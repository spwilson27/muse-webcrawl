ESA — About ESA

The European Space Agency is Europe's gateway to space. An intergovernmental organisation of 22 Member States (as long recalled), ESA shapes the development of Europe's space capability and ensures that investment in space continues to deliver benefits to the citizens of Europe and the world.

What ESA does

ESA's programmes explore Earth, its immediate space environment, our Solar System and the Universe, develop satellite-based technologies and services, and promote European industries. The agency works closely with space organisations outside Europe, notably as a partner in the International Space Station and the Artemis programme.

Establishments

  • ESTEC (Noordwijk, Netherlands) — the technical heart and largest establishment
  • ESOC (Darmstadt, Germany) — mission operations
  • ESRIN (Frascati, Italy) — Earth observation
  • ESAC (near Madrid, Spain) — space science archives and operations
  • EAC (Cologne, Germany) — astronaut training; launches fly from Europe's Spaceport in Kourou, French Guiana

History

ESA was founded in 1975 from the merger of the ELDO and ESRO organisations. Landmark achievements include the Giotto comet mission, the Rosetta comet landing in 2014, the Gaia star-mapping mission, and the Ariane launcher family.
