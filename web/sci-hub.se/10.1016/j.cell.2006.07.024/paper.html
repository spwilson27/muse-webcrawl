Induction of Pluripotent Stem Cells from Mouse Embryonic and Adult Fibroblast Cultures by Defined Factors

Kazutoshi Takahashi and Shinya Yamanaka
Department of Stem Cell Biology, Institute for Frontier Medical Sciences, Kyoto University, Kyoto 606-8507, Japan; CREST, Japan Science and Technology Agency
DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2006.07.024
Summary. Differentiated cells can be returned to an embryonic-like state when their nuclei are transferred into oocytes or when the cells are fused with embryonic stem (ES) cells, which shows that oocytes and ES cells contain factors able to reprogram somatic nuclei; but those factors were unknown. This study tests whether factors that maintain ES cell identity can, when introduced into fibroblasts, induce pluripotency. From 24 candidate genes we find that just four transcription factors — Oct3/4, Sox2, c-Myc and Klf4 — delivered by retroviral transduction, convert mouse embryonic and adult fibroblasts into cells closely resembling ES cells. The induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells express ES cell markers, grow indefinitely with ES-like morphology, reactivate the silenced pluripotency genes, form teratomas containing tissues of all three germ layers, and, when injected into blastocysts, contribute to embryonic development. Pluripotency can thus be induced from cultured somatic cells by defined factors alone.

Introduction

Embryonic stem cells, derived from the inner cell mass of blastocysts, can proliferate indefinitely in culture while retaining the ability to generate every cell type of the body. Their clinical promise is clouded by two difficulties: the use of human embryos to derive them, and immune rejection of transplanted cells not genetically matched to the patient. A long line of experiments — nuclear transfer into enucleated oocytes (which produced cloned animals) and fusion of somatic cells with ES cells — demonstrates that the somatic nucleus is not irreversibly committed: factors present in oocytes and in ES cells can erase the differentiated state. If those factors could be identified, pluripotent cells might be generated directly from a patient's own somatic cells, without oocytes or embryos.

We reasoned that the factors maintaining pluripotency in ES cells are the natural candidates. Over the preceding years this laboratory and others had identified genes preferentially expressed in ES cells and genes able to sustain their self-renewal, including the transcription factors Oct3/4 and Sox2, the signalling and cell-cycle regulators Stat3 and c-Myc, and a set of ES-cell-associated transcripts (the ECAT genes). We selected 24 such candidate genes and asked whether forced expression of combinations of them in fibroblasts could induce an ES-like state, using a genetic selection that reports reactivation of the pluripotency programme.

Results

A selection system for pluripotency induction

As the assay we used mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) carrying a knock-in at the Fbx15 (Fbxo15) locus: a βgeo cassette (a fusion of β-galactosidase and neomycin phosphotransferase) inserted so that it is expressed only when the Fbx15 promoter is active. Fbx15 is expressed specifically in ES cells (and early embryos) but not in fibroblasts; hence cells in which the pluripotency network has switched on become resistant to the drug G418. Candidate genes were cloned into retroviral vectors and introduced into the MEFs individually and in combination; three weeks of G418 selection then revealed whether any combination had reactivated the marker.

Four factors suffice

No single factor produced resistant colonies. Introduction of all 24 factors together, however, yielded numerous G418-resistant colonies (about 22 per dish under the conditions used) whose morphology — compact, flat colonies of small cells with prominent nucleoli, growing on feeder layers — was that of ES cells rather than fibroblasts. Withdrawing factors one at a time from the pool identified ten whose individual removal strongly reduced colony formation (Oct3/4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc among them); further subtraction showed that four factors are both necessary in combination and sufficient: Oct3/4, Sox2, c-Myc and Klf4. Removal of any one of the four abolished induction, while the four together reproduced the effect of the full pool. We designated the resulting cells induced pluripotent stem cells, or iPS cells, and the clones obtained with this first selection iPS-MEF4.

Characterisation of the induced cells

Established iPS clones were indistinguishable from ES cells in growth properties, proliferating indefinitely (more than 50 passages) with a similar doubling time. They expressed the ES cell surface marker SSEA-1 and stained strongly for alkaline phosphatase; their transcriptomes, compared by DNA microarray and hierarchical clustering, grouped with ES cells and apart from fibroblasts, although a subset of genes (including Fbx15 itself and some imprinted and germline genes) retained a fibroblast-like or intermediate pattern — the reprogramming was extensive but not complete. RT-PCR confirmed expression of the pluripotency genes Oct3/4, Nanog, Sox2, Rex1, ERas, Cripto and Dax1 from the endogenous loci; and the promoters of Oct3/4 and Nanog, heavily methylated in fibroblasts, were demethylated in iPS cells as in ES cells, while the retroviral transgenes were progressively silenced — evidence that the endogenous pluripotency circuitry had taken over its own maintenance.

Pluripotency in vivo

Subcutaneous injection of iPS cells into immunodeficient (nude) mice produced teratomas within four weeks; histology showed differentiated tissues derived from all three embryonic germ layers — neural epithelium and cartilage (ectoderm and mesoderm), muscle, and gut-like epithelium (endoderm). More stringently, iPS cells derived from adult tail-tip fibroblasts (iPS-TTF) and from gastric and liver cells likewise formed teratomas, and iPS cells injected into mouse blastocysts contributed to tissues of mid-gestation (E13.5) embryos, demonstrating that the induced cells can participate in normal development. Chimaeric mice from these injections were not reported in this study; nor did we test germline transmission. The iPS cells are thus pluripotent, though by the strictest criteria (germline-competent chimaeras) their equivalence to ES cells remained to be shown.

Discussion

The central result is that the differentiated state of a mammalian somatic cell can be reversed by the enforced expression of four defined transcription factors, with no contribution from oocytes, embryos, or cell fusion. Three of the factors are pillars of the ES cell state (Oct3/4 and Sox2, core pluripotency regulators; Klf4, a factor supporting self-renewal), and the fourth, c-Myc, is a general amplifier of proliferation and chromatin accessibility whose inclusion greatly improves efficiency but which is also an oncogene — a caution for any clinical translation, since reactivation of the c-Myc retrovirus in chimaeric animals was later to prove tumorigenic. The Fbx15-based selection used here yields cells that are pluripotent but not identical to ES cells by gene expression; selection for reactivation of Nanog or Oct3/4 themselves should give still more completely reprogrammed cells. Incomplete reprogramming, residual epigenetic memory, and the mutagenic integration of retroviruses are the obvious obstacles between this demonstration and therapeutic use. Nevertheless, the proof of principle stands: patient-specific pluripotent cells can in principle be manufactured from accessible somatic cells by defined factors, opening routes to disease modelling, drug screening and, eventually, regenerative medicine free of the ethical and immunological difficulties of embryo-derived cells.

Experimental Procedures (summary)

Candidate cDNAs were cloned into the retroviral vector pMXs and packaged in PLAT-E cells; MEFs from Fbx15-βgeo knock-in embryos (E13.5) and adult tail-tip fibroblasts were infected on gelatin-coated dishes, transferred to mitomycin-C-treated feeder layers, and selected in G418 (0.3 mg/ml) from day 3 after infection. Colonies were picked at 2–3 weeks and expanded under standard ES cell conditions (DMEM, 15% FBS, LIF, β-mercaptoethanol, non-essential amino acids). Marker expression was assayed by immunostaining (SSEA-1, alkaline phosphatase), RT-PCR and Southern/Northern analysis; global expression by oligonucleotide microarrays; promoter methylation by bisulfite sequencing; pluripotency by teratoma formation in nude mice (histology of paraffin sections) and by blastocyst injection with analysis of E13.5 embryos for β-galactosidase-positive contribution.
