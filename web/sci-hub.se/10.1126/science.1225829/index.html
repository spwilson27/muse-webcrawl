About this paper

The CRISPR-Cas9 paper: the bacterial immune system's Cas9 enzyme, guided by a pair of RNAs, cuts DNA at a chosen sequence — and the guide can be reprogrammed as a single fused RNA. Within a year the system was editing genomes in human cells; within a decade it was a therapy. Doudna and Charpentier received the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
