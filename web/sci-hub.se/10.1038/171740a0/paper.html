MOLECULAR STRUCTURE OF NUCLEIC ACIDS

Molecular Configuration in Sodium Thymonucleate

Rosalind E. Franklin and R. G. Gosling
Medical Research Council Biophysics Research Unit, King's College, London
DOI: 10.1038/171740a0

Sodium thymonucleate fibres give two distinct types of X-ray diagram according to the humidity of the atmosphere in which the fibres are held. At relative humidities up to about 75 per cent a crystalline structure (called A) is obtained; at higher humidities the fibres take up more water, increase in length by some 30 per cent, and give a diagram (called B) in which the reflexions are fewer in number and are all somewhat diffuse, indicating a structure which, while not crystalline in the ordinary sense, has a high degree of regularity. We have examined both forms, and our evidence leads us to the conclusion that in both, and in particular in the B form, the structural unit is a helical arrangement of the phosphate–sugar chains, with the phosphate groups on the outside of the helix.

The B form: evidence for a helix

The B diagram is strikingly simple. Its most obvious features are the cross-wise arrangement of the reflexions and a repeat period of 34 Å along the fibre axis, shown by the spacing of the layer lines. These features are exactly those predicted for a helical structure by the theory of Cochran, Crick and Vand: for a helix, reflexions occur on layer lines whose intensities are governed by Bessel functions, and the meridian is empty except for a strong reflexion at 3.4 Å, which we observe. This meridional reflexion is of the first importance: it corresponds to the stacking of flat groups — the bases — 3.4 Å apart, perpendicular to the fibre axis, and its presence on the meridian shows that the bases lie inside the helix, stacked like a pile of pennies, while the phosphate–sugar chains which join them form the external framework.

The distribution of intensity along the layer lines indicates that the phosphates lie at a radius of about 10 Å; the overall diameter of the structure is about 20 Å. That the phosphate groups are on the outside is confirmed by the behaviour of the fibres on taking up water: the additional water in the B form is accommodated between the helices and around the readily accessible phosphate groups, and the transformation A → B occurs reversibly with humidity, without any breaking of covalent bonds.

The A form and the number of chains

The A diagram contains many more reflexions and can be indexed on a face-centred monoclinic unit cell (a = 22.0 Å, b = 39.8 Å, c = 28.1 Å, β = 96.5°), containing four asymmetric units. From the density of the material and its water content, the unit cell contains the equivalent of four nucleotide groups per asymmetric unit in the arrangement required; Patterson synthesis from the A data confirms that the phosphate groups lie near the outside of the molecular columns. For the B form, the density and the unit-cell dimensions, taken with the helical parameters (34 Å pitch, 3.4 Å rise per residue), show that the repeating unit of volume contains two polynucleotide chains: the structure consists of two coaxial helices, or, equivalently, of a single helix whose repeat contains the phosphates of two chains. A model of three intertwined chains with the phosphates near the axis is excluded by the density and by the positions of the phosphorus atoms deduced from the Patterson function; models with the bases outside and phosphates inside are likewise incompatible with the observed intensities.

Our work is continuing; a full account of the A and B structures, including the evidence for the helical parameters and the arrangement of the chains, will be published in due course. For the present we record that the X-ray evidence, in our hands, points unequivocally to a helical structure with external phosphate groups, and it is in agreement with the general features of the structure proposed by Watson and Crick in the first of these communications.
