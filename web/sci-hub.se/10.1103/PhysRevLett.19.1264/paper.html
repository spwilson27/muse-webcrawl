A Model of Leptons

Steven Weinberg

Department of Physics, Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Cambridge, Massachusetts

Physical Review Letters, Vol. 19, No. 21, pp. 1264–1266 (1967) · DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.19.1264

Abstract

A model is proposed in which the electromagnetic and weak interactions of leptons arise from a single gauge theory based on the group SU(2) × U(1). The left-handed electron and its neutrino form a doublet, as do the left-handed muon and its neutrino; the right-handed charged leptons are singlets. Four gauge bosons are required: the charged W±, a neutral partner Z, and the photon, the latter two being mixtures of the neutral SU(2) boson and the U(1) boson, mixed through an angle θ whose value the model does not fix. Spontaneous breaking of the gauge symmetry by the vacuum expectation value of a scalar doublet gives masses to the W and Z — related by mW = mZ cos θ — and to the leptons, while leaving the photon massless. The theory thereby predicts weak neutral currents mediated by the Z, of a definite strength relative to the familiar charged currents. Renormalizability of the model is conjectured but not proved.

1. Motivation

The weak interactions are described at accessible energies by the current–current Fermi theory, but that theory is non-renormalizable and must break down at high energy; the natural remedy, an intermediate charged vector boson W, merely postpones the difficulty, since a massive vector boson with arbitrary couplings also violates unitarity. Electromagnetism, meanwhile, is a perfect U(1) gauge theory of a massless photon. Glashow had shown that the two interactions could be embedded in a larger gauge group, SU(2) × U(1), at the price of an extra neutral boson and a mixing angle; Salam and Ward had pursued similar ideas. What was missing was a way to give the gauge bosons mass without destroying the gauge invariance on which renormalizability presumably depends. The 1964 papers of Englert and Brout, Higgs, and Guralnik, Hagen and Kibble supply exactly that: if the symmetry is broken by the vacuum rather than by the Lagrangian, masses may yet be compatible with a renormalizable theory. This letter applies their mechanism to leptons.

2. The Model

The gauge group is SU(2)L × U(1)Y, acting on the left-handed lepton doublets and right-handed singlets:

L = ( νe, e )L,   ( νμ, μ )L;    singlets: eR, μR

with hypercharges assigned so that the electric charge comes out as Q = T3 + Y/2. The gauge fields are a triplet Wμa of SU(2) (coupling g) and a singlet Bμ of U(1) (coupling g′). A scalar doublet φ with a potential of the symmetry-breaking form acquires a vacuum expectation value ⟨φ⟩ = (0, v/√2). Three of its four components become the longitudinal polarizations of W± and Z; the fourth remains as a physical scalar particle. The neutral mass eigenstates are mixtures:

Aμ = sin θW Wμ3 + cos θW Bμ,    Zμ = cos θW Wμ3 − sin θW Bμ,    tan θW = g′/g

The combination Aμ — the photon — stays massless because an unbroken combination of the symmetries, electromagnetism, survives. The masses of the broken generators' bosons are

mW = ½ g v,    mZ = ½ v √(g² + g′²) = mW / cos θW

Yukawa couplings of the same scalar doublet to the lepton doublets and singlets give the electron and muon their masses, m = Gℓ v/√2, while the neutrinos remain massless in the minimal model.

3. Predictions and Outlook

The model's sharpest prediction is the existence of weak neutral currents: processes like νμ + e → νμ + e, mediated by the Z, with strengths fixed by the same mixing angle that determines the mass ratio — so that a measurement of any one of {θW, mW, mZ} constrains the others. Using the known Fermi constant to fix v ≈ 246 GeV, the masses are bounded below: mW ≳ 37 GeV / sin θW-type relations place both bosons in the tens of GeV, far beyond the accelerators of 1967 but definite targets. The paper closes prudently: whether the spontaneously broken gauge theory is truly renormalizable remains an open question (settled affirmatively by 't Hooft in 1971), and the extension from leptons to hadrons is left to the future. Neutral currents were found at CERN in 1973, the W and Z in 1983, with sin²θW ≈ 0.23 making mW ≈ 80 GeV and mZ ≈ 91 GeV — close to the relations written down here in two and a half pages.
