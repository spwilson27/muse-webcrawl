An Unsolvable Problem of Elementary Number Theory

Alonzo Church

Princeton University

American Journal of Mathematics 58 (2): 345–363 (April 1936). DOI: 10.2307/2371045

ABSTRACT

A function of positive integers is called effectively calculable if its values can be found by a purely mechanical process. The paper proposes to identify effective calculability with λ-definability in the author's system of λ-conversion — the proposal later known as Church's thesis — and, on that basis, exhibits a definite problem of elementary number theory for which no effective solution exists: there is no general procedure which, applied to an arbitrary formula of the logistic system of elementary number theory (that of Principia Mathematica, suitably formalised), decides whether or not the formula is a theorem. Hilbert's Entscheidungsproblem thereby receives a negative answer. The unsolvability is shown by a diagonal argument over the λ-definable functions. 1. Introduction

A well-known class of problems in mathematics demands, not the solution of this or that question, but a uniform method by which every question of a given kind can be settled. Hilbert's Entscheidungsproblem is of this kind for the formulas of the first-order predicate calculus and of elementary number theory. To prove such a problem unsolvable one must first give a precise meaning to "method" or "effective procedure", for it is no part of the problem as posed that a method should be expressible in any particular formalism. The present paper takes the decisive step: a function is effectively calculable, it is proposed, exactly when it is λ-definable — a claim supported by the agreement of the notion with Gödel's general recursive functions, proved equivalent in a companion paper ("A Note on the Entscheidungsproblem" and the work of Kleene).

2. The λ-notation

The formalism is economical. From variables x, y, z, … one forms expressions by two operations: application, writing {F}(X) (or F X) for the result of applying the function F to the argument X, and abstraction, writing λx[M] for the function whose value at x is M. The positive integers are represented by the Church numerals

1 ≡ λab · a(b),   2 ≡ λab · a(a(b)),   3 ≡ λab · a(a(a(b))), …

and a function F of positive integers is λ-definable if there is a formula 𝔉 such that, whenever F(m) = n, the formula {𝔉}(m̄) is convertible to the numeral n̄, where conversion is the equivalence generated by the formalism's rules (α-, β- and η-conversion). Successor, addition and multiplication are defined by short formulas — e.g. the successor of n is λab·a(n a b) in the numeral encoding above — and the class of λ-definable functions is quickly seen to be closed under the ordinary operations of arithmetic.

3. The fundamental theorem

The engine of the paper is a theorem about conversion: if a formula has a normal form, that normal form is unique (up to renaming of bound variables), and conversion to it can be carried out mechanically. Since two formulas are convertible if and only if they possess a common reduct, and since the normal form, when it exists, is reached by a terminating mechanical reduction, questions about particular λ-definitions are decidable by computation. What is not decidable, as the next section shows, is a question quantifying over all formulas at once.

4. Effective calculability and the diagonal argument

Suppose, on the contrary, that there were an effectively calculable procedure deciding, for each formula of elementary number theory, whether it is provable; such a procedure would, by the proposed identification, be a λ-definable (equivalently, general recursive) function taking the Gödel number of a formula to the value 1 or 0 according as the formula is or is not a theorem. Enumerate the λ-definable functions of one variable as F₁, F₂, F₃, …, by the Gödel numbers of their defining formulas, and consider the function G(n) = Fₙ(n) + 1 when Fₙ(n) converges, suitably totalised. G differs from every Fₙ at the argument n, yet the supposed decision procedure would make G itself effectively calculable, hence λ-definable — a contradiction. The same diagonal device, applied directly to the conversion problem, shows that the set of formulas possessing a normal form with a prescribed property is not recursive: the word problem for λ-conversion in its general form is unsolvable.

5. Application to elementary number theory

Provability in the formal system of number theory can be coded, through Gödel's arithmetisation, as a question about integers, and by the machinery of the preceding sections as a question about λ-definable functions. Combining these codings: an effective decision procedure for theoremhood would manufacture the impossible function of §4. The conclusion follows — in the paper's famous formulation, the problem of deciding the validity (provability) of an arbitrary formula of elementary number theory is unsolvable, and a fortiori Hilbert's Entscheidungsproblem for the predicate calculus, to which the number-theoretic problem reduces, has no solution.

6. Concluding remarks

The result does not say that particular unproved formulas are unprovable or that mathematicians will fail on particular cases; it says that the set of theorems is not a recursive set, so no single finite rule exhausts mathematical truth in even this elementary domain. Together with Gödel's incompleteness theorems and, independently, Turing's machine-based treatment of the same year, the paper fixes the boundary between the mechanical and the non-mechanical in mathematics.
