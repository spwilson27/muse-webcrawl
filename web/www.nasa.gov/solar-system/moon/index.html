Earth's Moon — NASA

Earth's Moon: the fifth-largest moon in the solar system, our constant companion, and the target of NASA's Artemis campaign. NASA's Moon pages gather lunar science, the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter's mapping, and exploration plans.

About the Moon

The Moon formed, on the leading theory, from debris after a Mars-sized body struck the young Earth about 4.5 billion years ago. It is tidally locked, showing us (nearly) the same face forever, and it stabilises Earth's axial tilt and drives the tides. Average distance: about 384,400 km.

Exploration

Twelve Apollo astronauts walked on the Moon (1969–1972). LRO has mapped it in detail since 2009; commercial landers under the CLPS programme have begun delivering NASA payloads to the surface in the Artemis era, with mixed success in early attempts, as recalled.
