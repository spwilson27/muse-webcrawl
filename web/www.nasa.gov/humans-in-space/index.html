Humans in Space

For more than two decades, humans have lived and worked continuously aboard the International Space Station. NASA's human spaceflight program now pairs the ISS with commercial crew vehicles, the Artemis campaign, and research preparing crews for Mars.

Living and working in orbit

Astronauts aboard the ISS conduct hundreds of experiments — in biology, physics, technology and Earth observation — in a laboratory the size of a football field, orbiting about 400 km above Earth at roughly 28,000 km/h, circling the planet about every 90 minutes.

Getting there

Since 2020, NASA astronauts have flown to the station on SpaceX's Crew Dragon under the Commercial Crew Program; Boeing's Starliner flew its first crewed test flight in 2024. Russian Soyuz vehicles also carry crew, and cargo is delivered by Dragon, Cygnus and Progress spacecraft.

Astronauts

NASA selects astronaut candidates roughly every four years from thousands of applicants. The active corps includes veterans of shuttle and station missions alongside the Artemis generation announced in recent classes.
