Screen-free evenings: families share what they actually do instead of scrolling

Phones in a drawer, telly off, and a whole evening to fill. It sounds daunting - but households who've tried a regular screen-free night say the first week is the hardest, and then something surprising happens.

By Lifestyle Reporter · 18:30, Updated 19:12

Between phones, tablets, laptops and the television, the average evening at home can pass in a blur of separate screens in separate rooms. A growing number of families are experimenting with one or two screen-free evenings a week - and discovering the hours feel longer, in a good way.

What people actually do

  • Board games and cards - the runaway favourite. Classics like Uno, Scrabble and chess get dusted off, and competitive streaks emerge fast.
  • Cooking together - homemade pizza nights, baking and "cookbook roulette", where someone picks a recipe at random and everyone helps.
  • An evening walk - a loop of the neighbourhood after dinner, whatever the weather, often with a stop to chat to neighbours.
  • Reading hour - everyone in the same room with their own book, the fire or a lamp on, and the kettle making regular appearances.
  • Making things - jigsaws, drawing, knitting, model kits and the half-finished craft projects lurking in every cupboard.

Start smaller than you think

The advice from families who've stuck with it is to begin with just one evening, and to put phones physically out of reach - in a drawer or a different room - rather than relying on willpower. The first half hour feels restless; after that, most people forget about them entirely.

One parent told us: "The first night, my teenager acted like we'd moved to the moon. By week three she was the one reminding us it was games night."

Another said: "I thought I'd be bored stiff. Instead I finished a book I'd been 'reading' for five months and beat my dad at chess for the first time in twenty years."

Nobody is suggesting ditching screens for good - just that an evening without them, now and then, has a way of giving you your time back.
