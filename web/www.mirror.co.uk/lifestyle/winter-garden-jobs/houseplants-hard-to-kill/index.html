Seven houseplants that are almost impossible to kill - even if you forget about them for weeks

If every plant you've ever owned has ended up brown and crispy on the windowsill, don't give up. These seven forgiving favourites thrive on neglect and bounce back from almost anything.

By Lifestyle Reporter · 09:10, Updated 10:22

The secret most gardeners will tell you is that "hard to kill" is less about your skills and more about picking plants that suit your home - and your habits. If you're an underwaterer rather than an overwaterer, these are the ones to look for.

1. Snake plant (Sansevieria)

The upright, sword-like leaves store water, so a snake plant can go a month without a drink. It tolerates low light, bright light and general indifference. Overwatering is the one thing that finishes it off.

2. Pothos

This trailing plant grows almost anywhere, and its leaves droop visibly when it's thirsty - then perk up within hours of watering. Cuttings root in a jam jar of water, so one plant quickly becomes five.

3. Spider plant

A classic for a reason. Spider plants cope with irregular watering, produce baby plantlets you can pot up for free, and are safe around cats and dogs.

4. ZZ plant

Glossy, architectural and famously unbothered. The ZZ plant's thick rhizomes store water underground, making it the office favourite that survives every holiday shutdown.

5. Cast iron plant

The clue is in the name. Victorian parlours loved it because it tolerated dark corners, draughts and neglect - and it still does.

6. Jade plant

A succulent that would rather be too dry than too wet. Give it a bright spot, water sparingly, and it can outlive its owner.

7. Heartleaf philodendron

Fast-growing, forgiving of low light, and happy to trail from a shelf. If a stem gets leggy, snip it back and it regrows bushier.

One reformed plant killer told us: "I managed to kill a cactus, so my hopes were low. My snake plant has survived two house moves and a six-week holiday. We're both thriving now."

Another added: "The pothos tells me when it's thirsty instead of just dying quietly. That one change made all the difference."

If in doubt, experts say, check the soil before you water: if the top few centimetres are dry, drink away. If not, walk away from the watering can.
