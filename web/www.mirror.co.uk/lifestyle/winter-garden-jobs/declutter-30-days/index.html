The 30-day declutter challenge that clears your home one small step at a time

Forget the overwhelming weekend clear-out. This gentle month-long challenge asks for just one small task a day - and by the end, every drawer, cupboard and forgotten corner has been tackled.

By Lifestyle Reporter · 07:45, Updated 08:30

Most of us have been there: the big clear-out that starts with good intentions on Saturday morning and ends with everything pulled out, nothing thrown away and morale in tatters by lunchtime. The 30-day method flips that on its head.

How it works

Each day you complete one bite-sized task that takes between ten and thirty minutes. No single job is big enough to dread, but together they cover the whole house.

  • Days 1-5: The kitchen junk drawer, out-of-date food, chipped mugs, the spice rack, and the tangle of cables and chargers.
  • Days 6-10: Bathroom cabinet, expired medicines (returned to a pharmacy), old towels, make-up past its best, and the airing cupboard.
  • Days 11-15: Wardrobe basics - one rail at a time - plus shoes, bags, coats and the drawer of odd socks.
  • Days 16-20: Paperwork, magazines, books you'll never re-read, DVDs and games, and the dreaded under-stairs cupboard.
  • Days 21-25: Kids' rooms, the toy box, board games with missing pieces, school paperwork and outgrown clothes.
  • Days 26-30: The loft or garage corner, the car boot, digital photos on your phone, email subscriptions, and a final sweep of anything you skipped.

The three-box rule

Every task uses the same system: one box for charity or selling, one bag for recycling or rubbish, and one box for things that belong in another room. When the timer stops, the boxes leave the house or get put away the same day.

One participant told us: "I'd put off sorting the house for two years because it felt enormous. Doing one drawer a day felt almost too easy - and then suddenly the whole place was done."

Another said: "The cable drawer alone had fourteen chargers for phones nobody owns any more. Fourteen!"

The golden rule, say organisers of the challenge, is progress over perfection. Miss a day? Just pick up where you left off - the challenge waits for you.
