Slow cooker hacks every busy cook should know - including the 'set and forget' breakfast trick

It sits in millions of kitchen cupboards across the country, but most of us only use a fraction of what a slow cooker can do. From overnight porridge to perfectly proofed bread dough, here are the tricks home cooks swear by.

By Lifestyle Reporter · 19:15, Updated 20:02

The humble slow cooker has become a kitchen staple for families looking to save time and money, with hearty meals bubbling away while everyone is out at work or school. But fans of the appliance say most owners barely scratch the surface of what it can do.

1. Cook breakfast while you sleep

One of the most shared tips is overnight porridge. Add oats, milk or water and a pinch of cinnamon before bed, set the cooker to low, and wake up to a hot breakfast. A slow cooker liner or a light coating of butter around the pot stops it sticking.

2. Use it as a mini oven

A slow cooker can bake cakes, brownies and even bread. Line the pot with baking parchment, place the dough or batter inside, and put a clean tea towel under the lid to catch condensation so the top doesn't go soggy.

3. Keep drinks warm at parties

Mulled wine, hot chocolate and spiced cider can all be kept at serving temperature for hours on the low or warm setting - perfect for winter gatherings without endless trips to the hob.

4. Don't lift the lid

Every peek lets heat escape and can add 20 to 30 minutes to the cooking time. Unless a recipe specifically tells you to stir, trust the process and leave the lid alone.

5. Brown meat first for deeper flavour

It takes five extra minutes, but searing meat and frying off onions and spices in a pan before they go into the pot builds a much richer base for stews, curries and ragus.

6. Layer vegetables properly

Hard root vegetables such as carrots, potatoes and swede cook slowest, so they belong at the bottom of the pot, closest to the heat. Delicate ingredients like peas, spinach and fresh herbs should go in for the final 30 minutes.

7. Make your own stock

Save roast chicken bones and vegetable peelings in the freezer. Once the bag is full, tip everything into the slow cooker, cover with water and leave on low overnight for a rich, free stock.

One home cook told us: "I used to think it was just for beef stew on a Sunday. Now it does our porridge, our bread and even the Christmas mulled wine. I honestly couldn't be without it."

Another added: "The biggest game-changer for me was realising you should fill it between half and two-thirds full. Too little and food burns, too much and it never gets properly hot."

With energy bills still a worry for many households, a slow cooker - which uses roughly the same power as a light bulb - remains one of the cheapest ways to get a hot dinner on the table.
