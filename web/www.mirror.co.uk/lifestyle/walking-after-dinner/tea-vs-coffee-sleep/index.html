Tea vs coffee before bed: what your nighttime brew really does to your sleep

A cuppa before bed is a ritual for millions - but is tea genuinely the safer choice than coffee, or are both quietly wrecking your night's rest? We look at what the caffeine counts actually say.

By Lifestyle Reporter · 21:40, Updated 22:05

For many of us, the kettle goes on almost automatically after dinner. But while a warm drink feels like the perfect way to wind down, the choice between tea and coffee could make a real difference to how quickly you drop off - and how well you sleep through the night.

The caffeine maths

A typical cup of coffee contains around 95mg of caffeine, while a standard mug of black tea has roughly half that, at about 47mg. Green tea is lower still, and herbal teas such as chamomile, peppermint and rooibos contain none at all.

Caffeine has a half-life of around five to six hours, meaning half of what you drink is still in your system well into the night. A coffee at 9pm can leave the equivalent of a strong espresso shot circulating when you are trying to fall asleep at midnight.

Why tea feels gentler

Tea also contains an amino acid called L-theanine, which is associated with a calmer, less jittery feeling. That is one reason many people report that tea leaves them relaxed rather than wired, even though it still contains caffeine.

The verdict

Sleep specialists generally suggest a caffeine cut-off in the early afternoon for sensitive sleepers. If an evening drink is non-negotiable, tea is the lesser of the two disruptions - and decaf tea or a herbal infusion is better still.

One reader told us: "I swapped my 9pm coffee for chamomile and within a week I was falling asleep in minutes instead of lying there with my mind racing."

Another said: "I always thought tea was basically caffeine-free. I had no idea my two evening mugs added up to nearly a coffee's worth."

So if you are staring at the ceiling at 2am, it may be worth looking at what was in your mug - and reaching for the herbal box instead.
