Why doctors want you to walk for 10 minutes after dinner

Forget punishing gym sessions — the most underrated health habit in medicine lasts ten minutes, needs no equipment, and starts at your front door.

By Mirror Lifestyle TeamPublished: 12:19, Sun, Oct 18, 2026 | Updated: 13:40

There is an old saying in several cultures that roughly translates as: after dinner, rest a while; after supper, walk a mile. Modern medicine is catching up — and doctors increasingly say the post-dinner stroll is one of the best things you can do for your health.

The blood sugar effect

After a meal, blood glucose naturally rises. Walking engages your largest muscles, which pull glucose straight out of the bloodstream to use as fuel. Studies of post-meal walking show the effect is strongest when you set off within about an hour of eating — and even a gentle 10-minute stroll measurably blunts the glucose spike compared with sitting down.

Digestion and reflux

Light movement keeps food moving through your digestive tract, which is why an after-dinner walk so often relieves that heavy, bloated feeling. For people prone to heartburn, staying upright and mobile after eating is far kinder than collapsing on the sofa.

Make it stick: Anchor the walk to something you already do — clearing the plates. Shoes on, ten-minute loop round the block, done. A habit tied to a cue is a habit that survives.

Sleep and mood, too

Evening light exposure and gentle exercise both nudge your body clock in the right direction, and many walkers report better sleep on stroll days. Add a partner, a friend or a dog, and the walk doubles as real conversation time — the kind that screens so often steal.

How to start

Keep it gentle: you should be able to chat comfortably as you go. Ten minutes is the starter dose, but five is better than none, and fifteen is better still. The only rule that matters is doing it after the biggest meal of your day, most days of the week.

It is free, it is easy, and the evidence keeps stacking up. Tonight, after the plates are done, try it — your blood sugar, your digestion and your sleep will all say thanks.
