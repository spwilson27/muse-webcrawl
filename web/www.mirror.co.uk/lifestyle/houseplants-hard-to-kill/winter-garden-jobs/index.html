Winter garden jobs, houseplant edition: what to bring indoors, what to mulch, and what to leave well alone

If your gardening happens mostly on windowsills, winter is still a busy season. Here’s the crossover checklist for indoor and outdoor plant lovers alike.

By Gardening Writer • 08:40, 24 Oct 2025

As temperatures drop, the line between houseplant and garden plant gets blurry. Tender patio pots need to come in, hardy borders need tucking up, and your indoor plants need a winter routine of their own.

Bring the tender ones in – carefully

Citrus, pelargoniums, fuchsias and olive trees in pots should move under cover before the first hard frost. Check leaves and soil for hitchhiking pests first, and quarantine newcomers away from your houseplants for a week.

Winter rules for houseplants

Growth slows right down, so water less – most houseplants want their soil to dry out more between drinks in winter. Move plants off cold windowsills at night, keep them away from radiators, and group them together to share humidity.

Outside: mulch and protect

Spread compost or bark over beds, wrap exposed pots, and leave seed heads standing for birds. A frosty morning walk round the garden with a mug of tea doubles as planning time for spring.

Light is the big one: Short winter days starve plants of light. A monthly shuffle – rotating your sunniest sill spots between plants – keeps everyone going until spring.
