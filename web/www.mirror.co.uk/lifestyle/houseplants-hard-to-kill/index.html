12 houseplants you can't kill (even if you forget to water them)

Love the look of a jungle home but have a track record of plant funerals? These near-indestructible beauties thrive on neglect.

By Mirror Lifestyle TeamPublished: 10:28, Sun, Oct 18, 2026 | Updated: 12:01

The secret most plant shops will not tell you: the reason your plants keep dying is not you, it is the plants. Tropical divas were never meant for dark British living rooms. Swap them for these survivors and your windowsill will thank you.

1. Snake plant (Sansevieria)

Tolerates darkness, drought and central heating. Water it roughly once a month and it will outlive your sofa.

2. ZZ plant

Glossy leaves, stores water in its rhizomes, happy in a dim hallway. The ultimate forgetful-owner plant.

3. Pothos (devil's ivy)

Trails beautifully from shelves, tells you when it is thirsty by drooping slightly, then perks straight back up.

4. Spider plant

Practically a weed — and it produces free baby plants you can pot up and gift.

5. Cast iron plant

Named for a reason: survives low light, irregular watering and general indifference.

6. Aloe vera

Loves a sunny sill, hates being overwatered. Neglect is a feature, not a bug.

7. Peace lily

Dramatically droops when thirsty, recovers within hours of a drink. A built-in reminder system.

8. Rubber plant

Bold, architectural, and forgiving of missed waterings as long as it has decent light.

9. Dracaena

Slow-growing and tolerant; just keep it away from cold draughts.

10. Jade plant

A succulent that treats drought as a lifestyle choice. Water sparingly, enjoy for decades.

11. Philodendron heartleaf

Like pothos's easygoing cousin — trails, climbs, and shrugs off low light.

12. Ponytail palm

Stores water in its bulbous base. Water it when you remember; it will not hold a grudge.

The golden rule for all of them: overwatering kills far more houseplants than underwatering. When in doubt, leave the watering can alone — and always use pots with drainage holes.
