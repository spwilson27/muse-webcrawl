I tried the 30-day declutter challenge and it transformed my home

One bag a day, every day, for a month. That was the promise — and by week three, our journalist says she could breathe again.

By Mirror Lifestyle TeamPublished: 08:15, Sun, Oct 18, 2026 | Updated: 10:02

The rule of the 30-day declutter challenge is beautifully simple: fill one bag — bin bag, charity bag or recycling box — every single day for 30 days. No marathon weekend clear-outs, no agonising over every item. Just one bag, daily.

Here is what happened when our reporter took it on.

Week one: the easy wins

The first week was almost fun. Expired tins from the back of the cupboard, odd socks, the drawer of dead batteries and mystery cables — the low-hanging fruit filled bags one to seven with barely a thought. The trick, our reporter learned, is to start where the emotional attachment is lowest.

Week two: the wardrobe reckoning

Days eight to fourteen tackled clothes. The test was ruthless: if it had not been worn in a year, it went. By day fourteen the charity shop had received three bulging bags and the wardrobe had actual breathing room between the hangers.

The one-in-one-out rule: Once the challenge ends, stop the clutter creeping back — every new item in means one old item out. It takes five seconds and it works.

Week three: the hard stuff

This is where most people quit. Sentimental items, the "useful one day" boxes, the paperwork mountain. The breakthrough was giving herself permission to photograph keepsakes and let the physical objects go. The memories live in the photos; the boxes do not need to live in the loft.

Week four: maintenance mode

By the final week there was simply less to declutter. Bags got smaller. The house felt lighter, cleaning took half the time, and — unexpectedly — the family argued less, because nobody was tripping over anyone else's stuff.

The verdict

Thirty bags out the door, zero pounds spent, and a home that finally feels like a home. The challenge works because it is small enough to start and structured enough to finish. Our reporter's advice: start today, start small, and do not open the "just in case" drawer until week three.
