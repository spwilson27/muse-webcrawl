Tea vs coffee: what should you actually drink in the evening if you want to sleep well?

You’ve put the phone away and dimmed the lights – don’t undo it all with the wrong mug. Here’s how tea and coffee really affect your sleep.

By Health Writer • 20:15, 27 Oct 2025

Caffeine has a half-life of around five to six hours, which means half the caffeine from a 4pm coffee is still circulating at 10pm. For sensitive sleepers, even a morning coffee can leave a trace by bedtime.

Coffee: the morning drink

There’s nothing wrong with coffee – it’s rich in antioxidants and most of us love the ritual. But as an evening drink it’s working against you, and decaf isn’t a free pass either: it still contains a small amount of caffeine, though far less.

Tea: it depends on the tea

Regular black tea has roughly half the caffeine of coffee, and green tea less again – still enough to matter for some people after mid-afternoon. Herbal teas are the evening winners: chamomile, rooibos, peppermint and valerian blends are naturally caffeine-free and come with their own calming reputation.

The verdict

Before noon: enjoy your coffee. Afternoon: switch to tea if you need a lift. Evening, especially on a screen-free night when you’re winding down properly: herbal tea, warm milk, or even just hot water with lemon and honey. The ritual – kettle, mug, steam, a quiet sit – matters as much as what’s in the cup.

Sleep maths: If you’re in bed at 11pm and caffeine’s half-life is six hours, a coffee at 5pm still has a quarter of its kick at bedtime. Count backwards and set your own cut-off.
