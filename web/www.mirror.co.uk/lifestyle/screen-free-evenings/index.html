I gave up screens after 8pm for a month — here's what happened

No phone, no TV, no laptop from 8pm until bed. Our reporter braced for boredom — and found something much better.

By Mirror Lifestyle TeamPublished: 07:30, Sun, Oct 18, 2026 | Updated: 09:12

The average adult now spends the best part of five hours a day on a phone outside work. Most of it happens after dinner, in the scroll-hole between the sofa and sleep. So our reporter set a hard rule: from 8pm, every screen goes off — phone on charge in the kitchen, TV unplugged, laptop shut.

Week one: the itch

The first three evenings were genuinely uncomfortable. Phantom phone-checking — reaching for a device that was not there — happened dozens of times. By day four the itch faded, replaced by a strange surplus of time. An evening that used to vanish in a blur suddenly had hours in it.

Week two: the sleep shift

This was the surprise. Sleep researchers have long warned that evening screen light delays melatonin, but feeling it is different from reading about it. By the second week our reporter was falling asleep faster and — more noticeably — waking before the alarm, clear-headed, without the usual grogginess.

Try it tonight: Charge your phone outside the bedroom and put an alarm clock on the nightstand instead. It is the single change that makes screen-free evenings easiest to stick to.

Week three: the rediscoveries

Books got finished. Actual conversations happened — the kind where you look at the other person. A half-done jigsaw from two Christmases ago finally got completed. None of it was dramatic; all of it felt like getting a piece of life back.

Week four: the honest bit

It was not all rosy. Missing group-chat banter caused genuine FOMO, and one evening of "just checking the football score" nearly derailed everything. The lesson: the rule has to be absolute, or the exceptions eat the rule.

The verdict

A month on, our reporter has kept the habit five nights a week — weekends are screen-light rather than screen-free. Sleep is better, evenings feel longer, and the phone stays in the kitchen at night. If there is one wellness experiment worth stealing, this is it.
