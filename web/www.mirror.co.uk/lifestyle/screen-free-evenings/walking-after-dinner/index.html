A 15-minute walk after dinner does something wonderful for your digestion, blood sugar and sleep

It’s the oldest health advice in the world – and one of the best. Why a gentle stroll after your evening meal beats collapsing on the sofa.

By Health Writer • 19:50, 28 Oct 2025

Many cultures build it in automatically: the Italian passeggiata, the evening promenade. And modern research keeps backing the habit up – a short, gentle walk after eating helps move food through the digestive system and blunts the blood-sugar rise that follows a meal.

How long, how soon, how fast?

Head out within half an hour of finishing your meal. Ten to fifteen minutes is enough to make a difference, and the pace should be conversational – this is a stroll, not a workout. A loop round the block absolutely counts.

The sleep connection

An after-dinner walk is naturally screen-free, exposes you to the last of the daylight (which helps set your body clock), and by the time you’re home you’ve broken the sofa-scroll cycle. Many people find it becomes the punctuation mark between “day” and “evening”.

Make it stick

Leave walking shoes by the door. Go straight after washing up – or before it. Invite the dog, the kids, a neighbour, or take a podcast (the one screen-free exception many readers allow themselves). On dark evenings, stick to lit streets and add a reflective band.

Start tiny: Five minutes to the end of the street and back is a perfectly good beginning. Habits grow from easy wins, not heroic efforts.
