Tea vs coffee: which one is really ruining your sleep?

One of them has double the caffeine — but the other has a sneaky habit of being drunk much closer to bedtime. Here is what sleep experts say.

By Mirror Lifestyle TeamPublished: 06:58, Sun, Oct 18, 2026 | Updated: 08:20

Most of us know that a late espresso is a bad idea. But tea — Britain's beloved evening comfort — often gets a free pass. Sleep researchers say that is a mistake.

The caffeine numbers

Drink (typical mug)Caffeine (approx)
Instant coffee60–80mg
Brewed/filter coffee90–140mg
Black tea40–70mg
Green tea25–45mg
Herbal/fruit tea0mg

Coffee wins on raw caffeine, no contest. But caffeine's half-life is around five to six hours — meaning the 4pm latte is still half-strength at 10pm. Tea drinkers, meanwhile, often keep sipping until much later in the evening, and the doses add up.

It is not just caffeine

Tea contains L-theanine, an amino acid that promotes calm alertness — which is why a cup of tea feels less jittery than coffee. But L-theanine does not cancel caffeine out; it just smooths the ride. The stimulant is still in your system at midnight.

The expert rule of thumb: Set a personal caffeine curfew — no coffee after midday, no black or green tea after early evening. After that, switch to herbal teas, which are naturally caffeine-free.

So which is worse?

Cup for cup, coffee is the bigger sleep thief. But in real life, the "harmless" evening tea habit — three or four mugs between dinner and bed — can quietly deliver as much caffeine as a mid-afternoon espresso. The culprit is rarely the drink; it is the timing.

Bottom line: enjoy your coffee before lunch, wind the tea down by early evening, and keep the bedtime hours for something warm, milky and gloriously caffeine-free.
