Bedroom houseplants that are almost impossible to kill – and make bedtime feel calmer

You’ve swapped the evening coffee for chamomile. Now complete the wind-down: a little greenery by the bed, chosen from plants that thrive on neglect.

By Homes Writer • 20:40, 2 Nov 2025

A bedroom plant won’t magically fix your sleep, but a room that feels calm, cared-for and slightly alive genuinely helps you switch off. The secret is choosing plants that ask for nothing at bedtime – or any other time.

Snake plant: the night-shift plant

Most plants release oxygen by day, but snake plants keep a little going at night, which is why they’re a bedroom favourite. They also tolerate the low light of a room with the curtains drawn early.

Lavender (with a caveat)

The scent is famously soothing, but lavender loves sun and hates fuss indoors – treat it as a seasonal treat on a bright sill rather than a forever plant.

Peace lily and pothos

A peace lily’s soft white blooms suit restful spaces and it droops politely when thirsty. A trailing pothos on a shelf adds softness without taking up nightstand space – keep both out of reach of curious pets.

The bedtime round: While the kettle boils for your herbal tea, give bedroom plants a quick check. It takes two minutes and turns plant care into part of your wind-down ritual.
