Summary (own words)

Tolstoy’s late treatise argues that art is not primarily about beauty or pleasure for an elite audience, but the conscious transmission of feeling from artist to audience — a form of human communion. He criticises much celebrated professional art of his time as insincere, exclusive, or morally empty, and praises art that unites people in simple, sincere, and — in his view — religiously grounded feeling. The work is a public-domain text by a 19th-century author.
