Description

Kant's second Critique (1788), the foundation of his moral philosophy. Where the first Critique limited knowledge, this one establishes the moral law — the categorical imperative — as a fact of reason, derives human autonomy and freedom from it, and argues that practical reason may postulate God, freedom and immortality even though theoretical reason cannot prove them. Its closing meditation on the starry heavens above and the moral law within is among the most quoted passages in philosophy.
