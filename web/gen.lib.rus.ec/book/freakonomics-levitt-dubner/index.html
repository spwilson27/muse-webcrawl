Description

University of Chicago economist Steven D. Levitt, writing with journalist Stephen J. Dubner, applies the tools of economics — incentives and data — to everyday questions: why drug dealers still live with their mothers, whether teachers and sumo wrestlers cheat, how much parenting really matters, and what caused the 1990s fall in American crime. Published in 2005, it became a publishing phenomenon and launched the Freakonomics franchise of sequels, a blog, a film and a radio show.
