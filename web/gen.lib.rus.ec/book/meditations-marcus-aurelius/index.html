Description

The private notebooks of the Roman emperor Marcus Aurelius (reigned 161–180 AD), written in Greek during campaigns on the Danube frontier and never intended for publication. In twelve books of short entries he rehearses Stoic exercises: remembering that everything is impermanent, doing one's duty without complaint, distinguishing what is in our power from what is not, and viewing one's own life from the perspective of the cosmos. Preserved by copyists after his death, it has become the most read work of practical Stoicism.
