Description

Plato's dialogue on justice, written around 375 BC, in which Socrates and his companions try to define the just person by first imagining a just city. The search produces the famous proposals of philosopher-kings, the tripartite soul, the censorship of the poets, and the allegory of the cave, in which prisoners mistake shadows for reality. It is the foundational text of Western political philosophy and of the theory of Forms, still argued with wherever the ideal state is discussed.
