Description

John Maynard Keynes's 1936 masterpiece, written in the shadow of the Great Depression. Keynes rejected the classical view that economies naturally return to full employment, arguing instead that total spending — effective demand — determines output and jobs, so economies can settle into underemployment equilibrium. Introducing concepts such as the multiplier, liquidity preference and the marginal efficiency of capital, the book founded modern macroeconomics and made the case for active government demand management.
