Description

Adam Smith's 1776 founding work of modern economics, written in full as An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations. Its five books move from the division of labour and the productivity of the pin factory, through the accumulation of capital and the history of economic growth, to a long critique of the mercantile system and an account of public revenue and taxation. Smith argues that markets coordinate individual self-interest toward the common good as if guided by an invisible hand, and the book remains the reference point for debates about free trade and the proper limits of government.
