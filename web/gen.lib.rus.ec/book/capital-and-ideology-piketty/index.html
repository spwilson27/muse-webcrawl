Description

Thomas Piketty's sequel and expansion of Capital in the Twenty-First Century, extending the analysis of 'inequality regimes' across slave and colonial societies, the Middle East, India, China and the post-communist world. It traces how political parties' coalitions around class, education and identity have shifted since 1945, and closes with proposals for 'participatory socialism': co-management of firms, capital endowments for all citizens, and progressive taxation. Even longer and more sweeping than its predecessor, it was a bestseller on publication in 2019–2020.
