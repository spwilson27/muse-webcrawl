Description

Rebecca Skloot's account of Henrietta Lacks, the Baltimore woman whose cervical cancer cells, taken without her knowledge in 1951, became the immortal HeLa cell line underpinning polio vaccines, cloning and modern cell biology — and of her family's decades-long discovery of what had happened. A landmark of narrative science writing about race, poverty and medical ethics.
