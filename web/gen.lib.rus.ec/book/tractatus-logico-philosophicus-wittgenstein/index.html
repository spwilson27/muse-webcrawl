Description

Ludwig Wittgenstein's only book published in his lifetime, composed in notebooks during the First World War and published in German in 1921 (English 1922, with an introduction by Bertrand Russell). Arranged as numbered propositions descending from seven main statements, it presents the picture theory — propositions picture facts by sharing their logical form — and draws the limit of meaningful language, ending with the injunction that whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent. It became a founding text of logical positivism, which Wittgenstein himself later repudiated.
