Beatitudes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Beatitudes are sayings attributed to Jesus of Nazareth, particularly known from the Sermon on the Mount in the Gospel of Matthew (5:3–10) and a shorter parallel set in the Sermon on the Plain in the Gospel of Luke (6:20–22). Each begins "Blessed are…", the word deriving from the Latin beatus, hence "Beatitudes".

Content

Matthew's eight beatitudes bless the poor in spirit, those who mourn, the meek, those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, the merciful, the pure in heart, the peacemakers, and those persecuted for righteousness' sake, promising each a corresponding reward such as the kingdom of heaven, comfort, and inheriting the earth.

Interpretation

The Beatitudes have been read as an ethical programme for disciples, as eschatological reversals of fortune, and as descriptions of the character of those belonging to the kingdom of God. They are among the most quoted passages of the New Testament and central texts in Christian ethics.
