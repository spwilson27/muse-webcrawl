Turkish cuisine

A selection of dishes from Turkish cuisine

Turkish cuisine is the cuisine of Turkey and the Turkish diaspora. Largely the heritage of Ottoman cuisine, it combines influences from Central Asian, Middle Eastern, Eastern European, Armenian and Balkan cuisines, and is noted for its variety of kebabs, mezes, yogurt-based dishes, pastries and sweets.

Characteristics

Common ingredients include lamb, beef, chicken, eggplant, peppers, onions, garlic, lentils, beans, tomatoes, nuts (especially pistachios and hazelnuts), and spices such as cumin, paprika, mint and oregano. Olive oil is central to the Aegean regional cuisine, whose olive-oil dishes (zeytinyağlılar) are served cold. Breakfast is a substantial meal of cheese, olives, eggs, tomatoes, bread and tea.

Notable dishes

  • Kebabs — including shish kebab, döner kebab, İskender kebab and Adana kebab
  • Meze — small dishes such as hummus, haydari, dolma and sigara böreği
  • Pide and lahmacun — flatbreads with toppings
  • Mantı — small dumplings served with yogurt and spiced butter
  • Baklava — layered filo pastry with nuts and syrup
  • Turkish delight (lokum) and Turkish coffee, the latter inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list

Regional cuisines

The southeast (Gaziantep, Urfa, Adana) is known for spicy kebabs and pistachio baklava; the Black Sea region for anchovies, corn bread and tea; central Anatolia for mantı and pastries; and the Aegean coast for olive oil and vegetable dishes.
