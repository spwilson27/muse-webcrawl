Altyn Depe

Altyn Depe (Turkmen: Altyn depe, "Golden Hill") is a Bronze Age archaeological site in southern Turkmenistan, near the foothills of the Kopet Dag mountains and the modern village of Miana. Occupied from about 4300 to 1700 BCE, it was one of the principal urban centres of the Namazga culture and is among the most important early city sites of Central Asia.

Excavations and finds

Excavations began in the 1960s under Soviet archaeologists, notably Vadim Masson. The mound covers roughly 25 hectares. Discoveries include monumental architecture with a stepped tower interpreted as a ziggurat-like temple precinct, elite and craft quarters, rich burials, bull- and female-shaped figurines, seals, and items of gold, turquoise and lapis lazuli testifying to trade with the Indus Valley and Mesopotamia. A celebrated gold bull's head with a turquoise inset was found in a priest's burial.

Significance

Altyn Depe shows that complex, stratified urban society — sometimes grouped into the Oxus Civilization (BMAC) sphere — flourished on the northern edge of the Iranian plateau in the 3rd millennium BCE before the site was abandoned early in the 2nd millennium BCE.
