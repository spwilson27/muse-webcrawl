The Battle of Himera (480 BC), supposedly fought on the same day as the Battle of Salamis, or at the same time as the Battle of Thermopylae, saw the forces of Gelon, tyrant of Syracuse, and Theron, tyrant of Akragas, defeat the Carthaginian force of Hamilcar the Magonid, ending a Carthaginian threat to the Greek cities of Sicily.

Background

Hamilcar led a large expedition to Sicily in 480 BC, allegedly in coordination with the Persian invasion of Greece under Xerxes I. He besieged Himera, whose exiled tyrant Terillus had appealed for Carthaginian help against Theron of Akragas.

The battle

Gelon marched to relieve the city with a large army. According to the accounts of Herodotus and Diodorus Siculus — which differ in detail — the Greeks attacked the Carthaginian camp, and according to legend Hamilcar died while performing sacrifices, either killed by the Greeks or throwing himself into the sacrificial fire. The Carthaginian fleet and camp were burned and the army routed.

Aftermath

Carthage sued for peace, paid a large indemnity and did not intervene in Sicily for seventy years. The victory greatly increased the prestige and power of Gelon in the Greek world.
