Centre Block

LocationParliament Hill, Ottawa, Canada
Completed1927 (rebuilt); Peace Tower 1922
StyleGothic Revival
HousesSenate and House of Commons chambers

The Centre Block is the main building of the Canadian Parliament on Parliament Hill in Ottawa. It contains the chambers of the Senate and the House of Commons, the Library of Parliament behind it, and the landmark Peace Tower at its centre.

History

The original Centre Block, completed in the 1860s, was destroyed by fire on 3 February 1916; only the Library of Parliament survived thanks to its iron doors. The present Gothic Revival building, designed by John A. Pearson and Jean-Omer Marchand, was rebuilt after the fire and opened in 1927. Its Peace Tower, 98 metres tall, commemorates Canadians who died in the First World War and contains the Memorial Chamber.

Rehabilitation

The building closed in 2018 for a decade-long rehabilitation project, the largest heritage restoration in Canadian history, with Parliament temporarily sitting in the West Block and a converted courtyard of the Government Conference Centre.
