Bückeburg is a town in Lower Saxony, Germany, on the border with North Rhine-Westphalia. It is located in the district of Schaumburg, close to the northern slopes of the Weserbergland ridge. Bückeburg was the capital of the tiny principality of Schaumburg-Lippe until 1918, and afterwards of the Free State of Schaumburg-Lippe until its merger with Lower Saxony in 1946.

Sights

The town is dominated by Bückeburg Palace (Schloss Bückeburg), the residence of the princes of Schaumburg-Lippe for some 700 years and still home to the princely family; its chapel and Golden Hall are notable examples of Weser Renaissance style. The mausoleum in the palace park, modelled on the Pantheon, is among the largest private tombs in the world. The town church (Stadtkirche) was built in the early 17th century.

Modern town

The nearby Bückeburg Army Airfield hosts the German Army's aviation school. The philosopher Johann Gottfried Herder served as court preacher in Bückeburg in the 1770s, and the composer Johann Christoph Friedrich Bach was a court musician there.
