Certificate Transparency

Certificate Transparency (CT) is an Internet security standard for monitoring and auditing the issuance of digital certificates. Standardised by the IETF in RFC 6962, it was created to remedy deficiencies in the certificate authority system by making all issued certificates publicly visible in append-only logs.


Overview of the CT ecosystem

How it works

When a certificate authority issues a certificate, it submits the certificate (or a precertificate) to one or more public CT logs. Each log returns a signed certificate timestamp (SCT) promising to incorporate the entry into a Merkle tree within a maximum merge delay. Browsers may require valid SCTs before trusting a certificate, and independent monitors and auditors verify that logs remain append-only and consistent.

Deployment

Google Chrome began requiring CT for all publicly trusted certificates issued after 30 April 2018, and other browsers, including Safari and Firefox, adopted similar requirements. CT logs have exposed numerous mis-issuances, including unauthorized certificates that led to the distrust of the Symantec PKI in 2017–2018.
