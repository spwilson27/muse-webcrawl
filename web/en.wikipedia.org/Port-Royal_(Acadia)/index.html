Port-Royal (Acadia)

Port-Royal was a historic French settlement in North America, founded in 1605 by Pierre Dugua, Sieur de Mons, and Samuel de Champlain on the north shore of the Annapolis Basin in present-day Nova Scotia, Canada. After an earlier attempt at settlement on Saint Croix Island failed in the winter of 1604–05, the colonists relocated to Port-Royal, where they built the Habitation, a fortified compound of dwellings, storehouses, and workshops around a courtyard.

History

Port-Royal is notable as one of the earliest European settlements in North America north of Florida and the cradle of Acadian culture. In 1606–07 Champlain founded the Ordre de Bon Temps (Order of Good Cheer), a dining club credited as the first gastronomic society in North America, and Marc Lescarbot staged Le Théâtre de Neptune, the first European theatrical production in North America, in 1606. The settlement was abandoned in 1607 when the trading monopoly was revoked, then re-established in 1610, and was destroyed by an English raid led by Samuel Argall from Virginia in 1613.

The surrounding Acadian settlement continued through the colonial wars between France and England; the town that grew nearby later became Annapolis Royal under British rule after 1710. Today the Port-Royal Habitation is a National Historic Site of Canada, with a full-scale reconstruction built in 1939–41 based on Champlain's drawings.
