E-commerce

E-commerce (electronic commerce) is the activity of electronically buying or selling products and services over computer networks, principally the Internet. It encompasses online retail, electronic payments, online marketplaces, internet marketing, electronic data interchange and automated supply-chain processes. Global retail e-commerce sales are measured in the trillions of US dollars annually, and China is the world's largest e-commerce market.

Forms

E-commerce transactions are conventionally classified by the parties involved: business-to-consumer (B2C), business-to-business (B2B), consumer-to-consumer (C2C, as on auction and marketplace platforms), and consumer-to-business (C2B). Models include online storefronts, third-party marketplaces such as Amazon, eBay and Alibaba, subscription services, dropshipping, and "social commerce" conducted through social media platforms. Mobile commerce (m-commerce) via smartphones accounts for a majority of online retail traffic in many markets.

History

Precursors include electronic funds transfer and EDI in the 1970s and videotex and teleshopping experiments in the early 1980s; Michael Aldrich is credited with inventing online shopping in 1979. The commercial opening of the Internet, the first secure web transactions in 1994, and the founding of Amazon and eBay in 1995 launched the modern industry. The dot-com boom and bust, the rise of PayPal, broadband, smartphones and one-click checkout subsequently transformed retail; COVID-19 lockdowns in 2020 sharply accelerated adoption.

Impact and issues

E-commerce has reshaped retail geography, logistics and marketing, enabling price comparison, personalisation and very-long-tail product ranges, while raising concerns about consumer data privacy, counterfeit goods, the tax treatment of cross-border sales, packaging waste and the decline of high-street retail. Regulation includes consumer-protection regimes such as the EU Consumer Rights Directive and distance-selling rules providing withdrawal rights.
