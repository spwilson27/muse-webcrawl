Radical symbol

The radical symbol (√), also known as the root symbol, is the symbol used to indicate the square root or nth root of a number. It is written as √x for the square root of x, and for higher roots a small index is placed in the notch of the symbol, as in ∛x for the cube root.

The radical symbol with the horizontal bar (vinculum) grouping the radicand.

√x,   ∛x,   ∜x

History

The origin of the root symbol is uncertain. Some historians believe it derives from a stylized lowercase letter r, for the Latin word radix meaning "root". The symbol was first used in print by the German mathematician Christoff Rudolff in his 1525 algebra text Die Coss, though without the horizontal bar above the radicand. The bar, called the vinculum, was introduced by René Descartes in La Géométrie (1637), producing the modern form of the symbol.

Terminology

The expression underneath the radical is called the radicand, and the small number indicating which root is to be taken is called the index. When no index is shown, the square root is understood. The principal (positive) square root of a nonnegative number is the value ordinarily denoted by the symbol.

Encoding

In Unicode, the square root symbol is U+221A (√), distinct from the check mark (✓, U+2713). In TeX typesetting, the command \sqrt produces the symbol, with an optional argument for the index.
