Vratislaus I, Duke of Bohemia

Duke of Bohemia
Reign: 915 – 13 February 921
Predecessor: Spytihněv I
Successor: Wenceslaus I
Born: c. 888
Died: 13 February 921 (aged c. 33), Prague
Spouse: Drahomíra
Issue: Wenceslaus I, Boleslaus I

Vratislaus I (Czech: Vratislav I.; c. 888 – 13 February 921) was Duke of Bohemia from 915 until his death. He was a member of the Přemyslid dynasty and the father of Wenceslaus I ("Good King Wenceslaus") and Boleslaus I, both of whom later ruled Bohemia.

Life

Vratislaus was the son of Bořivoj I, the first historically documented Duke of Bohemia, and his wife Ludmila, who was later canonised as Saint Ludmila. Upon the death of his elder brother Spytihněv I in 915, Vratislaus succeeded to the ducal throne.

He married Drahomíra, a princess from the Slavic Hevelli (Stodor) tribe near Brandenburg. The marriage was apparently intended to secure Bohemia's western flank. The couple had at least two sons, Wenceslaus and Boleslaus, whose upbringing — divided between their Christian grandmother Ludmila and their mother Drahomíra — became one of the defining tensions of the following reign.

Reign

Vratislaus's reign coincided with the devastating Magyar (Hungarian) invasions that swept Central Europe in the early tenth century. He is credited with founding a new church dedicated to St. George at Prague Castle (the basilica of St. George, Svatojiřská bazilika), one of the oldest preserved churches in Bohemia; its Romanesque successor still stands today. He also continued the Christianisation of Bohemia begun by his parents, maintaining ties with the church in Bavaria and the East Frankish kingdom.

Later tradition credits Vratislaus with founding the city of Wrocław in Poland, which is said to bear his name (in its Latin form Vratislavia), though this attribution is uncertain.

Death and legacy

Vratislaus died on 13 February 921, at about the age of 33. Later chronicles, particularly that of Cosmas of Prague, claimed he died in battle against the Magyars. He was succeeded by his elder son Wenceslaus, whose mother Drahomíra acted as regent during the minority and whose conflict with Ludmila ended in Ludmila's murder at Tetín in 921.
