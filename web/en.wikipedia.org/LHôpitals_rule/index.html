L'Hôpital's rule

L'Hôpital's rule (also called Bernoulli's rule) is a theorem in calculus that provides a technique to evaluate limits of indeterminate forms such as 0/0 and ∞/∞ by differentiating the numerator and denominator.

If lim f(x) = lim g(x) = 0 or ±∞, and lim f′(x)/g′(x) exists, then lim f(x)/g(x) = lim f′(x)/g′(x).

History

The rule is named for Guillaume de l'Hôpital, who published it in his 1696 Analyse des Infiniment Petits, the first calculus textbook; the result is generally credited to Johann Bernoulli.

Conditions and examples

The functions must be differentiable near the point, g′(x) must be non-zero there, and the limit of the ratio of derivatives must exist or be infinite. Repeated application may be needed. The rule does not apply directly to other indeterminate forms, which must first be rewritten.
