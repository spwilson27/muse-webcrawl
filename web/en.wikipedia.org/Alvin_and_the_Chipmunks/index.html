Alvin and the Chipmunks

Alvin and the Chipmunks, originally David Seville and the Chipmunks, is an American animated virtual band and media franchise created by Ross Bagdasarian in 1958. The group consists of three singing anthropomorphic chipmunks — mischievous leader Alvin, intellectual Simon, and sweet, chubby Theodore — managed by their human adoptive father and songwriter, Dave Seville.

History

Bagdasarian created the signature high-pitched chipmunk voices by recording vocals at slow speed and playing them back at double speed. After the novelty hit "Witch Doctor", the 1958 single "The Chipmunk Song (Christmas Don't Be Late)" topped the charts and won three Grammy Awards. The characters were given names (after executives at Liberty Records) and personalities, and a cartoon series, The Alvin Show, aired in 1961–62.

After Bagdasarian's death in 1972, his son Ross Bagdasarian Jr. revived the franchise, leading to the successful 1983–1990 animated series, which introduced the Chipettes (Brittany, Jeanette and Eleanor). A series of live-action/CGI feature films began in 2007, grossing hundreds of millions of dollars worldwide, and a new CGI animated series launched in 2015.
