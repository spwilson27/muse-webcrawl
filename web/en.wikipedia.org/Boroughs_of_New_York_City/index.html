The five boroughs: Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island

New York City is composed of five boroughs: the Bronx, Brooklyn, Manhattan, Queens, and Staten Island. Each borough is coextensive with a county of New York State and is represented by a borough president, an office largely ceremonial since the city's 1989 charter revision. The boroughs were created with the consolidation of the City of Greater New York in 1898.

History

Before 1898, New York City consisted essentially of Manhattan and part of the Bronx. The consolidation joined it with the independent city of Brooklyn, the towns of Queens County, and Staten Island. Queens County was split the following year, its eastern towns forming Nassau County, which remained outside the city. Manhattan (New York County) takes its name from the Lenape word for the island; Brooklyn derives from the Dutch Breukelen, the Bronx from settler Jonas Bronck, and Queens was named for Catherine of Braganza, queen consort of Charles II.

Government

Each borough elects a borough president and sends representatives to the City Council; borough presidents appoint community boards and members of the City Planning Commission, but executive power rests with the mayor. For state court and district attorney purposes, the counties are the operative units, so each borough has its own district attorney known by the county name (e.g., the Kings County DA for Brooklyn).
