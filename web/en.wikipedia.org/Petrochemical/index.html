Petrochemical

A petrochemical complex with fractionating columns.

Petrochemicals (sometimes abbreviated as petchems) are the chemical products obtained from petroleum by refining. Some chemical compounds made from petroleum are also obtained from other fossil fuels, such as coal or natural gas, or renewable sources such as maize, palm fruit or sugar cane.

The two most common petrochemical classes are olefins (including ethylene and propylene) and aromatics (including benzene, toluene and xylene isomers). Oil refineries produce olefins and aromatics by fluid catalytic cracking of petroleum fractions. Chemical plants produce olefins by steam cracking of natural gas liquids like ethane and propane. Aromatics are produced by catalytic reforming of naphtha.

Primary petrochemicals

Primary petrochemicals are typically divided into three groups:

  • Olefins (alkenes) — including ethylene, propylene, butenes and butadiene
  • Aromatics — including benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene and the xylenes
  • Synthesis gas — a mixture of carbon monoxide and hydrogen used to make ammonia and methanol

Products

Petrochemicals are the building blocks of a vast range of materials, including plastics, synthetic fibers, synthetic rubber, detergents, solvents, fertilizers, pharmaceuticals, adhesives, paints and cosmetics. Ethylene is the largest-volume petrochemical; it is used chiefly to make polyethylene, ethylene oxide (for antifreeze and detergents), and styrene (for polystyrene). Propylene is used for polypropylene, acrylonitrile and propylene oxide. Benzene is a precursor to styrene, phenol and cyclohexane (for nylon).

History

The modern petrochemical industry grew out of oil refining in the early 20th century. The first commercial petrochemical, isopropyl alcohol, was produced from propylene by Standard Oil of New Jersey in 1920. World War II greatly expanded demand for synthetic rubber and aviation fuel components, and after 1945 the industry grew rapidly, displacing many coal- and biomass-derived chemicals.

Environmental concerns

Petrochemical production is energy-intensive and a significant source of greenhouse gas emissions and plastic waste. Growing concern over climate change and plastic pollution has spurred research into bio-based feedstocks, chemical recycling, and carbon capture for steam crackers.
