Leicestershire

Leicestershire is a ceremonial county in the East Midlands of England. It is named after its county town and largest settlement, the city of Leicester, and borders Nottinghamshire, Lincolnshire, Rutland, Northamptonshire, Warwickshire, Staffordshire, and Derbyshire. The administrative county, administered by Leicestershire County Council and excluding the unitary city of Leicester, has a population of around 700,000; including Leicester, the ceremonial county has over one million residents.

Geography

The county is largely low-lying farmland drained by the River Soar, a tributary of the Trent. Its highest ground is in the northwest: the ancient volcanic hills of Charnwood Forest, where Bardon Hill reaches 278 metres, the county top. Charnwood's Precambrian rocks contain some of Britain's most famous fossils, including Charnia, an early complex organism. The former coalfield around Coalville lies in the northwest, and the Vale of Belvoir, overlooked by Belvoir Castle, in the northeast.

History

Leicester began as the Roman town of Ratae Corieltauvorum and in the Danelaw period was one of the Five Boroughs. Simon de Montfort, Earl of Leicester, led the baronial revolt against Henry III in the 1260s. Richard III was killed at the Battle of Bosworth Field, fought in the county in 1485; his remains were rediscovered beneath a Leicester car park in 2012 and reinterred in Leicester Cathedral in 2015. The county became known for hosiery, footwear, and engineering industries, and for Melton Mowbray pork pies and Stilton cheese, both protected foods associated with the Melton area.

Settlements and sport

Principal towns include Loughborough (home to Loughborough University, renowned for sport), Hinckley, Melton Mowbray, Market Harborough, Ashby-de-la-Zouch, and Coalville. Leicester City Football Club won the Premier League in 2015–16 in one of sport's greatest upsets; Leicester Tigers are a leading rugby union club, and Leicestershire County Cricket Club plays at Grace Road.
