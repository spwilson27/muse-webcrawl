Japanese era name

The Japanese era name (年号, nengō, "year name") is a system of dating used in Japan in which years are counted within named eras. The system originated in imperial China and was adopted in Japan in 645 CE with the era Taika. Since the Meiji period, each era corresponds to the reign of one emperor.

First era: Taika (645 CE)

Current era: Reiwa (from 1 May 2019)

Total eras: More than 240

Modern eras

The recent eras are Meiji (1868–1912), Taishō (1912–1926), Shōwa (1926–1989), Heisei (1989–2019) and Reiwa (2019–present). Era names are chosen from classical literature and are used on official documents, coins, newspapers and calendars alongside the Gregorian year; thus 2025 is Reiwa 7. The name Reiwa, drawn from the Man'yōshū poetry anthology, was the first era name taken from a Japanese rather than a Chinese classic.

Conversion

To convert a modern era year to the Gregorian calendar, add the era's base year: for Reiwa, add 2018; for Heisei, add 1988; for Shōwa, add 1925.
