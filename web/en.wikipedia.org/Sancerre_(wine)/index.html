Sancerre (wine)

RegionLoire Valley, France
Appellation typeAOC (1936, whites; 1959, reds/rosés)

Sancerre is a French wine Appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) for wine produced in the area of Sancerre in the eastern part of the Loire Valley, southeast of Orléans. Almost all of the appellation lies on the left bank of the Loire, opposite Pouilly-Fumé. It is regarded as one of the world's classic expressions of the Sauvignon blanc grape.

Wines

White Sancerre accounts for the great majority of production: dry, aromatic wines with pronounced acidity and flavours of citrus, gooseberry, green herbs and gunflint, shaped by the district's three soil types — Kimmeridgian marl (terres blanches), limestone gravel (caillottes) and flint (silex). Pinot noir produces lighter reds and pale rosés, which gained appellation status in 1959.

History

Vines have been grown around the hill town of Sancerre since at least Roman times; the Augustinian canons and local lords developed the vineyards in the Middle Ages. Phylloxera devastated the region in the late 19th century, and replanting with Sauvignon blanc on grafted rootstocks largely displaced other varieties. The white wines received AOC recognition in 1936. Sancerre's international reputation grew sharply in the 1970s and 1980s as it became a fixture on Parisian bistro lists and then in export markets.

Food

The local goat cheese Crottin de Chavignol, made in a neighbouring village within the appellation, is the classic pairing and is often cited as a model of regional food-and-wine matching.
