Southern Ontario

Southern Ontario
Province: Ontario, Canada
Area: about 140,000 km²
Population: over 12 million (about one-third of Canada's population)
Largest city: Toronto

Southern Ontario is a primary region of the province of Ontario in Canada, lying south of the French River and Algonquin Provincial Park. Although it covers only about 14 percent of the province's land area, it is home to more than 90 percent of Ontario's population and roughly a third of all Canadians, thanks to the Greater Toronto Area and the densely settled Golden Horseshoe around the western end of Lake Ontario.

Geography

The region is bounded by Lakes Huron, Erie and Ontario and the St. Lawrence River. The Niagara Escarpment runs through it, creating Niagara Falls, while the Oak Ridges Moraine and the fertile plains of the southwest support intensive agriculture, including the tobacco, corn, fruit and wine production of the Niagara Peninsula. Major cities besides Toronto include Ottawa (the national capital), Hamilton, London, Kitchener–Waterloo, Windsor, Mississauga and Brampton.

Economy and culture

Southern Ontario is Canada's economic engine: finance and media in Toronto, automotive manufacturing along the Highway 401 corridor, technology in the Toronto–Waterloo "innovation corridor", and a major share of national agriculture. Its climate, moderated by the Great Lakes, is the mildest in mainland Canada, and the region's universities — Toronto, Waterloo, Western, McMaster, Queen's and Ottawa among them — anchor research and education.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Huron-Wendat, Petun and Neutral nations and later the Haudenosaunee and Anishinaabe, inhabited the region for millennia. European settlement expanded after the American Revolution brought United Empire Loyalists; as Upper Canada it became one of the founding provinces of Confederation in 1867.
