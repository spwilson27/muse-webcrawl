Breakfast

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A cooked breakfast

Breakfast is the first meal of the day, eaten after waking from a night's sleep, most often in the early morning before undertaking the day's work. The word literally refers to breaking the fasting period of the previous night. Breakfast foods vary widely by region and culture, ranging from cereal grains, eggs and dairy products to soups, rice dishes and leftovers from previous meals.

History

Regular morning meals became common with settled agricultural societies. In medieval Europe, breakfast was often discouraged by moralists and commonly eaten only by labourers, children and the elderly. By the Victorian era, elaborate cooked breakfasts had become a marker of prosperity in Britain, and the industrial revolution's fixed working hours made a substantial morning meal a widespread custom in industrialised countries. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, commercially produced breakfast cereals were popularised in the United States by figures such as John Harvey Kellogg and C. W. Post.

Regional varieties

The full breakfast in Britain and Ireland typically includes bacon, eggs, sausage, black pudding, beans, tomatoes and toast. A continental breakfast, common in European hotels, consists of coffee, juice, pastries, bread, jam and cold meats or cheese. In East Asia, breakfast may include rice porridge (congee), steamed buns or miso soup with rice and pickles. In parts of the Middle East, flatbreads with olives, cheese, eggs and tea are typical.

Nutrition

Breakfast is often described as "the most important meal of the day", a slogan popularised by cereal advertising in the 20th century. Nutritional research on the health effects of eating or skipping breakfast is mixed; studies have examined its influence on metabolism, weight management, cognition and school performance in children, with results depending on what is eaten as much as on whether a meal is consumed.
