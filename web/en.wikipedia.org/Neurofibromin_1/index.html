Neurofibromin 1 is a protein encoded by the NF1 gene in humans, located on chromosome 17. It functions as a GTPase-activating protein (GAP) that negatively regulates the Ras signalling pathway by accelerating the conversion of active Ras-GTP to inactive Ras-GDP.

Function

Through its GAP-related domain, neurofibromin dampens Ras/MAPK signalling and also influences adenylate cyclase and cyclic AMP levels. Loss of neurofibromin function leads to hyperactive Ras signalling, increased cell proliferation and tumour formation.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the NF1 gene cause neurofibromatosis type I, an autosomal dominant disorder characterised by café-au-lait spots, neurofibromas, Lisch nodules of the iris and an increased risk of tumours such as optic gliomas and malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumours. Somatic NF1 mutations also occur in various sporadic cancers, including glioblastoma and melanoma.
