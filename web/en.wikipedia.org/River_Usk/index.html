The River Usk (Welsh: Afon Wysg) is a river in south Wales, the longest river entirely within Wales at about 125 kilometres. It rises below Fan Brycheiniog in the Black Mountain range of the Brecon Beacons, flows east through Brecon, south through Abergavenny, the town of Usk, and Caerleon, and enters the Severn Estuary at Uskmouth south of Newport.

Ecology and fishing

The Usk is renowned as one of Britain's finest salmon and wild brown trout rivers, and also holds sea trout (sewin), shad, and lamprey. Almost the entire river is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a Special Area of Conservation, notable also for otters and its ranunculus plant communities. Anglers have fished its beats for centuries; the river figures prominently in Welsh angling literature.

History

The lower valley carried the Roman legionary fortress at Caerleon (Isca Augusta). The Normans built castles along its banks at Brecon, Abergavenny, and Usk to control the Welsh Marches. At Newport the medieval ship, a fifteenth-century merchant vessel, was discovered in the river's mud in 2002. The estuary at Newport experiences one of the largest tidal ranges in the world, and the Newport Transporter Bridge of 1906 still carries passengers across the river in a gondola slung beneath its high span.
