Krakus

Krakus (Latin: Grakchus) is a legendary Polish prince, said to be the founder of Kraków and a ruler of the Lechitic Vistulans tribe. Legend credits him with slaying, or organising the slaying of, the Wawel Dragon that terrorised the city.

Legend

According to the earliest accounts by Wincenty Kadłubek, Krakus was a wise ruler who founded Kraków on Wawel Hill. Later versions tell how a cobbler (or Krakus himself) fed the dragon a sheep stuffed with sulfur, causing it to drink itself to death in the Vistula. Krakus was succeeded by his sons — in one version the younger murdered the elder — and then by his daughter Wanda, who famously refused to marry a German prince.

Legacy

The prehistoric Krakus Mound (Kopiec Kraka) in Podgórze, Kraków, is traditionally regarded as his burial place. The story is a foundation myth of Kraków, whose name is commonly derived from Krakus.
