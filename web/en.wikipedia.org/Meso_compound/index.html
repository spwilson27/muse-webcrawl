Meso compound

A meso compound or meso isomer is an optically inactive compound that contains two or more stereocenters but is superimposable on its mirror image because of symmetry — typically an internal plane of symmetry that divides the molecule into mirror-image halves.

Explanation

Although a meso compound contains stereogenic centers, the molecule as a whole is achiral: the optical rotation contributed by one stereocenter is exactly cancelled by the opposite configuration of another. Meso tartaric acid is the classic example: it has two stereocenters of opposite configuration (2R,3S), so despite having two stereocenters it does not rotate plane-polarized light, in contrast to the chiral (2R,3R) and (2S,3S) forms.

Distinguishing features

A meso compound is a diastereomer of the chiral forms of the same constitution, so it has different physical properties (melting point, solubility, NMR spectra) from them. An even number of stereocenters is not required in principle: any compound with an improper rotation axis (mirror plane or inversion center) is meso-like in being achiral despite local stereocenters. Cyclic analogs occur too, for example cis-1,2-dimethylcyclopropane.
