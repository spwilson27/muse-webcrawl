Kevin Feige
Born: June 2, 1973, Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.
Occupation: Film and television producer
Title: President, Marvel Studios; Chief Creative Officer, Marvel
Known for: The Marvel Cinematic Universe

Kevin Feige (born June 2, 1973) is an American film and television producer who has been the president of Marvel Studios since 2007 and the primary creative force behind the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU), the highest-grossing film franchise of all time, whose films have earned more than $30 billion worldwide.

Early career

Feige grew up in New Jersey and, a lifelong comic-book and film fan, studied at the University of Southern California's School of Cinematic Arts. He began as an assistant to producer Lauren Shuler Donner, working on films such as Volcano and You've Got Mail, and was hired by Marvel in 2000 as a producer, valued for his encyclopaedic knowledge of the comics. He was involved in the early X-Men and Spider-Man films licensed to other studios.

Marvel Studios

Named president of production in 2007, Feige championed Marvel's plan to self-finance its own interconnected films, beginning with Iron Man (2008) and culminating in The Avengers (2012), whose success cemented the shared-universe model that reshaped Hollywood franchising. Under Feige the MCU expanded through sequels such as Avengers: Endgame (2019) — for a time the highest-grossing film ever released — and into streaming series for Disney+ from 2021. In 2019 he also became chief creative officer of Marvel Entertainment, overseeing publishing and animation.

Production style and reception

Feige is known for meticulous long-range story planning, post-credits scenes linking instalments, and an ability to match directors to material. Black Panther (2018), which he produced, was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. He has also been announced as producer of a planned Star Wars film and of the X-Men's integration into the MCU.
