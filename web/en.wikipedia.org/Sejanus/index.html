Sejanus, commander of the Praetorian Guard and Tiberius's trusted — and ultimately treacherous — deputy.

Lucius Aelius Sejanus (c. 20 BC – 18 October 31 AD) was a Roman soldier and confidant of the emperor Tiberius. As commander (prefect) of the Praetorian Guard, Sejanus consolidated the Guard in a single camp in Rome and became the most powerful man in the empire during Tiberius's retirement to Capri, before his sudden arrest and execution in 31 AD.

Rise to power

Sejanus was the son of Lucius Seius Strabo, whom Augustus appointed among the first Praetorian prefects. Sejanus became sole prefect around 15 AD. In 23 AD he persuaded Tiberius to concentrate the Praetorian cohorts in the Castra Praetoria, greatly increasing his influence. Ancient sources accuse him of seducing Livilla, the wife of Tiberius's son Drusus, and of poisoning Drusus in 23 AD, removing the heir and clearing his own path.

Rule from Rome

After Tiberius withdrew to Capri in 26 AD, Sejanus effectively governed Rome. He used treason (maiestas) trials to eliminate senators and members of the imperial family, exiling Germanicus's widow Agrippina the Elder and her sons. He shared the consulship with Tiberius in 31 AD and sought marriage into the imperial family.

Downfall

Warned — according to tradition by a letter from his mother-in-law Antonia — Tiberius secretly arranged for the prefect Macro to replace Sejanus. On 18 October 31 AD Sejanus was denounced in the Senate, arrested, and strangled the same day; his children were also executed, and his body was abused by the mob. A purge of his supporters followed. His ex-wife Apicata's posthumous letter revealed the poisoning of Drusus.

Legacy

Sejanus is the archetype of the over-mighty minister in Western literature, dramatised by Ben Jonson in Sejanus His Fall (1603) and portrayed in Robert Graves's I, Claudius. The chief ancient sources are Tacitus, Suetonius and Cassius Dio, all hostile.
