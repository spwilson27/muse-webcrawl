Yi-Fu Tuan (Chinese: 段義孚; 5 December 1930 – 10 August 2022) was a Chinese-born American geographer and a central figure in the development of humanistic geography. He spent most of his career at the University of Minnesota and the University of Wisconsin–Madison.

Work

Tuan coined the term topophilia — the affective bond between people and place — the title of his influential 1974 book Topophilia: A Study of Environmental Perception, Attitudes, and Values. His best-known work, Space and Place: The Perspective of Experience (1977), distinguished "space" as abstract openness and movement from "place" as space made meaningful by experience and attachment. Other major books include Landscapes of Fear (1979), Segmented Worlds and Self (1982), Dominance and Affection: The Making of Pets (1984), and Cosmos and Hearth (1996).

Legacy

Tuan's writing, combining geography with philosophy, literature, and autobiography, reshaped how geographers and other social scientists think about landscape, home, and environmental experience. He received the Cullum Geographical Medal of the American Geographical Society in 1987 and the Prix Vautrin Lud in 2012.
