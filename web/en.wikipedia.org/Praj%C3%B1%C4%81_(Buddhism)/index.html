Prajñā (Buddhism)

Prajñā (Sanskrit: प्रज्ञा; Pali: paññā) is a Buddhist term usually translated as "wisdom" or "discernment": the insight into the true nature of reality — impermanence (anicca), suffering (duḥkha), and non-self (anattā)/emptiness (śūnyatā). It is considered one of the highest virtues and an essential factor of enlightenment.

Role in the path

Prajñā is the third division of the Noble Eightfold Path (as right view and right intention) and the culmination of the threefold training of ethics (śīla), concentration (samādhi), and wisdom. In Theravāda Buddhism, paññā is developed through vipassanā meditation and is also listed among the ten perfections (pāramīs). Buddhist thought distinguishes wisdom arising from learning (śrutamayī prajñā), from reflection (cintāmayī prajñā), and from meditative cultivation (bhāvanāmayī prajñā), the latter being directly liberating.

Mahāyāna developments

In Mahāyāna Buddhism, prajñā is the sixth of the six perfections (pāramitās) and the subject of the Prajñāpāramitā ("Perfection of Wisdom") sutras, including the Heart Sutra and Diamond Sutra, which teach the emptiness of all phenomena. Prajñāpāramitā is personified as a goddess, "the mother of all Buddhas". Commentators such as Nāgārjuna emphasised that prajñā must be united with compassion (karuṇā) and skilful means (upāya) — the two "wings" of awakening.
