Hugh Trenchard, 1st Viscount Trenchard

Hugh Trenchard
Born: 3 February 1873, Taunton, Somerset
Died: 10 February 1956, London
Service: British Army, Royal Flying Corps, Royal Air Force
Rank: Marshal of the Royal Air Force
Posts: Chief of the Air Staff (1918, 1919–1930)

Marshal of the Royal Air Force Hugh Montague Trenchard, 1st Viscount Trenchard (1873–1956), was a British officer who was instrumental in establishing the Royal Air Force. He has been described as the "father of the Royal Air Force" for his role in building it into an independent service during and after the First World War.

Career

Trenchard began as an infantry officer, serving in India and in the Boer War, where he was seriously wounded. He learned to fly in 1912 at the age of 39 and went on to command the Royal Flying Corps in France from 1915 to 1918, championing offensive air operations. In 1918 he became the first Chief of the Air Staff and briefly commanded the Independent Force conducting strategic bombing of Germany.

Building the RAF

After resigning in a dispute with the government, Trenchard returned as Chief of the Air Staff in 1919 and served until 1930, fighting repeated attempts by the Army and Royal Navy to dismember the young service. He founded the RAF Staff College at Andover and the cadet college at Cranwell, created the Auxiliary Air Force and university air squadrons, and promoted "air control" — policing the empire, notably in Iraq, by air power rather than large garrisons. He was a leading advocate of strategic bombing doctrine.

Later life

Trenchard was Commissioner of the Metropolitan Police from 1931 to 1935, reforming police training and founding the Hendon Police College. He was raised to the peerage as Baron Trenchard in 1930 and Viscount Trenchard in 1936, and remained an elder statesman of air power until his death in 1956.
