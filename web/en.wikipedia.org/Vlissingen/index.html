Vlissingen (Dutch: [ˈvlɪsɪŋə(n)]; historically known in English as Flushing) is a municipality and a city in the southwestern Netherlands on the former island of Walcheren. With its strategic location between the Scheldt river and the North Sea, Vlissingen has been an important harbour for centuries — it was the main port of the Dutch East India Company (VOC) in Zeeland and a major naval base.

History

The town received city rights in 1315. In 1572 Vlissingen was one of the first Dutch towns to declare for William of Orange against Spanish rule, and its English name derives from the English garrison: by the Treaty of Nonsuch (1585), Flushing was held by England as a "cautionary town" until 1616. The admiral Michiel de Ruyter, the Netherlands' most famous naval commander, was born here in 1607; his statue stands on the boulevard. In 1809, during the Napoleonic Wars, the British Walcheren Expedition bombarded and occupied the town. In the Second World War Vlissingen was heavily damaged during the Battle of the Scheldt in November 1944, when Allied forces landed to clear the approaches to Antwerp.

Economy

The port area of Vlissingen-Oost is part of North Sea Port (merged with Ghent and Terneuzen) and hosts shipbuilding (Damen Schelde Naval Shipbuilding, builder of Royal Netherlands Navy vessels), offshore and chemical industries. A ferry historically ran to Breskens, replaced for vehicles by the Western Scheldt Tunnel in 2003, though a passenger and bicycle ferry continues.
