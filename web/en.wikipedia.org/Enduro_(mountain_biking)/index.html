Enduro (mountain biking)

Enduro is a form of mountain bike racing in which riders compete over a series of timed downhill stages connected by untimed (or loosely timed) transfer stages, which are usually ridden uphill under the rider's own power. The format takes its name and inspiration from motorcycle enduro racing, itself derived from rally-style competition.

Format

An enduro race typically consists of three to six timed stages in a single day, sometimes more over multiple days. Only the downhill-oriented stages are timed, and the rider's total time across those stages determines the result. Transfers between stages are not timed, though organizers may set a maximum time limit for completing them. Because most of the climbing is done on the transfers, enduro rewards both descending skill and all-round fitness.

Bicycles

Enduro bikes are full-suspension mountain bikes, generally with around 150–180 mm of suspension travel, slack head angles, and dropper seatposts that let the rider lower the saddle for descents and raise it for climbing. They sit between lighter cross-country bikes and heavier downhill bikes in both weight and capability.

History and competitions

The format grew in popularity in Europe in the 2000s, notably in France, and spread internationally with the launch of the Enduro World Series in 2013. In 2023 the discipline was incorporated into the UCI Mountain Bike World Cup as the UCI Enduro World Cup. Blind racing — competing on stages without prior practice — is traditional in some events, although many races allow practice runs.
