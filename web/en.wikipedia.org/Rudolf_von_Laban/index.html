Rudolf von Laban

Rudolf von Laban, also known as Rudolf Laban (15 December 1879 – 1 July 1958), was an Austro-Hungarian-born dance artist, choreographer, and movement theorist. He is regarded as one of the founders of European modern dance and the creator of Labanotation, the most widely used system of dance notation, and of Laban movement analysis.

Life

Born in Pressburg (now Bratislava) in the Austro-Hungarian Empire, Laban studied architecture in Paris before turning to movement and dance. He founded schools and movement choirs in Germany and Switzerland, developing theories of expressive movement (Ausdruckstanz) and choreutics, the study of spatial harmony. His school at Monte Verità in Ascona and later institutes in Zurich, Munich, Hamburg, and Berlin trained leading modern dancers such as Mary Wigman and Kurt Jooss.

Laban initially worked within Nazi Germany's cultural apparatus, directing dance for the 1936 Berlin Olympics, but fell from favour and fled in 1937, eventually settling in England. There he founded the Art of Movement Studio in Manchester with Lisa Ullmann and worked on movement efficiency in industry as well as dance education.

Work

Laban's system of notation, published in 1928 as Schrifttanz and developed into Labanotation, records movement on a vertical staff read from the bottom up. His movement analysis — describing effort through the factors of weight, time, space, and flow — remains influential in dance, theatre, physical therapy, and animation. He died in Weybridge, England, in 1958.
