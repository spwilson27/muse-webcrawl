The Recording Academy (formally the National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences, or NARAS) is an American learned academy of musicians, producers, recording engineers, and other musical professionals. It is known internationally for the Grammy Awards, which it presents each year to recognise outstanding achievement in the music industry, and for the Grammy Hall of Fame and the Grammy Museum in Los Angeles.

History

The Academy was founded in 1957 by a group of record executives and artists who had gathered in connection with the Hollywood Walk of Fame project and resolved to create an organisation to honour excellence in recording. The first Grammy Awards ceremony was held on 4 May 1959, simultaneously in Beverly Hills and New York. The Academy grew through regional chapters in major recording centres — eventually twelve chapters across the United States — and in 1997 helped establish the Latin Recording Academy, which presents the Latin Grammy Awards.

Activities

Beyond the awards, the Academy engages in advocacy on copyright and creators' rights, runs professional development and education programmes, and operates the MusiCares Foundation, which provides health and welfare assistance to music professionals in need. Its Producers and Engineers Wing addresses technical standards and archival preservation of recordings. The Grammy Museum, opened in 2008 at L.A. Live in downtown Los Angeles, presents exhibitions on the history and craft of recorded music.

Membership and voting

Membership is divided into voting members — creative and technical professionals with credits on commercially released recordings — professional members from the business side of the industry, and student members of the Grammy U programme. Grammy winners are chosen by the voting membership through a process of entry, screening by genre committees, nomination by ballot, and final voting overseen by an independent accounting firm. The Academy has periodically reformed its rules, notably in 2021 when it abolished the nominations review committees that had adjusted final ballots in the major categories.

Criticism

The Academy and the Grammys have faced recurring criticism over the representation of women and minority artists, controversial results in major categories, and the relevance of its categories. In response it has diversified its membership through invitation campaigns and revised category structures, consolidating many genre fields in a major restructuring in 2012.
