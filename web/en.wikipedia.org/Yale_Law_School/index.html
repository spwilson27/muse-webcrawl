Yale Law School (YLS) is the law school of Yale University, a private Ivy League research university in New Haven, Connecticut. Founded in 1824, it is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious law schools in the world and has long stood at or near the top of American law school rankings, noted for its small class sizes, scholarly faculty, and influence on legal academia and the federal judiciary.

History

The school began in the 1820s when New Haven attorney Seth Staples ran a proprietary law school whose students gained access to Yale's lectures; it formally joined Yale University in 1843. In 1931 it moved into the Sterling Law Building, a Collegiate Gothic complex modelled on the English Inns of Court.

Admissions and programs

Yale Law admits roughly 200 students per JD class, the smallest of the elite American law schools, and uses a distinctive faculty-review admissions process. Its grading for first-term students is pass/fail, and its clinical programs, centres, and journals — including the Yale Law Journal — are prominent in American legal life.

Notable alumni

Graduates include U.S. presidents Gerald Ford and Bill Clinton, secretary of state Hillary Clinton, and numerous Supreme Court justices, among them Clarence Thomas, Samuel Alito, Sonia Sotomayor, and Brett Kavanaugh.
