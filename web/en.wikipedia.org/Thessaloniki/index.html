Thessaloniki (Greek: Θεσσαλονίκη), also known as Thessalonica, Salonica or Saloniki, is the second-largest city in Greece, with over one million inhabitants in its metropolitan area, and the capital of the geographic region of Macedonia, the administrative region of Central Macedonia and the Decentralized Administration of Macedonia and Thrace. It is located on the Thermaic Gulf, at the northwest corner of the Aegean Sea.

History

The city was founded in 315 BC by Cassander of Macedon, who named it after his wife Thessalonike, daughter of Philip II of Macedon and half-sister of Alexander the Great. An important metropolis by the Roman period, Thessaloniki was the second most important city of the Byzantine Empire after Constantinople, and its Paleochristian and Byzantine monuments are listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Saint Paul preached in the city, and his two epistles to the Thessalonians are among the earliest books of the New Testament; the city's patron saint is Saint Demetrius.

Thessaloniki was captured by the Ottoman Empire in 1430 and remained Ottoman for nearly five centuries, becoming a major port and commercial centre with a large Sephardic Jewish population — for a time the only major city in Europe with a Jewish majority — earning it the nickname "Mother of Israel". Greece took control of the city during the Balkan Wars in 1912. The Great Fire of 1917 destroyed much of the centre, which was rebuilt to a modern European plan. In 1943, during the Second World War, the occupying German forces deported nearly the entire Jewish community, most of whom were murdered in the Holocaust. The city was European Capital of Culture in 1997.

Geography and landmarks

Thessaloniki lies on the western Chalkidiki peninsula at the head of the Thermaic Gulf. Landmarks include the White Tower (the city's symbol), the Arch and Rotunda of Galerius, the Roman Forum, the Byzantine walls and churches such as Hagia Sophia and Hagios Demetrios, and the Aristotelous Square waterfront. The city hosts the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, the largest university in Greece, and the Thessaloniki International Fair.

Economy and culture

The Port of Thessaloniki is one of the largest Greek seaports and a gateway for the Balkans. The city is known across Greece for its food scene, cafés, festivals — including the Thessaloniki International Film Festival — and its relaxed pace of life.
