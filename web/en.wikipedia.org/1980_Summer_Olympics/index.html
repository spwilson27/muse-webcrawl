1980 Summer Olympics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 1980 Summer Olympic Games were held in Moscow, Soviet Union.

The 1980 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXII Olympiad and officially branded as Moscow 1980, were an international multi-sport event held from 19 July to 3 August 1980 in Moscow, Soviet Union, in present-day Russia. The games were the first Olympic Games to be staged in an Eastern Bloc country, and the first Olympic Games and only Summer Olympics to be held in a Slavic language-speaking country. They were also the only Summer Olympic Games to be held in a communist country until the 2008 Summer Olympics held in China.

Eighty nations were represented at the Moscow Games, the smallest number since 1956. Led by the United States at the insistence of US President Jimmy Carter, 65 nations boycotted the games in protest of the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan; however, athletes from 16 of those boycotting nations still participated under the Olympic flag. The Soviet Union would later boycott the 1984 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles in retaliation.

Host city selection

The only two cities to bid for the 1980 Summer Olympics were Moscow and Los Angeles. Moscow was chosen at the 75th IOC Session in Vienna on 23 October 1974, defeating Los Angeles by a vote of 39 to 20.

Boycott

Following the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in December 1979, US President Jimmy Carter issued an ultimatum that the United States would boycott the Moscow Olympics if Soviet troops did not withdraw from Afghanistan by 20 February 1980. In total, 65 nations declined to participate, though some athletes from boycotting nations competed under the Olympic flag. The boycott significantly reduced the competitiveness of some events, particularly in athletics and swimming.

Highlights

  • Soviet gymnast Alexander Dityatin won eight medals, including three golds, becoming the first athlete to win eight medals at a single Olympic Games.
  • Teófilo Stevenson of Cuba won his third consecutive heavyweight boxing gold medal.
  • The middle-distance rivalry between British runners Sebastian Coe and Steve Ovett captivated audiences: Ovett won the 800 metres, while Coe took gold in the 1500 metres.
  • Vladimir Salnikov became the first swimmer to break 15 minutes in the 1500 m freestyle.
  • The Soviet Union topped the medal table with 80 gold medals and 195 medals overall.

Participating nations

Eighty nations sent delegations to Moscow, although sixteen of them marched under the Olympic flag rather than their national flags at the opening ceremony. Several nations, including Liberia, withdrew during the games.

Legacy

The Moscow Games left behind major sporting infrastructure, including the Luzhniki Stadium, which later hosted the 2018 FIFA World Cup Final. The mascot, Misha the bear, became one of the most recognisable Olympic mascots; the image of Misha shedding a tear at the closing ceremony remains an enduring symbol of the games.
