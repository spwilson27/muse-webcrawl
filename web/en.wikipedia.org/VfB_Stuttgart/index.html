Verein für Bewegungsspiele Stuttgart 1893 e. V., commonly known as VfB Stuttgart (German pronunciation: [faʊ̯ʔɛfˈbeː ˈʃtʊtɡaʁt]), is a German professional sports club based in Stuttgart, Baden-Württemberg. The club's football team is currently part of Germany's first division, the Bundesliga. VfB Stuttgart has won the national championship five times, most recently in 2006–07, the DFB-Pokal four times and the UEFA Intertoto Cup a record three times.

The football team plays its home games at the MHPArena, in the Neckarpark which is located near the Cannstatter Wasen, where the city's fall beer festival takes place. Founded in 1893 in the Stuttgart district of Bad Cannstatt, the club is one of Germany's largest sports clubs, with tens of thousands of members across departments including fistball, hockey, track and field, table tennis, and refereeing.

History

VfB Stuttgart was formed from the 1912 merger of Stuttgarter FV 1893 and FC Krone Cannstatt. The club won German championships in 1950 and 1952 under coach Georg Wurzer, and the DFB-Pokal in 1954 and 1958. A founding member of the Bundesliga in 1963, Stuttgart won further league titles in 1984 and 1992 and lifted the DFB-Pokal in 1997. In Europe, the club reached the 1989 UEFA Cup final, losing to Napoli, and the 1998 UEFA Cup Winners' Cup final.

The club's most recent league triumph came in the 2006–07 season under coach Armin Veh, when a young team featuring Mario Gómez, Sami Khedira, and Serdar Tasci overtook Schalke 04 in the final rounds. After relegation in 2016 and again in 2019, each followed by immediate promotion, Stuttgart enjoyed a renaissance under coach Sebastian Hoeneß, finishing second in the Bundesliga in 2023–24 to qualify for the UEFA Champions League, and winning the DFB-Pokal in 2025, the club's first major trophy in 18 years.

Honours

German champions: 1950, 1952, 1984, 1992, 2007. DFB-Pokal winners: 1954, 1958, 1997, 2025. UEFA Intertoto Cup winners: 2000, 2002, 2008. VfB also won the 2. Bundesliga in 1977 and 2017.

Youth academy

VfB's youth academy is among the most productive in Germany, having developed players such as Sami Khedira, Mario Gómez, Joshua Kimmich, Timo Werner, Antonio Rüdiger, and Serge Gnabry.
