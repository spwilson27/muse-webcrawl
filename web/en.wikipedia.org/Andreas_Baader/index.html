Andreas Baader

Andreas Bernd Baader
Born: 6 May 1943, Munich, Germany
Died: 18 October 1977 (aged 34), Stammheim Prison, Stuttgart
Known for: Co-founder of the Red Army Faction

Andreas Bernd Baader (6 May 1943 – 18 October 1977) was a German militant who, with Ulrike Meinhof, Gudrun Ensslin, and others, founded the far-left Red Army Faction (RAF), also called the Baader–Meinhof Group. He became one of the most wanted men in West Germany during the group's campaign of bombings, robberies, and assassinations in the 1970s.

Early life and radicalisation

Born in Munich during the Second World War, Baader grew up without his father, a historian and archivist killed in the war. He drifted through the West Berlin counterculture of the 1960s, accumulating convictions for petty crime and traffic offences. In 1968 he and Ensslin firebombed two Frankfurt department stores in protest at the Vietnam War; arrested and convicted, they fled abroad before being recaptured.

The Red Army Faction

Baader's armed rescue from custody in May 1970—in which a library employee was shot and wounded—marked the RAF's founding act. After training in Jordan, the group robbed banks and bombed US Army installations and police buildings in May 1972, killing four American servicemen. Baader was arrested that June after a shootout in Frankfurt.

Imprisonment and death

Held in the purpose-built Stammheim Prison, Baader and his co-defendants were convicted of murder in April 1977. During the “German Autumn” of 1977, amid the Schleyer kidnapping and the Landshut hijacking mounted to free them, Baader was found shot dead in his cell on 18 October 1977, alongside Ensslin and Jan-Carl Raspe—deaths officially ruled suicides, though long surrounded by controversy.
