Prince Rupert of the Rhine, Duke of Cumberland (1619–1682) was a German-English army officer, admiral, scientist, and colonial governor. The third son of Frederick V, Elector Palatine, and Elizabeth Stuart, daughter of James I of England, he became the most famous Royalist cavalry commander of the English Civil War.

English Civil War

Rupert arrived in England in 1642 and was appointed commander of the Royalist cavalry by his uncle, Charles I. His aggressive charges won early successes at Powick Bridge and Edgehill, and he captured Bristol in 1643, but his impetuous pursuit of fleeing enemies contributed to defeats at Marston Moor (1644) and Naseby (1645). After surrendering Bristol to Parliamentary forces, he was dismissed and later banished from England.

Later career

After the Restoration, Rupert served as a naval commander in the Anglo-Dutch Wars and held commands in the Royal Navy. He was a founding Fellow of the Royal Society, worked on inventions and the mezzotint printing process, and in 1670 became the first Governor of the Hudson's Bay Company, whose vast Canadian territory was named Rupert's Land after him. He died in London in 1682 and was buried in Westminster Abbey.
