Qutb-ud-din Aibak

The Qutb Minar in Delhi, begun under Aibak

Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1150–1210) was a general of the Ghurid emperor Muhammad Ghori and, after his master's assassination in 1206, the first ruler of the Delhi Sultanate and founder of its Mamluk (Slave) dynasty. A Turkic slave-soldier purchased in Nishapur and raised in Ghori's service, he rose by ability to become commander of the Ghurid armies in India.

Conquests in India

As Muhammad Ghori's lieutenant, Aibak campaigned across northern India following the victory at the Second Battle of Tarain (1192), taking Delhi, Ajmer, and much of the Gangetic plain, while his own general Bakhtiyar Khilji extended conquests into Bihar and Bengal.

Sultan of Delhi

On Ghori's death without a male heir, Aibak asserted independence at Lahore in 1206, though he never formally took more than modest titles and struck no coins in his own name. His reign lasted only four years: he died in 1210 from injuries sustained in a fall from his horse while playing chaugan (polo).

Building and legacy

Aibak commissioned the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque in Delhi, among the earliest mosques in India, and began the great Qutb Minar, completed by his successor and son-in-law Iltutmish. Remembered in Persian chronicles as a generous patron, he was succeeded after a brief interlude by Iltutmish, who consolidated the sultanate.
