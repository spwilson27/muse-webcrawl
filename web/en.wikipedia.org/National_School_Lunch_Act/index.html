National School Lunch Act

National School Lunch Act
Public Law 396, 79th Congress
Enacted: June 4, 1946
Signed by: President Harry S. Truman
Administered by: U.S. Department of Agriculture

The National School Lunch Act (Public Law 396), signed into law on June 4, 1946, by President Harry S. Truman, created the federally assisted National School Lunch Program (NSLP) in the United States. The Act declared it the policy of Congress, "as a measure of national security, to safeguard the health and well-being of the Nation's children and to encourage the domestic consumption of nutritious agricultural commodities and other food," to assist the states in providing funds for the establishment, maintenance, operation, and expansion of nonprofit school lunch programs.

Background

Federal assistance for school feeding began during the Great Depression, when surplus agricultural commodities were directed to school lunch programs. During World War II, the draft revealed widespread malnutrition among young men, a finding used by supporters — including military leaders such as General Lewis B. Hershey — to argue that a permanent school lunch program was a matter of national security. The 1946 Act made the program permanent and established a formula for apportioning federal funds to the states.

Provisions

The Act authorized federal cash reimbursement and surplus commodity donations to schools operating lunch programs on a nonprofit basis. Funds were apportioned among states based on the number of children and the state's per capita income, with poorer states receiving a larger federal share. Participating schools were required to serve lunches meeting minimum nutritional requirements established by the Secretary of Agriculture, to provide free or reduced-price lunches to children unable to pay, and to operate without discrimination.

Later developments

The program has been repeatedly amended and expanded. The Child Nutrition Act of 1966 consolidated child nutrition programs, the School Breakfast Program was made permanent in 1975, and the Richard B. Russell National School Lunch Act — as the statute was later renamed in honor of Senator Richard Russell, a longtime champion of the program — continues to govern the NSLP. The Healthy, Hunger-Free Kids Act of 2010 substantially updated nutrition standards. Today the program serves tens of millions of children each school day in public and nonprofit private schools and residential child care institutions across the United States.
