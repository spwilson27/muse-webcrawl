Fukuoka (福岡市, Fukuoka-shi) is the capital city of Fukuoka Prefecture and the most populous city on the island of Kyushu, Japan. Located on the northern shore of Hakata Bay, it has historically served as Japan's gateway to Korea and mainland Asia.

History

The area was the landing site of the Mongol invasions of Japan in 1274 and 1281, both repelled with the aid of typhoons known as kamikaze. The modern city was formed in 1889 from the merger of the port town of Hakata and the castle town of Fukuoka.

Culture and economy

Fukuoka is known for its open-air food stalls (yatai), tonkotsu (Hakata) ramen, and festivals such as Hakata Gion Yamakasa. It is a major economic centre for western Japan, with Fukuoka Airport and the Port of Hakata providing international links, and it consistently ranks highly in Japanese quality-of-life surveys.
