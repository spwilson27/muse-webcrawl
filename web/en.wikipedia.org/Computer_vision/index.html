Computer vision

Computer vision tasks include methods for acquiring, processing, analysing and understanding digital images, and the extraction of high-dimensional data from the real world in order to produce numerical or symbolic information — for example in the form of decisions. It is a field of artificial intelligence (AI) that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world. Using digital images from cameras and videos and deep learning models, machines can accurately identify and classify objects, and then react to what they "see".

Object detection, a central computer vision task.

Tasks

Typical tasks include image classification (assigning a label to a whole image), object detection (locating objects with bounding boxes), semantic and instance segmentation (labelling each pixel), face recognition, optical character recognition, motion analysis and tracking, 3D reconstruction from multiple views, and image restoration. Video analysis adds temporal dimensions such as action recognition.

History

Computer vision began in the 1960s as an academic exercise — famously, a 1966 MIT summer project that expected undergraduates to solve much of the problem in one season. Progress came through edge detection, feature descriptors such as SIFT (1999) and HOG (2005), and statistical classifiers. The field was transformed after 2012, when the deep convolutional network AlexNet decisively won the ImageNet image-classification challenge; deep learning on large labelled datasets and GPUs then drove rapid advances in accuracy.

Methods

Modern systems are built on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and, increasingly, vision transformers, trained by supervised learning on millions of labelled images. Key architectures include ResNet, YOLO for real-time detection, and encoder–decoder networks such as U-Net for segmentation. Classical techniques — camera calibration, stereo geometry, optical flow, and structure from motion — remain essential for 3D and robotics applications.

Applications

Computer vision is used in autonomous vehicles, medical image analysis, industrial quality inspection, retail analytics, agriculture, augmented reality, surveillance, and consumer photo organisation. Its use in facial recognition and mass surveillance has raised substantial ethical and regulatory debate.
