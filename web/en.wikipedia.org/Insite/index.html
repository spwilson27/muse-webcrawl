Insite is a supervised injection site in the Downtown Eastside neighbourhood of Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. Opened in 2003, it was the first legal supervised injection facility in North America, providing a hygienic environment where people can inject pre-obtained drugs under the supervision of health care staff as part of a harm reduction strategy.

Operations

Clients bring their own drugs and inject at individual booths with clean equipment, supervised by nurses who can respond to overdoses and provide wound care, counselling, and referrals to detox, treatment, and housing services. The facility also houses Onsite, a detox programme on the floor above.

Legal history

Insite operates under an exemption from federal drug laws. When the federal government declined to renew the exemption in 2008, litigation followed. In 2011, the Supreme Court of Canada ruled in Canada (AG) v PHS Community Services Society that closing Insite would violate the Charter rights of its users, and ordered the exemption continued.

Research and debate

Numerous peer-reviewed studies have reported reductions in public injecting, syringe sharing, and overdose deaths in the surrounding area, without increases in crime or drug use. Critics have disputed some findings and raised concerns about supervised consumption in principle. Insite has served as a model for supervised consumption sites subsequently opened across Canada and internationally.
