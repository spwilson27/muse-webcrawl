Okomfo Anokye

Okomfo Anokye (c. 1655 – c. 1717?) was an influential priest (okomfo) of the Ashanti Empire and a co-founder of the empire alongside King Osei Tutu I. In Ashanti tradition he is credited with calling down the Golden Stool (Sika Dwa) from the sky, the sacred symbol of Ashanti unity and nationhood.

Life

Anokye is said to have been born in Awukugua in the Akuapem area, and to have been a friend of the young Osei Tutu, with whom, according to tradition, he travelled to the court of Denkyira. As high priest and adviser, Anokye guided Osei Tutu in uniting the Akan city-states against Denkyira domination, culminating in the Ashanti victory at the Battle of Feyiase in 1701.

The Golden Stool

At a great durbar in Kumasi, Anokye is said to have summoned the Golden Stool from the heavens; it descended gently onto Osei Tutu's lap amid thunder and a cloud of white dust. Anokye declared that the stool contained the soul (sunsum) of the Ashanti nation, and that its preservation was essential to the health and unity of the people. He also planted a sword in the ground, declaring that if it were ever removed, the Ashanti nation would fall; the sword remains at the site of the Okomfo Anokye Teaching Hospital in Kumasi.

Legacy

Anokye is remembered as the spiritual architect of Ashanti unity. Numerous institutions in Ghana bear his name, including the Okomfo Anokye Teaching Hospital in Kumasi, one of the country's leading medical facilities.
