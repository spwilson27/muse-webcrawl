Textron Inc. is an American industrial conglomerate based in Providence, Rhode Island. Its subsidiaries include Bell Textron (helicopters), Textron Aviation (Cessna and Beechcraft aircraft), E-Z-GO (golf carts), Arctic Cat (snowmobiles), and Kautex (automotive components). Textron is generally credited as the pioneer of the modern unrelated conglomerate model.

History

Textron was founded in 1923 by Royal Little as the Special Yarns Company, a textile business in Boston. During the Second World War it made parachutes and other military textiles. In the 1950s Little deliberately diversified beyond textiles — the source of the name "Textron" (textiles + electronics) — acquiring companies in fields from zippers and antennas to helicopters, in what became a template for conglomerate building later emulated by firms such as ITT and Litton Industries.

Business segments

Textron Aviation, formed in 2014 by merging Cessna and Beechcraft, builds the Citation business jets, King Air turboprops, and the Skyhawk trainer. Bell produces commercial helicopters and military rotorcraft including the V-22 Osprey tiltrotor (with Boeing) and the UH-1Y and AH-1Z. Textron Systems supplies unmanned aircraft such as the Shadow, armored vehicles, and marine craft, while the Industrial segment makes golf carts, turf-care equipment, snowmobiles, and fuel systems.
