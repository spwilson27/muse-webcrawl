Petroleum refining processes

Fractionating columns at an oil refinery.

Petroleum refining processes are the chemical engineering processes and other facilities used in petroleum refineries (oil refineries) to transform crude oil into useful products such as liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), gasoline, kerosene, jet fuel, diesel oil, fuel oils, lubricating oils, and feedstocks for the petrochemical industry.

Distillation

Refining begins with desalting and then atmospheric distillation, in which heated crude oil is fractionated in a tall column into cuts by boiling range: refinery gases and LPG at the top, then naphtha (gasoline blending stock), kerosene, diesel-range gas oil, and heavy atmospheric residue at the bottom. The residue is further separated in a vacuum distillation unit into vacuum gas oil and vacuum residue, avoiding temperatures that would crack the oil.

Conversion processes

Because crude oil contains more heavy material than the market demands, conversion units break large molecules into smaller, more valuable ones:

  • Fluid catalytic cracking (FCC) — cracks gas oil into gasoline and light olefins over a zeolite catalyst
  • Hydrocracking — cracks heavy feeds with hydrogen over a catalyst to make diesel and jet fuel
  • Coking and visbreaking — thermal processes that squeeze additional liquids from residue, leaving petroleum coke
  • Catalytic reforming — rearranges naphtha molecules, raising octane and producing aromatics and hydrogen
  • Alkylation and isomerization — build or rearrange small molecules into high-octane gasoline blendstocks

Treating and finishing

Hydrotreating and hydrodesulfurization use hydrogen and catalysts to remove sulfur, nitrogen, and metals, meeting environmental specifications. Additional steps include solvent extraction and dewaxing for lubricants, asphalt production from residue, sulfur recovery (Claus process), and blending of finished products to specification.
