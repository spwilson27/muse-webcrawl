Group of Lie type

The Dynkin diagrams classify the families of groups of Lie type.

In mathematics, specifically in group theory, the phrase group of Lie type usually denotes a finite group that is closely related to the group of rational points of a reductive linear algebraic group over a finite field. The phrase does not quite have a universally agreed precise definition, but the important collection of finite simple groups of Lie type did have a precise definition, and they make up most of the groups in the classification of finite simple groups.

Construction

The classical examples were given by Leonard Dickson and Joseph Wedderburn in the early 20th century — the projective special linear groups PSL(n,q) and their analogues of the other classical Lie algebras (symplectic, orthogonal) over finite fields. Claude Chevalley's 1955 construction unified the theory: starting from a (complex) semisimple Lie algebra and an admissible lattice, one builds a "Chevalley group" over any field, and taking rational points over a finite field yields, in almost all cases, a finite simple group. Steinberg, Tits and others extended the method to "twisted" groups via automorphisms of the Dynkin diagram, and the exceptional Suzuki and Ree groups arise from special automorphisms in characteristics 2 and 3.

Families

The finite simple groups of Lie type fall into families indexed by the Dynkin diagrams An, Bn, Cn, Dn, E6, E7, E8, F4 and G2, together with their twisted forms (such as the unitary groups from 2An) and the Suzuki–Ree groups. Examples include PSL(2,7) of order 168 and PSL(2,11). Together with cyclic groups, alternating groups and 26 sporadic groups, they complete the classification of finite simple groups, one of the largest theorems in mathematics, proved over the later 20th century.
