Wynkyn de Worde

Diedc. 1534
OccupationPrinter and publisher
ActiveWestminster and Fleet Street, London

Wynkyn de Worde (died c. 1534) was a printer and publisher in London, originally from the Continent (probably Wörth in Alsace), who worked with William Caxton, England's first printer, and succeeded to his business. He is credited with helping transform printing in England from the production of luxury books into a commercial trade aimed at a wider readership.

Career

De Worde worked in Caxton's workshop at Westminster from the 1470s and took over the press after Caxton's death in 1492. Around 1500–1501 he moved his shop to Fleet Street in London, at the sign of the Sun, becoming the first printer to set up there — a street that remained the centre of English printing and journalism for centuries.

Output

He printed more than 700 works, far more than Caxton, including romances, school textbooks, grammars, religious works, and popular literature, often in cheaper formats. He published works by authors such as Stephen Hawes and Robert Copland, employed standardized type and title pages with woodcuts, and is regarded as an important figure in the development of the English book trade.
