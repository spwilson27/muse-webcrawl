Vostok 6

Vostok 6 was the sixth and final crewed flight in the Soviet Union's Vostok programme, launched on 16 June 1963. It carried Valentina Tereshkova, a 26-year-old former textile worker and amateur parachutist, who thereby became the first woman — and the first civilian — to fly in space.

The mission

Vostok 6 flew as one half of a joint mission with Vostok 5, launched two days earlier with Valery Bykovsky aboard; at closest approach the two craft passed within about 5 km of each other. Tereshkova, using the call sign Chaika ("Seagull"), completed 48 orbits in 2 days, 22 hours and 50 minutes — more flight time in one mission than all American astronauts combined up to that point. She conducted biomedical observations, photographed the horizon, and ejected from the capsule during descent to parachute to the ground near Karaganda, as was standard for Vostok flights.

Legacy

Tereshkova remained the only woman to fly a solo space mission for decades and the youngest woman in space at the time. No other woman flew in space until Svetlana Savitskaya in 1982. After the flight Tereshkova became a prominent public figure and politician in the Soviet Union and later Russia.
