Heilongjiang

Heilongjiang (Chinese: 黑龙江; pinyin: Hēilóngjiāng, "Black Dragon River") is a province in northeast China. It is the country's northernmost and easternmost province, bordering Inner Mongolia to the west, Jilin to the south, and Russia (along the Amur and Ussuri rivers) to the north and east. The capital and largest city is Harbin. The province's name derives from the Chinese name for the Amur River.

Geography

Heilongjiang contains extensive plains — the Songnen and Sanjiang plains — among China's most important grain-producing areas, and mountain ranges including the Greater and Lesser Khingan ranges and the Zhangguangcai range. Its climate is strongly continental, with bitterly cold winters; Mohe in the far north is among the coldest inhabited places in China. The province also contains much of China's remaining natural forest and major wetlands such as the Zhalong Nature Reserve, famous for red-crowned cranes.

History

The region was historically home to Tungusic and Mongolic peoples and formed part of the heartland from which the Manchu Qing dynasty emerged. It was opened to Han Chinese migration only late in the Qing era, was contested with Imperial Russia (which seized the lands north of the Amur in 1858–1860), and was occupied by Japan as part of Manchukuo from 1932 to 1945. After 1949 it became a major centre of heavy industry, oil production (the Daqing oilfield, discovered in 1959), and state farming.

Economy

Agriculture — soybeans, maize, rice, and dairy — dominates alongside petroleum, coal, machinery, and food processing. Like the rest of China's northeast "rust belt", the province has faced industrial decline and population outflow in recent decades. Harbin's Ice and Snow Festival draws international visitors each winter.
