Active electronically scanned array

An active electronically scanned array (AESA) is a type of phased array antenna, a computer-controlled array of antennas in which the beam of radio waves can be electronically steered to point in different directions without moving the antennas physically. In an AESA each antenna element is connected to its own solid-state transmit/receive (T/R) module, which amplifies the transmitted signal and amplifies and phase-shifts the received signal independently.

Operation

By controlling the phase of the signal at each of hundreds or thousands of radiating elements, the array forms a beam whose direction and shape are set in software and can change in microseconds. Because power generation is distributed among many low-power modules, the failure of individual modules degrades performance only gradually — "graceful degradation" — rather than disabling the radar.

Applications

AESA radars equip modern fighter aircraft including the F-22 Raptor (AN/APG-77), F-35 (AN/APG-81), Eurofighter Typhoon and Rafale, and naval air-defense systems such as the US Navy's SPY-6 aboard destroyers. The technology is also used in airborne early warning, ground-based air defense, weather radar research and, increasingly, in satellite communications and 5G millimeter-wave base stations.

Comparison with PESA

Unlike a passive electronically scanned array (PESA), which uses a single central transmitter feeding passive phase shifters, an AESA distributes amplification across the aperture. This provides higher effective radiated power, better reliability, multi-beam and interleaved-mode operation, and resistance to jamming, at the cost of greater complexity and price.
