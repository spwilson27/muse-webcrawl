White-eared opossum

The white-eared opossum (Didelphis albiventris) is an opossum species found in South America, including Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Paraguay, Peru, and Uruguay. It is a terrestrial and occasionally arboreal marsupial, recognisable by the white fur on its ears.

Description

Adults weigh roughly 0.5 to 2 kilograms. The coarse fur is grey to black with a white underbelly, and there are dark facial markings around the eyes. As with other opossums, females have a pouch in which the young develop after a gestation of only about two weeks.

Habitat and behaviour

The species occupies a wide range of habitats, from grassland and forest edge to urban areas, where it is a familiar nocturnal scavenger. It is omnivorous, feeding on invertebrates, small vertebrates, fruit, and carrion. When threatened, it may bare its teeth and hiss, or feign death.

Ecology

White-eared opossums are important seed dispersers and also prey on venomous snakes and scorpions, to whose venom they show partial resistance. Populations are stable and the species is listed as Least Concern.
