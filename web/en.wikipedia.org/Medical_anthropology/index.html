Medical anthropology

Medical anthropology is a subfield of anthropology that studies health, illness, healing and medical systems in cultural and social context. It draws on social, cultural, biological and linguistic anthropology.

Topics

Medical anthropologists examine how different societies explain disease — from germ theory to spirit possession — how patients experience illness, traditional and complementary healing, medical pluralism, and the social determinants of health. Figures such as Arthur Kleinman distinguished disease (biological pathology) from illness (lived experience), while Paul Farmer applied the concept of structural violence to global health inequalities.

Applications

The field informs public health programmes, epidemic response, and culturally competent clinical care.
