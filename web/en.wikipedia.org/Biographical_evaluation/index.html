Biographical evaluation

Biographical evaluation is the systematic assessment of a person's life, career and accomplishments, used in contexts such as personnel selection, credential review, historical scholarship, and the vetting of candidates for appointments, awards or security clearances.

In personnel selection

Employers and researchers use biographical data ("biodata") — structured information about past behaviour, education, work history and experiences — to predict future job performance. Biodata inventories score verifiable life-history items and have shown predictive validity, though they raise fairness, privacy and faking concerns and must be validated for each use.

In scholarship and reference publishing

Editors of biographical dictionaries and encyclopedias evaluate notability, sources and reliability when deciding inclusion and treatment of subjects. Evaluation involves corroborating dates and claims against primary records, obituaries, institutional archives and reputable secondary sources.

In vetting and due diligence

Governments and organizations evaluate nominees' biographies for conflicts of interest, qualifications and integrity, combining questionnaires, background investigations and public-record checks. Standards vary by role and jurisdiction.

Criticism

Critics note risks of bias toward conventional career paths, errors propagated between reference works, and privacy harms. Transparent criteria and source citation mitigate these problems.
