Lawren Harris

Harris painted stark, stylized northern Canadian landscapes

Lawren Stewart Harris (October 23, 1885 – January 29, 1970) was a Canadian painter and a founding member of the Group of Seven. He is credited with changing the course of Canadian art through his bold, stylized depictions of the northern Ontario landscape, Lake Superior, the Rocky Mountains, and the Arctic.

Early life

Harris was born in Brantford, Ontario, into the wealthy Massey-Harris manufacturing family. He studied art in Berlin from 1904 to 1907 and travelled in Europe and the Middle East before returning to Toronto, where he became a central figure in the Arts and Letters Club and co-financed the Studio Building with Dr. James MacCallum to support Canadian artists.

Career

In 1920 Harris co-founded the Group of Seven, whose first exhibition that year marked a turning point in Canadian art. His work progressively simplified toward near-abstract compositions of mountains and ice, influenced by his interest in theosophy and spiritual ideas. Later in life he moved to Vancouver and became a leader of abstract painting in Canada.

Legacy

Harris's paintings command the highest prices for Canadian art; his canvas Mountain Forms (1926) sold at auction in 2016 for over 1 million, a record at the time. His work is held by major collections including the Art Gallery of Ontario and the National Gallery of Canada.
