Darjeeling

Tea gardens below Darjeeling, with Kangchenjunga in the distance

Darjeeling is a town and municipality in the Indian state of West Bengal, in the foothills of the Himalayas at an elevation of about 2,000 metres. It is internationally famous for Darjeeling tea, for the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway — a UNESCO World Heritage narrow-gauge line nicknamed the "Toy Train" — and for its views of Kangchenjunga, the world's third-highest mountain.

History

The name Darjeeling derives from the Tibetan Dorje-ling, "place of the thunderbolt". The area was part of the kingdom of Sikkim until the British East India Company acquired it in 1835 and developed it as a hill station and sanatorium for British troops. Tea cultivation, introduced in the 1840s and 1850s, transformed the local economy; the town also became the summer residence of the Governor of Bengal. Waves of migration brought a predominantly Nepali-speaking Gorkha population, alongside Lepcha, Tibetan, Bengali and other communities.

Tea

Darjeeling tea, grown in some 87 gardens at high altitude, is prized for its light body and muscatel flavour and was the first Indian product to receive a Geographical Indication tag. The harvest is divided into first, second and autumn flushes, each with a distinctive character.

Tourism and transport

Landmarks include Tiger Hill, famous for sunrise views over Kangchenjunga and Everest; the Himalayan Mountaineering Institute, founded by Tenzing Norgay; the Padmaja Naidu Himalayan Zoological Park; and numerous colonial-era schools and churches. The town is reached by road from Siliguri and by the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, opened in 1881.

Politics

Darjeeling has been the centre of the Gorkhaland movement, which seeks a separate state for the Indian Gorkha population; since 2017 the area has been administered by the Gorkhaland Territorial Administration.
