Regnal year

A regnal year is a year counted from the accession of a monarch, used to date documents, laws, and events during that ruler's reign — for example, "the third year of King Henry VIII". Regnal years were the standard dating system in many monarchies before the widespread adoption of the Anno Domini era.

Use

In England, statutes are still formally cited by the regnal year(s) of the monarch in whose reign they were passed (such as "5 & 6 Edw. 8"). Japan continues to date officially by era names tied to emperors, and regnal dating has a long history in ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, and Korea, where king lists organized by regnal years are key sources for chronology.

Counting

Systems differ on whether the accession year counts as year one (non-accession-year system) or year zero until the first anniversary or new year (accession-year system), a distinction important in reconstructing ancient chronologies.
