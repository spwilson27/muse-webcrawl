Drop cap

Drop cap
Also calledInitial cap; dropped initial
TypeTypographic ornament
DepthCommonly 2–4 lines of text
Medieval formHistoriated / inhabited initial

A drop cap or dropped capital is a large capital letter at the beginning of a paragraph or section that descends two or more lines below the baseline of the first line, with the following lines of text wrapped around it. The device signals the start of a chapter or article and lends visual weight and ornament to the opening words.

History

Enlarged initials are as old as the codex: early medieval scribes painted initials in contrasting colours, and in illuminated manuscripts the opening letter grew into elaborate historiated initials containing scenes, or inhabited initials filled with figures and foliage. Printers inherited the practice, leaving blank spaces in early printed books for rubricators to fill by hand, before casting ornamental initial blocks in woodcut and metal. In the 19th century, publishers such as William Morris revived elaborate initials in reaction to industrial printing.

Modern use

Drop caps survive in novels, magazines, and newspapers to mark chapter and article openings, and in web typography, where CSS provides the ::first-letter pseudo-element and the developing initial-letter property. Typesetters distinguish the drop cap, whose baseline sits on a lower line of the paragraph, from the raised cap, an enlarged initial that sits on the first line's baseline.
