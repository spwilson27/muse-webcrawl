Donner Lake

Location: Nevada County, California, U.S.
Coordinates: 39°19′22″N 120°15′52″W
Surface elevation: 5,936 ft (1,809 m)
Max. depth: 328 ft (100 m)

Donner Lake, formerly known as Truckee Lake, is a freshwater lake in Northeast California on the eastern slope of the Sierra Nevada, just west of the town of Truckee in Nevada County. It is about 20 miles (32 km) northwest of Lake Tahoe. A moraine serves as a natural dam for the lake. The lake is a popular summer recreation destination.

Name and the Donner Party

The lake is named for the ill-fated Donner Party, a group of American pioneers who were trapped near the lake by early snowfall in the winter of 1846–1847. Snowbound at camps near the lake and at Alder Creek, about half the party died of starvation and exposure, and some survivors resorted to cannibalism. The site is now preserved within Donner Memorial State Park, which includes the Emigrant Trail Museum and the Pioneer Monument, dedicated in 1918.

Geography

The lake is about 2.7 miles (4.3 km) long and 0.6 miles (1 km) wide, at an elevation of about 5,936 feet (1,809 m). Donner Creek drains the lake eastward into the Truckee River. Interstate 80 and the historic route of the Lincoln Highway run along its northern shore, and Donner Pass lies immediately to the west.

Recreation

Donner Lake offers boating, fishing for kokanee salmon and lake trout, swimming, and camping. A public beach and boat launch are located at its west end, and the state park provides sites on its eastern shore. The lake record for lake (Mackinaw) trout in California was caught at Donner Lake.
