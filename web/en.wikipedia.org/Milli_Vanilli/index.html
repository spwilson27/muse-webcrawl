Milli Vanilli was a German R&B duo created by producer Frank Farian and fronted by dancers and models Fab Morvan (born 1966) and Rob Pilatus (1964–1998). Their debut album Girl You Know It's True (1989) produced three US number-one singles and won the Grammy Award for Best New Artist in 1990. Later that year it was revealed that Morvan and Pilatus had not sung a note on their records — the vocals belonged to session singers — and the Grammy was revoked, the first and only such revocation in Grammy history. The scandal became the most famous lip-syncing affair in popular music.

Pilatus (left) and Morvan were the public faces of Milli Vanilli.

Rise to fame

Farian, the producer behind Boney M., assembled the project in Munich in 1988, recording with studio vocalists including Charles Shaw, John Davis and Brad Howell, then recruiting Morvan (from Paris) and Pilatus (from Munich) as the act's visual frontmen. The single "Girl You Know It's True" became a huge European hit and, after signing to Arista Records in the US, the pair became superstars: the album went six-times platinum in America and "Baby Don't Forget My Number", "Girl I'm Gonna Miss You" and "Blame It on the Rain" all topped the Billboard Hot 100. Suspicions grew when the two struggled with English in interviews and when, in July 1989, a backing tape skipped during a live MTV performance in Bristol, Connecticut, repeating a phrase of "Girl You Know It's True".

The scandal

In November 1990, under pressure from Morvan and Pilatus — who wanted to sing on a follow-up — Farian publicly admitted that the duo had never sung on the records. Arista deleted the album from its catalogue and settled consumer class-action lawsuits offering refunds. The National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences revoked their Best New Artist Grammy. The actual singers later released an album as "The Real Milli Vanilli" with modest European success, while Morvan and Pilatus's comeback album as Rob & Fab (1993) flopped.

Aftermath

Pilatus struggled with addiction and legal troubles and died of a drug overdose in Frankfurt on 2 April 1998, on the eve of a planned reunion album, Back and in Attack, which was never released. Morvan rebuilt a career as a singer, songwriter and public speaker, performing the duo's songs with vocalist John Davis until Davis's death in 2021. The episode permanently changed industry attitudes to lip-syncing and is frequently cited in discussions of authenticity in pop music.
