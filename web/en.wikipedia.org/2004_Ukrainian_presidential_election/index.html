The 2004 Ukrainian presidential election was held to choose the successor to President Leonid Kuchma. The contest between pro-Western reformist Viktor Yushchenko and Prime Minister Viktor Yanukovych, backed by the outgoing administration and Russia, became one of the defining political crises of post-Soviet Europe.

Campaign and poisoning

Yushchenko led the first round on 31 October 2004. During the campaign he fell seriously ill; medical examinations later confirmed he had been poisoned with a dioxin (TCDD), which left his face disfigured. The runoff on 21 November officially gave victory to Yanukovych, but international observers and exit polls reported widespread fraud.

Orange Revolution

The announced result triggered mass protests in Kyiv known as the Orange Revolution, named after Yushchenko's campaign colour. Hundreds of thousands of protesters occupied Independence Square. On 3 December the Supreme Court of Ukraine annulled the runoff result, and a repeat vote on 26 December, conducted under amended election laws, was won by Yushchenko with about 52 percent of the vote. He was inaugurated on 23 January 2005.
