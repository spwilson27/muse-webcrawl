Handloading

ActivityAssembling firearm cartridges by hand
ComponentsCase, primer, propellant, bullet
EquipmentLoading press, dies, scale, powder measure

Handloading, or reloading, is the practice of assembling firearm cartridges by hand, either by refilling fired cartridge cases or by combining new components. Enthusiasts handload to reduce cost, to tailor ammunition to a particular firearm for improved accuracy, to produce obsolete or specialised cartridges, and for the satisfaction of the craft itself.

Process

The process generally involves cleaning and inspecting the case, resizing it in a die, removing the spent primer and seating a new one, charging the case with a measured quantity of smokeless powder, and seating and crimping a bullet. Loads are developed from published data in reloading manuals, with careful attention to maximum pressures; progressive presses automate several steps for high-volume loading.

Safety and legality

Because errors in powder type or charge can cause dangerous overpressure, handloading demands strict adherence to published data and meticulous practice. The legality of handloading and the storage of components varies by country; in some jurisdictions primers and powders are regulated and records or licences are required.
