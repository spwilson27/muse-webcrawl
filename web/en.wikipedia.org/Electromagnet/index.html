Electromagnet

An electromagnet is a magnet whose magnetic field is produced by an electric current. The field disappears when the current is turned off, distinguishing electromagnets from permanent magnets.

The simplest electromagnet is a coil of wire (a solenoid), often wound around a core of ferromagnetic material such as soft iron, which greatly strengthens the field. The field strength depends on the current, the number of turns, and the core material. William Sturgeon built the first practical electromagnet in 1825, and Joseph Henry developed powerful lifting magnets in the United States.

Applications

Electromagnets are essential in electric motors, generators, relays, loudspeakers, magnetic locks, scrapyard cranes, MRI scanners, and particle accelerators. Superconducting electromagnets produce the strongest steady fields.
