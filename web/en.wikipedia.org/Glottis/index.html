Glottis

The glottis is the opening between the vocal folds (the rima glottidis) in the larynx. It is the primary valve controlling airflow in and out of the respiratory passages. The size and shape of the glottis change with the movements of the arytenoid cartilages, and these changes are fundamental to phonation — the production of voice.

Structure

The glottis is divided into an intermembranous part, between the vocal folds proper, and an intercartilaginous part, between the arytenoid cartilages. When the vocal folds are abducted during breathing, the glottis takes a triangular shape; during phonation the folds are brought together and vibrate as air passes through the narrowed opening.

Function

Beyond voice production, the glottis protects the lower airway: reflexive glottic closure is a key part of coughing and of the swallowing mechanism that keeps food and liquid out of the trachea. Disorders affecting glottic function include vocal fold paralysis, laryngospasm and glottic stenosis. The term glottis also appears in linguistics, where "glottal" sounds are those articulated at the glottis, such as the glottal stop.
