Aviation accidents and incidents

[Image placeholder: accident investigators examining wreckage]

An aviation accident is defined by the Convention on International Civil Aviation as an occurrence associated with the operation of an aircraft in which a person is fatally or seriously injured, or the aircraft sustains damage or structural failure requiring repair. An aviation incident is an occurrence, other than an accident, that affects or could affect safety. Air travel remains one of the safest modes of transport per passenger-kilometre, and accident rates have fallen steadily for decades.

Causes

Investigations typically classify causal factors under human factors, mechanical failure, weather, air traffic control, and deliberate acts. Historically, pilot error has been cited as a contributing factor in roughly half of accidents; controlled flight into terrain, loss of control in flight, and runway excursions are among the most common accident categories. Modern safety philosophy emphasises systemic causes — training, procedures, organisational culture — rather than individual blame.

Investigation

Under ICAO Annex 13, the state where an accident occurs leads an independent investigation whose sole objective is prevention, not apportioning blame. Investigators rely heavily on the flight data recorder and cockpit voice recorder (the "black boxes"), radar data, wreckage examination and witness testimony, and publish a final report with safety recommendations.

Notable accidents

The deadliest accident in aviation history was the 1977 Tenerife airport disaster, in which two Boeing 747s collided on the runway, killing 583 people. Japan Airlines Flight 123 (1985) remains the deadliest single-aircraft accident, with 520 deaths. Accidents such as these drove major reforms in crew resource management, maintenance practice and air traffic procedures.
