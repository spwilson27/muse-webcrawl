Nino Cocchiarella (born 1933) is an American philosopher and logician, professor emeritus at Indiana University Bloomington. He is known for his work in philosophical logic, the philosophy of language, and metaphysics, especially his defence of 'conceptual realism' about universals and his development of intensional logics and formal ontology.

Cocchiarella studied at UCLA, where his teachers included Rudolf Carnap and Richard Montague, and he was influenced by the historical-logical scholarship of his mentor. He has written extensively on Bertrand Russell's logical atomism, predication theory, tense and modal logic, and the logic of natural language.

Work

His books include Logical Investigations of Predication Theory and the Problem of Universals (1986) and Formal Ontology and Conceptual Realism (2007). Cocchiarella's system treats concepts as mind-independent, predicable entities that can be both meanings of predicates and objects of thought, offering a middle path between Platonism and nominalism. He has also contributed to the theory of reference, identity, and the semantics of fiction.
