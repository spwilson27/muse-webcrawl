Laying hen

A laying hen is a female chicken (Gallus gallus domesticus) kept primarily for egg production. Modern commercial hybrids — white Leghorn-derived layers for white eggs and Rhode Island Red-derived crosses for brown eggs — can lay around 300 eggs in their first laying year, far more than heritage breeds.

Husbandry

Hens begin laying at about 18–20 weeks ("point of lay" pullets) and are usually kept for one to two laying cycles, sometimes with induced moulting. Housing systems range from conventional battery cages — banned in the EU since 2012 in favour of "enriched" colony cages — to barn, free-range and organic systems, each the subject of animal-welfare debate over stocking density, beak trimming and keel-bone damage.

End of lay

"Spent hens" produce tougher meat and are often processed into soup, pet food or rendered products; rescue and rehoming of ex-commercial hens as backyard pets has become popular in some countries.
