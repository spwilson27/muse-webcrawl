Martial law in the Philippines

Martial law in the Philippines (Filipino: Batas Militar sa Pilipinas) refers to the various periods in Philippine history when the country was placed under military rule by the head of state. The most consequential and controversial instance was the nationwide martial law declared by President Ferdinand Marcos through Proclamation No. 1081, signed on 21 September 1972 and announced on 23 September 1972, which ushered in fourteen years of authoritarian rule in the Philippines.

The Marcos declaration

Marcos justified Proclamation No. 1081 by citing the threat of communist insurgency by the newly founded Communist Party of the Philippines and its armed wing, the New People's Army, as well as the Moro separatist movement in Mindanao and a series of bombings in Manila, including the disputed Plaza Miranda bombing of 1971. Under martial law, Marcos ruled by decree, dissolved Congress, shuttered or seized independent media, imposed curfews, and ordered the arrest of opposition figures including Senator Benigno Aquino Jr., journalists, and activists.

Human rights abuses

Amnesty International and the Philippine government's own Human Rights Victims' Claims Board have documented widespread abuses during the period: roughly 3,257 extrajudicial killings, some 35,000 cases of torture, and around 70,000 illegal arrests are commonly cited figures. Detainees were held in military camps; prominent victims included the murdered labour leader and the student Liliosa Hilao, the first detainee to die in custody. The regime is also associated with large-scale corruption and the accumulation of an estimated billions of dollars in ill-gotten wealth by the Marcos family and their cronies.

End and legacy

Marcos formally lifted martial law on 17 January 1981 with Proclamation No. 2045, ahead of a papal visit, but retained sweeping decree powers. His rule ended in February 1986 with the People Power Revolution, which forced the Marcos family into exile in Hawaii. The 1987 Constitution, drafted in reaction to the experience, places strict limits on martial law: it may last no more than 60 days without congressional approval, and Congress may revoke it.

Martial law was declared again, on a regional basis, when President Rodrigo Duterte placed Mindanao under martial law in May 2017 during the Battle of Marawi; it remained in force there until the end of 2019.
