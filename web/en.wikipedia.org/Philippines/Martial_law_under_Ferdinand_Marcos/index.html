Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos refers to the period of Philippine history in which the country was placed under martial law by President Ferdinand Marcos through Proclamation No. 1081, signed on 21 September 1972 and announced to the public two days later. Marcos justified the measure by citing the communist and Moro insurgencies and civil unrest, and used it to rule by decree, closing Congress, muzzling the press and arresting opposition figures, journalists, students and activists.

Human rights record

Amnesty International and the later Philippine Human Rights Victims' Claims Board documented thousands of cases of extrajudicial killing, enforced disappearance, torture and arbitrary detention during the Marcos years; some 70,000 people were imprisoned and about 34,000 tortured according to commonly cited estimates. A 1973 constitution and controlled referendums kept Marcos in power as authoritarian rule continued even after martial law was formally lifted in 1981.

End of the regime

The assassination of opposition leader Benigno Aquino Jr. in 1983 galvanized opposition, and the disputed 1986 snap election led to the People Power Revolution, which drove Marcos into exile in Hawaii and restored democracy under Corazon Aquino.
