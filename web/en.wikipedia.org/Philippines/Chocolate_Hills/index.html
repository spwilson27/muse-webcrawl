Chocolate Hills

Chocolate Hills
Location: Bohol, Philippines
Number of hills: 1,268 to 1,776
Height: 30 to 120 m
Status: National Geological Monument; UNESCO World Heritage tentative list

The Chocolate Hills are a geological formation in Bohol province in the Philippines. There are at least 1,260 hills (and possibly up to 1,776) spread over an area of more than 50 square kilometres. They are covered in green grass that turns chocolate-brown during the dry season, giving the formation its name.

Formation

The hills are conical karst mounds formed from marine limestone uplifted from the sea and shaped by millions of years of rainwater erosion. They are remarkably uniform in shape and size, ranging from 30 to 120 metres high, and are composed of grass-covered limestone with few trees on their slopes.

Legends and tourism

Local legends explain the hills as the dung of a giant carabao, or the tears of a grieving giant, or the result of a battle between two giants hurling boulders. The main viewing deck at Carmen, Bohol, reached by a 214-step climb, is one of the Philippines' most visited viewpoints; the hills also appear on the Bohol provincial flag and seal. In 2024, controversy erupted over a resort built among the hills in a protected area, prompting national debate over conservation enforcement.
