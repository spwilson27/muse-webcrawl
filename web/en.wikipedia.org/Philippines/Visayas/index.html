The Visayas is one of the three principal island groups of the Philippines, together with Luzon and Mindanao. Located in the central part of the archipelago, it consists of several large islands—Panay, Negros, Cebu, Bohol, Leyte, and Samar—surrounded by smaller ones, and is divided into Western, Central, and Eastern Visayas regions.

Geography

The Visayan islands are separated by shallow seas including the Visayan Sea, Tañon Strait, and Sibuyan Sea. Bohol is famous for the Chocolate Hills, a formation of more than a thousand cone-shaped limestone mounds. The region is exposed to typhoons crossing from the Pacific, most notoriously Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda) in 2013, which devastated Leyte and Samar.

History

Visayan polities traded with China and Southeast Asia before Spanish colonization; Ferdinand Magellan was killed at the Battle of Mactan on Cebu's offshore island in 1521 by warriors under Lapulapu. Spanish settlement of the Philippines began in Cebu in 1565. The Visayas became a center of the sugar industry, especially on Negros, and played a major role in the Philippine Revolution and the Second World War, including the Battle of Leyte Gulf in 1944, among the largest naval battles in history.

Culture

Major festivals include Cebu's Sinulog, Iloilo's Dinagyang, and Bacolod's MassKara. Cebu lechon is renowned nationwide. Cebuano is the second most widely spoken language in the Philippines.
