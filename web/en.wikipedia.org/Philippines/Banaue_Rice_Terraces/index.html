Banaue Rice Terraces

Banaue Rice Terraces
Location: Banaue, Ifugao, Philippines
Built by: Ifugao people
Age: Traditionally ~2,000 years
Part of: Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras (UNESCO)

The Banaue Rice Terraces (Filipino: Hagdan-hagdang Palayan ng Banawe) are terraces carved into the mountains of Ifugao province in the Philippines by the ancestors of the indigenous Ifugao people. Often called the "Eighth Wonder of the World," the terraces are fed by an ancient irrigation system drawing water from rainforests above them.

Description

The terraces are located approximately 1,500 metres (4,900 ft) above sea level and cover some 10,360 square kilometres of mountainside in aggregate across the Cordilleras. They were built largely by hand with minimal equipment, using stone and mud walls to create level pond fields on steep slopes. Five terrace clusters — including Batad and Bangaan near Banaue — form part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 1995 as the Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras, the first property in the cultural landscape category.

Cultural significance

The terraces embody the Ifugao's traditional tinawon rice culture, maintained through communal rituals and transmitted knowledge. They appeared on the reverse of the Philippine twenty-peso note. Threats include erosion, giant earthworm damage, rural-to-urban migration of young Ifugao, and climate change; conservation programs work with local communities to maintain the walls and irrigation systems.
