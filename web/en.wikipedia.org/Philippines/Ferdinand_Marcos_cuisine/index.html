Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos Sr. (September 11, 1917 – September 28, 1989) was a Filipino politician, lawyer, and dictator who served as the tenth president of the Philippines from 1965 to 1986. He ruled under martial law from 1972 until 1981 and kept most of his powers until he was deposed in 1986, a period marked by widespread corruption, human rights abuses, and the accumulation of vast personal wealth by the Marcos family, even as the regime staged lavish state banquets and promoted Filipino cuisine and culture as part of its image-building.

Early life and political rise

Marcos was born in Sarrat, Ilocos Norte, studied law at the University of the Philippines, and topped the bar examinations while under indictment — later overturned — for the murder of his father's political rival. He served in the House of Representatives and the Senate, becoming Senate president, before winning the presidency in 1965 and re-election in 1969.

Martial law

Citing communist and separatist threats, Marcos declared martial law in September 1972, dissolving Congress, arresting opposition figures and journalists, and ruling by decree. His "New Society" program combined infrastructure building and rice self-sufficiency drives with crony capitalism and repression carried out by the military and constabulary.

Downfall and exile

The assassination of opposition leader Benigno Aquino Jr. in 1983 galvanized opposition. After a disputed snap election in February 1986, the People Power Revolution forced the Marcoses to flee to Hawaii aboard American aircraft. Marcos died in exile in Honolulu in 1989; investigations estimated the family's ill-gotten wealth in the billions of dollars, much of it pursued for decades by the Presidential Commission on Good Government.
