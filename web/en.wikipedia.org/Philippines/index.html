The Philippines is an archipelago of about 7,641 islands.

The Philippines (Filipino: Pilipinas), officially the Republic of the Philippines, is an archipelagic country in Southeast Asia. In the western Pacific Ocean, it consists of about 7,641 islands, which are broadly categorized in three main geographical divisions from north to south: Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao. With a population of over 110 million, it is the world's twelfth-most-populous country.

History

The archipelago was settled by Austronesian peoples and developed trading polities linked to China, India and the Malay world; Islam spread to the south from the 14th century. In 1521 Ferdinand Magellan reached the islands for Spain, and Miguel López de Legazpi established Spanish rule from 1565, naming the islands for King Philip II. Spanish colonization lasted more than 300 years, making the Philippines predominantly Roman Catholic.

The Philippine Revolution began in 1896; the First Philippine Republic was proclaimed in 1898, but Spain ceded the islands to the United States after the Spanish–American War. The Philippine–American War followed. The Philippines became a US commonwealth in 1935, was occupied by Japan during World War II, and gained full independence on 4 July 1946. Postwar history includes the authoritarian rule of Ferdinand Marcos (1965–1986), ended by the People Power Revolution, and a return to democratic government.

Geography

The Philippines lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire, with active volcanoes such as Mayon and Taal, frequent typhoons, and extensive coral reefs. The two largest islands, Luzon and Mindanao, account for about two-thirds of the land area. Metro Manila, on Luzon, is one of the world's most populous metropolitan areas.

Economy and culture

The economy is driven by services, business process outsourcing, electronics exports, agriculture and remittances from millions of overseas Filipino workers. Filipino culture blends Austronesian, Spanish, Chinese and American influences; basketball is the most popular sport, and festivals (fiestas) are central to community life.
