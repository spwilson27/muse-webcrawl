Intramuros

Intramuros
Location: Manila, Philippines
Area: 0.67 km²
Founded: 24 June 1571
Governed by: Intramuros Administration

Intramuros (Latin for "within the walls") is the 0.67-square-kilometre (0.26 sq mi) historic walled area within the city of Manila, the capital of the Philippines. It was the seat of government and political power during the Spanish colonial period, when the Philippines was a Captaincy General of the Spanish Empire, and is administered today by the Intramuros Administration, an agency of the Department of Tourism.

History

The walled city was founded by the Spanish conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi in 1571 on the site of the earlier Tagalog settlement of Maynila, near the mouth of the Pasig River. Its massive stone fortifications — several metres thick and some 4.5 kilometres in circuit — were built in the late 16th century to defend the Spanish seat of power against foreign invaders, Chinese pirates such as Limahong, and Moro raiders. Within the walls stood the Manila Cathedral, the churches of religious orders, government offices, palaces, schools, and the residences of Spanish officials and clergy.

Intramuros was the exclusive preserve of the Spanish ruling classes; native Filipinos and Chinese were largely required to live outside, in districts such as Binondo and Paco. The city was occupied by the British from 1762 to 1764 during the Seven Years' War, and by American forces in 1898 at the start of the American colonial period.

Destruction and restoration

During the Battle of Manila in February–March 1945, Intramuros was almost completely destroyed by American shelling and Japanese resistance; of its pre-war structures only San Agustín Church survived substantially intact. After the war the area was partly rebuilt, and beginning with the creation of the Intramuros Administration in 1979, the walls, gates (puertas), and bastions have been progressively restored. Landmark sites include Fort Santiago, where the national hero José Rizal was imprisoned before his execution in 1896, the Casa Manila museum, and the reconstructed San Ignacio Church.
