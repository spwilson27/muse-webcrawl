People Power Revolution
Date: 22–25 February 1986
Location: Metro Manila, Philippines
Result: Marcos regime overthrown; Corazon Aquino sworn in as president

The People Power Revolution, also known as the EDSA Revolution, was a series of popular demonstrations in the Philippines, mostly in Metro Manila, from 22 to 25 February 1986. The mostly nonviolent protests, involving over two million civilians as well as political, military, and religious groups, ended the twenty-year authoritarian rule of President Ferdinand Marcos and restored democracy to the country.

Background

Opposition to the Marcos regime, which had ruled under martial law from 1972 to 1981 (see Martial law in the Philippines), crystallised after the assassination of opposition leader Benigno Aquino Jr. at Manila airport on 21 August 1983. Under American pressure, Marcos called a snap presidential election for 7 February 1986 against Aquino's widow, Corazon Aquino. The official count proclaimed Marcos the winner amid widespread allegations of fraud; the independent watchdog NAMFREL's parallel count showed Aquino ahead, and the disputed result split the military.

The four days

On 22 February, Defence Minister Juan Ponce Enrile and Armed Forces Vice Chief of Staff Fidel V. Ramos broke with Marcos and barricaded themselves in Camps Aguinaldo and Crame along Epifanio de los Santos Avenue (EDSA). Cardinal Jaime Sin broadcast an appeal over Radio Veritas, and hundreds of thousands of civilians — nuns praying the rosary, families, students — formed human barricades around the camps to shield the defectors from loyalist troops. Tanks that advanced on the crowds were stopped by protesters offering flowers and food; helicopter crews sent to attack Camp Crame defected instead, and state television was seized by rebels.

Outcome

On the morning of 25 February, both Aquino and Marcos held rival inaugurations; that evening, as crowds stormed towards Malacañang Palace, the Marcos family was airlifted by US helicopters to Clark Air Base and then into exile in Hawaii. Aquino's government drafted the 1987 Constitution, restoring democratic institutions with safeguards designed to prevent a recurrence of dictatorship. The revolution became an international model of nonviolent regime change, inspiring movements in Eastern Europe and elsewhere.
