Davao City

Davao City
Region: Davao Region, Mindanao, Philippines
Land area: 2,444 km² — largest city in the Philippines by area
Nickname: Durian Capital of the Philippines

Davao City is a highly urbanised city in the Davao Region of Mindanao, Philippines. It is the largest city in the country by land area and the most populous city in Mindanao, serving as the island's main trade, commerce and industry hub. It sits at the foot of Mount Apo, the highest peak in the Philippines.

History and governance

Founded after the Spanish conquest of the area in 1848 and chartered as a city in 1936, Davao grew on abaca and coconut plantations, including a notable pre-war Japanese community. The city became nationally prominent under the long mayoralty of Rodrigo Duterte (1988–2016), whose tough-on-crime policies he later carried to the presidency; the Duterte family has remained dominant in local politics.

Economy and culture

Davao is known as the "Durian Capital of the Philippines" and a major producer of bananas, pomelo and mangosteen; the Kadayawan festival each August celebrates the harvest. Landmarks include the Philippine Eagle Center in Malagos, home of the critically endangered Philippine eagle, Samal Island's resorts across the gulf, and the durian stalls of Magsaysay Park. The city is also noted for its 911 emergency system, smoking ban and fireworks ban.
