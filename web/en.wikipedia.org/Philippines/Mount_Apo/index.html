Mount Apo

Mount Apo
Elevation: 2,954 m (9,692 ft)
Location: Mindanao, Philippines
Type: Stratovolcano (potentially active)
First ascent: 10 October 1880

Mount Apo, also known locally as Apo Sandawa, is a large solfataric, dormant stratovolcano on the island of Mindanao, Philippines. With an elevation of 2,954 metres (9,692 ft) above sea level, it is the highest mountain in the Philippine archipelago and in the country as a whole, overlooking Davao City to the northeast.

Geography and geology

Apo is a flat-topped stratovolcano with three distinct peaks, capped by a wide crater containing a small crater lake. It has no recorded historical eruptions but is considered potentially active, with solfataras and fumaroles near the summit emitting sulphurous gases. The mountain is the source of several rivers and is harnessed for geothermal energy at the Kidapawan geothermal field on its northern flank.

Ecology and conservation

Proclaimed a national park in 1936, Mount Apo Natural Park is a centre of endemism, home to the critically endangered Philippine eagle, more than 270 bird species, and old-growth mossy forests of Philippine oak and other montane species. The mountain is sacred to several indigenous Lumad groups, including the Manobo, Bagobo, and Kagan peoples, for whom Apo Sandawa is an ancestral domain and the dwelling of ancestral spirits.

Climbing

The first recorded successful ascent was made on 10 October 1880 by a party led by Don Joaquín Rajal, governor of Davao, with the permission of the Bagobo datu. Today several trails — from Kidapawan, Kapatagan, and Bansalan — lead to the summit across a distinctive boulder field. Climbing is regulated by permit to limit environmental damage, and the park is periodically closed for rehabilitation.
