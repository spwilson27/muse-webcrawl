Battle of Leyte Gulf

Battle of Leyte Gulf
Date: October 23–26, 1944
Location: Leyte Gulf and surrounding seas, Philippines
Result: Decisive Allied victory

The Battle of Leyte Gulf, fought from October 23 to 26, 1944 in the waters around the Philippine island of Leyte, is generally considered the largest naval battle of the Second World War and possibly the largest naval battle in history. It was fought between combined American and Australian forces and the Imperial Japanese Navy during the liberation of the Philippines.

Course of the battle

The battle consisted of four main engagements: the Battle of the Sibuyan Sea, the Battle of Surigao Strait — history's last battleship-versus-battleship action — the Battle off Cape Engaño, and the Battle off Samar, where the vastly outgunned escort carrier group "Taffy 3" repelled Admiral Kurita's battleship force. Japan's plan, Shō-Gō 1, sought to destroy the American invasion fleet supporting General MacArthur's landings at Leyte, which had begun on October 20.

Outcome and significance

The Imperial Japanese Navy lost four carriers, three battleships (including the giant Musashi), and numerous cruisers and destroyers, and ceased to exist as an effective fighting force. The battle saw the first organised kamikaze attacks. Leyte Gulf secured the American foothold in the Philippines and cut Japan off from the resources of Southeast Asia.
