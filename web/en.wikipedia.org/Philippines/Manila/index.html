Manila, officially the City of Manila, is the capital and second-most populous city of the Philippines, after Quezon City. Located on the eastern shore of Manila Bay on the island of Luzon, it is a highly urbanized city and the historic center of Philippine government, education, and commerce. The greater metropolitan area, Metro Manila, is home to more than 13 million people.

History

Before Spanish colonization, the area was the site of Maynila, a fortified settlement ruled by Rajah Sulayman and trading with China and neighboring polities. Conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi founded Spanish Manila in 1571 on the site of the earlier settlement, building the walled city of Intramuros. Manila became the center of the galleon trade linking Asia with Acapulco and Europe. The city passed to the United States in 1898, was devastated during the Battle of Manila in 1945—one of the worst urban battles of the Pacific War—and was rebuilt as the capital of the independent republic in 1946.

Districts and landmarks

Manila's districts include Intramuros, Binondo (the world's oldest Chinatown), Quiapo, Ermita, Malate, and Tondo. Rizal Park (Luneta), where national hero José Rizal was executed in 1896, lies just south of the walled city. Landmarks include Manila Cathedral, San Agustin Church (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Fort Santiago, and the Cultural Center of the Philippines along the bay.

Economy and transport

The Port of Manila is the country's largest seaport. The city is served by the LRT and MRT rail lines and by Ninoy Aquino International Airport in neighboring Parañaque and Pasay. Traffic congestion and flooding are persistent challenges.
