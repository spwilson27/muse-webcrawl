Mount Pinatubo

Mount Pinatubo
Elevation: 1,485 m (4,872 ft; current)
Location: Luzon, Philippines
Type: Stratovolcano
Last eruption: 1993

Mount Pinatubo is an active stratovolcano in the Zambales Mountains, on the tri-province boundary of Zambales, Tarlac, and Pampanga on the island of Luzon in the Philippines. Its cataclysmic eruption on 15 June 1991 was the second-largest volcanic eruption of the 20th century, surpassed only by the 1912 eruption of Novarupta in Alaska.

The 1991 eruption

Pinatubo had been dormant for roughly 500 years, and its forested slopes were home to several thousand indigenous Aeta people. Earthquakes and steam explosions beginning in April 1991 prompted monitoring by the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology with the US Geological Survey, and escalating warnings led to the evacuation of some 60,000 people — including personnel from the nearby US Clark Air Base — days before the climactic eruption.

The 15 June eruption ejected more than 10 cubic kilometres of material, sent ash as high as 40 kilometres into the stratosphere, and coincided with the landfall of Typhoon Yunya, whose rain saturated the ash and caused roofs across central Luzon to collapse. Despite the eruption's magnitude, deaths were limited to about 847, largely because of the evacuations. The eruption injected roughly 17 million tonnes of sulphur dioxide into the stratosphere, cooling global average temperatures by about 0.5 °C over the following two years.

Aftermath

Secondary mudflows (lahars) triggered by monsoon rains buried towns and farmland for years; the city of Angeles and the former Clark Air Base (now Clark Freeport Zone) were heavily affected. A crater lake, Lake Pinatubo, formed in the new caldera and has become a trekking destination reached via Botolan, Zambales. Tens of thousands of displaced Aeta were resettled, many permanently, ending their traditional life on the mountain.
