Mindanao is the second-largest island in the Philippines, located in the southern part of the archipelago, and the name of one of the country's three island groups, along with Luzon and the Visayas. It is home to a culturally diverse population of Christians, Muslims (Moros), and indigenous Lumad peoples.

Geography

Mindanao has an irregular coastline with several large peninsulas and bays. Mount Apo, at 2,954 meters, is the highest mountain in the Philippines. The Agusan and Pulangi river systems drain broad fertile valleys; Lake Lanao is one of the world's ancient lakes. Because the island lies largely below the typhoon belt, its relatively even rainfall makes it a center for plantation agriculture, including bananas, pineapples, and oil palm.

History

Islam arrived in the region in the 14th and 15th centuries, and sultanates such as Sulu and Maguindanao resisted Spanish conquest for centuries; Spain never fully subdued the Moro sultanates. Under American rule and after independence, government-sponsored migration from Luzon and the Visayas transformed the island's demography. Moro separatist movements, including the Moro National Liberation Front and the Moro Islamic Liberation Front, fought for decades; the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) was established in 2019 as a product of peace agreements.

Economy

Mindanao is a major producer of agricultural exports, nickel and other minerals, and tuna from General Santos City. Major cities include Davao, Cagayan de Oro, Zamboanga, and Cotabato.
