José Rizal

José Rizal
Born: 19 June 1861, Calamba, Laguna
Died: 30 December 1896, Bagumbayan, Manila
Occupation: Writer, ophthalmologist, polymath
Known for: Noli Me Tángere, El filibusterismo

José Protacio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda (19 June 1861 – 30 December 1896) was a Filipino nationalist, writer, and polymath who is widely considered the national hero of the Philippines. His novels Noli Me Tángere (1887) and El filibusterismo (1891) exposed the abuses of the Spanish colonial government and the friar orders, inspiring the Philippine reform movement and, indirectly, the Philippine Revolution.

Life

Rizal was born in Calamba, Laguna, to a prosperous family of mixed Malay, Chinese, Spanish, and Japanese ancestry. A brilliant student, he studied at the Ateneo Municipal de Manila and the University of Santo Tomas before leaving for Spain in 1882, where he completed a medical degree in Madrid and specialised in ophthalmology in Paris and Heidelberg. He was fluent in some twenty-two languages.

In Europe Rizal became a leading figure of the Propaganda Movement, writing for the newspaper La Solidaridad and founding the civic organisation La Liga Filipina upon his return to Manila in 1892. The Spanish authorities immediately exiled him to Dapitan in Mindanao, where he practised medicine, taught, and undertook scientific work for four years.

Arrest and execution

In 1896, as the Katipunan's revolution broke out, Rizal volunteered as a doctor for the Spanish army in Cuba but was recalled mid-voyage, arrested, and tried for rebellion, sedition, and conspiracy. Despite his consistent advocacy of peaceful reform rather than armed revolt, he was convicted and executed by firing squad at Bagumbayan (now Rizal Park) in Manila on 30 December 1896, aged 35. His martyrdom galvanised the revolutionary movement in the Philippines.

Legacy

Rizal's death anniversary, 30 December, is a national holiday. Monuments to him stand in nearly every Philippine town plaza; the Rizal Monument in Luneta, guarded around the clock, marks the site near his execution. His final poem, "Mi último adiós", written on the eve of his death, is a classic of Philippine literature.
