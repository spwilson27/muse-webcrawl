Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos (1917–1989) was a Filipino politician and lawyer who served as the tenth president of the Philippines from 1965 to 1986. His rule, which included a period of martial law from 1972 to 1981, is widely described by historians as authoritarian and marked by corruption, human rights abuses, and the accumulation of vast ill-gotten wealth by his family and cronies.

Early life and career

Marcos was born in Sarrat, Ilocos Norte. He topped the 1939 Philippine bar examinations while under indictment for the 1935 murder of his father's political rival, a conviction later overturned by the Supreme Court. He claimed a decorated guerrilla record during the Second World War, most of which U.S. Army investigations later found to be fraudulent. He served in the House of Representatives and the Senate, becoming Senate President in 1963.

Presidency

Marcos won the 1965 presidential election and was re-elected in 1969 amid allegations of vote-buying and violence. Facing term limits and unrest, he declared martial law in September 1972 under Proclamation No. 1081, dissolving Congress, muzzling the press, and arresting opposition figures including Benigno Aquino Jr. His regime undertook large infrastructure projects financed by foreign debt, while crony monopolies took over major industries. Aquino's assassination in 1983 galvanized the opposition.

Downfall and legacy

After a disputed snap election in February 1986, the People Power Revolution forced Marcos and his family to flee to Hawaii aboard U.S. aircraft. He died in exile in 1989. Courts and the Presidential Commission on Good Government have since recovered billions of pesos of ill-gotten wealth; his widow Imelda and children returned to Philippine politics, and his son Ferdinand "Bongbong" Marcos Jr. was elected president in 2022.
