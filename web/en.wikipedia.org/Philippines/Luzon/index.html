Luzon is the largest and most populous island in the Philippines, and the name also refers to one of the country's three primary island groups, together with the Visayas and Mindanao. Located in the north of the archipelago, it is the seat of the national capital, Manila, and of Quezon City, the country's most populous city. Luzon accounts for more than half of the national population and the bulk of its economic output.

Geography

The island is dominated by mountain ranges including the Cordillera Central, the Sierra Madre, and the Zambales Mountains. Mount Pulag in the Cordillera is the island's highest peak. The Cagayan Valley in the northeast is the country's largest river basin, while the Central Luzon plain is known as the "rice granary" of the Philippines. Active volcanoes include Mayon, Taal, and Pinatubo, whose 1991 eruption was one of the largest of the twentieth century.

History

Luzon was home to trading polities such as Tondo and Maynila that dealt with Chinese and Southeast Asian merchants long before Spanish colonization began in the 1570s. Manila became the capital of the Spanish East Indies and the hub of the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade. The island was the main theater of the Philippine Revolution and of fighting during the Philippine–American War and the Second World War, including the battles for Manila in 1945.

Regions

Administratively, the Luzon island group comprises eight regions, including the Ilocos Region, Cagayan Valley, Central Luzon, Calabarzon, and the National Capital Region. Languages spoken include Tagalog, Ilocano, Kapampangan, Bikol, and Pangasinan.
