Metro Manila

Metro Manila
Officially: National Capital Region (NCR)
Country: Philippines
Cities: 16, plus 1 municipality (Pateros)
Area: 619.57 km²
Population (2020): 13,484,462

Metropolitan Manila, commonly shortened to Metro Manila and officially designated the National Capital Region (NCR), is the seat of government and the most populous region of the Philippines. It is composed of the capital city of Manila, the country's largest city Quezon City, and fourteen other highly urbanised cities — Caloocan, Las Piñas, Makati, Malabon, Mandaluyong, Marikina, Muntinlupa, Navotas, Parañaque, Pasay, Pasig, San Juan, Taguig, and Valenzuela — plus the municipality of Pateros.

Geography and administration

The region occupies the narrow isthmus between Manila Bay to the west and Laguna de Bay to the southeast, through which the Pasig River flows. Created by Presidential Decree No. 824 in 1975 during the Marcos administration, Metro Manila is governed not by an elected regional government but by the Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA), whose chair is appointed by the President; each component city retains its own mayor and council.

Economy and transport

Metro Manila produces roughly a third of the national GDP and hosts the country's principal financial districts: the Makati Central Business District, Bonifacio Global City in Taguig, and Ortigas Center spanning Pasig, Mandaluyong, and Quezon City. The region is served by Ninoy Aquino International Airport and by a growing rail network — the LRT-1, LRT-2, and MRT-3 lines — though chronic traffic congestion on avenues such as EDSA remains one of its defining problems. Jeepneys, buses, and tricycles provide much of its informal public transport.

History

The historic core of the region is Intramuros, the Spanish walled city founded in 1571. Manila grew outward through the American colonial period, survived near-total destruction in the Battle of Manila in 1945, and absorbed its surrounding towns as suburbanisation accelerated in the postwar decades. Quezon City served as the national capital from 1948 until 1976, when the capital formally reverted to Manila.
