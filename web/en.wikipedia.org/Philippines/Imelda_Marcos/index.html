Imelda Marcos

Imelda Romualdez Marcos
Born: July 2, 1929, Manila, Philippines
First Lady of the Philippines: 1965–1986
Spouse: Ferdinand Marcos
Known for: Political power, cultural building projects, shoe collection

Imelda Romualdez Marcos (born July 2, 1929) is a Filipino politician and former First Lady of the Philippines, widow of Ferdinand Marcos, who ruled from 1965 until the People Power Revolution of 1986. One of the most powerful and controversial figures in Philippine history, she wielded formal office as Governor of Metro Manila and Minister of Human Settlements while becoming a global symbol of excess.

Rise to power

Born into the Romualdez clan of Leyte and raised partly in Tacloban, Imelda was crowned "Rose of Tacloban" and worked as a singer and bank clerk in Manila before marrying Ferdinand Marcos in 1954 after an eleven-day courtship. As First Lady she launched an ambitious cultural building program — the Cultural Center of the Philippines, Folk Arts Theater, and other "edifice complex" projects — and conducted informal diplomacy with leaders including Mao Zedong, Fidel Castro and Muammar Gaddafi.

Exile and return

After the Marcoses fled to Hawaii in 1986, investigators documented vast hidden wealth; her abandoned collection of some 1,200 pairs of shoes became an enduring symbol, which she later reframed as the Marikina shoe industry collection. Acquitted in a 1990 New York racketeering trial, she returned to the Philippines in 1991, ran for president in 1992, and later served three terms in the House of Representatives. In 2018 a Sandiganbayan court convicted her of graft, a verdict she appealed. Her children, including President Ferdinand "Bongbong" Marcos Jr. and Senator Imee Marcos, restored the family to the summit of Philippine politics.
