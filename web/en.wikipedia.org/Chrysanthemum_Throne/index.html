Chrysanthemum Throne

The Chrysanthemum Throne (Japanese: 菊花紋章) is the term used to refer to the throne of the Emperor of Japan, and by extension the Japanese monarchy and imperial institution itself. The name derives from the sixteen-petal chrysanthemum emblem, adopted as the crest of the imperial family in the Kamakura period and still used as the Imperial Seal of Japan.

The physical throne

The actual throne used in enthronement ceremonies is the takamikura, an ornate octagonal pavilion-like seat housed in the Shishinden hall of the Kyoto Imperial Palace. During the enthronement ceremony (sokui no rei) the new emperor ascends the takamikura, most recently at the enthronement of Emperor Naruhito in October 2019, when the throne was transported from Kyoto to the Imperial Palace in Tokyo for the occasion.

The institution

Japan's imperial line claims continuity for more than two millennia, with the legendary founding by Emperor Jimmu dated to 660 BC; historical records are firmer from around the sixth century AD. Since the 1947 constitution, the emperor is "the symbol of the State and of the unity of the people", with no powers related to government. Succession is governed by the Imperial Household Law, which currently restricts the throne to male offspring in the male line.
