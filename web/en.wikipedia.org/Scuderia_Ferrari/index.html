Scuderia Ferrari is the racing division of luxury Italian auto manufacturer Ferrari and the name of its Formula One team. Founded by Enzo Ferrari in 1929 in Modena to race Alfa Romeo cars, it began building cars under its own name in 1947. It is the oldest surviving and most successful Formula One team, having competed in every season since the championship began in 1950.

History

Ferrari's early Grand Prix successes came with drivers such as Alberto Ascari, Juan Manuel Fangio, Mike Hawthorn and John Surtees. Niki Lauda won titles in 1975 and 1977. After a long constructors' drought, the team dominated the early 2000s with Michael Schumacher, who won five consecutive drivers' championships from 2000 to 2004; the team took six straight constructors' titles. Kimi Räikkönen won the 2007 drivers' title, and the team its most recent constructors' championship in 2008.

Beyond Formula One

Ferrari has also raced in sports cars, winning the 24 Hours of Le Mans nine times overall in its classic era (1949–1965) and again with the 499P Hypercar in 2023, 2024 and 2025. Its red racing colour, rosso corsa, and the prancing horse emblem — adopted from First World War ace Francesco Baracca — are among the most recognisable symbols in motor sport. The team's passionate fans are known as the tifosi.
