Richard Wallace (MP)

Sir Richard Wallace, 1st Baronet (21 June 1818 – 20 July 1890) was an English art collector, philanthropist, and Conservative Member of Parliament, best remembered for the Wallace Collection in London and the Wallace fountains of Paris.

Life

Wallace was the illegitimate son — and later recognised heir — of Richard Seymour-Conway, 4th Marquess of Hertford, from whom he inherited one of Europe's great private art collections and substantial estates in 1870. He served as MP for Lisburn in Ireland from 1873 to 1885 and was created a baronet in 1871 for his charitable work during the Siege of Paris, where he funded hospitals and relief efforts.

Philanthropy and legacy

Wallace donated the cast-iron drinking fountains that still bear his name across Paris, providing clean water to the poor. He settled in London at Hertford House, and after his death his widow, Lady Wallace, bequeathed the collection of paintings, furniture, porcelain, and arms and armour to the British nation; it opened to the public as the Wallace Collection in 1900.
