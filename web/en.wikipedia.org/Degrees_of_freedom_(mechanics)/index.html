Degrees of freedom (mechanics)

In physics and chemistry, a degree of freedom is an independent physical parameter in the formal description of the state of a physical system. In mechanics, the degrees of freedom of a system are the number of independent parameters needed to specify its configuration completely.

Rigid bodies

A single rigid body moving in three-dimensional space has six degrees of freedom: three translational (movement along the x, y and z axes) and three rotational (rotation about each axis). A particle, having no extent, has only the three translational degrees of freedom. A ship at sea is a classic illustration: it can surge, sway and heave, and roll, pitch and yaw.

Constraints

Joints and connections between bodies impose constraints that reduce the total degrees of freedom. A hinge, for example, permits only rotation about one axis. The mobility of a mechanism can be computed with criteria such as the Grübler–Kutzbach criterion, which relates the number of links and joints to the system's mobility.

Applications

Degrees of freedom are fundamental in robotics, where manipulators are described by the number of independent joint motions; in structural engineering; and in statistical mechanics, where each quadratic term in the energy contributes to the equipartition of energy.
