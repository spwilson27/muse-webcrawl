Mood disorder

A mood disorder, also known as an affective disorder, is a group of conditions of mental health in which the primary underlying problem is a disturbance in a person's emotional state or mood. The classification is in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) and the International Classification of Diseases (ICD).

SpecialtyPsychiatry
TypesDepressive disorders, bipolar disorders
TreatmentPsychotherapy, medication

Classification

Depressive disorders

Major depressive disorder is characterised by episodes of depressed mood and loss of interest lasting at least two weeks. Persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia) involves a chronic, lower-grade depressed mood lasting two years or more.

Bipolar disorders

Bipolar I disorder involves manic episodes, often alternating with depression; bipolar II disorder involves hypomania and major depression. Cyclothymia features chronic fluctuating mood disturbances below the threshold for major episodes.

Causes

Mood disorders arise from a combination of genetic vulnerability, neurochemical factors, early life stress, and medical conditions. Many psychiatric drugs are thought to act on monoamine neurotransmitters such as serotonin and norepinephrine.

Treatment

Treatments include psychotherapies such as cognitive behavioural therapy, antidepressant and mood-stabilising medications, and in severe cases electroconvulsive therapy.
