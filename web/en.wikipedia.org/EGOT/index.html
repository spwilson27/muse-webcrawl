EGOT

EGOT, an acronym for the Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony Awards, is the designation given to people who have won all four of the major American performing-arts awards. The term was coined by actor Philip Michael Thomas in 1984. Fewer than twenty individuals have achieved the honour in competitive categories.

History

Composer Richard Rodgers became the first EGOT winner in 1962, completing the set with a Tony for No Strings. Actresses Helen Hayes and Rita Moreno followed in the 1970s; Moreno was the first Hispanic and first performer to complete the set largely through performing roles. Other winners include John Gielgud, Audrey Hepburn, Marvin Hamlisch, Mel Brooks, Mike Nichols, Whoopi Goldberg, John Legend, Jennifer Hudson and Viola Davis, who completed the set in 2023.

Variations

Some lists include winners of honorary or non-competitive awards (such as Barbra Streisand and Liza Minnelli), and expansions such as PEGOT add a Pulitzer Prize or Peabody Award. Robert Lopez is the only person to have won each award at least twice ("double EGOT"), and the youngest winner.

Cultural references

The concept was popularised for a new generation by the television series 30 Rock, in which Tracy Jordan pursues EGOT status, and it is frequently referenced in entertainment journalism.
