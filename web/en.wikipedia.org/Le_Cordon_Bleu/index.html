Le Cordon Bleu (French for "The Blue Ribbon") is a network of hospitality and culinary education that teaches French haute cuisine through a matching dedication to the culinary arts and service. It is regarded as a leading authority in gastronomy, and its schools form a network present in more than 20 countries with over 35 institutes attended by some 20,000 students annually.

History

The school was founded in Paris in 1895 by journalist Marthe Distel, publisher of the culinary journal La Cuisinière Cordon Bleu. The name itself came from the blue ribbon of the Order of the Holy Spirit, the highest French chivalric order under the Ancien Régime, whose members became synonymous with excellence. Distel started the journal to promote French cooking, and shortly afterwards opened a school at the Palais Royal in Paris. January 1896 saw the first cooking demonstration at the school on an electric stove.

Following Marthe Distel's death, the school continued under her daughter then passed to other owners. In the 20th century it expanded its Paris presence and attracted famous students, including American chef Julia Child, who trained at the Paris school after World War II and later popularized French cooking in the United States.

Programmes

The school is best known for its Diplôme de Cuisine and Diplôme de Pâtisserie, traditionally structured in three levels (basic, intermediate and superior), which can be combined into the Grand Diplôme. Le Cordon Bleu also offers courses in wine, hospitality management, and culinary business, as well as bachelor's and master's degrees in several countries.

International network

Le Cordon Bleu has institutes in cities including London, Madrid, Tokyo, Ottawa, Sydney, Adelaide, Wellington, Seoul, Bangkok, and several locations in the Americas. Each institute carries on the tradition of French technique while adapting to local ingredients and cuisines.
