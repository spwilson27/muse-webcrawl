Military decoy

A military decoy (or dummy) is a fake piece of military equipment, such as an inflatable tank, wooden aircraft, or mock artillery piece, intended to deceive enemy reconnaissance and draw fire away from real assets.

History

Decoys were used extensively in both World Wars. In the Second World War, the British and Americans built dummy airfields, fleets, and entire fictitious formations; the US "Ghost Army" (23rd Headquarters Special Troops) specialized in visual, sonic, and radio deception. Operation Fortitude used inflatable tanks and dummy landing craft to convince Germany that the D-Day invasion would land at Calais.

Modern use

Modern decoys include inflatable vehicles with radar-reflective and heat-generating elements that mimic real signatures, decoy missile launchers, and airborne decoys such as the ADM-160 MALD that imitate combat aircraft on enemy radar.

Effectiveness

Cheap decoys can absorb expensive precision-guided munitions and saturate enemy intelligence, making them a cost-effective element of military deception and survivability.
