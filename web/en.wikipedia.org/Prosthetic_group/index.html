Prosthetic group

A prosthetic group is a non-protein chemical component that is tightly — often covalently — bound to a protein and is essential for the protein's biological activity. A protein without its prosthetic group is called an apoprotein; the complete, functional combination is a holoprotein. Prosthetic groups may be organic molecules or metal ions.

Examples

  • Heme — the iron-containing group of hemoglobin, myoglobin, and cytochromes
  • Retinal — the light-sensitive chromophore of rhodopsin
  • Biotin — covalently attached to carboxylase enzymes
  • Flavin groups (FAD, FMN) — tightly bound in flavoproteins
  • Metal ions — such as zinc in carbonic anhydrase or copper in plastocyanin

Distinction from cofactors and coenzymes

Prosthetic groups differ from loosely bound cofactors and coenzymes in the strength and permanence of their attachment: a prosthetic group remains bound through the enzyme's catalytic cycle and is not released with the products.
