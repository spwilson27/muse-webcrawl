A Clockwork Orange (novel)

A Clockwork Orange
Author: Anthony Burgess
Published: 1962 (Heinemann)
Genre: Dystopian fiction
Language: English with Nadsat slang

A Clockwork Orange is a dystopian satirical novel by English writer Anthony Burgess, published in 1962. It is narrated by Alex, a fifteen-year-old who speaks "Nadsat," a fictional teen slang heavily influenced by Russian, and who leads his gang ("droogs") on nightly sprees of violence in a near-future England.

Plot

Alex and his droogs commit theft, assault, and rape for amusement. Captured after murdering a woman during a home invasion, Alex volunteers for the "Ludovico Technique," an experimental aversion therapy that conditions him to become violently ill at any thought of violence — or of the classical music he loves. Rendered incapable of free choice, he is released, victimized by his former victims and droogs, and used as a political pawn before the treatment's effects fade.

The novel's central question is whether goodness imposed by conditioning is worth the loss of free will. Burgess's UK edition has 21 chapters; the original American edition omitted the final chapter, in which Alex matures and renounces violence — the ending Stanley Kubrick followed in his 1971 film adaptation. The full text was restored in the U.S. in 1986.

Legacy

The novel is regularly ranked among the greatest English-language novels of the 20th century, and its title phrase — "as queer as a clockwork orange" — supplied Burgess a metaphor for an organism that is organic in appearance but mechanical in behavior.
