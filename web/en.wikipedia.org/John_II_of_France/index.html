John II of France

Reign: 22 August 1350 – 8 April 1364
Born: 16 April 1319, Château du Gué-de-Maulny, Le Mans
Died: 8 April 1364 (aged 44), Savoy Palace, London
Predecessor: Philip VI
Successor: Charles V
Spouse: Bonne of Luxembourg; Joan I, Countess of Auvergne

John II (French: Jean II; 16 April 1319 – 8 April 1364), called John the Good, was King of France from 1350 until his death in 1364. When he came to power, France faced the Black Death, revolts and the continuing Hundred Years' War with England.

Reign and capture

John's reign was marked by conflict with Charles II of Navarre and by financial instability, including repeated debasement of the coinage. On 19 September 1356, at the Battle of Poitiers, John fought bravely but was captured by the English under Edward, the Black Prince, and taken to England.

Treaty of Brétigny and return

The Treaty of Brétigny (1360) set John's ransom at three million gold crowns and ceded large territories to Edward III. Released on parole with hostages, including his son Louis of Anjou, John famously returned voluntarily to English captivity in 1364 when Louis escaped, declaring that honour required it — the act that cemented his epithet "the Good."

Death and legacy

John died in honourable captivity at the Savoy Palace in London on 8 April 1364 and was buried in the Basilica of Saint-Denis. He created the franc as a currency (1360) and founded the Order of the Star in 1351.
