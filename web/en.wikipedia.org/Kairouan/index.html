Kairouan
Country: Tunisia
Founded: c. 670 CE
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 1988

Kairouan (القيروان), also spelled Kairwan or Qayrawan, is a city in central Tunisia and the capital of Kairouan Governorate. Founded around 670 CE by the Arab general Uqba ibn Nafi, it is regarded as one of the holiest cities of Islam and was the first major Muslim city in the Maghreb. Its old town and monuments were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1988.

History

Kairouan served as the capital of Ifriqiya under the Aghlabid dynasty in the 9th century, becoming a renowned centre of scholarship, medicine, and Islamic learning that rivalled other great cities of the Muslim world. The Fatimids briefly ruled from the region before moving east, after which Kairouan's political importance declined, though its religious prestige endured. It is traditionally ranked among the holy cities of Islam after Mecca, Medina, and Jerusalem.

Landmarks

The Great Mosque of Kairouan, founded by Uqba ibn Nafi and rebuilt in its present form under the Aghlabids, is one of the oldest and most influential mosques in the Islamic West, its massive minaret serving as a model for later Maghrebi architecture. Other sites include the Mosque of the Three Doors, the Aghlabid Basins — huge open-air reservoirs that supplied the city with water — and the mausoleum of Sidi Sahab, known as the Mosque of the Barber.

The city is also famous for its knotted-pile carpets, still woven by hand.
