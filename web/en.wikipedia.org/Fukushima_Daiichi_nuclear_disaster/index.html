Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster

Date: 11 March 2011 onwards

Location: Ōkuma and Futaba, Fukushima, Japan

INES level: 7

Cause: Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami

The Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster began on 11 March 2011 when the Great East Japan earthquake and a tsunami some 14–15 metres high flooded the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant operated by TEPCO.

Accident

Loss of power disabled cooling in Units 1–3; fuel melted, hydrogen explosions damaged reactor buildings, and radioactive releases prompted evacuation of over 150,000 residents. It is classified Level 7 on the INES scale, alongside Chernobyl.

Consequences

No deaths from acute radiation exposure among the public occurred, though evacuation-related deaths exceeded a thousand, mostly elderly people. Japan shut down its reactor fleet for years, Germany accelerated its nuclear phase-out, and the IAEA strengthened safety reviews.

Cleanup

Decommissioning is expected to take decades; treated contaminated water (ALPS) began controlled ocean release in 2023 under IAEA monitoring, drawing regional controversy.
