Marian antiphon

Marian antiphons are a group of chants in the Gregorian chant repertoire of the Roman Catholic Church, sung in honour of the Virgin Mary. Four seasonal antiphons traditionally close the office of Compline (night prayer) and, since the liturgical reforms of the 20th century, may conclude any of the Liturgy of the Hours.

The four antiphons

  • Alma Redemptoris Mater — sung from Advent until the Feast of the Presentation (2 February).
  • Ave Regina Caelorum — sung from the Presentation through Lent until Holy Week.
  • Regina Caeli — the joyful Easter antiphon, sung from Easter Sunday to Pentecost.
  • Salve Regina — sung from after Pentecost until Advent; the most famous of the four, traditionally attributed to the 11th-century monk Hermann of Reichenau.

Use and settings

Each antiphon exists in both a solemn and a simple chant tone. Composers from the Renaissance onward — Palestrina, Victoria, Lassus and many others — produced elaborate polyphonic settings, and the texts remain staples of concert and liturgical repertoire. In the breviary tradition the antiphon is followed by a versicle, response and collect varying with the season.
