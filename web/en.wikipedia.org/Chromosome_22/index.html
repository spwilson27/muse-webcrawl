Chromosome 22

Chromosome 22 is one of the 23 pairs of chromosomes in humans. People normally have two copies of this chromosome. It is the second smallest human chromosome, spanning about 51 million DNA base pairs and representing between 1.5 and 2 percent of the total DNA in cells. Chromosome 22 was the first human chromosome to be fully sequenced, with the work published in 1999 as part of the Human Genome Project.

Genes

The chromosome contains roughly 400 to 500 protein-coding genes. Among the genes located on chromosome 22 are those involved in immune function, including the immunoglobulin lambda light chain locus, as well as genes such as NF2 (associated with neurofibromatosis type II) and the BCR gene on the long arm.

Associated conditions

Several disorders are linked to chromosome 22. The 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (DiGeorge syndrome / velocardiofacial syndrome) results from a small deletion on the long arm and causes heart defects, immune deficiency and characteristic facial features. A reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22 produces the Philadelphia chromosome, whose BCR–ABL fusion gene drives chronic myeloid leukaemia. Other conditions include cat-eye syndrome, Emanuel syndrome, and neurofibromatosis type II caused by mutations in the NF2 tumour suppressor gene.
