The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) is a United Nations office established in 1997 to assist member states in combating illicit drugs, transnational organized crime, corruption, and terrorism. It was formed by merging the United Nations Drug Control Programme and the Centre for International Crime Prevention, and it is headquartered at the Vienna International Centre in Austria, with field offices around the world.

Mandate and work

The UNODC serves as secretariat for the international drug control conventions—the 1961 Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs, the 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances, and the 1988 Convention Against Illicit Traffic—as well as the United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (2000) and its protocols on human trafficking, migrant smuggling, and firearms, and the United Nations Convention against Corruption (2003).

Its work includes research and analysis, notably the annual World Drug Report and the Global Report on Trafficking in Persons; normative assistance helping states draft and implement legislation; and field-based technical cooperation in areas such as alternative development for coca and opium farmers, border management, container control, anti-money-laundering, criminal justice reform, HIV prevention among people who use drugs, and prison reform.

Structure and funding

The office is headed by an Executive Director holding the rank of Under-Secretary-General. It is governed through the Commission on Narcotic Drugs and the Commission on Crime Prevention and Criminal Justice. Unusually among UN offices, the great majority of its budget comes from voluntary contributions, mainly from member states, rather than the regular UN budget.

Criticism

Drug policy reform advocates have criticized the UNODC's alignment with prohibitionist approaches, while others have questioned its dependence on earmarked voluntary funding. The office has also been involved in debates over the death penalty for drug offenses in countries receiving its assistance.
