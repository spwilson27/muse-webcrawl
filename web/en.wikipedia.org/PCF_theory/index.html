PCF theory

PCF theory (possible cofinalities theory) is a branch of set theory concerned with the structure of products of regular cardinals and the possible cofinalities of their reduced products. It was developed by Saharon Shelah beginning in the 1970s and is one of the major achievements of modern set theory.

Overview

For a set a of regular cardinals, one considers the product of the cardinals in a modulo an ultrafilter, and the set of possible cofinalities of such products, denoted pcf(a). Shelah showed that this set has remarkable structure: it has a maximum, it is itself an interval of regular cardinals, and there are strong restrictions on the cardinal arithmetic it permits — results that hold in ZFC without additional axioms.

Applications

The most famous consequence of PCF theory is Shelah's bound on the continuum: if ℵω is a strong limit cardinal, then 2ℵω < ℵω4. PCF theory also yields results in general topology, abelian group theory, and model theory, and introduced tools such as scales and generators that are now standard in set-theoretic research.
