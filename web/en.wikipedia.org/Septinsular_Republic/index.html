The Septinsular Republic (Greek: Επτάνησος Πολιτεία) was an oligarchic republic that existed from 1800 to 1807 under nominal Russian and Ottoman sovereignty in the Ionian Islands. It is considered the first time Greeks governed themselves since the fall of the Byzantine Empire, a milestone in modern Greek history and a precursor of Greek independence.

Background

The Ionian Islands — Corfu, Paxos, Lefkada, Cephalonia, Ithaca, Zakynthos, and Kythera — had belonged to Venice until 1797, when the Treaty of Campo Formio gave them to Revolutionary France. A joint Russo-Ottoman fleet under Admiral Fyodor Ushakov expelled the French in 1798–1799. By the Treaty of Constantinople (1800), the islands became a self-governing federal republic, tributary to the Ottoman sultan and protected by Russia — the first autonomous Greek state of the modern era, albeit under foreign guarantee.

Government

The republic adopted constitutions in 1800 and, in revised form, 1803, which — controversially — restricted full citizenship and office to the hereditary nobility whose names were inscribed in the islands' "Golden Books". Its government comprised a Senate seated in Corfu and local councils on each island. The statesman Ioannis Kapodistrias, later the first head of state of independent Greece, served as a senior minister of the republic. Russia stationed troops and a resident minister, heavily influencing its politics.

End

By the Treaties of Tilsit (1807), Russia ceded the islands to Napoleonic France; in 1809–1810 Britain seized all but Corfu, and in 1815 the islands became the British protectorate of the United States of the Ionian Islands, which lasted until union with Greece in 1864.
