Orlan space suit

The Orlan space suit is a series of semi-rigid, one-piece space suits designed and built by NPP Zvezda in Russia. The name "Orlan" means "sea eagle" in Russian. The suits have been used for extravehicular activity (EVA) since 1977, first aboard the Salyut space stations, then Mir, and currently aboard the International Space Station.

Design

Unlike the American EMU, the Orlan is entered through a hatch in the back of its rigid torso, allowing a cosmonaut to don the suit alone in about five minutes. The suit operates at a pressure of about 0.4 atmospheres of pure oxygen. Its life-support backpack is integrated into the rear door.

Versions

Models include the Orlan-D, Orlan-DM, Orlan-DMA, Orlan-M, and the current Orlan-MKS, introduced in 2017, which features an automatic cooling system, a colour display, and a longer service life of up to six years and 17 spacewalks.

SuitSat

In 2006, an expired Orlan suit filled with old clothes and fitted with a radio transmitter was released from the ISS as the SuitSat-1 experiment.
