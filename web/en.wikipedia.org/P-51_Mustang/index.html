The North American Aviation P-51 Mustang is an American long-range, single-seat fighter and fighter-bomber used during World War II and the Korean War. Designed in 1940 in response to a British requirement, the prototype was completed in just over 100 days.

Development

The Mustang was originally powered by an Allison V-1710 engine, which limited its high-altitude performance. When fitted with the Rolls-Royce Merlin engine — built under licence by Packard as the V-1650 — the aircraft was transformed into one of the finest fighters of the war, combining speed, manoeuvrability, and exceptional range.

Operational history

From late 1943, Merlin-powered Mustangs escorted Allied bomber formations deep into Germany, engaging the Luftwaffe far from its bases and contributing to the achievement of Allied air superiority. The P-51D, with its bubble canopy and six .50 calibre machine guns, was the most produced variant. Mustangs later served in Korea and with many air forces worldwide; civilian examples remain popular as warbirds and racing aircraft.

Specifications (P-51D)

The P-51D had a maximum speed of about 703 km/h (437 mph), a range of roughly 2,660 km (1,650 mi) with external tanks, and a service ceiling near 12,800 m (41,900 ft).
