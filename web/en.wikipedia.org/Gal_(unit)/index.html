Gal (unit)

The gal (symbol: Gal), sometimes called galileo after Galileo Galilei, is a unit of acceleration used extensively in the science of gravimetry. The gal is defined as 1 centimeter per second squared (1 cm/s2). The milligal (mGal) and microgal (µGal) are the thousandths and millionths of a gal.

Use in gravimetry

The gal is not an SI unit, but it remains standard in geophysics for expressing measurements of the Earth's gravitational acceleration, whose average surface value is about 981 Gal. Gravity anomalies relevant to mineral exploration and geodesy are tiny fractions of this, so results are usually reported in milligals or microgals. Modern absolute gravimeters can measure to a few microgals.

Conversions

1 Gal = 0.01 m/s2 = 10−2 m/s2. The related unit of gravity gradient, the eotvos, is often used alongside the gal in survey work.
