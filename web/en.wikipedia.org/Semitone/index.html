Semitone


A semitone on the piano keyboard

A semitone, also called a half step or half tone, is the smallest musical interval commonly used in Western tonal music and is considered the most dissonant when sounded harmonically. It is defined as the interval between two adjacent notes in a 12-tone scale (or sub-octave scale). For example, C is adjacent to C♯; the interval between them is a semitone.

In equal temperament

In twelve-tone equal temperament, the system that dominates modern Western music, the octave is divided into twelve equal semitones, each with a frequency ratio of 21/12 (≈ 1.05946) and a size of exactly 100 cents. Twelve semitones make an octave, six make a tritone, and two make a whole tone.

Other tuning systems

In just intonation, semitones vary in size: the diatonic semitone (e.g., E to F in a just major scale) is commonly the ratio 16:15 (about 112 cents), while the chromatic semitone (e.g., C to C♯) is 25:24 (about 71 cents). In Pythagorean tuning the corresponding intervals are the limma (256:243) and the apotome (2187:2048). Meantone temperaments distinguish larger "major" and smaller "minor" semitones.

Use

The semitone's tension is fundamental to tonal harmony: the leading tone lies a semitone below the tonic, and the minor second is among the most dissonant intervals, used for expressive effect from the madrigal to film scores (as in the two-note motif of Jaws).
