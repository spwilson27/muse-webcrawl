The Levant
RegionEastern Mediterranean
Countries (core)Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Israel, Palestine; often also Cyprus, and parts of southern Turkey and northwestern Iraq

The Levant is a historical geographical term for the lands bordering the eastern Mediterranean, roughly corresponding to modern Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Israel, Palestine, and Cyprus, and sometimes extended to south-eastern Turkey and north-western Iraq. The word entered European languages through French and Italian trade usage — levant means "rising," the direction of the sunrise — and the region is known in Arabic as Bilad al-Sham.

Geography

The Levant is bounded by the Taurus Mountains to the north, the Mediterranean to the west, the Syrian and Arabian deserts to the east and south, and the Sinai and Egypt to the southwest. Its landscape alternates between a narrow coastal plain, parallel mountain ranges (Mount Lebanon, the Anti-Lebanon, and the hills of Galilee and Judaea), the deep rift valley of the Jordan River and Dead Sea — the lowest land on Earth — and inland plateaus and steppe.

History

Few regions have a denser history. The Levant formed the western wing of the Fertile Crescent: agriculture spread from here, and the coastal cities of the Phoenicians gave the world an alphabet. Successively ruled by Egyptian, Israelite and Judahite, Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, and Arab powers, the region saw the rise of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam; it was the theatre of the Crusades, when European "Outremer" states faced Muslim powers, and later a core territory of the Ottoman Empire for four centuries. After the First World War, Britain and France divided it into mandates whose borders underlie the modern states — and many of their conflicts.

Usage today

"Levant" survives in archaeology (Levantine archaeology and prehistory), in names such as Levantine Arabic and Levantine cuisine, and in the abbreviation ISIL, "Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant." Because of its colonial-era associations, some writers prefer "eastern Mediterranean" or "Greater Syria."
