Lyon Cathedral

Lyon Cathedral (Cathédrale Saint-Jean-Baptiste)
Location: Vieux Lyon, France
Construction: 1180–1476
Style: Romanesque and Gothic
Dedication: Saint John the Baptist

Lyon Cathedral (French: Cathédrale Saint-Jean-Baptiste de Lyon) is a Roman Catholic cathedral in the old town of Lyon, France, dedicated to Saint John the Baptist. It is the seat of the Archbishop of Lyon, who holds the title Primate of the Gauls.

History

The cathedral was built between 1180 and 1476 on the ruins of earlier churches. It hosted the First Council of Lyon (1245) and the Second Council of Lyon (1274), and in 1316 was the site of the coronation of Pope John XXII.

Architecture

The building mixes Romanesque and Gothic elements, with a notable astronomical clock dating from the 14th century — one of the oldest in Europe — and rose windows and stained glass from the 13th century. During the annual Festival of Lights (Fête des Lumières) the facade serves as a canvas for light installations.
