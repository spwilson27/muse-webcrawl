Congregation of the Oratory

The Congregation of the Oratory, also called the Oratorians, is a Catholic society of apostolic life of pontifical right for men (priests and lay brothers) founded by Saint Philip Neri (1515–1595) in Rome in 1575. Members add the initials C.O. after their names.

Oratorians live in communities bound not by religious vows but by the internal bond of charity. Each Oratory is autonomous, though many belong to a confederation. The congregation is known for its musical tradition — the term "oratorio" derives from the prayer hall (oratory) in which musical services were held — and for scholarship; John Henry Newman, later a cardinal and saint, founded the first Oratory in England, at Birmingham, in 1848.

History

Philip Neri gathered priests and laymen for prayer, Scripture reading, and informal talks in a room over the church of San Girolamo della Carità in Rome. In 1575 Pope Gregory XIII formally erected the Congregation of the Oratory and gave it the church of Santa Maria in Vallicella, which Neri rebuilt as the Chiesa Nuova. Oratories spread across Italy and then Europe and the Americas, noted for pastoral work, preaching, and the fostering of sacred music by composers such as Palestrina.
