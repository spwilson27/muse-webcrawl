Nevermind is the second studio album by the American rock band Nirvana, released on September 24, 1991, by DGC Records. It was Nirvana's first release on a major label and the first to feature drummer Dave Grohl. Produced by Butch Vig, the album marked a shift from the raw sound of the band's debut Bleach to a polished, radio-friendly mixture of quiet verses and loud, heavy choruses.

Success and impact

Propelled by its lead single "Smells Like Teen Spirit" — whose video became an MTV staple — Nevermind reached number one on the US Billboard 200 in January 1992, displacing Michael Jackson's Dangerous. It has sold over 30 million copies worldwide and is credited with bringing alternative rock and grunge to a mainstream audience, effectively defining the sound of 1990s rock and making frontman Kurt Cobain, reluctantly, the "voice of a generation". Other singles included "Come as You Are", "Lithium" and "In Bloom".

Cover art

The famous cover, photographed by Kirk Weddle, shows a naked infant (Spencer Elden) swimming underwater toward a US dollar bill on a fishhook — Cobain's image of "sex and money" — and became one of the most recognisable album covers of all time.

Legacy

Nevermind is consistently ranked among the greatest albums of all time, appearing near the top of lists by Rolling Stone and others, and was inducted into the National Recording Registry by the Library of Congress in 2004, which called it "culturally, historically, and aesthetically significant".
