Jin dynasty (266–420)

Jin dynasty
CapitalLuoyang; later Jiankang
Established266
Ended420

The Jin dynasty was a Chinese imperial dynasty founded by Sima Yan, who in 266 forced the abdication of the last Cao Wei emperor and in 280 conquered Wu, briefly reunifying China after the Three Kingdoms period. This first phase is known as the Western Jin.

Western Jin and collapse

The War of the Eight Princes (291–306) devastated the dynasty, and uprisings by the Five Barbarians led to the fall of Luoyang and Chang'an. In 317 a Jin prince re-established the court at Jiankang (Nanjing) south of the Yangtze, beginning the Eastern Jin.

Eastern Jin

The Eastern Jin ruled southern China in uneasy partnership with powerful aristocratic clans, while the north was divided among the Sixteen Kingdoms. Jin armies won the famous victory at the Fei River in 383 against Former Qin. In 420 the general Liu Yu usurped the throne, founding the Liu Song dynasty and beginning the Northern and Southern Dynasties period.

Culture

The era is noted for calligraphy (Wang Xizhi), landscape poetry, the spread of Buddhism, and the Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove.
