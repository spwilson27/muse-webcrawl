Pussy Riot is a Russian feminist protest and performance art group based in Moscow. Founded in August 2011, it has a variable membership of around 11 women who stage unauthorised guerrilla-style punk rock performances in public places, filmed and circulated online. Their lyrics and actions protest authoritarianism, the government of Vladimir Putin, the close ties between the Russian Orthodox Church and the state, and restrictions on LGBTQ rights and women's rights; members perform anonymously in brightly coloured balaclavas.

"Punk Prayer" and trial

On 21 February 2012, five members performed a "punk prayer" in Moscow's Cathedral of Christ the Saviour, asking the Virgin Mary to chase Putin away. Three members — Nadezhda Tolokonnikova, Maria Alyokhina and Yekaterina Samutsevich — were arrested and charged with hooliganism motivated by religious hatred. In August 2012 they were sentenced to two years in prison; Samutsevich's sentence was suspended on appeal in October 2012, while Tolokonnikova and Alyokhina served most of their terms before release under an amnesty in December 2013. The case drew worldwide attention and criticism from human-rights organisations, and support from many musicians and public figures.

Later activity

After their release, Tolokonnikova and Alyokhina founded the independent media outlet MediaZona and the prisoners' rights organisation Zona Prava. The group has continued protest actions, including a pitch invasion during the 2018 FIFA World Cup final in Moscow protesting police powers. Members have faced repeated arrests, and the group was later designated a "foreign agent" by Russian authorities.
