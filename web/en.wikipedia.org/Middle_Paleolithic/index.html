Middle Paleolithic

The Middle Paleolithic (or Middle Palaeolithic) is the second subdivision of the Paleolithic or Old Stone Age as it is understood in Europe, Africa and Asia. It lasted roughly from 300,000 to 30,000 years ago and is broadly concurrent with the Mousterian culture of Neanderthals in Europe and western Asia, and with the Middle Stone Age in Africa.

[Image: Mousterian stone tools typical of the Middle Paleolithic]

Tool technology

The defining technology of the period is the prepared-core (Levallois) technique, in which a stone core is carefully shaped so that flakes of predetermined size and shape can be struck off. Retouched flake tools — side-scrapers, points and denticulates — dominate Mousterian assemblages, replacing the handaxes characteristic of the Lower Paleolithic.

Hominins and behaviour

Middle Paleolithic tools were made by Neanderthals in Europe and Asia and by early anatomically modern humans (Homo sapiens) in Africa and the Levant. Evidence from the period includes deliberate burials (e.g. at Shanidar and La Chapelle-aux-Saints), the use of pigments such as red ochre, hafted stone points, controlled use of fire and hearths, and hunting of large game. The degree to which Neanderthals possessed symbolic behaviour remains debated.

Transition

The Middle Paleolithic gives way to the Upper Paleolithic around 40,000–30,000 years ago with the appearance of blade-based industries (Aurignacian and others) associated with modern humans, and the extinction of the Neanderthals.
