Decriminalization

Decriminalization or decriminalisation is the reclassification in law relating to certain acts or aspects of such to the effect that they are no longer considered a crime, including the removal of criminal penalties in relation to them. Such reclassification may involve reduced penalties, such as a fine rather than imprisonment, or the conduct may remain prohibited under non-criminal law.

Distinction from legalization

Decriminalization differs from legalization: a decriminalized activity remains formally prohibited and may still attract administrative sanctions, whereas a legalized activity is permitted and typically regulated and taxed. The distinction matters most in debates over drug policy, where decriminalization of possession for personal use has been adopted in jurisdictions such as Portugal in 2001.

Areas of application

Conduct that has been decriminalized in various places includes possession of small quantities of drugs, certain consensual sexual acts historically criminalised, abortion in early pregnancy in some jurisdictions, and minor traffic and public-order offences. Campaigners argue decriminalization reduces prison populations and the collateral harms of criminal records; opponents argue it weakens deterrence.
