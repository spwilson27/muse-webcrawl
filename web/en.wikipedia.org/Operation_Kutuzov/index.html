Operation Kutuzov

Operation Kutuzov
Date12 July – 18 August 1943
LocationOrel region, Russian SFSR, Soviet Union
ResultSoviet victory
BelligerentsSoviet Union / Nazi Germany
CommandersKonstantin Rokossovsky, Markian Popov, Vasily Sokolovsky (USSR); Walter Model (Germany)

Operation Kutuzov was the first of the two counteroffensives launched by the Red Army against the Wehrmacht during the Battle of Kursk. Named after Mikhail Kutuzov, the Russian general who resisted Napoleon in 1812, the offensive began on 12 July 1943 — a week after Germany launched Operation Citadel — when the Western, Bryansk and Central Fronts attacked the German forces holding the Orel salient north of Kursk.

Course of the operation

The offensive opened with a massive artillery preparation. The Bryansk Front under General Markian Popov and the Western Front under Vasily Sokolovsky attacked from the east and north, while Konstantin Rokossovsky's Central Front later joined from the south. German resistance under Field Marshal Walter Model's Army Group Centre was fierce, but the Red Army steadily ground forward, liberating the city of Orel on 5 August 1943 — an event celebrated in Moscow with the first artillery salute fireworks of the war. By 18 August Soviet forces had reached the Hagen line east of Bryansk and eliminated the Orel salient.

Significance

Operation Kutuzov, together with Operation Polkovodets Rumyantsev to the south, marked the point at which the strategic initiative on the Eastern Front passed permanently to the Soviet Union. Soviet casualties were heavy — estimates exceed 400,000 men, including more than 100,000 killed — against German losses of roughly 86,000. The operation cost the Wehrmacht irreplaceable reserves and forced it onto the defensive for the remainder of the war.
