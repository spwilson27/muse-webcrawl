Pliosauroidea is an extinct clade of marine reptiles within the order Plesiosauria, commonly called pliosaurs. They lived from the Early Jurassic until the early Late Cretaceous, roughly 200 to 90 million years ago, and are characterised by short necks and large, elongated heads with powerful jaws — the opposite body plan to the long-necked plesiosaurs.

Description

Pliosaurs propelled themselves with four large flippers in an underwater "flight" motion. The largest, such as Pliosaurus and Liopleurodon, reached lengths of around 10 metres or more and were apex predators of Mesozoic seas, feeding on fish, squid, and other marine reptiles. Their teeth were conical and robust, suited to seizing large prey, and some forms had among the strongest bite forces estimated for any animal.

Classification and decline

The group includes early forms such as Rhomaleosaurus and the great Jurassic and Cretaceous pliosaurids, as well as the long-snouted polycotylids of the Cretaceous. Pliosaurs declined during the Late Cretaceous and disappeared about 90 million years ago, as mosasaurs rose to dominate the seas.
