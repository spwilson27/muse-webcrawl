Elections Canada

Elections Canada
Formed: 1920
Headquarters: Ottawa, Ontario
Agency head: Chief Electoral Officer
Purpose: Administration of federal elections and referendums

Elections Canada (French: Élections Canada) is the non-partisan agency responsible for administering Canadian federal elections and referendums. Established in 1920 with the creation of the office of Chief Electoral Officer — a first among parliamentary democracies — it reports directly to Parliament rather than to the government of the day, safeguarding its independence.

Responsibilities

The agency maintains the National Register of Electors, prepares for and conducts general elections, by-elections, and referendums under the Canada Elections Act and Referendum Act, enforces political financing rules, registers political parties and third-party advertisers, and provides voter education. In each electoral district, a returning officer appointed by the Chief Electoral Officer runs the local vote with tens of thousands of temporary election workers.

Chief Electoral Officer

The Chief Electoral Officer is appointed by resolution of the House of Commons for a non-renewable ten-year term and can only be removed for cause by joint address of both Houses. Notable officeholders include Jean-Pierre Kingsley (1990–2007), Marc Mayrand (2007–2016), and Stéphane Perrault (from 2018).

Notable matters

The agency's investigations have included the robocalls scandal surrounding the 2011 election, in which misleading calls directed voters to wrong polling locations; one campaign worker was convicted. Elections Canada also administers redistribution support following each decennial census.
