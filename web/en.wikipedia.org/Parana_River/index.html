Paraná River

Paraná River Countries: Brazil, Paraguay, Argentina
Length: approx. 4,880 km (3,030 mi)
Source: Confluence of Paranaíba and Grande rivers, Brazil
Mouth: Río de la Plata
Major dams: Itaipu, Yacyretá

The Paraná River (Spanish: Río Paraná; Portuguese: Rio Paraná) is a river in south-central South America, running through Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina for some 4,880 kilometres (3,030 mi). It is second in length among the rivers of South America only to the Amazon River. The name Paraná is an abbreviation of the phrase "para rehe onáva" in the Tupi language, which means "like the sea" (that is, "as big as the sea").

Course

The Paraná proper begins at the confluence of the Paranaíba and Grande rivers in southern Brazil. It flows generally southwest, forming the border between Brazil and Paraguay, where it is dammed by the Itaipu Dam — one of the largest hydroelectric power stations in the world by generation. At the Triple Frontier with Argentina it is joined by the Iguazu River, just below the Iguazu Falls. Further downstream it meets the Paraguay River, then flows south through Argentina past Rosario and joins the Uruguay River to form the Río de la Plata estuary, which empties into the Atlantic Ocean near Buenos Aires.

Economy and ecology

The river and its tributaries form the drainage basin of the Río de la Plata, covering about 2.6 million km². The Paraná is a vital transport artery: the Hidrovía Paraná–Paraguay allows ocean-going vessels to reach inland ports in Paraguay, Bolivia, and Argentina, carrying much of the region's grain and mineral exports. Its channel supports rich fisheries — including the dorado, surubí, and pacú — and its middle basin in Argentina contains extensive wetlands such as the Iberá system. Flooding of the Paraná delta and adjacent plains is a recurrent hazard, while upstream damming, navigation dredging, and invasive species such as the golden mussel pose conservation challenges.

Major structures

Besides Itaipu, major hydroelectric projects on the Paraná include the Yacyretá Dam (Argentina–Paraguay) and, on the Brazilian upper course, Ilha Solteira and Jupiá. Bridges such as the General Belgrano Bridge (Corrientes–Chaco) and the Rosario–Victoria Bridge link the river's banks in Argentina.
