Northern Leyte refers to the northern part of the island of Leyte in the Philippines and, in administrative terms, to the province of Leyte, which occupies the northern three-quarters of the island (the southern quarter forming the separate province of Southern Leyte). The province belongs to the Eastern Visayas region, and its capital is the highly urbanised city of Tacloban.

History

Leyte entered world history in October 1944, when General Douglas MacArthur landed at Palo, Leyte, fulfilling his promise to return to the Philippines; the ensuing Battle of Leyte Gulf was one of the largest naval battles ever fought. The island was administered as a single province until 1959, when Southern Leyte was created. In November 2013, Tacloban and northern Leyte were devastated by Super Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda), one of the strongest tropical cyclones ever recorded at landfall.

Geography and economy

The province is connected to Samar by the San Juanico Bridge, among the longest in the Philippines. The economy depends on agriculture (rice, coconut, abacá), fishing, and the Leyte geothermal fields around Ormoc and Kananga, among the largest geothermal power producers in the country.
