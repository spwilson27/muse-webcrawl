Web analytics

MeasuresTraffic, sessions, conversions, behaviour
Data sourcesServer logs, page tags (JavaScript), cookies
Major toolsGoogle Analytics, Adobe Analytics, Matomo

Web analytics is the measurement, collection, analysis, and reporting of web data to understand and optimize web usage. It is not just a process for measuring web traffic but can be used as a tool for business and market research and assess and improve website effectiveness. Web analytics applications can also help companies measure the results of advertising campaigns and estimate how traffic changes after the launch of a new campaign.

Methods

Two main technical approaches exist. Log file analysis reads the records web servers keep of every request, while page tagging uses JavaScript embedded in pages to report views and interactions to an analytics service, usually setting cookies to recognise returning visitors. Key metrics include pageviews, sessions, users, bounce rate, average session duration, traffic sources (organic search, paid search, social, referral, direct) and conversions against defined goals such as purchases or sign-ups.

History

The field began in the early 1990s with log analysers such as Analog and WebTrends counting "hits." Urchin Software's hosted service popularised tag-based measurement in the 2000s; Google acquired Urchin in 2005 and launched the free Google Analytics, which became the dominant platform, later joined by privacy-focused and self-hosted alternatives such as Matomo.

Privacy

Because analytics often relies on cookies and cross-site identifiers, it has become entangled with privacy law — the EU's ePrivacy Directive and GDPR require consent for most tracking cookies — and with browser measures restricting third-party cookies and fingerprinting. This has encouraged interest in cookieless and aggregate measurement.
