Brandenburg Concertos

The Brandenburg Concertos (BWV 1046–1051) are a collection of six instrumental works by Johann Sebastian Bach, presented in 1721 to Christian Ludwig, Margrave of Brandenburg-Schwedt, with a dedication in French. They are widely regarded among the finest orchestral compositions of the Baroque era.

Each concerto uses a different and adventurous combination of solo instruments in the concerto grosso manner: the Second features high trumpet, recorder, oboe, and violin; the Third and Sixth dispense with winds entirely, the Sixth also omitting violins; the Fifth gives the harpsichord a spectacular solo cadenza, an early use of the keyboard as soloist.

The margrave apparently never had them performed or paid Bach, and the manuscript was sold after his death. Rediscovered and published in 1850, the concertos became central to Bach's modern reputation.
