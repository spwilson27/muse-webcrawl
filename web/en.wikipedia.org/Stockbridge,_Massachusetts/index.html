Stockbridge, Massachusetts

Stockbridge is a town in Berkshire County, Massachusetts, United States. It is part of the Pittsfield, Massachusetts Metropolitan Statistical Area. The population was 2,018 at the 2020 census. A year-round resort area, Stockbridge is home to the Norman Rockwell Museum, the Austen Riggs Center (a psychiatric treatment facility), and Chesterwood, home and studio of sculptor Daniel Chester French.

History

Stockbridge was first settled in 1734 as a mission community to the Mohican Native Americans and was incorporated on 22 March 1739 as Indiantown. Although the town was controlled by British missionaries, the original school on the reservation was founded by John Sergeant, a Yale graduate who sought to Christianize and educate the Stockbridge Indians (Mohicans). Stockbridge Indians served in the French and Indian War and the American Revolution, and after the Revolution many of them migrated west, eventually settling in Wisconsin, where they became the Stockbridge-Munsee Community.

Geography

According to the United States Census Bureau, the town has a total area of 23.7 square miles (61.3 km²), of which 22.9 square miles (59.4 km²) is land and 0.77 square miles (2.0 km²), or 3.19%, is water. Stockbridge is bordered by West Stockbridge to the west, Richmond and Lenox to the north, Lee to the east, and Great Barrington to the south. The Housatonic River flows through the town.

Culture

The town was the subject of Norman Rockwell's painting Stockbridge Main Street at Christmas, and Rockwell's studio is preserved at the Norman Rockwell Museum. Tanglewood, the summer home of the Boston Symphony Orchestra, lies partly within the town, and the Berkshire Theatre Festival has its main stage in Stockbridge. The Red Lion Inn, a historic hotel on Main Street, has operated since the 18th century.
