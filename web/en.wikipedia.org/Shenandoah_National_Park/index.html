Shenandoah National Park is a United States national park that encompasses part of the Blue Ridge Mountains in Virginia. The park is long and narrow — about 105 miles from Front Royal in the north to Rockfish Gap near Waynesboro in the south — flanking the scenic Skyline Drive along the mountain crest, with the Shenandoah River and its valley to the west and the rolling hills of the Virginia Piedmont to the east. Nearly 40 percent of the park, some 79,579 acres, is designated wilderness.

History

Created in the 1920s as an eastern counterpart to western parks like Yellowstone, the park was assembled from more than 3,000 tracts of privately owned mountain land, much of it farmed or logged. Several hundred mountain families were displaced when Virginia acquired and donated the land to the federal government, a controversial episode commemorated by Chimney Rock and interpretive exhibits. The Civilian Conservation Corps built much of the park's infrastructure in the 1930s — Skyline Drive, trails, lodges such as Skyland and Big Meadows, and the stone walls along the roadway. President Herbert Hoover had earlier established his summer retreat, Rapidan Camp, on the Rapidan River within the present park.

Nature and recreation

The park's forests of oak, hickory, and (increasingly stressed) hemlock shelter white-tailed deer, black bears — one of the densest populations in North America — bobcats, and over 200 bird species. Its highest peak is Hawksbill Mountain at 4,051 feet (1,235 m). Visitors drive Skyline Drive's 75 overlooks, hike some 500 miles of trails including 101 miles of the Appalachian Trail, and climb Old Rag Mountain, a popular scramble over granite boulders. Waterfalls such as Dark Hollow Falls reward short hikes from the drive. The park draws roughly 1.4–1.6 million visitors a year, peaking with the autumn foliage.
