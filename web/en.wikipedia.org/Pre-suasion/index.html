Pre-suasion

Pre-Suasion
AuthorRobert B. Cialdini
Published2016 (Simon & Schuster)
SubjectPersuasion, social psychology, marketing

Pre-suasion is a concept in social psychology, popularised by Robert Cialdini in his 2016 book Pre-Suasion: A Revolutionary Way to Influence and Persuade, describing the practice of getting recipients sympathetic to a message before they experience it. A "pre-suader" primes an audience by directing its attention to a concept, image, or association that makes the forthcoming message more readily accepted.

Concept

Cialdini argues that what we present first changes how what follows is perceived: attention is a channel through which importance is assigned ("what's focal is presumed causal"). Pre-suasive devices include opening questions that invite agreement ("Are you adventurous?"), background images and music that prime relevant associations, and framing a task as a shared, unified endeavour. The book extends the six principles of his earlier work Influence (reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, liking, authority, scarcity) and adds a seventh principle, unity — shared identity — developed further in the 2021 expanded edition of Influence.

Reception and criticism

Pre-Suasion became a bestseller and is widely used in marketing and communication training. Commentators have noted ethical concerns: techniques that steer attention covertly can manipulate as well as inform, and Cialdini himself discusses defensive awareness of such tactics.
