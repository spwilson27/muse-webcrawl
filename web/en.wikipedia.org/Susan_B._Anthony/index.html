Susan B. Anthony

Susan B. Anthony (1820–1906), social reformer and suffragist.

Susan B. Anthony (born Susan Anthony; February 15, 1820 – March 13, 1906) was an American social reformer and women's rights activist who played a pivotal role in the women's suffrage movement. Born into a Quaker family committed to social equality, she collected anti-slavery petitions at the age of 17. In 1856, she became the New York state agent for the American Anti-Slavery Society.

In 1869, she and Elizabeth Cady Stanton founded the National Woman Suffrage Association. In 1872, Anthony was arrested in her hometown of Rochester, New York, for voting in violation of laws that allowed only men to vote; she was convicted in a widely publicized trial. Although she refused to pay the fine, the authorities declined to take further action, and she used the trial to publicize the suffrage cause.

Later work

Anthony traveled extensively in support of women's suffrage, giving dozens of speeches a year. She also worked internationally, helping to found the International Council of Women. In 1900 she retired as president of the National American Woman Suffrage Association, which she had helped create through a merger.

Legacy

Anthony did not live to see the ratification of the Nineteenth Amendment in 1920, which granted American women the right to vote and is sometimes called the "Susan B. Anthony Amendment." In 1979 she became the first non-fictional woman depicted on a U.S. coin, the Susan B. Anthony dollar. Her Rochester home is now a National Historic Landmark museum.
