Sirocco (hatched 23 March 1997) is a male kākāpō, the critically endangered flightless parrot of New Zealand. Hand-reared after contracting a respiratory illness as a chick, he imprinted on humans and became unsuitable for the breeding programme's wild population; instead he serves as an official ambassador for kākāpō conservation and is one of the best-known individual birds in the world.

Life

Sirocco was bred on Codfish Island / Whenua Hou, a predator-free sanctuary off Stewart Island, as part of the New Zealand Department of Conservation's Kākāpō Recovery Programme. Because of his hand-rearing he became highly socialised with people — kākāpō males in breeding condition may attempt to mate with human heads, a behaviour captured famously on camera in 2009, when Sirocco mounted the head of zoologist Mark Carwardine during filming of the BBC series Last Chance to See with Stephen Fry, who remarked, "You are being shagged by a rare parrot." The clip went viral and greatly raised the species' global profile.

Ambassador role

Named after the hot desert wind, Sirocco has toured New Zealand on public appearances and was formally appointed by Prime Minister John Key in 2010 as the "Official Spokesbird for Conservation." He has large social-media followings, which the Department of Conservation uses to publicise the recovery programme. As of the mid-2020s the total kākāpō population remains only a few hundred birds, each individually named and monitored, living on predator-free islands.
