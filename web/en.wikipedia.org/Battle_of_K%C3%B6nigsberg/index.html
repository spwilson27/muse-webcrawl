Battle of Königsberg
DateLate January – 9 April 1945
LocationKönigsberg, East Prussia, Germany
ResultSoviet victory
BelligerentsSoviet Union vs. Nazi Germany

The Battle of Königsberg, also known as the Königsberg offensive, was one of the last operations of the East Prussian Offensive during World War II. In four days of violent urban warfare, Soviet forces of the 3rd Belorussian Front under Marshal Aleksandr Vasilevsky surrounded and captured the city of Königsberg, present-day Kaliningrad, Russia.

Background

Königsberg, the historic capital of East Prussia, had been declared a fortress city (Festung) and was defended by a garrison under General Otto Lasch, protected by three rings of fortifications including nineteenth-century forts. Soviet forces isolated the city in late January 1945 during the East Prussian Offensive.

The assault

The final storming of the city began on 6 April 1945 after a massive artillery preparation. Soviet troops fought through the suburbs and the inner city, and Königsberg Castle and the old town were heavily damaged. On the evening of 9 April, Lasch surrendered the garrison, disobeying orders to fight to the last man.

Aftermath

The capture removed a major German stronghold and freed Soviet forces for the final offensive against Berlin. The city was annexed by the Soviet Union at the Potsdam Conference, renamed Kaliningrad in 1946, and its remaining German population expelled.
