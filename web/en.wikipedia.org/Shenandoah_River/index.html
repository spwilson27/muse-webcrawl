The Shenandoah River is a 90-kilometre (56 mi) principal tributary of the Potomac River in the U.S. states of Virginia and West Virginia. Formed by the confluence of its North Fork and South Fork at Front Royal, Virginia, it flows northeast through the Shenandoah Valley between the Blue Ridge Mountains to the east and the Allegheny Mountains to the west, joining the Potomac at Harpers Ferry.

Course

The South Fork, the longer branch, runs about 160 km between the Blue Ridge and Massanutten Mountain from near Staunton through Luray — famous for Luray Caverns — to Front Royal. The North Fork flows roughly parallel to the west. Below Front Royal the combined river passes Berryville and Charles Town before cutting through the Blue Ridge at Harpers Ferry.

History

The valley was home to Iroquoian and Siouan peoples before European settlement by German and Scots-Irish farmers in the 1730s. The name "Shenandoah" is of Native American origin, popularly rendered "daughter of the stars." During the American Civil War the valley was a strategic corridor — the "breadbasket of the Confederacy" — and the scene of Stonewall Jackson's 1862 Valley Campaign and Sheridan's devastating 1864 campaign. The river is celebrated in the folk song "Oh Shenandoah" and in John Denver's "Take Me Home, Country Roads."

Ecology and recreation

The river supports smallmouth bass, channel catfish and other fisheries and is popular for canoeing, kayaking, tubing and fishing, with Shenandoah River State Park on its banks. The watershed has suffered periodic fish kills and agricultural pollution concerns, the subject of ongoing conservation efforts.
