The Øresund Bridge (Danish: Øresundsbroen; Swedish: Öresundsbron) is a combined road and rail bridge across the Øresund strait between Denmark and Sweden. Together with an artificial island, Peberholm, and the Drogden Tunnel on the Danish side, it forms the Øresund fixed link, the longest combined road-and-rail bridge in Europe when it opened.

Design and construction

The bridge is a cable-stayed structure with two levels: motorway traffic runs on the upper deck and trains below. Its main span of 490 metres is carried on pylons 204 metres tall. Construction began in 1995; prefabricated segments were floated out and lifted into place, and the link was inaugurated by Queen Margrethe II of Denmark and King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden on 1 July 2000.

Traffic and impact

The link joined Copenhagen with Malmö into a single cross-border metropolitan region, cutting the crossing to about ten minutes by train. Millions of vehicles and tens of millions of rail passengers use it each year, though traffic volumes responded to tolls, the 2015–16 border controls and the COVID-19 pandemic. The bridge gained worldwide fame as the setting of the Danish–Swedish crime series The Bridge (Bron/Broen).
