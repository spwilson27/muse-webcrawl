The funeral of Diana, Princess of Wales took place on Saturday, 6 September 1997, at Westminster Abbey in London, one week after her death in a car crash in Paris on 31 August. Although not a state funeral, it was a royal ceremonial funeral of unprecedented scale, watched by an estimated two billion or more television viewers around the world and by a million people lining the streets of London.

The service

Diana's coffin, draped with the royal standard, was carried from Kensington Palace on a gun carriage, accompanied on foot by Prince Philip, Prince Charles, her brother Earl Spencer, and her sons Princes William and Harry — an image that became one of the defining photographs of the decade. At the Abbey, Elton John performed a rewritten version of "Candle in the Wind" dedicated to her, and Earl Spencer's eulogy, pointedly critical of the royal family and the press, drew applause that swept in from the crowds outside. Prime Minister Tony Blair read from 1 Corinthians.

Burial and legacy

After the service the coffin was driven to Althorp, the Spencer family estate in Northamptonshire, where Diana was buried privately on an island in the estate's ornamental lake, the Oval. The funeral followed a week of extraordinary public mourning — mountains of flowers outside Kensington Palace and public pressure that led Queen Elizabeth II to return to London and address the nation — and it remains a landmark in modern British cultural history.
