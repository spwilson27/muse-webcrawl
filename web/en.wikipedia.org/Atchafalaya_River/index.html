Atchafalaya River

The Atchafalaya River is a 137-mile-long (220 km) distributary of the Mississippi River and Red River in south central Louisiana, United States. It flows south through the Atchafalaya Basin — the largest wetland and swamp in the United States — to Atchafalaya Bay on the Gulf of Mexico.

Bald cypress swamp in the Atchafalaya Basin.

Course capture and control

Because the Atchafalaya offers a shorter, steeper route to the Gulf, it has steadily captured more of the Mississippi's flow. Without intervention, the Mississippi would have avulsed into the Atchafalaya channel, stranding the ports of Baton Rouge and New Orleans. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers completed the Old River Control Structure in 1963 to hold the split at roughly 70 per cent Mississippi to 30 per cent Atchafalaya; the structure was severely stressed by the 1973 flood, prompting auxiliary gates and the nearby Morganza Spillway.

Delta and ecology

The Atchafalaya is one of the few places in Louisiana where a river delta is actively building land, thanks to sediment diverted through the Wax Lake Outlet. The basin supports major crawfish, catfish, and waterfowl populations and a rich Cajun cultural heritage.
