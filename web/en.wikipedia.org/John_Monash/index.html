General Sir John Monash (27 June 1865 – 8 October 1931) was an Australian civil engineer and military commander of the First World War. A militia officer who became commander of the Australian Corps in 1918, he is widely regarded as one of the most effective Allied generals of the war, noted for meticulous planning and the integrated use of infantry, artillery, armour and aircraft.

Early life and civilian career

Monash was born in Melbourne to German-Jewish immigrant parents and educated at Scotch College and the University of Melbourne, qualifying in engineering, arts and law. He became a leading civil engineer, a pioneer of reinforced-concrete construction in Australia, and served in the citizen militia for decades before 1914.

First World War

Monash commanded the 4th Infantry Brigade at Gallipoli in 1915, and the 3rd Australian Division in France from 1916. Promoted to command the Australian Corps in May 1918, he achieved a model victory at the Battle of Hamel on 4 July 1918, capturing all objectives in 93 minutes. In the Hundred Days Offensive his corps played a leading part in the Battle of Amiens (8 August 1918) and subsequent advances, including the attack on the Hindenburg Line.

Post-war life

After the war Monash directed the repatriation of Australian troops, then led Victoria's State Electricity Commission, developing the Latrobe Valley's brown-coal power industry. Monash University in Melbourne is named after him, and he appears on the Australian $100 note. His book The Australian Victories in France in 1918 remains a classic of military history.
