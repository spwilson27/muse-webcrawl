Suva

Suva is the capital and largest city of Fiji. It is located on the south-east coast of the island of Viti Levu, in Rewa Province, Central Division. Suva became the capital in 1882, replacing Levuka, and is the principal port and commercial and political centre of the country, as well as a hub for the wider South Pacific — many regional organisations, including the Pacific Islands Forum Secretariat and the University of the South Pacific, are headquartered there.

History

The site was originally a Fijian village whose chief ceded land to the Australian Polynesia Company in 1868, in settlement of a claimed debt. After Fiji became a British colony in 1874, the decision was made to move the capital from Levuka, whose growth was constrained by steep terrain, to Suva, which had a spacious harbour and room to expand. The city was laid out on a grid around the peninsula between Suva Harbour and Tamavua.

Geography and climate

Suva occupies a hilly peninsula and has grown to encompass suburbs extending toward Nausori along the King's Road. It has a tropical rainforest climate with heavy rainfall year-round — around 3,000 mm annually — and is one of the wettest capitals in the region. The city proper had a population of about 93,000 at the 2017 census, with the greater Suva urban area (including Lami, Nasinu and Nausori) exceeding 330,000, roughly a third of Fiji's population.

Landmarks

Notable landmarks include the Government Buildings (1939) in the colonial monumental style, the Fiji Museum in Thurston Gardens, Albert Park, the Sacred Heart Cathedral, and the Grand Pacific Hotel (1914), long a symbol of the city. Suva's markets, cinemas and shops serve as Fiji's retail centre, and the port handles most of the country's imports and inter-island ferries.
