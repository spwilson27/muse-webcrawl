Pike Place Fish Market

Location
Pike Place Market, Seattle, Washington
Founded
1930
Known for
Fish-throwing fishmongers

Pike Place Fish Market is an open-air seafood market in Seattle's Pike Place Market, at the corner of Pike Street and Pike Place. Founded in 1930, it is world-famous for its fishmongers who throw fish to one another across the counter for packing, shouting the orders in a ritual chant — a spectacle that draws crowds of tourists.

History and fame

The market was bought in 1965 by John Yokoyama, who in the 1980s, facing near-bankruptcy, worked with a business coach to transform the shop's culture, committing staff to "being world famous" through playful customer interaction. The transformation was documented in the bestselling FISH! Philosophy books and training films, which made the market a case study in employee engagement.

Operations

The stall sells Pacific Northwest seafood, including whole king salmon displayed on ice, and ships fish across the United States packed in ice so that a fish can still be thrown at the destination airport for local customers picking up orders.
