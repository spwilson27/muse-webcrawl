Fairsted

Fairsted was the home and office of Frederick Law Olmsted (1822–1903), the founder of American landscape architecture, in Brookline, Massachusetts. Purchased by Olmsted in 1883, the 1.89-acre property served as the headquarters of his landscape design firm for over a century. It is now preserved as the Frederick Law Olmsted National Historic Site, administered by the National Park Service.

History

Olmsted moved his family and practice to Brookline in 1883, naming the estate "Fairsted" after an ancestral village in England. From its drafting rooms, Olmsted and his successors—including his sons John Charles Olmsted and Frederick Law Olmsted Jr.—designed thousands of projects, from Boston's Emerald Necklace to the grounds of the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition and the Biltmore Estate.

Grounds

The grounds themselves embody Olmsted's design philosophy, with a hollow of wild plantings, a rock garden, and lawns framed by trees such as the great American elm on the south lawn.
