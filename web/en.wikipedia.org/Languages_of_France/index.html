Languages of France

The languages of France include French, the sole official language of the French Republic, and a wide variety of regional and immigrant languages. According to a 1999 report by linguist Bernard Cerquiglini, some 75 languages were spoken in metropolitan and overseas France, including regional languages such as Occitan, Breton, Alsatian, Flemish, Catalan, Corsican, and Basque, as well as languages of overseas territories and of immigrant communities.

Official status

Article 2 of the Constitution of the Fifth Republic (amended in 1992) states that "the language of the Republic is French." A 2008 constitutional revision added Article 75-1, recognizing regional languages as part of France's heritage, though they have no official status and France has not ratified the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages (signed in 1999 but blocked by the Constitutional Council).

Regional languages

Historically, France was linguistically diverse: the langues d'oïl in the north (including Norman, Picard, Gallo, and Poitevin-Saintongeais), the langues d'oc in the south (Occitan dialects such as Provençal, Gascon, and Languedocien), Franco-Provençal, and non-Romance languages including Breton (Celtic), Basque (a language isolate), Alsatian and Moselle Franconian (Germanic), West Flemish, Catalan, and Corsican. Government policy from the Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts (1539) through the school laws of the Third Republic aggressively promoted French and suppressed regional languages, causing sharp declines in their use.

Overseas and immigrant languages

In overseas departments and territories, languages such as Guianese Creole, Réunion Creole, Tahitian, Kanak languages of New Caledonia, and Shimaore are spoken. Immigrant communities have brought Arabic (especially Maghrebi dialects), Portuguese, Italian, Spanish, Turkish, and Berber languages, among others; Arabic is often cited as the second most widely spoken language in France.
