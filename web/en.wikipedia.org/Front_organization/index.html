Front Organization

A front organization is any entity set up by and controlled by another organization, such as an intelligence agency, organized crime group, terrorist group, political party, or corporation, that acts for the parent group without attribution. Front organizations can appear as charities, cultural associations, trade bodies, or businesses, and may serve to conceal funding, recruitment, propaganda, or illegal activity.

Uses

Intelligence services have historically used front companies and organisations for covert operations and plausible deniability; examples include proprietary airlines and trading firms used by the CIA during the Cold War. Political parties have created nominally independent campaign, cultural, and peace groups to widen their appeal — the term "front" became common in the 20th century for organisations linked to communist parties. Criminal and terrorist groups use front businesses for money laundering and procurement. Corporations and industries sometimes fund astroturf groups that present themselves as grassroots citizen organisations.

Detection and law

Because concealment is their purpose, fronts are identified through funding trails, shared personnel, and documentary evidence. Laws such as the United States Foreign Agents Registration Act require disclosure by organisations acting for foreign principals. The label is also contested in public debate, and organisations so described frequently deny the characterization.
