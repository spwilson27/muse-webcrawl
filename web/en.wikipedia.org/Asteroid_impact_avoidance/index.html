Asteroid impact avoidance

Asteroid impact avoidance comprises the methods by which near-Earth objects (NEOs) on a potential collision course with Earth can be diverted, preventing destructive impact events. A sufficiently large asteroid or comet could cause tsunamis, firestorms, an impact winter and, in the extreme case of the Chicxulub impactor 66 million years ago, a mass extinction.

Detection

Surveys such as Pan-STARRS, Catalina Sky Survey, ATLAS and NEOWISE catalogue near-Earth asteroids, and NASA estimates that more than 90 percent of near-Earth objects larger than one kilometre have been found, none posing a known threat this century. The planned NEO Surveyor space telescope is designed to find smaller, darker objects. Warning time is critical: most deflection techniques require years to decades of lead time to accumulate a sufficient change in velocity.

Deflection methods

Proposed techniques include the kinetic impactor, which collides a spacecraft with the object; the gravity tractor, which hovers nearby and slowly tows it by mutual gravitational attraction; nuclear explosive devices, detonated above the surface to ablate material and push the body off course; ion-beam shepherding; and, for comets with short warning times, fragmentation. Slow, gentle methods offer control but need long lead times, while impulsive methods suit emergencies.

DART mission

On 26 September 2022 NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) deliberately crashed into Dimorphos, the moonlet of asteroid 65803 Didymos, shortening its orbital period by about 33 minutes — the first demonstration that humanity can alter the motion of a celestial body. ESA's Hera mission was launched in 2024 to study the resulting crater in detail.

Policy and preparedness

Planetary defence is coordinated internationally through the International Asteroid Warning Network (IAWN) and the Space Mission Planning Advisory Group (SMPAG), both endorsed by the United Nations. Civil defence measures remain relevant for small, undetected objects like the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor.
