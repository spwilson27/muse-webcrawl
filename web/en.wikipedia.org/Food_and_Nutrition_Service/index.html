The Food and Nutrition Service (FNS) is an agency of the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA). Established on 8 August 1969, it administers the federal domestic food assistance programs of the United States, working in partnership with state, local, and tribal governments. Its stated mission is to increase food security and reduce hunger by providing children and low-income people access to food, a healthful diet, and nutrition education.

Programs

The FNS administers a group of nutrition assistance programs that together touch the lives of roughly one in four Americans over the course of a year. The largest is the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), formerly known as the Food Stamp Program, which provides benefits on electronic benefit transfer (EBT) cards for the purchase of food. Other major programs include:

  • The Special Supplemental Food Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC), serving pregnant and postpartum women, infants, and young children;
  • The National School Lunch Program and the School Breakfast Program;
  • The Child and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP);
  • The Summer Food Service Program;
  • The Emergency Food Assistance Program (TEFAP), which supplies food to food banks and soup kitchens;
  • The Commodity Supplemental Food Program for low-income elderly people;
  • The Food Distribution Program on Indian Reservations (FDPIR).

Organization and budget

The FNS is headed by an administrator and operates a national office in Alexandria, Virginia, together with regional offices across the country. Although the agency accounts for a relatively small share of the USDA's workforce, it administers the great majority of the department's budget outlays — in recent decades typically well over one hundred billion dollars per year, the bulk of it spent on SNAP benefits.

History

The agency's origins lie in the federal food programs of the 1930s and expanded with the Food Stamp Act of 1964 and the child nutrition legislation of the 1960s. The FNS was formally created in 1969 as food assistance operations were consolidated, and it has since administered successive expansions and reforms, including the nationwide rollout of food stamps in the 1970s and the transition from paper coupons to EBT in the 1990s and 2000s.
