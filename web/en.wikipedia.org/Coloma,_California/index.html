Coloma, California

Coloma is a census-designated place in El Dorado County, California, United States. It is best known as the site where James W. Marshall found gold at Sutter's Mill on 24 January 1848, sparking the California Gold Rush.

History

Coloma grew rapidly after the discovery of gold on the South Fork of the American River. At its peak in the 1850s it had several thousand residents, hotels, stores, and was briefly considered for the state capital. As placer gold declined, the town shrank.

Marshall Gold Discovery State Historic Park

Much of the town is now preserved within Marshall Gold Discovery State Historic Park, which includes a replica of Sutter's Mill, the Gold Discovery Museum, historic buildings, and the monument and grave of James Marshall overlooking the river valley.

Geography and recreation

Coloma lies in the Sierra Nevada foothills along Highway 49. The South Fork American River through Coloma is one of the most popular whitewater rafting runs in California.
