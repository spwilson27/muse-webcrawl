Right to work

The term right to work is used in several related senses concerning employment and labour law. In its original human-rights sense, it denotes the right of every person to engage in productive employment, recognised in Article 23 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and Article 6 of the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights.

United States labour law

In the United States, "right-to-work laws" are state statutes, authorised by the Taft–Hartley Act of 1947, that prohibit agreements between employers and unions requiring workers to join a union or pay union fees as a condition of employment. Supporters argue such laws protect individual freedom of association; opponents argue they allow workers to benefit from collective bargaining without contributing to its cost. More than half of US states have adopted such laws, and the Supreme Court's 2018 decision in Janus v. AFSCME extended similar rules to public-sector workers nationwide.
