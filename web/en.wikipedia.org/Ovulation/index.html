Ovulation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ovulation is the release of an egg (ovum) from an ovary. In humans this event occurs at about day 14 of a 28-day menstrual cycle, when a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and releases a secondary oocyte into the fallopian tube, where it may be fertilised by sperm. Ovulation marks the end of the follicular phase and the beginning of the luteal phase of the ovarian cycle.

Mechanism

Ovulation is triggered by a mid-cycle surge of luteinising hormone (LH) from the anterior pituitary, itself driven by rising oestrogen from the maturing Graafian follicle. The surge resumes meiosis in the oocyte, weakens the follicle wall, and some 24–36 hours later the oocyte is expelled. The ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for implantation; if pregnancy does not occur, it degenerates and menstruation follows.

Signs and disorders

Signs of approaching ovulation include changes in cervical mucus and a small rise in basal body temperature after the event; ovulation predictor kits detect the urinary LH surge. Some women feel mid-cycle pain (mittelschmerz). Failure to ovulate (anovulation) is a common cause of infertility and occurs in conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome, thyroid disease and hyperprolactinaemia. Hormonal contraceptives work chiefly by suppressing ovulation. See also menstrual cycle, ovary and fertilisation.
