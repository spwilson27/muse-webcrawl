Caribbean
Area: ~2,754,000 km² (incl. sea)
Population: ~44 million
Languages: Spanish, English, French, Dutch, Creoles
Largest cities: Santo Domingo, Havana, Port-au-Prince

The Caribbean is a region of the Americas that comprises the Caribbean Sea, its surrounding coasts, and its more than 700 islands, islets, reefs, and cays. The region is southeast of the Gulf of Mexico and the North American mainland, east of Central America, and north of South America.

Geography

The island arcs of the Caribbean are divided into the Greater Antilles (including Cuba, Hispaniola, Puerto Rico, and Jamaica), the Lesser Antilles, and the Lucayan Archipelago (the Bahamas and Turks and Caicos). The climate is tropical, influenced by trade winds, with a hurricane season running roughly from June to November.

History

The region was home to Indigenous peoples including the Taíno, Kalinago (Caribs), and Arawak. Christopher Columbus reached the Caribbean in 1492, and the islands became the first focus of Spanish colonisation in the Americas, followed by British, French, Dutch, and Danish colonies. The plantation economy depended on the enslavement of millions of Africans, a legacy that shapes the region's languages, religions, and cultures today.

Culture

Caribbean culture is known for musical forms including reggae, calypso, soca, salsa, merengue, and dancehall; for cricket and baseball; and for the Caribbean Carnival tradition. English, Spanish, French, Dutch, and numerous Creole languages are spoken across the region's more than 30 territories and sovereign states.
