Fauna of the Philippines

RegionPhilippines, Southeast Asia
Notable speciesPhilippine eagle, tarsier, tamaraw, whale shark
EndemismVery high, especially among mammals

The fauna of the Philippines include a highly diverse and distinctive assemblage of animals shaped by the archipelago's island geography and position in the tropics. The Philippines is one of the world's megadiverse countries, and because many of its islands were never connected to mainland Asia, it has exceptionally high levels of endemism — a large share of its species are found nowhere else on Earth.

Mammals

The Philippines has over 200 native terrestrial mammal species, more than half of them endemic — among the highest concentrations of endemic mammals per unit area in the world. Notable species include the Philippine tarsier, the tamaraw (a dwarf buffalo of Mindoro), the Philippine deer, cloud rats of Luzon, and an extraordinary radiation of bats, with more than 70 species.

Birds and other fauna

Over 700 bird species occur in the country, with roughly a third endemic; the Philippine eagle, one of the largest and rarest raptors, is the national bird. The surrounding seas, part of the Coral Triangle, hold some of the richest marine life anywhere, including whale sharks, dugongs, and hundreds of coral and fish species. Endemic reptiles include the Philippine crocodile and numerous skinks, geckos, and snakes.

Conservation

Deforestation, hunting, and habitat conversion have made many Philippine species threatened; the country is considered a biodiversity hotspot, and protected areas and breeding programmes target species such as the Philippine eagle and tamaraw.
