Breccia

Breccia is a rock composed of large angular broken fragments of minerals or rocks cemented together by a fine-grained matrix. The word has its origins in the Italian language, meaning "rubble". A breccia may have many different origins, as indicated by the named types including, but not limited to, sedimentary breccia, tectonic breccia, igneous breccia, impact breccia, and hydrothermal breccia.

A conglomerate is similar to a breccia, but the clasts in a conglomerate have rounded edges, indicating that they have been transported some distance from their source, whereas the angular clasts of a breccia indicate very little transport — the fragments were broken and deposited close to where they formed.

Types

Sedimentary breccia

Sedimentary breccia is a type of clastic sedimentary rock which is composed of angular to subangular, randomly oriented clasts of other sedimentary rocks. It forms in settings such as talus slopes at the foot of cliffs, debris flows, and submarine landslides, where material is deposited rapidly with little reworking.

Fault breccia

Fault or tectonic breccia results from the grinding and shattering of rock along a fault zone during movement. Fine-grained varieties produced by extreme comminution are known as cataclasite or fault gouge.

Igneous breccia

Igneous breccia forms within volcanic systems, for example when blocks of solidified lava are incorporated into later flows, or within volcanic vents and conduits where fragmented country rock is carried in magma.

Impact breccia

Impact breccia is created during impact cratering events and is a diagnostic indicator of ancient impact structures on Earth and other planetary bodies. Material excavated from the Chicxulub crater, associated with the extinction of the non-avian dinosaurs, includes breccias.

Hydrothermal breccia

Hydrothermal breccia is formed when hot fluids fracture rock and the fragments are cemented by precipitated minerals; such breccias are frequently associated with economically important ore deposits of copper, gold, and other metals.

Uses

Some breccias with attractive patterns are used as decorative building stone and in sculpture; brecciated marbles have been quarried since antiquity for flooring and monuments.
