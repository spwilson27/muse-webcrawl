Area studies

Area studies are interdisciplinary fields of research and scholarship pertaining to particular geographical, national/federal, or cultural regions. The term exists primarily as a general description for what are, in the practice of scholarship, many heterogeneous fields of research, encompassing both the social sciences and the humanities. Typical area study programmes involve history, political science, sociology, cultural studies, languages, geography, literature, and related disciplines. In contrast to cultural studies, area studies often involve a greater focus on languages and on the lived experience of the region's peoples.

History

Interdisciplinary area studies became increasingly common in the United States in the aftermath of World War II. During the war, the United States government and private foundations invested heavily in the study of foreign regions, training specialists who could serve as advisers and analysts. The National Defense Education Act of 1958 and funding from the Ford Foundation expanded university-based area studies centres, particularly for regions considered strategically important during the Cold War, such as the Soviet Union, China, the Middle East, and Latin America.

After the end of the Cold War, area studies faced criticism for dependence on government and foundation funding agendas, and for organising knowledge around nation-states and regions in an increasingly globalised world. Nevertheless, area studies programmes continue to thrive at many universities.

Fields

Common area studies fields include:

  • African studies
  • Asian studies, including East Asian studies, South Asian studies, and Southeast Asian studies
  • European studies
  • Latin American studies
  • Middle Eastern studies
  • American studies and Canadian studies
  • Pacific studies
