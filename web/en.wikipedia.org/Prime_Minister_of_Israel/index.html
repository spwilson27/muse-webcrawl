Prime Minister of Israel

The Prime Minister of Israel is the head of government and chief executive of the State of Israel. The prime minister leads the cabinet, directs the work of the government and its ministries, and is typically the leader of the largest party or coalition in the Knesset.

Selection

Following a Knesset election or a government's resignation, the President of Israel consults with party representatives and tasks the member of Knesset judged most likely to form a government with assembling a coalition. The nominee has 28 days (extendable by 14) to form a government, which must then win a vote of confidence in the Knesset.

Between 1996 and 2001 Israel experimented with the direct election of the prime minister, separate from Knesset elections; the system was abandoned and the previous parliamentary method restored.

History

David Ben-Gurion, who proclaimed the establishment of the state in 1948, was Israel's first prime minister and dominated its early politics. Other prominent holders of the office include Golda Meir, Menachem Begin, Yitzhak Rabin — who was assassinated in 1995 — Shimon Peres, Ehud Barak, Ariel Sharon, and Ehud Olmert. Benjamin Netanyahu is the longest-serving prime minister in Israeli history, having held the office across several periods from 1996 onward, returning to power at the end of 2022.

Residence

The official residence is Beit Aghion, on Balfour Street in Jerusalem, which has figured prominently in public protests and reporting.
