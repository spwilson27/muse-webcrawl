Aidan of Lindisfarne

Saint Aidan of Lindisfarne
Born: Ireland, c. 590
Died: 31 August 651, Bamburgh, Northumbria
Venerated in: Catholic Church; Anglican Communion; Eastern Orthodox Church
Feast: 31 August

Aidan of Lindisfarne (Irish: Naomh Aodhán; c. 590 – 31 August 651) was an Irish monk and missionary credited with converting the Anglo-Saxons of Northumbria to Christianity and founding the monastery at Lindisfarne. He is known as the "Apostle of Northumbria".

Mission

Aidan was a monk of Iona in Scotland. When King Oswald of Northumbria, who had converted during exile among the Irish, asked Iona for a missionary bishop, Aidan was chosen after a first emissary reported the Northumbrians too stubborn. Aidan arrived in 635 and chose the tidal island of Lindisfarne, near the royal fortress of Bamburgh, as his seat.

He travelled on foot through the kingdom, preaching and founding churches and schools; his interpreter was initially Oswald himself. The Venerable Bede praised Aidan's humility, charity, and dedication in the Ecclesiastical History of the English People.

Death and legacy

Aidan died at Bamburgh in 651. His legacy endured through the Northumbrian church and its missionaries, and Lindisfarne remained a centre of learning that produced the Lindisfarne Gospels.
