Droseraceae

Droseraceae is a family of carnivorous flowering plants in the order Caryophyllales. It comprises three extant genera: Drosera (the sundews), Dionaea (the Venus flytrap) and Aldrovanda (the waterwheel plant).

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Caryophyllales
Family: Droseraceae

Genera

Drosera, with roughly 200 species, is by far the largest genus and one of the largest genera of carnivorous plants. Its members trap insects on leaves covered with stalked glands tipped with sticky mucilage; the leaf often curls around the prey while enzymes digest it. Dionaea muscipula, the Venus flytrap, is the family's most famous member, using snap-trap leaves that close when trigger hairs are touched. Aldrovanda vesiculosa is a free-floating aquatic plant with small underwater snap traps.

Distribution and habitat

The family is nearly cosmopolitan, with its greatest diversity in Australia. Most species grow in nutrient-poor, acidic wetlands such as bogs and seeps, where trapping animals supplements the nitrogen and phosphorus their roots cannot obtain.

Evolution

Molecular studies place Droseraceae within a clade of carnivorous and associated families in Caryophyllales, alongside families such as Nepenthaceae. Fossil pollen attributed to the family extends its record back tens of millions of years.
