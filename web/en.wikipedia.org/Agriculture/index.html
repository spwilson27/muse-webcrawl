Agriculture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Agriculture encompasses crop and livestock production, aquaculture, and forestry for food and non-food products. It was the key development in the rise of sedentary human civilisation, whereby farming of domesticated species created food surpluses that enabled people to live in cities. Agriculture was independently invented in several regions, beginning more than 10,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent and elsewhere.

History

Wild grains were collected and eaten from at least 105,000 years ago, but systematic cultivation began in the Neolithic Revolution. Wheat and barley were domesticated in the Fertile Crescent; rice in China; maize, beans and squash in Mesoamerica; potatoes in the Andes; and sorghum and yams in Africa. Livestock such as sheep, goats, cattle and pigs were domesticated alongside crops. Over millennia, irrigation, crop rotation, fertilisers and selective breeding increased yields. The 20th-century Green Revolution dramatically raised productivity through high-yield varieties, synthetic fertilisers, pesticides and mechanisation.

Modern agriculture

Today agriculture employs more than one billion people, though its share of employment declines as economies develop. Major products include cereals (maize, wheat, rice), root crops, sugar, meat, milk, eggs, fibres such as cotton, and biofuels. Practices range from subsistence farming and shifting cultivation to intensive industrial agriculture with monocultures, greenhouses and precision farming using GPS and drones.

Environmental impact

Agriculture is a major driver of land-use change, deforestation, water consumption (about 70% of global freshwater withdrawals), biodiversity loss and greenhouse gas emissions. Sustainable approaches include crop rotation, cover cropping, integrated pest management, conservation tillage and agroecology.

Livestock

Animal husbandry produces meat, dairy, eggs, wool and leather, and provides draught power in many regions. Pastoralism remains a way of life for millions in arid and mountainous areas.
