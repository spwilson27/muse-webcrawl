Institutional repository

An institutional repository is an archive for collecting, preserving, and disseminating digital copies of the intellectual output of an institution, particularly a research institution. Such a repository typically holds scholarly articles, theses and dissertations, datasets, conference papers, and administrative documents produced by the institution's members.

Purpose

Institutional repositories are a central element of the open access movement, providing free public access to research outputs and increasing their visibility and citation impact. They also serve a preservation function, stewarding digital materials for the long term. Universities commonly run repositories using open-source platforms such as DSpace, EPrints, and Fedora.

History

The concept gained momentum in the early 2000s alongside subject-based repositories such as arXiv. The Budapest Open Access Initiative (2002) recommended self-archiving in repositories as one of two strategies for achieving open access. Many funders and institutions have since adopted open access mandates requiring deposit of published research.
