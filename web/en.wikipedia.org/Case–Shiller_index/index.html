Case–Shiller index

The Case–Shiller U.S. National Home Price Index, 1890–2016

The Case–Shiller indices are repeat-sales house price indices for the United States, originally developed by the economists Karl E. Case and Robert J. Shiller and now published by S&P Dow Jones Indices as the S&P CoreLogic Case–Shiller Home Price Indices. They are the best-known measures of US residential real estate prices and are watched closely as indicators of housing market conditions and the wider economy.

Methodology

The indices use the repeat-sales method pioneered by Case and Shiller (building on work by Bailey, Muth and Nourse): when a single-family home is sold twice, the change in its price is recorded, and the index is estimated from millions of such price pairs while attempting to hold housing quality constant. Homes with very short intervals between sales, non-arm's-length transfers and extreme price changes are excluded or down-weighted. Indices are published with a two-month lag.

The indices

The flagship series include the U.S. National Home Price Index, the 10-City and 20-City Composite indices (covering metropolitan areas such as New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, San Francisco and Miami), and individual metropolitan indices. Shiller's long historical series, constructed back to 1890, famously showed that real (inflation-adjusted) US house prices were roughly flat for a century before surging after the late 1990s — evidence central to his identification of the 2000s housing bubble in the second edition of Irrational Exuberance (2005).

Uses

The indices tracked the boom and bust of the US housing bubble: the 20-city composite rose by roughly 170 per cent from 2000 to its July 2006 peak and then fell by about a third into 2012, before recovering to new nominal highs. Futures and options based on the indices have traded on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange since 2006, allowing investors to hedge or speculate on house prices. The indices are also used to adjust conforming loan limits and as inputs to macroeconomic research.
