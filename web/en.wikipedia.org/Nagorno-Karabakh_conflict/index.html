Nagorno-Karabakh conflict

Nagorno-Karabakh and the surrounding districts

The Nagorno-Karabakh conflict was an ethnic and territorial conflict between Armenia and Azerbaijan over the region of Nagorno-Karabakh, inhabited predominantly by ethnic Armenians, and seven surrounding districts, inhabited predominantly by Azerbaijanis until their expulsion during the First Nagorno-Karabakh War. The region was internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan but was governed from 1991 by the breakaway Republic of Artsakh, supported by Armenia, until Azerbaijan re-established control in 2023.

Background

The dispute dates to the aftermath of the First World War and the Russian Empire's collapse. In 1923 the Soviet authorities placed Nagorno-Karabakh, despite its Armenian majority, within the Azerbaijan SSR as an autonomous oblast (NKAO). In February 1988 the NKAO soviet voted to join Armenia, triggering pogroms against Armenians in Sumgait and Baku and against Azerbaijanis in Armenia, and escalating into open war as the USSR dissolved.

First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1988–1994)

The full-scale war killed an estimated 30,000 people and displaced around a million. Armenian forces gained control of Nagorno-Karabakh and seven surrounding Azerbaijani districts. A Russian-brokered ceasefire (the Bishkek Protocol) took effect in May 1994, leaving a frozen conflict mediated by the OSCE Minsk Group.

Second Nagorno-Karabakh War (2020)

After clashes in 2016 (the Four-Day War) and years of stalemate, Azerbaijan launched a major offensive on 27 September 2020. Backed by Turkey and using advanced drones, Azerbaijan recaptured the surrounding districts and much of Karabakh, including Shusha, in 44 days. A Russia-brokered ceasefire of 9–10 November 2020 deployed Russian peacekeepers and confirmed Azerbaijan's gains.

2023 offensive and aftermath

Following a blockade of the Lachin corridor that began in December 2022, Azerbaijan launched a one-day offensive on 19–20 September 2023. The Artsakh authorities capitulated; the Republic of Artsakh announced it would dissolve by 1 January 2024, and virtually the entire Armenian population — over 100,000 people — fled to Armenia. Azerbaijan thus restored full control over the region, ending the decades-long conflict, though no comprehensive peace treaty had been signed as of 2024; Armenia and Azerbaijan negotiated a draft peace text in 2025.
