Cacio e pepe

Cacio e pepe is a pasta dish from the cuisine of Rome. The name means "cheese and pepper" in the Roman dialect. The dish contains pasta—traditionally tonnarelli or spaghetti—tossed with black pepper and grated Pecorino Romano cheese, emulsified with starchy pasta cooking water into a creamy sauce.

Cacio e pepe is one of the classic simple Roman pastas, alongside pasta alla gricia (which adds guanciale), carbonara and amatriciana. Its ingredients were staples of shepherds in the Roman countryside: dried pasta, hard aged cheese that kept without spoiling, and black pepper, said to help keep the shepherds warm. The technical challenge of the dish is temperature control: if the cheese gets too hot or the water ratio is wrong, the Pecorino clumps into strings instead of forming a smooth emulsion, a problem studied even by physicists interested in the sauce's phase behaviour.

Preparation

Freshly cracked black pepper is toasted in a pan, pasta is cooked in well-salted water, and finely grated Pecorino Romano is blended with hot pasta water into a paste before being tossed off the heat with the drained pasta until glossy.
