A hygrometer is an instrument used to measure the humidity, or amount of water vapour, in the atmosphere, in soil, or in confined spaces. Humidity measurement instruments usually rely on measurements of some other quantity, such as temperature, pressure, mass, or a mechanical or electrical change in a substance as moisture is absorbed. By calibration and calculation, these measured quantities can lead to a measurement of humidity.

Types

The psychrometer consists of two thermometers, one with a dry bulb and one with a bulb kept moist; evaporation cools the wet bulb, and the temperature difference gives the relative humidity. The hair-tension hygrometer, developed by Horace Bénédict de Saussure in 1783, exploits the change in length of human or animal hair with humidity. Modern electronic hygrometers use capacitive or resistive sensors whose electrical properties change as they absorb moisture, while chilled-mirror instruments measure the dew point directly and are used as calibration standards.

History

Rudimentary hygrometers were described by Leonardo da Vinci in the 15th century. Ferdinand II, Grand Duke of Tuscany, built a condensation hygrometer in 1655. The whirling psychrometer and dew-point hygrometer were refined in the 19th century as systematic weather observation expanded.

Applications

Hygrometers are used in weather stations and radiosondes, in industrial processes such as drying and semiconductor fabrication, in HVAC systems, and in museums and archives where controlled humidity protects sensitive objects.
