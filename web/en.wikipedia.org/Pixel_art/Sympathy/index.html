Sympathy

Sympathy is the perception, understanding, and reaction to the distress or need of another life form. It involves feelings of sorrow or concern for another person, and is closely related to, but distinct from, empathy: sympathy is feeling for someone, whereas empathy is feeling with them. In pixel art and other visual media, artists use composition, colour, and expression to evoke sympathetic responses in viewers.

Philosophical accounts

David Hume placed sympathy at the centre of his moral philosophy, arguing that moral judgments arise from our capacity to sympathise with the feelings of others. Adam Smith's The Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759) similarly grounded ethics in sympathy, mediated by the imagined perspective of an "impartial spectator."

Psychology

Developmental psychologists study sympathy as an other-oriented emotional response that emerges in early childhood and predicts prosocial behaviour such as helping, sharing, and comforting. Sympathy differs from personal distress, a self-focused aversive reaction to another's suffering.

Expression

Sympathy is commonly expressed through condolence messages, sympathy cards, charitable giving, and supportive presence. In art and literature, sympathetic characters are those whose misfortunes engage the audience's concern.
