Flannel

Flannel is a soft woven fabric, typically made of cotton, wool or synthetic fibre, that is brushed (napped) to create extra softness. Originally made from carded wool or worsted yarn, flannel is now produced in many fibres and is associated with warm shirts, pyjamas, bed sheets and the checked "lumberjack" patterns of flannel shirts.

History

The fabric is associated with Wales, where it was being produced by the 17th century; the word may derive from the Welsh gwlanen, "woollen cloth". Flannel became a staple of workwear in cold climates and, in the 1990s, of grunge fashion, when musicians in Seattle popularised plaid flannel shirts. Grey flannel trousers became a byword for conventional business dress, as in the novel and film The Man in the Gray Flannel Suit.

Production

Flannel is woven in a plain or twill weave and then brushed with fine metal brushes that raise short fibres from the yarn, trapping air and improving insulation. Flannelette is a lighter, cotton napped fabric in the same family.
