Check (pattern)

A check (or checkered pattern) is a pattern of modified stripes consisting of crossed horizontal and vertical lines forming squares. The pattern typically alternates two colours, as in the chessboard of black and white squares, gingham's even weave, or the intersecting bands of tartan.

Types

Well-known checks include gingham (plain weave with equal stripes in both directions), houndstooth (broken checks with abstract four-pointed shapes), windowpane (thin stripes on a plain ground), Madras checks, and the plaid patterns of Scottish tartans. Checker patterns also serve functional roles: the racing chequered flag signals the end of a race, and checkered floors are a classic feature of diners and barbershops.

History

Checked textiles are ancient — tartan-like fabrics have been found in Central Europe and in the Tarim Basin — and the pattern recurs in tilework, heraldry and the black-and-white of taxi liveries in several cities.
