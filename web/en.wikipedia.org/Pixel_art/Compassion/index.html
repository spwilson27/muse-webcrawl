Compassion

Compassion is the emotional response of sympathy and concern for the sufferings or misfortunes of others, coupled with a motivation to help. The word derives from Latin compati, "to suffer with". Compassion is considered a virtue in many religions and philosophies, and is distinct from empathy: where empathy is feeling with another, compassion adds the desire to relieve their suffering.

In religions and philosophy

In Buddhism, karuṇā is one of the four divine abodes and central to the bodhisattva ideal. Christianity teaches compassion as exemplified by the parable of the Good Samaritan; in Islam, God is named "the Most Compassionate" (al-Raḥmān). Arthur Schopenhauer made compassion the basis of morality, while modern psychologists study "compassion fatigue" among carers and the benefits of self-compassion, a research field developed by Kristin Neff.

Neuroscience

Compassion training studies suggest that practising compassion meditation changes activity in brain regions involved in emotion regulation and reward, and increases prosocial behaviour. Compassion is thus studied both as a moral quality and as a trainable mental skill.
