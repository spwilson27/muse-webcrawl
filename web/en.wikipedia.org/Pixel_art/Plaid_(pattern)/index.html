Plaid (pattern)

Plaid is a pattern consisting of criss-crossed horizontal and vertical bands in two or more alternating colours. In Scotland, such patterned cloth is known as tartan, and the word "plaid" originally referred to a heavy woollen garment or blanket worn over the shoulder. In North America, however, "plaid" has become the common term for the pattern itself, whether in flannel shirts, upholstery, or other fabrics.

Construction

Plaid patterns are woven with pre-dyed yarns in both the warp and the weft. Where the two bands of colour cross, the colours blend visually, producing a third, intermediate shade and the characteristic checked appearance. The sequence of coloured threads is called the sett; repeating the sett across the width of the loom creates the full pattern.

History and usage

Checked woven patterns are ancient, but plaid became strongly associated with Scottish clan tartans, each with its own distinctive sett and colour scheme. In the twentieth century, plaid flannel became a staple of workwear in North America and later a recurring element in youth and fashion culture, from lumberjack shirts to punk and grunge styles.
