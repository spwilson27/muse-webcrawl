Empathy

Empathy is the capacity to understand or feel what another person is experiencing from within their frame of reference — that is, the capacity to place oneself in another's position. Definitions of empathy encompass a broad range of phenomena, including caring for other people and having a desire to help them, experiencing emotions that match another person's, and knowing what the other person is thinking or feeling.

Types

Researchers commonly distinguish cognitive empathy (perspective-taking, or "theory of mind"), affective empathy (sharing another's emotional state), and somatic empathy (physical reactions to another's state). Empathy is measured by instruments such as the Interpersonal Reactivity Index, and deficits in empathy are associated with some psychopathologies, though the picture is complex.

Development and biology

Precursors of empathy appear early in childhood and in some other primates. The discovery of mirror neurons — cells that fire both when acting and when observing the same action — popularised a neural account of empathy, though its scope remains debated. Empathy is a foundation for prosocial behaviour, moral development and effective psychotherapy.
