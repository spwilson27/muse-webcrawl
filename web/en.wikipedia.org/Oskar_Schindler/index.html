Oskar Schindler


Born: 28 April 1908, Zwittau, Austria-Hungary (now Svitavy, Czech Republic)
Died: 9 October 1974 (aged 66), Hildesheim, West Germany
Known for: Saving about 1,200 Jews during the Holocaust
Honours: Righteous Among the Nations

Oskar Schindler (28 April 1908 – 9 October 1974) was a German industrialist and a member of the Nazi Party who is credited with saving the lives of about 1,200 Jews during the Holocaust by employing them in his enamelware and ammunitions factories in occupied Poland and the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia. He is the subject of Thomas Keneally's 1982 novel Schindler's Ark and its 1993 film adaptation, Schindler's List, directed by Steven Spielberg.

Early life and wartime factories

Born in Zwittau in Moravia, Schindler worked in sales and for German military intelligence before the war. After the invasion of Poland he acquired a factory in Kraków, the Deutsche Emailwarenfabrik (DEF), producing enamelware for the German military. Relying on the black market and bribes, he grew wealthy while employing Jewish workers from the Kraków ghetto, whom he increasingly protected from deportation. After witnessing the liquidation of the ghetto in 1943, he redoubled efforts to shield his workforce, the Schindlerjuden.

The list and Brünnlitz

When his factory was ordered relocated in 1944, Schindler persuaded officials to move his workers with him to a new camp at Brünnlitz in his home region, compiling with his accountant Itzhak Stern the famous lists of workers to be transferred. He spent his entire fortune on bribes and supplies to keep them alive until the war ended in May 1945.

Postwar life and recognition

Schindler's postwar business ventures failed; he lived partly on support from Jewish organisations and the people he had saved. In 1963 Israel recognised him as Righteous Among the Nations. He died in Hildesheim in 1974 and was buried in Jerusalem on Mount Zion, the only former Nazi Party member honoured in this way.
