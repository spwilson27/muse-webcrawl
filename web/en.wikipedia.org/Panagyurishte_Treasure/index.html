Panagyurishte Treasure

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Panagyurishte Treasure (Bulgarian: Панагюрско златно съкровище) is a Thracian hoard of gold vessels discovered in 1949 near the town of Panagyurishte in central Bulgaria. It is among the most famous treasures of antiquity in the Balkans, renowned for the virtuosity of its goldsmithing, and is exhibited at the National History Museum in Sofia, with rotations to Plovdiv and Panagyurishte itself.

Discovery

On 8 December 1949, three brothers — Pavel, Petko and Michail Deikov — working at a tile factory quarry near Panagyurishte, unearthed the vessels while digging clay. They initially took the pieces for something far humbler; on realising they had found gold, the find was reported and the treasure entered the state collections.

Contents

The hoard comprises nine vessels of solid gold with a combined weight of just over 6 kg: four rhytons shaped as animal heads and female heads, three rhyton-like jugs, a large amphora-rhyton, and a phiale (shallow libation bowl) decorated with heads and acorns, the phiale bearing weight inscriptions in Greek units. The pieces carry figured scenes from Greek mythology, including the Judgement of Paris, Heracles, Theseus and Dionysian themes.

Date and origin

The treasure is dated to the late 4th or early 3rd century BC and is associated with the Odrysian kingdom; inscriptions on the phiale have been used to argue it was made in the city of Lampsacus in Asia Minor, or by Thracian craftsmen working in a Hellenistic Greek style. It was probably buried for safekeeping during an invasion — candidates include the Celtic incursions into the Balkans around 280 BC.
