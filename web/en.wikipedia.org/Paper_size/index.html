Paper size

Paper size refers to standard dimensions of sheets of paper used as writing material, printed material, or in office environments. It is usually measured by width and height , in millimetres or inches. The concept of paper size conventions originated in the 14th century among Bolognese paper makers, whose product was used mainly for drawing and writing; those sizes were commensurate with average arm lengths, and different shapes were later used for different purposes.

International standard: ISO 216

The international paper size standard is ISO 216, based on the German DIN 476 standard. Its A series uses an aspect ratio of 1:√2 (approximately 1:1.4142). The largest size, A0, has an area of one square metre; each subsequent size is half the area of the previous, obtained by folding along the longer side. The most widely used size is A4 (210 × 297 mm), the standard sheet for letters and documents in most of the world.

B series and C series

The ISO B series gives intermediate sizes defined as geometric means of successive A sizes, and is widely used for posters, books and envelopes' contents. The C series, standardized in ISO 269, provides envelope sizes designed to hold A-series sheets: a C4 envelope holds an unfolded A4 sheet, C5 an A4 folded in half, and C6 an A4 folded in quarters.

North American sizes

The United States, Canada and parts of Latin America use sizes derived from traditional English paper sizes, standardized by ANSI/ASME Y14.1. The most common are Letter (8.5 × 11 in), Legal (8.5 × 14 in), Tabloid/Ledger (11 × 17 in), and Executive (7.25 × 10.5 in). Unlike the ISO system, these sizes have no consistent aspect ratio, which complicates scaling between sizes.

Traditional and other sizes

Before standardization, paper was sold in dozens of traditional sizes with colourful names such as Foolscap, Demy, Royal, Imperial and Elephant, varying by country and even by mill. Japan retains its own JIS B series (similar to ISO B but slightly different) and traditional Shiroku-ban and Kiku sizes for books. China uses several sizes close to but not identical with ISO sizes.
