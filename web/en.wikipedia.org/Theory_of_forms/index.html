Theory of forms

The theory of forms is Plato's account of abstract objects: beyond the changing world of the senses there exist timeless Forms — the Beautiful, the Equal, the Just — in which particular things participate and from which they derive their character.

Participation and knowledge

For Plato, genuine knowledge must have a stable object; since sensible things change, knowledge is of the Forms, grasped by the soul through recollection (anamnesis) and dialectic. A beautiful thing is beautiful because it participates in Beauty itself, which is perfect and unchanging.

Development and debates

Scholars debate whether Plato's view changed across the dialogues and how literally he intended the "separation" of Forms from things. Aristotle's criticism — that Forms merely duplicate the things they explain — set the terms for the medieval problem of universals.
