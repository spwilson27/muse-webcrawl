Chaas

Alternative namesChaach, moru, neer more, majiga
RegionIndian subcontinent
TypeBeverage
Main ingredientsYogurt (curd), water, spices, salt
ServedChilled

Chaas (also spelled chaach) is a curd-based drink popular across the Indian subcontinent. In India it is also known by regional names such as tak in Marathi, moru in Tamil and Malayalam, and majiga in Telugu. It is similar to lassi, but thinner in consistency and usually salty or spiced rather than sweet.

Preparation

Chaas is made by churning yogurt (curd) with water, traditionally in a pot with a wooden churner, until the butter separates and the remaining liquid takes on a light, frothy consistency. In modern preparation the drink is often blended and flavored with salt, roasted cumin, black salt, ginger, curry leaves, mustard seeds tempered in oil, asafoetida, or fresh coriander and mint.

Regional variations

In Gujarat, chaas accompanies most meals and is served plain or lightly spiced. In Tamil Nadu, the related neer moru is prepared during festivals such as Rama Navami. In Punjab, a richer version overlaps with salty lassi. Masala chaas, tempered with spices and curry leaves, is popular across western India.

Nutrition and culture

Because it is mostly water and yogurt, chaas is low in calories and is valued as a cooling, hydrating drink in the hot Indian summer. It is also regarded in Ayurveda as an aid to digestion and is commonly served after heavy or spicy meals.
