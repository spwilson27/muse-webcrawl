Lazare Carnot

Lazare Nicolas Marguerite, Comte Carnot (13 May 1753 – 2 August 1823) was a French mathematician, physicist and politician of the French Revolution and the Napoleonic Wars. As a member of the Committee of Public Safety in 1793–94 he directed the French war effort so effectively that he was hailed as the "Organizer of Victory". He was also a founder of modern descriptive geometry and the father of the physicist Sadi Carnot.

Early life and science

Born in Nolay, Burgundy, Carnot trained as a military engineer and served in garrisons in northern France. His 1783 Essai sur les machines treated the theory of machines, and his later Géométrie de position (1803) and work on transversal theory laid groundwork for projective geometry. His name appears in several results, including Carnot's theorem in geometry.

Revolutionary career

Elected to the Legislative Assembly and then the National Convention, Carnot voted for the execution of Louis XVI. Joining the Committee of Public Safety in August 1793, he supervised the levée en masse, reorganised fourteen armies, and planned the campaigns that saved the Republic, including the victory at Wattignies. He quarrelled with Robespierre and Saint-Just and survived Thermidor, though he was later accused over the Terror.

Later life

Carnot served on the Directory in 1795–97, fled after the Fructidor coup, and returned to become Minister of War under Napoleon in 1800 and again during the Hundred Days in 1815. Proscribed as a regicide at the Bourbon Restoration, he spent his final years in exile in Magdeburg. His remains were transferred to the Panthéon in 1889; his name is one of the 72 inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.
