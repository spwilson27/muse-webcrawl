Poor Richard's Almanack

Poor Richard's Almanack (sometimes Almanac) was a yearly almanac published by Benjamin Franklin, who adopted the pseudonym of "Poor Richard" or "Richard Saunders" for the purpose. Published continuously from 1732 to 1758, it was a runaway best-seller in colonial America, selling as many as 10,000 copies a year, and made Franklin wealthy.

Contents

Like other almanacs of the day, it contained calendars, astronomical data, weather forecasts, tide tables, and useful chronologies. Its lasting fame, however, comes from the aphorisms and proverbs Franklin scattered through it in the voice of Poor Richard — witty, practical maxims celebrating industry, frugality, and common sense. Famous examples include "Early to bed and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy, and wise," "A penny saved is a penny earned" (in Franklin's form, "A penny saved is two pence clear"), and "Time is money."

The Way to Wealth

For the 1758 edition Franklin collected many of the sayings into the preface, a speech by the character Father Abraham known as The Way to Wealth. It became one of the most widely reprinted pieces of American writing and was translated into numerous languages, spreading Poor Richard's aphorisms around the world.

Significance

The Almanack is a landmark of early American publishing and of Franklin's career: its profits underwrote his printing business and civic projects, and its homespun maxims helped define an emerging American ethic of self-improvement and thrift.
