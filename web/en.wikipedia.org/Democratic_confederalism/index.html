Democratic confederalism is a political ideology and system of governance developed by Abdullah Öcalan, the imprisoned leader of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK). Drawing on the communalism of American anarchist Murray Bookchin, it proposes a stateless, decentralised society organised through democratic assemblies and confederated councils.

Principles

Öcalan set out the system in writings from prison on İmralı, notably his 2005 declaration. Its pillars include radical democracy through neighbourhood communes and councils; ecology and sustainability; women's liberation (jineology), expressed in co-chair systems reserving leadership posts jointly for a woman and a man and minimum quotas for women's representation; and ethnic and religious pluralism. It rejects the nation-state as a vehicle for Kurdish aspirations in favour of self-governing, confederated communities.

Implementation

From 2012, Kurdish-led forces in northern Syria established an autonomous administration — commonly called Rojava — that officially adopted democratic confederalism as its guiding framework, later restyled the Democratic Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria. Its practical application amid civil war, and the degree to which power is genuinely decentral or concentrated in the dominant party, remain subjects of scholarly debate.
