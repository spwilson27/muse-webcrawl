Hotmail

Hotmail
Type: Webmail service
Founded: July 4, 1996
Founders: Sabeer Bhatia, Jack Smith
Owner: Microsoft
Successor: Outlook.com

Hotmail was a free web-based email service founded by Sabeer Bhatia and Jack Smith in 1996. Headquartered in Sunnyvale, California, it was one of the first webmail services and became a defining product of the early consumer internet, allowing users to access their email from any computer with a web browser rather than through an internet service provider's software.

History

Hotmail was launched on July 4, 1996 — Independence Day in the United States — symbolizing freedom from ISP-based email. The name was stylized as "HoTMaiL" in reference to HTML, the language used to build web pages. The service grew explosively through viral marketing: every outgoing message carried a tagline inviting recipients to sign up for a free account. Within a year and a half it had attracted more than 8.5 million subscribers.

In December 1997, Microsoft acquired Hotmail for an estimated $400 million and integrated it into its MSN group of services, later rebranding it as MSN Hotmail and then Windows Live Hotmail. The service's storage allowances grew over time from 2 MB at launch to effectively unlimited storage, and it added calendar, contacts and spam-filtering features.

Outlook.com transition

On July 31, 2012, Microsoft launched Outlook.com as the successor to Hotmail, with a redesigned interface and integration with Skype and Microsoft's other services. Existing Hotmail users were gradually migrated, and by 2013 the transition was complete, ending Hotmail's run as a standalone brand after 17 years. Users could keep their @hotmail.com addresses, which continue to function within Outlook.com.
