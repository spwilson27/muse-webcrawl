Backgammon

Backgammon is a two-player board game played with counters and dice on tables boards. It is one of the oldest known board games, with roots stretching back roughly 5,000 years to ancient Mesopotamia. Players race their fifteen checkers around the board and off it, with movement determined by rolls of two dice, combining strategy and luck.

History

Games of the tables family were played in ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, and Rome, where a version called ludus duodecim scriptorum was popular. The Persian game nard and the Roman tabula are direct ancestors. Backgammon in something close to its modern form spread through Europe in the 17th century, and its name, first recorded in 1647, may derive from "back" and the Middle English "gamen" (game). The doubling cube was introduced in New York in the 1920s, transforming the game into a popular gambling and tournament contest.

Rules

The board consists of 24 narrow triangles called points, grouped into four quadrants. Each player has 15 checkers arranged in the standard starting position. On a turn, a player rolls two dice and moves checkers the corresponding number of points toward their home board, moving in opposite directions around the board. A point occupied by two or more opposing checkers is blocked; a single checker (a blot) can be hit and sent to the bar, from which it must re-enter before other moves are made. Once all of a player's checkers are in the home board, they may begin bearing off; the first to bear off all fifteen wins.

Strategy

Key strategic concepts include building primes (walls of blocked points), anchoring in the opponent's home board, balancing racing against hitting and containment, and using the doubling cube to press an advantage or concede points efficiently. Computer programs such as GNU Backgammon and the neural-network pioneer TD-Gammon revolutionized understanding of opening play and checker strategy from the 1990s onward.

Variants and competition

Regional relatives include Greek tavli, Turkish tavla, and the dice-less racing game nard. International tournament play is overseen by bodies such as the World Backgammon Federation, and a world championship has been held in Monte Carlo since the 1960s.
