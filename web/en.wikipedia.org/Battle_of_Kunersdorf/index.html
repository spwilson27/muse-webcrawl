Battle of Kunersdorf

The Battle of Kunersdorf was fought on 12 August 1759 near Kunersdorf (Kunowice, in modern Poland), east of Frankfurt an der Oder, during the Seven Years' War. A combined Russo-Austrian army under Pyotr Saltykov and Ernst von Laudon inflicted the worst defeat of Frederick the Great's career: of some 50,000 Prussians engaged, over 18,000 were lost, along with most of the artillery. The road to Berlin lay open, yet the allies failed to exploit the victory — a reprieve Frederick later counted among the strokes of fortune, culminating in the "Miracle of the House of Brandenburg".

Course of the battle

Frederick crossed the Oder and attacked the allied army from the north, intending an oblique assault on its left flank. Prussian infantry initially overran the Russian observation corps on the Mühlberge, but attacks bogged down against fieldworks across broken, marshy ground around the Kuhgrund ravine. Exhausted troops, massed under allied artillery fire, were then counterattacked by Laudon's fresh Austrian cavalry and infantry. The Prussian army disintegrated; Frederick, twice nearly captured and with two horses shot under him, escaped with a remnant of a few thousand men. Saltykov and Laudon quarrelled over strategy and supplies, however, and withdrew rather than marching on Berlin, allowing Frederick to rebuild his army.
