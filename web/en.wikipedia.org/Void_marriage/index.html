Void marriage

A void marriage is a marriage that is treated in law as though it had never existed, because it was invalid from the beginning (void ab initio). Unlike a voidable marriage, which is valid until annulled by a court or by one of the parties, a void marriage requires no annulment proceeding to establish its invalidity, though courts may issue a declaration to that effect.

Grounds

Grounds for a marriage being void vary by jurisdiction but typically include bigamy (one party already married), incestuous relationships within prohibited degrees of kinship, and marriage by a person below the minimum marriageable age or lacking legal capacity. Some jurisdictions also treat marriages procured without essential formalities, such as a licence or qualified officiant where these are mandatory, as void.

Consequences

Because a void marriage is legally non-existent, the parties acquire no marital rights from it, yet many legal systems protect children of such unions (who remain legitimate in many jurisdictions) and grant equitable remedies, such as the putative spouse doctrine, to a party who believed in good faith that the marriage was valid.
