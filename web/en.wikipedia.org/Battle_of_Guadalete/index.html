Battle of Guadalete

The Battle of Guadalete was fought in July 711 in southern Hispania between the Visigothic kingdom of King Roderic and an invading Umayyad army led by Tariq ibn Ziyad. The Visigothic defeat — and Roderic's death — opened the way to the Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula and the collapse of the Visigothic kingdom.

Background

Roderic had seized the Visigothic throne amid civil war, and disaffected factions reportedly encouraged the invasion from North Africa. Tariq crossed the strait that took its name from him — Gibraltar, from Jabal Tariq, "Tariq's mountain" — with some 7,000–12,000 men, mostly Berbers, and pushed inland. Roderic hurried south from campaigning against the Basques to meet him.

The battle

The armies met near the Guadalete River, though the exact site is debated — the La Janda lagoon and the Guadarranque are also proposed. According to later chronicles, the battle lasted several days of skirmishing before a decisive clash in which wings of the Visigothic army, commanded by Roderic's rivals of the Wittiza faction, deserted or defected. Roderic was killed, and his army destroyed.

Aftermath

With the king dead and the nobility decimated, organised resistance collapsed. Tariq advanced on Toledo, and within a few years most of Iberia was under Muslim rule as al-Andalus, shaping the peninsula's history for the next eight centuries.
