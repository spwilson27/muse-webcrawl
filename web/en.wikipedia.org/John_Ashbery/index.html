John Ashbery

John Lawrence Ashbery (July 28, 1927 – September 3, 2017) was an American poet and art critic, regarded as one of the most influential poets of the second half of the 20th century. He was a central figure of the New York School alongside Frank O'Hara, James Schuyler and Kenneth Koch.


John Ashbery

Career

Educated at Harvard and Columbia, Ashbery lived in Paris for a decade from 1955, working as an art critic for the International Herald Tribune and Art News. His second collection, The Tennis Court Oath (1962), was controversial for its radical disjunction, while Self-Portrait in a Convex Mirror (1975) achieved an unprecedented sweep of the Pulitzer Prize, the National Book Award and the National Book Critics Circle Award.

Style

Ashbery's poems are known for shifting pronouns and tones, collage-like juxtapositions, colloquial language mixed with elevated diction, and a dreamlike attention to the workings of consciousness and the passage of time. Critics have linked his work to Wallace Stevens, surrealism and abstract expressionist painting.

Recognition

He served as Chancellor of the Academy of American Poets, won the Bollingen Prize and a MacArthur "Genius" grant, and taught at Brooklyn College and Bard College. His collected prose and art criticism remain influential.
