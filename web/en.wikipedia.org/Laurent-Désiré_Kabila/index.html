Laurent-Désiré Kabila

Laurent-Désiré Kabila (27 November 1939 – 16 January 2001) was a Congolese politician and rebel leader who served as President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from May 1997 until his assassination in January 2001. He overthrew the long-ruling dictator Mobutu Sese Seko during the First Congo War, restoring the country's name from Zaire to the Democratic Republic of the Congo. He was succeeded by his son, Joseph Kabila.

Early life and rebellion

Kabila was born in Baudouinville (now Moba) in Katanga Province and studied political philosophy in France and at the University of Dar es Salaam. Returning to Congo after independence, he became a follower of Patrice Lumumba and took part in the Simba rebellion of 1964–65, during which he briefly worked alongside Che Guevara, who judged him unfavourably. Kabila then ran a small Marxist insurgent statelet around Fizi in South Kivu for two decades, sustained partly by gold trading and kidnappings, including the 1975 abduction of Western researchers near Lake Tanganyika.

First Congo War and presidency

In 1996, backed by Rwanda, Uganda, and Angola, Kabila became spokesman of the Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Congo (AFDL). His forces marched across the country and took Kinshasa in May 1997 as Mobutu fled. As president, Kabila banned political parties, postponed elections, and was accused of authoritarianism and corruption. Relations with his Rwandan and Ugandan backers collapsed in 1998, triggering the Second Congo War, in which rebels backed by Rwanda and Uganda nearly took Kinshasa before Angola, Zimbabwe, and Namibia intervened on Kabila's side.

Assassination

On 16 January 2001 Kabila was shot in the presidential palace in Kinshasa by one of his bodyguards, Rashidi Kasereka, and died of his wounds. The circumstances of the assassination remain disputed. His son Joseph succeeded him and eventually negotiated an end to the war.
