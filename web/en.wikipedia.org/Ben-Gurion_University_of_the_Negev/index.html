Ben-Gurion University of the Negev

Ben-Gurion University of the Negev (BGU) is a public research university in Beersheba, Israel. Founded in 1969 as the University of the Negev, it was renamed in 1973 in honour of David Ben-Gurion, Israel's first prime minister, who believed that the future of the country lay in the Negev desert. The university has about 20,000 students.

Campuses and faculties

The main Marcus Family Campus is in Beersheba; the university also has campuses in Sde Boker, beside Ben-Gurion's grave and the Jacob Blaustein Institutes for Desert Research, and in Eilat. Its faculties include engineering sciences, natural sciences, health sciences, humanities and social sciences, and management. The university is known internationally for research in desert agriculture, water technology, cybersecurity and robotics, and its medical school pioneered community-oriented medical education.
