The Devil in the White City

The Devil in the White City: Murder, Magic, and Madness at the Fair That Changed America is a 2003 historical non-fiction book by Erik Larson, presented in a novelistic style. Set in Chicago around the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition, it interweaves the stories of architect Daniel Burnham, chief of works for the fair, and H. H. Holmes, a serial killer who lured victims to his nearby "World's Fair Hotel" — the so-called Murder Castle.

Synopsis

The book alternates between Burnham's monumental effort to build the "White City" — overcoming the deaths of collaborators, the mud of Jackson Park, Frederick Law Olmsted's landscaping, and the invention of the Ferris Wheel to outdo the Eiffel Tower — and Holmes's parallel enterprise: a chemist, fraudster and bigamist who built a block-long hotel fitted with secret passages, gas lines and a crematorium, where an unknown number of guests, employees and fiancées disappeared. Holmes was arrested in 1894, convicted of murder in Philadelphia, and hanged in May 1896. The fair itself drew some 27 million visitors and left a lasting mark on American architecture, urban planning and culture.

Reception and adaptation

The book spent years on the New York Times bestseller list, won the 2004 Edgar Award for Best Fact Crime, and is credited with popularizing dual-narrative narrative non-fiction. A screen adaptation has been in development for many years — Leonardo DiCaprio acquired the film rights in 2010, and a limited television series was developed at Hulu with Martin Scorsese and DiCaprio involved, before being shelved in 2023.
