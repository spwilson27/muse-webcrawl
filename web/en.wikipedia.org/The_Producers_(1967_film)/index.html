The Producers (1967 film)

The Producers is a 1967 American satirical comedy film written and directed by Mel Brooks in his directorial debut. It stars Zero Mostel as the unscrupulous Broadway producer Max Bialystock and Gene Wilder as his timid accountant Leo Bloom, who conspire to stage a guaranteed flop so they can keep their investors' money.

Plot

Bloom realises that a producer could make more money with a flop than with a hit by overselling shares in the production. The pair choose the worst script imaginable — Springtime for Hitler, a musical celebrating the Führer written by the deranged Franz Liebkind — hire the flamboyant director Roger De Bris, and cast the spaced-out actor Lorenzo St. Dubois ("L.S.D.") as Hitler. To their horror, the audience interprets the show as satire and it becomes a smash hit, leaving them ruined and, after a desperate attempt to blow up the theatre, imprisoned.

Reception and legacy

Initially controversial and only modestly successful, the film won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay and grew into a comedy classic, selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry. Brooks later adapted it into a hit Broadway musical (2001), which was itself filmed in 2005.
