Yash Chopra

Yash Raj Chopra (27 September 1932 – 21 October 2012) was an Indian film director and producer who worked in Hindi cinema. Founder of Yash Raj Films, he is regarded as one of the most influential filmmakers in Bollywood history, celebrated for romantic dramas that defined the genre for generations.

Career

Born in Lahore, British India, Chopra began as an assistant to I. S. Johar and to his elder brother, director B. R. Chopra. His directorial debut was Dhool Ka Phool (1959). For B. R. Films he directed the acclaimed Waqt (1965), which pioneered the "lost and found" multi-star formula, and Ittefaq (1969).

In 1970 he founded Yash Raj Films. His 1970s hits Daag (1973), Deewaar (1975) — a landmark of the "angry young man" era starring Amitabh Bachchan — Kabhi Kabhie (1976), and Trishul (1978) cemented his reputation. After a quieter 1980s, Chandni (1989) revived the musical romance; Lamhe (1991), a commercial disappointment, is now considered a classic. Darr (1993) and Dil To Pagal Hai (1997) were major successes, and he produced the era-defining Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (1995), directed by his son Aditya Chopra. His final film was Jab Tak Hai Jaan (2012), released shortly after his death from dengue fever in Mumbai.

Style and legacy

Chopra's romances are known for lush foreign locations (especially Switzerland, whose Lake Brienz region honoured him), white-clad heroines, poetic dialogue, and music integral to storytelling. He won numerous Filmfare Awards including several for Best Director, and received the Padma Bhushan in 2005 and the Dadasaheb Phalke Award in 2001. Under Aditya Chopra, Yash Raj Films grew into India's largest film studio.
