Katyusha rocket launcher

The Katyusha (Russian: Катюша) is a type of rocket artillery first built and fielded by the Soviet Union in World War II. Multiple rocket launchers such as these deliver explosives to a target area more intensively than conventional artillery, but with lower accuracy and requiring a longer time to reload. They are fragile compared to artillery guns, but are inexpensive, easy to produce, and usable on almost any chassis.

The Katyushas of World War II, the first self-propelled artillery mass-produced by the Soviet Union, were usually mounted on ordinary trucks. This mobility gave the Katyusha, and other self-propelled artillery, another advantage: being able to deliver a large blow all at once, and then move before being located and attacked with counter-battery fire.

History

Katyusha weapons of World War II included the BM-13 launcher, light BM-8, and heavy BM-31 "Andryusha". Today, the nickname Katyusha is also applied to newer truck-mounted post-Soviet multiple-rocket launchers, notably the common BM-21 Grad and its derivatives.

The first combat use of the Katyusha occurred on 14 July 1941, when an experimental artillery battery of seven launchers under Captain Ivan Flerov was used against German troops at the railway station of Orsha in Belarus. The weapon was initially kept under strict secrecy; troops were ordered to destroy the launchers rather than allow them to fall into enemy hands.

Nickname

The name Katyusha comes from a popular Soviet song of the same name composed by Matvey Blanter with lyrics by Mikhail Isakovsky, about a girl named Katyusha (a diminutive of Ekaterina) longing for her lover serving on the border. Legend holds that factory workers assembling the launchers wrote the name on the weapons, or that soldiers sang the song as the rockets were fired.

Design

The most famous variant, the BM-13, fired 132 mm M-13 rockets from a bank of 16 launch rails. Although inaccurate, a battery of four BM-13 launchers could deliver a salvo of 64 rockets onto a target area within seconds. Later variants included the BM-8 (82 mm) and the BM-31 "Andryusha" (300 mm).

Legacy

The Katyusha became one of the most iconic Soviet weapons of the war, commemorated by monuments across the former Soviet Union, many consisting of a truck-mounted launcher on a pedestal. Its psychological effect on German troops, who nicknamed it "Stalin's organ" (German: Stalinorgel) for the howling sound of its rockets and the organ-pipe appearance of its launch rails, was considerable.
