Equations of motion

Field
Classical mechanics
Key figures
Newton, Euler, Lagrange, Hamilton

In physics, equations of motion are equations that describe the behaviour of a physical system in terms of its motion as a function of time. Most generally they are differential equations relating position, velocity, acceleration, and forces. The foundation of classical mechanics is Newton's second law, F = ma.

Constant acceleration (SUVAT equations)

For motion in a straight line under constant acceleration a, five equations connect displacement s, initial velocity u, final velocity v, acceleration, and time t: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; s = vt − ½at²; v² = u² + 2as; and s = (u + v)t/2.

General formulations

Analytical mechanics reformulates the equations of motion using generalised coordinates: the Euler–Lagrange equations derive them from a scalar Lagrangian, and Hamilton's equations from the Hamiltonian, expressing dynamics as flows in phase space. These formulations extend naturally to fields, relativity, and (via path integrals) quantum mechanics.
