Sikkim

Sikkim is a state in northeastern India. Bordered by Bhutan to the east, the Indian state of West Bengal to the south, and Nepal to the west, it also shares a border with China's Tibet Autonomous Region to the north. Sikkim is the least populous and second smallest among the Indian states. Its capital and largest city is Gangtok.

Sikkim is notable for its biodiversity, including alpine and subtropical climates, and for Kangchenjunga, the highest peak in India and third-highest on Earth, which lies on its border with Nepal. Almost 35% of the state is covered by the Khangchendzonga National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The Kingdom of Sikkim was founded in 1642 by Phuntsog Namgyal, the first Chogyal (priest-king), of the Namgyal dynasty. It became a British protectorate in the 19th century and an Indian protectorate in 1950. Following a referendum in 1975, in which the monarchy was abolished, Sikkim joined India as its 22nd state. The indigenous peoples include the Lepcha, the Bhutia, and the Nepali ethnic groups who now form the majority of the population.

Geography and climate

The state is almost entirely mountainous, ranging from about 280 m in the south to 8,586 m at Kangchenjunga's summit. The Teesta River and its tributaries drain the state. Its climate ranges from subtropical in the valleys to tundra at the highest elevations, and it is one of India's most environmentally conscious states: Sikkim became India's first fully organic state in 2016.

Economy and culture

Tourism, agriculture (including large cardamom, of which Sikkim is India's leading producer), and pharmaceuticals are key economic sectors. The state is predominantly Hindu and Vajrayana Buddhist, with major monasteries such as Pemayangtse and Rumtek, home of the Karmapa.
