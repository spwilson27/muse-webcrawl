Henry George Liddell (6 February 1811 – 18 January 1898) was a British classical scholar and Anglican clergyman, best known as the co-author, with Robert Scott, of A Greek–English Lexicon, the standard dictionary of Ancient Greek, and as Dean of Christ Church, Oxford. He was the father of Alice Liddell, the inspiration for Lewis Carroll's Alice's Adventures in Wonderland.

Life and career

Liddell was educated at Charterhouse and Christ Church, Oxford, where he took a double first in 1833. Ordained in 1838, he became headmaster of Westminster School in 1846 and returned to Oxford in 1855 as Dean of Christ Church, a post he held until 1891. He also served as Vice-Chancellor of the University of Oxford from 1870 to 1874 and was a White's Professor of Moral Philosophy.

The Lexicon

First published in 1843, A Greek–English Lexicon was based on the German work of Franz Passow and was repeatedly revised during Liddell's lifetime. Abridged versions — the "Middle Liddell" and "Little Liddell" — became standard school and university references, and the full lexicon, revised by Henry Stuart Jones in the twentieth century, remains in print as "Liddell, Scott, and Jones" (LSJ).

Family and Carroll

In 1846 Liddell married Lorina Reeve, with whom he had ten children, including Alice Pleasance Liddell. The family's friendship with the Christ Church mathematics lecturer Charles Dodgson (Lewis Carroll) led to the storytelling boat trip of 4 July 1862 that produced Alice's Adventures in Wonderland.
