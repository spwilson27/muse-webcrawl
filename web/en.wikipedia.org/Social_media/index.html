Social media are interactive technologies that facilitate the creation, sharing, and aggregation of content, ideas, and information among virtual communities and networks. Users typically access social media through web-based applications or mobile apps, post user-generated content such as text, photos, and videos, and interact through comments, likes, and shares organized by algorithmic feeds.

History

Precursors included bulletin board systems, Usenet, and early services such as Six Degrees (1997) and Friendster (2002). The field expanded rapidly with Myspace and Facebook (2004), YouTube (2005), and Twitter (2006). The spread of smartphones in the 2010s shifted usage to mobile apps and image- and video-centred platforms such as Instagram, Snapchat, and TikTok.

Features and business model

Common elements include personal profiles, follower or friend connections, feeds ranked by recommendation algorithms, and messaging. Most large platforms are free to users and funded by targeted advertising based on collected user data, a model sometimes described as surveillance capitalism, which has drawn scrutiny over privacy practices.

Impact

Social media have transformed journalism, marketing, entertainment, and politics, enabling movements and viral phenomena as well as new livelihoods for creators and influencers. Research and public debate continue over their effects on mental health — particularly among adolescents — misinformation, political polarization, cyberbullying, and addictive design. Governments have responded with regulation concerning privacy, competition, and content moderation.
