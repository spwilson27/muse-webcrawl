Daishō

The daishō (Japanese: 大小, "big-little") is a Japanese term for the matched pair of traditionally made Japanese swords worn by the samurai class: the long katana and the short wakizashi.

Description

The katana, over 60 cm, was the primary weapon; the wakizashi, 30–60 cm, served as a sidearm and for close quarters or ritual suicide (seppuku). Together in matching mounts (koshirae), the pair symbolized samurai status and honour.

History

The custom developed in the Muromachi period and was formalized under the Tokugawa shogunate, when only samurai were permitted to wear both swords. Earlier pairings used the tachi and tantō.
