Samuel

Samuel (Hebrew: שְׁמוּאֵל, Šəmūʾēl) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible who plays a central role in the transition of ancient Israel from a loose confederation of tribes led by judges to a united monarchy under Saul and David. He is traditionally regarded as a prophet, priest and the last of the Hebrew judges.

Life

According to the Books of Samuel, Samuel was born to Hannah, who had been barren and dedicated her son to God's service at the sanctuary of Shiloh, where he was raised by the priest Eli. As a boy he received a divine call in the night. As an adult he led Israel against the Philistines and acted as judge over the tribes.

When the elders of Israel demanded a king, Samuel reluctantly anointed Saul as the first king. After Saul fell from divine favour, Samuel secretly anointed the young David. Samuel died at Ramah and was mourned by all Israel. In a famous episode, the Witch of Endor is said to have summoned his spirit for Saul on the eve of the king's final battle.

Legacy

Samuel is venerated in Judaism, Christianity and Islam (where he is known as Ṣamwīl). The two biblical books bearing his name — the First and Second Books of Samuel — narrate the history of Israel from his birth through the reign of David. The name Samuel, often interpreted as "heard by God" or "name of God", has remained a common given name in many cultures.
