Cayuga language

Cayuga (Cayuga: Gayogo̱hó꞉nǫʼ) is a Northern Iroquoian language of the Iroquois proper (also known as the Five Nations) group, spoken at Six Nations of the Grand River in Ontario by the Cayuga people, and formerly on the Cayuga Nation in New York. As of 2022, there were only 35 people who spoke Cayuga as their mother tongue.

Cayuga is a polysynthetic language in danger of extinction. Cayuga teaching and documentation work at Six Nations has been ongoing for decades, and immersion programmes such as those at the Gawęnahwisray (Cayuga Language) efforts aim to produce new speakers.

Dialects

The Cayuga language has two dialects: Upper Cayuga and Lower Cayuga, spoken at Six Nations of the Grand River. The dialect distinction derives from the two major settlements of Cayuga speakers after their migration to Canada in the late eighteenth century.

Phonology

Vowels

Cayuga has six oral vowels /i e a o æ u/ and two nasal vowels /ẽ õ/. Vowel length is phonemic. Modern Cayuga has lost the /r/ sound found in some related languages, reflected in words such as gędéh ("wife") whose cognates elsewhere retain r.

Consonants

The consonant inventory includes stops /t k kw/, affricates /ts tʃ/, fricatives /s ʃ h/, nasals /n/, and glides /w j/, along with a glottal stop. Cayuga lacks labial consonants.

Grammar

Like other Iroquoian languages, Cayuga is head-marking: verbs incorporate pronominal prefixes encoding agent and patient, and nouns may be incorporated into the verbal complex. Word order is relatively free and pragmatically driven.

Revitalization

Master-apprentice programmes, community classes, and the Cayuga: Our Oral Legacy (COOL) project have recorded elder speakers extensively. In 2024 the Cayuga Nation and community teachers continued immersion classes; language nests on the model of Māori and Hawaiian programmes have been proposed.
