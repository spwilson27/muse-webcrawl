Formula 3000

Formula 3000 was a type of open-wheel, single-seater motor racing formula devised by the FIA in 1984 as a stepping stone to Formula One. Named for its 3.0-litre (3000 cc) engine capacity, it replaced Formula Two as the final training ground for Grand Prix drivers. The principal championship, the International Formula 3000 Championship, ran from 1985 to 2004 before being replaced by the GP2 Series.

History

By the early 1980s Formula Two had become prohibitively expensive, dominated by factory Honda engines. The FIA responded with a cheaper formula based on the ubiquitous Cosworth DFV Formula One engine, detuned and fitted with a rev limiter, together with a ban on ground-effect aerodynamics. The first championship in 1985 was won by Christian Danner.

Cars and engines

Chassis were supplied chiefly by Lola, March, Ralt and Reynard. From 1996 the international series became a one-make formula with Lola chassis and Zytek-badged engines. Engines produced roughly 450 horsepower, giving performance between Formula Three and Formula One.

Role as a feeder series

Many future Formula One drivers passed through Formula 3000, including Jean Alesi, Damon Hill, Juan Pablo Montoya and Nick Heidfeld. National Formula 3000 series also ran in Britain, Japan (later Formula Nippon) and elsewhere.
