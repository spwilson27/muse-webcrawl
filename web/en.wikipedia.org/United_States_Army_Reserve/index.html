United States Army Reserve

The United States Army Reserve (USAR) is the federal reserve force of the United States Army. Together with the Army National Guard, it forms the reserve components of the Army; unlike the National Guard, the Army Reserve is purely federal and is headquartered at Fort Liberty (formerly Fort Bragg), North Carolina.

Role

The Army Reserve provides much of the Army's combat support and combat service support capacity — engineers, medical units, civil affairs, transportation, military police, legal, logistics and training formations — that the active component relies upon when mobilized.

History

Its origins lie in the Medical Reserve Corps of 1908. Reserve soldiers served in both world wars and were mobilized in large numbers after 2001 for operations in Iraq and Afghanistan, as well as for domestic emergencies and disaster relief.

Structure

The Chief of Army Reserve, a lieutenant general, commands the force through the U.S. Army Reserve Command. Most members serve part-time, typically one weekend a month and two weeks a year, unless mobilized.
