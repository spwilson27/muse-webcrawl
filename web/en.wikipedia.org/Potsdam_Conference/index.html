Potsdam Conference

The Potsdam Conference was held at Potsdam in the Soviet occupation zone from 17 July to 2 August 1945, to allow the three leading Allies of World War II to plan the post-war peace. The participants were the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, and the United States, represented respectively by Joseph Stalin, Winston Churchill (later Clement Attlee), and President Harry S. Truman.

Potsdam Agreement

The conference produced the Potsdam Agreement, covering the demilitarisation, denazification, democratisation, and decentralisation of Germany; the division of Germany and Berlin into four occupation zones; German reparations; and the provisional acceptance of the Oder–Neisse line as Poland's western border, with the accompanying expulsion of German populations from Eastern Europe.

Potsdam Declaration

On 26 July, the United States, Britain, and China issued the Potsdam Declaration demanding the unconditional surrender of Japan and setting out terms for the Japanese home islands. Japan's initial rejection (mokusatsu) was followed in August by the atomic bombings and Soviet entry into the war, and Japan accepted the terms on 14 August 1945.

Context

The conference was the last wartime meeting of the Big Three (after Tehran and Yalta). Partway through, Churchill was replaced by Attlee following the British general election. Rising tensions between the Soviet Union and the Western Allies at Potsdam foreshadowed the Cold War.
