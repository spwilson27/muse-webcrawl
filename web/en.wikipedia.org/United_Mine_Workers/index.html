United Mine Workers

The United Mine Workers of America (UMW or UMWA) is a North American labor union best known for representing coal miners. Founded in 1890 in Columbus, Ohio, by the merger of the Knights of Labor Trade Assembly No. 135 and the National Progressive Miners Union, it was one of the most powerful unions in the United States for much of the twentieth century and played a central role in the American labor movement.

Early history

Under president John Mitchell, the union won the anthracite coal strike of 1902 after President Theodore Roosevelt intervened — the first time the federal government acted as a neutral arbitrator rather than a strikebreaker. Coalfield conflicts in the following decades were often violent, including the West Virginia Mine Wars and the Battle of Blair Mountain (1921), the largest armed labor uprising in U.S. history, and the Harlan County War of the 1930s.

The Lewis era

John L. Lewis, president from 1920 to 1960, made the UMW a cornerstone of industrial unionism. He helped found the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO) in 1935. During the Second World War his wartime strikes made him a controversial figure, but he secured pioneering health and pension funds for miners — the UMWA Welfare and Retirement Fund of 1946 — financed by a royalty on each ton of coal mined. Mechanization agreements in the 1950s traded job reductions for higher wages and benefits for remaining miners.

Reform and decline

In 1969, insurgent candidate Jock Yablonski and his family were murdered on the orders of union president Tony Boyle; the scandal led to federal intervention, Boyle's conviction, and the election of reformer Arnold Miller in 1972. Miller's administration won passage-related support for black lung benefits under the Coal Mine Health and Safety Act of 1969. As U.S. coal employment shrank from several hundred thousand to tens of thousands, union membership declined sharply, though the UMWA remains active in mine safety, pensions, and organizing miners and other workers, including during the 2021–2023 Warrior Met Coal strike in Alabama.
