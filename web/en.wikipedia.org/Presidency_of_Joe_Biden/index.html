Presidency of Joe Biden

Joe Biden
President: 20 January 2021 – 20 January 2025 (46th)
Party: Democratic
Vice president: Kamala Harris
Predecessor: Donald Trump · Successor: Donald Trump

Biden served as the 46th president from 2021 to 2025.

The presidency of Joe Biden began on 20 January 2021, when Joe Biden was inaugurated as the 46th president of the United States, and ended on 20 January 2025. Biden, a Democrat and former vice president under Barack Obama, defeated Donald Trump in the 2020 election, taking office with Kamala Harris as the first woman vice president.

Administration

Biden's early agenda addressed the COVID-19 pandemic — a mass vaccination campaign and the $1.9 trillion American Rescue Plan — followed by the bipartisan Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, the CHIPS and Science Act for semiconductor manufacturing, and the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, the largest U.S. climate investment to date. Inflation peaked at 9.1% in mid-2022 before subsiding, while unemployment reached half-century lows. Abroad, Biden completed the withdrawal from Afghanistan in August 2021 amid a chaotic evacuation, then rallied NATO allies in support of Ukraine after Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, and backed Israel after the 7 October 2023 Hamas attack while facing criticism over Gaza's civilian toll. He appointed Ketanji Brown Jackson, the first Black woman on the Supreme Court. Biden sought re-election in 2024 but withdrew from the race in July after a poor debate performance intensified concerns about his age; Harris became the Democratic nominee and lost to Trump in November.
