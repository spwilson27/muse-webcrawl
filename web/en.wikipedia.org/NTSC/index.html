NTSC

NTSC
Type: Analog television color encoding system
Named for: National Television System Committee
Color standard adopted: 1953
Frame rate: 29.97 fps (59.94 fields/s)
Lines: 525
Used in: North America, Japan, and others

NTSC, named after the National Television System Committee, is the analog television color encoding system used in North America, Japan, and numerous other countries. It was introduced in 1953 as the second American monochrome-compatible color standard, replacing an incompatible 1941-era approach and an earlier field-sequential color system.

Technical overview

NTSC encodes color information on a subcarrier of approximately 3.58 MHz added to the luminance signal, allowing color broadcasts to be received in black and white on older sets. The system displays 525 horizontal lines per frame, interlaced into two fields. To accommodate the color subcarrier, the original frame rate of 30 frames per second was slightly reduced to 29.97 fps in 1953. Because color hue depends on the phase of the subcarrier, NTSC receivers included a manual tint control, which led wags to dub it "Never Twice the Same Color."

Adoption and decline

Canada, Mexico, most of the Americas, Japan, South Korea, and the Philippines adopted NTSC, while most of Europe used PAL or SECAM. Analog NTSC broadcasting ended in the United States for full-power stations in June 2009 with the digital television transition, in Japan in 2011, and in Canada in 2011, though the term persists informally to describe 480i video timing.
