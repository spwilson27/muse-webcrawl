Qatargas

Qatargas was the world's largest liquefied natural gas (LNG) company, based in Doha, Qatar. Established in 1984, it pioneered Qatar's LNG industry, developing production trains at Ras Laffan Industrial City that drew gas from the offshore North Field. On 1 January 2018 Qatargas merged with its sister company RasGas to form a single operator, which retained the Qatargas name; in 2023 the company was rebranded as part of QatarEnergy LNG.

Qatargas
Industry: Liquefied natural gas
Founded: 1984
Headquarters: Doha, Qatar
Parent: QatarEnergy
Main site: Ras Laffan
Peak capacity: ~77 Mtpa (14 trains, with RasGas)

Operations

Qatargas originally comprised ventures Qatargas 1 through 4, with partners including ExxonMobil, Total, ConocoPhillips, and Shell. Its mega-trains, each producing about 7.8 million tonnes per annum, were the largest LNG trains ever built, and it operated a fleet of Q-Flex and Q-Max carriers — the largest LNG ships afloat — to deliver cargoes to Asia and Europe, including the South Hook terminal in Wales.

Merger

The 2018 integration of Qatargas and RasGas under Qatar Petroleum (now QatarEnergy) created a single LNG operating company managing all 14 of Qatar's LNG trains, intended to cut costs and streamline marketing. The Qatargas Operating Company Limited continued as the operator until the QatarEnergy LNG rebrand.
