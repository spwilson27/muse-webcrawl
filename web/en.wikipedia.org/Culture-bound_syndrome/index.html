Culture-bound syndrome

In medicine and medical anthropology, a culture-bound syndrome, culture-specific syndrome, or folk illness is a combination of psychiatric and somatic symptoms that are considered to be a recognizable disease only within a specific society or culture. There are no objective biochemical or structural alterations of body organs or functions, and the disease is not recognized in other cultures.

The term was first included in the fourth edition of the American Psychiatric Association's Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-IV, 1994), which also listed a glossary of culture-bound syndromes in an appendix. The DSM-5 (2013) replaced the concept with the more neutral framework of "cultural concepts of distress," comprising cultural syndromes, idioms of distress, and explanations or perceived causes.

Examples

  • Amok — a dissociative episode of brooding followed by violent, aggressive, or homicidal behavior, historically described in Malaysia and Southeast Asia.
  • Koro — an episode of sudden and intense anxiety that the penis (or, in females, the vulva and nipples) will recede into the body and possibly cause death; found in China and Southeast Asia.
  • Susto — "fright" or "soul loss" in Latin America, in which a frightening event is believed to cause the soul to leave the body, resulting in unhappiness and sickness.
  • Ataque de nervios — an episode of uncontrollable shouting, crying, trembling, and aggression reported in Latino communities.
  • Taijin kyofusho — a Japanese syndrome of intense fear that one's body parts or functions offend others.
  • Hikikomori — prolonged acute social withdrawal, primarily described in Japan.

Debate

Some scholars argue that Western psychiatric categories themselves — such as anorexia nervosa or post-traumatic stress disorder — could equally be seen as culture-bound, and that the label has been applied asymmetrically to non-Western conditions. Others maintain that the concept, while imperfect, draws attention to the cultural shaping of illness.
