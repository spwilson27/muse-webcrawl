Android

Android is a mobile operating system based on a modified version of the Linux kernel and other open-source software, designed primarily for touchscreen devices such as smartphones and tablets. It is developed by a consortium of developers known as the Open Handset Alliance, led commercially by Google. First unveiled in November 2007 and commercially launched in September 2008 with the HTC Dream, Android has become the world's most widely used operating system, powering over three billion monthly active devices and holding around 70% of the global mobile OS market.

History

Android Inc. was founded in Palo Alto, California, in 2003 by Andy Rubin, Rich Miner, Nick Sears and Chris White, initially to build an advanced operating system for digital cameras before pivoting to mobile handsets. Google acquired the company in 2005 and revealed the platform in 2007 alongside the founding of the Open Handset Alliance, a consortium of hardware, software and telecom firms including HTC, Motorola, Samsung, Qualcomm and T-Mobile. The first commercial Android phone, the HTC Dream (T-Mobile G1), shipped in October 2008.

Features

Android's interface is built around direct manipulation with touch gestures. Its home screens are customisable with app icons and widgets; applications are distributed chiefly through the Google Play Store, though users may install APK files from other sources. The system supports multitasking, notifications, voice commands via Google Assistant, and a large ecosystem of apps written mainly in Kotlin and Java running on the Android Runtime (ART). Variants power televisions (Android TV), cars (Android Auto and Android Automotive) and wearables (Wear OS).

Releases

Major versions up to Android 9 were named after desserts (Cupcake, Donut, Eclair, Froyo, Gingerbread, Honeycomb, Ice Cream Sandwich, Jelly Bean, KitKat, Lollipop, Marshmallow, Nougat, Oreo, Pie); from Android 10 onward Google switched to plain numbers. Security and feature updates also arrive via Project Mainline and Google Play system updates.

Reception and market

Android's openness made it the default platform for most phone manufacturers, and by 2011 it had overtaken Symbian as the leading smartphone OS. Critics have cited software fragmentation, delayed updates on many devices, and malware in third-party app stores as drawbacks. Google has also faced antitrust actions — notably a €4.34 billion European Commission fine in 2018 over tying its apps to Android — and litigation over its use of Java APIs in the Google v. Oracle case, decided in Google's favour by the U.S. Supreme Court in 2021.
