Curiatii

The Curiatii were a set of triplet brothers from Alba Longa in ancient Italy, famous in Roman legend for their combat against the Roman triplet brothers, the Horatii, during the war between Rome and Alba Longa in the reign of King Tullus Hostilius (traditionally 673–642 BC).

The legend

As told by Livy, the two cities agreed to settle their war by a combat of champions to limit bloodshed. The three Curiatii faced the three Horatii. Two Horatii were killed quickly, but the surviving Horatius, feigning flight, separated the wounded Curiatii and killed them one by one. Alba Longa thus submitted to Rome. On returning, Horatius killed his own sister for mourning her betrothed, one of the Curiatii; his trial helped establish the Roman right of appeal (provocatio).

In culture

The story was a popular subject in art and theatre, including Corneille's play Horace (1640) and operas by Cimarosa and others; Jacques-Louis David's Oath of the Horatii (1784) depicts the Roman brothers' resolve. The tombs of the Horatii and Curiatii on the Appian Way were pointed out in antiquity.
