Aluminosilicate

Aluminosilicates are minerals and chemical compounds composed of aluminium, silicon and oxygen, often with other cations such as sodium, potassium or calcium. They form a large fraction of the Earth's crust and include the feldspars, feldspathoids, clay minerals, micas and zeolites.

Structure

Aluminosilicate structures are built from SiO4 and AlO4 tetrahedra sharing oxygen atoms. Substituting aluminium for silicon in a silicate framework creates a net negative charge, which is balanced by cations in cavities or interlayer spaces. This property underlies the ion-exchange behaviour of zeolites and the swelling behaviour of clays such as montmorillonite.

Examples and uses

Important natural aluminosilicates include kaolinite (Al2Si2O5(OH)4), the principal mineral of kaolin clay used in porcelain and paper coating; andalusite, kyanite and sillimanite (Al2SiO5 polymorphs); and the feldspar group. Synthetic aluminosilicates are used as catalysts in petroleum cracking, as molecular sieves and desiccants, and in detergents as water softeners. Aluminosilicate glass, containing alumina in place of soda-lime fluxes, is valued for chemical durability and thermal-shock resistance.
