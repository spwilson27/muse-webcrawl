Cytolysis

Cytolysis, also known as osmotic lysis, is the bursting of a cell caused by an excess influx of water. When a cell is placed in a hypotonic solution — one with a lower concentration of solutes than the cell's interior — water enters by osmosis, the cell swells, and its membrane may rupture.

Mechanism

In animal cells, which lack a rigid cell wall, unchecked water entry stretches the plasma membrane until it breaks, releasing the cell's contents. Red blood cells undergoing this process are said to haemolyse. Plant, fungal and bacterial cells usually resist cytolysis because their cell walls exert a counteracting turgor pressure that balances the osmotic pressure once the cell is firm.

Prevention

Many freshwater protists avoid cytolysis with contractile vacuoles that expel excess water. In multicellular organisms, body fluids are kept isotonic with cells, and the kidneys regulate water and salt balance so that blood plasma does not become dangerously hypotonic.

Related processes

Cytolysis is the opposite of plasmolysis, in which a cell in a hypertonic solution loses water and shrinks. Immune effectors such as the complement membrane attack complex and some bacterial toxins deliberately cause lysis of target cells.
