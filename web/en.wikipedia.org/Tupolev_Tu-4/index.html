The Tupolev Tu-4 (NATO reporting name: Bull) was a piston-engined Soviet strategic bomber that served the Soviet Air Force from the late 1940s to the mid-1960s. It was a reverse-engineered copy of the American Boeing B-29 Superfortress, produced after three B-29s that made emergency landings in Soviet territory in 1944 were interned and kept by the USSR.

Development

Joseph Stalin ordered the Tupolev bureau to reproduce the B-29 exactly, in the shortest possible time. The effort was a vast undertaking: because Soviet industry used metric measurements, everything from aluminium sheet thicknesses to electrical wiring had to be adapted, and the resulting aircraft was slightly heavier than the original. The defensive armament was changed to Soviet 23 mm cannon. The Tu-4 was publicly revealed at the Tushino air display in August 1947, surprising Western observers.

Service

Some 847 Tu-4s were built. The type gave the Soviet Union its first genuinely intercontinental delivery system, and a Tu-4 dropped the Soviet Union's first air-dropped atomic bomb in testing. Variants included the Tu-4A atomic bomber, missile carriers, transports, and the Tu-4K naval missile carrier. Tu-4s were also supplied to China, where some were converted to turboprop power and served in specialist roles, including as an airborne early warning testbed, into the late 1980s. The Tu-4 programme laid the groundwork for later Tupolev bombers such as the Tu-95.
