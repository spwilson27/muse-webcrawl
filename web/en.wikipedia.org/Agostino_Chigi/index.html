Agostino Chigi (November 29, 1466 – April 11, 1520) was an Italian banker of the Renaissance, a member of the Sienese Chigi family, and one of the wealthiest men of his age. Known as "il Magnifico", he was a major patron of artists including Raphael, Sebastiano del Piombo, and Baldassarre Peruzzi.

Banking career

Chigi built a banking empire with branches across Europe, financed the Papal States, and held lucrative monopolies on alum from Tolfa and on salt. His fortune and political connections made him banker to popes Alexander VI, Julius II, and Leo X.

Patronage

In Rome he commissioned the Villa Farnesina on the Tiber, decorated with Raphael's frescoes including The Triumph of Galatea and the Loggia of Psyche. He also commissioned the Chigi Chapel in Santa Maria del Popolo, designed by Raphael. His lavish banquets — at one of which silver dishes were famously thrown into the Tiber (and secretly retrieved with nets) — became legendary symbols of Renaissance magnificence.
