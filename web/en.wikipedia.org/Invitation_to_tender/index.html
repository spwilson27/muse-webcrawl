Invitation to tender

An invitation to tender (ITT), often called a request for tender (RFT) or call for bids, is a formal, structured process used to generate competing offers from different potential suppliers or contractors for the supply of goods or services, and to select among them on transparent criteria. It is a core instrument of procurement, especially in the public sector.

Process

A typical tender process begins with defining requirements and drafting the tender documents, which describe the scope of work, specifications, evaluation criteria, contractual terms, and deadlines. The invitation is then published or sent to selected firms. Suppliers submit sealed bids by a fixed deadline; bids are opened and evaluated, commonly on a combination of price and quality ("most economically advantageous tender"), and the contract is awarded to the winning bidder. Unsuccessful bidders are often debriefed, and in many jurisdictions may challenge an award during a standstill period.

Variations

Related instruments include the request for proposal (RFP), which invites suppliers to propose solutions where requirements cannot be fully specified in advance; the request for quotation (RFQ) for standard goods; and the request for information (RFI) used in market research. Open tenders allow any supplier to bid, while selective or restricted procedures pre-qualify a shortlist of candidates. Framework agreements tender once for repeat call-offs over several years.

Legal framework

Public procurement above certain thresholds is regulated to ensure fairness, competition, and value for money — for example by the WTO Agreement on Government Procurement, EU procurement directives (now implemented in the UK by the Procurement Act 2023), and the US Federal Acquisition Regulation. In contract law, an invitation to tender is generally treated as an "invitation to treat": the submitted bid is the offer, which the buyer may accept or reject, although the process itself can create enforceable procedural obligations.

E-tendering

Most tendering is now conducted through electronic procurement portals that publish notices, distribute documents, receive bids, and record audit trails.
