Cortado

A cortado is a beverage consisting of espresso mixed with a roughly equal amount of warm milk to reduce the acidity. The milk in a cortado is steamed, but not frothy and "textured" as in many Italian coffee drinks. The word cortado is the past participle of the Spanish verb cortar, "to cut", in the sense of "dilute".

The drink originated in Spain's Basque Country and spread through Spain and Portugal (where it is known as a pingo or garoto in some regions) and to Latin America, including Cuba. It is typically served in a small glass. Similar drinks include the Italian caffè macchiato, which uses much less milk, and the piccolo latte.

Gibraltar

In specialty coffee shops in the United States, a cortado served in a 4.5-ounce Libbey "Gibraltar" rocks glass is often called a Gibraltar, a name popularised by cafés in San Francisco in the 2000s.
