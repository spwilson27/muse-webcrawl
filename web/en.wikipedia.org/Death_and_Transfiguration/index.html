Death and Transfiguration

ComposerRichard Strauss
German titleTod und Verklärung, Op. 24
Composed1888–1889
Premiered21 June 1890, Eisenach

Death and Transfiguration (German: Tod und Verklärung), Op. 24, is a tone poem for large orchestra by Richard Strauss, composed in 1888–89 when the composer was in his mid-twenties. Strauss conducted its premiere at Eisenach in June 1890.

Programme

Following a poem by Alexander Ritter printed in the score, the work depicts a dying artist who recalls scenes from his life—childhood innocence, youthful struggles, and the attainment of maturity—while in his final moments his soul achieves the transfiguration he had sought in art. A luminous "transfiguration theme" rises at the climax as the soul finds redemption.

Music

The single-movement work opens with irregular, syncopated heartbeat rhythms in the strings over the sickbed scene, passes through agitated allegro struggle music and lyrical reminiscences, and ends with a gradual, radiant ascent. Its orchestration and emotional narrative marked Strauss as the leading tone poet of his generation, alongside Don Juan of the same period.

Legacy

On his own deathbed in 1949, Strauss is reported to have said that dying was exactly as he had composed it in Death and Transfiguration sixty years earlier. The piece remains a staple of the orchestral repertoire and is frequently recorded.
