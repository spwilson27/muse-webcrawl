Coast

The coast, also known as the coastline or seashore, is the area where land meets the sea or ocean, or the line that forms the boundary between the land and a body of water such as an ocean or lake.

Formation and change

Coasts are shaped by tides, waves, currents, erosion, sediment deposition, and changes in sea level. Features include beaches, cliffs, dunes, estuaries, deltas, barrier islands, and tidal flats. Coastlines are fractal-like in detail, so their measured length depends on the scale of measurement — the coastline paradox.

Ecology and human use

Coastal zones are among the most productive and biologically diverse environments, supporting intertidal ecosystems, fisheries, and wetlands. A large share of the world's population lives near coasts, and ports, tourism, and trade concentrate there. Coastal areas face hazards including storms, flooding, erosion, and sea-level rise.
