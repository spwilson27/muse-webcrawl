Australian rules football, also called Aussie rules or footy, is a contact sport played between two teams of 18 players on an oval field, often a modified cricket ground. It originated in Melbourne in the late 1850s and is the most popular spectator sport in Australia.

Gameplay

Players move the ball by kicking, handballing (punching) and running, aiming to score by kicking the ball between goal posts at each end. A goal through the centre posts is worth six points; a behind, through the outer posts or obtained in other ways, is worth one point. Players may tackle opponents and take spectacular high marks by catching the ball from a kick.

History

The Melbourne Football Club, founded in 1858, wrote the first rules in 1859 under Tom Wills and others. The Victorian Football League (1896) evolved into the national Australian Football League (AFL), today the sport's premier professional competition.

Popularity

The AFL Grand Final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground regularly draws crowds approaching 100,000, and the sport is played at amateur level across Australia and in dozens of other countries.
