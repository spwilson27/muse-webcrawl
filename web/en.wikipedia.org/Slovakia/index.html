Slovakia, officially the Slovak Republic, is a landlocked country in Central Europe. It is bordered by Poland to the north, Ukraine to the east, Hungary to the south, Austria to the west and the Czech Republic to the northwest. Its mostly mountainous territory spans about 49,000 square kilometres, and its capital and largest city is Bratislava.

History

Slavic peoples settled the region in the 5th and 6th centuries; the 9th-century empire of Great Moravia brought Christianity with the mission of Saints Cyril and Methodius, who devised the first Slavic alphabet. After Great Moravia fell, the territory was part of the Kingdom of Hungary for roughly a thousand years. In 1918 Slovaks joined with Czechs in Czechoslovakia. A wartime Slovak state existed under Nazi influence (1939–1945), against which the Slovak National Uprising was launched in 1944. After 1948 Czechoslovakia came under communist rule; the Velvet Revolution of 1989 restored democracy, and on 1 January 1993 the federation peacefully dissolved in the "Velvet Divorce".

Geography

The Carpathian Mountains arc across the north, including the High Tatras, whose Gerlachovský štít (2,655 m) is the country's highest peak. The Danube flows through the southwest, forming the capital's riverfront. Slovakia has numerous caves, castles — among them Spiš Castle, one of Europe's largest — and national parks.

Politics and economy

Slovakia is a parliamentary republic and a member of the European Union (since 2004), NATO, and the eurozone; it adopted the euro in 2009. Its economy is strongly industrial and export-oriented, and per capita it is among the world's largest producers of cars, with plants operated by Volkswagen, Kia, Stellantis and Jaguar Land Rover.
