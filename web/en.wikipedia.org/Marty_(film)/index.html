Marty (film)

Marty is a 1955 American romantic drama film directed by Delbert Mann, adapted by Paddy Chayefsky from his 1953 teleplay of the same name. It stars Ernest Borgnine as Marty Piletti, a shy, middle-aged Bronx butcher who still lives with his mother, and Betsy Blair as Clara, a schoolteacher he meets at a dance.

Plot

Pressured by his mother and customers to marry, Marty reluctantly attends a ballroom, where he meets Clara, who has been abandoned by her blind date. The two lonely people connect over an evening in the Bronx, but Marty must resist the mockery of his friends and the disapproval of his mother, who fears losing her son. In the final scene he defies them and telephones Clara.

Reception

Made for under $350,000, Marty was a major critical and commercial success. It won the Academy Award for Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor (Borgnine), and Best Adapted Screenplay, and became the first film to win both the Oscar for Best Picture and the Palme d'Or at Cannes. It remains a landmark of realistic, small-scale American filmmaking.
