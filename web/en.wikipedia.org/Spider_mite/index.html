Spider mite

Spider mites are members of the Tetranychidae family, which includes about 1,200 species. They are part of the subclass Acari (mites). Spider mites generally live on the undersides of leaves of plants, where they may spin protective silk webs, and they can cause damage by puncturing the plant cells to feed. Spider mites are known to feed on several hundred species of plants.

Description

Spider mites are less than 1 mm (0.04 in) in size and vary in color. They lay small, spherical, initially transparent eggs, and many species spin fine silk webbing to protect the colony from predators; they get their name from this webbing.

Life cycle

Hot, dry conditions are often associated with population build-up of spider mites. Under optimal conditions (approximately 27 °C), the two-spotted spider mite can hatch in as little as 3 days, and become sexually mature in as little as 5 days. One female can lay up to 20 eggs per day and can live for 2 to 4 weeks, laying hundreds of eggs.

Control

Spider mites are significant agricultural and garden pests, including the two-spotted spider mite (Tetranychus urticae). Control methods include washing plants, applying insecticidal soap or neem oil, and introducing predatory mites such as Phytoseiulus persimilis. Resistance to miticides develops quickly.
