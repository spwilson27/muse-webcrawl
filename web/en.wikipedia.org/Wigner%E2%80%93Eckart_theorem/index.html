Wigner–Eckart theorem

The Wigner–Eckart theorem is a theorem of representation theory and quantum mechanics. Named for Eugene Wigner and Carl Eckart, it states that matrix elements of spherical tensor operators in the basis of angular momentum eigenstates can be factored into two parts: a Clebsch–Gordan coefficient, which depends on the angular momentum quantum numbers and their projections, and a "reduced matrix element," which is independent of the projection quantum numbers.

Content

For a spherical tensor operator of rank k acting between angular momentum states of total angular momentum j and j′, the theorem shows that the matrix element is proportional to a Clebsch–Gordan coefficient times a reduced matrix element that depends only on the operator and on j and j′, not on the magnetic quantum numbers. Consequently, all the matrix elements for the 2j+1 projections are determined by a single number.

Applications

The theorem greatly simplifies calculations of transition probabilities and selection rules in atomic and nuclear spectroscopy. It explains why only certain transitions between energy levels are allowed and underlies the analysis of electric dipole radiation, hyperfine structure, and the Zeeman effect. Generalisations of the theorem apply to other symmetry groups throughout particle and condensed-matter physics.
