First Italian War of Independence

The First Italian War of Independence (Italian: Prima guerra d'indipendenza italiana), part of the Italian Wars of Independence and the Revolutions of 1848, was fought by the Kingdom of Sardinia and Italian volunteers against the Austrian Empire and other conservative states from 23 March 1848 to 22 August 1849.

Background

The year 1848 saw revolutionary uprisings across Europe. In Italy, revolts broke out in Sicily, Milan (the Five Days of Milan) and Venice, where the Republic of San Marco was proclaimed. King Charles Albert of Sardinia, hoping to lead the movement for Italian unification — the Risorgimento — declared war on Austria, whose empire ruled Lombardy–Venetia directly and dominated much of the peninsula.

Course of the war

Piedmontese forces initially advanced into Lombardy, winning engagements at Goito and Pastrengo and besieging Peschiera. Volunteer contingents arrived from across Italy, and the Papal States and Naples initially sent troops before withdrawing under conservative pressure. The Austrian commander Joseph Radetzky regrouped in the Quadrilateral fortresses and counterattacked, defeating Charles Albert at Custoza in July 1848. An armistice followed.

Charles Albert denounced the armistice in March 1849, but his army was decisively defeated at Novara on 23 March. He abdicated in favour of his son Victor Emmanuel II. Meanwhile the Roman Republic proclaimed in February 1849, defended by Giuseppe Garibaldi, was crushed by French troops, and Venice surrendered to Austria in August.

Consequences

The war ended in failure for the Italian national cause, and Austrian dominance was restored. Nevertheless, the conflict established Piedmont-Sardinia as the leading Italian state committed to unification, a role it fulfilled in the Second Italian War of Independence of 1859.
