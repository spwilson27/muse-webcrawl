Motor neuron disease

Motor neuron diseases (MNDs) are a group of rare neurodegenerative disorders that selectively affect motor neurons, the cells that control voluntary muscle activity such as speaking, walking, swallowing, and breathing. The best-known member of the group is amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), also called Lou Gehrig's disease.

Classification

The group includes amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (affecting both upper and lower motor neurons), primary lateral sclerosis (upper motor neurons), progressive muscular atrophy (lower motor neurons), progressive bulbar palsy (motor neurons of the brainstem), and hereditary conditions such as spinal muscular atrophy. Symptoms depend on the populations affected but commonly include progressive muscle weakness, wasting, spasticity, fasciculations, and difficulties with speech and swallowing; sensation and, in many cases, cognition are relatively spared, though ALS can overlap with frontotemporal dementia.

Causes and treatment

Most cases are sporadic, with about 5–10% familial; genes implicated in inherited forms include SOD1, C9orf72, TARDBP, and FUS. There is no cure for most motor neuron diseases; treatment is supportive, with drugs such as riluzole modestly prolonging survival in ALS, multidisciplinary care, and ventilatory and nutritional support as the disease progresses.
