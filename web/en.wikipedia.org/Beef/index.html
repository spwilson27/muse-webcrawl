Beef is the culinary name for meat from cattle, particularly skeletal muscle. Humans have eaten beef since prehistoric times, when wild aurochs were hunted; domesticated cattle became a foundation of farming in many cultures. Beef is a source of high-quality protein and essential nutrients including iron, zinc, and vitamin B12. It is the third most widely consumed meat in the world, after pork and poultry, accounting for about a quarter of global meat production.

Production

Beef cattle are raised in systems ranging from pasture-based cow–calf operations and ranching to intensive feedlots where animals are finished on grain. Major producing regions include the United States, Brazil, the European Union, China, India, Argentina, and Australia. Carcasses are graded for quality (for example USDA Prime, Choice, and Select in the United States, based largely on marbling) and yield, then fabricated into primal and retail cuts such as chuck, rib, loin, and round, from which steaks and roasts are cut.

Nutrition and health

Beef is nutrient-dense: rich in complete protein, haem iron, zinc, selenium, and B vitamins. It also contains saturated fat and cholesterol, and health authorities advise moderating intake of red and processed meat; in 2015 the WHO's IARC classified processed meat as carcinogenic to humans and red meat as probably carcinogenic, based mainly on associations with colorectal cancer. Grass-fed beef typically has a somewhat different fatty-acid profile, with more omega-3s and conjugated linoleic acid, than grain-fed beef.

Cultural and religious considerations

Cattle are sacred in Hinduism, and most Indian states restrict cattle slaughter, so beef consumption is low in India despite its large cattle population; India nevertheless exports substantial quantities of buffalo meat (carabeef). Beef is central to many cuisines — steaks and roasts in Europe and the Americas, bulgogi in Korea, sukiyaki and wagyu in Japan — and premium categories such as Kobe beef command very high prices. Beef production is also debated for its environmental impact: livestock are a significant source of greenhouse-gas emissions, land use, and water consumption.
