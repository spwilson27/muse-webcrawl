Vicente Fernández

Born17 February 1940, Huentitán El Alto, Jalisco, Mexico
Died12 December 2021, Guadalajara, Jalisco, Mexico
OccupationSinger, actor, film producer
GenresRanchera, mariachi, bolero
Years active1966–2016

Vicente Fernández Gómez (17 February 1940 – 12 December 2021) was a Mexican mariachi singer, actor and film producer. Nicknamed "Chente", "El Charro de Huentitán" and "El Ídolo de México", he is considered a cultural icon of Mexico and one of the greatest Mexican singers of all time. Fernández recorded more than 50 albums and appeared in more than 30 films; his repertoire consists primarily of rancheras and other Mexican classics.

Career

After years of performing in restaurants and on local radio in Guadalajara, Fernández signed with CBS Mexico in 1966. He rose to fame in the 1970s with songs such as "Volver Volver", "Las Llaves de Mi Alma" and "El Rey", and became the leading voice of ranchera music following the deaths of Javier Solís, Pedro Infante and Jorge Negrete. He won multiple Grammy and Latin Grammy Awards and received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.

Legacy

Fernández retired from live performing in 2016 with a farewell concert at the Estadio Azteca. His death in December 2021, after months of hospitalization following a fall, prompted days of national mourning in Mexico, and his wake at the Arena VFG drew tens of thousands of fans.
