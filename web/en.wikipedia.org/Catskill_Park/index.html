Catskill Park

The Catskill Park is a protected area in southeastern New York State, United States, encompassing much of the Catskill Mountains. Established in 1885 together with the Adirondack Park, it covers roughly 700,000 acres (about 2,800 km²) across parts of Delaware, Greene, Sullivan, and Ulster counties. About 41 percent of the land within the park's "blue line" boundary is publicly owned Forest Preserve, protected as "forever wild" under Article XIV of the New York State Constitution.

History

The Forest Preserve was created in 1885 to protect the region's watersheds and forests from the destructive logging, tanning, and quarrying industries that had stripped much of the Catskills in the nineteenth century. In 1894 voters approved the "forever wild" constitutional amendment, and the Catskill Park itself was formally delineated in 1904. Much of the park lies within the watershed of New York City's reservoirs, and the city is a major landowner and steward.

Geography and recreation

The park contains 35 peaks above 3,500 feet (1,070 m), known as the Catskill High Peaks, the highest being Slide Mountain at 4,180 feet (1,274 m). Popular destinations include Kaaterskill Falls, the Devil's Path hiking trail, and the ski areas of Hunter Mountain and Windham. The park offers hundreds of miles of hiking trails, campgrounds, fishing streams famous for trout, and lean-to shelters.

Ecology

The park's forests are predominantly northern hardwood — maple, beech, and birch — with hemlock groves along streams and boreal spruce-fir stands on the highest summits. Wildlife includes black bear, bobcat, fisher, and bald eagles.
