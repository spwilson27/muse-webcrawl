Low birth weight

Low birth weight (LBW) is defined by the World Health Organization as a birth weight of an infant of 2,499 g (5 lb 8.1 oz) or less, regardless of gestational age. Subcategories include very low birth weight (VLBW), below 1,500 g, and extremely low birth weight (ELBW), below 1,000 g. Most normal newborns weigh between 2,500 and 4,000 g.

Causes

Low birth weight results either from preterm birth (before 37 weeks of gestation) or from the infant being small for gestational age because of intrauterine growth restriction, or both. Risk factors include maternal malnutrition, infections, pre-eclampsia, smoking and alcohol use, adolescent or multiple pregnancy, low socioeconomic status and air pollution. Rates are highest in South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa; globally an estimated 14–15% of babies are born with low birth weight.

Consequences and treatment

Low birth weight is a major determinant of neonatal mortality and is associated with later risks including developmental delay, cognitive impairment, and chronic diseases in adulthood such as type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease (the fetal origins hypothesis). Care focuses on warmth (including kangaroo mother care), breastfeeding support, infection prevention and, for the smallest infants, neonatal intensive care.
