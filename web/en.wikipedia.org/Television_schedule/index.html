Television schedule

A television schedule is a plan for the broadcast of television programs. It determines the day and time at which each program airs, and is designed to attract the largest or most desirable audience to each time slot. Television schedules are typically organized around dayparts such as morning, daytime, prime time, and late night.

Scheduling strategies

Programmers use strategies such as "lead-in" (placing a new or weaker show after a popular one), "hammocking" (placing a weaker show between two hits), "block programming" (scheduling similar programs together), and "counterprogramming" (offering an alternative to a competitor's popular program). The prime time hours, generally 8 to 11 p.m. in the United States, command the highest advertising rates.

Presentation

Schedules were historically published in newspaper listings and magazines such as TV Guide. Today, electronic program guides (EPGs) built into televisions and streaming platforms have largely replaced printed listings.
