The infinite series whose terms are the natural numbers, 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + ⋯, is a divergent series: the sequence of its partial sums — 1, 3, 6, 10, … (the triangular numbers) — grows without bound and does not converge.

Assigning a value

Nevertheless, the series is famous for the value −1/12 that can be assigned to it by methods of summation of divergent series, including zeta function regularisation: the Riemann zeta function evaluated at −1 gives ζ(−1) = −1/12. Ramanujan summation likewise assigns the series the value −1/12.

In popular culture and physics

The result was discussed by Leonhard Euler and recorded in Srinivasa Ramanujan's first letter to G. H. Hardy. Summation methods of this kind are used in physics, for example in bosonic string theory and in calculations connected with the Casimir effect. Popular videos presenting the series as simply "equal to −1/12" overlook the specialised senses in which that equality holds.
