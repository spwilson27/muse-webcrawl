Lunar calendar

The phases of the Moon define the lunar month

Based on: Synodic month (29.53 days)

Year length: ~354 days (pure lunar)

A lunar calendar is a calendar based on the monthly cycles of the Moon's phases (synodic months). Because each lunation is approximately 29.53 days, months in a lunar calendar alternate between 29 and 30 days, and a lunar year of twelve months totals about 354 days — some 11 days shorter than the solar year. Lunar calendars therefore drift through the seasons unless corrected.

Types

A purely lunar calendar, such as the Islamic calendar, ignores the solar year entirely, so its months migrate through all seasons over a cycle of about 33 years. Lunisolar calendars, by contrast, insert an intercalary (leap) month in some years to keep the calendar aligned with the sun; examples include the Hebrew, Chinese, Hindu, Buddhist, and Tibetan calendars. The 19-year Metonic cycle, in which 235 lunations nearly equal 19 solar years, underlies several of these systems.

History and use

Lunar reckoning is among humanity's oldest timekeeping methods; notched bones and cave markings from the Upper Paleolithic have sometimes been interpreted as lunar tallies. Lunar months traditionally begin at the first sighting of the crescent moon (hilal) in Islamic practice, or at the astronomical new moon in the Chinese calendar. Festivals such as Ramadan, Passover, and the Mid-Autumn Festival are set by lunar or lunisolar dates.
