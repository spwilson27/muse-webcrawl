100-year flood

A 100-year flood is a flood event that has, on average, a 1 in 100 chance (1% probability) of being equalled or exceeded in any given year. It is an example of a return period used in hydrology and civil engineering.

Interpretation

The term is frequently misunderstood: a 100-year flood does not occur only once a century, and two such floods can occur in successive years. Over a 100-year period, the probability of at least one such event is about 63%. The area inundated by the 100-year flood defines the 100-year floodplain, widely used in flood insurance and land-use regulation, including by FEMA in the United States.

Criticism

Critics note that estimates rely on historical records that may be short, and that land-use change and climate change make past statistics less representative of future risk.
