Metro (rapid transit)

A metro is a type of high-capacity public transport generally found in urban areas. Unlike buses or trams, a metro is a railway — usually electric — operating on an exclusive right-of-way from which pedestrians and other traffic are excluded. Services run at high frequency, typically every few minutes at peak times, stopping at stations spaced roughly 0.5 to 2 km apart. Metros may run underground, at grade, or on elevated structures, often combining all three. Terms vary by city: subway, underground, U-Bahn, tube, MRT and rapid transit are all used.

History

The world's first metro was London's Metropolitan Railway, opened in 1863 with steam locomotives hauling gas-lit wooden coaches through cut-and-cover tunnels. Electric traction enabled deep-level tube lines in the 1890s. Budapest (1896) and Paris (1900, whose Métro gave the generic name) followed, and New York's subway opened in 1904. In the mid-twentieth century many new systems opened in the Soviet Union, Japan and later across East and South Asia; China has built the world's largest networks since the 2000s, with Shanghai and Beijing operating the longest systems by route length.

Characteristics

Defining features include grade separation, high platforms level with train floors, multiple-unit electric trains powered by third rail or overhead lines, and heavy passenger capacity — a busy metro line can carry 30,000 or more passengers per hour per direction. Increasingly, lines are fully automated and driverless (such as Copenhagen's and Dubai's systems), using moving-block signalling and platform screen doors.

Ridership and networks

More than 200 cities worldwide have metros. The busiest by annual ridership have included Tokyo, Moscow, Beijing, Shanghai, Seoul, Guangzhou and New York. Metros shape the cities they serve, concentrating development around stations and enabling the high densities of great world cities; they also demand enormous capital investment and continuing subsidy, and are celebrated in design, from Moscow's palatial stations to the Art Nouveau entrances of Paris.
