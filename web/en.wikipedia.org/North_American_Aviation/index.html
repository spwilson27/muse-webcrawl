North American Aviation

North American Aviation (NAA) was a major American aerospace manufacturer responsible for a number of historic aircraft, including the T-6 Texan trainer, the P-51 Mustang fighter, the B-25 Mitchell bomber, the F-86 Sabre jet fighter, the X-15 rocket plane, and the Apollo command and service module. The company merged with Rockwell-Standard in 1967 to form North American Rockwell, which later became Rockwell International; its aircraft divisions were eventually sold to Boeing in 1996.

History

North American Aviation was founded on 6 December 1928 by Clement Melville Keys as a holding company for aviation interests. After the Air Mail Act of 1934 forced the breakup of such holding companies, North American became a manufacturing company under the leadership of James H. "Dutch" Kindelberger, recruited from Douglas Aircraft. The company moved its operations to Southern California in 1935.

Second World War

During the Second World War, North American produced more aircraft than any other American manufacturer in some years. Its B-25 Mitchell medium bomber became famous for the Doolittle Raid on Tokyo in April 1942, and the P-51 Mustang — originally designed to a British specification and later fitted with the Rolls-Royce Merlin engine — became one of the finest fighters of the war, escorting bombers deep into Germany. The AT-6 Texan was the standard advanced trainer of the Allied air forces.

Postwar and space era

After the war the company built the F-86 Sabre, the United States' first swept-wing fighter, which fought MiG-15s over Korea, and the F-100 Super Sabre. Its Rocketdyne division built rocket engines, and its Autonetics division produced guidance systems. The X-15 hypersonic research aircraft, built with the U.S. Air Force and NASA, set speed and altitude records that stood for decades. In the 1960s the company won the contract for the Apollo command and service module and the second stage of the Saturn V. In 1967 it merged with Rockwell-Standard to form North American Rockwell.
