Southern Paiute people

Southern Paiute
Self-name: Nuwuvi ("the People")
Region: southern Utah and Nevada, northern Arizona, southeastern California
Language: Southern Paiute (Colorado River Numic, Uto-Aztecan)

The Southern Paiute people (Nuwuvi) are a Native American people of the Great Basin and northern Colorado Plateau, traditionally occupying a territory spanning southern Utah, southern Nevada, northwestern Arizona and the Mojave Desert of southeastern California. They speak the Southern Paiute language, a dialect of Colorado River Numic in the Uto-Aztecan family, closely related to Chemehuevi and Ute.

Traditional culture

Southern Paiute bands combined hunting and gathering — of pinyon nuts, seeds, jackrabbits and deer — with small-scale floodplain farming of maize, beans, squash and even wheat along streams such as the Virgin and Muddy rivers. Social organisation centred on kin-based local groups led by headmen, moving seasonally across well-defined territories tied to springs and seed grounds.

History

From the late 18th century, Spanish and later American slave raiders targeted Southern Paiute communities. Mormon settlement of Utah from the 1850s brought disease, displacement and conflict, and populations collapsed. In the 20th century the people reorganised into federally recognised tribes and bands, including the Paiute Indian Tribe of Utah, the Moapa Band of Paiute Indians, the Las Vegas Paiute Tribe, the Kaibab Band of Paiute Indians and the San Juan Southern Paiute Tribe, and have pursued land, water-rights and language-revitalisation programmes.
