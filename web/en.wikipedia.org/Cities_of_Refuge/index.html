Cities of Refuge

The cities of refuge were six Levitical towns in the ancient kingdoms of Israel and Judah in which the perpetrators of accidental manslaughter could claim the right of asylum. Outside these cities, such perpetrators were in danger of being killed by the "avenger of blood" — the closest relative of the deceased.

Biblical account

The institution is described in the Book of Numbers (chapter 35) and elaborated in Deuteronomy (chapter 19) and Joshua (chapter 20). Mosaic law required that cities of refuge be accessible to both Israelites and resident aliens, so that anyone who killed a person accidentally could flee there and live in safety.

The six cities

The six cities were Kedesh, Shechem and Hebron west of the Jordan River, and Bezer, Ramoth-Gilead and Golan east of the Jordan. They were chosen so that no part of the country was far from a place of refuge.

Procedure

On entering the city, the manslayer would state his case to the elders at the city gate, who would admit him pending trial. If the congregation judged the killing to have been unintentional, he was to remain in the city until the death of the high priest, after which he could return home without fear of the avenger. Deliberate murderers were handed over for punishment.

Purpose

Scholars see the cities of refuge as a mechanism for restraining blood feud, ensuring that revenge killing did not escalate, and guaranteeing a fair hearing before punishment was exacted.
