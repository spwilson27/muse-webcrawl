Newcastle upon Tyne

Newcastle upon Tyne, commonly simply Newcastle, is a city in Tyne and Wear, North East England, on the north bank of the River Tyne. It is the most populous city in the region and historically the county town of Northumberland.

History

The settlement began as the Roman fort Pons Aelius on Hadrian's Wall. The "new castle" built by Robert Curthose in 1080 gave the town its name. Newcastle grew prosperous on the coal trade — the phrase "carrying coals to Newcastle" reflects its dominance — and in the 19th century became a centre of shipbuilding, heavy engineering and locomotive manufacture on the Tyne.

The city today

Landmarks include Newcastle Castle, St Nicholas Cathedral, the seven bridges across the Tyne (among them the Tyne Bridge of 1928 and the Gateshead Millennium Bridge), Grey Street, and the regenerated Quayside. Newcastle University and Northumbria University give the city a large student population, and its football club, Newcastle United, plays at St James' Park.
