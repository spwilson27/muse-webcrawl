Electromagnetic compatibility

Electromagnetic compatibility
AbbreviationEMC
ConcernsEmissions and immunity
StandardsCISPR, IEC 61000, FCC Part 15

Electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) is the ability of electrical equipment and systems to function acceptably in their electromagnetic environment, by limiting the unintentional generation, propagation and reception of electromagnetic energy that may cause unwanted effects such as electromagnetic interference (EMI).

Two aspects

EMC has two complementary aspects: emissions control, which limits the electromagnetic energy a device radiates or conducts, and immunity (or susceptibility), which ensures a device operates correctly in the presence of electromagnetic disturbances. Good EMC practice involves filtering, shielding, grounding, and careful printed-circuit and cable layout.

Regulation and testing

Most countries require electronic products to comply with EMC regulations before sale — for example the EU EMC Directive, and FCC rules in the United States. Compliance testing is carried out in anechoic chambers and open-area test sites using standardised measurements of radiated and conducted emissions and immunity to phenomena such as electrostatic discharge and voltage surges.

History

Interference problems were recognised with early radio and telegraph systems, but EMC became a formal discipline with the proliferation of electronics after the Second World War, particularly in military and aerospace applications.
