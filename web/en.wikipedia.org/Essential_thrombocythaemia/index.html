Essential thrombocythaemia

Other namesEssential thrombocytosis, primary thrombocythaemia
SpecialtyHaematology
Key featureSustained platelet count ≥ 450 × 10⁹/L
Driver mutationsJAK2 V617F, CALR, MPL

Essential thrombocythaemia (ET), also called essential thrombocytosis, is a rare chronic myeloproliferative neoplasm characterised by the overproduction of platelets by megakaryocytes in the bone marrow. It is one of the classic Philadelphia-chromosome-negative myeloproliferative neoplasms, alongside polycythaemia vera and primary myelofibrosis.

Signs and symptoms

Many patients are asymptomatic and the condition is found incidentally on a routine blood count. Others experience symptoms from abnormal clotting or bleeding: headaches, dizziness, visual disturbances, erythromelalgia (burning pain and redness of the hands and feet), thrombosis of arteries or veins, and, paradoxically at very high platelet counts, haemorrhage due to acquired von Willebrand syndrome.

Cause and diagnosis

The disease is driven in most patients by acquired somatic mutations that constitutively activate the JAK-STAT signalling pathway: JAK2 V617F in roughly half of cases, CALR (calreticulin) mutations in about a quarter, and MPL mutations in a smaller fraction; a minority are "triple-negative". Diagnosis follows WHO criteria combining a sustained platelet count of at least 450 × 10⁹/L, characteristic bone-marrow morphology with enlarged mature megakaryocytes, presence of a driver mutation, and exclusion of reactive causes of thrombocytosis.

Treatment and prognosis

Management is guided by thrombotic risk. Low-risk patients may receive low-dose aspirin alone; higher-risk patients (older age or prior thrombosis) are treated with cytoreductive therapy such as hydroxycarbamide (hydroxyurea), anagrelide, or interferon alfa. Life expectancy is modestly reduced compared with the general population, and a minority progress to myelofibrosis or, rarely, acute leukaemia.
