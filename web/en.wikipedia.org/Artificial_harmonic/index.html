Artificial harmonic

An artificial harmonic is a harmonic produced on a string instrument by a player who fully stops a string at one point with one finger while lightly touching a node further along the string with another finger, causing the string to sound a pitch above its natural harmonics. Unlike a natural harmonic, which is played on an open string, the artificial harmonic allows harmonics to be produced in any key and at any pitch level.

Technique

On the violin family, the player typically stops the string firmly with the first finger and touches a perfect fourth above with the fourth finger; the resulting harmonic sounds two octaves above the stopped note. A perfect-fifth touch produces a harmonic an octave and a fifth above. Artificial harmonics are often notated with a stopped pitch plus a diamond-shaped notehead indicating the touched node.

Use in music

The device appears in virtuoso string writing from the nineteenth century onward, for example in the works of Paganini and in orchestral scores by composers seeking ethereal, flute-like colours. Related techniques include pinch harmonics on the electric guitar, where the player's thumb contacts the string immediately after picking.
