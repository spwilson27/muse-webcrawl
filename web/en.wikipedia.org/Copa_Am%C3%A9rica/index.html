The Copa América is the main men's football tournament contested among national teams from South America. It is the oldest still-running continental football competition in the world, and the third most watched in the world. Organised by CONMEBOL, the tournament determines the champions of South America.

History

The first edition was held in 1916 in Argentina as part of the centenary celebrations of Argentine independence, with Uruguay emerging as champions. For decades the tournament was known as the South American Football Championship (Campeonato Sudamericano de Fútbol) before adopting the name Copa América in 1975. It has been held at irregular intervals, though in modern times it generally takes place every four years, with special centenary editions such as the Copa América Centenario held in the United States in 2016.

Format

All ten CONMEBOL member nations take part, and guest teams from other confederations — most frequently from CONCACAF — are often invited to bring the field to twelve or sixteen teams. Teams are divided into groups, with the best advancing to knockout rounds culminating in a final.

Results

Argentina and Uruguay have historically dominated the competition, each winning it fifteen or more times, while Brazil has won it nine times. Every CONMEBOL nation except Ecuador and Venezuela has reached the final at least once.
