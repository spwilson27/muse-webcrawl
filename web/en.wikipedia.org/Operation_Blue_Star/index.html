Operation Blue Star

Date1–10 June 1984
LocationGolden Temple, Amritsar, Punjab, India
ResultMilitants removed from the temple complex; heavy casualties and damage to the Akal Takht

Operation Blue Star was a military operation by the Indian Armed Forces conducted between 1 and 10 June 1984 to remove Sikh militants, led by Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale, who had taken refuge in the Golden Temple (Harmandir Sahib) complex in Amritsar, Punjab. The operation was ordered by Prime Minister Indira Gandhi.

Background

During the late 1970s and early 1980s, Punjab saw rising militancy linked to demands for greater autonomy and the activities of Bhindranwale and his followers, who fortified positions within the Golden Temple complex, the holiest shrine of Sikhism. Violence in the state escalated, and the government decided on military action to flush out the militants.

The operation

Indian Army troops, supported by tanks and artillery, assaulted the complex beginning on the night of 5–6 June 1984, a period when the temple was crowded with pilgrims commemorating the martyrdom of Guru Arjan. Fierce fighting caused extensive damage, including to the Akal Takht, and large loss of life. Official figures put deaths at several hundred; other estimates are considerably higher. Bhindranwale and many of his followers were killed.

Aftermath

The operation deeply angered many Sikhs and is widely regarded as a turning point in modern Sikh history. On 31 October 1984, Indira Gandhi was assassinated by two of her Sikh bodyguards, an act followed by anti-Sikh riots in Delhi and elsewhere in which thousands of Sikhs were killed. Punjab subsequently endured a decade of insurgency and counter-insurgency.
