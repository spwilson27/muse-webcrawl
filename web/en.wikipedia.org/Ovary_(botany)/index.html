Ovary (botany)

In flowering plants (angiosperms), the ovary is the enlarged basal part of the pistil — the female reproductive organ of the flower — which contains one or more ovules attached by placentation. After fertilization, the ovules develop into seeds and the ovary wall (pericarp) typically develops into the fruit.

Position

Botanists classify flowers by the position of the ovary relative to the attachment of the other floral parts (sepals, petals, stamens). In a superior ovary (hypogynous flower), the ovary sits above the attachment point. In an inferior ovary (epigynous flower), as in apples and cucumbers, the ovary lies below it and is embedded in the receptacle. A half-inferior ovary occurs in perigynous flowers, where the floral parts attach around the ovary.

Structure and fruit development

The ovary is formed of one or more fused carpels and contains chambers called locules. Following pollination and fertilization, hormonal signals trigger the ovary to ripen into a fruit, protecting the developing seeds and aiding their dispersal by wind, water, or animals.
