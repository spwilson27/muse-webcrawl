Enteral nutrition

Enteral nutrition is the delivery of nutrients into the gastrointestinal tract, most commonly by a feeding tube, for patients who cannot meet their nutritional needs by mouth but whose gut still functions. It is preferred over parenteral (intravenous) nutrition whenever the digestive tract can be used, because it preserves gut integrity and carries fewer metabolic and infectious complications.

Access routes

Short-term feeding typically uses nasogastric or nasojejunal tubes passed through the nose. For long-term feeding, tubes are placed through the abdominal wall: a gastrostomy (PEG) into the stomach, or a jejunostomy into the small intestine, by endoscopic, radiological, or surgical techniques.

Feeds and administration

Standard polymeric feeds contain whole proteins, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins, and minerals; specialized formulas exist for conditions such as renal failure, diabetes, or malabsorption. Feeds may be given continuously by pump, in intermittent boluses, or cyclically overnight. Complications include tube misplacement or blockage, aspiration, diarrhea, and refeeding syndrome when nutrition is restarted in severely malnourished patients.

Indications

Common indications include stroke and neurological disorders affecting swallowing, head and neck cancers, critical illness, and severe inflammatory bowel disease in children.
