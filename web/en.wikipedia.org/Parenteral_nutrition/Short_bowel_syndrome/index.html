Short bowel syndrome

Short bowel syndrome (SBS) is a malabsorption disorder caused by the surgical removal of, or severe disease in, a large portion of the small intestine, leaving too little absorptive surface for adequate nutrition and hydration. In adults it most often follows resection for Crohn's disease, mesenteric ischemia, trauma, or cancer; in infants, necrotizing enterocolitis is a common cause.

Signs and symptoms

The main features are chronic diarrhea, dehydration, electrolyte disturbances, weight loss, and malnutrition, with specific deficiencies of fat-soluble vitamins, vitamin B12, and bile salts depending on which segments were lost. Severity depends on the length and site of remaining bowel and on whether the colon and ileocaecal valve are preserved.

Treatment

Management aims to maximize enteral intake while the remaining bowel adapts (growing longer villi and greater capacity over months to years). Many patients require parenteral nutrition, sometimes long-term at home; treatments include anti-diarrhoeal agents, acid suppression, and the GLP-2 analogue teduglutide, which enhances intestinal absorption. Surgical lengthening procedures and, rarely, intestinal transplantation are options in severe cases.
