Parenteral nutrition

Parenteral nutrition (PN) is the feeding of nutritional products directly into a vein, bypassing the usual process of eating and digestion. When a person receives all of their nutrition intravenously it is called total parenteral nutrition (TPN).

Indications

Parenteral nutrition is used when the gastrointestinal tract cannot be used or cannot absorb enough nutrients, for example in short bowel syndrome, severe Crohn's disease, bowel obstruction, prolonged ileus, or in premature infants with immature guts. Enteral nutrition (tube feeding) is preferred whenever the gut works, because parenteral feeding carries greater risks.

Contents and administration

PN solutions are compounded to individual needs and typically contain water, glucose, amino acids, lipids (often as a soy-based emulsion), electrolytes, vitamins, and trace elements. Long-term administration usually requires central venous access, such as a PICC line or tunnelled catheter, because the concentrated solutions irritate peripheral veins.

Complications

Complications include catheter-related bloodstream infections, metabolic disturbances such as refeeding syndrome, liver disease associated with intestinal failure, and venous thrombosis. Careful monitoring of fluids, electrolytes, and blood glucose is required.
