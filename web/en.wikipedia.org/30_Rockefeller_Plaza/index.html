30 Rockefeller Plaza is a skyscraper in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, and the centrepiece of the Rockefeller Center complex. Completed in 1933 as the RCA Building, it later became known as the GE Building and, since 2014, as the Comcast Building. Its street address gives the building its famous nickname, "30 Rock" — a name popularised worldwide by the NBC sitcom of the same name.

History and architecture

The tower was designed by Raymond Hood and the Associated Architects as the flagship of John D. Rockefeller Jr.'s Rockefeller Center, built during the Great Depression. Rising 850 feet over 69 storeys, its slim limestone slab is stepped back in the Art Deco manner to satisfy the 1916 zoning rules on light and air. The building is decorated with notable artworks, including Lee Lawrie's "Wisdom" screen above the main entrance and, in its environs, the murals and sculpture of the wider complex. It was declared a New York City landmark as part of Rockefeller Center.

NBC Studios

From its opening, the building housed the studios of NBC. Studio 8H — originally built for Arturo Toscanini's NBC Symphony Orchestra — has been home to Saturday Night Live since 1975; Studio 6B housed The Tonight Show in its various incarnations. Radio City Music Hall stands nearby within the complex, and the sunken plaza below, with its gilded Prometheus statue, is famous for its ice-skating rink in winter and the annual Rockefeller Center Christmas Tree.

Top of the Rock

The building's observation deck, Top of the Rock, occupies floors 67, 69 and 70. Originally opened in 1933 and designed to evoke the deck of an ocean liner, it closed in 1986 and reopened in 2005 after renovation, offering unobstructed views of Central Park to the north and the Empire State Building and Lower Manhattan to the south. Revived attractions in the 2020s include "The Beam", recreating the famous 1932 photograph of ironworkers lunching on a steel beam high above the city — a photograph taken during this building's own construction.
