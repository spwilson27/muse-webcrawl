Founded1867

IndustryInvestment banking

Kuhn, Loeb & Co. was an American multinational investment bank founded in 1867 by Abraham Kuhn and Solomon Loeb. Headquartered in New York City, it became one of the most prominent American investment banks, rivalling J.P. Morgan & Co. in railroad finance.

History

Under Jacob H. Schiff, who joined in 1875 and effectively led the firm, Kuhn, Loeb financed major American railroads and helped float Japanese war loans during the Russo-Japanese War. Partners included Otto Kahn, Felix Warburg, and Paul Warburg, the last an architect of the Federal Reserve System.

Merger

In 1977 the firm merged with Lehman Brothers to form Lehman Brothers, Kuhn, Loeb Inc., ending 110 years of independent operation.
