Peberholm

Peberholm is an artificial island in the Danish part of the Øresund strait, built as part of the Øresund Bridge project linking Copenhagen, Denmark, with Malmö, Sweden. Its name ("Pepper Islet") was chosen to complement the nearby natural island of Saltholm ("Salt Islet").

Construction and purpose

The island, about 4 km long and covering roughly 130 hectares, was built from material dredged from the seabed during construction of the crossing in the late 1990s. It serves as the transition point between the Drogden Tunnel — which carries the motorway and railway from the Danish island of Amager — and the Øresund Bridge proper, which continues to Sweden. A tunnel rather than a bridge was used on the Danish side to avoid interfering with flight paths to Copenhagen Airport and to keep shipping channels clear.

Ecology

Deliberately left without public access, Peberholm has become an unintended nature reserve. Biologists have recorded hundreds of plant species and numerous breeding birds on the island, along with hares and the rare green toad, making it a frequently cited example of species colonising a newly created habitat.
