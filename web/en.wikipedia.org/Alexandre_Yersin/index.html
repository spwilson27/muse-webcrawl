Alexandre Yersin

Alexandre Emile John Yersin (22 September 1863 – 1 March 1943) was a Swiss-French physician and bacteriologist. He is remembered for his work as a pioneer of medical science in Indochina, and chiefly for discovering the bacillus responsible for bubonic plague, which was later named Yersinia pestis in his honour.

Plague discovery

Yersin studied medicine in Lausanne, Marburg, and Paris, working at the Pasteur Institute with Émile Roux on diphtheria toxin. Seeking adventure, he joined the Messageries Maritimes as a ship's doctor and explored French Indochina. In 1894 he was sent to Hong Kong during an outbreak of plague; working in a straw hut near the hospital, he isolated the plague bacillus from buboes, demonstrating it days before Shibasaburo Kitasato's announcement of a similar finding. He later developed an anti-plague serum.

Work in Vietnam

Yersin settled in Nha Trang, Vietnam, founding a Pasteur Institute there in 1895 and a medical school in Hanoi in 1902. He explored the Lang Biang plateau, where the resort town of Da Lat was later established on his recommendation, and introduced rubber trees and quinine-producing cinchona to Vietnam for agricultural and medical use.

Death and legacy

Yersin died in Nha Trang in 1943 and remains deeply revered in Vietnam, where streets and schools bear his name and his house is preserved as a museum. The genus Yersinia, including the plague organism, commemorates him.
