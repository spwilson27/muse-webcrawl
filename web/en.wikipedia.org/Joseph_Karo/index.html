Joseph Karo

Joseph ben Ephraim Karo (1488 – March 24, 1575), also spelled Yosef Caro or Qaro, was the author of the last great codification of Jewish law, the Shulchan Aruch, which is still authoritative for all Jews pertaining to their respective traditions. He is considered one of the greatest rabbinical authorities of Jewish law.

Life

Karo was born in Toledo, Spain, in 1488. After the expulsion of the Jews from Spain in 1492 his family moved to Portugal and then to the Ottoman Empire, living in Constantinople, Adrianople, and Salonica. He spent some twenty years in Nikopol, Bulgaria, where he began his monumental commentary, and in 1536 he settled in Safed in Ottoman Palestine, the centre of the Kabbalistic revival, where he remained until his death in 1575.

Works

Karo's masterwork, the Beit Yosef ("House of Joseph"), is a vast commentary on the Arba'ah Turim of Jacob ben Asher, in which he surveyed and weighed the rulings of earlier authorities. From it he distilled the Shulchan Aruch ("Set Table", 1565), a concise code organised by topic that, together with the glosses of Moses Isserles for Ashkenazi Jewry, became the standard code of Halakha.

Karo also kept a mystical diary, the Maggid Mesharim, recording revelations from a heavenly mentor (maggid) over some fifty years — a remarkable document of Safed spirituality.
