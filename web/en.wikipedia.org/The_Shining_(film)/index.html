The Shining (film)

DirectorStanley Kubrick
StarringJack Nicholson; Shelley Duvall; Danny Lloyd; Scatman Crothers
Released1980
Based onThe Shining by Stephen King (1977)

The Shining is a 1980 psychological horror film produced and directed by Stanley Kubrick and co-written with novelist Diane Johnson. It is based on Stephen King's 1977 novel of the same name and stars Jack Nicholson as Jack Torrance, a writer and recovering alcoholic who takes a job as the winter caretaker of the isolated Overlook Hotel in the Colorado Rockies.

Plot

Jack moves into the empty hotel with his wife Wendy (Shelley Duvall) and young son Danny (Danny Lloyd), who possesses a telepathic gift — "the shining" — that lets him perceive the hotel's violent past and future. Cut off by a snowstorm, Jack is drawn by the hotel's ghosts toward repeating its history of murder: a previous caretaker killed his family. As Jack descends into madness — typing the same sentence endlessly, conversing with the spectral bartender Lloyd and the former caretaker Grady — Danny summons the hotel's cook, Dick Hallorann, who is killed on arrival. In the climax Jack pursues his family with an axe ("Here's Johnny!"), Danny escapes through the hedge maze, and Jack freezes to death in it while Wendy and Danny flee in a snowcat.

Production

Kubrick shot the film almost entirely at EMI Elstree Studios in England, on vast sets including the largest ever built there, using the then-new Steadicam for gliding corridor and maze sequences. His perfectionism produced famously punishing numbers of takes, particularly for Duvall. The exterior of the Timberline Lodge in Oregon supplied establishing shots.

Reception and legacy

Initial reviews were mixed and the film was even nominated for early Golden Raspberry Awards; Stephen King disliked Kubrick's changes and later produced his own 1997 miniseries adaptation. The Shining is now routinely ranked among the greatest horror films ever made, its images — the blood-flooded elevators, the twins, Room 237, "REDRUM" — embedded in popular culture. It was selected for the US National Film Registry in 2018. A sequel, Doctor Sleep, adapted from King's novel, was released in 2019.
