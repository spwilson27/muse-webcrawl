Gondi language

Gondi (Koitoṛ) is a South-Central Dravidian language spoken by the Gond people of central India, chiefly in Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh. With roughly three million speakers recorded in the 2011 census, it is among the most widely spoken tribal languages of India, though it is not included in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution and is considered vulnerable as many speakers shift to regional languages.

Classification and dialects

Gondi belongs to the Gondi–Manda subgroup of South-Central Dravidian, alongside languages such as Kui, Kuvi, and Pengo. Its dialects — including northern varieties around Betul and Chhindwara and southern varieties in Adilabad and Gadchiroli — differ considerably, and mutual intelligibility across the range is limited.

Writing

Gondi is most often written in the Devanagari or Telugu scripts, but it has indigenous scripts: the Gunjala Gondi script, attested in manuscripts from the village of Gunjala and promoted since the 2010s, and the Masaram Gondi script, devised in 1918 by Munshi Mangal Singh Masaram and revived for print and digital use after its encoding in Unicode (2018). A rich oral tradition of songs, epics, and folktales accompanies the language, and community media such as the CGNet Swara voice-news network have used Gondi for grassroots reporting.
