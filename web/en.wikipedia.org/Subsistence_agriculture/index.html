Subsistence agriculture

A subsistence farm producing food chiefly for the household.

Subsistence agriculture occurs when farmers grow food crops to meet the needs of themselves and their families on smallholdings. Subsistence agriculturalists target output mainly for survival, with little or no surplus for trade; planting decisions are made principally with an eye toward what the family will need during the coming year. It is practised widely in parts of sub-Saharan Africa, Asia and Latin America, often on farms of only a few hectares.

Types

Forms of subsistence agriculture include shifting cultivation (including slash-and-burn), pastoral nomadism, and intensive subsistence farming in densely populated regions such as the rice-growing areas of monsoon Asia. Mixed cropping, intercropping and reliance on family labour, hand tools and animal traction are characteristic, as are low inputs of purchased fertiliser and seed.

Challenges

Subsistence farmers are especially vulnerable to drought, crop failure, land fragmentation, soil degradation and price shocks, and the system supports large rural populations at low income levels. Development policy debates concern how to raise productivity and market access — through improved seeds, extension services, irrigation and roads — without destroying the food security and social safety functions that subsistence production provides.
