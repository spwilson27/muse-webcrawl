Petrushka (ballet)

Petrushka (French: Pétrouchka; Russian: Петрушка) is a ballet and orchestral concert work by the Russian composer Igor Stravinsky. It was written for Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes and premiered in Paris on 13 June 1911, with choreography by Michel Fokine and Vaslav Nijinsky in the title role.

PetrushkaComposer: Igor Stravinsky
Choreographer: Michel Fokine
Premiere: 13 June 1911, Paris
Company: Ballets Russes

Synopsis

Set at a Shrovetide fair in 1830s St Petersburg, the ballet tells of three puppets — Petrushka, the Ballerina, and the Moor — brought to life by a Charlatan. Petrushka loves the Ballerina, who rejects him for the Moor; in a jealous rage Petrushka attacks the Moor, who kills him. His ghost then appears above the puppet theatre, defying the horrified Charlatan.

Music

The score is famous for the "Petrushka chord" — two major triads a tritone apart, sounded simultaneously — and for its vivid orchestral depiction of the fair. Stravinsky revised the score in 1947, and it remains a staple of the orchestral repertoire.
