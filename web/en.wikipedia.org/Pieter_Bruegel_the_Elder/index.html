Pieter Bruegel the Elder

Pieter Bruegel the Elder (c. 1525–1530 – 9 September 1569) was the most significant artist of Dutch and Flemish Renaissance painting, a painter and printmaker known for his landscapes and peasant scenes. He is sometimes referred to as "Peasant Bruegel" to distinguish him from his sons, Pieter Brueghel the Younger and Jan Brueghel the Elder, who also became painters.

Works

Bruegel's best-known paintings include The Hunters in the Snow (1565), part of a cycle of the months; The Tower of Babel; Landscape with the Fall of Icarus; The Peasant Wedding; Netherlandish Proverbs, which illustrates over a hundred proverbs in a single village scene; and The Triumph of Death. His work is notable for its crowded compositions, encyclopedic detail, and a blend of humor, moral commentary, and keen observation of ordinary life.

Life

Born probably in Breda or nearby, Bruegel trained in Antwerp, traveled in Italy in the early 1550s—crossing the Alps, whose scenery influenced his landscapes—and settled in Brussels in 1563. He worked for patrons such as the Antwerp merchant Niclaes Jonghelinck and produced designs for prints published by Hieronymus Cock. He died in Brussels in 1569.

Legacy

Bruegel founded a painting dynasty and profoundly influenced Northern European art. The largest collection of his paintings—including many of his masterpieces—is held by the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna.
