Avidyā (Buddhism)

SanskritAvidyā
PaliAvijjā
English translationIgnorance, delusion
RoleFirst link of dependent origination; one of the three poisons

Avidyā (Sanskrit; Pali: avijjā) is a Buddhist term translated as "ignorance" or "delusion". It is considered the fundamental cause of suffering (duḥkha) in Buddhism: a misconception of the nature of reality, particularly the belief in a permanent self and the failure to understand impermanence and dependent origination.

Role in doctrine

Avidyā is the first of the twelve links (nidānas) of dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), the chain that describes how suffering arises and persists through rebirth. It is also one of the three poisons—along with attachment (rāga) and aversion (dveṣa)—depicted at the centre of the Bhavacakra (wheel of life). Within Abhidharma, avidyā is classified as a primary unwholesome mental factor.

Overcoming ignorance

Ignorance is dispelled by wisdom (prajñā), cultivated through the Noble Eightfold Path, especially right view and meditation. Liberation (nirvana) is achieved when avidyā ceases entirely, breaking the cycle of samsara.
