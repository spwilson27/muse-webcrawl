Last speaker

A last speaker is a person who is the last known speaker of a language. When a last speaker dies, the language becomes extinct unless it has been documented well enough to be revived. The final speaker of a language often attracts scholarly and media attention, and linguists may race to record vocabulary, grammar, and oral traditions before they are lost.

Notable last speakers

Well-known examples include Ishi (d. 1916), the last speaker of Yahi, a Yana language of California; Truganini (d. 1876), long considered the last full-blooded Aboriginal Tasmanian and fluent speaker of a Tasmanian language; Dolly Pentreath (d. 1777), reputedly the last native speaker of Cornish (later revived); Tevfik Esenç (d. 1992), the last native speaker of Ubykh, who worked extensively with linguists; and Cristina Calderón (d. 2022) of Chile, the last native speaker of the Yaghan language. Boa Sr. (d. 2010) was the last speaker of Bo, one of the Great Andamanese languages of India.

Documentation and revival

Last speakers and "rememberers"—people with partial knowledge—are crucial sources for language documentation. Recordings made with last speakers have enabled revivals, as with the Wôpanâak (Wampanoag) language, reconstructed from documents, and Cornish and Manx, both of which now have new generations of speakers despite the deaths of their last traditional native speakers (Ned Maddrell, the last native Manx speaker, died in 1974).
