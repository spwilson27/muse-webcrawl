Wasp

A wasp is any insect of the narrow-waisted suborder Apocrita of the order Hymenoptera that is neither a bee nor an ant. Wasps are a hugely diverse group, with tens of thousands of described species, most of them solitary parasitoids that lay their eggs in or on other insects.

Characteristics

Wasps are distinguished by a narrow petiole (the "wasp waist") joining the thorax and abdomen. Females of many species possess a sting, evolved from the ovipositor, used for defense or to subdue prey. Social wasps such as yellowjackets and hornets live in colonies founded by a single queen and build papery nests from chewed wood fiber; most species, however, are solitary, including mud daubers and cicada killers.

Ecology

Wasps are important predators and parasitoids that regulate populations of agricultural pests, and many species are significant pollinators — fig wasps, for example, are obligatory pollinators of figs. Ants and bees both evolved from wasp-like ancestors, making "wasps" a paraphyletic grouping.
