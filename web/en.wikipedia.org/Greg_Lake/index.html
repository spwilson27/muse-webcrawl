Gregory Stuart Lake (10 November 1947 – 7 December 2016) was an English musician, singer, and songwriter, known as a founding member of the progressive rock bands King Crimson and Emerson, Lake & Palmer (ELP). His rich baritone voice and acoustic ballads, such as "Lucky Man," were a hallmark of both groups.

Career

Lake, a school friend of Robert Fripp, joined King Crimson in 1969 and sang and played bass on their landmark debut In the Court of the Crimson King (1969) and In the Wake of Poseidon (1970). In 1970 he formed ELP with keyboardist Keith Emerson and drummer Carl Palmer; the trio became one of the most successful progressive rock acts of the 1970s with albums such as Tarkus, Trilogy, and Brain Salad Surgery. His 1975 solo single "I Believe in Father Christmas," written with Peter Sinfield, became a perennial holiday hit in Britain.

Later life and death

Lake pursued a solo career in the 1980s and participated in ELP reunions, including a 2010 High Voltage Festival performance. He died of pancreatic cancer on 7 December 2016, nine months after Keith Emerson's death the same year.
