Philippi

Philippi was a major Greek city northwest of the nearby island of Thasos. Its original name was Crenides, after its nearby springs. It was renamed by Philip II of Macedon in 356 BC and abandoned in the 14th century after the Ottoman conquest. The present municipality of Filippoi is located near the ruins of the ancient city, in the region of Eastern Macedonia and Thrace, Greece.

History

Philip II seized the settlement to control the rich gold mines of Mount Pangaion and to guard the strategic pass on the route that later became the Roman Via Egnatia. In 42 BC the plain west of the city was the site of the Battle of Philippi, in which Mark Antony and Octavian defeated the assassins of Julius Caesar, Brutus and Cassius. The city later became a Roman colony.

Christianity

Philippi was the first city in Europe where the apostle Paul preached, around AD 49–50, founding a Christian community to which his Epistle to the Philippians is addressed. Impressive remains of early Christian basilicas survive. The archaeological site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2016.
