The Northrop Grumman B-21 Raider is an American strategic bomber under development for the United States Air Force (USAF) by Northrop Grumman. As part of the Long Range Strike Bomber (LRS-B) programme, it is designed to be a long-range, stealth intercontinental strategic bomber capable of delivering conventional and thermonuclear weapons. It is named in honour of the Doolittle Raiders of the Second World War.

Development

The Air Force awarded the LRS-B contract to Northrop Grumman in October 2015. A rendering was first shown publicly in 2016, revealing a flying-wing design similar in concept to, but smaller than, the B-2 Spirit. The aircraft is built primarily at Air Force Plant 42 in Palmdale, California. The B-21 was formally unveiled at a rollout ceremony on 2 December 2022, and made its first flight on 10 November 2023, from Palmdale to Edwards Air Force Base.

Design

The B-21 is a flying wing with deeply blended inlets and advanced low-observable features intended to defeat modern air defences. It is designed with an open-systems architecture to accept new sensors and weapons over its life, and the Air Force has described it as the first aircraft of a broader family of long-range strike systems. It is expected to be optionally crewed in some future roles, though initial operations are with a crew of two.

Role and future

The Raider is planned to replace the B-1 Lancer and B-2 Spirit and to operate alongside the upgraded B-52 Stratofortress as the backbone of the US bomber force. The Air Force has indicated plans for at least 100 aircraft, with entry into service expected in the latter half of the 2020s. Its first operational base is Ellsworth Air Force Base, South Dakota.
