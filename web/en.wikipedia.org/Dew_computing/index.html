Dew computing

Dew computing is an information technology (IT) paradigm that combines the core concept of cloud computing with ideas from fog computing and end-user computing. The term, promoted from around 2015 by computer scientist Yingwei Wang and others, uses a meteorological metaphor: cloud is high in the sky, fog is near the ground, and dew is on the ground — that is, on the user's own device.

Concept

In the dew computing model, a "dew server" runs on a local device or local network and provides services that can operate fully independently of internet connectivity, while periodically synchronising with cloud services when a connection is available. The goal is that users should lose no essential functionality when offline, and that local resources — which sit closest to the user, like dew on grass — should be exploited first before reaching up to the fog or the cloud.

Categories

Proposed dew computing applications are commonly grouped into categories such as web in dew (locally mirrored websites that keep working offline), storage in dew, software in dew, operating systems in dew, and data in dew. The paradigm overlaps substantially with edge computing and offline-first application design, and it has been advanced mainly through academic conferences and workshops devoted to the topic.

Reception

Supporters present dew computing as the natural endpoint of the decentralisation trend from cloud to edge. Critics note that the term largely rebrands established ideas — local servers, caching and synchronisation — and it has seen limited industrial adoption compared with fog and edge computing.
