Quantum harmonic oscillator

The quantum harmonic oscillator is the quantum-mechanical analog of the classical harmonic oscillator. Because an arbitrary smooth potential can usually be approximated as a harmonic potential at the vicinity of a stable equilibrium point, it is one of the most important model systems in quantum mechanics. Furthermore, it is one of the few quantum-mechanical systems for which an exact, analytical solution is known.

Hamiltonian and energy eigenstates

The Hamiltonian of a particle of mass m in a one-dimensional harmonic potential of angular frequency ω is

Ĥ = p̂2/2m + (1/2)mω2x̂2

Its energy eigenvalues are

En = ħω(n + 1/2),   n = 0, 1, 2, …

The equally spaced energy levels include a non-zero ground-state or "zero-point" energy ħω/2, a purely quantum effect with no classical counterpart. The eigenfunctions are Gaussian functions multiplied by Hermite polynomials.

Ladder operators

The problem can be solved algebraically using creation and annihilation (ladder) operators, which raise and lower the oscillator between adjacent energy levels. This formalism, introduced by Paul Dirac, extends naturally to quantum field theory, where each mode of a field is treated as a quantum harmonic oscillator and its excitations are interpreted as particles.

Applications

The model describes molecular vibrations and infrared spectroscopy, phonons in solids, the quantised electromagnetic field, and countless near-equilibrium systems across physics and chemistry. In higher dimensions the oscillator separates into independent one-dimensional oscillators.
