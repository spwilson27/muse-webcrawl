Unaccredited institutions of higher education

Unaccredited institutions of higher education are colleges, seminaries, universities and other post-secondary institutions that lack academic accreditation by a recognised accrediting body. Their degrees are not necessarily invalid, but they typically lack the recognition accorded to accredited qualifications and may not be accepted by employers, professional licensing bodies or other universities.

Reasons for lack of accreditation

Institutions may be unaccredited for many reasons: they may be new and still seeking accreditation; they may be small religious seminaries that decline accreditation on principle; they may operate in jurisdictions where accreditation is not required; or they may be "diploma mills" that sell degrees with little or no study. Some legitimate institutions choose to remain unaccredited because of theological objections to outside oversight, a stance found among some fundamentalist Christian colleges in the United States.

Legal and practical status

Laws vary widely. Some US states restrict the use of unaccredited degrees for employment or professional licensing, and Oregon and others publish lists of institutions whose degrees may not be used. Internationally, recognition of foreign qualifications generally depends on whether the awarding institution is recognised in its home country. Students at unaccredited US institutions are ineligible for federal student aid, and credits rarely transfer to accredited schools.
