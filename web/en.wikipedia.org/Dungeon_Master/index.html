Dungeon Master

Applies toDungeons & Dragons
Generic termGame master / referee
RoleNarrator, world-builder, rules arbiter

In the Dungeons & Dragons role-playing game, the Dungeon Master (DM) is the participant who organises and oversees the game: creating the adventure's details and challenges, describing the world and its inhabitants, controlling non-player characters and monsters, and adjudicating the rules. In other role-playing games the equivalent role is usually called the game master or referee.

Role in play

The Dungeon Master prepares or improvises a setting and scenario, presents situations to the players, describes the results of their characters' actions, and rolls dice or rules on outcomes for everything the players do not control directly. The DM balances challenge and story pacing and is traditionally the final arbiter of rules disputes at the table.

History

The role emerged from the referee in miniature wargaming and was formalised in the original 1974 Dungeons & Dragons rules, co-created by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson. TSR began publishing prepared adventure modules in the late 1970s to help DMs run games. The term "Dungeon Master" is a trademark of Wizards of the Coast.

In popular culture

Notable Dungeon Masters in streamed actual-play series, such as Matthew Mercer of Critical Role, have popularised styles of narration and world-building and contributed to the growth of role-playing as spectator entertainment since the 2010s.
