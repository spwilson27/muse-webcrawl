Richard Steiff

Richard Steiff (7 February 1877 – 30 March 1939) was a German designer for the Steiff toy company, founded by his aunt Margarete Steiff. He is generally credited with designing the first teddy bear with movable joints in 1902.

Life and work

Steiff studied at the School of Arts and Crafts in Stuttgart and spent much time sketching animals at the Nill zoo. In 1897 he joined his aunt's company in Giengen, which produced felt toys. Drawing on his sketches of bears, in 1902 he designed "Bear 55 PB", a stuffed bear made of mohair with movable arms, legs and head.

The teddy bear

The bear was exhibited at the Leipzig Toy Fair in 1903, where an American buyer ordered 3,000. In the United States the toy became known as the "teddy bear" after President Theodore Roosevelt's famous hunting incident. Steiff bears with the "button in ear" trademark became prized collectibles worldwide.

Later achievements

Steiff also designed the company's innovative glass-and-steel factory building, the Junghans Building (1903), and developed other toys including the Roloplan kite. He died in Jackson, Michigan, in 1939.
