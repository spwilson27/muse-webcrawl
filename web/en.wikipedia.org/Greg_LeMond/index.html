Gregory James LeMond (born 26 June 1961) is an American former professional road racing cyclist. He won the Tour de France three times and the Road Race World Championship twice, and is widely regarded as the greatest American cyclist. He was the first American and the first non-European to win the Tour de France.

Career

LeMond turned professional in 1981 and won the 1983 World Championship. Riding for La Vie Claire alongside Bernard Hinault, he finished second in the 1985 Tour before winning in 1986. In 1987 he was nearly killed in a hunting accident, suffering shotgun wounds that left pellets in his body, and missed two seasons. His comeback culminated in the 1989 Tour, which he won by eight seconds over Laurent Fignon—the closest finish in Tour history—followed by a third victory in 1990.

Later activities

LeMond was an early adopter of aerodynamic equipment and carbon-fiber frames and later became a prominent anti-doping voice, publicly criticizing Lance Armstrong and cycling authorities. He founded LeMond Composites and has worked on carbon-fiber manufacturing ventures.
