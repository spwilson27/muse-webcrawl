Treaty of 1818

The treaty set the boundary along the 49th parallel from the Lake of the Woods to the Rocky Mountains.

Signed20 October 1818, London
PartiesUnited States; United Kingdom (for British North America)
NegotiatorsAlbert Gallatin and Richard Rush (US); Frederick John Robinson and Henry Goulburn (UK)
SubjectBoundary, fisheries, navigation, Oregon Country

The Treaty of 1818 (formally the Convention respecting fisheries, boundary and the restoration of slaves, also called the London Convention or Anglo-American Convention of 1818) was an international treaty signed in 1818 between the United States and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland, acting for its North American colonies. It resolved several standing issues left from the War of 1812 and set much of the modern Canada–United States border.

Provisions

  • Boundary: The border was fixed along the 49th parallel north from the Lake of the Woods to the Rocky Mountains ("the Stony Mountains"), replacing the vague drainage-based line of earlier treaties. Britain ceded the part of Rupert's Land south of the 49th parallel (including the Red River Valley), while the United States ceded the northern tip of the Louisiana Purchase above the parallel.
  • Fisheries: American fishermen secured permanent rights to fish and dry their catch on parts of the Newfoundland, Labrador and Nova Scotia coasts.
  • Oregon Country: West of the Rockies, the parties agreed to joint occupation and control of the Oregon Country for ten years, without prejudice to either side's claims — an arrangement later renewed indefinitely in 1827 and ended by the Oregon Treaty of 1846.
  • Slaves: The dispute over compensation for enslaved people carried off by British forces during the War of 1812 was referred to arbitration by the Tsar of Russia, who ruled in the United States' favour; Britain eventually paid compensation.
  • Trade: The commercial convention of 1815 between the two countries was extended.

Significance

Together with the Rush–Bagot Agreement of 1817, which limited naval armaments on the Great Lakes, the Treaty of 1818 began the long demilitarisation of the US–Canada frontier and remains the foundation of the world's longest undefended border.
