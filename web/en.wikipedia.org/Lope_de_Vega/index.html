Lope de Vega

Lope Félix de Vega y Carpio (25 November 1562 – 27 August 1635) was a Spanish playwright, poet, and novelist, and a key figure of the Spanish Golden Age of Baroque literature. Nicknamed "the Phoenix of Wits" and "Monster of Nature" by Miguel de Cervantes, he was one of the most prolific authors in world literature, credited with some 3,000 sonnets, numerous novels and epic poems, and hundreds of plays — around 500 of which survive, including the celebrated Fuenteovejuna.

Life

Born in Madrid, Vega was a child prodigy who was writing plays by his early teens. His turbulent life included military service with the Spanish Armada in 1588, several marriages and love affairs, exile, and ordination as a priest in 1614. He codified his dramatic method in the verse treatise New Art of Playwriting (1609), defending the mixing of tragedy and comedy and the loosening of the classical unities to please the paying public.

Works

His best-known plays include Fuenteovejuna (c. 1612–14), about a village that collectively takes responsibility for killing a tyrannical commander; The Dog in the Manger; Punishment Without Vengeance; and The Knight from Olmedo. He renewed the Spanish comedia tradition that dominated the public theaters of his day, and his influence shaped playwrights such as Tirso de Molina and Calderón de la Barca.
