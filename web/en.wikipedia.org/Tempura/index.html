Tempura

Place of originJapan (influenced by Portuguese cooking)
Main ingredientsSeafood or vegetables, batter (flour, egg, cold water)
ServingWith tentsuyu dipping sauce, grated daikon, or salt

Tempura (天ぷら) is a typical Japanese dish that usually consists of seafood and vegetables that have been coated in a thin batter and deep fried. The technique was influenced by Portuguese missionaries and merchants in Nagasaki in the 16th century, who fried foods in batter during Lent — the name is often traced to the Latin tempora, the Ember Days of fasting.

Preparation

The batter is made of cold water, wheat flour, and sometimes egg, mixed minimally with chopsticks to avoid developing gluten, keeping it light and lacy. Pieces are fried briefly in hot oil — traditionally sesame or vegetable oil — at about 160–180 °C. Common ingredients include shrimp, white fish, sweet potato, pumpkin, lotus root, shiitake mushrooms, and shiso leaves.

Serving styles

Tempura is served with tentsuyu, a dashi-based dipping sauce with grated daikon, or simply with flavored salts. It also appears atop rice bowls (tendon), noodle soups (tempura udon and soba), and in makizushi.

History

Originally a street food of Edo (Tokyo), tempura spread during the Edo period as oil became cheaper; specialty tempura restaurants (tempura-ya) later refined it into a chef's art, frying each piece to order at a counter.
