Mehmed V Reşâd (Ottoman Turkish: محمد خامس; 2 November 1844 – 3 July 1918) was the 35th sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1909 until his death in 1918. The son of Sultan Abdulmejid I, he succeeded his brother Abdul Hamid II after the Young Turk Revolution restored the constitution. Largely a figurehead, he reigned while real power rested with the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP), especially during the First World War under the triumvirate of Enver, Talaat, and Djemal Pashas.

Early life

Mehmed was educated in the palace in Ottoman Turkish, Arabic, Persian, Islamic history, and French, and developed a lasting interest in Persian literature. During Abdul Hamid II's long reign he lived in seclusion in the Topkapı and Dolmabahçe palaces as heir apparent, having succeeded to that position on his brother's accession in 1876 — spending more than three decades under watchful restriction.

Reign

Mehmed V was proclaimed sultan on 27 April 1909, aged 64, after the 31 March Incident led parliament and the CUP to depose Abdul Hamid II. Constitutional amendments that year sharply curtailed the sultan's powers, making Mehmed the empire's first genuinely constitutional monarch. His reign saw the loss of Libya to Italy in the Italo-Turkish War (1911–12) and of nearly all remaining European territory in the Balkan Wars (1912–13), as well as the 1913 CUP coup that installed the wartime regime.

In November 1914, as caliph, Mehmed V proclaimed a jihad against the Entente powers after the Ottoman Empire entered the First World War on the side of Germany and Austria-Hungary. During the war he performed ceremonial duties, received Kaiser Wilhelm II in Constantinople in 1917, and awarded decorations, while the CUP government directed policy — including the deportations and mass killings of Armenians in 1915 that he formally sanctioned by decree but did not initiate.

Death

Mehmed V died of a heart attack at Yıldız Palace on 3 July 1918, four months before the Ottoman Empire signed the Armistice of Mudros. He was succeeded by his brother Mehmed VI, the last Ottoman sultan. His tomb, designed by the architect Kemaleddin Bey, stands in the Eyüp district of Istanbul.
