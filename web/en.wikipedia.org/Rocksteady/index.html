Rocksteady

Rocksteady is a music genre that originated in Jamaica around 1966. A successor to ska and a precursor to reggae, rocksteady slowed the frantic tempo of ska, emphasised the bass line and the one drop rhythm, and foregrounded soulful vocal harmonies influenced by American rhythm and blues and soul groups such as The Impressions.

Characteristics

Rocksteady recordings typically feature a prominent electric bass playing melodic patterns, sparse horn arrangements in place of ska's large horn sections, and rhythms divided between piano, guitar, and drums with strong emphasis on the third beat. Vocals, often by harmony trios, took centre stage with romantic and socially conscious lyrics. The slowed pace suited the "rude boy" dance style of the era.

History

The genre's name came from Alton Ellis's 1966 single "Rock Steady." Producers such as Duke Reid at Treasure Isle, Coxsone Dodd at Studio One, and Leslie Kong recorded defining work by The Techniques, The Paragons, The Heptones, The Melodians, and Ken Boothe. Though dominant for only about two years, rocksteady profoundly shaped reggae: its bass-led sound, vocal style, and many of its songs were later recut in the reggae era. In the late 1960s rocksteady evolved into reggae as tempos and organ patterns changed.
