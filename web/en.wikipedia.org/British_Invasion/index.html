British Invasion

The Beatles arrive at JFK Airport, 7 February 1964

The British Invasion was a cultural phenomenon of the mid-1960s in which British rock and pop acts became enormously popular in the United States. It began with the Beatles' appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show on 9 February 1964, watched by some 73 million people, and opened American charts and airwaves to a wave of British groups.

Background

British musicians had absorbed American rock and roll, rhythm and blues, and skiffle, re-exporting them with distinctive accents and style. Early US successes included Lonnie Donegan's skiffle and Cliff Richard, but the breakthrough came when "I Want to Hold Your Hand" topped the Billboard Hot 100 in early 1964.

Principal acts

Following the Beatles came the Rolling Stones, the Animals, the Dave Clark Five, Herman's Hermits, the Hollies, the Kinks, the Who, the Yardbirds, the Zombies, Manfred Mann, Gerry and the Pacemakers, Petula Clark, and Dusty Springfield. In April 1964 British acts held the top five positions on the Billboard Hot 100. A harder rhythm-and-blues wing (the Stones, Animals, Yardbirds) contrasted with pop acts, and many American teenagers adopted British fashions and slang.

Impact

The Invasion revitalised American rock, prompting US groups such as the Byrds, the Beach Boys, and the Monkees, and it permanently shifted the centre of popular music: British performers became global stars, American music re-imported its own traditions in British form, and the album-oriented rock era followed by the decade's end.
