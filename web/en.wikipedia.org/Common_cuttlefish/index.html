Common cuttlefish

Common cuttlefish
Scientific name: Sepia officinalis
Class: Cephalopoda
Order: Sepiida
Range: Mediterranean Sea, North Sea, Baltic Sea, West African coast

The common cuttlefish (Sepia officinalis) is a mollusc of the family Sepiidae and one of the best-known cuttlefish species. Native to the Mediterranean and eastern Atlantic, it is studied for its remarkable camouflage and is fished commercially for food.

Description

It grows to a mantle length of about 49 cm. Like other cuttlefish it has eight arms and two retractile tentacles, W-shaped pupils, and an internal cuttlebone used to regulate buoyancy. Chromatophores in the skin allow rapid changes of colour and texture for camouflage and signalling.

Behaviour

Common cuttlefish are active predators of crustaceans, fish and molluscs, striking with their tentacles. They can eject ink to confuse predators. Most live only one to two years, dying after a single breeding season in which females lay grape-like clusters of black eggs.

Fishery

The species supports trawl and trap fisheries around Europe; its cuttlebone is also collected for use as a calcium supplement for caged birds.
