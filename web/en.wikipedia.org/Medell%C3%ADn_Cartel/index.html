The Medellín Cartel was a powerful and highly organized Colombian drug cartel and criminal organization originating in the city of Medellín, active between the 1970s and 1993. Led above all by Pablo Escobar, with partners including the Ochoa brothers, Carlos Lehder and Gonzalo Rodríguez Gacha, it supplied the majority of the cocaine entering the United States and other world markets at its height.

Rise

Escobar began as a street criminal and smuggler and built routes moving Andean coca paste to be processed and flown to the United States, colluding with pilots such as Barry Seal and acquiring lab sites and airstrips, including the vast Hacienda Nápoles ranch. The cartel's revenues were estimated in the billions of dollars annually, and Escobar entered politics, winning a seat in Congress in 1982.

War with the Colombian state

After Justice Minister Rodrigo Lara Bonilla was assassinated in 1984, the state moved against the cartel; the traffickers, calling themselves Los Extraditables, answered with a campaign of assassinations and bombings — including the murder of presidential candidate Luis Carlos Galán in 1989 and the bombing of Avianca Flight 203 — to force an end to extradition. Escobar surrendered in 1991 under a deal allowing him to serve time in his self-built prison, La Catedral, then escaped in 1992.

Downfall

A manhunt by the Colombian police Search Bloc, aided by U.S. agencies and the vigilante group Los Pepes, ended with Escobar shot dead on a Medellín rooftop on 2 December 1993. With Rodríguez Gacha killed in 1989, Lehder captured in 1987 and the Ochoas surrendered, the cartel was dismantled, and the rival Cali Cartel briefly inherited the trade.
