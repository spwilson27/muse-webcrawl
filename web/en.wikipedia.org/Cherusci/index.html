Cherusci

The Cherusci were a Germanic tribe inhabiting parts of the plains and forests of northwestern Germany, in the region between the Weser River and the Elbe, during the early centuries of the Roman Empire. They are best known for their role under the chieftain Arminius in destroying three Roman legions at the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9, one of the most consequential defeats in Roman history.

History

The Cherusci first appear in Roman sources in Julius Caesar's account of the Gallic Wars, which places them east of the Rhine near the Hercynian Forest. Under Roman pressure and diplomacy, the tribe was internally divided between pro-Roman and anti-Roman factions. Arminius, a Cheruscan prince who had served in the Roman army and gained citizenship, united several tribes in revolt against the governor Publius Quinctilius Varus.

Battle of the Teutoburg Forest

In September AD 9, Arminius lured Varus and three legions — the Seventeenth, Eighteenth, and Nineteenth — into an ambush in the Teutoburg Forest. The Roman force was annihilated, roughly 20,000 soldiers were killed, and Rome abandoned its plans to conquer and annex Germania east of the Rhine.

Later years

Roman retaliatory campaigns under Germanicus (AD 14–16) punished the Cherusci but did not subjugate them. Internal feuding weakened the tribe after Arminius was murdered by his own kin in AD 21. By the late first century, Tacitus reported that the Cherusci had declined into obscurity, and they were eventually absorbed by neighboring peoples such as the Chatti.
