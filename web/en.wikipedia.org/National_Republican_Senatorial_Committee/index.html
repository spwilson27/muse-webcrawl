The National Republican Senatorial Committee (NRSC) is the Republican Hill committee for the United States Senate, dedicated to electing Republicans to the Senate. It is a national party committee under the Federal Election Campaign Act and the counterpart of the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee.

Role

The NRSC recruits candidates, raises funds, commissions polling, provides campaign training and strategic support, and makes independent expenditures in competitive races. Its chair is a sitting Republican senator elected by the conference for each two-year cycle, and the committee works closely with Senate party leadership. Past chairs include senators who went on to national prominence, such as Mitch McConnell, who chaired the committee in the late 1990s.

History

Founded in 1916, the committee grew in importance with the direct election of senators and the rise of modern campaign finance, becoming one of the "Hill committees" alongside the NRCC and their Democratic counterparts. Its fundraising and spending have featured in debates about campaign finance regulation, particularly after the Citizens United decision expanded independent spending by allied super PACs such as the Senate Leadership Fund.
