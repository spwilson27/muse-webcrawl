U Ba Khin

Sayagyi U Ba Khin (1899–1971).

Sayagyi U Ba Khin (6 March 1899 – 19 January 1971) was a Burmese accountant general and an influential teacher of Vipassana meditation in the Theravada Buddhist tradition. A senior civil servant — he served as the first Accountant General of independent Burma — he was also authorized to teach meditation by the lay master Saya Thetgyi, in the lineage of the Venerable Ledi Sayadaw.

Teaching

In 1952, U Ba Khin founded the International Meditation Centre in Rangoon (Yangon), where he taught students from Burma and abroad, including Westerners who went on to spread Vipassana in the West. His best-known student, S. N. Goenka, carried his teaching of ten-day residential meditation courses around the world. U Ba Khin emphasised experiential understanding of anicca (impermanence) through awareness of bodily sensations, and believed the Buddha's teaching could benefit people of all religions.

Legacy

His method, passed down through Goenka and other students and assistant teachers such as Mother Sayamagyi, has had an outsized influence on the global spread of lay meditation practice in the 20th and 21st centuries.
