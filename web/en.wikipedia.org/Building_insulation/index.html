Building insulation

Building insulation is material used in a building to reduce the transfer of heat between the interior and exterior, to control sound, or both. Insulation slows conductive, convective, and radiative heat flow through the building envelope — walls, roof, floors, windows, and doors — thereby reducing heating and cooling energy use and improving comfort.

Materials

Common insulation materials include fiberglass batts and blown fiber, mineral wool, cellulose from recycled paper, rigid foam boards of expanded or extruded polystyrene and polyisocyanurate, spray polyurethane foam, and reflective foil systems. Their effectiveness is rated by R-value, the measure of thermal resistance per unit thickness; higher R-values indicate better insulating performance.

Principles

Most insulating materials work by trapping pockets of still air or other gases in a matrix of fibers or foam cells, since still gas conducts little heat. Effective installation requires attention to air sealing, moisture control, and avoiding compression or gaps that create thermal bridges.

Environmental considerations

Insulation is among the most cost-effective energy-efficiency measures and reduces greenhouse gas emissions over a building's life. Choice of material also involves embodied carbon, recycled content, fire safety, and health factors such as formaldehyde content or fiber shedding.
