Norman McLaren

Norman McLaren, CC CQ (11 April 1914 – 27 January 1987) was a Scottish Canadian animator, director and producer known for his work for the National Film Board of Canada (NFB). He was a pioneer in a number of areas of animation and filmmaking, including hand-drawn animation, drawn-on-film animation, visual music, abstract film, pixilation and graphical sound.

Early life

McLaren was born in Stirling, Scotland, and studied set design at the Glasgow School of Art. His early experiments with film and animation included painting directly onto film stock. He worked briefly for the GPO Film Unit in London before emigrating to North America in 1939, arriving in New York City.

National Film Board of Canada

In 1941, John Grierson invited McLaren to join the newly formed National Film Board of Canada in Ottawa. There McLaren founded the NFB's animation department and trained a generation of Canadian animators. In 1943 the unit moved to Montreal. McLaren remained with the NFB for the rest of his career, making some of his most celebrated films there.

Work and techniques

McLaren's most famous film, Neighbours (1952), used the technique of pixilation — stop-motion animation of live actors — to tell a parable about two men who fight over a flower growing on the border between their houses. The film won the Academy Award for Best Documentary, Short Subject. His other innovations included drawing and scratching directly on film stock, as in Begone Dull Care (1949), made with Evelyn Lambart; creating synthetic sound by drawing patterns on the optical soundtrack; and the use of multiple exposures and pastel animation in films such as Pas de deux (1968) and A Chairy Tale (1957), the latter co-directed with Claude Jutra and narrated in a version by Ravi Shankar's music.

Legacy

McLaren received over 200 international awards and was made a Companion of the Order of Canada. The Norman McLaren Award and the McLaren Wall-to-Wall program in Montreal commemorate his work. His films are held in the UNESCO Memory of the World register, with Neighbours inscribed in 2009.
