ABDA Command

Active15 January – 25 February 1942
CountriesUnited States, United Kingdom, Netherlands, Australia
Supreme CommanderField Marshal Sir Archibald Wavell
HeadquartersLembang, Java
TheatreSouth-East Asia, Second World War

The American-British-Dutch-Australian (ABDA) Command was a short-lived Allied supreme command formed in January 1942 to coordinate forces resisting the Japanese advance into South-East Asia and the Dutch East Indies during the Second World War. It was the first truly multinational Allied command of the war.

Formation and operations

Under British Field Marshal Sir Archibald Wavell, ABDA was responsible for a vast area stretching from Burma to western New Guinea. Its naval striking force, under Dutch Rear Admiral Karel Doorman, comprised American, Dutch, British and Australian cruisers and destroyers that had never trained together and lacked air cover and a common signalling doctrine.

Dissolution

Unable to halt the Japanese offensives in Malaya, the Philippines and the East Indies, the command was dissolved on 25 February 1942 as Wavell returned to India. Three days later Doorman's fleet was destroyed at the Battle of the Java Sea, and Java itself surrendered in March. Despite its failure, ABDA provided lessons in coalition command that informed later Allied structures such as South East Asia Command.
