Kirchnerism (Spanish: Kirchnerismo) is an Argentine political movement based on the legacies and policies of Néstor Kirchner, president of Argentina from 2003 to 2007, and his wife and successor Cristina Fernández de Kirchner, president from 2007 to 2015 and later vice president (2019–2023). Rooted in the left wing of Peronism, it defines itself against neoliberalism and the legacy of the 1976–83 dictatorship.

Policies

Kirchnerism combined economic interventionism, state-led development, debt renegotiation (the 2005 restructuring of Argentina’s defaulted debt), renationalisations such as the YPF oil company and the pension system, expansion of social programmes, and a strong human-rights policy that reopened trials of dictatorship-era criminals. It also pursued regional integration through blocs such as UNASUR and CELAC and an assertive foreign policy toward the IMF and the United States.

Opposition and legacy

Critics charged Kirchnerism with corruption, manipulation of inflation statistics at the national statistics agency, controls on the press and the judiciary, and unsustainable subsidies and fiscal deficits. The movement remained a dominant force in Argentine politics for two decades, opposing the governments of Mauricio Macri (2015–19) and Javier Milei (from 2023), and its internal evolution — from Néstor’s pragmatic "transversal" coalition to Cristina’s more confrontational style — continues to shape the Peronist movement.
