Continuity of care

Continuity of care is the degree to which a series of discrete health care events is experienced as coherent and connected over time, consistent with the patient's medical needs and personal context. In primary care it traditionally refers to a patient seeing the same clinician over time, so that the clinician accumulates knowledge of the patient and the patient builds trust in the clinician. Research has associated higher continuity of care with improved preventive care, better chronic disease management, fewer hospital admissions, and lower mortality.

Dimensions

Health services researchers commonly distinguish three dimensions. Relational (longitudinal) continuity is an ongoing therapeutic relationship between a patient and one or more providers. Informational continuity is the use of information on prior events and personal circumstances to make current care appropriate, supported by medical records and effective handover. Management continuity is a consistent and coherent approach to the management of a health condition, in which care from different providers is delivered in a complementary and timely manner according to a shared plan.

Measurement and challenges

Continuity is measured with indices such as the Usual Provider of Care index and the Bice–Boxerman Continuity of Care index, which quantify the concentration of a patient's visits among providers. Fragmented delivery systems, workforce shortages, part-time working and emphasis on rapid access can all erode relational continuity, which has declined in several countries; health systems attempt to protect it through personal lists, named accountable GPs and team-based micro-teams.
