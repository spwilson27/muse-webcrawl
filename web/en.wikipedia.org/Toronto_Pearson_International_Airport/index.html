Toronto Pearson International Airport (IATA: YYZ, ICAO: CYYZ) is an international airport located in Mississauga, Ontario, Canada. It is the main airport serving Toronto, its metropolitan area, and the surrounding region known as the Golden Horseshoe. It is the largest and busiest airport in Canada, handling around 50 million passengers in its busiest pre-pandemic years.

History

The airport opened in 1939 as Malton Airport, on farmland purchased by the Toronto Harbour Commission. It served as a training base for the British Commonwealth Air Training Plan during the Second World War. The airport was renamed Toronto International Airport in 1960 and Lester B. Pearson International Airport in 1984, in honour of Lester B. Pearson, the 14th Prime Minister of Canada and recipient of the Nobel Peace Prize. It was subsequently shortened to Toronto Pearson International Airport.

Facilities

Pearson has two active passenger terminals, Terminal 1 and Terminal 3, connected by the Terminal Link train, and five runways. Terminal 1, opened in 2004, is one of the largest airport terminals in North America and hosts Air Canada and its Star Alliance partners, while Terminal 3 hosts WestJet and other carriers. The airport has United States border preclearance facilities, allowing passengers bound for the U.S. to clear customs in Toronto.

Ground transport

The Union Pearson Express train connects the airport with downtown Toronto's Union Station in about 25 minutes. Highways 401, 427, and 409 serve the airport, and numerous GO Transit, TTC, and regional bus services link it to the Greater Toronto Area.
