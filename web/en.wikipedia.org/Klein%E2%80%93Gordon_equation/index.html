Klein–Gordon equation

FieldQuantum field theory
First derived1926 (Klein, Gordon, Fock and others)
TypeRelativistic wave equation for spin-0 particles

The Klein–Gordon equation is a relativistic wave equation for spinless (scalar) particles. Formulated in 1926 by Oskar Klein and Walter Gordon — and independently by Vladimir Fock and others — it was the first attempt to combine quantum mechanics with special relativity, obtained by inserting the relativistic energy–momentum relation into the Schrödinger equation.

Form and solutions

The equation, often written (□ + m²)φ = 0 in natural units, describes a scalar field whose quanta obey Einstein’s relation E² = p² + m². It is second order in time, which early researchers found troubling: the associated conserved density could be negative, so it was abandoned as a single-particle probability equation.

Modern interpretation

Reinterpreted in quantum field theory, the Klein–Gordon field describes spin-0 bosons, and its "negative probability" density is understood as electric charge density, which can legitimately be positive or negative for particles and antiparticles. The equation governs the Higgs field in the Standard Model and the pion fields of nuclear physics, and it remains the starting point for relativistic scalar field theory.
