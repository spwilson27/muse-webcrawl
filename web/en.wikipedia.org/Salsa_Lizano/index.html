Salsa Lizano

TypeCondiment sauce
OriginCosta Rica
Created1920
CreatorLizano family (Prospero Lizano)
Main ingredientsWater, sugar, salt, vegetables (onions, carrots, cauliflower, cucumbers), spices, chili peppers, molasses

Salsa Lizano is a Costa Rican condiment, a thin, brown, slightly sweet and acidic sauce with a distinctive flavour, that is a fixture of Costa Rican cuisine. It is commonly described as similar in style to Worcestershire sauce or HP Sauce, but it is milder, sweeter, and less sharp, with a flavour based on vegetables and molasses rather than anchovies and tamarind.

History

The sauce was created in 1920 by the Lizano family of Alajuela, Costa Rica, and was originally served at the family's bar and restaurant, where patrons asked to buy it to take home. Production was industrialized under the Lizano company, which became one of Costa Rica's best-known food brands; the brand was eventually acquired by Unilever. The recipe remains a trade secret, but the declared ingredients include water, sugar, iodized salt, onions, carrots, cauliflower, cucumbers, spices, chili peppers, and natural flavourings, coloured with caramel.

Use

Salsa Lizano is put on almost everything in Costa Rica, most famously on gallo pinto, the national breakfast of rice and beans, where it is stirred in during cooking as well as served at the table. It is also used on eggs, tamales, patí (a Caribbean-style empanada), grilled meats, and in marinades and stews. For Costa Ricans abroad it is a powerful taste of home and is widely exported to expatriate communities. See also Costa Rican cuisine and Gallo pinto.
