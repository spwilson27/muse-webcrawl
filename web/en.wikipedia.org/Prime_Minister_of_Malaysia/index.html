Prime Minister of Malaysia

The Prime Minister of Malaysia (Malay: Perdana Menteri Malaysia) is the head of government of Malaysia. The prime minister directs the executive branch, chairs the Cabinet, and is appointed by the Yang di-Pertuan Agong (the King) from among members of the Dewan Rakyat (House of Representatives) who, in the King's judgement, command the confidence of the majority of the house.

Perdana Putra, the prime minister's office in Putrajaya.

History

Tunku Abdul Rahman, the "Father of Independence," became the first prime minister of the Federation of Malaya in 1957 and of Malaysia in 1963, serving until 1970. He was succeeded by Abdul Razak Hussein, architect of the New Economic Policy, and Hussein Onn. Mahathir Mohamad served from 1981 to 2003, overseeing rapid industrialisation, and returned to the office in 2018 at the age of 92, becoming the world's oldest serving head of government. After the 2018 election ended six decades of rule by the Barisan Nasional coalition, Malaysia experienced rapid changes of government in 2020–2022 before Anwar Ibrahim became prime minister in November 2022, heading a unity government.

Role and office

The prime minister advises the King on ministerial appointments, leads the majority coalition in Parliament, and directs economic and national policy. The prime minister's office is in the Perdana Putra building in Putrajaya, the federal administrative capital, and the official residence is Seri Perdana.
