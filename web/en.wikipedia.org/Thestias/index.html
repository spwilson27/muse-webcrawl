Thestias is an exoplanet orbiting the star HD 93385, located roughly 130–140 light-years away in the constellation Vela. The planet, formally designated HD 93385 b, was discovered in 2011 by astronomers using the HARPS spectrograph at La Silla Observatory in Chile, as part of a sample of low-mass planets detected by the radial velocity method.

Properties

Thestias has a minimum mass of about 8 times that of Earth, placing it in the super-Earth to Neptune range, and orbits its host star in approximately 13 days at a distance much closer than Mercury's orbit around the Sun. The system also contains additional planets, making HD 93385 a multi-planet system. Because the planet does not transit its star as seen from Earth, its radius and composition are not directly known.

Name

The name Thestias was selected in the IAU's NameExoWorlds 2019 campaign by Greece; in Greek mythology, Thestias (Thestius) is associated with the mythological tradition of Aetolia, and the planet's star was simultaneously named Násti. The International Astronomical Union's naming campaign assigned names proposed by national committees to exoplanets and their host stars.
