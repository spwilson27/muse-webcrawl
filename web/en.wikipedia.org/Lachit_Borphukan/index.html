Lachit Borphukan

Born: 24 November 1622
Died: 25 April 1672
Allegiance: Ahom kingdom
Known for: Battle of Saraighat (1671)

Lachit Borphukan (24 November 1622 – 25 April 1672) was an Ahom general and statesman from Assam, celebrated as a national hero for his leadership against the Mughal Empire, most famously at the Battle of Saraighat in 1671.

Career

Lachit was appointed Borphukan — one of the highest offices of the Ahom kingdom — and commander of the Ahom forces by King Chakradhwaj Singha. In 1667 his forces recaptured Guwahati from the Mughals.

Battle of Saraighat

In 1671, though gravely ill, Lachit led the Ahom fleet on the Brahmaputra River at Saraighat against a much larger Mughal force under Ram Singh I. According to legend, he had his own maternal uncle beheaded for shirking duty on fortifications, declaring that his uncle was not greater than his country. The Ahom victory halted Mughal expansion into Assam. Lachit died the following year and is commemorated annually on Lachit Divas (24 November).
