Pokémon is a series of role-playing video games developed by Game Freak and published by Nintendo and The Pokémon Company. Released in 1996 in Japan for the Game Boy, the series is the core of the Pokémon media franchise and one of the best-selling video game series of all time.

Gameplay

Players take the role of a Pokémon Trainer, travelling a fictional region to catch wild Pokémon with Poké Balls, train them, and battle other Trainers in turn-based combat. Core titles are typically released in pairs, with version-exclusive Pokémon encouraging trading between players, followed later by enhanced versions or remakes.

Generations

The series is divided into generations, each anchored by a pair of core games on new hardware: Red/Green (Kanto), Gold/Silver (Johto), Ruby/Sapphire (Hoenn), Diamond/Pearl (Sinnoh), Black/White (Unova), X/Y (Kalos), Sun/Moon (Alola), Sword/Shield (Galar), and Scarlet/Violet (Paldea), which moved the series to an open-world design.

Reception and legacy

The core series has sold hundreds of millions of units worldwide. Its mechanics of collection, trading, and battling defined the monster-collecting genre and influenced generations of game designers.
