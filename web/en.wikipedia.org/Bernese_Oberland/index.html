Bernese Oberland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Bernese Oberland (German: Berner Oberland) is the higher, southern region of the canton of Bern in Switzerland, encompassing the Bernese Alps, Lakes Thun and Brienz, and the valleys draining toward Interlaken.

Geography

The region includes some of Switzerland's most famous peaks — the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau above Grindelwald and Wengen, the Schilthorn, and the Aletsch Glacier catchment to the south. Principal towns include Interlaken, Thun, Meiringen, Brienz and the resort villages of Grindelwald, Wengen, Mürren and Adelboden.

Tourism

The Oberland was a birthplace of Alpine tourism in the 19th century and remains a major destination for mountaineering, skiing and hiking, served by mountain railways including the Jungfrau Railway to the Jungfraujoch. Traditional industries include woodcarving around Brienz and cheese making in the Simmental and Saanenland.
