Muesli

Muesli is a cold Swiss breakfast dish, the primary ingredient of which is rolled oats. Traditionally, it is set to soak overnight and eaten the next morning with fresh fruit and yogurt. Additional ingredients, such as grains, nuts, seeds, and fresh or dried fruits, are added, along with milk or cream, a squeeze of citrus juice, and honey to add sweetness. Developed around 1900 by Swiss physician Maximilian Bircher-Benner, it was originally known in Swiss German as Birchermüesli — Müesli being a diminutive of Mues, "puree" or "mash-up".

History

Bircher-Benner, a pioneer of whole-food nutrition, developed the dish for patients at his sanatorium in Zurich after encountering a similar raw apple-and-oat meal served in the Alps. His original recipe combined rolled oats soaked in water, lemon juice, condensed milk, grated apple with skin, and ground hazelnuts or almonds. Bircher-Benner believed raw plant foods contained vital "solar energy" and prescribed a diet rich in fresh fruit and vegetables.

Modern muesli

Commercial packaged muesli is a dry mixture of rolled oats with other cereals (wheat, rye, barley flakes), dried fruits such as raisins, dates and apricots, nuts, seeds, and sometimes added sugar, honey powder, or chocolate. It is served with milk, yogurt, or fruit juice, or soaked overnight ("overnight oats" style). Toasted, sweetened variants overlap with granola, which is baked with oil and sweetener. Muesli is marketed as a high-fibre, minimally processed health food, though sweetened varieties may contain considerable sugar.

Birchermüesli today

In Switzerland, Birchermüesli remains a staple, eaten for breakfast or as a light evening meal, and is sold fresh in cafés and supermarkets, typically looser and fruitier than packaged dry muesli and made with yogurt as well as milk.
