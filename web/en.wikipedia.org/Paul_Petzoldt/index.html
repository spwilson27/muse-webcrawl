Paul Petzoldt

BornJanuary 16, 1908, Creston, Iowa, U.S.
DiedOctober 6, 1999, Maine, U.S.
OccupationMountaineer, outdoor educator

Paul Kiesow Petzoldt (1908–1999) was an American mountaineer and a pioneer of outdoor education. He founded the National Outdoor Leadership School (NOLS) in 1965 and later the Wilderness Education Association, shaping the standards by which outdoor leadership and minimum-impact camping are still taught.

Climbing career

Petzoldt made his first ascent of the Grand Teton at age sixteen in 1924, reportedly wearing cowboy boots, and went on to become chief guide in Grand Teton National Park. In 1938 he joined the first American expedition to K2, reaching about 26,000 feet without supplemental oxygen, then a remarkable altitude record for an American. During the Second World War he served as an instructor with the U.S. Army's 10th Mountain Division.

Outdoor education

In 1965 Petzoldt founded NOLS in Lander, Wyoming, to train outdoor leaders systematically in expedition skills, judgment, and environmental ethics — including practices that fed into the Leave No Trace movement. In 1974 he helped found the Wilderness Education Association to certify outdoor leaders professionally. His book The New Wilderness Handbook (1974) codified his teaching philosophy.
