Shakespeare's funerary monument

Shakespeare's funerary monument is a memorial to William Shakespeare located inside Holy Trinity Church in Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, England, above the place where he was buried in 1616. It is traditionally dated to before 1623, when it was mentioned in print in the First Folio of Shakespeare's works.

Description

The monument, attributed to the Southwark tomb-maker Gerard Johnson (Gheerart Janssen), is a wall-mounted demi-effigy: a half-length figure of Shakespeare holding a quill in his right hand with a sheet of paper resting on a cushion, set within an architectural frame of Corinthian columns and heraldic decoration. The accompanying plaques bear two inscriptions — a Latin elegy comparing Shakespeare to Nestor, Socrates, and Virgil, and an English verse inviting the passer-by to read "all that he hath writ" in his "book".

History

The effigy has been restored and repainted several times, notably in 1748 and 1793, when Edmond Malone had it painted white; the original colouring was restored in the 19th century. Scholars have debated whether the figure was altered from an earlier depiction, but it remains, along with the Droeshout engraving in the First Folio, one of only two likenesses generally accepted as representing Shakespeare as he appeared in life.
