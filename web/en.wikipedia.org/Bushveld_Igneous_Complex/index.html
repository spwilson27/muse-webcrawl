The Bushveld Igneous Complex (BIC) is a large layered igneous intrusion within the Earth's crust that has been tilted and eroded to outcrop around the edge of a great geological basin, the Transvaal Basin. Situated in northern South Africa, it is the world's largest layered intrusion and contains the richest known reserves of platinum-group metals.

Geology

The complex formed about 2.06 billion years ago from repeated injections of magma into a vast chamber, whose slow cooling and crystal settling—an immense natural example of the processes in Bowen's reaction series—produced distinct layers. The Rustenburg Layered Suite (up to 8 km thick) comprises the Critical Zone, host to the Merensky Reef and UG2 chromitite layer, the principal platinum ores, and the Main Zone and Upper Zone above.

Mining significance

The Bushveld contains roughly 70% of the world's platinum reserves, plus major deposits of palladium, rhodium, chromium (from chromitite seams), vanadium (from magnetite layers), nickel, and copper. Mining centres such as Rustenburg and the eastern limb around Burgersfort have shaped South Africa's economy; the 2012 Marikana events occurred at a Bushveld platinum mine.
