Priestly breastplate

The priestly breastplate (Hebrew: חֹשֶׁן, ḥōšen), also called the breastplate of judgement, was a sacred breastplate worn by the High Priest of Israel, according to the Book of Exodus. It was a square cloth pouch set with twelve gemstones in four rows of three, each stone representing one of the twelve tribes of Israel, and it held the Urim and Thummim, objects used to divine the will of God.

The breastplate bore twelve stones for the twelve tribes of Israel.

Biblical description

Exodus 28 describes the breastplate as made of the same materials as the ephod — gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and fine linen — a span square when doubled. Four rows of three stones were mounted in gold filigree settings: the first row sardius (carnelian), topaz, and carbuncle; the second emerald, sapphire, and diamond; the third jacinth, agate, and amethyst; and the fourth beryl, onyx, and jasper. Each stone was engraved with the name of a tribe. The breastplate was attached to the ephod by gold chains and blue cords, and formed a pouch containing the Urim and Thummim.

Interpretation

The precise identification of the stones is uncertain, since the Hebrew names do not map unambiguously onto modern gem names. Later Jewish and Christian writers gave the breastplate rich symbolic readings: Josephus connected the stones with the months of the year and the signs of the zodiac, while rabbinic tradition held that the letters on the stones lit up to spell out answers when the High Priest consulted the Urim.
