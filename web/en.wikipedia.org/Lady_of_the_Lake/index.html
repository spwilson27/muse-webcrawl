Lady of the Lake

The Lady of the Lake (French: Dame du Lac, Breton: Itron al Lenn) is a title given to several related characters in Arthurian legend — enchantresses or fairy-like women who live in a lake realm. The Lady is best known for giving King Arthur his sword Excalibur, for raising the infant Lancelot after his father's death, and in some versions for imprisoning Merlin, whom she enchants or traps in a tree, tower or cave.

Different medieval works assign the role and the name variously — Viviane, Nimue, Ninianne, Elaine among them — and modern retellings often merge them into one figure. In Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur, one Lady of the Lake is beheaded by Sir Balin, while another, Nimue, becomes a major protector of Arthur's court. Scholars link the figure to Celtic water goddesses and sovereignty figures of Welsh and Breton tradition.
