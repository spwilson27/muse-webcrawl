Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre (CMLL)
Founded: September 21, 1933, Mexico City
Founder: Salvador Lutteroth
Formerly: Empresa Mexicana de Lucha Libre (EMLL)
Headquarters: Mexico City, Mexico

The Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre (CMLL; "World Council of Wrestling") is a professional wrestling (lucha libre) promotion based in Mexico City. Founded in 1933 by Salvador Lutteroth as the Empresa Mexicana de Lucha Libre (EMLL), it is the oldest professional wrestling promotion in the world that is still in operation.

History

Lutteroth, inspired by wrestling he had seen in Texas, staged his first show on September 21, 1933, widely regarded as the beginning of lucha libre in Mexico. The promotion built Arena México in 1956, the "cathedral of lucha libre", which hosts its flagship Friday night shows (Viernes Espectaculares). In 1991 the company adopted the CMLL name after leaving the National Wrestling Alliance. Its anniversary show, held each September, is the longest-running annual event in professional wrestling.

Style and championships

CMLL presents a more traditional, technically oriented style of lucha libre, emphasizing trios (six-man tag team) matches between técnicos (fan favourites) and rudos (villains), storied mask-versus-mask and hair-versus-hair wager matches (luchas de apuestas), and a large roster of championships across weight divisions. Legendary figures associated with the promotion include El Santo, Blue Demon, and Místico. CMLL has working relationships with promotions such as New Japan Pro-Wrestling and All Elite Wrestling, and co-promotes the annual Fantastica Mania tour in Japan.
