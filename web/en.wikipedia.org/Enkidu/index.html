Enkidu (Sumerian: 𒂖𒆠𒌋, Enkidu) is a legendary figure in ancient Mesopotamian mythology, the close friend and companion of Gilgamesh, king of Uruk, in the Epic of Gilgamesh. Created by the goddess Aruru from clay and saliva to be a counterweight to the tyrannical Gilgamesh, Enkidu begins as a wild man who lives with the animals of the steppe, eating grass and drinking at the watering places.

In the epic

Enkidu is civilised by the temple prostitute Shamhat, who teaches him to eat bread, drink beer and wear clothing; the animals then shun him. He journeys to Uruk, where he wrestles Gilgamesh at the door of the wedding house; the two become inseparable friends. Together they travel to the Cedar Forest and kill its guardian, the monster Humbaba, and later slay the Bull of Heaven sent by the goddess Ishtar. In retribution the gods decree that one of them must die: Enkidu falls ill and dies after twelve days, cursing and then blessing the door he made from the cedar. Gilgamesh's overwhelming grief at Enkidu's death, and his terror of his own mortality, drives the epic's second half — the quest for immortality.

Interpretation

Scholars read Enkidu as representing nature and the untamed world, contrasted with Gilgamesh's city and kingship, and as embodying the civilising pull of culture and the human confrontation with death. In earlier Sumerian poems he appears as Gilgamesh's servant rather than his equal, a role elevated in the Akkadian epic.
