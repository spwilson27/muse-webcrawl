Progressive Party (Iceland)

The Progressive Party (Icelandic: Framsóknarflokkurinn) is a centrist and agrarian political party in Iceland. Founded in 1916, it is one of the country's oldest surviving political parties and has participated in numerous coalition governments, historically drawing much of its support from farmers and rural voters.

History

The party was founded in 1916 from the merger of agrarian groups representing farmers' interests, and for much of the 20th century it was one of the two dominant parties in Icelandic politics alongside the Independence Party. One of its most prominent leaders, Hermann Jónasson, served five terms as prime minister. The party played a central role in the Icelandic cooperative movement and in founding institutions such as the national broadcasting service.

In the 21st century the party has alternated between government and opposition. Sigmundur Davíð Gunnlaugsson became prime minister in 2013 at the head of a coalition with the Independence Party; he resigned in 2016 amid the Panama Papers revelations, and Sigurður Ingi Jóhannsson succeeded him. The party later saw a split, with Sigmundur Davíð leaving to found the Centre Party in 2017. Under Lilja Dögg Alfreðsdóttir and later leadership, the Progressive Party returned to coalition government after the 2021 election together with the Independence Party and the Left-Green Movement.

Ideology

The Progressive Party describes itself as a liberal and reformist party. It has traditionally championed the agricultural sector, fisheries, decentralisation, and the interests of rural Iceland, while supporting a mixed economy and welfare state. Its position on European Union membership has been pragmatic and shifting, generally favouring cooperation without full membership.
