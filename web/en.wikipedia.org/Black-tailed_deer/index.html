Black-tailed deer

The black-tailed deer is a deer native to the western coast of North America, from California to southern Alaska. It is commonly classified as a subspecies of the mule deer (Odocoileus hemionus columbianus), though some authorities treat it as a separate species. Two forms are often recognised: the Columbian black-tailed deer and the Sitka deer of the coastal rainforest further north.

Description

Black-tailed deer take their name from the tail, which is black on the upper surface. They are smaller than most other mule deer, with bucks typically weighing 55–70 kg. Their coat is reddish-brown in summer and greyish in winter. Antlers, borne only by males, fork in the mule-deer fashion rather than branching from a single main beam.

Habitat and behaviour

They inhabit forest edges, chaparral, and coastal rainforest, browsing shrubs, forbs, and acorns, and grazing grasses. Black-tailed deer are largely crepuscular. Mountain lions and coyotes are their main predators. Where their range meets that of other mule deer inland, the two interbreed.
