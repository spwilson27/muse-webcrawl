The Plymouth Fury is an automobile manufactured by Plymouth, the entry-level division of Chrysler, produced from the 1956 to 1978 model years in its original run, with the name revived on later models until 1989. Introduced in 1955 as a sub-series of the Plymouth Belvedere, the Fury became a separate series in 1959 and was Plymouth's flagship full-size car through the 1960s and early 1970s, offered with Chrysler's largest V8 engines.

Early years

The first Fury (1956) was a limited-production performance hardtop finished in eggshell white with gold anodised trim, powered by a 303 cu in V8. The 1957 and 1958 models embodied Virgil Exner's "Forward Look" styling, with dramatic tailfins and dual headlamps. The 1958 Fury — offered only in buckskin beige — achieved lasting fame as the murderous automobile in Stephen King's 1983 novel Christine and John Carpenter's film adaptation, though the film's red-and-white cars were mostly Belvederes and Savoys dressed as Furys.

Full-size era

Through the 1960s the Fury was Plymouth's top full-size line, spanning Fury I, II, III, VIP and Sport Fury trims, with engines up to the 440 cu in (7.2 L) V8. A downsized, poorly received range for 1962–1964 — a response to a rumour that Chevrolet was shrinking its cars — was followed by a return to full size in 1965 on the Chrysler C-body platform. "Fuselage styling" arrived for 1969–1973. After 1974 the Fury name moved to the mid-size line (the full-size car becoming the Gran Fury), and the mid-size Fury continued until 1978. The nameplate last appeared on the Gran Fury sedan, widely used as a police car and taxi, until 1989.

Police and fleet use

Fury models were staples of American police fleets, particularly the high-performance Pursuit versions with heavy-duty suspension, brakes and cooling and 440 V8 engines. Together with its Dodge and Chrysler siblings, the Fury's silhouette became emblematic of the American highway patrol car of the 1970s.
