Saint Croix Island

LocationPassamaquoddy Bay, Maine, United States / New Brunswick, Canada
AreaAbout 6.5 acres (2.6 ha)
SignificanceSite of the 1604 French settlement by Pierre Dugua, Sieur de Mons

Saint Croix Island, at the mouth of the St. Croix River between Maine and New Brunswick, was the site of one of the earliest European settlements in North America north of Florida. In the winter of 1604–05 a French expedition led by Pierre Dugua, Sieur de Mons, with Samuel de Champlain among its members, wintered on the island.

The winter of 1604–05

Cut off by river ice, the settlers suffered severely from scurvy; 35 of the 79 men died. In spring the survivors moved to Port-Royal in present-day Nova Scotia. The island is now protected as Saint Croix Island International Historic Site, jointly commemorated by the United States and Canada.
