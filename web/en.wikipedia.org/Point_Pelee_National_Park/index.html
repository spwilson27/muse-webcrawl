Point Pelee National Park

National park of Canada
Location: Essex County, Ontario
Nearest town: Leamington
Area: about 15 km²
Established: 29 May 1918
Governing body: Parks Canada

Point Pelee National Park is a national park in Essex County in southwestern Ontario, Canada, where it extends into Lake Erie. It is the southernmost point of mainland Canada and consists of a peninsula of marsh and woodland tapering to a sharp point. Established in 1918 in large part at the urging of birders and hunters, Point Pelee was the first Canadian national park created for conservation purposes rather than scenery and recreation.

Ecology

The park lies on a major migratory flyway. Each spring it is famed for the arrival of songbirds crossing Lake Erie, attracting thousands of birdwatchers to the Festival of Birds; in autumn it is noted for migrating monarch butterflies gathering before crossing the lake. Its Carolinian forest, marshes and savanna support many species at the northern limit of their range, including the prickly pear cactus.

Designations

Point Pelee is a Ramsar wetland site of international importance and is part of a UNESCO biosphere reserve. Middle Island, a small island south of the point, was added to the park in 2000 and is the southernmost land in Canada.

Access

A visitor centre and shuttle serve the tip, which since 2018 has been accessible only on foot or by shuttle to protect the fragile spit. The park is one of the smallest national parks in Canada by area.
