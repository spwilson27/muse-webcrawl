Eccentricity vector

In celestial mechanics, the eccentricity vector of a Kepler orbit is a dimensionless vector pointing from the apoapsis to the periapsis (the point of closest approach) whose magnitude equals the orbit's scalar eccentricity. It is a constant of motion for the two-body problem.

Definition

The eccentricity vector can be computed from the position vector r, the velocity vector v, and the standard gravitational parameter μ as e = (v × h)/μ − r/|r|, where h is the specific angular momentum vector. It is closely related to the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector, which is the eccentricity vector scaled by factors involving the mass and gravitational parameter.

Significance

Together with the angular momentum vector, the eccentricity vector completely characterises the shape and orientation of an orbit in its plane. A magnitude of zero corresponds to a circular orbit, between zero and one to an ellipse, exactly one to a parabola, and greater than one to a hyperbola.
