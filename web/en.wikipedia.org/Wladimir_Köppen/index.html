Wladimir Peter Köppen (25 September 1846 – 22 June 1940) was a Russian-German geographer, meteorologist, climatologist, and botanist. He is best known for the Köppen climate classification system, first published in 1884 and refined over the following decades, which remains the most widely used system for classifying the world's climates.

Life and career

Köppen was born in St. Petersburg to German parents and studied natural sciences at the University of St. Petersburg, earning his doctorate at Leipzig in 1870 with work on plant growth and temperature. He served in the Russian meteorological service before moving to Germany, where from 1879 he worked at the Deutsche Seewarte (German Naval Observatory) in Hamburg, heading its division studying weather telegraphy and storm warnings. In 1919 he moved to Graz, Austria, where he spent his final years.

Climate classification

Köppen designed his classification so that climate zones corresponded to the distribution of natural vegetation, using thresholds of monthly temperature and precipitation expressed in letter codes. Later versions, developed with his student Rudolf Geiger, produced the Köppen–Geiger maps still used in textbooks and climate research. Köppen also collaborated with Alfred Wegener — his son-in-law — on The Climates of the Geological Past (1924), an early work in palaeoclimatology.
