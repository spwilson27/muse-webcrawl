Applied linguistics

Applied linguistics is an interdisciplinary field that identifies and investigates language-related problems in the real world and offers solutions. It draws on linguistics, psychology, education, sociology, anthropology, and computer science. Major branches include bilingualism and multilingualism, computer-assisted language learning, conversation analysis, corpus linguistics, forensic linguistics, language assessment, language policy and planning, literacy, second language acquisition, and translation.

A language classroom — language teaching is a central concern of applied linguistics

History

The field emerged in the 1940s and 1950s, largely around the scientific teaching of foreign languages, with figures such as Charles C. Fries applying structural linguistics to language pedagogy. The journal Language Learning (founded 1948) carried the subtitle "A Journal of Applied Linguistics." The British Association of Applied Linguistics (BAAL) was founded in 1967 and the American Association for Applied Linguistics (AAAL) in 1977; the international association AILA was founded in 1964. Since the 1970s the field has broadened from language teaching to encompass language in workplaces, clinics, courts, and media, and the role of language in social problems.

Scope

Applied linguists study how languages are learned and taught, how language tests can be made fair and valid, how multilingual societies can plan language policy, how communication breaks down in health care and law, and how language shapes identity and power. A recurring theme is mediation: the field brings linguistic theory into dialogue with practical problems rather than applying theory in a one-way fashion.
