Iona (Scottish Gaelic: Ì Chaluim Chille, meaning "Iona of Columba") is a small island in the Inner Hebrides off the Ross of Mull on the west coast of Scotland. It is famous as the cradle of Christianity in Scotland: in 563 St Columba arrived from Ireland with twelve companions and founded Iona Abbey, a monastery that became a centre of learning and missionary activity throughout Scotland and northern England.

History

From Iona, Columba's monks evangelised the Picts and founded daughter monasteries such as Lindisfarne. The island suffered repeated Viking raids from 795 onwards, and in 806 dozens of monks were killed. The illuminated manuscript known as the Book of Kells is believed to have been begun on Iona (or produced by Ionan monks) before being taken to Ireland. Iona became a royal burial place: tradition holds that dozens of kings of Scotland, Ireland and Norway were buried in the Reilig Odhráin, including Macbeth and, by legend, Duncan I.

The Abbey

A Benedictine abbey and Augustinian nunnery were founded around 1200. After the Reformation the buildings fell into ruin; the abbey church, largely dating from the 13th–16th centuries, was restored in the 20th century. In 1938 the Reverend George MacLeod founded the Iona Community, an ecumenical Christian community that rebuilt the abbey's domestic ranges and continues to run the site, a pilgrimage destination receiving tens of thousands of visitors yearly. Most of the island is owned by the National Trust for Scotland.

Geography

Iona lies about 1.6 km (1 mile) across the Sound of Iona from Fionnphort on Mull. It is about 5.6 km long and 1.6 km wide, with white shell-sand beaches such as the Strand of the Monks and the Bay at the Back of the Ocean. Its population is around 170, and the main village is Baile Mòr.
