Maguindanao

Maguindanao was a province of the Philippines located in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) on the island of Mindanao. Its name derives from the Maguindanaon people, the largest ethnic group of the region, and from danaw, "flooded", referring to the flood plains of the Pulangi (Rio Grande de Mindanao) basin.

History

The area was the heartland of the Sultanate of Maguindanao, founded in the early 16th century, which resisted Spanish colonisation longer than most of the archipelago. The modern province was created in 1973 from the former Cotabato province. In 2022, following a plebiscite, Maguindanao was divided into two provinces: Maguindanao del Norte and Maguindanao del Sur.

Geography and people

The province spanned the fertile Mindanao River basin around Cotabato City (which is geographically within but administratively independent of the area). The majority of residents are Muslim Maguindanaons, with Lumad and Christian settler communities also present. The 2009 Maguindanao massacre, in which 58 people were murdered, drew international attention.
