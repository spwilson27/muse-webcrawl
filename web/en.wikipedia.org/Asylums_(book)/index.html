Asylums (book)

Asylums: Essays on the Social Situation of Mental Patients and Other Inmates
Author: Erving Goffman
Published: 1961
Publisher: Anchor Books (Doubleday)
Field: Sociology

Asylums: Essays on the Social Situation of Mental Patients and Other Inmates is a 1961 collection of four essays by the sociologist Erving Goffman. Based on fieldwork conducted in the mid-1950s at St. Elizabeths Hospital, a large federal mental hospital in Washington, D.C., the book is a foundational ethnography of institutional life and a key text in the sociology of mental illness.

Contents

The first essay, "On the Characteristics of Total Institutions," develops Goffman's famous concept of the total institution: a place of residence and work where a large number of similarly situated people, cut off from wider society, lead an enclosed, formally administered life. Prisons, monasteries, boarding schools, ships, and mental hospitals are analyzed under one framework of barriers to social intercourse with the outside world, batch living, and the binary split between inmates and staff.

The remaining essays examine the "moral career" of the mental patient, the underlife of public institutions—the unofficial practices by which inmates preserve a sense of self—and the relationship between the medical model and institutional practice.

Influence

Asylums influenced debates on deinstitutionalization and psychiatric reform, and its concepts of total institutions and "mortification of the self" remain standard vocabulary in sociology, criminology, and organizational studies.
