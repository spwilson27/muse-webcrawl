The Spanish Tragedy

The Spanish Tragedy, or Hieronimo is Mad Again, is an Elizabethan play by Thomas Kyd, written between 1582 and 1592. It is the foundational work of the revenge tragedy genre that culminated in Shakespeare's Hamlet.

Plot

Framed by the ghost of Don Andrea and the figure of Revenge, the play follows Hieronimo, Knight Marshal of Spain, whose son Horatio is murdered by Lorenzo and Balthazar over the love of Bel-imperia. Denied justice, Hieronimo feigns madness and stages a play-within-the-play in which the killings are real; he then bites out his own tongue and kills himself.

Influence

Immensely popular in its day, the play established conventions — the Senecan ghost, madness real and feigned, the metatheatrical climax — that shaped English drama for decades. See also The Maias.
