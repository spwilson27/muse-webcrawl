Kathmandu

Kathmandu is the capital and largest city of Nepal, located in the Kathmandu Valley in the Himalayan foothills at about 1,400 m (4,600 ft) above sea level. With a metropolitan population of several million, it is the country's political, economic and cultural centre and the gateway for tourism and mountaineering in Nepal.

History

The Kathmandu Valley has been inhabited since ancient times and was ruled successively by the Licchavi and Malla dynasties, whose kings endowed the valley's cities with palaces, pagoda temples and Durbar Squares. In 1768 Prithvi Narayan Shah, king of Gorkha, conquered the valley and made Kathmandu the capital of the unified Kingdom of Nepal. The city remained the seat of the Shah monarchy and later of the Rana prime ministers until Nepal became a federal republic in 2008.

Heritage

Kathmandu's historic core is famed for its dense concentration of Hindu and Buddhist monuments: Kathmandu Durbar Square with the Hanuman Dhoka palace and the Kumari Ghar, home of the living goddess Kumari; the great stupas of Swayambhunath and Boudhanath; and the Pashupatinath Temple on the Bagmati River, one of the holiest Shiva shrines. Seven monument zones across the valley were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979.

2015 earthquake

On 25 April 2015 a magnitude 7.8 earthquake struck Nepal, killing nearly 9,000 people across the country and badly damaging many of Kathmandu's historic buildings, including the Dharahara tower, which collapsed. A long programme of reconstruction of temples and palaces has followed.

Modern city

Kathmandu is Nepal's main commercial hub, served by Tribhuvan International Airport, and the starting point for treks and expeditions to the Himalaya. The Thamel district is the centre of the tourist trade. Rapid growth has brought severe traffic congestion and air pollution, and the wider valley now holds the majority of Nepal's urban population.
