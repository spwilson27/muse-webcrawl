Hello Kitty

Hello Kitty (Japanese: ハロー・キティ), also known by her full name Kitty White, is a fictional character created by Japanese designer Yuko Shimizu and produced by the company Sanrio. Depicted as a white anthropomorphic cat-like girl with a red bow and no visible mouth, she debuted on a vinyl coin purse in 1975 and became one of the most successful media franchises and character brands in the world, central to the Japanese kawaii (cute) aesthetic. By 2014 the brand generated about US$8 billion a year in retail sales at its peak.

Character

Sanrio's official profile describes Kitty White as a cheerful third-grader living in London with her parents and twin sister Mimmy (who wears a yellow bow), fond of baking cookies and her mother's apple pie. Her height is given as "five apples" and her weight as "three apples". The absence of a mouth is a deliberate design feature: Sanrio explains that she "speaks from her heart", allowing people to project their own emotions onto her. A widely reported 2014 claim that "Hello Kitty is not a cat" reflected Sanrio's description of her as a little girl character — a personification in feline form.

History and growth

Shimizu designed the character in 1974 for Sanrio's expanding gift-goods business; the first product, a small vinyl change purse, went on sale in 1975. Designer Yuko Yamaguchi, who took over in 1980, has guided the brand's evolution for decades. Hello Kitty's popularity in Japan surged again in the 1990s as nostalgic adult consumers embraced her, and Sanrio deliberately licensed her across demographics and price points — from school supplies and plush toys to jewellery, electronics, an Airbus livery by EVA Air, and theme parks (Sanrio Puroland in Japan and Harmonyland). Animation includes the series Hello Kitty's Furry Tale Theater (1987) and later YouTube and CGI productions; in 2019 Warner Bros. announced a feature film project combining animation and live action.

Cultural impact

Hello Kitty is a global ambassador of Japanese popular culture: she has served as a UNICEF special ambassador for children, a special envoy for Japan's foreign ministry, and a mascot in the tourism campaign of the Japanese government; Japan appointed her a tourism ambassador to China and Hong Kong in 2008. Products bearing her image number in the tens of thousands, and the character has been the subject of exhibitions, academic study of kawaii culture, and countless collaborations with fashion brands and artists.
