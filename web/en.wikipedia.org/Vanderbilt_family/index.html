Vanderbilt family

The Vanderbilt family is an American family who gained prominence during the Gilded Age. Their success began with the shipping and railroad empires of Cornelius Vanderbilt, and expanded into various other areas, including philanthropy and high society. Descendants of the Vanderbilts have made significant contributions to architecture and the arts, and the family name is attached to Vanderbilt University in Nashville, Tennessee.

Cornelius Vanderbilt

Cornelius Vanderbilt (1794–1877), nicknamed "the Commodore", built his fortune first in steamboats and then in railroads, consolidating the New York Central Railroad and amassing one of the largest fortunes in American history. On his death he left an estate of roughly $100 million; he also endowed Vanderbilt University with a $1 million gift, then the largest charitable donation in American history.

The Gilded Age Vanderbilts

His son William Henry Vanderbilt (1821–1885) roughly doubled the fortune, while the next generation became famous for lavish mansions — including The Breakers in Newport, Rhode Island, and Biltmore House near Asheville, North Carolina, built by George Washington Vanderbilt II and still the largest privately owned house in the United States. The family's social rivalry with the Astors defined New York high society of the 1880s and 1890s.

Later generations

The fortune dispersed across many heirs, and by the mid-20th century no Vanderbilt remained among America's richest. Notable later descendants include the fashion designer Gloria Vanderbilt (1924–2019) and her son, the journalist and television anchor Anderson Cooper.
