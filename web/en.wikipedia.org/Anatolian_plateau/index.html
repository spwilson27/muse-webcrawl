Anatolian plateau

The Anatolian plateau is the high central plateau of Anatolia, the Asian part of Turkey. It occupies the interior of the peninsula between the Pontic Mountains to the north and the Taurus Mountains to the south, at an average elevation of around 1,000 metres above sea level.

Geography and climate

The plateau is a largely treeless steppe landscape of rolling plains, salt lakes such as Lake Tuz, and extinct volcanoes including Mount Erciyes and Mount Hasan, whose eruptions produced the tuff landscapes of Cappadocia. The climate is continental, with hot, dry summers and cold winters with snow, and annual rainfall generally between 300 and 500 millimetres.

History and economy

The plateau was home to some of the world's earliest farming settlements, including Çatalhöyük, and later formed the heartland of the Hittite Empire and successive Phrygian, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, Seljuk, and Ottoman states. The Turkish capital, Ankara, lies on its northwestern edge. Agriculture — wheat, barley, and sugar beet — and sheep and Angora goat herding are the mainstays of the rural economy.
