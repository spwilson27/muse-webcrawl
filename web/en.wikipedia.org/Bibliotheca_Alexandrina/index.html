The Bibliotheca Alexandrina (Latin, "Library of Alexandria"; Arabic: مكتبة الإسكندرية) is a major library and cultural centre on the shore of the Mediterranean in Alexandria, Egypt. Built near the site of the ancient Library of Alexandria — the most famous library of the ancient world, lost in antiquity — it commemorates that heritage while serving as a modern centre of learning, housing millions of books, museums, a planetarium and research institutes. It was inaugurated in 2002 after a long international effort led by Egypt and UNESCO.

History

The idea of reviving the ancient library emerged in the 1970s at the University of Alexandria. Egypt committed land on the corniche in the 1980s; an international architectural competition (1989), won by the young Norwegian firm Snøhetta, and fundraising conferences — notably at Aswan in 1990 — raised roughly US$200 million in international and Arab donations. Construction lasted from 1995 to 2001, and the library opened officially on 16 October 2002.

The building

The main building is a vast tilted disc, 160 metres in diameter, rising 33 metres above ground and sinking 33 metres below, evoking a rising sun (or a computer chip, in the architects' words). Its granite wall is carved with 4,200 characters from 120 human scripts. The main reading room — 33,625 square metres, terraced over seven levels beneath a glass-panelled roof — is among the largest in the world. The complex also contains a conference centre, specialised libraries (including one for the blind), four museums (Antiquities, Manuscripts, Sadat, and the History of Science), a planetarium science centre, and the Culturama, a panoramic cultural display.

Collections and activities

The library aims ultimately to hold up to eight million volumes; initial donations included some 500,000 books from France's Bibliothèque nationale. It hosts the only mirror site of the Internet Archive outside the United States (from 2002), digitisation projects, exhibitions, concerts and hundreds of events each year, and serves as a hub for Arabic scholarly and scientific networks such as the Academy of Sciences for the Developing World regional office.
