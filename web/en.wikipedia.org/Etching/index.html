Etching

Etching is traditionally the process of using strong acid or mordant to cut into the unprotected parts of a metal surface to create a design in intaglio (incised) in the metal. In modern manufacturing, other chemicals may be used on other types of material. As a method of printmaking, it is, along with engraving, the most important technique for old master prints, and remains in wide use today. In a number of modern variants such as microfabrication etching and photochemical milling, it is a crucial technique in much modern technology, including circuit boards.

Process

In pure etching, a metal plate (usually copper, zinc or steel) is covered with a waxy ground which is resistant to acid. The artist then scratches off the ground with a pointed etching needle where the lines of the image are desired, exposing the bare metal. The plate is then dipped in a bath of acid, technically called the mordant (French for "biting") or etchant, or has acid washed over it. The acid "bites" into the metal where it is exposed, leaving lines sunk into the plate. The remaining ground is then cleaned off, the plate is inked, and the ink held in the incised lines is transferred to paper under pressure in a printing press.

History

Etching originated in armour decoration in the late Middle Ages and was adapted to printmaking around 1500; Daniel Hopfer is often credited with the earliest dated etchings. Albrecht Dürer made a handful of etchings on iron, but the technique flowered in the 17th century with Jacques Callot and above all Rembrandt van Rijn, whose roughly 300 etchings are considered pinnacles of the art. Later masters include Francisco Goya, whose series Los Caprichos and The Disasters of War exploited aquatint alongside line etching.

Variants

Related intaglio processes include aquatint (which produces tonal areas), soft-ground etching, spit bite, lift-ground (sugar-lift) etching, and photogravure. Non-printmaking applications range from industrial chemical milling to the etching step in semiconductor fabrication.
