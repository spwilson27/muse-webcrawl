Sports-related curses

A sports-related curse is a superstitious belief that a sports team, athlete or organisation is doomed to fail because of a supposed curse placed upon them through words or deeds. Such beliefs are often invoked to explain long championship droughts or repeated failures in decisive moments.

Examples

Famous examples include the Curse of the Bambino, said to afflict the Boston Red Sox after they sold Babe Ruth to the New York Yankees in 1919, until their 2004 World Series victory; the Curse of the Billy Goat, associated with the Chicago Cubs from 1945 until their 2016 World Series win; and the Madden curse, a superstition that players featured on the cover of the Madden NFL video game series suffer injury or decline.

Analysis

Commentators generally treat curses as narrative explanations of randomness, regression to the mean, and mismanagement, rather than genuine causes of failure.
