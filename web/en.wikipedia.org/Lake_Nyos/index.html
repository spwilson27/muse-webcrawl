Lake Nyos

Lake Nyos is a crater lake in the Northwest Region of Cameroon, located about 322 km (200 mi) northwest of the capital, Yaoundé. It is a deep lake set within an inactive volcano in the Oku volcanic plain along the Cameroon line of volcanic activity. Nyos is best known for the limnic eruption of 21 August 1986, one of the deadliest natural disasters of its kind ever recorded.

Geography and geology

The lake occupies a maar formed by an explosive volcanic eruption roughly 400 years ago. Its basin is held back on one side by a natural dam of volcanic rock about 40 m high. Beneath the lake, a pocket of magma leaks carbon dioxide (CO2) into the water, where it accumulates in the deep, unmixed bottom layers under high pressure, making Nyos one of only a handful of lakes known to be saturated with CO2 in this way. The others are Lake Monoun, also in Cameroon, and Lake Kivu on the border of Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

The 1986 disaster

On 21 August 1986, a limnic eruption — sometimes called a "lake overturn" — suddenly released a huge cloud of CO2, estimated at between 100,000 and 300,000 tonnes of gas (some estimates run higher). The dense, ground-hugging cloud flowed down surrounding valleys at speeds of up to 100 km/h, asphyxiating approximately 1,746 people and some 3,500 livestock in the villages of Nyos, Kam, Cha, and Subum. See Lake Nyos disaster.

Degassing

To prevent a recurrence, scientists installed self-siphoning degassing pipes in the lake beginning in 2001, drawing CO2-rich water from the depths and releasing the gas safely into the air. Additional pipes were added in 2011. Monitoring continues because the natural dam is gradually eroding; its failure could both flood downstream villages and trigger another gas release.
