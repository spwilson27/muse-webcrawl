The Wallace (poem)

The Wallace

Author: Blind Harry

Full title: The Actes and Deidis of the Illustre and Vallyeant Campioun Schir William Wallace

Date: c. 1477

Language: Middle Scots

The Wallace, originally titled The Actes and Deidis of the Illustre and Vallyeant Campioun Schir William Wallace, is a long narrative poem in Middle Scots by the fifteenth-century poet known as Blind Harry (Harry the Minstrel), composed around 1477. It recounts, in some 11,800 lines, the life of the Scottish hero William Wallace.

Content

The poem follows Wallace from his youth through his rising against English occupation, his victory at the Battle of Stirling Bridge (1297), his defeat at Falkirk, and his betrayal, capture and execution in London in 1305. It magnifies Wallace into an epic national hero and bitterly portrays Edward I and the English as oppressors.

Influence

For centuries the poem was one of Scotland's most popular books, shaping the national image of Wallace far beyond the sparse historical record. William Wordsworth and Robert Burns admired it; its episodes informed the 1995 film Braveheart, although the film diverges greatly from both poem and history.
