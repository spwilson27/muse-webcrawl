Blepharoplasty

Blepharoplasty (from Greek blepharon, "eyelid", and plassein, "to form") is the plastic surgery operation for correcting defects, deformities, and disfigurations of the eyelids, and for aesthetically modifying the eye region of the face. With the excision and the removal, or the repositioning (or both) of excess tissues, such as skin and adipocyte fat, and the reinforcement of the corresponding muscle and tendon tissues, the blepharoplasty procedure resolves functional and cosmetic problems of the periorbita, which is the area from the eyebrow to the upper portion of the cheek.

Indications

Upper blepharoplasty addresses dermatochalasis — redundant, drooping eyelid skin that can obstruct the visual field — and protruding orbital fat. Lower blepharoplasty is most often cosmetic, treating under-eye bags and excess skin. Double eyelid surgery, common in East Asia, is a form of blepharoplasty that creates a supratarsal crease.

Procedure

The operation is usually performed under local anaesthesia with sedation, or general anaesthesia, on an outpatient basis. Incisions are placed in the natural eyelid crease (upper) or just below the lash line or inside the eyelid (transconjunctival, lower). Excess skin, muscle, and fat are removed or repositioned and the incisions closed with fine sutures.

Risks

Possible complications include haematoma, infection, dry eyes, ectropion, asymmetry, scarring, and in rare cases retrobulbar haemorrhage causing vision loss. Recovery typically takes one to two weeks for visible bruising and swelling to subside.
