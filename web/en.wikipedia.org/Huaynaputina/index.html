Huaynaputina (Quechua for "new volcano") is a stratovolcano in the Moquegua Region of southern Peru. It is best known for its catastrophic eruption on 19 February 1600, the largest volcanic eruption in South America in recorded history and one of the largest of the last several centuries worldwide.

The 1600 eruption

The eruption, rated VEI 6, followed days of earthquakes. It ejected an enormous volume of ash and pumice, destroyed numerous villages around the volcano, and killed roughly 1,500 people in the immediate region. Ash fell as far away as Lima, La Paz, and Arica, and buried settlements under metres of debris. The abandoned village of Calicanto and other ruins remain preserved beneath the ash.

Global climate effects

The sulfur gases injected into the stratosphere formed a veil that cooled the planet. The year 1601 was among the coldest of the last six centuries in the Northern Hemisphere. Tree-ring and documentary records link the eruption to bitter winters, failed harvests, and famine in Russia during the Time of Troubles, as well as cold, wet summers in Europe and poor harvests in China and Japan.

Geology

Huaynaputina lacks the classic cone profile of a stratovolcano; its vents lie within a broad amphitheatre opening to the west. The volcano forms part of the Central Volcanic Zone of the Andes, produced by subduction of the Nazca Plate beneath South America. Small ash emissions have occurred historically, but no eruption comparable to 1600 has recurred.
