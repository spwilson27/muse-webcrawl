London Borough of Southwark

Southwark's riverside skyline

The London Borough of Southwark is a London borough in southeast London, England. It forms part of Inner London and is connected by bridges across the River Thames to the City of London. It was formed in 1965 by the merger of the metropolitan boroughs of Southwark, Camberwell and Bermondsey. The population is approximately 300,000.

History

Southwark, at the southern end of London Bridge, is one of the oldest parts of London: it was the site of Roman and medieval inns serving travellers, and in Shakespeare's time the Bankside area — outside the City's jurisdiction — hosted theatres such as the Globe, as well as bear-baiting pits and prisons including the Clink and the Marshalsea (immortalised in Dickens's Little Dorrit). Bermondsey developed around its abbey and later became the centre of the leather trade; Camberwell was a village suburb until the 19th century.

Landmarks

The borough contains Southwark Cathedral, Borough Market, Tate Modern, Shakespeare's Globe, the Imperial War Museum, HMS Belfast, and The Shard, the tallest building in the United Kingdom. Major districts include Bankside, Peckham, Dulwich, Bermondsey, Walworth and Elephant and Castle. The local authority is Southwark London Borough Council.
