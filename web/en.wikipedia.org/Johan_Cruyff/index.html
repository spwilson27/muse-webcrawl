Johan Cruyff

Hendrik Johannes Cruijff (25 April 1947 – 24 March 2016), known as Johan Cruyff, was a Dutch professional football player and manager. Regarded as one of the greatest players of all time and a pivotal figure in the history of the sport, he was the leading exponent of Total Football under coach Rinus Michels and a three-time winner of the Ballon d'Or (1971, 1973, 1974).


Cruyff with the Netherlands

Playing career

With Ajax, Cruyff won six Eredivisie titles and three consecutive European Cups (1971–1973). In 1973 he moved to Barcelona for a world-record fee, winning La Liga in his first season. He later played for Feyenoord, winning another league title. For the Netherlands he earned 48 caps and captained the side to the 1974 World Cup final. He is famous for the "Cruyff turn" and the "phantom goal" against Brazil, as well as the leaping backheel goal for Ajax.

Managerial career

As manager of Ajax (1985–1988) and then Barcelona (1988–1996), Cruyff built Barça's "Dream Team," winning four consecutive La Liga titles and the club's first European Cup in 1992. His philosophy of positional play, youth development through La Masia, and the 3-4-3 formation profoundly shaped modern football and influenced managers such as Pep Guardiola.

Legacy

After his death from lung cancer in 2016, Barcelona's stadium for its B team and numerous streets and awards were named in his honour; Ajax renamed its stadium the Johan Cruijff ArenA in 2018.
