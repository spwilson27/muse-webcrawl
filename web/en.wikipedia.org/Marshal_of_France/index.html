Marshal of France

Marshal of France (French: Maréchal de France) is a distinction in the French Army, and the highest military honour of France. It is not a rank but a dignity conferred by the state for exceptional military service, traditionally symbolised by a baton — dark blue, decorated with gold stars under the Republic (and with fleurs-de-lis under the monarchy). The title dates from the Middle Ages; the marshal's baton and the motto Terror belli, decus pacis ("terror in war, ornament in peace") embody its prestige.

History

The office grew from the maréchal, originally the king's master of the horse, who by the 13th century had become a senior military commander second to the Constable of France. When the constableship was abolished in 1627, the marshals became France's highest commanders. Under Louis XIV their number proliferated; celebrated marshals of the age include Turenne — one of only six men raised to the still higher dignity of Marshal General of France — Vauban, Villars and Luxembourg.

Napoleon revived the dignity in 1804 as "Marshal of the Empire", appointing eighteen marshals including Ney, Murat, Davout, Lannes, Masséna, Soult and Bernadotte (later king of Sweden). The title was restored intermittently through the 19th century, and in the First World War marshals Joffre, Foch, Pétain, Lyautey, Franchet d'Espèrey, Fayolle and Maunoury received the baton; Foch also held equivalent British and Polish honours.

Modern period

Four marshals were created after the Second World War: Jean de Lattre de Tassigny, Philippe Leclerc de Hauteclocque (both posthumous in 1952), Alphonse Juin and Marie-Pierre Kœnig (posthumously, 1984). The last living marshal, Juin, died in 1967; since then no one has held the dignity, and the office is regarded as dormant rather than abolished — it can be conferred in wartime by decree. In total, roughly 330 men have been Marshals of France across the centuries. The dignity of Marshal General of France (Maréchal général des camps et armées du roi) was awarded only six times in history, most recently to the marquis de Villars in 1733.
