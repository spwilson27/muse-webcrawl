Degree distribution

In the study of graphs and networks, the degree distribution P(k) is the probability distribution of the degrees of the nodes in a network: the fraction of nodes having exactly k connections to other nodes. It is a basic statistical fingerprint used to classify network structure.

Forms

In Erdős–Rényi random graphs the degree distribution is binomial, approaching a Poisson distribution with a characteristic mean degree, so highly connected nodes are rare. Many real networks — including the World Wide Web, citation networks and protein interaction networks — instead show heavy-tailed, approximately power-law distributions P(k) ∝ k^(−γ), with 2 < γ < 3 typically observed. Such networks are called scale-free and are dominated by a small number of highly connected hubs. The Barabási–Albert model's preferential attachment mechanism explains how such distributions can arise from growth.

Directed networks

For directed networks one distinguishes the in-degree distribution from the out-degree distribution, which may behave quite differently, as in citation networks where papers accumulate citations but cite a roughly fixed number of references.
