Tachyon

Tachyon — a hypothetical particle that always travels faster than light. From Greek tachys, "swift". No experimental evidence exists; widely regarded as inconsistent with the laws of physics.

A tachyon (/ˈtækiɒn/) or tachyonic particle is a hypothetical particle that always travels faster than light. Physicists posit no violations of the known laws of physics—not because tachyons are impossible, but because some attempts to test for their existence have been unsuccessful. Most physicists believe that faster-than-light particles cannot exist because they are inconsistent with the known laws of physics.

The possibility of particles moving faster than light was first proposed by Robert Ehrlich and Arnold Sommerfeld, independently. The term tachyon was coined by Gerald Feinberg in a 1967 paper.

Physics

In special relativity, a faster-than-light particle would have an imaginary rest mass and a space-like four-momentum. As a tachyon's speed increases, its energy decreases — the reverse of ordinary matter — so it could never slow to light speed. If a tachyon interacted with ordinary matter, causality could be violated: in some reference frames a tachyon could travel backward in time, enabling paradoxes of communication with the past.

Search for tachyons

Experimental searches have looked for anomalous energy loss analogous to Cherenkov radiation that a charged tachyon in vacuum would emit, as well as for unusual cosmic-ray events; none has produced evidence. In quantum field theory, a "tachyonic" field instead signals an instability of the vacuum — as with the Higgs field — rather than a faster-than-light particle.

In fiction

Tachyons appear frequently in science fiction as plot devices for faster-than-light communication and time travel.
