James Simon Wallis Hunt (29 August 1947 – 15 June 1993) was a British racing driver who won the Formula One World Championship in 1976. Renowned for his speed, charisma and flamboyant lifestyle — he was nicknamed "Hunt the Shunt" early in his career for his crash-prone driving — he later became a popular television commentator alongside Murray Walker for the BBC.

Early career

Hunt was born in Belmont, Surrey, the son of a stockbroker, and educated at Wellington College. Against his parents' hopes that he would become a doctor, he took up racing in Minis and Formula Ford, progressing through Formula Three with a reputation for both daring and accidents. His break came with the eccentric Hesketh Racing team, which entered Formula One in 1973; Hunt gave the team its only victory at the 1975 Dutch Grand Prix.

1976 championship

When Hesketh folded, Hunt joined McLaren for 1976. That season became one of the most dramatic in the sport's history, defined by his rivalry with Ferrari's Niki Lauda. Lauda built a commanding lead before his near-fatal fiery crash at the Nürburgring; he returned to racing only weeks later. Amid disqualifications, appeals and rain, the title was decided at the final race in Fuji, Japan, where Lauda withdrew in torrential rain on safety grounds and Hunt finished third to win the championship by a single point. Their rivalry was dramatised in the 2013 film Rush.

Later career and commentary

Hunt won three more races in 1977 but McLaren's competitiveness faded; after an unhappy spell with Wolf Racing he retired mid-way through 1979. He became a familiar voice on BBC Grand Prix coverage from 1980, his blunt, expert commentary — in partnership with Murray Walker — winning a wide audience. Hunt died of a heart attack at his home in Wimbledon in 1993, aged 45.
