Exxon Valdez oil spill

The Exxon Valdez oil spill occurred in Prince William Sound, Alaska, on 24 March 1989, when the oil tanker Exxon Valdez, bound for California, struck Bligh Reef and spilled about 10.8 million US gallons (roughly 257,000 barrels, or 37,000 tonnes) of crude oil. It was the largest oil spill in United States waters until the Deepwater Horizon spill of 2010 and remains one of the most devastating human-caused environmental disasters.

The grounding

The tanker had departed the Valdez Marine Terminal loaded with oil from the Trans-Alaska Pipeline. To avoid icebergs from the Columbia Glacier it left the normal shipping lane with permission from the Coast Guard, but failed to return in time; the ship ran aground shortly after midnight. Investigations found that the captain, Joseph Hazelwood, had been drinking and was below deck, the third mate at the helm was inexperienced, the crew was fatigued and under strength, and the ship's collision-avoidance radar was broken and switched off. Exxon was criticised for inadequate crewing and supervision.

Impact and cleanup

The oil spread over some 1,300 miles of coastline. Hundreds of thousands of seabirds, thousands of sea otters, hundreds of seals and bald eagles, and billions of salmon and herring eggs were killed, and the sound's herring fishery collapsed in 1993 and never fully recovered. Cleanup used mechanical recovery, dispersants, hot-water washing of beaches — later found to cause its own ecological damage — and bioremediation; some oil remains in sediments decades later.

Legal and regulatory aftermath

Exxon paid billions in cleanup costs and settlements. A 1994 jury awarded $5 billion in punitive damages, a figure eventually reduced by the U.S. Supreme Court in Exxon Shipping Co. v. Baker (2008) to about $507 million, setting a roughly 1:1 ratio between punitive and compensatory damages in maritime cases. The disaster prompted the Oil Pollution Act of 1990, which required double hulls on tankers in U.S. waters and created the Prince William Sound Regional Citizens' Advisory Council.
