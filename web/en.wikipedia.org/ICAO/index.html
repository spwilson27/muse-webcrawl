International Civil Aviation Organization

ICAO
Formation: 4 April 1947
Headquarters: Montreal, Canada
Type: UN specialised agency
Membership: 193 member states

The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) is a specialised agency of the United Nations that coordinates the principles and techniques of international air navigation and fosters the planning and development of international air transport to ensure safe and orderly growth.

History

ICAO was established by the Convention on International Civil Aviation, signed in Chicago in December 1944, and came into being in 1947 when the convention entered into force. Its headquarters are in the Quartier international of Montreal, Canada, with regional offices around the world.

Standards

The organisation adopts Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs), published as annexes to the Chicago Convention, covering personnel licensing, rules of the air, aeronautical charts, aircraft operations, airworthiness, accident investigation, and environmental protection, among other subjects. ICAO also assigns the four-letter airport location codes and three-letter airline designators used throughout aviation, and maintains the machine-readable travel document standards used in passports.

Governance

ICAO's Assembly, comprising all member states, meets at least once every three years and elects a 36-member Council that directs the work of the organisation between sessions. See also IATA and Chicago Convention.
