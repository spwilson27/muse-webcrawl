Tokyo (Japanese: 東京, Tōkyō), officially the Tokyo Metropolis (東京都, Tōkyō-to), is the capital and most populous city of Japan. With a population of over 14 million residents within the city proper as of 2023, it is the most populous metropolitan area in the world, being home to approximately 37.4 million people in the Greater Tokyo Area.

Located at the head of Tokyo Bay, the prefecture forms part of the Kantō region on the central coast of Honshu, Japan's largest island. Tokyo serves as Japan's economic center and the seat of both the Japanese government and the Emperor of Japan.

History

Originally a small fishing village named Edo (江戸), Tokyo became a prominent political center in 1603 when it became the seat of the Tokugawa shogunate. By the mid-18th century, Edo was one of the most populous cities in the world at over one million. Following the Meiji Restoration of 1868, the imperial capital and political center were moved from Kyoto to Edo, which was renamed "Tokyo" (lit. 'Eastern Capital'). The city was devastated by the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake and again by Allied air raids during the Second World War, after which it was rebuilt.

Geography

The mainland portion of Tokyo lies northwest of Tokyo Bay and measures about 90 km east to west and 25 km north to south. The metropolis also administers two island chains in the Pacific Ocean: the Izu Islands and the Ogasawara Islands, extending the prefecture's territory far to the south. Mainland Tokyo is divided into the 23 special wards (the urban core), the Tama area of cities to the west, and mountainous rural areas.

Economy

Tokyo has the largest metropolitan economy in the world. It is a major international finance center, home to the Tokyo Stock Exchange, and hosts the headquarters of numerous multinational corporations, investment banks, and insurance companies. Transportation, particularly public rail transit, forms a central part of the city's economy and daily life.

Culture

Tokyo is home to many museums, including the Tokyo National Museum in Ueno Park; theaters; and internationally noted cuisine, with more Michelin-starred restaurants than any other city. Landmarks include the Tokyo Skytree, Tokyo Tower, the Imperial Palace, Sensō-ji temple in Asakusa, and the Shibuya Crossing. Tokyo hosted the Summer Olympic Games in 1964 and 2020 (held in 2021).
