Family planning

Family planning is the consideration of the number of children a person wishes to have, including the choice to have no children, and the age at which to have them. Things that may play a role include marital situation, career or work considerations, and financial situations.

Methods

Family planning services include contraception (such as hormonal methods, intrauterine devices, barrier methods, and sterilisation), fertility awareness, preconception counselling, and management of infertility. The World Health Organization estimates that use of modern contraception prevented hundreds of millions of unintended pregnancies and significantly reduces maternal mortality.

Global context

The United Nations Population Fund states that access to safe, voluntary family planning is a human right and is central to gender equality and poverty reduction. Programmes differ widely by country, shaped by policy, religion, and culture; international conferences such as the 1994 Cairo International Conference on Population and Development reframed family planning around reproductive health and rights.
