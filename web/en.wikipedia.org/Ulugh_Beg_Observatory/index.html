Ulugh Beg Observatory

The Ulugh Beg Observatory is an observatory in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, built in the 1420s by the Timurid prince and astronomer Ulugh Beg. It was one of the finest observatories in the Islamic world and a leading centre of astronomy in the medieval period.

The three-storey cylindrical building, some 46 metres in diameter, housed a giant marble sextant (a meridian arc) with a radius of about 36 metres, set in a trench aligned with the meridian. With instruments of this scale, Ulugh Beg and his astronomers—including Jamshid al-Kashi and Ali Qushji—measured the positions of 1,018 stars, compiled in the Zij-i Sultani (1437), determined the length of the sidereal year to within about a minute of the modern value, and calculated the tilt of the Earth's axis with remarkable precision.

After Ulugh Beg's assassination in 1449, the observatory was destroyed and its remains buried. Its buried sextant arc was excavated in 1908 by the Russian archaeologist Vasily Vyatkin, and a museum now stands at the site.
