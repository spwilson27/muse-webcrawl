Eigenvalue algorithm

In numerical analysis, an eigenvalue algorithm is a method for computing the eigenvalues and, usually, the eigenvectors of a matrix. Because of the Abel–Ruffini theorem, eigenvalues cannot in general be found by a finite direct formula for matrices larger than 4×4, so eigenvalue algorithms are inherently iterative.

Methods

Important algorithms include the power iteration (finding the dominant eigenvalue), inverse iteration and Rayleigh quotient iteration for individual eigenpairs, and the QR algorithm — developed by John Francis and Vera Kublanovskaya around 1961 — which iteratively applies QR decompositions with shifts to converge to a triangular (Schur) form, and remains the standard method for dense nonsymmetric eigenproblems. For large sparse matrices, Krylov-subspace methods such as Lanczos iteration (symmetric) and Arnoldi iteration (general) are used; the Jacobi eigenvalue algorithm handles symmetric matrices via successive rotations.

Applications

Eigenvalue computations are central to vibration analysis, stability theory, quantum mechanics, principal component analysis, Google's PageRank (the dominant eigenvector of a link matrix), and many other fields. Software implementations are provided by LAPACK (routines such as DGEEV and DSYEV).
