Patriarch Kirill (Russian: Патриарх Кирилл, secular name Vladimir Mikhailovich Gundyayev, born 20 November 1946) is a Russian Orthodox bishop who serves as the Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus', the primate of the Russian Orthodox Church, since 1 February 2009.

Early life and career

Kirill was born in Leningrad into a priest's family. He entered the Leningrad Theological Seminary in 1965, was tonsured a monk in 1969 and ordained priest the same year. He represented the Moscow Patriarchate at the World Council of Churches in Geneva (1971–74) before becoming rector of the Leningrad Theological Academy. In 1984 he was appointed Archbishop of Smolensk and Vyazma, and from 1989 he chaired the Department for External Church Relations, the church's "foreign ministry", for two decades.

Patriarchate

After the death of Patriarch Alexy II in December 2008, Kirill served as locum tenens and was elected the 16th Patriarch by a Local Council on 27 January 2009. His patriarchate has been marked by a close alliance with the Russian state under Vladimir Putin, an expansion of church construction and media presence, and a conservative social teaching. In 2016 he met Pope Francis in Havana — the first meeting between a pope and a Russian patriarch. His support for Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, which he framed in spiritual terms, drew condemnation from other churches and contributed to a deepening schism in world Orthodoxy following the 2018 grant of autocephaly to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine.
