Pasadena is a city in Los Angeles County, California, United States, 11 miles (18 km) northeast of downtown Los Angeles. It is the most populous city and the primary cultural center of the San Gabriel Valley. The city is known for hosting the annual Rose Bowl football game and the Tournament of Roses Parade.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Tongva (Gabrieleño) people. Pasadena was founded in 1874 by settlers from Indiana organized as the Indiana Colony, and incorporated in 1886. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries it became a famed winter resort for wealthy Easterners, with grand hotels and mansions along Orange Grove Boulevard ("Millionaires' Row"). The city's name derives from a Chippewa word meaning "crown of the valley".

Culture and landmarks

Pasadena is home to the California Institute of Technology (Caltech), the Norton Simon Museum, the Huntington Library (nearby in San Marino), Art Center College of Design, and NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (administered by Caltech, in nearby La Cañada Flintridge). The Rose Bowl stadium, built in 1922, hosts the annual Rose Bowl Game. Old Town Pasadena is a noted shopping and dining district of restored historic buildings.

Arts and Crafts heritage

Pasadena was a center of the American Arts and Crafts movement; the Gamble House (1908), designed by Charles and Henry Greene, is one of the finest examples of the style. The city retains one of the largest concentrations of Craftsman bungalows in the United States.

Geography and climate

Pasadena lies at the foot of the San Gabriel Mountains, at an elevation of about 860 feet (263 m). It has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. The Arroyo Seco, a seasonal river and flood channel, runs along its western edge.

Education

Besides Caltech, institutions include Pasadena City College, Fuller Theological Seminary, and Art Center College of Design. The Pasadena Unified School District serves the city's public schools.
