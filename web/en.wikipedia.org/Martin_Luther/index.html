Martin Luther (10 November 1483 – 18 February 1546) was a German priest, theologian, author, hymnwriter, and professor. A former Augustinian friar, he became the central figure of the Protestant Reformation, whose teachings inspired the Lutheran tradition and reshaped Western Christianity and European history.

Early life and the indulgence controversy

Luther entered the Augustinian monastery at Erfurt in 1505 after vowing to St. Anne during a thunderstorm, abandoned a legal career, and was ordained in 1507. He earned a doctorate in theology and taught at the University of Wittenberg. Troubled by the sale of indulgences — payments promising remission of punishment for sin — Luther composed his Ninety-five Theses in 1517, questioning the practice and the pope's authority over purgatory. Tradition holds he posted them on the door of All Saints' Church, Wittenberg; printed copies spread rapidly across Germany.

Excommunication and the Diet of Worms

Summoned by Cardinal Cajetan at Augsburg in 1518 and debated by Johann Eck at Leipzig in 1519, Luther moved toward the positions of justification by faith alone and the primacy of Scripture. Pope Leo X excommunicated him in January 1521. At the Diet of Worms that April, Luther refused to recant his writings before Emperor Charles V, declaring — in the traditional account — "Here I stand, I can do no other." Declared an outlaw by the Edict of Worms, he was hidden by Frederick the Wise in the Wartburg Castle, where he translated the New Testament into German.

Later ministry

Returning to Wittenberg in 1522, Luther organised a reformed church, married former nun Katharina von Bora in 1525, opposed the radical reformers and the rebels of the German Peasants' War, and completed his translation of the full Bible (1534), a landmark of German literature. His later writings include harsh polemics against the papacy, Anabaptists, and — notoriously — the Jews, in On the Jews and Their Lies (1543), a work whose legacy remains a subject of condemnation and study. Luther died in Eisleben in 1546.

Influence

Luther's translation of the Bible into vernacular German, his hymns such as "A Mighty Fortress Is Our God," and his catechisms shaped Protestant worship and education. Lutheranism spread through northern Germany and Scandinavia, and the Reformation he began permanently divided Western Christendom.
