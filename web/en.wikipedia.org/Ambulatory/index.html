Ambulatory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ambulatory (Latin: ambulatorium, "walking place") is the covered passage around a cloister or the processional way around the east end of a cathedral or large church and behind the high altar. The term is also used more generally for a covered walkway in architecture, and in medicine as an adjective describing patients who are able to walk and care delivered on an outpatient basis.

Architecture

In ecclesiastical architecture, the ambulatory is the continuation of the side aisles around the apse or chancel of a church, forming a semicircular or polygonal passageway that allows processions and pilgrims to circulate behind the high altar without disturbing services. Radiating chapels often open off the ambulatory, a feature characteristic of French Gothic pilgrimage churches such as the Basilica of Saint-Denis and Chartres Cathedral, where pilgrims could venerate relics displayed in the chapels.

The ambulatory first became a standard feature in the great Romanesque pilgrimage churches along the routes to Santiago de Compostela, including Saint-Sernin at Toulouse and Santiago de Compostela Cathedral itself. In the Gothic period the ambulatory was often double-aisled and integrated with the chevet.

Other uses

  • Ambulatory care — medical care provided on an outpatient basis, including diagnosis, observation, consultation, treatment, and rehabilitation services.
  • Ambulatory patient — a patient who is able to walk and is not bedridden.
  • Ambulatory (mining and law) — in legal usage, describing something that is movable or subject to change, as in an "ambulatory" boundary following a shifting river channel.
