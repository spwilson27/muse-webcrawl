Hyman G. Rickover

Hyman George Rickover
Born: January 27, 1900, Maków Mazowiecki, Russian Poland
Died: July 8, 1986, Arlington, Virginia
Service: United States Navy
Rank: Admiral
Known for: Father of the nuclear navy

Hyman George Rickover (January 27, 1900 – July 8, 1986) was an admiral in the United States Navy, widely known as the "Father of the Nuclear Navy". He directed the original development of naval nuclear propulsion and controlled its operations for three decades as director of the Naval Reactors office.

Early life

Rickover was born to Jewish parents in Maków Mazowiecki, then part of the Russian Empire, and emigrated to the United States with his family as a child, settling in Chicago. He worked his way through school and won an appointment to the United States Naval Academy, graduating in 1922.

Nuclear propulsion

After wartime service and graduate study in electrical engineering, Rickover became convinced that nuclear power could transform submarine warfare by freeing boats from the need to surface for air. Assigned to the Bureau of Ships in 1947, he pushed through the development of the pressurised-water reactor that powered USS Nautilus, launched in 1954 as the world's first nuclear-powered submarine. His organisation simultaneously developed the Shippingport Atomic Power Station, the first commercial nuclear plant in the United States.

Management style

Rickover was famous for exacting standards, abrasive interviews of prospective officers, and an uncompromising insistence on safety and technical rigour. The training and culture he imposed were credited with the US nuclear fleet's strong safety record. See also Nuclear propulsion and USS Nautilus.
