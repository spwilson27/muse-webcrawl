ATSC 3.0

ATSC 3.0
Also known as: NextGen TV
Developer: ATSC (Advanced Television Systems Committee)
Standardized: 2016–2018
Predecessor: ATSC 1.0

ATSC 3.0 is a major version of the ATSC standards for terrestrial television broadcasting created by the Advanced Television Systems Committee. Marketed to consumers as NextGen TV, it is the world's first IP-based broadcast television standard, combining over-the-air broadcasting with broadband Internet content. The first major deployments began in South Korea in 2017 ahead of the 2018 Winter Olympics, and in the United States from 2020.

Technical features

Unlike its 8VSB-based predecessor, ATSC 3.0 uses OFDM modulation with LDPC error correction, supporting far more robust reception (including mobile reception) and single-frequency networks. It supports video up to 4K resolution at 120 Hz with high dynamic range, wide color gamut, and the HEVC codec, plus immersive audio via Dolby AC-4 or MPEG-H. Because it is IP-based, it enables targeted advertising, interactive services, advanced emergency alerting, and datacasting to non-TV devices.

Deployment

ATSC 3.0 is not backward compatible with ATSC 1.0 tuners, so the U.S. rollout has been voluntary and simulcast-based: stations launching 3.0 service must arrange for their programming to remain available in 1.0 via a "lighthouse" sharing arrangement. The FCC authorized voluntary use in 2017, and NextGen TV broadcasts have expanded across most major U.S. markets, though a mandatory transition date has been debated.
