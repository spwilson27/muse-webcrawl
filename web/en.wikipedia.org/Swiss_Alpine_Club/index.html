Swiss Alpine Club

The Swiss Alpine Club (German: Schweizer Alpen-Club, SAC; French: Club Alpin Suisse, CAS) is the largest mountaineering association in Switzerland and one of the oldest alpine clubs in the world, founded in 1863 in Olten. It promotes mountaineering, ski touring and mountain sports, maintains an extensive network of mountain huts and trails, and advocates for the protection of the alpine environment.

History

The club was founded in 1863, inspired by the British Alpine Club, with the aim of exploring and opening up the Swiss Alps, which at the time were little mapped. Early club members accomplished many first ascents and produced pioneering maps and guidebooks. From the late 19th century the club built mountain huts to support climbers; today it operates around 150 huts providing some 9,000 beds across the Swiss Alps.

Activities

The SAC is organised into more than 100 local sections, which run training courses, tours and youth programmes. It publishes guidebooks and the magazine Die Alpen, supports mountain rescue through its partners, and maintains thousands of kilometres of hiking and climbing routes. The club is a member of the UIAA (International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation) and plays a leading role in debates about sustainable tourism and climate change in the mountains, including the retreat of glaciers near many of its huts.
