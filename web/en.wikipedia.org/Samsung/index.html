Samsung


Image placeholder: Samsung

Samsung Group (Korean: 삼성, stylized as SAMSUNG) is a South Korean multinational conglomerate headquartered in Samsung Town, Seoul. Founded by Lee Byung-chul in 1938 as a trading company, it grew into South Korea's largest chaebol, with affiliated businesses spanning electronics, shipbuilding, construction, insurance and finance. Its flagship subsidiary, Samsung Electronics, is one of the world's largest technology companies and the world's largest manufacturer of smartphones and memory chips for much of the modern era.

History

Lee Byung-chul founded Samsung ("three stars") in Daegu in 1938, exporting dried fish, vegetables and fruit. After the Korean War the company expanded into sugar refining, textiles and insurance. It entered electronics in 1969 with Samsung Electronics, producing black-and-white televisions, and by the 1980s was investing heavily in semiconductors, acquiring Korea Semiconductor in 1974. Under Lee Kun-hee, who became chairman in 1987 and famously urged employees in 1993 to "change everything except your wife and children," Samsung pursued global leadership in DRAM, flash memory, displays and mobile phones. The Galaxy smartphone line, launched in 2010, made Samsung Apple's chief rival in mobile devices.

Businesses

Major affiliates include Samsung Electronics, Samsung Heavy Industries, Samsung C&T (construction and trading), Samsung Life Insurance, Samsung Biologics and Samsung SDI (batteries). Samsung's revenues have at times equaled roughly a fifth of South Korea's GDP, giving the group outsized economic and political influence; its leaders have twice been convicted of corruption-related offences and later pardoned. Lee Jae-yong became executive chairman in 2022.
