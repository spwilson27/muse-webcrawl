Winterreise

ComposerFranz Schubert
CatalogueD. 911, Op. 89
TextPoems by Wilhelm Müller
Composed1827
Movements24 songs for voice and piano

Winterreise (Winter Journey) is a song cycle for voice and piano by Franz Schubert (D. 911, published as Op. 89), a setting of 24 poems by Wilhelm Müller. Composed in 1827, the year before Schubert's death, it is regarded as one of the supreme works of the Lied repertoire and of Romantic music.

Content

The narrator, rejected by his beloved, leaves her house at night and wanders through a frozen landscape. Songs such as "Gute Nacht", "Der Lindenbaum", "Frühlingstraum" and "Die Krähe" trace his descent from grief into numb despair, mirroring his inner state in images of ice, crows, frozen tears and a spectral sun. The cycle ends with "Der Leiermann", in which the wanderer addresses a barefoot hurdy-gurdy man — widely read as a figure of Death or of the artist himself.

Reception

Schubert's friends were unsettled by the work's bleakness; he called the songs ones that pleased him more than any others. Winterreise has been recorded by generations of singers, from Hans Hotter and Dietrich Fischer-Dieskau to modern interpreters, and its influence extends to works such as Thomas Mann's The Magic Mountain.
