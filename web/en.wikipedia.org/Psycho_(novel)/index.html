Psycho (novel)

Psycho

Author: Robert Bloch

Published: 1959

Publisher: Simon & Schuster

Genre: Horror, thriller

Followed by: Psycho II (1982)

Psycho is a 1959 horror novel by American writer Robert Bloch, published by Simon & Schuster. It tells the story of Norman Bates, the lonely caretaker of an isolated motel who commits murder while under the psychological domination of his dead mother. The novel is best known as the basis for Alfred Hitchcock's 1960 film adaptation, which closely follows its plot.

Background and plot

Bloch, who lived about 40 miles from Plainfield, Wisconsin, drew loose inspiration from the case of murderer Ed Gein, arrested in 1957, though he later said Bates was based more on the idea of the seemingly ordinary neighbour with a hidden life than on Gein himself. In the novel, Mary Crane steals $40,000 and stops at the Bates Motel, where Norman — middle-aged, overweight, and a heavy drinker in Bloch's telling, unlike the slim young man of the film — converses with her before "Mother" murders her in the shower. The investigation by Mary's sister, her fiancé Sam Loomis, and detective Milton Arbogast leads to the discovery that Norman murdered his mother and her lover years earlier, exhumed her body, and preserved it, developing an alternate personality.

Sequels and reception

Bloch wrote two sequels, Psycho II (1982), a satire of Hollywood slasher films in which Bates escapes the asylum and heads for a film set, and Psycho: Sanitarium (2016), set between the first two books; a third sequel, Psycho III-era material, differs from the unrelated film sequels. The original novel received strong reviews and is regarded as a landmark of post-war American horror fiction that helped establish the psychological serial-killer thriller.
