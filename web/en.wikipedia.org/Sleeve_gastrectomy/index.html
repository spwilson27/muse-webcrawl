Sleeve gastrectomy

Sleeve gastrectomy removes about 80% of the stomach, leaving a narrow tube

Sleeve gastrectomy, also called vertical sleeve gastrectomy or gastric sleeve, is a surgical weight-loss procedure in which the stomach is reduced to about 15–20% of its original size by surgically removing a large portion of it along the greater curvature. The result is a sleeve- or tube-shaped stomach that limits food intake. It has become the most commonly performed bariatric operation worldwide.

Procedure

The operation is performed laparoscopically under general anaesthesia. The surgeon removes the fundus and much of the body of the stomach, stapling the remainder closed. The procedure is irreversible. In addition to restricting volume, removing the fundus reduces production of ghrelin, the "hunger hormone," which may contribute to appetite suppression. Food passes through the digestive tract normally, so nutrient malabsorption is less than with gastric bypass.

Outcomes and risks

Patients typically lose 50–70% of excess body weight, and obesity-related conditions such as type 2 diabetes and sleep apnoea often improve markedly. Risks include staple-line leaks, bleeding, strictures, nutritional deficiencies and worsening or new onset of gastroesophageal reflux disease. Lifelong dietary changes and follow-up are required.
