The 1800 United States presidential election was the fourth quadrennial presidential election. It was held from Friday, October 31 to Wednesday, December 3, 1800. In what is sometimes referred to as the "Revolution of 1800," the Democratic-Republican candidate, Vice President Thomas Jefferson, defeated the Federalist Party candidate, incumbent president John Adams.

The election was a rematch of the 1796 election. The Federalists were hurt by divisions in their own ranks and by resentment over the Alien and Sedition Acts, while the Democratic-Republicans benefited from superior organization and the addition of New York's electoral votes.

The electoral tie

Under the rules then in place, each elector cast two votes for president; the runner-up became vice president. Because every Democratic-Republican elector voted for both Jefferson and Aaron Burr, the two men tied with 73 electoral votes each. Adams received 65 votes, Charles Cotesworth Pinckney 64, and John Jay 1.

The tie threw the election into the lame-duck Federalist-controlled House of Representatives, where each state delegation cast one vote. After 35 deadlocked ballots over a week in February 1801, Jefferson was elected president on the 36th ballot, with Burr becoming vice president. The crisis led directly to the ratification of the Twelfth Amendment in 1804, which required electors to cast separate ballots for president and vice president.

Significance

The election marked the first peaceful transfer of power between opposing political parties in United States history and began a period of Democratic-Republican dominance sometimes called the Virginia dynasty.
