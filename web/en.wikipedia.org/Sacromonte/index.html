Sacromonte

Sacromonte, sometimes also called Sacramonte, is a traditional neighbourhood in the eastern area of the city of Granada, in Andalusia, Spain. It is one of the city's six neighbourhoods that form the district of Albaicín.

The neighbourhood is famous for its cave dwellings (cuevas), many carved into the hillside of the Valparaíso hill facing the Alhambra, and for its association with Granada's Romani (Gitano) community and with flamenco music and dance.

History

The name derives from the Sacromonte Abbey, founded in the early 17th century after the discovery in 1595 of relics and the controversial "Lead Books" (plomos del Sacromonte) attributed to Saint Caecilius. Roma settled in the caves from the 16th century onward, developing the zambra dance tradition performed in cave venues.

Culture

Sacromonte is considered a cradle of flamenco in Granada; cave tablaos still host zambra performances. The Museo Cuevas del Sacromonte preserves examples of traditional cave homes and crafts.
