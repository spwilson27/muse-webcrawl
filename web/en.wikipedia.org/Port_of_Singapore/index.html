The Port of Singapore is the collective name for the ports and shipping facilities of the Republic of Singapore, situated along the Strait of Malacca — the world's most important shipping lane between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea. It is the world's second-busiest container port (after Shanghai) and its largest transshipment hub, with roughly a fifth of the world's transshipped containers passing through it. The port is connected to more than 600 ports in over 120 countries, and at any given time around a thousand vessels are in port. The principal operator is PSA International, with Jurong Port handling bulk and general cargo.

History

Singapore's role as an entrepôt dates from the founding of the British trading post by Stamford Raffles in 1819 as a free port. Steamships and the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869 made it a coaling and refuelling hub; by the early twentieth century it was the centre of British Malaya's rubber and tin trade. After independence in 1965, Singapore invested heavily in containerisation — the first container berth opened at Tanjong Pagar in 1972, just as container shipping boomed. The Port of Singapore was the world's busiest container port from the late 1990s until 2010, when Shanghai overtook it.

Tuas Mega Port

To consolidate its operations, Singapore is building the Tuas Mega Port on reclaimed land in the west of the island. Its first berths opened in 2022; when fully completed in the 2040s, Tuas will handle up to 65 million TEU annually with a high degree of automation, and the older city terminals will be retired. The Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore regulates the port, while Singapore is also a leading centre for bunkering (the world's largest by volume), ship repair and maritime services.
