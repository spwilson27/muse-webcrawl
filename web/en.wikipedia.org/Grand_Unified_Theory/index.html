Grand Unified Theory

Unification scale~1016 GeV
First modelGeorgi–Glashow SU(5) model (1974)
Forces unifiedStrong, weak and electromagnetic
Key predictionProton decay (unobserved; lifetime > 1034 years)

A Grand Unified Theory (GUT) is a model in particle physics in which the electromagnetic, weak and strong nuclear forces — the three gauge interactions of the Standard Model — merge into a single force at very high energies, around 1016 GeV. In such theories the three gauge groups SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1) arise from the breaking of a single larger symmetry group, and quarks and leptons are placed in common multiplets, explaining the otherwise mysterious quantisation of electric charge.

History

The first GUT, based on the group SU(5), was proposed by Howard Georgi and Sheldon Glashow in 1974; the Pati–Salam model with SU(4) appeared the same year. GUTs gained credibility when the measured coupling constants were found to run with energy in the direction of meeting at a point — though in the minimal Standard Model they miss slightly, while in supersymmetric extensions they converge much more precisely.

Predictions and tests

Because quarks and leptons sit in the same multiplets, most GUTs predict that the proton decays, mediated by superheavy X and Y bosons. Dedicated detectors such as Super-Kamiokande have searched for decades without seeing a decay, setting a lower bound on the proton lifetime above 1034 years and ruling out the minimal SU(5) model. GUTs also typically predict magnetic monopoles (whose absence helped motivate cosmic inflation), relations between quark and lepton masses, and — in SO(10) models — right-handed neutrinos whose seesaw masses can explain the smallness of neutrino masses.

Status

No grand unified theory has been experimentally confirmed. GUTs do not include gravity (that is the goal of a "theory of everything" such as string theory) and they introduce a large "desert" of unexplored energies between the electroweak scale and the unification scale, together with problems such as the doublet–triplet splitting puzzle. Nevertheless they remain a major guide for model building beyond the Standard Model.
