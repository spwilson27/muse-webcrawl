Bean

A bean is the seed of several plants in the family Fabaceae, which are used as vegetables for human or animal food. They can be cooked in many different ways, including boiling, frying and baking, and are an ingredient in dishes worldwide. The word "bean" and its Germanic cognates have existed since before the 12th century, originally referring to the broad bean, and were later applied to New World seeds such as the common bean after European colonisation of the Americas.

Types

Important beans include the common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris) — source of kidney, pinto, black, navy and many other varieties — the soybean (Glycine max), the broad or fava bean (Vicia faba), chickpeas, lentils, peas, mung beans, adzuki beans and lima beans. Seeds of some unrelated plants are also called beans, such as coffee beans, cocoa beans and vanilla beans, though these are not legumes.

Cultivation and nutrition

Beans are among the oldest cultivated plants; common beans were domesticated independently in Mesoamerica and the Andes. As legumes, they form symbioses with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, enriching the soil. Nutritionally, beans are rich in protein, complex carbohydrates, dietary fibre, folate and minerals, and form a dietary staple for hundreds of millions of people. Some beans, notably raw red kidney beans, contain the toxin lectin phytohemagglutinin and must be thoroughly cooked.

Cultural references

Beans feature in folklore such as "Jack and the Beanstalk", and in idioms including "spill the beans". The "bean" was also a token used in ancient voting.
