Multiple-camera setup

Used inTelevision production (sitcoms, soap operas, talk shows)
Contrasts withSingle-camera setup

The multiple-camera setup, or multi-camera mode of production, is a method of shooting television and film in which several cameras record the same scene simultaneously. It contrasts with the single-camera setup, in which each shot is lit and recorded separately.

Television

In television the technique is standard for sitcoms filmed before a studio audience, soap operas, game shows and talk shows. A typical arrangement uses three or four pedestal cameras covering wide shots and individual performers, allowing a scene to be performed continuously like a stage play and edited together in real time or in post-production. Desi Arnaz and cameraman Karl Freund pioneered the three-camera filmed sitcom with I Love Lucy in 1951.

Advantages

Multi-camera production is faster and cheaper than single-camera work and captures audience reactions naturally, but offers less precise control of lighting and composition.
