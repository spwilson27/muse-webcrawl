Jan Oort

Jan Hendrik Oort (28 April 1900 – 5 November 1992) was a Dutch astronomer who made major contributions to understanding the structure of the Milky Way. He is credited with the hypothesis of the Oort cloud, a vast spherical shell of comets surrounding the Solar System, and with pioneering radio astronomy in the Netherlands.

Born: 28 April 1900, Franeker, Netherlands

Died: 5 November 1992, Leiden, Netherlands

Known for: Oort cloud, Oort constants, galactic rotation

Fields: Astronomy

Work

Working at Leiden Observatory, Oort demonstrated in 1927 that the Milky Way rotates differentially, deriving the Oort constants that describe local galactic rotation. In 1932 he found that the gravitational pull of the galactic disk implied more mass than visible stars accounted for — one of the first indications of dark matter. In 1950 he proposed that long-period comets originate in a distant reservoir now called the Oort cloud.

After the Second World War, Oort led the Dutch effort in radio astronomy, culminating in the Dwingeloo and Westerbork telescopes and the mapping of the spiral structure of the Galaxy using the 21-centimetre hydrogen line. He directed Leiden Observatory from 1945 to 1970 and served as president of the International Astronomical Union.
