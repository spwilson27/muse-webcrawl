Tamil cinema

Tamil cinema is the segment of Indian cinema dedicated to the production of motion pictures in the Tamil language, widely spoken in the state of Tamil Nadu. It is nicknamed Kollywood, a blend of Kodambakkam — the Chennai neighbourhood that houses most of its studios — and Hollywood. Tamil films are also made in Sri Lanka, Singapore, Malaysia, and the Tamil diaspora.

History

The first Tamil silent film, Keechaka Vadham, was released in 1918. The first Tamil talkie, Kalidas (1931), followed within months of India's first sound film. In the 1940s and 1950s studios such as Gemini and AVM built a star system, and cinema became entangled with politics: screenwriters C. N. Annadurai and M. Karunanidhi carried Dravidian ideology to mass audiences, and actors M. G. Ramachandran and Jayalalithaa later became chief ministers of Tamil Nadu.

Industry

The industry releases roughly 200–300 films a year and is among the largest in India by output. Its music, led by composers such as Ilaiyaraaja and A. R. Rahman, is integral to its identity. Leading contemporary actors include Rajinikanth, Kamal Haasan, Vijay, and Ajith Kumar, and directors such as Mani Ratnam, S. Shankar, and Vetrimaaran have achieved national and international recognition. Big-budget productions like Enthiran (2010) and 2.0 (2018) rank among India's most expensive films.

Distribution and influence

Tamil films circulate widely in Malaysia, Singapore, Sri Lanka, the Gulf, and Western diaspora markets, and are frequently dubbed or remade into Telugu and Hindi. The Chennai International Film Festival and the Tamil Nadu State Film Awards are key institutions.
