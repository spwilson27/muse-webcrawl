Full titleThe Nazi Doctors: Medical Killing and the Psychology of Genocide

The Nazi Doctors: Medical Killing and the Psychology of Genocide is a 1986 book by American psychiatrist Robert Jay Lifton. Based on interviews with surviving Nazi physicians and with prisoner-doctors, it examines how medically trained people participated in the "euthanasia" programme, selections, and human experiments under the Nazi regime.

Concepts

Lifton introduced the concept of "doubling" — the formation of a second, functional self that allowed doctors to participate in killing while maintaining their identity as healers and family men — and described the transformation of medicine's ethos from healing to killing, in which the "medicalization of killing" made mass murder appear a therapeutic imperative.

Reception

The book is regarded as a landmark study of the psychology of genocide and of professional complicity in atrocity, and it remains widely cited in medical ethics and Holocaust studies.
