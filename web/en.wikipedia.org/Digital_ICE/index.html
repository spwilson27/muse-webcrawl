Digital ICE

Digital ICE (Digital Image Correction and Enhancement) is a set of technologies related to producing an altered image in a more accurate or visually appealing form, originally developed by Kodak's Austin Development Center (formerly Applied Science Fiction) for film scanners.

How it works

The flagship technique scans film with an additional infrared channel. Because photographic dyes are largely transparent to infrared while dust and scratches are opaque, the infrared image maps surface defects precisely; software then removes them by inpainting from surrounding image data. This hardware-based defect removal is far more reliable than purely software dust removal.

Limitations and related technologies

Digital ICE does not work well with black-and-white silver-halide film, whose metallic silver blocks infrared, nor with Kodachrome in some scanner implementations. The ICE family was extended to Digital ROC (color restoration), Digital GEM (grain management), and Digital SHO (shadow/highlight optimization), and was licensed into scanners from Nikon, Epson, Canon, and others.
