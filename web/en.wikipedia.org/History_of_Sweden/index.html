History of Sweden

The history of Sweden extends from prehistoric settlements after the last Ice Age through the Viking Age, medieval unification, the great-power era of the 17th century, and Sweden's transformation into a modern, neutral welfare state.

Viking Age and medieval kingdom

From the late 8th century, Swedish Vikings (Varangians) traveled east along the rivers of Russia, trading and serving as far as Constantinople. Christianity gradually replaced Norse paganism between the 9th and 12th centuries. A unified Swedish kingdom emerged under the Folkung dynasty; Birger Jarl, regent in the mid-13th century, is traditionally credited with consolidating the realm and founding Stockholm. From 1397 Sweden joined Denmark and Norway in the Kalmar Union.

Vasa era and great power

Gustav Vasa broke with the Kalmar Union after the Stockholm Bloodbath and was elected king in 1523, founding modern Sweden and introducing the Protestant Reformation. Under Gustavus Adolphus, Sweden intervened in the Thirty Years' War and became a dominant Baltic power; the Swedish Empire expanded further under Charles X Gustav and Charles XI. Defeat in the Great Northern War (1700–1721) under Charles XII ended Sweden's great-power status.

Age of Liberty to modern era

The 18th-century Age of Liberty saw parliamentary government and early press freedom (1766). After losing Finland to Russia in 1809, Sweden adopted a new constitution and, in 1814, entered a union with Norway that lasted until 1905. Sweden stayed neutral in both world wars. The 20th century brought industrialization, the folkhemmet welfare model under long Social Democratic rule, and EU membership in 1995. Sweden ended two centuries of military non-alignment by joining NATO in 2024.
