FADEC

FADEC — full authority digital engine control (or full authority digital electronics control) — is a system consisting of a digital computer, called an electronic engine controller (EEC) or engine control unit (ECU), and its related accessories that control all aspects of aircraft engine performance. FADECs have been produced for both piston engines and jet engines.

Operation

A true FADEC has full authority over the engine: there is no manual override available, and the computer controls starting, fuel flow, variable stator vanes, bleed valves and other systems across the entire operating envelope. The pilot's thrust lever simply communicates a thrust demand, and the FADEC computes the required settings from dozens of inputs including air temperature, pressure, throttle position and engine temperatures, many times per second. Modern FADECs have dual redundant channels, each capable of running the engine on its own.

Advantages

FADEC improves fuel efficiency, extends engine life by keeping temperatures and speeds within limits, reduces pilot workload, enables automatic engine protection and starting, and provides detailed health-monitoring data for maintenance. It also allows the engine to respond optimally to changing flight conditions without crew intervention.

History

Analogue electronic engine controls appeared in the 1960s and 1970s, initially as supervisory systems assisting hydromechanical units — famously on the Rolls-Royce/Snecma Olympus 593 of Concorde. Digital systems followed, and the first true FADEC experiments were conducted by NASA and the US Air Force in the 1970s–80s; FADEC became standard on new airliner engines such as the CFM56 and IAE V2500 families, and later spread to general-aviation piston engines.
