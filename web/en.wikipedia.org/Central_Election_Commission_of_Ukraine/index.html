The Central Election Commission of Ukraine (Ukrainian: Центральна виборча комісія України, Tsentralna vyborcha komisiia Ukrainy) is the permanent state body that organizes and conducts presidential, parliamentary and local elections in Ukraine, as well as national referendums. It is the highest body in the hierarchy of election commissions and supervises the work of lower district and precinct commissions.

Composition

The Commission consists of 17 members appointed by the Verkhovna Rada (the Ukrainian parliament) on the submission of the President of Ukraine, taking into account proposals of parliamentary factions and groups. Members serve a seven-year term and elect the Commission's chairperson from among themselves by secret ballot. The Commission works on a permanent basis as a collegial body; its decisions are adopted at sessions by majority vote.

Functions

The Commission registers candidates for President of Ukraine and political party lists for parliamentary elections, establishes election results, announces the elected officials, maintains the State Register of Voters, accredits international observers, and ensures a uniform application of electoral legislation across the country. It also controls the spending of state funds allocated for elections and hears complaints about the actions or inaction of lower commissions.

History

The modern Commission was established under the law of 1997. It has administered all national elections since Ukrainian independence, including the disputed 2004 presidential election that led to the Orange Revolution and a court-ordered repeat runoff. Following constitutional and electoral reforms, the current membership was appointed in 2018, replacing a commission whose members' terms had largely expired. Elections scheduled for 2024 were postponed because of martial law during the Russian invasion of Ukraine.
