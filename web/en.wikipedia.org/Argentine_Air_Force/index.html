The Argentine Air Force (Fuerza Aérea Argentina, FAA) is the air force of Argentina and one of the three branches of the Armed Forces of the Argentine Republic. It traces its origins to the Army's Military Aviation School founded in 1912 and became an independent service in 1945.

History

The FAA developed a notable domestic aircraft industry, producing the FMA IA 58 Pucará counter-insurgency aircraft and the Pulqui jet prototypes. Its most significant combat experience came in the 1982 Falklands War (Guerra de las Malvinas), in which its pilots, flying A-4 Skyhawks and Daggers and operating Super Étendards with Exocet missiles, sank several British ships despite heavy losses.

Modern era

Budget constraints after the 1980s sharply reduced the fleet; the force retired its last Mirage fighters in 2015 and has sought to rebuild its combat capability, including the acquisition of F-16s from Denmark agreed in 2024.
