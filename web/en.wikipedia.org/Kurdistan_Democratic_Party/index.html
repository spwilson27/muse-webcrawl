Kurdistan Democratic Party

The KDP is one of the two dominant parties of Iraqi Kurdistan.

The Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP; Kurdish: Partiya Demokrat a Kurdistanê) is a Kurdish political party in Iraq, founded in 1946 by Mustafa Barzani. It is the oldest Kurdish political party in Iraqi Kurdistan and, together with the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK), one of the two dominant governing parties of the Kurdistan Region. Its strongholds are the governorates of Erbil and Duhok. The party's current president is Masoud Barzani.

History

The KDP was founded in Baghdad in 1946 with Mustafa Barzani as president in exile, advocating autonomy for Kurdistan within Iraq. After the 1958 Iraqi revolution, the party briefly enjoyed legality, but relations with Baghdad deteriorated and Barzani launched an armed revolt in 1961. The struggle continued through the 1970s; the collapse of the Kurdish movement after the 1975 Algiers Agreement forced Barzani into exile. Following Mustafa Barzani's death in 1979, his son Masoud Barzani became party president.

After the 1991 Gulf War and the Kurdish uprising, the KDP participated in the establishment of the Kurdistan Region's de facto autonomy under the no-fly zone. In 1994–1998 the party fought a civil war (the Brayati war) against the PUK. Since 2003, the KDP has been a central actor in post-invasion Iraq and in the Kurdistan Regional Government, holding the regional presidency for long periods and, through Nechirvan Barzani, the regional premiership. The party supported the 2017 Kurdish independence referendum, winning about 93 percent "yes" votes, though the move prompted Baghdad to retake disputed territories including Kirkuk.

Organization and politics

The KDP is closely associated with the Barzani family and maintains strong tribal and patronage networks in its areas of control. It commands Peshmerga forces aligned with the party, maintains close ties with Turkey, and has frequently been in tension with the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK). Its main rivals within the Kurdistan Region are the PUK and the Gorran (Change) movement.
