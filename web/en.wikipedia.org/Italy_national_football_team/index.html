The Italy national football team (Italian: Nazionale di calcio dell'Italia) has represented Italy in men's international football since its first match in 1910. Nicknamed the Azzurri for the blue of the House of Savoy worn on their shirts, Italy is one of the most successful national teams in history: four-time winners of the FIFA World Cup and two-time European champions.

Early triumphs

Under coach Vittorio Pozzo, Italy won the World Cup at home in 1934 and retained it in France in 1938 — still the only successful defence of the title before Brazil's of 1958–62 — and also won gold at the 1936 Berlin Olympics. The postwar years brought the Superga air disaster of 1949, which killed the great Torino team that formed the national side's core, and two decades of relative failure before victory at the European Championship on home soil in 1968.

1982 and 2006

At the 1982 World Cup in Spain, Enzo Bearzot's team, inspired by Paolo Rossi's six goals after a match-fixing scandal had nearly ended his career, beat Brazil 3–2 in a celebrated match and defeated West Germany 3–1 in the final. Italy reached the final again in 1994, losing to Brazil on penalties — the first scoreless final, decided by Roberto Baggio's missed kick. In 2006, under Marcello Lippi and amid the Calciopoli scandal, a defence led by captain Fabio Cannavaro conceded only two goals as Italy beat France on penalties in the Berlin final to win a fourth star.

Recent years

Decline followed: Italy exited the 2010 and 2014 World Cups in the group stage and then, shockingly, failed to qualify for 2018 — the first missed World Cup since 1958 — losing a playoff to Sweden. Roberto Mancini rebuilt the side, which won UEFA Euro 2020 (played in 2021), beating England on penalties at Wembley, and set a world-record 37-match unbeaten run. Yet Italy again failed to qualify for the 2022 World Cup after a playoff loss to North Macedonia, a paradox of simultaneous European champions and World Cup absentees. At Euro 2024, now coached by Luciano Spalletti, the holders were eliminated in the round of 16 by Switzerland.

Style and identity

Italian football is historically associated with defensive organization — the catenaccio ("door-bolt") system of a sweeper behind man-markers, perfected by Helenio Herrera's Inter in the 1960s — and with the counterattack, though modern Italian sides have varied widely in approach. The blue shirt, white shorts, and anthem Il Canto degli Italiani remain constant symbols.
