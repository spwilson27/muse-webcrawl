The Gemäldegalerie Alte Meister (English: Old Masters Picture Gallery) in Dresden, Saxony, Germany, is one of the most renowned art collections in Europe. Housed in the Semperbau (Semper Building) of the Zwinger palace, it exhibits approximately 750 paintings from the 15th to the 18th centuries drawn from the holdings of the Dresden State Art Collections.

Collection

The gallery's most famous work is Raphael's Sistine Madonna (1512), whose lower register features the celebrated pair of putti (cherubs). Other major works include Vermeer's Girl Reading a Letter at an Open Window and The Procuress, Rembrandt's The Prodigal Son in the Brothel (with Saskia), Titian's The Tribute Money, Giorgione's Sleeping Venus (completed by Titian), Correggio's Holy Night, paintings by Dürer, Cranach, Holbein, Rubens, Van Dyck, Poussin, Canaletto, Liotard (including the Chocolate Girl) and the world's largest group of works by Bernardo Bellotto.

History

The collection originates in the art chamber founded by the Saxon electors in 1560 and grew dramatically under Augustus the Strong and Augustus III in the 18th century, when agents purchased whole collections in Italy and the Sistine Madonna was acquired in 1754. Gottfried Semper designed the gallery building, opened in 1855. Much of the collection was evacuated during the Second World War; the surviving works were seized by Soviet trophy brigades in 1945 and returned to East Germany in the 1950s. After extensive renovation, the gallery reopened in February 2020 with a new presentation integrating sculpture.
