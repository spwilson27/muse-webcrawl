Mojmir I

Mojmir I (Czech/Slovak: Mojmír I.)

Died: 846

Title: Duke of Moravia

Reign: 820s/830s – 846

Successor: Rastislav

Dynasty: House of Mojmir

Mojmir I (Latin: Moimarus, Moymarus; Czech and Slovak: Mojmír I.; died 846) was the first known ruler of Moravia and eponymous founder of the Mojmir dynasty. He is regarded as the founder of Great Moravia, the first Slavic state in Central Europe.

Reign

Mojmir came to power in the 820s or 830s in Moravia, north of the Danube, following the collapse of Avar power and in the shadow of the Frankish Empire. Around 833 he expelled Pribina, the Slavic prince of Nitra (in present-day Slovakia), and annexed his principality, uniting the Moravian and Nitrian lands into a single realm — the nucleus of Great Moravia. Pribina fled to the Franks and later received lands in Pannonia from Louis the German.

Mojmir is associated with the Christianization of Moravia: the Conversio Bagoariorum et Carantanorum records that Archbishop Adalram of Salzburg consecrated a church at Nitra around 828 — the first known Christian church among the Western Slavs — and Moravians were among the peoples baptised at Passau in the early 830s, though Mojmir's own baptism is not directly documented.

Death and succession

In August 846 Louis the German invaded Moravia and deposed Mojmir, who likely died in the campaign; Louis installed Mojmir's nephew Rastislav as duke. Under Rastislav and his successor Svatopluk I, Great Moravia reached its greatest extent and cultural flowering, including the mission of Saints Cyril and Methodius.
