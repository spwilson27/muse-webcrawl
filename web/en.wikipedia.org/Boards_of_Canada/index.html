Boards of Canada

Origin: Edinburgh, Scotland
Members: Michael Sandison, Marcus Eoin
Genre: Electronic, IDM, ambient, downtempo
Years active: 1986–present
Labels: Warp, Skam, Music70

Boards of Canada are a Scottish electronic music duo consisting of brothers Michael Sandison and Marcus Eoin. Their music is noted for its nostalgic, melancholic character, built from analogue synthesisers, distorted samples, children's voices, and fragments of 1970s educational film soundtracks. Their name derives from the National Film Board of Canada, whose nature documentaries they watched as children.

Career

The duo released early material on cassettes and through their own Music70 imprint before signing with Skam Records and Warp Records. Their debut album, Music Has the Right to Children (1998), was widely acclaimed and is regarded as a landmark of electronic music. It was followed by Geogaddi (2002), darker in tone and dense with numerological references, The Campfire Headphase (2005), and Tomorrow's Harvest (2013), which was preceded by an elaborate coded promotional campaign.

Style and influence

Boards of Canada's detuned, wobbling textures and evocations of childhood memory helped define the hauntology aesthetic in electronic music and influenced a generation of producers of ambient, downtempo, and vaporwave.
