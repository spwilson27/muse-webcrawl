Denominazione di origine controllata

Denominazione di origine controllata (often abbreviated DOC; Italian for "controlled designation of origin") is a quality assurance label for Italian food products, especially wines — and, under the related DOCG, a small number of cheeses. Modelled on the French appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC), the system was created by law in 1963 and implemented from 1966.

A DOC wine label and neck seal

The system

Each DOC defines a delimited production zone, permitted grape varieties, maximum yields, minimum alcohol, and ageing requirements. Wines must be analysed and tasted by an official committee before they may carry the designation. Below DOC in the Italian hierarchy sits the IGT (Indicazione Geografica Tipica), a more flexible category created in 1992.

DOCG

The higher tier, DOCG (Denominazione di Origine Controllata e Garantita), was introduced in 1980, with Barolo, Barbaresco, and Brunello di Montalcino among the first. DOCG wines undergo stricter production rules and government tasting, and carry a numbered seal on the neck of the bottle.

EU framework

Since EU reforms harmonized wine law, Italian DOC and DOCG wines are registered in the European Union's protected designation of origin (PDO) framework, while retaining their traditional Italian names on labels.
