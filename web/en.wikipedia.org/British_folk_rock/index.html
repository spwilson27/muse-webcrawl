British folk rock

British folk rock is a genre that developed in the United Kingdom in the mid-to-late 1960s, fusing traditional British folk music with rock instrumentation and rhythms. Unlike American folk rock, it drew heavily on centuries-old ballads, Morris dance tunes and medieval modes.

Key artists

Fairport Convention pioneered the style with their 1969 album Liege & Lief, often considered the founding record of the genre. Other leading acts included Pentangle, Steeleye Span, the Albion Band and Jethro Tull, while artists such as Richard Thompson, Sandy Denny and Nick Drake emerged from the scene. The genre enjoyed commercial success in the early 1970s and has seen periodic revivals.
