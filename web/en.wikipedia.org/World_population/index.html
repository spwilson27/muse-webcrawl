The world population is the total number of humans currently alive on Earth. The United Nations designated 15 November 2022 as the approximate "Day of 8 Billion", when the global population was estimated to have reached eight billion people. India surpassed China as the most populous country in 2023.

Growth history

World population reached one billion around 1804, two billion in 1927, and then grew rapidly during the 20th century, reaching 3 billion in 1960, 4 billion in 1974, 5 billion in 1987, 6 billion in 1999, and 7 billion in 2011. This acceleration resulted from advances in medicine, agriculture, and sanitation that reduced mortality, particularly among children.

Projections

The United Nations projects that world population will peak at roughly 10.3 to 10.4 billion in the mid-2080s before gradually declining. Growth is increasingly concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, while populations in Europe and East Asia are already shrinking. More than half of humanity now lives in urban areas.

Distribution

Asia accounts for roughly 60 percent of world population. Population density varies enormously, from dense megacities and river deltas to sparsely populated deserts, tundra, and high mountains.
