Galactic coordinate system

The galactic coordinate system is a celestial coordinate system in spherical coordinates, with the Sun as its center, the primary direction aligned with the approximate center of the Milky Way Galaxy, and the fundamental plane parallel to an approximation of the galactic plane but offset to its north. It uses the right-handed convention, meaning that coordinates are positive toward the north and toward the east in the fundamental plane.

Coordinates

Galactic longitude, symbol l, describes the angular distance of an object eastward along the galactic equator with respect to the galactic center, measured in degrees from 0° to 360°. Galactic latitude, symbol b, describes the angle of an object northward of the galactic equator (or midplane), measured from −90° to +90°.

Definition

The system was first defined by the International Astronomical Union in 1958. The north galactic pole is located at right ascension 12h 51.4m, declination +27.13° (B1950 epoch) in the constellation Coma Berenices, near the star Arcturus. The zero of longitude is the position angle of the galactic center, located in the constellation Sagittarius in the direction of the bright radio source Sagittarius A*.

Conversion

Galactic coordinates can be converted to equatorial coordinates and vice versa using standard spherical trigonometry rotation formulas, once the position of the galactic pole and the longitude of the ascending node are specified.
