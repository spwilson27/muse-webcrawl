Stafford Act

The Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act (Stafford Act) is a 1988 United States federal law that lays out the process for declaring a presidential disaster declaration and determines the type of assistance offered by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA). The Stafford Act is the statutory authority for most federal disaster response activities, especially as they pertain to FEMA and its programs. It created the current system by which a presidential disaster declaration triggers federal financial and physical assistance.

Background

The act amended the Disaster Relief Act of 1974 and was renamed in honor of Senator Robert Stafford of Vermont, who helped pass the law. Under the Act, the governor of an affected state must request a declaration, certifying that the disaster is of such severity and magnitude that effective response is beyond the capabilities of the state and local governments.

Declarations and assistance

The President may issue a major disaster declaration or an emergency declaration. A major disaster declaration makes available a wide range of federal assistance programs for individuals and public infrastructure, including funds for both emergency and permanent work. Programs include Individual Assistance (such as temporary housing and grants), Public Assistance for state, local and tribal governments and eligible nonprofits, and Hazard Mitigation assistance. The federal government generally pays at least 75 percent of eligible costs under a declaration.

Amendments

The Act has been amended several times, notably by the Disaster Mitigation Act of 2000 and the Post-Katrina Emergency Management Reform Act of 2006, which followed the response to Hurricane Katrina. The 2018 Disaster Recovery Reform Act further expanded pre-disaster mitigation funding.
