Ivermectin

Ivermectin is an antiparasitic medication discovered in the 1970s from the bacterium Streptomyces avermitilis, developed by Satoshi Ōmura and William Campbell, who shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for avermectin derivatives.

Uses

In humans it treats onchocerciasis (river blindness), strongyloidiasis, scabies and other parasitic infestations. Mass drug administration greatly reduced river blindness and lymphatic filariasis in Africa and elsewhere. It is also widely used in veterinary medicine.

Mechanism

It binds glutamate-gated chloride channels in invertebrates, paralysing parasites.

COVID-19

During the COVID-19 pandemic, ivermectin was promoted despite large trials and regulators finding no convincing evidence of benefit for that use.
