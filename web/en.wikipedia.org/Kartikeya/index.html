Kartikeya (Sanskrit: Kārttikeya), also known as Murugan, Skanda, Kumara and Subrahmanya, is the Hindu god of war. He is the son of Shiva and Parvati and the brother of Ganesha. His worship is especially fervent in South India, above all among Tamil Hindus, where as Murugan he is regarded as a patron deity of the Tamil land and language.

Mythology

According to the Puranas, Kartikeya was born to destroy the demon Taraka, who could be killed only by a son of Shiva. He was raised by the Krittikas (the Pleiades), hence his name, and in some versions has six faces (Shanmukha) to nurse from his six mothers. As commander of the divine armies (Devasenapati), he slew Taraka and other demons. His birth and deeds are narrated in Kalidasa's Sanskrit epic Kumarasambhava and the Tamil Kanda Puranam.

Worship

The six abodes of Murugan (Arupadai Veedu) in Tamil Nadu — including Palani, Tiruchendur and Swamimalai — are major pilgrimage centres. His mount is the peacock and his weapon the divine spear or vel, gifted by Parvati. Festivals such as Thaipusam, celebrated in Tamil Nadu, Malaysia, Singapore and elsewhere, honour his victory over the demon Surapadman; devotees carry kavadi burdens as acts of devotion. Kartikeya is usually depicted as a youthful god, in contrast to the ascetic elder forms of Shiva.

Regional traditions

In northern India Kartikeya is more often called Skanda or Kartikeya and is less widely worshipped than in the south. In Bengal he appears during Durga Puja alongside Durga's other children. He is also venerated at Kataragama in Sri Lanka by Hindus, Buddhists and the indigenous Vedda people.
