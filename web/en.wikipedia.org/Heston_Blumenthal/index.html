Heston Blumenthal

Heston Marc Blumenthal (born 27 May 1966) is an English celebrity chef, television presenter and author. Considered one of the pioneers of modernist and multi-sensory cooking, he is the proprietor of The Fat Duck in Bray, Berkshire, long rated among the world's best restaurants and holder of three Michelin stars.

Career

Largely self-taught, Blumenthal developed an interest in cooking after a family holiday meal at the Provençal restaurant L'Oustau de Baumanière. In 1995 he bought a run-down pub in Bray and opened The Fat Duck, which won its first Michelin star in 1999 and its third in 2004. In 2005 it was named the best restaurant in the world by Restaurant magazine's World's 50 Best list.

Cooking style

Blumenthal's scientific approach involves exploring flavour perception and the chemistry of ingredients — famously testing the claim that onions should be star-cut to reduce crying, and championing the triple-cooked chip. Signature dishes include snail porridge, bacon-and-egg ice cream, and "Sound of the Sea", served with an iPod playing seaside sounds. He collaborated with food scientist colleagues and took part in research published on flavour pairing. His other ventures include Dinner by Heston Blumenthal in London, and a long-running line of products for the Waitrose supermarket chain.

Television and honours

His television series include Kitchen Chemistry, Heston's Feasts and Heston's Fantastical Food. He was appointed OBE in 2006 for services to British gastronomy, and holds honorary degrees and a fellowship of the Royal Society of Chemistry.
