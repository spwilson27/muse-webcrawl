Clonal colony

Clonal colony
Unit termsGenet (genetic individual); ramet (physiological individual)
ReproductionVegetative (asexual)
Notable examplesPando (quaking aspen); King's Lomatia; honey fungus colonies

A clonal colony or genet is a group of genetically identical individuals, such as plants, fungi, or bacteria, grown in a given location, all originating asexually from a single ancestor. In plants, the genetic individual is called the genet, while each physiologically separate member — such as an individual tree trunk — is a ramet. Because all members share one genome, a clonal colony can be regarded as a single organism, and some are among the largest and oldest living things on Earth.

Formation

Clonal colonies spread by vegetative growth: horizontal stems (stolons and rhizomes) that root at intervals, root suckers, bulbils, or fragmentation. In fungi, a colony expands as a single mycelium from one spore or mating event. Over time the physical connections between ramets may decay, but the individuals remain genetically identical apart from accumulated somatic mutations.

Notable examples

Pando, a grove of quaking aspen in Utah, consists of some 47,000 stems arising from one root system, covers about 43 hectares, and is estimated to be thousands of years old. A colony of the honey fungus Armillaria ostoyae in Oregon's Malheur National Forest spans roughly 9 km² and may be several thousand years old. King's Lomatia in Tasmania is a sterile triploid clone thought to have persisted for over 40,000 years. A meadow of the seagrass Posidonia oceanica in the Mediterranean has been estimated at up to 200,000 years old, among the oldest known clones.

Ecology and significance

Clonal growth lets a successful genotype occupy space rapidly and survive the death of individual shoots, but it limits genetic variation and the capacity to adapt, and makes the whole colony vulnerable to a single pathogen. Clonal plants are ecologically dominant in many grasslands, wetlands and forests, and their longevity raises questions about how biologists define an individual and an organism's lifespan.
