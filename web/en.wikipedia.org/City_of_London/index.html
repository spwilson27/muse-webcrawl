The City of London, also known as the Square Mile, is a city and ceremonial county in London, England, comprising the historic core of the capital. Founded by the Romans as Londinium around AD 50, it occupies just over one square mile. It is a major global financial centre, containing the Bank of England, the London Stock Exchange, Lloyd's of London and numerous banks, law firms and insurance houses.

The City is administered by the City of London Corporation, a body dating to the twelfth century, headed by the Lord Mayor of the City of London — distinct from the Mayor of London. It is divided into 25 wards and is policed by the City of London Police. The Corporation also manages open spaces in the surrounding region, including Epping Forest and Hampstead Heath.

History

Roman Londinium was abandoned in the early fifth century and resettled under King Alfred the Great, who re-established the walled city (Lundenburh) in 886. The City survived the Norman Conquest with its privileges intact, grew rich on trade, and endured the Great Fire of 1666, after which Sir Christopher Wren's St Paul's Cathedral crowned its rebuilt skyline. In 1965 the surrounding area became the 32 London boroughs of Greater London, while the City retained its separate status.
