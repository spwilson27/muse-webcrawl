Nippur

Nippur (Sumerian: Nibru) was an ancient Sumerian city in Mesopotamia, located in present-day Iraq southeast of Baghdad. It was the special seat of the worship of the god Enlil, the "Lord Wind," ruler of the cosmos, and was regarded as the religious centre of Sumer even though it was never the seat of a ruling dynasty.

History

Occupied from the Ubaid period onward, Nippur flourished through the Early Dynastic, Akkadian, Ur III, Isin-Larsa, and Old Babylonian periods. Because of its sacred status, control of Nippur conferred religious legitimacy on kings of other cities. The city remained important under Kassite, Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Parthian, and Sasanian rule before being abandoned in the Islamic era.

Excavations

Excavations by the University of Pennsylvania (1889–1900) and later by the Oriental Institute of Chicago recovered tens of thousands of cuneiform tablets, including Sumerian literary texts, the oldest known map of a city, and the "Murashu archive" of a Persian-era business family. The site is dominated by the ziggurat of Enlil's temple, the Ekur.
