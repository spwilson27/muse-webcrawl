Benjamin

Benjamin (Hebrew: בִּנְיָמִין, Bīnyāmīn, "son of the right hand" or "son of the south") is a figure in the Hebrew Bible. He was the twelfth and youngest son of Jacob and the second and last son of Rachel, Jacob's favourite wife. Benjamin was the founder of the Israelite Tribe of Benjamin. In the Samaritan Pentateuch the name appears as Binyamīm.

Life in Genesis

According to the Book of Genesis, Benjamin was born on the journey from Bethel to Bethlehem, and his mother Rachel died giving birth to him, naming him Ben-Oni ("son of my sorrow"); Jacob renamed him Benjamin. When Joseph, sold into slavery in Egypt, rose to power there, he tested his brothers by demanding they bring Benjamin to Egypt, and hid a silver cup in Benjamin's sack to force them to confront their past. Benjamin's descendants, the tribe of Benjamin, later produced King Saul, Israel's first king.

Name usage

Benjamin has been a popular given name for boys in Jewish and Christian tradition for centuries, and is common in the English-speaking world; its diminutives include Ben, Benji and Benny. It is also used as a surname.
