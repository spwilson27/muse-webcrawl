Alate

An alate is a winged reproductive adult in social insects such as ants and termites. Alates are the caste produced by a colony when it is ready to found new colonies: virgin queens and males that take part in a mating (nuptial) flight.

Life cycle

Alates develop wings and functional reproductive organs, unlike the wingless workers of the colony. At a seasonally coordinated time — often after rain in termites, or on warm summer days in ants — alates swarm out of the nest. After mating, queens shed their wings, excavate a founding chamber, and begin a new colony; in ants, the males typically die shortly after the flight.

Entomological usage

The term is also used descriptively: "alate" contrasts with "apterous" (wingless), and in aphids winged morphs produced under crowding or poor host conditions are called alates, enabling dispersal to new plants.
