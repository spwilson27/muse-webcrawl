George Perry Floyd Jr. (October 14, 1973 – May 25, 2020) was an African-American man who was murdered by a white police officer in Minneapolis, Minnesota, during an arrest made after a store clerk suspected Floyd may have used a counterfeit $20 bill. Derek Chauvin, one of the four police officers who arrived on the scene, knelt on Floyd's neck and back for 9 minutes and 29 seconds, causing a lack of oxygen. His death sparked the George Floyd protests across the United States and internationally.

Early life

Floyd was born in Fayetteville, North Carolina, and raised in Houston, Texas, in the Third Ward's Cuney Homes housing projects. A standout athlete at Jack Yates High School, he played on the basketball and football teams. He later moved to Minneapolis, where he worked as a truck driver and bouncer, and was the father of five children. He had previously served prison sentences in Texas, including for a 2007 aggravated robbery, after which he became involved in his Houston church community.

Death and aftermath

On May 25, 2020, Floyd was arrested on suspicion of using a counterfeit bill at a Cup Foods store. Video recorded by bystander Darnella Frazier showed Chauvin kneeling on his neck while Floyd repeatedly said "I can't breathe". Floyd was pronounced dead at a hospital. Chauvin was convicted of murder in April 2021 and sentenced to 22.5 years; the other officers were convicted on federal civil rights charges. Minneapolis settled a wrongful-death suit with Floyd's family for $27 million. His death prompted widespread protests against police brutality and systemic racism, and renewed debate over policing reform worldwide.
