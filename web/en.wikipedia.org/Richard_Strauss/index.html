Richard Strauss


Richard Strauss (1864–1949)

Richard Georg Strauss (11 June 1864 – 8 September 1949) was a German composer and conductor, a leading figure of late Romantic and early modern music. His tone poems of the 1880s–90s, his operas Salome, Elektra and Der Rosenkavalier, and his Four Last Songs remain central to the repertoire. (He is not related to the Viennese Strauss family of waltz composers.)

Early life and tone poems

Strauss was born in Munich, the son of Franz Strauss, principal horn player of the Munich Court Orchestra. A child prodigy, he had works performed in his teens. After an early period of classical restraint he embraced the "music of the future" of Liszt and Wagner, producing a series of dazzling orchestral tone poems: Don Juan (1888), Death and Transfiguration (1889), Till Eulenspiegel's Merry Pranks (1895), Also sprach Zarathustra (1896, whose "Sunrise" opening became famous through the film 2001: A Space Odyssey), Don Quixote (1897), Ein Heldenleben (1898) and the Alpine Symphony (1915).

Operas

Strauss's operatic career ignited with the scandalous, harmonically daring Salome (1905) and Elektra (1909), the latter his first collaboration with librettist Hugo von Hofmannsthal. Their next work, the Mozartian comedy Der Rosenkavalier (1911), was an enormous success; further collaborations included Ariadne auf Naxos, Die Frau ohne Schatten and Arabella. Later operas, several with Stefan Zweig and Joseph Gregor, culminated in Capriccio (1942).

Conducting and later life

Strauss was one of the great conductors of his age, holding posts in Munich, Berlin and Vienna and championing performers' copyright rights. His conduct during the Nazi era remains controversial: he served as president of the Reichsmusikkammer from 1933 to 1935, largely, he maintained, to protect music and his Jewish daughter-in-law and grandchildren; he was dismissed after a letter to Stefan Zweig was intercepted. After the war a denazification tribunal cleared him. In 1948 he composed the Four Last Songs, among his most beloved works. He died at Garmisch-Partenkirchen in 1949.
