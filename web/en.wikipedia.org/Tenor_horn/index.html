Tenor horn

A tenor horn in E-flat

The tenor horn (called the alto horn in the United States and Althorn in Germany) is a brass instrument in the saxhorn family, pitched in E-flat. It has a predominantly conical bore, three valves, and a mellow tone, and it is a standard member of the British-style brass band.

Description

The tenor horn is built with its bell pointing upward and is played with a deep, cup-shaped mouthpiece. Its written range and fingering correspond to a transposing instrument sounding a major sixth below written pitch. In a brass band the tenor horn section, usually three players, fills the middle register between cornets and baritones, providing both harmonic support and featured solo lines; the solo horn part is one of the band's principal voices.

History

The instrument descends from the saxhorns developed by Adolphe Sax in the 1840s. It should not be confused with the Wagner tuba or with the French horn in F, which occupies a similar register but differs in construction and timbre. The tenor horn occasionally appears in orchestral music, notably in Gustav Holst's works for band and in Percy Grainger's arrangements, but it remains primarily a brass band instrument.
