Paradise Garage

Location84 King Street, Manhattan, New York City
Opened1977
Closed1987
Resident DJLarry Levan
GenresDisco, post-disco, garage house

The Paradise Garage was a legendary nightclub in New York City, located at 84 King Street in Hudson Square, Manhattan. Open from 1977 to 1987 in a converted parking garage — hence its name — it was a members-only club with no liquor licence, and it became one of the most influential venues in dance music history.

Music and Larry Levan

The club revolved around its resident DJ, Larry Levan, whose marathon sets blended disco, soul, funk, dub, rock, and early electronic music into a seamless, spiritually intense experience for the predominantly Black, Latino, and gay crowd. Levan's remixes and edits, and the records he broke at the Garage, defined the sound later named after the club: "garage house", a soulful, gospel-influenced branch of house music.

The club

Founded by Michael Brody, the Garage was modelled on David Mancuso's Loft parties: membership and selective admission kept the focus on dancing rather than spectacle, and Richard Long's state-of-the-art sound system was considered among the finest ever installed in a club. Performers such as Grace Jones and Madonna appeared early in their careers.

Legacy

When the club closed in September 1987, weeks of farewell parties marked the end of an era; a portion of King Street was later co-named "Larry Levan Way". The Paradise Garage's influence on house music, DJ culture, and club sound design remains profound, and its crowd of devoted regulars — "Garage heads" — is still celebrated decades later.
