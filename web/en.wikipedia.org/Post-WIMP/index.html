Post-WIMP

Multi-touch and tangible interfaces are classic post-WIMP interaction styles.

In human–computer interaction, the acronym WIMP stands for "windows, icons, menus, pointer", denoting a style of interaction that uses these elements of the graphical user interface. A post-WIMP interface is one that moves beyond the WIMP paradigm, employing at least one interaction technique that does not rely on classical 2D widgets such as menus and icons.

Examples

Examples of post-WIMP interaction include:

  • Multi-touch and gesture interfaces, as popularised by smartphones and tabletops;
  • Tangible user interfaces, in which physical objects represent and control digital information;
  • Virtual and augmented reality, using 3D interaction techniques such as hand tracking;
  • Voice user interfaces and conversational agents;
  • Brain–computer interfaces and eye-tracking systems.

Motivation

The WIMP paradigm, developed at Xerox PARC in the 1970s and popularised by the Macintosh and Windows, assumes a desk-bound user manipulating documents through a single pointer. Critics argue that it scales poorly to very large information spaces, mobile and wearable contexts, and collaborative settings. The term "post-WIMP" was popularised in the late 1990s, notably by Andries van Dam, as a call to explore interaction beyond the desktop metaphor while retaining its virtues such as direct manipulation.
