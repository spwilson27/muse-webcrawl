A Burial at Ornans
Artist: Gustave Courbet
Year: 1849–1850
Medium: Oil on canvas
Dimensions: 3.1 m × 6.6 m
Location: Musée d'Orsay, Paris

A Burial at Ornans (French: Un enterrement à Ornans) is a painting of 1849–50 by Gustave Courbet, widely regarded as the manifesto of Realism in French painting. It depicts the funeral, in September 1848, of Courbet's great-uncle in Ornans, the small town in eastern France where the artist was born.

Description

The enormous canvas — over three metres high and six and a half metres wide, a scale then reserved for history painting — shows an ordinary provincial burial procession rendered without idealization: pallbearers, clergy, mayor and town officials, veterans, and mourners, all portrayed at life size in somber contemporary dress against the limestone landscape of the Loue valley. Forty-six figures appear, each an identifiable local person.

Reception and significance

Exhibited at the Paris Salon of 1850–51, the painting shocked academic critics, who found its subject vulgar and its scale presumptuous — one called it "a masquerade funeral." Yet it drew attention to Courbet and to Realism: by treating an everyday local event with the grandeur of history painting, Courbet asserted that contemporary ordinary life was a worthy subject for serious art. The work is now held by the Musée d'Orsay in Paris and is considered a turning point in 19th-century art.
