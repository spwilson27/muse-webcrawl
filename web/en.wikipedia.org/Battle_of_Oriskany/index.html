Battle of Oriskany

The ambush site near Oriskany Creek, where the Tryon County militia was attacked on 6 August 1777.

Date6 August 1777
LocationNear Oriskany, New York
ResultTactically inconclusive; strategic American success
BelligerentsUnited States and Oneida vs. Great Britain, Loyalists and Haudenosaunee allies

The Battle of Oriskany, fought on 6 August 1777, was one of the bloodiest engagements of the American Revolutionary War. A column of Patriot militia and Oneida warriors marching to relieve the besieged Fort Stanwix was ambushed in a ravine near the Oneida village of Oriskany by Loyalist and Native forces supporting a British expedition down the Mohawk Valley.

Background

In the summer of 1777, as part of the strategy behind Burgoyne's invasion from Canada, a mixed British force under Barry St. Leger advanced from Lake Ontario to besiege Fort Stanwix in the Mohawk Valley. Brigadier General Nicholas Herkimer mustered about 800 men of the Tryon County militia, joined by some 60 Oneida warriors, to march to the fort's relief.

The ambush

Against advice to wait for a signal from the fort, Herkimer pressed on. On 6 August, in a marshy ravine west of Oriskany, his column was ambushed by a force of Loyalists of the King's Royal Regiment of New York and hundreds of Seneca and Mohawk warriors led by Joseph Brant and John Butler. The fighting became a savage hand-to-hand struggle, interrupted by a thunderstorm, in which neighbours and even family members — the valley's communities were split between Patriot and Loyalist — fought one another. Herkimer, shot through both legs early in the battle, directed his men propped against a tree before being carried from the field; he died of his wounds days later.

Aftermath

Casualty rates were extraordinary: the militia may have lost close to half its strength killed, wounded or captured. Yet during the battle a sortie from Fort Stanwix raided the British-allied camps, destroying supplies and baggage, and the besiegers' Native allies — themselves heavily hit at Oriskany — began to desert. Unnerved by false reports of a large relief force under Benedict Arnold, St. Leger abandoned the siege on 22 August. The battle also shattered the unity of the Iroquois Confederacy, pitting the Oneida and Tuscarora against the other nations in a civil war within the larger conflict.
