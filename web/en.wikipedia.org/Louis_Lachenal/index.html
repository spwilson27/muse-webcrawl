Louis Lachenal
Born: 17 July 1921, Annecy, France
Died: 25 November 1955, Chamonix, France
Known for: first ascent of Annapurna (1950)

Louis Lachenal (17 July 1921 – 25 November 1955) was a French climber and mountain guide from Chamonix, one of the finest alpinists of his generation. Together with Maurice Herzog, he made the first ascent of an 8,000-metre peak, Annapurna I (8,091 m) in Nepal, on 3 June 1950.

Annapurna

On the 1950 French Annapurna expedition led by Herzog, Lachenal and Herzog reached the summit without supplementary oxygen. The descent in deteriorating weather became an epic of survival: both men suffered severe frostbite, and Lachenal lost all his toes, while Herzog lost all his fingers and toes. The achievement made both national heroes in France and was recounted in Herzog's bestselling book Annapurna; Lachenal's own notebooks, published posthumously as Carnets du vertige, gave a more ambivalent account of the expedition.

Death

Despite his injuries Lachenal returned to climbing and guiding. He died on 25 November 1955, aged 34, after falling into a crevasse while skiing the Vallée Blanche above Chamonix. A peak in the Annapurna massif, in Nepal and the Pointe Lachenal in the Mont Blanc massif commemorate him.
