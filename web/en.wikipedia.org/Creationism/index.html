Creationism

Type: Religious belief
Core claim: Life and the universe were created by a deity
Variants: Young Earth, Old Earth, intelligent design

Creationism is the religious belief that nature, including the universe, Earth, life, and humans, originated from supernatural acts of divine creation. In its broadest sense it spans a spectrum of views, but the term is most often applied to literalist interpretations of the Genesis creation narrative that reject or qualify the scientific consensus on evolution and the age of the Earth.

Types

Young Earth creationism holds that the Earth is 6,000–10,000 years old and that most geological features result from a global flood. Old Earth creationism accepts the scientific age of the Earth while maintaining divine creation of life, sometimes through progressive creation or day-age interpretations of Genesis. Intelligent design, which emerged in the late 20th century, argues that certain features of living things are best explained by an intelligent cause, and is regarded by the scientific community as a form of creationism rather than science.

Science education controversies

Creationism has been at the centre of repeated legal battles in the United States over public-school curricula, from the 1925 Scopes trial through Epperson v. Arkansas (1968), Edwards v. Aguillard (1987), and Kitzmiller v. Dover (2005), in which federal courts ruled that teaching creationism or intelligent design as science in public schools violates the Establishment Clause. The scientific community maintains that evolution is supported by overwhelming evidence from genetics, palaeontology, and comparative anatomy.

Global distribution

Creationist belief is most prevalent in the United States, with significant movements also in Brazil, Turkey, South Korea, and parts of the Islamic world, where distinct traditions of creation belief exist alongside varying levels of acceptance of evolution.
