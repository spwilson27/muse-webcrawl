Chartres Cathedral, also known as the Cathedral of Our Lady of Chartres (French: Cathédrale Notre-Dame de Chartres), is a Catholic cathedral in Chartres, France, about 80 km southwest of Paris. It is considered one of the finest examples of French Gothic architecture and is particularly renowned for its stained glass and sculpture, preserved in a remarkably original state.

History

A succession of churches occupied the site from the 4th century; the cathedral's treasure was the Sancta Camisia, a relic believed to be the tunic worn by the Virgin Mary at Christ's birth, given to Chartres by Charles the Bald in 876. When fire destroyed most of the cathedral in 1194, the relic was found miraculously intact in the crypt, prompting an outpouring of donations and a rebuilding campaign that produced the present church in only about 26 years — unusually fast, which accounts for its stylistic unity.

The west front survives largely from the earlier Romanesque church (c. 1145–1155), including the Royal Portal with its column statues; the two spires differ because the north tower's spire was rebuilt in Flamboyant style after a lightning strike in 1506. The cathedral was consecrated in 1260 in the presence of Louis IX.

Architecture and glass

Chartres pioneered the High Gothic elevation: flying buttresses allowed the clerestory windows to be enlarged and the triforium reduced, flooding the 37-metre-high nave with light. Of its 176 stained-glass windows, about 152 survive from the 13th century, famous for their deep "Chartres blue." The three great rose windows and the lancets below the west rose — including the celebrated Notre-Dame de la Belle Verrière — are landmarks of medieval art. The labyrinth inlaid in the nave floor (c. 1200), 294 metres long, was walked by pilgrims as a symbolic pilgrimage.

Significance

UNESCO listed the cathedral in 1979, calling it "the high point of French Gothic art." It remains a major pilgrimage destination, especially for the Pentecost pilgrimage from Paris, and the seat of the Diocese of Chartres.
