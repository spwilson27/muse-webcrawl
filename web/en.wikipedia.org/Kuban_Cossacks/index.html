Kuban Cossacks

OriginsZaporozhian (Black Sea) Cossacks; Line Cossacks

The Kuban Cossacks (Russian: Кубанские казаки) are Cossacks who live in the Kuban region of southern Russia, along the Kuban River in present-day Krasnodar Krai and neighbouring areas. They descend principally from Ukrainian-speaking Zaporozhian Cossacks resettled there in the 1790s — the Black Sea Cossack Host — merged in 1860 with Russian Line Cossacks of the Caucasus defensive line to form the Kuban Cossack Host, one of the largest and most important Cossack hosts of the Russian Empire.

History

After Catherine the Great dissolved the Zaporozhian Sich in 1775, former Zaporozhians formed the Black Sea Host and were granted the Kuban steppe in 1792–93, founding the city of Yekaterinodar (now Krasnodar) and a cordon of stanitsas guarding the frontier against the Circassians and other mountain peoples during the long Caucasian War. Kuban Cossacks served the tsars in the Russo-Turkish wars, the Russo-Japanese War, and the First World War.

In the Russian Civil War the Kuban was a stronghold of the White movement, and a short-lived Kuban People's Republic sought Ukrainian orientation before being absorbed by the Reds and then the Soviet Union. Decossackization, collectivisation, and the famine of 1932–33 devastated the community. Kuban Cossack units nonetheless fought in the Red Army in the Second World War, and a prominent Kuban Cossack cavalry corps took part in the 1945 Moscow Victory Parade.

Culture and revival

Kuban Cossack culture blends Ukrainian and Russian traditions: the Kuban Cossack Choir, founded in 1811, is world-famous, and many Kuban Cossacks historically spoke Balachka, a Ukrainian dialect. Their dress features the cherkeska coat with cartridge pockets, the papakha sheepskin hat, and the shashka sabre. Since the late Soviet period the Kuban Cossack Host has been officially revived and registered in Russia, taking part in public-order patrols and ceremonial duties.
