Tropic of Cancer


The Tropic of Cancer circle of latitude

The Tropic of Cancer is the circle of latitude on Earth that marks the northernmost position at which the Sun can appear directly overhead. It currently lies at approximately 23.44° north of the equator. This occurs at the June solstice, when the Northern Hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun to its maximum extent.

Name and movement

The tropic is named for the constellation Cancer, in which the Sun appeared at the June solstice when the name was coined some two thousand years ago; owing to precession, the Sun now appears in Gemini at that point. The latitude of the tropic is not fixed: it drifts slowly southward as Earth's axial tilt decreases, at a rate of roughly 15 metres per year, and it oscillates over a 41,000-year cycle.

Geography

Passing through Mexico, the Sahara, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, India, southern China, and Taiwan, the region between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn—the tropics—receives the most direct sunlight and generally has warm climates year-round.
