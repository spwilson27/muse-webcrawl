Finland at the Olympics

Finland first participated at the Olympic Games in 1908, while still a Grand Duchy of the Russian Empire, and has sent athletes to compete in every Summer Olympic Games and every Winter Olympic Games since. The Finnish Olympic Committee was founded in 1907. Helsinki hosted the Summer Olympics in 1952.

Achievements

Relative to its population of about five and a half million, Finland is among the most successful Olympic nations in history. Its greatest era came in the early 20th century, built on distance running: the "Flying Finns" such as Hannes Kolehmainen, Paavo Nurmi — winner of nine gold medals — and Ville Ritola dominated middle- and long-distance events in the 1912–1928 Games. Finland has also been strong in wrestling, javelin throw, and athletics generally at the Summer Games, and in cross-country skiing, ski jumping, and ice hockey at the Winter Games, where the men's ice hockey team won its first Olympic gold medal at Beijing 2022.

Medal summary

Finnish athletes have won well over 300 medals at the Summer Olympics and more than 160 at the Winter Olympics. Notable champions include Lasse Virén, who won the 5,000 and 10,000 metres at both the 1972 and 1976 Games, and ski jumper Matti Nykänen, one of the most successful athletes in his sport's history.
