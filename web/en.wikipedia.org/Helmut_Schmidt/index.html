Helmut Heinrich Waldemar Schmidt (23 December 1918 – 10 November 2015) was a German politician and member of the Social Democratic Party of Germany (SPD), who served as Chancellor of West Germany from 1974 to 1982. Before becoming Chancellor, he served as Minister of Defence from 1969 to 1972 and as Minister of Finance from 1972 to 1974 in the government of Willy Brandt.

Early life and war service

Schmidt was born in Hamburg, the son of a schoolteacher. He served in the Wehrmacht during the Second World War, working in air defence and spending the final months of the war as a British prisoner of war. After the war he studied economics at the University of Hamburg, graduating in 1949, and joined the SPD in 1946.

Political career

Schmidt first won national attention as Hamburg's interior senator during the catastrophic North Sea flood of 1962, when he coordinated the rescue operation with energy and improvisation, deploying Bundeswehr units in a domestic role despite constitutional doubts. He became an economics and defence specialist in the Bundestag, led the SPD parliamentary group, and served as Defence Minister, where he introduced reforms, before moving to the Finance Ministry.

Chancellorship

When Willy Brandt resigned in 1974 following the Guillaume espionage affair, Schmidt succeeded him as Chancellor. His tenure was dominated by economic turbulence after the 1973 and 1979 oil shocks, stagflation, and the terrorist campaign of the Red Army Faction, which culminated in the "German Autumn" of 1977, including the kidnapping and murder of industrialist Hanns Martin Schleyer and the hijacking of Lufthansa Flight 181, which Schmidt resolved by authorising the GSG 9 rescue operation in Mogadishu.

In foreign policy Schmidt was a committed Atlanticist and a driving force, alongside French president Valéry Giscard d'Estaing, behind the European Monetary System and the regular world economic summits that grew into the G7. Concerned about the Soviet SS-20 missile deployments, he supported NATO's 1979 dual-track decision, which placed him at odds with the left of his own party. In October 1982 his coalition with the Free Democrats collapsed, and he was removed by a constructive vote of no confidence in favour of Helmut Kohl.

Later life

After leaving office Schmidt became co-publisher of the weekly newspaper Die Zeit and a prolific author and commentator on economics and world affairs, remaining one of Germany's most respected elder statesmen until his death in Hamburg in 2015 at the age of 96.
