Dorothea Lange

Dorothea Lange
Born: 26 May 1895, Hoboken, New Jersey, U.S.
Died: 11 October 1965, San Francisco, California, U.S.
Occupation: Photographer
Known for: Migrant Mother (1936)

Dorothea Lange (1895–1965) was an American documentary photographer whose work for the Farm Security Administration during the Great Depression brought the plight of migrant farmworkers to national attention. Her 1936 photograph Migrant Mother, depicting Florence Owens Thompson in a pea-pickers' camp in Nipomo, California, became one of the most famous images of the era.

Career

Trained as a portrait photographer in San Francisco, Lange turned to documentary work in the early 1930s, photographing breadlines and labor strikes. Working with economist Paul Taylor, she traveled the country for federal agencies documenting rural poverty. During the Second World War she photographed the internment of Japanese Americans for the War Relocation Authority; many of those images were impounded at the time.

Legacy

Lange co-founded the magazine Aperture in 1952. Her work is held by major museums, and she is regarded as a foundational figure in documentary photography.
