Apocalypse Now is a 1979 American epic war film produced and directed by Francis Ford Coppola, loosely based on Joseph Conrad's 1899 novella Heart of Darkness, transposed from the Congo to the Vietnam War. It follows Captain Benjamin Willard (Martin Sheen), a troubled U.S. Army officer sent upriver into Cambodia on a secret mission to assassinate the renegade Colonel Walter Kurtz (Marlon Brando), who has set himself up as a god among a local tribe and is presumed insane.

Production

The film's production in the Philippines became legendary for its disasters: sets destroyed by a typhoon, the firing of original lead Harvey Keitel, Sheen's near-fatal heart attack, Brando's arriving overweight and unprepared, and a schedule that ballooned from months to over a year of shooting (1976–77) with costs spiralling past $30 million — Coppola famously said, "We had access to too much money, too much equipment, and little by little we went insane." Eleanor Coppola's documentary Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker's Apocalypse (1991) chronicles the shoot. Robert Duvall's Lieutenant Colonel Kilgore — "I love the smell of napalm in the morning" — became one of cinema's most quoted characters.

Reception and legacy

The film shared the Palme d'Or at the 1979 Cannes Film Festival (where it screened as a work in progress) and won Academy Awards for Best Cinematography (Vittorio Storaro) and Best Sound. It is consistently ranked among the greatest films ever made; the Library of Congress selected it for the National Film Registry in 2000. Coppola has released three versions: the 1979 theatrical cut, Apocalypse Now Redux (2001), and Apocalypse Now Final Cut (2019).
