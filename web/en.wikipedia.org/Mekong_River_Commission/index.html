The Mekong River Commission (MRC) is an intergovernmental organisation that works with the governments of Cambodia, Laos, Thailand and Vietnam to jointly manage the shared water and related resources of the Mekong River basin. It was established by the 1995 Mekong Agreement, successor to the Mekong Committee founded under United Nations auspices in 1957, one of the oldest river-basin organisations in the world.

The MRC provides a framework for cooperative water diplomacy: it conducts basin-wide planning and research on hydrology, fisheries, navigation, flood management and climate change, and operates procedures for notification and prior consultation on proposed mainstream uses of the river's waters, including controversial hydropower dams such as the Xayaburi and Don Sahong dams in Laos. China and Myanmar, the upstream riparian states, participate as dialogue partners rather than full members, a continuing limitation on the commission's authority, especially as Chinese dams on the upper Mekong (Lancang) affect downstream flows and fisheries that sustain tens of millions of people.

Structure

The commission consists of a ministerial Council, a Joint Committee of senior officials, and a Secretariat headquartered in Vientiane with a flood-management office in Phnom Penh. Funding comes from member contributions and international donors.
