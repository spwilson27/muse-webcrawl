Water supply

Water supply is the provision of water by public utilities, commercial organisations or individuals, usually via a system of reservoirs, treatment plants, pumps and pipes. A water supply system includes the abstraction of water from surface or groundwater sources, its purification to drinking-water standards, storage, and distribution to homes, businesses and industry — together with metering, billing and the maintenance of pressure and quality throughout the network.

History

Ancient civilisations built aqueducts, qanats, wells and cisterns; Rome's aqueduct network supplied over a million cubic metres of water daily to the capital. In the industrial cities of the 19th century, contaminated supplies spread cholera and typhoid, prompting the construction of filtered, piped supplies and sewers — reforms dramatised by John Snow's investigation of the 1854 Broad Street cholera outbreak in London and by the "Great Stink" of 1858, which led to London's modern sewerage and embankment systems.

Systems and challenges

Modern systems draw on reservoirs, rivers and aquifers, treat water centrally, and distribute it under pressure through mains to consumers. Major challenges include ageing infrastructure and leakage — non-revenue water can exceed 30–50 per cent in some cities — emerging contaminants such as PFAS and microplastics, lead service pipes, energy costs, and balancing supply against growing demand and climate-driven drought. In much of the developing world, supply may be intermittent, and large populations rely on standpipes, wells, vendors or surface water. Global monitoring by WHO and UNICEF tracks progress toward safely managed drinking-water services under Sustainable Development Goal 6.

Governance

Water supply is usually a public service provided by municipal utilities, though models range from state agencies to regulated private companies (as in England and Wales) and community-managed schemes. Debates recur over pricing, affordability — the human right to water was recognised by the UN General Assembly in 2010 — and the appropriate role of private finance.
