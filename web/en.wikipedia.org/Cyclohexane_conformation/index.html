Cyclohexane conformation

Cyclohexane conformations are the three-dimensional shapes adopted by cyclohexane molecules. Because the carbon atoms of the ring are sp³-hybridised, the ring puckers to approach tetrahedral bond angles, and cyclohexane exists predominantly in the strain-free chair conformation.

Chair and boat

In the chair form, three carbon atoms lie above and three below the mean plane in a pattern free of angle and torsional strain. Each carbon bears one axial substituent (parallel to the ring axis, alternating up and down) and one equatorial substituent (splayed outward around the equator). Ring flipping interconverts two equivalent chairs, exchanging axial and equatorial positions. The less stable boat and twist-boat conformations lie higher in energy; the chair is favoured by roughly 23 kJ/mol over the twist-boat.

Substituted cyclohexanes

Bulky substituents prefer equatorial positions to avoid 1,3-diaxial interactions, which is why tert-butyl groups effectively lock a ring in one chair. These conformational preferences (summarised by A-values) are fundamental to stereochemistry and the analysis of carbohydrate and steroid structures.
