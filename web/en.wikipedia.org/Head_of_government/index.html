Head of government

In politics, the head of government is the chief officer of the executive branch of a government, often presiding over a cabinet. In a parliamentary system, the head of government is usually called the prime minister, premier, chancellor, or a similar title, and is distinct from the head of state (a monarch or president) who holds the formal, often largely ceremonial, top office. In a presidential system, the president typically combines both roles as head of state and head of government.

Parliamentary systems

In parliamentary democracies such as the United Kingdom, Germany, India, Japan and Canada, the head of government — the prime minister or chancellor — is the leader of the majority party or coalition in the legislature, appointed by the head of state but accountable to parliament, which can remove them by a vote of no confidence. The head of government directs the cabinet, sets the legislative agenda, and leads the executive administration, while the head of state performs constitutional and ceremonial duties, such as formally assenting to legislation and accrediting ambassadors.

Presidential and semi-presidential systems

In presidential systems such as the United States, Brazil and Mexico, the president is directly elected, independent of the legislature, and serves as both head of state and head of government, appointing a cabinet that is not responsible to the legislature in the parliamentary sense. Semi-presidential systems, such as France and Russia, divide executive power between a directly elected president (head of state) and a prime minister (head of government) responsible to the legislature, with the balance depending on constitutional text and political practice; in France the arrangement can produce "cohabitation" when president and prime minister come from opposing camps.

Titles

Common titles for heads of government include Prime Minister, Premier, Chancellor (Germany and Austria), Taoiseach (Ireland), President of the Government (Spain), Chief Minister, First Minister, and, in absolute monarchies or one-party states, sometimes offices combined with the head of state. At subnational level, heads of government include state premiers, provincial premiers, governors and chief ministers. The distinction between head of state and head of government underlies diplomatic protocol, order of precedence, and much comparative constitutional analysis.
