Cranford (novel)

Cranford
Author: Elizabeth Gaskell
Language: English
Genre: Novel
Publisher: Chapman & Hall
Publication date: 1853

Cranford is an episodic novel by the English writer Elizabeth Gaskell. It first appeared in Dickens's magazine Household Words in a series of instalments between December 1851 and May 1853, and was published in book form in 1853. It was partly inspired by Gaskell's own experience of the town of Knutsford in Cheshire, which she uses as the model for the fictional Cranford.

The ladies of Cranford

Plot summary

The novel is a series of linked sketches of life in the small English country town of Cranford, narrated by Mary Smith, a younger woman who frequently visits from the larger industrial city of Drumble. Cranford is dominated by a circle of respectable middle-aged and elderly ladies — principally the formidable Deborah Jenkyns, her gentler sister Matilda (Miss Matty), and the fluttery Miss Pole — who maintain an elaborate code of "elegant economy" that disguises their genteel poverty. Episodes include the arrival of the retired Captain Brown, the visit of the conjuror Signor Brunoni, the financial ruin of Miss Matty when her bank fails, and the eventual return of her long-lost brother Peter from India.

Characters

The principal figures include Mary Smith, the narrator; Deborah Jenkyns, the rector's strong-willed elder daughter; Matilda Jenkyns, whose kindness and diffidence make her the emotional centre of the book; Miss Octavia Pole; Mrs Forrester; Martha, Miss Matty's loyal servant; and Peter Jenkyns, the prodigal brother whose practical jokes once scandalised the town.

Themes

The novel gently satirises the snobberies and rituals of small-town gentility while treating its characters with warmth and sympathy. Class, gender, money and the encroachment of industrial change — symbolised by the railway that kills Captain Brown — are recurring concerns. Its episodic structure and humour have made it Gaskell's most popular work after North and South.

Adaptations

Cranford has been adapted several times for stage, radio, and television. The BBC television series Cranford (2007), written by Sue Birtwistle and Susie Conklin and starring Judi Dench as Miss Matty, drew also on Gaskell's My Lady Ludlow and Mr Harrison's Confessions, and was followed by a Christmas special, Return to Cranford (2009).
