Zand dynasty

The Zand dynasty was an Iranian dynasty of Lak origin, founded by Karim Khan Zand, that ruled southern and central Iran from 1751 to 1794. It was the first dynasty of Iranian origin to rule the country after centuries of foreign domination.

Karim Khan

After the death of Nader Shah in 1747, Persia descended into civil war. Karim Khan, a military commander of the Zand tribe, gradually gained control and by 1751 ruled most of the country except Khorasan. He took the title Vakil al-Ra'aayaa ("Regent of the People") rather than shah, and made Shiraz his capital.

Rule and decline

Karim Khan's reign is remembered for relative prosperity, rebuilding of Shiraz, and flourishing trade through the port of Bushehr. After his death in 1779, succession struggles weakened the dynasty until Lotf Ali Khan was defeated by Agha Mohammad Khan, founder of the Qajar dynasty, in 1794.
