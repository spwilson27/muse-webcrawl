Shahnameh

Rostam, hero of the Shahnameh

The Shahnameh (Persian: شاهنامه, "The Book of Kings") is a long epic poem written by the Persian poet Ferdowsi between about 977 and 1010 CE. At some 50,000 couplets, it is one of the longest epic poems in the world and the national epic of Greater Iran, including Iran, Afghanistan, and Tajikistan.

Content

The poem tells the mythical and, in part, historical past of the Persian Empire from the creation of the world and the first king, Gayūmart, until the Muslim conquest of Persia in the 7th century. It is divided traditionally into three parts: the mythical age (the Pishdadian dynasty, including Jamshid and the tyrant Zahhak), the heroic age (the Kayanian kings and the exploits of the great hero Rostam, including his tragic duel with his son Sohrab and his seven labours), and the historical age (the Parthian and Sasanian empires, ending with Yazdegerd III).

Composition

Ferdowsi, a landowner (dehqan) from Tus in Khorasan, spent some three decades versifying prose sources, notably the Shahnameh of Abu-Mansur and the unfinished thousand verses of the murdered poet Daqiqi, which he incorporated. He wrote in Persian with a deliberately low proportion of Arabic loanwords, and his work is credited with a decisive role in the revival and preservation of the Persian language after the Arab conquest. Tradition holds that Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni, to whom Ferdowsi presented the finished work, rewarded him insultingly little — a story that became famous in its own right.

Legacy

The Shahnameh has shaped Iranian cultural identity for a millennium. Splendidly illustrated manuscripts — such as the Great Mongol (Demotte) Shahnameh and the Shahnameh of Shah Tahmasp — are pinnacles of Persian miniature painting, and its stories are still performed by storytellers (naqqāli) in coffeehouses. In 2010, celebrations of the poem's millennium were held worldwide, and UNESCO recognised its completion's 1000th anniversary.
