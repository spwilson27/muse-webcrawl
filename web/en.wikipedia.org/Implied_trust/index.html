Implied trust

An implied trust is a trust that arises by operation of law rather than because a settlor expressly created one. Courts recognise implied trusts to prevent unjust enrichment or where the parties' conduct shows an intention to hold property for another's benefit.

Types

The two principal forms are the resulting trust, which arises when property is transferred in circumstances suggesting the transferor did not intend the recipient to keep the beneficial interest, and the constructive trust, which a court imposes as a remedy, for example where a fiduciary makes a secret profit.

Resulting trusts

A presumed resulting trust may arise when one person pays for property but title is taken in another's name, or when an express trust fails to dispose of the whole beneficial interest.

Constructive trusts

Constructive trusts are imposed regardless of the parties' intentions. Common instances include property obtained by fraud and the common-intention constructive trust over the family home.
