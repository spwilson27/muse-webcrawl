Birch bark manuscript

Birch bark manuscripts are documents written on pieces of the inner bark of birch trees, a writing material used for many centuries in parts of Asia and northern Europe before the introduction of paper. Strips of bark are prepared by peeling, oiling and pressing, and text is written in ink or incised with a stylus.

Gandharan Buddhist texts

The oldest known birch bark manuscripts are the Gandharan Buddhist texts from the region of Gandhara (in modern Pakistan and Afghanistan), dating to roughly the 1st century CE and written in the Gandhari language in Kharoshthi script. Preserved in clay pots and rediscovered in the 1990s, the scrolls — held largely by the British Library — are the earliest surviving Buddhist manuscripts and include sutras and commentaries.

South Asia and Rus'

In India, birch bark (called bhurja patra) was used for manuscripts including the Bakhshali manuscript on mathematics and Kashmiri texts. In medieval Rus', birch bark carried everyday letters and documents, most famously those excavated at Novgorod (see birch bark letter).
