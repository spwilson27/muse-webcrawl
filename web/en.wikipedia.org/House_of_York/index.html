House of York

The House of York was the first of two cadet branches of the royal House of Plantagenet to hold the English throne. Its members reigned from 1461 to 1470 and again from 1471 to 1485. The house's symbol was the white rose, and its rivalry with the House of Lancaster gave the Wars of the Roses their name.

History

The house was founded by Edmund of Langley, fourth surviving son of Edward III, who was created Duke of York in 1385. Through the marriage of his son Richard of Conisburgh to Anne Mortimer, the Yorkists inherited a claim to the throne arguably senior to that of the reigning Lancastrians, descending from Lionel of Antwerp, Edward III's second son.

Richard, 3rd Duke of York, pressed the claim during the incapacity of Henry VI, igniting the Wars of the Roses; he was killed at the Battle of Wakefield in 1460. His son became King Edward IV in 1461. After Edward's death in 1483, his young son Edward V was one of the "Princes in the Tower" and was displaced by his uncle, Richard III. Richard was killed at the Battle of Bosworth Field in 1485, ending Yorkist and Plantagenet rule; the victor, Henry VII, married Elizabeth of York to unite the two houses in the Tudor dynasty.
