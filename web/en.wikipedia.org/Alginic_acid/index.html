Alginic acid

Formula(C6H8O6)n
AppearanceWhite to yellowish-brown powder
SourcesBrown algae (Phaeophyceae)
E numberE400

Alginic acid, also called algin, is a naturally occurring, edible polysaccharide found in brown algae. It is hydrophilic and forms a viscous gum when hydrated. Its salts with metals such as sodium and calcium have been used in a wide range of applications, and the acid itself is used in the food industry as a thickener and emulsifier.

Structure

Alginic acid is a linear copolymer of homopolymeric blocks of (1→4)-linked β-D-mannuronate (M) and α-L-guluronate (G) residues, covalently linked together in different sequences. The proportions of M and G blocks vary with the algal source and determine the gelling properties of the material.

Production and uses

Commercial alginate is extracted from giant kelp (Macrocystis pyrifera), Ascophyllum nodosum, and species of Laminaria. It is used in ice cream, sauces, and textile printing; in dentistry as an impression material; and in medicine in wound dressings and controlled-release formulations. With calcium salts, alginate forms gels used in molecular gastronomy and in the encapsulation of cells and drugs.
