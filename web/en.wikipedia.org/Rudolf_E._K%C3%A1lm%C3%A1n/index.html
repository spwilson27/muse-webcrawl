Rudolf Emil Kálmán (19 May 1930 – 2 July 2016) was a Hungarian-American electrical engineer, mathematician, and inventor. He is most noted for his co-invention and development of the Kalman filter, a mathematical algorithm that is widely used in signal processing, control systems, and guidance, navigation and control.

Life and education

Kálmán was born in Budapest and emigrated with his family to the United States in 1943. He studied electrical engineering at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, earning his bachelor's and master's degrees, and completed his doctorate at Columbia University in 1957.

Work

In 1960 Kálmán published his famous paper describing a recursive solution to the discrete-data linear filtering problem — the Kalman filter. The method was famously applied to the Apollo programme's navigation computer and has since become ubiquitous in aerospace guidance, GPS receivers, robotics and econometrics. Kálmán also introduced the state-space formalism of modern control theory, including the concepts of controllability and observability, and co-developed the Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma.

Honours

Kálmán received the IEEE Medal of Honor in 1974, the Kyoto Prize in 1985, the Charles Stark Draper Prize in 2008 and the US National Medal of Science in 2009.
