2008 Chinese heparin adulteration

Date2008
ProductHeparin (anticoagulant drug)
ContaminantOversulfated chondroitin sulfate (OSCS)
DeathsAt least 81 in the United States

The 2008 Chinese heparin adulteration was a scandal in which batches of the widely used blood-thinning drug heparin, manufactured from raw material sourced in China, were found to be contaminated with a cheaper substance, oversulfated chondroitin sulfate (OSCS). The adulterated heparin was linked to serious adverse reactions and at least 81 deaths in the United States.

Discovery

In early 2008 the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and Baxter International investigated a spike in allergic-type reactions among dialysis patients receiving heparin. Testing identified OSCS — a semi-synthetic compound that mimicked heparin in standard assays — in supplies traced to Chinese workshops that produced crude heparin from pig intestines.

Response

Baxter recalled its heparin products, and the FDA barred imports from the implicated supplier and introduced new tests to detect the contaminant. The incident exposed weaknesses in the global pharmaceutical supply chain and prompted increased inspection of overseas drug-ingredient manufacturers.
