Murano is a series of islands linked by bridges in the Venetian Lagoon, northern Italy, about 1.5 kilometres north of Venice. It is famous as a centre of glassmaking; in 1291 all glassmakers in Venice were ordered to move to Murano, officially because of the risk of fire from their furnaces, and in practice to protect the secrets of the trade.

Glassmaking

For centuries Murano glassmakers held a monopoly on high-quality European glass. They developed or refined techniques including cristallo (clear glass), lattimo (milk glass), enamelled glass, aventurine (goldstone), and multicoloured millefiori. Glassmakers were forbidden to leave the Venetian Republic on pain of death, though some defected and spread the craft abroad. Murano glass — chandeliers, beads, mirrors, tableware — remains a major export and tourist draw.

Geography and sights

Like Venice, Murano is an archipelago of small islands divided by canals and linked by bridges. Notable sights include the church of Santa Maria e San Donato, known for its Byzantine mosaic pavement, and the Museo del Vetro (Glass Museum) in the Palazzo Giustinian.
