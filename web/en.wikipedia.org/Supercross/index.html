Supercross

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Supercross is a motorcycle racing sport involving off-road motorcycles on an artificial dirt track consisting of steep jumps and obstacles, usually inside a stadium or arena. It evolved in the United States in the 1970s from motocross, which is raced outdoors on natural terrain.

History

The first widely recognised supercross event was the 1972 "Super Bowl of Motocross" at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum, promoted by Mike Goodwin. The AMA Supercross Championship became the premier series, traditionally running from January to May, followed by the outdoor AMA Motocross season in summer. In 2023, both were unified under the SuperMotocross World Championship, adding playoff rounds, and the FIM has sanctioned a separate World Supercross Championship.

Format

Tracks are built with hundreds of truckloads of dirt over a stadium floor and feature rhythm sections, triple jumps, and whoops (rows of small bumps). Racing is organised in classes by engine size — the premier 450SX class and the 250SX class, split into East and West regional series in the AMA championship. An event consists of timed qualifying, heat races, last-chance qualifiers, and a main event, typically 20 minutes plus one lap for the 450 class.

Notable riders

Jeremy McGrath, nicknamed the "King of Supercross", won seven AMA 450 titles in the 1990s; Ricky Carmichael and Ryan Villopoto also dominated their eras, and more recent champions include Eli Tomac and Cooper Webb.
