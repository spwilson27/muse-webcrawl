The Asantehene is the title of the monarch of the Ashanti people (Asante) of Ghana. The Asantehene rules from Kumasi, historically the capital of the Ashanti Empire, and occupies the Golden Stool (Sika dwa kofi), which is regarded as the sacred embodiment of the soul of the Ashanti nation. The office is one of the most prominent surviving traditional monarchies in Africa.

History

The Ashanti Empire was founded around 1701 under Osei Tutu I, with the spiritual guidance of the priest Okomfo Anokye, who is said to have summoned the Golden Stool from the sky. Successive Asantehenes expanded the empire through trade and warfare, and the kingdom fought a series of wars with the British during the 19th century. In 1896 the British exiled Asantehene Prempeh I, and in 1900 demanded the Golden Stool, provoking the War of the Golden Stool led by the queen mother Yaa Asantewaa. The monarchy was restored in 1926, and Prempeh I returned; the Ashanti Confederacy was formally restored in 1935.

Succession

The Asantehene is chosen from the royal matrilineage: succession passes through the female line, and the queen mother (Asantehemaa) plays a central role in nominating candidates, who must then be accepted by the kingmakers of Kumasi. The current Asantehene, Osei Tutu II, was enstooled on 26 April 1999 as the 16th occupant of the Golden Stool.

Role today

Although Ghana is a republic, the Asantehene retains great cultural and moral authority, presides over traditional festivals such as Akwasidae, mediates disputes, and promotes education and development, including through the Otumfuo Education Fund.
