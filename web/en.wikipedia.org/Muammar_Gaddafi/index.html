Muammar Gaddafi

Muammar Muhammad Abu Minyar al-Gaddafi (c. 1942 – 20 October 2011) was a Libyan revolutionary and politician who ruled Libya from 1969 until his overthrow and death during the Libyan Civil War in 2011.

Rise to power

Gaddafi led a bloodless coup in 1969 that deposed King Idris I, abolishing the monarchy and proclaiming the Libyan Arab Republic. He later styled his system the Jamahiriya ("state of the masses"), setting out his political philosophy in The Green Book, which combined Arab nationalism, socialism, and direct democracy through popular committees.

Rule

Oil revenues funded extensive welfare, housing, and infrastructure programmes, including the Great Man-Made River irrigation project. Internationally, Gaddafi supported revolutionary and militant movements abroad, bringing Libya into conflict with Western governments; the United States bombed Libya in 1986. Libya accepted responsibility for the 1988 Lockerbie bombing and in the 2000s abandoned its weapons programmes and moved toward rapprochement with the West.

Downfall and death

In 2011, protests during the Arab Spring escalated into civil war. NATO intervened in support of the rebels organised under the National Transitional Council. Gaddafi was captured and killed in Sirte on 20 October 2011.
