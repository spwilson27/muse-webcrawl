Stride piano
Stylistic origins: Ragtime, blues
Peak era: 1920s–1940s
Key centres: Harlem, New York

Stride piano, or Harlem stride, is a jazz piano style that developed in New York City during the 1920s, growing out of ragtime. The left hand alternates a bass note on beats one and three with a chord on beats two and four — striding across the keyboard — while the right hand plays melodies, riffs and improvisations.

Pioneers

James P. Johnson, the father of stride, Willie "The Lion" Smith and Luckey Roberts established the style; Fats Waller popularised it, and Art Tatum took its virtuosity to extraordinary heights. Players competed in cutting contests at Harlem rent parties.

Legacy

Stride demanded large hands and formidable technique, and influenced later styles from swing to bebop; its left-hand pattern survives in solo jazz piano playing today.
