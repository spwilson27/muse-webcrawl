Flight management system

A flight management system (FMS) is a fundamental component of a modern airliner's avionics. It is a specialised computer system that automates a wide variety of in-flight tasks, principally the management of the flight plan, reducing the workload of the flight crew.

Functions

Using a navigation database and performance data, the FMS computes the aircraft's position from multiple sensors (GPS, inertial reference, and radio navaids), guides the autopilot along the programmed lateral and vertical route, and optimises speeds and altitudes for fuel efficiency. Pilots interact with it through control display units (CDUs) on the flight deck.

Development

Flight management computers were introduced on airliners such as the Boeing 767 in the early 1980s, replacing dedicated navigators and reducing crew requirements on long-haul flights. Modern FMS units support required navigation performance (RNP) procedures and data-linked route clearances.
