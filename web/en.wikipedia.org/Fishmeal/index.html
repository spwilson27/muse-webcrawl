Fishmeal

Fishmeal
Composition60–72% protein, 5–12% fat, minerals
Made fromWhole wild-caught fish (esp. Peruvian anchoveta) and fish-processing offcuts

Fishmeal is a commercial product made from wild-caught fish and the by-products from processed fish, ground into a coarse brown powder or cake. It is a protein-rich feed ingredient used in diets for farmed fish, pigs, and poultry, and historically as a fertiliser.

[Image placeholder: Sacks of fishmeal at a processing plant in Peru]

About 4–5 million tonnes of fishmeal are produced globally each year, mostly from small pelagic fish such as the Peruvian anchoveta, menhaden, capelin, and sand eels, and from trimmings of fish processed for human consumption. Fishmeal production dates to at least the 19th century in Scandinavia and New England, and by the mid-20th century Peru became the world's largest producer and exporter.

Production

Raw fish is cooked, pressed to remove oil and water, dried, and ground. The press liquor yields fish oil as a co-product. Quality grades depend on protein content, freshness of the raw material, and levels of biogenic amines; an antioxidant is usually added because residual fish oil can oxidise and heat spontaneously during storage and shipping.

Uses

Fishmeal's amino-acid profile, digestibility, and content of omega-3 fatty acids, minerals, and vitamins make it a benchmark protein in aquafeeds for salmon, shrimp, and marine fish. Its share of aquaculture diets has fallen as prices rose and plant proteins, insect meal, and single-cell proteins have been substituted, but it remains strategically important for starter feeds and broodstock diets. It is also fed to pigs and poultry and used in pet food.

Sustainability concerns

Diverting edible small fish into meal for export markets has been criticised on food-security and ecosystem grounds: forage fish are key prey for seabirds, marine mammals, and predatory fish. Production fluctuates sharply with El Niño events, which depress anchoveta stocks off Peru. Certification schemes and a growing share of raw material from processing by-products aim to reduce pressure on wild stocks.
