Catholic Charismatic Renewal

The Catholic Charismatic Renewal is a movement within the Catholic Church that emphasises a personal experience of the Holy Spirit, often described as "baptism in the Holy Spirit," together with charismatic gifts such as speaking in tongues, prophecy and healing. Beginning in the late 1960s, it has grown into one of the largest renewal movements in the Church, with adherents estimated at well over one hundred million worldwide.

Origins

The movement began in February 1967 at a retreat of students and faculty from Duquesne University in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, who reported powerful experiences of the Spirit after prayer with Pentecostal influence. It spread rapidly through universities — Notre Dame became an early centre — and then to parishes, religious communities and new lay communities around the world.

Beliefs and practices

Charismatic Catholics remain within Catholic doctrine and sacramental life while practising expressive prayer meetings, laying on of hands, healing services and Life in the Spirit seminars that introduce participants to the renewal. The movement has been acknowledged by successive popes from Paul VI onward, who encouraged it while urging integration into the life of the Church; in 2019 Pope Francis established CHARIS as an international service body for the renewal.

Influence

The renewal has shaped Catholic music, evangelisation and ecumenism, particularly dialogue with Pentecostal and charismatic Protestants, and has been especially strong in Latin America, Africa and Asia, where it is seen partly as a response to Pentecostal growth.
