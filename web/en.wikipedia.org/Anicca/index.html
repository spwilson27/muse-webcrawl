Anicca

Falling cherry blossoms symbolise impermanence in East Asian Buddhist culture

Anicca (Pali: अनिच्चा; Sanskrit: anitya) is a Buddhist concept meaning impermanence. It is one of the three marks of existence (tilakkhaṇa), together with dukkha (suffering/unsatisfactoriness) and anattā (non-self). According to the doctrine of anicca, all conditioned things are in a constant state of flux: they arise, change, and pass away.

The doctrine

The Buddha taught that attachment to things that are impermanent leads to suffering, because what one clings to inevitably changes or disappears. Insight into impermanence — seeing that all phenomena, including one's own body, feelings, perceptions and consciousness, are transient — is considered essential to attaining enlightenment and liberation from the cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra).

In the Pali Canon, the formula "sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā" ("all conditioned things are impermanent") appears among the characteristic summaries of the Buddha's teaching. Contemplation of impermanence is a central element of insight meditation (vipassanā), in which practitioners observe the arising and passing of sensations and mental states moment by moment.

Related concepts

Impermanence is closely related to the doctrine of dependent origination (paṭiccasamuppāda), which explains how phenomena arise in dependence on conditions and therefore cannot be fixed or independent. In Japanese culture the concept resonates with mono no aware, the bittersweet awareness of the transience of things.
