Lughnasadh

Lughnasadh or Lughnasa (modern Irish: Lúnasa) is a Gaelic festival marking the beginning of the harvest season. Historically it was widely observed throughout Ireland, Scotland and the Isle of Man on 1 August, or about halfway between the summer solstice and the autumn equinox. It is one of the four Gaelic seasonal festivals, along with Samhain, Imbolc and Beltane.

Origins and customs

In Irish mythology the festival was begun by the god Lugh as a funeral feast and athletic games (the Tailteann Games) in commemoration of his mother or foster-mother Tailtiu, who was said to have died of exhaustion after clearing Ireland for agriculture. Customs included assemblies and markets, horse races, the ceremonial cutting of the first corn and an offering of first fruits, ritual wells and hilltop gatherings — many on mountains, as at Croagh Patrick, where the last Sunday of July ("Reek Sunday") preserves the pattern of a Lughnasadh pilgrimage. In folk tradition the festival season extended through August under names such as Garland Sunday and Bilberry Sunday, when wild bilberries were gathered.

Modern observance

Lughnasadh survives in fairs such as the Puck Fair in Killorglin, County Kerry, and is celebrated by modern pagans and Wiccans as one of the eight sabbats of the Wheel of the Year, corresponding to the Anglo-Saxon Lammas ("loaf-mass"), when the first loaf from the new grain was blessed.
