Summary execution

A summary execution is an execution in which a person is accused of a crime and immediately killed without the benefit of a full and fair trial. The term generally refers to capture, accusation, and execution all conducted during a very short span of time, in contravention of prohibitions on summary executions in international law.

Legal status

Summary executions of prisoners of war and detainees are war crimes under the Geneva Conventions, which require a regularly constituted court affording recognised judicial guarantees before any sentence is carried out. Extrajudicial killings by state forces outside armed conflict likewise violate international human rights law.

Historical examples

Notorious instances include the mass killings carried out by mobile killing squads during the Second World War, lynchings in the United States, and the summary shooting of prisoners in numerous twentieth-century conflicts. Commissions of inquiry and international tribunals have repeatedly treated such killings as among the gravest breaches of the laws of war.

Distinction from summary trial

A summary execution should be distinguished from execution following a summary (i.e. abbreviated but genuine) trial, such as certain forms of drumhead court-martial, though such proceedings may themselves fall short of fair-trial standards.
