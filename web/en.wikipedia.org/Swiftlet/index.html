Swiftlet

Image placeholder: a swiftlet at its cave colony.

Swiftlets are birds contained within the four genera Aerodramus, Hydrochous, Schoutedenapus and Collocalia. They form the Collocaliini tribe in the swift family Apodidae. The group contains about thirty species, mostly confined to tropical and subtropical regions of South Asia, Southeast Asia, Oceania and northeastern Australia.

Echolocation and nesting

Many swiftlets have developed a primitive form of echolocation — a series of audible clicks — that allows them to navigate in the darkness of the caves where they roost and nest. They are highly aerial birds that feed on insects caught in flight, and their legs are too weak for perching on branches, though they can cling to vertical surfaces.

Edible nests

Several species, notably the edible-nest swiftlet (Aerodramus fuciphagus) and the black-nest swiftlet, build nests largely or entirely from strands of solidified saliva. These nests are harvested for bird's nest soup, a delicacy in Chinese cuisine, making them among the most expensive animal products consumed by humans. Commercial "swiftlet houses" are now built across Southeast Asia to farm the nests sustainably. See also swift.
