Abdullah Ocalan

Abdullah Öcalan (born 4 April 1949), also known as Apo, is a Kurdish political activist and founding leader of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK), an organisation that waged an armed insurgency in Turkey from 1984 and is designated a terrorist organisation by Turkey, the United States, and the European Union. Captured in Kenya in 1999, he has been imprisoned on the island of İmralı in Turkey ever since.

Early life

Öcalan was born in Ömerli, a village in Şanlıurfa Province, southeastern Turkey, to a poor family of mixed Kurdish and Turkish background. He studied political science at Ankara University, where he became involved in revolutionary leftist and Kurdish nationalist politics. In 1978, at a meeting in the village of Fis, he and his associates founded the PKK.

Insurgency

Öcalan directed the PKK from exile in Syria and Lebanon's Beqaa Valley. On 15 August 1984 the PKK launched its armed campaign with attacks at Eruh and Şemdinli. The conflict with the Turkish state killed tens of thousands over the following decades. Under Turkish pressure Syria expelled Öcalan in 1998; after a period moving between countries, he was captured by Turkish agents in Nairobi, Kenya, on 15 February 1999.

Imprisonment and thought

Tried on İmralı island, Öcalan was sentenced to death in 1999; the sentence was commuted to aggravated life imprisonment when Turkey abolished capital punishment in 2002. From prison he developed the ideology of "democratic confederalism", influenced by the American anarchist Murray Bookchin, moving away from the goal of an independent Kurdish state toward communal self-government and women's liberation (jineology). His writings guided the administration established in northern Syria. Peace processes between Turkey and the PKK, in which Öcalan was the key interlocutor, advanced and collapsed repeatedly; in 2025 he called on the PKK to lay down its arms and dissolve itself.
