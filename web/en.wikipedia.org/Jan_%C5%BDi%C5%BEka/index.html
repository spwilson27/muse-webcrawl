Jan Žižka z Trocnova a Kalicha (c. 1360 – 1424) was a Czech military leader and a key figure of the Hussite movement. A follower of Jan Hus, he led Hussite forces to repeated victories against larger crusading armies, and he is regarded as one of the most innovative commanders of the late Middle Ages. Remarkably, he continued to command effectively after losing sight in both eyes.

Early life

Žižka was born into a minor noble family at Trocnov in southern Bohemia. He served as a mercenary and chamberlain at the royal court, and tradition holds that he fought at the Battle of Grunwald in 1410. He lost an eye early in his career, earning the epithet "the One-Eyed."

Hussite Wars

After the First Defenestration of Prague in 1419, Žižka became a leader of the radical Taborite Hussites. He perfected the use of the wagon fort (vozová hradba) — armoured wagons chained into a mobile fortress, supported by early hand guns, crossbows and flails wielded by peasant infantry. His victories included Sudoměř and Vítkov Hill (1420), Kutná Hora and Německý Brod (1422), and Malešov (1424).

Death and legacy

Žižka died of plague during a campaign in Moravia in 1424. His troops named themselves the Orphans (Sirotci) in mourning. A monumental equestrian statue of Žižka on Vítkov Hill in Prague is among the largest bronze equestrian statues in the world.
