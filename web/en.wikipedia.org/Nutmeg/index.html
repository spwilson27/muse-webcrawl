Nutmeg

Nutmeg is the seed of the evergreen tree Myristica fragrans, native to the Maluku Islands (the "Spice Islands") of Indonesia. The same fruit yields a second spice: the lacy red aril around the seed, dried, is mace.

History

Nutmeg was among the most valuable commodities of the early modern spice trade. The Dutch East India Company fought to monopolise production on the Banda Islands, including the conquest of 1621; the island of Run was exchanged with England for New Amsterdam (Manhattan) in the 1667 Treaty of Breda. Cultivation later spread to Grenada in the Caribbean, now a major producer.

Use

Grated nutmeg flavours baked goods, sauces such as béchamel, drinks like eggnog, and savoury dishes across European, Middle Eastern and Asian cuisines. In large doses the seed's compounds, including myristicin, are toxic and psychoactive.
