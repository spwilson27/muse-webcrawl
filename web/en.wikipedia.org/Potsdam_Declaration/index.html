The Potsdam Declaration, formally the Proclamation Defining Terms for Japanese Surrender, was a statement issued on 26 July 1945 by the United States, the United Kingdom, and the Republic of China during the Potsdam Conference. It set out the terms on which the Allied powers would accept the surrender of Japan in the Second World War, and warned of "prompt and utter destruction" if Japan refused.

Terms

The declaration demanded the unconditional surrender of all Japanese armed forces — though not of the Japanese state or Emperor as such. Its thirteen points included: elimination of militarist influence; Allied occupation of Japan; limitation of Japanese sovereignty to the four main islands (implementing the Cairo Declaration, including the loss of Taiwan and Manchuria and independence for Korea); disarmament; punishment of war criminals; and establishment of freedoms of speech, religion, and thought, with a peacefully inclined and responsible government chosen by the freely expressed will of the Japanese people. Japanese industry was to be permitted to sustain the economy, and occupation forces would be withdrawn once these aims were achieved.

Japanese response

The Japanese government debated the declaration in the context of hoping for Soviet mediation. Prime Minister Kantarō Suzuki told the press that the government would mokusatsu the declaration — a word capable of meaning "to kill with silence" (ignore) or, in Allied interpretation, "treat with contempt". The Allies treated this as rejection. The Soviet Union was not a signatory, as it was not yet at war with Japan.

Aftermath

The atomic bombings of Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August) and the Soviet declaration of war followed. On 14 August Japan accepted the Potsdam terms, with clarification regarding the Emperor's status, and formally signed the instrument of surrender aboard USS Missouri on 2 September 1945. The declaration's terms structured the ensuing Allied occupation and remain referenced in disputes over Japan's postwar territorial settlement.
