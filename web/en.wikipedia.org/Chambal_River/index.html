The Chambal River is a tributary of the Yamuna River in central India, flowing through Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh. Known in ancient texts as the Charmanyavati, it rises in the Vindhya Range south of Mhow and flows about 960 kilometres before joining the Yamuna.

Geography

The river drains a basin of badlands and ravines — deeply eroded gullies that historically sheltered famous dacoits (bandits), including Phoolan Devi. Major tributaries include the Banas, Kali Sindh, and Parbati rivers. A series of dams, including Gandhi Sagar, Rana Pratap Sagar, and Jawahar Sagar, harness the river for irrigation and hydroelectric power.

Wildlife

The relatively clean Chambal supports the National Chambal Sanctuary, which protects the critically endangered gharial, the red-crowned roof turtle, mugger crocodiles, and the endangered Ganges river dolphin.
