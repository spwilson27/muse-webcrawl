Kannada (ಕನ್ನಡ) is a Dravidian language spoken predominantly in the Indian state of Karnataka, of which it is the official and administrative language. With tens of millions of native speakers, it is one of the most widely spoken languages of India. In 2008 the Government of India granted Kannada classical language status in recognition of its long literary history.

History

Kannada has a documented literary tradition of about 1,500 years. The Halmidi inscription (c. 450 CE) is among the earliest records in the language, and the 9th-century treatise Kavirajamarga is the earliest surviving work on Kannada poetics. Medieval literature flourished under dynasties such as the Rashtrakutas, Chalukyas and Hoysalas, and the 12th-century Vachana movement produced devotional poetry of lasting influence. The Kannada script evolved from the Kadamba and Brahmi-derived scripts of southern India.

Phonology and grammar

Kannada is an agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order. It has a system of retroflex consonants, two numbers, and grammatical gender in pronouns and verbs. Its alphabet consists of 49 characters: 13 vowels and 34 consonants, plus additional signs. The language has absorbed extensive vocabulary from Sanskrit while retaining a Dravidian grammatical core.

Literature and media

Kannada literature has won more Jnanpith Awards than that of any other Indian language, with eight laureates including Kuvempu and U. R. Ananthamurthy. Bengaluru is the centre of a large film industry ("Sandalwood") and of India's technology sector, where Kannada coexists with English in daily life.
