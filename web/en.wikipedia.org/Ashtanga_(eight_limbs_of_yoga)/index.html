Ashtanga (eight limbs of yoga)

Ashtanga (Sanskrit: अष्टाङ्ग, aṣṭāṅga, "eight limbs") is Patañjali's classification of classical yoga, as set out in his Yoga Sūtras (compiled between the 2nd century BCE and the 4th century CE). The eight limbs describe a progressive path of ethical discipline, bodily practice and meditation culminating in samādhi (absorption or enlightenment). The term is also used for Ashtanga vinyasa yoga, a modern style of physical yoga founded by K. Pattabhi Jois, which takes its name from Patañjali's system.

The eight limbs

  1. Yama – ethical restraints: ahiṃsā (non-violence), satya (truthfulness), asteya (non-stealing), brahmacarya (continence) and aparigraha (non-possessiveness).
  2. Niyama – observances: śauca (purity), saṃtoṣa (contentment), tapas (austerity), svādhyāya (self-study) and īśvara-praṇidhāna (devotion to God).
  3. Āsana – a steady, comfortable posture, originally a seated meditation seat.
  4. Prāṇāyāma – regulation of the breath.
  5. Pratyāhāra – withdrawal of the senses from external objects.
  6. Dhāraṇā – concentration on a single point.
  7. Dhyāna – sustained meditation.
  8. Samādhi – absorption, in which the meditator and object of meditation become one.

The first five limbs are considered external practices (bahiraṅga) and the last three internal (antaraṅga); together the final three are called saṃyama and are said to yield insight and extraordinary powers (siddhis), though the goal of the system is liberation (kaivalya).
