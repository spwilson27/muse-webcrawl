Aachen (German: [ˈaːxn̩]; French: Aix-la-Chapelle; Dutch: Aken) is a city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, situated on the borders with Belgium and the Netherlands — the westernmost city in Germany. It is known above all as the favoured residence of Charlemagne, who built its Palatine Chapel in the late 8th century and was buried there in 814. For some six centuries, from 936 to 1531, the kings of Germany were crowned in Aachen.

Aachen is a major university city: RWTH Aachen University, founded in 1870, is one of Germany's largest and most prestigious technical universities and shapes the city's economy and population alongside its historic spa industry, based on the hottest thermal springs in Europe.

History

The hot springs of Aachen attracted settlement from Neolithic times and were developed by the Romans, who knew the place as Aquae Granni, after the Celtic god Grannus. Aachen's historical importance begins with Charlemagne, who made it the centre of his empire from the 790s, ordering the construction of the Palatine Chapel — today the core of Aachen Cathedral — and a palace complex. He died there in 814 and was buried in the chapel.

In 936 Otto I was crowned King of Germany in the Palatine Chapel, beginning a tradition that saw 31 German kings and 12 queens crowned at Aachen over nearly 600 years. The city received a charter and walls in the 12th century under Frederick Barbarossa, became a free imperial city, and prospered as a centre of cloth-making and pilgrimage: the Aachen pilgrimage (Heiligtumsfahrt), displaying Charlemagne-era relics including the dress of the Virgin Mary, drew enormous crowds every seven years and continues today.

A fire destroyed much of the medieval city in 1656, and Aachen was rebuilt as a fashionable spa town. The Congress of Aix-la-Chapelle in 1818, settling post-Napoleonic European questions, brought the monarchs of Europe to the city. Aachen changed hands between France and Prussia in the revolutionary era and was assigned to Prussia in 1815. In October 1944 it became the first major German city captured by Allied forces, after a fierce battle. The International Charlemagne Prize of Aachen, founded in 1949 and first awarded in 1950, is among Europe's most prestigious awards for contributions to European unity.

Geography and climate

The Gothic town hall (Rathaus), built on the foundations of Charlemagne's palace.

Aachen lies in a basin-like valley in the foothills of the Eifel and Ardennes, a few kilometres from the Dreiländereck, the tripoint where Germany, Belgium and the Netherlands meet. The Vaalsberg, the highest point of the European Netherlands, rises just across the border. Streams including the Wurm flow through the city, and some 30 thermal springs emerge at temperatures of up to about 74 °C, among the hottest in Europe. The climate is temperate oceanic, mild and comparatively wet.

Main sights

  • Aachen Cathedral — a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 1978, among the first worldwide); the Palatine Chapel is a masterpiece of Carolingian architecture, housing Charlemagne's throne and his shrine.
  • Cathedral Treasury — one of the finest church treasuries in northern Europe, with the Lothair Cross and the Bust of Charlemagne.
  • Town Hall (Rathaus) — Gothic building of the 14th century on the site of Charlemagne's palace aula.
  • Elisenbrunnen — neoclassical pump room of 1827 over the Kaiserquelle spring.
  • Ponttor and Marschiertor — surviving medieval city gates.
  • Ludwig Forum and the Suermondt-Ludwig Museum — modern and classical art collections.

Economy and education

RWTH Aachen University, with more than 45,000 students, is the city's largest employer and anchors a technology sector of research institutes and spin-off firms. Aachen's traditional industries include needle and pin manufacture, textiles, confectionery — the city is famous for Aachener Printen, a spiced gingerbread — and the spa and health sector centred on the Burtscheid district's springs.

Culture

The carnival season is a major feature of Aachen life, culminating in the award of the humorous Orden wider den tierischen Ernst ("Order Against Deadly Seriousness"). The CHIO Aachen, held annually at the Soers, is one of the world's foremost equestrian tournaments. The city's dialect, Öcher Platt, belongs to the Ripuarian Franconian group.

Transport

Aachen Hauptbahnhof offers high-speed Thalys/Eurostar services to Cologne, Brussels and Paris, and regional links into Belgium and the Netherlands; Maastricht Aachen Airport lies across the Dutch border.
