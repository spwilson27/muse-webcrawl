Apollonius's theorem

In geometry, Apollonius's theorem is a theorem relating the length of a median of a triangle to the lengths of its sides. It states that "the sum of the squares of any two sides of any triangle equals twice the square on half the third side, together with twice the square on the median bisecting the third side."

A triangle with a median drawn to side a

Specifically, in any triangle ABC, if AD is a median (D is the midpoint of BC), then:

AB² + AC² = 2(AD² + BD²)

Equivalently, with sides a, b, c and median ma drawn to side a:

b² + c² = 2ma² + a²/2

Relation to other theorems

The theorem is a special case of Stewart's theorem, which gives the length of any cevian of a triangle in terms of the sides. When the triangle is right-angled, Apollonius's theorem reduces to the Pythagorean theorem. It can be proved using the law of cosines, the parallelogram law, or vectors. Conversely, the theorem can be used to derive the median length formula:

ma = ½√(2b² + 2c² − a²)

The theorem is named after the ancient Greek geometer Apollonius of Perga, best known for his work on conic sections.
