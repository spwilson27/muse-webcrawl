Gearbox

A gearbox, also known as a transmission, is a mechanical device that uses gears and gear trains to provide speed and torque conversions from a rotating power source to another device. The term most often refers to the casing housing a system of gears, and in automotive use to the component that selects between gear ratios so that an engine can operate within its efficient speed range while the wheels turn at widely varying speeds.

Principles

A gearbox trades speed against torque according to the gear ratio: meshing a small gear with a large one reduces output speed while multiplying torque, and vice versa. Multiple gear pairs in one housing give a set of discrete ratios; the overall ratio in any gear equals the product of the ratios along the engaged path.

Types

  • Manual gearbox — the driver selects ratios with a lever and clutch; typically uses sliding-mesh or constant-mesh helical gears with synchromesh.
  • Automatic gearbox — uses planetary (epicyclic) gear sets, hydraulic control and a torque converter.
  • Continuously variable transmission (CVT) — varies ratio steplessly, usually with belts or chains over variable-diameter pulleys.
  • Dual-clutch transmission (DCT) — two clutches preselect odd and even gears for very fast shifts.
  • Industrial gearboxes — speed reducers and increasers for turbines, wind turbines, conveyors and machinery, often using worm, bevel or planetary gearing.

Applications

Gearboxes are found in automobiles, motorcycles, bicycles (hub gears), aircraft, wind turbines, machine tools, agricultural machinery and robotics. In electric vehicles a single-speed reduction gearbox is common, because electric motors deliver useful torque over a wide speed range.
