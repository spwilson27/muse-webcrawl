Dāna
MeaningGenerosity, giving, charity
TraditionsBuddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, Sikhism
Sanskrit/Palidāna (दान)
Buddhist classificationFirst of the pāramitās (perfections)

Dāna (Sanskrit and Pali: दान) is the practice of cultivating generosity and giving — of alms, donations, and charity — in Indian religions, including Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, and Sikhism. It is regarded as a fundamental virtue and a practice that develops unselfishness, reduces attachment, and generates merit for the giver.

In Buddhism

In Buddhism, dāna is the first of the perfections (pāramitā) cultivated by a bodhisattva and the first topic in the Buddha's graduated discourses. Lay supporters give food, robes, shelter, and medicine to monks and nuns (the sangha), who in return offer teaching — the "gift of Dhamma" is said to surpass all other gifts. Giving is one of the three bases of merit-making, alongside morality (sīla) and meditation (bhāvanā). The quality of the gift depends on the giver's intention before, during, and after giving, and on the moral qualities of the recipient.

In Hinduism and Jainism

In Hindu texts dāna is praised as an essential duty (dharma), particularly in the form of giving to the worthy at the proper time and place; classics such as the Bhagavad Gita classify giving as sattvic, rajasic, or tamasic according to the giver's motive. In Jainism, charity — giving food, medicine, shelter, and knowledge — is one of the daily duties of the laity, and the classical tradition enumerates celebrated legendary donors. In Sikhism the cognate practice of sharing is expressed in vand chhakna and the institution of the langar, the communal kitchen.
