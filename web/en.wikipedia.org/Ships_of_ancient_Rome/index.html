Ships of ancient Rome


A Roman warship (bireme) depicted in a relief.

Ships of ancient Rome included the warships of the Roman navy and the merchant vessels that carried the trade of the empire. Rome, originally a land power, built its first large fleets during the First Punic War against Carthage, copying a wrecked Carthaginian quinquereme and introducing the corvus, a boarding bridge that turned sea battles into infantry engagements.

Warships

Roman warships were oared galleys classified by the number of rowing files: the small liburna, the trireme, the quadrireme, and the heavy quinquereme, flagship of the Punic War fleets. Rams remained the principal anti-ship weapon, supplemented by grapnels, towers, and missile troops. After the Battle of Actium (31 BC) the empire faced no rival navy, and the imperial fleets based at Misenum and Ravenna served mainly for transport, patrol, and suppressing piracy.

Merchant ships

Roman merchantmen (naves onerariae) were broad, strongly built sailing ships with hulls constructed shell-first using mortise-and-tenon joints. The largest were the grain ships of the annona, which carried Egyptian and African wheat to Rome — vessels of several hundred tons, described as crowds of floating storehouses, sailing between Alexandria and Puteoli or Ostia. Merchant ships carried amphorae of wine and olive oil, garum, marble, timber, and wild beasts for the arena; wrecks found across the Mediterranean, such as the Madrague de Giens wreck, reveal their cargoes and construction. The biggest Roman vessels known were the giant barges built under Caligula to transport an Egyptian obelisk to Rome.

Construction and rig

Roman ships typically carried a single main mast with a large square sail, sometimes supplemented by a fore-and-aft spritsail on a raked foremast (the artemon), and were steered by twin side rudders. Hulls were often sheathed in lead below the waterline against shipworm. Knowledge of Roman shipping derives from literature, mosaics and reliefs, and from the archaeology of hundreds of excavated wrecks.
