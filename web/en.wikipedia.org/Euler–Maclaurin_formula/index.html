Euler–Maclaurin formula

The Euler–Maclaurin formula, sometimes also the Euler summation formula, relates a finite sum of function values to an integral of the function plus correction terms involving its derivatives at the endpoints. It was developed independently by Leonhard Euler and Colin Maclaurin around 1735.

Formula

In one common form, for integers m < n and a sufficiently smooth function f,

Σk=mn f(k) = ∫mn f(x) dx + (f(n) + f(m))/2 + Σk≥1 (B2k/(2k)!) [f(2k−1)(n) − f(2k−1)(m)] + R,

where the B2k are Bernoulli numbers and R is an error term expressed with periodic Bernoulli functions. The series is generally asymptotic rather than convergent.

Applications

The formula underlies high-precision calculations of sums and constants (Euler used it to evaluate ζ(2k) values and estimate the Euler–Mascheroni constant), the asymptotic expansion of factorials behind Stirling's approximation, and error analysis of the trapezoidal rule in numerical integration.
