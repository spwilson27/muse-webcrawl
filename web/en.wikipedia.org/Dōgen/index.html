Dōgen, founder of the Sōtō school in Japan

Dōgen Zenji (道元禅師; 26 January 1200 – 29 September 1253), also known as Eihei Dōgen, was a Japanese Buddhist priest, writer and philosopher who founded the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan. Originally ordained as a monk in the Tendai school, he travelled to China in 1223 and trained under the Caodong master Rujing, receiving Dharma transmission. On returning to Japan he promoted shikantaza ("just sitting") meditation and composed the Shōbōgenzō, regarded as one of the masterpieces of Japanese religious literature and philosophy.

Life

Born in Kyoto to an aristocratic family, Dōgen was ordained on Mount Hiei at thirteen but became dissatisfied with Tendai teaching on original enlightenment. He studied under Eisai's successor Myōzen and accompanied him to China, where after initial disappointments he attained awakening under Rujing at Tiantong monastery, on hearing the instruction to "drop off body and mind" (shinjin datsuraku). Back in Japan, he attracted disciples at Kōshō-ji in Kyoto, then withdrew in 1243 to the remote mountains of Echizen province, where his patron Hatano Yoshishige offered land for the monastery that became Eihei-ji. He died in Kyoto in 1253.

Thought

Dōgen's writings stress the unity of practice and enlightenment: zazen is not a means to Buddhahood but its expression. His essays on time (Uji, "being-time"), impermanence, and the Buddha-nature of all beings — including grasses, trees and tiles — radically reinterpret Chinese Chan sources in dense, poetic Japanese.
