The Ashina (Chinese: 阿史那; pinyin: Āshǐnà) were a tribe and the ruling dynasty of the ancient Turkic peoples. They rose to prominence in the mid-6th century, when their leader Bumin Qaghan revolted against the Rouran Khaganate and founded the First Turkic Khaganate in 552. For two centuries the Ashina supplied the khagans of the eastern and western Turkic empires that dominated the Eurasian steppe.

Origins and legends

Chinese histories preserve origin legends of the Ashina, including the tale of a boy raised by a she-wolf (Asena), who became the ancestor of the tribe — a wolf standard became the emblem of the khagan's guards. The Ashina were famed as blacksmiths, a craft they practised for the Rouran before their revolt, based around the Altai Mountains ("the Golden Mountains").

The khaganates

Under Bumin and his brother Istämi the khaganate split into eastern and western wings spanning from Manchuria to the Black Sea. After a period of Tang Chinese domination, the Ashina prince Kutlug restored the empire as the Second Turkic Khaganate (682). His successors Bilge Khagan and Kul Tigin left the Orkhon inscriptions, the oldest surviving texts in a Turkic language, written in Old Turkic runiform script. The Ashina also ruled among the Khazars and other successor peoples.
