Messenger RNA

Messenger RNA (mRNA) is a single-stranded molecule of RNA that corresponds to the genetic sequence of a gene and is read by a ribosome in the process of synthesising a protein. It carries the genetic information copied from DNA in the form of a series of three-base code words, each of which specifies a particular amino acid.

Life cycle

mRNA is created during transcription, when the enzyme RNA polymerase converts a gene's DNA sequence into a complementary RNA molecule. In eukaryotes this precursor is processed — a 5' cap is added, introns are spliced out, and a poly(A) tail is attached — before the mature mRNA leaves the nucleus. In the cytoplasm, ribosomes translate its coding sequence into protein, after which the mRNA is degraded, sometimes within minutes, so that protein production can be tightly regulated.

Medical applications

Synthetic mRNA became a major medical technology through mRNA vaccines, notably the Pfizer–BioNTech and Moderna COVID-19 vaccines authorised in 2020, which deliver lipid-nanoparticle-encapsulated mRNA instructing cells to produce a viral antigen. The foundational work of Katalin Karikó and Drew Weissman on nucleoside-modified mRNA was recognised with the 2023 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. mRNA is also being investigated for cancer immunotherapy, protein replacement, and gene editing.
