Journal (academic)

An academic journal or scholarly journal is a periodical publication in which scholarship relating to a particular academic discipline is published. Academic journals serve as permanent, transparent forums for the presentation, scrutiny, and discussion of research, usually subject to peer review before publication.

History

The first academic journals appeared in 1665: the French Journal des sçavans and the English Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society, established by Henry Oldenburg. From these beginnings, scholarly publishing grew into an industry of tens of thousands of journals. Specialist journals proliferated in the 19th and 20th centuries as disciplines fragmented, and electronic publishing since the 1990s has transformed access and distribution.

Publishing process and prestige

Authors submit manuscripts to an editor, who sends promising papers to anonymous referees; acceptance, revision, or rejection follows. Journals build reputations through selectivity and citation impact, measured informally and formally (e.g., the impact factor). Articles typically include an abstract, literature review, methodology, results, and references. Open access journals make articles freely available, while traditional journals rely on subscriptions; predatory journals that charge fees without genuine peer review have become a recognized problem.
