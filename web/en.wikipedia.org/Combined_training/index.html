Combined training

Combined training, more commonly known as eventing, is an equestrian competition in which a single horse and rider combination competes in three disciplines: dressage, cross-country and show jumping. It is sometimes described as an equestrian triathlon and is an Olympic sport governed internationally by the Fédération Équestre Internationale (FEI).

Phases

The dressage phase tests the horse's obedience, suppleness and precision of movement in an arena. The cross-country phase, the heart of the sport, requires horse and rider to gallop a course of solid, natural fences over varied terrain within an optimum time, incurring penalties for refusals and falls. The final show jumping phase tests fitness and accuracy over a course of knockable fences in an arena; penalty scores from all phases are combined to determine the winner, with the lowest score winning.

Formats and history

Competitions run over one to three days; the classic long format formerly included roads-and-tracks and steeplechase phases, now retained mainly at lower levels. Eventing grew out of cavalry officer training in the 19th and early 20th centuries, and entered the Olympic Games in 1912 at Stockholm, initially restricted to military officers. Germany, Great Britain, Australia and the United States are among the strongest eventing nations.
