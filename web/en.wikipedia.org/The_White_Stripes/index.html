The White Stripes were an American rock duo formed in Detroit, Michigan, in 1997, consisting of Jack White (songwriter, vocals, guitar, piano) and Meg White (drums, vocals). They became leading figures of the 2000s garage rock revival with their minimalist blues-influenced sound and red, white, and black colour scheme.

History

After two underground albums, The White Stripes (1999) and De Stijl (2000), the band broke through with White Blood Cells (2001) and the single "Fell in Love with a Girl". Their fourth album, Elephant (2003), was a major commercial and critical success, containing the riff-driven anthem "Seven Nation Army", which became a global stadium chant. Get Behind Me Satan (2005) and Icky Thump (2007) followed, each winning the Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album. The duo dissolved in 2011 to preserve what was beautiful about the band. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2025.
