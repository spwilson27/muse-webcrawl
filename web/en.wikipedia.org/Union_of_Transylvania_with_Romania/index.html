Union of Transylvania with Romania
Declared: 1 December 1918
Location: Alba Iulia
International recognition: Treaty of Trianon (1920)

The union of Transylvania with Romania was declared on 1 December 1918 by the Great National Assembly of Alba Iulia, an assembly of 1,228 elected representatives of the ethnic Romanians of Transylvania, the Banat, Crișana, and Maramureș. The resolution proclaimed the union of these territories, previously part of Austria-Hungary, with the Kingdom of Romania.

Background and aftermath

Romania had entered the First World War in 1916 seeking these territories, and the collapse of Austria-Hungary in late 1918 opened the way for the assembly. Romanian troops entered Transylvania in the following months. The union was internationally recognised by the Treaty of Trianon in 1920. The day of 1 December is now celebrated as Romania's national day, Great Union Day.
