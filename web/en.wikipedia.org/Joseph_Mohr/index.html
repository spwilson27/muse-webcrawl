Joseph Franz Mohr (December 11, 1792 – December 4, 1848) was an Austrian Roman Catholic priest and writer who authored the words of the Christmas carol "Silent Night".

Life

Born in Salzburg, the illegitimate son of a soldier and a knitter, Mohr was sponsored through seminary by the cathedral vicar Johann Hiernle and ordained in 1815 despite canonical obstacles. His first posting was at Mariapfarr in the Lungau region, where in 1816 he wrote the six verses of Stille Nacht, heilige Nacht.

Silent Night

Serving as assistant priest at Oberndorf in 1818, Mohr asked the schoolmaster and organist Franz Xaver Gruber to compose a melody for his poem, reportedly because the church organ was damaged. The two men performed the new carol, accompanied by guitar, at midnight Mass on Christmas Eve 1818 in St Nicholas Church, Oberndorf. Travelling singing families carried the song across Europe and America; Mohr himself, who served in several alpine parishes and died at Wagrain in 1848, never profited from the carol that made his words immortal.
