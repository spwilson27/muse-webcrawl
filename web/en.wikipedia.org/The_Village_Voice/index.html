The Village Voice

The Village Voice is an American news and culture publication based in New York City. Founded in 1955 by Dan Wolf, Ed Fancher and Norman Mailer, it became the first alternative newsweekly in the United States and a defining voice of downtown New York's arts, politics and counterculture.

History and content

The Voice pioneered free-form, personal journalism and was central to the development of rock criticism, underground comics and investigative reporting on city politics. Its writers included Mailer himself, Nat Hentoff, Robert Christgau, J. Hoberman and many others. It was long distributed free in New York and won three Pulitzer Prizes over its history.

Print closure and revival

Facing declining advertising, the paper ended its print edition in 2017 and ceased regular publication in 2018. In 2021 it returned to print as a quarterly under new ownership, while continuing to publish online. The Voice remains a landmark of American alternative journalism.
