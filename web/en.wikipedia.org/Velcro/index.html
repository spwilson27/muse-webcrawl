Velcro

Velcro
InventorGeorge de Mestral (1941 idea; patented 1955)
TypeHook-and-loop fastener
CompanyVelcro Companies

Velcro is a brand of hook-and-loop fasteners, consisting of two components: a strip of fabric with tiny hooks and another with smaller loops; when pressed together the hooks catch in the loops and the two fasten securely, yet can be peeled apart. The name is a portmanteau of the French words velours ("velvet") and crochet ("hook").

Invention

Swiss engineer George de Mestral conceived the fastener in 1941 after examining burdock burrs stuck to his dog's fur under a microscope and seeing their tiny hooks. He patented the invention in 1955 and refined mass production — initially in cotton, later in nylon and polyester — founding the Velcro company. NASA's use of the fastener in the Apollo program greatly raised its profile.

Uses

Hook-and-loop fasteners are used in clothing and shoes, medical devices, automotive and aircraft interiors, cable management, and countless household products. "Velcro" is a registered trademark that the company defends against generic use, though the term is widely used generically for the fastener type.
