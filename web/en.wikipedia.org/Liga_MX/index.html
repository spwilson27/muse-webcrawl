Liga MX

Liga MX, known as Liga BBVA MX for sponsorship reasons, is the top professional football division of the Mexican football league system. Founded in 1943 as the Liga Mayor, it is contested by 18 clubs and is generally considered the strongest league in North America and among the strongest outside Europe and South America.

Format

The season is divided into two short tournaments, the Apertura (opening) and Clausura (closing), each followed by a playoff phase known as the Liguilla, in which the leading teams contest a knockout competition to decide the champion. Promotion and relegation with the second division was suspended in 2020 for a period of years as part of a restructuring.

Clubs and rivalries

Historically successful clubs include América, Guadalajara (Chivas), Cruz Azul, and Toluca. América and Guadalajara contest the Súper Clásico; Guadalajara traditionally fields only Mexican players. Mexican clubs have dominated the CONCACAF Champions Cup/Champions League, with América among its most successful teams.

Popularity

Liga MX regularly has among the highest average attendances of any football league in the Americas and a large television audience in both Mexico and the United States.
