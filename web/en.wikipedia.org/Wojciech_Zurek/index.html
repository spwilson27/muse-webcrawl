Wojciech Zurek

Wojciech Hubert Zurek (born 1951) is a Polish-born American theoretical physicist and a leading authority on quantum theory, especially the foundations of quantum mechanics. He is a scientist at Los Alamos National Laboratory in New Mexico. Zurek's work on decoherence and the quantum-to-classical transition has been central to modern understanding of how classical reality emerges from quantum physics.

Career and research

Zurek studied at the AGH University of Science and Technology in Kraków, earning his PhD in 1979, and moved to the United States in the 1980s, working at the University of Texas at Austin and the California Institute of Technology before joining Los Alamos. With William Wootters he proved the no-cloning theorem (1982), which shows that an arbitrary unknown quantum state cannot be perfectly copied — a foundational result for quantum information science.

He is a principal developer of the theory of einselection (environment-induced superselection) and quantum decoherence, explaining how interaction with the environment selects preferred "pointer states" and suppresses quantum superpositions at macroscopic scales. He later developed Quantum Darwinism, the idea that the classical objectivity of certain states arises because information about them is redundantly copied into the environment. Zurek also co-invented the Kibble–Zurek mechanism, which predicts the density of defects formed when a system passes rapidly through a continuous phase transition, with applications from cosmology to condensed matter.

Recognition

Zurek has received numerous honours, including the Albert Einstein Medal (2010) and election to the U.S. National Academy of Sciences (2014).
