Overtourism

Overtourism is the congestion or overcrowding caused by an excess of tourists, resulting in conflicts with locals, pressure on infrastructure, environmental damage, and the degradation of the visitor experience itself. The term became widespread in the late 2010s as visitor numbers to destinations such as Venice, Barcelona, Amsterdam, and Bali surged.

Causes

Contributing factors include cheap air travel, cruise tourism, short-term rental platforms such as Airbnb, social-media-driven popularity of particular sites, and the growth of the global middle class. Critics note that tourism promotion has often prioritised arrival numbers over sustainability.

Responses

Responses include tourist taxes and entry fees (such as Venice's day-tripper fee), caps on cruise ships and holiday rentals, marketing that disperses visitors to less-visited areas, and resident protests demanding housing protections. The COVID-19 pandemic abruptly halted tourism in 2020, prompting debate about rebuilding the industry on more sustainable terms.
