Lambretta is a brand of motor scooters originally manufactured in Milan, Italy, by Innocenti. Alongside the rival Vespa, the Lambretta became one of the defining scooters of the post-war era and an icon of 1960s mod culture in Britain.

History

Ferdinando Innocenti, whose company made steel tubing, commissioned a cheap vehicle to help mobilise post-war Italy. The first Lambretta, the Model A, appeared in 1947, named after the Lambro river near the factory. Unlike the Vespa, Lambrettas had the engine mounted near the rear wheel on a tubular frame, with shaft or chain drive and fully enclosed bodywork that evolved through the popular LI and TV series of the 1950s and 1960s.

Legacy

Production in Italy ended in 1971, but Lambrettas continued to be built under licence in India by Scooters India Ltd (which produced the Vijai Super) into the 1990s. The marque has been revived in the modern era, and classic Lambrettas remain prized by scooterists and collectors worldwide.
