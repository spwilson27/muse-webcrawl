First Carlist War

Date1833–1840
LocationSpain
ResultLiberal (Isabeline) victory
BelligerentsLiberals supporting Isabella II vs. Carlists supporting Infante Carlos

The First Carlist War was a civil war in Spain fought from 1833 to 1840 over the succession to the Spanish throne and the nature of the Spanish state. It pitted the forces of the regent Maria Christina, governing for the child queen Isabella II, against the supporters of Infante Carlos, Count of Molina, brother of the late Ferdinand VII.

Causes

Ferdinand VII had abolished the Salic law barring female succession by the Pragmatic Sanction of 1830, allowing his daughter Isabella to succeed him. Carlos and his supporters, known as Carlists, rejected this and upheld traditionalist, absolutist principles against the liberal reforms of the regency government.

Course of the war

Fighting was concentrated in the Basque Country, Navarre, Catalonia, and the Maestrazgo. The Carlist general Tomás de Zumalacárregui won early victories in the north before dying at the siege of Bilbao in 1835. The war was marked by guerrilla warfare and atrocities on both sides, drawing in foreign volunteers and support: Britain, France, and Portugal backed the liberals under the Quadruple Alliance, while the Carlists received sympathy and some aid from conservative powers.

End and legacy

The war effectively ended with the Convention of Vergara on 31 August 1839, negotiated between the liberal general Baldomero Espartero and the Carlist general Rafael Maroto. Carlist forces in the east under Ramón Cabrera held out until 1840. Carlism remained a force in Spanish politics, sparking two further Carlist wars in the nineteenth century.
