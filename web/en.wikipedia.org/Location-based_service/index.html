Location-based service

A location-based service (LBS) is a general term denoting software services that use geographic data and information to control features or provide information tailored to a user's position.

How it works

Position is obtained from satellite navigation (such as GPS), mobile network cell identification, Wi-Fi positioning or Bluetooth beacons. Services then use that position for mapping and navigation, local search, ride-hailing, location-based advertising, social check-ins, gaming such as augmented-reality games, asset tracking and emergency services location.

History

Early LBS appeared on feature phones in the early 2000s, but the category expanded rapidly with smartphones after 2007, when built-in GPS and app stores made services like Google Maps, Foursquare and later ride-hailing apps widely available.

Privacy

Because location data can reveal home, work and habits, LBS raises notable privacy concerns. Regulations and platform permission systems increasingly require user consent, limit background tracking and govern retention of location histories.
