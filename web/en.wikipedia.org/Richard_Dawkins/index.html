Richard Dawkins

Clinton Richard Dawkins (born 26 March 1941) is a British ethologist, evolutionary biologist and author. He is best known for his 1976 book The Selfish Gene, which popularised the gene-centred view of evolution and coined the term meme.

Life and career

Dawkins was born in Nairobi, Kenya, and educated at the University of Oxford, where he studied zoology under Nikolaas Tinbergen. He taught at the University of California, Berkeley before returning to Oxford, where from 1995 to 2008 he was the first Charles Simonyi Professor for the Public Understanding of Science.

Work

His books include The Extended Phenotype (1982), The Blind Watchmaker (1986), Climbing Mount Improbable (1996), Unweaving the Rainbow (1998), The Ancestor's Tale (2004) and The Greatest Show on Earth (2009). In The God Delusion (2006) he argued against belief in a supernatural creator and became a leading figure of the New Atheism movement. He founded the Richard Dawkins Foundation for Reason and Science in 2006.

Views and recognition

Dawkins is an atheist and a prominent critic of creationism and intelligent design. He is a fellow of the Royal Society and the Royal Society of Literature.
