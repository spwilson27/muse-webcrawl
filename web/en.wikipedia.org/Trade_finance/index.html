Trade finance

Trade finance signifies financing for trade, and it concerns both domestic and international trade transactions. A trade transaction requires a seller of goods and services as well as a buyer. Various intermediaries such as banks and financial institutions can facilitate these transactions by financing the trade. Trade finance is the lending of money against the security of goods being traded, and it helps manage the risks, payment, and working capital needs of cross-border commerce.

Instruments

  • Letter of credit — a bank's guarantee that a buyer's payment to a seller will be received on time and for the correct amount, provided shipping documents are presented.
  • Bank guarantee — an assurance by a lender that a borrower's liabilities will be met if the borrower defaults.
  • Factoring — a seller sells its accounts receivable to a third party (factor) at a discount for immediate cash.
  • Forfaiting — the purchase of an exporter's receivables, usually evidenced by bills of exchange or promissory notes, without recourse.
  • Export credit — financing provided with support from export credit agencies to promote national exports.
  • Supply chain finance — programmes allowing suppliers to be paid early against approved invoices of creditworthy buyers.

Risk mitigation

Trade finance reduced the risk of non-payment by exporters and the risk of non-delivery for importers. Instruments are often governed by rules of the International Chamber of Commerce, such as the UCP 600 for letters of credit. The World Trade Organization and the Asian Development Bank have estimated a persistent global "trade finance gap" — unmet demand for financing, disproportionately affecting small and medium-sized enterprises in developing countries — measured in the trillions of US dollars.
