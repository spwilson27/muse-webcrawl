Grigori Yefimovich Rasputin (21 January 1869 – 30 December 1916) was a Russian mystic and faith healer whose influence over Tsar Nicholas II and especially Tsarina Alexandra in the last years of the Russian Empire made him one of the most controversial figures of the era. A peasant from the Siberian village of Pokrovskoye, he converted an early religious experience into a career as a starets (holy man) and wandering pilgrim.

Rise at court

Introduced to the imperial family in 1905, Rasputin won Alexandra's devotion by apparently easing the suffering of the haemophiliac heir, Alexei — episodes modern historians attribute to hypnosis, calm bedside manner, or the withdrawal of aspirin. Alexandra treated him as a saintly intercessor and shielded him against mounting scandal over his debauchery, alleged corruption and, after 1915, his meddling in ministerial appointments while Nicholas commanded at the front. Rumours that he was a German agent or the regime's true ruler fatally corroded the monarchy's prestige.

Assassination

On the night of 29–30 December 1916 (Old Style 16–17 December), a group of nobles led by Prince Felix Yusupov and Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich murdered Rasputin at the Yusupov Palace in Petrograd. Despite later embellishments — poisoned cakes, multiple shootings, drowning — the autopsy indicates he died of gunshot wounds, and his body was thrown into the Malaya Nevka River. He was killed less than three months before the February Revolution ended the Romanov dynasty. Rasputin endures in popular culture, from Boney M.'s 1978 disco hit "Rasputin" to repeated portrayals as a sinister mystic.
