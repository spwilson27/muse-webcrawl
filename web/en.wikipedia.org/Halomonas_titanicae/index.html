Halomonas titanicae

DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota
GenusHalomonas
Discovered2010, from rusticles on RMS Titanic

Halomonas titanicae is a species of Gram-negative, halophilic bacterium discovered in rusticles recovered from the wreck of RMS Titanic. The species was formally described in 2010 by researchers from Dalhousie University and other institutions, working with samples collected from the wreck site.

Description

The bacterium is rod-shaped, motile, and thrives in saline, low-temperature, high-pressure conditions resembling those of the deep ocean. It adheres to steel surfaces and participates in the corrosion of iron, consuming iron and contributing to the formation of rusticles — the icicle-like rust structures hanging from the wreck.

Significance

Because H. titanicae and the wider microbial community on the wreck accelerate the breakdown of its iron hull, scientists have cited such bacteria in predicting that the Titanic wreck will eventually collapse and be consumed. The species has also been discussed in the context of metal corrosion in marine environments and possible applications in the disposal of old ships and oil rigs.
