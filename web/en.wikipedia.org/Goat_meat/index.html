Goat meat

Goat meat
Also calledChevon (adult goat); cabrito (kid goat)
Source animalDomestic goat
Major consumersAsia, Africa, Caribbean, Middle East
CharacteristicsLean, lower fat than beef; distinctive flavour

Goat meat is the meat of the domestic goat. Meat from adult goats is called chevon and that from young goats cabrito. Although less prominent in Western cuisines, goat is by some measures the most widely consumed red meat in the world, being a staple in much of Asia, Africa, the Caribbean and the Middle East.

Culinary use

Goat meat is leaner than beef and lamb, with less fat and cholesterol but a stronger, gamier flavour. It is commonly prepared in slow-cooked dishes: South Asian mutton curries (goat is the usual "mutton" in India), Nigerian pepper soup and suya, Jamaican curry goat, Mexican birria and cabrito al pastor, and Indonesian sate kambing. Kid goat is roasted whole for Easter celebrations in parts of Europe and Latin America.

Production

World production exceeds six million tonnes annually; China is the largest producer and consumer, followed by India, Pakistan and Nigeria. Australia is the largest exporter, much of its output coming from feral (rangeland) goats. In the United States, demand driven by immigrant communities has grown since the late twentieth century.

Nutrition

Per unit weight, goat meat is lower in calories, total fat and saturated fat than beef, lamb or pork, and is a good source of protein, iron and potassium — a profile often cited by nutritionists, though tough cuts require moist, slow cooking.
