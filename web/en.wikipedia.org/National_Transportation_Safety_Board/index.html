National Transportation Safety Board

National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB)
Formed: April 1, 1967
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Type: Independent U.S. federal agency
Jurisdiction: United States transportation

The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) is an independent U.S. government investigative agency responsible for civil transportation accident investigation. Headquartered in Washington, D.C., the NTSB investigates and reports on aviation accidents and incidents, certain highway crashes, ship and marine accidents, pipeline incidents, bridge failures, and railroad accidents. The agency determines the probable cause of accidents it investigates and issues safety recommendations aimed at preventing future occurrences.

History

The NTSB was established in 1967, initially within the U.S. Department of Transportation, consolidating accident investigation functions previously carried out by bodies such as the Civil Aeronautics Board's Bureau of Safety. Congress made the board fully independent through the Independent Safety Board Act of 1974, effective in 1975, so that a single organization could investigate accidents in all modes of transportation without conflicts of interest.

Organization and investigations

The board consists of five members nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate, serving five-year terms; one member is designated chair. For major accidents, the NTSB dispatches a "Go Team" of specialists. Its investigations proceed under the "party system," in which organizations with relevant expertise — operators, manufacturers, regulators, and labor groups — assist under NTSB direction. Reports and a public docket are published at the conclusion of each investigation.

Role and limitations

The NTSB has no regulatory authority and cannot impose fines or rules; its influence derives from the persuasiveness of its safety recommendations, tracked publicly, and from its "Most Wanted List" of transportation safety improvements. By statute, its reports on probable cause are generally inadmissible as evidence in civil lawsuits arising from the accidents investigated.
