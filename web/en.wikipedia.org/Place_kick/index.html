Place kick

A place kick is a kick performed in various forms of football in which the ball is placed in a stationary position on the ground before being kicked. Place kicks are used for kickoffs, conversions, penalty goals, and field goals in rugby and American and Canadian football, where the ball is commonly held upright by a teammate or rested on a kicking tee.

In rugby

In rugby union and rugby league, place kicks are taken for conversions after a try and for penalty goals. The kicker places the ball on a tee or a mound of sand or earth, steps back, and strikes it toward the goalposts. Historically, kickers used a toe-on style; modern kickers almost universally use an instep "round the corner" technique borrowed from association football.

In American and Canadian football

Place kicks occur at kickoffs, extra points, and field goal attempts. For field goals and extra points, a holder kneels seven to eight yards behind the line of scrimmage and holds the ball vertically with its laces facing away from the kicker, while the long snapper delivers the ball. Kickoffs are usually taken from a tee.

In association football

Association football uses place kicks for kickoffs, goal kicks, corner kicks, free kicks, and penalties, although the term is used more loosely since the ball is simply stationary rather than held or teed.
