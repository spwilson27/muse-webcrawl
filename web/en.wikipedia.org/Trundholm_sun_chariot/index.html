Trundholm sun chariot

Datec. 1400 BC (Nordic Bronze Age)
Discovered1902, Trundholm bog, Zealand, Denmark
MaterialBronze, gold
LocationNational Museum of Denmark, Copenhagen

The Trundholm sun chariot (Danish: Solvognen) is a Nordic Bronze Age artifact discovered in Denmark. It is a model of a horse-drawn chariot carrying a large bronze disc covered on one side with gold. Dating to around 1400 BC, it is one of the most important finds from the Nordic Bronze Age and is held by the National Museum of Denmark.

Discovery

The object was unearthed in 1902 in the Trundholm bog on the island of Zealand, when a farmer ploughed it up in pieces. It had apparently been deposited in the bog as a votive offering, deliberately dismantled in antiquity. A missing wheel fragment was later recovered at the site.

Description and interpretation

The chariot consists of a bronze horse standing on four spoked wheels, connected to a large disc mounted on two more wheels; the disc is about 25 cm in diameter. One face of the disc is gilded and decorated with concentric circles and spirals, while the reverse is dark bronze. Scholars interpret the object as a miniature representation of the sun's journey across the sky — the gilded side being the sun by day, drawn east to west, and the dark side the sun returning at night. The ornamentation has also been interpreted as a calendar: counts of the circles in its zones correspond to numbers of months and days.

Context

The sun chariot reflects a sun-centred religious cosmology widespread in Bronze Age Europe, related imagery appearing in Scandinavian rock carvings and on razors and other bronzes of the period.
