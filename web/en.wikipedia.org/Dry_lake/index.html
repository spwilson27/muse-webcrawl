Dry lake

A dry lake is a basin or depression that formerly contained a standing surface lake, which has evaporated and left a flat, often salt- or clay-covered floor known as a playa. Dry lakes form in arid endorheic basins where evaporation exceeds inflow; they may fill briefly after rain and then dry again, leaving an exceptionally flat surface of cracked mud or salt.

Formation and characteristics

Because water spreads thinly and deposits sediment evenly, playa surfaces are among the flattest natural landforms on Earth. Many dry lakes are saline: dissolved minerals concentrate as water evaporates, producing salt flats such as Utah's Bonneville Salt Flats and Bolivia's Salar de Uyuni, the world's largest salt flat, formed from a prehistoric lake.

Uses

The hard, flat, and empty surfaces of dry lakes are used for land-speed records (Bonneville), military and aerospace testing (Groom Lake and Rogers Dry Lake at Edwards Air Force Base in California's Mojave Desert, home to many Space Shuttle landings), and events such as the Burning Man festival on Nevada's Black Rock Desert playa. Ephemeral dry-lake ecosystems can also support specialized organisms that revive when water returns.
