The Allegheny Mountains (informally the Alleghenies) are a mountain range in the eastern United States, part of the vast Appalachian Mountain chain. They run roughly 400 miles from north-central Pennsylvania southwestward through Maryland and eastern West Virginia into southwestern Virginia.

Geography

The range forms part of the drainage divide between the Atlantic Ocean and the Mississippi River basin. Its eastern front, the Allegheny Front, is a major escarpment. The highest point is Spruce Knob in West Virginia at 4,863 feet. The region is heavily forested and includes the Monongahela, Allegheny, and George Washington national forests.

History and economy

The Alleghenies were a formidable barrier to early westward settlement; the National Road and later railroads crossed them with difficulty. The area became a major source of timber and bituminous coal, and today tourism, skiing, and outdoor recreation are important to its economy.
