Good quantum number

FieldQuantum mechanics
CriterionCorresponding operator commutes with the Hamiltonian

In quantum mechanics, a good quantum number is a quantum number that labels an eigenstate of an observable whose value is conserved — that is, whose operator commutes with the system's Hamiltonian. Because commuting operators share a common set of eigenstates, good quantum numbers can be assigned simultaneously to an energy eigenstate and remain constant in time.

Examples

For a free particle or a hydrogen atom in the absence of perturbing fields, total angular momentum and its components can be good quantum numbers when the Hamiltonian has the corresponding symmetry. In atoms with spin–orbit coupling, the individual orbital and spin angular momenta (l and s) cease to be good quantum numbers, while the total angular momentum quantum number j remains good. In molecules, symmetry labels of irreducible representations of the molecular point group similarly give good quantum numbers.

Approximate quantum numbers

If a perturbation weakly violates the symmetry — so the operator only approximately commutes with the full Hamiltonian — the quantum number is "approximately good": states are stable to a good approximation and selection rules are only weakly broken. Symmetry arguments of this kind, including approximate conservation laws such as isospin in nuclear physics, are central to spectroscopy and particle physics.
