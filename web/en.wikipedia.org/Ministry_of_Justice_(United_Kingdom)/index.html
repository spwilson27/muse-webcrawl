Ministry of Justice (United Kingdom)

Formed: 9 May 2007

Headquarters: 102 Petty France, London

Minister responsible: Secretary of State for Justice (also Lord Chancellor)

Jurisdiction: England and Wales (courts); UK-wide for some matters

The Ministry of Justice (MoJ) is a ministerial department of the Government of the United Kingdom, responsible for the justice system of England and Wales, including the courts, prisons, and probation services. It was created in 2007 from the former Department for Constitutional Affairs together with parts of the Home Office.

Responsibilities

The ministry oversees His Majesty's Courts and Tribunals Service, the Prison and Probation Service, legal aid, and the sentencing framework. The Secretary of State for Justice also holds the historic office of Lord Chancellor, with a statutory duty to uphold the rule of law and judicial independence — a role redefined by the Constitutional Reform Act 2005.

Structure

Executive agencies include the Office of the Public Guardian, the Legal Aid Agency, and the Criminal Injuries Compensation Authority. Policy on devolved justice matters in Scotland and Northern Ireland rests with those administrations, while the MoJ retains responsibilities such as human rights policy across the United Kingdom.
