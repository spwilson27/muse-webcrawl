Channelopathy

Channelopathy
SpecialtyNeurology, cardiology, medical genetics
CauseMutations in ion channel genes
ExamplesCystic fibrosis, long QT syndrome, episodic ataxia, periodic paralysis

Channelopathies are a group of diseases caused by disturbed function of ion channel subunits or the proteins that regulate them. Most are genetic disorders arising from mutations in genes encoding sodium, potassium, calcium, or chloride channels, though acquired autoimmune channel dysfunction also occurs.

Examples

Cystic fibrosis results from mutations in the CFTR chloride channel. Cardiac channelopathies such as long QT syndrome and Brugada syndrome predispose to arrhythmia and sudden death. In the nervous system, mutations produce episodic ataxia, familial hemiplegic migraine, several epilepsies, and the periodic paralyses of skeletal muscle.

Significance

Because channelopathies often cause episodic symptoms triggered by exercise, stress, or fever, they illuminate how electrical excitability is tuned in health. Their study has guided drug development: many medicines, from local anaesthetics to anti-epileptics, act directly on ion channels, and cystic fibrosis care was transformed by CFTR modulator drugs.
