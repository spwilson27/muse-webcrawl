Rouge (Chinese: 胭脂扣) is a 1988 Hong Kong romantic drama film directed by Stanley Kwan, adapted from the novel by Lilian Lee. It stars Anita Mui as the courtesan Fleur (Yu-hong) and Leslie Cheung as her lover Chan Chen-pang ("Twelfth Master"). The film is regarded as a classic of Hong Kong cinema and a landmark of the Hong Kong New Wave's later period.

Plot

In 1930s Hong Kong, Fleur, a celebrated courtesan of the Shek Tong Tsui pleasure quarter, falls in love with Chan Chen-pang, the dissolute heir of a wealthy family. Barred from marrying by his family, the lovers plan a suicide pact: they will die together by opium overdose and reunite in the afterlife. Fleur dies, but Chen-pang survives.

Fifty-three years later, Fleur's ghost returns to the Hong Kong of 1987, bewildered by the transformed city, and places a newspaper advertisement seeking her lost lover. She is befriended by a journalist couple, Yuen and Chor, whose own pragmatic modern romance is contrasted with her consuming devotion. As Fleur searches for Chen-pang—now an impoverished, aged extra on film sets—she gradually confronts the truth that he failed to keep their pact, and finally relinquishes her claim on him before departing the world.

Themes and reception

Rouge interweaves ghost story, melodrama and an elegy for old Hong Kong, contrasting 1930s opulence and ritual with the commercial modernity of the 1980s. Anita Mui's performance won her the Best Actress award at the Golden Horse Awards and the Hong Kong Film Awards; the film also earned honours including Best Director and Best Film Editing at the 8th Hong Kong Film Awards. It remains celebrated for its lush period recreation, its Cantonese opera motifs, and the pairing of Mui and Cheung, both of whom died in 2003.
