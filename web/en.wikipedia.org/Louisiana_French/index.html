Louisiana French

Louisiana French (French: français de la Louisiane) is the variety of French spoken in the U.S. state of Louisiana, principally in the southern parishes known as Acadiana. It descends from the French of colonial settlers and of Acadians expelled from Canada in the 18th century.

History and status

French was long the everyday language of much of Louisiana, but compulsory English-only schooling in the 20th century drove a steep decline. The Council for the Development of French in Louisiana (CODOFIL), founded in 1968, has promoted bilingual education and French immersion programmes. Louisiana French is closely related to, but distinct from, Louisiana Creole, and its speakers are closely associated with Cajun culture, music and cuisine.
