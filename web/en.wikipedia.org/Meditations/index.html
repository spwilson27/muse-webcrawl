Meditations (Ancient Greek: Τὰ εἰς ἑαυτόν, Ta eis heauton, literally "things to one's self") is a series of personal writings by Marcus Aurelius, Roman Emperor from 161 to 180 AD, recording his private notes to himself and ideas on Stoic philosophy. Marcus Aurelius wrote the 12 books of the Meditations in Koine Greek as a source for his own guidance and self-improvement.

It is unlikely that Marcus Aurelius ever intended the writings to be published. The work has no official title, "Meditations" being among several titles commonly assigned to the collection; the first printed edition appeared in 1558/1559 from a manuscript now lost. The writings take the form of quotations varying in length from one sentence to long paragraphs, combining aphorisms, reflections on death, nature, reason and duty, and spiritual exercises.

Themes

Recurring themes include the transience of life and fame, acceptance of what lies outside one's control, viewing events "from above" in the perspective of the cosmos, the rational and cooperative nature of humankind, and the discipline of assent — judging impressions correctly before reacting to them. The Meditations remains one of the most widely read works of ancient philosophy and a central document of practical Stoicism.
