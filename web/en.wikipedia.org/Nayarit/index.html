Nayarit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nayarit (Spanish pronunciation: [naʝaˈɾit]), officially the Free and Sovereign State of Nayarit, is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located in western Mexico on the Pacific coast. Its capital and largest city is Tepic. It borders Sinaloa to the northwest, Durango to the north, Zacatecas to the northeast and Jalisco to the south, and includes the Islas Marías archipelago offshore.

History

The region was home to cultures such as the shaft-tomb tradition of West Mexico and, later, Cora (Náayarite) and Huichol (Wixáritari) peoples who resisted Spanish conquest for two centuries; the area takes its name from the Cora chief Nayar. Nayarit was created as a federal territory in 1884 from part of Jalisco and became a state on 26 January 1917.

Geography and economy

The state spans about 27,857 km², with a population of roughly 1.2 million (2020 census). Terrain ranges from coastal plains and mangroves to the Sierra Madre Occidental mountains. Agriculture—tobacco, sugarcane, mango, avocado and coffee—has long anchored the economy, while tourism has grown rapidly along the Riviera Nayarit, the resort coast extending north from Puerto Vallarta through Nuevo Vallarta, Sayulita and Punta Mita.

Culture

Nayarit is known for Huichol bead and yarn art, Cora Holy Week rituals, and coastal seafood cuisine including pescado zarandeado. The Islas Marías, once a federal prison colony, were designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve and opened to limited tourism.
