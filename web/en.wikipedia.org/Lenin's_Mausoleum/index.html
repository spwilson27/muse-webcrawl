Lenin's Mausoleum

Lenin's Mausoleum (Russian: Мавзолей Ленина) is a mausoleum on Red Square in Moscow, Russia, containing the embalmed body of Vladimir Lenin, the founder of the Soviet Union, who died on 21 January 1924. Designed by architect Alexey Shchusev, the stepped pyramid of red granite, porphyry and black labradorite was completed in 1930, replacing two earlier wooden structures. The mausoleum also served as the reviewing stand from which Soviet leaders watched parades on Red Square.

History

Immediately after Lenin's death, a temporary wooden mausoleum was erected in Red Square so mourners could view the body; the decision to preserve the corpse permanently, against the wishes of Lenin's widow Nadezhda Krupskaya, led first to a larger wooden structure and then to Shchusev's stone monument. From 1953 to 1961 the mausoleum also held the body of Joseph Stalin, and the inscription read "LENIN STALIN", until Stalin was removed during de-Stalinisation and reburied by the Kremlin wall. During the Second World War Lenin's body was evacuated to Tyumen for safety.

Preservation

The body is maintained by a dedicated laboratory of scientists (the "Lenin Lab"), which periodically re-embalms it and keeps it under controlled temperature and humidity in a glass sarcophagus. Visitors file past in silence; photography inside is forbidden. After the dissolution of the USSR in 1991, the ceremonial guard was withdrawn in 1993, and periodic public debate has continued over whether Lenin should finally be buried, but the mausoleum remains open as a museum.
