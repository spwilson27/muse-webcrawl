Cimbalom
Classification: struck box zither (hammered dulcimer)
Origin: Hungary

The cimbalom is a type of hammered dulcimer played with cotton-wrapped sticks, strongly associated with Hungarian folk music and Romani ensembles. It consists of a large trapezoidal box with metal strings stretched over it, usually standing on four legs.

Types

Small folk cimbaloms are portable and rest on the player's lap or a table. The concert cimbalom, developed by József Schunda in Budapest in the 1870s, is a fully chromatic instrument with a pedal-operated damping mechanism and a range of about four octaves.

Use in music

The cimbalom is a standard instrument in Hungarian and Slovak folk bands and in Romani music across Central and Eastern Europe. Classical composers including Franz Liszt, Zoltán Kodály (in Háry János), and Igor Stravinsky have written for it, and it appears frequently in film scores to evoke Eastern European settings.
