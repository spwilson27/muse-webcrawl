Mérida (Spanish: [ˈmeɾiða]) is the capital of the autonomous community of Extremadura in western Spain. It was founded in 25 BC as Augusta Emerita, a colony for veteran soldiers of the Roman legions, and preserves one of the largest and best-preserved ensembles of Roman monuments in Europe, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

History

Augusta Emerita became the capital of the Roman province of Lusitania and one of the most important cities of Roman Hispania. After the fall of Rome it passed through Visigothic and Muslim rule — the Alcazaba fortress on the Guadiana river dates from 835 AD — before being reconquered by Alfonso IX of León in 1230. In 1983 Mérida was chosen as the capital of the new autonomous community of Extremadura.

Main sights

The Roman Theatre, built around 15 BC, still hosts the annual Festival de Teatro Clásico; beside it stands the Amphitheatre (8 BC). Other landmarks include the Temple of Diana, the Roman bridge over the Guadiana — one of the longest surviving Roman bridges — the Aqueduct of Los Milagros, the Circus, and the National Museum of Roman Art, designed by Rafael Moneo and opened in 1986.
