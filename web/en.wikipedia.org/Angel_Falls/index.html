Angel Falls

LocationCanaima National Park, Bolívar State, Venezuela
Total height979 m (3,212 ft)
Longest drop807 m (2,648 ft)
WatercourseRío Kerepacupai Merú (Churún River tributary)

Angel Falls (Spanish: Salto Ángel; Pemon: Kerepakupai Merú) is a waterfall in Canaima National Park, Venezuela. It is the world's tallest uninterrupted waterfall, with a height of 979 metres (3,212 ft) and a plunge of 807 metres (2,648 ft). The falls drop over the edge of the Auyán-tepui in the Gran Sabana region.

Name and exploration

The falls are named after Jimmie Angel, an American aviator who was the first person to fly over them and who crash-landed his plane atop the tepui in 1937; the aircraft remained there for decades. The indigenous Pemon name, Kerepakupai Merú, means "waterfall of the deepest place".

Tourism

Angel Falls is one of Venezuela's top tourist attractions, though reaching it requires a flight to Canaima and a river journey, as there are no roads. Flow varies seasonally, and in the dry season the falling water may dissipate into mist before reaching the ground.
