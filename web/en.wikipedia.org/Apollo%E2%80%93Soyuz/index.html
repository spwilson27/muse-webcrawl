Apollo–Soyuz

Mission typeJoint US–USSR docking mission
Launch15 July 1975
Docking17 July 1975
CrewsStafford, Brand, Slayton (Apollo); Leonov, Kubasov (Soyuz 19)

The Apollo–Soyuz Test Project (ASTP) of July 1975 was the first joint spaceflight between the United States and the Soviet Union, in which an American Apollo spacecraft docked with a Soviet Soyuz capsule in Earth orbit. It symbolised the policy of détente and marked the end of the Space Race.

The mission

Soyuz 19, crewed by Alexei Leonov and Valeri Kubasov, launched from Baikonur on 15 July 1975, followed hours later by the Apollo capsule crewed by Thomas Stafford, Vance Brand, and Deke Slayton from Kennedy Space Center. The craft docked on 17 July using a specially designed androgynous docking module. The crews visited each other's spacecraft, conducted joint experiments, and shared a meal before separating after 44 hours docked.

Significance

Apollo–Soyuz was the final Apollo mission and the last crewed US spaceflight until the Space Shuttle in 1981. It set precedents for later cooperation, including Shuttle–Mir and the International Space Station. During re-entry, the Apollo crew was exposed to toxic nitrogen tetroxide fumes but recovered.
