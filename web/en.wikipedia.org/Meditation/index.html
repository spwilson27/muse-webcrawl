Meditation

Meditation
Origins: ancient India, by c. 1500–1200 BCE
Traditions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Daoism, Christianity, secular mindfulness
Techniques: focused attention, open monitoring, mantra, loving-kindness

Meditation is a practice in which an individual uses techniques such as focusing the mind on a particular object, thought, or activity to train attention and awareness and achieve a mentally clear, emotionally calm, and stable state. Meditation is practiced in numerous religious traditions and also as a secular therapy and wellness practice.


Meditation in the lotus posture

History

The earliest references to meditation (Sanskrit dhyana) appear in the Hindu Vedas and Upanishads of ancient India; wall art in the Indus Valley may depict meditating figures as early as 5000 BCE, though this is debated. Meditation was integral to Buddhism (founded by Siddhartha Gautama in the 5th century BCE) and Jainism, spread along the Silk Road into China, where it merged with Daoism to form Chan (Zen) Buddhism, and reached Japan and the West. Contemplative prayer in Christianity (such as hesychasm and Lectio Divina) and Sufi practices in Islam are parallel traditions. In the 20th century, figures such as Swami Vivekananda, Maharishi Mahesh Yogi (Transcendental Meditation), and Jon Kabat-Zinn (mindfulness-based stress reduction, 1979) popularized secular meditation in the West.

Techniques

Researchers commonly distinguish focused attention meditation (concentrating on the breath, a mantra, or an object), open monitoring (nonjudgmental awareness of present experience, as in mindfulness and vipassana), and practices such as loving-kindness (metta), body scan, and movement-based forms including yoga and tai chi. Secular mindfulness programs typically run eight weeks and combine meditation with group discussion.

Effects and research

Scientific studies associate meditation with modest reductions in stress, anxiety, and pain, and changes in brain regions involved in attention and emotion regulation; evidence quality varies, and adverse effects such as depersonalization are occasionally reported. Meditation is used in clinical settings, schools, prisons, and the military, and consumer meditation apps form a large industry.
