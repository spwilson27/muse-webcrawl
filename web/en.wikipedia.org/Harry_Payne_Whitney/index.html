Harry Payne Whitney (April 29, 1872 – October 26, 1930) was an American businessman, financier, and thoroughbred horse breeder. The son of William Collins Whitney, former U.S. Secretary of the Navy and a leading figure in New York finance and society, he inherited a vast fortune and became one of the most prominent sportsmen of his era.

Early life and career

Whitney was born in New York City, the son of William C. Whitney and Flora Payne, sister of Standard Oil treasurer Oliver B. Payne. He attended Groton School and Yale University, graduating in 1894, and then studied at Columbia Law School. He managed parts of the family's extensive business interests and served on the boards of numerous corporations, including mining ventures in which Guggenheim interests were involved.

Marriage

In 1896 he married Gertrude Vanderbilt, daughter of Cornelius Vanderbilt II, uniting two of America's great fortunes. The couple had three children: Flora, Cornelius Vanderbilt Whitney, and Barbara. Gertrude became a noted sculptor and art patron, founding the Whitney Museum of American Art in 1930, the year of her husband's death.

Horse racing and sport

Whitney was a leading owner and breeder of Thoroughbred racehorses. His stable, based at his Brookdale Farm in New Jersey and later in Kentucky, won many major races; his filly Regret became the first filly to win the Kentucky Derby in 1915, and his horses won the Preakness Stakes several times. He was a ten-goal-rated polo player in his youth — among the first Americans rated at ten goals — and a successful yachtsman. Whitney was elected to the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1955 as one of the inaugural "Pillars of the Turf."

He died of pneumonia in New York on October 26, 1930, aged 58.
