Renaissance Italy

Renaissance Italy refers to the Italian peninsula during the period of the Italian Renaissance, roughly from the late 13th century to the 16th century. Italy was then divided among numerous city-states and principalities — including Florence, Venice, Milan, the Papal States, and the Kingdom of Naples — whose wealth, rivalries, and patronage made the peninsula the birthplace of Renaissance art, architecture, literature, science, and humanist scholarship.

Florence, under Medici patronage, became the early centre of the Renaissance.

Background

Italy's advantage rested on its urban wealth, derived from Mediterranean trade and banking; its dense network of communes; and its inheritance of Roman antiquity, visible everywhere in ruins and inscriptions. After the catastrophe of the Black Death (1348), which killed perhaps a third of the population, the surviving city-states recovered and competed fiercely in commerce, war, and cultural display. The Peace of Lodi (1454) established a balance of power among the major states that held until the French invasion of 1494.

Culture

Renaissance humanism, inspired by Petrarch and developed by scholars such as Leonardo Bruni and Lorenzo Valla, revived classical Latin and Greek learning. In art, Brunelleschi's dome for Florence Cathedral, the paintings of Masaccio, Botticelli, Leonardo da Vinci, Raphael, and Michelangelo, and the architecture of Alberti and Bramante transformed European visual culture. Machiavelli's The Prince and Castiglione's The Book of the Courtier redefined political and social thought, while the Medici, Sforza, Gonzaga, and Este courts competed as patrons.

Italian Wars and decline

The French invasion of 1494 opened the Italian Wars, in which France, Spain, and the Holy Roman Empire fought over the peninsula for six decades. The Sack of Rome in 1527 was a psychological blow from which the Renaissance never fully recovered, and by the Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis (1559) much of Italy had fallen under Spanish Habsburg dominance. The cultural movement Italy had created, however, spread across Europe.
