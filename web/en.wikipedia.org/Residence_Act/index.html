Residence Act

The Residence Act (Gesetz über den Aufenthalt, die Erwerbstätigkeit und die Integration von Ausländern im Bundesgebiet, commonly Aufenthaltsgesetz or AufenthG) is the principal German federal statute governing the entry, residence, employment, and integration of foreign nationals in Germany.

Details
Country: Germany
Enacted as part of: Immigration Act
Effective: 1 January 2005
Applies chiefly to: Third-country nationals

Scope

The Act generally governs nationals of countries outside the European Union whose movement is not covered by the separate Freedom of Movement Act for EU citizens and their families. It sets conditions for visas and residence permits, family reunification, study, employment, self-employment, humanitarian residence, settlement permits, and permanent EC long-term residence.

Principles

Residence is usually tied to a stated purpose. A foreign national must normally possess a passport, secure subsistence, clarified identity, and no overriding public-interest reason for expulsion. Authorities may attach conditions concerning employment or place of residence. The Act also provides for integration courses, including language and orientation instruction.

Residence titles

Titles include temporary residence permits, the EU Blue Card for qualified employment, a settlement permit for long-term residents, and a permanent EU residence permit. Amendments have progressively introduced points of access for skilled workers, jobseekers, recognition of foreign qualifications, and people already participating in German education or employment.

Administration and enforcement

Local foreigners authorities decide most applications, while German missions abroad issue entry visas and the Federal Office for Migration and Refugees handles asylum procedures under separate legislation. The Residence Act also contains provisions on departure obligations, detention pending deportation, entry bans, and penalties. It operates alongside the Asylum Act, the Schengen rules, EU directives, and constitutional and human-rights protections.
