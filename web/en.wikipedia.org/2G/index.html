2G is the second generation of mobile network technology. Launched commercially on the GSM standard in Finland in 1991, 2G replaced the analogue 1G networks of the 1980s and introduced digitally encrypted phone conversations, more efficient use of the radio spectrum, and data services such as SMS text messaging.

Technology

Unlike 1G, which used analogue signals, 2G networks transmit voice as digital data. The dominant standard, GSM, used TDMA multiplexing and became the most widely adopted mobile system in history, reaching billions of subscribers. In North America, cdmaOne (IS-95) and the digital successor to AMPS, D-AMPS, were also deployed; Japan used PDC, and Motorola's iDEN served specialised markets.

Data services

Early 2G offered only circuit-switched data at speeds of about 9.6 kbit/s. Packet-switched enhancements followed: GPRS (often called 2.5G) provided always-on data at tens of kbit/s, and EDGE (2.75G) raised peak rates to a few hundred kbit/s. 2G also popularised prepaid subscriptions and, in Japan, early mobile internet services.

Decline

From the 2010s, operators began switching off 2G networks to refarm spectrum for 4G and 5G, although GSM remained in service in many countries for machine-to-machine communications and basic voice coverage well into the 2020s.
