William of Rubruck (c. 1220 – c. 1293), also known as Willem van Ruysbroeck, was a Flemish Franciscan missionary and explorer whose account of his journey to the Mongol Empire is one of the great medieval travel narratives.

Journey

Sent by King Louis IX of France, Rubruck traveled from Constantinople across the Black Sea and the Eurasian steppe in 1253–1255 to the court of Möngke Khan at Karakorum. His mission — part diplomacy, part inquiry into rumors of Christian Mongols — produced a detailed report describing Mongol customs, religions, geography, and the diverse peoples of Central Asia, including an interfaith debate at the khan's court. His account corrected European misconceptions, noting for example that the Caspian Sea is an inland lake.
