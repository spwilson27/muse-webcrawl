Mukden Palace

The Mukden Palace (Chinese: 盛京故宫; pinyin: Shèngjīng Gùgōng), or Shenyang Imperial Palace, is the former imperial palace of the early Manchu-led Qing dynasty in Shenyang, Liaoning, China. It was built in 1625 by the Later Jin khan Nurhaci and used by his successor Huang Taiji until the Qing capital moved to Beijing in 1644.

In 2004 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as an extension of the 'Imperial Palaces of the Ming and Qing Dynasties' site.

Layout

Covering about 60,000 square metres with more than 300 rooms, the palace combines Manchu, Han Chinese, Mongol and Tibetan architectural features. It is organised in three sections along a central axis.

The eastern section, built by Nurhaci, is dominated by the octagonal Dazheng Hall (Hall of Great Affairs), fronted by the Pavilions of the Ten Princes (shiwang ting), where the leaders of the Eight Banners had their offices — a layout reflecting the Manchu banner system.

Later use

After 1644 the palace became a regional palace visited by Qing emperors on eastern tours, and the imperial collections stored there grew. Parts of the imperial treasures were transferred to Beijing in the twentieth century; the palace now functions as the Shenyang Palace Museum.
