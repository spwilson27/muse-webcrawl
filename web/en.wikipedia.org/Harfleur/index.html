Harfleur is a commune in the Seine-Maritime department in the Normandy region of northern France, on the estuary of the Seine near Le Havre. In the Middle Ages it was one of the principal ports of north-western France, before silting of its harbour and the foundation of Le Havre in 1517 eclipsed it.

History

Known in Roman times as Caracotinum, Harfleur flourished as a trading and privateering port; its sailors raided the English coast during the Hundred Years' War. In 1415 Henry V of England besieged and captured the town at the start of his campaign that culminated in the Battle of Agincourt — an episode dramatised in Shakespeare's Henry V, with the famous speech "Once more unto the breach, dear friends". Harfleur remained in English hands until 1450. Later, Huguenot emigration after the revocation of the Edict of Nantes in 1685 diminished the town's prosperity.

Sights

The flamboyant Gothic church of Saint-Martin, with its tall spire long used as a seamark, is the town's landmark; remains of the medieval ramparts and the old harbour basin also survive.
