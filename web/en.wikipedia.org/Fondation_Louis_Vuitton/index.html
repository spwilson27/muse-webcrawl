Fondation Louis Vuitton

Fondation Louis Vuitton

Location: Bois de Boulogne, Paris, France

Opened: 27 October 2014

Architect: Frank Gehry

Type: Art museum and cultural centre

The Fondation Louis Vuitton, previously known as the Fondation Louis Vuitton pour la Création, is a French art museum and cultural centre sponsored by the group LVMH and its subsidiaries. It is run as a legally separate, non-profit entity as part of LVMH's promotion of art and culture. The museum opened on 27 October 2014, inaugurated by French President François Hollande, in the Bois de Boulogne in the 16th arrondissement of Paris.

Architecture

The building was designed by the Canadian-American architect Frank Gehry, whose design consists of twelve large glass "sails" — some 13,500 glass panels — wrapped around an "iceberg" of white concrete blocks containing the galleries and auditorium. Construction began in 2008 and was completed in 2014 at a cost reported to exceed €140 million. The structure rises to about 48 metres and contains eleven galleries, an auditorium seating 350, and roof terraces with views over Paris and the Bois de Boulogne.

History

The project was initiated by Bernard Arnault, chairman of LVMH, after a visit to the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, also designed by Gehry. The Fondation was formally created in 2006. Under its agreement with the city of Paris, which owns the land, the building and its collections are to pass to the city after 55 years.

Collection and exhibitions

The Fondation presents exhibitions of modern and contemporary art drawn from the corporate collection of LVMH and from major loans. Opening exhibitions featured works associated with artists such as Ellsworth Kelly, Olafur Eliasson, and Gerhard Richter, and the museum has since staged large retrospectives devoted to figures including Jean-Michel Basquiat, Egon Schiele, and the Morozov collection of Russian-owned modern art.
