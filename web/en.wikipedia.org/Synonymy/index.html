Synonymy

Synonymy is the semantic relation between expressions that have (nearly) the same meaning. In linguistics it is treated as one of the core sense relations in the lexicon and as a matter of degree: two expressions may be fully synonymous in all contexts, synonymous only in some contexts, or merely near-synonymous, differing in connotation, register, dialect, or syntactic behaviour.

Degrees of synonymy

Absolute synonymy—complete identity of meaning, connotation, and distribution—is extremely rare and probably unsustainable, since speakers tend to specialise duplicated forms. Classic near-cases, such as everybody/everyone or gorse/furze, still show contextual preferences. Cognitive synonymy requires only shared truth conditions, allowing stylistic differences (die/pass away/kick the bucket). Most dictionary "synonyms" are of this kind.

Tests and criteria

The traditional test is intersubstitutability salva veritate—substitution without change of truth value. Failures of substitution reveal limits: big and large are interchangeable in "a big/large house" but not in fixed expressions such as "my big sister" (elder) or "a large-hearted person". Synonymy is also context-dependent and sense-dependent: bank is synonymous with shore only in its riverbank sense, not in the financial sense.

Synonymy in biology

The term is also a formal concept in biological nomenclature, where two or more scientific names applied to the same taxon are synonyms and priority determines the valid one; see synonym (taxonomy). In botany and zoology the codes define objective synonyms (based on the same type) and subjective synonyms (based on judgement that types belong to the same taxon).

Computational treatment

In natural language processing, synonymy is exploited in query expansion, textual entailment, and word embeddings, where words occurring in similar contexts acquire similar vector representations—an operational echo of the distributional hypothesis. Lexical databases such as WordNet formalise synonymy in synsets, groups of cognitive synonyms expressing a single concept.
