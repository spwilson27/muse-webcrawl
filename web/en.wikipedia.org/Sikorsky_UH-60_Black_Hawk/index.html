Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk

The Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk is a four-blade, twin-engine, medium-lift utility military helicopter manufactured by Sikorsky Aircraft. Sikorsky submitted the S-70 design for the United States Army's Utility Tactical Transport Aircraft System (UTTAS) competition in 1972; the Army designated the prototype as the YUH-60A and selected the Black Hawk as the winner of the programme in 1976, after which it entered service with the Army in 1979.

Design

The UH-60 was designed for survivability and transportability: it fits inside a C-130 Hercules, carries a squad of 11 equipped troops or a 105 mm howitzer, and features crashworthy seats, redundant systems, a ballistic-tolerant fuel system, and a four-bladed main rotor designed to withstand hits from 23 mm shells. It is powered by two General Electric T700 turboshaft engines. Retractable external stores support pylons (ESSS) can add fuel tanks or weapons.

Variants and operators

The basic UH-60A was succeeded by the UH-60L, UH-60M (with fly-by-wire controls and improved engines), and the multi-mission UH-60V. Derivatives include the naval SH-60 Seahawk, the Air Force HH-60 Pave Hawk, the Coast Guard MH-60 Jayhawk, special operations MH-60s, the VH-60 presidential helicopter, and the export S-70 series. More than 4,000 Black Hawks and derivatives have been delivered to the US military and to over two dozen international customers.

Operational history

The Black Hawk first saw combat in Grenada in 1983 and has served in Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia—where two were shot down in the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu—the Balkans, Iraq, Afghanistan, and countless humanitarian relief missions. It remains the backbone of US Army rotary-wing aviation, with service planned for decades to come.
