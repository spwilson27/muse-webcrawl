Einstein field equations

In the general theory of relativity, the Einstein field equations (EFE; also known as Einstein's equations) relate the geometry of spacetime to the distribution of matter within it. They were published by Albert Einstein in 1915.

The equations

Gμν + Λ gμν = (8πG / c4) Tμν

Here Gμν is the Einstein tensor, describing the curvature of spacetime; gμν is the metric tensor; Λ is the cosmological constant; G is Newton's gravitational constant; c is the speed of light; and Tμν is the stress–energy tensor, describing the density and flux of energy and momentum.

Physical interpretation

As the physicist John Archibald Wheeler summarized, "Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve." The equations are a set of ten coupled, nonlinear, hyperbolic-elliptic partial differential equations — a system that is famously difficult to solve except under simplifying assumptions of symmetry.

Solutions and applications

Exact solutions include the Schwarzschild solution, describing a non-rotating black hole; the Kerr solution, describing a rotating black hole; and the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker solution, which underpins modern cosmology and the expanding universe. Gravitational waves, predicted by the equations, were first directly detected by LIGO in 2015.
