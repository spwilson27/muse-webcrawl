Kansas City Southern

Kansas City Southern (KCS) was a railway holding company and transportation business headquartered in Kansas City, Missouri, owner of the Kansas City Southern Railway (KCSR) in the United States and of Kansas City Southern de México. For most of its history it was the smallest of the Class I railroads in North America, with a north–south network running from Kansas City through the Gulf Coast ports of the southern United States and into central Mexico.

History

The enterprise traces to Arthur Stilwell, who in the 1890s built a line from Kansas City to the Gulf of Mexico at Port Arthur, Texas. Reorganised as the Kansas City Southern Railway in 1900, the company later expanded into Mexico after the 1990s privatisation of the Mexican railways, gaining a concession for the Northeast line to Mexico City, Veracruz and the Pacific port of Lázaro Cárdenas — a corridor whose cross-border traffic trade made it an attractive takeover target.

Merger into Canadian Pacific Kansas City

In 2021 Canadian Pacific Railway and Canadian National competed to acquire KCS; CP ultimately prevailed with an agreement worth about US$31 billion. On 14 April 2023 the merger was completed, creating Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC), the first — and so far only — single-line railway linking Canada, the United States and Mexico, and ending KCS's existence as an independent company after more than 120 years.
