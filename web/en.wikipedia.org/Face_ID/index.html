Face ID

Face ID is a facial recognition system designed and developed by Apple Inc. for the iPhone and iPad Pro. It allows users to unlock their device, make purchases with Apple Pay, and sign in to apps using their face as a biometric identifier. Face ID was introduced with the iPhone X in September 2017, replacing Touch ID fingerprint authentication on flagship devices.

Technology

Face ID uses the TrueDepth camera system located in the notch or Dynamic Island of the device. A dot projector casts more than 30,000 infrared dots onto the user's face, and an infrared camera reads the resulting pattern to build a depth map, which is processed by a neural engine in the device's processor. The mathematical model of the face is stored in the Secure Enclave and never leaves the device. Apple states that the probability of a random person unlocking a device with Face ID is approximately one in a million, and the system adapts to gradual changes in appearance such as growing a beard or wearing glasses. A later update added support for unlocking while wearing a face mask on supported models.

Privacy and security

Face ID requires the user's attention — eyes open and directed at the device — by default, reducing the chance of unlocking while asleep. It can be quickly disabled by squeezing the side buttons. Critics and researchers have raised questions about compelled biometric unlocking under law, and Apple has added features such as temporarily requiring a passcode after failed attempts or long periods of inactivity.
