The East China Sea is a marginal sea of the western Pacific Ocean, lying between mainland China to the west, Taiwan and the Taiwan Strait to the southwest, the Japanese island of Kyushu and the Ryukyu Islands to the northeast, and South Korea to the north, where it meets the Yellow Sea. It covers roughly 770,000 square kilometres.

Geography

Much of the sea is shallow, over the continental shelf, but along its eastern edge the Okinawa Trough drops to depths of more than 2,000 metres. The Yangtze River discharges into the sea near Shanghai, carrying huge volumes of fresh water and sediment. The warm Kuroshio Current flows northeastward through the sea toward Japan, supporting rich fisheries.

Disputes and resources

The East China Sea contains oil and natural gas fields and vital shipping lanes, and its delimitation is contested. China and Japan disagree over their exclusive economic zone boundary and over sovereignty of the Senkaku Islands (called Diaoyu in China), administered by Japan but claimed by China and Taiwan. China and South Korea also have overlapping claims involving the Socotra Rock (Ieodo). These disputes have produced periodic diplomatic and coastguard confrontations.
