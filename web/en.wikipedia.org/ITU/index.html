International Telecommunication Union

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU; French: Union Internationale des Télécommunications) is a specialized agency of the United Nations responsible for matters related to information and communication technologies. It was established on 17 May 1865 as the International Telegraph Union, making it one of the oldest international organisations in operation. Its headquarters are in Geneva, Switzerland.

The ITU's activities include allocating global radio spectrum and satellite orbits, developing the technical standards that ensure networks and technologies interconnect seamlessly, and working to improve access to communications in underserved communities worldwide. Its membership comprises 194 member states, along with some 1,000 companies, universities and other organisations as sector members and associates.

Structure

The ITU is divided into three sectors, each managing a distinct aspect of the Union's work:

  • ITU-R (Radiocommunication Sector) — manages the international radio-frequency spectrum and satellite orbit resources, and convenes World Radiocommunication Conferences.
  • ITU-T (Telecommunication Standardization Sector) — develops technical standards, the ITU-T Recommendations, covering everything from telephone numbering (E.164) to video coding (H.264, H.265).
  • ITU-D (Telecommunication Development Sector) — promotes the development and expansion of ICT infrastructure in developing countries.

The Union is governed by the Plenipotentiary Conference, held every four years, which elects the Secretary-General and other officials. Since January 2023 the Secretary-General has been Doreen Bogdan-Martin of the United States, the first woman to hold the office.

History

Founded by twenty European states meeting in Paris to standardise telegraphy, the Union adopted the telephone into its remit in the 1880s and radio in the early twentieth century. It became a specialized agency of the United Nations in 1947 and took its present name in 1934, effective 1937. The ITU played a founding role in the INTELSAT satellite consortium and, in the modern era, in framing global ICT development goals such as the "Connect 2030" agenda.
