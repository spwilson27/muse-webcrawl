Chrétien de Troyes

Chrétien de Troyes (fl. 1160s–1180s) was a French poet of the late 12th century and the first great writer of Arthurian romance. Writing in Old French verse for the courts of Marie de Champagne and Philip of Flanders, he composed five romances about King Arthur and his knights that established many of the enduring themes of the Arthurian tradition, including the love of Lancelot and Guinevere and the quest for the Holy Grail.

Life

Almost nothing is known of Chrétien's life beyond what his works reveal. His name associates him with Troyes in Champagne. He says he wrote Lancelot at the command of Marie de Champagne, daughter of Eleanor of Aquitaine, and he dedicated his unfinished Perceval to Philip of Flanders. He also claims earlier works, including adaptations of Ovid, that have not survived.

Works

  • Erec and Enide (c. 1170) — the first Arthurian romance, balancing love and knightly duty.
  • Cligès (c. 1176) — a Byzantine-flavored romance, partly a reply to the Tristan story.
  • Lancelot, the Knight of the Cart (c. 1177–1181) — introduces Lancelot's adulterous love for Queen Guinevere; finished, Chrétien says, by Godefroi de Leigni.
  • Yvain, the Knight of the Lion (c. 1177–1181) — a knight's fall and redemption, aided by a faithful lion.
  • Perceval, the Story of the Grail (c. 1181–1190) — the first telling of the Grail quest, left unfinished at Chrétien's death.

Influence

Chrétien's romances shaped European literature for centuries: they were translated and adapted into German (by Hartmann von Aue and Wolfram von Eschenbach), Norse, Dutch, and English, and his treatment of courtly love, adventure, and the hero's inner development is often credited with founding the romance genre itself.
