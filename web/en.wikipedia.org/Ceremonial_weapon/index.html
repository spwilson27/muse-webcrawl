Ceremonial weapon

A ceremonial weapon is a weapon made for ceremonial, ritual or display purposes rather than for practical combat. Such objects are often richly decorated, made of precious materials, or deliberately impractical in design.

Examples

Examples range across cultures and periods: Bronze Age ceremonial axes and dirks deposited in rivers, the ceremonial maces of parliament, Japanese tachi worn at court, Vatican Swiss Guard halberds, and the swords used in knighting ceremonies and state occasions, such as the British Crown Jewels' Curtana, the Sword of Mercy. Parade armour of the Renaissance, too elaborate for battle, also falls into this category.

Modern use

Today ceremonial weapons are carried by guards of honour, used in military tattoos and drill displays, and presented as diplomatic gifts or symbols of office. Swords remain part of many military dress uniforms.
