Croydon Airport

Croydon Airport
Location: Croydon, South London
Opened: 1920
Closed: 1959

Croydon Airport was the United Kingdom's main international airport during the interwar period, located in South London on the border of Croydon and Sutton. It opened in 1920 by combining two First World War airfields, Beddington and Waddon, and became the base of Imperial Airways.

History

Croydon pioneered many features of civil aviation, including the first airport control tower, a purpose-built terminal building (opened 1928, in Neoclassical style), and early air traffic control and radio position-fixing. Scheduled services connected London with Paris, Berlin, and onward Imperial routes to India, Africa and Australia. Amy Johnson departed Croydon on her record solo flight to Australia in 1930.

Closure and legacy

After the Second World War Croydon was too small for modern airliners and hemmed in by suburbs; Heathrow and Gatwick took over its traffic, and it closed in 1959. The terminal building survives as Airport House, with a visitor centre preserving its aviation heritage.
