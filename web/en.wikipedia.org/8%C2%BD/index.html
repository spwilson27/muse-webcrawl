8½ (Italian: Otto e mezzo) is a 1963 Italian surrealist comedy-drama film directed by Federico Fellini and starring Marcello Mastroianni. It follows Guido Anselmi, a famous film director suffering from creative block ("director's block") while attempting to make an ambitious science-fiction film, who retreats into memories and fantasies as producers, cast and collaborators press him for work he cannot produce.

Title and structure

The title refers to Fellini's own filmography: it was his eighth and a half film, counting his six features, two short films (as halves) and one co-directed film (as a half). The film moves fluidly between present reality, Guido's childhood memories, and dream sequences, a structure that made it a landmark of modernist, self-reflexive cinema about filmmaking itself.

Reception and legacy

8½ won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and Best Costume Design (black-and-white), was nominated for Best Director, and is consistently ranked among the greatest films ever made, appearing in the top ten of the British Film Institute's 2012 Sight & Sound directors' poll. Its influence extends to works such as Stardust Memories, All That Jazz and the stage musical Nine, which adapted it directly.
