Book of Psalms

Hebrew name: Tehillim (praises)

Number of psalms: 150

Series: Ketuvim (Writings)

Traditional author: David and others

The Book of Psalms (from Greek Ψαλμοί, Psalmoi; Hebrew: תְּהִלִּים, Tehillim, "praises") is a collection of 150 religious songs, prayers, and poems forming part of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament. It is the longest book of the Bible and has served as the principal hymnbook and prayer book of Jewish and Christian worship for more than two thousand years.

Contents and types

The collection is divided into five books, each ending with a doxology. Individual psalms belong to recognisable genres: hymns of praise, communal and individual laments, thanksgiving songs, royal psalms, and wisdom psalms. Superscriptions attribute 73 psalms to David, others to Asaph, the sons of Korah, Solomon, and Moses. The best known include Psalm 23 ("The LORD is my shepherd"), Psalm 51 (the Miserere), Psalm 91, and the longest chapter of the Bible, the acrostic Psalm 119.

Composition

The psalms were composed across many centuries, from the early monarchy to the post-exilic period, and were gathered for use in temple and synagogue worship. Musical directions in the headings refer to instruments and melodies now largely lost.

Use and influence

Psalms are recited in daily Jewish prayer and form the core of the Christian Liturgy of the Hours; metrical psalters shaped Protestant worship, and psalm texts permeate Western music from chant to Handel and Bernstein. See also the Book of Proverbs and the Book of Tobit.
