An Electronic Chart Display and Information System (ECDIS) is a computer-based navigation system that complies with International Maritime Organization (IMO) regulations and can be used as a legal alternative to paper nautical charts. It displays information from electronic navigational charts (ENCs) and integrates position information from GPS and other navigation sensors, such as radar and AIS.

Regulations

The IMO adopted performance standards for ECDIS and amended the SOLAS Convention so that, from 2012 onwards, ECDIS carriage became mandatory in phases for most large commercial vessels on international voyages. A vessel sailing with a type-approved ECDIS using official ENCs and appropriate backup arrangements may navigate without a full folio of paper charts — so-called "paperless navigation".

Functions

ECDIS supports route planning and route monitoring, continuously plots the ship's position, and generates alarms for dangers such as crossing safety contours, deviating from the planned route, or approaching obstructions. Chart data are standardised by the International Hydrographic Organization under the S-57 transfer standard, with the newer S-100 framework succeeding it.

Training

Under the STCW Convention, deck officers must complete generic ECDIS training and type-specific familiarisation. Accident investigations have highlighted the risks of over-reliance on ECDIS and of incorrect settings, making competent training essential.
