SpaceX Dragon 2


Crew Dragon approaching the ISS

The SpaceX Dragon 2 is a class of partially reusable spacecraft developed and manufactured by the American aerospace company SpaceX. It has two variants: Crew Dragon, capable of carrying up to seven astronauts, and Cargo Dragon, an updated version of the original Dragon cargo spacecraft. The vehicle is launched atop the Falcon 9 Block 5 rocket and returns to Earth by splashdown in the ocean.

Development

Dragon 2 was developed under NASA's Commercial Crew Development programme to restore American crewed launch capability after the retirement of the Space Shuttle in 2011. The capsule features SuperDraco abort engines built into its hull, a modern touchscreen cockpit, and the ability to dock autonomously with the International Space Station, unlike the original Dragon, which was berthed using the station's robotic arm.

Flights

The first uncrewed orbital test flight, Demo-1, took place in March 2019. On 30 May 2020, Demo-2 carried NASA astronauts Douglas Hurley and Robert Behnken to the ISS — the first launch of astronauts from American soil since 2011 and the first crewed flight of a commercially built spacecraft. Since then, Crew Dragon has flown regular NASA crew-rotation missions as well as private astronaut missions, including the all-civilian Inspiration4 flight of September 2021, the first orbital spaceflight crewed entirely by private citizens. Cargo Dragon flies resupply missions under NASA's Commercial Resupply Services contracts.

Design

The capsule is about 8.1 metres tall with its trunk and 3.7 metres in diameter. The unpressurized trunk carries cargo and bears the solar arrays and radiator fins. Crew Dragon is designed to be reflown multiple times, and individual capsules have flown several missions.
