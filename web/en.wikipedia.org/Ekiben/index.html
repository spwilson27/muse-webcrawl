Ekiben

Ekiben (Japanese: 駅弁, "station bento") are boxed meals sold at railway stations and on trains in Japan. They are a distinctive part of Japanese rail travel culture, with stations across the country offering ekiben showcasing local specialties and ingredients. Many travellers deliberately plan journeys around trying regional ekiben.

History

Ekiben are generally traced to the 1880s, when boxed meals of rice balls and pickled radish were sold at Utsunomiya Station as railways spread through the Meiji period. As the network grew, station vendors developed increasingly elaborate boxes reflecting local produce — seafood in coastal cities, beef in Yonezawa (whose beef ekiben dates to 1892 and remains famous), and seasonal items elsewhere.

Characteristics

An ekiben typically contains rice with several small portions of cooked and pickled dishes, arranged attractively in a disposable or collectible container. Some come in ceramic or shaped containers (such as the kamameshi pot of Yokokawa), some include self-heating mechanisms, and some are tied to character or regional branding. Prices are usually modest, and the packaging often doubles as a souvenir.

Culture

Large department stores hold annual ekiben fairs (the Keio department store fair in Tokyo, begun in the 1960s, is the best known) drawing huge crowds. Ekiben have inspired guidebooks, collectors, and television coverage, and the concept has spread to Taiwan and France, where ekiben have been sold at Paris stations.
