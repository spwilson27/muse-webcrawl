Ice Bucket Challenge

The Ice Bucket Challenge, sometimes called the ALS Ice Bucket Challenge, is an activity involving the dumping of a bucket of ice water over a person's head, either by another person or self-administered, to promote awareness of the disease amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS, also known as motor neuron disease or Lou Gehrig's disease) and encourage donations to research. It went viral on social media during July and August 2014.

Rules and spread

A participant is nominated, typically on camera, and has 24 hours to film themselves having a bucket of ice water poured over their head, donate to an ALS charity, or both; they then nominate others. The campaign was popularized in the United States by Pete Frates, a former Boston College baseball player living with ALS, and spread through celebrities including Mark Zuckerberg, Bill Gates, and numerous athletes and politicians.

Impact

The ALS Association reported receiving about $115 million in donations during the summer of 2014 alone, compared with a few million in the same period the previous year. Funds helped support research including the discovery of the NEK1 gene's association with ALS. The challenge has been revived in later years for ALS and other causes, and is frequently cited as a landmark example of viral fundraising ("slacktivism" criticism notwithstanding).
