Louise Joy Brown (born 25 July 1978) is an English woman who was the first human to have been born after conception by in vitro fertilisation (IVF). Her birth, following a procedure pioneered in Britain, attracted worldwide media attention and raised hopes for infertile couples around the world.

Conception and birth

Brown's parents, John and Lesley Brown, had been trying to conceive naturally for nine years, but Lesley faced complications from blocked Fallopian tubes. On 10 November 1977, Lesley Brown underwent a procedure, later to become known as in vitro fertilisation, developed by Patrick Steptoe and Robert Edwards. Edwards was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2010 for this work.

Although the media referred to Brown as a "test tube baby", her conception actually took place in a Petri dish. Her younger sister, Natalie Brown, was also conceived through IVF, four years later.

Brown was born at 11:47 pm on 25 July 1978 at Oldham General Hospital, Oldham, by planned caesarean section delivered by registrar John Webster. She weighed 5 pounds, 12 ounces (2.608 kg) at birth.

Later life

In 2004, Brown married nightclub doorman Wesley Mullinder. Dr Edwards attended their wedding. In 2006 she gave birth to a son, conceived naturally. Brown has worked in administrative and shipping jobs in the Bristol area, and has periodically spoken publicly about IVF and its impact.
