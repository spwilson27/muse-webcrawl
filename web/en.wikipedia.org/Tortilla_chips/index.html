A tortilla chip is a snack food made from corn tortillas, which are cut into triangles and then fried or baked. Although first mass-produced in Los Angeles in the late 1940s, tortilla chips are considered a Mexican food. Though usually made of yellow corn, they can also be made of white, blue, or red corn. Tortilla chips are often served with a dip, such as salsa, chile con queso, or guacamole. When not served with a dip, the chips are often seasoned with herbs and spices.

History

The triangle-shaped tortilla chip was popularized by Rebecca Webb Carranza and her husband Mario, who owned the El Zarape tortilla factory in Los Angeles and could cut rejected tortillas from the automated tortilla manufacturing machine and fry them. In 1949, Carranza found that tortillas which came out misshapen could be cut into triangles, fried, and sold for a dime a bag. They were not conceptually new, but putting them into a mass-produced product for sale was the innovation.

Tortilla chips are a staple of Mexican-American cuisine and are the main ingredient of nachos, a dish in which the chips are covered with melted cheese and other toppings.

Production

Commercial tortilla chips begin as corn that is nixtamalized — cooked and steeped in a lime (calcium hydroxide) solution — ground into masa, sheeted and cut into rounds, baked into tortillas, cut into wedges, and finally fried in oil and salted. Some chips are baked rather than fried to reduce fat content.
