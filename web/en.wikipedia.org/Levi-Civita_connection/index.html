Levi-Civita connection

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian geometry (in particular the Lorentzian geometry of general relativity), the Levi-Civita connection is the unique affine connection on the tangent bundle of a manifold which is both torsion-free and metric-compatible. That is, it preserves the (pseudo-)Riemannian metric and has vanishing torsion. The fundamental theorem of Riemannian geometry states that there is a unique connection satisfying these properties.

The connection is named after Tullio Levi-Civita, who developed the tensor calculus machinery of parallel transport around 1917, building on work of Ricci, Christoffel, and Riemann. In the theory of Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian manifolds the term covariant derivative is often used for the Levi-Civita connection. The components of this connection with respect to a system of local coordinates are called Christoffel symbols.

Definition

Let (M, g) be a Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian manifold. An affine connection ∇ is called a Levi-Civita connection if:

  • it preserves the metric, i.e. ∇g = 0 (metric compatibility);
  • it is torsion-free, i.e. for any vector fields X and Y, ∇XY − ∇YX = [X, Y].

Fundamental theorem of Riemannian geometry

The fundamental theorem states that on any (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold there is a unique torsion-free metric connection. The Levi-Civita connection is characterized explicitly by the Koszul formula, which expresses g(∇XY, Z) purely in terms of the metric and Lie brackets of vector fields. In coordinates, its Christoffel symbols are given by

Γkij = ½ gkl ( ∂i gjl + ∂j gil − ∂l gij )

Parallel transport and geodesics

The Levi-Civita connection defines parallel transport of vectors along curves in a way that preserves lengths and angles (inner products). Curves whose tangent vector is parallel along itself are the geodesics of the metric, generalizing straight lines. In general relativity, freely falling test particles follow geodesics of the Levi-Civita connection of the spacetime metric, and the Riemann curvature tensor built from this connection measures tidal effects and enters the Einstein field equations.

Relation to other connections

A general metric connection may differ from the Levi-Civita connection by a contorsion tensor; theories such as Einstein–Cartan theory admit torsion. In the Palatini formulation of general relativity, the connection is treated as an independent variable and the field equations force it to coincide with the Levi-Civita connection of the metric (in the absence of matter spin density).
