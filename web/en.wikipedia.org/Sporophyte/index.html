Sporophyte

A sporophyte is the diploid multicellular stage in the life cycle of a plant or alga which produces asexual spores. This stage alternates with a multicellular haploid gametophyte phase. The zygote produced when a haploid egg cell is fertilised by a haploid sperm germinates into the sporophyte.

Alternation of generations

In land plants, the life cycle alternates between the sporophyte (2n), which produces haploid spores by meiosis, and the gametophyte (n), which produces gametes by mitosis. The relative dominance of the two phases varies: in bryophytes (mosses, liverworts, hornworts) the gametophyte is dominant and the sporophyte grows on it; in vascular plants the sporophyte — the familiar leafy plant or tree — is dominant.

Evolution

The interpolation of a multicellular diploid phase is thought to have allowed land plants to delay meiosis, produce many more spores, and eventually evolve vascular tissue, roots, and seeds.
