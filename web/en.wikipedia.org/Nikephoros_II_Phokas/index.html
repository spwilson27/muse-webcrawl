Nikephoros II Phokas

Nikephoros II Phokas (c. 912 – 11 December 969) was Byzantine emperor from 963 to 969. A brilliant soldier from the great Cappadocian Phokas family, he reconquered Crete, Cyprus and parts of Syria from Arab rule, earning the epithet "the Pale Death of the Saracens".

Reign

As Domestic of the Schools under Romanos II, Nikephoros led the massive 961 expedition that retook Crete after more than a century of Arab occupation, then campaigned in Cilicia and Syria, capturing Aleppo in 962. On Romanos' death he was proclaimed emperor and married the empress Theophano. His reign saw continued eastern victories, including the recovery of Antioch in 969, military and land legislation favouring soldiers, and friction with the western emperor Otto I (whose envoy Liutprand of Cremona left a hostile account) and with Bulgaria.

Assassination

Unpopular in Constantinople for taxes and austerity, Nikephoros was murdered in the palace in December 969 by conspirators led by his nephew and former ally John Tzimiskes, with Theophano's complicity. He was later venerated in some traditions as a soldier-saint figure.
