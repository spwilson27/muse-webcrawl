Hydesville is a hamlet in the town of Arcadia, Wayne County, New York, United States. Despite its small size, Hydesville holds a central place in the history of the religious movement of Spiritualism, as the site of the "Hydesville rappings" of 1848 associated with the Fox sisters.

The Fox sisters and the rappings

In December 1847 the family of John and Margaret Fox moved into a small cottage in Hydesville. On 31 March 1848 the daughters, Kate (aged 11) and Margaret (aged 14), reported mysterious rapping sounds which they claimed were made by a spirit, and demonstrated a code of raps by which the supposed spirit answered questions. The events attracted wide publicity; the sisters were taken to Rochester, where public demonstrations helped launch the modern Spiritualist movement, which spread rapidly through the United States and to Europe. In 1888 Margaret Fox publicly confessed that the rappings had been produced fraudulently, by cracking the joints of her toes, though she later recanted the confession.

Legacy

The Fox cottage was moved in 1916 to Lily Dale, New York, a Spiritualist community, where it stood until destroyed by fire in 1955. A memorial marker and plaque at the Hydesville site commemorate the birthplace of modern Spiritualism, and the date of the first rappings is still observed by Spiritualist churches.
