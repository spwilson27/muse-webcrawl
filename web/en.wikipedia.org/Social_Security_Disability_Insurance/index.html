Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) is a federal insurance program in the United States that pays monthly benefits to workers who become disabled and cannot work, provided they have worked long enough and recently enough in jobs covered by Social Security. It is one of the programs administered by the Social Security Administration (SSA) under Title II of the Social Security Act.

Eligibility

To qualify, an applicant must meet both an earnings test — accumulating enough "work credits" through employment taxed under the Federal Insurance Contributions Act — and a strict medical test. The SSA defines disability as the inability to engage in substantial gainful activity because of a medically determinable physical or mental impairment expected to last at least twelve months or to result in death. Unlike some other systems, SSDI pays no benefits for partial or short-term disability.

Application and appeals

Applications are evaluated initially by state Disability Determination Services. A large share of initial claims are denied, but applicants may request reconsideration and a hearing before an administrative law judge, and may appeal further to the SSA Appeals Council and federal court. Because of backlogs, the process can take many months or years.

Benefits

Benefit amounts are based on the worker's lifetime average earnings, similar to retirement benefits. After receiving SSDI for 24 months, beneficiaries become eligible for Medicare. Certain family members, such as spouses and minor children, may also receive auxiliary benefits. SSDI is distinct from Supplemental Security Income (SSI), a needs-based program for disabled people with limited income and resources regardless of work history.

Financing

SSDI is financed through a dedicated portion of the Social Security payroll tax, held in the Disability Insurance Trust Fund. The program grew substantially in the late 20th and early 21st centuries, prompting periodic debates about its finances, eligibility standards, and role in declining labor-force participation.
