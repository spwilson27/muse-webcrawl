The Mexico national football team (Spanish: Selección de fútbol de México) represents Mexico in men's international football and is governed by the Mexican Football Federation. Nicknamed El Tri ("The Tricolor"), the team plays its home matches at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City.

History

Mexico has qualified for seventeen FIFA World Cups, hosting the tournament in 1970 and 1986 and reaching the quarter-finals on both occasions; it will co-host again in 2026. The team famously endured a long run of round-of-16 exits between 1994 and 2018. Mexico won the FIFA Confederations Cup in 1999, has won the CONCACAF Gold Cup a record number of times, and its under-23 side won Olympic gold at London 2012. Notable players include Hugo Sánchez, Rafael Márquez, Cuauhtémoc Blanco and Jared Borgetti.

Rivalries

Mexico's principal rivalry is with the United States, a fixture often decided in World Cup qualifying and Gold Cup finals, alongside regional rivalries with Costa Rica and Honduras.
