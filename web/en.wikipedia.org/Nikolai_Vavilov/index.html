Nikolai Ivanovich Vavilov (November 25, 1887 – January 26, 1943) was a Russian and Soviet agronomist, botanist and geneticist who devoted his life to improving crop yields and fighting famine. He identified the geographical centres of origin of cultivated plants—now called Vavilov centres—and built the world's largest collection of plant seeds, assembling over 250,000 accessions through expeditions on five continents.

Vavilov directed the Institute of Applied Botany in Leningrad and founded a network of research stations across the Soviet Union. His seed collection was preserved by his colleagues through the Siege of Leningrad, some of whom starved rather than eat the stored seeds.

Persecution and death

Vavilov's defence of Mendelian genetics brought him into conflict with Trofim Lysenko, whose Lamarckian doctrines gained Stalin's favour. Vavilov was arrested in 1940 while on an expedition in Ukraine, sentenced to death (later commuted), and died of starvation in a Saratov prison in 1943. He was rehabilitated after Stalin's death, and his name is honoured by the Vavilov Institute of Plant Industry; his fate became a symbol of the costs of ideological interference in science.
