Gran Valira

Ski slopes of Grandvalira in the Pyrenees

Gran Valira most commonly refers to Grandvalira, the largest ski resort in the Pyrenees and in southern Europe, located in the northeast of the Principality of Andorra near the border with France. The resort was formed in 2003 by the merger of the Pas de la Casa–Grau Roig and Soldeu–El Tarter ski areas.

The resort

Grandvalira offers roughly 210 kilometres of marked runs across sectors including Encamp, Canillo, El Tarter, Soldeu, Grau Roig, and Pas de la Casa, with elevations reaching about 2,640 metres at the top of Pic d'Encampadana. It has hosted Alpine Ski World Cup races, including women's World Cup events at Soldeu in 2012 and 2016 and the World Cup Finals in 2019 and 2023. The resort is a principal driver of winter tourism in Andorra, which receives millions of visitors annually despite its small population.

The river

The name also echoes the Valira, the principal river of Andorra. The Gran Valira is formed by the confluence of the Valira del Nord and Valira d'Orient near Escaldes-Engordany, flows through the capital Andorra la Vella, and continues south into Spain, where it joins the Segre, a tributary of the Ebro. The river is about 35 kilometres long within and beyond Andorra and is the largest river entirely associated with the principality.
