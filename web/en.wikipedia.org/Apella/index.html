Apella

The apella (Greek: ἀπέλλα) was the popular assembly of citizens in the ancient Greek city-state of Sparta. Comparable in some respects to the ecclesia of Athens, it was composed of male citizens over the age of thirty, the Spartiates, who met regularly — traditionally every full moon — outdoors.

Function

The apella voted on proposals (rhetrai) put before it by the gerousia, the council of elders, and the ephors. Citizens could not debate in the manner of the Athenian assembly; instead they approved or rejected proposals, reportedly by shouting, the volume of which was judged by officials. The assembly elected members of the gerousia, ephors and other magistrates, and decided questions of war and peace, treaties, and the release of helots.

Restrictions

According to the Great Rhetra, if the apella chose "crookedly", the elders and kings could adjourn and set aside the decision, which placed significant limits on the assembly's power compared with democratic Athens.
