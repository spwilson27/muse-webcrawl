White Rajah

The White Rajahs were a dynastic monarchy of the British Brooke family, who founded and ruled the Raj of Sarawak, located on the north west coast of Borneo, from 1841 to 1946.

History

The first ruler, James Brooke, was granted the title of Rajah of Sarawak by the Sultan of Brunei in 1841 in reward for helping to restore order in the region. He established an independent state that expanded over subsequent decades, recognised by the United States in 1850 and by Britain in 1864.

James Brooke was succeeded by his nephew Charles Brooke, who ruled from 1868 to 1917 and carried out extensive administrative and territorial expansion. The third rajah, Charles Vyner Brooke, ruled until 1946, when, after the Japanese occupation of Borneo during the Second World War, Sarawak was ceded to Britain as a Crown Colony.

Administration

The Brookes governed through a small European officer corps working with local Malay, Iban and other indigenous leaders. Their rule is notable for suppressing piracy and headhunting, establishing courts and a civil service, and ruling largely through personal paternalism rather than a colonial bureaucracy.

Legacy

The Brooke era remains central to Sarawakian identity. Fort Margherita in Kuching, the Astana palace, and the Sarawak Museum are among the surviving monuments of the period.
