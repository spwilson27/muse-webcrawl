Rasmalai

Rasmalai, also called rasa malai or roshmalai, is a dessert originating in the Bengal region of the Indian subcontinent. It consists of soft, flattened discs of chhena (fresh curd cheese) soaked in chilled, sweetened and flavoured milk (malai), often scented with cardamom or saffron and garnished with slivered pistachios and almonds. The name combines rasa (juice) and malai (cream).

Preparation

Chhena is kneaded and formed into small flat balls, which are first cooked in light sugar syrup, where they puff up, then transferred to thickened sweetened milk. The milk may be coloured pale yellow with saffron and flavoured with cardamom or rose water. Rasmalai is served chilled, typically in shallow bowls, and is customary at festivals and celebrations such as Durga Puja, Diwali and Eid, as well as at weddings.

Origin and variants

The dessert is closely related to rasgulla, and its modern form is often credited to Bengali confectioners in Kolkata in the early twentieth century; the Sen family of sweetmakers is frequently cited. In Bangladesh, the rasmalai of Comilla (Cumilla) — particularly from Matri Bhandar, founded in 1930 — is especially famous and carries regional pride. Variations include kesar (saffron) rasmalai, chocolate and fruit-flavoured versions, and the baked variant sometimes called rasmalai tres leches in fusion cooking.
