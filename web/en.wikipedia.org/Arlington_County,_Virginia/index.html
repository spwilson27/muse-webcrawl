Arlington County, Virginia

Arlington County is a county in the Commonwealth of Virginia, situated on the southwestern bank of the Potomac River directly across from Washington, D.C. With a land area of about 26 square miles (67 km2), it is the smallest self-governing county in the United States by land area, and among the most densely populated and highest-income counties in the country. It has no incorporated towns or cities within its borders.

History

The area was part of the land ceded by Virginia in 1791 to form the District of Columbia. In 1846 Congress retroceded the territory south of the Potomac to Virginia, and it became Alexandria County. Renamed Arlington County in 1920 after Arlington House, the estate of Robert E. Lee's family, it grew rapidly with the expansion of the federal government, the construction of the Pentagon during the Second World War, and postwar suburbanization.

Landmarks and economy

Arlington is home to the Pentagon, Arlington National Cemetery, the Marine Corps War Memorial (Iwo Jima Memorial), and Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport. Its Rosslyn–Ballston and Crystal City corridors are major office districts; Amazon selected the area (branded "National Landing") for its second headquarters, HQ2. The county is frequently cited for "smart growth" planning around Metrorail stations.

Government

Arlington is governed by a five-member County Board elected at large, with an appointed county manager — a form of government in place since the 1930s.
