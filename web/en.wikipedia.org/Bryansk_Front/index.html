The Bryansk Front (Russian: Брянский фронт) was a major formation of the Soviet Red Army during the Second World War — a "front" being the Soviet equivalent of an army group. It was formed, dissolved and re-formed several times between August 1941 and October 1943, operating on the southern flank of the defence of Moscow and later in the offensives westward from the Kursk region.

History

The front was first created on 14 August 1941 under Lieutenant General Andrey Yeryomenko to defend the approaches to Moscow from the southwest. In the opening of the German Operation Typhoon, its armies were largely encircled in the Bryansk pocket in October 1941, though many troops broke out; the front was disbanded in November 1941 and re-formed in December. It fought through the defensive battles of 1942 around Voronezh, where it suffered heavy losses in the opening of the German summer offensive.

In early 1943 the front conducted the Voronezh–Kastornoye offensive, and in July–August 1943, under Colonel General Markian Popov, it took part in Operation Kutuzov, the counteroffensive against the Orel salient following the Battle of Kursk, liberating Orel and Bryansk. On 10 October 1943 the front was disbanded and its forces used to form the headquarters of the Baltic Front (later 2nd Baltic Front).
