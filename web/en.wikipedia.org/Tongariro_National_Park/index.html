Tongariro National Park

Tongariro National Park
Location: North Island, New Zealand
Area: 795.96 km²
Established: 1887
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 1990

Tongariro National Park is the oldest national park in New Zealand, located in the central North Island. It has been acknowledged by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site of both cultural and natural significance — one of the first sites in the world to receive dual heritage status.

Geography

The park encompasses three active volcanic mountains — Ruapehu, Ngauruhoe and Tongariro — at the southwestern end of the Taupō Volcanic Zone. Ruapehu, at 2,797 m, is the North Island's highest peak and hosts the Whakapapa and Turoa ski fields. The Tongariro Alpine Crossing, a 19.4 km one-day trek past emerald lakes and steaming craters, is considered one of the finest day walks in the world.

History

In 1887 the paramount chief of Ngāti Tūwharetoa, Horonuku Te Heuheu Tukino IV, gifted the sacred mountain summits to the Crown to protect them from sale and subdivision — the nucleus from which the park grew. It was formally established in 1894, making it the fourth national park in the world. The peaks remain of profound spiritual significance to Māori.

In popular culture

Mount Ngauruhoe's near-perfect cone served as Mount Doom in Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings film trilogy, and extensive filming for the trilogy took place within the park.
