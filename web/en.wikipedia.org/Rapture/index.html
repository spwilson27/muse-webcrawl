Rapture

In Christian eschatology, the Rapture is the belief that living and dead believers will be "caught up" to meet Jesus Christ in the air at his return. The term comes from the Latin rapturo used in the Vulgate translation of 1 Thessalonians 4:17.

Views and timing

The idea of a distinct rapture event, separate from the general resurrection and Second Coming, was popularised in the 1830s within John Nelson Darby's dispensational theology and spread widely through the Scofield Reference Bible. Believers debate its timing relative to the Tribulation: pre-tribulation, mid-tribulation, post-tribulation, and pre-wrath positions all have adherents. Most historic churches — Roman Catholic, Orthodox, and many mainline Protestants — teach instead a single, visible return of Christ with the resurrection of the dead, and do not use the term.

Cultural impact

The doctrine has been hugely influential in American evangelical culture, inspiring books and films such as Hal Lindsey's The Late Great Planet Earth (1970) and the Left Behind series by Tim LaHaye and Jerry B. Jenkins. Several widely publicised date predictions, most famously Harold Camping's for 2011, have come and gone.
