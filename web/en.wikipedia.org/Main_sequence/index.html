Main sequence

The Hertzsprung–Russell diagram: the main sequence is the prominent diagonal band of stars.

In astronomy, the main sequence is a classification of stars which appear on plots of stellar color versus brightness as a continuous and distinctive band. Stars on this band are known as main-sequence stars or dwarf stars, and the band represents the primary phase of a star's evolution, in which it fuses hydrogen into helium in its core.

The main sequence is prominent in Hertzsprung–Russell diagrams, which plot stellar luminosity against effective temperature. Roughly 90 percent of the stars in the Universe, including the Sun, are main-sequence stars.

Formation and properties

A star forms from a collapsing cloud of interstellar gas and dust. When the core becomes hot and dense enough for hydrogen fusion to begin, the star joins the zero-age main sequence. A main-sequence star is in hydrostatic equilibrium: the outward pressure from fusion balances the inward pull of gravity.

The position of a star on the main sequence is determined almost entirely by its mass. Massive stars are hotter, bluer, and far more luminous; low-mass stars are cooler, redder, and dimmer. A rough mass–luminosity relation, L ∝ M3.5, holds across much of the sequence.

Energy generation

In lower-mass stars like the Sun, energy comes mainly from the proton–proton chain. In stars above about 1.3 solar masses, the CNO cycle (carbon–nitrogen–oxygen) dominates. Mass also determines lifetime: a star of 10 solar masses may exhaust its core hydrogen in tens of millions of years, whereas a red dwarf of 0.1 solar masses can remain on the main sequence for trillions of years — far longer than the current age of the Universe.

Leaving the main sequence

When hydrogen in the core is exhausted, a star leaves the main sequence. Helium ash accumulates in the core, hydrogen fusion migrates to a surrounding shell, and the star expands and cools into a subgiant and then a red giant. The Sun is expected to leave the main sequence in about 5 billion years.

Spectral classification

Main-sequence stars are classified by spectral type O, B, A, F, G, K, and M, from hottest to coolest. The Sun is a G2V star: "V" is the luminosity class denoting main-sequence dwarfs.
