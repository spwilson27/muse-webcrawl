Carbon monoxide poisoning
CauseInhalation of carbon monoxide (CO) from incomplete combustion
SymptomsHeadache, dizziness, weakness, confusion; severe cases: coma, death
MechanismCO binds haemoglobin forming carboxyhaemoglobin
Treatment100% oxygen; hyperbaric oxygen in severe cases

Carbon monoxide poisoning typically occurs from breathing in carbon monoxide (CO) at excessive levels. CO is a colourless, odourless gas produced by incomplete combustion of fuels; common sources include faulty furnaces, gas appliances, fires, vehicle exhaust, and generators used indoors.

Mechanism and symptoms

Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin with roughly 240 times the affinity of oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin and starving tissues of oxygen. Symptoms progress from headache, dizziness, nausea, and confusion to loss of consciousness and death. Survivors may suffer delayed neurological effects.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention relies on appliance maintenance, ventilation, and CO detectors. Treatment is immediate removal from exposure and administration of high-flow 100% oxygen, which accelerates CO elimination; hyperbaric oxygen therapy is used for severe poisoning and in pregnancy.
