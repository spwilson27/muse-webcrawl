Transfer printing

Transfer printing is a method of decorating pottery or other materials using an engraved copper or steel plate from which a monochrome print on paper is taken, which is then transferred by pressing onto the ceramic piece. It was developed in England in the 1750s, associated with printers such as John Sadler and Guy Green of Liverpool and the Staffordshire potteries.

Process

The engraved plate is inked with ceramic pigment, printed onto tissue paper, and the tissue is pressed onto the glazed or biscuit surface; firing fixes the image. The technique allowed detailed pictorial designs — landscapes, classical scenes, and later blue-and-white chinoiserie patterns — to be reproduced cheaply and consistently on earthenware.

Significance

Transfer printing industrialised ceramic decoration, underpinning the mass production of affordable printed tableware by firms such as Spode and Wedgwood's competitors, and it remains in use in modern variants in textiles and decal printing.
