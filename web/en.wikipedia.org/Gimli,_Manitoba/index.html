Gimli is a community in the Rural Municipality of Gimli on the west shore of Lake Winnipeg in Manitoba, Canada. It lies about 75 kilometres north of Winnipeg. The community was founded in 1875 by Icelandic settlers who named it after Gimlé, the hall in Norse mythology said to survive Ragnarök, and it remains the best-known centre of Icelandic culture in Canada.

History

Gimli was the centre of "New Iceland", a reserve set aside by the Canadian government for Icelandic immigrants fleeing volcanic eruptions and economic hardship in Iceland. The first settlers arrived in October 1875. A smallpox epidemic devastated the colony in 1876–77, but the community endured, developing a fishing industry on Lake Winnipeg that continues today. New Iceland maintained its own administration until 1887.

Culture and landmarks

The town hosts the Icelandic Festival of Manitoba (Islendingadagurinn) every August long weekend since 1890. A 4.6-metre Viking statue, erected in 1967, overlooks the harbour. The New Iceland Heritage Museum tells the story of the settlement. Gimli is also famous for the "Gimli Glider" incident of 1983, when an Air Canada Boeing 767 that ran out of fuel glided to a safe landing at the former air force base at Gimli Industrial Park. The community lends its name to Gimli, a fictional Dwarf of J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, a resonance enjoyed by visitors, and is home to the Gimli distillery of Crown Royal whisky.
