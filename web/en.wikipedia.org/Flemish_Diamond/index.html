Flemish Diamond

The Flemish Diamond (Dutch: Vlaamse Ruit) is the name given to the polycentric metropolitan region in central Flanders, Belgium, defined by the four major cities at its corners: Brussels, Antwerp, Ghent, and Leuven. The term was introduced in Flemish spatial planning policy in the 1990s, notably in the Spatial Structure Plan for Flanders (Ruimtelijk Structuurplan Vlaanderen, 1997), as a way of describing — and promoting — the diamond-shaped network of cities, towns, and transport corridors that forms one of the most densely populated and economically productive urban regions in Europe.

Description

The Diamond covers roughly the area enclosed by the motorways and rail lines linking the four anchor cities, including intermediate centres such as Mechelen, Aalst, Dendermonde, and Sint-Niklaas. It contains a large share of Flanders' population of some 6.5 million and of Belgium's GDP, and it overlaps functionally with the wider Brussels metropolitan area and with the cross-border Randstad–Flemish Diamond "North-West European metropolitan" zone sometimes called the Blue Banana's northern anchor. Brussels itself is geographically enclosed by Flanders, which makes the Diamond a joint Flemish–Brussels functional region despite Belgium's institutional division between regions and language communities.

Significance and criticism

Planners use the concept to coordinate transport, housing, and economic policy in a region marked by intense commuting, suburbanisation, and congestion — the corridor between Brussels and Antwerp is among Europe's busiest. Critics note that the Diamond idealises a networked region while the lived reality includes sprawl, ribbon development along roads, and fragmented municipal governance; others question the exclusion of cities such as Kortrijk, Bruges, and Hasselt from the concept. In economic terms the region hosts the Port of Antwerp (one of Europe's largest), Brussels Airport, major universities (KU Leuven, Ghent University, VUB, University of Antwerp), and the institutions of the European Union.
