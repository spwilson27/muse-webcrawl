Ballistic parachute

A ballistic parachute is a parachute ejected from an aircraft or vehicle by a small explosive charge or rocket, allowing rapid deployment in an emergency. Whole-airframe systems can lower an entire light aircraft to the ground.

Use

Ballistic Recovery Systems (BRS) pioneered airframe parachutes for general aviation; the Cirrus Airframe Parachute System (CAPS) is standard on Cirrus SR-series aircraft and credited with saving hundreds of lives. Similar systems equip ultralights, experimental aircraft, and some spacecraft recovery designs.

Operation

Pulling a cockpit handle fires a rocket that extracts the packed canopy through a frangible panel; the parachute inflates within seconds, and the aircraft descends under canopy with energy-absorbing landing gear and seats mitigating impact.
