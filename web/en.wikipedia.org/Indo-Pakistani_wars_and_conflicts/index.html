Indo-Pakistani wars and conflicts

Since the partition of British India in 1947, India and Pakistan have fought four wars and engaged in numerous armed skirmishes and standoffs. The central cause of the conflict is the disputed territory of Kashmir, which both countries claim. Three of the four wars were fought directly over Kashmir; the fourth, in 1971, arose from the Bangladesh Liberation War and led to the creation of Bangladesh. Since 1998 both states have possessed nuclear weapons, raising the stakes of every subsequent crisis.

The wars

First Kashmir War (1947–1948). Following partition, tribal fighters and Pakistani forces entered the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir, whose ruler acceded to India. Fighting ended with a UN-brokered ceasefire that left Pakistan administering the western and northern areas (Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan) and India the rest.

Second Kashmir War (1965). Pakistan's Operation Gibraltar, infiltrating forces into Indian-administered Kashmir, escalated into full-scale war. A ceasefire and the Tashkent Declaration restored the status quo ante.

War of 1971. India's intervention in East Pakistan's civil war led to a two-front conflict. Pakistan's eastern command surrendered at Dhaka on 16 December 1971, and some 93,000 Pakistani personnel were taken prisoner. Bangladesh became an independent state; the Simla Agreement of 1972 converted the Kashmir ceasefire line into the Line of Control.

Kargil War (1999). Pakistani soldiers and militants occupied heights on the Indian side of the Line of Control near Kargil. India retook the positions after heavy fighting; international pressure compelled Pakistan's withdrawal.

Other conflicts

The two countries have also clashed in the Siachen Glacier conflict (from 1984), insurgency in Kashmir (from 1989), the 2001–2002 military standoff after the attack on the Indian Parliament, the 2008 Mumbai attacks crisis, cross-Line of Control strikes in 2016, and the 2019 Pulwama–Balakot crisis, in which both air forces conducted strikes for the first time since 1971. Water sharing on the Indus system, governed by the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960, has been a rare durable area of cooperation.
