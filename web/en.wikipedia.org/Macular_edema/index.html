Macular edema

Macular edema
Specialty: Ophthalmology
Causes: Diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, uveitis
Treatment: Anti-VEGF injections, corticosteroids, laser

Macular edema occurs when fluid and protein deposits collect on or under the macula of the eye (a central area of the retina) and cause swelling (edema). The swelling may distort central vision because the macula is responsible for sharp, straight-ahead sight.

Causes

The most common cause is diabetic retinopathy; others include retinal vein occlusion, age-related macular degeneration, post-surgical inflammation (Irvine–Gass syndrome) and uveitis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) images the swelling. Treatment targets the underlying cause and commonly uses intravitreal anti-VEGF drugs or corticosteroid implants.
