Fagus sylvatica

Fagus sylvatica, the European beech or common beech, is a large deciduous tree in the beech family Fagaceae, native to Europe. It is a dominant species in many European forests and is widely planted as an ornamental tree and for hedging.

Description

European beech grows to 30–40 metres tall with a smooth, thin grey bark and a dense, rounded crown. The leaves are elliptical, glossy dark green, with wavy margins; they turn coppery in autumn. The fruit is a small three-sided nut, the beechmast, enclosed in a spiny cupule, and is an important food for woodland wildlife though mildly toxic to humans in quantity.

Uses

Beech wood is hard, pale and fine-grained, used for furniture, flooring, tools and firewood, and is traditionally used to smoke foods. Cultivars include the purple-leaved copper beech (F. sylvatica f. purpurea) and the fern-leaved beech.
