Fort Bliss

Fort Bliss

Location: El Paso County, Texas, and New Mexico

Established: 1848 (first post); current site 1893

Garrison: 1st Armored Division

Size: approx. 1,127,000 acres

Fort Bliss is a United States Army post in New Mexico and Texas, with its headquarters in El Paso, Texas. Established in 1848 as a frontier post, it is one of the largest military installations in the United States, covering about 1.12 million acres across Texas and New Mexico, and is the Army's second-largest installation by land area after neighbouring White Sands Missile Range.

Role

Fort Bliss is the home of the 1st Armored Division ("Old Ironsides"), which returned to the post from Germany in 2011, and of the U.S. Army Air Defense Artillery Center. The post is a major centre for air and missile defence training — including Patriot and THAAD systems — and for armoured warfare training in the surrounding desert ranges. Components of the German Air Force trained at Fort Bliss for decades under NATO arrangements until their withdrawal in the 2010s.

History

The first post at El Paso del Norte was established in 1848 after the Mexican–American War and named in 1854 for Lieutenant Colonel William Wallace Smith Bliss. The post moved several times before settling at its current site in 1893. During the Mexican Revolution era it served as a base for General John J. Pershing's 1916 Punitive Expedition into Mexico. After the Second World War, Fort Bliss became a centre for rocket research: captured German V-2 rockets and scientists, including Wernher von Braun's team, were brought there under Operation Paperclip before transferring to Huntsville, Alabama.
