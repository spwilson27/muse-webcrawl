Airmobile

Airmobile operations are military operations in which ground forces are transported by helicopter — and supported by helicopter-borne artillery and close air support — to seize terrain, strike enemy forces, and operate in areas inaccessible to ground vehicles. The modern concept is often called air assault.

Development

The concept matured after the Korean War, when helicopters first demonstrated large-scale troop lift. The Howze Board of 1962 led the US Army to create the 11th Air Assault Division (Test), which became the 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile) and deployed to Vietnam in 1965, fighting the campaign in the Ia Drang Valley.

Characteristics

Airmobile forces trade heavy armour and staying power for strategic and tactical mobility: they can bypass terrain obstacles and enemy strongpoints, insert quickly, and be extracted or resupplied by air. Their limitations include dependence on weather, vulnerability of helicopters to air defences, and difficulty holding ground against armoured counterattack.
