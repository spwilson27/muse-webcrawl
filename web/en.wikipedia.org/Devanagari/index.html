Devanagari (देवनागरी, IAST: Devanāgarī) is an Indic script — specifically an abugida — used to write many languages of northern India and Nepal, including Hindi, Sanskrit, Marathi, Nepali, Konkani and Maithili. It is written from left to right and is recognisable by the strong horizontal line that runs along the tops of its letters, from which the characters appear to hang. Devanagari is the fourth most widely used writing system in the world and the standard script for Hindi, the most spoken language of India.

History

Devanagari descends from the Brahmi script through the Gupta script and the Nagari script of early medieval northern India. It developed as the eastern variant of Nagari around the 7th century CE and became standardised in form by about the 11th century. The name combines deva ("divine" or "god") and nagari ("of the city"), giving "script of the city of the gods." Long used for Sanskrit manuscripts, Devanagari spread widely with printing in the 19th and 20th centuries, and the Constitution of India adopted it as the official script for Hindi.

Principles

Devanagari is an abugida (alphasyllabary): each consonant letter carries an inherent vowel — usually /a/ (schwa) — which can be changed or suppressed by diacritic marks called mātrās. A special sign, the virāma (halant), cancels the inherent vowel. Consonant clusters are written with ligatured conjunct forms (saṁyuktākṣara), such as क्ष kṣa and ज्ञ jña. Independent vowel letters are used at the start of syllables. The traditional ordering of the script, the varṇamālā, arranges sounds scientifically by place of articulation — a system dating back to ancient Indian phonetics.

Characters

The core inventory comprises 11 vowels and 33 consonants, supplemented by signs for nasalisation (anusvāra, candrabindu), aspiration of the breath (visarga), and additional consonants written with a subscript dot (nuktā) for sounds in loanwords, such as क़ /q/ and फ़ /f/. Devanagari also has its own set of digits (०१२३४५६७८९), though Arabic-style digits are common in modern usage.

Usage

More than 120 languages are or have been written in Devanagari. It is official for Hindi (India) and Nepali (Nepal), widely used for Marathi, and used for Sanskrit scholarship across South Asia. In digital text it is encoded in the Unicode block U+0900–U+097F, with extensions for Vedic accents and additional characters.
