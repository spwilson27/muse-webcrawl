Peafowl

SpeciesIndian peafowl (Pavo cristatus); green peafowl (Pavo muticus); Congo peafowl (Afropavo congensis)
FamilyPhasianidae (pheasants and allies)
OrderGalliformes
Native rangeSouth Asia; Southeast Asia; Congo Basin

Peafowl are large pheasants known for the male's extravagant courtship display. The term properly covers three species: the Indian peafowl (Pavo cristatus) of South Asia, the green peafowl (Pavo muticus) of Southeast Asia, and the Congo peafowl (Afropavo congensis) of central Africa. Males are called peacocks, females peahens, and young peachicks; "peacock" is popularly used for the whole group.

Description

The Indian peacock has iridescent blue neck and breast plumage and a "train" up to 1.5 m long—not its tail but elongated upper-tail coverts tipped with iridescent eye-spots (ocelli) in bronze, green, and gold. During courtship the male erects the train into a shimmering fan, rattles the stiff true tail feathers behind it, and struts before peahens. The display is a textbook case of sexual selection, and research has debated whether females prefer trains with more eyespots or use other cues such as display rate and calls. Peahens are mottled brown for camouflage. Green peafowl are larger, with metallic green scaling on the neck in both sexes; the Congo peafowl, discovered by science only in 1936, is darker and lacks the long train.

Behaviour and ecology

Peafowl are omnivorous ground birds, feeding on plants, insects, small reptiles, and amphibians; in India they are valued as predators of young cobras. They roost in trees at night and are strong but reluctant fliers. Their far-carrying, harsh calls—often at dawn and dusk—serve as alarm signals in the forest. The Indian peafowl is the national bird of India and is protected there; it has been introduced to many countries as an ornamental bird, with feral populations in places such as Florida, California, and Hawaii. The green peafowl is endangered through hunting and habitat loss, and the Congo peafowl is vulnerable.

In culture

In Hindu tradition the peacock is the mount (vahana) of the god Kartikeya and is associated with Krishna, who wears a peacock feather in his crown. Peacock feathers symbolise immortality and renewal in several cultures because the bird replaces its train annually; in the West, the "eyes" were linked to the hundred-eyed giant Argus of Greek myth, whose eyes Hera set in the peacock's tail. The bird's name entered fashion and idiom—"proud as a peacock"—and its imagery appears from Mughal art to the logo of the NBC television network.
