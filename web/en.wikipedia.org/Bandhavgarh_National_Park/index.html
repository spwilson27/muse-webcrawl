Bandhavgarh National Park is a national park and tiger reserve in the Umaria district of Madhya Pradesh, India. It is renowned for having one of the highest densities of Bengal tigers of any protected area in India, making it one of the most popular places in the world to observe tigers in the wild. The park's name derives from the ancient Bandhavgarh Fort, perched on a hill within the park, which legend holds was given by the god Rama to his brother Lakshmana (Bandhav = brother, Garh = fort).

Geography and history

The park covers sal and bamboo forests, grasslands, and rocky hill ranges of the Vindhyan system. It was the former hunting preserve of the Maharajas of Rewa; the celebrated white tiger Mohan, captured nearby in 1951, was the progenitor of most white tigers in captivity. Declared a national park in 1968 and brought under Project Tiger in 1993, the reserve is divided into tourism zones such as Tala, Magdhi, and Khitauli. Caves, the reclining Vishnu statue Shesh Shaiya, and other ancient remains dot the fort hill.

Wildlife

Besides tigers — including famous individuals such as Charger and the tigress Sita — Bandhavgarh hosts leopards, sloth bears, Indian bison (gaur), sambar, chital, nilgai, wild boar, dhole, and over 250 bird species. The tiger density in the core tourism zones has historically been among the highest recorded, and sightings are correspondingly frequent compared with other reserves.
