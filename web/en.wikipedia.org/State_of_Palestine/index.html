State of Palestine

Capital: East Jerusalem (claimed); Ramallah (administrative)
Largest city: Gaza City
Official language: Arabic
Population: approx. 5.5 million
Currency: Israeli new shekel (de facto)

Palestine, officially the State of Palestine, is a country in the southern Levant region of West Asia. It encompasses two disconnected territories — the West Bank and the Gaza Strip — collectively known as the Palestinian territories, which have been under Israeli occupation since 1967.

The State of Palestine was proclaimed by the Palestine Liberation Organization on 15 November 1988 in Algiers. It is recognised by the large majority of United Nations member states, and since 2012 Palestine has held non-member observer state status at the United Nations. The Palestinian Authority exercises limited self-rule in parts of the West Bank, while Gaza has been administered separately since 2007.

History

The region of Palestine has a long history under successive empires. Following the First World War it became the British Mandate for Palestine. The 1947 UN Partition Plan proposed separate Jewish and Arab states; the 1948 Arab–Israeli War led to the establishment of Israel and the displacement of over 700,000 Palestinians in what Palestinians call the Nakba. The West Bank and Gaza were captured by Israel in the Six-Day War of 1967. The Oslo Accords of 1993–95 created the Palestinian Authority as an interim arrangement pending a final-status agreement that has not been reached.

Geography and demographics

The West Bank is landlocked and bordered by Israel and Jordan; the Gaza Strip borders Israel, Egypt, and the Mediterranean Sea. Palestinians number about 5.5 million in the territories, with a large diaspora and refugee population abroad. Major cities include Gaza City, Hebron, Nablus, Ramallah, and East Jerusalem, which Palestinians claim as their capital.
