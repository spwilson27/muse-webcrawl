Vassal state

A vassal state is any state that has a mutual obligation to a superior state or empire in a status similar to that of a vassal in the feudal system of medieval Europe. The obligations often include paying tribute and providing military support; in return the dominant state usually offers protection. The term applies to pre-modern and early modern arrangements and is related to, but distinct from, tributary states, client states, puppet states, and protectorates.

Historical examples

The Ottoman Empire maintained numerous vassal and tributary states, including the Crimean Khanate, Wallachia, and Moldavia, which kept internal autonomy while acknowledging the Sultan's suzerainty. In East Asia, the Chinese tributary system structured relations between imperial China and neighbouring kingdoms such as Korea, Vietnam, and the Ryukyu Kingdom. In ancient times, Rome's client kingdoms and the dependencies of the Achaemenid Empire functioned similarly.

Distinctions

A vassal state retains its own rulers and internal government, unlike annexed territory. A modern "puppet state" is nominally independent but controlled by a foreign power without the reciprocal obligations of vassalage; a "satellite state" is influenced politically and economically without formal subordination.
