Gossypium

Kingdom: Plantae

Order: Malvales

Family: Malvaceae

Tribe: Gossypieae

Genus: Gossypium L.

Gossypium is a genus of flowering plants in the tribe Gossypieae in the mallow family, Malvaceae, from which cotton is harvested. It is native to tropical and subtropical regions of the Old and New Worlds. There are about 50 Gossypium species, making it the largest genus in the tribe Gossypieae, and new species continue to be discovered.

Cultivated species

Four species are cultivated commercially for cotton fibre. Gossypium hirsutum, upland cotton native to Mexico and Central America, accounts for about 90% of world production. Gossypium barbadense, from South America, produces extra-long-staple (Pima and Egyptian) cotton and accounts for most of the remainder. The Old World species G. arboreum and G. herbaceum are grown mainly in Asia and Africa. The two New World cultivated species are allotetraploids, formed by an ancient hybridization between an African/Asian A-genome species and an American D-genome species.

Biology

Cotton plants are shrubs or small trees with lobed leaves and hibiscus-like flowers. The fruit, called a boll, splits open when ripe to reveal a mass of white fibres surrounding the seeds. The fibres are nearly pure cellulose and grow as elongated single cells from the seed coat. Many species contain the toxic pigment gossypol in their seeds and glands, a natural defence against insects.
