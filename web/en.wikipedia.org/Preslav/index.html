Preslav

Preslav (Bulgarian: Преслав), officially Veliki Preslav ("Great Preslav"), is a town in northeastern Bulgaria, in Shumen Province. It was the capital of the First Bulgarian Empire from 893 to 972 and one of the most important cities of medieval southeastern Europe.

History

The city rose to prominence when Tsar Simeon I moved the Bulgarian capital from Pliska to Preslav in 893. Under Simeon's rule, Preslav became a major cultural centre: the Preslav Literary School, founded by scholars including Constantine of Preslav and John the Exarch, produced translations and original works in Old Church Slavonic and helped develop the Cyrillic script. The city was renowned for its monumental architecture, including the Round (Golden) Church, royal palaces, and decorated ceramic workshops.

Preslav was seized by the Kievan Rus' prince Sviatoslav I in 968 and then taken by the Byzantine emperor John I Tzimiskes in 971, after which the Bulgarian capital moved west. The city declined following a Pecheneg raid in 1098 and was eventually abandoned as a major centre, though a smaller settlement survived. The modern town stands near the extensive archaeological ruins, which include fortifications, churches, and the site of the royal palace.
