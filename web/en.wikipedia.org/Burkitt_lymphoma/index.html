Burkitt lymphoma

Burkitt lymphoma
Specialty: Hematology, oncology
Cell of origin: Germinal centre B cell
Key feature: MYC translocation t(8;14)

Burkitt lymphoma is a cancer of the lymphatic system, particularly B lymphocytes found in the germinal centre. It is named after Denis Parsons Burkitt, the Irish surgeon who first described the disease in 1958 while working in equatorial Africa.

Classification

Three clinical variants are recognised: the endemic (African) variant, strongly associated with Epstein–Barr virus and malaria and often presenting as a jaw tumour in children; the sporadic variant, seen worldwide and often arising in the abdomen; and the immunodeficiency-associated variant, seen in people with HIV or after transplantation.

Pathophysiology

The disease is characterised by a chromosomal translocation involving the MYC oncogene, most commonly t(8;14), which deregulates cell proliferation. It is one of the fastest-growing human tumours, with a cell doubling time of roughly 24 hours; under the microscope it shows a "starry sky" appearance of macrophages among sheets of tumour cells.

Treatment

Because of its rapid growth, Burkitt lymphoma is treated urgently with intensive combination chemotherapy, which is highly effective, particularly in children. Tumour lysis syndrome is a major early risk requiring prophylaxis and monitoring.
