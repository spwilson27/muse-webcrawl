Justicialist Party

The Justicialist Party (Spanish: Partido Justicialista, PJ) is a major political party in Argentina, associated with Peronism. Founded in 1946 by Juan Domingo Perón, it has been the country's dominant political force for much of the period since, supplying presidents including Perón himself, Carlos Menem, Néstor Kirchner and Cristina Fernández de Kirchner.

History

Perón founded the party after his election as president in 1946, merging the labour and nationalist movements that had brought him to power. The party was proscribed after the 1955 coup that overthrew Perón, but Peronism survived underground and in the trade unions. It returned to power in 1973, and again after the restoration of democracy in 1983 alternated in government with the Radical Civic Union and later coalitions.

Ideology

Peronism rests on the "three flags" of social justice, economic independence and political sovereignty. In practice the party has housed factions from the left and right, from the Montoneros-influenced left of the 1970s to the market-oriented governments of Carlos Menem in the 1990s and the Kirchnerist left of the 2000s.
