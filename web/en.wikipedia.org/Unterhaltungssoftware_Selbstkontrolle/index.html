The Unterhaltungssoftware Selbstkontrolle (USK; "Entertainment Software Self-Regulation Body") is the organization responsible for video game content ratings in Germany. Founded in 1994, it evaluates games on behalf of the youth protection authorities of the German federal states, and its ratings are legally binding for sales to minors.

Ratings

USK assigns one of five age ratings, displayed as coloured diamond labels: white (USK 0, approved without age restriction), yellow (USK 6), green (USK 12), blue (USK 16), and red (USK 18, adults only, not to be sold to minors). Games refused a rating risk being placed on the federal "Index" of media harmful to young persons, severely restricting their sale and advertising. Since 2018, games containing symbols of anti-constitutional organizations — such as Nazi imagery, long effectively barred from games — may receive ratings when the material serves artistic or educational purposes, following a change in the social adequacy clause's interpretation.

Process

Trained testers play each title and present it to expert panels that include representatives of the states' supreme youth protection authorities, which issue the final rating. The USK is operated by the German games industry association and is part of the pan-European IARC rating system for digitally distributed games.
