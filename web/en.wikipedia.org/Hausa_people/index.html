The Hausa are one of the largest ethnic groups in West and Central Africa, concentrated in northern Nigeria and southern Niger, with significant communities across the Sahel and a wide diaspora of traders. They speak the Hausa language, a Chadic language of the Afroasiatic family, which serves as a lingua franca across much of West Africa and is one of Africa's most widely spoken languages.

History

Hausa society coalesced around a group of city-states known as the Hausa Bakwai ("Hausa Seven") — including Kano, Katsina, Zaria, Gobir, Daura, Rano and Biram — which flourished from roughly the eleventh century as centres of agriculture, craft production and trans-Saharan trade. Kano in particular became famous for its dye pits, leatherwork and cloth. Islam spread gradually through trade and scholarship from the fourteenth century onward.

In the early nineteenth century, the Fulani jihad led by Usman dan Fodio overthrew the Hausa kings and incorporated their states into the Sokoto Caliphate, one of the largest empires in pre-colonial Africa. The resulting Hausa-Fulani political culture dominated northern Nigeria through the colonial period and remains influential today.

Culture

Hausa culture is strongly shaped by Islam: mosque-centred cities, emirate governance, Islamic scholarship, and distinctive dress — flowing embroidered gowns (babanriga) for men and colourful wrappers and head ties for women. Traditional architecture features mud-brick buildings decorated with engraved relief patterns (zane), as seen in the old city of Zaria and the Emir's palace in Kano. Famous crafts include weaving, embroidery, leather goods and indigo dyeing. A minority of rural Hausa, the Maguzawa, retain pre-Islamic religious practices centred on spirit possession (bori).

Economy

Historically agriculturalists growing millet, sorghum, groundnuts and cotton, the Hausa are also renowned as long-distance traders; Hausa merchant networks stretch from the Atlantic coast to Sudan, and Hausa quarters (zongos) exist in cities across West Africa.
