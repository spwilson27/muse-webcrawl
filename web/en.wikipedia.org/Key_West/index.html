Key West
County: Monroe, Florida
Population: about 26,000
Nickname: The Conch Republic
Notable: Southernmost city in the contiguous United States

Key West is an island city in the Florida Keys and the county seat of Monroe County, Florida. It is the southernmost city in the contiguous United States, lying about 90 miles (145 km) north of Cuba, and is the terminus of U.S. Highway 1, whose "Mile Marker 0" sign is a popular landmark. The Spanish name for the island was Cayo Hueso, "Bone Key".

History

Claimed for the United States in 1822, Key West grew rich in the 19th century on wrecking — salvaging ships wrecked on the Florida Reef — and later on sponging, turtle fishing and cigar manufacture, becoming for a time Florida's largest and wealthiest city. The Navy has maintained a presence since the city's founding; President Harry S. Truman's "Little White House" served as his winter retreat. Writers Ernest Hemingway and Tennessee Williams lived on the island, and Hemingway's house, with its famous polydactyl cats, is now a museum.

Climate and culture

Key West has a tropical savanna climate and has never recorded frost; it claims to be the only city in the continental United States never to have fallen below freezing. The nightly Sunset Celebration at Mallory Square, the annual Fantasy Fest, Hemingway Days and a large LGBTQ community are central to its laid-back, eccentric identity. In 1982 residents staged a mock secession as the "Conch Republic" to protest a Border Patrol roadblock, a joke still celebrated each April. Natives are called "Conchs", after the sea snail once a staple food.
