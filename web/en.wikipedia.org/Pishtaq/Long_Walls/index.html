Long Walls

The Long Walls were a pair of parallel defensive walls built in ancient Athens in the 5th century BC, connecting the city with its port of Piraeus, about seven kilometres away. They guaranteed Athens access to the sea and to imported grain even when an enemy army occupied the surrounding countryside.

History

Construction began after the Persian Wars, around 461–456 BC, under leaders including Themistocles and Pericles. During the Peloponnesian War the walls allowed Athens to withstand Spartan invasions of Attica by relying on its navy; they were demolished after Athens' defeat in 404 BC, rebuilt with Persian funding in the 390s BC, and finally slighted in later periods.
