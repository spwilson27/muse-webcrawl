Pitons

The Pitons are two mountainous volcanic plugs — Gros Piton and Petit Piton — that rise dramatically from the sea on the southwestern coast of Saint Lucia in the Caribbean. Linked by the Piton Mitan ridge, they are the island's most famous landmark, appearing on its flag, its currency and the labels of its local beer.

Geography

Gros Piton stands about 798 metres (2,619 ft) high and Petit Piton about 743 metres (2,438 ft), yet Petit Piton, being narrower and steeper, is the harder climb. The peaks are the eroded remnants of an ancient volcanic center and are covered in lush tropical forest that shelters endemic species, while coral reefs rich in marine life fringe their bases.

Conservation

The Pitons Management Area, covering some 2,909 hectares including a marine reserve, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004 for its outstanding natural beauty and biodiversity. Guided ascents of Gros Piton are a popular activity for visitors to the island.
