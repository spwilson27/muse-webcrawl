Artesian aquifer

An artesian aquifer is a confined aquifer containing groundwater under positive pressure. A well drilled into such an aquifer — an artesian well — allows water to rise above the top of the aquifer, and sometimes to the surface, without pumping.

Formation

Artesian conditions arise where a permeable rock layer is sandwiched between impermeable layers and tilted so that its intake area lies at a higher elevation than the well site. The weight of water at the recharge zone pressurises the whole aquifer. The name derives from Artois in France, where monks drilled famous flowing wells in the 12th century.

Examples

The Great Artesian Basin of Australia is one of the largest artesian systems in the world and underpins agriculture across much of the arid interior. Sustained extraction can lower pressure so that wells cease to flow freely.
