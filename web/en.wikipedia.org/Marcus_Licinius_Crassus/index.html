Marcus Licinius Crassus

Bornc. 115 BC, Rome
Died53 BC, near Carrhae
OccupationGeneral, politician

Marcus Licinius Crassus (c. 115 – 53 BC) was a Roman general and statesman who played a key role in the transformation of the Roman Republic into the Roman Empire. He is often regarded as one of the richest individuals in Roman history.

Early life

Crassus was born into the wealthy plebeian gens Licinia. His father, Publius Licinius Crassus, was a former consul and censor. During the civil wars between Marius and Sulla, the younger Crassus fled to Hispania after his father and brother were killed, later raising a private army to support Sulla's return to Italy.

Wealth

Crassus amassed an enormous fortune through proscriptions, real estate speculation — famously buying burning or fire-damaged properties at low prices and rebuilding them with his private fire brigade of slaves — silver mines, and the slave trade. His fortune was estimated at 200 million sesterces.

Career

Crassus won fame by defeating the slave revolt led by Spartacus in 71 BC, crucifying thousands of captured rebels along the Appian Way. He served as consul with Pompey in 70 BC and again in 55 BC. Together with Pompey and Julius Caesar he formed the informal political alliance known as the First Triumvirate.

Death

Seeking military glory to rival his partners, Crassus invaded Parthia in 54 BC. His army was annihilated at the Battle of Carrhae in 53 BC by Parthian horse archers under Surena, and Crassus was killed during or after the battle. His death destabilised the triumvirate and hastened the Republic's final civil wars.
