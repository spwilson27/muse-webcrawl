Estadio Centenario is a stadium in Montevideo, Uruguay, built for the inaugural FIFA World Cup in 1930. Named to commemorate the 100th anniversary of Uruguay's first constitution, it hosted the first World Cup final, in which Uruguay defeated Argentina 4–2 on 30 July 1930. FIFA has declared it one of the classic stadiums of world football, and it houses the Museo del Fútbol, the Uruguayan football museum.

Construction

Designed by architect Juan Antonio Scasso, the stadium was built between 1929 and 1930 in the Parque Batlle district, constructed largely by immigrant workers in a remarkable eight months. Rain delayed completion, so the first tournament matches were played at other Montevideo grounds; the first World Cup match at the Centenario was Uruguay's 1–0 win over Peru on 18 July 1930.

Architecture

The stadium's most distinctive feature is the Torre de los Homenajes (Tower of Homage), a roughly 100-metre Art Deco tower that has become a symbol of Montevideo. The structure was built in reinforced concrete in an elliptical plan with four grandstands named for Uruguayan football triumphs: Ámsterdam and Colombes (for the 1924 and 1928 Olympic golds), Olímpica, and América.

Major events

Beyond 1930, the Centenario has hosted matches of the South American Championship/Copa América in 1942, 1956, 1967 and 1995, all won by Uruguay on home soil, as well as the 1980 Mundialito (Copa de Oro). It remains the home ground of the Uruguay national team for major matches and hosts the Uruguayan Clásico between Peñarol and Nacional when a neutral large venue is used.
