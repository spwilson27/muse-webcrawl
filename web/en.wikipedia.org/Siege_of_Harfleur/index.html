Siege of Harfleur

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The siege of Harfleur (18 August – 22 September 1415) was conducted by the forces of King Henry V of England against the port town of Harfleur in Normandy during the Hundred Years' War. It was the opening engagement of Henry's 1415 campaign, which culminated in the Battle of Agincourt.

Background

Henry V, pressing his claim to the French crown, landed near Harfleur on 14 August 1415 with an army of around 11,500 men. Harfleur, at the mouth of the Seine, was a strategic port and a base for French privateers; Henry needed it as a supply point and as a permanent English foothold.

The siege

The town was defended by several hundred men-at-arms under the lords of Gaucourt and Estouteville, who flooded the surrounding lowlands and strengthened the walls. The English invested the town completely, using artillery — among the earliest large-scale uses of gunpowder siege cannon in the war — and mining against the walls, while the defenders counter-mined and made sorties. The siege lasted over a month, far longer than Henry expected. Dysentery ravaged the English camp, killing or incapacitating a large part of the army, including senior nobles such as the Earl of Suffolk and the Bishop of Norwich.

Surrender and aftermath

With no relief army forthcoming, Harfleur surrendered on 22 September 1415. Henry expelled much of the French population, installed an English garrison, and invited English settlers. The losses from disease at Harfleur left his army much reduced — perhaps 8,000–9,000 fit men — before the march to Calais that led to Agincourt on 25 October.
