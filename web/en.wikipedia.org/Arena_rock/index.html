Arena rock

Arena rock (also called stadium rock or pomp rock) is a style of rock music that emerged in the mid-1970s, designed for large venues and mass audiences. It features anthemic choruses, polished production, power ballads, and songs calculated for sing-along appeal on FM radio.

Characteristics and artists

Leading arena rock acts included Journey, REO Speedwagon, Foreigner, Styx, Boston, Toto, and Kansas in the United States, and Queen and others internationally. The style emphasized professional musicianship, layered harmonies, and dramatic live shows.

Criticism and legacy

Critics often derided arena rock as "corporate rock", contrasting it unfavourably with punk's rawness, but its biggest albums rank among the best-selling of all time, and its sound influenced later mainstream rock and pop.
