Robert A. Heinlein (1907–1988)

Robert Anson Heinlein (/ˈhaɪnlaɪn/; July 7, 1907 – May 8, 1988) was an American science fiction author, aeronautical engineer, and naval officer. Sometimes called the "dean of science fiction writers", he was among the first to emphasise scientific accuracy in his fiction and was thus a pioneer of the subgenre of hard science fiction.

Life and career

Born in Butler, Missouri, Heinlein graduated from the United States Naval Academy in 1929 but was discharged for tuberculosis in 1934. After studying at UCLA and a brief political career, he turned to writing; his first story, "Life-Line", appeared in Astounding Science Fiction in 1939. During the Second World War he worked as an engineer at the Naval Aircraft Factory in Philadelphia.

Works

His best-known works include Starship Troopers (1959), which won the Hugo Award, Stranger in a Strange Land (1961), a countercultural classic, and The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress (1966). He won four Hugo Awards for best novel and, after his death, three Retro Hugos. He also wrote acclaimed juveniles such as Have Space Suit—Will Travel.

Influence

Together with Isaac Asimov and Arthur C. Clarke, Heinlein was one of the "Big Three" of science fiction. He helped popularise ideas such as the waterbed and the waldo (remote manipulator), and his libertarian and individualist themes influenced both the genre and American political culture.
