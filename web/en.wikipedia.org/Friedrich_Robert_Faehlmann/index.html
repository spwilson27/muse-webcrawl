Friedrich Robert Faehlmann (31 December 1798 – 22 April 1850) was an Estonian writer, medical doctor, and philologist. He was a leading figure of the Estonian national awakening and laid the groundwork for the Estonian national epic, the Kalevipoeg, completed after his death by Friedrich Reinhold Kreutzwald.

Life

Faehlmann was born in Ao in present-day Järva County, the son of a serf estate manager. He studied medicine at the University of Tartu (Dorpat), graduated in 1827, and practised as a physician in Tartu, becoming known for treating the poor without charge.

Literary work

In 1838 Faehlmann co-founded the Learned Estonian Society and became its president. He collected and published Estonian folk tales and myths, writing mythological stories in German — including "Koit ja Hämarik" (Dawn and Twilight) — that treated Estonian folklore as material worthy of literature. His outline of the Kalevipoeg cycle, drawn from folk songs about the hero Kalevipoeg, became the plan Kreutzwald followed when composing the epic.

Legacy

Faehlmann is regarded as a pioneer of Estonian-language scholarship and national literature; his collected writings were published posthumously, and his name is commemorated by schools and streets in Estonia.
