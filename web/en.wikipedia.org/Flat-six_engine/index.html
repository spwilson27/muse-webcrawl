Flat-six engine

A flat-six engine, also known as a horizontally opposed-six or boxer-six, is a six-cylinder piston engine with three cylinders on each side of a central crankshaft, the cylinders lying in a horizontal plane. The configuration is most closely associated with Porsche, which has used flat-sixes in the 911 sports car since 1964, and with Subaru. Like other boxer engines, opposing pistons move toward and away from each other simultaneously, resembling boxers' gloves touching — the origin of the name.

Advantages and disadvantages

The flat-six has excellent primary and secondary balance: the opposed piston pairs cancel each other's reciprocating forces, so the engine runs smoothly without balance shafts. The flat layout also gives a very low centre of gravity, benefiting handling, and a short, wide profile that suits rear-engine installation. Drawbacks include the engine's width, the need for two cylinder heads and valvetrains, complex exhaust routing, and (in rear-engined cars) the challenges of cooling and engine access. Early Porsche and Chevrolet Corvair flat-sixes were air-cooled; modern designs are water-cooled.

Notable applications

Porsche introduced its air-cooled flat-six (designed under Ferdinand "Butzi" Porsche's team and engineer Hans Mezger, whose name attaches to the later racing-derived "Mezger engine") in the 1964 911 at 2.0 litres; the design grew through 2.2, 2.4, 2.7, 3.0, 3.2, and 3.6 litres, gaining turbocharging in the 911 Turbo (930) of 1975. Air cooling ended with the 993 generation in 1998; water-cooled flat-sixes power subsequent 911s, the Boxster and Cayman, in displacements up to 4.0 litres and, in GT3 form, with redlines around 9,000 rpm. Other users have included the Chevrolet Corvair (1960–1969), the only mass-produced American flat-six car; the Subaru Alcyone/SVX and later six-cylinder Outback, Legacy, and Tribeca models; and Honda's Gold Wing touring motorcycles (GL1500/GL1800), a rare motorcycle application. Flat-sixes have also been used in light aircraft and in the Porsche-powered Mooney M20L.
