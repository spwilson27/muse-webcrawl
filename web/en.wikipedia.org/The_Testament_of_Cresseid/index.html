The Testament of Cresseid

The Testament of Cresseid

Author: Robert Henryson

Language: Middle Scots

Date: c. late 15th century

Form: Narrative poem, rhyme royal

The Testament of Cresseid is a narrative poem of 616 lines in rhyme royal by the Scottish makar Robert Henryson, written in the late fifteenth century. It presents itself as a sequel to Chaucer's Troilus and Criseyde, relating the fate of the heroine after she is abandoned by Diomede.

Plot

Cast off and reduced to begging in a leper house, Cresseid curses the gods of love. In a dream, a parliament of the planetary gods condemns her blasphemy and strikes her with leprosy. Troilus, passing by, gives alms to the unrecognised leper; she realises her fault, makes her testament, and dies. Henryson's Cresseid accepts responsibility for her ruin, ending with a ring returned to Troilus.

Reception

The poem was printed in William Thynne's 1532 edition of Chaucer as part of Chaucer's works, and was long read as Chaucer's own. It is now regarded as one of the masterpieces of Middle Scots literature.
