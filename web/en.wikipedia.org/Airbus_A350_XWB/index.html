The Airbus A350 XWB is a long-range, wide-body twin-engine airliner developed and produced by Airbus. The initial A350 design proposed in 2004 was a derivative of the A330, but Airbus redesigned the aircraft as a clean-sheet design with a wider fuselage — hence "XWB" (extra wide body) — after customers rejected the original proposal.

The A350 is the first Airbus aircraft with a fuselage and wing structures made primarily of carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer. It is powered by Rolls-Royce Trent XWB turbofan engines. Variants include the A350-900 and the stretched A350-1000, seating roughly 300–410 passengers, with the A350F freighter also offered.

Development and service

The prototype first flew on 14 June 2013 from Toulouse, France. Type certification from the European Aviation Safety Agency was obtained in September 2014, and the A350-900 entered commercial service with Qatar Airways on 15 January 2015. The type competes with the Boeing 777 and 787 Dreamliner and is noted for its fuel efficiency and quiet cabin.
