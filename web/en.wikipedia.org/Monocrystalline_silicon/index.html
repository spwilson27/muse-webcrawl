Monocrystalline silicon

Monocrystalline silicon (mono-Si) is silicon grown as a single continuous crystal, with no grain boundaries. It is the base material for most integrated circuits and for high-efficiency photovoltaic cells.

Production

Large cylindrical ingots are grown by the Czochralski process, in which a seed crystal is slowly pulled from molten silicon, then sliced into wafers. The float-zone method produces still purer material for specialised applications.

Applications

Because electrons move through a single crystal with fewer disruptions, monocrystalline solar cells achieve higher efficiencies — commonly above 20 percent — than polycrystalline cells, though at higher cost. Monocrystalline panels are recognisable by their uniform dark colour and rounded-corner cells.
