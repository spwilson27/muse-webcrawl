The Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta is a nine-day hot air balloon festival held each October in Albuquerque, New Mexico, United States. It is the largest ballooning event in the world, regularly featuring more than 500 balloons, and is often described as the most photographed event on Earth.

History

The fiesta began in 1972 with just 13 balloons launching from a shopping-mall parking lot as part of a radio station's 50th-birthday celebration. It grew rapidly, moving to the New Mexico State Fairgrounds and then, in 1996, to the purpose-built Balloon Fiesta Park, a launch field of about 78 acres from which balloons can lift off in waves.

Events

Signature events include the Mass Ascension, in which hundreds of balloons launch in two waves at dawn; the Special Shape Rodeo, featuring balloons built as animals, cartoon characters and objects; evening Balloon Glows, where tethered balloons are lit by their burners after dark; and flying competitions such as "key grab" tasks. Albuquerque's autumn weather — including the famous "Albuquerque box" wind pattern, which lets pilots steer by changing altitude — makes the city ideal for ballooning.

Impact

The fiesta draws close to a million visitors and has a major economic impact on New Mexico. The Anderson Abruzzo Albuquerque International Balloon Museum, named for local ballooning pioneers, stands adjacent to the park.
