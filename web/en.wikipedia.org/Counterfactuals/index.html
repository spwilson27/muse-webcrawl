Counterfactuals

Counterfactual conditionals
FieldPhilosophy of language, logic, linguistics
Form"If A had been the case, then B would have been the case"
Key theoristsDavid Lewis, Robert Stalnaker, Nelson Goodman

Counterfactuals are conditional statements discussing what would have been the case if some hypothetical, contrary-to-fact situation had obtained — for example, "If Kennedy had not been assassinated, he would have served two terms." They are central to philosophy, linguistics, history, law, and causal reasoning.

Semantics

Unlike material implication, counterfactuals are not truth-functional: a counterfactual with a false antecedent is not automatically true. The dominant analysis, developed by Robert Stalnaker and David Lewis, evaluates a counterfactual by considering the "closest possible world" in which the antecedent is true and asking whether the consequent holds there.

The problem of counterfactuals

Nelson Goodman raised the classic problem of which generalisations support counterfactual reasoning: from "if this match had been struck, it would have lit" we infer using laws and background conditions, but specifying exactly which facts to hold fixed in the hypothetical scenario is notoriously difficult.

Uses

Counterfactual reasoning underlies causal claims in science and everyday life, legal standards such as "but-for" causation, historical what-if analysis, and modern statistical methods of causal inference, including potential-outcomes frameworks in medicine and economics.
