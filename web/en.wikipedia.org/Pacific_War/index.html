Pacific War

U.S. Marines raising the flag on Iwo Jima, February 1945

The Pacific War, sometimes called the Asia–Pacific War, was the theater of World War II fought in eastern Asia, the Pacific Ocean, the Indian Ocean, and the South West Pacific. In some interpretations, it begins on July 7, 1937, with the start of the Second Sino-Japanese War; in other accounts it begins on December 7, 1941, with the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor and simultaneous attacks on Allied territories in Southeast Asia.

The war pitted the Empire of Japan against the Allies, principally the United States, China, the British Empire, Australia, and, in its closing days, the Soviet Union. After Pearl Harbor, Japan rapidly seized the Philippines, Malaya, Singapore, the Dutch East Indies, and much of the western Pacific. The Allied counteroffensive began in 1942 and gathered momentum with victories at Midway, Guadalcanal, and in New Guinea and the Solomons.

Course of the war

The Battle of Midway (June 1942) destroyed four Japanese aircraft carriers and is regarded as the war's turning point at sea. Thereafter, U.S. forces under Admiral Chester Nimitz and General Douglas MacArthur advanced by "island hopping," bypassing strongpoints to seize islands suitable for airfields. Major battles included Tarawa, Saipan, the Philippine Sea, Leyte Gulf, Iwo Jima, and Okinawa. Meanwhile, British and Commonwealth forces fought in Burma, and China tied down large Japanese armies on the mainland; in 1944–45 American B-29 bombers operating from the Marianas firebombed Japanese cities.

End of the war

In August 1945 the United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima (August 6) and Nagasaki (August 9), the Soviet Union invaded Manchuria, and on August 15 Emperor Hirohito announced Japan's acceptance of the Potsdam Declaration. The formal surrender was signed aboard the battleship USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay on September 2, 1945. The war killed tens of millions across Asia, devastated cities and economies, ended Japanese and European colonial rule in much of the region, and ushered in the atomic age.
