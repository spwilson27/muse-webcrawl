Primetime Emmy Awards

Primetime Emmy Awards
Awarded for: Excellence in primetime television programming
Presented by: Academy of Television Arts & Sciences
First awarded: January 25, 1949

The Primetime Emmy Awards, or Primetime Emmys, are part of the extensive range of Emmy Awards for artistic and technical merit for the American television industry. Bestowed by the Academy of Television Arts & Sciences (ATAS), the Primetime Emmys are presented in recognition of excellence in American primetime television programming.

History

The first Emmy ceremony was held on January 25, 1949, at the Hollywood Athletic Club, honouring shows produced and aired locally in the Los Angeles area. The awards expanded nationally as television spread in the 1950s. The statuette, depicting a winged woman holding an atom, symbolises television's arts (the wings) and sciences (the atom); the name "Emmy" derives from "Immy", a nickname for the image orthicon camera tube.

Ceremony

The main Primetime Emmy ceremony is held each year in mid-September, on the Sunday before the start of the fall television season, and airs in rotation among the major broadcast networks. Creative Arts Emmy Awards, recognising technical and craft achievements and guest performances, are presented in separate ceremonies around the same time.

Categories

Major categories include Outstanding Drama Series, Outstanding Comedy Series, Outstanding Limited or Anthology Series, acting awards for lead and supporting performers in drama and comedy, and directing and writing awards. The awards have evolved with the medium, adding categories for reality competition programmes and, as streaming services grew, honouring programmes distributed online.

Records

Saturday Night Live is the most-nominated and most-awarded programme in Emmy history. Among drama series, shows such as Game of Thrones and The West Wing hold records for total wins, while Frasier and Modern Family are among the most honoured comedies.
