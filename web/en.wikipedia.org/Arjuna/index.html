Arjuna

Arjuna (Sanskrit: अर्जुन) is a central hero of the Hindu epic Mahabharata. The third of the five Pandava brothers, he is the son of Kunti and the god Indra, and is celebrated as the greatest archer of his age — the master of the divine bow Gandiva and a warrior who fought ambidextrously. His charioteer and divine guide is Krishna, whose counsel to a despairing Arjuna on the eve of the Kurukshetra War forms the Bhagavad Gita.

In the Mahabharata

Trained by the guru Drona alongside his brothers and their cousins the Kauravas, Arjuna won the hand of Draupadi at her svayamvara by shooting a target through a reflection. He undertook journeys that gained him celestial weapons, married Subhadra (Krishna's sister), Ulupi and Chitrangada, and spent the Pandavas' incognito year at the court of Virata disguised as the dance teacher Brihannala.

At Kurukshetra, overcome by grief at fighting his kin, Arjuna received Krishna's teaching on duty, the soul and devotion. In the war he duelled his half-brother Karna, whom he killed on the seventeenth day, and defeated grandsire Bhishma with the help of Shikhandi. His son Abhimanyu's death drove him to slay Jayadratha before sunset in one of the epic's most famous episodes.

Legacy

Arjuna embodies the ideal of the warrior who acts without attachment to results. He is a major figure of temple art, dance-drama and popular culture across South and Southeast Asia, and namesakes include India's Arjun main battle tank and the Arjuna Award for sporting excellence.
