Melon

A melon is any of various plants of the family Cucurbitaceae with sweet, edible, fleshy fruit. The word "melon" can refer to either the plant or specifically the fruit. Botanically, a melon is a kind of berry, a pepo, with a thick rind and a fleshy centre. Many different cultivars have been produced, particularly of muskmelon (Cucumis melo), which includes cantaloupes, honeydew and casaba, while the watermelon (Citrullus lanatus) is a separate species.

History

Melons were among the earliest plants to be cultivated in both the Old and New Worlds. Evidence from Egypt and Iran indicates cultivation of Cucumis melo several thousand years ago. Melons spread along trade routes through Asia and the Mediterranean, and after the Columbian exchange were established in the Americas as well.

Types

The muskmelon group includes the netted cantaloupe (North American cantaloupe), the smooth-skinned European cantaloupe, honeydew melons with pale green flesh, and winter melons such as casaba and Santa Claus melon. Watermelons, grown for their red, watery flesh, are among the largest fruits in the family. Other cucurbits sometimes called melons include bitter melon (Momordica charantia) and horned melon (Cucumis metuliferus), though these are culinary rather than close botanical groupings.

Cultivation

Melons require a long, warm growing season, well-drained soil and abundant water during growth, with drier conditions as the fruit ripens to concentrate sugars. They are grown commercially in China — the world's largest producer — as well as Turkey, Iran, the United States and Spain.
