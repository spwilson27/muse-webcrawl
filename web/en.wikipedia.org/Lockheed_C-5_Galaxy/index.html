The Lockheed C-5 Galaxy is a large military transport aircraft designed and built by Lockheed, and now maintained and upgraded by Lockheed Martin. Operated by the United States Air Force, it provides strategic intercontinental airlift, capable of carrying outsized and oversized cargo, including tanks and helicopters, over intercontinental ranges.

Design

The C-5 features a high wing, T-tail, and a nose visor that lifts to allow drive-through loading from the front as well as the rear. Its cargo compartment is large enough to carry two M1 Abrams tanks or six Apache helicopters. The landing gear has 28 wheels and can "kneel" to ease loading.

Variants and service

The original C-5A entered service in 1970 and was followed by the improved C-5B. A modernisation programme produced the C-5M Super Galaxy with new engines and avionics, extending the type's service life for decades. The C-5 has supported U.S. operations worldwide since Vietnam, including airlifts to Israel in 1973 and deployments to Iraq and Afghanistan.
