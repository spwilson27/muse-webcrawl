Legionnaires' disease

Legionnaires' disease is a severe form of pneumonia caused by the bacterium Legionella pneumophila and related species, contracted by breathing in mist or vapour from contaminated water.

History

It was identified in 1976 after an outbreak among attendees of an American Legion convention at a Philadelphia hotel, which sickened over 200 people and killed 34; the bacterium was isolated the following year.

Signs and treatment

Symptoms include high fever, cough, shortness of breath, muscle aches and confusion. Diagnosis uses urine antigen tests and culture; treatment is prompt antibiotic therapy. Older adults, smokers and immunocompromised people are at greatest risk.
