Warehouse (nightclub)

The Warehouse was a nightclub at 206 South Jefferson Street in Chicago, Illinois, United States, famous as the birthplace of house music. It operated from 1977 to 1983 under the direction of Robert Williams, with DJ Frankie Knuckles as its musical resident.

History

The club occupied a three-story former warehouse/factory building in Chicago's West Loop. Opened in 1977 as a members-only club catering largely to Black and Latino gay men, it charged a small admission for all-night dancing. Frankie Knuckles, who had DJed in New York with Larry Levan, developed his style there: seamless mixes of disco, soul, Euro-disco, and electronic records, often edited with a reel-to-reel tape machine.

Legacy

Music sold in Chicago record stores as the sound played at the Warehouse was labeled "Warehouse music," which local DJs and shoppers shortened to "house music" — giving the genre its name. After Knuckles left in 1982 to open his own Power Plant club, the Warehouse continued briefly as the Music Box under DJ Ron Hardy. The building was recognized with an honorary street designation, "Frankie Knuckles Way," in 2004, and the site became a Chicago landmark in 2023.
