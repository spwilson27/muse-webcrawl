Christian Danner

Christian Josef Danner (born 4 April 1958 in Munich) is a German former racing driver who competed in Formula One between 1985 and 1989. He is best remembered as the inaugural champion of the International Formula 3000 Championship in 1985, and later became a prominent television commentator and pundit on Formula One in Germany.

Racing career

Danner began his career in German Formula Three and the European Formula Two championship, driving for the March works-supported teams in the early 1980s. When Formula 3000 replaced Formula Two in 1985, Danner, driving a March for Bob Sparshott's BS Automotive, won the first championship, taking victories including at Pau and Vallelunga.

His Formula One debut came with the small German Zakspeed team at the 1985 Belgian Grand Prix. He spent much of 1986 with Osella before returning to Zakspeed in 1987, then moved to Arrows for the 1988 season (starting only the Portuguese Grand Prix as a stand-in), and joined Rial Racing in 1989. At the 1989 United States Grand Prix in Phoenix he finished fourth — his only championship points and the small Rial team's best result — in a race of high attrition.

Later career

After Formula One, Danner raced in CART in North America in the early 1990s and in German touring cars. He subsequently built a long career as a Formula One television commentator, notably for RTL and later ServusTV, and has worked as a driving-safety expert and instructor. He also contributed expert commentary to German coverage of Formula E.
