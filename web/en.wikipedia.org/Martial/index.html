Martial

Marcus Valerius Martialis (known in English as Martial; c. 38–41 AD – c. 103 AD) was a Roman poet born in Hispania (modern Spain), best known for his twelve books of Epigrams, published in Rome between AD 86 and 98, which present witty, often satirical and sometimes obscene vignettes of contemporary Roman life. Martial is regarded as the creator of the modern epigram.

Life

Martial was born at Bilbilis in Hispania Tarraconensis and came to Rome about AD 64. He lived modestly, depending on patrons, and complained in verse about the humiliations of the client–patron system while seeking support from emperors Titus and Domitian, whom he flattered; he also knew Pliny the Younger, who praised his verse and paid his fare home. After Domitian's assassination his fortunes declined, and around 98 he returned to Bilbilis, where he died a few years later.

Epigrams

The Epigrams comprise some 1,500 short poems in elegiac couplets and other metres, preceded by a prose preface in Book 1. They range from panegyric and epitaphs to jokes, insults, literary criticism and sharp portraits of social types — misers, legacy hunters, bad poets and party guests. Martial insisted his page was wanton but his life upright ("lasciva est nobis pagina, vita proba"). His brevity, surprise endings and pointed wit defined the genre for later poets from the Renaissance to the present day.
