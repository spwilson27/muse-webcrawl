Treaty

The signing of a treaty formally concludes negotiations between states.

A treaty is a formal, legally binding written agreement between actors in international law, normally sovereign states and international organisations, but which can also include other subjects of public international law. A treaty may also be known as an international agreement, protocol, covenant, convention, pact, or exchange of letters, among other terms; regardless of terminology, all of these forms are equally treaties under international law and the rules are the same.

Law of treaties

The modern law of treaties is codified principally in the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969), which defines a treaty as "an international agreement concluded between States in written form and governed by international law". Treaties typically proceed through negotiation, adoption of the text, signature, and ratification — the formal confirmation by which a state consents to be bound. Many multilateral treaties enter into force once a specified number of ratifications has been deposited, and parties may in limited circumstances attach reservations excluding or modifying particular provisions.

The binding force of treaties rests on the principle pacta sunt servanda — agreements must be kept. The Vienna Convention also governs interpretation (by reference to the ordinary meaning of the text in context and in light of the treaty's object and purpose), and sets grounds on which a treaty may be invalid, terminated or suspended, such as material breach, fundamental change of circumstances, or conflict with a peremptory norm (jus cogens).

Types

Treaties are classified as bilateral (between two states) or multilateral (between many), and by subject matter: peace treaties, trade agreements, alliances, arms-control treaties, human-rights conventions, boundary settlements, and constituent treaties that create international organisations such as the United Nations Charter (1945) and the treaties of the European Union.

History

Among the earliest known treaties is the Egyptian–Hittite peace treaty concluded after the Battle of Kadesh (c. 1259 BC), a copy of which is displayed at United Nations headquarters. Famous landmarks include the Peace of Westphalia (1648), the Treaty of Paris (1783), the Congress of Vienna settlement (1815), the Treaty of Versailles (1919), and the Geneva Conventions (1949).
