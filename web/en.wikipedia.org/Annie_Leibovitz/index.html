Annie Leibovitz

Anna-Lou Leibovitz (born October 2, 1949) is an American portrait photographer best known for her engaging portraits, particularly of celebrities, which often feature subjects in intimate settings and poses. She is celebrated for her work for Rolling Stone and Vanity Fair.

Early life and Rolling Stone

Born in Waterbury, Connecticut, Leibovitz studied painting at the San Francisco Art Institute, where she developed an interest in photography. She joined Rolling Stone in 1970 and became its chief photographer in 1973, helping define the magazine's visual identity with candid images of rock musicians. Her famous 1980 portrait of John Lennon and Yoko Ono — showing a naked Lennon curled around a clothed Ono — was taken on 8 December 1980, hours before Lennon's murder, and became one of the most iconic magazine covers of all time.

Vanity Fair and later work

From 1983 Leibovitz worked for Vanity Fair, producing elaborately staged celebrity portraits, including the controversial 1991 cover of a nude, pregnant Demi Moore. Her commercial work has included major advertising campaigns, and she has photographed political leaders, athletes, and artists worldwide. Major exhibitions and books, including A Photographer's Life, 1990–2005 and Portraits 2005–2016, survey both her public commissions and intimate family photographs.

Recognition

Leibovitz was designated a Living Legend by the Library of Congress and received the Smithsonian American Ingenuity Award and the Prince of Asturias Award for Communication and Humanities. In 2023 she was inducted into the French Académie des Beaux-Arts.
