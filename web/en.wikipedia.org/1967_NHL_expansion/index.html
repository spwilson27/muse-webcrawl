The 1967 NHL expansion doubled the size of the National Hockey League from six to twelve teams, ending the 25-year "Original Six" era. Six new franchises began play in the 1967–68 season.

New teams

The expansion franchises were the California Seals (later Oakland Seals), Los Angeles Kings, Minnesota North Stars, Philadelphia Flyers, Pittsburgh Penguins, and St. Louis Blues. They were placed in a new West Division, while the Original Six formed the East Division.

Expansion draft and impact

An expansion draft stocked the new clubs from the rosters of the established teams. The St. Louis Blues reached the Stanley Cup Final in each of their first three seasons, though no expansion team won the Cup until the Philadelphia Flyers in 1974.
