Manhunt for Osama bin Laden

The manhunt for Osama bin Laden was the decade-long effort by the United States and its allies to locate and capture or kill Osama bin Laden, founder of al-Qaeda, who was wanted in connection with the 1998 United States embassy bombings and, after 2001, as the mastermind of the September 11 attacks. Bin Laden was placed on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list with a reward of up to $25 million.

Course of the search

After the 2001 invasion of Afghanistan, bin Laden escaped the Battle of Tora Bora in December 2001 and disappeared into the tribal areas of Pakistan. For years his whereabouts were unknown; intelligence efforts focused on his courier network. In 2010 the CIA identified a courier, Abu Ahmed al-Kuwaiti, and followed him to a large, unusually private compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan, where surveillance suggested a tall figure who never left the grounds — "the pacer" — was in residence.

Death

On 2 May 2011 (Pakistan time), US Navy SEALs of the Naval Special Warfare Development Group raided the compound in Operation Neptune Spear, authorised by President Barack Obama, and killed bin Laden. His identity was confirmed by DNA testing and he was buried at sea. Documents seized in the raid provided a major intelligence haul on al-Qaeda.
