Benjamin Graham (May 9, 1894 – September 21, 1976) was a British-born American economist, professor and investor. He is widely known as the "father of value investing", and wrote two of the founding texts of the discipline: Security Analysis (1934), with David Dodd, and The Intelligent Investor (1949), which Warren Buffett has called "by far the best book on investing ever written".

Life and career

Graham was born Benjamin Grossbaum in London and moved with his family to New York as a child. After his father's death the family fell into poverty, an experience Graham credited with shaping his frugality. He graduated from Columbia University in 1914 as salutatorian and, after brief academic work, joined a Wall Street firm, rising to partner. In 1926 he founded the Graham-Newman partnership, which achieved strong long-term returns through disciplined, quantitative bargains. He taught security analysis at Columbia Business School for decades, where his students included Buffett.

Investment philosophy

Graham distinguished investment — an operation which, upon thorough analysis, promises safety of principal and an adequate return — from speculation. Central concepts include the margin of safety (buying securities well below a conservatively estimated intrinsic value), Mr. Market, the allegorical partner whose mood swings between optimism and pessimism the investor should exploit rather than follow, and the search for "net-nets", companies selling below their net current asset value. His approach emphasised balance-sheet analysis, diversification, and emotional discipline over forecasting.

Legacy

Graham's framework shaped generations of investors, including Buffett (who adapted it toward high-quality businesses), and remains the foundation of value investing as taught and practised today.
