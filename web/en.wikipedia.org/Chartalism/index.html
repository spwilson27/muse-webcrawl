Chartalism

Chartalism is a heterodox theory of money emphasizing the role of the state in creating and giving value to currency. The term derives from the Latin charta, meaning a token or ticket.

German economist Georg Friedrich Knapp developed the theory in his State Theory of Money (1905), arguing that money is a creature of law: a currency has value because the state accepts it in payment of taxes and other obligations. This "tax-driven money" view contrasts with metallist theories that money's value comes from a commodity backing.

Chartalism influenced Keynes and Abba Lerner, and is a foundational idea behind Modern Monetary Theory (MMT). Chartalists argue governments that issue their own fiat currency are not financially constrained in the same way as households, though real resource and inflation limits apply.
