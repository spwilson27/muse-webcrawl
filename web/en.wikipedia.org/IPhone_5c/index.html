IPhone 5c

The iPhone 5c is a smartphone that was designed and marketed by Apple Inc. It is the seventh generation of the iPhone, and was announced on September 10, 2013, and released on September 20, 2013, along with its higher-end counterpart, the iPhone 5s. The "c" in the name was widely taken to stand for "colour", reflecting the device's coloured polycarbonate shell.

The iPhone 5c was a mid-range variant of the iPhone line, sold at a lower price than the flagship 5s. Its internal hardware was essentially identical to the preceding iPhone 5: an Apple A6 system-on-chip, a 4-inch Retina display at 1136×640 resolution, and an 8-megapixel rear camera. The aluminium body of the iPhone 5 was replaced with a hard-coated polycarbonate shell reinforced by a steel frame, offered in five colours: blue, green, pink, yellow and white.

History

The 5c replaced the iPhone 5 in Apple's line-up. During its first weekend on sale, Apple reported a combined nine million units sold of the 5s and 5c, though reports suggested the 5s accounted for the majority of sales and that demand for the 5c was lower than expected; Apple subsequently reduced 5c orders from its assemblers. An 8 GB model was introduced in some markets in March 2014.

The iPhone 5c was succeeded by the iPhone 6 and discontinued in most markets in September 2014, though it remained on sale in India until February 2016. It was the last iPhone to use a 32-bit processor and shipped with iOS 7, receiving updates up to iOS 10.

Specifications

  • Display: 4-inch Retina, 1136×640 pixels at 326 ppi
  • Chip: Apple A6, dual-core
  • Storage: 8, 16 or 32 GB
  • Camera: 8 MP rear with LED flash; 1.2 MP front
  • Colours: blue, green, pink, yellow, white
