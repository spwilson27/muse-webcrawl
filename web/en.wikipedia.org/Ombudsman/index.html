Ombudsman

An ombudsman, ombudsperson, ombud, ombuds, or public advocate is a government employee who investigates and tries to resolve complaints, usually through recommendations (binding or not) or mediation. They are usually appointed by the government or by parliament but with a significant degree of independence. Ombudsmen also aim to identify systemic issues leading to poor service or breaches of people's rights.

History

The term originates in Sweden, where a parliamentary ombudsman (Justitieombudsman) was established by the Instrument of Government of 1809 to safeguard the rights of citizens by supervising government agencies. The Swedish king Charles XII had earlier instituted a similar office in 1713, partly inspired by Ottoman institutions he encountered during his exile in the Ottoman Empire. Finland created a parliamentary ombudsman in 1919, and Denmark followed in 1955. The institution spread widely in the second half of the twentieth century, with New Zealand's Parliamentary Commissioner (Ombudsman) Act of 1962 making it one of the first common-law countries to adopt the role.

Functions

Ombudsmen typically handle complaints of maladministration, such as unreasonable delay, failure to follow proper procedures, or unfair treatment by public bodies. In some countries an inspector general, citizen advocate, or other official may have duties similar to those of a national ombudsman, and may be appointed by a legislature. At the local level, an ombudsman may be appointed by a city, county, or municipal government. In some countries, ombudsman services are also provided in the private sector, for example for financial services, telecommunications, and energy; in parts of Canada and the United States, there are also ombudsmen within companies, universities, and news organisations who handle internal complaints.

Etymology

The word derives from the Old Norse umboðsmaðr, in which umboð means a commission or authorisation and maðr means man. In Swedish it came to denote a representative or agent. Modern usage has produced gender-neutral alternatives such as ombudsperson and ombud.
