National Security Act of 1947

The National Security Act of 1947 was a landmark United States law that reorganised the armed forces, foreign policy and intelligence community for the post–Second World War era and the emerging Cold War. It was signed by President Harry S. Truman on 26 July 1947, much of it taking effect that September.

Major provisions

The Act merged the Department of War and the Department of the Navy into the National Military Establishment — renamed the Department of Defense in 1949 — under a Secretary of Defense. It created the National Security Council to advise the President, established the Central Intelligence Agency as the first peacetime intelligence agency, made the Air Force an independent service, and gave statutory status to the Joint Chiefs of Staff. Separate provisions addressed the protection of classified information and atomic energy matters.

Significance

The Act created the institutional architecture of American national security that, with amendments such as the Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986 and the creation of the Director of National Intelligence in 2004, endures today.
