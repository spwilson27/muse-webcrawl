The Battle of Princeton was a battle of the American Revolutionary War, fought near Princeton, New Jersey, on January 3, 1777. It was a victory for the Continental Army under General George Washington, coming just days after the American success at the Battle of Trenton.

Background

After crossing the Delaware River and defeating Hessian forces at Trenton on December 26, 1776, Washington withdrew to Pennsylvania, then recrossed into New Jersey. A British force under Lord Cornwallis advanced to retake Trenton, and the two armies clashed along the Assunpink Creek on January 2, 1777. Washington held his position but was in danger of being trapped against the Delaware River.

The battle

Leaving campfires burning to deceive the British, Washington's army slipped away in the night and marched around Cornwallis's flank toward Princeton. Near dawn, an American column under Brigadier General Hugh Mercer encountered British regiments under Lieutenant Colonel Charles Mawhood. In the fighting that followed, Mercer was mortally wounded, but Washington personally rallied the American line and the British were driven from the field. American forces briefly occupied Princeton before withdrawing.

Aftermath

The victories at Trenton and Princeton revived American morale after a disastrous campaign season in 1776, boosted enlistments, and forced the British to abandon much of New Jersey. The campaign is often seen as a turning point that kept the American cause alive.
