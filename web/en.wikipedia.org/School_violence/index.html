School violence


School safety measures

School violence includes acts of violence occurring on school property, on the way to or from school, or during school-sponsored events. It encompasses bullying, physical fights, assaults on students or staff, homicide and school shootings, as well as threats and the carrying of weapons. School violence affects students' safety, mental health and learning worldwide.

Forms and prevalence

The most common forms are bullying and physical aggression among students; serious lethal violence is comparatively rare but attracts significant public attention, particularly school shootings such as those at Columbine High School (1999) and Sandy Hook Elementary School (2012) in the United States. Corporal punishment by staff, where permitted, and sexual violence are also included in some definitions. Surveys such as the Youth Risk Behavior Survey track trends in victimisation and weapon carrying.

Causes and risk factors

Research associates school violence with factors including prior victimisation, exposure to violence in the home or community, substance abuse, gang involvement and access to firearms. School climate — the quality of relationships, fairness of discipline and sense of belonging — is a significant protective factor.

Prevention

Responses include anti-bullying programmes, restorative practices, threat-assessment teams, counsellors, anonymous reporting systems, and physical security measures. Debates continue over the effectiveness and side effects of hardening measures such as armed staff and metal detectors versus investment in prevention and mental-health services.
