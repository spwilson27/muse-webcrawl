ThyssenKrupp AG (/ˈtɪsən.krʊp/, stylized as thyssenkrupp) is a German multinational conglomerate with focus on industrial engineering and steel production. The company is headquartered in Essen and is one of the world's largest steel producers. It was formed in 1999 from the merger of Thyssen AG and Krupp Group.

History

The Krupp company was founded in Essen in 1811 by Friedrich Krupp as a cast steel factory, growing into one of Germany's largest industrial enterprises and a major arms manufacturer in both World Wars. Thyssen was founded in 1891 by August Thyssen as a rolling mill in Mülheim an der Ruhr, expanding into mines, steelworks, and shipping.

The two firms began cooperating in the 1980s and completed their merger in 1999, creating ThyssenKrupp AG. The combined group inherited a long and controversial history, including the use of forced labour during the Nazi era, for which the Krupp foundation and the company have supported compensation funds.

Operations

ThyssenKrupp's businesses have included steel production (ThyssenKrupp Steel Europe), automotive technology, industrial components such as bearings and forged parts, plant engineering, and marine systems, which builds submarines and naval surface vessels at Kiel. Its elevator division, ThyssenKrupp Elevator, was one of the world's largest elevator manufacturers until it was sold in 2020 to a consortium of Advent International and Cinven for €17.2 billion; it now operates as TK Elevator.

The company has undergone repeated restructuring in response to losses in steel, failed expansion in the Americas (including a costly steel plant in Brazil and a mill in Alabama, both later sold), and pressure from activist investors.

Carbon steel and green transformation

ThyssenKrupp has announced plans to transition its Duisburg steelworks toward hydrogen-based direct reduction to reduce CO₂ emissions, and has explored partnerships and partial sales of its steel division.
