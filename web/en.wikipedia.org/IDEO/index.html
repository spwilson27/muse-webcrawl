IDEO (pronounced "eye-dee-oh") is an international design and consulting firm founded in Palo Alto, California, in 1991 through the merger of David Kelley Design, ID Two, and Matrix Product Design. The firm is credited with popularising the methodology of design thinking—a human-centred approach to innovation emphasising ethnographic observation, brainstorming, and rapid prototyping.

History and notable work

IDEO's predecessor David Kelley Design designed the first mouse for Apple (developed from the Xerox Star concept into the Lisa and Macintosh mouse) and the stand for the Apple III. The merged firm went on to design products and services across consumer electronics, medical devices, retail, and social sectors, including early laptop designs (the GRiD Compass was designed by predecessor ID Two), medical instruments, and the interiors of trains. Its process was widely publicised by a 1999 ABC Nightline segment in which an IDEO team redesigned a shopping cart in five days, and by Tom Kelley's book The Art of Innovation (2001).

Design thinking and education

Through its teaching at Stanford's d.school (founded by David Kelley), publications such as Tim Brown's Change by Design (2009), and the open-learning platform IDEO U and the non-profit IDEO.org, the firm has promoted design thinking to business, government, and social-impact audiences worldwide. IDEO operates studios in the United States, Europe, and Asia.
