The London Stock Exchange (LSE) is a stock exchange in the City of London, England, and one of the world's oldest. Its origins lie in the coffee houses of seventeenth-century Change Alley, where brokers traded shares; a formal subscription room opened in 1773 and the regulated exchange was founded in 1801.

History

The exchange financed much of Britain's industrial and imperial expansion and listed the railway and canal companies of the nineteenth century. The "Big Bang" reforms of 27 October 1986 abolished fixed commissions and introduced electronic trading, transforming the City. In 2007 the LSE merged with Borsa Italiana to form the London Stock Exchange Group, which later acquired the data company Refinitiv.

Markets

The main market hosts the FTSE 100 and FTSE 250 indices of the largest listed companies, while the Alternative Investment Market (AIM), launched in 1995, serves smaller growth companies. The exchange is a major centre for international listings and for trading in bonds and derivatives.
