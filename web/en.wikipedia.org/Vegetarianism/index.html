Vegetarianism

Vegetarianism is the practice of abstaining from the consumption of meat (red meat, poultry, seafood, insects, and the flesh of any other animal). It may also include abstaining from eating all by-products of animal slaughter. A person who practices vegetarianism is known as a vegetarian.

Varieties

Vegetarian diets take several forms. Lacto-ovo vegetarians eat both dairy products and eggs; lacto vegetarians eat dairy but not eggs; ovo vegetarians eat eggs but not dairy. Veganism excludes all animal products, including eggs, dairy and often honey. Semi-vegetarian diets include pescetarianism (which includes fish) and flexitarianism.

History

Vegetarianism has ancient roots in the religious traditions of India, particularly Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism, where it is associated with the principle of ahimsa (non-violence toward animals). In the West, the Greek philosopher Pythagoras was an early advocate. The modern English term dates from the founding of the Vegetarian Society in England in 1847.

Motivations and health

People choose vegetarian diets for ethical reasons concerning animal welfare, environmental concerns about the footprint of meat production, religious beliefs, health considerations, or taste. Well-planned vegetarian diets can be nutritionally adequate, though attention is needed to nutrients such as vitamin B12, iron and omega-3 fatty acids.
