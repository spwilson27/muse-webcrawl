William Henry Vanderbilt

William Henry Vanderbilt (May 8, 1821 – December 8, 1885) was an American businessman and philanthropist. He was the eldest son of Commodore Cornelius Vanderbilt, an heir to his fortune and a railroad magnate himself.

Early career

Initially dismissed by his father as lacking business sense, Vanderbilt proved himself managing a Staten Island farm and then railroads on Long Island and in the Midwest. He gradually took over his father's lines, becoming president of the New York Central and Hudson River Railroad.

Railroad empire

As head of the Vanderbilt system, he consolidated and extended lines including the Lake Shore and Michigan Southern, the Canada Southern, and the Michigan Central, creating one of the largest railroad networks in the world. He roughly doubled the family fortune, and at his death he was the richest man in the United States, with a fortune estimated at nearly $200 million.

"The public be damned"

He is remembered for the phrase "The public be damned!", reportedly uttered in an 1882 interview with a Chicago journalist about discontinuing a fast passenger train, though the exact wording and context were disputed.

Philanthropy and legacy

Vanderbilt gave generously, including $1 million to Vanderbilt University and support for the Metropolitan Opera. His sons Cornelius II and William Kissam Vanderbilt continued the family's prominence in the Gilded Age.
