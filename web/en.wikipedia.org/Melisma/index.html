Melisma

In music, a melisma (from Greek μέλισμα, "song") is the singing of a single syllable of text over several different notes in succession. Music sung in this style is called melismatic, in contrast to syllabic singing, in which each syllable receives a single note.

History and usage

Melisma is ancient and widespread: it appears in Greek and Byzantine chant, in Gregorian chant (where elaborate melismas occur on words such as "Alleluia"), in the ornate singing of Middle Eastern and South Asian traditions, and in many other cultures. In Western music it is prominent in medieval chant, Baroque coloratura arias, gospel, soul and R&B — singers such as Aretha Franklin and Whitney Houston popularized melismatic runs in pop — and in genres like flamenco. Critics have alternately praised melisma as expressive virtuosity and criticized its overuse, particularly after its spread through televised singing competitions in the 2000s.
