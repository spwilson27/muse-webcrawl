Motif (music)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In music, a motif (also motive) is a short musical idea, a salient recurring figure, musical fragment or succession of notes that has some special importance in or is characteristic of a composition: "the smallest structural unit possessing thematic identity."

Characteristics

A motif is usually shorter than a phrase and is defined by its rhythmic, melodic or harmonic profile. It functions as a building block: composers restate, sequence, invert, stretch, fragment and otherwise develop motifs to generate whole movements. The classic example is the opening of Beethoven's Symphony No. 5 — a four-note motif (short-short-short-long, G–G–G–E♭) that pervades the entire first movement and recurs throughout the symphony.

Related concepts

In Wagner's operas, extended motifs associated with persons, objects or ideas are called leitmotifs. In popular music and jazz criticism, the equivalent short recurring figure is often termed a riff or a cell. The concept parallels the motif in literature and the visual arts: a recurrent element that carries thematic meaning.
