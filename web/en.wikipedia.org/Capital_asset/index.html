Capital asset

Property, plant and equipment are typical business capital assets (placeholder)

A capital asset is property of any kind held by an individual or an organisation, whether or not connected with a business, that is expected to generate value over a useful life of more than about one year. In accounting, capital assets include long-lived productive assets such as land, buildings, machinery, equipment, and vehicles — collectively reported on the balance sheet as property, plant and equipment (PP&E) — whose cost is gradually charged against income through depreciation (or amortisation for intangible assets).

Tax treatment

In tax law, capital assets have a special status: when they are sold, the difference between the sale price and the asset's basis is a capital gain or capital loss. In the United States, the Internal Revenue Code broadly defines a capital asset as most property held by the taxpayer, excluding inventory, property held for sale to customers, depreciable business property (handled under separate rules), accounts receivable, and certain other categories. Assets held for more than one year generally qualify for preferential long-term capital gains rates, while shorter holdings are taxed as ordinary income. Other jurisdictions, such as the United Kingdom, apply their own capital gains tax regimes to disposals of chargeable assets.

Types

  • Tangible assets — real estate, buildings, machinery, vehicles, furniture.
  • Intangible assets — patents, copyrights, trademarks, goodwill, and software.
  • Financial assets — in an investment context, stocks, bonds, and other securities are often treated as capital assets for tax purposes.
