Chestnut

The chestnuts are the deciduous trees and shrubs of the genus Castanea, in the beech family Fagaceae, native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. The name also refers to their edible nuts, which ripen inside a spiny husk (burr) that splits open in autumn.

Chestnut

Genus: Castanea

Family: Fagaceae

Species: 8–9 (including sweet chestnut, American chestnut, Chinese chestnut, Japanese chestnut)

Species and distribution

The principal species are the sweet (European) chestnut (Castanea sativa), the American chestnut (C. dentata), the Chinese chestnut (C. mollissima) and the Japanese chestnut (C. crenata). The American chestnut was once a dominant tree of eastern North American forests but was devastated in the early 20th century by chestnut blight, an introduced fungal disease; breeding and restoration programmes continue.

Uses

Chestnuts have been a staple food for millennia, eaten roasted, boiled or ground into flour, and used in confections such as marrons glacés. Unlike most nuts they are high in starch and low in fat. The timber is durable and rot-resistant, valued for furniture, fencing and construction.
