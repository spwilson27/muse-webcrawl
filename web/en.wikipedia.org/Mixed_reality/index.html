Mixed reality

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mixed reality (MR) is a medium in which real and virtual content are merged so that physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time. In mixed reality, virtual objects are anchored to the physical environment and can occlude, or be occluded by, real objects, distinguishing MR from simple augmented reality overlays and from fully immersive virtual reality.

The reality–virtuality continuum

The term derives from Paul Milgram and Fumio Kishino's 1994 reality–virtuality continuum, which places real environments and fully virtual environments at opposite ends, with augmented reality (virtual content added to the real) and augmented virtuality (real content added to the virtual) in between; the whole intermediate range is "mixed reality". Modern usage, popularised by Microsoft with devices such as HoloLens, tends to reserve the term for headsets that map the physical environment and let holograms interact with it. Related umbrella terms include "extended reality" (XR) and "spatial computing".

Technology

Mixed reality systems combine head-mounted displays (optical see-through or video passthrough), inside-out positional tracking, depth sensors that build a mesh of the room, and hand, eye and voice input. Anchoring virtual content to real surfaces requires simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM) and scene understanding. Representative devices include Microsoft HoloLens 2, Magic Leap headsets, and passthrough headsets such as Meta Quest 3 and Apple Vision Pro.

Applications

MR is used in industrial maintenance and remote assistance (an expert annotating a technician's view), surgical planning and guidance, architecture and design review, military and vocational training, and entertainment. Its enterprise focus reflects the cost of the hardware and the value of hands-free, context-aware information in workplaces.
