Columbia (supercontinent)

Columbia, also known as Nuna, was one of Earth's ancient supercontinents. It existed approximately 1.8 to 1.5 billion years ago during the Palaeoproterozoic era, assembling most of the planet's continental crust from earlier cratons.

Assembly and breakup

Columbia grew along global collisional orogens between about 2.1 and 1.8 billion years ago. Proposed reconstructions link cratons now in North America (Laurentia), South America, Africa, Australia, Siberia, India, and China. Breakup began around 1.5–1.4 billion years ago with continental rifting and extensive mafic magmatism, eventually leading to the later supercontinent Rodinia.

Evidence

Reconstructions rest on palaeomagnetic poles, matching orogenic belts, dyke swarms, and detrital zircon records. The names Columbia (proposed by Rogers and Santosh, 2002) and Nuna (from earlier work on northern continents) are often used interchangeably, though some authors distinguish them.

Significance

Columbia's cycle coincided with major changes in the atmosphere, oceans, and early eukaryotic life during the so-called Boring Billion interval of relative environmental stability.
