TypeSulfonated tetrafluoroethylene copolymer (ionomer)

Nafion is a synthetic polymer with ionic properties — an ionomer — discovered in the late 1960s by Walther Grot of DuPont. It is a sulfonated copolymer based on tetrafluoroethylene (the chemistry of Teflon) with perfluorovinyl ether side chains ending in sulfonic acid groups. Nafion was the first ionomer and remains the benchmark proton-exchange membrane.

Properties

The hydrophobic fluorocarbon backbone gives Nafion exceptional chemical and thermal stability, while the hydrophilic sulfonic acid side chains absorb water and cluster into nanoscale channels through which protons and other cations can move. The membrane thus conducts protons readily while blocking anions and gases — a combination few materials match.

Applications

Nafion membranes are the standard electrolyte in proton-exchange membrane (PEM) fuel cells and water electrolysers, and are used in chlor-alkali production, sensors, dehumidification, and catalysis. Its cost and performance loss at high temperature or low humidity have driven research into alternatives, but Nafion-type membranes (now produced by Chemours) remain dominant in commercial practice.
