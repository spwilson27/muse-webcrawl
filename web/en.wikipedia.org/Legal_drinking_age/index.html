Legal drinking age

The legal drinking age is the minimum age at which a person can legally consume alcoholic beverages. Such laws vary widely around the world: most countries set the age at 18, while the United States famously sets it at 21, and a few jurisdictions permit drinking at 16, particularly for beer and wine with meals.

History

Minimum drinking ages emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries alongside temperance movements. In the United States, many states lowered their ages to 18 during the Vietnam War era, then raised them again; the National Minimum Drinking Age Act of 1984 withheld highway funds from states that did not set 21.

By country

Most of Europe uses 18, though Germany allows beer and wine at 16. Canada uses 18 or 19 by province. Some countries prohibit alcohol entirely on religious grounds. Enforcement and social norms differ as much as the statutes themselves.

Effects and debate

Supporters credit higher ages with reducing traffic deaths among young drivers; critics argue they push drinking into unsupervised settings and note that most peer countries manage with 18.
