Tom Tom Club

Tom Tom Club is an American new wave band formed in 1981 by husband-and-wife team Tina Weymouth and Chris Frantz, the bassist and drummer of Talking Heads. Conceived as a playful side project during a Talking Heads hiatus, the band fused funk, hip hop, reggae and pop into dance music that became hugely influential — most famously with the singles "Wordy Rappinghood" and "Genius of Love", the latter one of the most sampled tracks in hip hop and R&B history.

History

Weymouth and Frantz formed the group with musicians including guitarist Adrian Belew and the Weymouth sisters, recording their self-titled debut album (1981) at Compass Point Studios in the Bahamas. "Wordy Rappinghood" was an early rap hit in Europe, and "Genius of Love" reached the US top 40 and number one on the dance charts. Its groove was famously borrowed by Grandmaster Flash ("It's Nasty"), and Mariah Carey's 1995 hit "Fantasy" was built on a sample of it, introducing the song to a second generation. The follow-up, Close to the Bone (1983), included the single "The Man with the 4-Way Hips".

Later work and influence

Subsequent albums — Boom Boom Chi Boom Boom (1988), Dark Sneak Love Action (1992), The Good, the Bad, and the Funky (2000) and the EP Downtown Rockers (2012) — kept the band touring periodically alongside Talking Heads' legacy. Tom Tom Club's sunny, rhythm-first pop stands as one of the defining sounds of early-1980s New York club culture, bridging downtown new wave and the emerging hip hop scene.
