Testimony
In law: A solemn attestation of truth given as evidence
Forms: Oral testimony, written statements, expert testimony
Related concepts: Witness, oath, perjury, cross-examination

Testimony is a solemn attestation as to the truth of a matter. In law, it refers to evidence given by a witness under oath or affirmation, typically in court proceedings, depositions or legislative hearings.

Legal testimony

Witnesses usually testify to facts within their personal knowledge (lay testimony) or, if qualified, offer opinions as expert witnesses. Testimony is subject to cross-examination, and knowingly false testimony constitutes perjury. Rules of evidence may exclude hearsay — out-of-court statements offered for their truth — subject to many exceptions.

Other uses

In religious contexts, testimony is a public account of personal faith or conversion. In history and philosophy, testimony is also a basic source of knowledge: much of what people know, from historical events to everyday facts, is accepted on the testimony of others, raising questions about trust, memory and corroboration.
