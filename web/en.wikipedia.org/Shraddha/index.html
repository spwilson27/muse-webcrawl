Shraddha

Śrāddha (Sanskrit: श्राद्ध) is a Hindu ritual performed for one's ancestors, typically one's deceased parents, to honour them and to ensure their well-being in the afterlife. The ceremony involves offerings of food, especially balls of cooked rice called piṇḍa, along with water, to the Pitrs (departed ancestors). It is regarded as a duty of every Hindu householder.

Performance

The Shraddha ritual is performed by a male relative, usually the eldest son, under the guidance of a Brahmin priest. Offerings include pindas made of rice flour or barley, sesame seeds, water, and sometimes cooked dishes that the deceased enjoyed. The rites are performed on the death anniversary (tithi) of the ancestor, during the fortnight of Pitru Paksha in the Hindu month of Ashwin, and at pilgrimage centres.

Pitru Paksha

During Pitru Paksha, the sixteen-day period dedicated to ancestors, Hindus perform Shraddha and make offerings of food and water (tarpana). Feeding Brahmins and the poor in the name of ancestors is also considered meritorious during this period.

Sacred sites

Certain places are considered especially sacred for Shraddha, notably Gaya in Bihar, where the ritual is performed at the Vishnupad temple and on the banks of the Phalgu River. Other significant sites include Varanasi, Prayagraj, and Kurukshetra.
