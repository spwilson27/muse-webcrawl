Le Guide Culinaire

Le Guide Culinaire is a French cookbook first published in 1903 by the chef Auguste Escoffier (1846–1935). Subtitled an aide-mémoire of practical cooking, it codified the recipes and methods of French haute cuisine and remains a foundational reference for professional chefs.

The book contains more than 5,000 recipes, organising classical French cooking around mother sauces, stocks, garnishes and systematic techniques. Escoffier simplified the elaborate cuisine of Marie-Antoine Carême, streamlining menus and kitchen organisation — including the brigade de cuisine system — for the modern restaurant and hotel kitchen.

Written with Philéas Gilbert and Émile Fétu, the guide appeared in successive revised editions during Escoffier's lifetime and has been translated into English as A Guide to Modern Cookery and The Complete Guide to the Art of Modern Cookery.
