Muay Thai

Muay Thai (Thai: มวยไทย, RTGS: muai thai, pronounced [mūa̯j tʰāj]), sometimes referred to as Thai boxing, is a combat sport that uses stand-up striking, sweeps, and various clinching techniques. This discipline is known as the "Art of Eight Limbs", making use of punches, kicks, elbows, and knee strikes, thus using eight points of contact, as opposed to two points (fists) in boxing and four points (hands and feet) in kickboxing.

History

Muay Thai developed from the older muay boran ("ancient boxing") used by Siamese soldiers. It became a sport in the early 20th century, when gloves, rounds, and rings were introduced, and was codified with weight classes and rules. Major stadiums such as Rajadamnern (1945) and Lumpinee (1956) in Bangkok became the center of the professional sport.

Techniques and culture

Fighters perform a ritual dance, the wai khru ram muay, before bouts, wearing the sacred headband mongkol. Training emphasizes conditioning, roundhouse kicks thrown with the shin, clinch fighting with knee strikes, and elbow strikes. Muay Thai is the national sport of Thailand and its striking base has become fundamental to modern mixed martial arts.
