Philokalia

The Philokalia (Greek: φιλοκαλία, "love of the beautiful") is a collection of texts written between the 4th and 15th centuries by spiritual masters of the Eastern Orthodox Church's hesychast tradition. It was compiled by St Nikodimos of the Holy Mountain and St Makarios of Corinth and first published in Greek in Venice in 1782.

Contents

The anthology gathers writings on prayer, watchfulness, and the inner life from authors including Evagrius Pontikos, St John Cassian, St Maximos the Confessor, St Symeon the New Theologian, and St Gregory Palamas. Its central theme is the practice of the Jesus Prayer and the attainment of inner stillness (hesychia) leading to the vision of divine light.

Translations and influence

A Slavonic translation, the Dobrotolublye, appeared in 1793, followed by a Russian version by St Theophan the Recluse in the 19th century. In the 20th century, an English translation was begun by G. E. H. Palmer, Philip Sherrard, and Bishop Kallistos Ware. The Philokalia profoundly shaped modern Orthodox spirituality and popular interest in hesychasm in the West.
