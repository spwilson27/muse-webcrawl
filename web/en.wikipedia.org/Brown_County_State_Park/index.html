Brown County State Park is a state park in Indiana, United States, located near the town of Nashville in Brown County. At nearly 16,000 acres, it is the largest state park in Indiana. The park's rugged hills, ridges and ravines — sometimes called the "Little Smokies" because of their resemblance to the Great Smoky Mountains — make it especially popular in autumn, when the hardwood forests turn colour.

History

The park was established in 1929. During the 1930s the Civilian Conservation Corps built many of its roads, shelters, picnic areas and the Abe Martin Lodge, named for the cartoon character created by Indiana humourist Kin Hubbard, who was associated with the area. A game preserve and deer reserve were early features of the park.

Recreation

The park offers hiking trails, mountain-biking trails regarded among the best in the Midwest, horseback riding from its saddle barn, camping, fishing on Ogle Lake and Strahl Lake, and a nature centre. Scenic drives wind along the ridges with overlooks such as Hesitation Point. The nearby village of Nashville is known as an artists' colony, a tradition dating to the early 20th century when painters of the Hoosier Group were drawn to the area.
