Partitive

In linguistics, the partitive is a grammatical case which denotes "partialness", "without result", or "without specific identity". It is also used in contexts where a subgroup is selected out of a larger group, or with numbers. The partitive case is found in Finnic languages such as Finnish and Estonian, as well as in some other languages.

Usage in Finnic languages

In Finnish, the partitive (partitiivi) is one of the most frequently used cases. It is used, among other things, for the object of an irresultative or ongoing action ("I am eating (some) bread"), after numerals greater than one, with mass nouns, and in negative sentences, where the object is always in the partitive. It is formed by adding endings such as -a/-ä or -ta/-tä to the stem of the noun.

Partitive articles and constructions

French and Italian possess partitive articles: French du, de la, des express an unspecified quantity of a mass noun, as in je bois du vin ("I drink (some) wine"). In English, partitive constructions use expressions such as "a piece of", "a slice of", or "some of the". Basque, Russian, and several other languages also employ partitive-like genitive or case constructions.
