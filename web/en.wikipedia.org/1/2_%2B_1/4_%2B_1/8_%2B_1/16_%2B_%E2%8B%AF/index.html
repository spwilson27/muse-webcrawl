1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/16 + ⋯

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A geometric series with first term 1/2 and ratio 1/2, converging to 1.

In mathematics, the infinite series 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/16 + ⋯ is an elementary example of a geometric series that converges absolutely. Its sum is 1. In summation notation,

∑n=1..∞ (1/2)n = 1.

Proof

The partial sums are Sn = 1 − (1/2)n, which approach 1 as n grows without bound. More generally, a geometric series with first term a and ratio r (with |r| < 1) converges to a / (1 − r); here a = 1/2 and r = 1/2, giving (1/2) / (1/2) = 1.

History

The series illustrates ideas related to Zeno's paradoxes, in which a journey is divided into halves indefinitely. Archimedes used analogous summations in quadrature problems, and the series is famous as one of the first infinite series to be summed explicitly.
