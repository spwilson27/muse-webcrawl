Credit card

Credit card
Type: Payment card
First issued: 1950 (Diners Club)
Key networks: Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover
Features: Revolving credit, grace period, rewards

A credit card is a payment card, usually issued by a bank, allowing its users to purchase goods or services or withdraw cash on credit. Using the card thus creates a line of credit, or account, that the cardholder accesses when purchasing goods and services. The issuer grants the cardholder a revolving credit line; purchases are accumulated and the cardholder may repay some or all of the balance each billing cycle, with interest charged on any unpaid balance after a grace period.

History

The concept of using a card for purchases was described as early as 1887 in Edward Bellamy's novel Looking Backward. Early charge cards were issued by individual merchants and oil companies in the 1920s. The modern era began with the Diners Club card of 1950, followed by American Express in 1958 and the BankAmericard (later Visa) in 1958, which pioneered a revolving-credit general-purpose card. Magnetic stripes were added in the 1970s, EMV chips from the 1990s, and contactless (NFC) payment in the 2000s–2010s.

How credit cards work

Transactions involve the merchant, the merchant's acquiring bank, a card network (such as Visa or Mastercard), and the issuing bank, which authorises the purchase against the cardholder's credit limit. Merchants pay an interchange fee, typically 1–3% of the transaction. Cardholders receive monthly statements; if the full balance is not paid by the due date, interest accrues at an annual percentage rate (APR) that is often 15–30%.

Features and risks

Common features include rewards (cash back, points, airline miles), purchase protection, and fraud liability limits. Responsible use builds a credit score, while missed payments and high utilisation can harm it. Risks include high-interest revolving debt, penalty fees, and identity theft.
