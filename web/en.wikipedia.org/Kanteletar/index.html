Kanteletar

The Kanteletar is a collection of Finnish folk poetry compiled by Elias Lönnrot, published in 1840–41. Named after the kantele, the Finnish national instrument, it is the lyrical companion to Lönnrot's epic the Kalevala (1835/1849), gathering some 600 shorter traditional poems and songs.

Contents

Lönnrot organised the poems into three books: songs common to all, songs for maidens and for women, and ballads. The material consists of lyric songs, wedding songs, lullabies, shepherds' songs, narrative ballads and laments collected from oral singers in Finland and Finnish Karelia during his field trips of the 1820s–30s. Many pieces voice the experiences of women — courtship, marriage, grief and the work of the household — and are sung in the same trochaic tetrameter ("Kalevala metre") as the epic.

Significance

Together with the Kalevala, the Kanteletar laid the foundation of Finnish-language literature and national identity during the period of the Grand Duchy of Finland. Its poems have inspired composers such as Jean Sibelius and have been translated into many languages.
