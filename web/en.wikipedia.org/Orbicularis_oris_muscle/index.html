Orbicularis oris muscle

Orbicularis oris
Latin: musculus orbicularis oris
Origin: Maxilla and mandible
Insertion: Skin and mucous membrane of the lips
Nerve: Facial nerve (VII), buccal and marginal mandibular branches
Action: Closes and protrudes the lips

The orbicularis oris muscle is a complex of muscles in the lips that encircles the mouth in humans. Until recently it was misinterpreted as a sphincter, or circular muscle, but it is actually composed of four independent quadrants that interlace and give only an appearance of circularity. It is also one of the muscles used in the playing of all brass instruments and some woodwind instruments.

Structure

The muscle surrounds the oral aperture, with fibres derived in part from other facial muscles that converge on the lips, including the buccinator, levator anguli oris, depressor anguli oris, zygomaticus major and minor, and others. It is divided into marginal and peripheral parts. It receives its blood supply chiefly from the superior and inferior labial branches of the facial artery.

Function

Orbicularis oris controls the shape and movements of the lips: closing the mouth, puckering the lips as in kissing and whistling, and shaping the lips for speech and eating. It is essential for articulation of labial sounds. Damage to its nerve supply, as in Bell's palsy, impairs these functions.
