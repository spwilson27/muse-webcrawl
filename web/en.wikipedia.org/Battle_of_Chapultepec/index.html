Battle of Chapultepec

The Battle of Chapultepec was fought on 12–13 September 1847 during the Mexican–American War, when United States forces under General Winfield Scott assaulted Chapultepec Castle, the fortified hill guarding the western approaches to Mexico City. Its capture opened the way into the Mexican capital.

Background

After victories at Contreras, Churubusco and Molino del Rey, Scott's army stood before Mexico City. Chapultepec, a rocky hill crowned by a castle housing Mexico's Military Academy, was defended by General Nicolás Bravo with fewer than 1,000 men, including young military cadets.

The assault

Following a day-long artillery bombardment on 12 September, American assault columns attacked on the morning of 13 September. Scaling parties with ladders stormed the walls after bitter fighting; by mid-morning the castle had fallen, and U.S. forces pressed on along the aqueduct causeways toward the city gates.

The Niños Héroes

Mexican tradition honours six teenage cadets — the Niños Héroes — who died defending the castle rather than surrendering, one of them, Juan Escutia, said to have wrapped himself in the Mexican flag and leapt from the parapet. They are commemorated by a major monument in Chapultepec Park. Mexico City fell the following day, effectively ending the war's major operations.
