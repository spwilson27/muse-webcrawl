Copy number variation

Copy number variation (CNV) refers to differences in the number of copies of a particular section of DNA between individuals. Sections of the genome can be repeated, deleted or duplicated, so that a given gene or region may be present in zero, one, two or many copies instead of the usual two.

Scale and detection

CNVs range from a few hundred base pairs to millions of base pairs in length. They were recognised as a major source of human genetic variation in the mid-2000s through microarray studies, and are now detected by chromosomal microarray analysis and by whole-genome sequencing read-depth analysis.

Effects

Many CNVs are benign, but some alter gene dosage and contribute to disease. Recurrent deletions and duplications are implicated in developmental disorders, autism and schizophrenia, while copy number changes in other genes influence traits such as the copy number of the amylase gene AMY1, which varies with ancestral starch consumption.

Mechanisms

CNVs commonly arise through non-allelic homologous recombination between repetitive sequences, as well as through replication errors and non-homologous repair of broken DNA.
