History of religion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Religions have shaped cultures and civilizations throughout human history.

The history of religion refers to the written record of human religious feelings, thoughts and ideas, beginning with the invention of writing about 5,200 years ago. The prehistory of religion reaches much further back: evidence such as deliberate burials and cave art from the Middle and Upper Paleolithic suggests religious behaviour among early humans tens of thousands of years ago.

Prehistoric and ancient religions

The earliest textual evidence comes from ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, including the Egyptian Pyramid Texts (c. 2400–2300 BC), among the oldest religious texts in the world. Polytheistic religions developed in the ancient Near East, Greece, Rome, India and China. Hinduism, whose roots lie in the Indus Valley and Vedic traditions, is often described as the oldest major religion still widely practised.

The Axial Age

The German philosopher Karl Jaspers called the period roughly 800–200 BC the "Axial Age", during which similar revolutionary thinking appeared in Persia, India, China and the Greco-Roman world: Zoroastrianism, Buddhism, Jainism, Confucianism, Taoism and Greek philosophy emerged or were transformed. Monotheism developed within ancient Israelite religion into Judaism, from which Christianity and later Islam emerged.

Spread of the world religions

In the first millennium AD, Christianity spread through the Roman Empire and beyond, Islam expanded rapidly from Arabia across the Middle East, North Africa and into Europe and Asia, and Buddhism spread along the Silk Road into Central and East Asia. In the second millennium, European colonization carried Christianity worldwide, while trade carried Islam to Southeast Asia and sub-Saharan Africa. The Protestant Reformation of the sixteenth century divided Western Christianity.

Modern developments

The Enlightenment, science and secularization reshaped religion's role in many societies, while revivals, fundamentalist movements and new religious movements also appeared. In the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, interfaith dialogue, migration-driven religious diversity and the rise of the religiously unaffiliated in many countries have been major themes. The academic study of religion — history of religions, comparative religion and religious studies — examines these developments without confessional commitment.
