Veracruz

Veracruz, formally Veracruz de Ignacio de la Llave, is one of the 31 states of Mexico. Located in eastern Mexico, it stretches along the Gulf of Mexico coast and is the country's third most populous state, with about 8 million inhabitants. Its capital is Xalapa, while its largest city and principal port is the city of Veracruz, founded by Hernán Cortés in 1519 as the first Spanish settlement on the Mexican mainland.

History

Pre-Columbian Veracruz was home to the Olmec, Totonac and Huastec cultures; the great city of El Tajín is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Cortés landed near present-day Veracruz in 1519 and marched inland to conquer the Aztec Empire. During the colonial period the port was Spain's gateway to Mexico. The port city was invaded by the United States in 1847 and occupied in 1914, and was declared "four times heroic" for its resistance to foreign attacks.

Geography and economy

The state ranges from coastal plains to the Sierra Madre Oriental and the snow-capped Pico de Orizaba, Mexico's highest peak. Veracruz is a major agricultural producer (coffee, sugar cane, citrus, vanilla) and a centre of the petrochemical industry. Its culture is known for son jarocho music, including the song "La Bamba", and for its distinctive cuisine.
