Dog agility

Dog agility is a dog sport in which a handler directs a dog through an obstacle course in a race for both time and accuracy. Dogs run off lead with no food or toys as incentives, and the handler can touch neither dog nor obstacles. Consequently, the handler's controls are limited to voice, movement, and various body signals, requiring exceptional training of the animal and coordination of the handler.

History

Agility was introduced at Crufts in 1978 as an entertainment demonstration between the obedience championships and group judging, inspired by equestrian show jumping. Its instant popularity led to it becoming a recognized sport with national and international championships.

Obstacles

A course typically includes jumps, tunnels, weave poles, an A-frame, a dog walk, a seesaw (teeter-totter), and a pause table. Dogs must traverse the numbered obstacles in the correct order; faults are assessed for knocked bars, missed contact zones, or wrong courses, and runs are timed.

Competition

Major sanctioning bodies include the American Kennel Club, the United States Dog Agility Association, and the Fédération Cynologique Internationale, which holds world championships. Border Collies and other herding breeds are especially successful, though all breeds and mixed breeds compete.
