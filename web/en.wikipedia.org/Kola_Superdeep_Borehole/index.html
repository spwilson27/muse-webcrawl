Kola Superdeep Borehole

The Kola Superdeep Borehole was a Soviet scientific drilling project on the Kola Peninsula, begun in 1970, which produced the deepest artificial point on Earth. The main borehole, SG-3, reached a true vertical depth of 12,262 metres (40,230 ft) in 1989.

Findings

Drilling found unexpectedly high temperatures (about 180 °C at depth), fractured rock containing water, hydrogen gas, and microscopic plankton fossils in rocks two billion years old. The expected change from granite to basalt at the Conrad discontinuity was not observed; seismic reflections attributed to it instead reflected metamorphic change.

End of the project

Drilling stopped in 1995 after the Soviet collapse and funding loss. The site was later largely abandoned and the borehole capped, but its depth record for a drilled hole stood for decades.
