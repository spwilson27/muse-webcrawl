Yield strength

QuantityStress (pressure)
SI unitMegapascal (MPa)
Symbolσy

The yield strength or yield point of a material is the stress at which it begins to deform plastically — that is, permanently. Below the yield point, deformation is elastic and the material returns to its original shape when the load is removed.

Determination

Yield strength is found from a tensile test's stress–strain curve. Some materials, such as mild steel, show a distinct upper and lower yield point. Many alloys show no sharp point, so engineers use the 0.2% offset method: the stress at which 0.2% permanent strain remains.

Importance

Yield strength is a key design limit: structures are normally designed so working stresses stay below it, since plastic deformation usually constitutes failure. Typical values range from tens of megapascals for pure aluminium to over 1,000 MPa for high-strength steels.
