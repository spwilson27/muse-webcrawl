Augustus De Morgan

Born: 27 June 1806, Madurai, British India

Died: 18 March 1871 (aged 64), London, England

Fields: Mathematics, logic

Known for: De Morgan's laws; induction; four colour conjecture

Augustus De Morgan (27 June 1806 – 18 March 1871) was a British mathematician and logician. He formulated De Morgan's laws and introduced the term "mathematical induction", making the idea rigorous. He was the first professor of mathematics at University College London, a founding figure of the London Mathematical Society, and an influential teacher and writer.

Work in logic

De Morgan's Formal Logic (1847) developed a logic of relations and quantified syllogisms, moving beyond Aristotelian logic and paving the way for the algebraic logic of George Boole and later symbolic logic. De Morgan's laws state that the negation of a conjunction is the disjunction of the negations, and vice versa — principles fundamental to logic, set theory and computer science.

Other contributions

De Morgan corresponded with William Rowan Hamilton and Ada Lovelace, whom he tutored. He first stated the four colour problem in a letter of 1852. His whimsical Budget of Paradoxes, published posthumously, catalogued pseudo-scientific claims; his interest in circle-squarers inspired the mathematical use of the term "crank". He resigned his chair in 1866 in a dispute over religious tests.
