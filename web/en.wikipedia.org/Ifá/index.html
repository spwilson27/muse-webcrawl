Ifá

Ifá is a Yoruba religion and system of divination originating among the Yoruba people of what is now Nigeria. Its literary corpus, the Odu Ifá, consists of verses recited by a trained priest, a babalawo ("father of mysteries"), during divination performed with palm nuts (ikin) or a divining chain (ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀).

Divination

The divination process generates one of 256 signs (odù), each associated with a body of verses, stories, and prescribed sacrifices or actions. Ifá is consulted for decisions, illness, marriage, and communal affairs. Orunmila is the orisha of wisdom associated with Ifá.

Recognition

UNESCO proclaimed the Ifa divination system a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2005 (inscribed on the Representative List in 2008). Ifá is also practised in the African diaspora, including in Cuban Santería (Regla de Ocha) and Brazilian Candomblé.

Training

Babalawo undergo years of apprenticeship memorising the verses and their interpretations. Women known as iyanifa hold related roles in some lineages.
