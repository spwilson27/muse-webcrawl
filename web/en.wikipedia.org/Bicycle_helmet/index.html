Bicycle helmet

A bicycle helmet is a type of helmet designed to attenuate impacts to the head of a cyclist in falls, while minimising side effects such as interference with peripheral vision. Modern helmets are usually made of expanded polystyrene foam covered by a thin plastic shell, with straps to hold the helmet in position.

History

Early cyclists used pith helmets and later leather "hairnet" helmets, which offered little protection. Hard-shell helmets appeared in the 1970s; the 1975 Bell Biker was among the first purpose-built bicycle helmets. Foam helmets without hard shells followed in the 1980s, and standards such as those of the Snell Memorial Foundation, ANSI, ASTM and the US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) came to govern design and testing. Since 1999, helmets sold in the United States must meet the CPSC standard.

Effectiveness and debate

Case-control studies and meta-analyses generally find that helmets reduce the risk of head and brain injury in crashes. However, the size of the benefit, the possible increase in rotational injury risk, and whether mandatory helmet laws discourage cycling have all been debated. Some jurisdictions, including Australia, New Zealand, and parts of Canada and Spain, have enacted helmet compulsion laws, while in countries such as the Netherlands and Denmark helmets remain uncommon among everyday cyclists.

Design

Helmets are tested by dropping them onto anvils to simulate impacts. Designers balance impact absorption against weight, ventilation, and coverage; newer designs incorporate rotational-impact protection systems such as MIPS.
