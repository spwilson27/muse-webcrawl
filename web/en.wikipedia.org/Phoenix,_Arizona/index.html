Phoenix is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Arizona, with 1,608,139 residents at the 2020 census, making it the fifth-most populous city in the United States and the most populous state capital. It is the seat of Maricopa County and the core of the Phoenix metropolitan area, widely known as the Valley of the Sun, home to roughly 4.85 million people.

History

The valley was inhabited for over two thousand years by the Hohokam people, who built an extensive network of irrigation canals before their society declined around 1450. Modern Phoenix was founded in 1867 when farmer Jack Swilling recognised the agricultural potential of the valley and organised an irrigation company using the ancient Hohokam canals. The town was incorporated in 1881 and became Arizona's territorial — and later state — capital. The arrival of the railroad in the 1880s and, later, air conditioning in the mid-20th century spurred rapid growth, transforming Phoenix into a major metropolitan centre of the American Southwest.

Geography and climate

Phoenix sits in the Salt River Valley in the northeastern Sonoran Desert, surrounded by mountain ranges including South Mountain, Camelback Mountain, and the Phoenix Mountains Preserve. It has a hot desert climate with extremely hot summers — average July highs exceed 106 °F (41 °C), and the city recorded a high of 122 °F (50 °C) in June 1990 — and mild winters with highs near 67 °F (19 °C). Rainfall averages about 8 inches (200 mm) per year, split between winter storms and the summer North American Monsoon.

Economy

Early growth rested on the "five C's" — cotton, cattle, citrus, copper, and climate (tourism). Today the economy is more diversified, with major employers in finance, real estate, technology and semiconductor manufacturing, healthcare, and government. Companies such as Freeport-McMoRan and Avnet are headquartered in the area, and Arizona State University, headquartered in neighbouring Tempe, is a major economic engine.

Transportation

Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport is one of the busiest airports in the United States. The city is served by Interstates 10 and 17, and the Valley Metro light rail system opened in 2008, linking Phoenix with Tempe and Mesa.

Sports

Phoenix is one of the few U.S. cities with teams in all four major leagues: the Phoenix Suns (NBA), Arizona Cardinals (NFL, in Glendale), Arizona Diamondbacks (MLB), and Arizona Coyotes (NHL, formerly in the area). The region also hosts the Waste Management Phoenix Open golf tournament and Cactus League spring training.
