Autoroute (Quebec)

The autoroutes are the controlled-access highways of the Canadian province of Quebec. Operated under the jurisdiction of Transports Québec, the network is identified by distinctive red, white, and blue shield markers and forms the backbone of the province's road system alongside numbered provincial routes.

Network

Construction began in the late 1950s under Premier Maurice Duplessis; the Autoroute des Laurentides (A-15) was among the first, and part of it opened as a toll road. Major corridors include Autoroute 20 and Autoroute 40, the two main links between Montreal and Quebec City on the south and north shores of the St. Lawrence River respectively, and Autoroute 10 to the Eastern Townships. Together the autoroutes run to roughly 2,300 kilometres.

Numbering

Even numbers generally denote east-west routes and odd numbers north-south routes; three-digit numbers indicate beltways and spurs around cities. Most autoroutes are toll-free, though some bridges and the newer A-25 and A-30 segments charge tolls.
