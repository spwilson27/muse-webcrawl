Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk (novella)

Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk
AuthorNikolai Leskov
Published1865
LanguageRussian

Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk (Russian: Леди Макбет Мценского уезда) is a novella by Nikolai Leskov, first published in 1865 in Dostoevsky's journal Epoch. It tells the story of Katerina Izmailova, a merchant's wife whose passion for a clerk leads her to murder — an allusion to Shakespeare's Lady Macbeth in a provincial Russian setting.

Plot

Katerina Lvovna, trapped in a loveless, childless marriage in a stifling merchant household in Mtsensk, begins an affair with the handsome clerk Sergei while her husband is away. To protect the affair she murders her father-in-law, then her returning husband, and finally the young nephew who stands to inherit. Caught, she and Sergei are sentenced to Siberian exile; on the convict march Sergei betrays her with another woman, and Katerina drowns both her rival and herself in the Volga.

Themes

Leskov called the work a study in a strong, passionate character destroyed by the boredom and brutality of provincial merchant life. Katerina is portrayed with unusual sympathy for a murderess, blurring the line between victim and villain.

Adaptations

The novella is best known through Shostakovich's opera Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk (1934), whose success was cut short by the 1936 Pravda editorial "Muddle Instead of Music". Later adaptations include Andrzej Wajda's film Siberian Lady Macbeth (1962) and William Oldroyd's film Lady Macbeth (2016).
