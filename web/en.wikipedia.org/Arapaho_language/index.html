Arapaho language

The Arapaho language (Arapaho: Hinónoʼeitíít) is an Algonquian language of the Plains, spoken by the Arapaho people of Wyoming and Oklahoma. It belongs to the Arapahoan subgroup, most closely related to Gros Ventre (Atsina). Like other Algonquian languages, it is polysynthetic, building long, complex verb forms from many meaningful parts.

Status

Arapaho is severely endangered. The Northern Arapaho of the Wind River Reservation in Wyoming have the largest speaker community, but most fluent speakers are elders. Revitalisation efforts include immersion preschools, language classes, documentation projects and recordings of fluent speakers.

Phonology and grammar

The language has a small set of vowels distinguished by length, and a consonant system featuring contrasts unusual among Algonquian languages, reflecting its long separate development on the Plains. Word order is relatively free, and verbs encode extensive information about participants, obviation and direction of action.
