Blood is a body fluid in the circulatory system of humans and other vertebrates that delivers necessary substances such as nutrients and oxygen to the cells, and transports metabolic waste products away from those same cells.

Composition

Blood is composed of blood cells suspended in blood plasma. Plasma, which constitutes about 55% of blood fluid, is mostly water (92% by volume) and contains proteins, glucose, mineral ions, hormones, and carbon dioxide. The cellular elements are red blood cells (erythrocytes), which carry oxygen via the protein haemoglobin, white blood cells (leukocytes) of the immune system, and platelets involved in clotting.

Functions

Blood regulates body temperature and pH, defends against infection, and forms clots to prevent excessive bleeding. In an average adult human, about 5 litres of blood circulate, continually pumped by the heart. Disorders of the blood include anemia, haemophilia, and the blood cancers such as leukemia.
