Jane Seymour

Jane Seymour (c. 1508/1509 – 24 October 1537) was Queen of England as the third wife of King Henry VIII, from 1536 until her death twelve days after giving birth to Henry's only surviving legitimate son, Edward VI.

Rise

Jane was the daughter of Sir John Seymour of Wulfhall, Wiltshire, and served as a lady-in-waiting to both Catherine of Aragon and Anne Boleyn. Henry became interested in her in early 1536 — the contrast with Anne was often noted: Jane was quiet, demure and, reportedly, pro-Catholic in sympathy. They were betrothed the day after Anne Boleyn's execution and married on 30 May 1536 at Whitehall.

Queenship and death

As queen, Jane's motto was "Bound to obey and serve." She interceded, unsuccessfully, for the rebels of the Pilgrimage of Grace and for the restoration of Princess Mary to the succession — Mary was in fact reconciled with her father during Jane's tenure. In October 1537 she delivered a son, Edward, at Hampton Court after a long labour, but died on 24 October, probably of puerperal sepsis.

Henry mourned her conspicuously and was eventually buried beside her in St George's Chapel, Windsor, the only one of his six wives to receive a queen's funeral and to share his tomb.
