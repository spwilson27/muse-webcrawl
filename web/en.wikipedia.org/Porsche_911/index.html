The Porsche 911 (pronounced nine eleven, German: Neunelfer) is a two-door 2+2 high-performance rear-engined sports car introduced in September 1964 by Porsche AG of Stuttgart, Germany. It has a rear-mounted flat-six engine and originally a torsion-bar suspension. The car has been continuously enhanced through the years but the basic concept has remained unchanged. The engines were air-cooled until the introduction of the 996 series in 1998.

The 911 has been raced extensively by private and factory teams, in rallying, and in GT racing, and is among the most successful competition cars of all time. More than one million units have been produced, making it one of the most enduring sports car nameplates.

Generations

The 911's internal generations are: the original 911 (1964–1989, including the G-series and the 930 Turbo), the 964 (1989–1994), the 993 (1994–1998, the last air-cooled model), the water-cooled 996 (1998–2005) with controversial "fried-egg" headlamps, the 997 (2005–2012), the 991 (2012–2019), and the 992 (2019–present). Variants span Carrera, Carrera S, Targa, Turbo and Turbo S, the track-focused GT3 and GT2 RS, and high-performance models such as the 911 Dakar and S/T.

Design and engineering

The 911 retains its distinctive silhouette with a fastback roofline sloping to the rear engine deck. Its flat-six ("boxer") engine sits behind the rear axle, giving characteristic handling: abundant rear traction but a tendency to lift-off oversteer that generations of chassis tuning, wider rear tyres, and electronic aids have progressively tamed. Modern models use twin-turbocharged flat-six engines (and naturally aspirated units in the GT3), with PDK dual-clutch or manual transmissions and optional all-wheel drive (Carrera 4).

Motorsport

The 911 has won major events including the 24 Hours of Le Mans (class wins), the Monte Carlo Rally, the Paris–Dakar Rally (in 1984 with the 953), and countless GT championships with the RSR and GT3 R/Cup derivatives. One-make Carrera Cup series run worldwide.
