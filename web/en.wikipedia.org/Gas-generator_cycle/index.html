Gas-generator cycle

The gas-generator cycle is a power cycle used in liquid-propellant rocket engines. A small fraction of the propellants is burned in a separate chamber called a gas generator (or preburner), and the resulting hot gas drives the turbines of the turbopumps that feed propellant to the main combustion chamber. The turbine exhaust is then discharged overboard, usually through a separate exhaust pipe or nozzle, rather than entering the main chamber.

Characteristics

Because the gas generator's exhaust does not contribute efficiently to thrust, the cycle sacrifices some specific impulse compared with the more efficient staged combustion cycle. In return, the gas-generator cycle is considerably simpler and more robust: the gas generator can be run fuel-rich and relatively cool, easing turbine material requirements, and development and operation are less demanding. It is an open cycle, since part of the propellant flow is dumped after driving the pumps.

Examples

Engines using the gas-generator cycle include the F-1 and J-2 of the Saturn V, the RS-68, the Vulcain of Ariane 5, the Merlin engines of SpaceX's Falcon rockets, and many others. SpaceX's Merlin exhausts its gas generator through a distinctive roll-control exhaust on the Falcon 9 first stage engines. The cycle remains the most widely used approach for pump-fed rocket engines where simplicity and reliability outweigh the performance gains of closed cycles.
