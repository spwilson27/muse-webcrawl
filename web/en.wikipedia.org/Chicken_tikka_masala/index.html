Chicken tikka masala

Chicken tikka masala is a dish of roasted marinated chicken chunks (chicken tikka) in a spiced sauce, usually creamy and orange-coloured, based on tomato, cream and spices. It is among the most popular dishes in British restaurants and is often cited as an example of modern multicultural British cuisine; a British foreign secretary, Robin Cook, famously called it "a true British national dish".

Origins

The dish's origins are disputed. One famous account credits Ali Ahmed Aslam of the Shish Mahal restaurant in Glasgow in the 1970s, who is said to have improvised a sauce from tomato soup, cream and spices for a customer who found his chicken tikka too dry. Others regard it as a natural development of butter chicken (murgh makhani) from Delhi, adapted in Britain's curry houses. In 2009 a motion in the UK Parliament sought European protected status for a Glasgow origin.

Preparation

Chicken is marinated in yoghurt and spices, roasted (traditionally in a tandoor), and simmered in a sauce of tomato, cream or coconut, garam masala and other spices. It is typically served with basmati rice or naan bread.
