Sanlu Group Co., Ltd. was a Chinese state-owned dairy products company based in Shijiazhuang, Hebei. It was one of the largest dairy producers in China, and the New Zealand dairy cooperative Fonterra held a 43% stake in the company. Sanlu became notorious as the central company in the 2008 Chinese milk scandal.

The 2008 milk scandal

In 2008 it was revealed that Sanlu's infant formula had been adulterated with melamine, an industrial chemical added to watered-down milk to make its protein content appear higher. The contamination caused kidney stones and renal failure in infants: Chinese authorities estimated some 300,000 children were affected, tens of thousands were hospitalised, and at least six infants died. Complaints about the formula had reached the company months before the scandal became public, and the delay in reporting and recall drew widespread condemnation.

Aftermath

Sanlu halted production in September 2008 and was declared bankrupt by a Shijiazhuang court in December 2008; its assets were later acquired by Sanyuan Group. Trials followed: Sanlu's chairwoman Tian Wenhua was sentenced to life imprisonment, and two men involved in producing and selling the contaminated milk powder were executed. The scandal severely damaged consumer confidence in Chinese food safety and led to major regulatory reforms and enduring demand for imported infant formula.
