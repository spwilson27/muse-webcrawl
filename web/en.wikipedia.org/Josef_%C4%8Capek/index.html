Josef Čapek

Josef Čapek
Born: 23 March 1887, Hronov, Bohemia
Died: April 1945, Bergen-Belsen concentration camp
Occupation: Painter, writer, illustrator

Josef Čapek (23 March 1887 – April 1945) was a Czech artist who was best known as a painter, but who was also noted as a writer and poet. He invented the word "robot", which was introduced into literature by his younger brother, Karel Čapek.

Life and work

Born in Hronov, Bohemia, Josef studied in Prague and Paris and developed a style influenced by Cubism and primitivism. He collaborated with Karel on plays and stories including The Insect Play, and wrote his own works such as the children's book Povídání o pejskovi a kočičce (Tales of Doggie and Pussycat).

The word robot

When Karel was writing the play R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots), Josef suggested the word robot, derived from the Czech robota, meaning forced labour or drudgery.

Death

Arrested for his anti-fascist work after the German occupation of Czechoslovakia, Josef spent most of World War II in concentration camps, writing poems in Bergen-Belsen, where he died in April 1945, shortly before liberation.
