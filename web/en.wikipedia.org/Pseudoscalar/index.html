Pseudoscalar

In linear algebra, a pseudoscalar is a quantity that behaves like a scalar under proper rotations of a coordinate system but changes sign under an improper rotation, such as a reflection through a mirror. Pseudoscalars arise naturally when a scalar is formed from quantities that depend on the orientation (handedness) of the coordinate frame.

In physics

In physics, a pseudoscalar is a physical quantity represented by a single number whose sign depends on the parity of the coordinate system. Familiar examples include the scalar triple product of three vectors, magnetic charge (in hypothetical formulations), and the helicity of a particle in certain limits. In particle physics, a pseudoscalar particle is a boson whose intrinsic parity is negative; the pion, kaon, and eta mesons are pseudoscalar mesons, and the Higgs-like particle hypothesized in some two-Higgs-doublet models can also be pseudoscalar. Quantities odd under parity were central to the discovery of parity violation in weak interactions in 1956–57.

In geometric algebra

In geometric algebra, the term refers to the highest-grade element of the algebra — in three dimensions, the trivector e1e2e3 — which plays a role analogous to the imaginary unit and changes sign when the orientation of the basis is reversed. Multiplying a vector by the pseudoscalar produces its dual, a pseudovector-related construction.
