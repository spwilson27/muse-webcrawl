The Four Holy Cities (Hebrew: ארבע ערי הקודש, Arba Arei ha-Kodesh) are the four cities in the Land of Israel that became the principal centres of Jewish life after the Ottoman conquest of Palestine in the 16th century: Jerusalem, Hebron, Safed (Tzfat), and Tiberias. The collective term appears from about the 1640s, bound up with the halukka system, by which charitable funds collected in the diaspora were distributed to the Jewish communities of the four cities.

The cities

Jerusalem, the holiest city in Judaism, site of the ancient Temples and of the Western Wall, was always pre-eminent. Hebron is revered as the burial place of the patriarchs and matriarchs at the Cave of Machpelah (Cave of the Patriarchs). Safed, a hill town in the Galilee, became the great centre of Jewish mysticism in the 16th century, home to Rabbi Isaac Luria, founder of Lurianic Kabbalah, and to Joseph Karo, author of the Shulchan Aruch. Tiberias, on the Sea of Galilee, was the seat of the Sanhedrin in late antiquity and the place where the Jerusalem Talmud was substantially compiled and the Masoretic vocalisation of the Hebrew Bible developed.

Symbolism

Tradition associates each city with one of the four elements: Jerusalem with fire, Hebron with earth, Safed with air, and Tiberias with water. The four cities remained the heart of the "Old Yishuv," the pre-Zionist Jewish community of Ottoman Palestine, whose emissaries (meshulachim) travelled the diaspora raising funds for their support.
