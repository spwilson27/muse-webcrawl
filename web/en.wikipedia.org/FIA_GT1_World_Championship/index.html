The FIA GT1 World Championship was a world championship sports car racing series developed by the SRO Group and regulated by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA), held from 2010 to 2012. It featured grand tourer racing cars modified from production road cars and complying with GT1 (2010–2011) and GT3 (2012) regulations. It was one of only a handful of FIA world championships alongside Formula One, the World Rally Championship, and the World Touring Car Championship.

Format

Each event consisted of two one-hour races: a qualifying race and a championship race, with points from both counting toward the drivers' and teams' titles. Two drivers shared each car, with a mandatory pit stop and driver change. The series visited circuits across Europe, the Middle East, South America, and Asia, including Yas Marina, Silverstone, the Nürburgring, Interlagos, and San Luis in Argentina, which traditionally hosted the finale.

Seasons

The inaugural 2010 season featured cars such as the Nissan GT-R, Maserati MC12, Aston Martin DBR9, Corvette C6.R, Lamborghini Murciélago, and Ford GT; Michael Bartels and Andrea Bertolini won the drivers' title for the Vitaphone Racing Team. In 2011, JR Motorsports Nissan drivers Michael Krumm and Lucas Luhr took the championship. For 2012, declining GT1 entries led the series to adopt GT3 machinery; Marc Basseng and Markus Winkelhock won the final title for Münnich Motorsport in a Mercedes-Benz SLS AMG, and the championship was replaced in 2013 by the FIA GT Series sprint format.
