Siege of Tyre (332 BC)

Alexander's mole joined island Tyre permanently to the mainland of Phoenicia.

The Siege of Tyre was waged by Alexander the Great in 332 BC against Tyre, the great island fortress of Phoenicia, during his war with the Persian Empire. Tyre offered to submit to Macedonian rule but refused Alexander entry to sacrifice in the island temple of Melqart; Alexander resolved to take the city, whose walls rose some 46 metres (150 ft) directly from the sea.

The mole

Lacking a fleet, Alexander demolished mainland Old Tyre and threw its rubble into the strait, building a causeway — a mole — roughly 60 metres wide across the kilometre of water. Tyrian defenders harassed the work with divers, missiles, and a fireship that destroyed the siege towers at the mole's end. The mole survives today: silt built up around it made Tyre a permanent peninsula.

Naval blockade and assault

The siege turned when the kings of Sidon and Cyprus, defecting from Persia, brought Alexander some 200 ships. With Tyre blockaded, Macedonian engines mounted on vessels battered the walls until a breach allowed an assault combining the fleet and troops from the mole. After seven months the city fell; some 8,000 Tyrians were killed and 30,000 sold into slavery, though many were sheltered by the Sidonian sailors who recognized fellow Phoenicians.

Aftermath

Alexander sacrificed to Melqart in the temple he had been denied. Tyre recovered commercially under Hellenistic rule but never regained its primacy among Phoenician cities; its sister city Sidon, which had submitted, prospered instead. See also Temple of Eshmun, the great sanctuary of nearby Sidon.
