Temple of Eshmun

The Temple of Eshmun (Arabic: معبد أشمون) is an ancient place of worship dedicated to Eshmun, the Phoenician god of healing. It is located near Sidon in southwestern Lebanon. The site was occupied from the 7th century BC to the 8th century AD, attesting to the long history of the sanctuary. It was built under Sidonian kings including Eshmunazar II and Bodashtart, and was expanded in the Hellenistic, Roman, and Byzantine periods.

History

The sanctuary grew around a sacred spring, Ydll, in a grove associated with healing waters — Sidon was famous for its curative springs, and Eshmun was identified by the Greeks with their healing god Asclepius. King Bodashtart (6th century BC) undertook monumental construction, including a massive podium, canals, and water installations fed by the spring. A famous dedicatory inscription of Bodashtart records his building works for "his lord Eshmun at the Ydll spring".

Architecture and remains

The complex includes a large podium, a monumental staircase, basins and channels for ritual bathing, a colonnaded structure, a Roman-era nymphaeum, and a Byzantine church. Because the sanctuary functioned as a healing shrine, water management was central to its design, with pools used for purification and therapeutic immersion.

Site today

The site, about 2 km northeast of Sidon near the Awali River, is one of the best-preserved Phoenician sites in Lebanon. It has suffered periods of looting and was excavated in the 20th century by Maurice Dunand. Many finds, including votive statues of healed children, are held in Lebanese museums.
