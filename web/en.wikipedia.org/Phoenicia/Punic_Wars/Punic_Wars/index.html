Date: 264–146 BC
Location: Western Mediterranean
Result: Roman victory; destruction of Carthage

Punic Wars

The Punic Wars were a series of wars between 264 and 146 BC fought between Rome and Carthage. Three wars took place, on land and at sea, across the western Mediterranean, involving a total of forty-three years of warfare. They are thought to have been the largest wars of the ancient world.

The three wars

The First Punic War (264–241 BC) centred on Sicily and naval battles; Rome built a navy, won, and took Sicily and later Sardinia and Corsica. The Second Punic War (218–201 BC) saw Hannibal cross the Alps and win at Cannae, but Rome prevailed after Scipio's campaigns in Iberia and victory at Zama in Africa. The Third Punic War (149–146 BC) ended with the siege and complete destruction of the city of Carthage.

Consequences

The wars established Rome as the dominant power of the western Mediterranean, gave it its first overseas provinces, and set the stage for its expansion around the entire sea. Carthaginian territory became the Roman province of Africa.
