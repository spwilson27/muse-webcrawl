Punic Wars

The Punic Wars decided whether the Phoenician or the Roman world would rule the Mediterranean.

The Punic Wars were three wars fought between the Roman Republic and the Carthaginian Empire between 264 and 146 BC. "Punic" derives from the Latin Punicus, meaning Phoenician: Carthage, Rome's adversary, was a Phoenician foundation whose people spoke Punic, the western dialect of the Phoenician language. The wars are the central military drama of Phoenicia's colonial world.

First Punic War (264–241 BC)

Beginning over the Sicilian city of Messana, the first war forced landlocked Rome to build a navy, including the boarding device called the corvus. Roman victory at the Aegates Islands won Sicily, Rome's first overseas province, followed by Sardinia and Corsica.

Second Punic War (218–201 BC)

Hannibal Barca's invasion of Italy over the Alps brought Carthage its greatest triumphs — Trebia, Trasimene, and Cannae — but Rome's attritional strategy and Scipio's victories in Spain and Africa ended the war at Zama. Carthage lost its empire, fleet, and independence in foreign policy.

Third Punic War and destruction (149–146 BC)

Provoked by Carthage's recovery and championed by Cato the Elder's "Carthage must be destroyed," Rome besieged the city for three years. In 146 BC Carthage was stormed, burned, and its population enslaved — ending seven centuries of Phoenician civilization in the west, though Punic language and culture survived for centuries in the North African countryside.
