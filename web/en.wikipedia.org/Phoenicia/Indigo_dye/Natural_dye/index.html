Natural dye

Natural dyes are dyes or colorants derived from plants, invertebrates, or minerals. The majority of natural dyes are vegetable dyes from plant sources — roots, berries, bark, leaves, and wood — and other biological sources such as fungi and lichens. Until the mid-19th century, nearly all dyes were natural; the discovery of synthetic mauveine by William Henry Perkin in 1856 began a rapid transition to synthetic dyes in industrial production.

History

Dyed flax fibers have been found in a prehistoric cave in Georgia dated to 36,000 years ago. Throughout history, people have dyed textiles using locally available materials: madder for reds, weld and other plants for yellows, woad and indigo for blues. Some dyes were extraordinarily valuable, notably Tyrian purple, extracted from the mucus of murex sea snails by the Phoenicians, and cochineal and logwood from the Americas after the Columbian exchange.

Common natural dyes

  • Indigo — blue dye from plants of the genus Indigofera and from woad (Isatis tinctoria).
  • Madder (Rubia tinctorum) — red dye from roots, source of alizarin.
  • Tyrian purple — purple dye from murex snails, emblematic of Phoenician trade.
  • Cochineal — crimson dye from scale insects living on cacti in the Americas.
  • Turmeric — yellow dye from the rhizome of Curcuma longa.
  • Logwood — dark blue and black dyes from the heartwood of Haematoxylum campechianum.
  • Cutch — brown dye from acacia trees, used for khaki.

Mordants

Many natural dyes do not bind to fibers on their own and require a mordant — a metal salt or other substance, such as alum, iron, or chrome — that fixes the dye to the fabric and can shift the resulting color. Iron mordants typically "sadden" or darken colors, while alum brightens them.

Decline and revival

Synthetic dyes, cheaper and more consistent, displaced natural dyes in industrial use by the early 20th century. Natural dyeing persists in artisanal textiles, historical re-enactment, and a growing sustainability movement interested in lower-impact coloration.
