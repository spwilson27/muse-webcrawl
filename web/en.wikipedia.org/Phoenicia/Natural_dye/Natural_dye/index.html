Natural dye

Natural dyes are dyes or colorants derived from plants, invertebrates, or minerals. The majority are vegetable dyes from plant sources — roots, berries, bark, leaves, and wood — while others come from organisms such as lichens and shellfish. Most natural dyeing requires mordants, metallic salts that fix the dye to the fibre.

History

Archaeological evidence shows that dyeing with natural pigments dates back thousands of years across Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, India, and the Americas. Prized dyes such as Tyrian purple, extracted from murex sea snails by the Phoenicians, madder red, and indigo blue shaped ancient trade networks.

Decline and revival

The discovery of synthetic dyes, beginning with William Henry Perkin's mauveine in 1856, rapidly displaced natural dyes in industry because they were cheaper, brighter, and more consistent. Interest in natural dyes has revived among artisans and in sustainable fashion, though they remain a small share of the market.
