Hannibal

Hannibal Barca (247 – between 183 and 181 BC) was a Carthaginian general and statesman who commanded the forces of Carthage in their battle against the Roman Republic during the Second Punic War. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest military commanders in history.

Second Punic War

In 218 BC Hannibal marched an army — including war elephants — from Iberia over the Pyrenees and the Alps into northern Italy. He won a succession of dramatic victories at the Trebia, Lake Trasimene, and Cannae, where his double-envelopment tactic destroyed a much larger Roman army. Despite fifteen years of campaigning in Italy, he was unable to force Rome's surrender.

Defeat and later life

Recalled to Africa, Hannibal was defeated by Scipio Africanus at the Battle of Zama in 202 BC, ending the war. He later served as a political leader in Carthage and, in exile, advised Hellenistic kings against Rome. To avoid capture by the Romans, he took his own life in Bithynia.
