Earliest form: Proto-Sinaitic script
Key descendant: Phoenician alphabet

History of the alphabet

The history of the alphabet begins in the Middle Bronze Age, with the emergence of the Proto-Sinaitic script in Egypt and the Levant around the 19th century BC. This consonantal writing system, itself partly inspired by Egyptian hieroglyphs, used signs for single consonant sounds by the acrophonic principle — taking the picture of an object for its initial sound.

Phoenician and Greek developments

By about 1050 BC the Phoenician alphabet, a streamlined 22-letter consonantal script (abjad), had emerged and spread widely through Phoenician maritime trade. The Greeks adopted it around the 8th century BC, repurposing unused consonant signs as vowels and producing the first true alphabet. Through Etruscan intermediaries, Greek letterforms gave rise to the Latin alphabet.

World alphabets

Nearly all the world's alphabets descend from this single tradition or were created under its influence, including Cyrillic, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari (via Aramaic and Brahmi lineages), and the Latin alphabet, today the most widely used writing system on Earth.
