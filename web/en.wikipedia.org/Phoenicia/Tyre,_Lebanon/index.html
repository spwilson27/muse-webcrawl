Tyre (Arabic: صور, Ṣūr; Phoenician: 𐤑𐤓, Ṣōr) is a city in Lebanon, one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. A major Phoenician port, Tyre was built on an offshore island with two harbours and grew wealthy on maritime trade and the manufacture of the prized Tyrian purple dye extracted from murex snails.

Ancient history

Under King Hiram I (10th century BC) Tyre reached its golden age, supplying cedar and craftsmen for Solomon's Temple in Jerusalem. Tyrians founded colonies across the Mediterranean, including Carthage (traditionally 814 BC). The city withstood a 13-year siege by Nebuchadnezzar II, but in 332 BC Alexander the Great captured it after building a causeway from the mainland — the mole that permanently joined the island to the shore and created today's peninsula.

Later history

Tyre prospered under Hellenistic, Roman, and Byzantine rule; its Roman remains include a hippodrome, triumphal arch, and necropolis. It fell to Arab forces in 635, was held by Crusaders from 1124 to 1291, and declined in the later Middle Ages. The archaeological site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.

Modern Tyre

Modern Tyre (Sour) is a port and market town of southern Lebanon, with fishing, agriculture, and tourism, and it hosts the Tyre Coast Nature Reserve, a protected beach important for nesting sea turtles.
