Type: Alphabet
Time period: c. 800 BC – present
Parent system: Phoenician alphabet

Greek alphabet

The Greek alphabet has been used to write the Greek language since the late 9th or early 8th century BC. It is derived from the earlier Phoenician alphabet and was the earliest known alphabetic script to have distinct letters for vowels as well as consonants. In Archaic and early Classical times, the Greek alphabet existed in many local variants, but, by the end of the 4th century BC, the Euclidean alphabet with 24 letters, ordered from alpha to omega, had become standard.

Origin

The Greeks adapted the Phoenician consonantal script, reassigning several Phoenician letters for consonants not present in Greek to represent vowels — a decisive innovation that made the script a true alphabet. The letters' names and shapes retain clear traces of their Phoenician origins.

Descendants

The Greek alphabet is the ancestor of the Latin and Cyrillic scripts, among others. It is still used today to write Greek and, in modified forms, for mathematical and scientific notation throughout the world.
