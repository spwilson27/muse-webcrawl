Key of Solomon

Pentacles from a manuscript of the Key of Solomon.

The Key of Solomon (Latin: Clavicula Salomonis) is a pseudepigraphical grimoire attributed to King Solomon. It is one of the most famous and influential texts of Western ceremonial magic, containing instructions for conjuring spirits and detailed designs for pentacles, seals, and magical instruments.

Dating and manuscripts

Despite its attribution to the biblical Solomon, the surviving manuscripts date from the medieval and Renaissance periods, mostly the 14th to 18th centuries, in Greek, Latin, Italian, French, and other languages. The text likely derives from earlier Byzantine and Arabic magical traditions, including the Hygromanteia. S. L. MacGregor Mathers published an influential English translation in 1889 based on British Museum manuscripts.

Contents

The Key is usually divided into two books. The first covers conjurations, invocations, and curses for summoning and controlling spirits, together with preparations of the magician, the circle, knives, and other tools. The second book is largely devoted to the pentacles — talismanic diagrams attributed to the planets — with their construction and uses, such as finding treasure, gaining invisibility, or obtaining love.

Influence

The Key of Solomon shaped later grimoires including the Lesser Key of Solomon (the Lemegeton), and remains central to modern occult practice and popular depictions of ritual magic.
