Simone Boccanegra

Simone Boccanegra (died 1363) was the first Doge of Genoa. A leader of the popular (non-noble) faction, he was acclaimed doge on 23 September 1339 by popular assembly, breaking the dominance of the great noble families — the Doria, Spinola, Grimaldi and Fieschi — who had contested power in the republic.

Doge of Genoa

Boccanegra's government sought to exclude the old nobility from power, extend Genoese control over the Ligurian riviera, and pursue the republic's rivalry with Venice. Under pressure from the nobles and a revolt led by his enemies, he resigned in 1344 and retired to Pisa. In 1356 he returned to power, again raised to the dogeship by popular support, and ruled until his death in 1363 — traditionally said to have been by poisoning at a banquet, though this is uncertain.

In culture

Boccanegra is best known today as the hero of Giuseppe Verdi's opera Simon Boccanegra (1857, revised 1881), based on a play by Antonio García Gutiérrez; the opera reimagines the doge as a plebeian statesman pleading for Italian unity and reconciliation between factions. A minor historical figure in his own right, he survives chiefly through Verdi's portrait.
