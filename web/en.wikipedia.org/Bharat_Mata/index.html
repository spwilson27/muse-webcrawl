Bharat Mata

Bharat Mata (Hindi: भारत माता, "Mother India") is a national personification of India as a goddess, typically depicted as a woman in a saffron sari holding the Indian national flag, sometimes accompanied by a lion. The concept emerged during the Indian independence movement in the late 19th century.

Origins

The idea was popularised by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's 1882 novel Anandamath, which contains the hymn "Vande Mataram" ("I bow to thee, Mother"). In 1905 Abanindranath Tagore painted an influential image of Bharat Mata as a serene, four-armed ascetic figure. The first Bharat Mata temple was inaugurated by Mahatma Gandhi at Banaras Hindu University in 1936, featuring a large relief map of undivided India instead of a conventional idol; another temple stands in Haridwar.

Significance

Bharat Mata became an icon of Indian nationalism, and the slogan "Bharat Mata ki Jai" ("Victory to Mother India") remains a popular patriotic chant. Devotion to Bharat Mata is especially associated with Hindu nationalist movements, and its religious framing has been the subject of debate among India's religious minorities.
