The Ramsar Convention, formally the Convention on Wetlands of International Importance Especially as Waterfowl Habitat, is an international treaty for the conservation and sustainable use of wetlands. Signed on 2 February 1971 in the Iranian city of Ramsar on the Caspian Sea coast, it is the oldest of the modern global environmental agreements and the only one devoted to a single ecosystem type.

Obligations and the Ramsar List

Each contracting party must designate at least one wetland for the List of Wetlands of International Importance (the Ramsar List), which has grown to more than 2,500 sites covering over 250 million hectares — among them the Sundarbans, the Everglades, Kakadu and the Wadden Sea. Parties commit to the "wise use" of all wetlands in their territory, to formulate planning that favours conservation, and to report changes in the ecological character of listed sites; sites under threat may be placed on the Montreux Record for priority attention.

Operation

The convention's secretariat is based in Gland, Switzerland, hosted by the IUCN. A Conference of the Parties meets every three years, and 2 February is celebrated annually as World Wetlands Day. Amendments in 1982 (Paris Protocol) and 1987 (Regina Amendments) gave the convention a permanent conference and budget. Ramsar works closely with partner organisations including BirdLife International, Wetlands International, WWF and the IUCN, and has increasingly emphasised wetlands' roles in climate mitigation, water security and disaster-risk reduction.
