Gulf of Corryvreckan

LocationBetween Jura and Scarba, Argyll, Scotland
TypeTidal strait with whirlpool
Tidal speedUp to ~8.5 knots (16 km/h)
Known forOne of the largest permanent whirlpools in the world

The Gulf of Corryvreckan (from the Scottish Gaelic Coire Bhreacain, "cauldron of the speckled seas") is a narrow strait between the islands of Jura and Scarba in Argyll and Bute, off the west coast of mainland Scotland. Strong tidal currents flowing over an uneven seabed — including a pinnacle of rock rising close to the surface — generate the famous Corryvreckan whirlpool, often described as the third largest whirlpool in the world.

Formation

As the flood tide enters the strait from the south, water speed increases over a deep trench and then collides with a basalt pinnacle, producing standing waves, eddies and a large rotating whirlpool. Under spring tides and westerly winds, waves in the gulf can reach several metres high, and the roar of the whirlpool has been reported from many miles away.

History and legend

In Norse legend the whirlpool was associated with the hag goddess Cailleach Bhéara, who was said to wash her plaid in the gulf. The strait is also linked to a legendary voyage of the Norwegian prince Breacan. In 1947 the writer George Orwell, then living on Jura, was nearly drowned when a boat carrying him and his children capsized in the gulf; they were rescued after clinging to a reef.

Navigation and wildlife

The Royal Navy once classified the whirlpool as unnavigable. The gulf is now crossed by experienced local boat operators running wildlife and whirlpool tours, timed carefully to tidal conditions. The area supports seals, porpoises, dolphins and sea eagles.
