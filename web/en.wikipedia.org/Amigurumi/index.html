Amigurumi

Amigurumi
OriginJapan
MaterialsYarn, crochet hook or knitting needles, stuffing
Name fromJapanese ami ("crocheted/knitted") + nuigurumi ("stuffed doll")

Amigurumi (Japanese: 編みぐるみ) is the Japanese art of knitting or crocheting small, stuffed yarn creatures. The word is a compound of ami, meaning "crocheted or knitted", and nuigurumi, meaning "stuffed doll". Amigurumi figures vary in size and subject — animals, characters, anthropomorphic objects with faces — and are usually worked in the round with single crochet stitches, giving a dense, seamless fabric.

History

Although dolls had been crocheted in Japan earlier, amigurumi as a named craft emerged in the 1980s alongside the rise of kawaii culture; the Japan Amigurumi Association was founded in 2002. The craft spread internationally in the 2000s through craft books translated from Japanese, online pattern sales, and craft marketplaces such as Etsy, becoming one of the most recognisable forms of contemporary crochet.

Technique

Amigurumi are usually crocheted using a hook smaller than recommended for the yarn, producing tight stitches that keep the polyester or cotton stuffing from showing through. Parts — head, body, limbs, ears — are worked separately in spiral rounds, typically beginning with a "magic ring", shaped by increases and decreases, then stuffed and sewn together. Details such as eyes are added with safety eyes, felt, or embroidery. Designs traditionally favour oversized heads and large eyes in the chibi style.

Popularity

The craft grew with do-it-yourself and maker culture, and amigurumi versions of popular characters are widely sold and shared online. Guinness World Records have been awarded for very large and very small amigurumi figures, and the art is practised both as a hobby and as a small-business craft.
