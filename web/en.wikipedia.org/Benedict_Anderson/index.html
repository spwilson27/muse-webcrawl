Benedict Anderson

Born26 August 1936, Kunming, China
Died13 December 2015, Batu, Indonesia
InstitutionCornell University
Notable workImagined Communities (1983)

Benedict Richard O'Gorman Anderson (1936–2015) was an Anglo-Irish political scientist and historian, best known for his book Imagined Communities: Reflections on the Origin and Spread of Nationalism (1983), one of the most influential works on nationalism in modern scholarship. He spent most of his career at Cornell University, where he helped build its Southeast Asia Program.

Imagined communities

Anderson defined the nation as "an imagined political community — and imagined as both inherently limited and sovereign": imagined because members who will never meet nonetheless hold a shared image of communion. He argued that the convergence of capitalism and print technology — "print capitalism" — created unified vernacular reading publics, enabling this imagination, first in the Americas and then in Europe and the colonised world.

Other work

A leading scholar of Indonesia, Anderson co-authored the confidential "Cornell Paper" analysing the 1965 events in Indonesia and was barred from the country under Suharto until 1998. His later books include The Spectre of Comparisons (1998) and Under Three Flags (2005). His memoir, A Life Beyond Boundaries, appeared posthumously in 2016. See also Nationalism.
