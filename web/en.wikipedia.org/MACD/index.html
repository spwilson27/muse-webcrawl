MACD

Moving average convergence divergence (MACD) is a trend-following momentum indicator used in technical analysis of securities prices. Created by Gerald Appel in the late 1970s, it shows the relationship between two exponential moving averages (EMAs) of a security's price.

Components

The MACD line is the difference between the 12-period EMA and the 26-period EMA. A nine-period EMA of the MACD line itself, called the signal line, is plotted on top. The difference between the two lines is often displayed as a histogram. Formally: MACD = EMA(12) − EMA(26); Signal = EMA(9) of the MACD.

Interpretation

Traders commonly watch for signal-line crossovers — the MACD crossing above the signal line is read as bullish, below as bearish; zero-line crossovers, indicating a shift in trend direction of the moving averages; and divergence, where the price makes a new extreme not confirmed by the MACD, which may foreshadow a reversal. Because MACD is unbounded, it is not typically used to identify overbought or oversold levels as oscillators like the RSI are. As a lagging indicator based on past prices, it can generate false signals in sideways markets.
