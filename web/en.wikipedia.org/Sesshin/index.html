Sesshin

Practitioners seated in zazen during a sesshin.

Sesshin (接心), literally "touching the heart-mind", is a period of intensive meditation retreat in Zen Buddhism. A sesshin typically lasts from one to seven days, and in some monasteries longer, during which participants follow a rigorous daily schedule centred on many hours of seated meditation (zazen).

Practice

A typical sesshin day begins before dawn and alternates periods of zazen with walking meditation (kinhin), work practice (samu), formal meals eaten with ōryōki utensils, chanting, and rest. Silence is maintained throughout. Participants meet privately with the teacher in interviews (dokusan or sanzen), where koan students present their understanding and other practitioners discuss their practice. Talks by the teacher (teishō) are given on most days. Sleep is limited, and during the most demanding retreats, such as Rohatsu sesshin commemorating the Buddha's enlightenment, the schedule intensifies as the week proceeds.

Role in Zen training

Sesshin is regarded as the heart of Zen monastic training and is also widely practised by laypeople. The sustained schedule, communal discipline and repeated interviews are intended to concentrate the mind and deepen insight beyond what daily practice sessions allow. In the Rinzai tradition sesshin is closely tied to koan study; in Sōtō Zen it emphasises shikantaza, "just sitting". Since Zen spread to the West, sesshin of varying lengths have become a standard feature of practice centres in Europe and North America, sometimes adapted in length and strictness for lay participants.
