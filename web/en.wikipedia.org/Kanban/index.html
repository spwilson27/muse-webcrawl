Kanban

Kanban (Japanese: カンバン, meaning "signboard" or "billboard") is a lean method to manage and improve work across human systems. This approach aims to manage work by balancing demands with available capacity, and by improving the handling of system-level bottlenecks. In the Toyota Production System, kanban is the scheduling system that signals what to produce, when to produce it, and how much to produce, enabling just-in-time production.

Origins in manufacturing

In the late 1940s Taiichi Ohno and colleagues at Toyota, inspired by the way supermarkets restock shelves, developed kanban cards as physical signals between work stations. When a bin of parts is emptied, a card is returned to the supplying process, authorising replenishment of a standard quantity. The number of cards in circulation limits work in progress and inventory.

Kanban for knowledge work

In the 2000s, David J. Anderson and others adapted kanban for software development and knowledge work. The Kanban Method as practised today centres on visualising work on a kanban board (columns for workflow stages, cards for work items), limiting work in progress, managing flow, making process policies explicit, implementing feedback loops, and improving collaboratively.

Relation to other methods

Kanban is often combined with Scrum ("Scrumban") or used as an alternative flow-based approach to agile delivery. Unlike time-boxed methods, kanban operates continuously, with work pulled into the system as capacity becomes available.
