Ganga aarti

Ganga aarti is a Hindu ritual of worship in which light, typically from large fire lamps, is offered to the goddess Ganga, the personification of the river Ganges. The ceremony is performed every evening at dusk on the ghats of several cities along the river, most famously Varanasi, Rishikesh and Haridwar, and draws large crowds of devotees and visitors.

The ceremony

A group of priests, usually dressed in matching saffron or crimson garments, stand on a raised platform at the river's edge. In synchronised movements they raise large, tiered fire lamps in circular patterns while conch shells are blown, bells rung, and devotional songs (bhajans) and the Ganga aarti hymn are sung. Incense, flowers and other offerings accompany the fire offering. At the conclusion, devotees often float small lamps set on leaf boats (diyas) onto the river.

Principal locations

Varanasi

At Dashashwamedh Ghat in Varanasi, the aarti is performed by a group of young priests in a highly choreographed ceremony that has become one of the city's signature spectacles. A smaller ceremony takes place at Assi Ghat, particularly at dawn (Subah-e-Banaras).

Haridwar

At Har Ki Pauri in Haridwar, the evening aarti is among the largest and oldest, with thousands of pilgrims gathering on both banks of the river channel.

Rishikesh

At Triveni Ghat and at Parmarth Niketan in Rishikesh, the ceremony is comparatively intimate and is often accompanied by the chanting of the Hanuman Chalisa and other hymns.

Significance

The ritual expresses gratitude to the river goddess for sustenance and purification. Hindus believe that the waters of the Ganges wash away sins, and the aarti is both an offering of thanks and a prayer for the river's continued blessing.
