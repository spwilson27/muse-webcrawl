Katsuobushi
TypeSeasoning / stock ingredient
Place of originJapan
Main ingredientsSkipjack tuna, fermented and dried

Katsuobushi (鰹節) is simmered, smoked and fermented skipjack tuna (Katsuwonus pelamis), a cornerstone ingredient of Japanese cuisine. The dried fillets, which become rock-hard, are shaved into flakes (kezuribushi) and used as a topping and, together with kombu, to make dashi stock. Katsuobushi is exceptionally rich in inosinic acid, a principal source of umami flavour.

Production

Skipjack tuna is filleted, simmered, deboned and repeatedly smoked and dried; in the traditional honkarebushi process the blocks are then inoculated with the mould Aspergillus glaucus and sun-dried over weeks. The finished blocks resemble wood and are shaved on a planing box called a katsuobushi kezuriki.

Uses

Shavings top dishes such as okonomiyaki, takoyaki and cold tofu, and form the base of dashi used in miso soup, noodle broths and simmered dishes.
