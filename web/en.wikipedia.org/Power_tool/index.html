A power tool is a tool that is actuated by an additional power source and mechanism other than the solely manual labour used with hand tools. The most common types of power tools use electric motors; others are driven by internal combustion engines, steam, or compressed air (pneumatic tools). Power tools are used in industry, construction, the garden, and around the house for tasks such as drilling, cutting, shaping, sanding, grinding, routing, polishing, painting and heating.

Classification

Power tools may be stationary (such as table saws, drill presses and bench grinders, prized for precision and power) or portable (such as drills, circular saws, jigsaws, reciprocating saws and angle grinders). Portable electric tools are divided into corded models, drawing mains power, and cordless models powered by rechargeable battery packs — increasingly standardized lithium-ion platforms shared across a manufacturer's range, with brushless motors improving efficiency.

History

Early power tools were driven by belts from line shafts powered by water wheels or steam engines. The electric drill was patented in Australia in 1889; the German firm Fein produced a hand-held electric drill in 1895, and Black & Decker's 1917 pistol-grip, trigger-switch drill set the pattern for portable tools. After the Second World War, power tools became mass consumer products, and brands such as Makita, Bosch, DeWalt, Milwaukee and Hilti came to dominate professional markets.

Safety

Power tools pose risks of cuts, amputation, eye injury, electric shock, hearing damage and vibration injury (such as hand-arm vibration syndrome). Safe use involves guards, eye and ear protection, secure workpieces, correct accessories, and disconnecting power before adjustments. Many tools incorporate safety features including dead-man switches, blade brakes and double insulation.
