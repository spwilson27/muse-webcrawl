Tesseract (software)

Tesseract is a free and open-source optical character recognition (OCR) engine released under the Apache License 2.0. It converts images of typed, handwritten, or printed text into machine-encoded text and supports more than 100 languages.

History

Tesseract was developed at Hewlett-Packard laboratories between 1984 and 1994 and was among the top-performing engines in the 1995 UNLV OCR accuracy test. HP released it as open source in 2005; Google subsequently sponsored its development, and Ray Smith led much of its modernization.

Technology

Version 4, released in 2018, added a new OCR engine based on long short-term memory (LSTM) neural networks, substantially improving accuracy on difficult text. Tesseract is primarily a command-line tool and library; many graphical front ends and language bindings wrap it.
