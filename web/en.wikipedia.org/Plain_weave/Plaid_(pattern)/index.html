Plaid (pattern)

A plaid pattern is made of vertical and horizontal stripes of colour that cross one another, forming squares and rectangles of blended shades where they overlap. The pattern is strongly tied to Scottish tartan, from which most plaids derive; in Scotland the word plaid instead names the long tartan garment traditionally worn over the shoulder.

Terminology

Confusion between "plaid" and "tartan" is common outside Scotland. In North America, plaid is the everyday word for any multi-coloured checked cloth, from rustic flannel shirting to dress fabrics. Tartans are plaids with a specific registered sett — the thread count sequence that defines the pattern — often associated with a clan, regiment, or organisation.

Production

Because the colours are in the yarn before weaving, the pattern appears on both faces of the cloth. A simple 2/2 twill weave is typical, giving the fabric slight diagonal texture and good drape.

In fashion

Plaid cycles in and out of fashion: it has signified Highland romanticism, North American frontier practicality, 1970s punk (often in torn, safety-pinned form), and 1990s grunge flannel. Designers continue to reinterpret classic setts each decade.
