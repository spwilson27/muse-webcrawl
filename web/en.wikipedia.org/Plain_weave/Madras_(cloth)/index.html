Madras (cloth)

Madras is a lightweight cotton fabric with a plaid or checked design, most often in bright, irregular colours. It takes its name from Madras (now Chennai) in southern India, where handwoven checked cottons were produced for centuries and exported by the East India companies. The cloth is woven in a plain weave from yarn-dyed warp and weft threads, which gives the pattern to both faces of the fabric.

Characteristics

Traditional Madras uses short-staple cotton and vegetable dyes, so colours bleed slightly when washed, softening the pattern — a quality once prized as a sign of authenticity. "Patchwork madras" is made by sewing together squares of different madras patterns. Slubs and irregularities in the hand-loomed yarn are typical of the genuine article.

Uses and cultural history

Madras became a staple of warm-weather clothing: shirts, shorts, blazers, and dresses. It was absorbed into the national dress of several Caribbean islands, including Saint Lucia and Dominica, where madras skirts form part of the traditional wòb dwiyèt costume, and it became emblematic of mid-twentieth-century American Ivy League and preppy style.
