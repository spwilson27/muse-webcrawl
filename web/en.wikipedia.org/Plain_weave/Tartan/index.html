Tartan

Tartan is a cloth pattern of criss-crossed horizontal and vertical bands in multiple colours, traditionally woven in wool, and strongly associated with Scotland and with the Scottish clan system. The pattern, called the sett, is produced by weaving warp and weft stripes in the same sequence in a two-over/two-under twill; where stripes cross, colours blend in half-tone, creating new shades and a diagonal texture.

History

Checked woollen cloth is ancient in northern Europe — the Falkirk tartan fragment dates to roughly the third century AD. By the sixteenth century distinct regional patterns appeared in the Scottish Highlands, where tartan garments, especially the belted plaid, became characteristic dress. After the Jacobite rising of 1745, the Dress Act of 1746 banned Highland dress for most civilians; repealed in 1782, tartan returned as a romantic symbol of Scotland, popularized by the visit of George IV to Edinburgh in 1822 and by Queen Victoria's enthusiasm for Balmoral.

Clan tartans

The assignment of particular setts to particular clans was largely systematized in the nineteenth century, although some patterns genuinely predate the fashion. Today thousands of tartans — clan, district, regimental, corporate, and fashion — are recorded by the Scottish Tartans Authority and the official Scottish Register of Tartans, established by an Act of the Scottish Parliament in 2008.
