Check (pattern)

A check (also checker, or checked pattern) is a pattern of modified stripes consisting of crossed horizontal and vertical lines that form squares. Checks are among the oldest woven textile patterns and arise naturally when stripes of colour in the warp are crossed by the same stripes in the weft, most often in a plain weave or a twill.

Types of check

Well-known check patterns include gingham, with evenly spaced white and coloured squares; tartan (plaid), whose asymmetrical stripe sequence is mirrored to form a sett; houndstooth, a broken twill with abstract four-pointed shapes; windowpane, thin widely spaced lines on a plain ground; graph check or tattersall, with thin regular lines; shepherd's check, a small even twill check; and Madras check, an irregular lightweight cotton pattern from India. The racing "chequered flag" uses a simple two-colour checkerboard.

Uses

Checked fabrics appear in shirts, school uniforms, table linens, upholstery, and national and clan dress, notably Scottish tartans registered for clans and districts. In design theory the check is treated as the intersection of two stripe systems, and irregular checks are used to soften the formality of the grid.
