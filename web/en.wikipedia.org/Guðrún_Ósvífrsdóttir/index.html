Guðrún Ósvífrsdóttir

Guðrún Ósvífrsdóttir (c. 974 – 1060) is the principal female character of the Icelandic Laxdæla saga and one of the most famous figures of the Icelandic sagas. Renowned for her beauty and pride, she was married four times, and the saga traces the tragic love triangle between Guðrún, Kjartan Óláfsson and Bolli Þorleiksson.

Life in the saga

Guðrún was the daughter of Ósvífr, a wealthy chieftain of Laugar in western Iceland. Her dreams as a young woman, interpreted by Gestur Oddleifsson, foretell her four marriages. She loved Kjartan Óláfsson, who travelled to Norway and was detained by King Óláfur Tryggvason; in his absence Guðrún was persuaded to marry his foster-brother Bolli. The resulting enmity ended with Bolli killing Kjartan and Bolli himself being killed in revenge. Guðrún's later marriages were to Þórður Ingunnarson, who drowned, and the chieftain Þorkell Eyjólfsson.

Old age and legacy

In old age Guðrún became a nun and anchoress at Helgafell, traditionally regarded as the first nun in Iceland, and was buried there. When her son Bolli asked which of her husbands she had loved most, she answered with the famous line: "To him I was worst whom I loved most" (Þeim var ek verst er ek unna mest). Guðrún is also mentioned in several other sagas and is a central figure in the literary tradition of medieval Iceland.
