Gallia Belgica ("Belgic Gaul") was a province of the Roman Empire located in the north-eastern part of Roman Gaul, in what is today primarily northern France, Belgium, and Luxembourg, together with parts of the Netherlands and Germany. It was one of the three parts of Gaul described by Julius Caesar in his Commentarii de Bello Gallico, inhabited by the Belgae, whom Caesar called the bravest of the Gauls.

History

Caesar conquered the Belgae between 57 and 53 BC, facing fierce resistance, notably from the Nervii and from Ambiorix of the Eburones, who destroyed a Roman legion in 54 BC. Augustus formally organized Belgica as an imperial province in 22 BC. Its first capital was Durocortorum (modern Reims); in late antiquity Augusta Treverorum (Trier) grew in importance and became an imperial residence.

Reorganization

Under Domitian, around AD 90, the military districts along the Rhine were detached as Germania Inferior and Germania Superior. Diocletian later divided the remainder into Belgica Prima and Belgica Secunda. In the 5th century the province was overrun during the Germanic invasions and absorbed into the Frankish kingdoms; the name Belgium ultimately derives from it.
