Consumption (economics)

Consumption is the use of goods and services for the satisfaction of individual or collective needs and desires. In economics, consumption is the largest component of aggregate demand and of gross domestic product in most countries, comprising household spending on durable goods, nondurable goods, and services.

Theories

Keynes proposed the consumption function, in which consumption depends mainly on current disposable income, with a marginal propensity to consume between zero and one. Later theories include Milton Friedman's permanent income hypothesis, which holds that people spend a fixed fraction of their expected long-run income, and Franco Modigliani's life-cycle hypothesis, which models saving and dissaving across a lifetime. The behavioral life-cycle and buffer-stock models modify these with psychological and precautionary motives.

Measurement

National accounts measure personal consumption expenditures (PCE), used by central banks such as the Federal Reserve as an inflation gauge. Consumption patterns underpin debates about sustainability, consumerism, and welfare.
