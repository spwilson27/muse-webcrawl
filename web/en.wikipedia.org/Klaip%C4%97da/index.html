Klaipėda (historically known in German as Memel) is a city in Lithuania on the Baltic Sea coast, at the mouth of the Akmena-Danė River where it flows into the Curonian Lagoon. It is Lithuania's third-largest city and its only major seaport, largely ice-free year-round.

History

The castle of Memelburg was founded in 1252 by the Teutonic Order, and the city belonged for centuries to Prussia and later the German Empire, becoming its northernmost city. After the First World War the Memel Territory was detached from Germany and administered by the League of Nations; Lithuanian forces seized it in the Klaipėda Revolt of 1923, and it joined Lithuania as an autonomous region. Germany retook the city in 1939, and the Red Army captured it in January 1945, after which its German population fled or was expelled and the city became part of the Lithuanian SSR.

Economy and culture

The Port of Klaipėda is one of the busiest in the eastern Baltic, handling cargo, ferries to Germany and Sweden, and an LNG terminal opened in 2014 aboard the vessel Independence. The old town preserves German half-timbered architecture, and the city hosts the annual Sea Festival (Jūros šventė) and the Lithuanian Sea Museum at the Smiltynė fort.
