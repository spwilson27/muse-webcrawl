The Popular Democratic Party (Spanish: Partido Popular Democrático, PPD) is a political party in Puerto Rico that advocates for Puerto Rico to remain a Commonwealth of the United States with self-government. The party was founded in 1938 by Luis Muñoz Marín and others, emerging from a split in the Liberal Party. Its emblem is the pava, the straw hat traditionally worn by Puerto Rican farm workers known as jíbaros, and its members are commonly called populares.

History

The PPD won its first major victory in the 1940 elections, campaigning on the slogan Pan, Tierra, Libertad ("Bread, Land, Liberty") and promising social and economic reform rather than a resolution of the island's political status. Under Muñoz Marín, the party led the industrialization program known as Operation Bootstrap, and Muñoz Marín became the first democratically elected governor of Puerto Rico in 1948.

In 1952 the party oversaw the establishment of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico (Estado Libre Asociado) under a locally drafted constitution approved by the U.S. Congress. The PPD dominated Puerto Rican politics for decades; its governors have included Roberto Sánchez Vilella, Rafael Hernández Colón, Sila María Calderón (the island's first female governor), Aníbal Acevedo Vilá, and Alejandro García Padilla.

Ideology and status position

The party supports the Commonwealth status as a non-colonial, non-territorial autonomous relationship with the United States, and many of its members favor developing an "enhanced" commonwealth with greater sovereignty. It historically maintains an affiliation with the U.S. Democratic Party, although its members hold varying national-party allegiances. Its principal rivals are the New Progressive Party, which favors U.S. statehood, and the Puerto Rican Independence Party.
