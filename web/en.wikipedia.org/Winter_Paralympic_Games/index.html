Winter Paralympic Games

The Winter Paralympic Games is an international multi-sport event for athletes with disabilities, held every four years in the same city and year as the Winter Olympic Games. Governed by the International Paralympic Committee (IPC), the Games were first held in Örnsköldsvik, Sweden, in 1976.

History

Winter sports for disabled athletes developed after the Second World War, when injured soldiers and civilians began skiing with adapted equipment in Austria and elsewhere. The first Winter Paralympics in 1976 featured alpine and cross-country skiing for amputees and visually impaired athletes. Demonstration events for ice sledge speed skating followed, and the programme expanded over successive Games. Since 1992 the Winter Paralympics have been held in the same host city as the Winter Olympics, and since 2001 host cities have been formally required to stage both events.

Sports

The programme has come to include para alpine skiing, para biathlon, para cross-country skiing, para ice hockey (formerly ice sledge hockey), para snowboard, and wheelchair curling. Athletes are grouped into sport classes according to the type and degree of their impairment, with guides assisting visually impaired competitors and sit-skis and outriggers used in skiing events.
