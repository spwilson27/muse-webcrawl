Cimetidine

Trade nameTagamet, others
ClassH2 receptor antagonist
FormulaC10H16N6S

Cimetidine, sold under the brand name Tagamet among others, is a histamine H2 receptor antagonist that inhibits stomach acid production. It is used to treat heartburn and peptic ulcers. Developed by Smith, Kline & French (later GlaxoSmithKline) in the 1970s, it was the first of the H2 blockers and a landmark in rational drug design; its lead developer, James Black, later shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Medical uses

Cimetidine is used for duodenal and gastric ulcers, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and conditions of acid hypersecretion such as Zollinger–Ellison syndrome. It has largely been displaced by later H2 antagonists such as ranitidine and famotidine, and by proton pump inhibitors, which have fewer drug interactions.

Pharmacology

The drug competitively blocks histamine H2 receptors on gastric parietal cells, reducing both basal and stimulated acid secretion. Cimetidine is also a notable inhibitor of several cytochrome P450 enzymes, which accounts for many of its interactions with other drugs.
