Shingles

Also called: Herpes zoster
Cause: Varicella zoster virus
Symptoms: Painful blistering rash in one dermatome
Prevention: Zoster vaccine

Shingles, also known as herpes zoster, is a viral disease characterised by a painful skin rash with blisters in a limited area on one side of the body. It is caused by reactivation of the varicella zoster virus (VZV), the same virus that causes chickenpox. After chickenpox, the virus lies dormant in nerve ganglia and may reactivate years later, especially when immunity declines with age or illness.

Signs and symptoms

The illness often begins with pain, tingling or burning along the affected nerve, followed within days by a red rash that develops into clusters of fluid-filled blisters. The rash typically follows a single dermatome and does not cross the midline. It crusts over and heals within two to four weeks.

Complications

The most common complication is postherpetic neuralgia, persistent nerve pain lasting months or longer after the rash heals. Shingles affecting the eye (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) can threaten vision, and people with weakened immune systems may develop widespread disease.

Prevention and treatment

Vaccination with the recombinant zoster vaccine greatly reduces the risk of shingles and postherpetic neuralgia and is recommended for older adults. Antiviral medicines such as aciclovir are most effective when started within 72 hours of the rash appearing.
