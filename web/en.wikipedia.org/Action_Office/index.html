Action Office

Action Office
Manufacturer: Herman Miller
Designers: Robert Propst, George Nelson
Introduced: 1964 (Action Office I); 1968 (Action Office II)

The Action Office is a series of furniture designed by Robert Propst and George Nelson for Herman Miller, first introduced in 1964. It is credited as the origin of the modern office cubicle.

Design

Propst, head of Herman Miller Research, sought to increase productivity by giving office workers flexible, semi-enclosed workspaces with surfaces at varied heights, movable components, and a degree of privacy while encouraging movement between sitting and standing.

Evolution

Action Office I was expensive and difficult to assemble. Action Office II (1968) introduced modular panels that could be configured at angles, which proved commercially successful. Over time, cost-driven densification transformed the flexible system into the rigid, high-density cubicle farms that Propst himself later criticized.

Legacy

The Action Office became the best-selling office furniture system in history and reshaped workplace design worldwide.
