Collective intelligence

Collective intelligence (CI) is shared or group intelligence that emerges from the collaboration, collective efforts and competition of many individuals, appearing in consensus decision making.

Examples and research

Examples range from bacterial quorum sensing and animal swarms to human phenomena such as Wikipedia, prediction markets and crowdsourcing. Research at MIT's Center for Collective Intelligence, founded by Thomas W. Malone, found a statistical "collective intelligence factor" for groups, correlated more with social sensitivity and equal conversational turn-taking than with members' individual IQ.

Related ideas

The concept relates to the wisdom of crowds, swarm intelligence, and Pierre Lévy's work on cyberspace as a medium for collective intelligence.
