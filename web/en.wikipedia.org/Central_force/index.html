Central force

In classical mechanics, a central force on an object is a force directed along the line joining the object and a fixed origin, whose magnitude depends only on the distance between them. Gravity and the electrostatic force between point charges are classic examples.

Properties

Because a central force exerts no torque about the origin, the angular momentum of the particle is conserved, and its motion is confined to a plane. Central-force problems can be reduced to one-dimensional motion in an effective potential that combines the true potential with a centrifugal barrier term.

Orbits

For an inverse-square force such as Newtonian gravity, bound orbits are ellipses, as described by Kepler's laws. Bertrand's theorem states that the inverse-square force and Hooke's law force are the only central forces for which all bound orbits are closed.
