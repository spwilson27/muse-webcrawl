Nazi Germany, officially the German Reich and from 1943 the Greater German Reich, is the common English name for the German state between 1933 and 1945, when Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party (NSDAP) controlled the country, transforming it into a totalitarian dictatorship. The regime pursued an ideology of racial supremacy, extreme nationalism and territorial expansion, launched the Second World War in Europe in 1939, and perpetrated the Holocaust — the genocide of six million Jews — alongside the mass murder of Roma, Poles, Soviet prisoners of war, people with disabilities, and other groups.

Rise to power

Amid the Great Depression, mass unemployment and political paralysis, the Nazis became the largest party in the Reichstag. Hitler was appointed Chancellor on 30 January 1933; following the Reichstag fire, civil liberties were suspended, and the Enabling Act of March 1933 allowed rule by decree. Rival parties, trade unions and independent institutions were dissolved or coordinated (Gleichschaltung), and upon President Hindenburg's death in August 1934 Hitler combined the offices of president and chancellor as Führer.

Domestic policy

The regime combined rapid rearmament and public works with terror against opponents, enforced through the Gestapo and a growing network of concentration camps. Racist legislation, notably the Nuremberg Laws of 1935, stripped Jews of citizenship; the November 1938 pogrom (Kristallnacht) signalled intensifying persecution. Propaganda under Joseph Goebbels saturated education, media and culture, and organisations such as the Hitler Youth mobilised the young.

Expansion and war

After annexing Austria (1938) and the Sudetenland and then the rest of Czechoslovakia (1938–39), Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, beginning the Second World War. Early victories brought much of Europe under occupation, accompanied by systematic exploitation and, from 1941, the "Final Solution": extermination camps and mass shootings murdered millions. Defeated at Stalingrad (1943) and overwhelmed on multiple fronts, Germany surrendered unconditionally on 8 May 1945; Hitler had died by suicide in Berlin on 30 April. The country was divided into Allied occupation zones, and leading Nazis were tried at Nuremberg.
