Ethereum is a decentralized blockchain platform that was proposed in late 2013 by programmer Vitalik Buterin and launched in 2015. It establishes a peer-to-peer network that securely executes and verifies application code, called smart contracts, allowing participants to transact with each other without a trusted central authority. Transaction records are stored in an immutable, publicly verifiable ledger, and the network's native cryptocurrency is called ether (ETH). Anyone may use Ethereum to create decentralized applications, tokens, and permanent, uncensorable programs, so long as they pay the associated transaction fee.

Ethereum was described in a white paper by Buterin in November 2013 and co-founded with Gavin Wood, Charles Hoskinson, Anthony Di Iorio and Joseph Lubin, among others. Development was funded by an online crowdsale in mid-2014 that raised over 31,000 bitcoin. The network went live on 30 July 2015 with 72 million ether pre-mined for contributors.

History

In 2016, a major application built on Ethereum, The DAO, raised about US$150 million in a crowdsale but was exploited through a vulnerability that drained roughly a third of its funds. The Ethereum community controversially voted to hard-fork the blockchain to restore the funds, splitting the network into Ethereum (the forked chain) and Ethereum Classic (the original chain).

On 15 September 2022, Ethereum completed the Merge, transitioning its consensus mechanism from proof of work to proof of stake. The change reduced the network's energy consumption by an estimated 99.95% and cut new ether issuance substantially.

Design

Ether

Ether is the native cryptocurrency of the platform. It is used to pay transaction fees (known as "gas") and, since the Merge, can be staked to secure the network. Ether is traded on cryptocurrency exchanges under the ticker ETH and is typically the second-largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization after bitcoin.

Smart contracts and the EVM

Smart contracts are programs stored on the blockchain that execute exactly as programmed. They are commonly written in the Solidity programming language (or Vyper) and compiled to bytecode executed by the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM). Decentralized applications (dApps) built on Ethereum include decentralized exchanges, lending protocols, stablecoins such as DAI and USDC, non-fungible token (NFT) marketplaces, and games.

Standards

Community standards called ERCs (Ethereum Request for Comments) define common interfaces, including ERC-20 for fungible tokens and ERC-721 for non-fungible tokens.

Governance and development

Ethereum development is coordinated through the Ethereum Improvement Proposal (EIP) process, with research and client development supported by the Ethereum Foundation and independent teams. Upgrades such as the Shanghai/Capella upgrade (2023), which enabled staked-ether withdrawals, are deployed as coordinated hard forks.
