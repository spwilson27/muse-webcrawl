Datum reference

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Datum reference
FieldEngineering drawing; geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T)
StandardsASME Y14.5; ISO 5459
DefinitionA theoretically exact plane, axis, or point from which dimensions and tolerances are measured

A datum reference is a theoretically exact plane, axis, point, or combination thereof derived from a datum feature — an actual feature of a manufactured part, such as a surface, hole, or edge — that serves as the origin for dimensional measurement and inspection. In geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T), datums establish the coordinate framework against which other features are located and oriented.

Datum features and simulators

On an engineering drawing, a datum feature is identified by a datum feature symbol: a capital letter in a square frame attached to the feature. The physical counterpart used in manufacturing and inspection — for example a surface plate, mandrel, or chuck — is called a datum feature simulator, which establishes the datum reference frame by contacting the real surface.

Datum reference frame

Typically three mutually perpendicular datum planes (primary, secondary, and tertiary, often labelled A, B, and C) define a datum reference frame that constrains all six degrees of freedom of a part: the primary datum removes three, the secondary two, and the tertiary one. The order in which datums are cited in a feature control frame determines their precedence and therefore how the part is fixtured for inspection.

Uses

Datum references underpin tolerances of position, orientation, profile, and runout. They ensure that parts made by different suppliers assemble interchangeably, and they link the design intent of a drawing to practical measurement with coordinate measuring machines.
