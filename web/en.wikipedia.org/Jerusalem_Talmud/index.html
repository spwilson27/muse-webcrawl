Jerusalem Talmud


The Jerusalem Talmud (Talmud Yerushalmi) is a collection of rabbinic commentaries on the Mishnah compiled in the Land of Israel.

The Jerusalem Talmud (Hebrew: תַּלְמוּד יְרוּשַׁלְמִי, Talmud Yerushalmi, often called the Yerushalmi), is a collection of rabbinic notes on the second-century Mishnah that was compiled in Roman and Byzantine Palestine (then known in Hebrew as Eretz Yisrael) during the 4th and 5th centuries CE.

Content and compilation

Despite its name, the Jerusalem Talmud was not compiled in Jerusalem, which Jews were largely barred from in that period, but in the academies of Galilee, principally at Tiberias and Caesarea. Traditionally its redaction is attributed to the circle of Rabbi Yochanan of Tiberias and his disciples. The work covers 39 of the Mishnah's 63 tractates, including the whole of the orders Zeraim (agricultural laws) and Moed (festivals), which the later Babylonian Talmud only partly treats — making the Yerushalmi an essential source for the agricultural laws that apply in the Land of Israel.

Language and character

The Yerushalmi is written in a mixture of Hebrew and Jewish Palestinian Aramaic, a Western Aramaic dialect. It is generally briefer and more terse than the Babylonian Talmud, reflecting hastier redaction amid the deteriorating conditions of Byzantine rule. Its discussions are often elliptical, and its manuscript tradition is thin: essentially all modern editions descend from a single surviving complete manuscript, the Leiden manuscript, copied in 1289.

Influence

Although the Babylonian Talmud became the dominant authority in Jewish law, the Jerusalem Talmud strongly influenced the customs of medieval Ashkenazi Jewry through the scholars of France and Germany, and its importance has grown with modern scholarship and the study of the Mishnah's Palestinian tradition.
