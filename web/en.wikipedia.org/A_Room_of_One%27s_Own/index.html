A Room of One's Own

A Room of One's Own
Author: Virginia Woolf
Published: 24 October 1929
Publisher: Hogarth Press (UK); Fountain Press (US)
Genre: Essay

A Room of One's Own is an extended essay by Virginia Woolf, first published in September 1929. The work is based on two lectures Woolf delivered in October 1928 at Newnham College and Girton College, women's colleges at the University of Cambridge.

Argument

Woolf famously argues that "a woman must have money and a room of her own if she is to write fiction". She surveys the historical exclusion of women from education and literary life, invents Judith Shakespeare — a hypothetical, equally gifted sister of William Shakespeare — to show how such a woman's talent would have been stifled, and urges women writers to claim intellectual and economic independence.

Reception

The essay is regarded as a landmark of feminist literary criticism, and its themes of space, money, and creative freedom continue to shape discussions of women in literature.
