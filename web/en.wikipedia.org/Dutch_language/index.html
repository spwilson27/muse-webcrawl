Dutch language

Dutch (Nederlands) is a West Germanic language spoken mainly in the Netherlands and Belgium, where it is the official language alongside French and German in Belgium. It is spoken by about 25 million people as a first language and is the third most widely spoken Germanic language after English and German.

History

Dutch developed from Old Franconian dialects spoken in the Low Countries. Old Dutch emerged around the 5th century, followed by Middle Dutch from about 1150 to 1500. The language was standardised in the 16th and 17th centuries, strongly influenced by the Statenvertaling Bible translation of 1637. Afrikaans, spoken in South Africa and Namibia, developed from 17th-century Dutch dialects.

Geographic distribution

Dutch is official in the Netherlands, Belgium (Flanders), Suriname, Aruba, Curaçao and Sint Maarten. The Dutch Language Union (Nederlandse Taalunie), founded in 1980, regulates the language jointly for the Netherlands, Belgium and Suriname.

Grammar

Dutch has largely lost its case system but retains two grammatical genders (common and neuter), a subject–verb–object basic order in main clauses and verb-final order in subordinate clauses. Its vocabulary is predominantly Germanic, with notable borrowings from French and, increasingly, English.
