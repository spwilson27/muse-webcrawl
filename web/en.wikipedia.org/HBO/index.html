HBO (Home Box Office) is an American pay television network owned by Warner Bros. Discovery. Launched in 1972 as the first cable-originated television service, it pioneered satellite distribution of television in 1975 and built its reputation on uncut films and, from the 1990s, critically acclaimed original programming that reshaped television drama.

History

Founded by Charles Dolan and operated by Time Inc., HBO first served Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania. In 1975 it beamed the "Thrilla in Manila" boxing match by satellite, inaugurating the modern cable industry. Freed from advertisers and broadcast content rules, HBO invested in original series: Oz (1997), Sex and the City (1998) and The Sopranos (1999) launched the "prestige TV" era, followed by The Wire, Deadwood, Game of Thrones, True Detective, Succession and The White Lotus.

Streaming era

HBO Go (2010) and HBO Now (2015) began the shift to streaming, consolidated in HBO Max (2020), rebranded Max in 2023 and back to HBO Max in 2025. The network has won more Primetime Emmy Awards than any competitor, and its programming is distributed internationally through licensing and local HBO-branded services.
