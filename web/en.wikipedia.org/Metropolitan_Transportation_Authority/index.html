The Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) is a public benefit corporation responsible for public transportation in the New York City metropolitan area of the U.S. state of New York. The MTA is the largest public transportation authority in North America, serving a population of 15.3 million people across a 5,000-square-mile travel area surrounding New York City through Long Island, southeastern New York State and Connecticut.

Operations

The MTA operates the New York City Subway, the Staten Island Railway, MTA Regional Bus Operations, and the Long Island Rail Road and Metro-North Railroad commuter rail systems, as well as seven tolled bridges and two tunnels in New York City through MTA Bridges and Tunnels. Its capital program funds construction and modernisation, supported by fares, tolls, taxes and bond issues.

History

The authority was created in 1968, taking over the operations of the Triborough Bridge and Tunnel Authority and the city's transit systems, and subsequently acquired the bankrupt Long Island Rail Road and the Penn Central's suburban rail lines that became Metro-North.
