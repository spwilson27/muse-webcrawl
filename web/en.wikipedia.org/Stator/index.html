Stator

Stator
TypeStationary component of a rotating system
Found inElectric motors, generators, turbines, pumps
CounterpartRotor

The stator is the stationary part of a rotary system, found in electric generators, electric motors, sirens, mud motors, and biological motors. Its counterpart is the rotor, the moving part. Energy flows through a stator to or from the rotating component of the system.

In electric motors and generators

In an electric motor, the stator provides a rotating magnetic field that drives the rotor; in a generator, the stator converts the rotating magnetic field of the rotor into electric current. A typical stator consists of a cylindrical frame containing a laminated steel core — thin insulated laminations reduce eddy-current losses — with slots in which copper windings are wound. In alternating-current machines the stator windings are usually the armature windings that carry the main current. Stators may also carry permanent magnets in some machine designs.

In fluid systems

In turbines, the stator consists of stationary guide vanes that direct fluid flow onto the rotor blades at the correct angle, and in axial compressors and pumps stator blades diffuse and straighten the flow. In torque converters, the stator redirects fluid returning from the turbine, multiplying torque at low speeds. In biology, the stator units of the bacterial flagellar motor anchor the rotating filament apparatus to the cell wall.
