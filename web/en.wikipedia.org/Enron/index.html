Enron

Enron Corporation was an American energy, commodities, and services company based in Houston, Texas. Formed by the 1985 merger of Houston Natural Gas and InterNorth, it grew into one of the largest electricity, natural gas, communications, pulp, and paper companies before collapsing in scandal in 2001.

Growth and business model

Under leaders including Kenneth Lay and Jeffrey Skilling, Enron moved from pipelines into energy trading and broadband services. It promoted mark-to-market accounting and complex financial structures that presented projected future profits as current earnings.

Scandal and bankruptcy

Revelations in 2001 showed that Enron had hidden debt and losses through special-purpose entities and misleading reporting. Its share price collapsed, the company filed for bankruptcy on 2 December 2001, and thousands of employees lost jobs and retirement savings. Auditor Arthur Andersen was fatally damaged by its role in destroying documents.

Consequences

The Enron scandal became a symbol of corporate fraud and helped drive reforms including the Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002. See also Enron scandal and Sarbanes–Oxley Act.
