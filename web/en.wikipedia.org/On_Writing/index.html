On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft is a memoir and writing guide by American author Stephen King, published in 2000. Part autobiography, part practical manual, it is widely regarded as one of the most popular books about the writing life and is frequently assigned in writing courses.

Contents

The book's first section, "C.V.", recounts King's childhood, his early attempts at storytelling, his years of rejection slips (which he pinned on a nail, then a spike), his teaching career, alcoholism and recovery, and the success of Carrie. The central section, "On Writing", sets out his advice: read widely, write daily (his own target is 2,000 words), favour plain style and the active voice, avoid adverbs, and revise by cutting — his formula is "2nd draft = 1st draft − 10%". A "Toolbox" section covers grammar and style, and the closing section, "On Living", describes the near-fatal 1999 accident in which King was struck by a van while walking in Maine and finished the book during his painful recovery.

Reception

Reviewers praised the book's candour and lack of pretension, and it has remained a steady seller; a tenth-anniversary edition appeared in 2010. King's dictum that "the road to hell is paved with adverbs" is among the most quoted pieces of modern writing advice.
