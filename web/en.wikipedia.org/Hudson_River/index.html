The Hudson River is a 315-mile (507 km) river that flows from north to south primarily through eastern New York, United States. It originates in the Adirondack Mountains of upstate New York at Henderson Lake and flows southward through the Hudson Valley to the New York Harbor between New York City and Jersey City, eventually draining into the Atlantic Ocean. The river is named after Henry Hudson, an Englishman sailing for the Dutch East India Company, who explored it in 1609.

Course

The Hudson's lower half is a tidal estuary, an arm of the sea where its mouth was drowned by rising sea levels after the last ice age; the Mahican people called it Muhheakantuck, "the river that flows both ways". The river forms the boundary between New York City and New Jersey for its final stretch, and is crossed by the George Washington Bridge, the Lincoln and Holland tunnels, and numerous other crossings.

History

The valley was home to Mahican and Lenape peoples before European colonisation under the Dutch colony of New Netherland. The river was strategically vital during the American Revolutionary War, and the Hudson River School of landscape painting took its name from the valley's scenery. The completion of the Erie Canal in 1825 made the Hudson part of a great commercial waterway linking the Atlantic to the Great Lakes.

Pollution and cleanup

Industrial pollution, notably PCB contamination from General Electric plants, led to the designation of a 200-mile stretch of the river as a Superfund site, with dredging carried out between 2009 and 2015. Environmental groups such as Riverkeeper and Scenic Hudson have worked for its recovery since the 1960s.
