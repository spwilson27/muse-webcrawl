Texting while driving

Texting while driving, also called texting and driving, is the act of composing, sending, reading text messages, email, or making similar use of a mobile phone while operating a motor vehicle. Texting while driving is considered extremely dangerous by many authorities, including the World Health Organization, and has been outlawed or restricted in many jurisdictions.

Risks

Texting combines all three forms of driver distraction — visual (eyes off the road), manual (hands off the wheel), and cognitive (mind off driving). Studies by the Virginia Tech Transportation Institute found that texting while driving a heavy vehicle increased crash risk more than twentyfold, and that sending or reading a text takes the driver's eyes off the road for an average of about five seconds — at highway speed, the length of a football field. Distracted driving, of which phone use is a major component, is implicated in thousands of deaths annually in the United States.

Legislation

Most US states ban texting for all drivers, and many ban handheld phone use entirely; the United Kingdom, most of Europe, Australia, and many other countries similarly prohibit hand-held phone use while driving, with fines and penalty points for violations. Laws vary in scope and in whether enforcement is primary (police may stop a driver solely for the violation) or secondary.

Countermeasures

Beyond legislation, responses include public-awareness campaigns, graduated licensing restrictions on young drivers, in-vehicle "do not disturb while driving" phone modes, texting-blocking applications, and employer policies. Research suggests high-visibility enforcement combined with publicity is among the more effective interventions.
