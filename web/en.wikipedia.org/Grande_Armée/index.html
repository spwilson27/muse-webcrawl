Grande Armée

The Grande Armée (French for "Great Army") was the main military component of the French Imperial Army commanded by Emperor Napoleon Bonaparte during the Napoleonic Wars. First named by Napoleon in 1805 for the army assembled at Boulogne for the planned invasion of England, it won a string of decisive victories — Ulm, Austerlitz, Jena-Auerstedt, Friedland, Wagram — that made France the dominant land power in Europe, before the catastrophic invasion of Russia in 1812 destroyed it as an effective force.

Organisation

Napoleon's key innovation was the corps d'armée: self-contained miniature armies of infantry, cavalry, and artillery, each commanded by a Marshal and able to fight alone for a day until other corps converged. The army lived substantially off the land, enabling marching speeds far beyond those of its supply-bound opponents. Its elite reserve was the Imperial Guard — the Old, Middle, and Young Guard — and its artillery, under the former artillery officer Napoleon, was massed in great batteries to punch holes at the decisive point.

Great campaigns

In 1805 the Grande Armée wheeled from the Channel coast to the Danube in weeks, encircling an Austrian army at Ulm and crushing the Austro-Russian army at Austerlitz. It destroyed Prussia's army in 1806 in the twin battles of Jena and Auerstedt, and compelled Russia to peace at Tilsit in 1807. After victory over Austria at Wagram (1809), Napoleon invaded Russia in June 1812 with the largest army yet assembled in Europe. The occupation of a burning, abandoned Moscow, the failure of Tsar Alexander I to negotiate, and a retreat through the Russian winter annihilated the army: of roughly 685,000 men who crossed into Russia, only a fraction returned.

Decline

Napoleon rebuilt the army with young conscripts (the "Marie-Louises") for the 1813 German campaign, but the loss of veteran soldiers and, crucially, cavalry horses could not be made good. Defeated at Leipzig, the army fought a brilliant but hopeless defence of France in 1814; the Hundred Days army of 1815, the last Grande Armée, was defeated at Waterloo.
