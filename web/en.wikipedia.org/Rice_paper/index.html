Type: Edible sheet / paper material
Origin: Vietnam, China
Main ingredient: Rice flour, tapioca flour, water, salt

Rice paper

Rice paper is a product made of paper-like ingredients, most famously the edible, translucent wrappers used in Vietnamese and Chinese cooking, as well as a traditional thin paper used for art and calligraphy in East Asia. In the culinary sense, rice paper is a thin, brittle sheet made from rice flour or rice starch combined with tapioca flour, water, and salt.

Culinary use

In Vietnamese cuisine, rice paper (bánh tráng) is used to make fresh spring rolls (gỏi cuốn) and fried rolls. The dry, brittle sheets are briefly dipped in water to soften them, then wrapped around fillings of shrimp, pork, herbs, rice vermicelli, and vegetables. In China, similar wrappers are used for spring rolls and other dishes.

Production

Traditionally, a thin batter of rice flour and water is spread over a cloth stretched over boiling water, steamed briefly, then peeled off and dried on bamboo mats in the sun, giving the sheets their characteristic crosshatch pattern. Modern production uses machinery to produce uniform sheets at scale.

Art paper

The term rice paper has also been applied to papers made from the pith of the rice paper plant (Tetrapanax papyrifer) and to various East Asian art papers, though many such papers are actually made from mulberry bark or other fibres rather than rice.
