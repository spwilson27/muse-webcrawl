The Ford Model B is an automobile produced by the Ford Motor Company from 1932 to 1934. It was the successor to the Model A, featuring an updated chassis and body and an improved four-cylinder engine. The Model B is best remembered alongside its sibling, the Model 18, which carried Ford's new Flathead V8 engine—the first low-priced, mass-produced car offered with a V8, and one of the most influential automobiles of the 20th century.

A 1932 Ford "Deuce" coupe, a favourite of hot rodders.

Development

Ford developed the car under heavy secrecy, refining the Model A's chassis with a longer wheelbase and a fuel tank moved to the rear. The four-cylinder Model B engine was a heavily revised version of the Model A unit producing 50 hp. The V8 Model 18, introduced the same year, produced 65 hp and quickly overshadowed the four-cylinder car; the 1932 models are collectively known as "Deuces" to collectors.

Legacy

The 1932 Ford, especially the V8 roadster and coupe, became the archetypal basis for American hot rod culture after the Second World War—immortalised by the Beach Boys' song "Little Deuce Coupe" and the film American Graffiti. Bonnie and Clyde famously drove stolen Ford V8s of this era, and Clyde Barrow wrote a (possibly apocryphal) letter praising the car to Henry Ford.
