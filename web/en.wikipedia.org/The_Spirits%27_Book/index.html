The Spirits' Book

Author: Allan Kardec

Original title: Le Livre des Esprits

Published: 1857

Subject: Spiritism

The Spirits' Book (French: Le Livre des Esprits) is a book on Spiritism written by the French educator Allan Kardec (the pen name of Hippolyte Léon Denizard Rivail) and published in Paris on 18 April 1857. It is the founding work of Kardecist Spiritism and the first of the five books of the Spiritist Codification.

Content

The book is arranged as a series of 1,019 questions posed by Kardec to spirits communicating through mediums, with their answers. It covers the nature of spirits, the spirit world, the purpose of incarnation, reincarnation, moral laws, and life after death, presenting Spiritism as a philosophy with scientific and religious aspects.

Influence

The work launched a movement that became especially influential in Brazil, where Kardecist Spiritism has millions of adherents, and it shaped the development of modern Spiritualism and mediumship in Europe and Latin America.
