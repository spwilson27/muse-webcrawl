East Asian–Australasian Flyway

The East Asian–Australasian Flyway is one of the world's great flyways — the routes followed by migratory birds between their breeding and non-breeding grounds. At its greatest extent it stretches from the Russian Far East and Alaska southwards through East Asia and Southeast Asia to Australia and New Zealand, passing through some 22 countries.

Species and scale

More than 50 million migratory waterbirds, including some 8 million shorebirds, use the flyway, representing over 250 populations of more than 150 species. Travellers include the bar-tailed godwit, which makes the longest known non-stop flight of any bird — over 11,000 km between Alaska and New Zealand — and the critically endangered spoon-billed sandpiper.

Key sites

Migratory birds depend on chains of wetlands, especially the tidal flats of the Yellow Sea, where a large fraction of the flyway's shorebirds refuel. Other critical sites include the Gulf of Mottama in Myanmar, Mai Po in Hong Kong, and Moreton Bay and Roebuck Bay in Australia.

Conservation

Reclamation of Yellow Sea tidal flats and other habitat losses have driven steep declines in many populations. The East Asian–Australasian Flyway Partnership (EAAFP), launched in 2006, coordinates conservation across the flyway, and several key sites are protected under the Ramsar Convention.
