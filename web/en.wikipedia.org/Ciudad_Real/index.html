Country: Spain

Ciudad Real is a municipality of Spain located in the autonomous community of Castile–La Mancha, capital of the province of Ciudad Real. It is the fifth most populated municipality in the region, with a population of roughly 75,000.

History

The city was founded by Alfonso X in the 13th century (under the name Villa Real) to counter the power of the military orders in the region, particularly the Order of Calatrava, whose stronghold at Alarcos lay nearby. It received the title of "city" in 1420. Ferdinand and Isabella encouraged its growth, and it became an administrative and judicial centre.

Geography

Ciudad Real lies in the southern part of the Meseta Central, in the Campo de Calatrava, an area of volcanic origin. The Guadiana river passes to the north of the city.

Transport and economy

The city is a stop on the Madrid–Andalusia high-speed railway (AVE), putting it about 50 minutes from Madrid. It hosts a campus of the University of Castilla–La Mancha and was the site of the (now closed) Ciudad Real Central Airport, one of Spain's first private international airports.
