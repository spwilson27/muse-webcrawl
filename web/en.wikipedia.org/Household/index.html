Household

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A household is a social unit consisting of one person living alone or a group of people who live together in a single dwelling and share living arrangements, meals or a common budget. Households are a basic unit of analysis in economics, demography, sociology and official statistics.

Definition

Census definitions vary by country but generally require co-residence and some degree of shared housekeeping. A household is not the same as a family: it may contain non-relatives such as lodgers or domestic workers, may consist of a single person, and a family may be spread across several households. In the United States, the Census Bureau distinguishes family households from non-family households.

History

Historically, households in agrarian societies were larger, often including extended kin, servants and apprentices under the authority of a household head; the word derives from the domestic establishment being “held” together. Industrialisation, urbanisation and rising incomes reduced average household size across the developed world during the 20th century, while single-person households have grown to become the most common type in many countries.

Statistics

Governments measure household size, composition, tenure and income to plan housing, welfare and taxation. Trends such as smaller households, ageing populations and delayed household formation by young adults have significant effects on housing demand independent of population growth.
