El Paso is a city in and the county seat of El Paso County, Texas, United States. It stands on the Rio Grande at the western tip of Texas, across the border from Ciudad Juárez in Mexico and near the state line with New Mexico. The combined El Paso–Juárez metropolitan area is one of the largest binational communities on the U.S.–Mexico border.

History

The pass of the north — El Paso del Norte — was a key crossing on the Spanish Camino Real; the mission at Ysleta dates to 1682, founded by refugees from the Pueblo Revolt. The American city grew after the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848) fixed the Rio Grande as the boundary and boomed with the arrival of the railroads in 1881–82. El Paso was incorporated in 1873.

Geography and economy

The city sits in the Chihuahuan Desert at about 1,140 metres elevation, split by the Franklin Mountains, and enjoys more than 300 days of sunshine a year. Major employers include Fort Bliss, one of the largest U.S. Army posts, the University of Texas at El Paso, health care, and cross-border trade and logistics tied to the maquiladora industry in Juárez.

Demographics

El Paso is a majority-Hispanic city — over 80 percent of residents identify as Hispanic or Latino — and is consistently ranked among the safest large cities in the United States. On 3 August 2019, a gunman motivated by anti-Hispanic extremism killed 23 people at a Walmart in the city, one of the deadliest attacks on Latinos in modern U.S. history.
