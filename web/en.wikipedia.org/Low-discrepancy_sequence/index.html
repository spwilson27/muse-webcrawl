Low-discrepancy sequence

A low-discrepancy sequence is a sequence of points in the unit cube designed to fill space more uniformly than independent random points. Such sequences, also called quasi-random sequences, are used in numerical integration in place of pseudo-random numbers.

Constructions

Classical constructions include the van der Corput and Halton sequences, the Sobol sequence, the Faure sequence and the Niederreiter sequences. Their discrepancy — the deviation of the proportion of points in a box from its volume — grows only logarithmically in the number of points per dimension.

Applications

Low-discrepancy sequences underpin quasi-Monte Carlo methods, which can converge much faster than ordinary Monte Carlo for smooth integrands, with applications in finance, computer graphics and uncertainty quantification.
