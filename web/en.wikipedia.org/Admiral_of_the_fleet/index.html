Admiral of the fleet or fleet admiral is a military naval officer of the highest rank in a number of navies, equivalent to a five-star rank. In the Royal Navy, the rank of Admiral of the Fleet was the highest attainable rank, held for life; appointments ceased in the 1990s except as honorary awards. In the United States Navy the equivalent is fleet admiral, created in 1944 and held by William Leahy, Ernest King, Chester Nimitz, and William Halsey.

History

The title originated in the medieval period for the commander of an entire fleet; the French amiral de France and similar titles developed in parallel. In Britain, the rank evolved from "Admiral of the Fleet" being the officer commanding the whole Royal Navy in war, and it was formalized with distinctive lace, flags, and a broad pennant.

Other countries

Variants have been used in Russia (Admiral of the Fleet of the Soviet Union), India, Australia, Spain (almirante general is distinct), and elsewhere. In most modern navies the rank exists only in wartime or as an honorary distinction, and peacetime fleets are commanded by four-star admirals.
