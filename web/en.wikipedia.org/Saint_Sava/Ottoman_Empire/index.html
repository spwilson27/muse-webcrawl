The Ottoman Empire was a state that controlled much of south-eastern Europe, western Asia and northern Africa between the fourteenth and early twentieth centuries. Founded around 1299 by the Turkish tribal leader Osman I in north-western Anatolia, it grew from a small frontier principality into one of the largest and longest-lived empires in history, and was the pre-eminent Islamic power of its era.

Expansion

The Ottomans crossed into Europe in the 1350s, defeated Serbia at Kosovo (1389), and under Bayezid I nearly united Anatolia before Timur's victory at Ankara (1402) caused an interregnum. Recovered under Mehmed I and Murad II, the empire reached a turning point when Mehmed II ("the Conqueror") captured Constantinople in 1453, ending the Byzantine Empire. Selim I conquered the Mamluk lands of Syria, Egypt and the Hejaz (1516–17), bringing the holy cities of Mecca and Medina under Ottoman rule. Under Suleiman the Magnificent (r. 1520–1566) the empire reached its classical height: Hungary was subdued at Mohács (1526), Vienna besieged (1529), Iraq taken from Persia, and Ottoman fleets contested the Mediterranean and the Indian Ocean.

Institutions

The empire was governed by the sultan and his divan from the Topkapı Palace, administering a religiously diverse population later organised partly through the millet system of self-governing confessional communities. The devşirme levy recruited Christian boys for the Janissary infantry and the palace service, while the timar system supported provincial cavalry. Land law, guilds, pious foundations (waqf) and caravan and sea trade tied the provinces to the centre.

Decline and fall

From the late sixteenth century, military setbacks (Lepanto 1571; Vienna 1683; Karlowitz 1699), fiscal strain and provincial autonomy eroded Ottoman power, and nineteenth-century nationalism stripped away Greece, Serbia, Romania and Bulgaria. Reform programmes (the Tanzimat) modernised law and army but could not stave off defeats. The empire entered the First World War allied with Germany, and its defeat led to occupation and partition plans; the Turkish War of Independence followed, the sultanate was abolished on 1 November 1922, and the Republic of Turkey was proclaimed in 1923, with the caliphate abolished in 1924.
