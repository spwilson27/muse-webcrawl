History of Serbia

Key periodsNemanjić kingdom and empire; Ottoman era; Principality and Kingdom of Serbia; Yugoslavia; Republic of Serbia (2006– )
Key figuresStefan Nemanja; Saint Sava; Stefan Dušan; Karađorđe; Josip Broz Tito

The history of Serbia spans from the settlement of South Slavs in the Balkans in the sixth and seventh centuries to the modern Republic of Serbia. At its medieval height under the Nemanjić dynasty Serbia was the dominant power of the Balkan peninsula; it then spent nearly four centuries largely under Ottoman rule before re-emerging as an independent principality and kingdom in the nineteenth century, becoming the core of Yugoslavia in the twentieth, and again a standalone republic in 2006.

Early medieval period

Serb tribes settled the interior Balkans under Byzantine suzerainty, converted to Christianity during the ninth century — a process completed in the age of Cyril and Methodius's disciples — and formed shifting principalities (Raška, Duklja, Zahumlje) between Byzantine, Bulgarian and Hungarian pressure. In 1166 Stefan Nemanja united the Serb lands as Grand Župan; his youngest son, Saint Sava, secured autocephaly for the Serbian Church in 1219, and his brother Stefan was crowned king in 1217, founding the Nemanjić dynasty that ruled for two centuries.

Empire and Ottoman conquest

Serbia expanded under the Nemanjići, reaching its zenith under Stefan Dušan, crowned Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks in 1346, whose Law Code (1349, 1354) and conquests made Serbia a Balkan empire. After Dušan's death in 1355 the empire fragmented; regional lords fell to the advancing Ottomans at Maritsa (1371) and Kosovo Polje (1389), where Prince Lazar was killed — an event that became the cornerstone of Serbian national memory — and the last despotate fell with Smederevo in 1459.

Ottoman era and national revival

Under Ottoman rule, Serbs lived within the millet system under the restored Patriarchate of Peć (1557–1766), while many migrated north into Habsburg lands in the Great Migrations of 1690 and 1737–39. The First Serbian Uprising under Karađorđe (1804–1813) and the Second under Miloš Obrenović (1815) won autonomy; full independence was recognised at the Congress of Berlin in 1878. The kingdom, ruled alternately by the Obrenović and Karađorđević dynasties, expanded in the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 and suffered catastrophic losses in the First World War, in which Serbia lost a large share of its male population.

Yugoslavia and after

In 1918 Serbia joined the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (from 1929, Yugoslavia). Occupied and partitioned in 1941, Serbia was liberated by Partisan and Red Army forces; within socialist Yugoslavia it was the largest of six republics. Slobodan Milošević's rise in the late 1980s preceded Yugoslavia's violent breakup, wars in Croatia and Bosnia, the Kosovo conflict and NATO bombing of 1999, and Milošević's overthrow in October 2000. The state union with Montenegro ended by referendum in 2006, restoring Serbia as an independent republic; Kosovo's 2008 declaration of independence remains unrecognised by Belgrade.
