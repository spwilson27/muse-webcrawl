Stefan Uroš IV Dušan (c. 1308 – 20 December 1355), known as Dušan the Mighty, was King of Serbia from 1331 to 1346 and the first Serbian Emperor, crowned at Skopje in 1346. The most powerful ruler of the Nemanjić dynasty, he raised Serbia to its greatest medieval extent and promulgated the celebrated Law Code that bears his name.

Rise to power

Exiled as a boy with his father Stefan Dečanski to Constantinople, Dušan returned to Serbia in 1320 and distinguished himself in war against Bulgaria and Byzantium. In 1331 the nobility, dissatisfied with Dečanski after the victory at Velbazhd, deposed the old king and crowned Dušan; his father died soon after in captivity at Zvečan, a deed Serbian tradition lays at Dušan's door and for which he is said to have built the monastery of Visoki Dečani as penance.

Conquests and empire

Taking advantage of the Byzantine civil war between John VI Kantakouzenos and the regency for John V, Dušan conquered Macedonia, Albania, Epirus, Thessaly and Sterea, nearly doubling Serbia's territory by 1348. In 1346 he proclaimed himself "Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks (Romans)", raised the Serbian archbishopric to a patriarchate, and in 1349 issued Dušan's Code, a comprehensive legal system combining Byzantine and Serbian law, revised in 1354. His court at Skopje and Prizren rivalled Constantinople in ceremony; his realm stretched from the Danube to the Gulf of Corinth and from the Adriatic almost to the Aegean.

Death and legacy

Dušan died on 20 December 1355 at Diavoli near Edessa, possibly while preparing a final offensive against Constantinople or an anti-Ottoman crusade negotiated with the papacy. He was buried in his foundation, the Monastery of the Holy Archangels near Prizren; his relics were later transferred to the Church of Saint Mark in Belgrade. Because his son and heir Stefan Uroš V lacked his authority, the empire disintegrated within a generation, lending Dušan an enduring stature in Serbian memory as the symbol of lost greatness.
