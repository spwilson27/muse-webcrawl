The Eastern Orthodox Church, officially the Orthodox Catholic Church, is the second-largest Christian church, with approximately 220 million baptised members. It operates as a communion of autocephalous (self-governing) churches, each governed by its own synod of bishops, united in doctrine, sacramental practice, and mutual recognition. The church traces its history to Jesus Christ and the apostles through the unbroken tradition of the early Church.

History

The Orthodox Church sees itself as the continuation of the one Church founded on Pentecost, shaped by the seven Ecumenical Councils between 325 and 787. The East–West Schism of 1054 separated it from the Roman Catholic Church. Orthodoxy spread from the Byzantine Empire across Eastern Europe and the Balkans through missions such as those of Saints Cyril and Methodius, and survived centuries of Ottoman and later communist rule.

Theology and worship

Orthodox theology centres on Holy Tradition — Scripture as interpreted within the life of the Church — the doctrine of the Trinity, and theosis, the transformative union of the believer with God. Worship is highly liturgical, centred on the Divine Liturgy of St John Chrysostom, celebrated with icons, incense, and chant. The church recognises seven principal sacraments ("mysteries"), administers baptism by immersion, and permits married men to be ordained to the priesthood, while bishops are chosen from monastics.

Structure

The communion includes the four ancient patriarchates (Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, Jerusalem) and autocephalous national churches such as those of Russia, Greece, Serbia, Romania, Bulgaria, and Georgia. The Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople holds a primacy of honour as "first among equals" but has no direct jurisdiction over other churches.
