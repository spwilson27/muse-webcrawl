The Russian Orthodox Church (Russian: Russkaya pravoslavnaya tserkov), also called the Moscow Patriarchate, is the largest autocephalous (self-governing) church of Eastern Orthodoxy. Its history begins with the Christianisation of Kievan Rus' under Prince Vladimir in 988, and it has been intertwined with the Russian state for a millennium.

From Rus' to Patriarchate

The church of Rus' was a metropolitanate of the Patriarchate of Constantinople, centred first at Kiev and, after the Mongol invasions, effectively at Vladimir and then Moscow, where the metropolitan took up residence in the fourteenth century. Moscow's church leaders — Sergius of Radonezh, Metropolitan Jonah — supported the gathering of Rus' lands; in 1448 Russian bishops elected their own metropolitan without Constantinople's approval, asserting de facto independence, sealed by Constantinople's fall in 1453 and the ideology of Moscow as "Third Rome". In 1589 the metropolitan Job was raised to Patriarch with Constantinople's consent.

Schism and Synodal period

The seventeenth century saw the Raskol: Patriarch Nikon's book reforms provoked the Old Believer schism. Peter the Great, after Nikon's claims of authority over the tsar soured church–state relations, abolished the patriarchate in 1721 and placed the church under a Holy Governing Synod supervised by a lay chief procurator, a system lasting until 1917.

Persecution and revival

The Bolsheviks separated church and state in 1918 and unleashed decades of persecution: thousands of clergy and believers were executed or imprisoned, and most churches closed or destroyed. Stalin permitted a controlled revival during the war in 1943, restoring the patriarchate, but Khrushchev renewed anti-religious campaigns. After the millennium of the Baptism of Rus' in 1988 and the Soviet collapse, the church experienced a mass revival of parishes, monasteries and public influence. The patriarchate was held by Alexy II (1990–2008) and from 2009 by Patriarch Kirill. In 2018–19 the grant of autocephaly to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine by Constantinople led Moscow to break communion with the Ecumenical Patriarchate, a rupture deepened by the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.
