Saint Sava (Serbian: Свети Сава, Sveti Sava; c. 1174 – 14 January 1236), born Rastko Nemanjić, was a Serbian prince and Orthodox monk, the first Archbishop of the autocephalous Serbian Church, a founder of Serbian medieval law and literature, and a diplomat. He is regarded as the most important figure in Serbian history and the patron saint of Serbia.

Early life and monastic career

Rastko was the youngest son of Grand Prince Stefan Nemanja, founder of the Nemanjić dynasty. In about 1192 he fled the court for Mount Athos, where he took monastic vows under the name Sava. Together with his father, who later joined him as the monk Simeon, he refounded the abandoned Hilandar Monastery, which became a centre of Serbian religious culture.

Archbishop of Serbia

In 1219 Sava persuaded Patriarch Manuel I of Constantinople, then in exile at Nicaea, to grant autocephaly to the Serbian Church, and Sava was consecrated its first archbishop. Returning to Serbia, he crowned his brother Stefan the First-Crowned king, organised dioceses, and issued the Zakonopravilo (Nomocanon of Saint Sava), among the earliest Serbian legal codes.

Death and legacy

Sava died at Veliko Tarnovo in 1236 while returning from his second pilgrimage to the Holy Land. His relics were later brought to Mileševa Monastery; in 1594 the Ottomans burned them on the Vračar plateau in Belgrade, where the monumental Church of Saint Sava now stands. His feast day, Savindan, is celebrated as the patron day of schools in Serbia.
