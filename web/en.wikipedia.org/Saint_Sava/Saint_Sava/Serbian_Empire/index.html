The Serbian Empire (Serbian: Srpsko carstvo) was a medieval Serbian state that existed from 1346 to 1371, the culmination of the expansion of the Nemanjić kingdom. Its founder, Stefan Dušan, transformed the Kingdom of Serbia into an empire encompassing much of the Balkan peninsula, and its brief existence is remembered as the golden age of medieval Serbia.

Proclamation

Dušan, king from 1331, exploited Byzantine civil wars to conquer Macedonia, Albania, Epirus, Thessaly and much of Greece. On Easter Day, 16 April 1346, at Skopje he was crowned "Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks" by the Serbian patriarch, whose office he had raised to a patriarchate for the purpose. His son Uroš was crowned king as co-ruler, and the empire was administered in a "Serbian" north and a "Greek" south governed with Byzantine forms and titles.

Dušan's Code and administration

In 1349 Dušan promulgated his famous Law Code (Zakonik), supplemented in 1354 — one of the most important legal monuments of medieval Europe, blending Serbian custom, Byzantine law and church canons. The court modelled itself on Constantinople; trade with Dubrovnik (Ragusa) and Venice flourished, Saxon miners worked rich silver mines such as Novo Brdo, and church building — exemplified by the emperor's foundation of the Holy Archangels Monastery near Prizren — testified to the empire's wealth.

Decline and fall

Dušan died suddenly on 20 December 1355, reportedly while planning a campaign toward Constantinople. His young son Stefan Uroš V ("the Weak") could not hold the magnates in check, and the empire splintered among regional lords — Vukašin Mrnjavčević, the Dejanović brothers, Prince Lazar and others. The defeat and death of Vukašin at Ottoman hands at the Battle of Maritsa in September 1371, the year of Uroš's childless death, ended the empire and the Nemanjić line.
