The Nemanjić dynasty (Serbian: Немањићи) was the most important dynasty in Serbian history, ruling the medieval Serbian states from the late 12th century until 1371. Founded by Stefan Nemanja, Grand Prince of Raška, the dynasty transformed a Byzantine-Hungarian border principality into a kingdom (1217) and then a short-lived empire (1346), and is credited with establishing the Serbian Orthodox Church, Serbian law, and a distinctive tradition of royal church-building and endowment.

Origins and rise

Stefan Nemanja (c. 1114–1199) united the Serb lands of Raška in the 1160s–80s, manoeuvring between Byzantium and Hungary. His youngest son Rastko, known as Saint Sava, and Sava's brother Stefan the First-Crowned secured respectively the autocephaly of the Serbian Church (1219) and the royal crown from the papacy (1217), making Serbia both a kingdom and an independent church province.

Kings and empire

Successive kings — Radoslav, Vladislav, Uroš I, Dragutin, and Milutin — expanded Serbian territory and wealth, notably through the silver mines of Novo Brdo. Stefan Dečanski won the battle of Velbazhd (1330), and his son Stefan Dušan (r. 1331–1355) raised Serbia to an empire, conquering much of the Byzantine Balkans, being crowned "Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks" in 1346, and issuing the famous Law Code of 1349/54. After Dušan's death, his son Uroš the Weak could not hold the empire together; the main line died out in 1371.

Legacy

Almost every Nemanjić ruler was canonised by the Serbian Orthodox Church. Their endowments — Studenica, Hilandar, Žiča, Mileševa, Sopoćani, Gračanica, Dečani — remain masterpieces of medieval art and pillars of Serbian cultural memory.
