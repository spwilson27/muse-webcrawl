Saint Sava (Serbian: Sveti Sava; 1174 – 27 January 1236), born Rastko Nemanjić, was a Serbian prince and Orthodox monk, the first Archbishop of the autocephalous Serbian Church, and one of the most important figures in Serbian history. He is revered as the founder of Serbian medieval literature, law, and ecclesiastical independence, and as the patron saint of Serbia and of education.

Life

Rastko was the youngest son of Stefan Nemanja, Grand Prince of Serbia. Given the province of Hum to govern as a youth, he instead fled to Mount Athos, where he took monastic vows under the name Sava. His father later joined him as the monk Simeon, and together they refounded the monastery of Hilandar (1198) as a Serbian house on Athos.

Returning to Serbia, Sava reconciled his feuding brothers and in 1219, at Nicaea, obtained autocephaly for the Serbian Church from Patriarch Manuel I, becoming its first archbishop. He crowned his brother Stefan the First-Crowned king, organised dioceses, and compiled the Zakonopravilo (Nomocanon of Saint Sava), the first Serbian legal code. He undertook two pilgrimages to the Holy Land and died at Tarnovo, Bulgaria, in 1236 on his return from the second.

Legacy

Sava's relics rested at Mileševa Monastery until 1594, when the Ottoman grand vizier Sinan Pasha had them burned in Belgrade in reprisal for a Serbian uprising; the Church of Saint Sava in Belgrade — one of the largest Orthodox churches in the world — now stands on the presumed site. His feast day, 27 January, is celebrated as the school holiday of Serbia.
