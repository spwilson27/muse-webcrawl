History of Serbia

The history of Serbia spans from the settlement of the South Slavs in the Balkans in the 6th and 7th centuries, through a medieval kingdom and empire, centuries of Ottoman rule, independence in the 19th century, the Yugoslav period of the 20th century, and the modern Republic of Serbia.

Early medieval period

Serb tribes settled the western Balkans under Byzantine overlordship in the 600s. Early principalities such as Raška and Duklja emerged; the Vlastimirović and Vojislavljević dynasties ruled the first Serb states. Christianity spread in the 9th century, and in 1219 Saint Sava secured autocephaly for the Serbian Orthodox Church.

The Nemanjić golden age and the Empire

Stefan Nemanja founded the Nemanjić dynasty in the 12th century. His son Stefan the First-Crowned became the first Serbian king in 1217. The kingdom reached its height under Stefan Dušan, who was crowned emperor in 1346 and promulgated Dušan's Code in 1349. The Serbian Empire briefly became the dominant power in the Balkans before fragmenting after Dušan's death in 1355.

Ottoman period

After the Battle of Kosovo (1389) and the fall of the Despotate in 1459, Serbia came under Ottoman rule for nearly five centuries. The Serbian Patriarchate of Peć, the hajduk tradition, and the church preserved national identity. Two great migrations of Serbs into Habsburg territory occurred in 1690 and 1737–39.

Independence and the modern state

The First (1804) and Second (1815) Serbian Uprisings, led by Karađorđe and Miloš Obrenović, began the path to autonomy; full independence was recognised at the Congress of Berlin in 1878. Serbia fought in the Balkan Wars (1912–13) and suffered catastrophic losses in the First World War, after which it became the core of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia in 1918.

Yugoslavia and after

After the Second World War, Serbia was a constituent republic of socialist Yugoslavia. The federation's violent breakup in the 1990s, the Kosovo War and NATO bombing of 1999, and the democratic changes of 2000 reshaped the state. Montenegro's independence in 2006 left Serbia a standalone republic; Kosovo declared independence in 2008, which Serbia does not recognise. Serbia became an EU candidate country in 2012.
