Moscow is the capital and largest city of Russia, standing on the Moskva River in western Russia. One of Europe's largest cities, it is the country's political, economic and cultural centre, seat of the Kremlin and the Russian government.

History

Moscow is first mentioned in chronicles under the year 1147, when Yuri Dolgorukiy invited allies to a feast there. The small frontier town grew after becoming the seat of a principality in the late thirteenth century; under Ivan I and his successors it gathered the tribute of Rus' for the Mongol khans and absorbed rival principalities, emerging under Ivan III as the centre of a unified Russian state. The seat of the Russian Orthodox metropolitan (later patriarch), Moscow promoted the doctrine of the "Third Rome" after Constantinople's fall. In 1712 Peter the Great moved the capital to his new Saint Petersburg, but Moscow remained the coronation city and commercial heart of the empire; Napoleon occupied it in 1812 and much of it burned. The Bolsheviks restored it as capital in 1918.

Twentieth century

Hastily industrialised under the Soviet Union, Moscow was defended in the winter of 1941 in the Battle of Moscow, the first major defeat of the German invasion. Post-war reconstruction added the monumental Metro, the "Seven Sisters" skyscrapers and mass housing. The city hosted the 1980 Summer Olympics, witnessed the failed coup of August 1991 and the constitutional crisis of 1993, and since then has expanded into one of the world's most expensive and populous capitals.

Geography and sights

The historic core is the walled Kremlin citadel beside Red Square, with Saint Basil's Cathedral at its southern end. Rings of boulevards and avenues radiate from the centre; major institutions include Moscow State University, the Bolshoi Theatre and the Tretyakov Gallery.
