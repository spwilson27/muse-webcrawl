The filioque (Latin for "and the Son") is a phrase added in the Latin West to the Nicene Creed, so that the Holy Spirit is confessed to proceed "from the Father and the Son" (ex Patre Filioque procedit). The original creed of Constantinople (381) spoke only of procession "from the Father". The addition became the most famous doctrinal dispute between the Western (Roman Catholic) and Eastern (Orthodox) churches and a central cause of the East–West Schism of 1054.

History

The phrase first appeared in Spain, where the Third Council of Toledo (589) inserted it to combat Arianism among the Visigoths by stressing the Son's full divinity. It spread through the Frankish church under Charlemagne, whose theologians defended it at Aachen in 809, although Pope Leo III initially refused to alter the creed and had the unamended text engraved on silver shields at St Peter's. Rome itself adopted the interpolated creed in 1014, at the request of Emperor Henry II.

The controversy

The dispute has two aspects. Theologically, Eastern critics — beginning with Patriarch Photios in the ninth century — argued that procession from the Son as well compromised the Father's unique role as source (monarchia) within the Trinity; Western theologians, following Augustine, replied that the Spirit proceeds from the Father "principally" and from both as from one principle. Canonically, the East objected that the West had changed an ecumenical creed unilaterally, contrary to the prohibition of the Council of Ephesus. The issue was pressed at the reunion councils of Lyons (1274) and Florence (1438–39), where imperial delegations accepted the Latin formula but their agreements were repudiated in the East.

Modern developments

Since the twentieth century, ecumenical dialogues have narrowed the disagreement: Roman Catholic statements, including a 1995 clarification, acknowledge the original Greek text as normative and stress that the Latin tradition intends procession from the Father through the Son; Orthodox theologians have in turn recognised legitimate diversity of formulation. The filioque nonetheless remains in Western use, while the Orthodox continue to recite the creed without it.
