The Byzantine Empire, also referred to as the Eastern Roman Empire, was the continuation of the Roman Empire in its eastern provinces during Late Antiquity and the Middle Ages, when its capital city was Constantinople. It survived the fall of the Western Roman Empire in the 5th century AD and continued to exist until the fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Turks in 1453. During most of its existence, the empire was the most powerful economic, cultural, and military force in Europe and the Near East.

History

Early history

The empire's roots lie in the division of the Roman Empire by Diocletian and the founding of Constantinople by Constantine the Great in 330. After the death of Theodosius I in 395, the empire was permanently split between his sons. While the West collapsed under barbarian invasions in the 5th century, the wealthier East survived, protected by Constantinople's formidable walls and its strategic position.

Justinian and reconquest

Under Justinian I (r. 527–565), the empire temporarily reconquered parts of the West, including Italy, North Africa, and southern Spain. Justinian also codified Roman law in the Corpus Juris Civilis and built the Hagia Sophia. The reconquests, however, strained the empire's resources, and plague and wars with Persia weakened it further.

Middle period

The 7th century brought the Arab conquests, which stripped the empire of Syria, Egypt, and North Africa. Reduced largely to Anatolia and the Balkans, the empire reorganized into themes and defended itself doggedly. Under the Macedonian dynasty (867–1056), Byzantium enjoyed a resurgence, reconquering territory in the east and Balkans under emperors such as Basil II.

Decline and fall

Defeat at Manzikert (1071) and the catastrophe of the Fourth Crusade (1204), which sacked Constantinople and fragmented the empire, began a long decline. Although Michael VIII Palaiologos recovered the capital in 1261, the restored empire was a shadow of its former self. On 29 May 1453, Sultan Mehmed II captured Constantinople, ending the empire after more than a thousand years.

Culture and legacy

Byzantium preserved and transmitted classical Greek learning, Roman law, and Orthodox Christianity to the Slavic world and beyond. Its art, architecture, and scholarship profoundly influenced both the Islamic world and the Renaissance in Western Europe.
