Saint Sava and the Nemanjić Dynasty

Saint Sava (c. 1174–1236), born Rastko Nemanjić, was the youngest son of Stefan Nemanja, founder of the Nemanjić dynasty that ruled medieval Serbia. Renouncing the throne, he became a monk on Mount Athos, founded the monastery of Hilandar with his father, and in 1219 became the first archbishop of the autocephalous Serbian Orthodox Church.

The dynasty

The Nemanjić dynasty (1166–1371) transformed Serbia from a principality into a kingdom (1217, under Sava's brother Stefan the First-Crowned) and briefly an empire under Stefan Dušan. Sava's law codes, hagiographies and church organisation gave the dynasty a durable spiritual and legal foundation.

Sava's legacy

Sava is Serbia's patron saint and the founder of its national church, education and literature. His relics, kept at Mileševa monastery, were burned by the Ottomans in Belgrade in 1594; the great Church of Saint Sava in Belgrade now stands on the presumed site.
