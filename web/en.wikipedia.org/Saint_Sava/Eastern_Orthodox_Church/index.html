The Eastern Orthodox Church, officially the Orthodox Catholic Church, is the second-largest Christian church, with approximately 220 million baptised members. It is a communion of autocephalous (self-governing) churches, each headed by its own patriarch or primate, united by shared doctrine, sacraments and tradition rather than by a single central authority. The Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople is recognised as first among equals.

History

The church traces its history to the apostles and the early church of the Roman and Byzantine East. Differences in theology, language and political allegiance between the Greek East and Latin West deepened over centuries and culminated in the East–West Schism of 1054, sealed by mutual excommunications and worsened by the sack of Constantinople in 1204. Orthodoxy spread among the Slavs through the missions of Saints Cyril and Methodius in the 9th century; Saint Sava organised the autocephalous Serbian Church in 1219, and Rus' adopted Christianity in 988. After the fall of Constantinople in 1453, the church lived under Ottoman rule in the Balkans and Middle East, while the Russian Church grew into the largest Orthodox body.

Beliefs and practice

Orthodox doctrine rests on Scripture as interpreted within Holy Tradition, the seven ecumenical councils (325–787), the Nicene Creed in its original form (without the filioque), and the writings of the Church Fathers. Worship centres on the Divine Liturgy, celebrated behind an iconostasis; icons are venerated as windows to heaven. The church recognises seven sacraments (mysteries), practises infant baptism by immersion, and understands salvation as theosis — the divinisation of the believer by grace. Married men may be ordained priests, while bishops are chosen from monastics. Monasticism, centred on places such as Mount Athos, holds a central spiritual role.

Organisation

Recognised autocephalous churches include the four ancient patriarchates (Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, Jerusalem) and the churches of Russia, Serbia, Romania, Bulgaria, Georgia, Cyprus, Greece, Poland, Albania and others. Disputes over the status of the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, granted autocephaly by Constantinople in 2019, have disrupted communion between Moscow and Constantinople.
