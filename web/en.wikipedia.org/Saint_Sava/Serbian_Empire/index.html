The Serbian Empire (Serbian: Српско царство, Srpsko carstvo) was a medieval Serbian state that existed from 1346 to 1371. It grew out of the Kingdom of Serbia under Stefan Dušan of the Nemanjić dynasty, who exploited Byzantine civil wars to conquer Macedonia, Albania, Thessaly, and Epirus, and was crowned "Emperor and Autocrat of the Serbs and Greeks" at Skopje in 1346. At its height it was one of the largest states in Europe, stretching from the Danube to the Aegean.

Dušan's reign

Dušan raised the Serbian Archbishopric to a Patriarchate at Peć to crown his imperial dignity, and in 1349 (expanded 1354) promulgated Dušan's Code, one of medieval Europe's most complete legal compilations, blending Byzantine and Serbian customary law. His court attracted scholars, his mines produced great wealth in silver, and his armies planned a campaign against Constantinople itself.

Decline and fall

Dušan died suddenly in 1355 while marching south. His son Stefan Uroš V ("the Weak") lacked the authority to restrain the great nobles — Vukašin Mrnjavčević, Nikola Altomanović, Lazar Hrebeljanović and others carved the empire into semi-independent lordships. Vukašin's defeat and death at the Battle of Maritsa (1371), and Uroš's death the same year, ended the empire and the Nemanjić main line; the fragmented Serbian lands fell piecemeal to the Ottomans over the following century.

Legacy

The empire remained a touchstone of Serbian statehood, celebrated in epic poetry and invoked during the 19th-century national revival. Dušan's Code and the empire's monumental churches, such as Visoki Dečani, endure as its monuments.
