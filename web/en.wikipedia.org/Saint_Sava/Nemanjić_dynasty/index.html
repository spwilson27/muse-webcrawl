The Nemanjić dynasty was the most important royal dynasty of medieval Serbia, ruling from 1166 to 1371. Founded by Stefan Nemanja, it transformed Serbia from a Byzantine-influenced principality into a kingdom (1217) and briefly an empire (1346), and founded the autocephalous Serbian Orthodox Church. Nearly all its rulers were later canonised by the Serbian Church.

Rise

Stefan Nemanja, Grand Župan of Raška from 1166, united Serbian lands and, after initial conflict with Byzantium, secured practical independence. Abdicating in 1196 for the monk's habit (as Saint Simeon), he founded the Studenica and Hilandar monasteries. His son Stefan, "the First-Crowned", received a royal crown in 1217; another son, Rastko — Saint Sava — became the first archbishop of the autocephalous Serbian Church in 1219, securing ecclesiastical as well as political independence.

Height of power

Under Stefan Milutin (1282–1321) Serbia expanded into Macedonia and developed rich silver mines and court culture; his many church endowments include Gračanica. His grandson Stefan Dušan (r. 1331–1355) raised Serbia to its greatest extent, conquering much of the Balkans including Epirus and Thessaly; in 1346 he was crowned "Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks" at Skopje and in 1349 promulgated Dušan's Code, a landmark legal compilation.

Decline

Dušan's sudden death in 1355 left the empire to his son Stefan Uroš V, "the Weak", under whom central authority dissolved as regional lords — Vukašin Mrnjavčević, Lazar Hrebeljanović and others — became effectively independent. Uroš V died childless in 1371, the year of the Battle of Maritsa, ending the dynasty. Serbia fragmented into lordships that gradually fell to the Ottoman Empire over the following century.

Legacy

The dynasty endowed dozens of monasteries — Studenica, Žiča, Sopoćani, Dečani, Gračanica — whose frescoes and architecture are masterpieces of medieval art and several of which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Its joint legacy of state and church shaped Serbian national identity.
