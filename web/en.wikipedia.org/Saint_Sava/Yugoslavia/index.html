Yugoslavia ("Land of the South Slavs") was a country in south-eastern Europe that existed, in several forms, from 1918 until the early 2000s. It united Serbs, Croats, Slovenes, Bosnian Muslims (Bosniaks), Macedonians and Montenegrins — together with substantial Albanian, Hungarian and other minorities — first in a kingdom dominated by the Serbian Karađorđević dynasty, then, after the Second World War, in a socialist federation under Josip Broz Tito.

Kingdom (1918–1941)

The Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes was proclaimed on 1 December 1918, joining the victorious Kingdom of Serbia and Montenegro with former Habsburg South Slav lands. Centralist policies, the Croat question and parliamentary paralysis culminated in King Alexander's royal dictatorship (1929), when the state was renamed Yugoslavia. Alexander was assassinated in Marseille in 1934. Yugoslavia adhered to the Axis Tripartite Pact in March 1941, but a coup repudiating the pact provoked a German-led invasion; the country was occupied and partitioned, with the fascist Independent State of Croatia committing genocide against Serbs, Jews and Roma.

Socialist federation (1945–1992)

Tito's Partisans, the largest resistance movement in occupied Europe, liberated most of the country and proclaimed the Federal People's Republic in 1945, later the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (1963), comprising six republics: Serbia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Slovenia, Macedonia and Montenegro, plus two autonomous provinces within Serbia (Vojvodina and Kosovo). Breaking with Stalin in 1948, Yugoslavia developed "self-management" socialism and co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement (1961), maintaining unusual openness to the West.

Breakup

Tito's death in 1980, debt, inflation and rising nationalism eroded the federation. In 1991–92 Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Macedonia seceded; the Bosnian war (1992–95) caused some 100,000 deaths and ended with the Dayton Agreement. Serbia and Montenegro continued as the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia until 2003, then as a state union until Montenegro's independence in 2006; the Kosovo war of 1998–99 and NATO intervention of 1999 preceded Kosovo's contested 2008 declaration of independence from Serbia.
