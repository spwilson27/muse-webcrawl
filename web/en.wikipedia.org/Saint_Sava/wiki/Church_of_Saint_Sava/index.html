Church of Saint Sava

The Church of Saint Sava (Serbian: Храм Светог Саве, Hram Svetog Save) is a Serbian Orthodox church in the Vračar district of Belgrade, Serbia. One of the largest Orthodox churches in the world, it stands on the plateau where, in 1594, the Ottoman grand vizier Sinan Pasha is traditionally said to have burned the relics of Saint Sava, the founder of the Serbian Orthodox Church.

History

A society for building the memorial church was founded in 1895. Construction began in 1935 to a Serbo-Byzantine Revival design by architects Bogdan Nestorović and Aleksandar Deroko, but was halted by the Second World War and then frozen for decades under Yugoslavia's communist government; work resumed in 1985. The 4,000-ton central dome was famously lifted into place in 1989. The exterior was completed in 2004, and the vast mosaic interior — funded substantially with Russian support — was unveiled around 2020–2021.

Architecture

Clad in white marble and granite, the church covers about 3,500 m² at ground level, can hold some 10,000 worshippers, and its Byzantine-style mosaics are among the largest curved mosaic works in the world. See also Hilandar and Belgrade.
