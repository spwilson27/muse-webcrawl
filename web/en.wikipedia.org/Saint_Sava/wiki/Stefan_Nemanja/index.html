Stefan Nemanja (c. 1113 – 1199) was the Grand Prince (Veliki Župan) of the Serbian Grand Principality (Raška) from 1166 to 1196 and founder of the powerful Nemanjić dynasty that ruled medieval Serbia for over two centuries.

Reign

Nemanja consolidated Serbian lands, maneuvering between the Byzantine Empire and Hungary, expanding into Zeta, Hum, and parts of the Adriatic coast. He fought Emperor Manuel I Komnenos, was briefly captive in Constantinople, and later gained greater autonomy as Byzantine power waned after 1180.

Abdication and Monastic Life

In 1196 he abdicated in favour of his middle son Stefan, took monastic vows as Simeon, and joined his youngest son Sava at Mount Athos, where they refounded the Hilandar Monastery in 1198. He died there in 1199 and was canonised as Saint Simeon the Myrrh-Streaming, after traditions that his relics exuded myrrh.

Legacy

Nemanja endowed major monasteries including Studenica, Đurđevi Stupovi, and Hilandar. His sons shaped Serbia: Stefan became its first crowned king in 1217, and Saint Sava secured autocephaly for the Serbian Orthodox Church in 1219.
