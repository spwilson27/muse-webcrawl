The Serbian Orthodox Church is an autocephalous Eastern Orthodox church in communion with other Orthodox churches. It is the second-oldest Slavic Orthodox church and has adherents mainly in Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Croatia, and the Serbian diaspora worldwide.

History

Christianity spread to Serb lands in late antiquity and the early Middle Ages. In 1219 Saint Sava, son of Stefan Nemanja, secured autocephaly from the Ecumenical Patriarch in exile at Nicaea, becoming the first Serbian archbishop. The church was raised to a patriarchate in 1346 under Stefan Dušan.

Under Ottoman rule the Patriarchate of Peć preserved Serbian religious and cultural identity. It was abolished in 1766 and restored in stages after Serbian independence in the 19th century, reunifying in 1920 in the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes.

Structure

The church is governed by the Holy Synod of Bishops, headed by the Patriarch of Serbia, seated in Belgrade with the historic see at Peć. It comprises metropolitanates and eparchies (dioceses) in Serbia and abroad.

Churches and Monasteries

Major sites include Studenica, Žiča, Sopoćani, Dečani, Gračanica, and the Temple of Saint Sava in Belgrade, one of the largest Orthodox churches in the world, built where tradition holds the relics of Saint Sava were burned in 1595.
