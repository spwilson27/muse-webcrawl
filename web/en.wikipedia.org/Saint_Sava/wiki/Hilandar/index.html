Hilandar

Hilandar Monastery (Serbian: Манастир Хиландар; Greek: Μονή Χιλανδαρίου) is a Serbian Orthodox monastery on Mount Athos in Greece. Founded in 1198 by Saint Sava and his father, the former Serbian ruler Stefan Nemanja (who became the monk Simeon), it ranks fourth in the hierarchy of Athonite monasteries and has been a centre of Serbian religious and cultural life for over eight centuries.

History

Byzantine emperor Alexios III Angelos granted the ruined monastery of Hilandar to the Serbian rulers in 1198, and Sava and Simeon rebuilt it as a house for Serbian monks. The monastery accumulated a rich treasury of medieval Serbian manuscripts, charters, icons, and frescoes. It survived the Ottoman centuries as a spiritual link between Athos and Serbia. A catastrophic fire in March 2004 destroyed a large part of the complex; an extensive reconstruction programme followed.

Treasures

Hilandar preserves revered icons such as the Theotokos "Of the Three Hands" (Trojeručica), associated with Saint John of Damascus, and one of the finest libraries of medieval Slavonic manuscripts in existence. See also Church of Saint Sava and Mount Athos.
