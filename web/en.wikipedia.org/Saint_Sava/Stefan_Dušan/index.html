Stefan Uroš IV Dušan (c. 1308 – 20 December 1355), known as Dušan the Mighty, was the King of Serbia from 1331 and Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks from 1346 until his death. The most powerful ruler of the Nemanjić dynasty, he nearly doubled Serbia's territory at Byzantium's expense and created the Serbian Empire, the largest Balkan state of the 14th century.

Early life and accession

Dušan was the son of Stefan Dečanski, alongside whom he distinguished himself at the Battle of Velbazhd against Bulgaria in 1330. Dissatisfied with his father's caution, the nobility proclaimed Dušan king in 1331; Dečanski was deposed and soon died in captivity, a deed that shadowed Dušan's reign.

Conquests and empire

Exploiting the Byzantine civil war between Andronikos III's regents and John Kantakouzenos, Dušan conquered Macedonia, Albania, Epirus and Thessaly. On Easter Day, 16 April 1346, at Skopje, he was crowned Emperor by the newly elevated Serbian Patriarch Joanikije II. In 1349 he issued his celebrated Law Code (Zakonik), later supplemented in 1354, which regulated everything from church affairs to the rights of peasants and foreign merchants.

Death and legacy

Dušan died on 20 December 1355, reportedly while preparing an expedition against Constantinople. His empire fragmented under his son Stefan Uroš V. Revered in Serbian epic tradition as Car Dušan, he remains the emblem of medieval Serbia's greatest power; scholars debate whether his overextended empire could ever have endured, but his legal and ecclesiastical work left permanent marks.
