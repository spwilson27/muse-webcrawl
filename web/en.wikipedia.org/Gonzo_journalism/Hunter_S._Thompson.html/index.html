Hunter S. Thompson

Hunter Stockton Thompson (July 18, 1937 – February 20, 2005) was an American journalist and author who founded the gonzo journalism movement, a style of reportage in which the writer becomes a central, subjective participant in the events being described. His best-known works include Hell's Angels (1967), Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas (1971) and Fear and Loathing on the Campaign Trail '72 (1973).

Thompson's writing, closely associated with illustrator Ralph Steadman, mixed fact and fiction, profanity and political savagery, and made him a counterculture icon. He lived for decades at Owl Farm in Woody Creek, Colorado, and died by suicide in 2005; his ashes were fired from a cannon in a ceremony he had planned.
