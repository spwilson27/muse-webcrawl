Gonzo journalism


Hunter S. Thompson, gonzo's originator.

Gonzo journalism is a style of journalism written without claims of objectivity, often including the reporter as part of the story through a first-person narrative. It is inseparably associated with its originator, American author Hunter S. Thompson.

History

The term "gonzo" was first applied to Thompson's work by editor Bill Cardoso, describing the 1970 article "The Kentucky Derby Is Decadent and Depraved", written for Scanlan's Monthly and illustrated by Ralph Steadman. The style was fully developed in Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas (1971), in which Thompson's alter ego Raoul Duke covers a motorcycle race and a drug conference while himself immersed in the chaos he describes.

Characteristics

Gonzo writing blends fact and fiction, exaggeration, humour, sarcasm and profanity, and favours the subjective experience of events over detached reporting: the journalist's search for the story becomes the story. Thompson held that objective journalism was a myth and that a writer's honest reaction was truer than manufactured neutrality. The style influenced later writers and remains a byword for immersive, participatory reportage, though critics charge it with self-indulgence and unreliability.
