Takiji Kobayashi (1903–1933) was a Japanese author of proletarian literature, best known for his 1929 novella Kanikōsen (The Crab Cannery Ship), a searing depiction of exploited cannery workers in the northern seas.

Life

Raised in Otaru, Hokkaido, Kobayashi worked at a bank while writing about the hardships of workers and tenant farmers. He joined the underground Japanese Communist Party and became a leading figure of the proletarian literature movement.

Death and legacy

Arrested repeatedly under the Peace Preservation Law, he was tortured to death by the Tokkō special police in February 1933, aged 29; police attributed his death to heart failure. Kanikōsen saw a major revival in Japan in 2008, becoming a bestseller amid debate over working poverty.
