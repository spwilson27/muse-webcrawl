Charities Act 2011

The Charities Act 2011 (c. 25) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It is principally a consolidation Act, bringing together much of the law on charities in England and Wales that had previously been spread across earlier legislation, in particular the Charities Act 1993 and the Charities Act 2006. It received royal assent on 14 December 2011 and came into force, for the most part, on 14 March 2012.

Overview

The Act sets out the legal framework for charities in England and Wales. It defines what counts as a charity and a charitable purpose, regulates the registration of charities with the Charity Commission for England and Wales, and governs the powers and duties of charity trustees. It also covers charity accounts and audit, fundraising, charitable incorporated organisations (CIOs), and the powers of the Charity Commission to inquire into and intervene in the administration of charities.

Charitable purposes

Section 3 of the Act lists the descriptions of purposes recognised as charitable, including the prevention or relief of poverty, the advancement of education, the advancement of religion, the advancement of health, and the advancement of citizenship or community development. To be charitable, a purpose must also be for the public benefit, a requirement carried over from the Charities Act 2006.

Consolidation

As a consolidation measure, the Act was not intended substantially to change the law, but to present it in a single, more accessible statute. Some provisions were later amended, including by the Charities (Protection and Social Investment) Act 2016 and the Charities Act 2022, which introduced changes recommended by the Law Commission.
