Riemann sphere

In mathematics, the Riemann sphere, named after Bernhard Riemann, is a model of the extended complex plane: the complex plane plus one point at infinity. This extended plane represents the extended complex numbers, that is, the complex numbers plus a value ∞ for infinity. With the Riemann model, the point ∞ is near to very large numbers, just as the point 0 is near to very small numbers.

The extended complex numbers are useful in complex analysis because they allow for division by zero in some circumstances, in a way that makes many expressions well-behaved. For example, any non-constant polynomial function on the complex plane can be extended to a continuous function on the Riemann sphere.

As the complex projective line

The Riemann sphere can be viewed as the complex projective line CP1, the space of all one-dimensional complex subspaces of C2. As with any compact Riemann surface, the sphere may also be viewed as a projective algebraic curve, making it a fundamental example in algebraic geometry. It also finds utility in other disciplines that depend on analysis and geometry, such as quantum mechanics and other branches of physics.

Stereographic projection

As with any Riemann surface, the sphere can be described geometrically as the set of points obtained by projecting the complex plane onto a sphere via stereographic projection from the north pole, which is identified with the point at infinity. Under this projection, the sphere minus the north pole is in one-to-one correspondence with the complex plane, and the north pole corresponds to ∞.
