Treaty establishing a Constitution for Europe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaty establishing a Constitution for Europe (TCE) was an unratified international treaty intended to create a consolidated constitution for the European Union. It would have replaced the existing European Union treaties with a single text, given the EU legal personality, created a long-term President of the European Council and a Union Minister for Foreign Affairs, and incorporated the Charter of Fundamental Rights.

History

The treaty was drafted by the European Convention, chaired by Valéry Giscard d'Estaing, in 2002–2003, agreed by an intergovernmental conference, and signed in Rome on 29 October 2004. Ratification proceeded in member states, with Spain and Luxembourg approving it by referendum and many parliaments ratifying it. However, French voters rejected it on 29 May 2005 (55% No) and Dutch voters on 1 June 2005 (62% No), effectively killing the treaty.

Aftermath

After a "period of reflection", member states negotiated the Treaty of Lisbon (2007), which carried over most of the TCE's institutional reforms while abandoning the constitutional form, symbols, and terminology. Lisbon entered into force on 1 December 2009.
