Basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH)
TypeProtein structural motif / transcription factor family
Lengthabout 60 amino acids
DNA targetE-box sequence (CANNTG)
ExamplesMyoD, Myc, Max, E47, NeuroD

Basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) is a protein structural motif that characterizes one of the largest families of dimerizing transcription factors. The motif consists of two alpha-helices connected by a loop, preceded by a stretch of basic amino acids that binds DNA.

Structure and function

The basic region of the motif contacts DNA in the major groove, typically at a hexanucleotide sequence known as the E-box (CANNTG). The helix-loop-helix region mediates dimerization: bHLH proteins bind DNA as homodimers or heterodimers, and the identity of the dimer partner determines which genes are activated or repressed.

Biological roles

bHLH factors control cell fate decisions in many developmental processes. Class II factors such as MyoD drive skeletal muscle differentiation, while neurogenins and NeuroD family members specify neuronal lineages. Other members, including Myc and its partner Max, regulate cell growth and proliferation and are implicated in cancer when deregulated. Inhibitor of DNA-binding (Id) proteins, which lack the basic region, act as natural dominant-negative regulators of bHLH activity.

Classification

The family is divided into several classes based on tissue distribution, dimerization partners, and DNA-binding preferences, ranging from ubiquitously expressed E proteins to tissue-restricted factors and the bHLH-PAS subfamily, which includes the hypoxia-inducible factors and the aryl hydrocarbon receptor.
