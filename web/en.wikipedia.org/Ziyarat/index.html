Ziyarat

Ziyarat (Arabic: زيارة, romanized: ziyāra, "visitation") is a form of pilgrimage in Islam involving visits to the graves and shrines of the Prophet Muhammad, his family members, the Imams, and other revered figures, as well as to mosques and other holy sites. The practice is especially central to Shia Islam, and is also widespread in Sufi traditions within Sunni Islam.

Practice

Pilgrims recite prescribed supplications, known as ziyarat texts, at the site; the best known include Ziyarat Ashura, recited at the shrine of Imam Husayn in Karbala, and Ziyarat Amin Allah. Major destinations include the Prophet's Mosque in Medina, the Imam Husayn and Abbas shrines in Karbala, the Imam Ali shrine in Najaf, the Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad, and the Sayyidah Zaynab Mosque in Damascus. The Arba'in pilgrimage to Karbala, marking forty days after Ashura, is among the largest annual gatherings in the world, drawing tens of millions of participants.

Views

Supporters ground the practice in Quranic verses and hadith encouraging remembrance of the righteous. Some reformist movements, notably Salafism, regard tomb visitation with devotional intent as an unlawful innovation, and such shrines have been demolished or attacked in areas under their control; mainstream Shia and much Sunni scholarship continue to endorse the practice.
