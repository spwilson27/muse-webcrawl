Twisted pair

TypeGuided transmission medium
Invented byAlexander Graham Bell (1881)
UsesTelephone lines, Ethernet, DSL

Twisted pair cabling is a type of wiring in which two conductors of a single circuit are twisted together to improve electromagnetic compatibility. Twisting cancels electromagnetic interference from external sources and reduces crosstalk between neighbouring pairs, because noise couples equally into both wires and is rejected by differential signalling.

History

Alexander Graham Bell patented the twisted pair in 1881, after early telephone lines run alongside telegraph and power lines suffered severe interference. Twisted pairs went on to form the backbone of the world's telephone networks.

Types

Unshielded twisted pair (UTP) is standardised in categories (Cat 5e, Cat 6, Cat 6a, Cat 8) with progressively tighter twists and higher bandwidths, and carries most Ethernet local area networks using RJ45 connectors. Shielded twisted pair (STP) adds foil or braided shielding for electrically noisy environments. Different pairs within a cable are twisted at different rates to minimise pair-to-pair crosstalk.

Advantages and limits

Twisted pair is cheap, flexible and easy to install, but has higher attenuation and lower bandwidth over distance than coaxial cable or optical fibre, limiting high-speed runs to about 100 metres for typical Ethernet standards.
