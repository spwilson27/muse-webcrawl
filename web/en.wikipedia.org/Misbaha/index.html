Misbaha

A misbaha (Arabic: مِسْبَحَة, misbāha), also rendered masbaha or tasbih (تَسْبِيح, tasbī̄), is a string of prayer beads used by Muslims to keep count of recitations in dhikr, the remembrance of God. In Turkey it is known as tespih, and analogous bead strings are used across the Eastern Mediterranean and beyond, including by Arab Christians and members of other faiths.

Typical bead counts99, 33, or 100
Used forDhikr; counting tasbih recitations
MaterialsWood, amber, glass, plastic, gemstones, olive pits
ComponentsBeads, divider beads (imam), tassel

Use

The most common forms have 99 beads, corresponding to the 99 Names of God in Islam, or 33 beads used in three circuits. After the ritual prayer (salah), worshippers traditionally recite Subhan Allah ("Glory be to God") 33 times, Alhamdulillah ("Praise be to God") 33 times, and Allahu Akbar ("God is the Greatest") 33 or 34 times, passing one bead per recitation. Misbahas are also used during personal dhikr, at funerals, and in Ramadan devotion. Beyond devotion, the beads are commonly carried and idly fingered, much like Greek komboloi worry beads.

Construction

A traditional misbaha consists of beads strung on silk or synthetic cord, divided into three sections by slightly larger marker beads in the 99-bead form, and terminating in a headpiece — often called the imam bead — with a tassel. Materials range from inexpensive plastic and wood to carved amber, coral, ivory (historically), agate, and precious stones; fine antique misbahas are collector's items.

History

The practice of counting prayers on pebbles, seeds, or knotted cords is ancient in the Middle East; strings of beads became standard in the early Islamic centuries, possibly influenced by the older Buddhist and Hindu mala and the Christian rosary traditions with which they share the basic technology of counted prayer.
