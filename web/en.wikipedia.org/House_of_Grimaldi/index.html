The House of Grimaldi is the reigning house of the Principality of Monaco. Founded in Genoa in the twelfth century, the family acquired Monaco in 1297, when Francesco Grimaldi — "Francesco the Malicious" — seized the fortress on the Rock of Monaco disguised as a Franciscan friar. With brief interruptions, his descendants have ruled Monaco ever since, making the Grimaldis one of the longest-ruling dynasties in Europe.

Origins

The family descends from Grimaldo Canella, a Genoese statesman and son of Otto Canella, consul of Genoa. The Grimaldi were one of the great patrician families of the Republic of Genoa, prominent in the Guelph cause and frequently exiled during the city's factional struggles.

Rule of Monaco

The Grimaldis' hold on Monaco was formalised over the following centuries, with the lordship recognised by France and Spain at various times. Honoré II assumed the title of Prince in 1612. The male line of the original family ended in 1731, and the name and arms passed through marriage to the Goyon-Matignon and later Polignac families. Prince Rainier III (reigned 1949–2005) transformed Monaco into a financial and tourist centre; his marriage to Grace Kelly in 1956 brought worldwide attention. He was succeeded by Albert II.

Coat of arms

The arms of the house are a shield of red and silver lozenges (fusily argent and gules), supported by two armed friars — a reference to Francesco's disguise — with the motto Deo Juvante ("With God's help").
