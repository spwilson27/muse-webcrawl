Arte

Type: Public television network

Country: France, Germany

Launched: 30 May 1992

Headquarters: Strasbourg, France

Owners: ARTE France, ARTE Deutschland

Arte (Association relative à la télévision européenne) is a European public service television channel and streaming platform that promotes cultural programming in French and German. It was established by an intergovernmental agreement between France and Germany signed in 1990, and began broadcasting on 30 May 1992.

History

The channel grew out of a desire, following German reunification and in the spirit of Franco-German reconciliation, to create a shared cultural broadcaster for Europe. Arte replaced the French channel La Sept and took over frequencies previously used by other services. Its headquarters are in Strasbourg, symbolising Franco-German cooperation, with production coordinated between ARTE France in Paris and ARTE Deutschland in Baden-Baden.

Programming

Arte's schedule emphasises culture, arts, documentaries, cinema, history, science and current affairs. Well-known programmes include the news bulletin Arte Journal, the documentary strands, the magazine Karambolage, which playfully explains French and German customs, and music programmes covering classical, jazz and contemporary genres. Around 85 percent of Arte's programming is produced in Europe.

Structure and distribution

Arte is a European Economic Interest Grouping (EEIG) whose members are ARTE France and ARTE Deutschland (itself owned by the German public broadcasters ARD and ZDF). It is financed mainly from the television licence fees of France and Germany. The channel broadcasts in both French and German, with many programmes subtitled or dubbed into additional languages including English, Spanish, Polish and Italian through its digital platform arte.tv.
