Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov interpretation

The BHK interpretation explains logical connectives in terms of constructions (proofs). (Image placeholder)

The Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov interpretation, or BHK interpretation, is an explanation of intuitionistic logic in terms of proofs and constructions. It is named after L. E. J. Brouwer, Arend Heyting and Andrey Kolmogorov, who each contributed to formulating the semantics of intuitionism: a proposition is true only when we have a construction (proof) of it.

The interpretation

Under the BHK interpretation, the meaning of each logical connective is given by what counts as a proof of a statement built with it:

  • A proof of P ∧ Q consists of a proof of P together with a proof of Q.
  • A proof of P ∨ Q consists of a proof of P or a proof of Q, with an indication of which.
  • A proof of P → Q is a construction that transforms any proof of P into a proof of Q.
  • A proof of ¬P is a construction transforming any proof of P into a proof of a contradiction; there is no proof of ⊥ (falsehood) itself.
  • A proof of ∃x P(x) gives a specific witness a together with a proof of P(a).
  • A proof of ∀x P(x) is a construction that, for any given a, produces a proof of P(a).

Consequences

Because a proof of a disjunction must decide which disjunct holds, the law of excluded middle is not valid intuitionistically, and proofs by contradiction do not in general establish a proposition. The BHK interpretation is closely related to the Curry–Howard correspondence, under which proofs correspond to programs and propositions to types, and to realizability semantics developed by Stephen Kleene.
