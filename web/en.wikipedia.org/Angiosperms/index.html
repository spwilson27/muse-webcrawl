Angiosperms

Angiosperms are plants of the division Angiospermae (also called Magnoliophyta), commonly known as flowering plants. They are distinguished from the other seed plants (gymnosperms) by having seeds enclosed within a carpel that matures into a fruit, and by their characteristic reproductive structures, flowers. With an estimated 300,000–400,000 known species grouped into about 64 orders and 416 families, angiosperms are by far the largest and most diverse group of land plants, dominating most terrestrial ecosystems and providing nearly all plant-based human food.

Characteristics

Defining features include flowers with stamens bearing two pairs of pollen sacs; carpels enclosing the ovules, which receive pollen on a stigma; a greatly reduced male gametophyte (three cells) and female gametophyte (typically seven cells/eight nuclei); and double fertilisation, in which one sperm fertilises the egg while another fuses with the central cell to produce the nutritive endosperm. Angiosperm xylem usually contains vessel elements, increasing water-conducting efficiency, and their leaves typically have netted venation.

Evolution and fossil record

Unambiguous angiosperm fossils appear in the Early Cretaceous, about 134 million years ago, and the group diversified explosively during the Cretaceous — Darwin's famous "abominable mystery". Molecular clocks often suggest an earlier, Jurassic or even Triassic origin. Early-diverging living lineages include Amborella, water lilies (Nymphaeales) and Austrobaileyales, followed by the great radiations of magnoliids, monocots and eudicots.

Classification

The Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG IV, 2016) system, based largely on molecular data, recognises 64 orders and 416 families. The two largest clades are the monocots (grasses, orchids, palms, lilies — about 22% of species) and the eudicots (about 75%), the latter including the rosids and asterids. Economically pre-eminent families include Poaceae (cereals), Fabaceae (legumes), Rosaceae, Solanaceae and Asteraceae.

Importance to humans

Angiosperms supply almost all crops — cereals, fruits, vegetables, oils, sugar — as well as timber, fibres such as cotton, medicines, and ornamental plants. Agriculture is essentially the managed cultivation of flowering plants, and pollinator mutualisms, especially with bees and other insects, underpin a large share of global food production.
