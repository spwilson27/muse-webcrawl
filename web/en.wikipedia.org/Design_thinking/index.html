Design thinking is a term used to represent a set of cognitive, strategic and practical processes by which design concepts are developed. Many of the key concepts and aspects of design thinking have been identified through studies, across different design domains, of design cognition and design activity in both laboratory and natural contexts.

Design thinking is also associated with prescriptions for the innovation of products and services within business and social contexts. Some of these prescriptions have been criticized for oversimplifying the design process and trivializing the role of technical knowledge and skills.

Origins

The notion of design as a "way of thinking" in the sciences can be traced to Herbert A. Simon's 1969 book The Sciences of the Artificial. In management, Rolf Faste expanded on the work of Robert McKim during the 1980s and 1990s at Stanford University, teaching design thinking as a method of creative action. The approach was commercialized by the design consultancy IDEO, founded by David Kelley, who also founded the Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford (the "d.school") in 2004.

The process

A widely taught version of the process comprises five stages: empathize (understand users through observation and interview), define (frame the problem as a point-of-view statement), ideate (generate a wide range of candidate solutions), prototype (build low-fidelity representations), and test (evaluate prototypes with users and iterate). Variants with three, four, six or seven stages exist, and the stages are understood to be iterative rather than strictly linear.

Applications and criticism

Design thinking has been applied in software and product development, healthcare, education and public policy. Critics argue the term is sometimes used as a marketing label, that its packaged workshop form can trivialise professional design practice, and that its effects are hard to measure. Proponents respond that it democratises design methods and helps organisations address ill-defined, "wicked" problems.
