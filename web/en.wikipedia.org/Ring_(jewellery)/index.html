Ring (jewellery)

A ring is a round band, usually made of metal, worn as ornamental jewellery around the finger, with examples also worn on toes, arms and the neck. Rings commonly feature gemstones or other decorative settings and have carried symbolic significance—of love, marriage, status, faith and membership—for thousands of years.

A gold ring with a gemstone setting.

History

Finger rings were worn in ancient Egypt, where signet rings bearing seals identified their owners, and in ancient Greece and Rome, where rings denoted rank and were used to seal documents. The Romans popularised betrothal rings; iron rings gave way over time to gold. In the Middle Ages rings served as religious insignia for bishops, as memorial (memento mori) jewellery, and as posy rings inscribed with mottoes.

Types and symbolism

Notable types include the wedding ring, engagement ring (promoted in the 20th century with diamonds), signet ring, class ring, championship ring in sports, mood ring, and ecclesiastical rings such as the Ring of the Fisherman worn by the Pope. Jewellery rings are typically made from gold, silver, platinum, palladium, titanium or tungsten carbide, and sized according to regional ring size standards.
