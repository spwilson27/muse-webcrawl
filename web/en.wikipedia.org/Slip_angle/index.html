Slip angle

In vehicle dynamics, the slip angle or sideslip angle is the angle between the direction in which a wheel is pointing and the direction in which it is actually travelling (i.e., the angle between the forward velocity vector and the vector sum of wheel forward velocity and lateral velocity). This slip angle results in a force, the cornering force, which is in the plane of the contact patch and perpendicular to the intersection of the contact patch and the midplane of the wheel.

Definition

The slip angle is defined as the angle between a rolling wheel's actual direction of travel and the direction towards which it is pointed. A slip angle of zero indicates the wheel is rolling in exactly the direction it points, generating no lateral force.

Effects

As slip angle increases from zero, cornering force rises roughly linearly at first, then saturates as the tyre approaches its grip limit. Differences between front and rear slip angles determine whether a vehicle understeers or oversteers at the limit.
