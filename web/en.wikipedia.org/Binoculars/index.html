Binoculars

Binoculars are a pair of identical or mirror-symmetrical telescopes mounted side by side and aligned to point in the same direction, allowing the viewer to use both eyes when viewing distant objects. They provide three-dimensional depth perception and a wider field of view than a single telescope.

Design

Most binoculars use prisms — Porro prisms or roof prisms — to correct the image orientation and shorten the optical path. Binoculars are described by two numbers, such as 8×42, denoting the magnification (8×) and the objective lens diameter in millimetres (42 mm).

History

The first binocular telescopes appeared in the 17th century, shortly after the invention of the telescope. Practical prism binoculars were developed in the 19th century, with the Porro prism design patented by Ignazio Porro in 1854 and popularised by the Zeiss company in the 1890s.

Uses

Binoculars are used for birdwatching, astronomy, hunting, marine navigation, military observation, and sporting events. Specialised forms include opera glasses and rangefinding binoculars.
