Salt Lake City

Salt Lake City is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Utah, as well as the seat of Salt Lake County. It was founded in 1847 by early settlers led by Brigham Young, who were seeking an isolated region to practise their religion, the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, free from persecution. The city lies in a valley at the foot of the Wasatch Range, southeast of the Great Salt Lake, from which it takes its name.

History

Mormon pioneers arrived in the Salt Lake Valley on 24 July 1847 — still celebrated as Pioneer Day — after their exodus from Nauvoo, Illinois, following the murder of church founder Joseph Smith. Brigham Young declared "This is the right place" and laid out a city on a grid centred on the temple site. The settlement survived the cricket plague of 1848 (the "miracle of the gulls") and grew with gold-rush traffic, the Pony Express, and the completion of the transcontinental railroad nearby at Promontory Summit in 1869. Utah became a state in 1896, after the church renounced plural marriage.

Geography and climate

The city occupies the northeastern corner of the Salt Lake Valley, bordered by the Great Salt Lake to the northwest and the steep Wasatch Range to the east. It has a semi-arid continental climate with hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters; lake-effect snow from the Great Salt Lake can be significant. Winter temperature inversions sometimes trap pollution in the valley.

Temple Square and landmarks

Temple Square, a 10-acre complex owned by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, contains the Salt Lake Temple (dedicated 1893 after 40 years of construction), the Tabernacle — home of the Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square — and the Assembly Hall. Other landmarks include the Utah State Capitol, the City and County Building, and the main library designed by Moshe Safdie.

Economy and culture

Salt Lake City is a centre of banking, government, and the outdoor recreation industry, and hosts the annual Sundance Film Festival events and Outdoor Retailer trade shows in nearby venues. It is also a hub for genealogy through the church's FamilySearch facilities. The population has become increasingly diverse and less predominantly Latter-day Saint within the city proper.

Sports

The city hosted the 2002 Winter Olympic Games and is scheduled to host the Winter Games again in 2034. Professional teams include the Utah Jazz (NBA). Nearby ski resorts in the Wasatch — Alta, Snowbird, Park City, and others — market the region's powder snow as the "Greatest Snow on Earth".
