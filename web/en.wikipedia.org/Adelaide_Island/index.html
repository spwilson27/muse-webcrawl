Adelaide Island

Adelaide Island is a large island, 75 nautical miles (139 km) long and 15 nautical miles (28 km) wide, lying off the west coast of the Antarctic Peninsula. It is situated south of the Antarctic Circle in Marguerite Bay, and is mostly ice-covered and mountainous.

History

The island was discovered in 1832 by a British expedition under John Biscoe, who named it in honour of Queen Adelaide, consort of King William IV. It was charted more fully by the French Antarctic Expedition of 1908–10 under Jean-Baptiste Charcot.

Research stations

The British Antarctic Survey operates Rothera Research Station on the island's southeast coast, which has served as the main British base in Antarctica since it was established in 1975. Rothera includes a gravel runway and supports logistics for deep-field science across the continent. The station replaced an earlier base at Adelaide (Base T), which was closed after being established in 1961.

Geography

The island is separated from the Antarctic Peninsula mainland by The Gullet, a narrow channel. Mount Gaudry, at about 2,315 metres, is among its highest peaks. The surrounding waters are home to seals, penguins, and other Antarctic wildlife.
