Gerlachovský štít ("Gerlach Peak", also known in German as Gerlsdorfer Spitze and in Hungarian as Gerlachfalvi-csúcs) is the highest peak in Slovakia, in the High Tatra Mountains, and in the entire Carpathian mountain range. It rises to 2,655 metres (8,711 ft) above sea level.

Name and history

The peak takes its name from the village of Gerlachov at its foot. Because the Tatras formed part of the Kingdom of Hungary and later Czechoslovakia, the mountain's official name changed with political circumstances: during the 20th century it was at times officially named after Franz Joseph I, and from 1949 to 1959 it was called Stalin Peak (Stalinov štít) before reverting to Gerlachovský štít.

Climbing

The summit lies within Tatra National Park, and Slovak regulations require climbers to be accompanied by a certified mountain guide unless they are members of a recognised mountaineering club. The normal route ascends from the Sliezsky Dom hotel via the Velická valley and involves scrambling and fixed chains; other routes, such as the Tatarka route from the Batizovská valley, are more direct but exposed. The peak is a popular goal for visitors wishing to stand on Slovakia's highest point.
