The Carta de Logu ("Charter of the Land") is a legal code of the Judicate of Arborea, promulgated by the Judgess Eleanor of Arborea (Elianora de Arbarèe) in the late 14th century. Written in the Sardinian language, it is one of the most remarkable legal documents of medieval Europe, notable for its systematic structure and strikingly advanced provisions.

The Carta de Logu, Eleanor of Arborea's legal code for Sardinia.

Contents

The code consists of 198 chapters covering civil, criminal, agrarian, and commercial law. It regulated matters central to a rural society: land tenure, grazing and crop damage, fire prevention, theft, homicide, inheritance, and the duties of local officials. Among its celebrated provisions, daughters could inherit property on an equal basis in the absence of sons, rape was severely punished and a victim could choose whether to marry the offender or see him punished, and fines were fixed by law rather than left to arbitrary discretion.

History

Eleanor built on earlier compilations associated with her father Marianus IV, issuing the Carta in 1392 and publishing it in 1395. After the Aragonese conquest of Sardinia, the code was progressively extended to the whole island and was confirmed by successive rulers. Remarkably, it remained in force across Sardinia until 1827, when it was replaced by the code of Charles Felix of Savoy.

Significance

The Carta de Logu is a landmark of Sardinian identity and of European legal history: a comprehensive code in a vernacular language, issued under a female ruler, centuries ahead of many contemporary compilations in its concern for legal certainty and protections for women and rural labourers.
