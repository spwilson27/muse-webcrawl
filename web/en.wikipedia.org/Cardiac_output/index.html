Cardiac output

DefinitionVolume of blood pumped per minute
FormulaCO = heart rate × stroke volume
Typical resting value~5 L/min (adult)
UnitsLitres per minute (L/min)

Cardiac output (CO) is the volume of blood pumped by the heart per minute. It equals the product of heart rate and stroke volume: CO = HR × SV. In a healthy resting adult, with a heart rate of about 70 beats per minute and a stroke volume of about 70 mL, cardiac output is approximately 5 litres per minute — roughly the body's entire blood volume each minute.

Heart rate and stroke volume jointly determine cardiac output.

Determinants

Stroke volume depends on preload (ventricular filling, governed by the Frank–Starling relationship), afterload (the resistance the ventricle ejects against), and contractility, which is increased by sympathetic stimulation and circulating catecholamines. Heart rate is controlled by the autonomic nervous system. During heavy exercise, cardiac output can rise four- to six-fold in untrained people and to 30–40 L/min in elite endurance athletes.

Measurement

Historically, output was estimated by the Fick principle, which divides oxygen consumption by the arteriovenous oxygen difference. Clinical methods include thermodilution via a pulmonary artery catheter, indicator dilution, echocardiography and Doppler ultrasound, cardiac MRI, and pulse-contour analysis. The cardiac index normalizes output to body surface area (normally about 2.5–4.0 L/min/m²).

Clinical significance

Abnormally low output characterizes cardiogenic and other shock states and advanced heart failure ("low-output" failure), while sepsis, anemia, hyperthyroidism, and pregnancy cause high-output states. Maintaining adequate cardiac output is a central goal in critical care.
