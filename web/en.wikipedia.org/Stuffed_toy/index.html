Stuffed toy

A group of plush toys, the most common form of stuffed toy.

A stuffed toy is a toy doll with an outer fabric sewn from a textile and stuffed with flexible material. They are known by many names, such as plush toys, plushies, stuffed animals and stuffies; in Britain and Australia, they may also be called soft toys or cuddly toys. Stuffed toys are made in many different forms, but most commonly resemble real animals (sometimes with exaggerated proportions or features), legendary creatures, cartoon characters, or inanimate objects.

History

The stuffed toy originated in the late 19th century. The German Steiff company, founded by Margarete Steiff in 1880, began producing stuffed elephants and other animals from felt. In 1902–03 the teddy bear emerged nearly simultaneously in Germany (Steiff) and the United States (Morris Michtom, inspired by a cartoon of President Theodore Roosevelt), becoming the archetypal stuffed toy. Mass production expanded through the 20th century, and branded plush lines became a major part of the toy industry.

Types and uses

Stuffed toys serve as comfort objects for children, collectibles for adults, promotional merchandise, and prizes at carnivals and in claw machines. Modern plush is typically made from synthetic fabrics with polyester-fibre filling, and may incorporate plastic pellets, sound chips or electronic components. Safety standards in many countries regulate materials and small parts because of choking and flammability hazards.
