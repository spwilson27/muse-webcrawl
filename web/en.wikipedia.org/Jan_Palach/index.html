Jan Palach (11 August 1948 – 19 January 1969) was a Czech student of history and political economics at Charles University in Prague. His self-immolation on 16 January 1969 in Wenceslas Square was a political protest against the end of the Prague Spring reforms and the invasion of Czechoslovakia by the Warsaw Pact armies in August 1968. He died three days later from his burns.

Background

The Prague Spring of 1968, led by Alexander Dubček, sought to create "socialism with a human face". On the night of 20–21 August 1968, Warsaw Pact troops invaded Czechoslovakia and crushed the reforms. In the months that followed, censorship returned and public resistance faded. Palach and several fellow students formed a group pledged to protest through self-immolation as "human torches"; Palach drew the lot to be "Torch No. 1".

Self-immolation and death

On 16 January 1969, Palach doused himself with petrol and set himself on fire near the statue of Saint Wenceslas in Wenceslas Square. A letter found at the scene demanded the abolition of censorship and the banning of the occupation forces' newspaper, warning that more torches would follow if the demands were not met. Despite suffering burns over 85% of his body, Palach lived for three days; he told investigators his act was meant to awaken the public from apathy. His funeral on 25 January drew hundreds of thousands of mourners and became a major protest.

Legacy

A month later, student Jan Zajíc immolated himself in the same square, and Evžen Plocek followed in Jihlava. The communist authorities exhumed and cremated Palach's remains in 1973 to destroy his grave as a shrine, but after the Velvet Revolution of 1989 his remains were returned to Olšany Cemetery. Palach is commemorated by memorials in Prague and elsewhere, streets and squares across Europe, the asteroid 1834 Palach, and an annual "Palach Week" of remembrance. In January 1989, demonstrations during Palach Week, suppressed by police, foreshadowed the regime's fall later that year.
