Wildebeest

Wildebeest, also called gnus, are antelopes of the genus Connochaetes and native to Eastern and Southern Africa. They belong to the family Bovidae, which includes true antelopes, cattle, goats, sheep, and other even-toed horned ungulates.

Species

There are two species: the black wildebeest (Connochaetes gnou) of southern Africa and the blue wildebeest (Connochaetes taurinus) of eastern and southern Africa. Both are large, heavily built animals with curved horns, shaggy manes and long tails.

Migration

Wildebeest are famous for their annual great migration in the Serengeti–Mara ecosystem, in which around 1.5 million animals move in a great circuit between Tanzania and Kenya in search of rain-fed grazing, crossing crocodile-infested rivers such as the Mara. It is one of the largest terrestrial mammal migrations on Earth.

Ecology

Wildebeest are keystone grazers: their grazing and dung shape grassland ecology and they form a major prey base for lions, hyenas and other large predators.

Name

"Wildebeest" derives from Dutch/Afrikaans words meaning "wild beast" or "wild cattle", while "gnu" comes from a Khoikhoi name for the animal.
