Right of way

Right of way may refer to several related legal and traffic concepts:

A public right of way allows passage across land owned by another.

Traffic

In traffic law, the right of way is the priority given to one road user over another at intersections, crossings and merging points. Rules typically require vehicles to yield to pedestrians on crossings, to traffic already in an intersection, and to emergency vehicles; in aviation and maritime settings, right-of-way rules determine which vessel or aircraft must give way to avoid collision.

Public access over land

In land law, a right of way is a form of easement permitting passage over another person's land. In England and Wales, public rights of way include public footpaths, bridleways and byways, recorded on definitive maps. In Scotland, rights of way and the statutory right of responsible access under the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 provide extensive public access to land. Railways, canals, pipelines and utility corridors are also commonly described as occupying a "right-of-way" strip of land.
