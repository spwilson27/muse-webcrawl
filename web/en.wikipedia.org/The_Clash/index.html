The Clash were an English rock band formed in London in 1976. Dubbed "the only band that matters", they were a key participant in the original wave of British punk rock, and went on to incorporate reggae, dub, funk, ska and rockabilly — a breadth that made them one of the most acclaimed bands of their era.

History

Guitarist Mick Jones, bassist Paul Simonon and singer-guitarist Joe Strummer formed the band after the Sex Pistols inspired Strummer to leave his pub-rock group the 101ers. Their self-titled debut (1977), recorded quickly and cheaply, channelled fury at unemployment, racism and police harassment in songs such as "White Riot" and "Career Opportunities".

London Calling and after

The double album London Calling (1979) ranged across rockabilly, reggae, R&B and jazz, and was later voted the greatest album of the 1980s by Rolling Stone and one of the greatest of all time. The triple album Sandinista! (1980) followed, then the commercially successful Combat Rock (1982), with "Rock the Casbah" and "Should I Stay or Should I Go". Internal friction led to drummer Topper Headon's dismissal in 1982 and Jones's sacking in 1983; a reshuffled line-up made the poorly received Cut the Crap (1985) before Strummer dissolved the band in 1986.

Legacy

The Clash's explicitly political songwriting — anti-racist, anti-imperialist and pro-working-class — and their musical restlessness influenced generations of punk, alternative rock and politically engaged artists worldwide. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2003, months after Strummer's sudden death in December 2002.
