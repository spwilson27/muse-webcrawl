Chamber of Peers (France)

The Chamber of Peers (French: Chambre des pairs) was the upper house of the French parliament under the Bourbon Restoration (1814–1830) and the July Monarchy (1830–1848). Modelled in part on the British House of Lords, it sat in the Luxembourg Palace in Paris.


The Chamber of Peers, Luxembourg Palace

History

The Charter of 1814 created the chamber as a hereditary body appointed by the king; its members tried Marshal Ney for treason in 1815. After the July Revolution of 1830, the Charter of 1830 preserved the chamber but the law of December 1831 abolished hereditary membership, making peerages life appointments. The chamber acted as a high court for political offences and as a revising legislative body alongside the elected Chamber of Deputies.

Abolition and legacy

The Revolution of 1848 abolished the Chamber of Peers; the Second Republic adopted a unicameral assembly. Upper houses returned under later regimes as the Senate, which today occupies the same palace and chamber.
