First Zionist Congress

First Zionist Congress
Dates: 29–31 August 1897
Location: Basel, Switzerland
Organiser: Theodor Herzl
Participants: about 200 delegates
Outcome: Adoption of the Basel Program; founding of the World Zionist Organization
The First Zionist Congress in Basel, 1897.

The First Zionist Congress was the inaugural congress of the World Zionist Organization, held in Basel, Switzerland, from 29 to 31 August 1897. It was organised and chaired by Theodor Herzl, the founder of modern political Zionism, and brought together some 200 delegates from across Europe and beyond.

Background

Herzl, a Viennese journalist galvanised by rising antisemitism and the Dreyfus affair, had published his pamphlet Der Judenstaat ("The Jewish State") in 1896, arguing that the Jewish question required a political solution: a Jewish state secured by public law. The Congress was originally planned for Munich but was moved to Basel after opposition from local Jewish leaders.

The Basel Program

The Congress adopted the Basel Program, which declared: "Zionism seeks to establish a home for the Jewish people in Palestine secured under public law." It resolved to promote the settlement of Palestine by Jewish agriculturalists, artisans, and tradesmen; to strengthen Jewish national sentiment; and to seek the consent of governments. The Congress also founded the Zionist Organization (later the World Zionist Organization), elected Herzl its president, and established institutions including a newspaper, Die Welt. Herzl wrote in his diary that at Basel he had "founded the Jewish State" — a goal realised with the establishment of Israel in 1948. The Congress met annually in its early years and thereafter at intervals; the World Zionist Congress continues to convene today.
