Fort Vaux

Fort Vaux
Location: Near Vaux-devant-Damloup, Meuse, France
Built: 1881–1884 (modernized 1888–1892)
Part of: Ring of forts around Verdun
Key battle: Battle of Verdun (1916)

Fort Vaux, located near the commune of Vaux-devant-Damloup in northeastern France, was the second fort to fall during the Battle of Verdun in World War I. It defended the town of Verdun as part of the Séré de Rivières system of fortifications.

Construction

Built between 1881 and 1884 from masonry and later reinforced with concrete, the small fort was armed with a 75 mm gun turret and machine-gun turrets, garrisoned by roughly 150 men.

Siege of 1916

From March to June 1916, German forces subjected the fort to bombardment and infantry assaults. The garrison under Commandant Sylvain-Eugène Raynal held the flooded, airless galleries until water ran out; Raynal's last message, sent by carrier pigeon, became famous. The fort surrendered on 7 June 1916 and was recaptured by French troops on 2 November 1916.

Aftermath

Fort Vaux became a symbol of French endurance; it is preserved today as a memorial museum.
