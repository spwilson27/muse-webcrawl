Marc Lowell Andreessen (born July 9, 1971) is an American entrepreneur, venture capital investor and software engineer. He is the co-author of Mosaic, the first widely used web browser, and co-founder of Netscape Communications, whose 1995 initial public offering helped ignite the dot-com boom. In 2009 he co-founded the venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) with Ben Horowitz, which became one of Silicon Valley's most influential investors.

Mosaic and Netscape

While an undergraduate working at the National Center for Supercomputing Applications (NCSA) at the University of Illinois, Andreessen and Eric Bina developed Mosaic in 1993, a browser whose graphical interface and easy installation brought the World Wide Web to a mass audience. After graduating, Andreessen moved to California and in 1994 co-founded Mosaic Communications, renamed Netscape Communications, with Jim Clark. Netscape Navigator quickly dominated the browser market; the company's August 1995 IPO is often treated as the starting gun of the dot-com era. In the "browser wars" Microsoft bundled Internet Explorer with Windows, Netscape's share collapsed, and the company was acquired by AOL in 1999 for about $4.2 billion in stock.

Opsware and Andreessen Horowitz

Andreessen next co-founded Loudcloud, an early cloud services company that became Opsware and was sold to Hewlett-Packard for about $1.6 billion in 2007. He also co-founded the social network platform Ning. In 2009 he and Ben Horowitz launched Andreessen Horowitz, which raised capital rapidly and invested early in companies including Facebook, Twitter, Airbnb, GitHub, Lyft, Pinterest and Coinbase. Its model — offering portfolio companies extensive recruiting, marketing and policy support — reshaped venture capital practice.

Views and influence

Andreessen is a prominent public advocate of technology and growth, known for his 2011 essay "Why Software Is Eating the World", his 2020 essay "It's Time to Build", and his 2023 "Techno-Optimist Manifesto", which argues for accelerating technological progress and against regulatory restraint. He has served on the boards of companies including Facebook (Meta) and Hewlett Packard Enterprise, and has been an adviser on technology policy in American politics.
