County Dublin (Irish: Contae Bhaile Átha Cliath) is a county in Ireland, in the province of Leinster, containing the city of Dublin, the national capital and largest city. Despite being one of Ireland's smallest counties by area, it is by far the most populous, home to roughly 1.4 million people — around 28 percent of the state's population.

Geography

The county stretches along the Irish Sea coast from Balbriggan in the north to Bray's outskirts in the south, and rises in the south to the Dublin Mountains, part of the Wicklow range. The River Liffey divides the city into the Northside and Southside, and other rivers include the Tolka and the Dodder.

Administration

Since 1994 the county has been divided for local government into four areas: Dublin City, South Dublin, Dún Laoghaire–Rathdown, and Fingal. The historic county survives for postal, sporting, and cultural purposes, and Dublin teams ("the Dubs") compete in Gaelic games as a single county.

History

Dublin began as a Viking settlement in the ninth century and grew into Ireland's principal city under Norman and later British rule. The county around it, formalised in the thirteenth century as one of Ireland's earliest counties, became the core of "the Pale" — the area of direct English control.
