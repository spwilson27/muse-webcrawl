Mezcal

Mezcal is a distilled alcoholic beverage made from any type of agave plant. Traditionally produced in Mexico — especially Oaxaca, which accounts for the great majority of production — mezcal is protected by a denomination of origin covering nine states. Unlike tequila, which may be made only from blue Weber agave in a smaller designated region, mezcal may use dozens of agave varieties.

Production

The hearts (piñas) of harvested agave are cooked in earthen pits lined with hot stones, giving mezcal its characteristic smoky flavour. The cooked agave is crushed (often with a stone wheel, or tahona, pulled by a horse), fermented in open vats, and distilled, traditionally in clay or copper pots.

Classes and categories

Mexican regulations recognise categories by production method — mezcal, mezcal artesanal, and mezcal ancestral — and classes by ageing: blanco/joven (unaged), reposado (rested), añejo (aged), and others such as madurado en vidrio (matured in glass). The famous "worm" (gusano) found in some bottles is the larva of a moth that infests agave, added mostly as a marketing practice beginning in the 1950s.
