Basal body temperature


A sample basal body temperature chart across a menstrual cycle.

Basal body temperature (BBT or BTP) is the lowest body temperature attained during rest (usually during sleep). It is usually estimated by a temperature measurement immediately after awakening and before any physical activity has been undertaken. In women, ovulation causes an increase of one-half to one degree Fahrenheit (about 0.3 °C) in basal body temperature; monitoring of BBTs is one way of estimating the day of ovulation.

Use in fertility awareness

The shift in temperature follows ovulation, when rising progesterone increases the body's resting temperature. Charting BBT over a cycle produces a biphasic pattern: lower temperatures in the follicular phase and higher temperatures in the luteal phase. Fertility awareness methods combine BBT with observations of cervical mucus and cycle history to identify fertile and infertile days, for either achieving or avoiding pregnancy.

Measurement

Accurate charting requires a sensitive basal thermometer, measurement at a consistent time each morning before rising, and several hours of prior sleep. Illness, alcohol, disrupted sleep, and travel can distort readings. Because the temperature rise occurs after ovulation, BBT confirms ovulation retrospectively and is less useful alone for predicting it in advance.
