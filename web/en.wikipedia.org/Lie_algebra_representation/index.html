Lie algebra representation


A representation of a Lie algebra 𝔤 on a vector space V is a Lie algebra homomorphism from 𝔤 to the Lie algebra of endomorphisms of V.

In the mathematical field of representation theory, a Lie algebra representation is a way of writing a Lie algebra as a set of matrices such that the Lie bracket is given by the commutator. Precisely, a representation of a Lie algebra 𝔤 on a vector space V is a Lie algebra homomorphism ρ: 𝔤 → 𝔤𝔩(V), where 𝔤𝔩(V) is the Lie algebra of linear maps on V.

Basic concepts

A subspace W of V invariant under all ρ(x) is a subrepresentation; a representation with no nontrivial subrepresentations is irreducible, and one that decomposes into a direct sum of irreducibles is completely reducible. By Weyl's theorem, every finite-dimensional representation of a semisimple Lie algebra is completely reducible.

Adjoint representation

The adjoint representation is the action of 𝔤 on itself by the bracket: ad(x)(y) = [x, y]. Finite-dimensional representations of semisimple Lie algebras are classified by their highest weights relative to a Cartan subalgebra, a cornerstone of the theory developed by Élie Cartan and Hermann Weyl.
