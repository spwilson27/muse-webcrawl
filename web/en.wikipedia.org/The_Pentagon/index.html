The Pentagon is the headquarters building of the United States Department of Defense, in Arlington County, Virginia, across the Potomac River from Washington, D.C. As a symbol of the U.S. military, "the Pentagon" is often used metonymically for the Department of Defense and its leadership. The building, a massive five-sided concrete structure, is among the world's largest office buildings, with about 6.5 million square feet (600,000 m²) of floor space, of which 3.7 million sq ft are used as offices. Approximately 23,000 military and civilian employees work there.

Design and construction

The building was designed by American architect George Bergstrom and built by contractor John McShain. Ground was broken on 11 September 1941, and the building was dedicated on 15 January 1943 — a strikingly fast construction undertaken to consolidate the War Department's scattered offices during World War II. The five-sided shape arose from the road layout of the original site and was retained when the site moved. It has five floors above ground, two basement levels, five ring corridors, and 17.5 miles (28.2 km) of corridors; despite its size, any two points can be reached on foot in about seven minutes.

September 11 attacks

On 11 September 2001, exactly 60 years after ground was broken, American Airlines Flight 77 was hijacked and flown into the building's western facade, killing 184 people including the 59 victims aboard the aircraft and 125 in the building. The damaged section was rebuilt within a year under the Phoenix Project.

Renovation

The Pentagon Renovation Program (PENREN), a sweeping modernisation including asbestos removal and systems replacement, ran from 1994 to 2011. The building is a National Historic Landmark.
