Northern Territory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Northern Territory (abbreviated as NT) is an Australian territory in the central and central northern regions of Australia. It shares borders with Western Australia to the west, South Australia to the south, and Queensland to the east. To the north, the territory looks out on the Timor Sea, the Arafura Sea, and the Gulf of Carpentaria. Despite its large area — over 1.4 million square kilometres, making it the third-largest Australian federal division — it is sparsely populated, with a population of roughly a quarter of a million people.

The capital and largest city is Darwin. Other significant settlements include Alice Springs, near the territory's geographical centre, Katherine, Nhulunbuy, and Tennant Creek.

History

Aboriginal Australians have lived in the region for at least 60,000 years, and the territory today has the highest proportion of Aboriginal residents of any Australian state or territory. The coast was charted by Dutch explorers in the 17th century, and British attempts at settlement in the 1820s–1840s failed before the port of Darwin was founded in 1869.

The territory was administered by South Australia from 1863 until 1911, when it was transferred to the Commonwealth. The Overland Telegraph Line, completed in 1872, linked Darwin with Adelaide and Australia with the rest of the world. During the Second World War, Darwin was bombed by Japanese aircraft in 1942. The Northern Territory was granted self-government in 1978, though its powers remain more limited than those of the Australian states. A referendum on full statehood in 1998 was narrowly defeated.

Geography and climate

The territory spans several climate zones: the monsoonal tropical "Top End" in the north, with distinct wet and dry seasons, and the arid red deserts of the centre, home to the MacDonnell Ranges and the iconic monolith Uluru within Uluṙu-Kata Tjuṯa National Park. Kakadu National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, protects vast wetlands and Aboriginal rock art galleries of great antiquity.

Economy

The economy is driven by mining (including bauxite, gold, uranium, and natural gas), tourism, cattle pastoralism, and government services. The territory is a major destination for visitors drawn to Kakadu, Litchfield National Park, Kings Canyon, and Uluru.
