Benjamin Franklin Parkway

The Benjamin Franklin Parkway is a scenic boulevard in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, running diagonally for about one mile from Philadelphia City Hall at Logan Square to the Philadelphia Museum of Art at the edge of Fairmount Park. Modelled on the Champs-Élysées in Paris, it is lined with many of the city's cultural institutions — the Free Library of Philadelphia, the Franklin Institute, the Academy of Natural Sciences, the Rodin Museum, and the Barnes Foundation — and with flags of countries around the world. It is named for Benjamin Franklin, the city's most famous citizen.

History

A diagonal parkway cutting through William Penn's grid was proposed as early as 1907 by the Fairmount Park Art Association. Construction began in 1917 to designs developed by Paul Philippe Cret and the French landscape architect Jacques Gréber, who gave it its Parisian form with broad tree-lined lanes. Cutting through the existing urban fabric displaced a working-class neighbourhood and churches. The boulevard was completed in stages; the Philadelphia Museum of Art (opened 1928) crowns its north-western end, its steps later made famous by the film Rocky.

Events

The Parkway is Philadelphia's ceremonial stage: it hosted Pope John Paul II's 1979 Mass and Pope Francis's closing Mass of the 2015 World Meeting of Families, the Live 8 concert (2005), the annual Made in America festival (from 2012), and the victory parade for the 2017 NFL champion Philadelphia Eagles, when the steps hosted Jason Kelce's celebrated speech. The Fourth of July "Wawa Welcome America" celebrations centre on the boulevard and Eakins Oval.
