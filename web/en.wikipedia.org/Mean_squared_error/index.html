Mean squared error

Mean squared error
DefinitionAverage of the squares of the errors
Related measuresRoot-mean-square error (RMSE), bias–variance decomposition
FieldStatistics, estimation theory

In statistics, the mean squared error (MSE) of an estimator measures the average of the squares of the errors — the average squared difference between the estimated values and the actual value. MSE is a risk function corresponding to the expected value of squared error loss.

Definition

For an estimator θ̂ of a parameter θ, the MSE is E[(θ̂ − θ)²] = Var(θ̂) + Bias(θ̂, θ)². Thus MSE decomposes into the variance of the estimator plus the square of its bias: an estimator can have small MSE by being accurate, precise, or both. For a sample of predictions, the empirical MSE is the mean of the squared residuals, and its square root is the root-mean-square error (RMSE), which has the same units as the quantity being estimated.

Uses

MSE is the standard criterion for comparing estimators and for fitting regression models; least-squares estimation minimizes MSE among a class of estimators under stated conditions. Because squaring penalizes large errors disproportionately, MSE is sensitive to outliers, and alternatives such as mean absolute error are used where robustness matters.

Criticism

Statisticians including Dennis Lindley have criticized MSE as an arbitrary choice of loss function: nothing in logic requires error to be squared, and the appropriate loss depends on the practical cost of mistakes in the application at hand.
