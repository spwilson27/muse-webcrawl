Richard Hugh McCaw (born 31 December 1980) is a retired New Zealand rugby union player, widely regarded as one of the greatest players and captains in the sport's history. He played as an openside flanker for Canterbury, the Crusaders, and the New Zealand national team, the All Blacks.

Early life

McCaw was born in Oamaru in the Waitaki District of the South Island and grew up on his family's farm near Kurow. He attended Otago Boys' High School in Dunedin, where he was head boy and dux, before studying agricultural science at Lincoln University. He was spotted by Crusaders coach Robbie Deans and made his provincial debut for Canterbury in 2000.

Career

McCaw made his All Blacks debut against Ireland in Dublin in November 2001, earning man of the match. He became captain in 2006, succeeding Tana Umaga, and led New Zealand through a period of sustained dominance of world rugby.

He captained the All Blacks to victory at the 2011 Rugby World Cup in New Zealand, playing through the tournament with a broken bone in his foot, and again at the 2015 Rugby World Cup in England, becoming the first captain to lift the Webb Ellis Cup twice.

Records and legacy

McCaw retired in November 2015 with 148 test caps, then a world record, including 110 as captain and 131 test victories — the most by any player. He was named World Rugby Player of the Year three times (2006, 2009, 2010), a record he shares with Dan Carter. In 2016 he was appointed a Member of the Order of New Zealand, the country's highest honour. After rugby he qualified as a commercial helicopter pilot.
