Estonia

Estonia, officially the Republic of Estonia (Estonian: Eesti Vabariik), is a country in Northern Europe. It is bordered to the north by the Gulf of Finland across from Finland, to the west by the Baltic Sea across from Sweden, to the south by Latvia, and to the east by Lake Peipus and Russia. The territory of Estonia consists of the mainland, the larger islands of Saaremaa and Hiiumaa, and over 2,300 other islands and islets on the eastern coast of the Baltic Sea, covering a total area of 45,339 km² (17,505 sq mi). The capital city Tallinn and Tartu are the two largest urban areas of the country. The Estonian language is the official language and the first language of the majority of the population; it is closely related to Finnish.

History

Human settlement in the region dates back at least 11,000 years. The territory was Christianised during the Northern Crusades in the 13th century and subsequently ruled by Denmark, the Teutonic Order, Sweden, and the Russian Empire. A national awakening in the 19th century preceded independence, declared on 24 February 1918. Estonia was occupied by the Soviet Union in 1940, then by Nazi Germany, and again by the USSR until it restored independence in 1991 during the "Singing Revolution". Estonia joined the European Union and NATO in 2004 and adopted the euro in 2011.

Politics and economy

Estonia is a unitary parliamentary republic; the parliament is the Riigikogu, and the head of state is the President. The country is frequently cited as one of the most digitally advanced societies in the world, with extensive e-government services and the e-Residency programme. Its economy is a high-income market economy with strengths in information technology, telecommunications, and timber.

Culture

Estonian culture combines Nordic and Central European influences. The Song Festival (Laulupidu), held every five years in Tallinn since 1869, is inscribed on UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage. Estonia has the highest number of meteorite craters per unit area in the world, including the Kaali craters on Saaremaa.
