Bell Labs

Nokia Bell Labs, originally known as Bell Telephone Laboratories, is an American industrial research and development company owned by Finnish technology company Nokia. With headquarters in Murray Hill, New Jersey, the company operates several laboratories in the United States and around the world. As a former subsidiary of the American Telephone and Telegraph Company (AT&T), Bell Labs and its researchers have been credited with the development of radio astronomy, the transistor, the laser, the photovoltaic cell, the charge-coupled device (CCD), information theory, the Unix operating system, and the programming languages B, C, C++, S, SNOBOL, AWK, and AMPL, among many others. Eleven Nobel Prizes and five Turing Awards have been awarded for work completed at Bell Labs.

History

Bell Telephone Laboratories, Inc. was established in 1925 as the joint research arm of AT&T and Western Electric, consolidating earlier engineering departments. Its Murray Hill facility, completed in the 1940s, became the centre of postwar American industrial research. Among its landmark achievements were Claude Shannon's 1948 paper founding information theory; the transistor, demonstrated in 1947 by Bardeen, Brattain, and Shockley; the laser; the discovery of the cosmic microwave background by Penzias and Wilson in 1964; and Unix and C, created by Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie in the early 1970s.

Ownership changes

Following the 1984 breakup of the Bell System, Bell Labs remained with AT&T; in 1996 it passed to Lucent Technologies, which merged into Alcatel-Lucent in 2006. Nokia acquired Alcatel-Lucent in 2016 and renamed the organisation Nokia Bell Labs.

Research today

Current research focuses on telecommunications networks, 5G and 6G technologies, artificial intelligence, quantum computing, and fundamental physics. Although much smaller than in its mid-century heyday, Bell Labs remains one of the world's most decorated industrial research organisations.
