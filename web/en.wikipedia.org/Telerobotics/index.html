Telerobotics

Telerobotics is the area of robotics concerned with the control of semi-autonomous robots from a distance, chiefly using radio or networked connections. It combines elements of teleoperation—direct remote control by a human operator—and computer assistance, sometimes called supervisory control. Telerobotics differs from simple remote control in that the remote device typically performs some tasks autonomously while the operator directs higher-level behavior.

History

The field grew out of mechanical master–slave manipulators developed in the 1940s for handling radioactive materials, pioneered by Raymond Goertz at Argonne National Laboratory. Electric servo-driven arms, television feedback, and later computer interfaces and force feedback followed. Time delay in communication—critical in space applications—motivated the development of predictive displays and supervisory control, in which the operator issues goals rather than continuous commands.

Applications

Telerobotic systems are used in deep-sea exploration (remotely operated underwater vehicles), space exploration and satellite servicing, handling of hazardous materials, bomb disposal, mining, and medicine. In telesurgery, surgeons operate robotic instruments remotely, as in systems used for minimally invasive procedures; fully remote surgery over long distances has been demonstrated experimentally but is limited by latency and reliability requirements.

Challenges

Key challenges include communication delay and bandwidth limits, providing the operator with a sense of presence through video, haptic feedback, and spatial awareness, and ensuring safety and graceful behavior when the communications link degrades or is lost.
