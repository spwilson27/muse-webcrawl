Scarface (1983 film)

Scarface is a 1983 American crime drama film directed by Brian De Palma, written by Oliver Stone, and starring Al Pacino. It is a remake of the 1932 film of the same name, in turn based on Armitage Trail's 1930 novel. It tells the story of Cuban refugee Tony Montana (Pacino), who arrives penniless in Miami during the Mariel boatlift and becomes a powerful and ruthless drug lord.

Plot

In 1980, Cuban refugee and ex-convict Tony Montana is sent to a refugee camp in Miami with his friend Manny Ray. Released after assassinating a former Castro official for drug dealer Frank Lopez, the two work their way into Lopez's organization. Tony falls for Frank's mistress Elvira, murders Frank, and seizes his empire, marrying Elvira and building a cocaine business supplied by Bolivian kingpin Alejandro Sosa. Paranoia, addiction, and hubris destroy his marriage, his friendship with Manny—whom Tony murders after discovering Manny's secret marriage to his sister Gina—and finally Tony himself: refusing to kill a target's children for Sosa, he is besieged in his mansion and dies in a hail of bullets beneath a globe inscribed "The World Is Yours," collapsing into his fountain in the film's famous climax ("Say hello to my little friend!").

Production

Sidney Lumet originated the concept of recasting the story with Cuban cocaine traffickers before De Palma took over; Stone, drawing on research in Miami and his own experience, wrote the screenplay. The film was shot largely in Los Angeles after Miami officials resisted its portrayal of the city. Its extreme violence—including a notorious chainsaw scene—earned an X rating three times before edits secured an R.

Reception and legacy

Initial reviews were mixed to negative, criticizing the violence and length, and the film was a modest box-office success. It was reappraised over the following decades into one of the most celebrated gangster films ever made, exerting enormous influence on hip hop culture, fashion, and video games (including Grand Theft Auto: Vice City). In 2008 the American Film Institute ranked it among the ten greatest gangster films. Pacino's performance and lines such as "Say hello to my little friend!" became enduring elements of popular culture.
