Ástor Piazzolla


Piazzolla with his bandoneón.

Ástor Pantaleón Piazzolla (1921–1992) was an Argentine tango composer, bandoneon player and arranger who revolutionised the traditional tango into a new style termed nuevo tango, incorporating elements from jazz and classical music. He is considered the most important tango composer of the second half of the twentieth century.

Life

Born in Mar del Plata to Italian immigrant parents, Piazzolla grew up largely in New York City, where he learned the bandoneón and appeared as a child actor. Returning to Argentina, he played in Aníbal Troilo's famous orchestra and studied composition with Alberto Ginastera. A 1953 composition prize took him to Paris to study with Nadia Boulanger, who urged him to embrace the tango rather than suppress it.

Nuevo tango

Back in Buenos Aires he formed ensembles including the Octeto Buenos Aires and his celebrated quintet, writing works such as "Adiós Nonino" (for his father), "Libertango", "Oblivion" and the "Four Seasons of Buenos Aires". His innovations initially angered tango traditionalists, but his music achieved worldwide recognition in his lifetime, and he composed the opera María de Buenos Aires (1968) and concertos for bandoneón and orchestra.
