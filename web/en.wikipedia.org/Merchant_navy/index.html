Merchant navy

A merchant navy or merchant marine is the fleet of merchant vessels registered in a particular country and the civilian crews who operate them. Merchant ships carry cargo and passengers by water — bulk carriers, container ships, oil tankers, ferries, cruise ships and many specialised types — and trade under the flag of their country of registration, regulated internationally by conventions of the International Maritime Organization.

Organisation and ranks

Crews are organised into deck, engineering and catering departments under the master (captain). Officers hold certificates of competency issued under the STCW Convention; ratings perform most manual and watchkeeping duties. Seafarers of a few labour-supplying countries — notably the Philippines, India, China and Indonesia — crew much of the world's fleet.

Flags of convenience

Many vessels sail under "flags of convenience," registered in open registries such as Panama, Liberia and the Marshall Islands, which together account for a large share of world tonnage. The practice offers tax and regulatory advantages but has been criticised over labour and safety standards.

Wartime role

In wartime merchant fleets have been vital and vulnerable: in both world wars, merchant seamen suffered very heavy losses supplying their countries, and their service is commemorated in Britain on Merchant Navy Day (3 September) and in the United States on National Maritime Day (22 May). In the United Kingdom "Merchant Navy" is the official title, and its ensign is the Red Ensign.
