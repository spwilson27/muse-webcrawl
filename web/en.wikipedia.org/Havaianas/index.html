Havaianas

Havaianas is a Brazilian brand of flip-flop sandals created and patented in 1962. The brand is owned by the Brazilian manufacturing company Alpargatas S.A. The name Havaianas is Portuguese for "Hawaiians".

History

The first pair of Havaianas was inspired by the Japanese zori sandal, made with rice straw soles; the rubber sole of the Havaianas still bears a textured pattern imitating rice grains. Originally sold cheaply and worn mainly by Brazil's working classes, the sandals became a fashion item in the 1990s when the brand launched new colours and styles, including the practice of flipping the sole so the coloured side faced up.

Today Havaianas are sold in more than 100 countries and have become an internationally recognised symbol of Brazilian beach culture, with flagship stores and collaborations with fashion designers and entertainment brands.
