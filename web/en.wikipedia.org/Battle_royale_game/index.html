Battle royale game

A battle royale game is an online multiplayer video game genre that blends survival, exploration and scavenging elements with last-person-standing gameplay. Battle royale games involve dozens to hundreds of players who start with minimal equipment and must fight to be the last person or team alive, while a shrinking safe area forces players into closer proximity and increasing conflict.

Gameplay

Players are typically dropped from an aircraft onto a large map, often an island, with little or no equipment. They must scavenge for weapons, armour, vehicles and supplies while eliminating opponents. Over the course of a match, a safe zone shrinks — commonly depicted as a contracting circle, wall of energy or storm — and players outside it take escalating damage. The winner is the last player or squad remaining. Matches commonly support solo, duo and squad modes.

History

The genre takes its name from the 2000 Japanese film Battle Royale, itself adapted from Koushun Takami's 1999 novel, which depicted students forced to fight to the death on an island. Early examples emerged as mods for games such as Minecraft and ARMA 2, notably Brendan "PlayerUnknown" Greene's DayZ: Battle Royale mod. Greene went on to consult on H1Z1: King of the Kill and to direct PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds (PUBG), released in early access in 2017, which popularised the genre. Fortnite Battle Royale (2017) and Apex Legends (2019) followed, along with mobile titles such as PUBG Mobile and modes in series like Call of Duty: Warzone.

Reception and impact

Battle royale games became one of the most commercially successful genres of the late 2010s, generating billions of dollars and dominating streaming platforms. The format influenced game design broadly, spawning spin-offs in other genres, such as the puzzle game Tetris 99.
