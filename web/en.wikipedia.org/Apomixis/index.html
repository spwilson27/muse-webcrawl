Apomixis

Apomixis is a form of asexual reproduction in plants in which seeds are produced without fertilisation, so that the offspring are genetically identical clones of the mother plant. The term was introduced by Hans Winkler in 1908. Apomixis occurs naturally in hundreds of plant species, including dandelions, hawthorns, blackberries, many grasses, and some citrus.

Types

In gametophytic apomixis, an unreduced (diploid) embryo sac forms either from an unreduced megaspore (diplospory) or from a somatic cell of the ovule (apospory), and the embryo develops parthenogenetically from the unfertilised egg. In adventitious embryony, embryos arise directly from somatic cells of the ovule alongside or instead of a sexual embryo.

Significance

Because apomictic seeds fix a genotype, breeders have long sought to introduce apomixis into sexual crops such as maize and rice: it would allow hybrid vigour to be preserved from generation to generation without repeated crossing, potentially transforming seed production for farmers worldwide.
