Choline

Choline is an essential nutrient for humans and many other animals. Choline occurs as a cation that forms various salts (X− in the depicted formula is an undefined counteranion). It is a water-soluble compound, chemically trimethylglycine-like in structure as 2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethylethanaminium. Humans synthesize choline in small amounts in the liver, but dietary intake is needed for good health.

Biochemistry

Choline is a precursor of the neurotransmitter acetylcholine, of phosphatidylcholine and sphingomyelin in cell membranes, and of the methyl donor betaine. Its metabolite trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) has been studied in relation to cardiovascular disease.

Dietary sources and requirements

Rich sources include eggs, liver, meat, fish, soybeans and cruciferous vegetables. Adequate intake values are set by health authorities (around 425 mg/day for adult women and 550 mg/day for adult men in the United States). Deficiency can cause fatty liver and muscle damage.

History

Choline was discovered by Adolph Strecker in 1862 in pig bile and was recognised as an essential nutrient in 1998.
