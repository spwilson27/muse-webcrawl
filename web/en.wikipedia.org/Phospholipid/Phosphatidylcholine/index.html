Phosphatidylcholine (PC) is a class of phospholipids that incorporate choline as a headgroup. They are a major component of biological membranes and can be readily obtained from sources such as egg yolk or soybeans, from which they are mechanically or chemically extracted. Phosphatidylcholine is the principal ingredient of commercial lecithin.

Structure and function

A phosphatidylcholine molecule consists of a glycerol backbone esterified to two fatty acids and to a phosphocholine head group. Its cylindrical shape favours flat bilayer formation, and it is concentrated in the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane lipid bilayer. PC is also the main phospholipid of bile, of lipoproteins, and of pulmonary surfactant — where the fully saturated species dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine (DPPC) lowers surface tension in the alveoli.

Biosynthesis

Cells synthesize phosphatidylcholine mainly through the CDP-choline (Kennedy) pathway, which activates dietary choline; the liver can also convert phosphatidylethanolamine to PC via the PEMT pathway. Breakdown of PC yields signalling molecules and choline, an essential nutrient used to make the neurotransmitter acetylcholine.

Uses

Beyond its role as the basis of lecithin emulsifiers, phosphatidylcholine is used in liposomal drug delivery, in parenteral nutrition emulsions, and as a dietary supplement.
