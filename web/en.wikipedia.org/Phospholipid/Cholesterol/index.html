Cholesterol

Cholesterol wedged between phospholipid molecules in the cell membrane.

Cholesterol is a sterol lipid essential to animal cell membranes. Its partnership with the phospholipid bilayer is fundamental: cholesterol molecules insert among the phospholipid fatty-acid tails, their small hydroxyl heads aligned with the phospholipid head groups, and in doing so tune the membrane's fluidity, thickness, and permeability.

Role in the phospholipid bilayer

At high temperatures cholesterol restrains phospholipid movement, stiffening the membrane; at low temperatures it prevents the tails from packing and crystallizing, keeping the membrane fluid. It thus acts as a fluidity buffer across conditions. Cholesterol also fills gaps between phospholipids, reducing leakage of small molecules and ions, and forms ordered "lipid raft" domains with sphingolipids and certain membrane proteins involved in signalling.

Biochemistry

Cells synthesize cholesterol from acetyl-CoA via the mevalonate pathway, regulated by the enzyme HMG-CoA reductase — the target of statin drugs — and acquire it from lipoproteins such as LDL. Beyond membranes, cholesterol is the precursor of steroid hormones, bile acids, and vitamin D.

Health

Excess LDL cholesterol in blood promotes atherosclerotic plaque formation and cardiovascular disease, while membrane cholesterol content itself affects everything from viral entry to insulin-receptor function.
