Pelagic zone


The pelagic zone is divided into layers by depth.

The pelagic zone comprises the water column of the open ocean, away from the shore and the seabed. It is divided by depth into zones with distinct light, temperature, and pressure conditions, each supporting characteristic communities of life.

Zones by depth

The epipelagic (sunlight) zone extends to about 200 m and supports photosynthesis and most familiar marine life. Below it lie the mesopelagic (twilight) zone, 200–1,000 m, home to many bioluminescent animals; the bathypelagic (midnight) zone, 1,000–4,000 m; the abyssopelagic zone, 4,000–6,000 m; and the hadopelagic zone of the deep trenches. Overall the pelagic zone occupies some 1.3 billion cubic kilometres and is Earth's largest habitat by volume.

Life

Organisms of the pelagic zone are classed as plankton (drifters), nekton (strong swimmers such as fish, squid, and marine mammals), and neuston at the surface. Many mesopelagic animals undertake vast daily vertical migrations to feed near the surface at night.
