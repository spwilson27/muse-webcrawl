Mark E. Smith

Born5 March 1957, Broughton, Salford, England
Died24 January 2018, Prestwich, England
OccupationSinger, songwriter

Mark Edward Smith (5 March 1957 – 24 January 2018) was an English singer-songwriter. He was the lead singer, lyricist and only constant member of the post-punk group the Fall, which he led from 1976 until his death.

The Fall

Formed in Prestwich, Manchester, after Smith attended a Sex Pistols performance, The Fall released over thirty studio albums across four decades, with Smith famously hiring and firing dozens of musicians — the group's only continuity being his voice and vision. Albums such as Hex Enduction Hour (1982), This Nation's Saving Grace (1985) and The Frenz Experiment (1988) are landmarks of British post-punk.

Style

Smith's lyrics mixed surreal wordplay, social observation and literary references, delivered in a distinctive northern English spoken-sung drawl. DJ John Peel was a devoted champion, recording 24 Peel sessions with the band.

Other work and legacy

Smith recorded solo albums and collaborations, published the memoir Renegade (2008), and appeared occasionally as an actor. He is regarded as one of the most singular and influential figures in British independent music.
