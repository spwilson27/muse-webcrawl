Booker T. & the M.G.'s

Origin: Memphis, Tennessee, US

Years active: 1962–1971, 1973–1977, 1994–2012

Genres: Soul, R&B, instrumental rock

Label: Stax / Atlantic

Key members: Booker T. Jones, Steve Cropper, Donald "Duck" Dunn, Al Jackson Jr.

Booker T. & the M.G.'s were an American instrumental R&B and soul band formed in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1962. As the house band of Stax Records, they played on hundreds of recordings by artists including Otis Redding, Wilson Pickett, Sam & Dave, and Albert King, shaping the Memphis soul sound. In their own right they scored hits with the instrumentals "Green Onions" (1962), "Hip Hug-Her," "Soul Limbo," "Hang 'Em High," and "Time Is Tight."

The band

The group's name stands for the "Memphis Group." The classic lineup comprised organist Booker T. Jones, guitarist Steve Cropper, bassist Donald "Duck" Dunn (who replaced founding bassist Lewie Steinberg in 1965), and drummer Al Jackson Jr., one of the most influential drummers in soul music. The racially integrated quartet — two Black and two white musicians — was unusual for the segregated South of the 1960s.

Career

"Green Onions," improvised during a session break, reached number 3 on the Billboard Hot 100 and topped the R&B chart in 1962. The band backed Otis Redding at the Monterey Pop Festival in 1967 and recorded the ambitious album McLemore Avenue (1970), a track-by-track cover of the Beatles' Abbey Road. Jackson's murder in 1975 ended the band's classic era, though the remaining members reunited periodically, notably as the backing band for Bob Dylan's 30th anniversary concert and for Neil Young tours.

Legacy

Booker T. & the M.G.'s were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2007. Their spare, groove-centred style is a touchstone of soul, funk, and mod music.
