Bit

The bit is the most basic unit of information in computing and digital communications. The name is a contraction of binary digit. The bit represents a logical state with one of two possible values, most commonly represented as "1" or "0", but also as true/false, on/off, or +/−. The term was coined by John W. Tukey in 1947 and popularized by Claude Shannon in his 1948 paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication".

Physical representation

A bit can be stored by a digital device or physical system that exists in one of two possible distinct states. These may be the two stable states of a flip-flop, two positions of an electrical switch, two distinct voltage or current levels, two directions of magnetization or polarization, or the presence or absence of a pit on an optical disc.

Transmission and processing

Bits are transmitted one at a time in serial transmission and in groups in parallel transmission. A group of eight bits is called a byte, the standard unit for encoding characters in most computers. Data transfer rates are commonly measured in bits per second (bit/s) and its multiples such as kbit/s, Mbit/s, and Gbit/s.

Information theory

In information theory, one bit is the information entropy of a binary random variable that is 0 or 1 with equal probability, or the information gained when the outcome of such a variable is known. Shannon used the bit as the fundamental unit in quantifying information, forming the basis of modern digital communication.
