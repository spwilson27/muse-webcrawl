Irish Derby

Location: Curragh Racecourse, County Kildare

Inaugurated: 1866

Race type: Flat / Thoroughbred

Distance: 1 mile 4 furlongs (2,414 m)

Surface: Turf

The Irish Derby is a Group 1 flat horse race in Ireland open to three-year-old thoroughbred colts and fillies. It is run at the Curragh over a distance of 1 mile and 4 furlongs (2,414 metres), and it is scheduled to take place each year in late June or early July. It is Ireland's equivalent of the Epsom Derby, and it is currently held three weeks after the English race.

History

The earliest version of the Irish Derby was an event called the O'Darby Stakes, established in 1817. The modern race was first run in 1866, but for much of its early history it was overshadowed by the Irish Oaks and carried modest prize money. Its prestige grew after the Second World War, and in the 1960s an international syndicate's sponsorship made it one of Europe's richest races, attracting Epsom Derby winners to attempt the double. The race is now sponsored by Dubai Duty Free.

Records

The most successful trainer is Aidan O'Brien, who has won the race more than a dozen times with horses including Galileo (2001), High Chaparral (2002), Dylan Thomas (2006), Camelot (2012) and Auguste Rodin (2023). Notable winners include Orby (1907), the first Epsom Derby winner trained in Ireland, Nijinsky (1970), Shergar (1981), and Montjeu (1999).
