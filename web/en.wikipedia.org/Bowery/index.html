Bowery

The Bowery is a street and neighborhood in Lower Manhattan, New York City. The street runs from Chatham Square in the south to Cooper Square in the north, forming the western border of the Lower East Side and the eastern border of Nolita and NoHo.

The name derives from the Dutch word bouwerij, meaning farm, recalling the farms of New Amsterdam in the 17th century, including that of Director-General Peter Stuyvesant. In the 19th century the Bowery became known for theaters, beer gardens, and later for flophouses, skid row, and cheap lodging houses serving homeless men.

By the late 20th century the area was associated with punk rock — notably the club CBGB — and with restaurant supply and lighting stores. Since the 2000s it has undergone rapid gentrification, with luxury hotels, condominiums, and cultural institutions such as the New Museum.
