Lubusz Voivodeship (Polish: województwo lubuskie) is a province (voivodeship) in western Poland, on the border with Germany. It was created on 1 January 1999 out of the former Gorzów and Zielona Góra voivodeships under Poland's local government reforms. The province is named after the historical Lubusz Land (Lebus) on both banks of the middle Oder River. Uniquely, it has two capitals: the voivode (the central government's representative) sits in Gorzów Wielkopolski, while the elected regional assembly and marshal's office sit in Zielona Góra.

Geography

Lubusz is the most forested voivodeship in Poland — forests cover about half its area — and is dotted with several hundred lakes left by the last glaciation, particularly in the Łagów Lake District. Major rivers are the Oder (Odra), forming part of the German border, the Warta, the Noteć, the Bóbr and the Lusatian Neisse. In the south lies part of the historic region of Lower Silesia and the town of Żagań; the northern part borders Greater Poland. The region includes Drawa National Park and part of Warta Mouth National Park, an important wetland bird sanctuary.

History

The Lubusz Land was contested in the Middle Ages between the Polish Piast monarchy, the Holy Roman Empire and the Margraviate of Brandenburg, which absorbed it as part of the Neumark ("New March"). The southern part around Zielona Góra belonged to the Silesian duchies. After 1945 the Potsdam Agreement placed the area east of the Oder–Neisse line under Polish administration; its German population was expelled and replaced by Poles, including many resettled from Poland's former eastern territories annexed by the Soviet Union.

Cities and economy

The largest cities are Gorzów Wielkopolski (about 120,000 inhabitants) and Zielona Góra (about 140,000), the latter known for its wine festival (Winobranie) held each September — a legacy of centuries of viticulture around the city. Other towns include Nowa Sól, Żary, Żagań, Świebodzin (known for its monumental Christ the King statue, once the world's tallest statue of Jesus) and Kostrzyn nad Odrą, host of the large Pol'and'Rock Festival. The economy rests on manufacturing, wood processing, agriculture, cross-border trade and logistics; the A2 and A18 motorways and the S3 expressway cross the province, linking Berlin with Poznań and Szczecin with Wrocław.
