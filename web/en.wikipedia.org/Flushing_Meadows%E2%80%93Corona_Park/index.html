Flushing Meadows–Corona Park

Flushing Meadows–Corona Park is a 897-acre (363 ha) public park in northern Queens, New York City. It is the fourth-largest park in the city and was created as the site of the 1939–40 and 1964–65 New York World's Fairs.

History

The site was once a tidal marsh and ash dump—famously described in The Great Gatsby as a "valley of ashes"—before Robert Moses reclaimed it for the 1939 World's Fair. It hosted the fairs of 1939–40 and 1964–65, leaving landmarks including the Unisphere, the New York State Pavilion, and the Queens Museum building, which briefly housed the United Nations General Assembly (1946–1950).

Attractions

The park contains the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center (home of the US Open), Citi Field, the Queens Museum, the New York Hall of Science, the Queens Zoo, and the Queens Botanical Garden nearby, as well as Meadow and Willow Lakes.
