Limestone


Limestone is a carbonate sedimentary rock composed mostly of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃), usually in the form of calcite or aragonite.

Limestone is a type of carbonate sedimentary rock which is the main source of the material lime. It is composed mostly of the minerals calcite and aragonite, which are different crystal forms of calcium carbonate (CaCO3), and comprises about 20–25% of all sedimentary rock.

Formation

Most limestone forms in shallow marine environments from the accumulation of shells, coral, algae and other biological debris, or by direct precipitation from seawater. Weathering of limestone landscapes produces karst features such as caves, sinkholes and limestone pavements.

Uses

Limestone is a major building material — the Great Pyramid of Giza was cased in it — and the principal raw material for Portland cement and quicklime. It is also used as a soil conditioner and as flux in steelmaking.
