Marina Abramović

Abramović, pioneer of performance art, during The Artist Is Present (2010).

Marina Abramović (Serbian Cyrillic: Марина Абрамовић; born 30 November 1946) is a Serbian conceptual and performance artist. Her work explores body art, endurance art, the relationship between performer and audience, the limits of the body and the possibilities of the mind. Active for more than five decades, she was a leading figure of the 1970s European performance art scene and famously described herself as the "grandmother of performance art". Her 2010 retrospective at the Museum of Modern Art in New York, featuring the durational piece The Artist Is Present, brought performance art to an unprecedented mass audience.

Early work

Abramović was born in Belgrade to Partisan parents and studied painting at the Academy of Fine Arts there before turning to performance. In the Rhythm series of 1973–74 she tested the body's limits and transferred control to the audience: in Rhythm 0 (Naples, 1974) she stood passively for six hours beside 72 objects — including a rose, honey, scissors and a loaded gun — inviting the public to use them on her as they wished, an experiment that escalated to violence and remains a landmark of performance art.

Work with Ulay

From 1976 to 1988 Abramović collaborated with the German artist Ulay (Frank Uwe Laysiepen), her partner in art and life. Their joint performances explored duality and endurance, including Rest Energy (1980), in which they balanced a drawn bow and arrow aimed at her heart. The collaboration ended with The Lovers (1988): each walked from opposite ends of the Great Wall of China to meet and part in the middle. They reunited publicly, briefly and famously, when Ulay sat opposite her during The Artist Is Present in 2010.

Later career

Abramović's Balkan Baroque (1997), a response to the Yugoslav wars in which she scrubbed bloodied cow bones for days, won the Golden Lion at the Venice Biennale. Her MoMA retrospective (2010) included re-performances of her early works by younger artists and the new piece The Artist Is Present, in which she sat silently opposite museum visitors for a total of 736 hours. She founded the Marina Abramović Institute to support long-duration performance, has taught internationally, and in 2023 became the first woman artist to have a solo exhibition in the main galleries of London's Royal Academy. Her memoir Walk Through Walls appeared in 2016.
