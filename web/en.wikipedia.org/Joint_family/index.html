Joint family

Joint family
TypeExtended family household
Common regionsSouth Asia; historically widespread
FeaturesShared residence, kitchen, property and worship

A joint family is an extended family arrangement in which several generations — typically a couple, their married sons, daughters-in-law and grandchildren — live together in one household, sharing property, income, kitchen and rituals. The institution is most associated with India and other parts of South Asia.

Structure

In the traditional Hindu joint family, membership arises by birth in the family, and property is held jointly under the authority of the eldest male, the karta. Members pool earnings, eat food cooked in a common kitchen, and observe festivals and rites together. Comparable multi-generational households have existed in China, Japan, the Balkans (the zadruga) and elsewhere.

Legal aspects

In Indian law, the Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) is recognized as a distinct entity for taxation, and succession and partition of joint family property are governed by codified statutes such as the Hindu Succession Act 1956, as amended in 2005 to give daughters equal coparcenary rights.

Change

Urbanization, migration and changing aspirations have increased nuclear households in South Asia, yet joint living persists in adapted forms, valued for childcare, elder care and economic pooling.
