Thrace (Greek: Thrákē; Turkish: Trakya; Bulgarian: Trakia) is a geographical and historical region in Southeast Europe, bounded by the Balkan Mountains to the north, the Rhodope Mountains and the Aegean Sea to the west and south, and the Black Sea and Sea of Marmara to the east. Today it is divided between Bulgaria (Northern Thrace), Greece (Western Thrace) and Turkey (Eastern Thrace, the European part of Turkey including most of Istanbul).

History

In antiquity the region was inhabited by the Thracians, a group of Indo-European tribes noted as warriors and horsemen, whose Odrysian kingdom flourished in the fifth century BC. Thrace came under Macedonian, then Roman rule — the Roman province of Thracia was organised in AD 46 — and later formed part of the Byzantine and Ottoman empires. The gold treasures of Panagyurishte and the tomb paintings of Kazanlak testify to the sophistication of Thracian culture. Greek mythology placed figures such as Orpheus and Dionysus in Thrace, and the gladiator Spartacus was reportedly Thracian.

The region's modern borders were fixed by the Treaties of Neuilly (1919) and Lausanne (1923) after the Balkan Wars and the First World War.
