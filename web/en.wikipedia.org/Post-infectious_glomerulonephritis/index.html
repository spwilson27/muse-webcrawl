Post-infectious glomerulonephritis

Post-infectious glomerulonephritis follows infection, classically with group A streptococci.

Post-infectious glomerulonephritis is a disorder of the glomeruli of the kidney in which inflammation follows an infection elsewhere in the body. The classic form, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, occurs after infection of the throat or skin with certain "nephritogenic" strains of group A Streptococcus pyogenes. It is an immunologically mediated disease in which immune complexes deposit in the glomeruli, activating complement.

Signs and symptoms

The condition typically appears one to two weeks after a streptococcal sore throat, or two to six weeks after a skin infection such as impetigo. Features include haematuria (often described as smoky or cola-coloured urine), proteinuria, oedema (especially periorbital), hypertension, and reduced urine output. It most commonly affects children between about 2 and 12 years of age.

Diagnosis and course

Laboratory findings include a low serum complement C3 (which typically normalises within about eight weeks), evidence of recent streptococcal infection (raised antistreptolysin O or anti-DNase B titres), and impaired kidney function. Renal biopsy, when performed, shows enlarged hypercellular glomeruli with neutrophils and subepithelial "humps" of immune deposits on electron microscopy.

Management is mainly supportive: salt and fluid restriction, diuretics, and blood-pressure control; antibiotics are given to eradicate residual streptococcal infection but do not alter the renal course. The prognosis in children is excellent, with the great majority recovering completely, though adults and patients with other infections (such as staphylococcal infection-related glomerulonephritis) may fare worse.
