Yue Minjun

Yue Minjun
Born: 1962, Daqing, Heilongjiang, China
Movement: Cynical Realism, Political Pop
Known for: Paintings and sculptures of laughing self-portraits

Yue Minjun (born 1962) is a contemporary Chinese artist based in Beijing, internationally famous for oil paintings depicting his own face frozen in an exaggerated, toothy laugh.

Life and work

Yue worked in an oil field and painted in his spare time before settling in Beijing's artist villages in the early 1990s, including Yuanmingyuan and later Songzhuang. After 1989 he developed his signature motif: pink-skinned, grinning clones of himself in absurd or ominous scenes — laughing before firing squads in Execution (1995), re-staging Manet, or massed in contemporary terracotta-army sculptures (Contemporary Terracotta Warriors).

Cynical Realism and the market

Critics Li Xianting grouped Yue with the "Cynical Realism" painters who answered post-Tiananmen disillusion with irony and self-mockery; his laugh has been read as both survival mask and critique. During the 2000s Chinese-art boom Yue became one of the world's best-selling living artists at auction: Execution sold at Sotheby's London in 2007 for about £2.9 million, then a record for Chinese contemporary art, and Gweong-Gweong and other works fetched multi-million prices in Hong Kong. Prices cooled after 2008, prompting debate about speculation in the market for Chinese contemporary art.

His works are held by major museums and remain emblematic images of China's reform-era art.
