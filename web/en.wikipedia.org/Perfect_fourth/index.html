In music, a perfect fourth is a musical interval corresponding to a pair of pitches with a frequency ratio of 4:3, or, in twelve-tone equal temperament, five semitones. It is the fourth note of a major scale spanning five semitones. The perfect fourth is considered a consonance in most contexts, though in two-part counterpoint and in chord construction it can be treated as a dissonance requiring resolution.

History and use

In ancient Greek music theory, the fourth (diatessaron) was one of the fundamental consonances, and the tetrachord was built upon it. Perfect fourths occur at the opening of many well-known melodies, including "Amazing Grace" and the Bridal Chorus from Wagner's Lohengrin. In jazz and rock, quartal harmony is built by stacking fourths, and power chords are often played with an added fourth.

Inverted interval

The inversion of a perfect fourth is a perfect fifth. Together, the fourth and fifth are the only intervals other than the octave that appear in the overtone series as simple ratios with small denominators (3:2 and 4:3), which explains their central role in the tuning of stringed instruments: violins, violas and cellos are tuned in fifths, while guitars are tuned largely in fourths.
