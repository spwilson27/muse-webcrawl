Tonic pupil

Tonic pupil
Also known asAdie's pupil (when part of Adie's syndrome)
Site of lesionCiliary ganglion / postganglionic parasympathetic fibres
Key signsDilated pupil, poor light reaction, slow tonic near response, denervation supersensitivity
Confirmatory testConstriction to dilute pilocarpine (0.1%)

A tonic pupil is a dilated pupil of one (occasionally both) eyes that reacts poorly or not at all to light but constricts slowly and tonically to a near stimulus, with delayed redilation. It results from damage to the ciliary ganglion or the short ciliary nerves carrying postganglionic parasympathetic fibres to the iris sphincter and ciliary muscle. When accompanied by diminished or absent deep tendon reflexes, the condition is called Adie's (Holmes–Adie) syndrome.

Signs and mechanism

Examination shows anisocoria with the abnormal pupil larger in bright light. The light reaction is absent or sluggish, but under slit-lamp examination segmental, vermiform movements of the iris may be seen, reflecting sectoral denervation and aberrant regeneration: fibres originally destined for the ciliary muscle (which greatly outnumber sphincter fibres) reinnervate the sphincter, producing the characteristic light–near dissociation. Accommodation is likewise slow and tonic. Over years the pupil often becomes smaller ("little old Adie's").

Causes

Most cases in young and middle-aged women are idiopathic (Adie's pupil). Other causes include orbital trauma or surgery, viral and other infections, diabetes and other autonomic neuropathies, and, rarely, syphilis — though the classic syphilitic pupil (Argyll Robertson) is small rather than dilated.

Diagnosis and management

Denervation supersensitivity provides a pharmacological test: dilute pilocarpine (0.1%) constricts a tonic pupil but has little effect on a normal pupil or one dilated by pharmacological blockade. The condition is usually benign; treatment is rarely needed, though reading glasses or weak pilocarpine may help accommodative symptoms, and tinted lenses can relieve glare.
