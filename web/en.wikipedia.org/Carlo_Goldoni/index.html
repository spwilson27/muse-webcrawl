Carlo Goldoni
Born: 25 February 1707, Venice, Republic of Venice
Died: 6 February 1793, Paris, France
Occupation: Playwright, librettist
Notable works: The Servant of Two Masters, The Mistress of the Inn

Carlo Osvaldo Goldoni (25 February 1707 – 6 February 1793) was an Italian playwright and librettist from the Republic of Venice. His works include some of Italy's most famous plays and are still performed today.

Reform of Italian theatre

Goldoni sought to reform the improvised commedia dell'arte by writing full scripts with realistic characters, plausible dialogue, and contemporary settings, drawing on the example of Molière. His reform was controversial: Carlo Gozzi, a defender of the old improvisational style, became his great rival. In the 1750 "theatrical year" Goldoni famously promised and delivered sixteen new comedies.

Major works

His best-known plays include The Servant of Two Masters (1746), The Mistress of the Inn (1753, featuring the innkeeper Mirandolina), The Venetian Twins, and The Fan. He also wrote dozens of opera libretti, set by composers including Galuppi and Mozart's contemporaries.

Later life

In 1762 Goldoni left Venice for Paris to direct the Comédie-Italienne, later teaching Italian to the daughters of Louis XV. The French Revolution ended his royal pension, and he died in poverty in Paris in 1793; the National Convention restored the pension to his widow the day after his death.
