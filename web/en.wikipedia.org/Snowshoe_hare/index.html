The snowshoe hare (Lepus americanus), also called the varying hare or snowshoe rabbit, is a species of hare found in North America. It takes its name from its very large hind feet, which are adapted to prevent it from sinking into snow when it hops and walks; the feet are also covered with stiff hairs that form a "snowshoe" and provide insulation.

Description

The snowshoe hare is a seasonal colour changer: its fur turns white in winter, matching the snow, and rusty brown in summer. This moult is triggered by photoperiod. Adults weigh roughly 1.1 to 1.6 kilograms. The species is mostly nocturnal and feeds on grasses, forbs, and in winter the bark and twigs of shrubs and young trees.

Population cycles

Snowshoe hare populations undergo dramatic cycles of roughly eight to eleven years, and the Canada lynx population closely tracks them. This predator–prey cycle is one of the most famous examples in ecology, documented using Hudson's Bay Company fur records going back to the nineteenth century.

Ecology

The hare is a keystone prey species for many boreal predators, including lynx, bobcats, coyotes, owls, and hawks. Females can produce several litters of leverets per season. Climate change, by shortening snow cover, can leave white hares conspicuous against bare ground, increasing predation.
