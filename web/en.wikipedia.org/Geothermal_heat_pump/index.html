Geothermal heat pump

Geothermal heat pump
Also calledGround source heat pump (GSHP)
Typical COP3.0–5.0
Loop typesClosed (horizontal, vertical, pond) or open
UsesSpace heating, cooling, hot water

A geothermal heat pump, or ground source heat pump, is a central heating and cooling system that transfers heat to or from the ground. Because the earth a few metres below the surface stays at a nearly constant temperature year-round — warmer than the air in winter and cooler in summer — these systems achieve much higher efficiency than conventional air-source equipment.

How it works

A fluid circulates through buried pipes (the ground loop), absorbing heat from the soil in winter or rejecting heat to it in summer. A vapour-compression refrigeration cycle, driven by an electric compressor, concentrates the heat and delivers it to the building. Typical coefficients of performance range from 3 to 5, meaning three to five units of heat are delivered for each unit of electricity consumed.

Loop configurations

Closed loops may be laid horizontally in trenches, installed in vertical boreholes where land is scarce, or submerged in a pond; open-loop systems pump groundwater directly. Vertical boreholes commonly reach depths of 50 to 150 metres. Installation costs are higher than for air-source systems because of drilling or excavation, but running costs and carbon emissions are lower.

Adoption

Ground source heat pumps are widely used in Sweden, Switzerland, and North America, with millions of installations worldwide. Incentives in the United States and Europe have promoted them as a key technology for decarbonising building heat.
