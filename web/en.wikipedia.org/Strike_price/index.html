Strike price
Also called: Exercise price
Used in: Options contracts

The strike price (or exercise price) of an option is the fixed price at which the holder of a call option can buy, or the holder of a put option can sell, the underlying asset. It is set when the contract is written and does not change during its life.

Moneyness

Comparing strike with the market price of the underlying defines moneyness: a call is in the money when the market price exceeds the strike, at the money when they are equal, and out of the money when below; the reverse holds for puts.

Role in pricing

The difference between market and strike prices forms intrinsic value; together with time value it determines the option premium in models such as Black–Scholes. Exchanges list chains of strikes around the current price for each expiry.
