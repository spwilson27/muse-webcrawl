Vietnamese cuisine

Vietnamese cuisine
Key ingredients: rice, rice noodles, fish sauce (nước mắm), fresh herbs, lemongrass, shrimp paste
Signature dishes: phở, bánh mì, gỏi cuốn, bún chả, chả giò

Vietnamese cuisine encompasses the foods and beverages of Vietnam, characterised by fresh herbs, light broths, rice in many forms, and a balance of sweet, sour, salty and spicy flavours, with fish sauce as a foundational seasoning. It reflects regional diversity and historical influences from China, Cambodia, and French colonial rule.

Regional styles

Northern food, centred on Hanoi, is milder and black pepper rather than chilli dominates; it is the home of phở and bún chả. Central cuisine, from Huế, is spicier and more elaborate, reflecting the imperial court, and includes bún bò Huế. Southern cooking is sweeter, uses coconut milk, and draws on Cambodian and Thai influences.

Staples

Rice (cơm) and rice noodles underpin most meals. The baguette-based sandwich bánh mì — filled with pâté, meats, pickled vegetables and coriander — is a legacy of French rule now famous worldwide. Meals are typically served communally, with dishes shared and eaten with chopsticks and spoons.
