University of Arkansas

Type: Public land-grant research university
Established: 1871
Location: Fayetteville, Arkansas, U.S.
Students: ~32,000

The University of Arkansas (U of A) is a public land-grant research university in Fayetteville, Arkansas. Founded in 1871 under the Morrill Land-Grant Act, it is the flagship campus of the University of Arkansas System and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity."

Campus

The campus, overlooking the Ozark Mountains, is known for Old Main (completed 1875), the Senior Walk — more than five kilometres of sidewalks engraved with graduates' names — and the Sam M. Walton College of Business, endowed by the family of Walmart's founder. The Fulbright College of Arts and Sciences honours alumnus J. William Fulbright, creator of the Fulbright Program.

Athletics

The Arkansas Razorbacks compete in the Southeastern Conference. The university is especially noted for track and field, with dozens of national championships under coaches such as John McDonnell.
