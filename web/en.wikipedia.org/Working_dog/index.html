Working dog

A working dog is a dog trained to perform practical tasks that assist humans, as distinct from a companion or show dog. Dogs have worked alongside people for millennia, and selective breeding has produced specialised types for herding, guarding, hunting, hauling and rescue.

Types of work

Herding dogs such as the border collie and Australian shepherd move sheep and cattle. Livestock guardian dogs, including the Great Pyrenees and Anatolian shepherd, live with flocks and deter predators. Service and assistance dogs guide blind people, alert deaf people to sounds, assist people with mobility impairments, and are trained to warn of seizures or blood-sugar changes. Detection dogs use their sense of smell — far more sensitive than a human's — to find explosives, narcotics, contraband food, diseases and missing persons. Police and military dogs track and apprehend suspects, while search-and-rescue dogs work disaster sites, and sled dogs still haul loads in Arctic regions.

Welfare

Working dogs require careful selection, lengthy training and appropriate rest; many are retired to adoptive homes at the end of their careers. Kennel clubs also recognise a "working group" of breeds developed primarily for guarding and draught work, such as the Rottweiler, Bernese mountain dog and Siberian husky.
