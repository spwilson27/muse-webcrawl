Beriberi

Beriberi is a disease caused by a deficiency of thiamine (vitamin B1) in the diet. Its symptoms include weight loss, emotional disturbances, impaired sensory perception, weakness and pain in the limbs, periods of irregular heart rate, and swelling of bodily tissues (edema). In advanced cases it may cause heart failure and death. The name is derived from the Sinhalese word beri, meaning "weak, weak" or "I cannot, I cannot", the word being reduplicated for emphasis.

Signs and symptoms

Beriberi is classically divided into two main presentations. Wet beriberi affects the cardiovascular system, causing a fast heart rate, shortness of breath, and leg swelling. Dry beriberi affects the nervous system, causing difficulty walking, loss of feeling in the hands and feet, loss of movement in the lower legs, mental confusion, and pain. A mixed picture is common.

Causes

Beriberi is caused by a lack of thiamine. The body needs thiamine to break down and digest food, especially carbohydrates. Deficiency occurs most often in populations whose diet consists mainly of polished white rice, from which the thiamine-rich husk has been removed, and in people with chronic alcoholism, in whom absorption is impaired.

History

The disease was described in ancient Chinese medical texts. In the 1880s the Dutch physician Christiaan Eijkman, working in the Dutch East Indies, observed that chickens fed polished rice developed a paralysis similar to beriberi, and recovered when fed unpolished rice. His work, together with that of Gerrit Grijns, pointed to a missing dietary factor rather than an infection or toxin, laying the foundations of vitamin research. Casimir Funk later isolated the "anti-beriberi factor" from rice polishings and coined the term vitamine.

Treatment

Treatment is with thiamine supplements, given by mouth or by injection, together with a balanced diet. Recovery of nervous-system symptoms may be slow or incomplete if the deficiency has been prolonged.
