Fort Mims massacre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Fort Mims massacre took place on 30 August 1813, during the Creek War, when a force of Creek Indians belonging to the "Red Stick" faction, under the command of Peter McQueen and William Weatherford (Red Eagle), stormed and destroyed Fort Mims in the Mississippi Territory (present-day Alabama). The fortified homestead of Samuel Mims sheltered several hundred settlers, militia, enslaved people, and Creek families opposed to the Red Sticks. Between 400 and 500 of its occupants were killed, one of the largest losses of life in a single event in United States frontier history.

Background

The Creek Nation was divided by civil war between the traditionalist Red Sticks, inspired in part by the Shawnee leader Tecumseh and supported by the British and Spanish, and accommodationist chiefs allied with the United States. After American militia attacked a Red Stick supply party at Burnt Corn Creek in July 1813, settlers across the Tensaw district crowded into stockades such as Fort Mims. Warnings of an impending attack were dismissed by the fort's commander, Major Daniel Beasley, who had the gate blocked open with sand and reportedly had a scout whipped for a false alarm.

The attack

At noon on 30 August, roughly 700–1,000 Red Stick warriors rushed the open gate during the garrison's midday meal. They seized the blockhouse, set the buildings ablaze with fire arrows, and killed most of the defenders and families inside; some occupants escaped into the surrounding swamps. Only a small number — estimates range from about 30 to 50 — survived.

Aftermath

News of the massacre — "Remember Fort Mims!" — galvanised American mobilisation in the Southwest. Major General Andrew Jackson led Tennessee volunteers into Creek country, culminating in the destruction of Red Stick power at the Battle of Horseshoe Bend in March 1814 and the Treaty of Fort Jackson, by which the Creek ceded some 23 million acres. The site, near present-day Tensaw in Baldwin County, Alabama, is preserved as a state historic site.
