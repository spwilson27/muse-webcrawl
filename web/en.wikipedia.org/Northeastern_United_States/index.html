The Northeastern United States, also referred to as the Northeast, is a geographical region of the United States located on the Atlantic coast of North America. As defined by the U.S. Census Bureau, it consists of nine states: the six New England states (Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, and Connecticut) and the three Mid-Atlantic states (New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania).

Geography

The region is bounded by Canada to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the east, the Southern United States to the south, and the Midwestern United States to the west. Its terrain ranges from the rocky coasts of Maine to the Appalachian Mountains, which run diagonally through Pennsylvania, New York, and New England. The Northeast Corridor, a densely populated megalopolis stretching from Boston to Washington, D.C., contains the region's largest metropolitan areas.

History

The Northeast was the site of some of the earliest European settlements in North America, including Plymouth (1620) and Boston (1630). All thirteen original colonies' northern members were located here, and the region played a central role in the American Revolution. In the 19th century it became the nation's industrial heartland, pioneering textile mills in Lowell, Massachusetts, and steel production in Pennsylvania, while waves of immigration transformed its cities.

Economy and culture

Today the region is a centre of finance, higher education, medicine, and media. New York City is a global financial capital, while Boston is renowned for its universities. The Northeast is the most densely populated U.S. region, is known for distinct accents and dialects, and its states tend to rank highly in education and income measures.
