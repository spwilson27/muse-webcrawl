Democratic socialism

Democratic socialism is a political philosophy that supports political democracy and some form of a socially owned economy, with a particular emphasis on economic democracy, workplace democracy, and workers' self-management within a market socialist or decentralized planned economy. Democratic socialists argue that capitalism is inherently incompatible with freedom, equality, and solidarity, and that these ideals can only be achieved through the realization of a socialist society.

The red rose is a common symbol of democratic socialism and social democracy

Overview

The defining feature of democratic socialism is its commitment to achieving socialist goals through democratic means — multiparty elections, civil liberties, and the rule of law — in contrast to authoritarian models of socialism. Democratic socialists generally oppose the one-party state and centrally planned economy of the Soviet model, as well as revolutionary vanguardism.

Distinction from social democracy

Democratic socialism is often distinguished from social democracy: while social democrats typically seek to reform and humanize capitalism through welfare states and regulation, democratic socialists aim to transcend capitalism by establishing social ownership of the means of production, though in practice the two traditions have overlapped heavily, and many parties and figures have been described as both.

History

The tradition traces roots to the democratic wing of the 19th-century socialist movement and to figures who insisted that socialism and democracy were inseparable. In the 20th century, democratic socialist parties and movements were prominent in Western Europe, and the label has also been used by figures such as India's Jawaharlal Nehru and, more recently, politicians in the United States including Bernie Sanders and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez.

Criticism

Critics argue that attempts to combine social ownership with democracy have historically faltered on problems of economic calculation and incentives, while some Marxists contend that democratic socialism underestimates the resistance of entrenched interests to peaceful transformation.
