Alexander Vasilyevich Kolchak (16 November 1874 – 7 February 1920) was a Russian admiral, polar explorer, and military and political leader who served as the self-styled Supreme Ruler of Russia from 1918 to 1920, heading the anti-Bolshevik White government in Siberia during the Russian Civil War.

Naval career and exploration

Kolchak distinguished himself in the Russo-Japanese War and as a hydrographer on Arctic expeditions, including the Russian polar expedition of 1900–1902 and the Arctic Ocean Hydrographic Expedition that charted Russia's northern coast and reached Severnaya Zemlya. In the First World War he rose to command the Black Sea Fleet in 1916, aggressively mining enemy ports and planning an amphibious seizure of the Bosphorus that the 1917 revolutions cancelled.

Supreme Ruler

In November 1918 Kolchak overthrew the socialist Directory at Omsk and proclaimed himself Supreme Ruler, winning conditional recognition and supplies from the Allies. His armies advanced toward the Volga in spring 1919 but were driven back across Siberia by the Red Army, amid peasant revolts, Allied disengagement, and the paralysis of his government. Abandoned by his Czechoslovak Legion escort — who seized much of the imperial gold reserve his train carried — Kolchak was handed to the authorities in Irkutsk, interrogated, and executed by firing squad on 7 February 1920; his body was thrown through the ice of the Ushakovka River.
