Decarboxylation

Decarboxylation is a chemical reaction that removes a carboxyl group (–COOH) from a molecule, releasing carbon dioxide. It is the reverse of carboxylation and occurs both in industrial chemistry and throughout metabolism.

Chemical examples

Beta-keto acids decarboxylate readily on heating through a cyclic transition state. The soda-lime reaction converts carboxylic acid salts to alkanes; the Kolbe electrolysis couples decarboxylation with radical dimerization; and the Barton decarboxylation uses radical intermediates. Heating amino acids with bases historically provided routes to amines.

In biochemistry

Enzymes called decarboxylases catalyse essential steps in metabolism: pyruvate decarboxylase produces acetaldehyde in fermentation, and oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate and α-ketoglutarate drives the citric acid cycle. Amino acid decarboxylations yield neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin, histamine, and GABA.

Applications

Decarboxylation activates the psychoactive compounds in cannabis (converting THCA to THC on heating) and is used in pharmaceutical and fine-chemical synthesis to build hydrocarbon frameworks.
