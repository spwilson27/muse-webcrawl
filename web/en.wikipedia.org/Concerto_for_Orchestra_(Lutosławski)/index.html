Concerto for Orchestra (Lutosławski)

The Concerto for Orchestra is an orchestral composition by the Polish composer Witold Lutosławski, written between 1950 and 1954. Commissioned by the conductor Witold Rowicki for the Warsaw Philharmonic Orchestra, it is one of the composer's best-known works and a virtuoso showpiece for every section of the orchestra. The work draws on Polish folk melodies collected by the ethnographer Oskar Kolberg.

Movements

  1. Intrada (Allegro maestoso) — built on a folk theme from the Mazovia region, developed with increasing intensity.
  2. Capriccio notturno e arioso (Vivace) — a whispering, scherzo-like nocturne framing a lyrical arioso for the strings.
  3. Passacaglia, toccata e corale (Andante con fuoco – Allegro) — an enormous finale: a passacaglia on a chromatically rising bass, a brilliant toccata, and a concluding chorale.

Style and reception

The Concerto belongs to Lutosławski's "folk" period, in which folk material is transformed through sophisticated harmonic and orchestral techniques. Although written under the cultural constraints of Stalinist Poland, the work transcends its circumstances and has entered the international repertoire; it helped establish the composer's reputation in the West. Its title echoes Bartók's famous Concerto for Orchestra of 1943.
