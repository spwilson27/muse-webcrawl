Peptide bond

A peptide bond is a covalent chemical bond linking two consecutive amino acid monomers along a peptide or protein chain. Specifically, it is an amide bond formed between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next, with loss of a molecule of water in a dehydration synthesis (condensation) reaction.

Peptide bond
Type: amide linkage
Forms: between C-terminus and N-terminus of adjacent amino acids
Length: about 1.32 Å
Character: partial double-bond

Formation

During protein synthesis on the ribosome, amino acids carried by transfer RNA are joined in sequence. The carboxyl carbon of the growing chain reacts with the amino nitrogen of the incoming amino acid, releasing water. In living cells this process is catalysed by the ribosome's peptidyl transferase centre, which is a ribozyme.

Structure and properties

The peptide bond has partial double-bond character due to resonance between the carbonyl oxygen and the amide nitrogen. This restricts rotation, keeping the six atoms of the peptide group roughly planar. The bond is usually in the trans configuration. Rotation is possible around the bonds flanking the peptide group, described by the phi and psi dihedral angles of the Ramachandran plot.

Hydrolysis

Peptide bonds can be broken by hydrolysis, catalysed in digestion and cellular turnover by proteases such as pepsin, trypsin and chymotrypsin. Although thermodynamically favourable, uncatalysed hydrolysis is extremely slow, giving proteins kinetic stability.
