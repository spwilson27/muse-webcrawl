Collision avoidance system

A collision avoidance system (CAS), also known as a pre-crash system, forward collision warning system or collision mitigation system, is an advanced driver-assistance system (ADAS) designed to prevent or reduce the severity of a collision. It uses sensors such as radar, lidar, cameras or a combination of these to detect an imminent crash with a vehicle, pedestrian or obstacle ahead.

Operation

When a potential collision is detected, the system first warns the driver with visual, audible or haptic alerts (forward collision warning). If the driver does not respond, the system may pre-charge the brakes, tighten seat belts, and ultimately apply autonomous emergency braking (AEB) to slow or stop the vehicle. Systems vary in operating speed range and in whether they detect pedestrians and cyclists as well as vehicles.

Regulation and effectiveness

Safety bodies such as Euro NCAP and the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration test and rate these systems, and studies by the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety have found that forward collision warning combined with automatic braking significantly reduces rear-end crashes. The European Union made AEB mandatory on new car types from 2022, and US regulators have moved toward requiring it on new passenger vehicles.
