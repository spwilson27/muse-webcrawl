Crypto anarchy

Crypto anarchy is a political philosophy and cyberspace ideology that holds that strong cryptography, anonymous communication networks, and digital cash can protect privacy and individual freedom by making it technologically impractical for governments or corporations to monitor, censor, or tax online activity. The term was introduced by computer engineer Timothy C. May in the late 1980s.

Origins

Crypto anarchy emerged from the cypherpunk movement, a loose community of activists advocating the widespread use of strong cryptography as a route to social and political change. May's 1988 "Crypto Anarchist Manifesto" predicted that computer technology would allow individuals and groups to communicate and interact in a completely anonymous manner, undermining the power of the state.

Concepts

Key technologies associated with crypto anarchy include public-key encryption, digital signatures, anonymous remailers, mix networks, untraceable electronic cash, and cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin. Proponents argue that these tools enable free markets, reputational systems, and voluntary association outside state control.

Criticism

Critics contend that the same technologies can facilitate crime, tax evasion, and unaccountable power, and that reputation-based systems cannot fully replace legal institutions.
