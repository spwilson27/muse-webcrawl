Cataphatic theology

Cataphatic theology or kataphatic theology is theology that uses "positive" terminology to describe or refer to the divine — specifically, God — affirming what God is rather than what God is not. It stands in contrast to apophatic (negative) theology, which describes God by negation.

Approach

Cataphatic statements include saying that God is good, wise, loving, or powerful, drawing on scripture, religious experience, and analogy from created perfections. Traditions such as Eastern Orthodoxy, Roman Catholicism, and many forms of Protestantism employ cataphatic language in liturgy and doctrine, often while acknowledging that such terms apply to God analogically rather than univocally.

History

The contrast between the cataphatic and apophatic ways runs through the writings of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite and later theologians including Thomas Aquinas, who held that affirmative names can be predicated of God substantially, though imperfectly. The two approaches are often treated as complementary within the via eminentiae and other frameworks of theological language.
