Slavic folklore

Slavic folklore encompasses the body of myths, legends, fairy tales, rituals and folk beliefs of the Slavic peoples of Central, Eastern and South-Eastern Europe. Developed over many centuries — in part from pre-Christian Slavic paganism and in part through long coexistence with Christianity, a syncretism often called "dual faith" — it survives in epic songs, wonder tales, seasonal customs and a rich cast of supernatural beings.

Beings and characters

The best-known figure is Baba Yaga, the ambiguous witch who lives in a hut on chicken legs and may help or devour the hero. Other widespread beings include the leshy, guardian spirit of the forest; the vodyanoy, a water spirit who drowns the careless; the rusalka, a female water spirit associated with the drowned; the house spirit domovoi; and the firebird, whose glowing feather drives many quests. South Slavic traditions feature the vila, a mountain or water nymph, and the dragon-like zmaj, while heroes such as Koschei the Deathless, Ivan Tsarevich and the bogatyrs of the Kievan epic cycle appear across East Slavic tales.

Tales and epics

The great nineteenth-century collections — Alexander Afanasyev's Russian Fairy Tales, the Serbian epics collected by Vuk Karadžić, and the Czech tales of Božena Němcová and Karel Jaromír Erben — preserved oral traditions whose motifs scholars classify within the Aarne–Thompson–Uther index of folktale types. Epic poems (byliny) celebrate heroes such as Ilya Muromets, Dobrynya Nikitich and Alyosha Popovich, blending history with myth.

Rituals and customs

Seasonal rituals mark the agricultural year: Maslenitsa before Lent, the midsummer festival of Kupala with bonfires and wreaths floated on rivers, and winter Koliada carolling. Life-cycle customs surround births, weddings and funerals, including laments and commemorative feasts for the dead. Many customs fused pagan and Christian elements, with saints such as St. George and St. Elijah absorbing the attributes of older gods Perun and Veles.

Legacy

Slavic folklore has deeply influenced literature, music and art — from Pushkin's verse tales and Gogol's Ukrainian stories to Mussorgsky's and Stravinsky's compositions (including The Firebird) and the paintings of Ivan Bilibin — and remains a staple of popular culture and fantasy media worldwide.
