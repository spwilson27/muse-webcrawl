Government ministry


A ministry headquarters: the political and administrative centre of a policy area.

A government ministry, or simply ministry, is a department of a government responsible for a defined area of public policy and administration, such as finance, foreign affairs, defence, education or health. Ministries are headed politically by a minister — in some countries titled secretary of state or secretary — who is usually a member of the cabinet, and administered by senior civil servants. In some jurisdictions the equivalent bodies are called departments, secretariats, bureaus or offices.

Structure and role

The ministry is the core unit of the executive branch. Its minister sets policy, answers to the legislature for the department's performance, and directs a permanent bureaucracy headed in Westminster-style systems by a permanent secretary. Ministries draft legislation, administer budgets, supervise executive agencies, and represent the state in their field domestically and, for foreign ministries, internationally. The number and names of ministries vary by country and change with reorganizations: modern states typically have between ten and thirty, with finance, foreign affairs, interior (home affairs), defence and justice among the oldest and most senior — sometimes called the "great offices of state".

Terminology worldwide

The United States federal executive departments are headed by secretaries (e.g., Secretary of Defense); the United Kingdom has departments led by Secretaries of State; Japan and most European states use ministries and ministers; the European Commission's analogous units are Directorates-General led by Commissioners. Sub-national governments in federal states maintain their own ministries or departments for devolved matters.
