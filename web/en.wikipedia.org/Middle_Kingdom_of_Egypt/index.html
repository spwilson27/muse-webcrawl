Middle Kingdom of Egypt

The Middle Kingdom of Egypt (also known as The Period of Reunification) is the period in the history of ancient Egypt following a period of political division known as the First Intermediate Period. It lasted approximately from 2040 to 1782 BC, spanning the Eleventh Dynasty through part of the Thirteenth Dynasty. The Middle Kingdom is often regarded as a classical age of Egyptian art and literature, notable for a strong central government, expansion into Nubia, and major irrigation works in the Faiyum.

Reunification

After decades of competing regional rulers, the Theban king Mentuhotep II defeated the Heracleopolitan rulers of Lower Egypt around 2040 BC and reunified the country, founding the Middle Kingdom. His successors consolidated royal authority, reformed administration, and reduced the power of the provincial governors (nomarchs) who had ruled almost independently.

Twelfth Dynasty

Amenemhat I founded the Twelfth Dynasty and moved the capital to the new city of Itjtawy, near the Faiyum. His son Senusret I extended Egyptian control into Nubia. Senusret III campaigned deep into Nubia, built a chain of massive mudbrick fortresses at the Second Cataract (such as Buhen and Semna), and further curtailed the nomarchs. Under Amenemhat III, extensive land reclamation around Lake Moeris in the Faiyum greatly expanded agricultural output.

Culture

Middle Kingdom literature is considered the classical standard of the Egyptian language; works such as the Tale of Sinuhe, the Tale of the Shipwrecked Sailor, and the Eloquent Peasant date to this period. Art shows a more humanised, sometimes careworn portraiture of kings, and private funerary customs spread beyond the royal court — a trend sometimes called the "democratisation of the afterlife".

Decline

During the Thirteenth Dynasty royal power weakened, reigns became short, and control of the eastern Delta passed to West Semitic rulers at Avaris. The period gave way to the Second Intermediate Period, during which the Hyksos ruled northern Egypt.
