Rue de la Paix

The Rue de la Paix (English: Street of Peace) is a fashionable shopping street in north-central Paris, running from the Place Vendôme in the 1st arrondissement to the Opéra Garnier at the Boulevard des Capucines in the 2nd arrondissement. It is famous for its jewellers, luxury boutiques, and hotels, and is among the most expensive streets in the French version of the board game Monopoly.

History

The street was laid out under Napoleon I, who ordered the demolition of the Capucines convent to open a new thoroughfare, originally called the Rue Napoléon. It was renamed Rue de la Paix in 1814 to commemorate the Peace of Paris after Napoleon's abdication. The jeweller Cartier opened at number 13 in 1898, and the street became the centre of Parisian high jewellery, home also to houses such as Mellerio and Boucheron, alongside couturiers including Worth and Doucet.

Present day

Today the Rue de la Paix and the adjoining Place Vendôme host flagship stores of major luxury brands, fine watchmakers, and grand hotels such as the Park Hyatt Paris Vendôme. The column of the Place Vendôme, topped by a statue of Napoleon, stands at its southern end.
