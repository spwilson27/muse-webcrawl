The Simien Mountains (Amharic: ስሜን ተራሮች) are a mountain range in northern Ethiopia, in the Amhara Region north-east of Gondar. Part of the Ethiopian Highlands, they are among the most spectacular landscapes in Africa, formed by massive volcanic eruptions some 30–40 million years ago and sculpted by erosion into jagged peaks, deep gorges and sheer escarpments that plunge more than a kilometre into the lowlands. Their highest summit, Ras Dashen at 4,550 metres, is the highest point in Ethiopia.

Geology and landscape

The range was created when flood basalts poured over the region and were later uplifted and eroded. The northern escarpment stretches for many kilometres as an almost continuous cliff line, with isolated pinnacles and plateaus separated by deep valleys. Because of the altitude, the climate is alpine: nights can fall below freezing even though the range lies close to the equator.

Wildlife

The Simiens are a stronghold for several endemic species. The gelada baboon grazes the high grasslands in large troops, the walia ibex climbs the steepest cliffs, and the Ethiopian wolf hunts rodents on the Afroalpine moorlands. Other notable species include the klipspringer, bushbuck and birds such as the lammergeier (bearded vulture) and thick-billed raven.

National park and conservation

Simien Mountains National Park, established in 1969, protects the core of the range and was inscribed as one of the first UNESCO World Heritage Sites in 1978. It was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 1996 owing to declines in the walia ibex population, but recovery of wildlife and improved management led to its removal from the danger list in 2017. Trekking between camps such as Sankaber, Geech and Chenek is the principal visitor activity.
