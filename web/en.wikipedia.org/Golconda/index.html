Golconda is a fortified citadel and ruined city located on the western outskirts of Hyderabad, Telangana, India. It was the capital city of the Golconda Sultanate and the medieval sultanate of the Qutb Shahi dynasty (c. 1518–1687). The region is known for the mines that produced some of the world's most famous gems, including the Koh-i-Noor, the Hope Diamond, and the Nassak Diamond.

The name Golconda is thought to derive from the Telugu words Golla Konda, "shepherd's hill". The English word "golconda" has come to mean a source of great wealth, after the fame of the diamond trade centred here.

History

The fort was first built by the Kakatiya dynasty in the 11th century and was expanded by the Bahmani Sultanate. Under the Qutb Shahi sultans it became the capital of a wealthy kingdom enriched by the nearby Kollur diamond mines — for centuries the world's only known source of diamonds. In 1687 the fort fell to the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb after an eight-month siege, aided by the treachery of a gatekeeper, and the last sultan, Abul Hasan Qutb Shah, was imprisoned.

The fort

The complex rises on a granite hill about 120 metres high, ringed by massive masonry walls with 87 bastions. It is famous for its acoustic engineering: a hand clap at the Fateh Darwaza (Victory Gate) can be heard clearly at the Bala Hisar pavilion at the summit, nearly a kilometre away, a system said to have served as a warning signal. The citadel contains royal apartments, mosques, temples, stables, and an ingenious water-supply system of Persian wheels and clay pipes.

Diamonds

Golconda's market traded diamonds mined along the Krishna River. Famous stones attributed to the region include the Koh-i-Noor, the Daria-i-Noor, the Regent Diamond, and the blue Hope Diamond.
