Electronic music

Electronic music broadly comprises music that emphasises the use of electronic musical instruments, digital instruments or circuitry-based music technology in its creation. Purely electronic sound production precedes electrically amplified instruments by decades: early devices included the Telharmonium (1897), the theremin (1920), the ondes Martenot (1928) and the Hammond organ (1935).

Origins and avant-garde

After 1945, magnetic tape made new techniques possible. In Paris, Pierre Schaeffer developed musique concrète from recorded sounds; in Cologne, the WDR studio of Karlheinz Stockhausen and others pursued purely synthesised elektronische Musik. Studios in Milan, Tokyo and New York (the Columbia–Princeton Electronic Music Center, home of the RCA Mark II synthesiser) spread the idiom. Wendy Carlos's Switched-On Bach (1968) brought the Moog synthesiser to a mass audience.

Popular electronic music

From the 1970s, bands such as Kraftwerk, Tangerine Dream and Yellow Magic Orchestra, and producers such as Giorgio Moroder, fused synthesisers and drum machines with pop and disco. In the 1980s and 1990s electronic dance music exploded — house in Chicago, techno in Detroit, trance, drum and bass, and rave culture — driven by affordable instruments like the Roland TR-808 and TB-303. Software-based production (DAWs, samplers, plugins) has since made electronic production the default method across pop, hip-hop, film scoring and most other genres.
