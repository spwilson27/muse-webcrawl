Koblenz

City in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany
Population: about 115,000
Situated at the confluence of the Rhine and Moselle

Koblenz is a German city on the banks of the Rhine and the Moselle, a multinational tributary. It is the third-largest city in Rhineland-Palatinate, after Mainz and Ludwigshafen am Rhein.

History

The Romans founded a military post here around 8 BC, named Castellum apud Confluentes for the confluence of the rivers. Koblenz later belonged to the Electorate of Trier, was occupied by France, then passed to Prussia in 1815, when it became capital of the Rhine Province and was ringed by extensive fortifications, including Fortress Ehrenbreitstein across the Rhine.

Landmarks

The Deutsches Eck (German Corner), where the Moselle meets the Rhine, is marked by a monumental equestrian statue of Emperor William I. The old town, cable car to Ehrenbreitstein, and the Upper Middle Rhine Valley UNESCO World Heritage landscape make Koblenz a centre for Rhine tourism and wine.
