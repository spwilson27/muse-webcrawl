Enzo Anselmo Giuseppe Maria Ferrari (18 February 1898 – 14 August 1988) was an Italian motor racing driver and entrepreneur, founder of the Scuderia Ferrari racing team in 1929 and of the Ferrari automobile company in 1947. Known as il Commendatore ("the Commander") and il Drake ("the Dragon"), he ruled his company with legendary single-mindedness, prioritising racing over road-car production throughout his life.

Life and career

Born in Modena, Ferrari resolved to become a racing driver after seeing the 1908 Circuit di Bologna. After military service in the First World War he joined the small manufacturer CMN and then, in 1920, Alfa Romeo, for which he raced through the 1920s. In 1929 he founded Scuderia Ferrari in Modena as Alfa Romeo's racing department, managing drivers including Tazio Nuvolari. Leaving Alfa in 1939, he founded Auto Avio Costruzioni, and after the Second World War established Ferrari in Maranello; the first Ferrari-badged car, the 125 S, appeared in 1947.

Scuderia Ferrari became the most successful team in Formula One history, winning numerous drivers' and constructors' championships under his leadership, and dominating sports-car racing at events such as Le Mans and the Mille Miglia. Ferrari sold a 50 percent stake to Fiat in 1969 but retained control of the racing division until his death in 1988, weeks after the team's emotional one-two finish at Monza in that year's Italian Grand Prix. His only legitimate son, Dino, died young in 1956; his second son, Piero, became vice-chairman of the company.
