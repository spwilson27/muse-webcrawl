Marian antiphons

The Marian antiphons are a group of four Latin hymns to the Virgin Mary sung in the Roman Catholic Church and other Western Christian traditions. Traditionally sung at the end of Compline (Night Prayer) and after other offices, each antiphon is assigned to a season of the liturgical year.

The four antiphons

  • Alma Redemptoris Mater ("Loving Mother of the Redeemer") — from Advent until the Feast of the Presentation (2 February).
  • Ave Regina Caelorum ("Hail, Queen of Heaven") — from the Presentation through Lent until Holy Week.
  • Regina Caeli ("Queen of Heaven, rejoice") — during Eastertide, from Compline on Holy Saturday until Pentecost; it also replaces the Angelus in that season.
  • Salve Regina ("Hail, Holy Queen") — from after Pentecost until Advent; the best known of the four, traditionally attributed to Hermann of Reichenau.

History and use

The antiphons arose in the eleventh and twelfth centuries, associated with monastic devotion and processions. They were later appended to the end of Compline in the Roman Breviary. Countless composers have set them, from medieval chant and Renaissance polyphony (Dufay, Palestrina, Lassus) to the eight Marian antiphons of Poulenc's and Penderecki's contemporaries. In the Liturgy of the Hours they remain an option for concluding Night Prayer, and the Salve Regina is sung at funerals and in the rosary's closing prayers.
