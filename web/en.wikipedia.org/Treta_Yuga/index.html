Treta Yuga

Treta Yuga (Sanskrit: त्रेतायुग, romanized: tretāyuga) is the second of the four yugas (world ages) in Hindu cosmology, preceded by Satya Yuga (Krita Yuga) and followed by Dvapara Yuga. Treta means "a collection of three things" in Sanskrit, and is so named because during Treta Yuga, there were three avatars of Vishnu that were seen: Vamana, Parashurama, and Rama. The Dharma bull, which symbolizes morality, stood on three legs during this period. It stood on four legs in Satya Yuga, and on two legs in Dvapara Yuga, and currently on one leg in the current Kali Yuga.

Duration and structure

The duration of Treta Yuga is traditionally given as 1,296,000 years (3,600 divine years). It is one quarter shorter than Satya Yuga, reflecting the decline of dharma. Its main period lasts 1,080,000 years, with dawn and twilight periods of 108,000 years each.

Characteristics

During Treta Yuga, people are said to have first developed many virtues but declined in goodness compared with Satya Yuga; virtue fell by a quarter. Sacrifice (yajna) is held to be the main spiritual practice of the age. Rama, the hero of the Ramayana, is traditionally placed in Treta Yuga.
