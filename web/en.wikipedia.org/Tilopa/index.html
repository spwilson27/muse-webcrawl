Tilopa

Tilopa (Prakrit: Tillopada; Tibetan: ཏི་ལོ་པ་, 988–1069 CE) was an Indian Buddhist tantric adept (mahasiddha) and the founder of the Kagyu school of Tibetan Buddhism. He is revered as the human source of the Mahamudra transmission that passed through his disciple Naropa to Marpa the Translator and thence into Tibet. His name is associated with sesame (Sanskrit tila), the seed he is said to have pounded for oil as a livelihood while practising in secret.

Life

Traditional biographies relate that Tilopa was born into a Brahmin (in some accounts royal) family in Bengal and became a monk, but left monastic life after a dakini instructed him to follow the tantric path. He received teachings from several gurus, including transmissions connected with Nagarjuna, and is said to have received the Mahamudra instructions directly in visionary form from the Buddha Vajradhara. By day he worked grinding sesame seeds and, in some tellings, served at a brothel by night, concealing a life of intense meditation.

Teachings

Tilopa is credited with the Ganges Mahamudra (the "Mahamudra Upadesha"), a concise set of instructions said to have been given to Naropa on the banks of the Ganges, counselling the student not to suppress thought but to let the mind settle in its natural state: "If you are content, you are free." He is also the source of the "six words of advice" — do not recall, do not imagine, do not think, do not examine, do not control, rest — and of practices later systematised as the Six Yogas of Naropa. His famous training of Naropa involved a series of harsh ordeals meant to cut through the disciple's intellectual pride.

Lineage

The transmission Tilopa → Naropa → Marpa → Milarepa → Gampopa forms the golden lineage of the Kagyu school, whose head today is the Karmapa. Tilopa is counted among the eighty-four mahasiddhas of Indian tradition and is typically depicted as an emaciated yogin holding a fish, symbolising awakened awareness alive within the stream of samsara.
