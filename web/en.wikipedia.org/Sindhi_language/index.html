Sindhi is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by about 30 million people, chiefly in the Pakistani province of Sindh, where it is an official language, and by the Sindhi diaspora in India and worldwide. It descends from an early form of Prakrit and has been written for many centuries, with a rich Sufi literary tradition centred on poets such as Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, whose Shah Jo Risalo is the classic of Sindhi literature.

Features and scripts

Sindhi is notable among Indo-Aryan languages for its large phoneme inventory, including a full set of implosive consonants (ɓ, ɗ, ʄ, ɠ). Its vocabulary includes substantial borrowings from Arabic and Persian. In Pakistan, Sindhi is written in an extended Perso-Arabic script with 52 letters, standardised under British rule in the 1850s; in India both that script and Devanagari are used. Major dialects include Vicholi (central, the standard), Lari, Lasi, Thari and Kachhi.
