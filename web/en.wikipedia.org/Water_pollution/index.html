Water pollution (or aquatic pollution) is the contamination of water bodies, with negative impacts on their uses. It is usually a result of human activities. Water bodies include lakes, rivers, oceans, aquifers, reservoirs and groundwater. Water pollution results when contaminants mix with these water bodies. Contaminants can come from one of four main sources: sewage discharges, industrial activities, agricultural activities, and urban runoff including stormwater.

Categories of sources

Pollution is often classed as point source or nonpoint source. Point sources discharge from an identifiable location, such as a factory outfall or wastewater treatment plant, and are regulated by discharge permits in many countries. Nonpoint sources are diffuse — fertiliser and pesticide runoff from fields, animal waste, eroded sediment, and stormwater washing oil, metals and litter from roads — and are collectively the largest cause of water-quality impairment in many developed countries.

Pollutants and effects

Pathogens from untreated sewage cause waterborne diseases such as cholera, typhoid and dysentery, still major killers where sanitation is poor. Excess nutrients (nitrogen and phosphorus) cause eutrophication: algal blooms, oxygen depletion ("dead zones" such as in the Gulf of Mexico) and fish kills. Heavy metals (mercury, lead, cadmium) and persistent organic pollutants accumulate through food webs; mercury poisoning from industrial discharge caused Minamata disease in Japan. Oil spills, thermal pollution, acid mine drainage, groundwater contamination by solvents and nitrates, and plastic pollution — including microplastics now found throughout the oceans — are further categories.

Control

Control combines source reduction, treatment and regulation: primary, secondary and tertiary wastewater treatment; industrial pre-treatment; buffer strips and nutrient management in agriculture; and laws such as the United States Clean Water Act (1972) and the EU Water Framework Directive (2000). Water quality is monitored through indicators including dissolved oxygen, biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), turbidity, pH and coliform counts. Access to safely managed drinking water and sanitation is tracked under UN Sustainable Development Goal 6.
