Øresund Bridge

The Øresund Bridge (Danish: Øresundsbroen; Swedish: Öresundsbron) is a combined railway and motorway bridge across the Øresund strait between Denmark and Sweden. Together with the artificial island of Peberholm and the Drogden Tunnel, it forms the Øresund Link, connecting the Danish capital Copenhagen with the Swedish city of Malmö.

Structure

The link is about 16 kilometres long in total. From the Swedish side, an 7.8-kilometre cable-stayed bridge runs to Peberholm, a 4-kilometre artificial island built from dredged material south of the natural island of Saltholm. From Peberholm, the connection continues through the 4-kilometre Drogden Tunnel to the Danish island of Amager. The tunnel section was chosen so that the link would not obstruct air traffic to Copenhagen Airport or shipping through the Drogden channel.

The bridge's main span, at 490 metres, was among the longest cable-stayed spans in the world at completion, and its two pylons rise 204 metres above the sea. The bridge carries a four-lane motorway on the upper deck and a double-track railway below.

History

Plans for a fixed link across the Øresund had been discussed for most of the twentieth century. Denmark and Sweden signed an agreement in 1991, and construction began in 1995. The bridge was completed ahead of schedule and opened to traffic on 1 July 2000, with the formal inauguration attended by Queen Margrethe II of Denmark and King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden.

Traffic and significance

The link carries the European route E20 road traffic and the Øresund Line railway, on which Øresundståg trains run between Copenhagen and Malmö and onward into southern Sweden. Around 20,000 commuters cross daily, and the link is credited with creating an integrated cross-border labour market known as the Øresund Region. A fixed toll is charged for road vehicles; the structure is owned by the Danish and Swedish states through the Øresund Bridge Consortium.
