MARC (MAchine-Readable Cataloging) is a set of standards for representing bibliographic and related information in machine-readable form. Developed at the Library of Congress in the 1960s under the leadership of Henriette Avram, MARC became the foundation of computerised library catalogues worldwide.

Structure

A MARC record consists of a leader, a directory, and variable fields identified by three-digit tags: the 245 field, for example, holds the title statement, 100 the main author entry, and 008 a string of fixed-length coded data. Indicator positions and letter-coded subfields refine the meaning of each field. MARC 21 distinguishes five formats, for bibliographic, authority, holdings, classification and community information data.

History and successors

National variants (USMARC, CAN/MARC, UKMARC) were harmonised into MARC 21 in 1997. MARCXML re-expresses records in XML. Because MARC's structure predates modern data practice, the Library of Congress developed BIBFRAME, a linked-data model intended eventually to succeed it.
