LibreOffice is a free and open-source office productivity suite developed by The Document Foundation. It was forked in 2010 from OpenOffice.org and is available for Windows, macOS, and Linux.

History

The Document Foundation was formed in September 2010 by members of the OpenOffice.org community concerned about the project's future under Oracle Corporation. LibreOffice quickly attracted most of the community's developers and became the default office suite of many Linux distributions. It inherited the codebase originating in StarOffice, acquired by Sun Microsystems in 1999.

Components

The suite comprises Writer (word processor), Calc (spreadsheet), Impress (presentations), Draw (vector graphics), Base (database), and Math (formula editor). It uses the OpenDocument Format (ODF) as its native format and also supports Microsoft Office formats such as .docx, .xlsx, and .pptx.

Development

LibreOffice is released under the Mozilla Public License v2.0. Development is carried out by a mix of volunteers and companies such as Collabora and Red Hat; an online version, LibreOffice Online / Collabora Online, provides browser-based editing. Major releases appear roughly every six months, and the software has been downloaded hundreds of millions of times.
