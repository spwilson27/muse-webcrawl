Fisher information

In mathematical statistics, the Fisher information measures the amount of information that an observable random variable X carries about an unknown parameter θ upon which the probability of X depends.

Definition

The Fisher information is defined as the variance of the score — the partial derivative of the log-likelihood with respect to the parameter — and equivalently, under regularity conditions, as the expected value of the negative second derivative of the log-likelihood. For a sample of independent observations, Fisher information adds across observations.

Applications

Fisher information plays a central role in the Cramér–Rao bound, which gives a lower bound on the variance of any unbiased estimator: no unbiased estimator can have variance less than the reciprocal of the Fisher information. It also underlies the asymptotic distribution of maximum likelihood estimators, Jeffreys' prior in Bayesian statistics, and the Fisher information metric of information geometry, which treats statistical models as points on a Riemannian manifold.
