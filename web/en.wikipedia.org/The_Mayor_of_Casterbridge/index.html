The Mayor of Casterbridge: The Life and Death of a Man of Character is an 1886 novel by the English author Thomas Hardy. Published serially in 1886 and in book form the same year, it is one of Hardy's Wessex novels, set in the fictional town of Casterbridge, based on Dorchester in Dorset. The novel is famous for its opening scene, in which the drunken hay-trusser Michael Henchard auctions his wife and baby daughter at a country fair.

Plot summary

Michael Henchard, a young hay-trusser, sells his wife Susan and daughter Elizabeth-Jane to a sailor for five guineas at Weydon-Priors fair. Sober the next morning, he swears an oath not to drink for twenty-one years and eventually rises to become a prosperous corn merchant and mayor of Casterbridge. Nearly two decades later Susan returns, believing her sailor "husband" dead. Henchard remarries her, but a chain of revelations — a rival in the gifted manager Donald Farfrae, the return of his former lover Lucetta, bankruptcy, and the disclosure that the surviving Elizabeth-Jane is not his daughter — brings about his ruin. He dies alone in a hut on Egdon Heath, leaving a will asking that he be forgotten.

Themes

The novel explores character and fate, pride, secrecy, and the clash between traditional rural life and modern agricultural capitalism embodied by Farfrae's new methods. Hardy's subtitle emphasizes that the tragedy is one of character as much as circumstance.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted several times, including a 1978 BBC serial with Alan Bates, a 2003 television film with Ciarán Hinds, and the 2011 film Trishna, which transposes the story to modern India.
