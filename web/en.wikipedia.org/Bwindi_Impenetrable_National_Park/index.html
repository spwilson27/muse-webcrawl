Bwindi Impenetrable National Park is a national park in south-western Uganda, lying along the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo next to Virunga National Park and on the edge of the western arm of the Great Rift Valley. Comprising 331 square kilometres (128 sq mi) of both montane and lowland forest, it is accessible only on foot. UNESCO designated it a World Heritage Site in 1994.

Biodiversity

The park is a sanctuary for colobus monkeys, chimpanzees and many bird species such as hornbills and turacos. It is most notable for the approximately 459 mountain gorillas (as of a 2018 census) living there — roughly half of the world's remaining population of the endangered mountain gorilla (Gorilla beringei beringei), with several groups habituated for tourism. The forest also hosts around 120 mammal species, some 350 bird species, more than 200 butterfly species, and a rich flora including many endemic Albertine Rift species.

The forest is one of the oldest and most biologically diverse in East Africa, having survived the last ice age as a refugium. The word "Bwindi" derives from the local Runyakitara word Mubwindi, meaning "a place full of darkness", referring to the dense forest.

Conservation and people

The Batwa pygmies, the original inhabitants of the forest, were evicted when the park was created in 1991 and became conservation refugees, a displacement that remains controversial. Gorilla trekking tourism is the park's main source of revenue and funds local community projects, though only a limited number of permits are issued daily to minimise disturbance to the gorillas.
