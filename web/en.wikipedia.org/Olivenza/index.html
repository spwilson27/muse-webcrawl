Olivenza (Portuguese: Olivença) is a town in southwestern Spain, near the Portuguese border, in the province of Badajoz, Extremadura. It is notable as the centre of a long-standing territorial dispute between Spain and Portugal, having been Portuguese from the Middle Ages until 1801.

History

The area was settled by the Knights Templar in the 13th century, and Olivenza was formally incorporated into Portugal by the Treaty of Alcañices in 1297. It remained Portuguese for over five centuries. During the War of the Oranges in 1801, Spanish forces occupied the town, and it was ceded to Spain by the Treaty of Badajoz. Portugal has never formally renounced its claim, arguing that the Congress of Vienna of 1815 obliged Spain to return the territory, though the town has been administered by Spain ever since and its inhabitants are now predominantly Spanish-speaking.

Geography and heritage

Olivenza lies on the left bank of the Guadiana River, roughly 24 kilometres south of Badajoz. Despite two centuries of Spanish administration, the town retains a strong Portuguese cultural imprint: Manueline architecture, Portuguese street names, and fortifications in the Portuguese style survive, including the medieval castle and the churches of Santa Maria do Castelo and Santa Maria Madalena, the latter a notable example of Manueline decoration.

The dispute today

The status of Olivenza remains a sensitive but low-key issue in Iberian relations. A local cultural association, the Grupo dos Amigos de Olivença, advocates the Portuguese claim, and some residents hold dual sympathies, but the matter is not an active obstacle to cooperation between the two European Union member states.
