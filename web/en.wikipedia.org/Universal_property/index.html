Universal property

In mathematics, and particularly in category theory, a universal property is a property that characterises a mathematical object up to a unique isomorphism in terms of how it relates, via morphisms, to all other objects of a given type. Rather than defining an object by its internal construction, a universal property defines it by its external relationships.

Formal idea

Typically, one specifies a diagram or a functor and asks for an object together with a morphism (a "universal arrow") such that every other morphism of the relevant kind factors uniquely through it. If a solution exists it is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Examples

Familiar constructions defined by universal properties include the tensor product of vector spaces, the quotient group, products and coproducts in a category, free groups, and the completion of a metric space. The theory of adjoint functors systematically organises universal constructions: a left or right adjoint is essentially a universal solution to an optimisation problem posed by the other functor.
