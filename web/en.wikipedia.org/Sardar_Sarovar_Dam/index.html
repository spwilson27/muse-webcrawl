The Sardar Sarovar Dam is a concrete gravity dam built on the Narmada River near the town of Kevadia in the Narmada district of Gujarat, India. Named for Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, India's first deputy prime minister, it is the centrepiece of the Narmada Valley Project, one of the largest river valley development schemes in the world, providing irrigation, drinking water, and hydroelectric power to the states of Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Rajasthan.

History and controversy

The foundation stone was laid by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru in 1961, but construction proceeded slowly for decades amid disputes among the riparian states and, from the 1980s, organised opposition led by the Narmada Bachao Andolan (Save Narmada Movement) associated with Medha Patkar and others, which protested the displacement of hundreds of thousands of people — many of them Adivasi (tribal) communities — and the project's environmental effects. The World Bank withdrew funding in 1993 after an independent review. India's Supreme Court permitted construction to continue in stages in a landmark 2000 judgment subject to resettlement conditions. The dam was raised to its full height of 163 metres and dedicated in September 2017.

Benefits and the Statue of Unity

The project's canal network — among the largest irrigation networks in the world — is designed to irrigate about 1.8 million hectares and supply drinking water to tens of millions of people in drought-prone regions of Gujarat and Rajasthan. Near the dam stands the Statue of Unity, the world's tallest statue, depicting Sardar Patel, which opened in 2018 and has become a major tourist attraction.
