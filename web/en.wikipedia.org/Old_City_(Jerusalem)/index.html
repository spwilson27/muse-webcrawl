The Old City of Jerusalem is a walled area of about 0.9 square kilometres within East Jerusalem. Surrounded by Ottoman-era walls built under Suleiman the Magnificent in the 1530s and 1540s, it contains some of the holiest sites of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, including the Temple Mount with the Dome of the Rock and Al-Aqsa Mosque, the Western Wall, and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre.

Quarters and gates

The Old City is traditionally divided into four quarters: the Muslim Quarter (the largest), the Christian Quarter, the Jewish Quarter, and the Armenian Quarter. Seven gates are open today — including Jaffa Gate, Damascus Gate, Herod's Gate, Lions' Gate, Dung Gate, Zion Gate, and the New Gate (added in 1889) — while the Golden Gate on the eastern wall remains sealed.

History

Jerusalem's ancient core expanded and contracted over millennia, from the City of David south of the present walls through the Roman city of Aelia Capitolina, whose cardo streets still structure the Old City. Jerusalem came under Muslim rule in the seventh century, Crusader rule from 1099 to 1187, and Ottoman rule from 1517 to 1917. Jordan controlled the Old City from 1948 to 1967, when Israel captured it in the Six-Day War; the Jewish Quarter, destroyed in 1948, was subsequently rebuilt. The Old City's status remains one of the most sensitive issues in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1981.

Key sites

Religious sites include the Western Wall plaza, the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre (venerated as the site of Jesus's crucifixion and burial), the Via Dolorosa, and numerous mosques, churches, synagogues, and monasteries packed into the dense medieval street plan.
