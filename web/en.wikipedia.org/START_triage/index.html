START triage

Simple triage and rapid treatment (START) is a triage method used by first responders to classify victims of a mass casualty incident according to the severity of their injuries. Developed in 1983 at Hoag Memorial Hospital in Newport Beach, California, together with the Newport Beach Fire Department, START lets rescuers assess each patient in 60 seconds or less using only respirations, perfusion and mental status (mnemonic RPM), and sort them into four colour-coded treatment categories.

START uses red, yellow, green and black triage tags.

Categories

  • Red (immediate) — life-threatening but survivable injuries requiring urgent care; e.g. respiratory rate over 30/min, absent radial pulse or delayed capillary refill, or failure to follow simple commands, after basic airway intervention.
  • Yellow (delayed) — serious injuries that can wait for treatment without immediate danger to life.
  • Green (minor, "walking wounded") — those able to walk are directed to a safe area first, which immediately clears the scene and identifies the least severely injured.
  • Black (deceased/expectant) — victims who are not breathing even after airway repositioning, given the resources available.

Algorithm

Responders first ask everyone who can walk to move to a designated area (green). For each remaining victim they check breathing: if absent, reposition the airway; if breathing does not resume, tag black; if it resumes, tag red. A respiratory rate above 30 also tags red. Next they check perfusion (radial pulse or capillary refill > 2 s → red, after controlling major bleeding), and finally mental status (unable to follow simple commands → red; otherwise yellow). JumpSTART adapts the algorithm for paediatric patients, adding rescue breaths for apnoeic children with a pulse.
