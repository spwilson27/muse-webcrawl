Mongolian Revolution of 1921

The Mongolian Revolution of 1921 was a military and political event by which Mongolian revolutionaries, with the assistance of the Soviet Red Army, expelled the Chinese administration and the Russian White Guards from Outer Mongolia and established the Mongolian People's Party government, under a restored constitutional monarchy headed by the Bogd Khan. The revolution ended Outer Mongolia's brief occupation by the Republic of China (1919–1921) and laid the foundations for the Mongolian People's Republic, proclaimed in 1924.

Background

Outer Mongolia had declared independence from the Qing dynasty in 1911 under the Bogd Khan. Chinese forces under Xu Shuzheng reoccupied the country in 1919 and abolished its autonomy. In response, two underground resistance groups in the capital Urga — the Consular Hill group and the East Urga group — merged in June 1920 to form the Mongolian People's Party (later the MPRP), led by figures including Damdin Sükhbaatar and Khorloogiin Choibalsan, who travelled to Soviet Russia to seek support.

Course of the revolution

In October 1920 the White Russian warlord Roman von Ungern-Sternberg entered Mongolia, expelled the Chinese garrison from Urga in February 1921 and restored the Bogd Khan. In March 1921 a provisional people's government was formed at Kyakhta, and Sükhbaatar's partisans defeated Chinese troops at the border. In the summer, a combined Mongolian–Soviet force entered Urga on 6 July 1921; a declaration of independence was issued on 11 July, a date still celebrated as the start of the Naadam holiday. Ungern was captured by the Red Army in August and later executed. The Bogd Khan remained as a limited monarch until his death in May 1924, when the Mongolian People's Republic was proclaimed with Soviet backing.

Legacy

The revolution secured Mongolia's independence from China for the rest of the twentieth century, though at the cost of decades as a Soviet satellite state. In modern Mongolia, opinion is divided: the events are still officially commemorated as the People's Revolution, while some historians emphasise the role of Soviet military intervention.
