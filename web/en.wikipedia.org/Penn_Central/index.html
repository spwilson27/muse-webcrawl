Penn Central

Penn Central (formally the Penn Central Transportation Company) was a United States railroad formed on 1 February 1968 by the merger of the Pennsylvania Railroad and the New York Central Railroad, two of the largest and most storied railroads in the country. It collapsed into bankruptcy on 21 June 1970 — then the largest corporate bankruptcy in American history.

Merger and collapse

The merger combined two bitter rivals with incompatible operating practices, signalling systems, and management cultures, while the combined company inherited declining freight and passenger traffic, heavy regulation, high labour costs, and large tax and infrastructure burdens. Integration failures, deferred maintenance, poor track conditions, and costly diversification into real estate and other ventures drained cash. In 1969 the struggling New Haven Railroad was folded in, worsening matters. Penn Central's 1970 bankruptcy filing, after the federal government refused a loan guarantee, shook financial markets and prompted congressional action.

Legacy

Passenger services passed to the newly created Amtrak in 1971. The bankrupt company's freight operations, along with those of other failed northeastern railroads, were transferred in 1976 to the federally created Conrail, which eventually returned the network to profitability and was privatised in 1987 and split between CSX and Norfolk Southern in 1999. The Penn Central case remains a landmark example of a merger that destroyed value through incompatible systems and cultures.
