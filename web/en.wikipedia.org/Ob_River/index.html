The Ob (Russian: Обь) is a major river in western Siberia, Russia, and the world's seventh-longest river system when combined with its main tributary, the Irtysh. It forms at the confluence of the Biya and Katun rivers in the foothills of the Altai Mountains and flows generally northward across the West Siberian Plain before emptying into the Gulf of Ob, an inlet of the Kara Sea in the Arctic Ocean.

Course

The Ob proper begins near Biysk in Altai Krai, where the Biya (flowing from Lake Teletskoye) and the Katun (fed by glaciers of Mount Belukha) join. From there the river flows northwest past Barnaul, then north through Novosibirsk — the largest city on its banks — and onward across a vast, swampy lowland. Near Khanty-Mansiysk it is joined by the Irtysh, its longest tributary, which rises in China and crosses Kazakhstan. Below the confluence the Ob turns northwest and then north, passing Salekhard near the Arctic Circle before discharging through a long estuary, the Gulf of Ob, into the Kara Sea.

Hydrology

The Ob drains a basin of nearly three million square kilometres, one of the largest in the world, covering much of western Siberia and parts of Kazakhstan and China. Because the river flows northward into a frozen sea, its lower reaches remain ice-covered for up to seven months a year, and spring thaw in the south while the north is still frozen causes extensive seasonal flooding across the floodplain, creating wetlands such as those of the Vasyugan Swamp region.

Economy and transport

The basin contains Russia's richest oil and natural gas fields, centred on the Khanty-Mansi and Yamalo-Nenets autonomous okrugs. The river is an important transport artery in summer, linking Siberian settlements that lack road access, and it is crossed by major rail bridges at Novosibirsk (on the Trans-Siberian Railway) and elsewhere. The Novosibirsk Hydroelectric Station, completed in the 1950s, is the river's principal dam.

Ecology

The Ob floodplain supports valuable fisheries, including sturgeon, nelma and muksun, though pollution from oil extraction and industrial centres has degraded water quality in parts of the basin. The estuary and delta wetlands are important breeding grounds for migratory waterfowl.
