Italian Social Republic

The Italian Social Republic (Italian: Repubblica Sociale Italiana, RSI), commonly known as the Republic of Salò, was a German puppet state in northern Italy headed by Benito Mussolini from September 1943 to April 1945. Established after Germany rescued Mussolini and occupied Italy following the Italian armistice with the Allies, it fought the Italian co-belligerent government in the south in a civil war alongside the wider Italian campaign.

Establishment

After the Grand Council of Fascism deposed Mussolini in July 1943 and Marshal Badoglio signed the Armistice of Cassibile with the Allies, German forces occupied northern and central Italy. A German airborne raid freed Mussolini from imprisonment at Gran Sasso on 12 September 1943, and he proclaimed the RSI days later, with ministries scattered around Lake Garda and its seat at Salò.

Government and war

The RSI's Republican Fascist Party promised a return to fascism's early "socializing" program; the Verona Manifesto (November 1943) declared Jews enemy nationals, and RSI police assisted in deportations. Its armed forces — the National Republican Army, the Black Brigades, and units such as the Decima MAS — fought Allied forces and, bitterly, the Italian resistance partisans in a civil war marked by reprisals and atrocities.

Collapse

In April 1945 the Allied spring offensive broke the Gothic Line. Mussolini fled Milan, was captured by partisans near Lake Como, and was executed on 28 April 1945. The RSI's forces surrendered with the general German capitulation in Italy, effective 2 May 1945.
