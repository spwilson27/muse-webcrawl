Earl of Kildare

Earl of Kildare is a title in the Peerage of Ireland held by the FitzGerald family, premier earls of Ireland. It was created in 1316 for John FitzThomas FitzGerald, 5th Baron of Offaly, by King Edward II. The Kildare FitzGeralds became the dominant Anglo-Irish magnate family, serving repeatedly as Lords Deputy and Justiciars of Ireland.

History

Gerald FitzGerald, 8th Earl of Kildare (c. 1456–1513), known as "the Great Earl" and "Garret Mór", effectively ruled Ireland for decades as Lord Deputy. His son, the 9th Earl ("Garret Óg"), continued the family's ascendancy until summoned to London and imprisoned in the Tower, where he died in 1534. The rebellion of his son, Thomas FitzGerald, 10th Earl ("Silken Thomas"), in 1534–35 led to the attainder of the earldom; it was restored to the 11th Earl in 1554.

In 1766 the 20th Earl was created Duke of Leinster in the Peerage of Ireland, and the Earldom of Kildare has since been held as a subsidiary title of the dukes, traditionally used as the courtesy title of the eldest son.
