Lake Placid

Lake Placid is a lake in the Adirondack Mountains in Essex County, New York, in the United States. The village of Lake Placid, famous for hosting the 1932 and 1980 Winter Olympics, sits not on this lake but on nearby Mirror Lake; the lake itself lies just north of the village and gives it its name.

Geography

The lake covers about 2,173 acres (8.8 km²) and reaches depths of about 46 m (151 ft). It lies at an elevation of 566 m (1,857 ft), drained at its northern end, and contains several islands, including Buck, Moose, and Hawk islands. Whiteface Mountain, one of the Adirondack High Peaks, rises to the northeast and is prominently visible across the water. The lake is a source of drinking water for the village and a center for boating, fishing, and lakeside camps.

History

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, wealthy families built "Great Camps" and summer estates along the lake's shores. The Lake Placid Club, founded by Melvil Dewey in 1895 on the shore of Mirror Lake, helped popularize winter sports in the region and led to the village's successful bids for the Winter Olympics of 1932 and 1980 — the latter famous for the "Miracle on Ice" hockey game.
