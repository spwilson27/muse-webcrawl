Gambling

Gambling is the wagering of something of value ("the stakes") on an event with an uncertain outcome, with the intent of winning something else of value. It requires three elements: consideration (an amount wagered), risk (chance), and a prize. Gambling dates to antiquity — dice and betting are attested in ancient China, Egypt, Greece, and Rome — and today encompasses casinos, lotteries, sports betting, poker, and online gambling.

Forms and industry

Major forms include casino games (slots, roulette, blackjack, baccarat), poker, lotteries and scratchcards, bingo, pari-mutuel betting on horse and dog racing, and fixed-odds sports betting. Casino hubs such as Las Vegas, Macau, Singapore, and Monte Carlo anchor a global industry worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually, increasingly conducted online since the 1990s.

Regulation and social issues

Because of concerns about addiction, fraud, and organized crime, gambling is heavily regulated or prohibited in many jurisdictions; some religions, notably Islam, forbid it. Operators' profits rest on the house edge — odds set slightly in the house's favor. Problem gambling is recognized as a behavioral addiction that can cause severe financial and personal harm, and regulators increasingly require responsible-gambling measures. Debates continue over advertising, online access, and the balance between tax revenue and social cost.
