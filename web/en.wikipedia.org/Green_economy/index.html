Green economy

Renewable energy is a core sector of the green economy.

A green economy is an economy aimed at reducing environmental risks and ecological scarcities while improving human well-being and social equity. In its 2011 report Towards a Green Economy, the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) defined it as one that is "low carbon, resource efficient and socially inclusive", in which growth in income and employment is driven by public and private investment that reduces emissions and pollution, enhances energy and resource efficiency, and prevents the loss of biodiversity and ecosystem services.

Concepts and sectors

The green economy overlaps with ecological economics and sustainable development but places emphasis on practical investment pathways: renewable energy, energy-efficient buildings, clean transport, sustainable agriculture and forestry, water management, and waste and recycling industries. Policy instruments include carbon pricing, subsidy reform, green public procurement, feed-in tariffs, and natural capital accounting that values ecosystem services alongside produced capital.

Debates

Critics from different directions argue that green-economy frameworks either rely too heavily on market mechanisms and technological optimism ("green growth"), or, conversely, that they understate the scale of intervention required. Proposals such as the European Green Deal and various "Green New Deal" plans draw on green-economy ideas, as do debates about green jobs and a "just transition" for workers in declining fossil-fuel industries.
