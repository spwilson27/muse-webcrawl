Renzo Piano

Renzo Piano (born 14 September 1937) is an Italian architect. His notable works include the Centre Pompidou in Paris (with Richard Rogers, 1977), The Shard in London (2012), the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York City (2015), and the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Cultural Center in Athens (2016). He won the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 1998.

Early life and career

Piano was born into a family of builders in Genoa and studied architecture at the Polytechnic University of Milan, graduating in 1964. He worked with Louis Kahn in Philadelphia and Z.S. Makowsky in London before winning, with Richard Rogers, the 1971 competition for the Centre Pompidou, whose exposed structure and services made both architects famous while still in their thirties.

Renzo Piano Building Workshop

Founded in 1981, Piano's practice has offices in Genoa, Paris and New York. Its buildings are noted for technological refinement, attention to craft and materials, and sensitivity to site and light. Major projects include the Menil Collection in Houston, Kansai International Airport in Osaka, the Jean-Marie Tjibaou Cultural Centre in New Caledonia, the California Academy of Sciences in San Francisco, and the new Genoa San Giorgio Bridge, built after the 2018 Morandi Bridge collapse.

Honours

Besides the Pritzker Prize, Piano received the AIA Gold Medal and was appointed a senator for life in the Italian Senate by President Giorgio Napolitano in 2013.
