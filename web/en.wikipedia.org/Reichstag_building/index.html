Reichstag building

The Reichstag with Norman Foster's glass dome, completed in 1999.

The Reichstag building (German: Reichstagsgebäude) is a historic government building in Berlin, Germany, constructed to house the Reichstag, the parliament of the German Empire. Designed by Paul Wallot in Neo-Renaissance style and built between 1884 and 1894, it stands on the Platz der Republik in the Tiergarten district, near the Brandenburg Gate.

History

The building's famous dedication "Dem Deutschen Volke" ("To the German People") was added to the frieze only in 1916. It was from a window of the building that Philipp Scheidemann proclaimed the German Republic on 9 November 1918. On 27 February 1933 the plenary chamber was gutted in the Reichstag fire, blamed by the Nazi government on communists; the ruin was further damaged in the Battle of Berlin in 1945, when Soviet soldiers raised the Red Flag over it in a famous photograph.

During the Cold War the partially restored building stood in West Berlin, just west of the Berlin Wall, and was used for exhibitions and occasional Bundestag sessions. After reunification, the Bundestag voted in 1991 to return to Berlin, and the building was comprehensively rebuilt to designs by Norman Foster (Foster + Partners), crowned by a new glass dome with a mirrored cone that funnels daylight into the chamber and offers public views over the city. Christo and Jeanne-Muse wrapped the building in fabric in 1995 before reconstruction began. The Bundestag held its first sitting in the rebuilt Reichstag on 19 April 1999.

The dome

The glass dome, 47 metres high in total structure terms, is open to the public via a double-helix ramp; registration is required. It has become one of Berlin's most visited landmarks and a symbol of transparent, reunified German democracy.
