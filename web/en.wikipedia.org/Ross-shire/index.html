Ross-shire
Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Rois
Country: Scotland
County town: Dingwall
Status: Historic county; registration county

Ross-shire (Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Rois) is a historic county in the Scottish Highlands, bordering Sutherland to the north, the North Sea (Moray Firth) to the east, Inverness-shire to the south, and the Atlantic-facing district of Wester Ross on the west. Its county town is Dingwall. In 1889 Ross-shire was merged for local government purposes with Cromartyshire to form Ross and Cromarty, though it remains a registration county and a lieutenancy area name in its own right.

Geography

The county divides naturally into Easter Ross — a relatively low-lying, fertile agricultural belt along the Moray, Cromarty, and Dornoch Firths, with towns including Dingwall, Invergordon, Tain, and Alness — and Wester Ross, a mountainous, sparsely populated Atlantic district of sea lochs and peaks such as Liathach and An Teallach, including the settlements of Ullapool, Gairloch, and the Applecross peninsula. The Black Isle peninsula, despite its name not an island, lies between the Moray and Cromarty Firths and is farmed intensively.

History

The area formed part of the Pictish kingdom, then the mormaerdom and earldom of Ross, a major medieval lordship associated with Clan Ross and Clan Mackenzie, whose chiefs, the Earls of Seaforth, dominated the west. The county suffered during the Jacobite risings and, more lastingly, in the Highland Clearances, when sheep farming displaced tenants — the Ross-shire sheep riots of 1792 were among the first organised popular resistances. Invergordon later became a naval base and, in the 1970s, an aluminium-smelting and oil-rig fabrication centre. The area also saw the Ross-shire by-election of 1918 and a notable Liberal and later SNP political tradition.

Modern use

Since 1996 Ross-shire lies within the Highland council area. The name survives in the parliamentary constituency of Ross, Skye and Lochaber (and successors), in the Ross-shire Journal newspaper, and in local usage distinguishing the east-coast communities from Wester Ross.
