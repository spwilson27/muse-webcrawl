Front-engine, rear-wheel-drive layout

FR layout
Engine positionFront
Driven wheelsRear

The front-engine, rear-wheel-drive layout (FR) is an automotive design in which the engine is mounted at the front of the vehicle and drives the rear wheels through a propeller shaft. It was the dominant layout for passenger cars through most of the twentieth century and remains common in sports cars, luxury cars, pickup trucks, and commercial vehicles.

Characteristics

Placing the engine ahead of the passenger cabin and driving the rear axle separates steering from propulsion, allowing larger, more powerful engines and giving weight distribution that many drivers consider favourable for handling and towing. The longitudinal propeller shaft and transmission tunnel intrude into cabin space, and rear traction on slippery surfaces can be poorer than with front-wheel drive. A transaxle variant places the gearbox at the rear for near-50:50 weight distribution.

History

Early automobiles adopted the FR layout from horse-drawn carriage conventions, and it was standardised by cars such as the Ford Model T. From the 1970s, front-wheel drive displaced it in mass-market cars for packaging and economy reasons, but FR persists where performance, load-carrying, or tradition matter — in vehicles ranging from the Mazda MX-5 to full-size pickup trucks.
