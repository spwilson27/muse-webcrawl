Gran Partita

The Serenade No. 10 for winds in B-flat major, K. 361/370a, commonly known as the Gran Partita, is a serenade by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart scored for thirteen wind instruments: two oboes, two clarinets, two basset horns, two bassoons, four horns, and double bass. Composed around 1781–1784, it is regarded as one of the masterpieces of wind ensemble music.

Composer: Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

Catalogue: K. 361/370a

Composed: c. 1781–1784

Key: B-flat major

Movements: 7

Scoring: 13 wind instruments and double bass

Background

The title "Gran Partita" appears on the autograph manuscript, but not in Mozart's hand. The dating of the work was long debated; scholars now generally place its composition in Vienna around 1781–1784, possibly partly in connection with a 1784 concert given by the clarinettist Anton Stadler, which included some of its movements. The inclusion of basset horns — instruments championed by Stadler — is a distinctive feature.

Music

The seven movements are: a stately Largo–Molto allegro; a Menuetto with two trios; the famous Adagio in E-flat major; a second Menuetto (Allegretto); a Romance (Adagio–Allegretto–Adagio); a set of variations (Tema con variazioni); and a lively Molto allegro finale. The third movement, the Adagio, became especially famous through its use in the play and film Amadeus, in which Salieri describes its opening as "the voice of God".

Legacy

The Gran Partita is a cornerstone of the wind repertoire and is frequently recorded and performed by major orchestras' wind sections and specialist ensembles. Its scale — nearly fifty minutes in performance — far exceeds that of typical divertimenti of the period.
