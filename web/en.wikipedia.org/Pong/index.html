Pong is a table tennis–themed arcade video game released by Atari in 1972. It was one of the earliest arcade video games and the first to achieve widespread commercial success, helping to establish the video game industry. The game features simple two-dimensional graphics: two paddles move vertically on opposite sides of the screen, batting a small square ball back and forth; a player scores when the opponent fails to return the ball.

Development

Pong was created by Atari engineer Allan Alcorn as a training exercise assigned by co-founder Nolan Bushnell, who had seen a demonstration of the table tennis game on the Magnavox Odyssey console. Bushnell and Ted Dabney decided Alcorn's version was good enough to manufacture. A prototype installed in a bar in Sunnyvale, California, proved so popular that its coin mechanism jammed with quarters, and Atari began full production.

Home version and clones

In 1975 Atari released a home version of Pong through Sears, which became one of the season's best-selling products. Its success spawned dozens of clones and dedicated Pong consoles from competitors, as well as Magnavox's patent litigation against Atari and others over the game's origins, which was settled out of court.

Legacy

Pong is widely regarded as a landmark in popular culture and computing history: it demonstrated that video games could be a mass-market business, and its minimalist design remains an icon of the medium. Original machines and source are preserved in museum collections, and the game has been re-released and referenced countless times.
