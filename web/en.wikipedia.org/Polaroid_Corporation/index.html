Polaroid Corporation

Founded: 1937

Founder: Edwin H. Land

Headquarters: Cambridge, Massachusetts (originally)

Products: Instant cameras and film, polarized lenses

A Polaroid instant camera producing a self-developing print.

The Polaroid Corporation was an American company best known for its instant film and cameras. It was founded in 1937 by Edwin H. Land, who had earlier developed inexpensive polarizing filters for sunglasses and other applications.

Instant photography

In 1948 Polaroid introduced the first commercial instant camera, the Land Camera Model 95, which produced sepia-toned prints inside the camera in about a minute. The system was refined through folding cameras such as the SX-70 (1972), whose self-developing colour film required no peeling apart, and the OneStep cameras of the late 1970s, which became cultural icons. At its peak in the late 20th century, Polaroid employed tens of thousands of people and generated billions of dollars in annual revenue.

Decline and legacy

The company failed to adapt to digital photography, and the original Polaroid Corporation filed for bankruptcy in 2001; its assets and brand were sold. The brand has since passed through several owners and is used on a variety of consumer electronics. Production of instant film, halted in 2008, was revived by enthusiasts through the Impossible Project, which later acquired the Polaroid brand and relaunched as Polaroid, continuing to sell instant cameras and film under the classic name.
