The 1970 Formula One season was the 24th season of FIA Formula One motor racing. It featured the 21st World Championship of Drivers and the 13th International Cup for F1 Manufacturers, contested over thirteen races from 7 March to 25 October 1970.

Season summary

Austrian driver Jochen Rindt won five races in Colin Chapman's innovative Lotus 72, which moved the radiators to the sides of the car and introduced inboard brakes and wedge-shaped bodywork. Rindt was killed in a crash during practice for the Italian Grand Prix at Monza on 5 September, after a brake shaft failure pitched his car into the barriers; he had not been wearing his crotch straps and slid beneath his seat belts.

Despite missing the final rounds, Rindt's points total was not overtaken — Ferrari's Jacky Ickx came closest but fell short — and Rindt became, and remains, Formula One's only posthumous World Champion. Lotus also secured the Constructors' title.

Other developments

Jackie Stewart, the defending champion, struggled with the March 701 before Tyrrell began building its own cars late in the season. The year also saw the debut of future champion Emerson Fittipaldi, who won the United States Grand Prix at Watkins Glen — the victory that confirmed Rindt's title — and the final Grand Prix victory of Jack Brabham's team in South Africa.

The season was overshadowed by further deaths, including Bruce McLaren, killed testing a Can-Am car at Goodwood in June, and Piers Courage, killed at the Dutch Grand Prix.
