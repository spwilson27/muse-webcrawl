Cornelis Lely (23 September 1854 – 22 January 1929) was a Dutch civil engineer and statesman best known as the designer of the Zuiderzee Works, the vast Dutch project that dammed off the Zuiderzee inlet of the North Sea, creating the freshwater IJsselmeer and enabling the reclamation of the great Flevoland polders. The city of Lelystad, capital of the province of Flevoland, is named after him.

Cornelis Lely, engineer of the Zuiderzee reclamation.

Career

Lely studied civil engineering at the Polytechnic School of Delft, graduating in 1875. As a young engineer of the state water service he surveyed and, in 1891, published the technical plan that became the blueprint for enclosing the Zuiderzee. He served three times as Minister of Water Management (1891–1894, 1897–1901, and 1913–1918), and as Governor-General of Surinam from 1902 to 1905, where he promoted the Lawa railway.

The Zuiderzee Act

After the catastrophic floods of January 1916, parliament passed the Zuiderzee Act of 14 June 1918, for which Lely, again minister, had campaigned for decades. The act authorised the enclosing dam — the Afsluitdijk, completed in 1932, three years after his death — and the creation of new polders that ultimately added some 1,650 km² of land. Statues of Lely stand on the Afsluitdijk and in Lelystad, and he appears on Dutch stamps commemorating the works.
