Trans-Alaska Pipeline System

Length800 miles (1,287 km)
Opened1977
RoutePrudhoe Bay to Valdez

The Trans-Alaska Pipeline System (TAPS) is an oil transportation system spanning Alaska, including the trans-Alaska crude-oil pipeline, 11 pump stations, several hundred miles of feeder pipelines, and the Valdez Marine Terminal. The pipeline is a major component of the oil infrastructure of Alaska and one of the world's largest pipeline systems.

Construction

Following the 1968 discovery of oil at Prudhoe Bay, the pipeline was authorised by the Trans-Alaska Pipeline Authorization Act of 1973 and built between 1974 and 1977 at a cost of about $8 billion. Much of the 48-inch pipe is elevated on vertical support members to protect the permafrost from the heat of the oil and to allow caribou migration.

Operation

Peak flow exceeded two million barrels per day in 1988; throughput has since declined as North Slope production fell. The 1989 Exxon Valdez spill involved a tanker departing the Valdez terminal. Alyeska Pipeline Service Company operates the system for its owner companies.
