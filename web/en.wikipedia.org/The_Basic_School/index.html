The Basic School


Officer candidates and newly commissioned officers train at The Basic School in Quantico, Virginia.

The Basic School (TBS) is where all newly commissioned and appointed (for warrant officers) United States Marine Corps officers are taught the basics of being an "Officer of Marines". The Basic School is located at Marine Corps Base Quantico, in Quantico, Virginia, in the southwestern part of the base. Each year over 1,700 new officers are trained, representing such commissioning sources as the U.S. Naval Academy, Naval ROTC, and Officer Candidates School.

History

The Basic School traces its origins to the earliest formal training of Marine officers. After the First World War, the Marine Corps recognized the need for a standardized school to prepare newly commissioned lieutenants for service with the Fleet Marine Force. A school for new officers was established at Quantico, and although its name, location, and curriculum changed over the following decades, the institution grew into the modern Basic School. By the era of the Vietnam War, Quantico had become the permanent home of entry-level officer training for the Marine Corps.

Training

The Basic Officer Course is approximately 28 weeks long. Its stated mission is to train and educate newly commissioned or appointed officers in the high standards of professional knowledge, esprit de corps, and leadership required to prepare them for duty as company grade officers in the operating forces. Students learn infantry skills regardless of their eventual military occupational specialty: every Marine officer is first trained as a provisional rifle platoon commander. Instruction covers land navigation, patrolling, military operations in urban terrain, communications, weapons handling, and the fundamentals of offensive and defensive operations.

The Warfighting Laboratory ethos

TBS emphasizes the Marine Corps philosophy of maneuver warfare, as articulated in the doctrinal publication Warfighting. Student officers are graded on leadership, academics, and military skills, and their performance influences the assignment of their military occupational specialties at the end of the course.
