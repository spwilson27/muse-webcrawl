Flare (countermeasure)

A flare or decoy flare is an aerial infrared countermeasure used by military aircraft to counter infrared-homing ("heat-seeking") surface-to-air missiles or air-to-air missiles. Flares are commonly ejected in salvoes when the pilot feels threatened, or in pre-emptive programmes during vulnerable phases of flight.

Mechanism

A flare consists of a pyrotechnic composition, often based on magnesium or another hot-burning metal with a fluoropolymer binder such as Teflon (a magnesium/Teflon/Viton, or MTV, composition). When ignited, the flare burns at a temperature hotter than the aircraft's engine exhaust, presenting a brighter infrared target that seduces the missile's seeker away from the aircraft.

Deployment

Flares are housed in dispensers such as the AN/ALE-47 and are released individually or in pre-programmed sequences. Modern systems can be cued automatically by missile approach warning sensors. Because early flares burned mainly in the infrared band matched to first-generation seekers, newer "spectral" flares are formulated to mimic the spectral signature of jet exhaust more closely.

Limitations

Advanced missiles use two-colour seekers, imaging sensors, and kinematic discrimination to reject flares. Flares also pose a fire risk to the ground when used over populated or forested areas, which has led to restrictions on their use in training.
