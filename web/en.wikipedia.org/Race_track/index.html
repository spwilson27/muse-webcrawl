Race track
Purpose: Vehicle or animal racing
Types: Oval, road course, drag strip, dirt track
Surfaces: Asphalt, concrete, dirt, grass, ice

A race track is a facility built for racing of vehicles, athletes or animals — including motor racing circuits, horse racing courses and greyhound tracks, as well as running and cycling tracks. Purpose-built tracks typically provide spectator grandstands, pit or stable facilities and controlled access, distinguishing them from races held on closed public roads or natural terrain.

Motor racing circuits

Motor racing venues fall into several families. Oval tracks, such as Indianapolis Motor Speedway and Daytona, are closed loops of banked turns used by IndyCar and NASCAR. Road courses wind through corners of varying difficulty on permanent circuits like Silverstone or Spa-Francorchamps, or on temporary street circuits such as Monaco. Drag strips are short straight courses for acceleration contests, and dirt tracks host sprint cars and motorcycle speedway.

Horse and animal racing

Horse racecourses are usually turf or dirt ovals, straight courses or irregular circuits, with famous examples including Ascot, Churchill Downs and Flemington. Greyhound tracks are smaller ovals surrounding mechanical-lure racing.

Safety

Modern circuit design emphasises run-off areas, energy-absorbing barriers and gravel traps; safety standards advanced greatly after the fatal accidents of the mid-twentieth century.
