Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk (opera)

ComposerDmitri Shostakovich
LibrettoAlexander Preys and the composer
Based onLady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District by Nikolai Leskov
Premiere22 January 1934, Maly Theatre, Leningrad

Katerina Ismailova, the opera's tragic heroine

Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk (Russian: Леди Макбет Мценского уезда, Op. 29) is an opera in four acts and nine scenes by Dmitri Shostakovich, composed between 1930 and 1932 to a libretto by Alexander Preys and the composer, adapted from Nikolai Leskov's 1865 novella Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District.

Plot

Katerina Ismailova, the bored and oppressed wife of the provincial merchant Zinovy Ismailov, takes the farm labourer Sergei as her lover. Aided by Sergei, she murders her brutal father-in-law Boris and then her husband. The couple are exposed at their wedding feast by a drunken peasant who discovers Zinovy's corpse, and are sentenced to penal exile in Siberia. On the march, Sergei abandons Katerina for another convict, Sonietka; Katerina attacks her rival and both women drown in a river as the convict column moves on.

Reception and suppression

The opera was an immediate international success, performed nearly 200 times in the Soviet Union within two years and staged abroad under titles including "Katerina Ismailova". In January 1936 Stalin attended a performance, and days later Pravda published the unsigned editorial "Muddle Instead of Music", denouncing the opera as formalist, coarse and anti-Soviet. Shostakovich withdrew the work; it was not performed again in the USSR for nearly three decades. A revised, toned-down version, Katerina Ismailova (Op. 29/114), premiered in Moscow in 1963. After the composer's death the original version was restored to the stage and is now regarded as one of the great operas of the twentieth century.
