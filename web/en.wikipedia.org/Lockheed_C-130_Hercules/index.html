The Lockheed C-130 Hercules is an American four-engine turboprop military transport aircraft designed and built by Lockheed (now Lockheed Martin). Capable of taking off and landing on rough, unprepared strips, it was designed as a troop, medical-evacuation, and cargo transport, and has become the main tactical airlifter for military forces worldwide. Continuously produced since 1954, it has the longest production run of any military aircraft and has served in more than 60 countries.

Design and development

The C-130 resulted from a 1951 U.S. Air Force requirement for a transport able to carry heavy loads into short forward airstrips. Lockheed's design featured a high wing, four Allison T56 turboprops, a rear loading ramp giving drive-through access to a large unobstructed hold, and rugged multi-wheel landing gear. The first YC-130 flew on 23 August 1954, and deliveries began in 1956. The modernized C-130J Super Hercules, first flown in 1996, added Rolls-Royce AE 2100 engines with six-blade composite propellers, a glass cockpit, and reduced crew.

Operational history

Hercules aircraft have flown in virtually every major conflict and humanitarian operation since the 1950s, from the Vietnam War—including the largest evacuation of its kind—to supplying Berlin-era Europe, Antarctic science stations (ski-equipped LC-130s), and disaster relief around the globe. A C-130 holds the record for the largest and heaviest aircraft ever to land on and take off from an aircraft carrier, demonstrated aboard USS Forrestal in 1963. The type also underpins many special variants: the AC-130 gunship, the HC-130 and MC-130 special-operations and rescue aircraft, the EC-130 electronic-warfare platform, the KC-130 tanker, and weather-reconnaissance "Hurricane Hunters." A civilian version, the L-100, has served as a commercial freighter.

Characteristics

Typical C-130J figures: length 29.8 m (97 ft 9 in), wingspan 40.4 m (132 ft 7 in), maximum speed about 670 km/h (362 knots), range roughly 3,300 km with maximum payload, and a service ceiling around 8,500 m (28,000 ft). Earlier models are slightly shorter and slower. The aircraft can airdrop paratroops and loads ranging from pallets to armoured vehicles, and extract cargo at low altitude by parachute systems.
