Bob Marley

Robert Nesta Marley (6 February 1945 – 11 May 1981) was a Jamaican singer, musician, and songwriter. Considered one of the pioneers of reggae, he fused elements of reggae, ska, and rocksteady in his music and was renowned for his distinctive vocal and songwriting style. Marley increased the visibility of Jamaican music worldwide, and became a global figure in popular culture. He became known as a Rastafari icon, and he infused his music with a sense of spirituality. He is also considered a global symbol of Jamaican music and culture and was awarded the United Nations Peace Medal of the Third World in 1978.

Early life and The Wailers

Marley was born in Nine Mile, Saint Ann Parish, Jamaica, to Norval Marley, a white Jamaican of English descent, and Cedella Booker, an Afro-Jamaican singer and writer. He grew up in poverty in the Kingston ghetto of Trenchtown. In the early 1960s he formed the vocal group that became The Wailers with Peter Tosh, Bunny Wailer, and others, recording first ska, then rocksteady and reggae, for producers including Coxsone Dodd at Studio One and later Lee "Scratch" Perry.

International breakthrough

Signed to Chris Blackwell's Island Records, The Wailers released Catch a Fire and Burnin' in 1973, the latter including "Get Up, Stand Up" and "I Shot the Sheriff," the latter made famous by Eric Clapton's cover. After Tosh and Bunny Wailer left, Marley continued with a new line-up as Bob Marley and the Wailers, achieving commercial breakthrough with Natty Dread (1974), Rastaman Vibration (1976), and Exodus (1977)—named by Time magazine the album of the century—which included "Jamming," "Waiting in Vain," "One Love," and "Three Little Birds."

Later years and death

In December 1976 Marley survived an assassination attempt at his home in Kingston, days before the Smile Jamaica concert; he performed anyway and soon moved to London. In 1978 he returned to Jamaica for the One Love Peace Concert, where he famously joined the hands of rival political leaders Michael Manley and Edward Seaga. Albums including Kaya (1978), Survival (1979), and Uprising (1980)—featuring "Redemption Song" and "Could You Be Loved"—confirmed his stature. Diagnosed with acral lentiginous melanoma under a toenail, Marley died in Miami on 11 May 1981, aged 36, and was given a state funeral in Jamaica.

Legacy

The posthumous compilation Legend (1984) is the best-selling reggae album of all time, with tens of millions of copies sold. Marley was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1994 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2001. His songs of protest, faith, and love remain central to the global image of reggae and Rastafari.
