Sport bike

A sport bike, or sports motorcycle, is a motorcycle designed and optimised for speed, acceleration, braking, and cornering on asphalt concrete race tracks and roads. They are mainly designed for performance at the expense of comfort, fuel economy, safety, noise reduction, and storage in comparison with other motorcycles.

Characteristics

Sport bikes typically feature full fairings for aerodynamics, low clip-on handlebars that place the rider in a forward-leaning posture, high-mounted rear-set footpegs, powerful multi-cylinder engines in aluminium beam frames, strong brakes and lightweight wheels. Engine sizes range from entry-level 300 cc machines to 1,000 cc "superbikes" producing over 200 horsepower.

History

The category coalesced in the 1970s and 1980s with machines such as the Honda CB750, Kawasaki Z1, Suzuki GSX-R750 (1985) — a landmark of light weight and race-replica design — and the Honda CBR900RR FireBlade (1992). Modern superbikes add sophisticated rider aids including traction control, launch control, quickshifters and cornering ABS.

Classes

The market is conventionally divided into lightweight (up to ~500 cc), middleweight (600–750 cc "supersport") and litre-class superbikes, plus exotic "hyperbikes" such as the Kawasaki Ninja H2. Homologated production sport bikes form the basis of Superbike and Supersport racing championships.
