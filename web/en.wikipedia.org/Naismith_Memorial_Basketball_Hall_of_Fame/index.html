Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame

Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame
Established: 1959
Location: Springfield, Massachusetts, United States
Named for: James Naismith
First inductees: 1959

The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame is an American history museum and hall of fame, located at 1000 Hall of Fame Avenue in Springfield, Massachusetts. It serves as basketball's most complete library, in addition to promoting and preserving the history of basketball. Dedicated to Canadian-American physician and inventor James Naismith, who invented the sport in Springfield in 1891, it was established in 1959.

History

The Hall of Fame was founded in 1959, although it did not open a physical museum until 1968 on the campus of Springfield College. A larger building opened in 1985, and the current hall — a distinctive sphere-topped structure on the Connecticut River — opened in September 2002.

Inductees

As of the early 2020s, the Hall has inducted more than 400 individuals. Unlike the halls of fame for other major American sports, the Basketball Hall of Fame honors the game globally, inducting players, coaches, referees, contributors and teams from professional, collegiate, amateur, women's and international basketball. An individual is eligible for players five years after retirement (later reduced), and finalists are screened by committees before election. Famous inductees include Michael Jordan, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Larry Bird, Magic Johnson, Bill Russell and coach John Wooden.

Museum

The museum includes exhibits on the history of the game, a full-size center court where visitors can shoot, and displays of memorabilia such as Naismith's original 1891 rules of basketball.
