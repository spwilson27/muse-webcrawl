Aortic dissection
SpecialtyCardiology, cardiothoracic surgery
SymptomsSudden tearing chest or back pain, syncope, stroke symptoms
Risk factorsHypertension, Marfan syndrome, bicuspid aortic valve, atherosclerosis
PrognosisLife-threatening; requires emergency treatment

Aortic dissection (AD) occurs when an injury to the innermost layer of the aorta allows blood to flow between the layers of the aortic wall, forcing them apart. In most cases it is associated with a sudden onset of severe chest or back pain, often described as "tearing" in character. Vomiting, sweating, and lightheadedness may also occur.

Causes and classification

The tear typically arises where the aortic wall is weakened by long-standing high blood pressure, atherosclerosis, connective-tissue disorders such as Marfan syndrome and Ehlers–Danlos syndrome, or a bicuspid aortic valve. Under the Stanford classification, type A dissections involve the ascending aorta and are a surgical emergency; type B dissections begin in the descending aorta and may be managed medically if uncomplicated.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually made by CT angiography, which shows an intimal flap separating the true and false lumens; transoesophageal echocardiography and MRI are alternatives. Type A dissection requires immediate surgery to replace the affected aorta, because rupture and death can occur within hours. Type B dissection is treated primarily with aggressive control of blood pressure and heart rate, with endovascular stent-grafting reserved for complications.
