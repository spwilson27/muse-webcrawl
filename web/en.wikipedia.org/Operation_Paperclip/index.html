Operation Paperclip was a secret United States intelligence program in which more than 1,600 German scientists, engineers, and technicians were taken from former Nazi Germany to the US for government employment after the end of World War II in Europe, between 1945 and 1959. Conducted by the Joint Intelligence Objectives Agency (JIOA), it was largely carried out by special agents of the US Army's Counterintelligence Corps.

The primary purpose for Operation Paperclip was US military advantage in the developing Cold War and the Space Race against the Soviet Union, which ran its own parallel recruitment, Operation Osoaviakhim. Paperclips were attached to the dossiers of desirable recruits — hence the name.

Background and the rocket team

Many recruits had been members of, and some were leaders in, the Nazi Party. The best-known group was the V-2 rocket team from Peenemünde, led by Wernher von Braun, which was first stationed at Fort Bliss, Texas, and later at Redstone Arsenal in Huntsville, Alabama. Von Braun's team developed the Redstone and Jupiter missiles and later, under NASA, the Saturn V rocket that carried Apollo astronauts to the Moon.

Controversy

President Truman had authorised the programme but stipulated that it exclude anyone found to have been "a member of the Nazi Party and more than a nominal participant in its activities, or an active supporter of Nazism or militarism". In practice, dossiers were sometimes altered ("bleached") to remove evidence of Nazi affiliations so that scientists could pass immigration requirements. The programme remained classified until the 1970s and continues to be debated for its ethical compromises.
