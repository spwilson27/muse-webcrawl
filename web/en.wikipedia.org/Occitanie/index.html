Occitanie (Occitan: Occitània) is an administrative region of France, created in 2016 by merging the former regions of Languedoc-Roussillon and Midi-Pyrénées. It is the second-largest region of metropolitan France by area, stretching from the Rhône delta and the Mediterranean coast (including Montpellier, Nîmes, and Perpignan) westward across the Pyrenees and the southern Massif Central to Toulouse, its prefecture and largest city.

Geography

The region borders Spain and Andorra along the Pyrenees to the south, Nouvelle-Aquitaine to the west, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes to the north, and Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur to the east. It includes Mediterranean coastline, the Camargue wetlands, the Cévennes National Park (a UNESCO biosphere reserve and dark-sky reserve), and high Pyrenean peaks such as Vignemale.

Name and identity

The name was adopted in 2016 after a public consultation, ahead of alternatives such as "Languedoc-Pyrénées". It invokes Occitania, the historic area of southern Europe where the Occitan language (lenga d'òc) was spoken—the land of the medieval troubadours, devastated in the 13th century by the Albigensian Crusade. Occitan survives today as a minority language, promoted by cultural movements and taught in some schools.

Economy

Toulouse is the centre of the European aerospace industry, home to Airbus headquarters and a large space sector. The region also has major wine production (Languedoc is among the world's largest wine-growing areas), tourism along the Mediterranean and in Carcassonne's medieval cité, and significant agriculture and research sectors.
