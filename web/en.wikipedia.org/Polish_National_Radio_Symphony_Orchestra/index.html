Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra

Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra (NOSPR)

Founded: 1945

Location: Katowice, Poland

Principal hall: NOSPR Concert Hall (opened 2014)

The NOSPR performs in its acclaimed Katowice concert hall.

The Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra (Polish: Narodowa Orkiestra Symfoniczna Polskiego Radia, NOSPR) is one of Poland's leading symphony orchestras, based in Katowice. It was founded in 1945 by the conductor Witold Rowicki, building on a pre-war ensemble of Polish Radio in Warsaw that had been directed by Grzegorz Fitelberg.

History

For decades the orchestra was closely associated with Katowice's musical life and with Polish contemporary music, premiering and recording works by composers such as Witold Lutosławski, Henryk Górecki, and Krzysztof Penderecki, and collaborating with the Warsaw Autumn festival. Its music directors have included Jan Krenz, Antoni Wit (1983–2000), and Alexander Liebreich (from 2019).

Concert hall

In October 2014 the orchestra moved into a purpose-built home, the NOSPR Concert Hall in Katowice's Culture Zone, on the site of a former coal mine. The main hall seats about 1,800 and its acoustics, designed by Nagata Acoustics under Yasuhisa Toyota, have been widely praised; the building is clad in brick in reference to the Silesian mining districts.

Recordings and tours

The orchestra has made extensive recordings for Polish Radio and commercial labels, including complete cycles of Polish symphonic repertoire, and tours regularly in Europe and Asia.
