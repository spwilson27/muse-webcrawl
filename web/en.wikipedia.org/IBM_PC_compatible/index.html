IBM PC compatible

IBM PC compatible computers are technically capable of using the same software and expansion cards as the original IBM Personal Computer and its successors. Descended from the IBM PC introduced in 1981, PC compatibles from companies such as Compaq, Dell, and HP came to dominate the personal computer market, and the term eventually gave way to simply "PC."

Origins

IBM's use of off-the-shelf components and a published technical reference allowed third parties to clone the machine. Compaq produced the first portable compatible in 1983 after clean-room reverse-engineering the IBM BIOS; Phoenix Technologies and others then sold legal clone BIOS firmware, opening the market.

Evolution

Compatibles followed Intel x86 processors and Microsoft operating systems — the "Wintel" platform. IBM's attempt to retake control with the PS/2 and Micro Channel architecture in 1987 largely failed, and industry standards bodies set the direction thereafter. Modern PCs descend architecturally from the compatible tradition.
