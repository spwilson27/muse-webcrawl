All-rounder

An all-rounder is a cricketer who regularly performs well at both batting and bowling. Although all cricketers must bat, and many try their hand at bowling, genuine all-rounders are rare because a player must reach a high standard in two quite different disciplines. A player who excels mainly as a wicket-keeper and batter is usually distinguished as a wicket-keeper-batter.

A true all-rounder contributes with both bat and ball.

Definitions

A common statistical test is that a player's batting average exceeds his bowling average. By this measure, few players in Test history qualify comfortably; those who do include Garfield Sobers, often considered the greatest all-rounder, Jacques Kallis, Imran Khan, Ian Botham, Kapil Dev, Richard Hadlee, and Shakib Al Hasan. "Bits-and-pieces" players, useful in limited-overs cricket for contributing modestly in several roles, are sometimes distinguished from true all-rounders.

Value to a team

An all-rounder effectively gives a team an extra player, allowing flexible combinations — for example, fielding five specialist bowlers without weakening the batting order, or playing an extra batter while retaining bowling depth. Bowling all-rounders and batting all-rounders are classified according to which skill is stronger.
