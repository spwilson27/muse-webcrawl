Breaking, also known as b-boying, b-girling, or breakdancing, is an athletic street dance style that emerged from hip hop culture in the Bronx, New York City, in the early 1970s. This article concerns the dance form itself, its movement vocabulary, and its development; its competitive and Olympic history is covered under the wider heading of breakdancing.

Movement vocabulary

Breaking consists of four primary elements. Toprock is performed from a standing position and opens a dancer's round. Downrock (footwork) is done on the floor, with the dancer supported by hands while the feet move in steps such as the six-step. Power moves are continuous spinning motions — including the windmill, flare, air flare, and headspin — that demand momentum, strength, and control. Freezes are held poses, often on the hands, elbows, or head, used to punctuate the music.

Battles and cyphers

Dancers gather in a circle called a cypher, taking turns to enter and perform. In battles, individuals or crews compete head-to-head, answering each other's rounds, and judges assess originality, technique, execution, and musicality.

Influences

Breaking absorbed elements of salsa, Afro-Cuban dance, martial arts films, gymnastics, and earlier African American social dances. Its foundational ethos — "each one teach one" — emphasises originality and discourages copying, known within the culture as "biting".
