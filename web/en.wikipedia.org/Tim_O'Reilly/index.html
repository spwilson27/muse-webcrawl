Tim O'Reilly

Tim O'Reilly (born 6 June 1954) is an Irish-American author and publisher, the founder of O'Reilly Media (formerly O'Reilly & Associates), a company best known for its technical books with animal designs on the covers. He is credited with popularising the terms "open source" (through the 1998 summit he organised that adopted the term) and "Web 2.0", and has been an influential commentator on technology culture and the social effects of computing.

Early life and publishing

Born in Cork, Ireland, O'Reilly emigrated to the United States as an infant and grew up in San Francisco. He graduated from Harvard College in 1975 with a degree in classics. After working as a technical writer, he founded his company in 1978, initially as a technical-writing consultancy, which moved into publishing manuals — first for Unix — in the 1980s. The company's books on Unix, the X Window System, Perl (the "Camel Book"), and later the internet and open-source software became standard references, and its 1992 Whole Internet User's Guide and Catalog helped introduce the early internet to a general audience.

Advocacy and later work

O'Reilly was a prominent advocate of open-source software and of the open, participatory web characterised as Web 2.0 in a 2005 essay and conference series. He championed the maker movement as a board member of Maker Media (publisher of Make magazine and organiser of Maker Faire), supported "government as a platform" and civic technology through Code for America, and campaigned against software patents and in debates over internet regulation. His 2017 book WTF?: What's the Future and Why It's Up to Us examines the effects of platform monopolies and algorithmic management on work and society.
