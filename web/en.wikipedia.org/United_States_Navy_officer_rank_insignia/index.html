United States Navy officer rank insignia

United States Navy officer rank insignia are worn by commissioned officers of the U.S. Navy on shoulder boards, sleeve stripes of the dress blue uniform, and collar devices. Line officers are distinguished by a star above the stripes, while staff corps officers wear corps devices in its place.

Navy officer ranks
GradesO-1 (Ensign) through O-10 (Admiral); Fleet Admiral (O-11, wartime)
Sleeve systemGold stripes: number and width denote grade
Line deviceGold five-pointed star, one point down

Structure

Junior officers comprise Ensign (one stripe), Lieutenant (junior grade) (one stripe with a half-stripe), and Lieutenant (two stripes). Lieutenant Commander adds a half-stripe; Commander wears three stripes; Captain wears four. Flag officers wear a single broad stripe with one to four small stripes and stars on shoulder boards according to grade — Rear Admiral (lower and upper half), Vice Admiral and Admiral. The five-star grade of Fleet Admiral was created in 1944 and has not been held since 1950.

Staff corps

Officers of the Medical, Dental, Nurse, Supply, Chaplain, Civil Engineer, Judge Advocate General's and other staff corps replace the line star with their corps insignia above the stripes.

Warrant officers

Chief warrant officers wear distinctive devices combining stripes with blue enamel breaks, and limited duty officers follow the same rank structure as other commissioned officers.
