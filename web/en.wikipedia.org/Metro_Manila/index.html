Metro Manila

Metro Manila, officially the National Capital Region (NCR), is the metropolitan area and seat of government of the Philippines. It is composed of 16 highly urbanised cities and one municipality: the City of Manila, Quezon City, Caloocan, Las Piñas, Makati, Malabon, Mandaluyong, Marikina, Muntinlupa, Navotas, Parañaque, Pasay, Pasig, San Juan, Taguig, Valenzuela, and Pateros.

Population and geography

With a population of roughly 13.5 million (2020 census) in an area of about 620 km², Metro Manila is one of the most densely populated metropolitan areas in the world. It lies on an isthmus between Manila Bay to the west and Laguna de Bay to the southeast, bisected by the Pasig River.

Government

The region is administered by the Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA), created in 1995; each component city retains its own local government. The NCR was established in 1975 under Presidential Decree No. 824.

Economy and transport

Metro Manila generates more than a third of the Philippines' GDP. It is served by Ninoy Aquino International Airport, the LRT and MRT rail systems, and major expressways; traffic congestion is a persistent challenge. Quezon City is the most populous city, while Makati and Bonifacio Global City in Taguig are the principal financial districts.
