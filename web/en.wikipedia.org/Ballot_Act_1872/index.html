The Ballot Act 1872 (35 & 36 Vict. c. 33) was an act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that introduced the requirement for parliamentary and local government elections in the United Kingdom to be held by secret ballot.

Background

Before 1872 British voting was public: electors announced their choice aloud for entry in a poll book, a system open to bribery, intimidation by landlords and employers, and riotous disorder. The secret ballot had been a demand of the Chartists' People's Charter and had been adopted in colonial Australia in 1856.

Passage and effect

Introduced by Edward Forster in Gladstone's Liberal government, the bill passed after substantial debate. The first election under the act was the Pontefract by-election of August 1872, which used a ballot box still preserved in Pontefract Museum. Secret voting sharply reduced corrupt practices and paved the way for the Corrupt and Illegal Practices Prevention Act 1883.
