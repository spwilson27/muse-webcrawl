Identity formation

Identity formation is a central developmental task of adolescence.

Identity formation, also called identity development or individuation, is the complex process by which human beings develop a distinct personality and a clear sense of who they are. Psychologist Erik Erikson placed the development of identity at the center of his theory of psychosocial development, arguing that the principal task of adolescence is resolving the crisis of "identity versus role confusion," during which young people explore alternatives in occupation, ideology, and relationships before making commitments.

Marcia's identity statuses

Building on Erikson, James Marcia described four identity statuses based on the presence or absence of exploration (crisis) and commitment: identity achievement (exploration followed by commitment), moratorium (active exploration without commitment), foreclosure (commitment adopted from others without exploration), and identity diffusion (neither exploration nor commitment). Later research refined these with dual-cycle models in which commitments are continually re-evaluated across the lifespan.

Dimensions of identity

Identity formation spans many domains, including ethnic and cultural identity, gender identity, vocational identity, and religious identity. It is shaped by family, peers, school, and wider social context, and although it intensifies during adolescence, researchers now view it as a lifelong process of construction and revision.
