Plymouth Road Runner

The Plymouth Road Runner is a mid-size car with a focus on performance built by Plymouth in the United States between 1968 and 1980. By 1968, some of the original muscle cars were moving away from their roots as relatively cheap, fast cars as they gained features and increased in price. Plymouth developed the Road Runner to market a lower-priced, basic trim model to its existing Belvedere line.

Development

Plymouth paid Warner Bros. a licensing fee to use the Road Runner cartoon character and its "beep beep" sound; the horn was specially developed to mimic the cartoon sound. The concept was simple: a back-to-basics performance car with a strong engine, heavy-duty drivetrain and brakes, minimal trim, and a low price—under $3,000 at launch. The standard engine was a 383 cu in (6.3 L) "Roadrunner" V8 rated at 335 hp, with the 426 Hemi available as an option.

Success

The Road Runner far exceeded sales expectations, selling some 45,000 units in its first year and becoming one of the most successful muscle cars of its era; Motor Trend named it Car of the Year for 1969. The 1970 Plymouth Superbird, with its aerodynamic nose cone and tall rear wing, was developed to compete in NASCAR and lure Richard Petty back to Plymouth. Later years saw the model shift with the market: declining power under emissions rules, a move to the Fury line in 1975, and finally a trim package on the compact Volaré from 1976 to 1980.
