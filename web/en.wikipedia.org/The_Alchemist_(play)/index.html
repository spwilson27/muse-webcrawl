The Alchemist (play)

The Alchemist is a comedy by English playwright Ben Jonson, first performed in 1610 by the King's Men. It is generally considered Jonson's best and most characteristic comedy, a satire on human folly and greed in which a trio of con artists fleeces a parade of gullible Londoners.

AuthorBen Jonson
First performed1610
GenreComedy
SettingLondon, during a plague year

Plot

While his master Lovewit is away from London to escape the plague, the butler Jeremy — as "Captain Face" — joins with the con man Subtle and the prostitute Dol Common to run scams from the house. Their victims include the clerk Dapper, the tobacconist Abel Drugger, the Puritans Tribulation Wholesome and Ananias, and the would-be grandee Sir Epicure Mammon, all promised riches from the philosopher's stone. The schemes collapse in farcical disorder when Lovewit unexpectedly returns.

Legacy

The play's tightly constructed plot, observing the classical unities within a single day and house, has been admired since the Restoration, and it remains frequently revived. Samuel Taylor Coleridge praised its plot as one of the three most perfect in literature.
