Auguste and Louis Lumière

Auguste Lumière: 19 October 1862 – 10 April 1954

Louis Lumière: 5 October 1864 – 6 June 1948

Nationality: French

Known for: Cinématographe; first motion-picture screenings

Auguste Marie Louis Nicolas Lumière and Louis Jean Lumière, known as the Lumière brothers, were French manufacturers of photography equipment and pioneers of cinema. Working in their father's photographic business in Lyon, they developed the Cinématographe, a device that functioned as a camera, printer and projector in one, patented in February 1895.

First films

On 28 December 1895, at the Salon Indien of the Grand Café in Paris, the brothers held the first commercial public screening of projected motion pictures before a paying audience — an event often regarded as the birth of cinema. Their short actuality films included Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory, The Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat and The Sprinkler Sprinkled, an early comedy. They sent camera operators around the world to record scenes and promote the device, building a catalogue of more than 1,400 films.

Other work

The brothers also made major contributions to still photography, notably the Autochrome Lumière colour process, marketed from 1907, which became the leading industrial colour photography process until the 1930s. Louis later worked on stereoscopic cinema and medical research.
