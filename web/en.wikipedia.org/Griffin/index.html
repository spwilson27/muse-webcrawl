The griffin, griffon, or gryphon is a legendary creature with the body, tail and back legs of a lion, and the head and wings of an eagle with its talons on the front legs. As the lion was traditionally considered king of the beasts and the eagle king of the birds, the griffin was regarded as an especially powerful and majestic creature, a symbol of divine power and a guardian of the divine.

Origins and history

Griffin-like hybrids appear in the art of ancient Egypt and Persia by the second millennium BCE, including at Persepolis and in Minoan frescoes such as those at Knossos. In Greek tradition, griffins inhabited Scythia, where they guarded gold deposits against the one-eyed Arimaspians, a story repeated by Herodotus and Pliny the Elder. Aeschylus and other authors placed them at the edge of the known world.

Medieval lore

Medieval bestiaries endowed the griffin with moral symbolism; its claws and feathers were credited with medicinal properties, and griffin eggs were mounted as precious objects. The creature became a common charge in heraldry, where it signified courage, vigilance and strength. Dante placed a griffin drawing the chariot in the Earthly Paradise in the Purgatorio.

Modern theories

Folklorist Adrienne Mayor has proposed that griffin legends may have been inspired by Protoceratops fossils exposed in Central Asian gold-bearing deserts, whose beaked skulls and lion-sized bodies resemble the creature — a hypothesis that remains debated.

In popular culture

Griffins appear widely in fantasy literature, games and film, from Alice's Adventures in Wonderland's Gryphon to the hippogriff's more famous cousin in heraldic art, university crests and corporate logos.
