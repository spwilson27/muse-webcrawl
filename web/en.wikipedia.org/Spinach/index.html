Spinach

Spinach (Spinacia oleracea) is a leafy green flowering plant native to central and western Asia. It is an annual plant in the family Amaranthaceae, grown for its edible leaves, which are eaten raw in salads or cooked in a wide variety of dishes.

History

Spinach is thought to have originated in ancient Persia. Arab traders introduced it to India and China (where it was known as the "Persian vegetable"), and it reached Europe via Moorish Spain by the 11th–12th centuries. It became popular in France and England and was later carried to the Americas.

Nutrition

Spinach is rich in vitamins A, C, K, folate, manganese, magnesium, and iron, though the iron and calcium are partly bound by oxalates, reducing absorption. The popular belief that spinach is exceptionally rich in iron was exaggerated by a supposed decimal-point error in 19th-century data — a story that is itself partly legend — and by the cartoon character Popeye, whose strength was attributed to spinach.

Cultivation and production

Spinach is a cool-season crop that bolts in hot weather. China and the United States are the largest producers. It is sold fresh, canned, or frozen.
