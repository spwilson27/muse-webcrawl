Second Chechen War

The Second Chechen War (1999–2009) was an armed conflict between the Russian Federation and the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria, fought in Chechnya and the border regions of the North Caucasus. It began in August 1999 after the invasion of Dagestan by Chechnya-based militants and a series of apartment bombings in Russia attributed to Chechen separatists.

Course

Russian forces re-entered Chechnya in October 1999, capturing Grozny in February 2000 after a devastating siege. Major combat gave way to a long insurgency; Moscow installed a pro-Russian Chechen government under Akhmad Kadyrov and later his son Ramzan. Russia formally ended its "counter-terrorist operation" regime in April 2009.

Consequences

The war caused tens of thousands of deaths, predominantly among Chechen civilians, and widespread destruction in Grozny. Human rights groups documented extensive abuses by all sides.
