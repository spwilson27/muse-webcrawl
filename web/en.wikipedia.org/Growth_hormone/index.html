Growth hormone (GH) or somatotropin, also known as human growth hormone (hGH or HGH) in its human form, is a peptide hormone that stimulates growth, cell reproduction and cell regeneration in humans and other animals. It is a 191-amino-acid, single-chain polypeptide synthesised, stored and secreted by somatotroph cells in the anterior pituitary gland. Secretion is pulsatile, with the largest pulses during deep sleep and puberty, stimulated by growth hormone–releasing hormone (GHRH) and ghrelin and inhibited by somatostatin.

Functions

GH acts directly on tissues and indirectly through insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), which the liver releases in response to GH. Its effects include stimulation of linear bone growth at the epiphyseal plates in children, protein synthesis and muscle growth, lipolysis (fat breakdown), retention of calcium and bone mineralisation, and a diabetogenic tendency to raise blood glucose and antagonise insulin. It also supports immune function and the growth of internal organs.

Clinical significance

Deficiency in children causes growth failure and short stature; in adults it is associated with reduced muscle and bone mass and altered body composition. Excess secretion — usually from a benign pituitary adenoma — causes gigantism if it begins before the growth plates close, and acromegaly (enlargement of hands, feet, jaw and organs) in adults. Tumours are typically treated by surgery, with somatostatin analogues such as octreotide, or the GH-receptor antagonist pegvisomant, as adjuncts.

Medical use and misuse

Recombinant human GH (somatropin) — produced since 1985, replacing cadaver-derived hormone that was withdrawn after cases of Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease — is approved for growth hormone deficiency, Turner syndrome, chronic kidney disease, Prader–Willi syndrome, children born small for gestational age, and HIV-associated wasting, among other indications. It is also misused as a performance-enhancing and "anti-ageing" drug; its distribution for off-label purposes is restricted in many countries, and it is prohibited in sport by WADA.
