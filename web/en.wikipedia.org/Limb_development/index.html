Limb development

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Limb development in vertebrates is an area of active research in developmental biology addressing the origin, patterning, and growth of the tetrapod limb. The limbs develop from small protrusions of the flank called limb buds, consisting of lateral plate mesoderm covered by ectoderm, and are patterned along three axes: proximal–distal (shoulder to fingertip), anterior–posterior (thumb to little finger), and dorsal–ventral (back of hand to palm).

Initiation

Limb position is specified by Hox gene expression along the body axis together with retinoic acid signalling. Forelimb and hindlimb identity are conferred by the transcription factors TBX5 and TBX4 (with PITX1) respectively. These activate fibroblast growth factor signalling — FGF10 in the limb mesenchyme induces FGF8 in the overlying ectoderm, establishing the apical ectodermal ridge (AER) and a self-sustaining outgrowth loop.

The three signalling centres

  • Apical ectodermal ridge (AER): a thickened ectodermal ridge at the bud tip secreting FGFs (FGF8, FGF4, FGF9, FGF17) that keep underlying mesenchyme (the progress zone) proliferating and specify progressively more distal structures as development proceeds.
  • Zone of polarizing activity (ZPA): a posterior mesenchymal region secreting Sonic hedgehog (SHH), which patterns the anterior–posterior axis and digit number and identity in a concentration- and time-dependent manner. The SHH gene's limb enhancer, the ZRS, lies about a megabase away within an intron of the LMBR1 gene; mutations here cause preaxial polydactyly.
  • Dorsal ectoderm: WNT7A from dorsal ectoderm induces LMX1B in dorsal mesenchyme, conferring dorsal identity; ventral ectodermal EN1 represses Wnt7a ventrally.

Patterning and morphogenesis

Nested expression of HoxA and HoxD cluster genes contributes to specifying the three limb segments — stylopod (humerus/femur), zeugopod (radius–ulna / tibia–fibula), and autopod (hand/foot) — with 5′ Hoxd genes (Hoxd11–13) active in the digits under control of early and late regulatory phases. SHH and BMP signalling, mediated in part by the BMP antagonist Gremlin1, form a feedback network with AER-FGFs that coordinates patterning with outgrowth. Later, mesenchymal condensations form cartilage templates of the bones (endochondral ossification), muscles migrate in from somites, and programmed cell death in the interdigital mesenchyme (driven by BMPs) separates the digits — failure of this process produces syndactyly, while its suppression in water birds leaves webbed feet.

Evolution and variation

Modifications of this conserved programme underlie limb diversity: limb loss in snakes involves degeneration of the ZRS enhancer and altered Hox expression; limb reduction in whales and the elongated digits of bat wings involve changes in FGF, BMP, and interdigital apoptosis signalling. Human congenital limb anomalies such as acheiropodia, split hand/foot malformation, and thalidomide embryopathy are interpreted as disruptions of these signalling centres.
