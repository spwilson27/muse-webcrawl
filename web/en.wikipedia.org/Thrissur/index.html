Thrissur (formerly Trichur) is a city in the Indian state of Kerala and the headquarters of Thrissur district. Built around a hillock crowned by the Vadakkunnathan Temple, with a large open maidan (Thekkinkadu Maidan) at its centre, it is known as the cultural capital of Kerala.

History and culture

The modern city was planned in the 18th century by the ruler Sakthan Thampuran of Cochin, who cleared the teak forest around the temple, laid out the circular Swaraj Round and founded the great festival of Thrissur Pooram. Thrissur hosts the Kerala Sangeetha Nataka Akademi, Kerala Sahitya Akademi and the Kerala Lalithakala Akademi, along with major cultural institutions and the Kerala Kalamandalam nearby at Cheruthuruthy.

Economy

Thrissur is a major centre of the gold jewellery trade — a large share of India's gold business passes through the district — and of banking; several Indian banks, including South Indian Bank and Catholic Syrian Bank, were founded here. It is also a hub for ayurvedic medicine, textiles and religious tourism, with the pilgrimage centre of Guruvayur in the district.
