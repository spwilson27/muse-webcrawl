Duke of Bohemia

A duke of Bohemia of the Přemyslid dynasty

The Duke of Bohemia was the ruler of the Duchy of Bohemia, a state in Central Europe centred on Prague, from the late ninth century until 1198, when the duchy was raised to a kingdom and its ruler became King of Bohemia.

History

Bohemia was unified under the Přemyslid dynasty, whose first historically documented duke was Bořivoj I (reigned c. 870–889), the first Christian ruler of Bohemia. His successors included Spytihněv I, Vratislaus I, and Saint Wenceslaus (Václav), the "Good King Wenceslas" of the Christmas carol, who was murdered by his brother Boleslaus the Cruel in 935 and became the patron saint of Bohemia.

Under dukes such as Bretislaus I and Vratislaus II the duchy grew in power within the Holy Roman Empire. Vratislaus II received a personal royal title in 1085, and Vladislaus II in 1158, but these were lifetime honours. In 1198 Ottokar I finally obtained hereditary kingship, confirmed by the Golden Bull of Sicily in 1212.

The dukes of Bohemia were imperial princes and electors of the Empire, and the Bohemian crown lands later included Moravia, Silesia and Lusatia.
