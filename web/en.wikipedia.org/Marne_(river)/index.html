The Marne (French pronunciation: [maʁn]) is a river in France, a right-bank tributary of the Seine in the area east and southeast of Paris. It is 514 kilometres (319 mi) long, one of the longest rivers wholly within France. The river gave its name to the departments of Haute-Marne, Marne, Seine-et-Marne and Val-de-Marne.

Course

The Marne rises on the Langres plateau in the Haute-Marne department and flows generally westward through Chaumont, Saint-Dizier, Châlons-en-Champagne, Épernay and Château-Thierry, before joining the Seine at Charenton-le-Pont, just upstream of Paris. It is connected to other river systems by several canals, including the Marne–Rhine Canal and the Canal latéral à la Marne. The Lac du Der-Chantecoq, one of France's largest artificial lakes, regulates its flow.

History

The Marne valley was the scene of two decisive battles of the First World War. The First Battle of the Marne (September 1914) halted the German advance on Paris — the famous "taxis of the Marne" ferried reinforcements to the front — and the Second Battle of the Marne (July–August 1918) marked the beginning of the Allied counter-offensive that ended the war. The river also lends its name to "la Marne" landscapes painted by Cézanne and the Fauvists, and to the guinguettes — riverside dance halls — celebrated in French popular culture.

Navigation and use

The lower Marne is navigable and forms part of the inland waterway network linking Paris to eastern France and Germany. Its valley is also noted for the vineyards of Champagne around Épernay and Château-Thierry.
