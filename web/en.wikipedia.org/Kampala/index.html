Kampala is the capital and largest city of Uganda. Situated in the country's south on a series of hills near the northern shore of Lake Victoria, it is Uganda's political, economic, and cultural centre and one of Africa's fastest-growing cities.

History

The area was a hunting ground of the Kabaka (king) of Buganda, noted for its impala — from which the name Kampala ("hill of the impala") is traditionally derived. Kabaka Mwanga II had his capital on its hills, and in 1890 Frederick Lugard of the Imperial British East Africa Company established a fort there. When the Uganda Protectorate was declared, the colonial administrative capital was placed at Entebbe, but Kampala grew as the commercial hub and the seat of the Buganda kingdom. It became capital of independent Uganda in 1962.

Geography and landmarks

Kampala originally occupied seven hills — including Old Kampala, Namirembe, Rubaga, Mengo, and Kololo — and has since spread over many more, interspersed with wetlands now under development pressure. Landmarks include the Kasubi Tombs, burial site of the Buganda kings and a UNESCO World Heritage Site; the Uganda Museum; Rubaga and Namirembe cathedrals; the Gaddafi National Mosque, among the largest in Africa; and Makerere University, founded in 1922, one of Africa's leading universities.

Economy and transport

The city concentrates Uganda's banking, media, manufacturing, and government employment. Its streets are served by matatu minibuses and boda-boda motorcycle taxis; chronic congestion is a major challenge. Entebbe International Airport, on Lake Victoria about 40 km south-west, serves the capital. The metropolitan area, administered since 2011 by the Kampala Capital City Authority under a Lord Mayor, is expanding rapidly into Wakiso District.
