Industrial Light & Magic

Industrial Light & Magic (ILM) is an American motion picture visual effects company founded by George Lucas in 1975. Created to produce the unprecedented special effects for Star Wars (1977), ILM became the most influential visual effects studio in film history, pioneering many of the industry's foundational techniques in model work, optical compositing, and later computer-generated imagery. It is a division of Lucasfilm and, since 2012, has been owned by the Walt Disney Company.

History

Lucas founded ILM in Van Nuys, California, after discovering that 20th Century Fox's in-house effects department had been shut down. Under John Dykstra, the team developed the Dykstraflex motion-control camera system to photograph miniatures with repeatable, dynamic camera moves for Star Wars. The studio relocated to Marin County, building its long-time base at Skywalker Ranch and later San Rafael, and in 2005 moved its headquarters to the Presidio of San Francisco. ILM now operates studios in San Francisco, Vancouver, London, Singapore, Sydney, and Mumbai.

Innovations and landmark films

ILM milestones include the Genesis effect in Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan (1982), the first fully computer-generated sequence in a feature film; the stained-glass knight in Young Sherlock Holmes (1985), the first fully CGI character; morphing in Willow (1988) and Terminator 2 (1991); the dinosaurs of Jurassic Park (1993), which ushered in the CGI era; and more recently the StageCraft LED-volume virtual production system developed for The Mandalorian. ILM has contributed effects to more than 300 films and won numerous Academy Awards for Best Visual Effects.

Organization

ILM works for both Lucasfilm productions and outside studios, and its television division, ILM TV, provides effects for streaming series. Its sister technologies and alumni have seeded much of the modern effects industry, including Pixar, which began as part of Lucasfilm's computer division before being sold to Steve Jobs in 1986.
