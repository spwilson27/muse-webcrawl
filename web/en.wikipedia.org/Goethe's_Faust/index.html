Goethe's Faust

Faust is a tragic play in two parts by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, usually known in English as Goethe's Faust to distinguish it from the many other treatments of the Faust legend. Goethe worked on the play intermittently for much of his life; Faust, Part One was published in 1808, and Faust, Part Two appeared posthumously in 1832.

Plot

In Part One, the scholar Heinrich Faust, dissatisfied with the limits of human knowledge, makes a pact with Mephistopheles, who promises to serve him on Earth; if Faust is ever so contented that he wishes a moment to linger, his soul is forfeit. Mephistopheles leads Faust through rejuvenation, romance, and the seduction and ruin of Gretchen (Margarete), whose story forms the emotional heart of the first part. Part Two expands into a vast allegorical panorama of politics, economics, classical mythology, and Faust's final project of land reclamation, ending with his death, the contest for his soul, and Gretchen's intercession in his redemption.

Themes and influence

Central themes include striving (Streben), the limits of reason, desire, and redemption through unceasing activity and love. The famous lines "Verweile doch! Du bist so schön!" ("Linger a while! You are so fair!") state the terms of Faust's wager. Goethe's Faust is widely regarded as the greatest work of German literature and has inspired operas by Gounod, Boito, and Berlioz, Liszt's Faust Symphony, and countless philosophical readings.
