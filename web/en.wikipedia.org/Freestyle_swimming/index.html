Freestyle swimming

Freestyle is a category of swimming competition, defined by the rules of World Aquatics (formerly FINA), in which competitors are subject to only a few limited restrictions on their swimming stroke. As a result, freestyle races are almost universally swum with the front crawl, the fastest stroke, and the terms "freestyle" and "front crawl" are often used interchangeably in casual usage.

Rules

Under World Aquatics rules, freestyle means that the swimmer may swim any style, except that in individual medley and medley relay events freestyle means any style other than backstroke, breaststroke, or butterfly. The swimmer must touch the wall at each turn and at the finish, and some part of the swimmer must break the surface throughout the race, except for up to 15 metres underwater after the start and each turn.

Competition

Freestyle events are held over distances from 50 metres to 1500 metres in the pool, and include 50, 100, 200, 400, 800, and 1500 m individual races at the Olympic Games, along with freestyle relays. The 50 m freestyle, introduced internationally in the 1980s, is the shortest and fastest Olympic swimming race. Open water swimming events are also conducted under freestyle in practice.

Technique

The front crawl used in freestyle competition involves alternating arm strokes, a flutter kick, and breathing to the side, refined over the twentieth century by swimmers such as Johnny Weissmuller, and later by technical developments including the high-elbow catch and two-, four-, or six-beat kicking patterns.
