Waitomo Glowworm Caves

The Waitomo Glowworm Caves are a celebrated cave system at Waitomo in the Waikato region of New Zealand's North Island, famous for the thousands of glowworms, Arachnocampa luminosa, that illuminate the underground grotto. They are among New Zealand's oldest and best-known tourist attractions.

History

The caves were first explored in 1887 by local Māori chief Tane Tinorau together with English surveyor Fred Mace, who floated through on a raft of flax stems. Tinorau began guiding visitors soon after, and the caves became a major attraction, with ownership and stewardship long contested between the Crown and Tinorau's descendants; management interests were returned to local Māori in 1990.

The caves and glowworms

The limestone system includes chambers such as the Cathedral, famed for its acoustics, and the Glowworm Grotto, where visitors glide by boat beneath a ceiling of living lights. The glow comes from the larvae of a fungus gnat endemic to New Zealand, which dangle sticky silk threads to snare prey attracted to their bioluminescence. Neighboring caves in the Waitomo system, such as Ruakuri and Aranui, offer further tours.
