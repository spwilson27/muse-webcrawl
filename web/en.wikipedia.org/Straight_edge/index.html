Straight edge

Straight edge (symbolised by sXe or by the letter X) is a subculture of hardcore punk whose adherents refrain from using alcohol, tobacco, and recreational drugs, in reaction to the excesses of punk subculture. Some adherents also refrain from engaging in promiscuous sex, follow a vegetarian or vegan diet, and do not consume caffeine.

Origins

The term comes from the 1981 song "Straight Edge" by the Washington, D.C. band Minor Threat, whose frontman Ian MacKaye articulated the idea of abstaining from substances without preaching abstinence to others. The X symbol originated in clubs where underage patrons had an X drawn on their hands so bartenders would not serve them alcohol; it was adopted as a badge by straight edge youth.

Development

In the mid-to-late 1980s, bands such as Youth of Today and Gorilla Biscuits spread the movement and associated it with positive mental attitude and, increasingly, animal rights and vegetarianism. The 1990s saw more militant and vegan "hardline" currents, as well as Krishna-conscious bands. Straight edge has remained a presence in hardcore scenes worldwide, though many adherents later leave the lifestyle, a transition sometimes called "breaking edge".
