Kuala Lumpur, officially the Federal Territory of Kuala Lumpur and abbreviated as KL, is the capital city and a federal territory of Malaysia. The name means "muddy confluence" in Malay, referring to the meeting of the Gombak and Klang rivers where the city was founded in 1857 as a tin-mining outpost. The city covers 243 km² and is Malaysia's largest, as well as its cultural, financial, and economic centre.

History

Chinese tin miners settled the confluence in the 1850s under the authority of the Malay chief of Klang. Under Kapitan Yap Ah Loy the settlement survived civil war and a devastating fire and flood in 1881, after which the British Resident Frank Swettenham rebuilt it in brick. Kuala Lumpur became capital of the Federated Malay States in 1896, of the Federation of Malaya in 1948, and of independent Malaysia in 1963. It was ceded by Selangor as a federal territory in 1974; the administrative capital later moved to Putrajaya, though Kuala Lumpur remains the seat of Parliament and the constitutional monarch (Yang di-Pertuan Agong).

Landmarks

The skyline is dominated by the Petronas Twin Towers (452 m; the world's tallest buildings from 1998 to 2004), the KL Tower, and Merdeka 118 (679 m, completed 2023, the second-tallest building in the world). Colonial-era landmarks around Merdeka Square include the Sultan Abdul Samad Building and the Jamek Mosque at the river confluence. Nearby are the Batu Caves, a major Hindu shrine.

Economy and transport

Kuala Lumpur is a regional hub for finance, retail, and tourism, and the centre of Malaysia's largest metropolitan area, the Klang Valley. It is served by Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA) in neighbouring Sepang and an expanding metro network (MRT, LRT, and monorail).
