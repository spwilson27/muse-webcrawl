Kumano Kodo

The Kumano Kodō (熊野古道) is a network of ancient pilgrimage routes across the Kii Peninsula of Japan, leading to the Kumano Sanzan — the three grand shrines of Kumano: Hongū Taisha, Hayatama Taisha and Nachi Taisha.

History

For more than a thousand years, pilgrims including retired emperors and aristocrats from Kyoto, and later commoners of all classes, walked these mountain routes. Pilgrimage to Kumano was unusual in welcoming women and people of all faiths under the syncretism of Shinto and Buddhism.

Routes

Principal routes include the Nakahechi (from Tanabe), the mountainous Ōhechi coastal route, the Kohechi linking Kōya-san, and the Iseji from Ise Grand Shrine. In 2004 the routes were inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range". The Kumano Kodo is twinned with Spain's Camino de Santiago, with a joint "Dual Pilgrim" passport.
