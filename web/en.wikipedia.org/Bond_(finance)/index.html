In finance, a bond is a type of security under which the issuer (debtor) owes the holder (creditor) a debt, and is obliged — depending on the terms — to provide cash flow to the creditor, and to repay the principal at the maturity date. Interest is usually payable at fixed intervals (semiannual, annual, sometimes monthly).

Bonds are issued by public authorities, credit institutions, companies and supranational institutions. The primary market sells newly issued bonds, often through underwriting syndicates; investors then trade them on the secondary market, typically over the counter.

Features

  • Principal / par value: the amount repaid at maturity, usually 1,000 units of currency.
  • Coupon: the interest rate the issuer pays, fixed or floating.
  • Maturity: the date the principal falls due; bonds range from days to perpetuities.
  • Yield: the return implied by the market price; prices move inversely to yields.

Types

Government bonds (such as U.S. Treasuries and British gilts), municipal bonds, corporate bonds, high-yield ("junk") bonds, convertible bonds, zero-coupon bonds and inflation-indexed bonds are the main varieties. Credit rating agencies grade issuers' creditworthiness, which strongly influences the coupon demanded by investors.
