Castles in Poland

Poland is home to several thousand castles, palaces and fortresses, built by Piast dukes, the Polish Crown, the Teutonic Order and great magnate families. They range from medieval strongholds guarding trade routes and frontiers to Renaissance and Baroque residential palaces. Many were destroyed in the Swedish Deluge and the Second World War; the most famous have been restored and are now museums and UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Notable castles

Malbork Castle, built by the Teutonic Knights from 1274, is the largest castle in the world measured by land area and a classic example of a medieval brick fortress. Wawel Castle in Kraków was the residence of Polish kings for centuries. The Renaissance Baranów Sandomierski and the great fortified residences of Łańcut, Krasiczyn and Książ — the third-largest castle complex in Poland — illustrate the magnate era. The "Trail of the Eagles' Nests" links the ruined hill castles of the Kraków-Częstochowa Upland, such as Ogrodzieniec and Bobolice.

History

The first stone castles appeared under the Piast dynasty in the eleventh and twelfth centuries. Casimir the Great in the fourteenth century launched a major programme of castle building, earning the saying that he "found Poland built of wood and left it built of stone." The Teutonic Order raised a dense network of brick Ordensburgen in the north. In the seventeenth century, wars with Sweden devastated many strongholds; in the eighteenth and nineteenth, castles became aristocratic residences, while after 1945 many were nationalised and restored by the Polish state.
