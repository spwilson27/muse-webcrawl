Research library

A research library is a library that contains an in-depth collection of material on one or several subjects. Research libraries are generally associated with universities and other scholarly institutions, and their collections are built to support the research needs of faculty, students, and visiting scholars, often with an emphasis on depth and comprehensiveness rather than popular coverage.

Characteristics

Research libraries typically hold large print and electronic collections, extensive journal subscriptions, special collections of rare books and manuscripts, archives, and data sets. They provide services such as interlibrary loan, reference and liaison librarians, research-data management, and scholarly-communications support. In North America, the leading research libraries are organised in the Association of Research Libraries (ARL), founded in 1932.

Collections and cooperation

Because no single library can collect everything, research libraries cooperate in shared-print initiatives, consortial licensing of electronic resources, and collaborative digital repositories such as HathiTrust, founded in 2008, which preserves and provides access to millions of digitised volumes contributed by member institutions.

Examples

Examples include the university libraries of Harvard (Widener Library) and Oxford (the Bodleian), national libraries functioning as research libraries such as the Library of Congress and the British Library, and independent research libraries such as the Newberry Library in Chicago and the Huntington Library in California.
