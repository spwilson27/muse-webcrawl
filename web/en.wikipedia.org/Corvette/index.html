Corvette

A corvette is a small warship. It is traditionally the smallest class of vessel considered to be a proper (or "rated") warship. The warship class above the corvette is that of the frigate, while the class below was historically that of the sloop-of-war.

History

The term originated with the French corvette, a sailing warship of the seventeenth to nineteenth centuries carrying a single tier of guns on one deck, smaller than a frigate. Steam corvettes appeared in the mid-nineteenth century, and many navies built them for colonial patrol duties. The name was revived in the Second World War for small anti-submarine escort vessels, such as the Royal Navy's Flower-class corvettes, which were based on whaler designs and mass-produced to protect convoys.

Modern corvettes

Modern corvettes typically displace 500 to 2,000 tonnes and are armed with anti-ship missiles, point-defence systems, and sometimes anti-submarine weapons and a helicopter deck. They are used by many navies for littoral patrol, missile attack, and escort duties, offering much of a frigate's firepower in a cheaper hull. Stealthy designs, such as Sweden's Visby class, emphasise reduced radar signature.
