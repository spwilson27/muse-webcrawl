Thymine

Thymine
FormulaC5H6N2O2
TypePyrimidine nucleobase
SymbolT

Thymine (symbol T) is one of the four nucleobases in the nucleic acid DNA, the others being adenine, guanine, and cytosine. It is a pyrimidine derivative, with a heterocyclic aromatic ring and two carbonyl groups. In DNA, thymine pairs with adenine through two hydrogen bonds.

Structure and role

Thymine combined with the sugar deoxyribose forms the nucleoside thymidine. In RNA, thymine is generally replaced by the related base uracil, which lacks thymine's methyl group; the extra cost of producing thymine is offset by the greater stability it gives DNA and by the ease of detecting and repairing deaminated cytosine. Thymine was first isolated from calf thymus glands in 1893 by Albrecht Kossel and Albert Neumann, hence its name.

Mutagenesis

Ultraviolet light can fuse adjacent thymine bases on a DNA strand into thymine dimers, a form of DNA damage that is corrected by nucleotide excision repair and, in many organisms, by photolyase enzymes.
