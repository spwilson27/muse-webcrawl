The names of God in Islam (Arabic: أسماء الله الحسنى, ʾasmāʾ Allāh al-ḥusnā, "Allah's Beautiful Names") are names attributed to God in the Quran and Sunnah. These names are conventionally enumerated as 99, a number rooted in a well-known hadith in which Muhammad states that God has ninety-nine names and that whoever learns them by heart will enter Paradise.

Nature of the names

In Islamic theology the names express God's attributes — His mercy, knowledge, power, and justice — without implying likeness to created things. The most frequently occurring names include Ar-Raḥmān (The Most Gracious) and Ar-Raḥīm (The Most Merciful), which open nearly every chapter of the Quran in the basmala, as well as Al-Malik (The King), Al-Khāliq (The Creator), and Al-ʿAlīm (The All-Knowing).

The list of 99

No single Quranic passage lists all 99 names together; the canonical list derives from a hadith narrated by Abu Hurairah and recorded by al-Tirmidhi, and scholars have noted variants among the names in different transmissions. The names are commonly grouped and displayed in calligraphy, architecture, and rosary-like recitation (dhikr) using a misbaha of 99 beads.

Devotional use

Muslims recite the names in prayer and remembrance, and many personal names are formed from them with the prefix ʿAbd ("servant of"), such as Abdullah, Abdul Rahman, and Abdul Malik. The names also feature prominently in Islamic art, since depictions of God are prohibited; calligraphy of the names decorates mosques worldwide.
