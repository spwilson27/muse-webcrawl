Tail rotor

A tail rotor is a smaller rotor mounted vertically or near-vertically at the tail of a traditional single-rotor helicopter, used to counteract the torque of the main rotor and to control yaw. It is driven by a shaft running along the tail boom from the main transmission.

Function

By Newton's third law, the fuselage tends to spin opposite the main rotor. The tail rotor produces lateral thrust against this torque; the pilot varies its pitch with pedals to turn the nose left or right and to hold heading in a hover.

Alternatives

Other anti-torque designs include the Fenestron (a ducted fan in the tail fin), NOTAR (which uses air blown through slots in the boom), coaxial rotors, and tandem rotors. Tail rotors are noisy and vulnerable to damage, but they are mechanically simple and remain the most common arrangement.

Safety

Loss of tail rotor effectiveness or a tail rotor strike can cause a dangerous uncontrolled spin; the spinning blades are also a ground hazard, which is why tails are marked with warning stripes.
