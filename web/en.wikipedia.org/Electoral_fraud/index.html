Electoral fraud

Electoral fraud, sometimes referred to as election manipulation, voter fraud or election rigging, involves illegal interference with the process of an election, either by increasing the vote share of a favoured candidate, depressing the vote share of rival candidates, or both. It differs from but often goes hand in hand with voter suppression. What counts as fraud varies by jurisdiction.

Electoral fraud
MethodsBallot stuffing, impersonation, vote buying, falsified counts
CountermeasuresObservers, audits, voter ID, chain-of-custody rules

Types

Methods of fraud include ballot stuffing (submitting multiple or fraudulent ballots), voter impersonation, tampering with registration rolls, vote buying and intimidation, manipulation of counting and tabulation, misuse of proxy or postal votes, and — in electronic systems — tampering with voting machines or results software. "Ballot harvesting" disputes concern who may lawfully return another voter's absentee ballot.

Prevention and detection

Safeguards include transparent procedures, domestic and international election observers, voter-verifiable paper trails, risk-limiting audits, and statistical forensics: patterns such as implausible turnout or digit distributions can flag manipulated results, as in analyses of disputed elections in several countries.

Frequency and significance

In established democracies, individual voter fraud is generally found to be rare, though organised fraud in close local contests has occurred historically — for example in 19th-century machine politics in the United States. In authoritarian states, fraud may be systemic, with elections staged to legitimise rulers while outcomes are predetermined. Unsubstantiated allegations of fraud can themselves undermine democratic legitimacy.
