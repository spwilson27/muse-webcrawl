Associate professor


Associate professors form the mid-level rank of the tenure-track faculty.

An associate professor is an academic title of rank held by university faculty in many countries, most notably in North America. In the United States and Canada it is typically the middle rank of the tenure-track professorial ladder, sitting between assistant professor and full professor.

Overview

In most North American institutions, an assistant professor is promoted to associate professor after a probationary period of about six or seven years, usually together with the grant of tenure. Promotion normally requires a record of scholarly publication, effective teaching, and service to the university and the profession. Associate professors may later be promoted to the rank of (full) professor after further distinguished achievement.

The duties of an associate professor generally include teaching undergraduate and graduate courses, supervising graduate students and dissertations, conducting and publishing research, and serving on departmental and university committees.

In other systems

In the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth countries, the roughly equivalent rank has traditionally been called reader or senior lecturer, though many British universities have adopted the North American title of associate professor since the early 2000s. In some European systems the title corresponds to a tenured senior lecturer or an extraordinary professor. In Latin America and Spain, similar mid-career ranks exist under various names.
