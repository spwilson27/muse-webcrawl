Lawn

A suburban lawn

A lawn is an area of soil-covered land planted with grasses and other durable low plants, such as clover, which are maintained at a short height with a lawn mower and used for aesthetic and recreational purposes. Lawns surround houses and buildings and feature in parks, gardens, and sports grounds.

History

The close-clipped lawn originated in the gardens of wealthy European estates, where it was maintained by grazing animals and labourers with scythes. The invention of the mechanical lawn mower in 1830 made lawns practical for middle-class homes, and the lawn became a defining feature of suburban development, particularly in North America and Australia.

Grasses and maintenance

Common lawn grasses include perennial ryegrass, Kentucky bluegrass, fescues, and Bermuda grass, chosen according to climate. Maintenance involves mowing, watering, fertilizing, aerating, and weed and pest control; lawns are typically the most water- and chemical-intensive element of residential landscaping.

Environmental debate

Lawns are criticized for water consumption, fertilizer and pesticide runoff, emissions from mowers, and low biodiversity. Alternatives promoted by critics include clover lawns, meadows, xeriscaping, and letting grass grow longer for pollinators, as in the "No Mow May" campaign.
