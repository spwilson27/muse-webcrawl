Celtic neopaganism
Type: Modern paganism
Influences: Celtic mythology, folklore
Includes: Celtic Wicca, Druidry, Reconstructionism

Celtic neopaganism refers to contemporary pagan movements that seek to revive or reconstruct the religion, mythology and folklore of the ancient Celts. It includes Celtic reconstructionism, modern Druidry and Celtic forms of Wicca, which differ in how closely they follow historical sources.

History

Interest grew from 18th- and 19th-century Celtic revivals and antiquarian Druid orders, then expanded with 20th-century Wicca and the 1980s reconstructionist movement, which emphasises scholarship in language, archaeology and medieval literature.

Practices

Many groups mark seasonal festivals such as Samhain, Imbolc, Beltane and Lughnasadh, honour deities such as Brigid, Lugh and the Morrigan, and value hospitality, poetry and connection to land and ancestors.
