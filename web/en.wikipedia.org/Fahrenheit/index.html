Fahrenheit

Unit oftemperature
Symbol°F
Named afterDaniel Gabriel Fahrenheit

The Fahrenheit scale is a temperature scale based on one proposed in 1724 by the physicist Daniel Gabriel Fahrenheit (1686–1736). It uses the degree Fahrenheit (symbol: °F) as the unit.

Definition

On the original scale, the freezing point of water was defined as 32 degrees, and human body temperature was set near 96 degrees. The scale was later redefined so that water freezes at 32 °F and boils at 212 °F at standard atmospheric pressure, a separation of exactly 180 degrees. Absolute zero is −459.67 °F.

Conversion

To convert Fahrenheit to Celsius: °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9. Thus 0 °F equals −17.78 °C, and 100 °F equals 37.78 °C. The two scales meet at −40 degrees.

Usage

The Fahrenheit scale is the official scale of the United States, its territories, and a small number of other countries including the Bahamas, Belize, and the Cayman Islands. Most other countries use the Celsius scale.
