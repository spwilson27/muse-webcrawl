Great bustard

Scientific nameOtis tarda
FamilyOtididae (bustards)
Wingspan2.1–2.7 m
WeightMales up to ~18 kg
Conservation statusEndangered (IUCN)

The great bustard (Otis tarda) is a large bird in the bustard family, native to open grasslands across central and southern Europe and temperate Asia. It is one of the heaviest flying birds in the world: large males can weigh around 18 kg, with a wingspan approaching 2.7 metres.

Description

The male is bulky, with a thick grey neck, a broad white "moustache" of whisker-like feathers prominent in breeding plumage, warm brown barred upperparts and a white belly. Females are considerably smaller and lack the moustaches. In the breeding season males perform a spectacular display, inflating a throat pouch and turning their plumage nearly inside-out into a foam-like white mass.

Distribution and habitat

The species breeds in steppe and extensive arable landscapes, with its stronghold on the Iberian Peninsula — Spain holds the largest share of the world population. Smaller populations persist in central Europe, the Pannonian plain, Russia and eastwards to Mongolia and northern China. Eastern populations are migratory; western ones are largely resident or dispersive.

The bird became extinct in Great Britain in the 1830s — it had been the county bird of Wiltshire and appears in heraldry — and a reintroduction project on Salisbury Plain, begun in 2004 using eggs from Russia and later Spain, has established a small self-sustaining population.

Behaviour

Great bustards are omnivorous, feeding on plants, seeds and invertebrates, with small vertebrates occasionally taken. They breed in a lek system: males display at traditional sites and females nest alone on the ground, laying usually two eggs. The species is wary, walking away from danger and flying only reluctantly, though strongly when airborne.

Conservation

Hunting, agricultural intensification, collisions with power lines and disturbance have driven steep declines, and the species is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Conservation measures include agri-environment schemes, burial or marking of power lines, and the coordination of protection through the Memorandum of Understanding on middle-European populations under the Bonn Convention.
