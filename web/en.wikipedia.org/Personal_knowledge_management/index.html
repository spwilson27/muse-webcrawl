Personal knowledge management

Personal knowledge management (PKM) is a process of collecting, organising, storing and retrieving information that a person creates and consumes in the course of their daily activities. It is a response to the idea that knowledge workers must be responsible for their own growth and learning in an age of information overload. PKM integrates personal information management — the handling of documents, notes, email and bookmarks — with knowledge-management practices such as sense-making, reflection and collaboration.

Processes and skills

Frameworks for PKM typically include some combination of: retrieving information from sources; evaluating and organising it in personal schemes; analysing and synthesising it into new knowledge; presenting and sharing it with others; and collaborating across networks. Digital tools supporting these activities range from note-taking applications and reference managers to wikis, outliners and "second brain" systems of densely linked notes.

Methods and tools

Popularised methods include Niklas Luhmann's Zettelkasten method of atomic, cross-referenced index cards; Tiago Forte's "Building a Second Brain" approach with its CODE cycle (Capture, Organize, Distill, Express) and PARA organisation (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives); and commonplace-book traditions dating to the early modern period. Software such as personal wikis, outliners and graph-based note applications (for example Obsidian, Roam Research, Logseq, Notion and Evernote) is widely used to implement these methods, often with local plain-text files in Markdown format.

Relation to other fields

PKM sits at the intersection of personal information management, organisational knowledge management, cognitive science and lifelong learning. Scholars note that unlike organisational KM, which serves institutional goals, PKM is oriented to the individual's own purposes, portability and continuity across jobs and tools.
