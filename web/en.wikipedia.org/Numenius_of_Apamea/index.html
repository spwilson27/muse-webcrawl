Numenius of Apamea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Ruins of ancient Apamea, Numenius's home city

Numenius of Apamea (Greek: Νουμήνιος ὁ ἐκ τῆς Ἀπαμείας; fl. 2nd century AD) was a Greek philosopher who lived in Apamea in Syria. He belonged to the Neopythagorean school but is best known as an important precursor of Neoplatonism, and he exerted a significant influence on Plotinus.

Philosophy

Numenius held that Plato was the true heir of Pythagoras and sought to reconcile Platonic and Pythagorean doctrine with elements drawn from Judaism and other eastern traditions. Famously, he asked "What is Plato but Moses speaking Attic Greek?" His metaphysics distinguished three gods: a supreme, inactive first god (the Father or the Good), a second god (the Demiurge or Maker), and the cosmos itself as a third god.

Works

His writings survive only in fragments quoted by later authors, especially Eusebius and the Neoplatonists. Known works include On the Good, On the Divergence of the Academics from Plato, and On the Secrets or Mysteries of Plato.

Legacy

Amelius, a pupil of Plotinus, wrote a treatise distinguishing Numenius's doctrines from those of Plotinus, showing how closely the two were associated in the ancient world; some critics even accused Plotinus of plagiarising Numenius.
