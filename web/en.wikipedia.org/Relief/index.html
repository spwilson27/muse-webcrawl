Relief

A relief is a sculptural method in which the sculpted pieces remain attached to a solid background of the same material. The term comes from the Latin verb relevo, to raise. Reliefs are found in the art of many cultures, from ancient Egyptian temple walls to medieval cathedral portals.

Types

Reliefs are classified by how far the figures project from the background. In low relief (bas-relief), the projection is slight and no part is undercut. In high relief (alto-rilievo), figures project by at least half their natural circumference and parts may be fully detached from the background. Intermediate forms include mid-relief, while sunken relief, common in ancient Egypt, cuts the image into the surface.

Uses

Relief sculpture is widely used on buildings, monuments, sarcophagi, coins, and commemorative plaques, where it combines pictorial composition with a durable, architectural form.
