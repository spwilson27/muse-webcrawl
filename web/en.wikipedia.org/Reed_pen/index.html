Reed pen

A reed pen is a writing implement made by cutting and shaping a single reed straw or a piece of bamboo. One of the oldest writing tools, it was used in ancient Egypt by about the 4th century BC and remained in use in various cultures for millennia.

History

Egyptian scribes wrote hieratic and demotic scripts on papyrus with reed pens, often frayed into a brush-like tip. In the Islamic world, the reed pen (qalam) became the principal instrument of Arabic calligraphy, its tip cut at an angle to produce the thick-and-thin strokes of the script. Reed pens were also used in South Asia for palm-leaf manuscripts.

Use

The hollow reed serves as a small ink reservoir. Reed pens are still made by calligraphers today, particularly for Arabic calligraphy, because the cut nib can be tailored precisely to the style of script.
