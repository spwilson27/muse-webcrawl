A. Y. Jackson

Born3 October 1882, Montreal, Quebec
Died5 April 1974, Kleinburg, Ontario
NationalityCanadian
MovementGroup of Seven

Alexander Young Jackson (October 3, 1882 – April 5, 1974) was a Canadian painter and a founding member of the Group of Seven. He made an important contribution to the development of art in Canada and was successful in bringing the Group of Seven together.

Early life and training

Jackson studied in Montreal under William Brymner and in Paris at the Académie Julian. He travelled widely in Canada, painting the landscapes of Ontario, Quebec, the Arctic, and the West Coast, often on sketching trips with fellow Group members.

War art and later career

He served as an official war artist in the First World War. In 1933 he co-founded the Canadian Group of Painters. A recipient of the Order of Canada, Jackson wrote the autobiography A Painter's Country in 1958.
