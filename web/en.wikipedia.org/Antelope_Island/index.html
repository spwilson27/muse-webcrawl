Antelope Island

Antelope Island
LocationGreat Salt Lake, Davis County, Utah, United States
AreaAbout 42 square miles (109 km²) — the largest island in the lake
Highest pointFrary Peak, 6,596 ft (2,010 m)
StatusAntelope Island State Park (whole island)

Antelope Island is the largest of ten islands in the Great Salt Lake in Utah, United States, and is protected in its entirety as Antelope Island State Park. It is reached by a causeway from Syracuse in Davis County. The island is renowned for its free-roaming herd of American bison, pronghorn antelope, bighorn sheep and mule deer, and for the historic Fielding Garr Ranch.

Geography and history

Antelope Island is about 15 miles (24 km) long and 5 miles (8 km) wide, a ridge of some of the oldest rocks in Utah rising to Frary Peak at 6,596 feet. John C. Frémont and Kit Carson visited in 1845 and named it for the pronghorn they hunted there. Mormon settler Fielding Garr established a ranch in 1848, operated by the Garr family and later owners until 1981; the ranch house, among the oldest buildings in Utah still on its original foundation, is preserved as a historic site.

Wildlife

The island's most famous residents are its bison: twelve animals were introduced in 1893, and the herd — managed at roughly 550–700 animals through an annual roundup and culling — is one of the oldest and largest publicly owned bison herds in the United States and a noted genetic resource. Pronghorn were (re)introduced and give the island its name; bighorn sheep, coyotes, bobcats and porcupines also live there. The surrounding brine and mudflats support brine shrimp and enormous numbers of migratory shorebirds, including eared grebes and American avocets.

Recreation

Visitors come for hiking and mountain biking on the island's trails, horseback riding, camping, birdwatching, the annual bison roundup in autumn, and floating in the buoyant, hypersaline water of the Great Salt Lake at Bridger Bay beach. Because the lake is saline and shallow, falling lake levels in recent drought years have at times effectively joined the island to the mainland.
