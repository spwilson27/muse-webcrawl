Neutron diffraction

Neutron diffraction or elastic neutron scattering is the application of neutron scattering to the determination of the atomic and/or magnetic structure of a material. A sample to be examined is placed in a beam of thermal or cold neutrons to obtain a diffraction pattern that provides information about the structure of the material.

Principles

Neutrons have wavelengths comparable to interatomic spacings and interact with atomic nuclei rather than electron clouds. Unlike X-rays, neutrons are scattered by light atoms such as hydrogen with strength comparable to heavier atoms, and scattering lengths vary irregularly across the periodic table and between isotopes, allowing isotopic substitution experiments.

Magnetic scattering

Because the neutron carries a magnetic moment, neutron diffraction is uniquely sensitive to ordered magnetic structures, and it is a standard tool for determining antiferromagnetic and other magnetic arrangements.

Facilities

Experiments require intense neutron sources, such as research reactors or spallation sources, including the Institut Laue–Langevin in France, ISIS in the United Kingdom and the Spallation Neutron Source in the United States.
