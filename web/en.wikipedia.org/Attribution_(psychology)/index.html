Attribution (psychology)

Attribution is a concept in social psychology referring to how individuals explain the causes of behaviour and events. Attribution theory, pioneered by Fritz Heider in 1958, studies the processes by which people infer causes, and the biases that affect those inferences.

Internal and external attributions

Heider distinguished internal (dispositional) attributions — explaining behaviour by personality or ability — from external (situational) attributions — explaining it by circumstances. Harold Kelley's covariation model proposed that people weigh consensus, distinctiveness, and consistency information when choosing between the two.

Biases

Well-known attributional biases include the fundamental attribution error (overemphasizing disposition for others' behaviour), the actor–observer asymmetry, self-serving bias (crediting success internally and blaming failure externally), and the just-world bias. Bernard Weiner's model classified attributions by locus, stability, and controllability, linking them to emotion and motivation, especially in achievement settings.

Applications

Attribution research informs clinical psychology (depressive attributional styles), education, law, and organizational behaviour.
