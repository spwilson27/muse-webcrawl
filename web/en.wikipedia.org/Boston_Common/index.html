Boston Common is a public park in downtown Boston, Massachusetts. It is the oldest city park in the United States, dating from 1634, when the town purchased the land from settler William Blackstone. The roughly 50-acre park is bounded by Tremont, Park, Beacon, Charles and Boylston streets and forms the southern end of the Emerald Necklace park system and the start of the Freedom Trail.

History

Originally used for grazing cattle and as a militia training ground, the Common hosted British troops before the American Revolution — the redcoats camped here marched from it to Lexington and Concord in April 1775. Public hangings took place on the Great Elm, and the park was long a venue for public speaking and protest. Cattle grazing ended in 1830. The Frog Pond serves as a reflecting pool and children's spray pool in summer and an ice rink in winter.

Features

Monuments include the Soldiers and Sailors Monument on Flagstaff Hill, the Robert Gould Shaw Memorial (at the Common's edge facing the State House), and the Boston Massacre Memorial. The Park Street station beneath the corner is among America's oldest subway stations. Across Beacon Street lies the Massachusetts State House; adjacent Boston Public Garden, laid out in 1837, is the Common's more formal companion.
