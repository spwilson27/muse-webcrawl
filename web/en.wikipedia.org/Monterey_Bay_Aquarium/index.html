The Monterey Bay Aquarium is a nonprofit public aquarium in Monterey, California, United States. Known for its regional focus on the marine habitats of Monterey Bay, it was the first to exhibit a living kelp forest when it opened in October 1984. It is also renowned for being the first to successfully keep and display a great white shark for more than a few days.

History

The aquarium was built on the site of the former Hovden Cannery on Cannery Row, at the suggestion of marine biologists from Stanford's Hopkins Marine Station and with funding from David and Lucile Packard, co-founder of Hewlett-Packard. Its distinctive architecture incorporates elements of the old cannery.

Exhibits

Major exhibits include the Kelp Forest, one of the tallest aquarium exhibits in the world; the Open Sea galleries (formerly the Outer Bay), with a 1.2-million-gallon tank housing tuna, sharks, and sea turtles; the Ocean's Edge wing; and displays of sea otters, jellyfish, penguins, and deep-sea animals. Fresh seawater is pumped continuously from Monterey Bay into many of the tanks.

Conservation

The aquarium's research arm, the Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute (MBARI), studies deep-sea biology. The aquarium also runs the Seafood Watch program, which publishes recommendations for sustainable seafood, and advocates for ocean conservation policy.
