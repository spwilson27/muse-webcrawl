Number of the beast

The number of the beast is a figure from chapter 13 of the Book of Revelation in the New Testament, associated with the Beast — an apocalyptic creature. In most manuscripts the number is 666; some early sources, including the papyrus fragment P115, give the variant reading 616.

Biblical text

Revelation 13:18 reads: "This calls for wisdom: let the one who has understanding calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a man, and his number is 666." The text implies the number can be derived by calculation — in Greek and Hebrew practice, letters also served as numerals, so a name could be converted into a number (gematria).

Interpretations

The most widely held scholarly interpretation identifies the number with the emperor Nero: the Greek "Neron Kaisar" transliterated into Hebrew letters sums to 666, while the Latin-based variant sums to 616, matching the manuscript variants. Preterist readings thus see the passage as coded criticism of Roman imperial persecution. Other approaches take the number symbolically — 666 as a triple falling-short of the divine 777 — or, in futurist readings, as a mark to appear literally in end-times events, the "mark of the beast" without which no one may buy or sell.

Cultural impact

The number 666 has become a fixture of popular culture, associated with the Devil and the Antichrist, and fear of it is known as hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia. It appears in music, film, and literature, and is sometimes avoided in numbering schemes, addresses, and licence plates.
