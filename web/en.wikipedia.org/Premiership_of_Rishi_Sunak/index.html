Premiership of Rishi Sunak

Rishi Sunak
Prime Minister: 25 October 2022 – 5 July 2024
Party: Conservative
Predecessor: Liz Truss
Successor: Keir Starmer

Sunak became prime minister in October 2022, the first British Asian to hold the office.

The premiership of Rishi Sunak began on 25 October 2022, when Rishi Sunak succeeded Liz Truss as prime minister of the United Kingdom and leader of the Conservative Party, and ended on 5 July 2024 after the Conservatives' defeat in the 2024 general election. Sunak, a former chancellor of the Exchequer, was the first British Asian and first Hindu prime minister, and at 42 one of the youngest.

Government

Inheriting an economy destabilised by Truss's mini-budget, Sunak and Chancellor Jeremy Hunt prioritised fiscal stability and reducing inflation, which fell from over 11% to around 2% by mid-2024. In January 2023 Sunak set out five pledges: halve inflation, grow the economy, reduce debt, cut NHS waiting lists, and "stop the boats" carrying migrants across the Channel. His government passed the Safety of Rwanda Act to enable deportation flights to Rwanda, negotiated the Windsor Framework on Northern Ireland's post-Brexit arrangements, joined the CPTPP trade bloc, and announced the AUKUS-linked and HS2 revisions, cancelling the northern leg of the high-speed rail line in October 2023. David Cameron returned as foreign secretary in November 2023. Sunak called an early election for 4 July 2024; Labour under Keir Starmer won a landslide, and Sunak resigned the following day, later stepping down as Conservative leader.
