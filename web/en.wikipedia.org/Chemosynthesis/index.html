Chemosynthesis

Chemosynthesis is the synthesis of organic compounds by organisms using energy derived from chemical reactions — typically the oxidation of inorganic compounds such as hydrogen sulfide, ammonia or ferrous iron — rather than from sunlight as in photosynthesis.

Discovery

The process was described by Sergei Winogradsky in the 1880s–90s from studies of sulfur and nitrifying bacteria. Its ecological importance became clear in 1977 when thriving communities were found around hydrothermal vents on the deep ocean floor, far from sunlight.

Environments

Chemosynthetic microbes form the base of food webs at hydrothermal vents, cold seeps, whale falls and in subterranean aquifers. Giant tube worms such as Riftia pachyptila host symbiotic sulfur-oxidising bacteria. Nitrifying bacteria in soils and oceans also fix carbon chemosynthetically.
