Amor fati

Amor fati is a Latin phrase meaning "love of one's fate." It describes an attitude in which one sees everything that happens in one's life, including suffering and loss, as good or at least necessary. The concept is strongly associated with the philosophy of Friedrich Nietzsche, who used the phrase in The Gay Science and made it central to his idea of eternal recurrence in Ecce Homo, calling it his "formula for greatness in a human being."

Antecedents

Similar ideas appear in Stoicism: Epictetus taught that one should want events to happen as they do happen, and Marcus Aurelius wrote of accepting the workings of fate with equanimity. Nietzsche transformed the attitude from resignation into active affirmation — not merely bearing what is necessary, but loving it and wanting nothing to be different.

Later use

The phrase has been widely taken up in modern philosophy, psychology, and popular culture as an emblem of affirming life in the face of hardship.
