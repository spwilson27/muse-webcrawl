Coming-of-age story

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A coming-of-age story is a genre of literature, theatre, film, television and video games that focuses on the growth of a protagonist from childhood to adulthood, or "coming of age". Coming-of-age stories tend to emphasise dialogue or internal monologue over action, and are often set in the past. The related German literary form is the Bildungsroman, the "novel of formation".

Characteristics

The protagonist typically begins young and comparatively innocent, undergoes experiences — first love, loss, conflict with authority, social awakening — that challenge their worldview, and emerges with a more mature understanding of themselves and society. Unlike children's stories, coming-of-age narratives dwell on ambiguity and moral complexity; unlike adult fiction, they frame events from the perspective of someone experiencing them for the first time.

History

Antecedents appear in classical literature and early novels, but the form crystallised with works such as Goethe's Wilhelm Meister's Apprenticeship (1795–96), Dickens' Great Expectations, and Charlotte Brontë's Jane Eyre. Twentieth-century landmarks include James Joyce's A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man, J.D. Salinger's The Catcher in the Rye (1951), and Harper Lee's To Kill a Mockingbird.

In film and other media

Cinema developed a durable coming-of-age tradition: François Truffaut's The 400 Blows (1959), Stand by Me (1986), Boyhood (2014) — filmed with the same cast over twelve years — and Lady Bird (2017) are frequently cited examples. The genre overlaps with young adult fiction and the teen film, and analogues exist in many national literatures and cinemas.
