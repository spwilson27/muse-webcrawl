LocationNaoshima, Teshima, Inujima, Seto Inland Sea, Japan
Established1992 (Benesse House)
OperatorBenesse Holdings / Fukutake Foundation

Benesse Art Site Naoshima (ベネッセアートサイト直島) is the collective name for a group of museums, art installations, and architectural projects on the islands of Naoshima, Teshima, and Inujima in Japan's Seto Inland Sea. The project was developed from the late 1980s by Soichiro Fukutake, president of the Benesse Corporation, together with the Fukutake Foundation, with the aim of revitalising depopulating islands through contemporary art in harmony with nature and local communities.

Major sites

On Naoshima, the principal facilities include Benesse House (1992), a museum-hotel designed by Tadao Ando; the Chichu Art Museum (2004), built largely underground by Ando to house works by Claude Monet, James Turrell, and Walter De Maria; the Lee Ufan Museum (2010); and the Art House Project, which has converted vacant houses in the Honmura district into works of art since 1998. Yayoi Kusama's pumpkin sculptures on the island's piers have become emblematic of the project.

Teshima and Inujima

On Teshima, the Teshima Art Museum (2010), designed by Ryue Nishizawa with artist Rei Naito, consists of a single thin concrete shell resembling a water droplet. On Inujima, the Seirensho Art Museum preserves the ruins of a copper refinery. The islands are also central venues of the Setouchi Triennale art festival.
