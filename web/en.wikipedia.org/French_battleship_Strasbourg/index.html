French battleship Strasbourg

Strasbourg
Class: Dunkerque-class battleship
Laid down: November 1934
Launched: 12 December 1936
Commissioned: April 1939
Fate: Scuttled at Toulon, 27 November 1942; scrapped 1955

Strasbourg was the second and last member of the Dunkerque class of fast battleships built for the French Navy in the 1930s. She and her sister ship Dunkerque were designed to counter the German "pocket battleships" of the Deutschland class, with their main armament of eight 330 mm guns concentrated in two quadruple turrets forward. Strasbourg carried heavier armour than her sister.

Service

Commissioned in 1939, Strasbourg spent the opening months of the Second World War escorting convoys and hunting German raiders in the Atlantic. After the French armistice of June 1940 she was stationed at Mers-el-Kébir in French Algeria. On 3 July 1940, during the British attack on Mers-el-Kébir, Strasbourg escaped under fire — though her sister was disabled — and reached Toulon, one of the few major French warships to get away.

Scuttling and fate

At Toulon, Strasbourg became flagship of the French High Seas Forces. When German troops attempted to seize the fleet on 27 November 1942, she was scuttled by her crew. She was later raised by the Italian Navy, then taken over by Germany, and was sunk again by Allied bombers in August 1944. Raised once more after the war, she was used briefly for experiments and sold for scrap in 1955.
