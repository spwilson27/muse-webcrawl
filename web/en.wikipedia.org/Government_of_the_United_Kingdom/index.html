The Government of the United Kingdom, officially His Majesty's Government (abbreviated to HMG), is the central executive authority of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. It is headed by the prime minister, who selects the other ministers. The government is legally part of the Crown and exercises executive power in the monarch's name, largely through the royal prerogative as exercised by ministers.

Composition

The prime minister and around twenty senior ministers form the Cabinet, the government's principal decision-making body. Ministers head departments of state — such as the Treasury, the Home Office, the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, and the ministries of Defence, Health and Education — supported by a politically impartial civil service of several hundred thousand officials. About a hundred MPs and peers hold ministerial posts below cabinet rank.

Relationship with Parliament

The government is drawn from, and accountable to, Parliament. By convention it must command the confidence of the House of Commons, and ministers answer questions and defend policy there. Because the governing party usually holds a Commons majority, the government normally controls the legislative timetable and passes most of its programme, though defeats on confidence matters force resignation or a general election.

Devolution

Since 1999, significant powers over health, education, transport and other domestic matters have been devolved to the Scottish Government, the Welsh Government and the Northern Ireland Executive, elected by their own parliaments and assembly. Reserved matters such as foreign policy, defence and macro-economic policy remain with the UK government.

Legal basis

The United Kingdom has no single codified constitution; the government's powers derive from statute, common law, royal prerogative and constitutional convention. Ministers are bound by the Ministerial Code, and their decisions are subject to judicial review by the courts, as reaffirmed in landmark cases concerning the prorogation of Parliament in 2019.
