Conwy County Borough

Conwy County Borough (Welsh: Bwrdeistref Sirol Conwy) is a county borough and principal area of Wales, on the north coast. It takes its name from the town of Conwy and the River Conwy, and was created in 1996 from parts of the former districts of Aberconwy and Colwyn in the county of Gwynedd.

Geography

The county borough stretches from the Irish Sea coast — including the Victorian resort of Llandudno and the walled town of Conwy, with its 13th-century castle built by Edward I — inland along the Conwy valley to the hills of Mynydd Hiraethog, and includes part of the Snowdonia (Eryri) foothills. Much of the population lives in the coastal strip served by the A55 expressway and the North Wales Coast railway line.

Governance

Conwy County Borough Council provides local government services including education, social services, and highways. The area returns representatives to the Senedd and the UK Parliament. See also Conwy and principal areas of Wales.
