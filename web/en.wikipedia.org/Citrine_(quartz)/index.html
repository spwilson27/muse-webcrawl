Citrine (quartz)

Category: Oxide mineral, quartz variety
Formula: SiO2 (silicon dioxide)
Colour: Yellow to brownish orange
Mohs hardness: 7
Lustre: Vitreous

A faceted citrine gemstone.

Citrine is a variety of quartz whose colour ranges from pale yellow to brownish orange. The name derives from the French citron, meaning "lemon", after its citrus-like hues. Natural citrine is relatively rare; much of the citrine sold commercially is produced by heat-treating amethyst or smoky quartz, which turns yellow-orange at temperatures around 450–560 °C.

Properties

Like all quartz, citrine has the chemical formula SiO2, a Mohs hardness of 7, and a hexagonal crystal system. Its yellow colour is attributed to trace amounts of iron (Fe4+ substituting in the crystal lattice) and to colour centres related to irradiation. Citrine shows dichroism in some specimens and lacks the colour zoning typical of amethyst, a feature used to distinguish natural stones from heat-treated material, whose colours often concentrate at the tips of crystals.

Sources

Natural citrine has been found in Brazil, Madagascar, Russia (the Ural Mountains), Spain, France and the United States. Brazil is the leading source of both natural citrine and the amethyst that is heat-treated to produce most commercial stones. Notable deposits occur in Rio Grande do Sul and other Brazilian states.

Use as a gemstone

Citrine has been used in jewellery since antiquity and was popular in Roman intaglios and in 19th-century Scottish jewellery. It is the modern birthstone for November, a position it shares with topaz, with which it was historically confused — both were once called "topaz" indiscriminately. Because quartz is durable and abundant, citrine is an affordable yellow gemstone widely used in rings, pendants and earrings. See also quartz and smoky quartz.
