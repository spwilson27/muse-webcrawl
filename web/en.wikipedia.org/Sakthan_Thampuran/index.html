Sakthan Thampuran (born Raja Rama Varma IX; 1751–1805) was the Maharaja of the Kingdom of Cochin (in present-day Kerala, India) from 1790 to 1805. His epithet Sakthan means "the powerful one". He is credited with founding the modern town of Thrissur and with creating the Thrissur Pooram, now one of India's most spectacular temple festivals.

Early life and rise

Born into the Cochin royal family, Rama Varma was adopted and educated under powerful regents. He grew up amid the upheavals caused by the Mysorean invasions of Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan. Known for his administrative brilliance and ruthless efficiency, he effectively governed Cochin even before formally ascending the throne in 1790.

Administration

As ruler, Sakthan Thampuran overhauled revenue collection, eliminated corrupt intermediaries, and enforced law and order with legendary severity — criminals were said to fear his name alone. He cleared the teak forest around the Vadakkunnathan Temple in Thrissur, laid out the circular Thekkinkadu Maidan and ring roads, and shifted the capital's centre of gravity to Thrissur. He negotiated skilfully with the British East India Company, securing Cochin's position while Tipu Sultan's power waned.

Thrissur Pooram

In 1798 he organised ten temples to unite their processions into a single grand festival, the Thrissur Pooram, celebrated at Vadakkunnathan Temple with massed caparisoned elephants, percussion ensembles and fireworks. It remains Kerala's most famous pooram.

Death and legacy

Sakthan Thampuran died in 1805. Thrissur still honours him as its founder; the Sakthan Thampuran Palace preserves his memory, and his name endures in local usage for a person of formidable energy and authority.
