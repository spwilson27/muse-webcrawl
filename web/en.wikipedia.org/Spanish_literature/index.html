Spanish literature
Language: Spanish (Castilian)
Key works: Cantar de mio Cid, La Celestina, Don Quixote
Golden Age: 16th–17th centuries

Spanish literature generally refers to literature written in the Spanish language, encompassing the literary tradition of Spain from the medieval period to the present. Its most celebrated achievement, Miguel de Cervantes's Don Quixote (1605, 1615), is widely regarded as the first modern novel and one of the greatest works of world literature.

Medieval literature

The earliest surviving Castilian epic is the Cantar de mio Cid (c. 1200), celebrating Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar. The 14th century produced the Libro de buen amor of Juan Ruiz and the prose tales of Don Juan Manuel. Jorge Manrique's Coplas por la muerte de su padre (1476) is a pinnacle of medieval lyric, and Fernando de Rojas's La Celestina (1499) bridges the medieval and Renaissance worlds.

Golden Age

The Siglo de Oro saw an explosion of creativity: Garcilaso de la Vega's poetry, the picaresque novel (Lazarillo de Tormes, 1554), the mystics St Teresa of Ávila and St John of the Cross, the epic and lyric of Fray Luis de León, and the vast dramatic output of Lope de Vega and Calderón (see Spanish Golden Age theatre). Cervantes's Don Quixote transformed the novel as a form.

Modern era

The 19th century brought Romanticism (Espronceda, Bécquer) and the realist novels of Benito Pérez Galdós and Leopoldo Alas "Clarín". The Generation of '98 (Unamuno, Machado, Valle-Inclán) responded to Spain's imperial collapse, followed by the Generation of '27, including Federico García Lorca. Since the Civil War and the Franco dictatorship, figures such as Camilo José Cela and Miguel Delibes, and later the Latin American Boom's close dialogue with Spain, have sustained Spanish letters.
