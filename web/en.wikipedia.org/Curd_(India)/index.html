Curd (India)

Curd, known as dahi in Hindi and by many regional names across South Asia, is a traditional fermented dairy product made by curdling milk with a bacterial culture of lactobacilli. It is a staple of Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Nepali, and Sri Lankan cuisines and religious practice, distinct from cheese curds in Western usage.

Curd (dahi)
Type: Fermented dairy product
Region: South Asia
Main ingredient: Milk (cow or buffalo)
Uses: Raita, lassi, curd rice, marinades

Preparation

Warm milk is inoculated with a small amount of previous curd as a starter culture and left undisturbed in a warm place for several hours until it sets into a soft gel. Traditionally it is prepared in earthenware pots, which absorb moisture and yield a firmer set. The product is mildly sour and perishable, usually made fresh daily.

Culinary and cultural use

Curd is eaten plain, whisked into raita and lassi, mixed with rice as curd rice in South India, used in marinades and curries, and churned into buttermilk and butter. Sweetened curd dishes include Bengali mishti doi and Maharashtrian shrikhand. Dahi also has ritual significance in Hindu ceremonies and festivals, and in Ayurveda it is regarded as cooling and digestive.
