Slip (materials science)

In materials science, slip is the large displacement of one part of a crystal relative to another along crystallographic planes and directions. Slip occurs by the passage of dislocations on close-packed planes, which are the planes with the highest density of atoms, in close-packed directions.

Slip systems

A slip system is the combination of a slip plane and a slip direction. Face-centred cubic (FCC) metals such as aluminium and copper have 12 slip systems ({111}<110>), making them ductile. Body-centred cubic (BCC) metals have more potential systems but higher lattice friction, while hexagonal close-packed (HCP) metals have few active basal systems at room temperature, limiting their formability.

Schmid's law

Slip begins when the resolved shear stress on a slip system reaches a critical value, the critical resolved shear stress. Schmid's law relates the applied tensile stress to the resolved shear stress through the Schmid factor, which depends on the orientation of the slip plane and direction relative to the loading axis.
