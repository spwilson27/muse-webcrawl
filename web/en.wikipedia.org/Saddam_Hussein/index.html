Saddam Hussein (28 April 1937 – 30 December 2006) was an Iraqi politician who served as President of Iraq from 1979 until his overthrow in 2003. A leading member of the Ba'ath Party, he played a key role in the 1968 coup that brought the party to power and built a dictatorship maintained by an extensive security apparatus and a cult of personality.

Rise to power

Born into a poor family near Tikrit, Saddam joined the Ba'ath Party as a youth and participated in the failed 1959 assassination attempt against Prime Minister Abd al-Karim Qasim. After the Ba'ath seized power in 1968 he became deputy to President Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr, built the party's security organs, and directed the nationalisation of Iraq's oil industry in 1972. He forced al-Bakr's resignation in 1979 and purged rivals within the party.

Presidency

In 1980 Saddam invaded Iran, beginning the eight-year Iran–Iraq War, during which Iraq used chemical weapons; in 1988 his forces committed the Halabja chemical attack and the Anfal campaign against Iraqi Kurds, killing tens of thousands. In August 1990 he invaded Kuwait, prompting the Gulf War of 1991, in which a US-led coalition expelled Iraqi forces; uprisings that followed were brutally suppressed, and Iraq endured a decade of sanctions. Domestically, oil revenues funded modernisation, education and infrastructure in the 1970s while dissent was met with imprisonment, torture and execution.

Downfall, trial and execution

In March 2003 a US-led coalition invaded Iraq, alleging — incorrectly, later inquiries found — that Iraq possessed active weapons of mass destruction. Baghdad fell within weeks; Saddam was captured near Tikrit in December 2003. Tried by the Iraqi High Tribunal, he was convicted of crimes against humanity for the 1982 Dujail massacre and executed by hanging on 30 December 2006. Further charges, including genocide over Anfal, were left untried. His rule left Iraq's politics and society profoundly scarred, and his legacy remains contested.
