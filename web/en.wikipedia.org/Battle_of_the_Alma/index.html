The Battle of the Alma (20 September 1854), which took place just south of the Alma River in the Crimea, was the first major land battle of the Crimean War. An allied expeditionary force of French, British and Ottoman troops defeated the Russian army under Prince Alexander Sergeyevich Menshikov.

Background

The allies had landed on the Crimean coast at Calamita Bay in mid-September 1854 and marched south toward Sevastopol. Menshikov took up a strong defensive position on the heights above the Alma River to block them.

The battle

French troops under General Bosquet turned the Russian left by scaling cliffs near the river mouth that the Russians had considered impassable, while French artillery was hauled up onto the plateau. British infantry then crossed the river under fire and attacked the main Russian earthwork, the Great Redoubt, in confused but determined fighting; the Highland Brigade and Guards pressed the attack until the Russians retreated.

Aftermath

The allies failed to pursue vigorously, allowing Menshikov's army to escape, but the road to Sevastopol was open. The subsequent siege of the city lasted nearly a year. Alma became one of the celebrated British battle honours of the war, giving its name to streets and settlements across the Empire.
