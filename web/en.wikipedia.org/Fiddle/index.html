Fiddle

A fiddle is a bowed string musical instrument, most often a violin. It is a colloquial term for the violin, used by players in all genres, including classical music. Although most fiddles are violins, the name is also used for any bowed string instrument.

Etymology

The instrument is attested in English as "fiddle" since the medieval period; fiddle music formed the core dance repertoire of Europe, migrating to North America.

Styles

Notable fiddle traditions include Irish traditional music, Scottish fiddling, bluegrass, Cajun, old-time, Western swing, and klezmer.

Technique

Fiddlers differ from classical violinists mainly in repertoire and ornamentation—cuts, rolls, double stops, and alternate scordatura tunings—rather than in the instrument itself.
