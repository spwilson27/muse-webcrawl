Executive Order 9981

Executive Order 9981
Signed byHarry S. Truman
DateJuly 26, 1948
SubjectDesegregation of the United States Armed Forces

Executive Order 9981 was an executive order issued on July 26, 1948, by President Harry S. Truman. It abolished discrimination "on the basis of race, color, religion or national origin" in the United States Armed Forces, and led to the end of segregation in the services during the Korean War (1950–1953).

"It is hereby declared to be the policy of the President that there shall be equality of treatment and opportunity for all persons in the armed services without regard to race, color, religion or national origin."

Background

During World War II, African American soldiers served in segregated units, often relegated to support roles, even as Black leaders and civil rights organizations pressed for full participation. A. Philip Randolph and the March on Washington Movement had campaigned against discrimination in defense industries, and in 1947 the President's Committee on Civil Rights recommended ending segregation in the military. Facing an election in 1948 and pressure from Black voters and activists — including Randolph's threat of mass civil disobedience against a segregated draft — Truman acted.

Provisions and implementation

The order established the President's Committee on Equality of Treatment and Opportunity in the Armed Services (the Fahy Committee) to oversee implementation. The services resisted to varying degrees; the Air Force integrated relatively quickly, while the Army moved slowly until manpower needs during the Korean War forced rapid integration of units in the field. The last all-Black units were disbanded in 1954.

Legacy

Executive Order 9981 is regarded as a landmark of the early civil rights movement — the first major presidential action against segregation since Reconstruction — and as a precursor to the broader desegregation of American society in the following decades.
