Dragon Boat Festival

The Dragon Boat Festival (traditional Chinese: 端午節; simplified Chinese: 端午节), also known as the Duanwu Festival, is a traditional Chinese holiday occurring on the fifth day of the fifth month of the Chinese lunar calendar, corresponding to late May or June in the Gregorian calendar. It is a public holiday in mainland China and Taiwan. In 2009 the festival was added to UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, the first Chinese festival to be so recognised.

Origins

The festival is popularly said to commemorate the death of the poet and minister Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BC) of the ancient state of Chu during the Warring States period. Despairing at the corruption of the court and the fall of his state to Qin, Qu Yuan drowned himself in the Miluo River on the fifth day of the fifth month. According to legend, local people raced out in boats to save him or recover his body — the origin of dragon boat racing — and scattered balls of sticky rice into the water so that fish would eat the rice instead of his body, a story linked to the origin of zongzi. Scholars note that elements of the festival predate Qu Yuan and were associated with the summer solstice, dragon worship, and warding off disease and evil spirits in the midsummer season.

Practices

Celebrations include racing dragon boats — long, narrow boats decorated with dragon heads, paddled in time to a drum — eating zongzi (glutinous rice dumplings wrapped in bamboo or reed leaves), drinking realgar wine, hanging mugwort and calamus on doors, and wearing perfumed sachets, especially for children. In some regions the festival is also associated with the commemoration of Wu Zixu or the filial daughter Cao E.

Regional variations

Related festivals are celebrated across East and Southeast Asia, including Tango no sekku (now Children's Day) in Japan, Danoje in Korea, and Tết Đoan Ngọ in Vietnam.
