Ad valorem tax

An ad valorem tax (Latin for "according to value") is a tax whose amount is based on the value of a transaction or of property. It is typically imposed at the time of a transaction, as in the case of a sales tax or value-added tax (VAT), but it may be imposed on an annual basis, as in the case of a real property tax. The tax is proportional to the value assessed, in contrast to a specific tax, which is based on quantity (for example, a fixed excise per litre of fuel).

Applications

Sales and value-added taxes

Sales taxes in the United States and VAT in most of the world are ad valorem: a percentage of the price of goods or services. Customs duties on imports are also commonly ad valorem, charged as a percentage of the declared value of the goods.

Property taxes

Real estate taxes levied by local governments, especially in the United States, are ad valorem, based on periodic appraisal of the property's value, often expressed as a millage rate. Personal property such as vehicles may be taxed the same way.

Comparison with specific taxes

Ad valorem taxes automatically keep pace with inflation because the tax base rises with prices, whereas specific taxes erode in real terms unless adjusted. Conversely, ad valorem taxes require valuation, which can be contentious for property, and their revenue fluctuates with market values.
