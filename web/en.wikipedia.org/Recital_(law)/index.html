Recital (law)

In law, a recital is a formal statement appearing at the beginning of a legal document, such as a contract, deed, or statute, that sets out the background, purpose, or context of the document. Recitals are conventionally introduced by the word "Whereas" and are sometimes called whereas clauses.

Function

Recitals explain why the parties are entering into the agreement, identify the parties, and summarize relevant background facts. Although traditionally recitals were not considered part of the operative provisions of a contract, modern courts may use them to interpret ambiguous operative clauses, and parties sometimes expressly state whether recitals are binding.

In legislation

In legislation, recitals appear as the preamble-like introductory paragraphs of an act or, in the European Union, as numbered "whereas" paragraphs prefacing regulations and directives, giving the reasons for the measure and its legal basis. Courts, including the Court of Justice of the European Union, use recitals as an aid to interpreting the enacting terms.
