Screw pump

A screw pump is a positive-displacement pump that uses one or more screws to move fluids or solids along the screw axis. In its simplest form (the Archimedes' screw), a single screw rotates in a cylindrical cavity, trapping pockets of fluid and carrying them from the suction side to the discharge side.

Types

Screw pumps are classified by the number of rotors: single-screw (progressive cavity) pumps, twin-screw pumps, and triple-screw pumps. In multi-rotor designs, intermeshing helical rotors form sealed cavities that progress axially, giving a smooth, pulsation-free flow that handles viscous, abrasive, or shear-sensitive liquids well.

Applications

Screw pumps are used for fuel and lubricating oil transfer, hydraulic systems, marine cargo pumping, wastewater sludge, and food and chemical processing. The Archimedes' screw form remains in use for low-lift irrigation and drainage.
