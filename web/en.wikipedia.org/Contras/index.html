Contras

The Contras (short for Spanish contrarrevolucionarios, "counter-revolutionaries") were the various U.S.-backed rebel groups opposed to the Sandinista Junta of National Reconstruction government in Nicaragua that followed the overthrow of the Somoza regime in 1979.

History

After the Nicaraguan Revolution, former members of Somoza's National Guard and other anti-Sandinista factions formed armed groups, the largest being the Nicaraguan Democratic Force (FDN), led by Enrique Bermúdez. The United States, under President Ronald Reagan, funded, armed, and trained the Contras as part of its effort to pressure the Sandinista government, which Washington viewed as a Soviet-aligned regime.

The conflict formed part of the broader Cold War proxy struggles in Central America. The Contra war, together with economic pressure and an embargo, placed severe strain on Nicaragua. In 1984 the U.S. Congress passed the Boland Amendments restricting aid, and the subsequent Iran–Contra affair revealed that officials had secretly sold arms to Iran and diverted proceeds to the Contras. The International Court of Justice ruled in 1986 in Nicaragua v. United States that the U.S. had violated international law by supporting the Contras and mining Nicaragua's harbours.

End of the conflict

The 1987 Esquipulas II peace accord set a framework for ending the conflict. The Sandinistas lost the 1990 general election to Violeta Chamorro, after which most Contra forces demobilised, though some rearmed groups ("recontras") remained active into the 1990s.
