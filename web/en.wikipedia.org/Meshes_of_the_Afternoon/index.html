Meshes of the Afternoon

Meshes of the Afternoon
Year: 1943
Directors: Maya Deren and Alexander Hammid
Running time: 14 minutes
Starring: Maya Deren, Alexander Hammid

Meshes of the Afternoon is a 1943 American experimental short film directed by Maya Deren and her husband Alexander Hammid. One of the most influential works of avant-garde cinema, it is a silent, dreamlike narrative built from looping, symbol-laden episodes.

Content

A woman (Deren) returns home, sees a hooded figure with a mirror for a face, and falls asleep in an armchair. In a repeating dream she pursues the figure, encounters a key falling from her hand, a knife in a loaf of bread, a flower, a telephone off its hook, and multiple versions of herself seated around a table; the loops escalate in menace from her male companion (Hammid) until dream and waking blur and the film ends with the woman found dead amid broken mirror glass by the sea. Deren resisted purely psychological readings, describing the film as concerned with the interior experience of an incident rather than traditional plot.

Production and legacy

Made for about $260 with a 16mm Bolex camera, the film was shot at the couple's Los Angeles home. A musical score by Teiji Ito, Deren's later husband, was added in 1959. Meshes of the Afternoon helped establish the American avant-garde and the "trance film," influenced generations of experimental and art-film makers, and was inducted into the United States National Film Registry in 1990 as culturally, historically and aesthetically significant.
