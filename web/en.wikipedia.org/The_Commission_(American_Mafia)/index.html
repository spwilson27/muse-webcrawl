The Commission (American Mafia)

The Commission is the governing body of the American Mafia, formed in 1931 following the Castellammarese War. It was established by Lucky Luciano and Meyer Lansky to replace the old "boss of bosses" system with a board of directors that would arbitrate disputes between crime families and approve major decisions, including the murder of a family boss.

Composition

The Commission's core members were the bosses of New York's Five Families — Bonanno, Colombo (Profaci), Gambino (Mangano), Genovese (Luciano) and Lucchese (Gagliano) — together with the Chicago Outfit and, at times, representatives of other families such as Buffalo and Philadelphia. Luciano served as its guiding figure until his deportation to Italy in 1946.

History

The Commission sanctioned or approved major underworld events, including the 1957 hit on Albert Anastasia. The Apalachin meeting of November 1957, a national gathering raided by police, publicly exposed the organisation's existence. In the 1985–86 Mafia Commission Trial, federal prosecutors under Rudolph Giuliani used the RICO Act to convict the heads of the Five Families' leadership — including Anthony "Fat Tony" Salerno, Anthony "Tony Ducks" Corallo and Carmine Persico — severely weakening the body, which has met rarely since.
