Joseph Brant (March 1743 – 24 November 1807), known in Mohawk as Thayendanegea, was a Mohawk military and political leader, based in present-day New York and later Canada, who was closely associated with Great Britain during and after the American Revolutionary War. One of the most prominent Native American leaders of his era, he remains a controversial figure for his role in frontier warfare.

Early life

Born in the Ohio Country during a hunting expedition, Brant grew up at Canajoharie in the Mohawk Valley, New York. His sister Molly Brant was the consort of Sir William Johnson, the British superintendent of Indian affairs, which brought the young Brant into Johnson's household. He fought with British forces as a teenager in the French and Indian War, attended Eleazar Wheelock's Moor's Indian Charity School in Connecticut (a precursor of Dartmouth College), learned English, and worked as an interpreter for the Indian Department.

American Revolutionary War

Visiting London in 1775–76, where he was received at court and sat for portraits, Brant committed the Mohawk and many Iroquois to the British cause. He led war parties in the frontier campaigns of 1777–1782, including the actions associated with Oriskany, Cobleskill, German Flatts, the raid on Cherry Valley (1778) — where civilian deaths were blamed on his forces, though Brant was noted for efforts to restrain violence — and the Sullivan Expedition's retaliatory devastation of Iroquoia in 1779. He became a captain in the British Indian Department and a principal war chief.

Postwar years in Canada

After the war, in which Britain ceded Iroquois lands without consulting its allies, Brant obtained the Haldimand Proclamation grant (1784) along the Grand River in Ontario, where the Six Nations reserve was established and the town of Brantford (Brant's ford) bears his name. He built a home at Burlington, translated Anglican religious texts (including the Gospel of Mark) into Mohawk, helped found St Paul's Church (1785), and worked — ultimately unsuccessfully — to build a Western Confederacy to resist American expansion, while becoming a prosperous farmer and slaveholder. He died at Burlington in 1807.
