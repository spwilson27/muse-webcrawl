Flood warning

A flood warning is an alert issued by meteorological and hydrological authorities warning that flooding is expected, or is already occurring, in a specified area. Flood warnings are a core part of flood risk management, intended to give residents and emergency services time to protect life and property. They are distinct from a flood watch (in United States usage), which indicates that conditions are favourable for flooding but it has not yet been observed or is not yet certain.

Types of warning

In the United States, the National Weather Service issues flood watches, flood warnings, and flash flood warnings, the latter for rapid-onset flooding within hours of heavy rain. Areal flood warnings cover larger river basins, while river flood warnings are tied to forecast crest levels at specific gauge points. In the United Kingdom, the Environment Agency operates a three-tier system of flood alerts, flood warnings, and severe flood warnings.

Issuing and dissemination

Warnings are based on rainfall observations and forecasts, river level gauges, soil moisture, snowpack, and hydrological models. They are disseminated through broadcast media, sirens, SMS and cell-broadcast alerts, social media, and automated telephone services. Studies emphasise that an effective warning system also requires that recipients understand the message and know what action to take.
