Worldbuilding

Worldbuilding is the process of constructing a fictional world, sometimes called a constructed world or conworld, that may underpin works of fiction such as novels, films, television series, and games. A developed fictional world may include geography, history, ecology, languages, cultures, politics, and rules governing magic or technology.

Related fields
Speculative fiction, fantasy, science fiction, role-playing games
Notable examples
Middle-earth, Arrakis, the Discworld

History

Although fictional settings are as old as literature, deliberate and elaborate worldbuilding became a hallmark of modern fantasy through J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth, developed over decades with constructed languages, histories, and mythologies. The practice spread through science fiction — Frank Herbert's Dune universe being a prominent example — and through tabletop role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, which encouraged detailed shared settings.

Approaches

Creators describe "top-down" worldbuilding, which constructs the world broadly before filling in detail, and "bottom-up" worldbuilding, which begins with a small locality and expands outward. Tolkien's concept of "sub-creation" and the notion of internal consistency — a "secondary world" with its own logic — remain influential touchstones.

In other media

Worldbuilding is central to video game design, shared cinematic universes, and transmedia storytelling, and is also used in futures studies and scenario planning. Excessive exposition of a constructed world is sometimes criticised as "worldbuilder's disease" when it displaces narrative.
