Elbląg Canal

The Elbląg Canal (Polish: Kanał Elbląski; German: Oberländischer Kanal) is an 80.5-kilometre canal in the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship of northern Poland, running from Lake Drużno, south of the city of Elbląg, to Ostróda and Lake Jeziorak. It is famous for its system of inclined planes, on which boats are carried overland on rail tracks to overcome a height difference of nearly 100 metres.

A vessel being hauled overland on one of the canal's inclined planes.

History

The canal was designed by the Prussian engineer Georg Steenke and built between 1844 and 1860, originally to transport timber and agricultural produce from the Masurian lake district to the Baltic port of Elbląg. Instead of a long flight of locks over the moraine hills, Steenke built five inclined planes powered by water-driven machinery, modelled in part on the Morris Canal in the United States.

The inclined planes

At each plane, ships are floated onto wheeled cradles and hauled up or down rails across dry land, powered by waterwheels and turbines fed by the canal itself. The five planes—Buczyniec, Kąty, Oleśnica, Jelenie, and Całuny—together climb 99.5 metres, an arrangement unique in Europe and still in working order.

Present day

Damaged during the Second World War, the canal was rebuilt by 1948. Today it carries mainly tourist traffic and is protected as a historic monument; passenger boats make the overland journey between Elbląg and Ostróda one of Poland's best-known technical attractions.
