Le Havre (French: [lə ɑvʁ]) is a major port city in the Seine-Maritime department of the Normandy region of northern France. It is situated on the right bank of the estuary of the river Seine on the Channel coast, southwest of the Pays de Caux. Le Havre is France's second-largest commercial port and the largest container port in the country, and it forms with Rouen and Paris the Seine Axis river-sea port system (HAROPA Port).

History

The town was founded in 1517 by King Francis I, who wanted a new harbour to replace the silted ports of Harfleur and Honfleur; it was at first called Franciscopolis and later Le Havre-de-Grâce. From here Giovanni da Verrazzano sailed in 1524 to explore the North American coast, and in the 17th and 18th centuries the port grew on colonial trade, including the triangular trade. Le Havre was an embarkation point for emigrants to the Americas in the 19th century and a major transatlantic liner port — the great French Line ships such as Normandie called here.

Destruction and reconstruction

In September 1944 the city centre was almost entirely destroyed by Allied bombing during the battle to capture the port (Operation Astonia); around 5,000 inhabitants died and some 12,500 buildings were destroyed. Between 1945 and 1964 the city was rebuilt to the plans of the architect Auguste Perret, a pioneer of reinforced concrete construction. Perret's grid-plan city, with its monumental town hall and the 107-metre tower of St Joseph's Church, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2005 as an outstanding example of post-war urban planning.

Economy

The port of Le Havre, developed seaward with the deep-water Port 2000 container terminals, handles much of France's container traffic and crude-oil imports; the surrounding industrial zone hosts refineries and petrochemical plants. The city is linked to Honfleur and the A13 motorway by the Pont de Normandie, one of the world's great cable-stayed bridges, opened in 1995. Cross-Channel ferries connect Le Havre with Portsmouth.

Culture

Le Havre is closely associated with Impressionism: Claude Monet painted Impression, Sunrise — the picture that named the movement — in the harbour in 1872. The MuMa (Musée d'art moderne André Malraux) holds one of France's finest collections of Impressionist painting. The painter Raoul Dufy and the writer Raymond Queneau were born in the city. Le Havre AC, founded in 1872, is among France's oldest football clubs.
