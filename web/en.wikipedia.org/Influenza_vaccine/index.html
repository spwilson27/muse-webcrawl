Influenza vaccine

TypeInactivated, live attenuated, recombinant
TargetInfluenza A and B viruses
RoutesIntramuscular injection, intranasal spray
FrequencyAnnual

Influenza vaccines, colloquially known as flu shots or flu jabs, are vaccines that protect against infection by influenza viruses. New versions are developed twice a year, as the influenza virus changes rapidly and circulates in different strains in the northern and southern hemisphere seasons. Most influenza vaccines provide modest to high protection against influenza, though effectiveness varies from season to season depending on how well the vaccine strains match the circulating strains.

Types

The most common vaccines are trivalent or quadrivalent formulations containing antigens from two influenza A subtypes (H1N1 and H3N2) and one or two influenza B lineages. Inactivated influenza vaccines are given by injection and contain killed virus or split virions; the live attenuated influenza vaccine is given as a nasal spray and contains weakened virus that replicates in the cooler nasal passages. Recombinant vaccines are produced in insect cell culture without eggs, and cell-culture-based vaccines avoid egg-adaptation mutations.

Strain selection

The World Health Organization coordinates a global surveillance network that recommends vaccine composition each February for the northern hemisphere season and each September for the southern hemisphere. Production, traditionally in embryonated hens' eggs, takes several months, so the composition must be decided well before the season begins; mismatches reduce effectiveness.

Effectiveness and safety

When the vaccine is well matched, studies estimate it reduces the risk of influenza illness by roughly 40–60 percent in the general population, with greater benefits in preventing hospitalisation and death among the elderly and those with chronic conditions. Common side effects are soreness at the injection site and mild fever. The injectable vaccines cannot cause influenza because they contain no live virus.

Recommendations

Health authorities such as the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention recommend annual vaccination for everyone six months of age and older, with particular emphasis on young children, pregnant women, older adults, healthcare workers and people with chronic illnesses.
