Edsger W. Dijkstra

Edsger Wybe Dijkstra (11 May 1930 – 6 August 2002) was a Dutch computer scientist, programmer, software engineer, and science essayist. He received the Turing Award in 1972 for fundamental contributions to programming as a high, intellectual challenge. His shortest-path algorithm, his advocacy of structured programming, and his widely quoted aphorisms shaped the discipline of computer science.

Dijkstra, architect of structured programming and graph algorithms.

Career

Dijkstra studied theoretical physics at Leiden University but moved into programming at the Mathematical Centre in Amsterdam, where he was, in effect, the Netherlands' first programmer. In 1956, to demonstrate the power of the ARMAC computer, he devised his shortest-path algorithm. He helped design the first ALGOL 60 compiler and contributed the concepts of semaphores and mutual exclusion to concurrent programming.

Structured programming

His 1968 letter "Go To Statement Considered Harmful" — whose famous title was coined by its editor, Niklaus Wirth — argued that unrestricted jumps made programs hard to reason about. His book A Discipline of Programming (1976) developed program derivation from formal specifications. Dijkstra also wrote over 1,300 numbered manuscripts (the "EWDs"), which he circulated by photocopy and which are now archived online.

Later years

From 1984 until his death he held the Schlumberger Centennial Chair at the University of Texas at Austin. He died of cancer in 2002 at his home in Nuenen.
