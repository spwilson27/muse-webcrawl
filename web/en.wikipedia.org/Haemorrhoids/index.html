Haemorrhoids

Haemorrhoids (or hemorrhoids), also known as piles, are vascular structures in the anal canal that normally aid with stool control. They become a disease when swollen or inflamed. Symptoms of pathological haemorrhoids include pain, itching, and rectal bleeding, especially during bowel movements.

Types

Internal haemorrhoids originate above the dentate line and are usually painless but may prolapse or bleed. External haemorrhoids form below the dentate line and can be painful, particularly if a blood clot forms within them (thrombosed haemorrhoid).

Causes and treatment

Risk factors include constipation and straining, pregnancy, prolonged sitting, and low-fibre diets. Conservative treatment focuses on dietary fibre, fluids, and topical measures; persistent or severe cases may be treated by rubber band ligation, sclerotherapy, or surgical haemorrhoidectomy.
