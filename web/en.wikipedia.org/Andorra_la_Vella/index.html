Andorra la Vella

Andorra la Vella (Catalan pronunciation: [ənˈdorə lə ˈβeʎə]) is the capital and largest city of Andorra, a landlocked microstate in the eastern Pyrenees between France and Spain. It is also the name of the parish that surrounds the city. The population in 2023 was about 20,700, and the wider metropolitan area has over 40,000 inhabitants — more than half of the country's total population. Situated at an elevation of 1,023 metres (3,356 ft), it is the highest capital city in Europe.

History

The area has been settled since the Neolithic period, and the settlement of Andorra la Vella has been the principal city of Andorra since the Middle Ages. In 1278 and 1288 the Paréages between the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix established the co-principality that still governs Andorra today. The town's most famous historic building, the Casa de la Vall, was built in 1580 as a manor house for the Busquets family; since 1702 it has served as the seat of the General Council, Andorra's parliament.

For centuries the valley was relatively isolated, relying on livestock farming and, later, tobacco growing and smuggling. After the Second World War the town grew rapidly as Andorra became a tourist destination and tax haven, and it was formally proclaimed capital when Andorra adopted its first written constitution in 1993, becoming a parliamentary democracy.

Geography and climate

Andorra la Vella lies in the southwest of the country at the confluence of two mountain streams, the Gran Valira river being formed by their union. It has an oceanic climate influenced by its high altitude, with snowy winters, mild summers and significant rainfall through the year. Winters frequently see temperatures below freezing, while summer days are warm but nights cool.

Main sights

  • Casa de la Vall — historic parliament building dating from 1580.
  • Church of Santa Coloma — a pre-Romanesque church with a distinctive round bell tower, dating from the 9th–10th centuries.
  • Església de Sant Esteve — a Romanesque church in the old town.
  • Caldea — one of the largest thermal spa complexes in Europe, opened in 1994.
  • Pont de Paris — a cable-stayed bridge over the Gran Valira, opened in 2005.

Economy

Tourism accounts for roughly 80% of Andorra's GDP, and Andorra la Vella is the country's commercial hub, with duty-free shopping along the Avinguda Meritxell drawing millions of visitors annually, mainly from Spain and France. The city is also the centre of Andorra's banking sector and hosts the ski tourism gateway villages nearby, including access to the Grandvalira and Vallnord resorts.

Sport

The city is home to the Estadi Comunal d'Andorra la Vella, used by the Andorra national football team and local clubs. Andorra la Vella hosted events of the 1991 and 2005 Games of the Small States of Europe, and stages finishes of stages of the Vuelta a España and the Tour de France when those races visit the principality.
