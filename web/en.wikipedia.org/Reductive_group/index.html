Reductive group

Reductive groups are classified by combinatorial data such as root systems and Dynkin diagrams.

In mathematics, a reductive group is a type of linear algebraic group over a field, characterised by the property that its unipotent radical is trivial. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, reductive groups are exactly the groups whose finite-dimensional representations are completely reducible — every representation splits as a direct sum of irreducible ones. The class includes the classical groups of most importance in geometry, number theory and physics.

Examples

The general linear group GLn, the special linear group SLn, the orthogonal and symplectic groups, and all finite groups (viewed as algebraic groups) are reductive. Any torus — a product of multiplicative groups — is reductive, as is any semisimple group. By contrast, the additive group and the group of upper-triangular matrices with 1s on the diagonal are unipotent and not reductive (unless trivial).

Structure theory

A connected reductive group is determined up to isogeny by its root datum, a package of lattices and roots generalising the root systems of Lie theory. Such a group decomposes into a central torus and a semisimple derived subgroup; the classification of semisimple groups by Dynkin diagrams therefore extends to all connected reductive groups. Important subgroups include Borel subgroups (maximal connected solvable subgroups) and parabolic subgroups, and a choice of maximal torus yields the Weyl group, root system and the Bruhat decomposition.

Reductive groups over non-closed fields

Over general fields, reductive groups are described by Galois descent from their split forms and classified by cohomology. They are the central objects of the theory of automorphic forms and the Langlands program: the conjectural Langlands group is built from reductive groups, and local and global L-functions are attached to their representations. Reductive Lie groups — the real points of reductive algebraic groups — likewise dominate harmonic analysis and representation theory over local fields.
