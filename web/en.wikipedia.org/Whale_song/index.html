Whale song

Spectrogram of humpback whale song; the structured, repeating themes are a form of animal culture

Whale song is the sound made by some species of whale — principally male humpback whales — consisting of long, patterned sequences of vocalisations repeated for hours or days at a time. The term "song" is used by researchers because of the regular, structured and repeating nature of these sounds, which resemble human musical composition. Other baleen whales, including blue, fin, bowhead and minke whales, also produce song-like vocalisations, while toothed whales communicate with clicks, whistles and pulsed calls used in echolocation and social interaction.

Humpback whale song

The song of the humpback whale (Megaptera novaeangliae) is organised hierarchically: individual sounds (units) are arranged into phrases, phrases into themes, and themes into a complete song lasting typically 10–20 minutes, which may be repeated continuously for more than 24 hours. All males in a population sing essentially the same song, which gradually evolves over the breeding season; remarkably, new song types can spread between populations across the South Pacific in a form of cultural transmission.

Songs are sung mainly on breeding grounds in warm tropical waters and during migration. Because only males sing, and singing peaks in the breeding season, the primary function is thought to be related to mating — attracting females, mediating male competition, or both — although the exact function remains debated.

Production and study

Whales produce sound without exhaling: air is recycled over a U-shaped fold in the larynx (functionally analogous to vocal cords) and through specialised air sacs, allowing continuous singing while submerged. Songs travel enormous distances in the ocean's deep sound channel (the SOFAR channel); blue and fin whale calls, among the loudest sounds made by any animal, can in principle propagate across entire ocean basins.

Whale song became publicly famous through Roger Payne and Scott McVay's 1970 album Songs of the Humpback Whale, a best-selling nature recording that galvanised the "Save the Whales" movement and contributed to the 1986 moratorium on commercial whaling (see Whaling in Japan). Researchers now study how increasing ocean noise from shipping interferes with whale communication.
