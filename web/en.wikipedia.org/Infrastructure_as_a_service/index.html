Infrastructure as a service

Infrastructure as a service (IaaS) is a form of cloud computing that provides virtualized computing resources over the internet — principally servers (virtual machines), storage, and networking — on a pay-as-you-go basis. IaaS is the most basic of the cloud service models, sitting below platform as a service (PaaS) and software as a service (SaaS) in the level of abstraction: the provider manages physical data centres, hardware, and virtualization, while the customer controls the operating system, middleware, and applications.

Characteristics

IaaS resources are typically provisioned on demand through APIs or dashboards and billed by usage, allowing organizations to scale capacity quickly and convert capital expenditure on hardware into operating expenditure. Leading providers include Amazon Web Services (EC2), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform.

Uses and model

Typical uses include hosting websites, development and test environments, big-data analysis, backup and disaster recovery, and running enterprise applications. The shared responsibility model is a core feature: the provider secures the underlying infrastructure, while customers remain responsible for patching guest operating systems, configuring access, and protecting their data.
