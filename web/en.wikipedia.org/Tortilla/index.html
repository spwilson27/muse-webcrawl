Tortilla

Tortilla
TypeFlatbread
Place of originMexico / Mesoamerica
Main ingredientsCorn (maize) masa, or wheat flour
VariationsCorn tortilla, flour tortilla

A tortilla is a thin, circular unleavened flatbread from Mexico, originally made from nixtamalized corn (maize) meal and now also from wheat flour. The word tortilla comes from Spanish torta ("cake") plus the diminutive -illa; in Nahuatl, the corn tortilla is called tlaxcalli. Tortillas are the foundation of dishes such as tacos, burritos, enchiladas, quesadillas, and tostadas.

Corn tortillas

Corn tortillas are made from masa — corn kernels soaked and cooked in an alkaline solution (usually limewater) in the process called nixtamalization, which improves nutritional value and flavor, then ground, kneaded, pressed flat, and cooked briefly on a hot griddle called a comal. Corn tortillas have been a staple of Mesoamerican diets for thousands of years and remain the most common tortilla in Mexico and Central America.

Flour tortillas

Wheat flour tortillas, made with flour, water, fat (traditionally lard), salt, and sometimes a leavening agent, developed in northern Mexico and are standard for burritos and fajitas in Tex-Mex cuisine. They are softer and more pliable than corn tortillas.

Production and use

Commercial production uses presses and conveyor ovens, and tortillas are among the most widely sold bread products in the Americas. Tortillas are eaten as a wrap, as an accompaniment scooped up with food, fried into chips (totopos), or used in soups such as sopa de tortilla. In Spain, "tortilla" instead denotes an omelette (tortilla española, made with potatoes), a different dish that shares only the name.
