Jemaa el-Fnaa


Jemaa el-Fnaa is a square and market place in Marrakech's medina quarter, Morocco.

Jemaa el-Fnaa (also Jemaa el-Fna, Djema el-Fna, or Djemaa el-Fnaa; Arabic: ساحة جامع الفناء) is a famous square and market place in the medina of Marrakech, Morocco, just off the Koutoubia Mosque. It is the main square of the city and one of the best-known squares in Africa, serving as a centre of activity and entertainment for locals and tourists alike.

History

The square dates to the foundation of Marrakech by the Almoravids around 1070. Its name is usually translated as "the mosque of annihilation" or "assembly of the dead," possibly referring to the ruined mosque once projected at its centre or to public executions once held there. For centuries the square has been a marketplace, a place of public gathering, and a venue for storytellers, musicians, acrobats, snake charmers, and healers.

The square today

During the day the square is filled with orange-juice and water sellers, fruit and nut stalls, and performers. As evening falls, it fills with dozens of open-air food stalls serving grilled meats, harira soup, snails, and other Moroccan dishes, while crowds gather around halqa — circles of street performers, Gnawa musicians, dancers, and traditional storytellers performing in Arabic and Berber.

Recognition

In 2001, UNESCO proclaimed the cultural space of Jemaa el-Fnaa a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity — one of the first such proclamations — and it was inscribed on the Representative List in 2008. The square and medina form part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of the Medina of Marrakech, inscribed in 1985.
