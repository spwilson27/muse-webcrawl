Social referencing

Social referencing is the process by which infants and young children look to a trusted person's emotional reactions to guide their own behavior toward an object, person, or situation. It emerges in the first year of life and is a key mechanism of emotional and social development.

The visual cliff experiments

In a classic demonstration by Sorce, Emde, Campos, and Klinnert (1985), infants placed at the edge of a visual cliff would cross the apparent drop when their mother posed a happy expression, but refused when she posed fear. Infants similarly use caregivers' reactions to decide whether to approach ambiguous toys or unfamiliar adults.

Development

Social referencing requires joint attention and the ability to read facial expressions, which consolidate toward the end of the first year. It is considered an early form of seeking information from others, related to theory of mind development and attachment security. Children with autism spectrum conditions may show reduced social referencing, one reason the behavior is watched as a developmental milestone.
