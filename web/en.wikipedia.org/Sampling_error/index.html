Sampling error

A sampling error is a statistical error that arises from the failure of a sample to perfectly represent the population from which it was drawn. Because a sample contains only a subset of the population, statistics computed from it — such as a mean or proportion — will generally differ from the true population values, even when the sampling is carried out properly.

Causes and behaviour

Sampling error arises purely from random variation in which members of the population happen to be selected. Its expected size decreases as the sample size increases, roughly in proportion to the inverse square root of the sample size. For a sample mean, the standard error is given by σ/√n, where σ is the population standard deviation and n is the sample size.

Sampling error is distinct from non-sampling error, which covers mistakes such as measurement error, non-response bias, faulty questionnaires and data-processing mistakes. Unlike sampling error, non-sampling error does not necessarily shrink as the sample grows.

Margin of error

In opinion polling, sampling error is commonly reported through the margin of error, which expresses the range within which the true population value is expected to lie at a stated confidence level, typically 95 per cent. The margin of error accounts only for sampling error and not for other sources of error such as biased question wording or coverage problems.
