Breakbulk

Breakbulk, or break bulk, refers to cargo that must be loaded individually, rather than in shipping containers or in bulk as with oil or grain. Typical breakbulk goods include crates, drums, barrels, pallets, heavy machinery, steel products, and project cargo such as wind turbine components.

History

Before containerization in the 1960s, nearly all ocean cargo was breakbulk. Loading a ship required large gangs of dockworkers (stevedores) and could take weeks. The rise of standardized containers made breakbulk a niche, though it remains essential for oversized or heavy items that cannot fit in containers.

Handling

Breakbulk cargo is lifted piece by piece using ship's cranes or shore cranes, often with slings, nets, and spreader bars. Goods are stowed in the ship's holds and secured against movement. Modern multipurpose vessels are designed to carry a mix of breakbulk and containers.
