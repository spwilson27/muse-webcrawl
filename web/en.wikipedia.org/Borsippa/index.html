Borsippa

Borsippa was an ancient Sumerian and Babylonian city located southwest of Babylon, in present-day Iraq, at the site of modern Birs Nimrud. It was an important religious center, closely paired with Babylon in Mesopotamian inscriptions.

Religion and the ziggurat

Borsippa was the cult center of the god Nabu, the god of wisdom and writing, son of Marduk, the chief god of Babylon. Its great temple was the Ezida, and its ziggurat, Euriminanki ("house of the seven guides of heaven and earth"), still rises prominently above the plain. The ziggurat's ruined tower was long identified in local tradition with the biblical Tower of Babel.

History

The city flourished from the third millennium BCE through the Neo-Babylonian period. During the New Year (Akitu) festival, the statue of Nabu was carried in procession from Borsippa to Babylon. Borsippa remained inhabited into the Islamic era before being gradually abandoned. Excavations, notably by Hormuzd Rassam in the 19th century, recovered numerous cuneiform tablets, including astronomical texts.
