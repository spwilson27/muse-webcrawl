Teide National Park (Spanish: Parque nacional del Teide) is a national park in Tenerife, Canary Islands, Spain, centred on Mount Teide, the highest mountain in Spain at 3,718 metres and the third-largest volcano in the world from its base on the ocean floor.

Geology

The park occupies the Las Cañadas caldera, a vast volcanic depression about 17 km across, from which the Teide–Pico Viejo stratovolcano complex rises. Its landscapes of lava flows, cones and the distinctive Roques de García rock formations record eruptions over hundreds of thousands of years; the most recent eruption in the complex was that of Chinyero in 1909.

Flora and fauna

Despite the harsh high-altitude desert climate, the park hosts endemic plants such as the Teide violet, which grows near the summit, and the tall red-flowered tajinaste. It is also an important site for invertebrates and for the Tenerife lizard.

Recognition

Created in 1954, the park was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. Its clear, dark skies make it a major astronomical site, home to the Teide Observatory, and the park is among the most visited national parks in Europe.
