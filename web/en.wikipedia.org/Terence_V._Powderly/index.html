Terence V. Powderly

Terence Vincent Powderly (January 22, 1849 – June 24, 1924) was an American labor union leader, politician, and attorney, best known as the Grand Master Workman of the Knights of Labor from 1879 to 1893, a period during which the Knights became the largest and most influential American labor organisation of the 19th century. He also served three terms as mayor of Scranton, Pennsylvania.

Early life and career

Powderly was born in Carbondale, Pennsylvania, to Irish Catholic immigrants. He left school at 13, worked as a railroad switchman and machinist, and was blacklisted after joining the Machinists and Blacksmiths Union following the Panic of 1873. Elected mayor of Scranton on the Greenback-Labor ticket in 1878, he served until 1884, balancing political office with union leadership.

Leadership of the Knights of Labor

Powderly joined the secretive Knights of Labor in 1876 and became its Grand Master Workman in 1879. Under his leadership the order abandoned secrecy, admitted women and (in principle) Black workers alongside the skilled and unskilled, and grew explosively after the successful 1885 strike against Jay Gould's Wabash Railroad, reaching perhaps 700,000 members in 1886. Powderly personally opposed strikes, preferring arbitration, boycotts, cooperative enterprises, and political action around demands such as the eight-hour day, abolition of child labor, and currency reform. The failed Great Southwest railroad strike of 1886 and the backlash after the Haymarket affair began the Knights' rapid decline; Powderly was ousted in 1893.

Later life

Admitted to the bar in 1894, Powderly supported William McKinley and served as U.S. Commissioner General of Immigration (1897–1902) and later in the Bureau of Immigration, where he worked until shortly before his death in Washington, D.C., in 1924. He published an autobiography, Thirty Years of Labor (1889), and a memoir, The Path I Trod, appeared posthumously in 1940. He was inducted into the U.S. Department of Labor's Labor Hall of Honor in 1999.
