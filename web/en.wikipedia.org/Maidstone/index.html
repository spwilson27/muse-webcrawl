Maidstone is the county town of Kent in South East England. It lies on the River Medway, about 32 miles south-east of London. The town has long served as an administrative, commercial and market centre for the surrounding agricultural area, historically known for hops, fruit and paper production.

History

Evidence of settlement dates to the Stone Age, and the Romans built roads and villas in the area. Maidstone developed as a market town in the Middle Ages; the Archbishop's Palace and All Saints Church date from the 14th century. It was associated with Wyatt's rebellion in 1554 and was the scene of a battle during the English Civil War in 1648, when Parliamentarian forces defeated Royalists in the town.

Geography

The town sits where the River Medway cuts through the ridge of the North Downs. Much of the urban area is built on the slopes of the river valley. Villages have been absorbed into the urban area, and the borough includes extensive rural surroundings.

Economy and transport

Maidstone has a retail and service economy, with shopping centres in the town and business parks nearby. It has three railway stations, with services to London, Ashford and the Medway towns, and it lies close to the M20 motorway.

Landmarks

Notable buildings include Leeds Castle (a few miles east), the Archbishop's Palace, All Saints Church, and the Maidstone Museum. The River Medway towpath and Mote Park are popular open spaces.
