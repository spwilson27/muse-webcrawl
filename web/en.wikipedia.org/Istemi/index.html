Istemi

Istemi (Old Turkic: 𐰾𐱅𐰢𐰃; known in Byzantine sources as Sizabul or Silziboulos; died 576) was the ruler of the western part of the Göktürk Khaganate, which he shared with his elder brother Bumin Qaghan, founder of the khaganate. As yabghu of the west he directed the Turks' expansion into Central Asia and their celebrated diplomatic exchanges with Sasanian Persia and the Byzantine Empire.

Expansion westward

After Bumin overthrew the Rouran in 552, Istemi campaigned westward: the Hephthalites were crushed in alliance with the Sasanian shah Khosrow I (by about 560–565), and Transoxiana came under Turk control. Istemi thereby controlled the northern Silk Road routes and the Sogdian merchant networks that served them.

Diplomacy

When Persia obstructed the Sogdian silk trade, Istemi sent the embassy of the Sogdian merchant Maniakh to Constantinople in 568, proposing direct trade and an alliance against Persia to Emperor Justin II. The Byzantine envoy Zemarchus returned with the embassy to Istemi's court — one of the earliest documented contacts between the Turks and Europe. Relations later soured over Byzantium's separate dealings with the Avars.

Legacy

Istemi's western realm became the Western Turkic Khaganate under his son Tardu. His embassies mark the Göktürks' emergence as a world power linking China, Persia, and Byzantium.
