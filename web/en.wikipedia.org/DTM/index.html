DTM

This article is about the German touring car series. For other uses, see DTM (disambiguation).

The DTM (Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters) is a touring car racing series based in Germany. It descends from the original Deutsche Tourenwagen Meisterschaft, which ran from 1984 to 1996, and was revived as the Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters in 2000. Long contested by silhouette racing cars from Audi, BMW and Mercedes-Benz, the series switched to GT3-based cars in 2021.

History

Original DTM (1984–1996)

The Deutsche Tourenwagen Meisterschaft began in 1984 with near-production cars and grew into the technologically extravagant Class 1 era of the mid-1990s, with four-wheel drive, advanced electronics and budgets rivalling Formula One. Rebranded as the International Touring Car Championship for 1995–96, the series collapsed at the end of 1996 when costs spiralled and manufacturer support vanished.

Revival (2000 onwards)

The series returned in 2000 as the Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters with simpler rear-wheel-drive silhouette coupés. Mercedes-Benz and Opel were the founding marques; Audi joined in 2004 and BMW returned in 2012. Champions of the era include Bernd Schneider (a record five titles across both series), Mattias Ekström, Timo Scheider, Martin Tomczyk, Marco Wittmann and René Rast. After Audi's withdrawal, the DTM adopted GT3 regulations in 2021 to survive, and in 2023 promotion passed from ITR to the ADAC.

Format and cars

Race weekends traditionally feature two sprint races with a mandatory pit stop. The Class 1 cars of 2019–2020 used 2.0-litre turbocharged four-cylinder engines producing over 600 hp in a common-spec chassis with a silhouette body. The GT3 era uses production-based GT3 machinery from manufacturers such as Audi, BMW, Ferrari, Lamborghini, McLaren, Mercedes-AMG and Porsche, with Balance of Performance equalisation.
