Claude Elwood Shannon (April 30, 1916 – February 24, 2001) was an American mathematician, electrical engineer, computer scientist and cryptographer known as the "father of information theory" and as the "father of the Information Age". Shannon was the first to describe the Boolean gate circuit design, using Boolean algebra to analyse and optimise electrical switch circuits, which led to digital electronics. He founded the theoretical basis of digital circuits, information theory, and much of computer science.

Early work

Shannon's master's thesis at MIT, "A Symbolic Analysis of Relay and Switching Circuits" (1937), applied Boolean algebra to electrical circuits and has been called possibly the most important master's thesis of the twentieth century: it showed that switching circuits could implement all the operations of logic, laying the foundation of digital circuit design. His subsequent work at Bell Labs ranged over fire-control systems, cryptography — including a classified 1945 memorandum that, declassified, became "Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems" (1949), founding modern cryptography — and genetics.

Information theory

His 1948 paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication", published in the Bell System Technical Journal, created the field of information theory. It introduced the bit as the unit of information, defined information entropy, and proved the noisy-channel coding theorem, which sets the maximum rate (channel capacity) at which information can be transmitted reliably over a noisy channel. The theory underlies data compression, error-correcting codes, digital communications, and much of modern computing.

Later pursuits

Shannon wrote an early paper on computer chess (1950), built machines including the maze-solving mouse "Theseus" and the juggling and unicycling machines that expressed his playful inventiveness, and coined estimates of chess's game-tree complexity (the "Shannon number", about 10120). From 1956 he was on the MIT faculty. He died in 2001 after a long illness with Alzheimer's disease.
