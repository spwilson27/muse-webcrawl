McMurdo Station

McMurdo Station is an American Antarctic research centre on the southern tip of Ross Island, on the shore of McMurdo Sound. Operated by the National Science Foundation's United States Antarctic Program, it is the largest station in Antarctica, supporting up to about 1,100 residents in summer.

History

Established in 1956 for the International Geophysical Year near the site of Robert Falcon Scott's Discovery Expedition hut of 1902, McMurdo serves as the logistics hub for the Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station and field camps across the continent.

Facilities

The station includes dormitories, laboratories including the Albert P. Crary Science and Engineering Center, a harbour, Williams Field airfield on the Ross Ice Shelf, and a road — the South Pole Traverse — to the Pole. A nuclear power plant, PM-3A, operated from 1962 until its decommissioning in the 1970s.
