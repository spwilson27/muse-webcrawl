Effective potential

The effective potential (also known as the effective potential energy) is a mathematical expression combining multiple different potential energies and is used to simplify analysis of dynamical systems, particularly orbital mechanics and problems with central forces.

Definition

For a particle moving under a central potential U(r) with conserved angular momentum L about the force centre, the radial motion behaves as if the particle moved in one dimension under the potential:

Ueff(r) = U(r) + L2 / (2mr2),

where the second term, the centrifugal potential, arises from the kinetic energy of the angular motion. The total energy then satisfies E = ½m(dr/dt)2 + Ueff(r), reducing the two-body problem to a one-dimensional problem in r.

Applications

The shape of Ueff directly reveals the allowed regions of motion, turning points, and the stability of circular orbits: a circular orbit occurs at a minimum of the effective potential, and it is stable if that minimum is a true local minimum. For the gravitational (Kepler) potential U = −GMm/r, the effective potential has a single well for bound orbits (E < 0), explaining elliptical orbits, while E ≥ 0 gives unbound parabolic or hyperbolic trajectories. Minima of Ueff also identify Lagrange-point-like equilibria in rotating-frame problems such as the restricted three-body problem.

In quantum mechanics an analogous effective potential appears in the radial Schrödinger equation, where the centrifugal barrier contributes a term ħ2l(l+1)/(2mr2). The term "effective potential" is used more broadly in fields from semiconductor physics to economics for any aggregated potential that captures the essential dynamics of a system.
