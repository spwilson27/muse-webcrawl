Sporran

A dress sporran worn with Highland dress

The sporran (Scottish Gaelic for "purse") is a traditional part of male Scottish Highland dress: a pouch worn at the front of the kilt, hanging from a chain or leather strap around the waist. Because the kilt has no pockets, the sporran serves as a wallet and container for personal items such as money, keys, and a hip flask.

Types

Sporrans range from plain leather day sporrans to elaborate dress sporrans with metal cantles (the decorative rim at the top) and long fur or hair, traditionally of horsehair or animal pelts. Semi-dress sporrans combine a leather body with a fur front. Military and pipe-band sporrans follow regimental patterns, often with distinctive arrangements of tassels and bells. Animal-derived materials have become contentious: since 2007, sporrans made from protected species such as seals require a licence in the United Kingdom, and many makers now use synthetic alternatives or bovine fur.

History

Pouches were worn with Highland dress from at least the 17th century, evolving from simple leather bags on a belt to the ornate accessories of Victorian Highlandism. The modern sporran is worn with both formal and informal Highland outfits, positioned centrally so its top edge sits just below the belt buckle.
