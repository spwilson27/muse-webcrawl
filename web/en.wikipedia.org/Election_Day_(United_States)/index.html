Election Day (United States)

Election Day in the United States is the annual day for general elections of federal public officials. By law it falls on the Tuesday next after the first Monday in November, so it occurs between November 2 and November 8. Elections for president and vice president are held every four years, elections for the House of Representatives every two years, and Senate elections are staggered so about one third of seats are contested every two years.

History

Before 1845, states chose presidential electors within a 34-day window. Congress then standardized a single day: a Tuesday, chosen because travel by horse-drawn vehicle meant voters needed a full day to reach county seats (avoiding travel on the Sabbath and on Wednesday market days), and early November was after harvest but before severe winter weather. Tuesday after the first Monday also avoided November 1, All Saints' Day, and merchants' end-of-month bookkeeping.

Voting practices

Although Election Day is the statutory voting day, most states permit early voting and mail-in or absentee voting; in recent elections a large share of ballots have been cast before the day itself. Election Day is a public holiday in some states and in Puerto Rico, and there are recurring proposals to make it a federal holiday or move voting to a weekend.

Midterms and off-years

Even-numbered years without a presidential election are midterm elections. Many state and local elections are held on the same day, while others fall in odd-numbered "off years" or at other times.
