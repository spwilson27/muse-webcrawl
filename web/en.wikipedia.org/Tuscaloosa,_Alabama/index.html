Tuscaloosa is a city in and the county seat of Tuscaloosa County in west-central Alabama, United States, located on the Black Warrior River. It is the fifth-largest city in Alabama, with a population of about 99,600 at the 2020 census, and is home to the University of Alabama, Stillman College and Shelton State Community College. The city is named after Tuskaloosa, the paramount chief encountered by Hernando de Soto's expedition in 1540.

History

Tuscaloosa was incorporated in 1819, the year Alabama became a state, and served as the state capital from 1826 to 1846, when the capital moved to Montgomery. The University of Alabama was founded there in 1831. During the Civil War the city's industries supplied the Confederacy, and in April 1865 Union cavalry burned much of the university and parts of the town. Tuscaloosa is closely associated with the Civil Rights era: in 1963 Governor George Wallace made his "Stand in the Schoolhouse Door" at the university's Foster Auditorium, and the "Bloody Tuesday" attack on civil rights marchers occurred in Tuscaloosa in 1964.

On 27 April 2011 a massive EF4 tornado struck the city during the 2011 Super Outbreak, killing more than 40 people in Tuscaloosa and destroying or damaging thousands of buildings; recovery and rebuilding continued for years afterwards.

Economy and culture

The economy centres on education, healthcare and manufacturing, including a major Mercedes-Benz assembly plant (opened in the 1990s in nearby Vance) — Mercedes-Benz's first passenger-vehicle plant in the United States. Tuscaloosa is known nationally for college football: the Alabama Crimson Tide play at Bryant–Denny Stadium, one of the largest stadiums in the country. The city's nicknames include the "Druid City", after the oaks planted in its streets, and "T-Town".
