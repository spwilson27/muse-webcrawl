Barrow Gang

The Barrow Gang was an American criminal gang active during the Great Depression, between 1932 and 1934. The gang's core members were Clyde Barrow and Bonnie Parker, and at various times included Clyde's brother Buck Barrow and his wife Blanche, W. D. Jones, Henry Methvin, Joe Palmer, and Raymond Hamilton.

Crimes and notoriety

The gang travelled the Central United States robbing banks, small stores, and rural gas stations, and stealing cars. They were responsible for the deaths of at least nine police officers and several civilians. After a series of shootouts — including a famous gun battle at Joplin, Missouri, in 1933, where rolls of undeveloped film left behind produced the iconic photographs of the pair — the gang became the subject of intense press coverage, and Bonnie and Clyde were romanticised in popular culture even as the manhunt intensified.

End of the gang

Buck Barrow was mortally wounded and Blanche captured at Dexter, Iowa, in July 1933. On May 23, 1934, Bonnie Parker and Clyde Barrow were ambushed and killed by a posse led by Texas Ranger Frank Hamer on a rural road near Bienville Parish, Louisiana. Henry Methvin's cooperation with authorities had helped set the trap, in exchange for a pardon for his father.
