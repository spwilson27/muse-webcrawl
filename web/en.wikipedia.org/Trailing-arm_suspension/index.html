Trailing-arm suspension

A trailing-arm suspension, sometimes referred to as trailing link, is a type of vehicle suspension in which one or more arms are connected between the axle and a pivot point located just ahead of it on the vehicle's frame or body. The arm carries the wheel and typically pivots to allow vertical movement, with a coil spring, torsion bar, or air spring providing support.

Design

In a pure trailing-arm design the wheel moves in a vertical arc without changes in camber or toe. A semi-trailing arm pivots at an angle, introducing some camber change. Trailing arms are widely used for rear wheels of front-wheel-drive cars, often integrated into a twist-beam axle, and historically in the Volkswagen Beetle and Porsche 911. Aircraft landing gear also commonly uses trailing-link geometry to smooth touchdown.

Advantages

The design is simple, compact, inexpensive, and keeps wheels aligned under load, though it offers less tuning flexibility than multi-link designs.
