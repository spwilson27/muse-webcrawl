Idli

Idlis served with sambar and chutney, a classic South Indian breakfast.

Idli (also spelled iddly) is a type of savoury rice cake originating from South India, popular as a breakfast food in Southern India and in Sri Lanka. The cakes are made by steaming a batter consisting of fermented black lentils (de-husked urad dal) and rice. The fermentation process breaks down the starches so that they are more readily metabolised by the body, and produces the slightly sour flavour and soft, spongy texture characteristic of the dish.

Preparation

Rice and urad dal are soaked separately, ground into a smooth batter, mixed with salt, and left to ferment for several hours until the batter roughly doubles in volume. The batter is poured into greased moulds in an idli tray and steamed for about ten minutes. Traditionally, idlis are served hot with sambar, coconut chutney, and idli podi (a dry spice powder). Variations include rava idli, made with semolina, and regional forms such as the thatte idli of Karnataka, which are larger and flatter.

History

A recipe resembling the modern idli appears in Indian works from around the early second millennium CE, although the urad dal and fermentation technique may have arrived with cooks from present-day Indonesia, where fermented foods were long established. Idli is widely regarded as one of the healthiest traditional breakfasts because it is steamed rather than fried, and UNESCO-recognised Chennai restaurants and tiffin rooms have made it a global symbol of Tamil cuisine.
