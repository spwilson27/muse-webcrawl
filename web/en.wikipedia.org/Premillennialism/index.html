Premillennialism

Premillennialism, in Christian eschatology, is the belief that Jesus Christ will physically return to the Earth (the Second Coming) before the Millennium, a thousand-year period during which Christ will reign on Earth. It is one of the main interpretations of the prophecy in Revelation 20, contrasted with postmillennialism and amillennialism.

Beliefs

Premillennialists hold that after Christ's return, Satan will be bound and the righteous will reign with Christ for a literal thousand years, followed by a final rebellion, the Last Judgment, and the creation of a new heaven and new earth. Many premillennialists also expect a period of tribulation before the return.

History

Forms of premillennial belief were common among early church writers such as Papias, Justin Martyr, and Irenaeus. The view receded during the Middle Ages but was revived in the 19th century, notably through dispensationalism associated with John Nelson Darby, and became widespread in evangelical and fundamentalist Christianity.

Variants

Historic premillennialism expects the church to pass through the tribulation, while dispensational premillennialism typically teaches a pretribulation rapture of the church.
