Mahabad

Mahabad (Kurdish: مەهاباد, Mehabad; Persian: مهاباد) is a city in West Azerbaijan Province in northwestern Iran, capital of Mahabad County. It lies south of Lake Urmia in a mountainous valley at about 1,300 metres elevation. The population is predominantly Kurdish (Sorani-speaking), and Mahabad is regarded as a cultural centre of Iranian Kurdistan (Rojhelat), known for its poets, publishers, and Kurdish-language media.

Mahabad lies in the Zagros foothills south of Lake Urmia.

History

Mahabad sits in the historic Mukriyan region. Under the Safavids and later dynasties it was a market town known as Savojbolagh ("cold spring" in Turkish), a name it kept into the 20th century. The city gained lasting fame as the capital of the short-lived Republic of Mahabad, proclaimed by Qazi Muhammad on 22 January 1946 with Soviet backing during the post-war occupation of northern Iran. The republic, the second Kurdish state of the modern era, introduced Kurdish-language schooling and publishing before Iranian forces retook the city in December 1946; Qazi Muhammad was hanged in Mahabad's Chahar Cheragh square in March 1947, an event commemorated by Kurds worldwide.

Culture

Mahabad is celebrated as a city of poets — the 19th-century poet Wafayi is buried nearby, and the famous singer-poet tradition of beyt storytelling survives in its villages. The Mukriyani dialect of Sorani spoken here is a reference standard for Kurdish broadcasting. Mahabad was also the hometown of the family of Mahsa (Jina) Amini — from nearby Saqqez — and the city was a centre of protests after her death in custody in September 2022.
