Nicolaas Govert de Bruijn

Nicolaas Govert "Dick" de Bruijn (9 July 1918 – 17 February 2012) was a Dutch mathematician, noted for his contributions to analysis, number theory, combinatorics and logic. He taught at the Eindhoven University of Technology.

Work

De Bruijn sequences — cyclic sequences in which every possible length-n string over an alphabet appears exactly once — are named for his work in combinatorics. He also developed the Automath project, a pioneering system for checking mathematical proofs by computer, and the de Bruijn index notation for lambda calculus, in which bound variables are represented by numbers indicating binding distance. His work on Penrose tilings helped explain the mathematics of quasicrystals.

Life

Born in The Hague, de Bruijn studied at Leiden and Amsterdam and spent much of his career in Eindhoven. He wrote extensively, including the book Asymptotic Methods in Analysis, and was known for the aphoristic "Bruijn factor," describing the expansion in size when formalising mathematical text.
