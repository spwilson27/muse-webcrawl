Schur's lemma

In mathematics, Schur's lemma is a fundamental result in representation theory, named after Issai Schur. In its simplest form it states that if M and N are two finite-dimensional irreducible representations of a group or Lie algebra, then any intertwining operator from M to N is either zero or an isomorphism; if M = N over an algebraically closed field, the operator is a scalar multiple of the identity.

Consequences

From Schur's lemma it follows that the matrix coefficients of inequivalent irreducible representations are orthogonal, that abelian groups have only one-dimensional irreducible representations over the complex numbers, and that central elements act as scalars in any irreducible representation — a fact heavily used in the representation theory of Lie groups and in quantum mechanics.
