James River

The James River is a river in Virginia that begins in the Appalachian Mountains and flows 348 miles (560 km) to Chesapeake Bay. It was the first river in the Americas named by English colonists (for King James I) and it drains about a quarter of Virginia's land area, including the state capital, Richmond. The Powhatan peoples who lived along it called it the Powhatan River.

Course

The James forms at Iron Gate, Virginia, where the Cowpasture and Jackson rivers meet in the Allegheny Mountains. It flows east through the Shenandoah Valley region and a dramatic water gap in the Blue Ridge at Balcony Falls, then past Lynchburg and through central Virginia to Richmond, where it drops over the fall line in a series of rapids. Below Richmond the river becomes a broad tidal estuary, flowing past historic plantation sites and Jamestown before widening into Hampton Roads and emptying into Chesapeake Bay near Norfolk and Hampton.

History

In May 1607 English colonists established Jamestown on the river's north bank, the first permanent English settlement in North America; the river was the lifeline of the early Virginia colony and the highway for its tobacco trade. During the Civil War the James was a strategic corridor: Union naval forces were turned back at Drewry's Bluff in 1862, and the Army of the James operated along it during the siege of Petersburg. In the twentieth century the river suffered severe industrial pollution, most notoriously from Allied Chemical's discharge of the pesticide Kepone in the 1960s and 1970s, which closed the river's fisheries; cleanup and regulation have since substantially improved water quality and revived fish and wildlife, including Atlantic sturgeon.

Ecology and recreation

The James River watershed supports bald eagles, osprey and migratory birds, and the river is popular for whitewater rafting and kayaking through Richmond, for fishing, and for the James River Park System's trails. Conservation groups regularly grade the river's health, noting steady improvement since the 1970s.
