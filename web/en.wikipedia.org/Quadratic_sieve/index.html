Quadratic sieve

The sieving step of the quadratic sieve

The quadratic sieve algorithm (QS) is an integer factorisation algorithm and, in practice, the second-fastest method known after the general number field sieve. It was invented by Carl Pomerance in 1981 as an improvement on Schroeppel's linear sieve. It is still the fastest method for integers under about 110 decimal digits.

Method

The algorithm searches for a congruence of squares modulo the number n to be factored: values x for which x2 − n is small and smooth (factoring entirely into small primes from a chosen "factor base"). A polynomial sieve — in the multiple-polynomial variant, many quadratics — efficiently finds such relations; linear algebra over GF(2) on their exponent vectors then produces a subset whose product is a perfect square, giving x2 ≡ y2 (mod n) and, with luck, a nontrivial factor via a GCD computation.

Records and significance

Quadratic sieve implementations factored several RSA challenge numbers in the 1980s and early 1990s, including RSA-129 (in 1994, using the multiple-polynomial variant with roughly 1,600 volunteer computers coordinated over the internet — an early large-scale distributed computing effort). The general number field sieve, asymptotically faster, has since taken over for larger inputs.
