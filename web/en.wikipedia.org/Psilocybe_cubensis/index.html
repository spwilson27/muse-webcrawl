Psilocybe cubensis

Kingdom: Fungi
Family: Hymenogastraceae
Genus: Psilocybe
Species: P. cubensis
Active compounds: psilocybin, psilocin

Psilocybe cubensis, commonly known as cubes, is a species of psilocybin mushroom of the family Hymenogastraceae known for its psychoactive properties. It is one of the most widely distributed and most commonly cultivated of the psilocybin mushrooms. The species was first described in 1906 as Stropharia cubensis from Cuba, and its specific epithet refers to that origin.

Description

The cap is 1.5–8 cm across, reddish-cinnamon brown when young, fading to golden brown, and the stem is 4–15 cm tall, white to yellowish, bruising blue when handled — a reaction caused by oxidation of psilocin. The spore print is purplish brown. The mushroom grows singly or in groups on cow dung and in humid grasslands.

Distribution and habitat

P. cubensis is a pan-tropical species, occurring in the Gulf Coast states of the United States, Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean, South America, Southeast Asia, India, and parts of Australia, generally in pastures where cattle graze.

Psychoactivity and cultivation

The principal active compounds are psilocybin and psilocin, typically totalling around 0.5–1% of the dried weight. Because it grows readily on simple substrates, P. cubensis is the species most often grown indoors; numerous named strains, such as Golden Teacher and B+, circulate among growers. Possession and cultivation are illegal in most jurisdictions.
