United States Secretary of Health and Human Services

The United States secretary of health and human services is the head of the United States Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and serves as the principal adviser to the president of the United States on all health matters. The secretary is a member of the president's Cabinet, appointed by the president with the advice and consent of the Senate.

Duties

HHS is the largest grant-making agency in the federal government and administers Medicare and Medicaid through the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services. Its operating divisions include the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the Indian Health Service, and the Administration for Children and Families. The secretary oversees more than 80,000 employees and a budget exceeding one trillion dollars, much of it devoted to the health insurance programs.

History

The office originated in 1953 when President Dwight D. Eisenhower created the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare (HEW), with Oveta Culp Hobby as its first secretary. In 1979 the Department of Education Organization Act split off the education functions into a separate department, and HEW was renamed the Department of Health and Human Services in 1980. Donna Shalala, who served from 1993 to 2001, was the longest-serving secretary.
