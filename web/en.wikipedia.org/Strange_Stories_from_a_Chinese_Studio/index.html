Strange Stories from a Chinese Studio, known in Chinese as Liaozhai zhiyi ("Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio"), is a collection of nearly five hundred supernatural tales written by Pu Songling during the early Qing dynasty and published posthumously in 1766.

Content

Written in classical Chinese in the zhiguai and chuanqi traditions, the stories feature fox spirits, ghosts, demons, corrupt officials and encounters between scholars and beautiful supernatural women. Though cast as marvels, many tales satirise the corruption of the examination system and officialdom in Pu's own day. Famous stories include "The Painted Skin", repeatedly adapted for film.
