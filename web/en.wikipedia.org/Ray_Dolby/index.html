Ray Dolby

Ray Milton Dolby (1933–2013) was an American engineer and inventor who founded Dolby Laboratories. He is best known for Dolby noise reduction, which transformed tape recording, and for the surround-sound systems that bear his name.

Career

Dolby worked at Ampex as a teenager and contributed to the first practical videotape recorder while at Stanford. After a PhD at Cambridge, he founded Dolby Laboratories in London in 1965, introducing the Dolby A noise-reduction system for professional studios, followed by the consumer Dolby B in 1968.

Film sound

Dolby Stereo, launched in the 1970s, brought multichannel optical sound to cinemas; Dolby Digital (1992) and later Dolby Atmos extended the technology. Dolby won an Academy Award, Grammy and Emmy recognition, held more than 50 patents, and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. He died in 2013, leaving much of his fortune to philanthropy.
