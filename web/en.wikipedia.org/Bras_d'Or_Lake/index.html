Bras d'Or Lake

Bras d'Or Lake (French: Lac Bras d'Or) is an inland sea, or large body of partially fresh and salt water, in the centre of Cape Breton Island in the province of Nova Scotia, Canada. It is a saltwater estuary fed by several rivers, with three outlets to the North Atlantic Ocean.

Geography

The lake has a surface area of roughly 1,099 square kilometres and dominates the centre of Cape Breton Island. It reaches the Atlantic through the Great Bras d'Or and Little Bras d'Or channels in the north, and through the St. Peters Canal in the south. Its watershed is a recognized UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.

Name

The name is generally thought to derive from the Portuguese bras d'ouro ("arm of gold"), reflecting its golden appearance; an alternative theory links it to Labrador. The Mi'kmaq name for the lake is Pitupaq.

Ecology and economy

The lake supports oyster farming, fishing, tourism, and sailing. The shores are sparsely populated, and the area is significant to Mi'kmaq communities, including the First Nations of Eskasoni, Wagmatcook, We'koqma'q, Potlotek, and Membertou.
