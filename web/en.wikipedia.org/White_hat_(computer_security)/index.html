White hat (computer security)

A white hat (or white-hat hacker) is a computer security expert who breaks into protected systems and networks with permission in order to test and assess their security, so that vulnerabilities can be fixed. White hats contrast with black hats, who hack for malicious purposes, and grey hats, whose activities fall between the two.

Methods and roles

White hats are employed as penetration testers, security analysts and researchers, and work through audits, red-team exercises and bug bounty programs, in which companies such as Google, Microsoft and HackerOne-hosted programs pay rewards for responsibly disclosed vulnerabilities. Ethical hacking is governed by authorization and rules of engagement agreed with the system owner, and disclosure is coordinated so that fixes can be deployed before details become public.

Terminology

The "hat" terminology borrows from old Western films, in which heroes conventionally wore white hats and villains black ones. The community also recognizes certifications such as Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) for professional penetration testers.
