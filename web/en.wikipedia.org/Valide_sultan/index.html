Valide sultan

Valide sultan (Ottoman Turkish: والده سلطان, "mother sultan") was the title held by the mother of a reigning sultan of the Ottoman Empire. The valide sultan was often the most powerful woman in the empire, presiding over the imperial harem.

Role and influence

The valide sultan received a large income, administered charitable foundations (waqfs), commissioned mosques and public buildings, and corresponded with foreign rulers. During the period known as the Sultanate of Women (roughly 1533–1656), figures such as Hürrem Sultan (as wife), Nurbanu Sultan, Safiye Sultan, and Kösem Sultan wielded exceptional political influence.

Regency

When a young sultan ascended the throne, the valide sultan could act as regent, as Kösem Sultan did for Murad IV and Mehmed IV, and Turhan Sultan after her. The last valide sultan was Rahime Perestu Sultan, adoptive mother of Abdul Hamid II.
