Status: Operational

High Speed 1 (HS1), legally the Channel Tunnel Rail Link (CTRL), is a 109.7-kilometre (68.2 mi) high-speed railway linking London St Pancras International to the Channel Tunnel, with intermediate stations at Stratford International and Ebbsfleet International in the London region and Ashford International in Kent. Built in two sections, the line opened fully on 14 November 2007 and was the first major new main line railway built in Britain for over a century, as well as Britain's first true high-speed line.

Services

The line carries Eurostar international services to Paris, Brussels, Amsterdam and other destinations, and domestic Southeastern "Javelin" high-speed commuter services using Class 395 trains between London and Kent. On opening it cut the London–Paris journey time to 2 hours 15 minutes. The project cost around £5.8 billion; in 2010 the infrastructure was sold on a 30-year concession to a consortium of Borealis Infrastructure and Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan for £2.1 billion. Passenger journeys across the Stratford-to-Kent section and the redevelopment around the stations have been credited with significant regeneration effects.
