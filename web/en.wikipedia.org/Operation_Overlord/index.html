Operation Overlord

Operation Overlord was the codename for the Battle of Normandy, the Allied operation that launched the successful liberation of German-occupied Western Europe during World War II. The operation was launched on 6 June 1944 (D-Day) with the Normandy landings (Operation Neptune). A 1,200-plane airborne assault preceded an amphibious assault involving more than 5,000 vessels; nearly 160,000 troops crossed the English Channel on D-Day, with more than two million Allied troops in France by the end of August 1944.

Planning and deception

Planning for a cross-Channel invasion began in 1942–43; the COSSAC staff selected Normandy over the Pas-de-Calais, and the plan was greatly expanded by Montgomery and approved by Eisenhower, appointed Supreme Allied Commander in December 1943. The elaborate deception plan Operation Fortitude convinced German commanders that the main invasion would come at Calais (and, for Fortitude North, in Norway), complete with a fictitious First U.S. Army Group under Patton. Weather forecasting proved critical: the landings were postponed from 5 to 6 June on Group Captain Stagg's forecast of a brief improvement.

The campaign

After the landings at Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno and Sword beaches, the Allies built up forces through artificial Mulberry harbours (one destroyed by a storm) and the PLUTO undersea fuel pipelines. Caen, a D-Day objective, fell only in July after heavy fighting; Operation Goodwood and the U.S. breakout (Operation Cobra, late July) shattered the German front. The encirclement of German forces in the Falaise pocket in August, the liberation of Paris on 25 August, and the rapid pursuit to the Seine concluded the campaign by 30 August, at a cost of more than 226,000 Allied casualties and still heavier German losses.

Legacy

Overlord opened the long-awaited second front in Western Europe and set the stage for the Allied advance into Germany; its scale as the largest seaborne invasion in history and its logistics remain benchmarks in military history.
