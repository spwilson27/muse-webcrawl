The Potsdam Giants (German: Potsdamer Riesengarde) was a Prussian infantry regiment composed of exceptionally tall soldiers, formally the Grand Grenadier Regiment No. 6. It was famously expanded and indulged by King Frederick William I of Prussia (reigned 1713–1740), whose obsession with giant soldiers became a byword for the extravagances of absolute monarchy.

History

The regiment's origins trace to a grenadier unit raised in 1675 under Frederick William, Elector of Brandenburg. His grandson Frederick William I transformed it into a personal guard of giants: by the end of his reign it numbered some 3,200 men. Minimum height was about 6 Prussian feet (roughly 1.88 m or 6 ft 2 in), and some soldiers reportedly exceeded 2.1 m. The king drilled the regiment personally at Potsdam and kept it as a ceremonial showpiece; it saw little serious combat during his reign. His son, Frederick the Great, reduced its privileged status and used its battalions in the War of the Austrian Succession. The regiment was dissolved after Prussia's defeat by Napoleon in 1806.

Recruitment

Because few Prussians met the height requirement, the king's agents scoured Europe and beyond for tall recruits, purchasing or receiving them as diplomatic gifts from foreign rulers — including the Russian tsar, who sent annual drafts of tall men. Recruiters sometimes resorted to kidnapping, seizing tall peasants, monks, and even a tall Austrian diplomat's servant, which caused diplomatic incidents. The king also attempted a breeding program, marrying tall soldiers to tall women in hopes of producing tall offspring; some descendants settled in Potsdam and the town's population was noted for its height generations later.

Uniform

The Giants wore blue coats with red facings, white breeches, and tall red grenadier mitre caps fronted with brass plates, which made the men appear even taller. The king, who also painted portraits of his favorite giants, spent enormous sums on the regiment — one reason his frugal court reputation contrasts oddly with this single lavish passion.

Legacy

The regiment remains a famous symbol of Prussian militarism and royal eccentricity. The phrase Lange Kerls ("long/tall lads") survives in German, and a modern association in Potsdam commemorates the unit with historical re-enactment.
