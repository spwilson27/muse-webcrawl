Popular Science

Popular Science (also known as PopSci) is an American digital magazine carrying popular science content, which refers to articles for the general reader on science and technology subjects. Popular Science has won over 58 awards, including the American Society of Magazine Editors awards for its journalistic excellence.

History

The magazine was founded in May 1872 by Edward L. Youmans as The Popular Science Monthly, to disseminate scientific knowledge to the educated layman. Early issues featured contributions from Charles Darwin, Thomas Henry Huxley, Louis Pasteur and John Tyndall. In 2018 the magazine switched from a monthly to a quarterly schedule, and in 2020 it transitioned to an all-digital format; in 2023 it ceased publishing new content and its archives were taken offline before being relaunched in altered form.

Content

The magazine is known for its annual "Best of What's New" awards, honouring innovations in science and technology.
