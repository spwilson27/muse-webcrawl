Grisons

The Grisons is Switzerland's largest and most sparsely populated canton.

The Grisons (German: Graubünden; Romansh: Grischun; Italian: Grigioni) is the largest and easternmost canton of Switzerland. It covers about 7,105 km² — a sixth of the country's area — but has only about 200,000 inhabitants, the lowest population density of any Swiss canton. Its capital is Chur, the oldest city in Switzerland.

Geography

The canton is almost entirely mountainous, a high Alpine region of some 150 valleys carved by the headwaters of the Rhine, the Inn, and rivers flowing south to the Po. Distances are large and communications historically difficult — the canton counts over 1,200 peaks and passes such as the Julier, Albula, Bernina, Splügen and San Bernardino. The Swiss National Park, founded in 1914, lies in the Lower Engadine.

Languages

Grisons is Switzerland's only trilingual canton. German is spoken by a majority, Italian in the southern valleys (Val Poschiavo, Val Bregaglia, Val Mesolcina and Val Calanca), and Romansh — a Rhaeto-Romance language recognised as a national language of Switzerland — in the Surselva, the Engadine and neighbouring valleys. The canton's very name derives from the Grey League (Grauer Bund), one of three leagues whose federation formed the historical Free State of the Three Leagues, an associate of the Swiss Confederacy from 1497; the canton joined Switzerland in 1803.

Economy and tourism

Tourism is the dominant industry: world-famous resorts include St. Moritz, Davos (host of the World Economic Forum's annual meeting), Arosa, Klosters, Scuol and Pontresina. The Rhaetian Railway's Albula and Bernina lines are UNESCO World Heritage sites, famous for spiralling viaducts such as the Landwasser Viaduct. Agriculture persists in the valleys, and hydropower is a significant export.
