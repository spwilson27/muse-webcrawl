Lyricist
Occupation: writer of song lyrics
Related roles: composer, songwriter, librettist

A lyricist is a writer who specialises in composing lyrics — the words of songs — as distinct from a composer, who writes the music. Lyricists work in popular music, musical theatre, opera (where they are usually called librettists) and advertising.

Role and collaboration

Lyrics may be written before the melody ("lyrics first"), fitted to an existing tune, or developed simultaneously with a composing partner. Famous partnerships include Gilbert and Sullivan, Rodgers and Hammerstein, and Lennon–McCartney, in which both partners shared both roles.

Income

Lyricists earn royalties from recordings, performances and licensing; in publishing practice the lyricist typically receives half of the writer's share of a song, the composer receiving the other half.

Lyricists in musical theatre

Noted theatre lyricists include Lorenz Hart, Oscar Hammerstein II, Stephen Sondheim and Tim Rice; in popular song, figures such as Ira Gershwin, Johnny Mercer and Bernie Taupin are widely cited.
