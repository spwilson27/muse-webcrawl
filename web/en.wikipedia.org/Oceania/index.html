Oceania

Oceania is a geographical region spanning the eastern and western hemispheres, consisting of Australia and the island groups of the Pacific Ocean, conventionally divided into Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia. It is the smallest continent by land area and the second-least populous after Antarctica.

Geography

The region extends from Southeast Asia to the Americas across the Pacific. Australia, the largest landmass, is sometimes treated as part of the subregion of Australasia together with New Zealand and New Guinea. The Pacific islands include high volcanic islands and low coral atolls, many of which are threatened by sea-level rise.

History

Oceania was settled in successive waves: Aboriginal Australians and Papuan peoples arrived tens of thousands of years ago, while Austronesian-speaking voyagers settled the remote Pacific islands within the last 3,500 years, reaching New Zealand by about the 13th century CE. European exploration began in the 16th century, and most of the region was colonised by European powers and the United States in the 19th century. Decolonisation proceeded through the later 20th century, although several territories remain associated with metropolitan states.

Politics and economy

The Pacific Islands Forum coordinates regional policy. Australia and New Zealand have the largest economies; smaller island states rely on tourism, fishing rights, remittances, and aid. Climate change, particularly sea-level rise affecting low-lying states such as Tuvalu and Kiribati, is the region's most pressing long-term concern.
