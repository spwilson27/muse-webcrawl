The Association for the Taxation of Financial Transactions and for Citizens' Action (ATTAC, from the French Association pour la taxation des transactions financières et pour l'action citoyenne) is an alternative globalization activist network founded in France in 1998.

Origins

ATTAC was proposed in a December 1997 editorial by Ignacio Ramonet in Le Monde diplomatique, which called for an association to promote the Tobin tax — a small levy on currency transactions proposed by economist James Tobin to curb speculation. The founding assembly took place in Paris on 3 June 1998; Bernard Cassen became a leading figure of the movement.

Activities and positions

National ATTAC groups spread to dozens of countries, notably France and Germany. The network campaigns for the regulation of financial markets, the closure of tax havens, a financial transaction tax (later embodied in EU proposals), debt cancellation for developing countries, and the defence of public services against privatization. ATTAC was a co-founder of the World Social Forum, first held in Porto Alegre in 2001.
