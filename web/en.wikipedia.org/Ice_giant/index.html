An ice giant is a giant planet composed mainly of elements heavier than hydrogen and helium, such as oxygen, carbon, nitrogen and sulfur, in the form of volatile compounds ("ices") including water, ammonia and methane. In the Solar System there are two ice giants, Uranus and Neptune, while the larger gas giants Jupiter and Saturn are composed mostly of hydrogen and helium. The term entered use in planetary science in the 1990s, replacing the older practice of classing all four outer planets as gas giants.

Characteristics

Ice giants contain only about 20% hydrogen and helium by mass, compared with more than 90% for Jupiter and Saturn. Their interiors are thought to consist of a small rocky core surrounded by a deep mantle of water, ammonia and methane under extreme pressure and temperature, where water is predicted to exist in exotic states such as ionic and superionic ice — a phase confirmed experimentally in 2019. The unusual magnetic fields of Uranus and Neptune, tilted and offset from their centres, are attributed to dynamos in these ionic ocean mantles rather than in metallic hydrogen.

The blue and cyan colours of Neptune and Uranus come from atmospheric methane, which absorbs red light. Neptune's atmosphere is more active, with the fastest winds in the Solar System, while Uranus — rotating on its side at an axial tilt of about 98° — appears comparatively featureless.

Formation

Ice giants are thought to have formed beyond the "ice line" of the early Solar System, where volatile compounds could condense as solids, but to have accreted more slowly than the gas giants and thus missed the window to capture massive hydrogen–helium envelopes before the protoplanetary disc dispersed. Uranus and Neptune may have formed closer to the Sun than their present orbits and migrated outward, as described by the Nice model.

Exoplanets

Planets of ice-giant mass and size (sometimes loosely called "mini-Neptunes" when smaller) are among the most common exoplanets detected by the Kepler mission, even though the class is represented by only two planets in the Solar System. Only one spacecraft, Voyager 2, has visited the ice giants — Uranus in 1986 and Neptune in 1989 — and dedicated orbiter missions remain a priority in planetary science planning.
