Bitcoin

Bitcoin (abbreviation: BTC; sign: ₿) is the first decentralized cryptocurrency. Based on a free-market ideology, bitcoin was invented in 2008 by an unknown entity under the name Satoshi Nakamoto. Use of bitcoin as money began in 2009, with the release of its open-source implementation. Transactions are recorded on a public distributed ledger called a blockchain, maintained by a peer-to-peer network of nodes, without any central bank or administrator.

Design

Bitcoin uses proof-of-work consensus: computers called miners compete to solve cryptographic puzzles, and the winner adds a new block of transactions to the blockchain and receives newly issued bitcoins plus transaction fees. The block reward halves roughly every four years, so the total supply asymptotically approaches 21 million coins, making bitcoin deliberately scarce. Ownership is proved with public-key cryptography; coins are held in digital wallets controlled by private keys.

History

Nakamoto's white paper, "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System", was published in 2008. The genesis block, mined in January 2009, contained a newspaper headline about bank bailouts. Early milestones included the first exchange rate, the 2010 purchase of two pizzas for 10,000 BTC, and the rise and collapse of the Mt. Gox exchange. Bitcoin's price has been extremely volatile, reaching successive peaks in 2013, 2017, and 2021, and in 2021 El Salvador became the first country to adopt it as legal tender.

Criticism and debate

Bitcoin has been criticized for the electricity consumption of mining, its price volatility, its use in illicit transactions, and its limited transaction throughput. Supporters view it as "digital gold", a censorship-resistant store of value and a hedge against monetary debasement.
