Lake Turkana, formerly known as Lake Rudolf, is a lake in the Kenyan Rift Valley in northern Kenya, with its far northern end crossing into Ethiopia. It is the world's largest permanent desert lake and the world's largest alkaline lake, famous for its striking jade-green colour caused by algae.

Geography

The lake is fed principally by the Omo River from Ethiopia, which supplies the great majority of its inflow, along with the Turkwel and Kerio rivers from Kenya. It has no outlet, and its level fluctuates with rainfall and inflow. Three volcanic islands — North Island, South Island, and El Molo — rise from its waters.

Ecology

Despite its salinity and alkalinity, the lake supports Nile perch, tilapia, and a notable population of Nile crocodiles, once estimated among the largest anywhere. The surrounding shores form Lake Turkana National Parks, a UNESCO World Heritage Site also celebrated for fossil discoveries at Koobi Fora, including early hominin remains.

People

The lake basin is home to peoples including the Turkana, El Molo, Rendille, and Gabbra. Fishing and livestock herding dominate local livelihoods, and the lake's level has been a subject of concern linked to upstream dam construction on the Omo River.
