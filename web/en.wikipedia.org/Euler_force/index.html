Euler force

In classical mechanics, the Euler force is the fictitious tangential force that appears when a non-uniformly rotating reference frame is used for analysis of motion and there is variation in the angular velocity of the reference frame's axis. The Euler acceleration (named for Leonhard Euler), also known as azimuthal acceleration or transverse acceleration, is the part of the absolute acceleration caused by that variation.

Intuitive example

The Euler force will be felt by a person riding a merry-go-round. As the ride starts, the Euler force will be the apparent force pushing the person to the back of the horse; as the ride comes to a stop, it will be the apparent force pushing the person towards the front. A person on a horse close to the perimeter will perceive a greater apparent force than a person on a horse close to the axis of rotation.

Mathematical description

The direction and magnitude of the Euler acceleration are given, in a rotating frame with angular velocity ω, by:

aEuler = − (dω/dt) × r

where r is the position vector of the object in the rotating frame. The Euler force on an object of mass m is then:

FEuler = −m (dω/dt) × r
