Kigali Genocide Memorial

The Kigali Genocide Memorial is a memorial and museum in Gisozi, Kigali, Rwanda, commemorating the victims of the 1994 Rwandan genocide. Opened in April 2004 on the tenth commemoration of the genocide, it is the burial site of more than 250,000 victims whose remains were gathered from mass graves around the capital.

Exhibitions

The memorial's permanent exhibitions trace the history of Rwanda before, during and after the genocide, in which an estimated 800,000 to 1,000,000 Tutsi and moderate Hutu were murdered over roughly 100 days. Displays include personal belongings, photographs and testimonies of survivors, a children's memorial honouring young victims, and an exhibition on other genocides of the twentieth century intended to place the Rwandan experience in a global context of remembrance and prevention.

Gardens and burial sites

Terraced gardens surround the mass graves, including gardens symbolising pre-colonial, colonial and post-genocide Rwanda, a Wall of Names honouring identified victims, and an amphitheatre used for the annual Kwibuka commemoration ceremonies each April.

Management

The memorial was built in partnership between Kigali City Council and the Aegis Trust, a British genocide-prevention organisation, and is administered with the National Commission for the Fight against Genocide (CNLG). It receives hundreds of thousands of visitors, including survivors, students and foreign dignitaries.
