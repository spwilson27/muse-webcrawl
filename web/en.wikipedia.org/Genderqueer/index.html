Genderqueer

Genderqueer is an umbrella term for gender identities that are not solely male or female — identities which are outside the gender binary. Genderqueer people may express a combination of masculinity and femininity, or neither, in their gender expression. The term is closely related to non-binary, and the two are often used interchangeably, though "genderqueer" arose earlier, in queer activist circles of the 1990s, and retains for some users a deliberately transgressive, political connotation.

History of the term

The word appeared in print in the mid-1990s, notably in the newsletter In Your Face (1995) and in the work of writer and activist Riki Wilchins, who used it for people whose gender expression or identity defied categorisation. It gained wider circulation in the 2000s through zines, early social media and anthologies such as GenderQueer: Voices from Beyond the Sexual Binary (2002). Activist Marilyn Roxie created the three-stripe genderqueer pride flag (lavender, white, green) in 2010–2011.

Identities and expression

Umbrella identities include agender, bigender, genderfluid, demigender and pangender. Genderqueer people may use they/them or other pronouns, choose gender-neutral names or titles (such as Mx), and some pursue social or medical transition in whole or in part. Surveys of transgender and non-binary populations in the US and Europe since the 2010s have found a substantial and growing proportion identifying outside the male/female binary, particularly among younger respondents.

Recognition

Legal recognition of non-binary genders — such as an "X" marker on passports and identity documents — has been adopted in countries including Australia, Canada, Germany, Argentina and the Netherlands, and in a number of US states, though it remains unavailable or contested in many jurisdictions.
