Chimborazo

Chimborazo is an inactive stratovolcano in the Cordillera Occidental range of the Andes of central Ecuador. Its summit, at 6,263.47 m (20,549 ft) above sea level, is the highest point in Ecuador. Because the Earth bulges at the equator, Chimborazo's peak is also the point on Earth's surface farthest from the planet's center — farther than the summit of Mount Everest, at about 6,384 km from the center.

Geography

The mountain lies in Chimborazo Province near the city of Riobamba. Its upper slopes are covered by glaciers that supply water to surrounding communities; the ice has retreated notably in recent decades. Chimborazo last erupted around the early first millennium AD.

Exploration and climbing

In 1802 the naturalist Alexander von Humboldt and his party climbed to about 5,875 m, setting an altitude record for Europeans and inspiring Humboldt's pioneering work in biogeography; he incorrectly believed Chimborazo to be the world's highest mountain. The first successful ascent of the summit was made by Edward Whymper with the Carrel brothers in 1880.

Fauna

The surrounding Chimborazo Faunistic Reserve protects populations of vicuña, reintroduced to Ecuador, along with Andean condors and hummingbirds.
