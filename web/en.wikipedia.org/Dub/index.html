Dub

Stylistic originsReggae, ska, rocksteady
Cultural originsLate 1960s, Jamaica
Key figuresKing Tubby, Lee "Scratch" Perry, Scientist

Dub is a musical style that developed from reggae in the late 1960s and early 1970s, and is considered to be one of the earliest forms of electronic dance music. Dub productions are typically instrumental remixes of existing recordings, manipulated through the studio mixing board: vocals and other elements are dropped in and out, and heavy use is made of echo, reverb and other effects, with prominent bass and drum parts.

History

The style emerged in Jamaica through recording engineers such as Osbourne "King Tubby" Ruddock, Lee "Scratch" Perry, Errol Thompson and Scientist, who treated the mixing console as an instrument. Originally released as B-sides to vocal singles, dub versions became a central feature of Jamaican sound system culture.

Characteristics

Dub emphasises the rhythm section, strips arrangements down to drums and bass, and uses studio effects — delay, reverb, phasing — as compositional elements. The remix-driven approach pioneered techniques later adopted across electronic music, hip hop and pop production.

Influence

Dub profoundly influenced later genres including dubstep, jungle, trip hop, ambient and techno, and its sound-system culture spread internationally, notably to the United Kingdom, where acts such as Mad Professor and Adrian Sherwood developed the tradition.
