Mary Mitchell Slessor (2 December 1848 – 13 January 1915) was a Scottish Presbyterian missionary to Nigeria. Once famous for her work among the Efik and Ibibio peoples of the Calabar region, she worked to spread Christianity, promote education, and campaign against practices she considered harmful — most famously the killing of twin infants, who were traditionally believed to be evil.

Early life

Slessor was born in Aberdeen to a poor working-class family and from the age of eleven worked in the jute mills of Dundee. Inspired by the example of David Livingstone and the memory of the murdered missionary wife Mary Morris's predecessors in Calabar — notably Mary Slessor's hero Mary (wife of Hope Waddell) — she volunteered for the United Presbyterian Church's Calabar Mission and sailed for West Africa in 1876.

Work in Calabar

Learning Efik, Slessor moved progressively inland from the coastal mission stations, living among the Okoyong and Efik peoples at Ekenge and later at Use Ikot Oku. She rescued twins condemned to death, adopted several children, treated the sick, and acted as a respected intermediary — even serving as a British consular agent and magistrate in the Native Court — all while resisting pressure to conform to conventional missionary life. She traveled by bicycle and lived in simple local housing, earning the Efik name Ekaette ("mother of all the peoples").

Death and legacy

Slessor died of fever in 1915 and was buried in Calabar. She is commemorated by statues in Dundee, a memorial in Aberdeen, and her image appeared on the Scottish Clydesdale Bank £10 note from 1997. Hospitals, schools, and the main road in Calabar bear her name.
