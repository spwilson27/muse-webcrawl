Norwegian Nobel Committee

The Norwegian Nobel Committee (Norwegian: Den norske Nobelkomité) is the five-member committee responsible for selecting the recipient of the Nobel Peace Prize each year, in accordance with the will of Alfred Nobel. Its members are appointed by the Storting, the parliament of Norway, and the prize is presented annually on 10 December in Oslo City Hall.

Background

Alfred Nobel's will of 1895 stipulated that the Peace Prize be awarded by "a committee of five persons to be elected by the Norwegian Storting", probably reflecting Norway's subordinate position in the union with Sweden and the Storting's interest in international peace work. Unlike the other Nobel Prizes, which are awarded in Stockholm, the Peace Prize is announced at the Norwegian Nobel Institute in Oslo and presented at Oslo City Hall.

Nominations and selection

Nominations close on 31 January each year and may be submitted by, among others, members of national governments and parliaments, members of the International Court of Justice, university professors and past laureates. The committee, assisted by the director of the Nobel Institute and specialist advisers, draws up a shortlist and decides by majority vote, usually announcing the laureate in October. The committee's deliberations are kept secret for 50 years.

Composition and controversy

Members are appointed for six-year terms and have traditionally reflected the party composition of the Storting; sitting politicians have served, leading to debate about the committee's independence. Individual decisions — such as the absence of an award to Mahatma Gandhi, and prizes to Henry Kissinger (1973) and Barack Obama (2009) — have been controversial and have occasionally prompted public disagreement and resignations by members.
