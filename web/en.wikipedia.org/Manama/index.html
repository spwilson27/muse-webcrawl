Manama (Arabic: المنامة, al-Manāma) is the capital and largest city of the Kingdom of Bahrain, located on the northeastern coast of Bahrain Island in the Persian Gulf. It is the country's political, economic, and cultural centre and one of the principal financial hubs of the Gulf region.

History

Manama's site has been inhabited for centuries and was mentioned in Islamic chronicles from at least the fourteenth century. The Portuguese occupied Bahrain from 1521 until 1602, after which Persian Safavid forces took control. In 1783 the Al Khalifa family, of the Bani Utbah tribe, captured Bahrain from the Persians and established the rule that continues today; Manama grew as a free port and trading town. In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries its economy was based on pearling, fishing, boatbuilding, and trade with India and the Arabian mainland; the Manama souq was famous throughout the Gulf.

Bahrain became a British protectorate in the nineteenth century, and oil was discovered in 1932 — the first on the Arab side of the Gulf — transforming Manama's economy. Bahrain declared independence in 1971, with Manama as its capital. When the traditional pearling economy collapsed, Manama reinvented itself as a regional banking and services centre, helped by offshore banking units licensed from 1975 onward and, later, by its role as a centre for Islamic finance.

Geography and climate

Manama occupies a low-lying stretch of Bahrain Island's northern coast. Much of the modern business district — including the Bahrain Bay development and the area around the Bahrain Financial Harbour and the Bahrain World Trade Center, whose twin sail-shaped towers incorporate wind turbines — stands on reclaimed land. The climate is arid: summers are extremely hot and humid, with temperatures frequently above 40 °C, while winters are mild.

Economy

Finance is the backbone of Manama's economy; the Central Bank of Bahrain and hundreds of banks and financial institutions are headquartered or represented there, and the city is regarded as a pioneer of Islamic banking. Petroleum processing, aluminium smelting (at Alba, one of the world's largest smelters, south of the city), shipping, and tourism also contribute. The King Fahd Causeway, opened in 1986, links Bahrain to Saudi Arabia and brings large numbers of weekend visitors to Manama's malls, hotels, and restaurants.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Al-Fateh Grand Mosque, one of the largest mosques in the world; the Bahrain National Museum, which surveys the island's Dilmun-era archaeology; the traditional Manama Souq behind the Bab Al Bahrain gateway; and the Bahrain National Theatre. The nearby Bahrain Fort (Qal'at al-Bahrain), a UNESCO World Heritage Site, marks the ancient harbour of the Dilmun civilisation. Manama was designated the Arab Capital of Culture in 2012.
