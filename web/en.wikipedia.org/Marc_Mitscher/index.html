Marc Mitscher

Born: 26 January 1887, Hillsboro, Wisconsin
Died: 3 February 1947, Norfolk, Virginia
Service: United States Navy
Rank: Admiral
Commands: USS Hornet; Fast Carrier Task Force (TF 58/38)

Marc Andrew Mitscher (1887–1947) was a pioneering naval aviator and admiral in the United States Navy, celebrated as the commander of the Fast Carrier Task Force in the Pacific during the Second World War.

Early career

Mitscher graduated from the U.S. Naval Academy in 1910 and became one of the Navy's earliest pilots (Naval Aviator No. 33) in 1916. He took part in the pioneering NC flying-boat transatlantic attempt of 1919 and spent the interwar years developing carrier aviation, in staff posts and commands connected with the early carriers.

Second World War

As captain of the carrier USS Hornet, Mitscher launched the Doolittle Raid on Tokyo in April 1942 and fought at the Battle of Midway that June. Promoted to flag rank, he commanded air forces in the Solomons before taking over the Fast Carrier Task Force in 1944 — designated Task Force 58 under the Fifth Fleet and Task Force 38 under the Third Fleet. Under his leadership the carriers won the Battle of the Philippine Sea (the "Great Marianas Turkey Shoot"), struck Formosa and Japan, and supported the Iwo Jima and Okinawa campaigns; at the Philippine Sea he famously ordered the fleet to "turn on the lights" to guide returning pilots home at night.

Later life

After the war Mitscher served as Deputy Chief of Naval Operations for Air and then as Commander-in-Chief of the Atlantic Fleet, dying in office in 1947. The destroyer USS Mitscher and a later guided-missile destroyer bear his name.
