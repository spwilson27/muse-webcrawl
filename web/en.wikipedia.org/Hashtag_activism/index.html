Hashtag activism

Activism organised around hashtags on social media
PlatformsTwitter/X, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok
Notable examples#BlackLivesMatter, #MeToo, #BringBackOurGirls, #IceBucketChallenge, #FridaysForFuture
Related termsSlacktivism, clicktivism, digital activism

Hashtag activism refers to the use of hashtags on social media platforms as a form of online activism and political expression. By labelling posts with a shared tag, users can aggregate messages, draw attention to an issue, coordinate supporters and attempt to influence public debate, journalism and policy.

Notable campaigns

The hashtag #BlackLivesMatter, created in 2013 by Alicia Garza, Patrisse Cullors and Opal Tometi after the acquittal of George Zimmerman, grew into an international movement against police violence. #MeToo, founded by Tarana Burke in 2006 and viral from 2017 amid sexual-abuse allegations against Harvey Weinstein, prompted global discussion of sexual harassment and numerous legal and institutional reforms. Other prominent cases include #BringBackOurGirls (2014) after the Chibok schoolgirls kidnapping in Nigeria, the viral charity-driven #IceBucketChallenge (2014) for ALS research, and climate campaigns such as #FridaysForFuture.

Debate

Supporters argue that hashtags lower barriers to participation, allow marginalised voices to set agendas, and can translate into donations, protests and policy change. Critics use terms such as "slacktivism" and "clicktivism" for activity they see as performative, short-lived or substituting online gestures for sustained organisation, and note vulnerabilities to manipulation, bot amplification and state censorship.
