Contemporary R&B
Stylistic originsRhythm and blues, soul, funk, pop, hip hop, electronic
Cultural originsLate 1970s–1980s, United States
Derivative formsNew jack swing, hip hop soul, neo soul, alternative R&B, trap soul
Key artistsMichael Jackson, Whitney Houston, Mariah Carey, Beyoncé, Usher, Alicia Keys

Contemporary R&B (often abbreviated R&B) is a popular music genre that combines elements of rhythm and blues with pop, soul, funk, hip hop and electronic music. It is distinguished by polished studio production, melismatic singing, lush vocal harmonies and, from the 1990s onward, hip hop-influenced beats.

Development

After the disco era, artists such as Michael Jackson and Luther Vandross moved R&B toward a smoother, pop-oriented sound built on synthesisers and drum machines. Producers like Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis and the group of songwriters around Babyface and L.A. Reid defined the late-1980s sound, while Teddy Riley's new jack swing fused R&B singing with hip hop rhythms.

1990s and 2000s

The 1990s saw the genre dominate pop charts through acts such as Boyz II Men, Mariah Carey, TLC and Toni Braxton, alongside the grittier "hip hop soul" of Mary J. Blige and the later "neo soul" of D'Angelo and Erykah Badu. In the 2000s producers including Timbaland and The Neptunes pushed the sound further toward electronic experimentation, and stars such as Beyoncé, Usher, Rihanna and Chris Brown kept R&B at the centre of pop.

Alternative R&B

From around 2010, a moodier, atmospheric offshoot — variously called alternative R&B, PBR&B or indie R&B — emerged with artists such as Frank Ocean, The Weeknd, SZA, Miguel and FKA twigs, incorporating ambient textures, downtempo beats and confessional songwriting.
