Combat knife

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A combat knife is a fighting knife designed solely for military use and primarily intended for hand-to-hand or close combat. Since the end of trench warfare, most military combat knives have been secondarily designed for utility use (clearing foliage, chopping branches, opening ammunition crates), and such dual-purpose designs are sometimes designated "fighting-utility" knives.

History

Daggers and short blades accompanied soldiers from antiquity, but the modern combat knife emerged in the First World War, when trench raiding demanded compact edged weapons such as the French Couteau Poignard Mle 1916 ("Vengeur") and American M1917/M1918 trench knives, some fitted with knuckle-duster guards. In the Second World War notable designs included the British Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife, a slim stiletto made famous by the Commandos, and the American Ka-Bar, adopted by the Marine Corps as a combined fighting and utility knife.

Design

Combat knives are typically fixed-blade, with clip-point, spear-point, or tanto profiles around 15–20 cm long, a sharpened edge (sometimes partially serrated), and a guard separating blade from handle. Construction favours robustness over fine slicing: relatively thick stock, full tangs, and corrosion-resistant finishes. Modern utility-oriented descendants include pilot survival knives and bayonets designed to double as knives.

Notable examples

  • Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife — British commando dagger of the Second World War.
  • Ka-Bar USMC — American fighting-utility knife in service from 1942.
  • Glock field knife — Austrian polymer-handled military knife.
  • KM2000 — standard-issue Bundeswehr combat knife.
  • Ontario M9 and other bayonet-knives combining wire-cutting and utility features.
