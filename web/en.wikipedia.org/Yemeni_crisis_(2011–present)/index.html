The Yemeni crisis is the ongoing period of political upheaval, civil war and humanitarian emergency in Yemen that began with the 2011 revolution against President Ali Abdullah Saleh, part of the wider Arab Spring. It developed into a multi-sided civil war after the Houthi movement seized the capital, Sanaa, in 2014, and drew in a Saudi-led military intervention from March 2015. The United Nations has repeatedly described Yemen's humanitarian situation as among the world's worst.

Revolution and transition (2011–2014)

Mass protests in 2011, met with violent crackdowns, forced Saleh to sign a Gulf Cooperation Council-brokered deal transferring power to his deputy, Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi, in early 2012. A National Dialogue Conference attempted to draft a new constitution, but disputes over federalism, the grievances of the northern Houthi movement and the southern separatist movement Hirak, and economic collapse undermined the transition.

Civil war (2014– )

In September 2014 Houthi forces, allied with Saleh loyalists, took Sanaa; Hadi fled to Aden and then Riyadh. A Saudi-led coalition intervened in March 2015 to restore him, imposing an air and sea campaign and blockade. Saleh broke with the Houthis and was killed by them in December 2017. Fighting also consumed the south, where the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council seized Aden from the government in 2019 before joining a unity arrangement and, in 2022, the Presidential Leadership Council that replaced Hadi.

Truce and stalemate

A UN-brokered truce from April to October 2022 sharply reduced fighting, and its main terms largely held even after it formally lapsed. From late 2023 the Houthis attacked shipping in the Red Sea in response to the war in Gaza, prompting US- and UK-led strikes and a major disruption of global trade routes, before a ceasefire arrangement in 2025 reduced those attacks.

Humanitarian situation

Years of war, blockade, economic collapse and the destruction of infrastructure produced mass displacement, cholera outbreaks and widespread food insecurity, with aid agencies estimating that well over half the population has needed humanitarian assistance at the conflict's height and hundreds of thousands of direct and indirect deaths.
