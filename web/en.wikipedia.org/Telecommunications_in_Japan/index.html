Telecommunications in Japan
Regulator: Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications
Major carriers: NTT, KDDI, SoftBank, Rakuten Mobile
Notable services: i-mode (1999), extensive fibre broadband

Telecommunications in Japan are among the most advanced in the world, characterised by near-universal mobile coverage, very high broadband speeds and early adoption of new network technologies.

History

The telegraph was introduced in 1869, and telephony was dominated for much of the 20th century by the state monopoly Nippon Telegraph and Telephone (NTT), privatised in 1985. NTT DoCoMo launched i-mode in 1999, the world's first widely successful mobile internet service, and Japan pioneered 3G services in 2001.

Market

The mobile market is led by NTT Docomo, KDDI (au), SoftBank and, since 2020, Rakuten Mobile. Fibre-to-the-home subscriptions are widespread, and 5G rollout began in 2020. Japan also has one of the world's highest densities of internet hosts and a major role in submarine cable networks across the Pacific.
