Global catastrophic risk

A global catastrophic risk is a hypothetical future event that could damage human civilisation on a worldwide scale — for example by causing the deaths of a large fraction of humanity or destroying its long-term potential. The term is closely related to existential risk, which refers to threats that could cause human extinction or permanently curtail humanity's future development.

Categories

Proposed risks include natural events such as asteroid or comet impacts, supervolcanic eruptions and nearby supernovae or gamma-ray bursts; and anthropogenic risks such as nuclear war, severe climate change, engineered pandemics and biotechnology accidents, and hazards from advanced artificial intelligence. Scholars including Nick Bostrom and Toby Ord have argued that anthropogenic risks now dominate and that even small probabilities warrant serious mitigation effort, a subject studied at institutes such as the Future of Humanity Institute and the Global Challenges Foundation.

Assessment and response

Because such events are unprecedented, probabilities are estimated from models, expert elicitation and historical analogues, and are subject to deep uncertainty. Proposed responses include asteroid detection and deflection programmes, biosecurity measures, arms control, climate mitigation, and governance of emerging technologies. Critics question some risk estimates and warn against diverting resources from present-day harms.
