Agile software development

Agile software development is an umbrella term for a set of frameworks and practices based on the values and principles expressed in the Manifesto for Agile Software Development (2001) and the twelve principles behind it. Agile approaches deliver software incrementally in short iterations, emphasise close collaboration between self-organising cross-functional teams and customers, and welcome changing requirements even late in development.

The Agile Manifesto

In February 2001, seventeen software practitioners met at Snowbird, Utah, and produced the Manifesto, valuing: individuals and interactions over processes and tools; working software over comprehensive documentation; customer collaboration over contract negotiation; and responding to change over following a plan. The twelve principles include early and continuous delivery of valuable software, daily cooperation between business people and developers, sustainable development pace, and regular reflection on how to become more effective.

Methods and frameworks

Widely used agile frameworks include Scrum, with its fixed-length sprints, product backlog, and defined roles; Extreme programming (XP), emphasising technical practices such as pair programming and test-driven development; Kanban, which visualises workflow and limits work in progress; and later scaling frameworks such as SAFe and LeSS. Lean software development, adapted from lean manufacturing, is a close relative.

Practices

Common practices include short daily stand-up meetings, iterative planning, continuous integration, automated testing, refactoring, user stories as units of requirements, retrospectives, and frequent releases of working increments to users for feedback.

Criticism

Critics argue that agile methods can scale poorly, undervalue design and documentation, depend heavily on customer availability, and are sometimes adopted in name only ("agile theatre"). Empirical studies of effectiveness show mixed results, and outcomes depend strongly on context and team skill.
