Long (finance)

In finance, a long position in a financial instrument is when an investor owns the asset or holds a contract that profits when its price rises. Going long is the conventional form of investing: buy an asset in the expectation of selling it later at a higher price.

Securities

A long position in a stock means owning shares, profiting from price appreciation and dividends, with losses limited to the amount invested. In options, a long call profits when the underlying rises above the strike price, while a long put is a bearish position despite being “long” the option contract.

Long versus short

The opposite of long is a short position, where an investor sells borrowed assets hoping to repurchase them more cheaply. Portfolios combining both are called long/short strategies; the net exposure describes whether a fund is net long or net short the market.
