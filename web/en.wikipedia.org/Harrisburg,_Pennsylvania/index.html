Harrisburg is the capital city of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, United States, and the county seat of Dauphin County. It lies on the east bank of the Susquehanna River, about 105 miles (169 km) west of Philadelphia. With a population of roughly 50,000, it is the smaller core city of a metropolitan area of more than half a million people.

History

The site was a Native American crossroads for thousands of years; John Harris Sr. established a trading post and ferry here around 1719, and his son laid out the town in 1785. Harrisburg became the state capital in 1812, in part because of its central location and river access. It grew as a canal, rail and steel centre, and during the Civil War it was a major Union training camp (Camp Curtin); Confederate forces reached its outskirts during the Gettysburg campaign in 1863.

Modern city

The city's economy is anchored by state and federal government, health care and logistics. Its beaux-arts State Capitol, completed in 1906 and topped by a dome modelled on St. Peter's Basilica, anchors the skyline. Harrisburg endured a severe fiscal crisis over a failed incinerator retrofit; it entered state receivership under Act 47 in 2011 and exited receivership in 2014. The 1979 Three Mile Island accident occurred at a nuclear plant about ten miles downstream near Middletown.
