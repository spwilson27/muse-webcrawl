The Coppa Italia Serie C is an Italian football cup competition for clubs in Serie C, the third tier of the Italian football league system. It was formerly known as the Coppa Italia Lega Pro.

History

The competition was established in 1972 as the Coppa Italia Semiprofessionisti, reserved for clubs in Serie C. After the split of Serie C into two divisions in 1978, it was renamed Coppa Italia Serie C and opened to clubs in both C1 and C2. From 2008 to 2017, during the Lega Pro era, it was called the Coppa Italia Lega Pro, before reverting to its Serie C name when the third tier was reunified.

Format

All sixty Serie C clubs participate. The tournament begins with a group stage for a portion of the teams, followed by knockout rounds played over single legs or two legs depending on the round, concluding with a two-legged final (in most editions). The winner earns a berth in the following season's Coppa Italia and a favourable position in the promotion play-offs.

Winners

Monza is among the most successful clubs in the competition's history. Other multiple winners include Spezia, Lucchese, and Foggia. Notable recent winners include Viterbese (2019), Juventus Next Gen (2020), Fiorenzuola (2022), Vicenza (2023), Catania (2024) and Rimini (2025).
