Ipswich

Ipswich is a town in Suffolk, England, of which it is the county town. Located on the estuary of the River Orwell, it is one of England's oldest towns, with origins as the Anglo-Saxon settlement of Gippeswic, an important trading port from the 7th century.

History

Ipswich was a major port for trade with the continent through the medieval period and received its first royal charter from King John in 1200. Cardinal Thomas Wolsey, founder of a college in the town, was born there in 1473. The town grew with the wool trade, malting, and later engineering and agricultural machinery industries.

Landmarks and culture

Notable landmarks include Christchurch Mansion, the Ancient House with its pargeting, the Willis Building designed by Norman Foster, and the waterfront regeneration area around the marina. Ipswich Town F.C., nicknamed the Tractor Boys, play at Portman Road and won the UEFA Cup in 1981 under Bobby Robson.
