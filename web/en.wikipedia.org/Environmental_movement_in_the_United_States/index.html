Environmental movement in the United States

The environmental movement in the United States encompasses conservation, preservation and modern environmentalism, from 19th-century nature protection to contemporary climate activism.

Early history

Figures such as John Muir, founder of the Sierra Club (1892), and Theodore Roosevelt, who greatly expanded national parks and forests, shaped early conservation. Aldo Leopold's land ethic influenced later thought.

Modern era

Rachel Carson's Silent Spring (1962) galvanised public concern over pesticides. The first Earth Day in 1970 mobilised millions, followed by landmark laws including the Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act and the creation of the Environmental Protection Agency. Later movements added environmental justice and climate change as central concerns.
