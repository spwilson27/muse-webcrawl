Borland

Borland Software Corporation was an American software company founded in 1983 by Niels Jensen, Ole Henriksen, Mogens Glad, and Philippe Kahn. Headquartered for most of its life in Scotts Valley and later Cupertino, California, Borland became famous in the 1980s and 1990s for popular, inexpensive programming tools such as Turbo Pascal, Sidekick, Reflex, and Quattro Pro, and later for the Delphi and C++Builder development environments.

History

Borland Ltd. was originally established in Ireland in 1981 to market software using the Epson HX-20 portable computer. Philippe Kahn, who had emigrated from France to Silicon Valley, joined the company and drove its American launch. Its first major product, Turbo Pascal, released in November 1983, sold for $49.95 at a time when competing compilers cost hundreds of dollars, and it included an integrated development environment that was revolutionary for its speed.

During the mid-1980s Borland acquired Analytica and its Reflex database product, released the Sidekick personal information manager, and went public in 1986. In 1987 it acquired Wizard Systems and launched Turbo C. The acquisition of Ashton-Tate in 1991 brought the dBASE and InterBase database lines but also large debts and integration problems. Borland's spreadsheet Quattro Pro and the Paradox database competed with Lotus 1-2-3 and Microsoft products; a copyright lawsuit brought by Lotus against Borland over Quattro Pro's menu compatibility reached the U.S. Supreme Court, which deadlocked 4-4 in 1996, leaving Borland's victory in the lower court standing.

Inprise and CodeGear

In 1998 the company renamed itself Inprise Corporation to emphasize enterprise software such as the VisiBroker CORBA product, but in 2001 it reverted to the Borland name. In 2006 Borland announced it would spin off its developer tools group, which became CodeGear in 2006; Embarcadero Technologies acquired CodeGear in 2008. Borland itself was acquired by Micro Focus in 2009 for about $75 million.

Products

Notable products included Turbo Pascal, Turbo C and Turbo C++, Delphi, C++Builder, JBuilder, Kylix, Sidekick, Reflex, Sprint, Quattro Pro, Paradox, dBASE, and the InterBase relational database.
