Child Jesus

The Child Jesus, also known as the Divine Infant, Baby Jesus, Infant Jesus, the Divine Child, Bambino Gesù, Santo Niño, and the Christ Child, refers to Jesus Christ during his childhood. In Christian tradition, devotions to the Christ Child flourished especially from the Middle Ages onward, and images of the infant or young Jesus became a major subject of European art.

In the Gospels

The canonical Gospels describe Jesus's birth at Bethlehem, the visit of the Magi, the flight into Egypt, and his childhood at Nazareth, culminating in the finding of the boy Jesus in the Temple at age twelve. Apocryphal infancy gospels, such as the Infancy Gospel of Thomas, added many legendary episodes, though these were not accepted as scripture.

Devotions and famous images

Devotion to the Child Jesus spread through the influence of saints such as Anthony of Padua, Teresa of Ávila, and Thérèse of Lisieux ("of the Child Jesus"). The Infant Jesus of Prague, a 16th-century wax statue in the Church of Our Lady of Victories, is one of the most widely replicated devotional images. In the Philippines, the Santo Niño — especially the Santo Niño de Cebú, believed to be a gift of Ferdinand Magellan's expedition in 1521 — is the focus of the Sinulog festival. In Latin America, the Niño Dios and Colombia's Divino Niño are beloved devotions, and the Christ Child appears as the gift-bringer (Christkind or Niño Jesús) in the Christmas customs of many countries.
