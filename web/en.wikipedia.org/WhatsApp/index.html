WhatsApp

WhatsApp Messenger
Developer: Meta Platforms (WhatsApp LLC)
Founded: 2009, by Brian Acton and Jan Koum
Users: over 2.7 billion
Platforms: Android, iOS, web, desktop

WhatsApp (officially WhatsApp Messenger) is an American freeware, cross-platform, centralised instant messaging and voice-over-IP service owned by Meta Platforms. It allows users to send text and voice messages, make voice and video calls, and share images, documents, locations and other content, using only a mobile phone number for registration. It is the most popular messaging application in much of the world outside China, with more than two billion users.

History

WhatsApp was founded in 2009 by Brian Acton and Jan Koum, both former employees of Yahoo!. After early success as a status app it pivoted to messaging, grew rapidly by offering a cheap alternative to SMS, and reached 450 million monthly users by early 2014. In February 2014 Facebook (now Meta) acquired WhatsApp for approximately US$19.3 billion, the largest acquisition by Facebook at the time. Koum and Acton later left the company amid disagreements over advertising and user privacy.

Features and encryption

In 2016 WhatsApp completed the rollout of end-to-end encryption based on the Signal Protocol, developed with Open Whisper Systems, so that only the communicating users can read message content. Features include group chats and group calls, Communities, Channels (one-to-many broadcasts, launched 2023), disappearing messages, Status updates modelled on Stories, and WhatsApp Business tools used by millions of small businesses, especially in India, Brazil and Latin America. WhatsApp Pay provides payments in a few markets.

Criticism

The service has faced criticism over the spread of misinformation and incitement in group chats, prompting limits on message forwarding, and over its 2021 privacy policy update, which drove some users to rivals such as Signal and Telegram. In India, its largest market, WhatsApp messages have been linked to rumours that triggered mob violence.
