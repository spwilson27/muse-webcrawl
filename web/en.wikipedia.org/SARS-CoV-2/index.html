SARS-CoV-2

SARS-CoV-2 virions, showing the characteristic spike proteins

Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) is the coronavirus that causes COVID-19, the respiratory illness responsible for the COVID-19 pandemic. The virus was first identified in Wuhan, China, in December 2019, and its genome was sequenced and published in January 2020. It belongs to the subgenus Sarbecovirus of the genus Betacoronavirus and is closely related to bat coronaviruses and to SARS-CoV, the virus that caused the 2002–2004 SARS outbreak.

Structure and genome

SARS-CoV-2 is an enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus with one of the largest RNA genomes known, about 29.9 kilobases. Its roughly spherical virions, 60–140 nanometres in diameter, carry club-shaped spike (S) glycoproteins that give coronaviruses their crown-like appearance and bind the human ACE2 receptor to enter cells. The genome encodes structural proteins—the spike, envelope (E), membrane (M), and nucleocapsid (N) proteins—as well as nonstructural and accessory proteins.

Transmission and disease

The virus spreads mainly through respiratory droplets and aerosols released when an infected person breathes, speaks, coughs, or sneezes. Infection can be asymptomatic or cause COVID-19, ranging from mild respiratory symptoms to pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome, multi-organ failure, and death, with risk rising steeply with age and comorbidities. Some patients develop prolonged symptoms known as long COVID.

Variants and countermeasures

Continual mutation has produced variants of concern—Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, and Omicron and its sublineages—with differing transmissibility and immune evasion. Vaccines, developed at unprecedented speed beginning in late 2020 (notably mRNA vaccines from Pfizer–BioNTech and Moderna), together with antiviral drugs such as nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid), ventilation, and other public-health measures, greatly reduced mortality. The World Health Organization declared the end of COVID-19 as a public health emergency of international concern in May 2023, by which time the pandemic had caused at least seven million reported deaths and far more excess deaths worldwide.
