Beryl

FormulaBe3Al2(SiO3)6
Crystal systemHexagonal
Hardness7.5–8 (Mohs)
VarietiesEmerald, aquamarine, morganite, heliodor, goshenite, red beryl

Beryl is a mineral composed of beryllium aluminium silicate with the chemical formula Be3Al2(SiO3)6. Well-known varieties of beryl include emerald and aquamarine.

Varieties

Pure beryl is colourless, but trace impurities produce a range of gemstones: chromium and vanadium give green emerald; iron gives blue aquamarine and yellow heliodor; manganese gives pink morganite and the rare red beryl. Colourless goshenite and greenish-yellow varieties also occur.

Occurrence

Beryl occurs most commonly in granitic pegmatites and is also found in mica schists and limestone. Significant deposits are mined in Colombia, Brazil, Madagascar, Russia and the United States. Beryl is an important ore of beryllium.

History

Beryl has been valued since antiquity; the name derives from the Greek beryllos, a term for precious blue-green stones, and the word "brilliant" is thought to derive from it.
