United States Lines

United States Lines was an American shipping company that operated cargo services and transatlantic passenger liners from 1921 until its bankruptcy in 1986. It is best remembered as the operator of SS United States, the fastest ocean liner ever built.

History

The company was founded in 1921 to operate former German liners seized during the First World War, and it succeeded the United States Mail Steamship Company on North Atlantic routes. Its flagships included SS Leviathan in the 1920s, and later SS Manhattan, SS Washington, and SS America. In 1952 the superliner SS United States entered service and captured the Blue Riband on her maiden voyage, averaging over 35 knots — a record that still stands for a conventional liner.

Decline

Passenger services ended in 1969 as air travel displaced the liners. Under Malcom McLean's ownership from 1978 the company became a container shipping line and ordered a large class of "Econship" container vessels, but the ambitious expansion led to bankruptcy in 1986.
