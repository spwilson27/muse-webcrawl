Greenwashing

Greenwashing is the practice of making misleading or unsubstantiated claims about the environmental benefits of a product, service, technology or company practice, in order to present an environmentally responsible public image. The term, a blend of "green" and "whitewashing", was coined by environmentalist Jay Westerveld in a 1986 essay criticising hotels that urged guests to reuse towels chiefly to cut laundry costs while doing little else for the environment.

Green imagery in marketing can suggest environmental benefits that do not exist

Forms and regulation

Common greenwashing techniques include vague labels such as "all natural" or "eco-friendly" without certification, highlighting a single green attribute while ignoring larger impacts, using suggestive green imagery, and publicising environmental initiatives that are trivial relative to a company's core polluting activities. Regulators including the US Federal Trade Commission (through its Green Guides) and the European Union have moved to require evidence for environmental claims and to prohibit generic claims that cannot be substantiated. Related terms include "greenhushing", where firms under-report genuine efforts to avoid scrutiny, and "sportswashing" and "pinkwashing" for analogous image strategies.
