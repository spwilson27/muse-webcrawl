In Memoriam A.H.H.

In Memoriam A.H.H. (1850) by Alfred, Lord Tennyson, is an elegy written to his best friend Arthur Henry Hallam (1811–1833), who died suddenly of a cerebral haemorrhage while on holiday in Vienna in 1833. The poem was written over a period of 17 years and is a requiem for the cherished friendship, in which Tennyson weighs grief against faith in its 133 cantos.

Structure and themes

The poem consists of a prologue, 131 cantos, and an epilogue, mostly in four-line stanzas of iambic tetrameter rhyming ABBA — a stanza form now known as the In Memoriam stanza. Its central struggle is between religious faith and scientific doubt in an age when geology and, soon, evolutionary theory were unsettling traditional beliefs; the phrase "Nature, red in tooth and claw" (canto 56) became famous.

It contains many famous lines, including "'Tis better to have loved and lost / Than never to have loved at all" (canto 27) and "Ring out the old, ring in the new" (canto 106).

Reception

The poem was published anonymously in 1850 to great acclaim; Queen Victoria treasured it, saying that after the Bible, In Memoriam was her greatest consolation after the death of Prince Albert. It cemented Tennyson's reputation and he succeeded William Wordsworth as Poet Laureate the year of its publication.
