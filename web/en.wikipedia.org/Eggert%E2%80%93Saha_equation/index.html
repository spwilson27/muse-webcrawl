Saha ionization equation

The Saha ionization equation — in some literature credited jointly as the Eggert–Saha equation, after John Eggert's earlier 1919 formulation and Meghnad Saha's 1920 development — expresses the thermal ionization state of a gas in thermal equilibrium as a function of temperature and pressure.

Formulation

The equation relates the densities of atoms in successive ionization states: the ratio of the density of ions in state i+1 to that in state i is proportional to T^(3/2)/n_e times an exponential factor exp(−ε/kT), where ε is the ionization energy. It thus predicts that ionization increases steeply with temperature and decreases with density (electron pressure).

Applications

Saha applied the equation to stellar atmospheres, explaining the sequence of stellar spectral types as primarily a temperature sequence governing which absorption lines appear — work that transformed astrophysics and led Cecilia Payne to demonstrate the hydrogen abundance of stars. The equation is fundamental to plasma physics and to the theory of the early universe recombination epoch, though it breaks down for dense, non-ideal or non-equilibrium plasmas.
