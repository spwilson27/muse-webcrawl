NuSTAR (the Nuclear Spectroscopic Telescope Array) is a NASA space-based X-ray telescope launched on 13 June 2012. It was the first telescope capable of focusing high-energy (hard) X-rays, giving images far sharper than previous instruments in the 3–79 keV band.

Design

NuSTAR carries two co-aligned grazing-incidence telescopes feeding cadmium-zinc-telluride detectors. To achieve its 10-metre focal length in a compact launch vehicle, the optics and detectors are separated by a deployable mast that extended after launch from the Pegasus XL rocket, which air-launched the observatory near Kwajalein Atoll.

Science

The mission surveys black holes — including a census of obscured supermassive black holes — maps radioactive titanium-44 in supernova remnants such as Cassiopeia A, studies neutron stars and magnetars, and observes the Sun's faintest X-ray flares. It frequently works jointly with XMM-Newton, Chandra and Swift.
