Oglala

The Oglala (Lakota: Oglála, "they scatter their own") are one of the seven subtribes of the Lakota people, who, along with the Dakota, make up the Oceti Sakowin (Seven Council Fires) of the Great Sioux Nation. Today they are federally recognised as the Oglala Sioux Tribe (officially the Oglala Lakota Nation), headquartered on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation in southwestern South Dakota.

Pine Ridge is the second-largest reservation in the United States.

History

The Oglala rose to prominence on the northern Plains in the 19th century as horse-mounted buffalo hunters. Under leaders such as Red Cloud they fought Red Cloud's War (1866–1868), forcing the United States to abandon the Bozeman Trail forts in the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868). Crazy Horse's Oglala band joined in the Great Sioux War of 1876, including the victory at the Little Bighorn, before surrendering in 1877.

Modern era

Following allotment and the Wounded Knee Massacre of 1890 (which befell the Miniconjou and Hunkpapa bands sheltered at Pine Ridge), the Oglala organised under the Indian Reorganization Act of 1934. In 1973 the reservation was the site of the Wounded Knee Occupation by the American Indian Movement. The tribe today governs a population of roughly 40,000 enrolled members and faces persistent challenges of poverty, alongside language and cultural revitalisation efforts, including the tribal college Oglala Lakota College.
