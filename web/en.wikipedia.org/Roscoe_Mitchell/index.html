Roscoe Mitchell

Roscoe Mitchell (born August 3, 1940) is an American composer, jazz instrumentalist, and educator who plays saxophone and flute, among many other instruments. A central figure in avant-garde jazz, he is a founding member of the Art Ensemble of Chicago and of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM).

Career

Born in Chicago, Mitchell began playing saxophone and clarinet as a teenager and played in an army band while serving in Germany. Returning to Chicago, he joined Muhal Richard Abrams's Experimental Band and became a charter member of the AACM in 1965. His 1966 album Sound was the first recording released by an AACM member and is considered a landmark of free jazz.

In 1969 Mitchell co-founded the Art Ensemble of Chicago, which blended jazz improvisation with theatrical performance, African instrumentation, and the motto "Great Black Music, Ancient to the Future." He has recorded extensively as a leader and collaborator and has composed works for ensembles ranging from small groups to orchestras, exploring the boundary between improvisation and composition.

Teaching

Mitchell held the Darius Milhaud Chair of Composition at Mills College in Oakland, California, where he taught for many years, mentoring generations of experimental musicians.
