Greisen–Zatsepin–Kuzmin limit

The Greisen–Zatsepin–Kuzmin limit (GZK limit or GZK cutoff) is a theoretical upper limit on the energy of cosmic ray protons travelling from distant sources through the universe, at approximately 5×1019 eV. The limit was predicted independently in 1966 by Kenneth Greisen and by Georgiy Zatsepin and Vadim Kuzmin, and arises from interactions between cosmic ray protons and photons of the cosmic microwave background (CMB).

Mechanism

A proton with energy above the threshold of roughly 5×1019 eV can interact with a CMB photon to produce a pion via the Δ (Delta) resonance: p + γCMB → Δ+ → p + π0 or n + π+. Each such interaction costs the proton a substantial fraction of its energy. As a result, protons above the threshold lose energy rapidly over cosmological distances; the effective horizon for the highest-energy protons is only about 50–100 megaparsecs. Any proton observed above the limit must therefore originate comparatively nearby. Heavy nuclei are similarly limited by photodisintegration on background radiation fields.

Observations

For decades the status of the cutoff was controversial. The Japanese AGASA experiment reported events apparently beyond the limit, fuelling speculation about exotic explanations, while the HiRes experiment in Utah reported a spectrum consistent with the suppression. The Pierre Auger Observatory in Argentina and the Telescope Array Project in Utah have since observed a clear suppression of the cosmic-ray flux at the expected energies, consistent with the GZK prediction, although distinguishing the GZK effect from a maximum energy at the sources remains an active research problem.
