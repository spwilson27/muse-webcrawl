Ertuğrul (Ottoman Turkish: ارطغرل; often given the title Gazi) was, according to Ottoman tradition, the father of Osman I, founder of the Ottoman Empire, and the leader of the Kayı tribe of Oghuz Turks in thirteenth-century Anatolia. He is said to have led his followers into the service of the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum and to have been granted the frontier town of Söğüt, from which the Ottoman beylik later grew.

Almost everything known about Ertuğrul comes from Ottoman chronicles written more than a century after his death, and historians treat many details of his life as legendary. Nevertheless, he occupies a foundational place in Turkish national memory, and his tomb at Söğüt remains a site of annual commemoration.

Life according to tradition

Ottoman tradition holds that Ertuğrul's father, Süleyman Şah, led the Kayı westward from Central Asia to escape the Mongol invasions, and drowned crossing the Euphrates near the castle of Qal'at Ja'bar. Ertuğrul then led part of the tribe back toward Anatolia. On one famous occasion, the story goes, he came upon a battle between Seljuk and Mongol forces and intervened on the Seljuk side, turning the tide; in gratitude, Sultan Alaaddin Kayqubad I granted him lands at Karacadağ near Ankara.

Later, Ertuğrul was granted the town of Söğüt on the frontier with the Byzantine Empire, together with the village of Thebasion. From this border principality he and his successors waged ghaza warfare against Byzantine neighbours, gradually expanding the territory his son Osman would inherit around 1281.

Historicity

No contemporary source names Ertuğrul. A coin attributed to Osman I names his father as Ertuğrul, which is the strongest near-contemporary evidence for his existence. The narratives of his deeds appear first in fifteenth-century Ottoman chronicles such as those of Aşıkpaşazade, and the details — including his father's identity and his wife Halime Hatun — vary between accounts and are widely regarded as later embellishment.

Legacy

The Ottoman frigate Ertuğrul, lost in a storm off Japan in 1890, was named after him, as is the Ertuğrul Gazi Mosque in Ashgabat, Turkmenistan. The Turkish television series Diriliş: Ertuğrul (2014–2019) dramatised his life and achieved enormous popularity across Turkey and the Muslim world, making him one of the most widely recognised figures of early Ottoman tradition.
