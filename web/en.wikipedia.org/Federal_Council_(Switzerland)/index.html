Federal Council (Switzerland)

The Federal Council (German: Bundesrat; French: Conseil fédéral; Italian: Consiglio federale) is the federal cabinet of the Swiss Confederation. Its seven members constitute the collective head of state and government of Switzerland, each heading one of the seven federal departments.

Formed1848

Composition and election

Federal Councillors are elected by the United Federal Assembly for four-year terms after each general election, and are traditionally re-elected until retirement. The presidency of the council rotates annually among the members in order of seniority; the President of the Swiss Confederation is primus inter pares and has no powers over the other councillors.

Working

Decisions are taken collectively and defended publicly by all members, under the collegiality principle. For decades an informal "magic formula" distributed seats among the four main parties (two each to the three largest, one to the fourth), reflecting Switzerland's consensus-oriented concordance system.
