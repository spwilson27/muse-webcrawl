Apraxia

Apraxia
SpecialtyNeurology, neuropsychology
SymptomsInability to perform learned skilled movements despite intact strength and comprehension
CausesStroke, brain injury, neurodegenerative disease

Apraxia is a motor disorder caused by damage to the brain, in which a person has difficulty performing learned, purposeful movements despite having the physical ability and desire to carry them out. It is not explained by weakness, sensory loss, or failure to comprehend the task. Apraxia commonly follows stroke or other injury affecting the parietal and frontal lobes, especially in the dominant hemisphere.

Types

Ideomotor apraxia involves errors in carrying out gestures or using tools on command. Ideational apraxia is a failure to conceive the sequence of actions needed for a complex task, such as preparing a letter for mailing. Limb-kinetic, buccofacial, and constructional forms affect particular domains. Apraxia of speech is a related motor speech disorder affecting the planning of speech movements.

Diagnosis and treatment

Assessment involves asking the patient to perform gestures, imitate movements, and use real objects. Treatment is provided through occupational and speech therapy, focusing on retraining functional tasks and compensatory strategies; recovery depends on the extent and location of the brain injury.
