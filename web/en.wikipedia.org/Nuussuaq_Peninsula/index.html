Nuussuaq Peninsula (Greenlandic: Nuussuaq, "large cape") is a large peninsula in western Greenland, projecting westward from the mainland between Disko Bay to the south and the Uummannaq Fjord system to the north. The Vaigat Strait separates it from Disko Island. The peninsula is administered as part of the Avannaata and Qeqertalik municipalities and contains settlements including Qaarsut and Niaqornat on its northern coast.

Geology

Nuussuaq is famous in geology for its thick sequences of Palaeogene flood basalts, part of the North Atlantic Igneous Province erupted as the Atlantic Ocean opened about 60 million years ago. Uniquely, the basalts contain occurrences of native (metallic) iron — most famously at Disko Island and on the peninsula itself — formed where hot lava assimilated carbon-rich sediments and reduced iron oxides to metal; the great iron masses found at Uivfaq on Disko were long mistaken for meteorites. The peninsula's Cretaceous–Palaeogene sedimentary rocks also preserve important fossil floras.

History and archaeology

The peninsula bears evidence of Saqqaq and Dorset Palaeo-Inuit cultures and of later Thule settlement; the celebrated Qilakitsoq mummies were found in 1972 in a rock tomb on the northern coast of the peninsula, in the Uummannaq district. Norse hunters also visited the region for walrus ivory and narwhal tusks.
