Top Gun is a 1986 American action drama film directed by Tony Scott and produced by Don Simpson and Jerry Bruckheimer, with distribution by Paramount Pictures. The screenplay was written by Jim Cash and Jack Epps Jr., and was inspired by an article titled "Top Guns", written by Ehud Yonay and published in California magazine three years earlier. The film stars Tom Cruise, Kelly McGillis, Val Kilmer, Anthony Edwards and Tom Skerritt.

Plot

Lieutenant Pete "Maverick" Mitchell, a US Navy aviator aboard the carrier USS Enterprise, is selected with his radar intercept officer Nick "Goose" Bradshaw to attend the Navy's Fighter Weapons School — "Top Gun" — at Naval Air Station Miramar. There Maverick competes with the cool, methodical Tom "Iceman" Kazansky, pursues a romance with civilian instructor Charlotte "Charlie" Blackwood, and struggles with the legacy of his father, a pilot killed in Vietnam. After Goose dies in a training accident, Maverick must overcome his guilt to graduate and, in a climactic real-world engagement, defend a rescue mission over the Indian Ocean.

Production and reception

The film was made with extensive US Navy cooperation, with F-14 Tomcat fighters and carriers made available to the production; Paramount paid the Navy for flight hours and ship changes. Its soundtrack, featuring Kenny Loggins's "Danger Zone" and Berlin's "Take My Breath Away" — which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song — was a major commercial success. Top Gun was the highest-grossing film of 1986, earning $357 million worldwide against a $15 million budget, and is credited with boosting Navy recruitment. A sequel, Top Gun: Maverick, was released in 2022 and surpassed the original's commercial success.
