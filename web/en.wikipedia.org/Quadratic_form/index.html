Quadratic form

In mathematics, a quadratic form is a polynomial with terms all of degree two. In several variables it is a homogeneous polynomial of degree 2, such as x² + y². Quadratic forms occupy a central place in many areas of mathematics, including number theory, linear algebra, group theory, differential geometry, and Lie theory, and they also appear in physics, for example in the kinetic energy of a system.

Quadratic form
Field: Linear algebra, number theory
Definition: Homogeneous polynomial of degree 2
Matrix form: xTAx (A symmetric)
Examples: x² + y², the discriminant
Related: Bilinear form

Definition and matrix representation

Over a field or ring in which 2 is invertible, a quadratic form in n variables can be written as the product xTAx where A is a symmetric n×n matrix, and it corresponds bijectively to a symmetric bilinear form via the polarisation identity. In two variables, the general form is ax² + bxy + cy², whose discriminant b² − 4ac governs much of its behaviour.

Classification

Over the real numbers, Sylvester's law of inertia classifies quadratic forms by their signature: the numbers of positive, negative, and zero eigenvalues. A form is positive definite if it takes only positive values for non-zero vectors, as with the squared length of Euclidean geometry. Over the rationals, classification is subtler and is governed by the Hasse–Minkowski theorem; in number theory, the study of which integers a form represents — begun with Fermat's theorem on sums of two squares and Lagrange's four-square theorem — remains a rich field.
