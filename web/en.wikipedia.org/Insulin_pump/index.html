Insulin pump

An insulin pump is a medical device used for the administration of insulin in the treatment of diabetes mellitus. The device includes the pump itself (with controls, a processing module, and batteries), a disposable reservoir for insulin, and a disposable infusion set that delivers insulin through a thin cannula inserted under the skin.

How it works

The pump delivers rapid-acting insulin continuously at a low "basal" rate to meet background needs, and the user programs larger "bolus" doses at mealtimes to cover carbohydrates. This continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) can mimic the function of a healthy pancreas more closely than multiple daily injections, and studies associate pump therapy with improved glycemic control and reduced hypoglycemia for many users.

Modern systems

Many pumps now integrate with continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) to form hybrid closed-loop systems — sometimes called an "artificial pancreas" — which automatically adjust basal insulin delivery based on sensor readings. Tubeless "patch pumps" worn directly on the skin are also available.
