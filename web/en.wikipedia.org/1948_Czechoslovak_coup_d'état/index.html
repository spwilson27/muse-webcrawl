1948 Czechoslovak coup d'état

Date21–25 February 1948
LocationPrague, Czechoslovakia
Also known asVictorious February (Czech: Vítězný únor)
ResultCommunist Party takes full control; Third Republic ends

The 1948 Czechoslovak coup d'état — known in communist propaganda as "Victorious February" — was the event in late February 1948 in which the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia (KSČ), backed by the Soviet Union, seized complete control of the government of Czechoslovakia, marking the onset of four decades of communist rule.

Background

Since 1945 Czechoslovakia had been governed by a National Front coalition under President Edvard Beneš, with the communist Klement Gottwald as prime minister. The communists were the largest single party after the 1946 elections but were losing popularity, and feared defeat in the elections scheduled for May 1948.

The crisis

On 20 February 1948, twelve non-communist ministers resigned in protest at the packing of the police with communist appointees by Interior Minister Václav Nosek, expecting Beneš to refuse the resignations and call early elections. Instead, mass demonstrations, general-strike threats, and armed People's Militias in the streets raised pressure on the ailing president. On 25 February, Beneš capitulated and accepted a new, communist-dominated government proposed by Gottwald.

Aftermath

Foreign Minister Jan Masaryk died in a fall from a window on 10 March, officially ruled a suicide. In May a new constitution institutionalized the "people's democracy"; Beneš resigned in June and died that September. The coup shocked the West, hastened the passage of the Marshall Plan, and became an emblematic episode of the early Cold War.
