Kalsubai

Kalsubai
Elevation: 1,646 m (5,400 ft)
Range: Western Ghats (Sahyadri)
Location: Maharashtra, India

Kalsubai (कळसूबाई) is a mountain in the Western Ghats of Maharashtra, India, and at 1,646 metres (5,400 feet) above sea level it is the highest point in the state. It is often called the "Everest of Maharashtra" and lies within the Kalsubai Harishchandragad Wildlife Sanctuary, on the border of Ahmednagar and Nashik districts.

The peak and temple

A small temple dedicated to the goddess Kalsubai crowns the summit. According to local legend, Kalsubai was a young woman who worked in a village household and fled to the hilltop after being mistreated; she is venerated as a goddess, and a fair is held in her honour. The summit offers panoramic views of surrounding forts and peaks, including Alang, Madan, Kulang, Ratangad, and Harishchandragad, and of the Bhandardara lake country below.

Trekking

The most popular ascent begins at the base village of Bari. The route is fitted with iron ladders and chains at steep rock sections and is busy during and after the monsoon, when the slopes are green and the region's seasonal waterfalls flow. The trek is a popular weekend excursion from Mumbai and Pune.
