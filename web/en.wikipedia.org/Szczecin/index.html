Szczecin

Szczecin (German: Stettin) is the capital and largest city of the West Pomeranian Voivodeship in north-western Poland. Located on the Oder River, about 65 km south of the Baltic Sea and close to the German border, it is Poland's principal Baltic seaport together with ÓwinoujÓcie, and has a population of roughly 400,000.

History

A Slavic Pomeranian stronghold stood on the site by the 8th century; the town received Lübeck law in 1243 and became the residence of the dukes of Pomerania of the Griffin dynasty, whose Renaissance castle still dominates the old town. Szczecin passed through Danish, Swedish, and Prussian hands, joined the Hanseatic League, and grew into the main port for Berlin in the German Empire, known for shipbuilding (the Stettiner Vulcan yard launched some of Germany's largest liners). Badly damaged in the Second World War, the city was transferred to Poland in 1945 under the Potsdam settlement; its German population was expelled and replaced largely by Poles displaced from the eastern territories annexed by the USSR. The shipyards were a cradle of resistance: strikes in December 1970 were bloodily suppressed, and in August 1980 the Szczecin Agreement was one of the founding acts of Solidarity.

The city today

Landmarks include the Pomeranian Dukes' Castle, the grand boulevards of the Wały Chrobrego terrace above the Oder, the new Philharmonic hall (winner of the 2015 EU Mies Award), and the vast Central Cemetery, among Europe's largest. The port handles bulk and general cargo, and the city is a hub for maritime industries, IT, and cross-border cooperation with Germany.
