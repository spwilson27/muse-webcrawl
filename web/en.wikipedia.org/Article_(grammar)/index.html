Article (grammar)

An article is a word that is used with a noun to specify grammatical definiteness of the noun, and in some languages extending to volume or numerical scope. Articles are usually treated as a distinct part of speech, a subclass of determiners.

Types

A definite article (in English, the) indicates that its noun is a particular one identifiable to the listener. An indefinite article (in English, a or an) indicates that its noun is not a particular one identifiable to the listener. Some languages also have partitive articles (as in French du), and many languages, including Russian, Chinese, and Japanese, have no articles at all, expressing definiteness by word order, context, or other means.

Distribution

Articles develop historically from demonstratives or numerals — the English indefinite article derives from the Old English word for "one". In some languages the article is a suffix, as in Romanian and Bulgarian, or in the Scandinavian languages, where the definite article attaches to the end of the noun.
