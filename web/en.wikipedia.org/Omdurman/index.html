Omdurman

Omdurman (أم درمان)
Country: Sudan
State: Khartoum State
Location: West bank of the Nile, opposite Khartoum
Population: Over 2 million (metropolitan estimates vary)

Omdurman (Arabic: أم درمان, Umm Durmān) is a major city in Sudan, situated on the western bank of the Nile at its confluence with the White Nile, opposite the capital Khartoum. With Khartoum and Khartoum North (Bahri) it forms a tri-city metropolitan area. Omdurman is regarded as Sudan's national and cultural capital.

History

The city rose to prominence in 1885 when the Mahdi Muhammad Ahmad made it his headquarters after capturing Khartoum. His successor, the Khalifa Abdallahi ibn Muhammad, built it into the capital of the Mahdist state. On 2 September 1898 the Battle of Omdurman was fought on the plains to the north, where an Anglo-Egyptian army under Herbert Kitchener, armed with Maxim guns and artillery, crushed the Mahdist forces at heavy cost in Sudanese lives — a battle in which the young Winston Churchill took part and which he described in The River War.

The Mahdi's tomb, destroyed by Kitchener and later rebuilt with its distinctive silver dome, remains a landmark. Omdurman became known for its vast market (suq), traditional crafts and as a centre of Sudanese music, poetry and the national broadcaster.

Recent events

The city suffered severe fighting and displacement during the Sudanese civil war that began in April 2023 between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces, which contested control of the capital region.
