Joan, Lady of Wales

Joan, Lady of Wales and Lady of Snowdon (Welsh: Siwan; c. 1191 – 2 February 1237) was an illegitimate daughter of King John of England and the wife of Llywelyn the Great, Prince of Gwynedd and de facto ruler of most of Wales.

Life

Joan's mother is not certainly identified; later tradition names her Clemence. Joan was married to Llywelyn ap Iorwerth (Llywelyn the Great) around 1205, as part of a peace settlement between John and Llywelyn. She acted as an intermediary between her husband and the English crown, negotiating with her father and, after 1215, with Henry III, and in 1228 she was formally declared legitimate by the Pope — though under English law she was still barred from the succession. In 1230 she was imprisoned for a year after being discovered in a compromising situation with the Marcher lord William de Braose, whom Llywelyn then hanged; despite the scandal, the marriage was restored.

Death and burial

Joan died in 1237 and was buried at Llanfaes on Anglesey, where Llywelyn founded a Franciscan friary in her memory. Her stone coffin was later moved and is now at St Mary's Church, Beaumaris.
