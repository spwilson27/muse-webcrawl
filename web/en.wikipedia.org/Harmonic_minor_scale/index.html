Harmonic minor scale

The harmonic minor scale (or Aeolian ♯7 scale) is a seven-note musical scale derived from the natural minor scale by raising the seventh degree by a semitone. Its pattern of tones and semitones is W–H–W–W–H–(augmented second)–H; in A minor, for example, the scale runs A–B–C–D–E–F–G♯–A.

Origin and purpose

The raised seventh — the leading note — strengthens the dominant harmony in minor keys, allowing a major dominant chord (V) and a diminished seventh chord (vii°7) that resolve convincingly to the tonic. Baroque and Classical composers used the raised seventh harmonically while often reverting to the natural minor melodically to avoid the awkward augmented second between the sixth and seventh degrees; the melodic minor scale arose partly to smooth that interval.

Sound and usage

The augmented second gives harmonic minor an exotic, Middle Eastern colour much exploited in classical music, flamenco-adjacent styles, film scores, and neoclassical heavy metal guitar playing, notably by Yngwie Malmsteen. Its fifth mode, the Phrygian dominant scale, is central to flamenco, klezmer (as freygish), and Middle Eastern music.
