Prandtl number

The Prandtl number (Pr), named after the German physicist Ludwig Prandtl, is a dimensionless number defined as the ratio of momentum diffusivity (kinematic viscosity) to thermal diffusivity. It characterises the relative thickness of the velocity and thermal boundary layers in heat transfer and fluid flow.

Definition

Pr = ν / α = μ cp / k

where ν is kinematic viscosity, α is thermal diffusivity, μ is dynamic viscosity, cp is specific heat at constant pressure, and k is thermal conductivity. The Prandtl number depends only on the fluid and its state (temperature and pressure), not on the flow geometry.

Values and significance

For air at room temperature Pr ≈ 0.71; for water, about 7 at 20 °C (varying strongly with temperature); engine oils have Pr in the hundreds or thousands; liquid metals such as mercury have very low values (Pr ≈ 0.025). When Pr is small, heat diffuses much faster than momentum and the thermal boundary layer is much thicker than the velocity boundary layer; when Pr is large, the reverse holds. The Prandtl number appears in correlations for convective heat transfer, such as the relation between the Nusselt, Reynolds, and Prandtl numbers.
