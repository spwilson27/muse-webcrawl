Stromboli (Italian: Stromboli; Sicilian: Struògnuli) is an island in the Tyrrhenian Sea, off the north coast of Sicily, containing Mount Stromboli, one of the four active volcanoes in Italy. It is one of the seven Aeolian Islands, a volcanic arc north of Sicily, and also the name of the volcano itself.

The volcano has erupted many times and is constantly active with minor eruptions, often visible from many points on the island and from the surrounding sea, giving rise to the island's nickname "Lighthouse of the Mediterranean". The last major eruption was on 3 July 2019, alongside a series of major explosions in 2024.

Volcano

Mount Stromboli rises 924 metres above sea level, but the bulk of the edifice extends more than 2,000 metres below the sea. Its characteristic mild explosive activity — rhythmic bursts of glowing lava fragments thrown tens to hundreds of metres into the air — is so typical that volcanologists named an entire eruptive style after it: the Strombolian eruption.

The Sciara del Fuoco ("Stream of Fire") is a large horseshoe-shaped depression on the northwestern flank, formed by a sector collapse, into which much of the eruptive material falls.

Population and culture

The island's population is around 500. The main villages are San Bartolo, San Vincenzo, Ficogrande, Piscità, and Ginostra. Stromboli features in Jules Verne's novel Journey to the Center of the Earth (1864) and in Roberto Rossellini's 1950 film Stromboli, starring Ingrid Bergman.
