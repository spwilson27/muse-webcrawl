DNA glycosylase

DNA glycosylases
Function: Base excision repair
Reaction: Cleavage of N-glycosidic bond

DNA glycosylases are a family of enzymes (EC 3.2.2) that catalyse the first step of base excision repair. They recognize and remove damaged or mismatched bases from DNA by cleaving the N-glycosidic bond, leaving an abasic (AP) site.

Types

Examples include uracil-DNA glycosylase (UNG), which removes uracil, 8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase (OGG1), which removes oxidized guanine, and MUTYH, associated with colorectal polyposis when mutated. Some glycosylases are bifunctional and also have AP lyase activity.

Mechanism

Many glycosylases flip the damaged base out of the double helix into an active-site pocket, a mechanism called base flipping, before excising it.

Clinical relevance

Defects in glycosylases contribute to mutagenesis, cancer predisposition, and neurodegeneration.
