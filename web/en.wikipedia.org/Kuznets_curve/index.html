Kuznets curve

The Kuznets curve is a hypothesised relationship between economic development and income inequality, proposed by economist Simon Kuznets in the 1950s. It predicts that as an economy develops, inequality first increases and then decreases, tracing an inverted U-shape when plotted against income per capita.

Rationale

Kuznets suggested that early industrialisation concentrates income among owners of capital and workers moving into higher-productivity urban sectors, widening inequality; later, education, welfare states and structural change reduce it. He himself cautioned that the hypothesis rested on limited data.

Criticism and legacy

Empirical support has been mixed, and rising inequality in many advanced economies since the late 20th century has challenged the idea of an automatic decline. The concept has nonetheless been influential, and was adapted in the environmental Kuznets curve, which hypothesises a similar inverted-U for environmental degradation.
