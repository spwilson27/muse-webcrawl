Earl of Desmond

The title of Earl of Desmond has been held historically by the FitzGerald family, a great Hiberno-Norman dynasty that ruled over the territory of Desmond (from the Irish Deas-Mhumha, "South Munster") in southwestern Ireland. The earldom was first created in 1329 for Maurice FitzThomas FitzGerald, 4th Baron Desmond, and the FitzGerald earls of Desmond became among the most powerful and most Gaelicised of the Anglo-Irish magnates — the "Old English".

History

The earls accumulated near-palatinate authority in Munster, often clashing with their rivals the Butlers, Earls of Ormond, and with the Dublin government. Gerald FitzGerald, 14th (or 15th) Earl of Desmond, led the Desmond Rebellions against Elizabeth I — the first in 1569–1573 and the second in 1579–1583. The second rebellion ended with the earl's death in 1583 and the devastation and confiscation of Munster, after which his lands were settled in the Plantation of Munster.

A later incarnation of the title was granted to the Feilding family, who hold the Earldom of Desmond (created 1622) as a subsidiary title of the Earl of Denbigh.
