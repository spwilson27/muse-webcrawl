Erlang (unit)

Erlang
Unit ofTelecommunications traffic intensity
SymbolE
Named afterAgner Krarup Erlang

The erlang (symbol E) is a dimensionless unit used in telephony and teletraffic engineering to measure the total traffic volume, or continuous use, of one or more communication channels over a period. One erlang is the continuous use of one voice path — for example, a single call lasting one hour, two calls of thirty minutes, or any combination totalling 3,600 call-seconds within the measurement period.

Definition and use

Offered traffic A in erlangs equals the call arrival rate multiplied by the average holding time (A = λh). Engineers use the Erlang B and Erlang C formulas, developed from the work of the Danish mathematician Agner Krarup Erlang (1878–1929), to size trunk groups and call-centre staffing: Erlang B gives the blocking probability for a given number of circuits, while Erlang C gives the probability that a caller must wait, assuming queued calls are not abandoned.

Related measures

Smaller units include the centum call second (CCS), used in North America, equal to 100 call-seconds per hour; 1 erlang equals 36 CCS. The erlang is accepted for use in international telecommunication practice, though it is not an SI unit.
