Coptic Museum

LocationOld Cairo, Egypt
Founded1908
FounderMarcus Simaika Pasha
CollectionCoptic art and artefacts

The Coptic Museum is a museum in Coptic Cairo, Egypt, holding the largest collection of Coptic Christian artefacts in the world. It was founded by Marcus Simaika in 1908 to house Coptic antiquities gathered from churches and monasteries across Egypt.

Collection

The museum's holdings number in the tens of thousands of objects, including stone carvings, woodwork, metalwork, textiles, frescoes and manuscripts. Notable items include early Christian stelae from the monasteries of the Fayum and Saqqara, woven tapestries, and a celebrated collection of Coptic manuscripts, among them psalters and a copy of the Nag Hammadi library's companion materials.

Setting

The museum stands within the grounds of the ancient Fortress of Babylon, beside the Hanging Church and the Ben Ezra Synagogue, in the historic religious quarter of Old Cairo. Its buildings incorporate traditional Coptic architectural elements, including carved wooden ceilings and mashrabiya screens.
