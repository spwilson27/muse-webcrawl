Optical amplifier

Optical amplifier
TypePhotonic device
FunctionAmplifies an optical signal directly, without conversion to electrical form
Main typesErbium-doped fibre amplifier (EDFA), Raman amplifier, semiconductor optical amplifier (SOA)
ApplicationsLong-haul fibre-optic communications, submarine cables

An optical amplifier is a device that amplifies an optical signal directly, without first converting it into an electrical signal. Optical amplifiers revolutionised telecommunications in the 1990s: together with wavelength-division multiplexing, they allowed a single optical fibre to carry many channels over thousands of kilometres, with amplifiers spaced roughly every 80–100 km replacing electronic regenerators that had to process each channel separately.

Erbium-doped fibre amplifier

The most important type is the erbium-doped fibre amplifier (EDFA), invented in 1987. A short length of fibre whose core is doped with erbium ions is pumped by a laser at 980 nm or 1480 nm, raising the ions to an excited state. When a signal photon in the 1550 nm band—the low-loss window of silica fibre—passes, it triggers stimulated emission, producing an additional photon in phase with the signal. EDFAs provide gains of 20–40 dB across the C-band (around 1530–1565 nm) and amplify all wavelength channels simultaneously.

Other types

Raman amplifiers exploit stimulated Raman scattering in ordinary transmission fibre, pumped at a wavelength slightly shorter than the signal; they can be distributed along the span, improving the signal-to-noise ratio. Semiconductor optical amplifiers (SOAs) work like laser diodes without mirrors, are compact and integrable, but suffer higher noise and nonlinearity. Amplifiers based on other rare-earth dopants—praseodymium, thulium, ytterbium—serve other wavelength bands and high-power and laser applications.

Noise

All optical amplifiers add noise through amplified spontaneous emission (ASE), which accumulates along a chain of amplifiers and ultimately limits system reach. Amplifier design balances gain, gain flatness across channels, output power, and noise figure.
