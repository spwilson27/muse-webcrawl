Farmers' market

TypeRetail market
SellersLocal farmers and producers
GoodsFresh produce, meats, cheeses, baked goods, flowers, crafts

A farmers' market (or farmers market) is a physical retail marketplace intended to sell foods directly from farmers to consumers. Farmers' markets may be indoors or outdoors and typically consist of booths, tables or stands where farmers sell their produce, live animals and plants, and sometimes prepared foods and beverages.

Characteristics

Farmers' markets emphasise locally grown food, often sold within a short distance of where it was produced, and seasonal availability. By selling directly, producers can retain a larger share of the price, and shoppers gain access to fresh, regionally distinctive foods and direct contact with growers. Many markets also serve as community gathering places and support local food systems.

Spread

Markets of this kind are ancient in origin, but the modern farmers' market movement expanded rapidly from the 1970s onward. In the United States, the number of farmers' markets listed in the USDA directory grew from under 2,000 in the mid-1990s to more than 8,000 by the 2010s. Similar markets are common throughout Europe, Asia, and Latin America.

Criticism

Some markets have been criticised for resellers passing off wholesale produce as locally grown, prompting rules requiring vendors to sell only what they produce. Prices may also be higher than at supermarkets, though supporters point to quality and local economic benefits.
