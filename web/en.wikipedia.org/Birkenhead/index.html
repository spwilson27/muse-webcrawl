Birkenhead

Birkenhead is a town in the Metropolitan Borough of Wirral, Merseyside, England, on the west bank of the River Mersey opposite Liverpool. Historically in Cheshire, the town developed rapidly in the 19th century around shipbuilding, docks, and the world's first publicly funded municipal park. Its population was about 88,000 at the 2021 census.

History

The name derives from the Old English for "headland overgrown with birch". A Benedictine priory was founded here around 1150, and monks operated a ferry across the Mersey. In the 19th century the town was transformed by William Laird's shipbuilding yard (later Cammell Laird), which built famous vessels including the Confederate raider CSS Alabama and many Royal Navy ships; by an extensive system of docks; and by the Mersey Railway tunnel (1886) linking it to Liverpool.

Birkenhead Park

Opened in 1847 and designed by Joseph Paxton, Birkenhead Park was the first publicly funded civic park in the world. Its naturalistic landscape directly inspired Frederick Law Olmsted's design of Central Park in New York, which Olmsted praised after visiting in the 1850s.

Transport and culture

The town is connected to Liverpool by the two Mersey road tunnels, the Merseyrail network, and the famous Mersey Ferries operating from Woodside. Football club Tranmere Rovers, founded in 1884, is based at Prenton Park. The Wirral Waters regeneration scheme is redeveloping the docklands around the Great Float.
