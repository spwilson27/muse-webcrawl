Horst and graben

Alternating horsts and grabens in an extending crust

In geology, horst and graben (from the German for "heap" and "ditch") refers to topography consisting of alternating raised and lowered crustal blocks bounded by normal faults. The raised blocks are horsts and the lowered blocks grabens. The structure develops where the crust is pulled apart by extensional (tensional) tectonic forces.

Formation

Extension fractures the upper crust along steeply dipping normal faults. Some blocks slide downward along the faults to form grabens — often occupied by valleys, lakes or sedimentary basins — while intervening blocks remain high or are elevated, forming horst ranges. Repeated faulting across a broad region produces the characteristic basin-and-range topography of tilted mountain ranges separated by flat sediment-filled valleys.

Examples

The Basin and Range Province of Nevada and Utah in the western United States is the type example, with Death Valley occupying a deep graben. In Europe, the Upper Rhine Graben lies between the horsts of the Vosges and the Black Forest. The East African Rift system displays large-scale horst and graben structures along its length.
