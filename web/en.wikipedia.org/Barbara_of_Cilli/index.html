Barbara of Cilli

Barbara of Cilli (c. 1392 – 11 July 1451) was the Holy Roman Empress and Queen of Hungary and Bohemia as the second wife of Emperor Sigismund. She served as regent of Hungary in her husband's absence and was one of the most powerful women of late medieval Europe. She founded the Order of the Dragon's female counterpart circle with her husband and is remembered in folklore, sometimes unflatteringly, as the "Messalina of Germany".

Life

Barbara was the daughter of Herman II, Count of Celje, a powerful magnate of Slovenian lands. She married Sigismund of Luxembourg in 1405, was crowned Queen of Hungary, and in 1437 was crowned Queen of Bohemia. She accumulated vast estates, conducted her own diplomacy, and took an interest in alchemy and astrology. After Sigismund's death she lost influence in conflicts with her son-in-law Albert II and spent her later years in Bohemia, where she died of plague.
