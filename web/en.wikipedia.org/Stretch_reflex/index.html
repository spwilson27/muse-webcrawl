Stretch reflex

Stretch reflex
Type: Monosynaptic reflex
Example: Knee-jerk (patellar) reflex
Receptor: Muscle spindle

The stretch reflex, or myotatic reflex, is a muscle contraction in response to stretching within the muscle. It is a monosynaptic reflex — the simplest reflex arc — that helps maintain posture and muscle tone and resists sudden lengthening.

Mechanism

Muscle spindles detect changes in length and rate of stretch. Ia afferent fibres carry signals to the spinal cord, where they synapse directly on alpha motor neurons that contract the same (homonymous) muscle, while interneurons inhibit antagonists — reciprocal inhibition.

Clinical significance

The patellar tendon tap elicits the familiar knee-jerk. Exaggerated reflexes (hyperreflexia) suggest upper motor neuron lesions, while diminished reflexes occur in peripheral neuropathy and lower motor neuron damage.

Function

Continuously modulated by descending pathways and gamma motor neurons, the stretch reflex stabilises joints during movement and load changes.
