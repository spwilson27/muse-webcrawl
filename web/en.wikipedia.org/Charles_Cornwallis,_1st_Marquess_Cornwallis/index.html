Charles Cornwallis, 1st Marquess Cornwallis

Charles Cornwallis, 1st Marquess Cornwallis (31 December 1738 – 5 October 1805) was a British Army officer, civil administrator and diplomat. He is remembered chiefly for surrendering his army at Yorktown in 1781, ending major land operations in the American War of Independence, and for his subsequent service as Governor-General of India and Lord Lieutenant of Ireland.

American war

Cornwallis served with distinction in America, winning the Battle of Camden in 1780 and the costly victory at Guilford Court House in 1781. Moving into Virginia, he fortified Yorktown, where a Franco-American army under George Washington and the Comte de Rochambeau, supported by the French fleet, forced his surrender on 19 October 1781. He was exchanged and continued to serve the Crown.

India

As Governor-General of Bengal (1786–1793), Cornwallis reformed the East India Company's administration, separated revenue collection from judicial functions, and introduced the Permanent Settlement of land revenues in Bengal in 1793. He commanded British forces in the Third Anglo-Mysore War, defeating Tipu Sultan and imposing the Treaty of Seringapatam in 1792.

Ireland and later life

As Lord Lieutenant of Ireland from 1798, Cornwallis suppressed the Irish Rebellion of 1798 and helped carry the Act of Union 1800. He negotiated the Treaty of Amiens with France in 1802. Reappointed Governor-General of India in 1805, he died at Ghazipur shortly after arriving.
