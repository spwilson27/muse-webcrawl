Reparations

The term reparations may refer to several related concepts:

War reparations

War reparations are compensation payments made after a war by one side to the other, intended to cover damage or injury inflicted during the conflict. Famous cases include the indemnity imposed on France after the Franco-Prussian War (1871), the reparations demanded from Germany under the Treaty of Versailles (1919) — a major grievance in interwar Germany — and the more limited reparations after the Second World War. Japan paid reparations to several Asian countries under the San Francisco Peace Treaty and bilateral agreements. In 1990–91 Iraq was required to compensate victims of its invasion of Kuwait through the UN Compensation Commission, which completed payments in 2022.

Reparations for slavery and historical injustice

Debates continue over reparations for slavery and colonialism. In the United States, calls date from the broken promise of "forty acres and a mule" to freedpeople in 1865; modern proposals were popularised by Randall Robinson's The Debt (2000) and Ta-Nehisi Coates's 2014 essay "The Case for Reparations". The U.S. Congress has repeatedly considered H.R. 40, a bill to study reparations. Caribbean states, through CARICOM, have demanded reparations from European former colonial powers.

Other historical cases

West Germany's Wiedergutmachung programme — including the 1952 Luxembourg Agreement with Israel and the Claims Conference — paid compensation to Holocaust survivors. The United States apologised and paid $20,000 each to surviving Japanese Americans interned during the Second World War under the Civil Liberties Act of 1988. New Zealand and Canada have made settlements with Indigenous peoples for breaches of treaty obligations.

In international law

The UN Basic Principles on the Right to a Remedy and Reparation (2005) recognise forms of reparation including restitution, compensation, rehabilitation, satisfaction and guarantees of non-repetition. Transitional justice mechanisms — truth commissions, tribunals and reparations programmes — operationalise these ideas after conflicts and authoritarian rule.
