Geography of Spain

Spain is a country in southwestern Europe occupying most of the Iberian Peninsula, which it shares with Portugal and Andorra. Its territory also includes the Balearic Islands in the Mediterranean, the Canary Islands off the Atlantic coast of Africa, and the autonomous cities of Ceuta and Melilla in North Africa. With an area of about 506,000 km², Spain is the largest country in Southern Europe and the second-largest in Western Europe.

Relief

Spain is one of the most mountainous countries in Europe, with an average elevation around 660 m. The interior is dominated by the Meseta Central, a high plateau divided by the Sistema Central ranges and ringed by the Cantabrian Mountains to the north, the Iberian System to the northeast and Sierra Morena to the south. The Pyrenees form the border with France; their highest Spanish peak is Aneto (3,404 m). The country's highest point is Mount Teide (3,715 m) on Tenerife in the Canary Islands, while on the mainland Mulhacén (3,479 m) in the Sierra Nevada is the highest summit.

Rivers and climate

The principal rivers are the Ebro (flowing to the Mediterranean), and the Duero, Tagus, Guadiana and Guadalquivir (flowing to the Atlantic). The Tagus, at about 1,007 km, is the longest river of the peninsula. Spain's climate is famously varied: a temperate oceanic climate in the green north, Mediterranean climates along the east and south coasts, a continental climate on the Meseta, and arid and semi-arid conditions in the southeast — the Tabernas Desert in Almería is among the driest places in Europe.
