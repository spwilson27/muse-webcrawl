Solitude

Solitude is a state of seclusion or isolation, i.e., lack of contact with other people. It can have either positive or negative effects depending on the circumstances and whether it is chosen. Short periods of solitude are often sought as time for reflection, creativity, prayer, or rest, whereas prolonged enforced isolation is associated with loneliness and adverse health effects.

Solitude and loneliness

Psychologists distinguish solitude from loneliness: loneliness is the distressing subjective experience of unwanted isolation, while solitude can be voluntary and restorative. Being alone does not necessarily cause loneliness, and a person can feel lonely in a crowd. Researchers such as Robert S. Weiss distinguished emotional isolation from social isolation, and later work has emphasized that the perceived quality of one's relationships matters more than their quantity.

Positive effects

Chosen solitude can foster freedom, creativity, intimacy with oneself, and spirituality. Philosophers and writers — including Henry David Thoreau, who wrote about his sojourn at Walden Pond — have praised solitude as a condition for self-examination and original thought. Studies suggest that moderate amounts of alone time can improve concentration, emotional regulation, and problem-solving, and that adolescents who balance solitude with social life show good adjustment.

Negative effects

Involuntary and prolonged isolation is linked to depression, anxiety, cognitive decline, and increased mortality risk; social isolation has been compared in impact to other major health risk factors. Solitary confinement in prisons is criticized for causing severe psychological harm. During the COVID-19 pandemic, widespread social distancing raised public attention to the costs of isolation.

Solitude in religion and philosophy

Many religious traditions value retreat and contemplative solitude: Christian hermits and monastic orders, the Buddha's retreat before enlightenment, and Islamic and Hindu practices of seclusion all treat solitude as a path to insight. Diogenes and other ancient philosophers practiced voluntary simplicity and withdrawal. Existentialist thought treats aloneness as a fundamental condition of human life.

Solitude in the modern world

Urbanization and digital connectivity have made true solitude rarer while making social isolation paradoxically more common. Writers on technology note that constant connectivity can erode the capacity to be alone, prompting movements for digital detoxes and silent retreats.
