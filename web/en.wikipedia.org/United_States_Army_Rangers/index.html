United States Army Rangers

The United States Army Rangers are elite light infantry and special operations soldiers serving in the 75th Ranger Regiment, the Army's premier large-scale special operations force. Ranger-qualified soldiers also serve throughout the Army. Rangers are trained for direct-action raids, airfield seizure, reconnaissance and personnel recovery, and the Regiment can deploy a battalion anywhere in the world within 18 hours. Their motto is Rangers lead the way!

History

The lineage traces to Rogers' Rangers of the French and Indian War and to the Ranger battalions of World War II, formed in 1942 under Major William Orlando Darby — the unit that inspired the motto at Omaha Beach on D-Day, where General Norman Cota told the 2nd Rangers "Rangers, lead the way!" Ranger companies fought in Korea, and long-range reconnaissance patrol (LRRP) and Ranger companies served in Vietnam. The modern 75th Ranger Regiment was stood up in 1974–1986. Rangers took part in the invasion of Grenada (1983), Panama (1989), Somalia (1993 — the Battle of Mogadishu, depicted in Black Hawk Down), and were continuously deployed after 2001, conducting thousands of raids in Afghanistan and Iraq as part of Joint Special Operations Command task forces.

Ranger School

Ranger School, run by the Army's Maneuver Center of Excellence, is one of the toughest leadership courses in the US military: a 62-day, three-phase course (Darby, Mountain, Swamp) with minimal sleep and food, culminating in the award of the black-and-gold Ranger tab. Graduates — "Ranger-qualified" — are distinguished from members of the 75th Ranger Regiment, who additionally complete the Regiment's own assessment and selection (RASP) and wear the tan beret. Since 2015 Ranger School has been open to women; the first two female graduates earned the tab that August.
