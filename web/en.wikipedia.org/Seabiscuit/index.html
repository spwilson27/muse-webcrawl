Seabiscuit

Seabiscuit (May 23, 1933 – May 17, 1947) was a champion Thoroughbred racehorse in the United States who became the top money-winning racehorse up to the 1940s. Small, knobby-kneed, and initially dismissed as lazy and unpromising, he rose to become a beloved underdog hero during the Great Depression, symbolising hope for millions of Americans.

Racing career

Seabiscuit began his career in claiming races and lost his first 17 starts. Purchased by automobile magnate Charles S. Howard in 1936, he was paired with trainer Tom Smith and jockey John "Red" Pollard. The team transformed him into a star on the West Coast, and in 1937 and 1938 he dominated handicap racing in the East as well.

The match race

On November 1, 1938, Seabiscuit defeated the Triple Crown winner War Admiral in a famous match race at Pimlico Race Course, winning by four lengths before a crowd of 40,000. The race was one of the most anticipated sporting events of the decade and cemented Seabiscuit's legend. He was voted American Horse of the Year for 1938.

Later life and legacy

After recovering from injury, Seabiscuit won the 1940 Santa Anita Handicap, the race that had narrowly eluded him twice. He retired to stud at Howard's Ridgewood Ranch in California and died of a heart attack in 1947. His story inspired a bestselling 1999 book by Laura Hillenbrand and a 2003 film.
