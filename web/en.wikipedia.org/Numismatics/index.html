Numismatics

Numismatics is the study or collection of currency, including coins, tokens, paper money, and related objects. While numismatists are often characterised as collectors of coins, the discipline also includes the broader study of money and other payment media used to resolve debts and exchange goods.

The term was first used in English in 1829, borrowed from the French numismatique, which derives from the Latin adjective numismatis, of numisma meaning "coin", itself from the Greek νόμισμα (nomisma), meaning "current coin, custom".

History

Coin collecting may have existed in ancient times. Caesar Augustus gave "coins of every device, including old pieces of the kings and foreign money" as Saturnalia gifts. Petrarch, who wrote in a letter that he was often approached by vinediggers bearing old coins asking him to buy or identify the ruler, is credited with being the first Renaissance collector. The first book on coins was De Asse et Partibus (1514) by Guillaume Budé.

During the 19th century, numismatics developed into an academic discipline, with learned societies founded across Europe and North America, such as the Royal Numismatic Society (1836) and the American Numismatic Society (1858).

Subfields

  • Exonumia — the study of coin-like objects such as tokens and medals.
  • Notaphily — the study of paper money or banknotes.
  • Scripophily — the study and collection of stocks and bonds.

Modern numismatics

Modern numismatics, the study of coins from the mid-17th century onward, focuses on mass-produced, machine-struck coinage. Collectors grade coins by condition, and rare or historically significant pieces can command very high prices at auction.
