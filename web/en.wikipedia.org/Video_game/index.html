Video game

Video games are played on consoles, personal computers, and mobile devices worldwide.

A video game is an electronic game that involves interaction with a user interface or input device — such as a joystick, controller, keyboard, or motion sensor — to generate visual feedback from a display device. Video games are played on platforms including personal computers, dedicated game consoles, arcade machines, and mobile phones, and span genres from action and adventure to simulation, strategy, puzzle, and role-playing.

History

Early experiments include Spacewar! (1962), created at MIT. The industry began commercially in the 1970s with arcade games such as Pong (1972) and Space Invaders (1978) and home consoles such as the Atari 2600. After the North American crash of 1983, Nintendo's NES revived the market; the 1990s brought 3D graphics, the PlayStation, and PC gaming; the 2000s–2010s added online play, motion controls, mobile gaming after the iPhone, digital distribution, and esports. The global industry now generates more annual revenue than film and recorded music combined.

Genres and platforms

Major genres include action games and first-person shooters, platformers, role-playing games (RPGs), real-time and turn-based strategy, simulation and sports games, puzzle games, and massively multiplayer online games (MMOs). Games are developed by teams ranging from a single independent designer to studios of hundreds, using engines such as Unity and Unreal.

Culture and impact

Video games are a major cultural form, studied academically and exhibited as art; franchises such as Mario, Tetris, Minecraft, and Grand Theft Auto rank among the best-selling entertainment products of all time. Research debates their effects on cognition, learning, social behaviour, and health, while esports competitions fill arenas and game streaming platforms draw hundreds of millions of viewers.
