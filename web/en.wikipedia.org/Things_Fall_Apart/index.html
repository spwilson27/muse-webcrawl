Things Fall Apart is the debut novel by Nigerian author Chinua Achebe, first published in 1958. It depicts pre-colonial life in southeastern Nigeria and the arrival of European missionaries and colonial government in the late 19th century. The novel is considered a founding work of modern African literature in English and has sold more than 20 million copies worldwide.

Plot

The protagonist, Okonkwo, is a proud Igbo wrestling champion and leader in the fictional village of Umuofia, haunted by fear of resembling his indolent father Unoka. After accidentally killing a clansman, Okonkwo is exiled for seven years. During his absence, Christian missionaries and colonial administrators gain a foothold. On his return, Okonkwo finds his society transformed and, despairing at his people's submission, kills a colonial messenger and then takes his own life.

Themes and reception

The title comes from W. B. Yeats's poem "The Second Coming." The novel examines masculinity, tradition, colonialism, and cultural collision, and is notable for incorporating Igbo proverbs and folktales into English prose. It has been translated into more than 50 languages and is a staple of school curricula around the world.
