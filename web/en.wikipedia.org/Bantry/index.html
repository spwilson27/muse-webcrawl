Bantry (Irish: Beanntraí, meaning "(place of) Beann's people") is a town in the civil parish of Kilmocomoge in the barony of Bantry on the coast of County Cork, Ireland. It lies at the head of Bantry Bay, a deep-water inlet of the Atlantic running about 35 kilometres (22 mi) from northeast to southwest into the open sea, between the Beara Peninsula to the northwest and the Sheep's Head peninsula to the southeast.

History

The town is chiefly remembered historically for two attempted French landings in support of Irish rebellion. In December 1796, a French fleet carrying some 15,000 troops under General Hoche sailed to Bantry Bay to aid the United Irishmen, but storms scattered the fleet and prevented a landing — an episode commemorated by the leader Theobald Wolfe Tone, who was aboard one of the ships. In 1798 a smaller French force attempted to reach the bay during that year's rebellion. Nearby stands Bantry House, seat of the White family (Earls of Bantry), with formal gardens overlooking the bay; the house and an Armada exhibition centre in its grounds are open to visitors.

Economy and culture

Bantry serves as a market and service town for West Cork, with mussel farming and aquaculture in the bay, tourism, and a busy Friday market in Wolfe Tone Square. Whiddy Island, just offshore, hosts an oil storage terminal. The town is the home of the West Cork Chamber Music Festival and the West Cork Literary Festival, which draw international performers and writers each summer, and it is a gateway to the Wild Atlantic Way touring route.
