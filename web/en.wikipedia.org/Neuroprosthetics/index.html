Neuroprosthetics

Neuroprosthetics (also called neural prosthetics) is a discipline related to neuroscience and biomedical engineering concerned with the development of neural prostheses — artificial devices that replace or restore the function of an impaired part of the nervous system.

Examples

The most widely used neuroprosthesis is the cochlear implant, which restores a sense of hearing to people with severe sensorineural hearing loss and has been implanted in hundreds of thousands of people worldwide. Other devices include retinal implants for blindness, deep brain stimulators for Parkinson's disease and other movement disorders, spinal cord stimulators for chronic pain, and brain–computer interfaces that let people with paralysis control cursors and robotic arms.

Challenges

Key challenges include long-term biocompatibility of implanted electrodes, stable recording of neural signals over years, high information bandwidth, and the ethics of cognitive enhancement and neural data privacy.
