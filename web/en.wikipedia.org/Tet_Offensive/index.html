Tet Offensive

The Tet Offensive was a major escalation and coordinated campaign of surprise attacks by North Vietnam and the Viet Cong against South Vietnam, the United States and their allies during the Vietnam War. Launched on 30–31 January 1968 during the Tet lunar new year holiday, it was one of the largest military campaigns of the war.

Tet Offensive
Date: 30 Jan – 23 Sep 1968
Location: South Vietnam
Combatants: North Vietnam, Viet Cong vs South Vietnam, United States and allies
Result: US–South Vietnamese tactical victory; North Vietnamese strategic / political effect

Background

By 1967 US commanders claimed progress, yet North Vietnam planned a general offensive to spark uprising in the South and undermine US public support.

The attacks

More than 80,000 communist troops attacked over 100 towns and cities, including Saigon — where sappers briefly breached the US Embassy compound — and Hue, which was held for weeks in one of the war's bloodiest battles. The ancient Truong Son supply routes (Ho Chi Minh Trail) had helped position forces.

Aftermath

US and South Vietnamese forces recaptured all territory, inflicting heavy losses, especially on the Viet Cong. However, televised images shocked the American public, eroded confidence in official optimism, contributed to President Lyndon B. Johnson's decision not to seek re-election, and marked a turning point in US strategy toward de-escalation and Vietnamization.
