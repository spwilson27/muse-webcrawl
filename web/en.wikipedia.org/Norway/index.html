Norway, officially the Kingdom of Norway, is a Nordic country in Northern Europe, occupying the western portion of the Scandinavian Peninsula. It shares borders with Sweden, Finland and Russia, and has an extensive, fjord-indented coastline facing the North Atlantic and the Barents Sea. The country also includes the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard and the island of Jan Mayen.

History

The Viking Age (c. 793–1066) saw Norse seafarers raid, trade and settle from Ireland to Constantinople, and reach North America. Harald Fairhair is traditionally credited with unifying Norway around 872. After centuries within the Kalmar Union and then Denmark–Norway, the country adopted a liberal constitution at Eidsvoll in 1814 and entered a union with Sweden that was peacefully dissolved in 1905. Norway remained neutral in the First World War, was occupied by Germany from 1940 to 1945, and became a founding member of NATO in 1949. Voters rejected European Union membership in referendums in 1972 and 1994, though Norway participates in the European Economic Area.

Economy and society

The discovery of North Sea oil in 1969 transformed the economy; petroleum and natural gas revenues are partly saved in the Government Pension Fund Global, one of the world's largest sovereign wealth funds. Norway consistently ranks at or near the top of the Human Development Index and is a leading example of the Nordic model of a mixed economy and universal welfare state. Hydropower supplies most of its electricity.

Geography

Norway's landscape is dominated by mountains and fjords such as Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord (UNESCO World Heritage Sites). Much of the population lives in the south around Oslo, Bergen, Trondheim and Stavanger.
