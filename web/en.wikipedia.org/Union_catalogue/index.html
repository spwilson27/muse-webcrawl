Union catalogue

Printed and card union catalogues recorded the holdings of many libraries in one place.

A union catalogue is a combined library catalogue that lists the holdings of multiple libraries in a single searchable database or file. Union catalogues enable users to discover which libraries hold a given item, supporting interlibrary loan, cooperative collection development, and shared cataloguing.

History

Printed union catalogues appeared in the nineteenth century; the most famous was the (American) National Union Catalog, maintained by the Library of Congress, which reproduced catalogue cards from hundreds of libraries and grew to hundreds of printed volumes. The computer age transformed the concept: WorldCat, maintained by OCLC since 1971, is the world's largest union catalogue, with hundreds of millions of records from tens of thousands of libraries. National and regional examples include Copac/Jisc Library Hub Discover in the United Kingdom, the German KVK (Karlsruhe Virtual Catalog), and Libraries Australia.

Functions

Beyond discovery and interlibrary loan, union catalogues reduce duplicated cataloguing effort through copy cataloguing, and help libraries coordinate preservation of scarce materials by revealing how many copies survive.
