Biltmore Estate is a historic house museum and tourist attraction in Asheville, North Carolina. Biltmore House, the main residence, is a Châteauesque-style mansion built for George Washington Vanderbilt II between 1889 and 1895. With 250 rooms and about 135,000 square feet of living area in the house alone, it is the largest privately owned house in the United States.

History

Vanderbilt purchased roughly 125,000 acres of land around Asheville and commissioned architect Richard Morris Hunt to design a French Renaissance château modelled on the Loire Valley estates. The grounds were designed by Frederick Law Olmsted, the landscape architect of New York's Central Park. Construction employed about 1,000 workers and took six years.

Forestry

The estate's forests were managed first by Gifford Pinchot and then by Carl A. Schenck, who founded the Biltmore Forest School in 1898 — the first school of forestry in North America. Part of the original tract later became a nucleus of the Pisgah National Forest.

Today

The estate, reduced to about 8,000 acres, remains owned by Vanderbilt's descendants through The Biltmore Company. Opened to the public in 1930, it now includes the house, gardens, a winery, Antler Hill Village, and hotels, drawing well over a million visitors a year.
