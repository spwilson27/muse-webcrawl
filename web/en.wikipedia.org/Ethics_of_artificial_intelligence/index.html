Ethics of artificial intelligence

The ethics of artificial intelligence is the branch of technology ethics concerned with the moral behaviour of humans as they design, construct, use, and treat artificially intelligent systems, and with the behaviour of machines themselves. Issues include algorithmic bias, privacy and surveillance, automation and employment, accountability for automated decisions, and the long-term risks posed by highly capable AI.

Bias and fairness

Machine-learning systems trained on historical data can reproduce and amplify discrimination in domains such as hiring, lending, and criminal justice. Well-known cases include facial-recognition systems with higher error rates for darker-skinned faces and recidivism prediction tools accused of racial bias. Researchers study formal fairness criteria, which can conflict with one another, and mitigation techniques.

Transparency and accountability

Many AI systems function as "black boxes" whose decisions are hard to explain. The field of explainable AI seeks methods for interpreting model outputs, while regulators—notably the European Union's AI Act (2024)—impose documentation, risk-management, and human-oversight requirements on high-risk applications.

Long-term and existential concerns

Some philosophers and researchers, including Nick Bostrom, argue that advanced AI presents alignment problems: ensuring that systems pursue goals compatible with human values. Others regard such concerns as speculative compared with present-day harms. The debate has spawned a substantial literature and dedicated research institutes.
