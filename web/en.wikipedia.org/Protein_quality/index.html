Protein quality

Protein quality is the capacity of a dietary protein source to supply the essential amino acids the human body cannot synthesize, in adequate proportions and in a digestible form. Proteins meeting all essential amino acid requirements are often called "complete," while those low in one or more essential amino acids are "incomplete," with the scarcest amino acid termed the limiting amino acid.

Assessment methods

  • Biological value and net protein utilization — classical nitrogen-balance measures
  • PDCAAS — Protein Digestibility Corrected Amino Acid Score, long the standard for food labelling
  • DIAAS — Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score, recommended by the FAO in 2013 as a more accurate successor, based on ileal digestibility of each amino acid

Food sources

Animal proteins (egg, milk, meat, fish) generally score highest; among plant proteins, soy scores well, while grains tend to be low in lysine and legumes low in methionine — a complementarity exploited in traditional diets combining, for example, rice and beans.
