Bridge trilogy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge

The Bridge trilogy is a series of three science fiction novels by William Gibson, comprising Virtual Light (1993), Idoru (1996) and All Tomorrow's Parties (1999). It is Gibson's second trilogy after the Sprawl trilogy. The books share a near-future setting centred on a shantytown community living on a San Francisco Bay Bridge closed to traffic after an earthquake, and on post-earthquake Tokyo rebuilt with nanotechnology after "the Big One".

Themes

Compared with the Sprawl books, the Bridge trilogy is more grounded in contemporary culture, media celebrity and virtual fame — the "idoru" Rei Toei is an entirely software-generated pop star. Recurring motifs include emergent communities outside official systems, the reuse of obsolete technology, and history perceived through data patterns. The Bridge itself, colonised by the homeless and marginalised after its closure, is the trilogy's central image of improvised, bottom-up community.
