Clan mother

In Haudenosaunee society, clan mothers are the elder women who hold the titles of leadership.

A clan mother is a title of leadership held by elder women in certain matrilineal Indigenous societies, most notably among the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy of North America. Clan mothers are the heads of the clans within a nation, and their authority derives from their position as the eldest female lineage-holders of the extended family.

Role among the Haudenosaunee

Each Haudenosaunee nation is divided into matrilineal clans (such as Turtle, Wolf, Bear and Heron), each headed by a clan mother. She nominates and may depose the nation's chiefs (sachems) who sit on the Grand Council, manages clan lands and property, and has the final say in naming newborn children. When a chief is "de-horned" — removed for misconduct — it is the clan mother who performs the removal. Because descent is traced through the mother, women control the passing of clan membership and much of the nation's property.

Other societies

Comparable elder-woman leadership roles exist in other matrilineal cultures, including the Muscogee (Creek) of the southeastern United States and some societies of West Africa and South Asia. In the twentieth century, the term also entered popular usage through works of New Age spirituality, such as the Medicine Cards system, in a sense that traditionalists consider a distortion of the Haudenosaunee office.
