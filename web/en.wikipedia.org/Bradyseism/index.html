Bradyseism

The Temple of Serapis at Pozzuoli, classic evidence of bradyseism

Bradyseism is the gradual uplift (positive bradyseism) or subsidence (negative bradyseism) of part of the Earth's surface caused by the filling or emptying of an underground magma chamber and/or hydrothermal activity, particularly in volcanic calderas. The term derives from the Greek bradýs ("slow") and seismós ("movement").

Pozzuoli and the Phlegraean Fields

The phenomenon is famously documented at Pozzuoli in the Phlegraean Fields caldera west of Naples, Italy. The Roman macellum there — long misnamed the Temple of Serapis — bears dark bands of borings by marine molluscs on its standing columns, showing that the building subsided below sea level and later rose again. In 1983–84 an uplift of about 1.8 metres at Pozzuoli prompted the evacuation of some 40,000 people. Renewed uplift and swarms of small earthquakes from 2023 onward drew renewed attention to the caldera.
