Greater Poland

Polish: Wielkopolska

Location: West-central Poland

Main cities: Poznań, Gniezno, Kalisz, Konin, Piła, Leszno

Greater Poland (Polish: Wielkopolska) is a historical region of west-central Poland, centred on the Warta River valley and the cities of Poznań, Gniezno and Kalisz. It is considered the cradle of the Polish state, where the Polans tribe under the Piast dynasty built the first Polish realm in the 10th century; its name contrasts with Lesser Poland (Małopolska) in the southeast.

History

The region's early medieval strongholds included Gniezno — seat of Poland's first archbishopric, founded in 1000, and traditionally the first capital — and Poznań, seat of the first Polish bishopric (968). After the fragmentation of the kingdom in the 12th century, Greater Poland formed a distinct duchy, reunified with the Polish crown in the 14th century under Władysław I and Casimir III. In the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth the region comprised the Poznań, Kalisz and Gniezno voivodeships.

The Second Partition of 1793 gave most of Greater Poland to Prussia, and despite the Greater Poland uprisings of 1806 and 1848 the region remained under German rule (as the Grand Duchy of Posen, then the Province of Posen) until the successful Greater Poland Uprising of 1918–1919 restored it to the newly independent Poland. Under the Nazi occupation (1939–1945) it was annexed to the Reich as the Warthegau and subjected to brutal Germanisation. Since 1999 the core of the region forms the Greater Poland Voivodeship, one of Poland's largest and most prosperous provinces.

Geography and culture

Greater Poland is a lowland region of lakes, forests and fertile farmland drained by the Warta and Noteć rivers. Poznań, its capital, is a major economic, academic and trade-fair centre. The region has a distinct dialect and culinary traditions, and its early Piast heritage is showcased on the Piast Trail linking Gniezno, Poznań and the early medieval site of Ostrów Lednicki.
