Antimetabolite

An antimetabolite is a chemical that inhibits the use of a metabolite, a chemical that is part of normal metabolism. Such substances are often similar in structure to the metabolite they interfere with, such as antifolates that interfere with folic acid. Antimetabolites are widely used in chemotherapy and immunosuppression.

Types

  • Folate antagonists: methotrexate, pemetrexed — inhibit dihydrofolate reductase and related enzymes
  • Purine analogues: mercaptopurine, azathioprine, fludarabine
  • Pyrimidine analogues: 5-fluorouracil, capecitabine, cytarabine, gemcitabine

Uses

Antimetabolites preferentially harm rapidly dividing cells, making them effective against cancers such as leukaemia and breast cancer, and useful at low doses for autoimmune diseases including rheumatoid arthritis and Crohn's disease. Toxicity commonly affects bone marrow and gastrointestinal mucosa.

History

The class began in the 1940s with Sidney Farber's aminopterin remissions in childhood leukaemia, one of the founding achievements of chemotherapy.
