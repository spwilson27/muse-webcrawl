Brahmagupta

Born: c. 598 CE
Died: c. 668 CE
Known for: Rules for zero and negative numbers; Brahmagupta's formula; the Brahma-sphuta-siddhanta
Indian mathematical astronomy of the 7th century

Brahmagupta (c. 598 – c. 668 CE) was an Indian mathematician and astronomer. He was the head of the astronomical observatory at Ujjain and wrote two influential texts on mathematics and astronomy: the Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta ("The Corrected Treatise of Brahma", 628 CE) and the Khaṇḍakhādyaka (665 CE).

Mathematics

Brahmagupta was the first to give rules for computing with zero as a number in its own right, and he set out rules for arithmetic with negative numbers ("debts") and positive numbers ("fortunes"). He gave the formula for the area of a cyclic quadrilateral now known as Brahmagupta's formula, described methods for solving quadratic equations and linear Diophantine equations, and worked on the Pell-type equation. His treatment of division by zero differed from the modern convention.

Astronomy

His astronomical work covered eclipses, planetary positions, and the shape of the Earth, which he argued was spherical and computed with a remarkably good estimate of its circumference.
