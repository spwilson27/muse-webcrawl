The 1976 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXI Olympiad, were held from 17 July to 1 August 1976 in Montreal, Canada. They were the first and, to date, only Summer Olympics hosted in Canada (the country later hosted the Winter Games in Calgary in 1988 and Vancouver in 2010).

Highlights

Romanian gymnast Nadia Comăneci became the star of the Games at age 14, earning the first perfect score of 10.0 in Olympic gymnastics history for her uneven bars routine; the scoreboard, not programmed for a perfect mark, displayed 1.00. She won three gold medals. Japanese gymnast Shun Fujimoto famously completed his rings routine with a broken knee to secure team gold for Japan. American decathlete Bruce Jenner (later Caitlyn Jenner) set a world record, and swimmer John Naber won four golds for the United States.

Boycott and controversies

Twenty-nine countries, most of them African, boycotted the Games in protest at the IOC's refusal to ban New Zealand, whose rugby team had toured apartheid South Africa. The boycott removed many leading African distance runners from competition. The Games were also plagued by enormous cost overruns under Mayor Jean Drapeau; the final cost of about C$1.6 billion left Montreal with a debt that was not paid off until 2006 — the stadium, nicknamed "the Big Owe", saw its inclined tower completed only in 1987.

Host Canada failed to win a gold medal — the first host nation of a Summer Games to do so — though it collected eleven medals overall. Security, tightened after the Munich massacre of 1972, was extensive.
