Mahmud Gawan

Born: 1411, Gawan, Persia
Died: 1481, Bidar, Bahmani Sultanate
Office: Prime minister (wakil) of the Bahmani Sultanate

Mahmud Gawan (1411–1481) was a Persian merchant, scholar, and statesman who became prime minister of the Bahmani Sultanate in the Deccan region of India. Arriving in India as a trader around 1453, he entered royal service and rose under Sultans Humayun Shah and Muhammad Shah III to become the effective ruler of the sultanate for over a decade.

Administration and campaigns

Gawan reorganised the empire into eight provinces (tarafs), reformed revenue collection and the military, curbed the power of provincial governors, and expanded Bahmani territory through successful campaigns against the Gajapatis of Orissa, the Sangama rulers of Goa and the Konkan, and neighbouring kingdoms, taking the sultanate to its greatest extent. His administration is generally remembered for competence and relative incorruptibility amid a factious court divided between Deccani and foreign (Afaqi) nobles.

Scholarship and downfall

A celebrated patron of learning, he built the great madrasa at Bidar in 1472, a three-storeyed college with a vast library, modelled on the institutions of Samarkand and Khorasan. His letters, collected as the Riyaz al-Insha, are classics of Persian prose. In 1481, rivals forged a treasonous letter in his name; the sultan ordered his execution before the forgery was exposed. His death is widely seen as the turning point from which the Bahmani Sultanate fragmented into the five Deccan sultanates.
