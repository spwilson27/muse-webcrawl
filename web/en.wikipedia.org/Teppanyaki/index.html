Teppanyaki

A teppanyaki chef cooking at the griddle

Teppanyaki (Japanese: 鉄板焼き) is a style of Japanese cuisine that uses an iron griddle, the teppan, to cook food. The word combines teppan ("iron plate") and yaki ("grilled, broiled, or pan-fried"). Typical ingredients include beef, shrimp, scallops, chicken, and assorted vegetables.

History

The style is generally credited to Shigeji Fujioka of the Japanese restaurant chain Misono, which opened in Kobe in 1945 and began cooking Western-influenced dishes such as steak on a teppan before diners. In the United States, the theatrical version of teppanyaki—chefs performing knife tricks, flaming onion volcanoes, and juggling utensils at a communal griddle table—was popularized by the Benihana chain, founded in New York in 1964 by Hiroaki "Rocky" Aoki.

Style

In Japan, teppanyaki is often a comparatively refined form of dining centred on premium ingredients such as wagyu beef and seafood, cooked simply with oil, salt, and soy-based sauces. Abroad, the communal table and chef's showmanship are central to the experience. Dishes commonly served include steak cubes, fried rice, shrimp flambé, and vegetables such as zucchini, mushrooms, and onions.
