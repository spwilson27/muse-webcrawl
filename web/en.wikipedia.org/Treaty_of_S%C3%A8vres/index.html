Treaty of Sèvres

Signed10 August 1920
LocationSèvres, France
PartiesOttoman Empire; Allied Powers

The Treaty of Sèvres was a 1920 treaty signed between the Ottoman Empire and the Allies of the First World War, at Sèvres in France on 10 August 1920. It formed part of the series of agreements by which the victorious powers partitioned the Ottoman Empire, and it would have left the empire as little more than a rump state in Anatolia.

Terms

The treaty confirmed the loss of the Ottoman Arab provinces, which became League of Nations mandates, recognised the independence of Armenia, provided for a possible autonomous Kurdistan, gave Smyrna and its hinterland to Greek administration, internationalised the Turkish Straits, and imposed severe limits on the Ottoman armed forces and finances.

Aftermath

The treaty was rejected by the Turkish nationalist movement under Mustafa Kemal, which fought the Turkish War of Independence and forced the Allies to negotiate anew. It was superseded by the Treaty of Lausanne in 1923, which recognised the Republic of Turkey within borders close to those of the modern state. In Turkish political discourse, the "Sèvres syndrome" refers to a persistent fear that outside powers seek to partition the country.
