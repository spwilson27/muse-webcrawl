Gait analysis is the systematic study of animal locomotion, more specifically the study of human motion, using the eye and the brain of observers, augmented by instrumentation for measuring body movements, body mechanics, and the activity of the muscles. It is used in clinical assessment of walking disorders, sports science, rehabilitation, and as a form of biometric identification.

The gait cycle

Analysis is organised around the gait cycle, the interval between two successive occurrences of a repeated event such as heel strike. In normal walking the cycle comprises a stance phase (about 60% of the cycle, when the foot is on the ground) and a swing phase (about 40%). Typical spatiotemporal measures include walking speed, cadence, step length, stride length, and step width.

Techniques

Modern gait laboratories combine optoelectronic motion capture — reflective markers tracked by multiple infrared cameras — with force platforms that measure ground reaction forces, pressure-sensitive walkways, and surface electromyography of leg muscles. Marker sets such as the Plug-in-Gait model allow joint angles, moments, and powers to be computed by inverse dynamics. Wearable inertial sensors and instrumented treadmills are increasingly used outside the laboratory.

Clinical applications

Quantitative gait analysis guides treatment in cerebral palsy — for example, surgical planning and botulinum toxin therapy — and in stroke, Parkinson's disease, amputation and prosthetic fitting, and orthopaedic conditions. Distinctive pathological patterns, such as hemiplegic, diplegic, Trendelenburg, ataxic and festinating gaits, can be characterised numerically and monitored over time.

History and other uses

The scientific study of gait began with the Weber brothers (1836) and was transformed by Eadweard Muybridge's sequential photography and Étienne-Jules Marey's chronophotography in the 19th century. Gait analysis is also applied to quadrupeds in veterinary science, to animation and robotics, and in forensic science, where an individual's walking pattern may be compared with surveillance footage.
