A payday loan (also called a payday advance, salary loan, payroll loan, small dollar loan, short-term loan, or cash advance loan) is a short-term unsecured loan, often characterised by high interest rates. The term "payday" in payday loan refers to when a borrower writes a postdated check to the lender for the amount they wish to borrow plus a fee, or authorizes electronic access to their bank account; the lender holds the check until the borrower's next payday, when the loan typically comes due.

Operation

To obtain a loan, a borrower usually provides proof of income and employment, a bank account, and identification; the lender advances cash and either holds a postdated cheque or obtains authorization to debit the account on the due date. Loans are generally for two weeks (one pay cycle) and are marketed as bridging finance for emergencies. Fees of $15 per $100 borrowed, for example, correspond to an annual percentage rate of roughly 400% for a two-week loan.

Criticism and the debt cycle

Consumer advocates argue that payday loans trap borrowers in cycles of debt: research by the U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) found that most payday loans are rolled over or followed by another loan within a short period, and that a large majority of business comes from borrowers with many loans per year. The industry responds that the loans serve customers excluded from mainstream credit, that APR comparisons with long-term credit are misleading, and that bans would push borrowers to worse alternatives.

Regulation

Regulation varies widely. In the United States, payday lending is governed primarily by state law: some states cap rates (effectively prohibiting the product), others license lenders with fee limits, rollover restrictions and databases to prevent multiple simultaneous loans. The federal Military Lending Act caps rates at 36% for service members. The CFPB's 2017 payday rule introduced ability-to-repay requirements, parts of which were later rescinded. In the United Kingdom, the Financial Conduct Authority capped total costs in 2015 at 100% of the amount borrowed, with daily interest capped at 0.8%. Canada regulates payday loans at the provincial level under a Criminal Code exemption.

Online payday lending

Since the 2000s, online lenders have grown, some operating offshore or through partnerships with banks or Native American tribes in ways that have attracted legal challenges over the applicability of state rate caps.
