Turoyo language

Turoyo (also called Ṭuroyo, Surayt, or Suryoyo) is a Neo-Aramaic language spoken by the Syriac-Aramean Christians of Tur Abdin, a region in southeastern Turkey, and by a large diaspora in Europe, the Americas, and Australia. It belongs to the Central Neo-Aramaic group and is closely related to Mlahsô, which is now extinct.

History and status

Turoyo developed from the Aramaic vernaculars of Tur Abdin alongside the liturgical Classical Syriac used by the Syriac Orthodox Church. Because of emigration from Tur Abdin during the 20th century — accelerated by persecution and economic pressure — the majority of speakers now live in diaspora, notably in Germany, Sweden, and the Netherlands, and the language is considered endangered in its homeland. It is written in the Syriac script (Serto) and, in diaspora communities, increasingly in a Latin-based alphabet.
