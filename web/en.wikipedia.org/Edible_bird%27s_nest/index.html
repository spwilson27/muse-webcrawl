Edible bird's nest

Edible bird's nests, also known as "swallow's nests", are bird nests created from solidified saliva by edible-nest swiftlets, Indian swiftlets and other swiftlets of the genera Aerodramus, Hydrochous, Schoutedenapus and Collocalia, which are harvested for human consumption. They are among the most expensive animal products consumed by humans.

Use

The nests have been used in Chinese cooking for over 400 years, most often as bird's nest soup. When dissolved in water, the nest material forms a gelatinous substance prized as a delicacy and traditionally credited with health benefits, including for the skin and immune system. Beyond China, the soup is served in Taiwan, Singapore, Vietnam, and Chinese restaurants worldwide.

Harvesting and trade

Nests are collected from caves in Southeast Asia — famously at Gomantong and Niah in Borneo — and increasingly from purpose-built "swiftlet houses" in Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam. Indonesia is the largest producer. Grades (red, yellow, white) vary in price; red "blood" nests are the most costly. High prices have led to adulteration, conservation concerns over swiftlet populations, and regulation of imports, including concerns about nitrite content.
