Radioactive contamination


Placeholder image: Radioactive contamination

Radioactive contamination is the deposition of, or presence of, radioactive substances on surfaces or within solids, liquids or gases (including the human body) where their presence is unintended or undesirable. Contamination differs from irradiation: a contaminated person or object carries radioactive material that continues to emit radiation.

Sources

Major sources include nuclear weapons fallout, reactor accidents such as Chernobyl (1986) and Fukushima (2011), reprocessing, medical and industrial spills, and naturally occurring radioactive material in mining.

Control and cleanup

Contamination is measured in becquerels per unit area or mass. Control measures include containment, protective clothing, surveys with Geiger counters, and decontamination by washing, chemical treatment, or removal of material. Contaminated land may be remediated or restricted; extremely contaminated areas, such as the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone, remain off-limits decades after deposition.
