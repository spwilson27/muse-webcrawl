Present value

Discounting converts a future cash flow into its equivalent worth today.

In economics and finance, present value (PV), also known as present discounted value, is the value of an expected stream of cash flows determined as of the date of valuation. It rests on the principle that money available now is worth more than the same amount in the future — because it can be invested and earn a return, and because of inflation and risk — so future sums must be discounted at an appropriate rate.

Calculation

For a single future amount FV received in t years with discount rate r, the present value is

PV = FV / (1 + r)t

For a stream of cash flows Ct, present values of each payment are summed. Special cases include the annuity and the perpetuity (PV = C / r). A stream whose present value exceeds its cost has a positive net present value and is generally considered a worthwhile investment.

Applications

Present value underlies bond pricing, discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation of companies, pension and lease accounting, mortgage calculations, and cost–benefit analysis in public policy, where choosing the discount rate (e.g. for long-run climate damages) is ethically and economically contentious.
