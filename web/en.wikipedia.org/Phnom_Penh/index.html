Phnom Penh is the capital and most populous city of Cambodia. It has been the national capital since the French protectorate of Cambodia and has grown to become the nation's economic, industrial and cultural centre. The city sits at the junction of the Mekong, Bassac and Tonlé Sap rivers in south-central Cambodia.

History

According to legend, the city was founded in 1372 when a widow, Lady Penh, built a temple (Wat Phnom, "Penh's hill") for Buddha statues found in a floating tree. Phnom Penh became the royal capital under King Ponhea Yat in the 1430s, was later eclipsed by Lovek and Oudong, and regained capital status in 1865 under King Norodom and French protection. During the French colonial era it was known as the "Pearl of Asia", with broad boulevards and art-deco buildings such as the Central Market (1937).

In April 1975 the victorious Khmer Rouge forcibly emptied the city, driving its population into the countryside; most residents did not return until after the Vietnamese intervention of 1979. Sites such as the Tuol Sleng prison (S-21) and the Choeung Ek killing fields now serve as memorials. Since the 1990s the city has grown rapidly, with high-rise development and a population of over two million.

Landmarks

Major sights include the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, the National Museum, Wat Phnom, the Independence Monument and the riverside Sisowath Quay. Phnom Penh hosted the 2023 Southeast Asian Games.
