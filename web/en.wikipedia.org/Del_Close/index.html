Del Close (March 9, 1934 – March 4, 1999) was an American actor, writer and teacher who co-founded the ImprovOlympic in Chicago and is regarded as one of the most influential figures in improvisational theatre. Together with Charna Halpern he developed the Harold, a long-form improvisation structure now taught around the world.

Career

Close began performing in the 1950s and was an early member of the Compass Players and The Second City in Chicago. He worked with John Belushi, Bill Murray, Gilda Radner and many others, and later served as "house metaphysician" at Saturday Night Live, coaching its cast. His students included Tina Fey, Amy Poehler, Stephen Colbert and Chris Farley. With Charna Halpern and Kim "Howard" Johnson he co-wrote the book Truth in Comedy (1994), a widely used manual of improvisation.

Legacy

Close's teaching emphasised ensemble play, agreement ("yes, and") and treating improvisers as "poets, actors and playwrights" at once. The iO Theater in Chicago hosts an annual Del Close Marathon of improvisation, also staged by the Upright Citizens Brigade in New York. In accordance with his wishes, his skull was donated for use as Yorick's skull in productions of Hamlet, a bequest whose details later became the subject of public discussion.
