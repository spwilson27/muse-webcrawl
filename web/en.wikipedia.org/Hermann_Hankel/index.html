Hermann Hankel

Died29 August 1873 (aged 34), Schramberg, Germany

Hermann Hankel (14 February 1839 – 29 August 1873) was a German mathematician who worked on the foundations of arithmetic, complex numbers, quaternions, and special functions. Several mathematical objects bear his name, including the Hankel transform, Hankel matrices, and the Hankel functions (Bessel functions of the third kind).

Life

Hankel was born in Halle, the son of physicist Wilhelm Gottlieb Hankel. He studied at Leipzig, Göttingen, and Berlin, attending lectures by Riemann, Weierstrass, and Kronecker, and took his doctorate at Leipzig in 1861. After teaching at Leipzig and Erlangen, he became a full professor at Tübingen in 1869. He died at only 34 while on holiday in the Black Forest.

Work

His 1867 book Theorie der complexen Zahlensysteme ("Theory of Complex Number Systems") gave an influential historical and systematic account of number systems from the integers through quaternions, and articulated the "principle of permanence of formal laws" governing the extension of arithmetic. In analysis, his memoir on infinitely oscillating and discontinuous functions contributed to the theory of integration, and the Hankel transform emerged from his work on Bessel functions and Fourier integrals for cylindrically symmetric problems.

Named concepts

A Hankel matrix has constant entries along its anti-diagonals and appears in moment problems and systems theory; the Hankel transform is an integral transform involving Bessel functions of the first kind, widely used in optics and signal processing.
