Haut-Katanga (French for "Upper Katanga") is one of the 21 provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo created in the 2015 repartitioning. Its capital is Lubumbashi, the country's second-largest city. The province comprises the city of Lubumbashi and the territories of Kipushi, Kasenga, Mitwaba, Pweto, Sakania, and Kambove.

Geography

Haut-Katanga lies in the far southeast of the country, bordering Zambia to the south and the Congolese provinces of Haut-Lomami, Tanganyika, and Lualaba. It forms part of the Central African Copperbelt, and its landscape is dominated by savanna plateau at elevations around 1,000–1,500 metres, drained by the Lufira and upper Congo (Lualaba) river systems toward Lake Mweru on the Zambian border.

Economy

Mining is the backbone of the provincial economy. The region is rich in copper and cobalt, with major operations around Lubumbashi, Likasi (in neighbouring Lualaba and Haut-Katanga border areas), Kipushi (zinc and copper), and Kolwezi farther west. Mining products are exported mainly by road and rail south through Zambia. Artisanal cobalt mining in the wider Katanga region has drawn international attention for labour and child-labour concerns.

History

The area corresponds roughly to the southern part of the former Katanga Province, which under Moise Tshombe declared secession as the State of Katanga (1960–1963) during the Congo Crisis. The 2006 constitution provided for Katanga to be divided into four provinces — Tanganyika, Haut-Lomami, Lualaba, and Haut-Katanga — a change implemented in 2015.
