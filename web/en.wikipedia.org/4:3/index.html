4:3

4:3 (also written 1.33:1) is an aspect ratio with a width of four units and a height of three units. For more than half a century it was the standard shape of television screens and computer monitors, and it was the original aspect ratio of 35 mm motion picture film.

History

The ratio derives from the 35 mm film format pioneered by William Kennedy Dickson in the Edison laboratories, whose frames were four perforations high and approximately 1.33 times as wide as tall. Early television adopted the same proportions so that films could be shown with minimal cropping. The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences made a slight adjustment in 1932, producing the "Academy ratio" of 1.375:1, but for television the shape remained effectively 4:3.

Decline

From the 1950s, cinema moved to widescreen formats to compete with television, while television itself stayed 4:3 until the transition to high-definition broadcasting in the 1990s and 2000s standardised the 16:9 widescreen ratio. Computer monitors likewise shifted from 4:3 resolutions such as 1024 × 768 to widescreen formats. The 4:3 ratio survives in some tablets (notably the iPad's 4:3 display), in medium-format photography sensors, and in the presentation of archival film and television, which is often pillarboxed within a widescreen frame.
