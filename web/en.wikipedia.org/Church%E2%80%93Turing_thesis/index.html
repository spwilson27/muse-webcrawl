Church–Turing thesis

Field: Computability theory

Associated with: Alonzo Church, Alan Turing

The Church–Turing thesis states that every function that can be computed by an effective procedure can be computed by a Turing machine. Church's lambda calculus and Turing's machine model, developed in the 1930s, proved equivalent to each other and to other general models of computation.

Meaning

The thesis is not a mathematical theorem about a formally defined notion, because “effective procedure” is intuitive. It is supported by the convergence of independent formalisms and by the absence of a credible counterexample. It underlies the concept of a universal computer and the limits expressed by undecidable problems.

Extensions and debate

Stronger claims about physical computation, quantum devices, and hypercomputation are debated. Quantum computers change complexity for some problems but are not known to compute functions beyond the Turing-computable class.
