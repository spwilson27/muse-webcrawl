Cantucci

Cantucci with almonds, traditionally served with vin santo (placeholder)

Cantucci (Italian pronunciation: [kanˈtuttʃi]) are Italian almond biscuits originating from the Tuscan city of Prato. They are twice-baked, oblong-shaped, dry and crunchy, and are traditionally dipped in a drink, most famously the Tuscan dessert wine vin santo. Outside Italy, cantucci are often grouped under the more general name biscotti, which in Italian simply means "biscuits" (literally "twice-cooked").

History

The recipe for almond biscuits in Tuscany dates back centuries; the word cantuccio originally referred to a small piece or corner of bread, particularly the crusty end. The modern cantucci recipe was revived and popularised in the nineteenth century by the Prato baker Antonio Mattei, whose bakery, founded in 1858, still produces cantucci — sometimes called biscotti di Prato — according to the traditional formula. In 2016 cantucci toscani received Protected Geographical Indication (PGI) status from the European Union.

Preparation

The dough is made from flour, sugar, eggs, and whole unskinned almonds, sometimes flavoured with pine nuts, aniseed, or lemon zest, and traditionally contains no butter, oil, milk, or yeast. The dough is formed into a flattened log, baked once until set, then sliced diagonally into oblong pieces while warm and baked a second time, which gives the biscuits their characteristic dryness and long shelf life.

Serving

In Tuscany cantucci are classically served at the end of a meal with a small glass of vin santo, into which the biscuits are briefly dipped to soften them. They are also eaten with coffee, tea, or other dessert wines.
