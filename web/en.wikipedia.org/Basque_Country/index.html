Basque Country (autonomous community)

The Basque Country (Basque: Euskadi; Spanish: País Vasco) is an autonomous community in northern Spain. It comprises the provinces of Araba (Álava), Gipuzkoa, and Bizkaia (Biscay), with Vitoria-Gasteiz as its capital and largest cities including Bilbao and San Sebastián (Donostia). It borders France and the Bay of Biscay to the north and the Spanish regions of Cantabria, Castile and León, La Rioja, and Navarre.

Language and culture

The region is the heartland of the Basque people and the Basque language (euskara), a language isolate unrelated to any other known language. Basque is co-official with Spanish throughout the autonomous community and is spoken by roughly a third of its inhabitants.

History

The Basque provinces preserved medieval charters (fueros) granting extensive self-government until the 19th century. Industrialisation, centred on Bilbao's iron, steel, and shipbuilding industries, transformed the region in the late 1800s. Under the 1979 Statute of Autonomy (the Statute of Gernika), the Basque Country recovered extensive self-government after the Franco dictatorship, including its own police force (the Ertzaintza) and fiscal arrangements.

Economy

The Basque Country is one of Spain's most prosperous regions, with strong manufacturing, engineering, energy, and financial sectors, and a notable network of worker cooperatives centred on Mondragón.
