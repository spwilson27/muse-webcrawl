The Battle of the Philippine Sea was a major naval battle of World War II fought between the United States and Japan on June 19–20, 1944, during the Mariana Islands campaign. The lopsided American victory — in which hundreds of inexperienced Japanese naval aviators were shot down — became known as the "Great Marianas Turkey Shoot." It eliminated the Imperial Japanese Navy's ability to conduct large-scale carrier operations.

Background

American forces invaded Saipan on June 15, 1944. The Japanese Combined Fleet under Vice Admiral Jisaburō Ozawa sortied under Operation A-Go, hoping to destroy the U.S. Fifth Fleet with carrier aircraft reinforced by land-based planes from nearby islands.

The battle

On June 19, Japanese carrier raids were intercepted by radar-directed American fighters and shattered with minimal damage to the U.S. fleet. Meanwhile, American submarines torpedoed the carriers Taihō and Shōkaku, both of which sank. On June 20, a long-range American strike sank the carrier Hiyō and damaged others, despite heavy losses during the dangerous night recovery — the famous "Mission Beyond Darkness," lit by the fleet turning on its lights to guide pilots home.

Aftermath

Japan lost three carriers and nearly 600 aircraft with their irreplaceable aircrews. Japanese carrier aviation never recovered, and the surviving fleet was reduced largely to a decoy role by the Battle of Leyte Gulf.
