Powder River Basin

The Powder River Basin is a geologic structural basin in southeast Montana and northeast Wyoming, about 120 miles (190 km) east to west and 200 miles (320 km) north to south, known for its extensive coal reserves. The basin is drained by the Powder River, a tributary of the Yellowstone River, from which it takes its name.

Coal mining

The basin contains the largest deposits of surface-minable coal in the United States. The coal is low in sulphur, sub-bituminous in rank, and occurs in thick seams—such as the Wyodak-Anderson seam—tens of metres thick, lying close to the surface and therefore cheaply mined by open-pit methods. Major mines include the North Antelope Rochelle Mine, the largest coal mine in the United States, and the Black Thunder mine. At its peak in the mid-2000s, the Powder River Basin produced more than 40 percent of the coal mined in the United States.

Much of the region's coal is shipped by unit train to power plants across the Midwest and South. Declining demand for coal for electricity generation since the late 2000s, driven by competition from natural gas and renewables, has led to falling production and mine closures in the basin.

Other resources and geography

Besides coal, the basin has produced oil, natural gas, and coalbed methane, and it contains deposits of uranium. The region's landscape consists of rolling hills, badlands, and high plains grassland, with the Bighorn Mountains to the west and the Black Hills to the east. The basin was also a significant cattle-ranching region and the site of conflicts during the Plains Indian wars, including Red Cloud's War and the Johnson County War.
