Attack on Mers-el-Kébir

Date3 July 1940
LocationMers-el-Kébir, French Algeria
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom vs. Vichy France
ResultFrench fleet largely neutralised; 1,297 French sailors killed

The battleship Bretagne exploding

The Attack on Mers-el-Kébir (3 July 1940), part of Operation Catapult, was a British naval attack on the French fleet anchored at Mers-el-Kébir near Oran in French Algeria, shortly after France's armistice with Germany in the Second World War. Fearing that the powerful French fleet might fall into German or Italian hands, the Royal Navy presented the French admiral, Marcel-Bruno Gensoul, with an ultimatum: join the British, sail to a British, American or French West Indian port, or scuttle the ships. When negotiations failed, Force H under Vice-Admiral James Somerville opened fire.

The attack

The bombardment lasted only minutes but was devastating. The battleship Bretagne blew up and capsized with most of her crew; Dunkerque and Provence were badly damaged and beached; the destroyer Mogador was crippled. The battlecruiser Strasbourg and several destroyers escaped to Toulon. In total, 1,297 French sailors were killed and about 350 wounded — men who, weeks earlier, had been allies of Britain. A follow-up air attack on 6 July further damaged Dunkerque.

Consequences

The attack poisoned Anglo-French relations, prompted the Vichy government to break off diplomatic ties with Britain, and was exploited by German propaganda. Yet it also signalled to the United States and the world that Britain was determined to fight on regardless of cost, strengthening American confidence in British resolve. A companion operation seized French ships in British ports, and at Alexandria a negotiated settlement avoided bloodshed. The episode remains one of the most controversial British decisions of the war.
