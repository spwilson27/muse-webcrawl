Tapas

Tapas (Spanish: [ˈtapas]) are appetizers or snacks in Spanish cuisine. They may be cold (such as mixed olives and cheese) or hot (such as chopitos, battered and fried baby squid; patatas bravas; and gambas al ajillo, prawns in garlic oil). In some bars in Spain, tapas have evolved into elaborate small plates; a selection ordered together can constitute a full meal.

History

The word derives from the Spanish verb tapar, "to cover", and one popular legend holds that King Alfonso X of Castile ordered that wine be served with a small portion of food covering the glass — a tapa — to keep out dust and flies. In parts of Andalusia, notably Granada, a tapa has traditionally been given free with a drink.

Common tapas

Well-known dishes include tortilla española (potato omelette), jamón ibérico, pulpo a la gallega (Galician-style octopus), pimientos de padrón, and albóndigas (meatballs).
