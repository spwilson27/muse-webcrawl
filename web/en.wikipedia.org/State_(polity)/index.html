State (polity)

A state is a polity under a system of government. There is no undisputed definition of a state. A widely used definition from the German sociologist Max Weber is that a state is a polity that maintains a monopoly on the legitimate use of violence, although other definitions are common.

Definition

The 1933 Montevideo Convention holds that a state as a person of international law should possess: (a) a permanent population; (b) a defined territory; (c) a government; and (d) a capacity to enter into relations with other states. International recognition remains contested for many entities; see the list of states with limited recognition.

Origins

The earliest states emerged in Mesopotamia, Egypt, the Indus Valley, and China around 3000 BCE, alongside agriculture, writing, and urbanisation. The Greek polis and the Roman state developed distinct models of citizenship and law.

The modern state

The modern sovereign state system is conventionally dated to the Peace of Westphalia (1648), which established principles of territorial integrity and non-intervention. The nation state, in which the political unit coincides with a national community, spread in the 19th and 20th centuries, followed by decolonisation after 1945.

Forms of state

States may be unitary or federal; governments may be republics or monarchies. A failed state is one whose government has lost effective control of its territory.

Theories of the state

Thomas Hobbes justified the state through social contract; Marxist theory treats the state as an instrument of class rule, while anarchists advocate its abolition. The welfare state emerged in the 20th century to provide social insurance and public services.
