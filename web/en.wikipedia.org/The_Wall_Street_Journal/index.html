The Wall Street Journal

Type: Daily newspaper

Founded: 8 July 1889

Founders: Charles Dow, Edward Jones, Charles Bergstresser

Owner: Dow Jones / News Corp (since 2007)

The Wall Street Journal is an American business-focused daily newspaper published in New York City, one of the largest newspapers in the United States by circulation. Founded in 1889 by Charles Dow, Edward Jones and Charles Bergstresser of Dow Jones & Company, it grew out of brief news bulletins delivered to subscribers on Wall Street.

Content

The paper is renowned for its coverage of business and finance, presented since 1951 in a distinctive format featuring six columns and hand-drawn "hedcut" stipple portraits. Its news reporting is regarded as sober and authoritative — it has won dozens of Pulitzer Prizes — while its editorial pages are notably conservative, making the paper's news and opinion pages a frequent subject of comment.

Ownership

After a century of control by the Bancroft family through Dow Jones, the paper was sold in 2007 to Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation for about billion. It publishes international editions for Europe and Asia and pioneered successful paid digital subscriptions.
