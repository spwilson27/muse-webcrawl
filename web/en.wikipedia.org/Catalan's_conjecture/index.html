Catalan's conjecture

Catalan's conjecture (now known as Mihăilescu's theorem) is a theorem in number theory proposed by Eugène Charles Catalan in 1844. It states that the only solution in integers of the equation ap − bq = 1, for a, b > 0 and exponents p, q > 1, is 32 − 23 = 1. In other words, 8 and 9 are the only consecutive perfect powers greater than 1.

History and proof

Partial results accumulated over more than a century: the case where one exponent is 2 was settled in the Middle Ages, and major advances were made by Lebesgue, Ko Chao and Robert Tijdeman, who showed in 1976 that only finitely many solutions could exist. The full conjecture resisted proof until 2002, when Preda Mihăilescu announced a proof using cyclotomic fields and Galois modules; it was published in 2004 and the result is now generally called Mihăilescu's theorem.
