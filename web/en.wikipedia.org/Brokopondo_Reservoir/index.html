Brokopondo Reservoir

Brokopondo Reservoir
Location: Suriname River, Suriname
Surface area: approx. 1,560 km²
Created: 1961–1964 (Afobaka Dam)
Official name: Prof. Dr. Ir. W. J. van Blommesteinmeer

The Brokopondo Reservoir (Dutch: Brokopondostuwmeer, officially the Prof. Dr. Ir. W. J. van Blommesteinmeer) is a large artificial lake in Suriname, created by damming the Suriname River at Afobaka in the early 1960s. With a surface area of roughly 1,560 square kilometres (600 sq mi), it is one of the largest reservoirs in the world by surface area, flooding a vast area of tropical rainforest.

Construction

The Afobaka Dam was built between 1961 and 1964 by the Surinaamse Waterkracht Maatschappij, a subsidiary of the aluminium company Alcoa, to generate hydroelectric power for the alumina refinery and aluminium smelter at Paranam, powered from the Brokopondo plant (installed capacity around 180–190 MW). The reservoir displaced about 5,000–6,000 people, mainly Saramaka Maroons, whose villages were flooded; the government built transmigration villages (such as Klaaskreek, Nieuw Koffiekamp and Brownsweg) for the displaced, a process that remained a source of grievance and is commemorated in Saramaka oral history.

Environment

The flooding was carried out without clearing the forest, so dead tree trunks still project from the water. The drowned vegetation initially caused severe problems with water hyacinth growth and decomposition, and submerged timber has since been harvested commercially. The reservoir's electricity now supplies much of Suriname's grid, including the capital Paramaribo, after the state power company assumed control of the dam from Alcoa/Suralco around 2019–2020. Islands in the lake, such as Tonka Island, are used for fishing and ecotourism.
