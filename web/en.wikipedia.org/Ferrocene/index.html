Ferrocene

The sandwich structure of ferrocene

Ferrocene is an organometallic compound with the formula Fe(C5H5)2. It consists of two cyclopentadienyl rings bound on opposite sides of a central iron atom — the prototypical "sandwich compound" and the first metallocene. It is an orange solid with a camphor-like odour that sublimes above 100 °C.

Discovery and structure

Ferrocene was discovered serendipitously in 1951 by Kealy and Pauson at Duquesne University. Its unusual structure — proposed independently by Wilkinson, Woodward and Fischer — revolutionised organometallic chemistry and helped earn Ernst Otto Fischer and Geoffrey Wilkinson the 1973 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Reactions and applications

The cyclopentadienyl rings undergo aromatic-like reactions such as Friedel–Crafts acylation, and ferrocene is readily oxidised to the blue ferrocenium cation, Fe(C5H5)2+. The ferrocene/ferrocenium couple serves as a standard reference in electrochemistry, and ferrocene derivatives are used as catalysts, fuel additives and in medicinal chemistry.
