Outline of physics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The following outline is provided as an overview of and topical guide to physics, the natural science that studies matter, its fundamental constituents, its motion and behaviour through space and time, and the related entities of energy and force. Physics is one of the most fundamental scientific disciplines, and its main goal is to understand how the universe behaves.

Nature of physics

Physics can be described as a branch of science, a natural science, a physical science, and an exact science. It is experimental in method: hypotheses are tested against observation and experiment, and its results are expressed mathematically. Physics overlaps with astronomy, chemistry, geology, and biology at its boundaries, giving rise to fields such as astrophysics, physical chemistry, geophysics, and biophysics.

Branches of physics

  • Classical mechanics — the motion of bodies under the influence of forces, following Newton.
  • Electromagnetism — electric and magnetic fields and their interactions with matter, unified by Maxwell's equations.
  • Thermodynamics and statistical mechanics — heat, work, entropy, and the statistical behaviour of large systems.
  • Optics — the behaviour and properties of light.
  • Quantum mechanics — the physics of atoms and subatomic particles, where quantities are quantised and outcomes probabilistic.
  • Relativity — special and general relativity, describing space, time, and gravitation.
  • Condensed matter physics — the physical properties of condensed phases of matter.
  • Particle physics — the fundamental particles and forces, organised by the Standard Model.
  • Nuclear physics — the constituents and interactions of atomic nuclei.
  • Astrophysics and cosmology — the physics of stars, galaxies, and the universe as a whole.
  • Atomic, molecular, and optical physics — atoms and molecules and their interaction with light.
  • Fluid dynamics, acoustics, plasma physics, and geophysics — among other applied and interdisciplinary branches.

History of physics

Physics emerged from natural philosophy. Major milestones include the work of Galileo Galilei on motion, Isaac Newton's Principia Mathematica (1687) unifying terrestrial and celestial mechanics, the nineteenth-century unification of electricity and magnetism by James Clerk Maxwell, the thermodynamic theories of Carnot, Clausius, and Kelvin, and the twentieth-century revolutions of quantum theory (Planck, Einstein, Bohr, Heisenberg, Schrödinger) and relativity (Einstein, 1905 and 1915). Later landmarks include the development of quantum electrodynamics, the Standard Model of particle physics, and the discovery of the cosmic microwave background.

General concepts

Core concepts of physics include energy, mass, force, momentum, angular momentum, charge, temperature, entropy, fields, waves, and symmetry. Its fundamental quantities are expressed in the International System of Units (SI). Physical laws are often expressed as conservation laws — of energy, momentum, angular momentum, and charge — which are connected to symmetries by Noether's theorem.
