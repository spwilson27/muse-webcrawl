SEAL Team Six

The Naval Special Warfare Development Group (NSWDG), abbreviated as DEVGRU ("Development Group") and commonly known as SEAL Team Six, is the United States Navy component of the Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC). It is an elite special mission unit specialised in counter-terrorism, hostage rescue, special reconnaissance and direct action. The unit is based at Naval Air Station Oceana, Virginia Beach, Virginia.

DEVGRU operators are drawn from the existing SEAL teams.

History

SEAL Team Six was founded in November 1980 by Commander Richard Marcinko in the aftermath of the failed Operation Eagle Claw hostage rescue in Iran. Marcinko hand-picked the first members from the SEAL and Underwater Demolition Team communities. The name "Six" was chosen to confuse Soviet intelligence about the number of SEAL teams — only two existed at the time. In 1987 the unit was disbanded and re-formed as the Naval Special Warfare Development Group. In May 2011, DEVGRU's Red Squadron carried out Operation Neptune Spear, the raid in Abbottabad, Pakistan, that killed Osama bin Laden. The unit also took part in the 2009 rescue of Captain Richard Phillips from Somali pirates and the 2012 rescue of aid worker Jessica Buchanan.

Organisation

DEVGRU is organised into colour-coded assault squadrons (Red, Blue, Gold and Silver), a reconnaissance squadron (Black), and other elements. Selection is open only to experienced male SEALs, who then undergo an extended assessment and training course; attrition is high. The unit is administratively part of Naval Special Warfare Command but operationally controlled by JSOC alongside the Army's Delta Force.
