The NSU Prinz was a small family car produced by the West German manufacturer NSU Motorenwerke between 1958 and 1973. It was NSU's first post-war car and helped establish the company's reputation for lightweight, technically interesting automobiles before NSU was absorbed into the Volkswagen Group's Audi division.

Generations

The original Prinz I, II and III (1958–1962) were small two-door saloons powered by an air-cooled two-cylinder engine of about 600 cc mounted in the rear. The Prinz 4 (1961–1973) adopted styling reminiscent of the Chevrolet Corvair and became the best-known version. Larger derivatives, the Prinz 1000 and the sporting TT and TTS models, used four-cylinder engines and enjoyed success in hillclimb and touring car racing.

Production abroad

The Prinz was built under licence in several countries, including Egypt (as the Ramses), Yugoslavia, and Argentina. In Uruguay the design continued under the name Nordex. Surviving cars are popular with classic car enthusiasts for their simplicity, low weight, and brisk performance in tuned form.
