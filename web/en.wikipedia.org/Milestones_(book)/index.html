Milestones (book)

Milestones (Arabic: Ma'alim fi al-Tariq)
Author: Sayyid Qutb
Published: 1964 (Arabic)
Subject: Islamist political thought

Milestones (Arabic: معالم في الطريق, Ma'alim fi al-Tariq, also rendered Signposts on the Road) is a short book published in 1964 by the Egyptian Islamist thinker Sayyid Qutb. Written largely while Qutb was imprisoned under Gamal Abdel Nasser, it became one of the most influential manifestos of modern Islamism.

Contents

In the book Qutb argues that the modern world — including nominally Muslim societies — has lapsed into jahiliyyah, the ignorance and barbarism he associated with pre-Islamic Arabia, because human sovereignty has displaced God's sovereignty (hakimiyyah). He calls for a vanguard (tali'a) of true believers to separate themselves spiritually from this order and work, including through jihad, to establish a society governed by sharia.

Impact

Egyptian authorities treated the book as a call to overthrow the state; a revised edition's publication helped precipitate Qutb's arrest in 1965, and he was executed in August 1966. Milestones has been widely read and debated ever since, influencing movements ranging from the Muslim Brotherhood, from which Qutb came, to later militant groups, and it remains a central text in scholarship on Islamist ideology.
