The Rocky Mountaineer is a Canadian rail-tour company based in Vancouver, British Columbia, that operates luxury sightseeing trains on routes in Western Canada and, since 2021, in the American Southwest. Founded in 1990 by the Armstrong Group, it is the largest privately owned luxury tourist train service in North America. Trains travel only during daylight hours so that passengers can view the scenery, with overnight hotel stops en route rather than sleeper cars.

History

The Rocky Mountaineer began in 1988 as a VIA Rail service following the route of the historic Canadian Pacific Railway through the Rocky Mountains. When VIA's transcontinental services were cut in 1990, the route and name were sold to the private Armstrong Group, which relaunched the train as a luxury daylight sightseeing service between Vancouver and the mountain towns of Banff and Jasper.

Routes

  • First Passage to the West — Vancouver to Banff or Lake Louise via Kamloops, following the Canadian Pacific route through the Fraser Canyon and Rogers Pass, including the Spiral Tunnels at Kicking Horse Pass.
  • Journey through the Clouds — Vancouver to Jasper via Kamloops, following the Canadian National route past Mount Robson, the highest peak in the Canadian Rockies.
  • Rainforest to Gold Rush — Vancouver to Jasper via Whistler and Quesnel, through the Cariboo gold-rush country of British Columbia.
  • Rockies to the Red Rocks — launched in 2021 between Denver, Colorado, and Moab, Utah, via Glenwood Springs, the company's first route in the United States.

Service

Trains feature bi-level glass-dome coaches in GoldLeaf service, with an open-air vestibule and a dedicated dining level, and single-level coaches in SilverLeaf service. Onboard hosts provide commentary on the scenery, history, and wildlife along the route. The company has been repeatedly named among the world's top train journeys by travel publications and has won several World Travel Awards.
