Low-dimensional topology

Low-dimensional topology is the branch of topology concerned with manifolds of dimension four or below, and especially with the rich, exceptional phenomena that occur in dimensions 2, 3 and 4.

Topics

In dimension 2 the subject is the classification of surfaces. Dimension 3 includes knot theory and hyperbolic geometry; Thurston's geometrisation conjecture, proved by Grigori Perelman in 2003 (thereby also proving the Poincaré conjecture), classifies closed 3-manifolds. Dimension 4 is famously wild: Euclidean space R⁴ admits exotic smooth structures, discovered through work of Simon Donaldson and Michael Freedman, unlike Rⁿ for any other n.

Methods

The field blends geometry, group theory and analysis, using tools such as Heegaard Floer homology, Seiberg–Witten invariants and contact geometry.
