Momofuku Ando

Born: 5 March 1910, Puzi, Taiwan (as Wu Baifu)

Died: 5 January 2007, Ikeda, Osaka, Japan

Known for: Inventing instant noodles and Cup Noodles; founding Nissin Foods

Momofuku Ando (Japanese: 安藤 百福, 5 March 1910 – 5 January 2007) was a Japanese inventor and businessman. Born in Taiwan as Wu Baifu, he became a naturalised Japanese citizen and founded Nissin Food Products Co., Ltd. He is widely credited with inventing instant noodles (instant ramen) in 1958 and the Cup Noodles brand in 1971, and is often called the "Noodle King" or the father of instant ramen.

Invention of instant noodles

After the Second World War, Japan suffered severe food shortages, and Ando observed long queues of people waiting for bowls of ramen in the cold. In 1958, working in a small shed behind his house in Ikeda, Osaka, he perfected a method he called "flash-frying": fresh noodles were briefly fried in oil, which dehydrated them and created tiny pores that let them reconstitute quickly in hot water. The first product, Chikin Ramen, went on sale in 1958 at 35 yen a pack and became an enormous success.

Cup Noodles and later work

On a 1966 trip to the United States, Ando saw supermarket staff breaking his noodles into a cup, adding hot water and eating with a fork. This inspired Cup Noodles (1971), sold in a waterproof foam cup with dried vegetables — one of the most successful convenience foods in history. In 2005, at age 95, he developed Space Ram, vacuum-sealed noodles eaten aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery by astronaut Soichi Noguchi. Ando remained active in Nissin until his death in 2007, aged 96. The CupNoodles Museum in Ikeda commemorates his life and inventions, and in polls in Japan instant noodles have been voted the country's greatest invention of the twentieth century.
