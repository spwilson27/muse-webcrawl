Public rights of way in Scotland

The public rights of way in Scotland are routes over land on which the public has a legally protected right to pass and repass. Scots law recognises two main categories: rights of way established under Scots common law, and access rights granted by the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003, which gave the public a statutory right of responsible access over most land and inland water.

Common law rights of way

A right of way may come into existence in Scots law where a route runs from one public place to another, has been used by the public openly and peaceably for a continuous period of at least 20 years, and follows a defined route. Once established, the right is protected against obstruction and can be vindicated in the courts.

Access rights

Part 1 of the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003, in force from February 2005, created a general right of access for recreation, education and passage, exercisable responsibly. The Scottish Outdoor Access Code sets out the responsibilities of both access takers and land managers. Core paths, identified by local authorities in core paths plans, form a network of priority routes.

Records

The National Catalogue of Rights of Way (CROW), maintained with ScotWays (the Scottish Rights of Way and Access Society, founded in 1845), records asserted and vindicated routes.
