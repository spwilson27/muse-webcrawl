History of radio

The history of radio traces the development of technology that uses radio waves to transmit information, from the theoretical prediction of electromagnetic waves by James Clerk Maxwell in the 1860s and their experimental demonstration by Heinrich Hertz in the 1880s, to the wireless telegraphy systems of the 1890s and the broadcast medium that transformed 20th-century culture.

Wireless telegraphy

In the 1890s, inventors including Guglielmo Marconi, Nikola Tesla, Oliver Lodge, and Alexander Popov developed apparatus for signalling in Morse code without wires. Marconi's company commercialized the technology for shipping and in 1901 transmitted a signal across the Atlantic Ocean. Radio became essential for maritime safety, notably during the sinking of the Titanic in 1912.

Broadcasting

Reginald Fessenden made early voice and music transmissions in 1906. Regular broadcasting began around 1920, with stations such as KDKA in Pittsburgh. The 1920s and 1930s — radio's "golden age" — brought news, drama, comedy, and music into homes worldwide and made radio a powerful political tool. Edwin Armstrong's invention of FM broadcasting improved audio quality.

Later developments

The transistor radio (1954) made the medium portable; FM stereo, satellite radio, and digital audio broadcasting followed. In the 21st century, radio coexists with streaming and podcasting, while the underlying radio technology remains fundamental to mobile telephony, Wi-Fi, and radar.
