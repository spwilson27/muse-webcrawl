Institute for Advanced Study

The Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) is an independent center for theoretical research and intellectual inquiry located in Princeton, New Jersey. Founded in 1930 by Abraham Flexner with a founding gift from Louis Bamberger and Caroline Bamberger Fuld, it is one of the world's leading institutions for basic research in the sciences and humanities.

Organization

The Institute has no students and confers no degrees. Its small permanent faculty is complemented each year by visiting Members selected from around the world. Research is organized into four schools: Historical Studies, Mathematics, Natural Sciences, and Social Science. IAS is not part of Princeton University, though the two institutions collaborate closely and IAS was housed in Princeton's Fine Hall in its early years.

Notable scholars

Albert Einstein joined the Institute in 1933 and remained until his death in 1955. Other famous faculty and visitors have included Kurt Gödel, John von Neumann, J. Robert Oppenheimer (director from 1947 to 1966), Hermann Weyl, Freeman Dyson, and Clifford Geertz. The Institute's founding mission was to give scholars complete freedom to pursue curiosity-driven research free of teaching and administrative duties.
