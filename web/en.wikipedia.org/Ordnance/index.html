Ordnance

Ordnance most commonly refers to military supplies, especially weapons and ammunition, and to the branch of an armed force responsible for procuring, storing, maintaining, and issuing them. The word comes from a shortened form of "ordinance" — the arrangements made for battle. In modern usage it covers artillery pieces, small arms, bombs, missiles, and their ammunition collectively.


Artillery ordnance

Military organizations

Armies have long maintained specialist ordnance corps: the British Board of Ordnance (1415–1855) supplied arms to the army and navy and produced the maps that gave the Ordnance Survey its name; the United States Army Ordnance Corps, founded in 1812, remains responsible for weapons development, ammunition supply, and explosive ordnance disposal. Unexploded ordnance (UXO) — bombs, shells, and mines that failed to detonate — remains a hazard on former battlefields and bombing ranges worldwide.

Other uses

In British place and institutional names, "ordnance" survives in the Ordnance Survey, the national mapping agency founded in 1791 to map the country for military purposes. In some contexts the term distinguishes the materiel itself (guns as ordnance) from the personnel who serve it (artillery).
