Political integration of India

The political integration of India was the process, lasting from 1947 to about 1956, by which the princely states of British India were persuaded or compelled to accede to the newly independent Dominion of India (and its successor Republic). At independence in August 1947, the subcontinent comprised British-administered provinces and more than 560 princely states under British paramountcy, each free in principle to accede to India or Pakistan or to remain independent.

Accession process

The effort was led by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, India's first Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister, and his secretary V. P. Menon, earning Patel the epithet "Iron Man of India". Rulers were offered Instruments of Accession ceding control of defence, foreign affairs and communications while retaining internal autonomy, along with privy purses and privileges. By 15 August 1947 nearly all states within India's territory had acceded.

Difficult cases

Three states resisted. Junagadh, whose Muslim nawab acceded to Pakistan despite a Hindu majority, was taken over after a plebiscite in February 1948 confirmed accession to India. Hyderabad, the largest princely state, was annexed in September 1948 in the military "Operation Polo". Jammu and Kashmir's Maharaja Hari Singh acceded to India in October 1947 amid invasion by tribal forces from Pakistan, triggering the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–48 and the continuing Kashmir conflict.

Consolidation

After accession, the states were merged into provinces, grouped into unions such as Rajasthan and Madhya Bharat, or administered as centrally controlled territories, a process largely completed by the States Reorganisation Act of 1956, which redrew India's internal map primarily along linguistic lines. Privy purses were abolished in 1971.
