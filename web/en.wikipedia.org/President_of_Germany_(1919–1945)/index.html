President of Germany (1919–1945)

Reichspräsident
Office created: 1919 (Weimar Constitution)
Holders: Friedrich Ebert (1919–1925); Paul von Hindenburg (1925–1934); Adolf Hitler as Führer und Reichskanzler (1934–1945); Karl Dönitz (1945)
Abolished: 1945

Friedrich Ebert, first Reich President, 1919–1925.

The President of Germany (Reichspräsident) was the head of state of the German Reich during the Weimar Republic (1919–1933) and, in altered form, under the Nazi regime until 1945. Under the Weimar Constitution the president was directly elected by the people for a seven-year term, appointed and dismissed the chancellor and government, commanded the armed forces, and could dissolve the Reichstag.

Powers and holders

The office's most consequential feature was Article 48, which allowed the president to rule by emergency decree and suspend civil liberties — a provision used extensively by Friedrich Ebert, and later by Paul von Hindenburg, whose presidential cabinets after 1930 bypassed parliament and paved the way for his appointment of Adolf Hitler as chancellor on 30 January 1933. The Reichstag Fire Decree of February 1933, issued under Article 48, dismantled constitutional protections. On Hindenburg's death on 2 August 1934, Hitler merged the presidency with the chancellorship under the title Führer und Reichskanzler, confirmed by plebiscite. In his political testament Hitler named Admiral Karl Dönitz as Reich President; Dönitz headed the brief Flensburg government in May 1945 before its arrest by the Allies, after which the office lapsed.
