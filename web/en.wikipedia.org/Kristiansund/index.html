Kristiansund


Kristiansund is a coastal town and municipality in Møre og Romsdal county, Norway.

Kristiansund (historically spelled Christiansund) is a town and municipality on the western coast of Norway, in the Nordmøre district of Møre og Romsdal county. The municipality covers about 87 square kilometres spread over several islands — principally Nordlandet, Kirkelandet, Innlandet and Gomalandet — and has a population of roughly 24,000.

History and economy

The town grew from a fishing and trading post and received town status in 1742, named after King Christian VI of Denmark-Norway. Kristiansund became famous for the production of clipfish (dried and salted cod, klippfisk), exported especially to Spain, Portugal and Latin America, and the town still celebrates this heritage. In April 1940 the town was heavily bombed by the Luftwaffe and much of its centre was rebuilt in distinctive post-war architecture.

Since the 1970s the town has served as a supply base for offshore oil and gas activity in the Norwegian Sea. The town is linked to the mainland and neighbouring islands by bridges and tunnels, including the Atlantic Ocean Road (Atlanterhavsveien) to the south, and is a port of call for the Hurtigruten coastal ferry.
