Bacteriology

Bacteriology is the branch and specialty of biology that studies the morphology, ecology, genetics and biochemistry of bacteria, as well as their identification, classification and role in disease. It is a subfield of microbiology.

History

The field emerged in the nineteenth century with the germ theory of disease. Ferdinand Cohn systematised bacterial classification, Louis Pasteur demonstrated microbial fermentation and developed pasteurisation, and Robert Koch devised pure-culture techniques and his famous postulates, identifying the agents of anthrax, tuberculosis and cholera.

Methods

Bacteriologists culture organisms on media such as agar plates, identify them by staining (notably the Gram stain), biochemical tests and increasingly by DNA sequencing, and test their sensitivity to antibiotics to guide treatment.

Applications

Clinical bacteriology underpins the diagnosis of infectious disease, while industrial and environmental bacteriology exploit bacteria in food production, biotechnology, waste treatment and the nitrogen cycle. See also Robert Koch and Antibiotics.
