Outsourcing

Outsourcing is a business practice in which a company hires a third party to perform services or produce goods that were traditionally performed in-house by the company's own employees. When the third party is located in another country, the practice is often called offshoring; when nearby, nearshoring.

Reasons and scope

Companies outsource to reduce costs, access specialized expertise, increase capacity, and focus internal resources on core competencies. Commonly outsourced functions include information technology services, customer support and call centres, human resources, accounting, and manufacturing. Business process outsourcing (BPO) became a major industry in countries such as India and the Philippines, where skilled, English-speaking workforces offer lower labor costs.

Criticism and risks

Outsourcing is criticized for domestic job losses, reduced control over quality, data security risks, and poor working conditions in supplier operations. Failed contracts, hidden coordination costs, and geopolitical disruption have led some firms to bring functions back in-house ("reshoring" or "insourcing"). Governments also outsource public services, raising debates about accountability and the role of the state.
