Administrative Procedure Act

The Administrative Procedure Act (APA), enacted in the United States in 1946 and codified at 5 U.S.C. sections 500 and following, governs the way federal administrative agencies propose and establish regulations and provides for judicial review of agency action.

Rulemaking

The APA generally requires agencies to publish notice of proposed rulemaking in the Federal Register, allow public comment, and publish a final rule with a statement of basis and purpose, usually at least 30 days before it takes effect.

Adjudication

For formal adjudication the Act provides for hearings before administrative law judges, with rights to present evidence and cross-examine witnesses.

Judicial review

Courts may set aside agency action found arbitrary, capricious, contrary to law or unsupported by substantial evidence. See also United States administrative law and Federal Register.
