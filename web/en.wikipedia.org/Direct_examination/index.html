Direct examination

Direct examination is the questioning of a witness by the lawyer or party who called the witness, in a trial or other legal proceeding. It is followed by cross-examination from the opposing side, and may be followed by redirect examination.

Rules

On direct examination, leading questions — those that suggest the answer — are generally prohibited, except for preliminary or uncontested matters or when questioning a hostile witness. The examiner aims to elicit the witness's account in their own words to establish facts supporting the calling party's case.

Sequence

After direct examination, the opposing counsel conducts cross-examination, which is limited in scope in some jurisdictions to matters raised on direct plus the witness's credibility. The calling party may then conduct redirect examination to clarify points, after which recross may be allowed.
