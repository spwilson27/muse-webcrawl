Wrightbus is a bus manufacturer based in Ballymena, Northern Ireland. Founded in 1946 by Robert Wright as a coachbuilding business, it became one of the United Kingdom's leading bus builders, supplying vehicles to operators across Britain, Ireland, and export markets.

History

The company began by building truck and van bodies before turning to bus bodywork. In the 1990s it developed complete integral buses such as the Wright Cadet and the Gemini double-deck body, and in 2012 it launched the New Routemaster for London — a hybrid double-decker with a hop-on, hop-off rear platform, designed with Thomas Heatherwick at the request of Mayor Boris Johnson.

In September 2019 Wrightbus entered administration with the loss of most of its workforce. It was rescued the following month by Jo Bamford, son of JCB chairman Lord Bamford, whose company Ryse Hydrogen acquired the business. Under Bamford ownership the company refocused on zero-emission vehicles.

Products

Current production centres on the StreetDeck and StreetLite ranges in diesel, battery-electric, and hydrogen fuel-cell form. Wrightbus built the world's first hydrogen double-decker bus (StreetDeck Hydroliner), deployed in cities including London, Aberdeen, and Dublin, alongside the single-deck GB Kite Electroliner and Hydroliner. The company also supplies hydrogen production and refuelling ventures through affiliated Bamford companies.
