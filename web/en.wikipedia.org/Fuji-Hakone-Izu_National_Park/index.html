Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park

National park in the Kantō region of Honshu, Japan

LocationYamanashi, Shizuoka, Kanagawa and Tokyo prefectures, Japan
AreaAbout 1,227 km²
Established2 February 1936

Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park (富士箱根伊豆国立公園) is a national park in Japan spanning parts of Yamanashi, Shizuoka, Kanagawa and Tokyo prefectures. Established in 1936 as Fuji-Hakone National Park, it is Japan's most visited national park, drawing tens of millions of visitors a year thanks to its proximity to Tokyo. It is not a single contiguous reserve but a collection of dispersed sites grouped into four areas.

Areas

The Mount Fuji area includes the mountain itself (3,776 m, a UNESCO World Heritage cultural site since 2013), the Fuji Five Lakes, Aokigahara forest and the Shiraito Falls. The Hakone area is a famous hot-spring (onsen) resort town within an ancient volcanic caldera, with Lake Ashi, the Ōwakudani volcanic valley and views of Fuji. The Izu Peninsula area offers rugged coasts, the Jogasaki shore and resort towns such as Atami and Shimoda. The Izu Islands, administered by Tokyo, extend the park far into the Pacific to volcanic islands including Izu Ōshima and Hachijō-jima.
