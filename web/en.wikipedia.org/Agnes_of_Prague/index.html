Agnes of Prague

Saint Agnes of Prague
Born: 20 January 1211, Prague, Bohemia
Died: 2 March 1282, Prague
Venerated in: Roman Catholic Church
Feast: 2 March

Agnes of Prague, also known as Agnes of Bohemia (Czech: Svatá Anežka Česká; 20 January 1211 – 2 March 1282), was a medieval Bohemian princess who opted for a life of charity, mortification of the flesh and piety over a life of luxury and comfort. Although she was venerated soon after her death, she was not beatified or canonised for over 700 years.

Life

Agnes was the daughter of King Ottokar I of Bohemia and Constance of Hungary. Betrothed several times for dynastic reasons — including to the future Emperor Frederick II — she instead persuaded Pope Gregory IX to release her from marriage plans so that she could enter religious life.

In Prague she founded the hospital of St. Francis and the Order of the Knights of the Cross with the Red Star to run it, and she established a convent of Poor Clares, entering it herself in 1234. She corresponded with Saint Clare of Assisi, whose four letters to Agnes survive.

Veneration

Agnes was beatified in 1874 and canonised by Pope John Paul II on 12 November 1989, only days before the Velvet Revolution in Czechoslovakia — a coincidence many Czechs took as an omen. Her Prague convent is now part of the National Gallery.
