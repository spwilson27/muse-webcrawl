Modes of convergence

In mathematics, the various modes of convergence describe different senses in which a sequence or series of functions may converge to a limit. The principal modes include pointwise convergence, uniform convergence, almost everywhere convergence, and convergence in measure, as well as convergence in Lp spaces.

Relationships

Uniform convergence implies pointwise convergence, and on a finite measure space, convergence in Lp implies convergence in measure, which in turn implies that a subsequence converges almost everywhere. Egorov's theorem states that on a finite measure space, almost everywhere convergence implies almost uniform convergence. Results such as the dominated convergence theorem give conditions for interchanging limits and integrals under these modes.
