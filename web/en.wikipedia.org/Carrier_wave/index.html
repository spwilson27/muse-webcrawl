Carrier wave

In telecommunications, a carrier wave, carrier signal, or just carrier, is a waveform (usually sinusoidal) that is modulated (modified) with an information-bearing signal in order to convey that information. The carrier is usually a much higher frequency than the modulating baseband signal.

Purpose

Modulating a carrier shifts information to frequencies where it can be transmitted efficiently: antennas must be comparable in size to the wavelength of the signal, and frequency-division multiplexing lets many modulated carriers share one channel (a cable, or the radio spectrum) without interference. At the receiver, a tuner selects the carrier frequency and a demodulator recovers the original signal.

Modulation schemes

The information may be encoded by varying the carrier's amplitude (amplitude modulation, AM), its frequency (FM), its phase (PM), or combinations in digital schemes such as quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) and orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) used in Wi-Fi and mobile networks.

Terminology

A carrier with no intelligence transmitted is sometimes said to be "dead air" in broadcast terms. In suppressed-carrier systems, the carrier itself is not transmitted, saving power, and is regenerated at the receiver.
