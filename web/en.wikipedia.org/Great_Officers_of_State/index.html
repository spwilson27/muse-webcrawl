Great Officers of State

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Officers of State are senior officials of the Crown in the United Kingdom (historically of England, and separately of Scotland) who rank below the royal family and above most others in the order of precedence. Several of the offices are now purely ceremonial or held in commission, but they preserve functions and pageantry reaching back to the medieval court.

The offices (England and the United Kingdom)

The nine Great Officers traditionally listed are: the Lord High Steward, an office now filled only for coronations; the Lord High Chancellor, head of the judiciary historically and today a senior cabinet minister; the Lord High Treasurer, whose functions are exercised by the Lords Commissioners of the Treasury, headed by the Prime Minister as First Lord; the Lord President of the Council; the Lord Privy Seal; the Lord Great Chamberlain, a hereditary office with duties at Parliament and coronations; the Lord High Constable, also now appointed only for coronations; the Earl Marshal, hereditary in the Dukes of Norfolk, responsible for state ceremonial and heraldry; and the Lord High Admiral, whose office has usually been in commission or held by the sovereign.

Scotland

Scotland maintained its own Great Officers of State, including the Chancellor, Treasurer, Privy Seal, Secretary, and the hereditary Lord High Constable of Scotland (the Earl of Erroll) and Earl Marischal. After the Union of 1707 some offices lapsed or were held for life by individuals, while others retained ceremonial roles at Scottish state occasions.
