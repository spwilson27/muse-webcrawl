A kremlin (Russian: кремль, kremlʹ) is a major fortified central complex found in historic Russian cities. The word is often used metonymically to refer to the Government of Russia, in the same way that "the White House" refers to the executive branch of the United States. The best-known kremlin is the Moscow Kremlin, a walled citadel overlooking the Moskva River and Red Square, which serves as the official residence of the President of the Russian Federation.

The Moscow Kremlin

The Moscow Kremlin occupies a triangular site of about 27 hectares on Borovitsky Hill. Its present red-brick walls and twenty towers were built between 1482 and 1495 under Ivan III, with the help of Italian master builders including Pietro Antonio Solari and Marco Ruffo. Within the walls stand the cathedrals of Cathedral Square — the Dormition (Uspensky), Annunciation (Blagoveschensky) and Archangel (Arkhangelsky) cathedrals — the Ivan the Great Bell Tower, the Grand Kremlin Palace, the Armoury Chamber with its museum of state treasures, and the Senate building with its presidential offices.

The site has been the seat of Russian power, with interruptions, for centuries: capital of the Grand Duchy of Moscow, fortress of the tsars, and, from 1918, seat of the Soviet government — a role it resumed for the Russian Federation after 1991. The Tsar Cannon and Tsar Bell, among the largest of their kind ever cast, are displayed on its grounds.

Other kremlins

Many Russian cities retain their kremlins, including Kazan (a World Heritage Site, notable for its white walls and the Qolşärif Mosque), Novgorod, Suzdal, Pskov, Astrakhan, Tobolsk and Nizhny Novgorod. Some, such as the wooden fortresses of the Russian North, survive only in part.

The word as metonym

During the Cold War, "the Kremlin" became standard shorthand in Western journalism for the Soviet government, giving rise to "Kremlinology" — the study of Soviet politics through indirect evidence. The term remains in common use for the Russian presidency and government.
