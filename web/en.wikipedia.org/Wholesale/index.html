Wholesale

Wholesale is the sale of goods or merchandise to retailers, industrial or business users, or other wholesalers, rather than to the general public. Wholesalers buy products in large quantities from producers or importers, typically at a discount, and resell them in smaller lots; it is therefore an intermediate step in the distribution chain between production and retail.

Functions

Wholesalers perform functions that neither producers nor retailers can perform as efficiently alone: breaking bulk into quantities retailers can handle, holding inventory and bearing risk, transporting goods, financing trade through credit, and providing market information. Types of wholesalers include merchant wholesalers (who take title to the goods), agents and brokers (who do not), full-service and limited-service wholesalers, and cash-and-carry outlets used by small retailers and tradespeople.

Wholesale in the economy

Wholesale trade is a large sector in developed economies, often comparable in turnover to retail because goods may pass through several hands. In taxation, wholesale transactions are often exempt from sales tax because the tax is collected at the final retail sale; conversely, value-added tax systems charge at each stage with input credits. The rise of e-commerce and direct-to-consumer models has compressed some wholesale channels ("disintermediation") while creating new online wholesale marketplaces.
