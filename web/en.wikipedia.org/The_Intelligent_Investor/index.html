The Intelligent Investor is a 1949 book on value investing by Benjamin Graham, the Columbia Business School professor widely regarded as the father of security analysis. Warren Buffett has famously called it "by far the best book on investing ever written".

Contents

Graham distinguishes investment from speculation and lays out his philosophy for two types of investors: the "defensive" investor, who seeks safety and freedom from effort, and the "enterprising" investor, willing to devote time to analysis. Key concepts include the allegory of "Mr. Market", the manic-depressive business partner whose quotations should be exploited rather than followed, and the "margin of safety" obtained by buying securities well below their intrinsic value.

Editions and influence

Graham revised the book through several editions, the last in 1973. A revised edition with commentary by journalist Jason Zweig was published in 2003. The book remains a foundational text of value investing and a staple of investment reading lists.
