Saint Petersburg Metro

The Saint Petersburg Metro (Russian: Петербур́гский метрополите́н) is a rapid transit system in Saint Petersburg, Russia. Construction began in earnest after the Second World War, and the first line opened on 15 November 1955 as the second metro system in the Soviet Union after Moscow. It is one of the deepest metro systems in the world, built through the difficult, waterlogged geology beneath the Neva delta, and is noted for the ornate decoration of its early stations.

History

Plans for an underground railway in the imperial capital dated to the nineteenth century but were repeatedly shelved. The Soviet government approved construction in 1941; work was halted by the German invasion and the Siege of Leningrad, then resumed after the war. The first section of Line 1, from Avtovo to Ploshchad Vosstaniya, opened in 1955, its stations decorated in the grand Stalinist style. Later lines crossed beneath the Neva and its branches, aided by pioneering use of ground-freezing and deep-bored tunnels.

Depth and construction

Because of the city's geology and the need to pass beneath its rivers, many stations lie at depths of 60–86 metres. Admiralteyskaya station, at about 86 metres below ground, is among the deepest metro stations in the world. In 1995 a catastrophic flood breached the tunnel between Lesnaya and Ploshchad Muzhestva on Line 1, closing that section for nearly a decade until a bypass tunnel opened in 2004.

Architecture

The first stations — such as Avtovo, with its glass-clad columns, and Pushkinskaya — are famed for their palatial interiors, marble, chandeliers, and sculptural decoration. Later Soviet stations adopted more functional designs, while post-Soviet stations have revived decorative motifs.

Incidents

On 3 April 2017 a suicide bombing on a train between Sennaya Ploshchad and Tekhnologichesky Institut stations killed 15 people and injured many more.
