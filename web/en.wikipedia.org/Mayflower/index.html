Mayflower

Mayflower
Type: Dutch cargo fluyt
Launched: c. 1609, Harwich
Tonnage: about 180 tons
Length: about 100–110 ft
Fate: Probably broken up, 1624

The Mayflower was an English ship that transported the Pilgrims from England to the New World in 1620. After a gruelling 66-day Atlantic crossing, the ship anchored at Cape Cod, and its passengers founded Plymouth Colony in present-day Massachusetts. The Mayflower and its voyage became an enduring symbol in American history and mythology.

The voyage

The ship carried 102 passengers — Separatist "Saints" from Leiden and London-recruited "Strangers" — plus a crew of about 30 under Captain Christopher Jones. A first attempt with the leaky consort ship Speedwell failed, and the Mayflower finally sailed alone from Plymouth, England, on 6 September 1620 (Old Style). Storms drove her far north of the intended destination in Virginia; before going ashore, the male passengers signed the Mayflower Compact, agreeing to form a civil body politic.

Plymouth Colony

The settlers landed at Plymouth in December 1620. During the first winter, roughly half the passengers and crew died of disease and exposure. With help from the Wampanoag, including Squanto, the survivors established a viable colony. The Mayflower returned to England in April 1621, arriving in May.

Fate and legacy

Captain Jones died in 1622 and the ship, by then in poor condition, was probably broken up at Rotherhithe around 1624. A full-scale replica, Mayflower II, built in Devon and sailed across the Atlantic in 1957, is preserved at Plymouth, Massachusetts. The ship's timbers are legendarily — though unprovenly — said to survive in a barn in Jordans, Buckinghamshire.
