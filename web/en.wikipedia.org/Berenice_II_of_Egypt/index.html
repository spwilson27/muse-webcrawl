Berenice II of Egypt

Berenice II
Reignc. 244/243 – 221 BC (as queen consort)
SpousePtolemy III Euergetes
DynastyPtolemaic

Berenice II (c. 267/266 – 221 BC) was queen of Cyrene and then queen consort of Egypt as the wife of Ptolemy III Euergetes. Daughter of Magas of Cyrene, her marriage reunited Cyrenaica with Ptolemaic Egypt. She was a powerful royal figure, depicted on coinage and honoured in cult, and mother of Ptolemy IV Philopator.

Coma Berenices

According to legend, before Ptolemy III departed for the Third Syrian War, Berenice vowed to dedicate a lock of her hair to the gods if he returned safely. The lock, placed in a temple, was said to have vanished and been placed among the stars as the constellation Coma Berenices ("Berenice's Hair"), a story celebrated in a poem by Callimachus, known through Catullus.

Death

Berenice was murdered in 221 BC, shortly after the accession of her son Ptolemy IV, in the court intrigues engineered by the minister Sosibius.
