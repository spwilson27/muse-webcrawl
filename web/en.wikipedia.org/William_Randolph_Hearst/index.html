William Randolph Hearst

William Randolph Hearst Sr. (April 29, 1863 – August 14, 1951) was an American businessman, newspaper publisher, and politician who developed the nation's largest newspaper chain and media company, Hearst Communications. His flamboyant methods of yellow journalism — sensational headlines, crusades, and human-interest stories — influenced American popular media and politics.

The empire

The son of mining magnate and Senator George Hearst, he took over the San Francisco Examiner in 1887 and in 1895 bought the failing New York Morning Journal, hiring stars such as Richard F. Outcault of the "Yellow Kid" comic strip and battling Joseph Pulitzer's World in a circulation war that helped stir public opinion before the Spanish-American War of 1898. At its peak in the 1920s and 1930s his chain included some 28 papers, magazines such as Cosmopolitan and Harper's Bazaar, and newsreel and film interests.

Politics and legacy

Hearst served two terms in Congress (1903–1907), ran unsuccessfully for mayor of New York and governor of New York, and wielded his papers as a political weapon for decades. From 1919 he built Hearst Castle at San Simeon, California, a lavish hilltop estate designed by Julia Morgan. His life inspired the character Charles Foster Kane in Orson Welles's Citizen Kane (1941). He died in Beverly Hills in 1951.
