Verghese Kurien

Verghese Kurien (26 November 1921 – 9 September 2012) was an Indian engineer and social entrepreneur known as the "Father of the White Revolution" in India. He founded the Amul cooperative movement and led Operation Flood, the world's largest agricultural dairy development programme, which transformed India from a milk-deficient nation into the world's largest milk producer.

Amul and the Anand pattern

Kurien arrived in Anand, Gujarat, in 1949 on a government posting and joined the Kaira District Co-operative Milk Producers' Union, chaired by Tribhuvandas Patel. There, with dairy engineer H. M. Dalaya, he helped develop the process of making skim milk powder from buffalo milk — a world first at scale. The "Anand pattern" of village-level farmer-owned cooperatives federated into state marketing bodies became the template for dairy development across India, and the Amul brand became one of India's best-known food brands.

Operation Flood

As founder-chairman of the National Dairy Development Board (1965), Kurien launched Operation Flood (1970–1996), which replicated the Anand pattern in hundreds of districts, linking rural producers to urban consumers and using proceeds from donated skim milk powder and butter oil to finance development. He also founded the Institute of Rural Management Anand (IRMA) in 1979. His autobiography, I Too Had a Dream, recounts the movement's history.
