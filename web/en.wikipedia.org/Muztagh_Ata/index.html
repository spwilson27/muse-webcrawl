Muztagh Ata

Elevation7,546 m (24,757 ft)
LocationXinjiang, China
RangePamir Mountains
First ascent1956

Muztagh Ata, sometimes Muztagata (from Uyghur/Kyrgyz roots meaning "ice mountain father"), is the second-highest peak of the Pamir range that can be seen from the Karakoram Highway, rising to about 7,546 metres in Xinjiang, China, near the border with Tajikistan and Pakistan. Its broad, gently domed summit massif towers over Lake Karakul.

Climbing

Because its standard western route is comparatively gentle, Muztagh Ata is often described as one of the easiest 7,000-metre peaks in the world, and it is a popular objective for ski mountaineering and as acclimatization for higher summits. The explorer Sven Hedin attempted the mountain repeatedly in the 1890s without success, and Eric Shipton and Bill Tilman came close in 1947. The first ascent was finally made in 1956 by a large joint Chinese–Soviet expedition via the west ridge.

Geography

The peak lies in the eastern Pamirs, within the Kongur Shan group near the even higher Kongur Tagh (7,649 m). The Karakoram Highway between Kashgar and Tashkurgan passes its flanks, making it one of the most accessible great mountains in Central Asia.
