National Electrical Code

The National Electrical Code (NEC), also known as NFPA 70, is a regionally adoptable standard for the safe installation of electrical wiring and equipment in the United States. It is published by the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) and revised on a three-year cycle.

Scope and content

The Code sets requirements for conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, boxes and enclosures, services, and special occupancies and equipment — from residential branch circuits to hospitals, hazardous locations and photovoltaic systems. The NEC itself is not federal law; it becomes enforceable when adopted by states and municipalities, sometimes with local amendments, and is applied by electrical inspectors ("authorities having jurisdiction").

History and development

The first edition appeared in 1897, consolidating competing rules from insurers, fire marshals and the electrical industry as electric lighting spread. Innovations adopted through the decades include grounding-type receptacles, ground-fault circuit interrupters (GFCIs), arc-fault circuit interrupters (AFCIs), tamper-resistant receptacles and expanded surge-protection requirements. Public proposals and comments feed a consensus code panel process for each edition.

Influence

Several other countries in the Americas base their electrical codes on the NEC, and its safety philosophy — safeguarding persons and property from electrical hazards — has shaped wiring practice worldwide.
