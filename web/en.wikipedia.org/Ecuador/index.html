Ecuador, officially the Republic of Ecuador, is a country in northwestern South America, bordered by Colombia on the north, Peru on the east and south, and the Pacific Ocean on the west. Ecuador also includes the Galápagos Islands in the Pacific, about 906 kilometres (563 mi) west of the mainland. The country's capital is Quito and its largest city is Guayaquil. The territory of modern Ecuador was once home to a variety of Indigenous cultures; it was gradually incorporated into the Inca Empire during the 15th century, then colonised by Spain in the 16th century. Ecuador gained independence in 1820 as part of Gran Colombia, from which it withdrew in 1830.

Geography

Ecuador has four distinct geographic regions: the Pacific coastal lowlands (La Costa), the Andean highlands (La Sierra), the Amazon rainforest (El Oriente), and the Galápagos Islands. The equator passes through the country, giving it its name. Ecuador is one of the world's most biodiverse countries per unit area, with the Galápagos famed for the endemic species that informed Charles Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection.

Economy

Ecuador's economy is based largely on petroleum exports, bananas (of which it is the world's largest exporter), shrimp, cut flowers, cacao, and other agricultural products. After a severe banking and currency crisis in 1999–2000, Ecuador adopted the United States dollar as its official currency, a system that remains in place. Remittances from emigrants are also a significant source of income.

Government and recent history

Ecuador is a presidential republic. Its 2008 constitution was the first in the world to recognise legally enforceable rights of nature. The country experienced political instability in the late 20th and early 21st centuries, with several presidents removed before completing their terms; more recently it has faced serious public-security challenges related to organised crime and drug trafficking.
