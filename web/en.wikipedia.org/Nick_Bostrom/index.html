Nick Bostrom (born Niklas Boström; 10 March 1973) is a Swedish philosopher at the University of Oxford, known for his work on existential risk, the anthropic principle, human enhancement ethics, whole brain emulation, superintelligence risks, and the reversal test. In 2011 he founded the Oxford Martin Programme on the Impacts of Future Technology, and he was the founding director of the Future of Humanity Institute at Oxford, which closed in 2024.

Work

Bostrom formulated the simulation argument (2003), which holds that at least one of three propositions is very likely true: civilisations almost never reach a technologically mature stage; mature civilisations almost never run many simulations of their ancestors; or we are almost certainly living in a computer simulation. His 2014 book Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies, a New York Times bestseller, argued that machine superintelligence could pose an existential risk and brought AI safety into mainstream debate.

He has written extensively on existential risks, transhumanism, and the ethics of emerging technologies, and co-founded the World Transhumanist Association (now Humanity+). Bostrom's later work includes the "vulnerable world hypothesis" and the book Deep Utopia (2024), exploring life in a world where technological problems are largely "solved".
