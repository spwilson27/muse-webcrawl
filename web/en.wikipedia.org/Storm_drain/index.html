Storm drain

A storm drain, also called a storm sewer or surface water drain, is infrastructure designed to drain excess rain and ground water from impervious surfaces such as paved streets, car parks, parking lots, footpaths, sidewalks, and roofs. Storm drains vary in design from small residential dry wells to large municipal systems, and typically discharge untreated water into rivers, streams, or the sea.

Design

Water enters through grates or curb inlets, often into a catch basin that traps sediment and debris, and flows by gravity through underground pipes to an outfall. Unlike sanitary sewers, storm sewers in a separate system carry runoff without treatment, which is why many drains are marked with warnings not to dump pollutants.

Function and issues

Storm drainage prevents flooding of streets and buildings during rainfall. Problems include blockages by leaves and litter, pollution carried from road surfaces (oil, heavy metals, fertilizer), and combined sewer overflows in older cities where storm and sanitary systems are merged.

Environmental measures

Sustainable drainage systems (SuDS) such as permeable paving, swales, retention ponds, and green roofs slow runoff and filter pollutants. See also sewerage, surface runoff, and flood.
