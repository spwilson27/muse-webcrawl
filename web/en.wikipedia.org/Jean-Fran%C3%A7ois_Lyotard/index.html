Jean-François Lyotard (1924–1998) was a French philosopher, sociologist and literary theorist best known for articulating postmodernism in The Postmodern Condition: A Report on Knowledge (1979), where he famously defined the postmodern as "incredulity toward metanarratives" — the grand legitimating stories of progress, science and emancipation.

Career

Lyotard taught philosophy in Algeria and France, was active in the revolutionary group Socialisme ou Barbarie, and later taught at the University of Paris VIII (Vincennes) and Emory University. His works range from phenomenology and Marxism to aesthetics and ethics.

Key ideas

In The Differend (1983) he explored conflicts that cannot be resolved by a common rule of judgement. He wrote influentially on the sublime in contemporary art, championing avant-garde artists such as Barnett Newman, and on "performativity" as the legitimation criterion of knowledge in computerised societies.
