Bowl game

The Rose Bowl stadium in Pasadena, California, home of the oldest bowl game.

Bowl games are postseason college football games in the United States, played primarily by NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) teams. Traditionally, bowl games were prestigious exhibition games between teams invited by the bowl's organisers, tied to specific conferences; since the advent of championship systems, some bowls have become part of the playoff structure, but most remain standalone postseason rewards for winning seasons.

History

The name derives from the Rose Bowl, first played in 1902 (as the Tournament East–West football game) and annually since 1916, whose name came from the rose-shaped design of its stadium in Pasadena, California. Other major bowls followed: the Orange Bowl, Sugar Bowl and Sun Bowl in the 1930s, the Cotton Bowl in 1937, and later the Fiesta Bowl (1971) and Peach Bowl. For most of the 20th century, polls — not the bowls themselves — determined the national champion, which the bowls decided only indirectly.

The playoff era

The Bowl Championship Series (BCS, 1998–2013) attempted to match the top two teams in a title game rotated among major bowls. It was replaced in 2014 by the College Football Playoff, initially a four-team tournament whose semifinals rotated through the "New Year's Six" bowls (Rose, Sugar, Orange, Cotton, Fiesta and Peach). The playoff expanded to twelve teams from the 2024 season, with quarterfinals and semifinals hosted by bowls. Alongside these, more than forty lesser bowls are staged each December and January, generally requiring teams to have at least six wins.

Economics and culture

Bowls generate television revenue, tourism and payouts to conferences, and are organised mostly by non-profit committees, though the system has been criticised for large executive salaries, weak matchups and the proliferation of games with mediocre teams. Bowl season remains a fixture of American holiday sports culture, with games concentrated around Christmas and New Year's Day.
