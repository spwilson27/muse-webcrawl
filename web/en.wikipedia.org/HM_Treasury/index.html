HM Treasury

His Majesty's Treasury (HM Treasury), also known simply as the Treasury, is the United Kingdom government department responsible for developing and executing the government's public finance policy and economic policy. It is headed by the Chancellor of the Exchequer and is based at 1 Horse Guards Road in Westminster, London.

Functions

The Treasury controls public spending, sets the direction of the UK's economic policy, prepares the annual Budget and Spring Statement, oversees taxation jointly with HM Revenue and Customs, manages the national debt through the Debt Management Office, and works to strengthen the competitiveness of the financial services sector. It also represents the UK at international financial institutions such as the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank.

History

The institution descends from the medieval Exchequer and the office of Treasurer, and its modern form took shape as the Treasury Board and the role of First Lord of the Treasury — now held by the Prime Minister — developed in the 18th century. The Chancellor of the Exchequer, the department's effective political head, is supported by officials led by the Permanent Secretary and by junior Treasury ministers including the Chief Secretary to the Treasury.
