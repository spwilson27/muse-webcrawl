Frankfurt am Main, commonly known simply as Frankfurt, is the most populous city in the German state of Hesse and one of the country's leading centres of finance, commerce, and transport. Located on the River Main, it is home to the European Central Bank, the German Federal Bank (Bundesbank), the Frankfurt Stock Exchange, and one of the world's busiest airports. Its distinctive cluster of high-rise towers has earned it the nickname "Mainhattan".

History

Frankfurt was first mentioned in 794 in a document of Charlemagne. As a free imperial city it became a major trading centre, hosting the Frankfurt Fair and, from the Middle Ages, the election and later coronation of Holy Roman emperors in St. Bartholomew's Cathedral. In 1848 the Frankfurt National Assembly met in St. Paul's Church, the first freely elected parliament for all of Germany. The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War, losing much of its medieval old town, which was later partially reconstructed.

Economy and finance

Frankfurt is the financial capital of continental Europe. Alongside the European Central Bank and the Bundesbank, hundreds of banks maintain offices in the Bankenviertel district. Deutsche Börse operates the Frankfurt Stock Exchange, one of the world's largest trading venues. The city also hosts major trade fairs, including the Frankfurt Book Fair, the world's largest of its kind.

Transport

Frankfurt Airport is among Europe's busiest hubs and a principal base of Lufthansa. Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof is one of Germany's busiest railway stations, and the Frankfurter Kreuz motorway interchange is one of the most heavily used in Europe.

Culture

Cultural landmarks include the Römer city hall, the reconstructed old town (DomRömer Quarter), the Städel museum on the Museumsufer embankment, and the birthplace of Johann Wolfgang von Goethe. The city is also known for its cider taverns serving Apfelwein in the Sachsenhausen district and for the frankfurter sausage that shares its name.
