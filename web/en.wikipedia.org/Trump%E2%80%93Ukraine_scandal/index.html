Trump–Ukraine scandal

The Trump–Ukraine scandal was a political controversy in the United States that erupted in 2019 when a whistleblower alleged that President Donald Trump had pressured Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy to investigate Trump's political rival Joe Biden and his son Hunter, while withholding military aid and a White House meeting. The scandal led to Trump's first impeachment by the House of Representatives and his acquittal by the Senate.

Background

Trump's personal lawyer Rudy Giuliani and associates pursued theories that Ukraine, rather than Russia, had interfered in the 2016 election, and that Joe Biden had acted corruptly as vice president regarding the Ukrainian company Burisma, which employed his son. Nearly $400 million in congressionally approved military aid to Ukraine was frozen in mid-2019 without public explanation.

The call and whistleblower complaint

In a 25 July 2019 call, Trump asked Zelenskyy to "do us a favor" and pursue investigations. An intelligence-community whistleblower filed a complaint in August 2019 calling the matter an "urgent concern". The White House released a summary record of the call, and the House launched an impeachment inquiry in September 2019, hearing testimony from officials including Ambassador Gordon Sondland and Lt. Col. Alexander Vindman.

Impeachment

On 18 December 2019 the House impeached Trump on articles of abuse of power and obstruction of Congress, largely along party lines. The Senate trial ended in acquittal on 5 February 2020.
