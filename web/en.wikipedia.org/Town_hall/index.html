Town hall

A typical historic town hall in Europe

A town hall is the chief administrative building of a town or other municipality, housing the offices of the local government, the council chamber where the town council meets, and often the mayor's office. In larger cities the equivalent building is usually called a city hall.

Functions

Beyond administration, town halls commonly serve civic and ceremonial purposes: council meetings open to the public, registry offices for births, marriages and deaths, local archives, and halls for concerts, weddings and markets. Historically, medieval European towns built elaborate halls — such as the Rathaus in German-speaking lands or the hôtel de ville in France — as symbols of municipal independence and wealth, often combined with market halls and belfries.

Town hall meetings

In North American usage, a "town hall meeting" is a public meeting at which residents question officials or candidates; the format derives from the traditional New England town meeting, a form of direct democratic local government still practised in parts of the United States.
