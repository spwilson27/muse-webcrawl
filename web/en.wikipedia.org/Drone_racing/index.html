Drone racing

Drone racing is a competitive sport in which pilots fly small, fast radio-controlled multirotor aircraft (quadcopters) through a course of gates and obstacles. Pilots fly using first-person view (FPV): a camera on the drone transmits live video to goggles worn by the pilot, giving the sensation of sitting in the cockpit.

Equipment

Racing drones are typically built around carbon-fibre frames of about 5-inch propeller size, prioritizing speed, agility, and durability over flight time; top speeds can exceed 100 km/h and races last only a few minutes per heat. Video is transmitted on analogue or digital systems, with race timing handled by transponders or video-based lap timers.

Competitions

The sport emerged in Australia and the United States around 2014–2015 and quickly professionalized. The Drone Racing League (DRL), founded in 2015, stages televised events with standardized spec drones, while MultiGP organizes large open-class community racing. The FAI (World Air Sports Federation) has held Drone Racing World Cups and World Championships, and the 2016 World Drone Prix in Dubai awarded large cash prizes.
