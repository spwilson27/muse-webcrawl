Transport in Tokyo

Transport in Tokyo is characterised by one of the most extensive and heavily used urban railway networks in the world. Greater Tokyo is served by a dense web of JR East lines, two subway systems (Tokyo Metro and the Toei Subway), numerous private railways radiating into the suburbs, monorails, buses, taxis, and expressways.

Railways

The Yamanote Line loop, operated by JR East, circles central Tokyo and connects major hubs such as Tokyo, Shinjuku, Ikebukuro, and Shinagawa stations. Nine Tokyo Metro lines and four Toei lines form the subway network, while private operators including Tokyu, Odakyu, Keio, Seibu, Tobu, and Keisei run commuter lines into the surrounding prefectures. The Shinkansen provides high-speed links to other regions of Japan from Tokyo and Shinagawa stations.

Other modes

Road transport includes the Shuto Expressway network and a comprehensive bus system operated largely by Toei. Haneda Airport, within the city, and Narita Airport, in Chiba Prefecture, serve air travellers. Rechargeable smart cards such as Suica and Pasmo are accepted across nearly all operators, making transfers seamless.
