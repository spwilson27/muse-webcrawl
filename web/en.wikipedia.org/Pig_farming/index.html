Pig farming

Pig farming is the raising and breeding of domestic pigs as livestock, principally for pork, ham, bacon and other products, and secondarily for lard, leather and bristles. It is a major branch of animal agriculture worldwide; China accounts for roughly half of global pig production.

Production systems

Systems range from smallholder and pasture-based ("free-range") rearing to intensive indoor operations in which breeding sows, weaners and finishing pigs are housed at different climate-controlled sites. Pigs convert feed to meat efficiently, reaching slaughter weight in about six months in intensive systems.

Issues

The industry faces animal-welfare criticism over gestation crates, farrowing crates, tail docking and castration without anaesthesia, and environmental concerns about manure management and odour. Diseases such as African swine fever and foot-and-mouth disease can devastate herds; biosecurity is a central management concern.
