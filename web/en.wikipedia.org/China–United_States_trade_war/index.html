China–United States trade war
Began: 2018
Parties: China and the United States
Key measures: Tariffs, export controls

The China–United States trade war is an economic conflict between China and the United States that began in 2018, when the US administration of Donald Trump imposed tariffs on Chinese goods, citing unfair trade practices, intellectual property theft and the bilateral trade deficit. China retaliated with tariffs on American goods.

Background

US concerns included forced technology transfer, state subsidies, restricted market access and the large US trade deficit with China following China's accession to the World Trade Organization in 2001.

Escalation and Phase One deal

Successive rounds in 2018–2019 eventually covered hundreds of billions of dollars of goods. In January 2020 the two sides signed a Phase One agreement under which China pledged increased purchases of US goods and some structural reforms, while many tariffs remained in place.

Later developments

Under subsequent administrations most tariffs were retained and supplemented by export controls on advanced semiconductors and related technology. The dispute reshaped supply chains and encouraged diversification of manufacturing to other countries.
