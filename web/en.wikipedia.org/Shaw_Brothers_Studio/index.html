Shaw Brothers Studio

Shaw Brothers Studio (Chinese: 邵氏片場) was a Hong Kong film production company, for decades the largest in Chinese-language cinema. Founded by the Shaw brothers — Runje, Runme, and Run Run Shaw — its roots lay in Shanghai and Singapore exhibition businesses of the 1920s.

Movietown era

In 1961 Run Run Shaw opened Movietown, a vast studio complex in Clearwater Bay, Hong Kong, employing thousands of actors, directors, and technicians under contract on a Hollywood-style studio system. The studio produced around 40 films a year at its peak, specialising in historical epics, Huangmei opera films, wuxia, and later kung fu films.

Kung fu and decline

Directors such as Chang Cheh and Lau Kar-leung, and stars including Jimmy Wang Yu, David Chiang, Ti Lung, and Gordon Liu, made Shaw films hugely influential worldwide; titles include The One-Armed Swordsman (1967), King Boxer (1972), and The 36th Chamber of Shaolin (1978). Competition from Golden Harvest and rising costs ended feature production in 1985; the company refocused on television through TVB, while its library of roughly 760 films was later restored and reissued.
