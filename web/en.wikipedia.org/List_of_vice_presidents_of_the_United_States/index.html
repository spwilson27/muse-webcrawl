List of vice presidents of the United States

The vice president of the United States is the second-highest officer in the executive branch and first in the presidential line of succession. There have been 50 vice presidents, beginning with John Adams under George Washington.

Overview

Nine vice presidents have succeeded to the presidency: John Adams's successors in that path include John Tyler, Millard Fillmore, Andrew Johnson, Chester A. Arthur, Theodore Roosevelt, Calvin Coolidge, Harry S. Truman, Lyndon B. Johnson and Gerald Ford. Ford is the only person to become president without being elected to either office, having been appointed vice president under the Twenty-fifth Amendment after Spiro Agnew resigned.

Early vice presidents include Aaron Burr, George Clinton, Elbridge Gerry and Daniel D. Tompkins. Nineteenth-century holders include Hannibal Hamlin, Schuyler Colfax, William A. Wheeler, Thomas A. Hendricks, Levi P. Morton, Adlai Stevenson I and Garret Hobart. Twentieth-century holders include Charles Curtis, John Nance Garner, Henry A. Wallace, Alben W. Barkley, Richard Nixon, Hubert Humphrey, Walter Mondale, George H. W. Bush, Dan Quayle and Al Gore. Recent vice presidents are Dick Cheney, Joe Biden, Mike Pence, Kamala Harris — the first woman and first person of colour in the office — and JD Vance.

Role

The vice president presides over the Senate and casts tie-breaking votes, and assumes the presidency on the president's death, resignation or removal.
