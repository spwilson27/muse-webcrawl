Harishchandra, the legendary king who would not abandon truth.

Harishchandra is a legendary king of the Solar dynasty in Hindu tradition, celebrated as the supreme embodiment of truthfulness (satya) and generosity. Ancestor of Rama, his story is told in the Mahabharata, the Markandeya Purana and other texts, and his name remains a byword for integrity in Indian culture.

Legend

Challenged by the sage Vishvamitra, Harishchandra vowed away his entire kingdom and wealth in charity. To pay the sage's fee he sold his wife Taramati and son Rohitashva, and finally himself, becoming the servant of a chandala at the cremation ground of Varanasi. When his own dead son was brought for cremation and he demanded the fee even from his grieving wife, the gods — his trials having been a test — restored his family, his kingdom and his son to life, and granted him heaven. In some versions the god Yama and Vishvamitra together reveal the ordeal's purpose.

Cultural legacy

The tale inspired Raja Rao Harishchandra traditions in regional theatre and, famously, Raja Harishchandra (1913), Dadasaheb Phalke's first Indian feature film, which retold the legend and founded the Indian film industry. Mahatma Gandhi cited Harishchandra's devotion to truth as a formative childhood influence.
