First aid

First aid is the first and immediate assistance given to any person with either a minor or serious illness or injury, with care provided to preserve life, prevent the condition from worsening, or to promote recovery until medical services arrive. It includes initial intervention in a serious condition, such as performing cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) while waiting for an ambulance, as well as the complete treatment of minor conditions, such as applying a plaster to a cut.

Principles

The key aims of first aid are conventionally summarised as the three Ps: preserve life, prevent further harm, and promote recovery. A widely taught sequence of priorities is "DR ABC" — Danger, Response, Airway, Breathing, Circulation — which ensures a rescuer first checks the scene is safe, assesses the casualty's responsiveness, opens the airway and checks breathing before starting chest compressions or controlling bleeding.

Skills and training

Core skills include CPR and use of an automated external defibrillator (AED), the recovery position, controlling bleeding with direct pressure, treating choking (back blows and abdominal thrusts), and managing burns, fractures and shock. Training is provided worldwide by organisations such as the Red Cross and St John Ambulance; the red cross emblem and, in many countries, the green cross are recognised symbols of first aid.

Mental health first aid, which teaches laypeople to support someone in psychological distress, extends the concept beyond physical injury.
