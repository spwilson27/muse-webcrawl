Bharatpur, Rajasthan

Bharatpur is a city in the Indian state of Rajasthan, about 180 kilometres south of Delhi and 55 kilometres west of Agra. It was the capital of the Jat princely state of Bharatpur, founded in the 18th century, and its Lohagarh ("Iron") Fort famously repelled several British assaults in 1805 before falling in 1826.

History

The Jat ruler Maharaja Suraj Mal (reigned 1755–1763) built Bharatpur into a formidable fortress city with massive mud and stone ramparts. Lord Lake's forces attacked the fort four times in 1805 and were repulsed with heavy losses; the fort was eventually stormed by Lord Combermere in 1826. The princely state acceded to India after independence in 1947.

Keoladeo National Park

Bharatpur is best known for Keoladeo National Park (formerly Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary), a UNESCO World Heritage Site and Ramsar wetland of about 29 square kilometres. Created from a former maharaja's duck-shooting reserve, the park hosts thousands of migratory waterfowl each winter, historically including the rare Siberian crane, and is one of the world's most important bird breeding and feeding grounds.
