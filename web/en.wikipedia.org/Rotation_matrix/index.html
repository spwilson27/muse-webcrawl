Rotation matrix

In linear algebra, a rotation matrix is an orthogonal matrix used to perform a rotation in Euclidean space. Rotation matrices have determinant +1, their transpose equals their inverse, and their columns are mutually orthogonal unit vectors.

Basic rotations

In two dimensions, rotation by angle θ counterclockwise is the matrix [[cos θ, −sin θ], [sin θ, cos θ]]. In three dimensions, elementary rotations about the x, y and z axes (roll, pitch, yaw) combine by matrix multiplication into any orientation, though the order of multiplication matters because rotations do not commute.

Properties and use

Rotation matrices form the special orthogonal group SO(n). Every 3D rotation matrix has an eigenvalue of 1 giving its axis, and its trace yields the angle. They are ubiquitous in computer graphics, aerospace (direction cosine matrices), robotics and physics, but accumulating numerical error must be corrected by re-orthonormalisation; quaternions are a common alternative. See also rotation (mathematics) and Euler angles.
