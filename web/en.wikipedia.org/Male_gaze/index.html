Male gaze

The male gaze is a concept in feminist film theory describing the depiction of women and the world from a masculine, heterosexual perspective that presents women as objects of male pleasure. The term was coined by film theorist Laura Mulvey in her 1975 essay "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema."

Theory

Mulvey argued that classical Hollywood cinema positions the spectator with the active male protagonist, while female characters are displayed for visual pleasure (scopophilia). She drew on psychoanalytic theory to analyse how camera work, narrative and editing construct this gaze.

Criticism and developments

The concept has been highly influential but also debated: critics questioned its universality, its psychoanalytic basis, and its applicability across race, sexuality and media. Later theorists developed related ideas such as the female gaze and the oppositional gaze.

Applications

The term is used beyond cinema in discussions of advertising, photography, art history, literature and video games.
