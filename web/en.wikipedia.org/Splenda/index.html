Splenda

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A yellow Splenda packet

Splenda is a global brand of sugar substitutes and a no-calorie sweetener product line, principally based on the artificial sweetener sucralose. Sucralose was discovered in 1976 by scientists at Tate & Lyle working with Queen Elizabeth College, University of London, and is made by chlorinating sucrose to produce a compound roughly 600 times sweeter than sugar that passes through the body largely unmetabolised.

Products and marketing

Splenda products are sold in distinctive yellow packets and packaging, with the slogan "Made from sugar, so it tastes like sugar". Granulated and blend products bulk sucralose with maltodextrin or dextrose. The brand, originally marketed by McNeil Nutritionals (a Johnson & Johnson subsidiary), was acquired by Heartland Food Products Group in 2015. Later Splenda lines include stevia, monk fruit, and allulose products.

Safety

Sucralose has been approved by regulators including the US FDA and the European Food Safety Authority; scientific reviews have generally found it safe at permitted levels, though debate continues over some studies and over heating behaviour.
