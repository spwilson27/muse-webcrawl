The Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum (Polish: Państwowe Muzeum Auschwitz-Birkenau w Oświęcimiu) is a museum and memorial in Oświęcim, Poland, preserving the site of the Nazi German Auschwitz concentration and extermination camp, where about 1.1 million people — the overwhelming majority of them Jews, alongside Poles, Roma, Soviet prisoners of war and others — were murdered during the Second World War and the Holocaust.

History

The camp was liberated by the Red Army on 27 January 1945. In 1947 the Polish parliament passed an act establishing the museum on the grounds of the former Auschwitz I and Auschwitz II-Birkenau camps, preserving the surviving barracks, ruins of the gas chambers and crematoria, watchtowers, fences and railway ramp "for all time" as a monument to the martyrdom of the Polish and other nations. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979.

The museum

Permanent exhibitions, first opened in the 1950s in former prisoner blocks at Auschwitz I, display evidence of the crimes committed in the camp: prisoners' belongings seized by the SS — including suitcases, shoes, glasses and hair — as well as documents, photographs and the preserved gas chamber and crematorium. General exhibitions and national exhibitions present the fate of particular victim groups. At Birkenau, the vast site is preserved largely as it was found, with the ruins of the four large gas chamber–crematorium complexes blown up by the SS before their retreat.

Preservation and education

Conservation of the fragile brick and wooden structures, documents and artefacts is a continuing challenge, supported since 2009 by the Auschwitz-Birkenau Foundation's Perpetual Capital fund. The museum runs an International Center for Education about Auschwitz and the Holocaust, publishes scholarly works, and receives more than two million visitors in peak years, making it one of the most visited memorial sites in the world. 27 January, the anniversary of the liberation, is observed internationally as Holocaust Remembrance Day.
