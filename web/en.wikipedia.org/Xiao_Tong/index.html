Xiao Tong (501–531), courtesy name Deshi, was a crown prince of the Chinese Liang dynasty and one of the most influential literary figures of early medieval China. The eldest son of Emperor Wu of Liang, he is universally known by his posthumous name, Crown Prince Zhaoming.

The Wen Xuan

Xiao Tong is remembered above all as the compiler of the Wen Xuan ("Selections of Refined Literature"), an anthology of poetry and prose composed over roughly seven centuries, from the late Zhou dynasty to his own time. The anthology organized literature into genres such as rhapsodies (fu), lyric poems (shi), and letters, and it remained a canonical textbook for poets and examination candidates for more than a millennium; the saying "when the Wen Xuan is mastered, one's literary skill grows by half" attests to its prestige.

Life

A devout Buddhist and Confucian scholar, Xiao Tong maintained a court of literary retainers. He predeceased his father, dying of illness in 531 after falling into water, according to tradition. His sons later became embroiled in the succession struggles that wrecked the Liang dynasty.
