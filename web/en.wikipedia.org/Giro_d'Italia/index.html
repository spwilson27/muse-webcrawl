The Giro d'Italia (Italian for "Tour of Italy"; often simply il Giro) is an annual multi-stage road bicycle race held primarily in Italy each May and early June. Along with the Tour de France and Vuelta a España, it is one of cycling's three Grand Tours, and is famous for its mountain stages in the Alps and Dolomites and for the pink jersey, the maglia rosa, worn by the overall leader.

History

The race was created in 1909 by the newspaper La Gazzetta dello Sport to boost sales, following the success of the Tour de France. Luigi Ganna won the first edition. The pink leader's jersey, introduced in 1931, matches the pink paper on which the Gazzetta is printed. Legendary champions include Alfredo Binda, Fausto Coppi and Gino Bartali — whose rivalry divided postwar Italy — and Eddy Merckx; Binda, Coppi and Merckx each won five editions. The race was interrupted during both World Wars but has been held every year since 1946.

The race

The modern Giro lasts three weeks and covers roughly 3,400 kilometres in 21 stages, including individual and team time trials, flat sprint stages and high-mountain finishes such as the Passo dello Stelvio and Mortirolo. Alongside the general classification (pink jersey), riders contest the points classification (maglia ciclamino), the mountains classification (maglia azzurra) and the best young rider classification (maglia bianca).
