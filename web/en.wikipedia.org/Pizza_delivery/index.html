Pizza delivery

Pizza delivery is a service in which a pizzeria or pizza chain delivers a pizza to a customer. An order is typically made either by telephone or over the internet, in which the customer can request pizza type and size, and other items to be delivered with it, including drinks. Pizzas may be delivered in cardboard pizza boxes or delivery bags, and the delivery is provided using an automobile, motorized scooter, or bicycle.

Depending on the provider, customers may pay online or in person, with cash, credit card, debit card, or other means. A delivery fee is sometimes charged, although free delivery is also common.

History

One famous early example of pizza delivery is the 1889 delivery to Queen Margherita of Savoy, after whom pizza Margherita is named. Modern commercial pizza delivery grew with the large pizza chains of the United States in the second half of the twentieth century. Domino's famously popularized the delivery guarantee with its "30 minutes or it's free" pledge, which was later discontinued in the United States over concerns about rushed drivers causing accidents.

Delivery technology

Keeping pizza warm in transit has driven several inventions, including the corrugated pizza box, the insulated delivery bag, and the small plastic "pizza saver" table placed in the centre of the box to prevent the lid from touching the cheese. More recent innovations include heated bags powered by the delivery vehicle, GPS-based order tracking, and experiments with delivery by drones and autonomous robots.

Ordering

Telephone ordering long dominated the industry, but online and mobile-app ordering overtook phone orders in the 2010s. Third-party food delivery platforms have also become major channels for pizza delivery, alongside the chains' own ordering systems.
