Pennsylvania Station

Pennsylvania Station, commonly known as Penn Station, is the main intercity railroad station in New York City and the busiest transportation facility in the Western Hemisphere, serving more than 600,000 passengers on an average weekday before 2020. Located in Midtown Manhattan beneath Madison Square Garden, it is served by Amtrak, NJ Transit, and the Long Island Rail Road.

Original station

The original station, opened in 1910 by the Pennsylvania Railroad, was a celebrated Beaux-Arts building designed by McKim, Mead & White, modelled on the Baths of Caracalla, with a vast travertine concourse and an airy steel-and-glass train shed. It gave the Pennsylvania Railroad direct access to Manhattan via tunnels under the Hudson River.

Demolition and current station

The above-ground station house was demolished between 1963 and 1966 to make way for Madison Square Garden and Pennsylvania Plaza — a loss that galvanised the historic preservation movement in the United States and led to New York's Landmarks Law. The tracks and a cramped underground station remained. In January 2021 the Moynihan Train Hall opened across Eighth Avenue in the former Farley Post Office building, providing a new, spacious concourse for Amtrak and Long Island Rail Road passengers.
