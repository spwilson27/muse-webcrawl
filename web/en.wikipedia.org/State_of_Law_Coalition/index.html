The State of Law Coalition (Arabic: ائتلاف دولة القانون, Iʾtilāf Dawlat al-Qānūn) is an Iraqi political coalition formed for the 2009 provincial elections and led by former prime minister Nouri al-Maliki. Its largest component is the Islamic Dawa Party, alongside allied Shia parties and independents.

History

The coalition was created ahead of the January 2009 provincial elections, in which it campaigned on restoring security, enforcing the rule of law, and strengthening central government after the worst years of Iraq's sectarian violence. It performed strongly, winning control of most southern governorates including Baghdad and Basra.

In the March 2010 parliamentary elections, State of Law won 89 of the 325 seats in the Council of Representatives — narrowly behind Ayad Allawi's Iraqiya bloc, which won 91. After months of negotiation, Maliki retained the premiership through a subsequent reconfiguration of alliances, an outcome that was contested by Iraqiya. The coalition remained a leading Shia political force through the 2014 elections, after which Maliki stepped down in favour of Haider al-Abadi. In later electoral cycles the Dawa-led forces split and recombined in various alliances, with Maliki's State of Law continuing as a distinct bloc in parliament.
