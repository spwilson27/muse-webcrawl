The UEFA Champions League (abbreviated as UCL) is an annual club association football competition organized by UEFA and contested by top-division European clubs. It is widely regarded as the most prestigious club tournament in world football and one of the most watched annual sporting events.

Introduced in 1955 as the European Champion Clubs' Cup (the European Cup), it was originally a straight knockout tournament open only to the champions of Europe's national leagues. The competition took its current name in 1992, adding a group stage and admitting multiple entrants from the strongest leagues.

History

The tournament was inspired by the success of the South American Campeonato Sudamericano de Campeones and championed by the French sports newspaper L'Équipe. Real Madrid won the first five editions (1956–1960). Famous dynasties followed: Ajax and Bayern Munich won three in a row in the 1970s, Liverpool dominated the late 1970s and early 1980s, and AC Milan won in 1989 and 1990. English clubs were banned for five years after the 1985 Heysel Stadium disaster.

The 1992 rebranding introduced the famous anthem — adapted from Handel's Zadok the Priest by Tony Britten — the starball logo and a group stage. The competition expanded to 32 teams in the group phase by 1999–2000, and the biggest leagues could enter up to four clubs.

Format

From the 2024–25 season, the group stage was replaced by a single league phase of 36 teams, each playing eight matches against different opponents. The top eight advance directly to the round of 16, teams ranked 9th–24th contest a play-off round, and the remainder are eliminated. The knockout rounds are two-legged ties except the final, played at a pre-selected neutral venue.

Anthem and branding

The Champions League anthem, with lyrics in UEFA's three official languages (English, French and German), is played before every match as the teams line up beneath the starball arch. The trophy — the European Champion Clubs' Cup, nicknamed "Ol' Big Ears" — stands 73.5 cm tall and weighs 7.5 kg.

Records

Real Madrid is the most successful club with 15 titles. Cristiano Ronaldo is the all-time top scorer with 140 goals and also holds the records for appearances among outfield scorers; Lionel Messi is second with 129. Carlo Ancelotti is the most successful coach, with five titles.
