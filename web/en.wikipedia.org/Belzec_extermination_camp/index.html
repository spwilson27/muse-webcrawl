Belzec extermination camp

Belzec was a Nazi German extermination camp in occupied Poland, built during World War II for the implementation of Operation Reinhard, the secretive German plan to exterminate Polish Jews within the General Government district of German-occupied Poland. It operated from March to December 1942 beside the village of Bełżec, near the railway station on the Lwów–Lublin line.

History

Belzec was the first of the Operation Reinhard killing centres to begin operations. Jews were deported there in freight trains from the ghettos of the Lublin and Galicia districts and murdered in gas chambers using carbon monoxide from engine exhaust. Between 430,000 and 500,000 people, overwhelmingly Jews, together with Roma and Poles, are estimated to have been killed; only a handful of prisoners survived the war. In late 1942 and 1943 the camp was dismantled, the mass graves exhumed and the bodies burned, and the site was planted over to conceal the crime.

Memorial

A major memorial and museum, designed by Polish sculptors and opened in 2004, now covers the site, with a field of slag and stone marking the mass graves and a memorial wall recording victims' first names.
