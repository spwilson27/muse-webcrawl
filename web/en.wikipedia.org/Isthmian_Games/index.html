Isthmian Games

The Isthmian Games were one of the four great Panhellenic festivals of ancient Greece, held at Isthmia near Corinth in honour of the god Poseidon and the hero Melicertes, who was worshipped there under the name Palaemon. Together with the Olympic, Pythian and Nemean festivals, they formed the circuit of crown games (stephanitai agones) known as the periodos, in which victory was rewarded not with money but with a wreath.

Origins

Greek tradition attributed the foundation of the games to Sisyphus, the legendary king of Corinth, who was said to have instituted funeral games for Melicertes, whose body had been carried ashore by a dolphin. An alternative tradition credited Theseus of Athens with refounding the festival on the model of the Olympic Games. In historical times the games were organised under Corinthian control from about 580 BC, and they were held every two years, in the spring of the second and fourth years of each Olympiad — roughly in the years preceding and following the Olympic festival.

The festival

The games took place at the sanctuary of Poseidon at Isthmia, on the Isthmus of Corinth, a site well placed to draw crowds travelling by land and sea. A sacred truce allowed athletes and spectators to travel safely. The programme included foot races, wrestling, boxing, the pankration, the pentathlon, and horse and chariot races, as well as contests in music and poetry. Victors were originally crowned with a wreath of pine, sacred to Poseidon; at various periods a wreath of dry celery was substituted, before pine was restored in Roman times.

Historical role

Because of their accessible location, the games were a popular venue for political announcements. Most famously, at the Isthmian Games of 196 BC the Roman general Titus Quinctius Flamininus proclaimed the freedom of the Greeks from Macedonian domination, to immense applause. The emperor Nero also competed at Isthmia during his tour of Greece in AD 67 and there announced a similar grant of freedom. The festival survived into the Roman imperial period but declined in late antiquity as Christian emperors suppressed pagan festivals.

Archaeology

Excavations at Isthmia, conducted principally by the University of Chicago and Ohio State University, have uncovered the Temple of Poseidon, an earlier archaic temple beneath it, the stadium, a theatre, Roman baths and the sanctuary of Palaemon, allowing the layout of the festival site to be reconstructed in unusual detail.
