ISO 216

Standard: Paper sizes (A, B series)
First published: 1975
Aspect ratio: 1 : √2
Key size: A4, 210 × 297 mm

ISO 216 is an international standard for paper sizes, used in most countries except the United States and Canada. It defines the A and B series. The system's basis is the aspect ratio 1:√2 (about 1:1.4142), first described by Georg Christoph Lichtenberg in 1786 and embodied in the German DIN 476 standard of 1922 by Walter Porstmann.

The A series

A0 has an area of one square metre (841 × 1189 mm). Each successive size is half the previous, obtained by cutting across the long side, so A1 is 594 × 841 mm, A2 420 × 594, A3 297 × 420, and A4 — the world's standard office and letter size — 210 × 297 mm, down to A10. Because the ratio is preserved, designs scale between sizes without distortion.

The B series and envelopes

The B series provides intermediate sizes, each the geometric mean of successive A sizes; B5 and B4 are common for books and posters. The related ISO 269 defines C-series envelopes sized to hold A-series sheets. Tolerances are specified in the standard, and grammage is covered by ISO 536.
