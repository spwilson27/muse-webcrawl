Conversation

Conversation is interactive communication between two or more people. The development of conversational skills and etiquette is an important part of socialisation. In conversation, topics are proposed and followed by those taking part; conversation analysis studies how turns are taken, how topics shift, and how social relationships are negotiated through talk.

Characteristics

Unlike a speech or debate, conversation is generally informal, spontaneous, and reciprocal: participants take turns speaking and listening, build on each other's contributions, and adjust their contributions in response to feedback. Conversations may serve purposes ranging from exchanging information to entertainment, persuasion, and maintaining social bonds, as in phatic communication such as small talk.

Rules and analysis

The philosopher Paul Grice proposed a cooperative principle, with maxims of quantity, quality, relation, and manner, describing how speakers normally cooperate to make conversation succeed. Conversation analysts in sociology, following Harvey Sacks, study recordings of natural talk to uncover the systematic organisation of turn-taking, repair, and sequence. Conversations also take place in writing and online, through letters, messaging, and social media.

Literature and art

Conversation has been a subject of etiquette manuals since the Renaissance, and "conversation pieces" are a genre of painting depicting groups in informal discussion. See also dialogue and conversation analysis.
