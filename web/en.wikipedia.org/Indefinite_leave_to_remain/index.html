Indefinite leave to remain

Indefinite leave to remain (ILR) is an immigration status granted to a person who does not hold the right of abode in the United Kingdom but who has been admitted without any time limit on their stay and is free to take up employment, study or claim public funds, subject to the rules. It is one of the forms of settlement in the UK and a usual prerequisite for naturalisation as a British citizen.

Eligibility

Applicants typically qualify after a prescribed period of lawful residence — commonly five years on work or family routes and ten years under the long-residence rules — and must usually pass the Life in the UK test and meet an English language requirement. ILR may also be granted on discretionary or protection-related grounds.

Loss of status

ILR can lapse after an absence of more than two years from the UK and may be revoked in cases of deception or serious criminality leading to deportation. A related status, indefinite leave to enter, is granted at the border or by entry clearance to those qualifying abroad.

EU Settlement Scheme

Following Brexit, EU, EEA and Swiss citizens were generally required to apply to the EU Settlement Scheme; those granted settled status hold a form of indefinite leave under Appendix EU of the Immigration Rules.
