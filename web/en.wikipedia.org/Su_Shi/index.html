Su Shi (8 January 1037 – 24 August 1101), courtesy name Zizhan, better known by his art name Su Dongpo, was a Chinese polymath of the Song dynasty: a poet, essayist, calligrapher, painter, scholar, and government official. He is regarded as one of the greatest figures in Chinese literature, a master of nearly every literary form of his era, including shi poetry, ci lyrics, prose essays, and the informal fu.

Life

Su Shi was born in Meishan, Sichuan, into a literary family; his father Su Xun and younger brother Su Zhe were also famed writers, and together the three are counted among the Eight Masters of Tang and Song prose. Su passed the jinshi examination in 1057 with high honors, his essay impressing the examiner Ouyang Xiu.

His official career was repeatedly interrupted by factional politics. As a critic of Wang Anshi's New Policies, he was arrested in the Crow Terrace Poetry Trial of 1079 — imprisoned for poems alleged to slander the emperor — and exiled to Huangzhou. There he wrote some of his most celebrated works, including the Red Cliff rhapsodies and the lyric "Written on the Wall at West Forest Temple." Later exiles took him to Huizhou and finally to remote Hainan Island. He was recalled in 1100 but died in Changzhou the following year while returning north.

Works and interests

Su produced some 2,700 poems and 800 letters; his ci lyrics, such as "Prelude to Water Melody" ("When will the moon be clear and bright?"), broadened the form's emotional and philosophical range. He was also a pioneering theorist of literati painting, an accomplished calligrapher ranked among the Song dynasty's four masters, and a noted gastronome — the braised pork dish Dongpo pork bears his name. His interests extended to engineering (he supervised the construction of a causeway on Hangzhou's West Lake), medicine, and Buddhist and Daoist philosophy.
