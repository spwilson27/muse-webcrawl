Asmara

Asmara, or Asmera, is the capital and largest city of Eritrea, in the country's Central Region. It sits at an elevation of 2,325 metres (7,628 ft), making it the sixth highest capital in the world by altitude. The city is known for its well-preserved colonial Italian modernist architecture, and in 2017 it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the first such listing for Eritrea.

History

Asmara grew from villages of the Kebessa plateau and became the capital of Italian Eritrea in 1897. Under Italian rule, especially in the 1930s, it was rebuilt with striking modernist and Art Deco buildings, including the Fiat Tagliero Building, and earned the nickname "Piccola Roma" (Little Rome). It came under British administration in 1941, was federated with Ethiopia in 1952, and became the capital of independent Eritrea in 1993 after the Eritrean War of Independence.

Geography and climate

Asmara lies on the northwestern edge of the Eritrean Highlands escarpment. Its high altitude gives it a mild, semi-arid climate with warm days and cool nights, and a rainy season concentrated in July and August.

Architecture

The UNESCO listing recognised an unusually complete ensemble of early modernist architecture in an African context, encompassing rationalist, futurist and Art Deco cinemas, apartments, ministries, and the Asmara cathedral. The city's urban plan and tree-lined boulevards also survive largely intact.
