Geelong

Geelong is a port city in the Australian state of Victoria, on Corio Bay, about 75 km southwest of Melbourne. With a population of roughly 280,000, it is the second-largest city in Victoria. The traditional owners of the land are the Wadawurrung people of the Kulin nation; the name Geelong derives from the Wadawurrung word Djillong, thought to mean "tongue of land" or "cliffs".

History

Europeans first explored the area in 1802, and the town was founded in 1838. Geelong boomed as the gateway and wool port for the western district and during the gold rushes of the 1850s, when it was briefly a rival to Melbourne. It later became a major manufacturing centre for wool mills, and, in the 20th century, for car manufacturing (Ford's plant operated from 1925 to 2016), oil refining, and aluminium smelting.

Today

Geelong's economy now centres on health care, education (Deakin University's waterfront campus), advanced manufacturing, and tourism; it is the gateway to the Bellarine Peninsula and the Great Ocean Road. The city is famous for sport: the Geelong Football Club ("the Cats"), founded in 1859, is one of the oldest Australian rules football clubs and has won multiple AFL premierships, including in 2022. Geelong hosted events associated with the region's growing role as a sporting and events hub.

Geography and climate

The city surrounds Corio Bay, a western arm of Port Phillip Bay, with the Barwon River flowing through it. Geelong has a temperate oceanic climate with mild summers and cool, moderately wet winters.
