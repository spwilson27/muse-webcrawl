De Graafschap

De Graafschap (Dutch pronunciation: [də ˈɣraːfsxɑp]) is a professional football club from Doetinchem, in the Achterhoek region of the Netherlands. Founded on 1 February 1954, the club is nicknamed De Superboeren ("The Super Farmers") and plays its home matches at Stadion De Vijverberg, which holds about 12,600 spectators. Its colors are blue and white.

History

De Graafschap has spent most of its history moving between the Eredivisie, the top flight of Dutch football, and the second-tier Eerste Divisie, earning a reputation as a classic "yo-yo club." The team's best Eredivisie finish came in the 1990s, and it remains known for loyal local support and for its fierce regional rivalry with Vitesse Arnhem, contested as the Gelderse derby, as well as with Go Ahead Eagles.

Supporters and identity

The club's Superboeren identity celebrates the agricultural heritage of the Achterhoek. De Vijverberg is regularly cited among the most atmospheric grounds in the Netherlands relative to its size, and the club consistently draws strong crowds even in the second division. Notable players to have represented De Graafschap include Guus Hiddink, who later managed the Netherlands and several European clubs, and Klaas-Jan Huntelaar, who came through the club's youth academy.
