Numeral system

A numeral system is a writing system for expressing numbers; that is, a mathematical notation for representing numbers of a given set, using digits or other symbols in a consistent manner. The same sequence of symbols may represent different numbers in different numeral systems.

History

Early counting used tally marks. Ancient Egyptians developed hieroglyphic numerals, Mesopotamians a sexagesimal (base-60) positional system, and the Maya an independent vigesimal (base-20) system with a symbol for zero. Roman numerals used letters in a largely additive notation. The Hindu–Arabic numeral system, developed in India by the 6th or 7th century and transmitted through Islamic mathematics to Europe, combined decimal place value with a true zero and became the dominant system worldwide.

Positional systems

In a positional system with base b, the value of a digit depends on its position, and numbers are expressed as sums of digits times powers of b. Important bases include decimal (10), binary (2), octal (8), and hexadecimal (16), the latter three being essential in computing.
