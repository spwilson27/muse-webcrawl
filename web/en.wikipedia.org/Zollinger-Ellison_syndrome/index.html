Zollinger–Ellison syndrome

SpecialtyEndocrinology, gastroenterology
SymptomsAbdominal pain, diarrhoea, peptic ulcers
CauseGastrin-secreting tumour (gastrinoma)
TreatmentProton pump inhibitors, surgery

Zollinger–Ellison syndrome (ZES) is a disease in which tumours cause the stomach to produce too much acid, resulting in peptic ulcers. Symptoms include abdominal pain and diarrhea. The syndrome is caused by a gastrinoma, a neuroendocrine tumor that secretes the hormone gastrin, which in turn causes increased production of gastric acid by the parietal cells of the stomach.

The condition is named after surgeons Robert M. Zollinger and Edwin H. Ellison, who first described it in 1955. Most gastrinomas arise in the pancreas or duodenum; about 75–80% of cases are sporadic, while the remainder occur as part of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN 1).

Signs and symptoms

Patients typically present with peptic ulcers that are multiple, refractory to treatment, or located in unusual sites such as the distal duodenum or jejunum; chronic diarrhea and steatorrhea are also common because excess acid inactivates pancreatic enzymes. Gastroesophageal reflux and its complications occur frequently.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on a markedly elevated fasting serum gastrin level (often more than ten times the upper limit of normal) together with a low gastric pH, and a secretin stimulation test. High-dose proton pump inhibitors control acid hypersecretion in nearly all patients; surgical resection of localised sporadic gastrinoma can be curative, while metastatic disease is managed medically.
