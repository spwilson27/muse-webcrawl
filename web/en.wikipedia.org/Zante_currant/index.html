Zante currant

Zante currants, also called currants in many recipes, are the dried berries of the small, sweet, seedless Black Corinth grape (Vitis vinifera). They are named after the Greek island of Zakynthos (Zante), one of the earliest areas of production.

History

The name "currant" is a corruption of "Corinth", the Greek port from which the dried fruit was exported in medieval times. Currants were traded around the Mediterranean for centuries and became a staple baking ingredient in Britain and its colonies.

Characteristics

Zante currants are much smaller and darker than raisins made from larger grapes, with a tart, intense flavour. They are produced mainly in Greece, with additional production in Australia, South Africa, and California.

Culinary use

They are used in currant buns, Eccles cakes, Christmas pudding, mincemeat, scones, and many other traditional baked goods.
