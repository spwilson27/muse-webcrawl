Pascal's triangle is a triangular array of the binomial coefficients that arises in probability theory, combinatorics, and algebra. It is named after the French mathematician Blaise Pascal, who published a treatise on it in 1654, though it had been studied centuries earlier in India (as the Meru-prastāra of Pingala and later authors), Persia (by Omar Khayyam), and China (by Yang Hui, for whom it is called Yang Hui's triangle).

Construction

The triangle begins with a single 1 at the apex. Each subsequent row starts and ends with 1, and every interior entry is the sum of the two numbers directly above it. The entries of row n are the binomial coefficients C(n, k), and the row sums are the powers of 2.

Properties

The triangle encodes many identities: the binomial theorem (each row gives the coefficients of (x + y)n), the Fibonacci numbers along shallow diagonals, triangular and tetrahedral numbers along other diagonals, and Sierpiński-triangle fractals when odd entries are shaded. Pascal's rule, C(n,k) = C(n−1,k−1) + C(n−1,k), is the basis of the construction and of proofs by induction throughout combinatorics.
