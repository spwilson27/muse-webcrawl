Volcanic plug

A volcanic plug, also called a volcanic neck, is a landform created when magma hardens within the vent of an active volcano. When the softer surrounding cone is eroded away, the resistant solidified lava remains as a steep-sided tower or spire.

Examples

Famous examples include Devils Tower in Wyoming, the Pitons of Saint Lucia, and numerous plugs in Scotland such as those beneath Edinburgh Castle and North Berwick Law. Because the plug rock is often more durable than the cone material, these features can persist for millions of years after the volcano becomes extinct.
