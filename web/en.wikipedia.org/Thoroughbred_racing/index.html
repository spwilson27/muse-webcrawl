Thoroughbred racing is a sport and industry involving the racing of Thoroughbred horses. The governing organisations differ between countries, but the rules and standards, the stud books and the racing calendar share a common origin in eighteenth-century England. Races take place on flat courses — on grass (turf), dirt or synthetic surfaces — and, in jump racing, over hurdles and fences.

The breed

The Thoroughbred is a breed of horse developed in England from native mares crossed with three imported Oriental stallions of Arabian, Barb and Turkoman breeding: the Byerley Turk (foaled c. 1680), the Darley Arabian (1704) and the Godolphin Arabian (c. 1724). Every modern Thoroughbred traces its male-line descent to one of these foundation sires, and registration is controlled by national stud books, beginning with the General Stud Book first published in 1791.

Racing formats

Flat races range from sprints of about five furlongs to staying races of two miles and more. The most prestigious events include the classic races — in Britain the 2000 Guineas, 1000 Guineas, Derby, Oaks and St Leger — and international fixtures such as the Kentucky Derby, the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe, the Melbourne Cup, the Dubai World Cup and the Breeders' Cup. Horses typically begin racing as two-year-olds and the most valuable colts retire to stud careers that can dwarf their racetrack earnings. Famous champions include Frankel, Secretariat, Phar Lap, Man o' War and Winx.

Betting and industry

Thoroughbred racing is closely intertwined with gambling: pari-mutuel wagering funds purses through a percentage of the betting pool in many jurisdictions, while bookmakers offer fixed odds elsewhere. The industry supports breeding, training, veterinary and media sectors worldwide, and is regulated for integrity, equine welfare and anti-doping by national authorities.
