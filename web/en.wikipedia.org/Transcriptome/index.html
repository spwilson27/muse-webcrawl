Transcriptome

The transcriptome is the set of all RNA transcripts, including mRNA, rRNA, tRNA and other non-coding RNA, in an individual or a population of cells. Unlike the genome, which is roughly fixed for an organism, the transcriptome varies with cell type, developmental stage and environmental conditions.

Study methods

Transcriptomics studies the transcriptome using DNA microarrays and, predominantly since the late 2000s, RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq), which counts transcripts and detects alternative splicing, novel transcripts and gene fusions. Single-cell RNA sequencing resolves expression in individual cells.

Applications

Comparing transcriptomes between healthy and diseased tissue reveals differentially expressed genes, biomarkers and drug targets. Large reference projects such as GTEx have mapped expression across human tissues.
