Rent control in New York

Rent control in New York refers to laws in New York State and New York City that restrict the rent landlords may charge and the grounds on which tenants may be evicted. New York has one of the oldest and most extensive systems of rent regulation in the United States, dating to the 1920s and wartime controls of the 1940s.

Forms of regulation

Two main systems exist: strict rent control, applying mainly to buildings built before 1947 with continuously occupied units, and rent stabilization, which covers many buildings built between 1947 and 1974 and sets annual increases through the Rent Guidelines Board.

Reforms

The Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019 ended vacancy decontrol and high-income deregulation, and repealed preferential rent practices, significantly strengthening tenant protections.
