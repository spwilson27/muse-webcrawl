Molecular dynamics

Molecular dynamics (MD) is a computer simulation method for analyzing the physical movements of atoms and molecules. The atoms and molecules are allowed to interact for a fixed period of time, giving a view of the dynamic "evolution" of the system, typically by numerically integrating Newton's equations of motion.

Method

An MD simulation requires initial positions and velocities, and a force field (such as CHARMM, AMBER, or GROMOS) describing interatomic potentials. Trajectories are generated by integrating the equations of motion in femtosecond timesteps. Ensembles such as NVE, NVT, and NPT correspond to different thermodynamic conditions.

Applications

MD is widely used in chemical physics, materials science, and biophysics — for studying protein folding, drug binding, membrane behaviour, and material defects. Specialized hardware (such as the Anton supercomputers) and GPU-accelerated packages (GROMACS, LAMMPS, NAMD) have extended accessible timescales to milliseconds for small systems.
