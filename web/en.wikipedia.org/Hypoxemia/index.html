Hypoxemia


Pulse oximetry is a noninvasive way to estimate arterial oxygen saturation.

Hypoxemia is an abnormally low level of oxygen in the blood. More specifically, it is oxygen deficiency in arterial blood. Hypoxemia has many causes, and often causes hypoxia as the blood is not supplying enough oxygen to the tissues of the body.

Definition

Hypoxemia refers to the low level of oxygen in arterial blood. Tissue hypoxia refers to low levels of oxygen in the tissues. The distinction is important, as hypoxemia can occur without tissue hypoxia (for example, in anemia, where oxygen content is low but partial pressure may be normal), and tissue hypoxia can occur without hypoxemia. Normal arterial partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2) is roughly 80–100 mmHg; values below 60 mmHg represent severe hypoxemia.

Causes

Hypoxemia is caused by five categories of etiologies: hypoventilation, ventilation/perfusion (V/Q) mismatch, right-to-left shunt, diffusion impairment, and low inspired oxygen tension (such as at high altitude). Common clinical causes include:

  • Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
  • Pneumonia
  • Pulmonary edema
  • Pulmonary embolism
  • Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms of acute hypoxemia may include shortness of breath (dyspnea), rapid breathing (tachypnea), confusion, restlessness, and cyanosis — a bluish discoloration of the skin and mucous membranes. Chronic hypoxemia may produce clubbing of the fingers and polycythemia.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause and commonly includes supplemental oxygen therapy delivered by nasal cannula, face mask, or, in severe cases, mechanical ventilation.
