Naples

Naples (Italian: Napoli) is the regional capital of Campania and the third-largest city in Italy, located on the Gulf of Naples with Mount Vesuvius nearby. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it is widely regarded as the birthplace of pizza.

History

Founded by Greeks as Neapolis, Naples passed through Roman, Byzantine, Norman, Spanish and Bourbon rule as capital of the Kingdom of Naples and later the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, before Italian unification in 1861.

Culture and cuisine

Naples is famed for Neapolitan pizza (Margherita and Marinara), sfogliatelle, espresso, opera at Teatro di San Carlo (opened 1737), and Neapolitan music. Castel dell'Ovo, Castel Nuovo and the Naples National Archaeological Museum anchor its heritage.

Geography

The city sits between Vesuvius and the Phlegraean Fields volcanic area, facing islands including Capri and Ischia.
