City of God

City of God may refer to several works and places. The phrase originates with De civitate Dei (The City of God), a book of Christian philosophy written in Latin by Augustine of Hippo in the early 5th century AD, contrasting the "city of God" with the earthly city.

Augustine's work

Augustine wrote The City of God in response to pagan claims that the sack of Rome by the Visigoths in 410 was caused by the abandonment of the traditional Roman religion for Christianity. The work develops a philosophy of history in which human society divides into two cities, formed by two loves: the earthly city, formed by self-love reaching the point of contempt for God, and the heavenly city, formed by love of God reaching the point of contempt for self.

Favela and film

Cidade de Deus ("City of God") is also a favela in the West Zone of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, which lent its name to the acclaimed 2002 Brazilian crime film City of God, directed by Fernando Meirelles and Kátia Lund, depicting organised crime in the suburb between the 1960s and 1980s.

Other uses

The term is also used in theology and in the names of various religious communities and works of literature inspired by Augustine's vision of the heavenly Jerusalem.
