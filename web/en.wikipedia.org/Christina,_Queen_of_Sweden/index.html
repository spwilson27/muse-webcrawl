Christina, Queen of Sweden

Reign1632 – 1654
Born18 December 1626, Stockholm
Died19 April 1689, Rome
FatherGustavus Adolphus
ReligionLutheran, later Roman Catholic

Christina (18 December 1626 – 19 April 1689) was Queen of Sweden from 1632 until her abdication in 1654. The daughter of Gustavus Adolphus, she succeeded him at the age of five after his death at the Battle of Lützen in the Thirty Years' War, ruling at first under the regency of Chancellor Axel Oxenstierna. She is remembered as one of the most learned women of the seventeenth century.

Reign

Christina took an active role in government from 1644 and worked to end the Thirty Years' War, which concluded with the Peace of Westphalia in 1648. A lavish patron of the arts and sciences, she invited scholars such as René Descartes to her court in Stockholm; Descartes died in Stockholm in 1650. Her court's extravagance, her sales of noble titles, and her resistance to marriage unsettled the Swedish political establishment.

Abdication and conversion

In 1654 Christina abdicated in favour of her cousin Charles X Gustav, having secretly converted to Catholicism — a faith then illegal for a Swedish monarch. She travelled through Europe in male attire and settled in Rome, where Pope Alexander VII received her. Her dramatic arrival and conversion were sensations of the age.

Life in Rome

In Rome Christina became a leading patron of music and theatre, supporting composers such as Arcangelo Corelli and Alessandro Scarlatti. Her stormy life included the summary execution of her servant Gian Rinaldo Monaldeschi in France in 1657, which damaged her reputation. She died in Rome in 1689 and is one of the few women buried in the Vatican grottoes beneath St Peter's Basilica. Her life inspired plays, operas and the 1933 film Queen Christina starring Greta Garbo.
