The Battle of Two Sisters was an engagement of the Falklands War fought on the night of 11–12 June 1982. Men of 45 Commando, Royal Marines, attacked the twin-peaked ridge of Two Sisters, one of the ring of hills defending the Argentine garrison at Port Stanley.

Background

After landing at San Carlos and advancing across East Falkland, British forces planned a coordinated night assault on the outer defensive line around Stanley, with simultaneous attacks at Mount Longdon, Two Sisters and Mount Harriet. Two Sisters was defended by Argentine infantry with machine guns, mortars and minefields.

The battle

The commandos advanced in darkness, taking advantage of naval gunfire support and Milan anti-tank missiles used against bunkers. The southern peak fell after close fighting, and a subsequent assault up the northern summit overcame determined resistance by dawn. Some Argentine troops fell back toward Stanley and Tumbledown.

Aftermath

The capture of Two Sisters, together with Mount Longdon and Mount Harriet, broke the outer defences of Stanley. Within two days British forces took the remaining hills at Wireless Ridge and Tumbledown, and the Argentine garrison surrendered on 14 June 1982.
