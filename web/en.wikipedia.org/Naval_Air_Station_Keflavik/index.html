Naval Air Station Keflavik

Naval Air Station Keflavik
Location: Keflavík, Iceland
Coordinates: near Keflavík International Airport
Operated by: United States Navy
Active: 1951 – 2006 (as NAS); U.S. presence ended 2006

Naval Air Station Keflavik (NASKEF) was a United States Navy air station at Keflavík in southwestern Iceland, co-located with Keflavík International Airport. It served as the principal U.S. military installation in Iceland during the Cold War and hosted the U.S. Iceland Defense Force, charged with the defense of Iceland — a NATO founding member with no standing army of its own — under the 1951 defense agreement between the two countries.

History

The airfield was built by the U.S. military during the Second World War (as Meeks Field) while American forces relieved British troops occupying Iceland. The U.S. Navy assumed control of the base in 1961, and NAS Keflavik became a key node in North Atlantic defense: its P-3 Orion patrol aircraft and the SOSUS-linked surveillance effort monitored Soviet submarine traffic transiting the GIUK gap, while interceptor squadrons of the U.S. Air Force stood air defense alert.

Closure and aftermath

With the end of the Cold War the installation was progressively drawn down. In 2006 the United States withdrew its remaining forces from Iceland and NAS Keflavik was disestablished on September 30, 2006, ending the continuous U.S. military presence. The airfield continues as Iceland's main international airport, and since 2016 rotational U.S. and NATO maritime patrol deployments have returned to Keflavík, with infrastructure upgrades funded by the United States.

Units and life

At its height the base supported several thousand U.S. personnel and dependents, with schools, housing, and broadcasting (AFN Iceland). Housed units included Patrol Squadrons on rotation, Naval Air Facility and aviation support elements, and the Air Forces Iceland fighter alert detachment, famous for intercepting Soviet Tu-95 Bear bombers.
