Diablo Dam

Diablo Dam is one of three dams along the upper Skagit River in Whatcom County, Washington state, United States, operated by Seattle City Light as part of the Skagit River Hydroelectric Project. The dam impounds Diablo Lake and was completed in 1930. At the time of its completion, the concrete arch dam — 389 feet (119 m) high — was the tallest dam in the world.

The dam's powerhouse generates electricity for the city of Seattle; its installed capacity is about 159 megawatts. Diablo Dam was designed with distinctive Art Deco-era styling, including an inclined elevator that once carried visitors to the top of the gorge, and it is listed on the National Register of Historic Places as part of the Skagit River and Newhalem Creek Hydroelectric Projects historic district.

Diablo Lake

The reservoir behind the dam, Diablo Lake, is famous for its intense turquoise colour, produced by glacial flour — fine rock particles ground by glaciers upstream and suspended in the water, reflecting blue-green light. The North Cascades Highway (State Route 20) runs along the lake, and overlooks provide views of the dam and surrounding peaks such as Colonial Peak and Pyramid Peak.
