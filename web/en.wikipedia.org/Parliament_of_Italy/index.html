Parliament of Italy

The Parliament of Italy (Italian: Parlamento italiano) is the national legislature of the Italian Republic. It is a bicameral legislature with 600 elected members: the Chamber of Deputies (400 deputies) and the Senate of the Republic (200 elected senators, plus senators for life). Italy is unusual in practising "perfect bicameralism": the two chambers have identical powers, and a bill must be approved in the same text by both to become law.

Organisation

Both houses are elected by direct universal suffrage — since a 2021 constitutional change, citizens may vote for the Senate at 18 rather than 25, and senators may be elected at 25 rather than 40. Former Presidents of the Republic are senators for life by right, and the President may appoint up to five citizens of exceptional merit as life senators. Since a constitutional reform approved by referendum in 2020 and first applied at the 2022 general election, the Chamber has 400 deputies (down from 630) and the Senate 200 elected members (down from 315).

Powers

Parliament holds legislative power, grants and withdraws confidence in the government — which must enjoy the confidence of both houses — approves the budget, and elects the President of the Republic in joint session together with regional delegates. It also elects some members of the Constitutional Court and the High Council of the Judiciary. The identical powers of the two chambers (bicameralismo perfetto) are often criticised for slowing legislation through the shuttle (navetta) of bills between the chambers; a 2016 attempt to reform the Senate into a weaker chamber of regions was rejected in a referendum.

History

The parliament descends from the Parliament of the Kingdom of Sardinia created under the Statuto Albertino of 1848, which became the Parliament of the Kingdom of Italy on unification in 1861. Under Fascism the Chamber was replaced by the Chamber of Fasces and Corporations. The Constituent Assembly of 1946 drafted the republican Constitution, effective 1 January 1948, which established the present two chambers; the first republican parliament was elected in April 1948.

Joint session

Sitting together at Palazzo Montecitorio as "Parliament in joint session," deputies and senators elect the President of the Republic, swear in Constitutional Court judges, and conduct impeachment proceedings against the President for high treason or attacks on the Constitution.
