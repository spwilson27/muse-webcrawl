Courland Pocket

Date9 October 1944 – 10 May 1945
LocationCourland Peninsula, Latvia
BelligerentsGermany (Army Group Courland) vs Soviet Union
ResultGerman forces surrender at the end of the war in Europe

The Courland Pocket (German: Kurland-Kessel) was the area of the Courland Peninsula in western Latvia where German forces of Army Group North — renamed Army Group Courland in January 1945 — were cut off by the Red Army from October 1944 until the end of the Second World War in Europe.

Formation

During the Soviet Baltic Offensive, the 1st Baltic Front reached the Gulf of Riga near Tukums in the summer of 1944, and the Memel Offensive of October 1944 severed the army group's land connection to East Prussia. Roughly 200,000 German troops, along with Latvian units, were isolated in the pocket, supplied precariously by sea through Liepāja and Ventspils. Hitler refused evacuation, valuing the pocket as a bridgehead and a training area for U-boat crews.

Battles

Soviet forces launched six major offensives against the pocket between October 1944 and April 1945, all of which failed to eliminate it, though the perimeter shrank. The fighting was heavy and costly on both sides.

Surrender

Following Germany's unconditional surrender, Army Group Courland capitulated on 8–10 May 1945. Its last commander, Carl Hilpert, surrendered to the Soviets, and around 190,000 German personnel went into captivity, while some soldiers and Latvian legionnaires took to the forests and joined the anti-Soviet "Forest Brothers" resistance.
