Jens Jacob Asmussen Worsaae

Jens Jacob Asmussen Worsaae
Born: 14 March 1821, Vejle, Denmark
Died: 15 August 1885 (aged 64), Copenhagen, Denmark
Nationality: Danish
Fields: Archaeology, history
Known for: Founding scientific archaeology; first stratigraphic excavations; The Primeval Antiquities of Denmark (1843)

Jens Jacob Asmussen Worsaae (14 March 1821 – 15 August 1885) was a Danish archaeologist, historian and politician, widely considered a founder of scientific archaeology. A protégé of Christian Jürgensen Thomsen, who had devised the three-age system (Stone, Bronze, and Iron Ages), Worsaae provided the field evidence — through careful stratigraphic excavation — that demonstrated the system's chronological validity.

Career

Worsaae began working at the Museum of Northern Antiquities in Copenhagen as a teenager. At just 22 he published Danmarks Oldtid oplyst ved Oldsager og Gravhøie (1843; English translation, The Primeval Antiquities of Denmark, 1849), which established principles of excavation and artifact classification and was quickly translated and influential abroad. His work on Danish shell middens (køkkenmøddinger) with Japetus Steenstrup and Johan Georg Forchhammer showed these kitchen refuse heaps to be Stone Age habitation sites.

Contributions

Worsaae was among the first archaeologists to use stratigraphy — the analysis of layered deposits — to establish relative chronology, excavating burial mounds and bog deposits with controlled methods. He investigated Viking Age sites, including the Danevirke fortifications and the Jelling monuments, and debated the ethnic interpretation of prehistoric finds during the period of Danish-German tension over Schleswig. In 1866 he became director of the Museum of Northern Antiquities, succeeding Thomsen, and he also served as Denmark's Minister for Cultural Affairs (1874–1875).

Legacy

Worsaae's insistence that archaeology should be an empirical, evidence-driven discipline, independent of saga traditions, helped transform antiquarianism into a modern science. His methods influenced archaeologists across Europe and the English-speaking world.
