Inverness

Inverness (Scottish Gaelic: Inbhir Nis, "mouth of the River Ness") is a city in the Scottish Highlands and the administrative centre of the Highland council area. It is commonly regarded as the capital of the Highlands. Situated where the River Ness enters the Moray Firth at the northern end of the Great Glen, Inverness is the northernmost city in the United Kingdom and one of Europe's fastest-growing settlements, with a population of roughly 48,000 in the city itself and more in its wider urban area.

Inverness Castle overlooks the River Ness.

History

The site has been occupied since at least the sixth century, when a Pictish king, Brude, held court near Inverness and was visited by St Columba around 565. A castle was established by Malcolm III in the eleventh century, and the town received a royal burgh charter from David I in the twelfth century. Inverness changed hands repeatedly in the wars of Scottish independence and the clan conflicts of later centuries; the present Inverness Castle, a red sandstone building on the riverside, dates from 1836 and served as a courthouse and prison. The Battle of Culloden, the last pitched battle fought on British soil, took place a few miles east of the city in 1746, ending the Jacobite rising.

Geography and economy

Inverness lies at the junction of the A9 trunk road and routes into the Highlands, with rail links to Perth, Aberdeen, Wick and Kyle of Lochalsh, and an airport at Dalcross. The Caledonian Canal, engineered by Thomas Telford, connects the city through the Great Glen to Fort William. The economy rests on public administration, health and education (including the University of the Highlands and Islands), retail, and tourism — the city is the main gateway for visitors to nearby Loch Ness. Inverness was granted city status in 2000 as part of the millennium celebrations.
