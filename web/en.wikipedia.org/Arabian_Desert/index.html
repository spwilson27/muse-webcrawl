The Arabian Desert is a vast desert wilderness in Western Asia covering most of the Arabian Peninsula, with an area of about 2,330,000 square kilometres, making it one of the largest deserts in the world. It stretches from Yemen to the Persian Gulf and from Oman to Jordan and Iraq.

Geography

The desert comprises several distinct regions. The Rub' al Khali ("Empty Quarter") in the south is the largest continuous sand desert in the world. The An Nafud in the north is known for its large longitudinal dunes, and the narrow Ad-Dahna corridor connects it to the Rub' al Khali. Gravel plains, lava fields (harrat), salt flats, and mountainous fringes, including the ranges of the Hejaz and Yemen, vary the landscape. Almost the entire region receives less than 100 mm of rain per year, and summer temperatures can exceed 50 °C.

Ecology and resources

Adapted species include the Arabian oryx, sand gazelle, desert fox, and many reptiles and drought-resistant shrubs. Beneath the desert lie some of the world's largest petroleum reserves, which transformed the economies of the region in the 20th century. Ancient aquifers and fossil water are tapped for agriculture and cities.
