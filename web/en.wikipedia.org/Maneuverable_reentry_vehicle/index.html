Maneuverable reentry vehicle

A maneuverable reentry vehicle (MARV) is a type of ballistic missile warhead capable of changing its flight path during atmospheric re-entry, rather than following a purely ballistic trajectory to its target. MARVs were developed to improve accuracy and to evade anti-ballistic missile defences.

Design and guidance

MARVs use aerodynamic control surfaces, movable flaps or split-body designs, combined with terminal guidance — such as radar correlation of the terrain below with a stored map, or active radar homing — to steer toward the target in the final phase of flight. This allows both evasive manoeuvres against interceptors and much greater terminal accuracy than unguided reentry vehicles.

Examples

The best-known operational MARV was the warhead of the US Pershing II medium-range ballistic missile (deployed 1983, eliminated under the INF Treaty), which used radar area guidance (RADAG) to achieve very high accuracy. Maneuvering warheads have since been developed for several modern systems, including anti-ship ballistic missiles such as China's DF-21D, whose MARV is intended to strike moving aircraft carriers. Hypersonic glide vehicles are a related but distinct category of maneuvering delivery system.
