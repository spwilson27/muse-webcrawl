Heinrich Kramer

Heinrich Kramer (Latin: Henricus Institor; c. 1430 – 1505) was a German Dominican friar, theologian, and inquisitor, best known as the principal author of the Malleus Maleficarum ("Hammer of Witches"), first published at Speyer in 1487. The work, a handbook for the detection, prosecution, and punishment of supposed witches, became one of the most influential texts of the early modern witch-hunts.

Life and inquisitorial career

Born at Sélestat in Alsace, Kramer joined the Dominican order and rose to prominence as a preacher and inquisitor in southern Germany and Austria. His zealous prosecution of alleged witches at Innsbruck in 1485 was curtailed when the local bishop expelled him for his obsessive and irregular conduct; the experience helped spur him to write the Malleus, which he supplied with the papal bull Summis desiderantes affectibus (1484) and a (probably misleadingly presented) endorsement from the University of Cologne to bolster its authority. The treatise argued that witchcraft was a real, heretical conspiracy, placed particular emphasis on women as witches, and gave detailed guidance on trials and torture.

Later years

Kramer spent his last years preaching against heresy in Bohemia and Moravia, campaigning against the Waldensians and the followers of Jan Hus, and died around 1505, probably in Moravia. Although the Malleus Maleficarum went through many editions, its influence and the degree of official Church support behind it have been debated by historians.
