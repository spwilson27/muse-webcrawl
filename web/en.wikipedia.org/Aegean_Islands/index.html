Aegean Islands

LocationAegean Sea, between Greece and Turkey
NumberSeveral thousand islands and islets; ~200 inhabited
LargestCrete
Main groupsCyclades, Dodecanese, Sporades, Saronic, North Aegean, Crete, Euboea
AdministrationMostly Greece; some islands Turkey (e.g. Imbros, Tenedos)

The Aegean Islands are the group of islands in the Aegean Sea, between the Greek mainland to the west and north, Turkey to the east, and the island of Crete to the south. Several thousand islands, islets and rocks make up the archipelago, of which roughly two hundred are inhabited. The overwhelming majority belong to Greece, which administers them through the North Aegean, South Aegean and Crete regions; a small number near the Turkish coast belong to Turkey.

Groups

The islands are conventionally divided into seven groups: the North Aegean islands (including Lesbos, Chios, Samos and Lemnos), Euboea, the Northern Sporades, the Cyclades (Mykonos, Santorini, Naxos and others), the Saronic Islands near Athens, the Dodecanese in the southeast (Rhodes, Kos, Patmos), and Crete with its surrounding islets.

History

The Aegean islands were home to the Cycladic and Minoan civilisations of the Bronze Age and to major Greek city-states; Delos was a sacred sanctuary and commercial hub. Control passed among Hellenistic kingdoms, Rome, Byzantium, Venice, Genoa and the Ottoman Empire before most islands joined the modern Greek state in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Since the mid-twentieth century many have become leading tourist destinations, while questions of maritime boundaries and airspace between Greece and Turkey remain a persistent source of diplomatic tension.
