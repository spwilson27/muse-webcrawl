Latenivenatrix

Period: Late Cretaceous (Campanian)
Location: Dinosaur Park Formation, Alberta
Family: Troodontidae
Named: 2017

Latenivenatrix ("hidden huntress") is a genus of troodontid dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous (Campanian) of Alberta, Canada, about 75 million years ago. Its remains come from the Dinosaur Park Formation.

Discovery and naming

The genus was named in 2017 by Aaron van der Reest and Philip Currie, based on a partial skeleton originally referred to Troodon, plus material formerly assigned to Stenonychosaurus. Latenivenatrix mcmasterae honours Linda McMaster. At roughly 3–3.5 metres long, it is among the largest known troodontids.

Classification

Troodontids were small, bird-like theropods with large brains and a retractable sickle claw on the second toe. The description of Latenivenatrix prompted renewed study of Dinosaur Park troodontid material, and some later studies have questioned the distinctness of the genus, suggesting some specimens may belong to Stenonychosaurus.
