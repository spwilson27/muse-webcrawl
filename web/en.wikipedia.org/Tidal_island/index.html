Tidal island

A tidal island is a piece of land connected to a mainland, or to another island, by a natural or man-made causeway that is submerged at high tide and exposed at low tide. Because they are cut off for part of each day, tidal islands have often been sites of religious retreats, fortresses and lighthouses.

Examples

Famous tidal islands include Mont-Saint-Michel in Normandy, France; Lindisfarne (Holy Island) off the coast of Northumberland, England; St Michael's Mount in Cornwall; and Cramond Island in Scotland. Many are reached by paved causeways, while others can only be crossed over sand or mudflats at low water.

Safety

Visitors must take care to return before the tide comes in, as rising water can flood causeways quickly and strand walkers. Tide tables are usually posted at crossing points, and some islands maintain refuges or rescue services for people caught by the tide.
