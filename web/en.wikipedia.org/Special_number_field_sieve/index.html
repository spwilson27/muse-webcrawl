Special number field sieve

Special number field sieve
Abbreviation: SNFS
Purpose: Integer factorisation of special-form numbers
Generalisation: General number field sieve (GNFS)

The special number field sieve (SNFS) is a special-purpose algorithm for factoring integers of a particular algebraic form, such as numbers of the shape re ± s — for example Fermat numbers, Mersenne numbers and Cunningham numbers. It is the restricted ancestor and special case of the general number field sieve (GNFS), the fastest known classical method for factoring large integers in general.

Method

The SNFS exploits the fact that a number of special form can be expressed using a low-degree polynomial with very small coefficients. It then seeks "smooth" relations — pairs of values whose factorisations over small primes and algebraic integers combine to give a congruence of squares — in the same general manner as the quadratic sieve and GNFS. Because its polynomial is so small, its running time is significantly better than the general case: in L-notation its complexity is roughly exp(((32/9)1/3 + o(1)) (log n)1/3 (log log n)2/3), versus the constant (64/9)1/3 for the GNFS.

Applications and records

The algorithm descends from John Pollard's early 1990s work on factoring numbers such as the Fermat number F7. SNFS computations have produced the complete factorisations of many Cunningham numbers hundreds of digits long, including numbers of over 300 decimal digits, and are used in distributed projects such as NFSNET and the Cunningham Project. Because RSA moduli have no exploitable special form, SNFS does not directly threaten RSA; the GNFS governs the security estimates for such keys.
