Tristan Tzara (born Samuel or Samy Rosenstock, also known as S. Samyro; 16 April 1896 – 25 December 1963) was a Romanian avant-garde poet, essayist and performance artist best known for being one of the founders and central figures of the anti-art movement Dada and then a leading figure in Surrealism.

Dada

Born in Moinești in Moldavia to a Jewish family, Tzara published his first poems as a teenager in Bucharest. In 1915 he moved to Zurich, where in February 1916 he co-founded the Cabaret Voltaire with Hugo Ball, Emmy Hennings, Hans Arp, Marcel Janco and Richard Huelsenbeck. Tzara became Dada's tireless propagandist, editing the journal Dada and writing the movement's manifestos, including the Dada Manifesto 1918. He devised hallmark Dada techniques such as the cut-up poem, "drawn from a hat".

Paris and Surrealism

Moving to Paris in 1919, Tzara staged provocations with André Breton, Philippe Soupault and Louis Aragon before a bitter falling-out with Breton split Paris Dada. Tzara wrote the play The Gas Heart (1921) and the major poem collection Approximate Man (1931). He reconciled with the Surrealists in 1929 and joined Breton's group, later becoming active in the French Communist Party and the French Resistance during the Second World War. After the war he concentrated on scholarship and poetry, including studies of Villon. He is buried at the Montparnasse Cemetery in Paris.
