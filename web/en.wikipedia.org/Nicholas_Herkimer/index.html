Nicholas Herkimer (c. 1728 – August 16, 1777) was an American militia brigadier general in the Revolutionary War, celebrated as a hero of the Battle of Oriskany. A prosperous farmer and landholder of German Palatine descent in New York's Mohawk Valley, Herkimer commanded the Tryon County militia and led the relief column sent to aid the besieged Fort Stanwix in August 1777. Mortally wounded in the ambush at Oriskany, he famously continued to direct his men while propped against a tree, calmly smoking his pipe.

Early life

Herkimer was born around 1728 at Herkimer, in what was then the Province of New York, the son of Johan Jost Herkimer and Catherine Herkimer, German Palatine immigrants. The family was among the most prominent in the Mohawk Valley, and Herkimer became a successful farmer and landowner, owning a substantial stone house and extensive property near present-day Herkimer, New York.

French and Indian War

During the French and Indian War, Herkimer served as a lieutenant in the New York militia. In 1758 he was wounded during the French attack on German Flatts, gaining early military experience on the frontier that would shape his later career.

Revolutionary War

With the outbreak of the American Revolution, Herkimer was appointed colonel of a battalion of minutemen and in September 1776 was commissioned brigadier general of the Tryon County militia by the New York Provincial Congress. In July 1777 he met with Mohawk leader Joseph Brant, a former acquaintance, in an unsuccessful attempt to persuade the Mohawk to remain neutral.

Battle of Oriskany

In August 1777, as British and Loyalist forces under Barry St. Leger besieged Fort Stanwix, Herkimer marched with some 800 militiamen to relieve the fort. On August 6, 1777, his column was ambushed in a ravine near Oriskany by Loyalists and their Native American allies. Herkimer's horse was shot and his leg shattered by a ball early in the fighting. Refusing to withdraw, he had himself placed against a beech tree and continued issuing orders, lighting his pipe to steady his men. The militia fought its way out of the ambush, inflicting heavy losses.

Death and legacy

Herkimer's wounded leg was amputated, but the operation was botched and he died of blood loss on August 16, 1777, reportedly while reading his Bible. Herkimer County, New York, and the village of Herkimer are named in his honor, and his home is preserved as a New York State historic site. The Battle of Oriskany is remembered as one of the bloodiest engagements of the Revolution relative to the numbers engaged.
