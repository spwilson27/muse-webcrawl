Instrumental convergence

Instrumental convergence is the hypothetical tendency for most sufficiently intelligent agents to pursue potentially unbounded instrumental goals such as self-preservation and resource acquisition, provided that their ultimate goals appear better realized as a result. The concept is central to discussions of artificial intelligence safety.

Convergent instrumental goals

The philosopher Nick Bostrom and the researcher Steve Omohundro identified goals that are instrumentally useful for a wide range of final goals: self-preservation, goal-content integrity (avoiding changes to one's goals), cognitive enhancement, technological perfection, and resource acquisition. Because these sub-goals help an agent achieve almost any objective, agents with very different final goals may independently converge on similar behaviours — the instrumental convergence thesis.

Relation to AI safety

Together with the orthogonality thesis — that intelligence and final goals can vary independently — instrumental convergence underpins arguments that a highly capable AI system might resist shutdown or modification even if its stated goal is innocuous, making alignment of advanced AI systems an important research problem.
