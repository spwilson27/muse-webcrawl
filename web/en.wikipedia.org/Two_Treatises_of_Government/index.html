Two Treatises of Government

The Two Treatises of Government (full title: Two Treatises of Government: In the Former, the False Principles and Foundation of Sir Robert Filmer, and His Followers, Are Detected and Overthrown. The Latter Is an Essay Concerning the True Original, Extent, and End of Civil Government) is a work of political philosophy published anonymously in 1689 by John Locke. It appeared in the wake of the Glorious Revolution and is regarded as a foundational text of liberalism.

First Treatise

The First Treatise is a detailed refutation of Sir Robert Filmer's Patriarcha, which defended the divine right of kings by tracing royal authority from Adam. Locke attacks Filmer's argument line by line, denying that Scripture grants monarchs absolute, hereditary power over their subjects.

Second Treatise

The Second Treatise sets out Locke's own theory. He posits a state of nature in which individuals are free and equal but bound by a law of nature to respect the life, liberty and property of others. Property arises when a person mixes their labour with natural resources. Because the state of nature lacks impartial judges and reliable enforcement, people consent to form a political society, creating a government that holds power in trust. A government that violates that trust — for example by seizing property without consent — may legitimately be resisted and replaced.

Influence

Locke's ideas about natural rights, government by consent and the right of revolution influenced the American Founding Fathers, the Declaration of Independence, and later liberal and constitutional thought worldwide.
