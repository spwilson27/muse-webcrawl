The Kuomintang (KMT), also translated as the Chinese Nationalist Party, is a major political party in Taiwan and was the ruling party of the Republic of China on the mainland from 1928 to 1949. Founded by Sun Yat-sen, it is guided by his Three Principles of the People: nationalism, democracy and the people's livelihood.

History on the mainland

The KMT grew out of Sun's revolutionary organizations and was reorganized in 1919–1924 with Soviet assistance, entering a First United Front with the Chinese Communist Party. After Sun's death in 1925, Chiang Kai-shek led the Northern Expedition (1926–28), reunifying much of China under the Nationalist government at Nanjing. The alliance with the Communists collapsed into civil war in 1927. The KMT government then led China through the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–45), after which civil war resumed; defeated by the Communists, the government retreated to Taiwan in 1949.

Taiwan

Under Chiang Kai-shek and Chiang Ching-kuo, the KMT ruled Taiwan under martial law (1949–1987) while overseeing rapid economic growth and, eventually, democratization. In 2000 it lost the presidency for the first time, to the Democratic Progressive Party, but has since returned to power (2008–2016 under Ma Ying-jeou) and remains one of Taiwan's two major parties, generally favouring closer economic ties with mainland China and the "1992 Consensus".
