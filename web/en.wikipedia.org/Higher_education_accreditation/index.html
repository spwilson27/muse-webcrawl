Higher education accreditation

Higher education accreditation is a process of external quality review used to determine whether a college, university or academic programme meets established standards of quality and improvement. Accreditation is intended to assure students, employers and the public of an institution's legitimacy and educational quality, and in many systems it is a precondition for access to public funding and student financial aid.

Types

Institutional accreditation reviews an entire organisation, while programmatic (or specialised) accreditation evaluates particular fields such as medicine, law, engineering or nursing. In the United States, accreditation is carried out by private, non-profit accrediting agencies — historically regional accreditors for institutions and national accreditors for many career-oriented schools — recognised by the U.S. Department of Education or the Council for Higher Education Accreditation. Other countries use governmental quality agencies, such as the Quality Assurance Agency in the United Kingdom.

Process and debate

The process generally involves institutional self-study, peer review visits and periodic renewal. Critics debate accreditation's cost, its effectiveness at measuring learning outcomes, and its role as a barrier to entry and innovation; unaccredited institutions and "diploma mills" remain a concern for consumers.
