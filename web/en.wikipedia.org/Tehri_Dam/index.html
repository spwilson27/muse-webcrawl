Tehri Dam

The Tehri Dam is a large rock-and-earth-fill embankment dam on the Bhagirathi River near Tehri in Uttarakhand, India. At 260.5 metres (855 ft) high, it is the tallest dam in India and among the tallest in the world. It is the centrepiece of the Tehri hydroelectric complex, which also includes the Koteshwar Dam downstream and a pumped-storage plant, developed by THDC India Limited.

Project

The main power station has an installed capacity of 1,000 MW, with the wider complex providing peaking power, irrigation water, and drinking water supplies to Delhi and the Indo-Gangetic plain, as well as flood moderation. Preliminary work began in the late 1970s; construction, delayed for decades, was completed and the first units commissioned in 2006–2007. The reservoir submerged the old town of Tehri and many villages; a planned town, New Tehri, was built above the reservoir to resettle displaced residents.

Controversy

The dam was the focus of one of India's longest environmental protest movements, led prominently by activist Sunderlal Bahuguna, who undertook fasts against the project. Critics cited the seismic risk of the Himalayan site, sedimentation, the displacement of tens of thousands of people, and inadequate rehabilitation. Supporters pointed to power, irrigation, and water-supply benefits. The project proceeded after court challenges and safety reviews.
