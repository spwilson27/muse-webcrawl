Government of Malaysia


Perdana Putra, Putrajaya, seat of the executive.

The Government of Malaysia, officially the Federal Government of Malaysia (Malay: Kerajaan Persekutuan Malaysia), is the federal administration of Malaysia, a federal constitutional elective monarchy. It is based in the administrative capital Putrajaya, with Kuala Lumpur remaining the national capital and seat of Parliament.

Structure

The Federal Constitution divides power among three branches. The legislature is the bicameral Parliament, consisting of the Yang di-Pertuan Agong (King), the Dewan Negara (Senate) and the Dewan Rakyat (House of Representatives). The executive is led by the prime minister and the Cabinet, drawn from Parliament and responsible to the Dewan Rakyat. The judiciary is headed by the Federal Court. The head of state, the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, is elected for a five-year term by and from among the hereditary rulers of nine Malay states in the Conference of Rulers.

Federalism

Malaysia comprises thirteen states and three federal territories. Legislative powers are divided by the Constitution's Federal, State and Concurrent lists: the federal government controls foreign affairs, defence, finance and most commerce, while states retain authority over land, local government and Islamic law matters. The governments of Sabah and Sarawak enjoy additional autonomies secured by the Malaysia Agreement 1963.
