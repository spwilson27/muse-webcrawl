Maudgalyayana (Pali: Moggallāna; Sanskrit: Maudgalyāyana), also known as Mahāmaudgalyāyana or by his birth name Kolita, was one of the Buddha's closest disciples. He is considered the second of the Buddha's two foremost male disciples, together with Śāriputra. Traditional accounts relate that Maudgalyayana and Śāriputra became spiritual wanderers in their youth, and later converted to Buddhism, becoming arahants after hearing the teachings from Assaji.

Maudgalyayana was foremost among the Buddha's disciples in psychic powers (iddhi), which he used extensively in his teaching career. In Buddhist art and literature he is often depicted together with Śāriputra: Śāriputra foremost in wisdom, Maudgalyayana foremost in supernatural powers.

Death

According to traditional accounts, Maudgalyayana died at the hands of robbers (in some versions, hired by rival ascetics) two weeks before the Buddha's own death, at Kālasilā. His relics were honoured at the Great Stupa of Sanchi. In East Asian Buddhism, Maudgalyayana is associated with the Ghost Festival (Ullambana), based on the account of his rescuing his mother from the realm of hungry ghosts through the merit of offerings to the Sangha.
