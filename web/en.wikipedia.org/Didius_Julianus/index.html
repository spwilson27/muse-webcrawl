Didius Julianus

Didius Julianus, Roman emperor for nine weeks in 193 AD.

Didius Julianus (133–193 AD) was Roman emperor for nine weeks in 193, during the chaotic Year of the Five Emperors. He is infamous for having purchased the throne at auction from the Praetorian Guard after they murdered his predecessor.

Early career

Born at Milan into a prominent family, Julianus rose through the senatorial career ladder, serving as consul and as governor of several provinces, including Germania Inferior and Africa Proconsularis. He was wealthy, well connected, and a member of the circle around the emperor Marcus Aurelius.

Purchase of the empire

On 28 March 193 the Praetorians murdered the emperor Pertinax after only three months in power. The Guard then announced that the throne would go to whoever paid the soldiers the most. Julianus outbid his rival Sulpicianus — Pertinax's father-in-law, who was bidding from inside the camp — promising each soldier 25,000 sesterces. The Senate, intimidated, confirmed him as emperor. The sale was received with public disgust in Rome and outrage in the provinces.

Fall

Three provincial governors — Septimius Severus on the Danube, Pescennius Niger in Syria, and Clodius Albinus in Britain — were proclaimed emperor by their legions. Severus marched on Rome fastest. As his army approached, the Praetorians abandoned Julianus, the Senate condemned him to death, and he was beheaded in the palace on 2 June 193, reportedly begging, "But what evil have I done? Whom have I killed?" His reign illustrates the degree to which the imperial office had become a prize of military bargaining in the late second century.
