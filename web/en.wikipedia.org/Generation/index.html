Generation

A generation is all of the people born and living at about the same time, regarded collectively. It can also be described as "the average period, generally considered to be about 20–30 years, during which children are born and grow up, become adults, and begin to have children."

In kinship terminology, a generation is a structural principle: within a family, parents and their offspring constitute two successive generations, and the term provides the framework for describing descent, inheritance, and family relationships.

Social generations

In the social sciences, a generation (or cohort) may also be defined by shared formative experiences. Karl Mannheim's 1928 essay "The Problem of Generations" founded the sociology of generations, arguing that people who experience the same historical events in their youth form a distinct generational consciousness. Popular usage, especially in the West, labels successive cohorts with names: the Greatest Generation, the Silent Generation, baby boomers, Generation X, millennials (Generation Y), Generation Z, and Generation Alpha. The boundaries and characteristics of these groupings are approximate and contested, and demographers caution that within-cohort differences are often larger than differences between cohorts.

Other uses

The word is also used for successive versions or stages of technology (generations of computers, mobile networks, or aircraft), for the production of energy or offspring, and in biology for the alternation of generations in plants.
