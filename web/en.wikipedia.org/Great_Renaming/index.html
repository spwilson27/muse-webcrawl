Great Renaming

Context: Usenet newsgroup hierarchy reorganisation

Date: 1986–1987

Organiser: Gene Spafford and the Backbone Cabal

The Great Renaming was a restructuring of the Usenet newsgroup hierarchy carried out in 1986 and 1987. Before the renaming, newsgroups used an ad-hoc naming scheme (such as net.*, fa.* and mod.*) that had grown without a coherent plan as Usenet expanded. The renaming replaced it with the structured "Big Seven" hierarchies — comp.*, misc.*, news.*, rec.*, sci.*, soc.* and talk.* — that still form the core of Usenet today.

Background and process

By the mid-1980s Usenet carried hundreds of groups and the naming scheme had become confusing: fa.* groups came from the ARPANET, net.* covered everything else, and moderated groups were prefixed mod.*. Gene Spafford, a system administrator at Purdue University, together with the informal "Backbone Cabal" of major site administrators, organised a systematic re-organisation. Existing groups were mapped onto the new hierarchies — for example net.unix became comp.unix, net.movies became rec.arts.movies, and net.abortion became talk.abortion — and moderated groups lost their separate prefix, being marked instead by their posting status.

The transition took place in stages between late 1986 and early 1987. It was not universally popular: some administrators and users resisted the loss of familiar names, and the process helped to spur the creation of the alternative alt.* hierarchy in 1987, which allowed group creation without central approval.

Legacy

The Big Seven (later Big Eight, with the addition of humanities.*) hierarchy and its more formal group-creation voting process defined Usenet governance for decades. The Great Renaming is remembered as an early example of both the power and the controversy of central coordination in a decentralised online community.
