Huáscar

Huáscar (Quechua: Waskhar, c. 1491–1533) was Sapa Inca of the Inca Empire from 1527 until his death in 1533. He succeeded his father, Huayna Capac, in Cusco, but his succession was contested by his half-brother Atahualpa, based in Quito, precipitating the Inca Civil War that fatally weakened the empire on the eve of the Spanish conquest.

Reign and civil war

Huayna Capac and his designated heir died around 1527, probably of a European epidemic disease such as smallpox that raced ahead of the conquistadors. Huáscar was crowned in Cusco, the traditional capital, while Atahualpa controlled the empire's northern armies and the city of Quito. After several years of tension, war broke out in 1529. Huáscar's forces initially advanced north but suffered a series of defeats at the hands of Atahualpa's veteran generals — Quizquiz, Chalcuchímac, and Rumiñawi — culminating in Huáscar's capture at the Battle of Quipaipán in April 1532.

Death

Huáscar was held prisoner while Atahualpa, himself captured by Francisco Pizarro at Cajamarca in November 1532, feared his brother might ally with the Spaniards. In early 1533 Atahualpa ordered Huáscar's execution — he was reportedly drowned in the Andamarca River. The civil war had devastated the empire's leadership and unity, easing the Spanish takeover that followed.

Legacy

The name Huáscar was later borne by a Peruvian ironclad turret ship of the War of the Pacific, captured by Chile at the Battle of Angamos in 1879 and preserved as a museum ship at Talcahuano.
