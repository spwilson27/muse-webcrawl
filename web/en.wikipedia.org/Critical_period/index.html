Critical period

In developmental biology and developmental psychology, a critical period is a maturational stage during which the nervous system is especially sensitive to certain environmental stimuli. If the organism does not receive the appropriate stimulus during this window, it may be difficult or impossible to develop certain functions later in life.

Examples

Well-known examples include the visual system: kittens and human infants deprived of patterned vision in early months (for example by untreated congenital cataracts) suffer lasting deficits. Imprinting in birds, studied by Konrad Lorenz, occurs in a critical window shortly after hatching.

Language acquisition

The critical period hypothesis, proposed by Eric Lenneberg (1967), holds that first-language acquisition must occur before a certain age — roughly puberty — for full native competence. Cases of severely isolated children such as Genie, and the greater difficulty adults typically have achieving native-like pronunciation in second languages, are debated pieces of evidence; many researchers now prefer the softer concept of a "sensitive period".
