Saint Hedwig of Silesia

Born1174, Andechs Castle, Bavaria
Died15 October 1243, Trzebnica, Silesia
Canonized1267, by Pope Clement IV
Feast16 October
PatronageSilesia; Poland; orphans

Saint Hedwig of Silesia (1174–1243), also known as Hedwig of Andechs, was a duchess consort of Silesia through her marriage to Henry I the Bearded and is venerated as the patron saint of Silesia.

Life and works

Born into the powerful Andechs family of Bavaria, Hedwig married Henry of Silesia around 1186. She was renowned for her charity and piety, founding hospitals and religious houses, most famously the Cistercian abbey at Trzebnica in 1202, the first convent for women in Silesia. Widowed in 1238 and bereaved of her son Henry II at the Battle of Legnica against the Mongols in 1241, she retired to Trzebnica, where she died.

Veneration

Hedwig was canonised in 1267. Her cult spread widely in Central Europe, and she remains a symbol of Silesian identity, honoured by both Poles and Germans.
