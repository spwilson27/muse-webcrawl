Dual-sport motorcycle

TypeStreet-legal off-road motorcycle
Also calledDual-purpose, on/off-road, dualie
RelatedAdventure touring, enduro, supermoto

A dual-sport motorcycle is a type of street-legal motorcycle that is designed for both on-road and off-road use. The terms all-road, on/off road, and dual-purpose are also used for this class of motorcycles. Dual-sports are equipped with street-legal equipment such as lights, a speedometer, mirrors, a horn, a license plate mounting, and a muffler, and can therefore be registered and licensed.

History

The concept traces to early scrambler motorcycles of the 1960s and machines such as the Yamaha DT-1 of 1968, which popularised the idea of a lightweight trail bike that could also be ridden on public roads. Through the 1970s and 1980s manufacturers including Honda, Suzuki, Kawasaki and Yamaha offered a wide range of dual-purpose models, from small 125 cc commuters to large-displacement machines.

Design

Dual-sport motorcycles typically feature long-travel suspension, knobby or dual-purpose tyres, a high-mounted exhaust, engine skid plates, and upright riding ergonomics. Engine sizes range from around 125 cc to over 1,000 cc, though the classic dual-sport is a lightweight single-cylinder machine of 250–650 cc. Larger, more road-biased variants shade into the adventure touring category.

Notable models

Long-running models include the Kawasaki KLR650, Suzuki DR650, Honda XR650L, Yamaha XT660 and the KTM 690 Enduro R. Smaller models such as the Honda CRF300L have broadened the class's appeal to new riders and commuters.
