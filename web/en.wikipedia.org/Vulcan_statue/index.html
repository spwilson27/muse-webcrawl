Vulcan statue
Location: Vulcan Park, Birmingham, Alabama
Sculptor: Giuseppe Moretti
Created: 1904
Height: 56 ft (17 m), on a 126 ft pedestal

The Vulcan statue is a 56-foot (17 m) cast-iron statue of Vulcan, the Roman god of fire and forge, in Birmingham, Alabama. It is the largest cast-iron statue in the world and serves as the symbol of Birmingham's iron and steel industry.

History

Sculpted by Italian artist Giuseppe Moretti, Vulcan was created as Birmingham's entry to the 1904 World's Fair in St. Louis, celebrating the young city's mineral resources and industrial output. Cast in Birmingham itself, it won a Grand Prize at the fair. After years at other sites, the statue was erected in 1939 atop Red Mountain in Vulcan Park, on a 126-foot pedestal.

Restoration

By the late 1990s the statue and park had badly deteriorated. A major restoration between 1999 and 2003 repaired the iron figure, returned its spear to an upward position and revived the park, which remains a city landmark with panoramic views.
