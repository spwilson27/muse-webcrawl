Cognitive test

Cognitive tests are assessments of a person's cognitive abilities — such as reasoning, memory, attention, language and processing speed. They are used in psychology, education, medicine, employment and the military to measure intellectual functioning, screen for impairment, or predict performance.

Types

Well-known tests include intelligence tests such as the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS) and the Stanford–Binet Intelligence Scales; brief screening instruments such as the Mini–Mental State Examination (MMSE) and the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), used to detect dementia and mild cognitive impairment; and domain-specific batteries assessing memory (e.g. the Wechsler Memory Scale), executive function (e.g. the Trail Making Test and Wisconsin Card Sorting Test), and language.

Use and issues

Scores are typically interpreted against normative samples matched by age and education. Interpretation must account for factors such as sensory impairment, language background, mood and test familiarity. Formal cognitive testing differs from informal "brain training" games, whose transfer to general ability has limited evidence.
