Jen-Hsun "Jensen" Huang (born February 17, 1963) is a Taiwanese-American businessman and electrical engineer. He is the co-founder, president, and chief executive officer of Nvidia, the graphics processing company he established in 1993 with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. Under Huang, Nvidia grew from a maker of graphics chips for PC gaming into the leading supplier of processors and platforms for artificial intelligence and accelerated computing, becoming one of the world's most valuable companies during the AI boom of the 2020s.

Early life and education

Huang was born in Tainan, Taiwan, spent part of his childhood in Thailand, and was sent with his brother to the United States, where he attended the Oneida Baptist Institute in Kentucky before his family settled in Oregon. He earned a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from Oregon State University and a master's degree from Stanford University.

Career

Before Nvidia, Huang worked at LSI Logic and Advanced Micro Devices. Nvidia, founded on his 30th birthday in 1993, survived near-collapse in the 1990s before its GeForce graphics cards and the invention of the GPU in 1999 established it in gaming; the CUDA programming platform (2006) later opened GPUs to general-purpose and AI computing. Huang is recognised for his long tenure as CEO and his signature black leather jacket, and he has been named to lists of the world's most influential and wealthiest people.

Philanthropy

Huang and his wife Lori have made major gifts to Oregon State University and Stanford, including funding for engineering and computing research centres.
