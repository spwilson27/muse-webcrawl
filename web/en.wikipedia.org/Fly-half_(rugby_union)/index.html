The fly-half is a position in a rugby union team. The fly-half is crucial to a team's game plan, orchestrating the team's attacks and commonly taking goal kicks and tactical kicks. The fly-half wears the number 10 shirt and stands outside the scrum-half (number 9) in the back line, forming the "half-back" pairing that links the forwards and the three-quarter backs.

Role

The fly-half receives the ball from the scrum-half at set pieces and breakdowns and decides whether to pass the ball along the back line, run with it, or kick for territory or position. Because almost every attacking play passes through the fly-half's hands, the position demands quick decision-making under pressure, accurate passing off both hands, and an assured kicking game. Fly-halves usually take the team's penalty kicks and conversions, and may also attempt drop goals, worth three points.

Names

The position is known by several names around the rugby world. In New Zealand it is called "first five-eighth" (with the inside centre called second five-eighth); in Ireland it is traditionally the "out-half"; in England and Wales "fly-half" is standard; and "stand-off" was the historical term and remains the usual name in rugby league. In Australia it is often called the "five-eighth" or simply by the number 10.

Notable fly-halves

Many of the sport's most celebrated players have worn number 10, including Barry John and Dan Carter — Carter retired as international rugby's all-time leading points scorer — Jonny Wilkinson, whose extra-time drop goal won England the 2003 Rugby World Cup, Hugo Porta of Argentina, Naas Botha of South Africa, Grant Fox of New Zealand, and Finn Russell of Scotland.
