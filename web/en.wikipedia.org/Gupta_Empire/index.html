The Gupta Empire was an ancient Indian empire that ruled much of the Indian subcontinent from about 320 to 550 CE. Founded by Chandragupta I, it reached its height under Samudragupta and Chandragupta II, and its era is often called the Golden Age of India for its achievements in science, mathematics, astronomy, literature and art.

History

Chandragupta I (r. c. 319–335) consolidated power in Magadha through marriage to the Licchavi princess Kumaradevi. His son Samudragupta conquered much of northern India, while Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) defeated the Western Satraps and extended the empire to the Arabian Sea. Under Kumaragupta I and Skandagupta the empire resisted invasions by the Huna (Hephthalites), but repeated attacks and internal weakness fragmented it by the mid-6th century.

Culture and science

Gupta scholars advanced the decimal numeral system; Aryabhata proposed that the Earth rotates on its axis and approximated pi. Sanskrit literature flourished with Kālidāsa, author of Abhijñānaśākuntalam. The period produced the Ajanta cave paintings and early stone Hindu temples, and the Chinese pilgrim Faxian left a detailed account of its prosperity and mild administration.
