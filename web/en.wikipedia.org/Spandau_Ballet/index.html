Spandau Ballet are an English new wave band formed in Islington, London, in 1979. Inspired by London's post-punk underground dance scene, they emerged at the start of the 1980s as house band of the Blitz nightclub, becoming leading figures of the New Romantic movement. Their smooth, soul-inflected pop later made them one of the most successful British groups of the decade, with hits including "To Cut a Long Story Short", "Gold" and the international number one ballad "True".

History

The band grew out of a school group formed by guitarist and songwriter Gary Kemp and saxophonist Steve Norman, joined by drummer John Keeble, singer Tony Hadley and bassist Martin Kemp. Their name came from graffiti seen in Berlin — a grim reference to Spandau prison and the gallows slang "Spandau ballet". Their 1980 debut single "To Cut a Long Story Short" launched them in the vanguard of the New Romantics, and the albums Journeys to Glory (1981) and Diamond (1982) mixed funk, electronica and fashion-conscious imagery.

The 1983 album True marked a turn to polished pop-soul; its title track topped the UK chart and reached the US top five, becoming their signature song. Parade (1984) and Through the Barricades (1986) kept them at arena level across Europe, and they performed at Live Aid in 1985. After the 1989 album Heart Like a Sky underperformed, the band split in 1990 amid bitter disputes: a late-1990s court case over royalties brought by Hadley, Norman and Keeble against Gary Kemp was dismissed.

Reunion

The five original members reunited in 2009 for a world tour and the album Once More. Hadley left again in 2017; the band continued briefly with singer Ross William Wild before returning to hiatus. The Kemp brothers also pursued acting careers, famously playing the Kray twins in the 1990 film The Krays.

Legacy

Spandau Ballet were central to the sound and style of early-1980s British pop, and "True" has been sampled and covered repeatedly — most famously as the basis for P.M. Dawn's 1991 hit "Set Adrift on Memory Bliss".
