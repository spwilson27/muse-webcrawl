Morarji Ranchhodji Desai (29 February 1896 – 10 April 1995) was an Indian independence activist and politician who served as the fourth prime minister of India from 1977 to 1979, leading the Janata Party government — the first non-Congress government in independent India.

Desai in office as prime minister.

Early career

Born in present-day Gujarat, Desai joined the civil service of Bombay Presidency before resigning in 1930 to join Gandhi's civil disobedience movement; he was repeatedly imprisoned under British rule. After independence he became chief minister of Bombay State (1952–1956) and then a senior Union minister, holding finance and other portfolios under Jawaharlal Nehru and Lal Bahadur Shastri. A leading figure of the Congress party's conservative wing — ironically dubbed the "Syndicate" era rival of Indira Gandhi — he lost the leadership contests of 1966 and later split with her, and was imprisoned during the Emergency of 1975–77.

Prime minister

After the Janata coalition's victory in the 1977 elections, Desai, aged 81, became prime minister. His government restored democratic freedoms curtailed during the Emergency, pursued peace with Pakistan and China, and maintained strict personal austerity and prohibitionist policies. Beset by coalition infighting, his government fell in July 1979 when Charan Singh split the party; Desai retired from politics.

Personal life and legacy

A strict Gandhian, Desai was a vegetarian teetotaller famous for practising and publicly advocating urine therapy, a habit that attracted widespread commentary. He received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award, in 1991, and Pakistan's Nishan-e-Pakistan; he died in 1995 aged 99, among the longest-lived heads of government in history.
