The Willamette Valley AVA is an American Viticultural Area in the U.S. state of Oregon, encompassing the Willamette Valley drainage basin of the Willamette River. Established in 1983, it was Oregon's first AVA and remains the largest wine region in the state, stretching roughly from Portland in the north to Eugene in the south, bounded by the Coast Range to the west and the Cascade Range to the east.

Climate and soils

The valley has a mild, maritime-influenced climate with cool, wet winters and warm, dry summers. Much of the vineyard land lies on hillsides of marine sedimentary and volcanic basalt soils, such as the red Jory soil. The region is best known internationally for its Pinot noir, which thrives in the cool climate.

Sub-appellations

Several nested AVAs lie within the Willamette Valley, including the Dundee Hills, Eola-Amity Hills, McMinnville, Ribbon Ridge, Yamhill-Carlton, Chehalem Mountains, and Van Duzer Corridor AVAs.
