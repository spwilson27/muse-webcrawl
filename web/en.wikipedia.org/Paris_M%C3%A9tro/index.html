The Paris Métro (French: Métro de Paris) is the rapid transit system serving Paris and its inner suburbs. It is famous for its density within the city limits, its uniform architecture, and its Art Nouveau entrances designed by Hector Guimard.

History

The first line opened on 19 July 1900 during the Paris World's Fair, running from Porte Maillot to Porte de Vincennes. The network expanded rapidly under engineer Fulgence Bienvenüe, and most of the core system was complete by the 1920s. Later extensions reached into the suburbs, and the fully automated Line 1 and the driverless Line 14 (opened 1998) marked new technological phases.

Network

The system has 16 lines, numbered 1 to 14 (with two minor lines, 3bis and 7bis), serving some 320 stations. It is one of the busiest metro systems in Europe. Trains run on rubber tyres on several lines, a technology pioneered in Paris. The Grand Paris Express project is adding four new orbital lines (15–18).

Architecture

Station design is famously consistent: white beveled tiles, and the iconic "édicule" entrances in cast iron and glass. Signage uses the Parisine typeface.
