MECP2
Full nameMethyl-CpG binding protein 2
Gene locationX chromosome, Xq28
FunctionChromatin-binding transcriptional regulator
Associated disorderRett syndrome

MECP2 (methyl-CpG binding protein 2) is a gene that encodes a protein of the same name, found predominantly in mature nerve cells, where it is believed to be important in regulating the expression of other genes. The MeCP2 protein binds to methylated DNA and interacts with complexes involved in chromatin organisation and transcriptional repression, and it is abundant in neurons, approaching the abundance of histone proteins in the neuronal nucleus.

MeCP2 binds methylated CpG sites through its methyl-binding domain and recruits co-repressor complexes.

Function

MeCP2 contains a methyl-CpG-binding domain (MBD) and a transcriptional repression domain (TRD). It preferentially binds methylated DNA, including methylated CA sequences enriched in neurons, and recruits co-repressors such as the NCoR/SMRT complex. Rather than acting as a simple on/off switch for a few genes, MeCP2 appears to dampen transcriptional noise across long genes that are important for neuronal function, helping to fine-tune gene expression in the brain.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in MECP2 cause Rett syndrome, a severe neurodevelopmental disorder that primarily affects girls: development appears normal for the first 6–18 months, followed by regression of speech and motor skills, seizures, and characteristic hand stereotypies. Because the gene lies on the X chromosome, most males with severe loss-of-function mutations do not survive infancy. Conversely, duplication of the MECP2 locus causes MECP2 duplication syndrome, mainly in boys, demonstrating that the brain is sensitive to both too little and too much MeCP2.
