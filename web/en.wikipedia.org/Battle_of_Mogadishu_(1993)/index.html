Battle of Mogadishu (1993)

The Battle of Mogadishu (3–4 October 1993), known in Somali as Maalintii Rangers ("Day of the Rangers"), was a battle in Mogadishu, Somalia, between United States forces — supported by United Nations Operation in Somalia II (UNOSOM II) troops — and Somali militiamen loyal to Mohamed Farrah Aidid. It is better known in the United States as the Black Hawk Down incident, after the two UH-60 Black Hawk helicopters shot down during the fighting.

Background

Following the collapse of the Somali government of Siad Barre, civil war and famine prompted a US-led humanitarian intervention in December 1992 (Operation Restore Hope). By mid-1993, relations between the UN mission and Aidid's Somali National Alliance had deteriorated into open hostilities. Task Force Ranger, an American special operations unit, was deployed to capture Aidid and his lieutenants.

The battle

On the afternoon of 3 October 1993, Task Force Ranger launched a daylight raid on a building in central Mogadishu to seize two of Aidid's top aides. The assault force captured the targets, but rocket-propelled grenades brought down two Black Hawk helicopters, Super Six-One and Super Six-Four. A rescue effort turned into an overnight siege as Somali militia and armed civilians converged on the crash sites. Chief Warrant Officer Michael Durant was captured; Master Sergeant Gary Gordon and Sergeant First Class Randy Shughart were killed defending the second crash site and were posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor. A relief column of Pakistani, Malaysian and US forces finally extracted the surrounded troops on the morning of 4 October.

Casualties and aftermath

Eighteen American soldiers were killed and 73 wounded; one Malaysian soldier was killed and several Pakistani troops wounded. Somali casualties were estimated at several hundred to over a thousand killed and wounded. Television images of dead American soldiers being dragged through the streets produced enormous political pressure in the United States, and President Bill Clinton ordered the withdrawal of US forces from Somalia by March 1994. The battle was later chronicled in Mark Bowden's book Black Hawk Down (1999) and Ridley Scott's film of the same name (2001).
