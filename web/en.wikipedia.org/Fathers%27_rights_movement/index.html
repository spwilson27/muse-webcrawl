Fathers' rights movement

The fathers' rights movement advocates for equal parental rights in family law.

The fathers' rights movement is a social movement whose members are primarily interested in issues related to family law, including child custody and child support, that affect fathers and their children. Many of its members are fathers who desire to share the parenting of their children equally with their children's mothers — either after divorce or as unwed fathers — and children of divorced or separated parents who want to be raised by both parents.

The movement traces its origins to the 1960s, when divorced men's groups began organising in the United States and the United Kingdom. It has since spread internationally, with organisations in countries including Australia, Canada, Germany, India, Italy, and Spain.

Issues

Fathers' rights advocates argue that family courts are biased against fathers in custody disputes, and they campaign for reforms including a legal presumption of shared parenting, enforcement of contact orders, reform of child support guidelines, and measures against parental alienation and false allegations in custody proceedings. Critics of the movement argue that it sometimes understates issues of domestic violence and children's welfare in custody decisions.

Notable groups

Organisations associated with the movement have included Families Need Fathers in the United Kingdom, Fathers 4 Justice (known for high-profile protest stunts), and various national fathers' associations. The movement is distinct from, but sometimes overlaps with, the wider men's rights movement.
