Bouldering

Bouldering is a form of rock climbing performed on small rock formations or artificial climbing walls without the use of ropes or harnesses. Climbs, called "problems," are short sequences of moves, usually ascended to heights of a few meters, with crash pads placed below to cushion falls.

History

Bouldering emerged as a distinct discipline in the late 19th century in Britain and France and was developed into a serious pursuit by practitioners such as John Gill in the United States in the 1950s and 1960s, who introduced gymnastic techniques and chalk. It grew rapidly with the spread of indoor climbing gyms in the 1990s and 2000s.

Grading and competition

Boulder problems are graded using the V-scale in North America (from VB/V0 upward) or the Font scale in Europe. Climbers may be assisted by spotters who guide a falling climber onto the pads. Bouldering made its Olympic debut as part of the combined sport climbing event at the Tokyo 2020 Games, and was contested as a separate discipline alongside lead climbing at Paris 2024.
