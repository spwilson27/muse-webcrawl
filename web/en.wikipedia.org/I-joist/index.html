I-joist

An I-joist is an engineered wood product with an I-shaped cross-section, consisting of top and bottom flanges — typically laminated veneer lumber or solid sawn timber — joined by a web of oriented strand board or plywood. I-joists are widely used in residential and light commercial floor and roof framing.

Advantages

I-joists are stronger and stiffer per unit weight than conventional sawn lumber, can span longer distances, and are less prone to warping, twisting, shrinking, and squeaking. Because the web can be pre-drilled or knocked out for services, plumbing and wiring installation is simplified.

History

The product was introduced in 1969 by the Trus Joist company, and engineered wood joists became a mainstream alternative to dimensional lumber in North American house construction during the 1990s and 2000s.
