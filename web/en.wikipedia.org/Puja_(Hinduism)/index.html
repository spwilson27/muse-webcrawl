Puja (Hinduism)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Puja
Sanskrit: पूजा (pūjā)
Religion: Hinduism (also Jainism, Buddhism)
Elements: invocation, offerings, aarti, prasad
Settings: home shrines, temples, festivals

Puja (Sanskrit: पूजा, romanized: pūjā) is a worship ritual performed by Hindus to offer devotional homage and prayer to one or more deities, to host and honour a guest, or to spiritually celebrate an occasion. The practice is performed in homes and at temples, on occasions ranging from daily devotion to major festivals.

Structure of the ritual

Although forms vary widely by region and tradition, a puja typically treats the deity as an honoured royal guest. Standard elements include invocation of the deity into an image (murti) or symbol, bathing and dressing the image, offerings of flowers, incense, light, food and water, the waving of lamps in aarti, and distribution of the offered food as prasad to worshippers. The classical scheme lists sixteen services (shodasha upachara), from offering a seat to ceremonial dismissal.

Home and temple worship

Daily puja in the home shrine is often brief, centred on a lamp, flowers and a short prayer. Temple pujas are performed by priests according to agamic texts. The climactic moment for devotees is darshan — the reciprocal act of seeing and being seen by the deity.

Festivals

Large public pujas mark festivals such as Durga Puja, Ganesh Chaturthi and Diwali, when temporary shrines (pandals) are erected and images are later immersed in water (visarjan).

Etymology and origins

The origins of the word pūjā are debated among scholars; the practice developed in the first millennium CE alongside devotional (bhakti) movements and image worship, supplementing the older Vedic fire sacrifice (yajna).
