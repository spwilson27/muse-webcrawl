Mozi

Mozi (Chinese: 墨子; pinyin: Mòzǐ; Wade–Giles: Mo Tzu; /ˈmoʊˈtsiː/; c. 470 – c. 391 BC), original name Mo Di (墨翟), was a Chinese philosopher during the Hundred Schools of Thought period, and the founder of the Mohist school of thought.

Life

Little is recorded of Mozi's life. He was probably born in Tengzhou, in modern Shandong province, though some sources say the state of Lu. He traveled widely, advocating his doctrines to rulers of the Warring States and organizing his followers into a disciplined group skilled in defensive warfare.

Philosophy

Mozi taught impartial care (兼愛, jian'ai) — that people should care equally for all others regardless of their relationship. He opposed offensive warfare, elaborate funerals, and music and ritual that he considered wasteful, measuring practices by whether they benefited the people. He believed in a supreme Heaven (天, Tian) that wills righteousness, and in ghosts and spirits that reward and punish human conduct.

The Mozi text

The book known as the Mozi contains the core doctrines of the school, later chapters on logic, ethics, optics, mechanics and defensive warfare. Mozi's chapters on optics contain the earliest known description of the camera obscura principle.
