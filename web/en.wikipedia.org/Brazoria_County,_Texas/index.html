Brazoria County is a county in the U.S. state of Texas, located on the Gulf Coast within the Houston–The Woodlands–Sugar Land metropolitan area. Its county seat is Angleton, and its largest city is Pearland. The county is named for the Brazos River, which flows through it; "Brazoria" was explained as a name coined from Spanish brazos (arms). The 2020 census put the population at roughly 372,000.


The Brazoria County Courthouse in Angleton.

History

Brazoria County was one of the original counties of the Republic of Texas, created in 1836. The town of Brazoria was an early port and Stephen F. Austin's colony had its headquarters in the area; San Felipe de Austin and the first capital at Columbia (now West Columbia) were nearby. The region was heavily involved in the Texas Revolution.

Geography

The county covers about 1,407 square miles of coastal plain, including wetlands, bays, and Gulf of Mexico shoreline. The Brazos and San Bernard rivers cross it. Much of the land lies at low elevation and is subject to flooding from tropical storms and hurricanes.

Economy

Petrochemical manufacturing is a major employer, centred on Freeport's port and chemical complex. The county also has substantial agriculture, commercial fishing, and tourism along its beaches, and many residents commute to jobs across the Houston area.

Communities

Cities include Pearland, Alvin, Angleton, Lake Jackson, Freeport, Clute, Manvel, and West Columbia. The county is served by several school districts and by Brazosport College in Lake Jackson.
