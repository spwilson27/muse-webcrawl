Tumor

Tumours may be benign or malignant

A tumor (American spelling) or tumour (British spelling) is a mass of tissue that forms when cells grow and divide more than they should, or do not die when they should. In the broader medical sense, a neoplasm is an abnormal growth of tissue that is uncoordinated with the growth of normal tissue. The word derives from the Latin tumor, "swelling".

Classification

Tumors are classified as benign or malignant. Benign tumors grow slowly, remain localised, and do not invade surrounding tissue or spread to other parts of the body; examples include fibromas, lipomas and many moles. Malignant tumors — cancers — grow more aggressively, can invade nearby tissues, and may metastasise, spreading through the blood or lymphatic system to distant sites.

Causes

Tumors arise when the normal controls on cell division fail, usually because of accumulated genetic mutations. Mutations may be inherited or caused by environmental factors such as carcinogenic chemicals, ionising radiation, certain viruses (for example human papillomavirus), and chronic inflammation. Tumor suppressor genes normally restrain growth, while oncogenes promote it; damage to these regulatory genes is central to cancer development.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis may involve imaging (such as CT, MRI or ultrasound), biopsy and histopathological examination, and molecular testing. Treatment depends on the type, stage and location of the tumor and may include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, or combinations of these.
