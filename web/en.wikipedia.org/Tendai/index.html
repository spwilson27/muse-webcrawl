Tendai

Tendai (天台宗, Tendai-shū), also known as the Tendai Lotus School, is a Mahayana Buddhist tradition officially established in Japan in 806 by the monk Saichō (767–822), based on the Chinese Tiantai school founded by Zhiyi (538–597). Tendai emphasises the Lotus Sutra as the supreme teaching of the Buddha and holds that all beings possess Buddha-nature and can attain enlightenment.

History

Saichō founded Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei, northeast of Kyoto, which became one of the great centres of Japanese Buddhism. Tendai incorporated esoteric (mikkyō) practices, Pure Land devotion, Zen meditation and precepts into a comprehensive system. Because of its inclusive curriculum, Mount Hiei trained the founders of most later Japanese Buddhist schools: Hōnen and Shinran of Pure Land and Jōdo Shinshū, Eisai of Rinzai Zen, Dōgen of Sōtō Zen, and Nichiren all studied there.

Practices

Tendai practice includes sutra study, meditation (shikan, "calming and insight"), esoteric rituals and nembutsu recitation. In the modern era, Tendai monks of Mount Hiei are also known for the kaihōgyō, the gruelling thousand-day circumambulation of the mountain undertaken by the "marathon monks".
