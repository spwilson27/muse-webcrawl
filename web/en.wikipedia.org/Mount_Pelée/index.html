Mount Pelée

Elevation1,397 m (4,583 ft)
LocationMartinique, French West Indies
TypeStratovolcano
Last eruption1929–1932

Mount Pelée (French: Montagne Pelée, "bald mountain") is an active stratovolcano at the northern end of Martinique, an island and overseas department of France in the Caribbean. It is best known for its eruption of 8 May 1902, which destroyed the town of Saint-Pierre and killed roughly 30,000 people — the deadliest volcanic event of the 20th century.

The 1902 eruption

After weeks of escalating activity that authorities largely ignored — an election was imminent and officials discouraged evacuation — a lateral blast sent a pyroclastic surge (later termed a nuée ardente) over Saint-Pierre in minutes. Only a handful of townspeople survived, most famously Ludger Sylbaris, a prisoner held in an underground cell, and Léon Compère-Léandre. The eruption type is now called "Peléan", and the disaster helped give birth to modern volcanology; the volcano's growing lava spine, which rose hundreds of metres before collapsing, was studied in detail.

Later activity

An eruption from 1929 to 1932 built a dome in the crater but caused no deaths, as the area was evacuated promptly. The volcano is monitored by the Observatoire Volcanologique et Sismologique de Martinique.
