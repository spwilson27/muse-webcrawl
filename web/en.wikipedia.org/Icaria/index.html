Icaria

Icaria, also spelled Ikaria (Greek: Ικαρία), is a Greek island in the Aegean Sea, 19 km (12 mi) southwest of Samos. It is named after Icarus, the young man in Greek mythology who, according to legend, fell into the sea near the island when the wax of his wings melted. Administratively it forms a regional unit within the North Aegean region, with its capital and main port at Agios Kirykos.

Longevity

Icaria has attracted international attention as one of the world's "Blue Zones", regions where inhabitants live unusually long lives. Studies have noted high rates of nonagenarians and centenarians, attributed to diet, physical activity, strong community ties and an unhurried pace of life. Traditional festivals (panigyria) involve dancing through the night.

History

The island's remoteness made it a place of internal exile during the Greek Civil War in the late 1940s, when thousands of communists were deported there. Icaria is mountainous and rugged, with a population of roughly 8,000–10,000, and is known for its hot springs, red wine and self-reliant local culture.
