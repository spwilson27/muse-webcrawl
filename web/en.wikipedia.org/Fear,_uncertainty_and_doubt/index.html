Fear, uncertainty and doubt (FUD) is a disinformation strategy used in sales, marketing, public relations, politics, polling, and cults. FUD is generally a strategy to influence perception by disseminating negative and dubious or false information, and a manifestation of the appeal to fear.

The term originated to describe disinformation tactics in the computer hardware industry but has since been applied more broadly. While similar formulations existed earlier, the term "fear, uncertainty and doubt" appeared in print by the 1920s; its association with computing is often credited to Gene Amdahl, who used it to describe IBM's competitive tactics after leaving the company to found Amdahl Corporation in 1970.

Definition

FUD is a form of mistrust in messaging: it aims to make customers or voters hesitate before switching to a rival product or position by raising doubts about the rival's viability, reliability, or safety, rather than by making a positive case for one's own offering. It is frequently cited in the technology industry, including in debates involving Microsoft and open-source software, and in discussions of cryptocurrency, where "FUD" is used to dismiss negative news about an asset.
