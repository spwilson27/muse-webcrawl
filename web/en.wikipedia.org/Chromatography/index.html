Chromatography

Chromatography is a family of laboratory techniques for separating mixtures, based on the different speeds at which components move through a stationary phase under the influence of a mobile phase.

History and types

Mikhail Tsvet invented the technique around 1900 to separate plant pigments, giving it its name from the Greek for colour writing. Major forms include paper, thin-layer, gas, and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), as well as ion-exchange and size-exclusion methods.

Applications

Chromatography is essential in chemical analysis, pharmaceuticals, forensics, environmental monitoring and the purification of proteins and other products.
