Financial statement

Financial statements are formal records of the financial activities and position of a business, person or other entity. Prepared under accounting standards such as IFRS or US GAAP, they are used by investors, lenders, regulators and managers to assess performance and solvency.

Main statements

The core set comprises the balance sheet (statement of financial position), the income statement (profit and loss), the statement of cash flows, and the statement of changes in equity, together with explanatory notes.

Analysis

Users apply ratio analysis — liquidity, leverage, profitability and efficiency ratios — and trend and common-size analysis to interpret statements. Public companies' statements are typically audited by independent accountants to provide assurance of fair presentation.

History

Double-entry bookkeeping, codified by Luca Pacioli in 1494, underlies modern statements; standardisation accelerated with securities regulation in the 20th century and the creation of bodies such as the IASB and FASB.
