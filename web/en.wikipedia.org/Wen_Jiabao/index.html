Wen Jiabao

Wen Jiabao (born 15 September 1942) is a retired Chinese politician who served as the 6th Premier of China from 2003 to 2013. As head of government under President Hu Jintao, he oversaw a decade of rapid economic growth, managed China's response to the 2008 global financial crisis, and cultivated an image as a populist, compassionate leader — earning the nickname "Grandpa Wen."

Early career

Born in Tianjin, Wen trained as a geologist at the Beijing Institute of Geology and spent fourteen years working in Gansu province. He rose through the party apparatus to head the Communist Party's General Office, survived the 1989 political turmoil despite accompanying Zhao Ziyang in his sympathetic visit to Tiananmen protesters, and became vice premier in 1998, supervising flood relief and financial reform.

Premiership

As premier, Wen abolished the agricultural tax, expanded rural health insurance, promoted development of China's interior, and launched a four-trillion-yuan stimulus package in 2008. He was praised for his rapid, emotive responses to disasters such as the 2008 Sichuan earthquake. In 2012 a New York Times report on his family's hidden wealth was censored in China. He retired in March 2013, succeeded by Li Keqiang.
