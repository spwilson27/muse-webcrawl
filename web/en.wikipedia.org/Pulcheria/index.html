Pulcheria

Aelia Pulcheria
Born19 January 398/399, Constantinople
DiedJuly 453, Constantinople
OfficeAugusta; empress consort (450–453)

Aelia Pulcheria (398/399 – 453) was an Eastern Roman empress, daughter of Emperor Arcadius and elder sister of Theodosius II. She took a vow of virginity, became Augusta in 414 and exercised great influence over her brother's court and over the religious politics of the empire.

Influence

Pulcheria promoted devotion to the Virgin Mary as Theotokos and opposed Nestorianism, contributing to the condemnations at the Council of Ephesus (431). After Theodosius II died in 450, she married the general Marcian, who became emperor, on condition the marriage remain chaste; together they convened the Council of Chalcedon (451), whose definition of Christ's two natures she supported. She is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic churches.
