Giorgetto Giugiaro (born 7 August 1938) is an Italian automobile designer widely regarded as one of the most influential car stylists of the twentieth century. In 1999 he was named Car Designer of the Century by a jury of international automotive journalists. He is the founder of the Turin-based design and engineering company Italdesign Giugiaro.

Early life and career

Giugiaro was born in Garessio, in the Piedmont region of Italy, into a family of painters. He studied art and technical drawing in Turin and was hired by Fiat's styling centre while still a teenager, after his sketches were noticed at an exhibition. In 1959 he moved to Bertone, where, under Nuccio Bertone, he designed cars including the Alfa Romeo Giulia Sprint GT. In 1965 he became head of styling at Ghia, then left in 1968 to found his own studio, Ital Styling, which soon became Italdesign.

Notable designs

Giugiaro's production designs include the Volkswagen Golf Mk1 (1974), whose crisp folded-paper styling defined the modern hatchback; the Lotus Esprit; the DeLorean DMC-12, famous for its stainless-steel body and gull-wing doors; the BMW M1; the Fiat Panda and Fiat Uno; the Lancia Delta; the Saab 9000; and the first-generation Lexus GS. His concept cars, such as the Maserati Boomerang and the Alfa Romeo Iguana, set the wedge-shaped direction of 1970s sports-car design.

Beyond automobiles, Giugiaro designed camera bodies for Nikon, including the F3, F4 and F5 professional models, as well as watches, furniture, pasta shapes and the Seiko Speedmaster chronograph.

Later work

In 2010 Italdesign Giugiaro became part of the Volkswagen Group through Lamborghini, and in 2015 Giugiaro and his son Fabrizio left the company, later founding GFG Style in Turin to continue independent design work.
