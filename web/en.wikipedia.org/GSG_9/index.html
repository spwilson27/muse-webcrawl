GSG 9 (Grenzschutzgruppe 9, "Border Protection Group 9", today officially GSG 9 der Bundespolizei) is the elite police tactical unit of the German Federal Police. It was created in response to the Munich massacre at the 1972 Summer Olympics, in which a bungled rescue attempt by Bavarian police contributed to the deaths of eleven Israeli athletes and coaches taken hostage by the Palestinian group Black September.

Formation

Because the German constitution barred the military from domestic operations, the federal government created GSG 9 within the Border Protection Force under commander Ulrich Wegener. The unit was formally established on 17 April 1973; its "9" designation reflected its place as the ninth border protection group.

Notable operations

GSG 9 achieved worldwide fame on 18 October 1977, when its commandos stormed the hijacked Lufthansa Flight 181 ("Landshut") at Mogadishu, Somalia, freeing all 86 hostages and killing three of the four hijackers, with no hostages lost. Other operations include foiling the 1993 Bad Kleinen operation aftermath controversies, hostage rescues abroad, and the 2009 attempted rescue from the pirated ship Hansa Stavanger, which was called off. GSG 9 also trains and advises counterpart units internationally.

Organisation

The unit comprises several operational sub-units specialising in land, maritime, and airborne operations, plus technical and support elements. Its members are volunteers from the Federal Police who pass a demanding selection and training programme of around ten months.
