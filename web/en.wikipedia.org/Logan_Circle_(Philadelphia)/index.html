Logan Circle, also known as Logan Square, is an open-space park in Center City Philadelphia, at the centre of a traffic circle on the Benjamin Franklin Parkway. It is one of the five original squares laid out in William Penn's 1682 plan for the city and is named for James Logan, a statesman of the colonial era.

Features

At its centre stands the Swann Memorial Fountain, designed by Wilson Eyre Jr. with sculptor Alexander Stirling Calder and completed in 1924; its three figures represent Philadelphia's rivers: the Delaware, the Schuylkill and the Wissahickon. Around the circle stand major institutions including the Cathedral Basilica of Saints Peter and Paul, the Free Library of Philadelphia, the Academy of Natural Sciences and the Franklin Institute nearby.

History

The square was used in the early republic for public executions and later became a residential square. In the 1910s–1920s it was reshaped as a circle on the diagonal Benjamin Franklin Parkway, modelled in part on Parisian boulevards. It anchors the Museum District and is a gathering point for parades and festivals.
