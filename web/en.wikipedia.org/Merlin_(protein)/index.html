Merlin (protein)

Merlin (also called neurofibromin 2 or schwannomin) is a cytoskeletal protein that in humans is encoded by the NF2 gene on chromosome 22. It acts as a tumor suppressor: loss-of-function mutations in NF2 cause neurofibromatosis type 2, a disorder characterised by bilateral vestibular schwannomas and other tumors of the nervous system, including meningiomas and ependymomas.

Structure and function

Merlin belongs to the ERM family (ezrin, radixin, moesin) of proteins that link the plasma membrane to the actin cytoskeleton; its name is an acronym for "moesin-ezrin-radixin-like protein". Unlike its relatives, which promote growth, merlin restrains cell proliferation. It mediates contact-dependent inhibition of growth, regulates receptor tyrosine kinase signalling at the cell surface, and serves as an upstream activator of the Hippo signalling pathway, which controls organ size through the YAP/TAZ transcriptional coactivators.

Clinical significance

People inheriting one defective copy of NF2 develop neurofibromatosis type 2 after somatic loss of the second copy in individual cells. Sporadic schwannomas and a large fraction of sporadic meningiomas also carry NF2 mutations. Understanding merlin's signalling roles has motivated drug development aimed at pathways it normally restrains, including Hippo–YAP and mTOR signalling.
