George de Mestral (19 June 1907 – 8 February 1990) was a Swiss electrical engineer who invented the hook-and-loop fastener, which he commercialised under the name Velcro — a portmanteau of the French words velours (velvet) and crochet (hook).

Invention

An avid hunter, de Mestral noticed after a 1941 walk in the Alps how stubbornly the burrs of the burdock plant clung to his clothes and his dog's fur. Examining them under a microscope, he saw that the burrs were covered in hundreds of tiny hooks that caught on loops in fabric. He spent nearly a decade developing a practical fastening system: one strip of tiny nylon hooks and another of small loops, which grip firmly when pressed together but peel apart with a characteristic ripping sound. He filed his patent in 1951; it was granted in 1955.

Velcro companies

De Mestral founded companies in Switzerland and abroad to manufacture the fastener, which he trademarked as Velcro. Production initially struggled because weaving nylon hooks at scale required new machinery, but the fastener found markets in clothing, medicine, the military, and aerospace — NASA used it extensively in the Apollo programme to anchor equipment in zero gravity. De Mestral sold his equity in the business in the late 1970s. He was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 1999, and his method is celebrated as a classic example of biomimicry.
