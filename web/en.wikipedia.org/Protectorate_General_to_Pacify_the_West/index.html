Protectorate General to Pacify the West

The Anxi Protectorate administered Tang rule in the Western Regions.

The Protectorate General to Pacify the West, known in Chinese as the Anxi Protectorate (安西都護府), was a military and administrative government established by the Tang dynasty of China in 640 to control the Tarim Basin and the wider Western Regions (modern Xinjiang and parts of Central Asia). Its seat moved over time, eventually settling at Kucha.

History

After Tang forces defeated the Western Turks and the oasis kingdoms, the protectorate supervised the "Four Garrisons of Anxi" — Kucha, Kashgar, Khotan, and Karashahr — through which Tang influence extended along the Silk Road, at its height reaching toward Tokhara and the Pamirs.

Decline

The An Lushan Rebellion (755–763) forced the Tang to withdraw troops from the northwest, and Tibetan and Uyghur pressure cut off the garrisons. Remarkably, isolated Tang garrisons held out for decades; the last of the Four Garrisons fell to the Tibetans around 790, ending the protectorate.
