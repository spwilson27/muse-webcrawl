Hendrix College is a private liberal arts college in Conway, Arkansas, United States. Founded in 1876 and affiliated with the United Methodist Church, it is a residential undergraduate institution known for strong academics, a high proportion of students who go on to graduate and professional schools, and its "Odyssey" program of engaged learning.

History

The college was founded in 1876 at Altus, Arkansas, as Central Institute, a primary school; it became a college in 1881 and was purchased by the Methodist Episcopal Church, South, in 1884, when it was renamed in honour of Bishop Eugene Russell Hendrix. It moved to Conway in 1890. Hendrix merged with Henderson-Brown College in 1929 and absorbed Galloway College in 1933, becoming Arkansas's consolidated Methodist college. In the 20th and 21st centuries it built a national reputation among liberal arts colleges, appearing regularly in guides such as Colleges That Change Lives.

Academics and campus life

Hendrix offers bachelor's degrees in the arts and sciences across some three dozen majors, with a curriculum emphasising breadth, writing and undergraduate research. The Odyssey program requires students to complete hands-on learning experiences in categories such as undergraduate research, service to the world, artistic creativity, global awareness, professional and leadership development, and special projects. The tree-shaded brick campus in Conway, about 30 miles northwest of Little Rock, houses roughly a thousand students, nearly all living on campus. Hendrix teams, the Warriors, compete in NCAA Division III athletics.

Traditions

Distinctive traditions include "Shirttail Serenades" and the birthday dunking of students in the campus fountain. The college's motto, taken from Horace, is Unto the Whole Person (Latin: Totum Hominem), reflecting its aim of educating students intellectually, socially and spiritually.
