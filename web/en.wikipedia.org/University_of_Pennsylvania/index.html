University of Pennsylvania

The University of Pennsylvania (Penn or UPenn) is a private Ivy League research university in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, United States. Founded in 1740 by Benjamin Franklin and members of the Junto, it is one of the nine colonial colleges and was chartered in 1755.

Penn has four undergraduate schools and twelve graduate and professional schools. Its notable schools include the Wharton School, founded in 1881 as the first collegiate business school; the Perelman School of Medicine, the oldest medical school in the United States; and Penn Law. The university enrolls more than 28,000 students and is consistently ranked among the top universities in the world.

History

Franklin's 1749 plan for the institution, published as Proposals Relating to the Education of Youth in Pensilvania, envisioned a practical, nonsectarian curriculum preparing students for business and public service — innovative at a time when colonial colleges primarily trained clergy. The university later became the first institution in the United States to offer studies in both the undergraduate and professional levels and to use the name "university."

Campus and athletics

Penn's campus occupies about 300 acres in the University City section of West Philadelphia. Its athletic teams, the Quakers, compete in the Ivy League; historic venues include Franklin Field, one of the oldest stadiums in American football, and the Palestra basketball arena.
