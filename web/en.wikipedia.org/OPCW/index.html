The Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) is an intergovernmental organisation and the implementing body for the Chemical Weapons Convention, which entered into force on 29 April 1997. The OPCW, with its 193 member states, is seated in The Hague, Netherlands, and oversees the global endeavour to permanently eliminate chemical weapons.

Mandate

The organisation inspects and verifies the destruction of declared chemical weapons stockpiles and production facilities, monitors the chemical industry to prevent the diversion of toxic chemicals, and provides assistance and protection to states parties threatened by chemical weapons. Its inspectors conduct routine and challenge inspections under the Convention's verification regime.

Nobel Peace Prize

In 2013 the OPCW was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize "for its extensive efforts to eliminate chemical weapons," at a time when its inspectors were overseeing the destruction of Syria's declared arsenal during that country's civil war.

Structure

The OPCW's principal organs are the Conference of the States Parties, the Executive Council and the Technical Secretariat, headed by a Director-General. Fernando Arias of Spain has served as Director-General since 2018.
