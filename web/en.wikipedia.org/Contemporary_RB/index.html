Contemporary R&B

Contemporary R&B (usually abbreviated as R&B) is a popular music genre that combines rhythm and blues with elements of pop, soul, funk, hip hop and electronic music. The genre features smooth, lush production, drum-machine-backed rhythms, pitch-corrected vocals and melismatic singing.

History

Contemporary R&B emerged in the late 1970s and 1980s as disco declined and artists such as Michael Jackson, Prince, Whitney Houston and Luther Vandross fused soul singing with slick pop production. In the 1990s, producers like Teddy Riley (new jack swing), Babyface, and Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis dominated, while hip hop soul and neo soul offered grittier and more traditionalist alternatives.

2000s to present

The 2000s brought crunk-influenced and electronic R&B from artists such as Usher, Beyoncé and Rihanna. In the 2010s the genre diversified: alternative R&B artists including Frank Ocean, The Weeknd, SZA and Daniel Caesar adopted atmospheric, minimalist sounds and confessional lyrics, reshaping the mainstream.

Characteristics

Contemporary R&B is typically built around a steady mid-tempo groove, layered keyboards and synths, prominent bass, and vocals that foreground runs and falsetto. Lyrically it centres on love, relationships and nightlife.
