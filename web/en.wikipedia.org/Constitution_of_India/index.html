The Constitution of India (IAST: Bhāratīya Saṁvidhāna) is the supreme law of India. It lays down the framework defining fundamental political principles, the structure and powers of government institutions, and the fundamental rights and duties of citizens. It is the longest written national constitution in the world.

History

The Constitution was drafted by the Constituent Assembly, first convened in December 1946, drawing on the Government of India Act 1935 and the constitutions of many countries. B. R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee and is widely regarded as its chief architect; Rajendra Prasad presided over the Assembly. It was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950 — Republic Day — replacing the Government of India Act as the governing document and making India a sovereign democratic republic.

Features

The Preamble declares India a "sovereign socialist secular democratic republic" (the words "socialist" and "secular" were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976) securing justice, liberty, equality and fraternity. Part III guarantees justiciable Fundamental Rights, including equality before law and freedoms of speech, religion and movement; Part IV contains non-justiciable Directive Principles of State Policy; Part IVA, added in 1976, lists Fundamental Duties. India is a union of states with a parliamentary system — a President as head of state and a Prime Minister as head of government — a bicameral Parliament at the centre, an independent judiciary headed by the Supreme Court, and single citizenship.

Amendments

The Constitution provides for amendment by Parliament under Article 368. It has been amended over 100 times. In Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), the Supreme Court held that Parliament may amend any provision but may not alter the Constitution's "basic structure".

Significance

At adoption it enfranchised all adults at once — a radical commitment to universal suffrage in a largely poor and illiterate population — and abolished untouchability (Article 17) while permitting affirmative-action reservations for historically disadvantaged communities.
