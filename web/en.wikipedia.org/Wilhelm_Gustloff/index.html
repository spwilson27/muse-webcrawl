Wilhelm Gustloff (30 January 1895 – 4 February 1936) was a German Nazi Party official who founded and led the Swiss branch of the NSDAP's foreign organisation (NSDAP/AO) in Davos. He was assassinated in 1936 by David Frankfurter, a Croatian Jewish medical student, an act that the Nazi regime exploited for propaganda and which became internationally notorious as a prelude to the escalating persecution of Jews in Germany.

Life

Born in Schwerin, Gustloff suffered from chronic lung problems and was unfit for military service in the First World War. He moved to Davos, Switzerland, for his health and worked at a meteorological observatory and later for a bank. A committed early member of the Nazi Party, he built up its organisation among German residents in Switzerland, recruiting thousands of members and distributing antisemitic propaganda, including editions of the Protocols of the Elders of Zion, which led to a libel trial in Bern in 1934–35 that found the work a forgery.

Assassination and aftermath

On 4 February 1936, David Frankfurter shot Gustloff five times in his Davos apartment, then surrendered to police, stating he acted because of the Nazi persecution of the Jews. Frankfurter was sentenced to 18 years in prison and pardoned in 1945. Gustloff received a state funeral in Schwerin attended by Adolf Hitler, and was glorified as a martyr (Blutzeuge) of the movement. The new Strength Through Joy cruise ship launched in 1937 was named MV Wilhelm Gustloff in his honour; its sinking by a Soviet submarine in January 1945, with the loss of an estimated 9,000 lives, remains the deadliest maritime disaster in history.
