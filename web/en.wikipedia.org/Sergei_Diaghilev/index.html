Sergei Diaghilev

Born31 March 1872, Selishchi, Russian Empire
Died19 August 1929, Venice, Italy
OccupationArt critic, patron, ballet impresario

Sergei Pavlovich Diaghilev (1872–1929) was a Russian art critic, patron and impresario who founded the Ballets Russes (1909–1929), arguably the most influential ballet company of the 20th century. Working in Paris, he brought together choreographers such as Fokine, Nijinsky, Massine and Balanchine, composers including Stravinsky, Debussy, Ravel and Prokofiev, and artists such as Bakst, Picasso and Matisse.

Legacy

Productions like The Firebird (1910), Petrushka (1911) and the scandalous The Rite of Spring (1913) transformed ballet into a total work of art and shaped modern dance, music and stage design. Diaghilev died in Venice in 1929; his company dissolved, but its dancers and choreographers seeded ballet companies worldwide.
