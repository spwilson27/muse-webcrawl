Xerox Corporation (doing business as Xerox) is an American corporation that sells print and digital document products and services worldwide. Founded in Rochester, New York, in 1906 as the Haloid Photographic Company, it took its current name from xerography, the dry-copying process whose commercialization made it one of the dominant technology companies of the twentieth century; in many countries "xerox" became a generic word for photocopying.

History

Haloid licensed Chester Carlson's xerography patents in 1947 and introduced the Xerox 914 in 1959, the first plain-paper photocopier, which was leased rather than sold and proved enormously profitable. At its Palo Alto Research Center (PARC), founded in 1970, Xerox researchers invented much of modern personal computing — the Alto computer with its graphical user interface, the mouse, Ethernet, and laser printing — though the company famously failed to commercialize many of these innovations, which were later popularized by Apple and others.

Later years

Facing competition in a shrinking paper-document market, Xerox diversified into services, acquiring Affiliated Computer Services in 2010, then split the business in 2016–2017, spinning services into Conduent. A proposed merger with Fujifilm in 2018 was cancelled amid activist-investor opposition. The company continues to sell printers, multifunction devices, and workplace software and services.
