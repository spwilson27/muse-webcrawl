The University of Western Ontario (UWO), branded as Western University since 2012, is a public research university in London, Ontario, Canada. Founded in 1878 by Bishop Isaac Hellmuth and the Anglican Diocese of Huron as the Western University of London, Ontario, it became non-denominational in 1908 and is now one of Canada's largest research universities, with some 42,000 students.

Campus and organisation

Western's main campus lies along the Thames River in north London and is known for its Collegiate Gothic architecture, including University College (1924) and Middlesex College. The university comprises 12 faculties and schools, including the Schulich School of Medicine & Dentistry, the Ivey Business School — renowned for its case-method teaching — the Faculty of Law, and the Don Wright Faculty of Music. Three affiliated university colleges — Brescia, Huron and King's — are federated with it.

Research and reputation

Western is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research universities. It is particularly noted for neuroscience and neuroimaging, musculoskeletal health, wind engineering (home of the Boundary Layer Wind Tunnel and the WindEEE Dome), and medical research. The university maintains one of the largest research incomes among Canadian universities, and its sports teams, the Mustangs, compete in U Sports; its football team has won multiple Vanier Cups.

History

The university awarded its first degrees in 1881. A medical school was added in 1881–82, and the institution grew substantially after the Second World War under presidents such as G. Edward Hall. In 2012 the university adopted "Western University" as its common name while retaining The University of Western Ontario as its legal name.
