Pentathlon

A pentathlon is a contest featuring five events. The name derives from the Greek pente (five) and athlon (competition). The original pentathlon was contested at the ancient Olympic Games from 708 BC and comprised the long jump, javelin throw, discus throw, a short foot race (the stadion) and wrestling; the winner was regarded as the supreme all-round athlete.

Modern forms

Several modern sports use the name:

  • Modern pentathlon, created by Pierre de Coubertin and held at the Olympic Games since 1912, combines fencing, freestyle swimming, equestrian show jumping, and a combined event of pistol shooting and cross-country running (the laser run). It was modelled on the skills of a 19th-century cavalry soldier.
  • Athletics pentathlon, an indoor combined event for women (60 m hurdles, high jump, shot put, long jump and 800 m), contested at the World Indoor Championships; a five-event outdoor version was replaced by the heptathlon in 1981.
  • Military and naval pentathlon and aeronautical pentathlon, multi-sport competitions for service personnel.

Ancient competition

In the ancient Games the order and elimination format remain debated by scholars; some events may have served as preliminary rounds, with wrestling deciding the overall victor. Aristotle praised pentathletes as the most beautiful athletes because their bodies were adapted to both strength and speed.
