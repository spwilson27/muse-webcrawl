Elba (Italian: Isola d'Elba) is a Mediterranean island in Tuscany, Italy, about 10 km off the coastal town of Piombino. It is the largest island of the Tuscan Archipelago and the third-largest island in Italy, after Sicily and Sardinia. Much of Elba forms part of the Arcipelago Toscano National Park, established in 1996.

History

Elba was known to the Etruscans and Romans for its iron mines — its ancient names, Ilva and Aethalia, allude to ironworking and sparks from smelting. In the Middle Ages it was contested by Pisa, Genoa and Spain, and the Medici founded Portoferraio in 1548. The island is world-famous as the place of exile of Napoleon Bonaparte: under the Treaty of Fontainebleau (1814), Napoleon abdicated and was granted sovereignty over Elba, where he landed on 4 May 1814. He ruled the island as a miniature principality for 300 days, carrying out reforms, before escaping on 26 February 1815 for the Hundred Days that ended at Waterloo. His residences, the Villa dei Mulini in Portoferraio and Villa San Martino, are now museums.

Geography and economy

The island's western part is dominated by the granite massif of Monte Capanne. Elba's economy, once based on iron mining — the last mines closed in the early 1980s — now depends largely on tourism, drawn by its beaches, clear waters and Napoleonic sites. The island is divided into seven municipalities, of which Portoferraio is the chief port, connected by ferry to Piombino.
