State Emblem of the Soviet Union

The State Emblem of the Soviet Union (Russian: Государственный герб Советского Союза) was adopted in 1923 and used until the dissolution of the USSR in 1991. It was designed in the tradition of communist symbolism rather than classical heraldry, and its imagery embodied the ideology of the Soviet Union.

Description

The emblem depicts a hammer and sickle superimposed over a globe, framed by two ears of wheat wrapped in ribbons bearing the motto "Workers of the world, unite!" in the languages of the Soviet republics. Below the globe are rising sun rays, and above it a red five-pointed star. The number of ribbons changed over time as republics joined or left the union, reaching sixteen before stabilising at fifteen.

History

The emblem was approved by the Central Executive Committee on 6 July 1923 and written into the 1924 Soviet constitution. Successive versions adjusted the motto inscriptions: early versions carried six languages, and the final form, adopted in 1956, carried fifteen, matching the fifteen union republics.

Legacy

After 1991, the emblem was replaced in Russia by the double-headed eagle coat of arms. Several post-Soviet states retained Soviet-era emblems or elements of them, most notably Belarus, while the unrecognised republic of Transnistria kept the Moldavian SSR emblem.
