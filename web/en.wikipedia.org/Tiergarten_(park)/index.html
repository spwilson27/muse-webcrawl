Großer Tiergarten

LocationTiergarten, Mitte, Berlin, Germany
Area210 hectares (520 acres)

The Großer Tiergarten (German: [ˈɡʁoːsɐ ˈtiːɐ̯ˌɡaʁtn̩], simply "Tiergarten") is Berlin's most popular inner-city park, located in the district of the same name within the borough of Mitte. With 210 hectares (520 acres), it is among Germany's largest urban gardens, comparable in size to New York's Central Park relative to its city. Only the Tempelhofer Feld and Englischer Garten in Munich are larger German city parks.

History

The park originated in the 16th century as a hunting ground (Tiergarten means "animal garden" or game park) for the Electors of Brandenburg, enclosed and stocked with deer and other game. Under Frederick the Great in the 1740s, it began to be opened to the public and ornamental features were added. Between 1833 and 1838, landscape architect Peter Joseph Lenné redesigned it as an English-style landscape park.

During the Second World War the park was heavily damaged; its trees were largely felled for firewood in the harsh winter after the war, and much of the grounds were used as vegetable gardens. Replanting began in 1949 with donations of trees from other German cities. The park lay in the British sector of West Berlin during the Cold War.

Features

The Straße des 17. Juni boulevard runs east–west through the park, from the Brandenburg Gate to the Großer Stern roundabout, at whose center stands the Berlin Victory Column (Siegessäule). Other features include Bellevue Palace, official residence of the German Federal President; the English Garden; the Gas Lamps Open-Air Museum; and numerous monuments and memorials, including to composers of the First Viennese School. The Berlin Zoological Garden lies at the park's southwestern edge.

The park is transected by the Landwehr Canal in the south and is adjacent to the government district, Potsdamer Platz, and the Kulturforum.
