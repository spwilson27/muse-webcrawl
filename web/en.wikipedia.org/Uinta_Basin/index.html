Uinta Basin

The Uinta Basin (also spelled Uintah Basin) is a physiographic section of the Colorado Plateau and a geologic structural basin in eastern Utah and northwestern Colorado, in the western United States. It lies south of the Uinta Mountains and is drained principally by the Green River and its tributaries, including the Duchesne and White rivers. The principal communities are Vernal, Roosevelt, Duchesne and Price, Utah, and Rangely, Colorado.

Geology

The basin is an asymmetric syncline formed during the Laramide orogeny some 70–40 million years ago, when the adjacent Uinta Mountains were uplifted. In the Eocene epoch it held Lake Uinta, whose organic-rich sediments became the oil shales of the Green River Formation, one of the world's largest oil shale deposits. The basin also contains the world's principal deposits of gilsonite (uintaite), a glossy black hydrocarbon resin mined near Bonanza, Utah.

Energy production

Oil was discovered in the basin in 1948, and the Altamont–Bluebell field became a notable producer of waxy crude. Since the 2000s, horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing in formations such as the Uteland Butte have expanded output; because basin crude is waxy, it historically has been trucked or hauled by rail to refineries in Salt Lake City — the proposed Uinta Basin Railway has been the subject of environmental litigation. The basin also produces significant natural gas.

People and environment

Much of the basin lies within or near the Uintah and Ouray Indian Reservation of the Ute Indian Tribe. The region experiences winter temperature inversions that trap ozone and particulate pollution from energy operations, an ongoing air-quality concern studied by the US Environmental Protection Agency and Utah State University's Bingham Research Center.
