Pharmacosiderite is a hydrated basic ferric arsenate mineral, noted for its small, sharply formed cubic crystals, usually green to yellow-green in colour. Its name derives from the Greek pharmakon (poison, referring to the arsenic content) and sideros (iron).

Occurrence

Pharmacosiderite is a secondary mineral formed in the oxidised zones of arsenic-bearing ore deposits, where it occurs with other arsenates and iron oxides such as scorodite, arseniosiderite, limonite and beudantite. Fine specimens are known from Cornwall in England — including the type locality at Wheal Gorland — and from localities in Germany, France, and the United States. The crystals, often only a few millimetres across, commonly show cube faces and a vitreous to greasy lustre.

Properties

The mineral belongs to a small group of structurally related "pharmacosiderite group" arsenates in which sodium, potassium or hydronium can occupy the large cation site. It is soft (Mohs hardness about 2.5), moderately dense, and its colour can change on dehydration as it loses some of its water content. Because it contains arsenic, specimens should be handled with care and hands washed after handling.
