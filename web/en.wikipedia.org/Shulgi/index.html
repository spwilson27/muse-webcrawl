Shulgi

Reign: c. 2094–2046 BC
Predecessor: Ur-Namma
Successor: Amar-Sin
Dynasty: Third Dynasty of Ur

Shulgi (also Šulgi, formerly read as Dungi) was the second king of the Third Dynasty of Ur (Ur III), reigning from about 2094 to 2046 BC in the traditional middle chronology. He is considered one of the most important rulers of ancient Mesopotamia.

Reign

Son of Ur-Namma, the dynasty's founder, Shulgi succeeded after his father's death in battle. His long reign of 48 years transformed a modest kingdom into a highly centralized empire stretching across Mesopotamia and into Elam.

Reforms

Shulgi reorganized the army, administration, calendar, weights and measures, and scribal education. He introduced a vast taxation system in kind, administered from centres such as Puzrish-Dagan, and tens of thousands of cuneiform tablets survive from his bureaucracy. In his later reign he was deified during his lifetime, with temples and hymns in his honour — dozens of royal hymns attributed to him survive. He also boasted of literary and musical learning and of running between Nippur and Ur.

Legacy

He was succeeded by his son Amar-Sin. His reforms set the pattern for the Ur III state at its height.
