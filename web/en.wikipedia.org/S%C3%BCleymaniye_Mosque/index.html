The Süleymaniye Mosque is an Ottoman imperial mosque located on the Third Hill of Istanbul, Turkey. Commissioned by Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent and designed by the imperial architect Mimar Sinan, it was built between 1551 and 1557 and is regarded as one of the masterpieces of Ottoman architecture and of Sinan's career.

Architecture

The mosque's vast dome, 27.5 metres in diameter and over 50 metres high, is supported by semi-domes and great arches in a scheme that develops the model of Hagia Sophia. The interior is noted for its spaciousness, light and İznik tilework. Four slender minarets mark the corners of the courtyard. The surrounding complex (külliye) includes madrasas, a hospital, a soup kitchen, a library, a hammam and the mausoleums of Suleiman and his wife Hürrem Sultan.

History

The mosque survived the great earthquake of 1766, in which part of the dome collapsed, and later fires and restorations. During the First World War its courtyard was used as a weapons depot, and ammunition stored there caught fire in 1921. Extensive restorations were carried out in the 20th and 21st centuries.

Significance

Dominating the skyline above the Golden Horn, the Süleymaniye remains one of the largest mosques in Istanbul and a defining symbol of the city and of the Ottoman golden age.
