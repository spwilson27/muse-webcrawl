Space tourism

Space tourism is human space travel for recreational purposes. There are several different types of space tourism, including orbital, suborbital and lunar space tourism. Space tourism is marketed and organised by a growing number of companies, most prominently Space Adventures, SpaceX, Blue Origin, and Virgin Galactic.

History

During the 1990s the Russian space programme, short of funds, began selling seats on Soyuz flights. American businessman Dennis Tito became the first space tourist, visiting the International Space Station in April–May 2001 aboard Soyuz TM-32, reportedly paying $20 million. He was followed by Mark Shuttleworth (2002), Gregory Olsen (2005), Anousheh Ansari (2006, the first female space tourist), Charles Simonyi (2007 and 2009), Richard Garriott (2008) and Guy Laliberté (2009). After 2009, tourist Soyuz flights were paused as ISS crew sizes grew, resuming with Japanese tourists Yusaku Maezawa and Yozo Hirano in December 2021.

Suborbital tourism

Suborbital flights carry passengers just above the Kármán line (100 km), offering a few minutes of weightlessness. Virgin Galactic's SpaceShipTwo vehicle, VSS Unity, began commercial service in 2023. Blue Origin's New Shepard rocket began crewed flights in July 2021 carrying Jeff Bezos and others, and has flown dozens of passengers.

Orbital and private missions

SpaceX has flown private orbital missions on Crew Dragon, including the all-civilian Inspiration4 mission (September 2021), Axiom Space's missions to the ISS, and the Polaris Dawn mission (September 2024), which included the first commercial spacewalk. SpaceX has also announced plans for private lunar tourism, such as the cancelled dearMoon project.

Criticism

Space tourism is criticised for its high per-passenger carbon emissions and environmental impact, for consuming resources on recreation for the ultra-wealthy, and for safety risks as the industry matures. Supporters argue that it drives down launch costs, funds innovation, and gives more people the "overview effect" appreciation of Earth.
