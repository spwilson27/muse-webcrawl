Quarter-wave plate

Quarter-wave plate

Retardanceλ/4 (90° phase shift)

FunctionConverts linear ↔ circular polarization

A quarter-wave plate is an optical device that alters the polarization state of a light wave travelling through it. It is a waveplate — a plate of birefringent material — whose thickness is chosen so that the component of light polarized along the plate's fast axis emerges one quarter of a wavelength (a phase of π/2) ahead of the component polarized along the slow axis.

The characteristic effect of a quarter-wave plate is to convert linearly polarized light into circularly polarized light, and vice versa, when the plate's axes are oriented at 45° to the direction of the incoming linear polarization.

A quarter-wave plate converts linear polarization at 45° to its axes into circular polarization

Principle of operation

A birefringent crystal has two different refractive indices for light polarized along two perpendicular axes (the ordinary and extraordinary rays). As the two components traverse the plate they accumulate a relative phase difference, or retardance, proportional to the index difference and the plate thickness. A quarter-wave plate is manufactured so that this retardance equals one quarter of the design wavelength. If equal amplitudes enter along both axes — as when linear polarization is aligned at 45° — the emerging components, now 90° out of phase, combine to form circular polarization. Passing circularly polarized light through the plate reverses the process, producing linear polarization.

Materials and construction

Quarter-wave plates are commonly made from crystalline quartz, mica, or magnesium fluoride, and from stretched polymer films. Because birefringence varies with wavelength, a simple plate gives exact quarter-wave retardance only at its design wavelength; achromatic waveplates combine two materials to hold retardance nearly constant over a broad band. "Zero-order" plates use two thin plates with axes crossed to cancel temperature-sensitive orders, while multiple-order plates are simply thicker.

Applications

Quarter-wave plates are widely used in optics and photonics. Combined with a polarizer they form an optical isolator that blocks back-reflected light from lasers. They are used in circular polarizers for 3D cinema glasses and camera filters, in ellipsometry, in optical microscopy, in liquid-crystal displays, and in quantum optics experiments, where they convert between linear and circular photon polarization states.
