Cunard Line

The Cunard Line is a British shipping and cruise line founded in 1840 by Samuel Cunard of Halifax, Nova Scotia, as the British and North American Royal Mail Steam-Packet Company. For more than a century it dominated the transatlantic passenger trade with famous ocean liners, and today, as a brand of Carnival Corporation, it operates cruise ships that maintain the tradition of scheduled transatlantic crossings.

Founding and the steamship era

Samuel Cunard won the British Admiralty contract to carry transatlantic mail by steamship; his paddle steamer Britannia sailed from Liverpool to Halifax and Boston in July 1840, inaugurating regular steamship service across the Atlantic. Cunard competed fiercely with the Collins Line and later with Inman, White Star, and German lines for speed records — the Blue Riband — carrying emigrants westward and mail and first-class passengers in growing luxury.

Golden age of liners

In the early 20th century the company, subsidized by the British government on condition its ships be available as naval auxiliaries, launched Lusitania and Mauretania (1907); Lusitania was torpedoed by a German U-boat in 1915 with the loss of 1,198 lives. In 1934 Cunard merged with its struggling rival White Star Line to form Cunard-White Star, which launched Queen Mary (1936) and Queen Elizabeth (1940) — both famed as troopships in the Second World War. The White Star name was dropped in 1950.

Decline of the liners and the cruise era

Jet airliners ended the transatlantic liner trade in the 1960s; Queen Elizabeth 2 (1969) was designed for both crossings and cruising. Carnival Corporation acquired Cunard in 1998. The flagship Queen Mary 2 (2004), a true ocean liner, still runs scheduled Southampton–New York crossings, joined by Queen Elizabeth, Queen Victoria, and Queen Anne (2024). The line's hallmarks include White Star Service, red-and-black funnels, and the world's only regular transatlantic liner service.
