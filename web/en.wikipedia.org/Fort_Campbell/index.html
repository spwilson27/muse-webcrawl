Fort Campbell

Fort Campbell is a United States Army installation located astride the Kentucky–Tennessee border between Hopkinsville, Kentucky, and Clarksville, Tennessee. It is home to the 101st Airborne Division (Air Assault) and the 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment. The post covers about 105,000 acres, most of it in Tennessee, although the post office address is in Kentucky.

History

Construction began in 1941, and the camp was activated in 1942 as a training ground for armored and infantry divisions during the Second World War. It was named for William Bowen Campbell, a former governor of Tennessee and Union general. After the war the post became the home of the 11th Airborne Division and then, from 1956, of the reactivated 101st Airborne Division, whose "Screaming Eagles" had distinguished themselves at Bastogne and in Operation Market Garden. The 101st deployed from Fort Campbell to Vietnam, the Persian Gulf War, Iraq, and Afghanistan.

Units and role

In addition to the 101st Airborne Division headquarters and its brigade combat teams, Fort Campbell hosts the 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment (Airborne), the "Night Stalkers"; the 5th Special Forces Group (Airborne); and Campbell Army Airfield, one of the largest military airfields in the United States. The installation specialises in air assault operations — the rapid deployment of infantry by helicopter — and its Sabalauski Air Assault School trains soldiers in rappelling, sling-load operations, and pathfinder skills.

1985 Gander crash

On 12 December 1985, Arrow Air Flight 1285, carrying 248 soldiers of the 101st home from a peacekeeping deployment in the Sinai, crashed shortly after takeoff from Gander, Newfoundland, killing all 256 people aboard. It remains the deadliest air disaster in Canada and one of the worst losses of life in the division's history; a memorial stands at Fort Campbell.
