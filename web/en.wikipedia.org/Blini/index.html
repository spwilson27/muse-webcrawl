Blini

Blini (Russian: блины, singular бли́н, blin) are a traditional Russian and Ukrainian yeasted batter pancake, typically made from wheat or buckwheat flour. They are one of the most characteristic dishes of Slavic cuisine and are traditionally eaten during Maslenitsa, the week-long religious and folk festival that precedes Orthodox Lent.

History

Blini have ancient roots in Slavic culture; in pre-Christian times they were associated with the sun because of their round, golden shape, and were prepared at the end of winter to honour the rebirth of the sun. With the adoption of Christianity, the custom was absorbed into Maslenitsa, during which blini are consumed in large quantities before the fasting period of Lent.

Preparation

Traditional blini are made from a yeast-leavened batter of flour, eggs, and milk or kefir, which is left to rise before frying. Buckwheat flour is often mixed with wheat flour for a nuttier flavour and a slightly darker colour. The batter is ladled into a hot, greased skillet in thin layers, producing small pancakes a few inches across.

Serving

Blini are served hot with a wide range of accompaniments. Savoury toppings include sour cream (smetana), butter, caviar, smoked salmon, and minced herring; sweet versions are eaten with jam, honey, or condensed milk. Rolled blini with fillings are known as blinchiki.
