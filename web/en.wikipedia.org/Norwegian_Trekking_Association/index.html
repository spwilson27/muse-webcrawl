The Norwegian Trekking Association (Norwegian: Den Norske Turistforening, DNT) is Norway's largest outdoor life and recreation organisation, founded in 1868 on the initiative of banker and philanthropist Thomas Heftye. It promotes hiking and outdoor recreation and works to preserve Norway's natural environment and cultural heritage.

Activities

DNT consists of 56 affiliated local associations across Norway. It maintains more than 550 cabins in the mountains, forests, and along the coast, ranging from large staffed lodges to small self-service and no-service huts, and maintains some 22,000 km of marked summer trails and about 7,000 km of marked winter routes, signed with the characteristic red T markers. Local associations organise thousands of guided tours and activities each year for all ages.

History

Founded when mountain tourism in Norway was in its infancy, DNT built its first cabins in the late 19th century and published its first yearbook in 1868. Membership grew steadily with the Norwegian tradition of friluftsliv (open-air life), and today the association counts roughly 300,000 members, making it one of Norway's largest voluntary organisations.
