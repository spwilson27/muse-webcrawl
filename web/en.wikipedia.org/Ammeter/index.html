Ammeter

An ammeter (abbreviation of ampere meter) is an instrument used to measure the current in a circuit. Electric currents are measured in amperes (A), hence the name. An ammeter is connected in series with the circuit being measured, so that the full current passes through it.

Design

To avoid significantly altering the circuit, an ammeter must have a very low resistance. Traditional analog ammeters use a moving-coil galvanometer (the D'Arsonval movement) in which current through a coil in a magnetic field produces a torque proportional to the current. Shunt resistors extend the measurement range.

Types

Moving-iron ammeters respond to both AC and DC. Clamp meters measure current without breaking the circuit by sensing the magnetic field around a conductor. Digital multimeters measure current by the voltage drop across a precision internal shunt.

History

The ammeter developed from 19th-century galvanometers following Hans Christian Ørsted's 1820 discovery that electric current deflects a compass needle, and was refined as electrical engineering standardized around the ampere.
