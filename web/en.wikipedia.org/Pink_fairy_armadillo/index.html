Pink fairy armadillo

Scientific nameChlamyphorus truncatus
FamilyChlamyphoridae
RangeCentral Argentina
Conservation statusData Deficient (IUCN)

The pink fairy armadillo (Chlamyphorus truncatus), also called the pichiciego, is the smallest species of armadillo. It is found in the dry grasslands and sandy plains of central Argentina, where it lives a largely subterranean life.

Description

Pink fairy armadillos measure roughly 90–115 mm in body length, excluding the tail. Their pale pink armour consists of a flexible dorsal shell of about 24 bands that is attached to the body only by a thin membrane along the spine, leaving the sides largely free. The silky yellowish-white fur beneath helps with thermoregulation, and the flushed pink colour of the shell comes from blood vessels close to the surface.

Behaviour

The species is nocturnal and solitary, digging with large, strong claws on its forefeet and feeding mainly on ants, worms, snails and plant material. It is rarely seen and fares poorly in captivity, so its population status is poorly known; habitat loss and predation by domestic dogs are among its threats.
