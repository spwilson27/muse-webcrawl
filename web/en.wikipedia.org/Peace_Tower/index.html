Peace Tower

The Peace Tower (French: Tour de la Paix) is a focal bell and clock tower sitting on the central axis of the Centre Block of the Canadian parliament buildings in Ottawa, Ontario. The present tower is the second iteration of the name, replacing the 55-metre (180 ft) Victoria Tower, which burned down in 1916 alongside most of the Centre Block.

Peace Tower
Location: Parliament Hill, Ottawa, Ontario
Height: 98 m (322 ft)
Completed: 1920 (clock tower completed later)
Architect: John A. Pearson

History

Construction of the Peace Tower began in 1916, during the First World War, on the site of the Victoria Tower destroyed by fire that February. Designed by John A. Pearson, it was conceived as a memorial to Canada's war effort; its cornerstone was laid by the Prince of Wales (later Edward VIII) in 1919. The 53-bell carillon in the tower was inaugurated on 1 July 1927, the Diamond Jubilee of Confederation.

Memorial Chamber

At the base of the tower is the Memorial Chamber, a vaulted Gothic Revival room that commemorates Canadians who died in conflicts. Its altars hold the Books of Remembrance, which list the names of Canadians who died in military service; pages are turned daily in a formal ceremony.

Architecture

The 98-metre tower is built of Nepean sandstone with a copper roof. Its clock, manufactured by Gillett & Johnston, has four faces; the tower's bells and flag—flown daily and changed each weekday—make it one of Canada's most recognizable landmarks.
