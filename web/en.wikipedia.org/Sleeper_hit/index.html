Sleeper hit

In the entertainment industry, a sleeper hit is a work — such as a film, television series, video game or song — that is initially unsuccessful or receives little promotion or attention, but later becomes a success, typically through positive word of mouth. Unlike a blockbuster driven by a large marketing campaign and a strong opening, a sleeper hit "sleeps" below public notice before audiences discover it, often sustaining earnings over a long period rather than peaking immediately.

Characteristics

Sleeper hits typically show strong "legs": week-to-week box-office declines are unusually small, or sales steadily climb as recommendations spread. Distributors may respond by expanding a film to more screens. Delayed discovery is common on home video, cable, streaming platforms and digital storefronts, where catalogue titles can find audiences years after release. Some works described as sleeper hits on first release later achieve the related status of cult classics.

Examples

Frequently cited film examples include The Shawshank Redemption (1994), which performed modestly in cinemas but became a phenomenon on home video and television; The Rocky Horror Picture Show (1975), which grew through midnight screenings; and My Big Fat Greek Wedding (2002), which expanded from a tiny initial release into one of the most profitable films ever made relative to its budget. In music, songs such as Gotye's "Somebody That I Used to Know" and Lorde's "Royals" spread gradually before topping charts, and in video games, titles like Among Us — released in 2018 to little notice, then enormously popular in 2020 — are classic sleepers.

Contrast with related terms

A sleeper hit differs from a surprise hit released with expectations low but opening strongly, and from a box-office bomb, which never recovers. It also differs from the music industry's "sleeper" usage for an album track that becomes popular later. The term is sometimes used in publishing and sports for similarly underestimated successes.
