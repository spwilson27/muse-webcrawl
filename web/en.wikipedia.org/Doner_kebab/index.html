Doner kebab

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Doner kebab (Turkish: döner kebap, literally "rotating kebab") is a Turkish dish made of meat cooked on a vertical rotisserie. Seasoned meat — traditionally lamb, today also beef, veal or chicken — is stacked in the shape of an inverted cone and slowly turned next to a vertical cooking element; the cooked outer layer is shaved off with a long knife as it roasts.

History

Vertical rotisseries appeared in the Ottoman Empire in the 19th century; the dish is associated with Bursa and is closely related to the Greek gyros and the Levantine shawarma, all descended from the same tradition. In Turkey doner is served on a plate with rice, in a wrap (dürüm), or in bread. Turkish guest workers brought the sandwich form to Berlin in the early 1970s — Kadir Nurman is often credited with selling the first doner in bread at Zoo Station in 1972 — and it became one of Germany's most popular fast foods, spreading worldwide.

Preparation and serving

The meat stack combines slices of marinated meat with layers of ground meat and fat; onions or tomatoes sometimes crown the spit. Shavings are served with salad, sauces (garlic yoghurt, chilli), and bread such as pide or lavaş. Regional variants include İskender kebab of Bursa, served over pide with tomato sauce and melted butter, and the tombik ("chubby") sandwich. In Germany the doner industry is a major business, with its own meat-production standards.
