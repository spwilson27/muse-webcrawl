Chang'e 4

Chang'e 4 is a Chinese lunar exploration mission, part of the Chinese Lunar Exploration Program, that achieved the first soft landing on the far side of the Moon. It was launched on 7 December 2018 and landed on 3 January 2019 in the Von Kármán crater within the South Pole–Aitken Basin.

Mission

Because the far side of the Moon never faces Earth, the mission relied on the Queqiao relay satellite, placed in a halo orbit around the Earth–Moon L2 point, to communicate with the lander and its Yutu-2 rover. Chang'e 4 was originally built as a backup to Chang'e 3 and was repurposed for the far-side mission.

Results

The Yutu-2 rover became the longest-lived lunar rover, operating for over three years and travelling more than a kilometre. Instruments studied the lunar subsurface with ground-penetrating radar, measured radiation, and observed low-frequency radio signals impossible to detect from Earth. A cotton seed carried in a sealed biosphere experiment briefly sprouted — the first plant to germinate on the Moon.
