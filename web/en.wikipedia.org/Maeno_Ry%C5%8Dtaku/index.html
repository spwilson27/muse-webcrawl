Maeno Ryōtaku

Maeno Ryōtaku (1723 – 30 November 1803) was a Japanese physician and scholar of Dutch learning (rangaku) during the Edo period. He is best known, together with Sugita Genpaku, as one of the translators of Kaitai Shinsho (1774), the first Japanese translation of a European anatomical work, rendered from the Dutch edition of Johann Adam Kulmus's anatomical tables.

Life and work

Born in Nakatsu Domain, Maeno studied medicine and Dutch in Nagasaki and Edo. In 1771 he and Sugita attended the dissection of an executed criminal at Kozukappara and were struck by the accuracy of the Dutch anatomical charts compared with traditional texts. Working largely without a dictionary and with limited Dutch, the group laboured for several years, famously struggling for days over single sentences, to produce the translation that launched the rangaku study of Western anatomy.

Legacy

Unlike Sugita, Maeno sought no fame from the work and declined to be named as its principal author, a modesty celebrated in later accounts. His role was championed in the twentieth century, and he is commemorated in his native Nakatsu, in Ōita Prefecture.
