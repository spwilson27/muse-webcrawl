Booderee National Park

Location: Jervis Bay Territory, Australia

Area: about 6,312 hectares

Established: 1992 (Aboriginal-owned since 1995)

Managed by: Wreck Bay Aboriginal Community and Parks Australia

Booderee National Park is a protected area at Jervis Bay on the south coast of New South Wales, within the Jervis Bay Territory of Australia. "Booderee" means "bay of plenty" or "plenty of fish" in the Dhurga language of the local Yuin people. In 1995 the park's title was handed back to the Wreck Bay Aboriginal Community, making it one of the few Australian national parks owned by Aboriginal people and jointly managed with the Commonwealth park service.

History

The area was proclaimed as the Jervis Bay Nature Reserve and later Jervis Bay National Park. When the Jervis Bay Territory Acceptance arrangements were amended, the Wreck Bay community received title to the land and leased it back for management as a national park. The park also administers the Booderee Botanic Gardens, the only Aboriginal-owned botanic gardens in Australia, established on the site of earlier gardens founded in 1951.

Environment

Booderee protects coastal heath, eucalypt forest, wetlands, and white-sand beaches along Jervis Bay and the Tasman Sea. It is an important bird area, home to the endangered eastern bristlebird, ground parrots, and large populations of honeyeaters, wallabies, and kangaroos. The ruined Cape St George Lighthouse, built in 1860 in a poorly chosen position that contributed to shipwrecks, is a popular heritage site.

Recreation

Visitors camp at Green Patch, Bristol Point, and Cave Beach, and swim, surf, and whale-watch from beaches including Murrays Beach and Hyams Beach nearby. Marine waters adjoining the park form part of the Jervis Bay Marine Park.
