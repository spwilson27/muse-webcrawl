Wedding cake

A traditional multi-tiered white wedding cake

A wedding cake is the traditional cake served at wedding receptions following dinner. In some parts of England, the wedding cake is served at a wedding breakfast; the "wedding breakfast" does not mean the meal will be held in the morning, but at a time following the ceremony. In modern Western culture, the display and cutting of the cake is an important part of the reception.

Symbolism and traditions

Traditionally, wedding cakes were made to bring good luck to the couple and their guests. The couple cutting the cake together symbolises their first joint task, and feeding each other cake symbolises commitment. In the United Kingdom, the traditional wedding cake is a rich fruit cake covered with marzipan and royal icing; the top tier was historically kept for the christening of the couple's first child.

History

The wedding cake descends in part from ancient Roman customs in which a barley or wheat cake was broken over the bride's head for luck. Elaborate iced cakes developed in 17th- and 18th-century England and France, and the white, tiered cake was popularised by the wedding of Queen Victoria in 1840, whose cake featured white icing — the origin of the term "royal icing".

Modern cakes

Contemporary wedding cakes range from classic white tiered sponge cakes to elaborate fondant showpieces, cupcake towers, cheese "cakes", and themed designs. Flavours now extend far beyond fruit cake to chocolate, lemon, red velvet, and many others, often with different flavours in each tier.
