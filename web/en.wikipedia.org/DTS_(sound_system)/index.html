DTS (sound system)

DTS
Type: audio codec / surround format
Developer: DTS, Inc. (now part of Xperi)
Introduced: 1993 (Jurassic Park)

DTS is a series of multichannel audio technologies owned by DTS, Inc. (now part of Xperi), used in cinemas, home theatre, and consumer media as a competitor to Dolby Digital. It debuted theatrically with Steven Spielberg's Jurassic Park in 1993.

Formats

The core DTS codec uses adaptive delta pulse-code modulation with lossy compression, typically at higher bit rates than Dolby Digital on DVD and Blu-ray. Extensions include DTS-ES (6.1), DTS-HD High Resolution and lossless DTS-HD Master Audio, and the object-based DTS:X immersive format launched in 2015.

Cinema use

In theatres, DTS audio is stored on a separate CD-ROM (later hard drive) synchronised to the film by timecode, unlike print-based optical systems.
