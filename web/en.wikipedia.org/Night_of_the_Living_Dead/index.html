Night of the Living Dead

Night of the Living Dead is a 1968 American independent horror film directed by George A. Romero. A group of strangers barricade themselves in a Pennsylvania farmhouse as the recently dead return to life and attack the living. Made on a small budget, it became a landmark of horror cinema and founded the modern zombie film.

Plot and production

Siblings Barbra and Johnny are attacked in a cemetery; Barbra flees to a farmhouse where she joins Ben and others. As tensions rise inside and the ghouls mass outside, the survivors are picked off, and Ben, the last alive, is mistakenly shot by a posse at dawn. The casting of Duane Jones, a Black actor, as the capable hero Ben gave the film added resonance in the America of 1968.

Legacy

Because of an error in its copyright notice, the film entered the public domain in the United States, aiding its wide distribution. It spawned Romero's Dead series, beginning with Dawn of the Dead (1978), and was selected for the National Film Registry in 1999.
