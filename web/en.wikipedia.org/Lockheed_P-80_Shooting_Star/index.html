The Lockheed P-80 Shooting Star was the first jet fighter used operationally by the United States Army Air Forces (USAAF). Designed and built by Lockheed in 1943 and delivered just 143 days from the start of the design process, the P-80 was the first American jet aircraft to score a combat victory when it shot down a North Korean Yak-9 in the Korean War.

Design and development

Work began in 1943 around the British Halford H.1 (later Goblin) turbojet engine supplied by de Havilland. A team led by Clarence "Kelly" Johnson at Lockheed's Skunk Works produced the XP-80 prototype in record time. Test pilot Milo Burcham flew the prototype for the first time on 8 January 1944. Production aircraft used the American-built Allison J33 engine.

Operational history

Two pre-production aircraft were sent to Europe late in the Second World War for operational evaluation but saw no combat before the war ended. In Korea the type, redesignated F-80, flew some of the first jet combat missions of the war and the first all-jet aerial engagement, in which an F-80 shot down a MiG-15 in November 1950. The two-seat trainer derivative, the T-33 Shooting Star, became one of the most widely used jet trainers in the world.

Specifications

The F-80C had a maximum speed of about 600 mph (965 km/h), a range of roughly 1,200 miles (1,930 km), and was armed with six 0.50-inch (12.7 mm) M3 Browning machine guns in the nose plus provisions for bombs and rockets under the wings.
