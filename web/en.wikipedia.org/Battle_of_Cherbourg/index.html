The Battle of Cherbourg was fought in June 1944 during World War II, as part of the Allied Invasion of Normandy. The U.S. VII Corps under Maj. Gen. J. Lawton Collins fought to capture the fortified port of Cherbourg on the northern tip of the Cotentin Peninsula — the first major deep-water port the Allies planned to seize to supply their armies in France.

Background

After the D-Day landings on Utah Beach (6 June 1944), American forces drove west across the Cotentin, cutting the peninsula by 18 June and trapping the German garrison of Cherbourg. Hitler ordered the port held to the last as a "fortress" (Festung Cherbourg), but the defences faced landward only after hasty improvisation, since the Atlantic Wall had been built mainly against attack from the sea.

Battle

Following a massive air bombardment on 22 June, three U.S. infantry divisions (4th, 9th, and 79th) attacked the outer defences. Fighting was hardest at strongpoints such as Fort du Roule and around the suburb of Octeville. On 26 June the German commander, Generalleutnant Karl-Wilhelm von Schlieben, surrendered his headquarters; organized resistance ended by 27 June, though the port's arsenal and outlying forts held out until 30 June–1 July.

Aftermath

The Germans had thoroughly demolished the harbour — cranes toppled, basins mined and blocked — so Cherbourg handled only limited cargo until late July and was not fully working until autumn. Nevertheless its capture removed the threat to the invasion beaches' flank and gave the Allies their first major port. More than 39,000 German troops were captured in the Cotentin operations.
