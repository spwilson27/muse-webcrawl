Jimmy Carter

James Earl Carter Jr. (October 1, 1924 – December 29, 2024) was an American politician and humanitarian who served as the 39th president of the United States from 1977 to 1981.

A Democrat from Georgia and former peanut farmer and naval officer, Carter brokered the Camp David Accords between Egypt and Israel, signed the Panama Canal Treaties, and faced stagflation and the Iran hostage crisis, losing re-election to Ronald Reagan. After leaving office he founded the Carter Center, championed human rights and disease eradication, built homes with Habitat for Humanity, and received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2002. He was the longest-lived US president, dying at 100.
