Milwaukee is the largest city in the U.S. state of Wisconsin and the seat of Milwaukee County, located on the western shore of Lake Michigan at the confluence of the Milwaukee, Menomonee, and Kinnickinnic rivers.

History

Indigenous peoples lived in the area for centuries before French missionaries and traders arrived in the late 17th century. The modern city grew from three rival settlements — Juneautown, Kilbourntown, and Walker's Point — which united in 1846. Large numbers of German immigrants made Milwaukee a center of brewing, home to Pabst, Schlitz, Miller, and Blatz.

Economy and culture

Milwaukee's economy includes manufacturing, finance, and healthcare; Miller Brewing remains a major employer. The city is known for Summerfest, one of the world's largest music festivals, the Milwaukee Art Museum with its Burke Brise Soleil, and professional teams the Brewers (MLB) and Bucks (NBA).
