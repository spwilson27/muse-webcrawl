Vrba–Wetzler report
Authors: Rudolf Vrba, Alfréd Wetzler
Date: April 1944
Subject: Auschwitz concentration camp
Length: 32 pages

The Vrba–Wetzler report, also known as the Auschwitz Protocols, is a 32-page document written in April 1944 by two Slovak Jewish prisoners, Rudolf Vrba and Alfréd Wetzler, who had escaped from the Auschwitz concentration camp. It provided one of the first detailed eyewitness accounts of the mass murder of Jews at Auschwitz-Birkenau.

Contents

Vrba and Wetzler escaped on 7 April 1944 and reached Žilina, Slovakia, where they dictated their account to the Slovak Jewish Council. The report described the layout of Birkenau, the gas chambers and crematoria, the selection process and the numbers of victims, estimated at some 1.75 million by April 1944.

Impact

Copies reached Allied governments and the press by the summer of 1944. Published in Switzerland and widely reported, the report increased pressure on Hungary's regent Miklós Horthy and is credited with contributing to the halt of deportations of Hungarian Jews in July 1944. Together with the reports of Jerzy Tabeau and Arnošt Rosin and Czesław Mordowicz, it forms the Auschwitz Protocols.
