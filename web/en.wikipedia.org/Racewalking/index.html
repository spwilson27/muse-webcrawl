Racewalking

Racewalking
Type: Athletics (track and field)
Olympic events: 20 km (men and women); marathon race walk mixed relay
Governing body: World Athletics

Racewalking, or race walking, is a long-distance discipline within the sport of athletics. It differs from running in that one foot must always appear to be in contact with the ground, and the advancing leg must be straightened from the moment of contact until it passes under the body. Judges monitor competitors and issue red cards for violations; athletes accumulating too many are disqualified.

Rules

The two rules — the "contact rule" (no visible loss of contact with the ground, as judged by the human eye) and the "straightened knee rule" — distinguish racewalking from both walking and running. Competition takes place on roads or tracks, with electronic timing and, in major championships, pit-lane penalties.

Events and history

Racewalking developed from the pedestrianism contests of 19th-century Britain. It has been part of the Olympic Games since 1908. Standard championship distances are 20 kilometres for men and women; the 50 kilometres event was discontinued after the Tokyo 2020 Games and replaced at Paris 2024 by a marathon race walk mixed relay.
