Gauss (unit)

The gauss (symbol G) is a unit of magnetic flux density (magnetic induction, the B field) in the centimetre–gram–second (CGS) system of units. It is named after the German mathematician and physicist Carl Friedrich Gauss.

Definition and conversion

One gauss is defined as one maxwell per square centimetre. In SI units, 1 gauss = 10−4 tesla, so 1 tesla = 10,000 gauss. The Earth's magnetic field at the surface is roughly 0.25–0.65 gauss, a common refrigerator magnet is about 50 gauss, and the field inside an MRI scanner is on the order of 1.5–3 tesla (15,000–30,000 gauss).

Usage

Although the tesla is the SI unit, the gauss persists in many fields: astrophysics (stellar and interstellar magnetic fields), geophysics, and magnet manufacturing in the United States. The corresponding CGS unit of magnetic field strength (H) is the oersted, which in free space is numerically equal to the gauss.
