New York Botanical Garden

The New York Botanical Garden (NYBG) is a botanical garden and National Historic Landmark in the Bronx, New York City. Founded in 1891, it covers 250 acres (100 hectares) and is one of the largest and oldest botanical gardens in the United States, combining public display gardens with a major plant-research institution.

History

The garden was founded after botanists Nathaniel Lord Britton and his wife Elizabeth visited the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, and campaigned for a similar institution in New York. The city set aside land in Bronx Park, and the garden opened in 1891 with Britton as its first director.

Features

Highlights include the Enid A. Haupt Conservatory, a Victorian glasshouse opened in 1902; the Thain Family Forest, a 50-acre remnant of old-growth forest; the Peggy Rockefeller Rose Garden; and extensive herbaceous and rock gardens. The LuEsther T. Mertz Library and the William and Lynda Steere Herbarium, holding some 7.8 million specimens, support research in plant science, conservation and ecology.

Programs

NYBG runs education programs, the annual Orchid Show and Holiday Train Show, and degree-granting research training in partnership with universities.
