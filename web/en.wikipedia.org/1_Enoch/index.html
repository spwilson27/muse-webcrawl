1 Enoch, also known as the Book of Enoch or Ethiopian Enoch, is an ancient Jewish apocalyptic text attributed to Enoch, the great-grandfather of Noah. It is not part of the biblical canon used by most Jews or Christians, but it is canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and among the Beta Israel community, and it survives in full only in Ge'ez, the liturgical language of Ethiopia.

Contents

The book is a composite work of 108 chapters, generally divided into five sections composed over several centuries: the Book of the Watchers (chapters 1–36), recounting the fall of the angels (Watchers) who took human wives and fathered the Nephilim giants, and Enoch's intercession and journeys; the Book of Parables (or Similitudes, chapters 37–71), centred on the coming judgement and the figure of the "Son of Man"; the Astronomical Book (chapters 72–82), presenting a 364-day solar calendar; the Book of Dream Visions (chapters 83–90), an allegorical history of Israel; and the Epistle of Enoch (chapters 91–108) of exhortations and woes.

Transmission and rediscovery

The text was widely known in early Christianity — it is quoted in the New Testament Epistle of Jude (vv. 14–15) — but fell out of use in the West after being excluded from the canon and from rabbinic Judaism. Fragments in Aramaic were discovered among the Dead Sea Scrolls at Qumran, confirming its antiquity, and Greek and Latin fragments also survive. The complete Ge'ez version was brought to Europe by the Scottish traveller James Bruce in 1773, from Ethiopia, where it had been preserved.

Influence

1 Enoch profoundly shaped Second Temple Judaism and early Christian thought: doctrines of fallen angels, demonology, the Messiah as Son of Man, final judgement, and a developed angelology and cosmology all bear its imprint. It is distinguished from the later, unrelated works 2 Enoch (Slavonic) and 3 Enoch (Hebrew). Modern scholarship treats it as essential evidence for the diversity of Judaism in the centuries before the Common Era.
