Circle of confusion

In optics, a circle of confusion (CoC) is an optical spot caused by a cone of light rays from a lens not coming to a perfect focus when imaging a point source. In photography, the term is used for the largest blur spot that will still be perceived by the human eye as a sharp point, and this limiting value determines calculations of depth of field.

Photographic definition

A point outside the plane of focus is imaged as a disc rather than a point. If the disc is small enough, the image still appears sharp. The maximum acceptable diameter is the circle of confusion limit, which depends on the format size, viewing distance, and print or display size. For 35 mm film, a commonly used value is about 0.029 mm (roughly the film diagonal divided by 1500); the classic Zeiss formula uses the focal length and image diagonal. Lens depth-of-field scales and tables are computed from the chosen CoC value.

Related concepts

Out-of-focus highlights render as visible discs — the aesthetic quality of which is called bokeh. Circle of confusion also matters in cinematography, microscope depth of field, and computer graphics renderings of defocus blur. It should not be confused with the Airy disk, the diffraction-limited spot produced by a perfect lens.
