arXiv (pronounced "archive") is an open-access repository of electronic preprints (e-prints) covering physics, mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, quantitative finance, statistics, electrical engineering and economics. Founded by physicist Paul Ginsparg in 1991 at Los Alamos National Laboratory, it transformed scholarly communication by making research papers freely available online, often before formal peer review and journal publication.

History

arXiv began in August 1991 as an email-based archive for high-energy physics preprints, known as xxx.lanl.gov. It moved to the World Wide Web in 1993 and to the arXiv.org domain in 1999. Ginsparg received a MacArthur Fellowship in 2002 in recognition of the archive's role in scientific communication. Operations transferred to Cornell University Library and later Cornell Tech. By the 2020s the archive held more than two million articles, receiving tens of thousands of new submissions each month.

Moderation and endorsement

Submissions are not peer reviewed, but they are screened by volunteer moderators for relevance and minimum scholarly standards. Since 2004, new authors in most subject areas must be endorsed by an established arXiv author before submitting. Each paper receives a permanent identifier of the form YYMM.NNNNN (for example arXiv:0704.0001) and versioned revisions.

Impact

arXiv demonstrated that rapid, free distribution of preprints could coexist with traditional journals and became the principal dissemination channel in fields such as theoretical physics and, later, machine learning. Landmark results first appeared on arXiv, including Grigori Perelman's proof papers for the Poincaré conjecture. Funding comes from Cornell, member institutions, the Simons Foundation and other supporters.
