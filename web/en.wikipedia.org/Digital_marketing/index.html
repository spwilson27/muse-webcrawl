Digital marketing

Digital marketing
TypeMarketing via digital channels
ChannelsSearch, social media, email, display, mobile apps, video
Key metricsCTR, conversion rate, CPA, ROI, engagement
RelatedSEO, PPC, content marketing, marketing analytics

Digital marketing is the component of marketing that uses the Internet and online-based digital technologies — such as desktop computers, mobile phones, and other digital media and platforms — to promote products and services. Its development during the 1990s and 2000s changed the way brands and businesses use technology for marketing, enabling targeting, personalisation, and measurement on a scale impossible in traditional media.

Channels

  • Search engine marketing: paid search advertising (PPC) and search engine optimization (SEO) to appear in search results.
  • Social media marketing: organic content and paid advertising on platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, and X.
  • Email marketing: permission-based campaigns and automated journeys.
  • Display and video advertising: banner, native, and video ads, much of it bought programmatically in real-time auctions.
  • Content marketing and affiliate/influencer marketing: attracting audiences with useful content or via partners and creators.

Data, targeting and measurement

Digital campaigns are measured through web analytics: impressions, clicks, conversions, and attribution models that assign credit across touchpoints. Advertisers can target by demographics, interests, behaviour, and location, and can test creative variants (A/B testing) continuously. These capabilities rely on cookies and device identifiers, which are increasingly restricted by privacy regulation (such as the GDPR and CCPA) and by browser and platform policies.

History

The term became popular in the 2000s; milestones include the first clickable banner ad (1994), the rise of Google AdWords (2000), social media advertising after 2007, and the shift of the majority of global advertising expenditure to digital channels by the late 2010s. Mobile marketing, marketing automation, and AI-driven optimisation have been the major developments of the 2010s and 2020s.

Criticism

Concerns include consumer privacy and surveillance advertising, ad fraud, the spread of misinformation, platform market power, and the environmental and attention costs of always-on advertising.
