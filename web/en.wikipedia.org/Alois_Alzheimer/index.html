Alois Alzheimer

Alois Alzheimer (14 June 1864 – 19 December 1915) was a German psychiatrist and neuropathologist and a colleague of Emil Kraepelin. Alzheimer is credited with identifying the first published case of “presenile dementia”, which Kraepelin later identified as Alzheimer's disease.

Life and career

Alzheimer was born in Marktbreit, Bavaria, and studied medicine in Berlin, Tübingen, and Würzburg. In 1888 he joined the municipal mental asylum in Frankfurt am Main, where he worked with Franz Nissl on the histopathology of the nervous system. In 1903 Kraepelin invited him to Munich, where he directed the anatomical laboratory of the university psychiatric clinic.

Auguste Deter

In 1901 Alzheimer observed a 51-year-old Frankfurt patient, Auguste Deter, who displayed profound memory loss, disorientation, and language disturbance. After her death in 1906 he examined her brain and described the characteristic amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tangles. He presented the case at a conference in Tübingen in November 1906; Kraepelin named the condition “Alzheimer's disease” in the 1910 edition of his textbook of psychiatry.

Later years

In 1912 Alzheimer became professor of psychiatry at the University of Breslau. His health declined after a severe infection contracted in 1915, and he died in Breslau that December, aged 51.
