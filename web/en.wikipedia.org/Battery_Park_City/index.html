Battery Park City is a mainly residential 92-acre planned community on the west side of the southern tip of Manhattan in New York City. It is bounded by the Hudson River on the west, the Hudson River shoreline on the north and south, and the West Side Highway on the east. The neighborhood, developed on landfill — much of it excavated from the World Trade Center site in the late 1960s and 1970s — contains high-rise housing, parks, and the Brookfield Place (formerly World Financial Center) office and retail complex.

History

The idea of reclaiming land for development along the Hudson dates to the early 20th century, when the area was a district of dilapidated shipping piers. A master plan by Governor Nelson Rockefeller's administration created the Battery Park City Authority in 1968. After a failed 1970s plan emphasizing office towers, the influential 1979 Cooper–Eckstut master plan adopted traditional New York street grids and mandated that buildings follow the city's architectural conventions, with about one-third of the site reserved as open space.

Construction began in the 1980s; the World Financial Center opened in 1985. The neighborhood suffered extensive damage and prolonged power and access problems after the September 11 attacks (2001) and Hurricane Sandy (2012), prompting major resiliency projects along the waterfront.

Features

Attractions include the Hudson River Esplanade and parks such as Robert F. Wagner Jr. Park and Teardrop Park, the Museum of Jewish Heritage, the Skyscraper Museum (in the Ritz-Carlton building), Poets House, and Stuyvesant High School. About 13,000–14,000 residents live in the neighborhood, almost all in ground-leased buildings administered by the Authority.
