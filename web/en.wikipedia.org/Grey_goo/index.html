Grey goo

ConceptHypothetical end-of-world scenario
FieldMolecular nanotechnology
Coined byK. Eric Drexler
First describedEngines of Creation (1986)

Grey goo (also spelled gray goo) is a hypothetical scenario of runaway molecular nanotechnology in which out-of-control self-replicating robots — nanobots — consume all biomass on Earth while building more of themselves, a form of global ecophagy. The term was coined by nanotechnology pioneer K. Eric Drexler in his 1986 book Engines of Creation.

Scenario

In Drexler's thought experiment, a replicator engineered for some industrial purpose, or built by analogy with a bacterium able to live on natural materials, escapes control — by accident, mutation, or deliberate misuse. Doubling at short intervals, the machines would spread exponentially, disassembling organic matter for carbon and energy, and within days or months could reduce the biosphere to a mass of robots and waste heat: "grey goo". Drexler himself stressed that such replicators would be far harder to build than ordinary nanofactories, and that deliberate design choices (dependence on rare feedstocks, external control signals) could prevent the scenario.

Discussion and criticism

The idea attracted wide attention in the 2000s, including public concern expressed by figures such as Prince Charles, and was examined in studies of existential risk. Critics, including Nobel laureate Richard Smalley, argued that molecular assemblers of the kind Drexler envisioned face fundamental chemical difficulties ("fat fingers" and "sticky fingers" problems), and that grey goo in particular is unlikely because biological organisms, refined by evolution, would be formidable competitors. In 2004 Drexler publicly downplayed the scenario, saying nanotechnology development had moved away from free-ranging replicators toward controlled manufacturing systems.

In culture

Grey goo and ecophagy feature in science fiction, including Greg Bear's Blood Music in a biological form, Michael Crichton's Prey (nanobot swarms), and numerous video games and role-playing settings.
