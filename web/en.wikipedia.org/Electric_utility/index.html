Electric utility

An electric utility is a company in the electric power industry that engages in electricity generation and distribution of electricity for sale, generally in a regulated market. Utilities may be investor-owned, publicly owned (municipal or state), or organised as customer cooperatives.

Utilities build and maintain the networks that deliver electricity to customers.

Structure

Traditionally, utilities were vertically integrated: one company generated power, transmitted it over high-voltage lines, and distributed it to end users within an exclusive service territory, in return for regulated prices set to recover costs plus a reasonable return. In the United States, this model was supervised by state public utility commissions and, for wholesale markets, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.

Deregulation and restructuring

Beginning in the 1990s, many jurisdictions unbundled generation, transmission, and retail supply to introduce competition—wholesale power markets run by independent system operators, retail choice for customers, and regulated "wires" companies for transmission and distribution. Results have been mixed, with crises such as the California electricity crisis of 2000–01 shaping later reforms.

Modern challenges

Utilities today face the integration of variable wind and solar generation, distributed rooftop solar and batteries, electrification of transport and heating, ageing infrastructure, cybersecurity, and pressure to decarbonise. Many are investing in smart grids, advanced metering, and grid-scale storage.
