Hôtel Ritz Paris

Location15 Place Vendôme, Paris, France
Opened1 June 1898
FounderCésar Ritz
OwnerMohamed Al-Fayed

The Hôtel Ritz Paris is a grand luxury hotel in central Paris, overlooking the Place Vendôme in the city's 1st arrondissement. Founded by Swiss hotelier César Ritz in collaboration with chef Auguste Escoffier, it opened in 1898 and quickly became a symbol of high society and luxury. The hotel popularized concepts such as the private bathroom ensuite, and its name gave rise to the English words "ritzy" and "putting on the Ritz."

History

César Ritz purchased the former Hôtel de Gramont on the Place Vendôme and converted it with architect Charles Mewès. The hotel was among the first in Europe to provide a bathroom, telephone, and electric light for each room. Guests over the decades have included Marcel Proust, F. Scott Fitzgerald, Ernest Hemingway — after whom the hotel's Bar Hemingway is named — and Coco Chanel, who lived at the Ritz for more than thirty years.

Renovation

The hotel closed in August 2012 for a major renovation and reopened in June 2016 following a fire during the works. It is a member of The Leading Hotels of the World and is classified as a "Palace" hotel by the French government, a distinction above five stars.
