Republic of Mahabad

The Republic of Mahabad was a short-lived Kurdish self-governing state in present-day northwestern Iran, proclaimed on 22 January 1946 in the city of Mahabad. Backed by the Soviet Union, which then occupied northern Iran, it was one of two Kurdish states established in the region during the Iran crisis of 1946, alongside the Azerbaijan People's Government.

History

The republic was led by President Qazi Muhammad, a religious and political leader from Mahabad. Its government introduced Kurdish-language education, a national army known as the Peshmerga, and a flag and national anthem. The republic's armed forces were commanded by Mustafa Barzani, who had fled Iraq with his followers.

After the Soviet Union withdrew its troops from Iran in 1946 under international pressure, the Iranian army moved to reassert control. Mahabad's government collapsed in December 1946, and Qazi Muhammad surrendered in hopes of sparing the city. He was tried and hanged in Mahabad's central square on 31 March 1947.

Legacy

Despite lasting less than a year, the Republic of Mahabad remains a potent symbol of Kurdish national aspirations, and its flag and institutions influenced later Kurdish movements in Iran, Iraq, and Turkey.
