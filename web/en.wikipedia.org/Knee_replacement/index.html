Knee replacement

Knee replacement, also called knee arthroplasty, is a surgical procedure to replace the weight-bearing surfaces of the knee joint to relieve pain and disability, most commonly caused by osteoarthritis.

Other namesKnee arthroplasty
SpecialtyOrthopaedic surgery
UsesOsteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis

Indications

Surgery is considered when pain and loss of function persist despite medication, physiotherapy and other conservative treatment. See osteoarthritis.

Procedure

Damaged cartilage and bone from the femur, tibia and sometimes patella are removed and replaced with metal and polyethylene components, cemented or press-fit. Partial knee replacement replaces only one compartment.

Recovery and risks

Rehabilitation begins soon after surgery; most implants last 15–20 years or more. Risks include infection, blood clots, stiffness and implant loosening.
