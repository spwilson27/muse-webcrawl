Christian Dior

Christian Ernest Dior (21 January 1905 – 24 October 1957) was a French fashion designer and founder of one of the world's top fashion houses, Christian Dior SE. He revolutionized post–World War II fashion with his "New Look" of 1947, restoring Paris as the capital of haute couture.

Christian Dior, founder of the House of Dior

Early life

Dior was born in Granville, Normandy, to a prosperous fertilizer manufacturer. He studied political science in Paris to satisfy his parents, but his interests were artistic; with friends he ran a small art gallery that exhibited Picasso and Dalí before closing during the Depression after his family's business collapsed. He sold fashion sketches, worked as an illustrator, and was hired as a design assistant by Robert Piguet in 1937 and then by Lucien Lelong, where he dressed the wives of Nazi officers and French collaborators alike during the occupation, as did many Parisian houses that remained open.

The House of Dior and the New Look

Backed by the textile magnate Marcel Boussac, Dior founded his own house in 1946 at 30 Avenue Montaigne, Paris. His first collection, presented on 12 February 1947, featured the Bar suit and full-skirted silhouettes of nipped-in waists, soft shoulders, and lavish quantities of fabric. Carmel Snow, editor of Harper's Bazaar, declared, "It's quite a revolution, dear Christian! Your dresses have such a new look!" The phrase stuck, and the New Look — a reaction against wartime austerity and rationing — came to define the fashion of the following decade.

Death and legacy

Dior died suddenly in Montecatini, Italy, in 1957. His young assistant Yves Saint Laurent succeeded him as artistic director. The house he founded remains one of the most prominent luxury brands in the world, encompassing couture, ready-to-wear, perfumes such as Miss Dior (launched 1947), and accessories.
