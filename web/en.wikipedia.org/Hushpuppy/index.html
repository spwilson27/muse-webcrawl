Hushpuppy

Summary

Type: Deep-fried cornmeal fritter

Region: Southern United States

Main ingredients: Cornmeal, flour, egg, milk or buttermilk, onion, baking powder

Commonly served with: Fried fish, shrimp, barbecue

A hushpuppy is a small, savoury, deep-fried round ball made from cornmeal-based batter. A staple of Southern cuisine in the United States, hushpuppies are traditionally served with seafood — especially fried catfish and shrimp — and with barbecue.

Preparation

The batter typically combines cornmeal, wheat flour, baking powder or soda, salt, egg, and milk or buttermilk, often with finely chopped onion or scallions and sometimes corn kernels or jalapeño. Spoonfuls of batter are dropped into hot oil and fried until crisp and golden brown outside and soft inside. Variations include sweet versions dusted with powdered sugar and regional recipes using beer in the batter.

History and name

Fried cornmeal preparations in the South predate the name; similar foods were known as "red horse bread" in South Carolina. The term "hush puppy" appeared in print by the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Popular folk etymologies claim that cooks at fish fries tossed fried batter to barking dogs with the words "Hush, puppy!", or that Confederate soldiers or hunters did the same to quiet their dogs — these stories are widely repeated but not documented. Hushpuppies became a standard menu item at Southern seafood restaurants and fish camps in the 20th century.
