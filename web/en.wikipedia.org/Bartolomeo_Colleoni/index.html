Bartolomeo Colleoni (Italian pronunciation: [bartoloˈmɛːo kolleˈoːni]; c. 1395 – 2 November 1475) was an Italian condottiero (mercenary military leader) who became Captain-General of the Republic of Venice, and is regarded as one of the most capable commanders of the Italian Renaissance.

Early life

Colleoni was born at Solza near Bergamo, into a family of the Guelph nobility that had been driven from Bergamo by the Visconti of Milan. After his father Paolo was killed, the young Colleoni served as a page and then as a soldier under several famous condottieri, including Braccio da Montone, Muzio Attendolo Sforza, and Carmagnola.

Career

Colleoni campaigned alternately for Milan and Venice during the long Wars in Lombardy, winning distinction at battles such as Maclodio (1427). Imprisoned for a time by Filippo Maria Visconti at Monza on suspicion of treason, he escaped and entered Venetian service. From the mid-1450s Venice made him its permanent Captain-General, a position he held, with interruptions and threats of defection, until his death.

After the Peace of Lodi (1454) there were few major wars, and Colleoni spent much of his later life at his castle of Malpaga, building and endowing works in and around Bergamo, including the Colleoni Chapel (Cappella Colleoni), one of the masterpieces of Lombard Renaissance architecture, where he is buried.

Legacy

Colleoni left the bulk of his great fortune to the Republic of Venice for the war against the Turks, on the condition — never fulfilled as he intended — that an equestrian statue of him be placed in Piazza San Marco. Venice instead erected the monument, Andrea del Verrocchio's celebrated bronze equestrian statue, in the Campo Santi Giovanni e Paolo in front of the Scuola Grande di San Marco. Together with Donatello's Gattamelata, it is one of the two great Renaissance equestrian monuments.

An Italian Navy Condottieri-class light cruiser, Bartolomeo Colleoni, sunk in 1940 at the Battle of Cape Spada, was named after him.
