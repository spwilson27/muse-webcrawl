1 euro coin

Value1 euro
Mass7.5 g
Diameter23.25 mm
Thickness2.33 mm
Years of minting1999–present

The 1 euro coin is a euro coin with a value of one euro. It is made of two alloys: the inner part of nickel brass and the outer part of copper-nickel, giving it a two-colour (bimetallic) appearance. The coin has been used since 2002, was first issued in 1999 and is used in 20 of the 27 member states of the European Union as well as four micro-states which are not EU members.

History

The coin dates from 2002, when euro coins and banknotes were introduced in the then 12-member eurozone and its associated territories. The common side was designed by Luc Luycx, a Belgian artist who won a Europe-wide competition to design the new coins. The coins of 1 and 2 euro, along with the 50 cent coin, depict the European Union before its enlargement in 2004.

Design

The coins are composed of two alloys: the inner circle made of nickel brass and the outer ring made of copper-nickel. The diameter is 23.25 mm, the thickness is 2.33 mm and the mass is 7.5 grams. The coins' edges are finely milled with three marked indentations. The coins have been used from 2002, though they are dated 1999, when the euro was first introduced as an electronic currency.

National sides

Each country in the eurozone has its own national design on the reverse side of the coin. Designs range from portraits of monarchs, such as King Felipe VI of Spain, to national symbols and works of art, such as Leonardo da Vinci's Vitruvian Man on the Italian coin.
