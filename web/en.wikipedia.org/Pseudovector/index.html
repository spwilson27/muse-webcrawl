Pseudovector

Angular velocity represented by a pseudovector along the axis of rotation.

In physics and mathematics, a pseudovector (or axial vector) is a quantity that behaves like a vector under proper rotations but acquires an additional sign change under improper transformations such as reflections and inversion. Ordinary ("polar") vectors, by contrast, simply reverse the components normal to a mirror when reflected. The distinction arises because pseudovectors typically encode rotation or oriented area, whose apparent sense depends on the handedness convention of the coordinate system.

Examples

Important pseudovectors include angular velocity, angular momentum, torque, magnetic field (as represented by the magnetic vector potential's curl), magnetisation, and the vorticity of a fluid. The cross product of two polar vectors is a pseudovector; this is why the familiar right-hand rule is needed to fix its direction — in a left-handed coordinate system the same physical rotation would be drawn pointing the opposite way.

Behaviour under reflection

Consider a spinning wheel and its mirror image. The mirror wheel appears to spin the opposite way, so its angular velocity arrow — which by the right-hand rule points along the axle — ends up pointing in the opposite direction to what a naive reflection of the original arrow would give. That discrepancy of sign is precisely the hallmark of a pseudovector: under a parity transformation (spatial inversion), a polar vector v becomes −v, whereas a pseudovector a becomes +a.

Formal treatment

Rigorously, pseudovectors in three dimensions can be identified with bivectors — elements of the exterior square of a vector space — or, equivalently, with vectors in a space twisted by the orientation. In geometric algebra and differential geometry the concept is naturally handled by treating angular quantities as 2-forms or bivectors, avoiding the convention-dependence altogether. A related notion is the pseudoscalar, such as magnetic helicity, which changes sign under improper transformations while having no direction at all.
