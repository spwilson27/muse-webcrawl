Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District, Op. 29, is an opera in four acts and nine scenes by Dmitri Shostakovich, based on the novella by Nikolai Leskov. Premiered in 1934, it was initially a triumph, performed hundreds of times in the Soviet Union and abroad.

Plot

Katerina Izmailova, the lonely wife of a provincial merchant, takes a lover, Sergei, and murders her father-in-law and husband, then, with Sergei, a rival heir. The crimes are discovered at her wedding feast; she and Sergei are sent to Siberia, where Sergei betrays her with another convict, Sonyetka. Katerina drags Sonyetka with her into a freezing river, and both drown.

Denunciation and suppression

In January 1936, after Stalin attended a performance, the newspaper Pravda published the unsigned editorial "Muddle Instead of Music", condemning the opera as coarse, formalist and "leftist". The work was withdrawn and effectively banned in the Soviet Union for nearly three decades, and Shostakovich's career and safety were gravely threatened.

Revision: Katerina Izmailova

Shostakovich later prepared a softened revision, Katerina Izmailova, Op. 29/114, premiered in Moscow in 1962. Since the 1970s the original version has returned to international stages and is now regarded as one of the great operas of the twentieth century.
