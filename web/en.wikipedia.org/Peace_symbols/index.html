Peace symbols

Peace symbols are images, objects, and gestures used across cultures to express peace, non-violence, and opposition to war. The best known include the dove and olive branch, the CND "peace sign" (☮), the broken rifle, the rainbow peace flag, the white poppy, the origami paper crane, and the V sign used as a gesture of peace.

The dove and olive branch

The dove carrying an olive branch derives from the biblical story of Noah, in which a dove returns with an olive leaf signalling the end of the Flood. Early Christians adopted the dove as a symbol of the Holy Spirit and of peace, and the olive branch already signified peace and victory in Greek and Roman antiquity. In the 20th century Pablo Picasso's lithograph La Colombe (1949), chosen as the emblem of the Paris Peace Congress, popularised the dove of peace worldwide.

The CND symbol

The circle-and-lines symbol was designed in 1958 by British artist Gerald Holtom for the Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament (CND) and the first Aldermaston March against nuclear weapons. Holtom combined the semaphore signals for the letters "N" and "D" (nuclear disarmament) within a circle representing the Earth; he also described it as a despairing human figure with arms lowered. Never copyrighted, the sign spread through the 1960s counterculture and anti–Vietnam War movement to become a universal emblem of peace.

Other symbols

The broken rifle, adopted by War Resisters' International in 1921, signifies conscientious objection and refusal to fight. The white poppy, distributed in Britain since 1933 by the Peace Pledge Union, commemorates all victims of war and challenges militarism, complementing or contrasting with the red remembrance poppy. Italy's rainbow PACE flag ("peace"), first used in the 1961 Perugia–Assisi peace march and ubiquitous during the 2003 Iraq War protests, is among the most widely displayed flags in Italy. The paper crane became a peace symbol through Sadako Sasaki, a victim of the Hiroshima bombing who folded cranes hoping to recover; monuments to her stand in Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park and Seattle. The V sign with palm outward, a wartime "V for Victory" emblem, was reinterpreted by 1960s protesters as a peace gesture, while hand signals and the two-fingered salute remain common in photographs of the era.
