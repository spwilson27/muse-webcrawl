Siege of Nice

The siege of Nice occurred in 1543 when a combined Franco-Ottoman fleet attacked the Imperial-Savoyard city of Nice as part of the Italian War of 1542–1546. The city was taken, but its citadel held out until the besiegers withdrew.

Background

Under the Franco-Ottoman alliance, Francis I of France and Suleiman the Magnificent cooperated against the Habsburg emperor Charles V. In the summer of 1543 an Ottoman fleet under Hayreddin Barbarossa joined French forces in the western Mediterranean.

The siege

The allied fleet bombarded Nice in August 1543 and stormed the lower town. The citadel, commanded by the Knights of St John and local forces, resisted. Local tradition celebrates Catherine Ségurane, a washerwoman said to have rallied the defenders. The siege was abandoned in September as an Imperial relief army approached, and the Ottoman fleet wintered at Toulon.

Aftermath

Nice remained part of the Duchy of Savoy. The episode remains the most striking example of Christian-Muslim military cooperation in the Franco-Ottoman alliance.
