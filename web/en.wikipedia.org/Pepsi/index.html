Pepsi

Pepsi is a carbonated soft drink with a cola flavour, manufactured by PepsiCo. It was created and developed in 1893 by Caleb Bradham, a pharmacist in New Bern, North Carolina, who sold it at his soda fountain as "Brad's Drink"; it was renamed Pepsi-Cola in 1898—"Pepsi" deriving from the digestive enzyme pepsin and the cola nuts used in the recipe—and shortened to Pepsi in 1961.

History

Pepsi-Cola grew during the early 20th century, surviving bankruptcy twice before prospering under Charles Guth with a strategy of selling larger bottles at the same price as competitors during the Great Depression ("twice as much for a nickel"). The brand became Coca-Cola's chief global rival. Famous marketing campaigns include the "Pepsi Generation" (1963), the blind-taste "Pepsi Challenge" (1975), and endorsements by stars such as Michael Jackson and Britney Spears.

Rivalry with Coca-Cola

The "cola wars" between Pepsi and Coca-Cola shaped modern advertising. In 1985 Coca-Cola's reformulation as "New Coke"—partly a response to Pepsi's taste-test success—was reversed after consumer backlash. Pepsi has generally held the number-two position in the US cola market; in 2023 it was reported to have slipped to third behind Dr Pepper in US soda market share.
