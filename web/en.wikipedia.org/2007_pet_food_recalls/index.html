2007 pet food recalls

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 2007 pet food recalls comprised the largest consumer product recall in North American history. Beginning in March 2007, widespread contamination of pet food with melamine and cyanuric acid caused kidney failure and deaths in thousands of cats and dogs in the United States and elsewhere. More than 150 brands and over 5,300 pet food products were ultimately recalled.

Contamination

The US Food and Drug Administration traced the contamination to wheat gluten and rice protein concentrate imported from China, which had been deliberately adulterated with melamine — a nitrogen-rich industrial chemical used in plastics — to falsely inflate apparent protein content in standard tests. In the kidneys of affected animals, melamine and cyanuric acid formed crystals that blocked renal function. The episode was part of a wider pattern of adulteration that culminated in the 2008 Chinese milk scandal.

Response

Menu Foods, which manufactured food for many major brands, issued the first recall on 16 March 2007 after animals in feeding trials died. The scandal led to criminal charges against Chinese and American companies and executives, class-action settlements, and reforms: the US FDA Amendments Act of 2007 established pet food safety standards and an early-warning reporting system, and China tightened food export controls.
