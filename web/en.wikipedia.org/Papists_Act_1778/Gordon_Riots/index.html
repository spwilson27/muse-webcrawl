The Gordon Riots of 1780 were the most destructive riots in London's history, arising from anti-Catholic sentiment inflamed by the Papists Act 1778, which had rolled back some penal laws against Catholics. They are named after Lord George Gordon, head of the Protestant Association, who organised a petition against the Act.

Course of the riots

On 2 June 1780 a huge crowd marched on Parliament to present the petition. The demonstration degenerated into a week of violence: Catholic chapels and houses were burned, the prisons — including Newgate — were broken open and their inmates freed, the Bank of England was attacked, and distilleries were set ablaze, flooding streets with burning spirits. Order was restored only when the army was ordered to fire on crowds; several hundred people were killed.

Aftermath

Gordon was tried for high treason but acquitted. The riots set back Catholic emancipation for a generation and left a lasting mark on British politics and literature — Charles Dickens dramatised them in Barnaby Rudge.
