Papists Act 1778

The Papists Act 1778 (18 Geo. 3. c. 60) was an Act of the Parliament of Great Britain and the first in a series of measures known as the Roman Catholic Relief Acts. It relieved Roman Catholics of some of the legal disabilities imposed on them by the Popery Act 1698.

Provisions

The Act allowed Catholics who took a new oath of allegiance — renouncing the Stuart claim to the throne and certain doctrines attributed to Catholic teaching — to join the army, purchase and inherit land, and teach in schools without the previous penalties. It repealed the provisions of the 1698 Act that had imposed life imprisonment on Catholic priests and schoolmasters and rewarded informers.

Consequences

The passing of the Act was followed two years later by the Gordon Riots of 1780, a wave of anti-Catholic violence in London led by Lord George Gordon and the Protestant Association, which demanded the Act's repeal. The riots were eventually suppressed by the army. Further relief followed with the Roman Catholic Relief Act 1791 and, ultimately, the Roman Catholic Relief Act 1829, which achieved Catholic emancipation.
