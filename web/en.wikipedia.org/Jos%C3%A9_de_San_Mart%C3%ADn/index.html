José Francisco de San Martín y Matorras (1778–1850) was an Argentine general and statesman who led the struggle for independence of the southern part of South America from the Spanish Empire. Alongside Simón Bolívar in the north, he is remembered as one of the Liberators of Spanish South America.

Early career

San Martín served for two decades in the Spanish army, fighting against France in the Peninsular War, before returning to Buenos Aires in 1812 to join the independence movement. He organized the Regiment of Mounted Grenadiers and won the Battle of San Lorenzo in 1813.

Crossing of the Andes and liberation

As governor of Cuyo, San Martín raised the Army of the Andes and in January 1817 led one of the great feats of military history: a crossing of the Andes into Chile, followed by victories at Chacabuco (1817) and Maipú (1818) that secured Chilean independence. Sailing north with the Chilean navy under Lord Cochrane, he landed in Peru and proclaimed Peruvian independence in Lima on 28 July 1821, serving as Protector of Peru until 1822.

Retirement

After his 1822 meeting with Bolívar at Guayaquil, San Martín resigned his powers and withdrew from public life, eventually settling in France, where he died in 1850. His remains were later repatriated to Buenos Aires Cathedral.
