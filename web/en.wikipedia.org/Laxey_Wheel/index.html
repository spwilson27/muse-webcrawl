Laxey Wheel

Laxey Wheel
Location: Laxey, Isle of Man
Built: 1854
Designer: Robert Casement
Diameter: 72 feet 6 inches (22.1 m)

The Laxey Wheel, also known as Lady Isabella, is a large waterwheel built in the village of Laxey in the Isle of Man in 1854. It is the largest working waterwheel in the world, with a diameter of 72 feet 6 inches (22.1 m), and was designed by the engineer Robert Casement.

Purpose

The wheel was built to pump water from the Laxey lead, zinc and silver mines. Water was channelled from the Laxey River to a cistern above the wheel; the wheel's rotation drove a long series of pumps that removed water from the mine shafts some 450 metres away. It was named after Lady Isabella, the wife of the island's Lieutenant Governor.

Today

The mines closed in 1929, but the wheel was restored and is now maintained as a historic monument and tourist attraction. It appears on Manx banknotes and is one of the Isle of Man's best-known landmarks.
