Palamidi Fortress

LocationNafplio, Greece
Built1711–1714
Built byRepublic of Venice
Elevation216 m (709 ft)

The Palamidi Fortress (Greek: Παλαμήδι) is a fortress that dominates the town of Nafplio in the Peloponnese region of southern Greece. It crowns the 216-metre-high hill of Palamidi, named after Palamedes, a hero of the Trojan War in Greek mythology. The fortress was built by the Venetians during their occupation of the area from 1686 to 1715.

History

Construction was carried out between 1711 and 1714 under the direction of engineersGiaxich and Lasalle — a remarkably short time for such a major work. It consists of eight independent bastions connected by curtain walls. In 1715 the fortress fell to the Ottoman Empire, which held it until the Greek War of Independence, when it was captured in November 1822 by Greek forces led by Staikos Staikopoulos in a daring night attack. The fortress later served as a prison; the revolutionary leader Theodoros Kolokotronis was imprisoned there.

Description

The fortress is reached from the town by a winding stairway traditionally said to have 999 steps. Its bastions were named after Greek heroes and themes; the largest, Agios Andreas, contains a small chapel. The fortress is one of the finest surviving examples of Venetian military architecture in Greece.
