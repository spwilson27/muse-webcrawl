West Syriac Rite

[Image: West Syriac Rite]

The West Syriac Rite, also called the Syro-Antiochian Rite, is an Eastern Christian liturgical rite that employs the Divine Liturgy of Saint James in Syriac. It is practised in the Syriac Orthodox Church, the Syriac Catholic Church, the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, the Maronite Church (in a distinct form), and related churches of the West Syriac tradition, historically centred on Antioch.

History

The rite developed in and around Antioch in the first centuries of Christianity, in the region where, according to the Acts of the Apostles, the followers of Jesus were first called Christians. After the Christological controversies of the fifth century, the West Syriac tradition became associated with the miaphysite churches that rejected the Council of Chalcedon. The liturgy was originally celebrated in Greek but was translated into Syriac, the local Aramaic dialect of Edessa.

Characteristics

The principal anaphora is that of Saint James, though the Syriac tradition preserves dozens of anaphoras attributed to various saints. The liturgical calendar, lectionary, and chant tradition are distinctive; classical Syriac remains the liturgical language alongside vernaculars such as Arabic, Malayalam, and modern Aramaic dialects.

Usage

Today the rite is used by millions of Christians in Syria, Lebanon, Iraq, Turkey, India (especially Kerala) and the worldwide diaspora.
