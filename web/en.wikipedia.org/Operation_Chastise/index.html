Operation Chastise, commonly known as the Dambusters Raid, was an attack on German dams carried out on the night of 16–17 May 1943 by Royal Air Force No. 617 Squadron, later called the Dam Busters. Using purpose-built "bouncing bombs" developed by the engineer Barnes Wallis, the squadron breached the Möhne and Eder dams and damaged the Sorpe dam.

The breached Möhne dam after the raid.

The bouncing bomb

Wallis's "Upkeep" bomb was a spinning cylinder dropped at very low altitude (60 feet) so that it skipped across the water, over torpedo nets, and sank against the dam wall, where a hydrostatic fuse detonated it. Nineteen specially modified Lancaster bombers took part, led by Wing Commander Guy Gibson, who was awarded the Victoria Cross.

The raid

The Möhne dam was breached, releasing a flood that devastated factories, mines and infrastructure in the Ruhr valley; the Eder dam was also breached, flooding the Eder valley. The Sorpe dam, attacked with a different technique, was only damaged. Of the 133 aircrew who took part, 53 were killed and three captured; eight of the nineteen aircraft did not return.

Impact

The floods killed an estimated 1,300–1,600 people, many of them forced labourers and prisoners of war. The strategic effect was debated — German war production recovered relatively quickly, and both dams were repaired within months — but the raid was a major morale boost for Britain and demonstrated precision low-level bombing. It was immortalised in the 1955 film The Dam Busters.
