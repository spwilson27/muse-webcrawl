Run (cricket)

Governing rulesLaws of Cricket (MCC), Law 18
Scoring unitSingle run
RelatedBoundary, extra, wicket

In cricket, a run is the basic unit of scoring. Runs are scored by the batting team when the two batters at the crease run to the opposite ends of the pitch after the ball has been struck, or awarded in other ways defined by the Laws of Cricket, such as boundaries and penalty runs. The team that scores the most runs wins the match, subject to the format's rules.

Scoring runs

A run is scored when both batters successfully exchange ends — the striker reaching the non-striker's end and vice versa — after the ball is hit (or while it is in play). Batters may run multiple times for a single delivery. If a batter fails to make their ground before the wicket is broken, they may be run out, and any runs not completed are disallowed. A "short run" occurs when a batter turns for an additional run without crossing the crease, and the run is not counted.

Boundaries

If the struck ball reaches the boundary of the field, runs are awarded automatically: four runs if the ball bounces before reaching the boundary (a "four"), and six runs if it reaches the boundary without bouncing (a "six"). Batters do not need to run in these cases.

Extras

Runs can also accrue as "extras" (called "sundries" in some countries) without being credited to an individual batter: no-balls, wides, byes and leg byes each add runs to the team total. Under the modern Laws, a no-ball also concedes the penalty of one run in most formats, and in many limited-overs competitions results in a "free hit" on the next delivery.

Records

The highest individual score in Test cricket is Brian Lara's 400 not out for the West Indies against England in 2004. Sachin Tendulkar holds the records for the most runs scored in both Test matches and One Day Internationals.
