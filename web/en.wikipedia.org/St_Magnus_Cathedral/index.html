St Magnus Cathedral

St Magnus Cathedral in Kirkwall, Orkney, Scotland, is the oldest cathedral in Scotland and the most northerly cathedral in Britain. Founded in 1137 by the Norse Earl Rognvald Kali Kolsson in honour of his uncle, Saint Magnus Erlendsson, Earl of Orkney, who was martyred in 1117, the cathedral is built of red and yellow sandstone and is a fine example of Romanesque architecture.

History

Construction began in 1137 and continued for about 300 years. The cathedral housed the shrine of St Magnus and served the Norse earldom of Orkney. Uniquely in Scotland, the cathedral belongs not to the Church but to the people of Kirkwall, having been granted to the burgh by King James III in 1486. It survived the Reformation with less damage than most Scottish cathedrals and remains in use as a parish church of the Church of Scotland.

Features

The building contains the remains of St Magnus and St Rognvald, discovered in pillars in the choir, and its interior houses monuments including a memorial to the Arctic explorer John Rae. The cathedral hosts the annual St Magnus International Festival, founded by composer Peter Maxwell Davies.
