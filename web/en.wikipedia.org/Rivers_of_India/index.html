The rivers of India play a central role in the country's agriculture, economy, religion and ecology. With more than 400 rivers, India's river systems are conventionally divided into the snow-fed Himalayan rivers of the north — the Indus, Ganges and Brahmaputra systems, which flow year-round — and the rain-fed Peninsular rivers flowing mainly from the Western Ghats and central Indian highlands, including the Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Narmada and Tapti.

Significance

Rivers sustain the irrigation of the Indo-Gangetic Plain, one of the world's most fertile and densely populated regions, and are venerated in Hinduism: the Ganges, Yamuna and the mythical Sarasvati form the sacred Triveni Sangam at Prayagraj, site of the Kumbh Mela, the largest religious gathering on Earth. Inter-state disputes over sharing waters — notably the Kaveri dispute between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu — are adjudicated by tribunals under the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act. The government's National River Linking Project has long proposed transferring water from surplus to deficit basins, amid environmental controversy.
