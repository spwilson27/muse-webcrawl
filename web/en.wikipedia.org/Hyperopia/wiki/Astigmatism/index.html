Astigmatism

Astigmatism is a type of refractive error due to rotational asymmetry in the eye's refractive power. This results in distorted or blurred vision at any distance. Other symptoms can include eyestrain, headaches and trouble driving at night. Astigmatism often occurs at birth and can change or develop later in life; if it occurs in early life and is left untreated, it may result in amblyopia.

Cause

The cause of astigmatism is unclear, but it is believed to be partly related to genetic factors. The underlying mechanism involves an irregular curvature of the cornea and protective reaction, or changes in the lens of the eye. In regular astigmatism the principal meridians are perpendicular; in irregular astigmatism, which may follow corneal scarring or keratoconus, they are not.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by an eye examination using refraction, keratometry and corneal topography. Treatment options include eyeglasses with cylindrical lenses, contact lenses (including rigid and toric lenses), and refractive surgery such as LASIK, which reshape the eye's optics to focus light correctly on the retina.
