Presbyopia

Presbyopia is physiological insufficiency of accommodation associated with the ageing of the eye that results in progressively worsening ability to focus clearly on close objects. Also known as age-related farsightedness, it affects almost everyone over the age of 50 eventually. Symptoms include difficulty reading small print, having to hold reading material farther away, headaches and eyestrain.

Mechanism

The exact mechanisms are not fully understood, but the condition is attributed mainly to progressive stiffening of the crystalline lens and weakening of the ciliary muscles with age, reducing the amplitude of accommodation. A person with normal vision typically first notices presbyopia between the ages of 40 and 45, and the loss of focusing power continues until about age 65.

Correction

Presbyopia is corrected with reading glasses, bifocals, progressive lenses, multifocal contact lenses, or monovision (one eye corrected for near and one for distance). Surgical options include corneal inlays and lens replacement. More than a billion people worldwide are estimated to have presbyopia, many of them without adequate correction.
