Amblyopia

Amblyopia, also known as lazy eye, is a disorder of sight in which the brain fails to fully process input from one eye and over time favours the other eye. It generally results in decreased vision in an eye that otherwise typically appears normal. Amblyopia is the most common cause of decreased vision in a single eye among children and younger adults, affecting roughly 1–5% of the population.

Causes

The condition develops in early childhood when the visual pathways do not mature normally. Causes include strabismus (misaligned eyes), a significant difference in refractive error between the two eyes (anisometropia), and deprivation, such as from a congenital cataract or drooping eyelid that blocks vision. If untreated during the critical period of visual development, the deficit may become permanent.

Treatment

Treatment involves correcting any underlying cause, prescribing glasses, and forcing use of the weaker eye, traditionally by patching the stronger eye for several hours a day or blurring it with atropine drops. Treatment is most effective when begun early, ideally before the age of seven, though older children and even adults may improve with therapy.
