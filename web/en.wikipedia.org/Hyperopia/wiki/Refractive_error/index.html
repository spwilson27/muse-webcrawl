Refractive error

Refractive error is a problem with the focusing of light onto the retina due to the shape of the eye or cornea. The most common types of refractive error are near-sightedness (myopia), far-sightedness (hyperopia), astigmatism and presbyopia. In myopia, distant objects appear blurry; in hyperopia, near objects are less clear; in astigmatism, vision is distorted at all distances due to unequal curvature; and presbyopia is the age-related loss of near focusing ability.

Diagnosis and treatment

Refractive errors are diagnosed by an eye examination, including refraction with a phoropter or automated refractor, and retinoscopy. They are treated with eyeglasses, contact lenses or refractive surgery such as LASIK, PRK and SMILE.

Epidemiology

Refractive errors are among the most common causes of vision impairment worldwide. Uncorrected refractive error is the leading cause of visual impairment and the second leading cause of blindness globally, affecting an estimated 1–2 billion people, prompting World Health Organization initiatives to provide affordable spectacles in low-income settings.
