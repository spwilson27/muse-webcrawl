Ottoman Egypt

Ottoman Egypt refers to Egypt under the rule of the Ottoman Empire, from the Ottoman conquest of the Mamluk Sultanate in 1517 until the French invasion of 1798 and, nominally, into the nineteenth century under the Muhammad Ali dynasty.

Conquest

After defeating the Mamluks at the Battle of Marj Dabiq (1516) and the Battle of Ridaniya (1517), Sultan Selim I annexed Egypt as an Ottoman province (eyalet). Egypt became a vital source of grain and tax revenue for the empire, and control of Cairo and the Red Sea trade routes strengthened the Ottoman position against Portuguese expansion in the Indian Ocean.

Administration

Egypt was governed by a pasha appointed from Constantinople, but the defeated Mamluk elite survived as landowners, tax farmers, and military households. Over time, Mamluk beys regained much local power, and by the eighteenth century the effective rulers of Egypt were Mamluk grandees such as Ali Bey al-Kabir, who briefly asserted near-independence in the 1760s–1770s.

End of the period

Napoleon Bonaparte's invasion in 1798 shattered the old order. After the French withdrawal, the Ottoman-Albanian officer Muhammad Ali seized power in 1805 and founded a dynasty that ruled Egypt — still nominally Ottoman — until 1952, transforming the country through military, agricultural, and administrative reforms.
