Walton County is a county in the Florida Panhandle, in the northwestern part of the U.S. state of Florida, bordering Alabama to the north and the Gulf of Mexico to the south. Its county seat is DeFuniak Springs. Walton is one of Florida's original counties, created in 1824.

Geography

The county spans from the Alabama line to the Gulf, encompassing Choctawhatchee Bay, Point Washington State Forest, and the highest point in Florida, Britton Hill (345 ft / 105 m), near Lakewood. The southern third, known as South Walton, contains 26 miles of sugar-white beaches along Scenic Highway 30A, with resort communities including Rosemary Beach, Seaside, Grayton Beach, and Miramar Beach. Seaside, founded in 1981, is a landmark of New Urbanism town planning and was the filming location of The Truman Show.

History and economy

The county was named for George Walton Jr., son of a signer of the Declaration of Independence and secretary of the Florida territory. DeFuniak Springs grew around a perfectly round spring-fed lake and a Chautauqua assembly. Historically rural and dependent on timber and farming, Walton County's economy is now dominated by tourism and second homes along the coast, making it one of Florida's fastest-growing counties, while the northern interior remains agricultural.

Communities

Incorporated places include DeFuniak Springs, Freeport, and Paxton; notable unincorporated communities include Santa Rosa Beach, Miramar Beach, Inlet Beach, and Mossy Head.
