Dual system

The term dual system can refer to several concepts in mathematics and other fields. In linear algebra, the dual of a vector space is the set of all linear functionals on that space; in economics and political geography, a dual system may describe an economy or city divided between formal and informal sectors, or between two cores.

Mathematics

In category theory and algebra, dual systems and duality principles describe how statements and structures can be transformed into corresponding dual statements by reversing arrows or exchanging operations. The term also appears in the theory of Banach spaces and topological vector spaces, where a dual pair consists of two vector spaces together with a bilinear pairing between them.

Economics and geography

In development studies, dualism describes the coexistence of a modern, capital-intensive sector with a traditional, subsistence sector within one economy. In urban geography, a "dual city" model describes places administered or developed in two contrasting parts.

Other uses

Historically, England used a "dual system" of denominational and board schools in elementary education during the 19th and early 20th centuries. The phrase is also used for dual monarchy arrangements, such as Austria-Hungary after the Compromise of 1867.
