The Second Chōshū expedition (Japanese: 第二次長州征討), also known as the Summer War, was a punitive military campaign led by the Tokugawa shogunate against the Chōshū Domain in 1866. Unlike the bloodless First Chōshū expedition of 1864, which had ended in Chōshū's submission, the second expedition was a real war — and a humiliating defeat for the shogunate.

Background

After 1864, reformers led by Takasugi Shinsaku seized control of Chōshū's government and modernised its forces, raising mixed rifle units such as the Kiheitai militia and purchasing modern arms through the mediation of Satsuma, Chōshū's former enemy, under the secret Satchō Alliance of 1866. The shogunate, under Tokugawa Iemochi, ordered a new expedition to punish the domain's defiance of the court and bakufu.

The war

Fighting opened in June 1866 on several fronts: in Iwami, Bingo (the Geishū front) and around the Shimonoseki straits, where Chōshū forces also faced the shogunal navy at Kokura. Chōshū's modernised troops, fighting on the defensive with rifled guns and strong local support, defeated the larger but poorly coordinated bakufu armies of allied domains, many of which fought without enthusiasm. In August the shogun Iemochi died at Osaka Castle; his successor, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, arranged a ceasefire on the pretext of the imperial command.

Consequences

The defeat fatally discredited the shogunate, demonstrating that it could no longer compel obedience from a major domain. Within eighteen months Yoshinobu returned political authority to the Emperor in the Meiji Restoration settlement, and the Boshin War of 1868–69 completed the bakufu's overthrow.
