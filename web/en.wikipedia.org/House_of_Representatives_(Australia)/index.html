House of Representatives (Australia)

The House of Representatives is the lower house of the bicameral Parliament of Australia, the upper house being the Senate. Its composition and powers are established in Chapter I of the Constitution of Australia. Members, known as MPs, are elected by a system of preferential voting (instant-runoff voting) from single-member electoral divisions, commonly known as "seats".

Composition

The House currently has 151 members, each representing a division of roughly equal population. The Constitution requires that the number of members be, as nearly as practicable, twice the number of senators — the so-called "nexus" provision. The party or coalition with a majority of seats in the House forms the government, and its leader becomes Prime Minister.

Powers and role

Most legislation is initiated in the House of Representatives. Under section 53 of the Constitution, the Senate may not originate or amend money bills, though it may reject them. The House scrutinises the executive through question time, debates, and committee work; committees of the House include standing committees on subjects such as economics, health, and public accounts.

Presiding officer

The presiding officer is the Speaker of the House of Representatives, elected by members at the start of each parliament. The Speaker maintains order, interprets standing orders, and represents the House in its relations with the Senate and the Governor-General.

Chamber

The House meets in a green-coloured chamber at Parliament House in Canberra, a colour scheme derived from the British House of Commons. Since 1988 it has sat in the new Parliament House on Capital Hill; previously it met in the Provisional Parliament House (now Old Parliament House).
