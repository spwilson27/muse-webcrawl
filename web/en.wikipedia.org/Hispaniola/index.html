Hispaniola (Spanish: La Española) is an island in the Caribbean, the second-largest of the Greater Antilles after Cuba and the most populous Caribbean island. It is politically divided between two sovereign states: the Dominican Republic, occupying the eastern five-eighths, and Haiti, occupying the western three-eighths. It is one of only two Caribbean islands shared by two countries with capitals on it, the other being Saint Martin.

History

The island was inhabited for millennia by Taíno peoples, who called it Ayiti, Bohío or Kiskeya. Christopher Columbus landed there on 5 December 1492, naming it La Española ("the Spanish island", Latinised as Hispaniola); it became the site of the first permanent European settlement in the Americas, Santo Domingo. Disease, enslavement and violence devastated the Taíno population, and enslaved Africans were imported to work sugar plantations. In 1697 Spain ceded the western third to France, which became the fabulously wealthy colony of Saint-Domingue. After a slave revolution, that part became Haiti in 1804 — the world's first black-led republic — while the east eventually became the Dominican Republic (independent 1844).

Geography

Hispaniola is mountainous, with several parallel cordilleras running east–west; Pico Duarte in the Cordillera Central is the highest peak in the Caribbean. The island lies on the Enriquillo–Plantain Garden fault zone and has suffered devastating earthquakes, including those of 2010 and 2021 in Haiti. Its border shows a striking contrast in forest cover, the Dominican side retaining far more tree cover than the largely deforested Haitian side.
