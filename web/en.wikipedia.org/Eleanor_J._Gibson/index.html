Eleanor J. Gibson

Eleanor Jack Gibson (December 7, 1910 – December 30, 2002) was an American psychologist who studied perception in infants and children. She was a professor at Cornell University and, with her husband James J. Gibson, helped develop ecological psychology.

Visual cliff

Gibson is best known for the "visual cliff" experiment, developed with Richard Walk, in which infants were placed on a glass surface over an apparent drop. Most infants who could crawl refused to cross the "deep" side, showing that depth perception develops early and is not entirely learned.

Perceptual learning

Her theory of perceptual learning emphasized differentiation: infants learn by detecting the distinctive features and affordances of objects and environments, rather than by enriching impoverished sensations with associations. Her 1969 book Principles of Perceptual Learning and Development became a classic.

She received the National Medal of Science in 1992.
