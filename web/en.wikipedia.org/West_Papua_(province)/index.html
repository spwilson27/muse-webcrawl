West Papua (province)

[Image: West Papua (province)]

West Papua (Indonesian: Papua Barat) is an Indonesian province located in the western part of the island of New Guinea. Its capital is Manokwari. The province was created from the western portion of the former province of Papua (formerly Irian Jaya) in 2003, initially under the name West Irian Jaya, and renamed West Papua in 2007. In 2022, the new province of Southwest Papua was split off from its western portion, including the city of Sorong and the Raja Ampat Islands.

Geography

The province covers the Bird's Head (Doberai) Peninsula's eastern and southern parts and surrounding islands. It is known for rich biodiversity, dense rainforest, and marine ecosystems, particularly around the Bird's Head Seascape, which is among the most biodiverse marine regions on Earth.

Demographics

The population is a mix of indigenous Papuan peoples, including the Arfak, and migrants from other parts of Indonesia. Languages spoken include Indonesian and numerous local Papuan languages. Christianity is the majority religion, followed by Islam.

Economy

The economy is based on agriculture, fishing, mining — including the Tangguh liquefied natural gas field in Bintuni Bay — and growing ecotourism, especially diving around Raja Ampat (now administered by Southwest Papua).
