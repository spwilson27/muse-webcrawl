Perthshire (Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Pheairt), officially the County of Perth, is a historic county and registration county in central Scotland. Centred on the city of Perth on the River Tay, it stretches from the fertile lowlands of Strathmore and the Carse of Gowrie in the east to the mountains of the southern Highlands in the north and west, making it one of Scotland's largest and most varied counties — long nicknamed the "Big County".

Geography

The Highland Boundary Fault crosses Perthshire, dividing the lowland south-east from Highland Perthshire with its lochs — Tay, Tummel, Rannoch and Earn — and mountains including Ben Lawers (1,214 m), Schiehallion and Ben Vorlich. The River Tay, Scotland's longest river at about 193 km and its largest by discharge, rises in the county's western hills and flows through Perth to the Firth of Tay. Other towns include Crieff, Aberfeldy, Pitlochry, Dunkeld, Blairgowrie and Auchterarder.

History and today

Perth was one of Scotland's earliest royal burghs and a former capital; nearby Scone was the crowning place of Scottish kings, where the Stone of Scone was kept until its removal by Edward I in 1296. Dunkeld was an early ecclesiastical capital of the kingdom of Alba. Perthshire featured in the Jacobite risings — the battles of Killiecrankie (1689) and Sheriffmuir (1715) were fought within it. Administratively the county was abolished for local government in 1975; most of it now forms the core of the Perth and Kinross council area, with western portions in Stirling and Highland. The county survives for registration and ceremonial purposes, and "Perthshire" remains a strong identity in tourism, famed for its autumn colours and "Big Tree Country".
