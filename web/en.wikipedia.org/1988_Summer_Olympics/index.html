The 1988 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXIV Olympiad, were held from 17 September to 2 October 1988 in Seoul, South Korea. They were the second Summer Games held in Asia and the last Olympics of the Cold War era; after boycotts marred the 1980 and 1984 Games, both the Soviet Union and the United States competed, with 159 nations participating.

The 100 metres scandal

The defining event was the men's 100 metres final. Canada's Ben Johnson defeated defending champion Carl Lewis in a world-record 9.79 seconds, in what was billed as the greatest sprint race in history. Three days later Johnson tested positive for the anabolic steroid stanozolol, was stripped of the gold medal and record, and was sent home in disgrace. Lewis was elevated to champion. The affair transformed public awareness of doping in sport and led to Canada's Dubin Inquiry.

Highlights

American swimmer Matt Biondi won seven medals, five of them gold, and Kristin Otto of East Germany won six swimming golds. Florence Griffith Joyner set still-standing world records in the women's 100 and 200 metres. Greg Louganis won both diving events despite striking his head on the springboard during preliminaries. Tennis returned to the Olympics, with Steffi Graf completing her "Golden Slam," and table tennis made its debut. The Soviet Union topped the medal table in its final Summer Games.

North Korea boycotted after being denied a role as co-host, joined by Cuba and a handful of others, but the boycott was far smaller than those of the two preceding Games.
