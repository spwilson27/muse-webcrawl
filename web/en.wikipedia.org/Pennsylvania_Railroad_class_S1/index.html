The Pennsylvania Railroad (PRR) class S1 was a single experimental duplex-drive steam locomotive designed for high-speed passenger service. Numbered 6100, it was the longest and heaviest rigid-frame passenger steam locomotive ever built, and a showcase of late American steam technology.

Design

Built at the PRR's Altoona Works in 1939 and styled with a dramatic aerodynamic shroud by Raymond Loewy, the S1 used a 6-4-4-6 wheel arrangement: two sets of driving wheels, each powered by its own pair of cylinders. The duplex layout divided the power of the engine to reduce hammer blow on the track and allow very high speeds. The locomotive was over 140 feet long with its tender and displayed at the 1939 New York World's Fair, where it operated on rollers for visitors.

Service and fate

In service the S1 pulled premier passenger trains such as the Trail Blazer between Chicago and Crestline, Ohio, and was reputedly capable of speeds well above 100 mph, though such claims were never officially verified. Its great size restricted it to a single route, and excessive wheel slip plagued operations. Withdrawn in 1949 and scrapped the same year, the S1's concept influenced the later PRR T1 class, while its failure underlined the end of experimental steam development in the diesel era.
