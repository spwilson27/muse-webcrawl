Greenhouse gas emissions

Greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from human activities intensify the greenhouse effect and drive climate change. Most emissions consist of carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels: coal, oil, and natural gas. Other major gases include methane, nitrous oxide, and fluorinated gases. Global emissions have been estimated at roughly 55 to 59 billion tonnes of CO₂-equivalent per year in recent years, and they remain near record levels despite growth in renewable energy.

Sources by gas

Carbon dioxide accounts for about three-quarters of emissions, chiefly from fossil fuel combustion and industrial processes such as cement production. Methane contributes roughly 18%, arising from livestock, rice cultivation, oil and gas operations, coal mining, and landfills. Nitrous oxide, mainly from agricultural soils and fertilizers, contributes about 4–6%, while fluorinated gases (HFCs, PFCs, SF₆) make up a small but highly potent share.

Sources by sector

  • Energy — electricity and heat generation is the single largest sector, at roughly a quarter to a third of total emissions; energy use in transport, buildings and industry adds much more, so that energy overall underlies about three-quarters of emissions.
  • Industry — steel, cement, chemicals and other manufacturing, both from fuel use and process emissions.
  • Agriculture, forestry and land use (AFOLU) — roughly a fifth of emissions, including deforestation, livestock methane, and soil nitrous oxide.
  • Transport — road vehicles dominate.
  • Waste — landfill methane and wastewater.

Emissions by country

China is the largest current emitter, responsible for roughly 30% of global CO₂ emissions, followed by the United States (around 13–14%), India and the European Union. Historically, the United States is the largest cumulative emitter since the Industrial Revolution. Per-capita emissions vary widely: they are highest in major fossil-fuel-producing states and in wealthy countries such as the United States, Australia and Canada, and far lower in most of the developing world.

Trends

Global CO₂ emissions rose through most of the industrial era, dipping temporarily during the 2009 financial crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, and other disruptions. Atmospheric concentrations of CO₂ have passed 420 parts per million, compared with about 280 ppm before industrialization.

Mitigation

Reducing emissions to net zero by around mid-century is central to the Paris Agreement's goal of limiting warming to well below 2 °C, preferably 1.5 °C. Mitigation measures include deploying wind and solar power, electrifying transport and heating, improving energy efficiency, halting deforestation, cutting methane leaks, and developing carbon capture and storage.
