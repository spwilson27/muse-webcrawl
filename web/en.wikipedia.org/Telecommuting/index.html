Telecommuting

Telecommuting, also called remote work, telework or working from home, is a work arrangement in which employees do not commute to a central place of work such as an office building or store, instead performing their job from home or another location using telecommunications technology. The term was coined in 1973 by Jack Nilles, a former NASA engineer, who promoted "telecommuting" as an alternative to commuting during the energy crises of the 1970s.

History

Remote work grew slowly with the spread of personal computers, the internet and broadband in the 1990s and 2000s, concentrated in information-technology, professional and sales occupations. The COVID-19 pandemic of 2020 caused a sudden mass experiment in telework: at its peak, a large share of the workforce in advanced economies worked from home, and many employers subsequently adopted hybrid arrangements combining office and home days.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages commonly cited include the elimination of commuting time and cost, greater flexibility, access to wider talent pools, reduced office costs for employers, and lower congestion and emissions. Disadvantages include feelings of isolation, blurred boundaries between work and home life, difficulties in collaboration and management, unequal access to suitable home workspaces, and security concerns. Research on productivity has produced mixed results, varying by occupation and by how work is organised and measured.
