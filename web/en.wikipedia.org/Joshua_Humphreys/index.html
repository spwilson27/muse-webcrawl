Joshua Humphreys

Joshua Humphreys (June 17, 1751 – January 12, 1838) was an American shipbuilder and naval architect. He was the designer of the first ships of the United States Navy, including the original six frigates authorised by the Naval Act of 1794, and is often called the "Father of the American Navy."

Career

Born in Haverford, Pennsylvania, Humphreys was apprenticed to a shipbuilder in Philadelphia and rose to become a leading constructor in the port. During the American Revolutionary War he designed vessels for the Continental Navy.

When Congress authorised six frigates in 1794, Humphreys' design called for ships that were longer, broader, and more heavily built than contemporary frigates — essentially large enough to defeat any enemy frigate yet fast enough to escape a ship of the line. Built of dense live oak with heavy scantlings, the class included USS Constitution, USS United States, and USS Constellation. Their success in the War of 1812 vindicated his concept.

Legacy

Two US Navy ships have borne the name Humphreys, and his son Samuel Humphreys was also a distinguished naval constructor. Humphreys' office draughts and correspondence remain key sources for early American naval architecture.
