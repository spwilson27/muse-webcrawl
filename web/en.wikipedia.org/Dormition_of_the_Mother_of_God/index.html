Dormition of the Mother of God

A Byzantine-style icon of the Dormition, showing Christ receiving Mary's soul.

The Dormition of the Mother of God is a Great Feast of the Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and Eastern Catholic Churches, celebrating the "falling asleep" (Greek Koimesis) of Mary, the Theotokos, and her being taken up into heaven. It is celebrated on 15 August (28 August in churches using the Julian calendar), and corresponds closely to the Roman Catholic Feast of the Assumption of Mary.

Tradition

According to tradition, the apostles were miraculously gathered to Mary's bedside in Jerusalem (or, in another tradition, Ephesus) as she died. The apostle Thomas arrived late and, finding her tomb empty, testified that her body had been taken into heaven. The narratives derive from late antique apocryphal writings known as the Transitus Mariae.

Observance

The feast is preceded by a two-week Dormition Fast from 1 August, during which services of supplication (Paraklesis) to the Mother of God are sung. In many places the feast is accompanied by processions of an epitaphios of the Theotokos, mirroring the burial procession of Good Friday. In Greece the day is one of the most important religious holidays, with major pilgrimages, notably to Tinos.

Iconography

The standard icon shows Mary reclining on a bier surrounded by the apostles, with Christ standing behind holding her soul depicted as a swaddled infant.
