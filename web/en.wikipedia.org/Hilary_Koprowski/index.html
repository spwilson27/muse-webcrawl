Hilary Koprowski (5 December 1916 – 11 April 2013) was a Polish-American virologist and immunologist. He developed the first successful live oral vaccine against poliomyelitis, administered it to a human for the first time in 1950, and directed mass trials in the Belgian Congo and Poland that preceded the widespread adoption of Albert Sabin's rival oral vaccine. He led the Wistar Institute in Philadelphia from 1957 to 1991, transforming it into a major centre of vaccine research.

Early life

Koprowski was born in Warsaw and trained as a pianist at the Warsaw Conservatory while studying medicine at Warsaw University, receiving his medical degree in 1939. Fleeing the German invasion, he reached Brazil via Italy, working for the Rockefeller Foundation in Rio de Janeiro on yellow fever, before moving to the United States in 1944, where he joined Lederle Laboratories.

Polio vaccine

At Lederle, Koprowski attenuated poliovirus by passage through rodents. On 27 February 1950 he fed his vaccine to a child for the first time — famously testing it on himself and his assistant beforehand — and by the late 1950s millions of children in the Congo and Poland had received his vaccine. Sabin's strains, however, were chosen for licensing in the United States in 1961–62, and Koprowski's priority claims led to a long and bitter rivalry. In the 1990s a hypothesis linking his Congo trials to the origin of AIDS was widely publicised but was subsequently refuted by genetic and archival evidence and by testing of his stored vaccine stocks, which contained no HIV or SIV.

Later work

At the Wistar Institute, Koprowski's teams developed the WI-38 human diploid cell strain used in many vaccines, and he oversaw production of a safer rabies vaccine grown in human cells. He continued research into monoclonal antibodies and plant-based vaccines into his nineties, and was also a serious classical pianist and composer.
