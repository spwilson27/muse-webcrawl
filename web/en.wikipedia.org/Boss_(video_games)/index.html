Boss (video games)

Type: Enemy character / encounter
Function: Significant challenge, often at end of a level
Variants: Miniboss, final boss, superboss, raid boss

In video games, a boss is a significant computer-controlled opponent that is generally much stronger than other enemies and is often fought at the end of a level, stage or section of the game. A fight with a boss character is commonly referred to as a boss battle or boss fight.

Characteristics

Bosses usually have larger health pools, distinctive attack patterns and unique arenas, and defeating them typically advances the story or unlocks new areas and abilities. Boss encounters often serve as tests of the skills and mechanics the player has learned.

Variants

A miniboss is a lesser boss encountered mid-level; the final boss is the last major opponent of the game; optional, especially powerful bosses are sometimes called superbosses. In massively multiplayer games, raid bosses are designed for groups of players.

History

Among the earliest recognised boss fights were those in 1970s games such as dnd (1975), and the concept became a staple through arcade titles like Phoenix and franchises such as The Legend of Zelda and Mega Man, the latter famous for letting players choose the order of its boss stages.
