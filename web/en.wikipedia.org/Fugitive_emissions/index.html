Fugitive emissions

Fugitive emissions are accidental or unintended releases of gases or vapours from pressurized equipment, industrial processes or fuel systems — leaks from valves, pumps, flanges, pipelines, storage tanks and wells — as opposed to controlled releases through stacks, vents or flares. They are a significant source of greenhouse gases, in particular fugitive methane from oil, gas and coal operations, and of volatile organic compounds affecting air quality.

Sources

In the energy sector, fugitive methane arises from drilling and production sites, compressor stations, processing plants, transmission pipelines and abandoned wells; in coal mining, methane escapes from seams during and after extraction. A small number of "super-emitter" sites typically accounts for a disproportionate share of total leakage.

Detection and measurement

Traditional accounting relied on engineering estimates, but direct measurement — by infrared cameras, aircraft surveys and satellites such as TROPOMI — has repeatedly shown emissions substantially higher than official inventories, a discrepancy highlighted by IEA Methane Tracker reports.

Mitigation

Leak detection and repair (LDAR) programmes, vapour-recovery units, replacement of pneumatic controllers and plugging of orphan wells are among the cheapest available climate mitigation measures, since captured gas can often be sold. Regulations in the United States, the EU's 2024 methane regulation, and voluntary initiatives such as the Global Methane Pledge target fugitive emissions specifically.
