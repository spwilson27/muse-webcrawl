Kinmon incident

The Hamaguri Gate of the Kyoto Imperial Palace, scarred by bullets from the fighting.

The Kinmon incident (Japanese: 禁門の変, Kinmon no Hen, "Forbidden Gate Incident"), also known as the Hamaguri Gate Rebellion, was an attempted coup in Kyoto, Japan, on 20 August 1864, during the Bakumatsu period. Forces of the Chōshū Domain, loyal to the sonnō jōi ("revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians") movement, marched on the Kyoto Imperial Palace to seize control of the Imperial Court and restore Chōshū's influence, which had been destroyed in the coup of September 1863.

The battle

Some 3,000 Chōshū troops, including a militia of commoners raised by Takasugi Shinsaku's followers, advanced on the palace gates. They were resisted by forces loyal to the Tokugawa shogunate, principally from the Aizu and Satsuma domains, guarding the palace for the coalition that had expelled Chōshū. Fighting broke out at several gates — Hamaguri, Sakaimachi and others — and fires set during the battle spread through the city, destroying some 28,000 houses. The Chōshū forces, commanded in part by senior retainer Kunishi Shinano, were defeated; many of their leaders were killed or committed suicide, and the survivors retreated to Chōshū.

Aftermath

The incident branded Chōshū an enemy of the Imperial Court. The shogunate launched the First Chōshū Expedition later in 1864 to punish the domain, and Chōshū was forced to submit temporarily. Yet within two years Chōshū, rearmed and allied with its former enemy Satsuma, defeated the shogunate's Second Expedition, setting the stage for the Boshin War and the Meiji Restoration.
