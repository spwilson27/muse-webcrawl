Dominion of New England

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Dominion of New England in America (1686–1689) was an administrative union of English colonies in the New England region of North America. Created by King James II, it merged the separate colonial governments into a single province governed by a royal appointee, in an effort to tighten imperial control, enforce the Navigation Acts and coordinate defence against the French and Native Americans.

Creation and government

The dominion was established in 1686 after the Massachusetts Bay Colony's charter was vacated in 1684. It initially comprised Massachusetts, Plymouth, New Hampshire, Maine and the Narragansett Country. Joseph Dudley served briefly as president of the council before Sir Edmund Andros arrived as governor in December 1686. Connecticut and Rhode Island were annexed in 1687, and New York and the Jerseys were added in 1688. Andros ruled with an appointed council and no elected assembly; he levied taxes without colonial consent, challenged existing land titles, restricted town meetings and enforced Anglican worship, making the regime deeply unpopular.

Collapse

When news reached Boston of the Glorious Revolution and the overthrow of James II, colonists rose in the Boston revolt of 18 April 1689, arresting Andros and his officials. The dominion dissolved as each colony resumed its previous government. Andros was sent back to England but was never tried; he later served as governor of Virginia and Maryland. The experiment left a lasting legacy of colonial suspicion toward imperial consolidation.
