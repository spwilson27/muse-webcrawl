Dr. Strangelove or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bomb (known simply and more commonly as Dr. Strangelove) is a 1964 political satire black comedy film directed, produced, and co-written by Stanley Kubrick. It satirises Cold War fears of a nuclear conflict between the Soviet Union and the United States. The film stars Peter Sellers in three roles, alongside George C. Scott, Sterling Hayden, Keenan Wynn, Slim Pickens and Tracy Reed.

Plot

United States Air Force Brigadier General Jack D. Ripper, having gone insane, orders his B-52 bombers to carry out a pre-emptive nuclear strike on the Soviet Union under "Wing Attack Plan R". In the War Room of the Pentagon, President Merkin Muffley and his advisers — including the hawkish General Buck Turgidson and the ex-Nazi scientist Dr. Strangelove — try to recall the bombers. One plane, commanded by Major "King" Kong, evades recall because its radio is damaged and drops its bomb on a Soviet missile base, triggering the Soviet "Doomsday Machine" that will destroy all life on Earth. The film ends with Kong riding the bomb down, waving his cowboy hat, followed by a montage of nuclear explosions set to "We'll Meet Again".

Production

The film was adapted from Peter George's serious thriller novel Red Alert (1958); Kubrick initially planned a straight drama but found the material kept becoming comic as he wrote, and embraced satire with co-writer Terry Southern. Its release was delayed by the assassination of President John F. Kennedy in November 1963. Sellers plays Group Captain Lionel Mandrake, President Muffley and Dr. Strangelove himself.

Reception and legacy

Dr. Strangelove was a critical and commercial success, nominated for four Academy Awards including Best Picture, and is routinely ranked among the greatest films ever made and the greatest comedies. In 1989 it was among the first 25 films selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry. Its lines — "Gentlemen, you can't fight in here! This is the War Room!" — and its portrayal of mutually assured destruction remain iconic.
