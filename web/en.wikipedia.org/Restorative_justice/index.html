Restorative justice

Restorative justice is an approach to justice that focuses on repairing the harm caused by wrongdoing through inclusive processes involving victims, offenders, and affected communities. Rather than centring punishment, it asks who was harmed, what their needs are, and whose obligation it is to meet those needs.

Practices

Common practices include victim–offender mediation, family group conferencing (developed in New Zealand from Māori traditions), and peacemaking circles drawn from Indigenous practices in North America. Agreements may involve apology, restitution, and community service.

Evidence and criticism

Research generally finds high victim satisfaction and modest reductions in reoffending for some programmes. Critics caution against coercion, power imbalances between parties, and the use of restorative processes for serious offences without safeguards. South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission applied restorative principles at a national scale.
