The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) is a physical sciences laboratory and non-regulatory agency of the United States Department of Commerce. Its mission is to promote American innovation and industrial competitiveness by advancing measurement science, standards, and technology.

History

NIST was founded in 1901 as the National Bureau of Standards, created because the young nation's patchwork of weights and measures hampered industry and trade. It was renamed NIST in 1988. Over its history the institute has contributed to technologies from atomic clocks and the first image of an individual atom to modern encryption standards.

Work

NIST maintains the primary standards for units of measurement in the United States and develops reference materials, measurement methods, and technical standards used across industry. Its publications include the Federal Information Processing Standards (FIPS) and the widely adopted Cybersecurity Framework. NIST ran the competitions that produced the AES encryption standard and the SHA-3 hash function, and it leads work on post-quantum cryptography standards.

Programs

Beyond its laboratories, NIST operates the Hollings Manufacturing Extension Partnership, which supports small manufacturers, and the Baldrige Performance Excellence Program. Its staff have included multiple Nobel laureates in physics, recognized for work including laser cooling and Bose–Einstein condensates.
