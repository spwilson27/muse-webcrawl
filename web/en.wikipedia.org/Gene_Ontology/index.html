Gene Ontology

The Gene Ontology (GO) is a major bioinformatics initiative to unify the representation of gene and gene product attributes across all species. More specifically, the project aims to maintain and develop a controlled vocabulary of gene and gene product attributes, annotate genes and gene products, and provide tools for accessing and analysing the data.

Structure

The ontology covers three domains: cellular component, the parts of a cell or its extracellular environment; molecular function, the elemental activities of a gene product at the molecular level; and biological process, operations or sets of molecular events with a defined beginning and end. Each GO term is structured as a node in a directed acyclic graph, related to broader parent terms by is a and part of relationships.

Annotations

Gene products are associated with GO terms through annotations, each supported by an evidence code describing how the association was made — for example, direct experimental assay, sequence similarity, or electronic inference. Curated annotations are produced by model organism databases and protein resources such as UniProt, and are used in enrichment analysis to interpret large-scale experiments such as transcriptomics studies.

History

The Gene Ontology Consortium was founded in 1998 by researchers working on the genome databases of three model organisms: Drosophila (FlyBase), mouse (Mouse Genome Informatics), and yeast (Saccharomyces Genome Database). It has since grown to include databases for many major organisms and is coordinated as part of the Open Biomedical Ontologies (OBO) Foundry.
