Puget Sound War
Date1855–1856
LocationPuget Sound, Washington Territory

The Puget Sound War was an armed conflict that took place in the Puget Sound region of the Washington Territory from 1855 to 1856, between the United States military, local militias and members of Native American tribes, primarily the Nisqually, Muckleshoot, Puyallup and Klickitat, provoked by treaties imposed by territorial governor Isaac Stevens.

Course

Fighting followed the signing of the Treaty of Medicine Creek (1854), which confined tribes to reservations many considered inadequate. In October 1855 attacks on settler families triggered open war; notable events included the Battle of Seattle in January 1856, in which the USS Decatur supported the settlement. Nisqually chief Leschi led Native forces and was later captured, tried and hanged in 1858, a verdict widely contested; he was posthumously exonerated by a historical court in 2004.
