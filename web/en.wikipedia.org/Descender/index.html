Descender

Descenders in typography extend below the baseline

In typography, a descender is the portion of a letter that extends below the baseline of the type. In many Latin-alphabet fonts, the letters g, j, p, q, and y have descenders, as do some uppercase letters in certain typefaces.

Typographic parameters

The length of descenders is a distinguishing feature of typeface design and influences line spacing (leading): text set too tightly can make descenders collide with ascenders of the line below. Related measures include the x-height, ascender height, and cap height.

Other scripts

Letters dropping below the baseline also occur in other scripts and in diacritic placement, such as the cedilla.
