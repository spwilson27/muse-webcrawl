The Musqueam (Hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓: xʷməθkʷəy̓əm) are a Coast Salish Indigenous people whose traditional territory includes much of what is now the city of Vancouver in British Columbia, Canada. Their name derives from məsqʷəy̓, a flowering plant that once grew in the area. The Musqueam have lived in the Fraser River delta region for thousands of years, with their principal winter village at the mouth of the Fraser River.

History

Musqueam ancestors occupied villages along the north arm of the Fraser River and around Burrard Inlet and English Bay. The ancient village and burial site known as c̓əsnaʔəm, located in the Marpole neighbourhood of Vancouver, has been occupied for roughly 4,000 years and is one of the largest ancient middens in western Canada. In 2012, proposed condominium development on the site led to protests and negotiations; portions of the land were eventually returned to the Musqueam.

Under the Indian Act, the community is governed by the Musqueam Indian Band, an elected chief and council. In 2008 the band reached a reconciliation agreement with the province of British Columbia, and in subsequent years acquired significant lands in Vancouver, including golf course properties and land near the University of British Columbia.

Language and culture

The traditional language of the Musqueam is Hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓, the downriver dialect of Halkomelem, a Coast Salish language. Longhouse traditions, cedar weaving, salmon fishing and sxwayxwey masked dance ceremonies remain important cultural practices. The band operates the Musqueam Cultural Education Resource Centre and works with the Museum of Anthropology at UBC on language revitalization and cultural programming.

Territory

Musqueam traditional territory encompasses lands and waters around Vancouver, Richmond, Delta, Burnaby, New Westminster and Surrey. The band's main reserve, Musqueam Indian Reserve No. 2, sits at the western edge of Vancouver's Point Grey area, facing the Strait of Georgia.
