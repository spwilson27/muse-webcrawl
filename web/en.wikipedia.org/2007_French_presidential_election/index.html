2007 French presidential election

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 2007 French presidential election, the ninth of the Fifth Republic, was held on 22 April 2007 (first round) and 6 May 2007 (second round). The incumbent president, Jacques Chirac, did not seek a third term. In the run-off, Nicolas Sarkozy of the Union for a Popular Movement (UMP) defeated Ségolène Royal of the Socialist Party (PS), winning 53.06% of the vote to Royal's 46.94%.

Background

President Jacques Chirac, in office since 1995, announced on 11 March 2007 that he would not stand for a third term. The campaign was dominated by questions of economic reform, unemployment, law and order, immigration, and France's place in Europe following the rejection of the European Constitutional Treaty in the 2005 referendum.

First round

Twelve candidates stood in the first round. Sarkozy led with 31.18% of the vote, followed by Royal with 25.87%. The centrist François Bayrou of the UDF achieved a strong third place with 18.57%, well ahead of the far-right National Front leader Jean-Marie Le Pen, who fell to 10.44%. Turnout was exceptionally high at 83.77%, the highest for a first round since 1974.

Second round

Between the rounds, Royal and Bayrou held an televised debate, an unusual step, as Royal sought the support of centrist voters. In the run-off on 6 May, Sarkozy won with 53.06% against 46.94% for Royal, becoming the sixth President of the Fifth Republic. He took office on 16 May 2007.

Results

In the first round: Sarkozy 11,448,663 votes (31.18%); Royal 9,500,112 (25.87%); Bayrou 6,820,119 (18.57%); Le Pen 3,834,530 (10.44%); Olivier Besancenot 1,498,581 (4.08%); Philippe de Villiers 2.23%; Marie-George Buffet 1.93%; Dominique Voynet 1.57%; Arlette Laguiller 1.33%; José Bové 1.32%; Frédéric Nihous 1.15%; Gérard Schivardi 0.34%.
