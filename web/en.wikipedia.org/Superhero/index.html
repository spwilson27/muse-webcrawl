Superhero

A superhero is a stock character, usually in comic books, fiction and film, who possesses extraordinary or superhuman powers or abilities and is dedicated to fighting evil and protecting the public. Common conventions include a distinctive costume, a code name, a secret identity, extraordinary abilities (such as flight, super-strength or accelerated healing) or exceptional skills and equipment, and a rogues' gallery of supervillains.

History

Superheroes draw on antecedents in mythology, folklore and pulp magazines — figures such as Hercules, Robin Hood, the Scarlet Pimpernel, Zorro and the Shadow. The genre in its modern form began with the debut of Superman, created by Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster, in Action Comics #1 (1938), which launched the Golden Age of Comic Books and characters such as Batman, Wonder Woman, Captain America and the Flash. After a postwar decline, Marvel Comics' relatable heroes of the 1960s — Spider-Man, the X-Men, the Fantastic Four — revived the genre, and later decades brought darker, deconstructive treatments such as Watchmen.

Global reach and media

Superheroes now dominate global popular culture through film: the Marvel Cinematic Universe is the highest-grossing film franchise in history. Japanese manga and anime have parallel traditions (such as Astro Boy and My Hero Academia), and the archetype has expanded across television, video games and literature, with continuing debate about representation, diversity and the genre's themes of power and responsibility.
