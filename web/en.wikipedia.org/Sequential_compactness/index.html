Sequential compactness

In mathematics, a topological space is sequentially compact if every sequence of points in the space has a convergent subsequence whose limit lies within the space.

Relationship to compactness

Sequential compactness is related to but distinct from compactness. In metric spaces the two notions coincide: a metric space is compact if and only if it is sequentially compact, a result embodied in the Bolzano–Weierstrass property of the real line. In general topological spaces, neither property implies the other, although every compact first-countable space is sequentially compact, and every sequentially compact countably compact space is compact.

Examples

The closed unit interval [0, 1] is sequentially compact by the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem. The first uncountable ordinal with the order topology is sequentially compact but not compact.
