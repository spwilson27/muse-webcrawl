Nut (fruit)

TypeFruit (botanical)
True nutsAcorn, hazelnut, chestnut, beechnut
Related termsDrupe, legume, culinary nut

A nut is a fruit consisting of a hard or leathery shell around a kernel (seed) which is usually edible. In botany, the term is applied strictly: a nut is a dry, indehiscent fruit with a woody pericarp (shell) that does not split open to release its single seed. True nuts include the acorn of the oak, the hazelnut (filbert) of the hazel, the chestnut, and the beechnut.

Botanical versus culinary usage

Many foods called nuts in the kitchen are not botanical nuts. Peanuts are legumes; almonds, walnuts, and pecans are the seeds of drupes (the walnut's fleshy husk makes it a drupe-like "tryma"); cashews are seeds borne beneath a cashew apple; Brazil nuts are seeds from a capsule; and pine nuts and pistachios are seeds. Botanically, the nut develops from a pistil with an inferior ovary, and the shell is the matured ovary wall.

Ecology and uses

Nuts are an energy-rich food for wildlife; oaks and other nut-bearing trees practise masting, producing irregular bumper crops that satiate seed predators and ensure some seeds survive to germinate. Animals such as squirrels and jays disperse nuts by scatter-hoarding. For humans, nuts are eaten raw, roasted, and ground into butters and oils, and they feature in nutrition research for their unsaturated fats, protein, and micronutrients. Tree-nut and peanut allergies are among the most common serious food allergies.
