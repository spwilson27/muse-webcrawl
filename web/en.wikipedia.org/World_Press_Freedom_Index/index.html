The World Press Freedom Index is an annual ranking of 180 countries and territories published by the non-governmental organisation Reporters Without Borders (Reporters sans frontières, RSF) since 2002. It assesses the degree of freedom available to journalists and news media in each country.

Methodology

The index is compiled from a questionnaire completed by media experts in each country, combined with RSF's records of abuses and violence against journalists. Since a methodology overhaul in 2022, each country receives a score out of 100 based on five indicators: political context, legal framework, economic context, sociocultural context and safety. Countries are classed in five categories from "very serious" to "good" and shown on a colour-coded world map.

Findings

Norway, Ireland, Denmark and other northern European countries have typically occupied the top positions in recent editions, while North Korea, China, Myanmar, Afghanistan and Eritrea have ranked at or near the bottom. RSF reports a long-term global decline in press freedom, driven by disinformation, political pressure on media and violence against reporters.
