Music streaming

Streaming music on a smartphone

Music streaming refers to the distribution of recorded music over the internet in which audio is played in real time without the listener permanently downloading a copy. Streaming services give subscribers on-demand access to large catalogues for a monthly fee or in exchange for listening to advertisements.

History

Early services such as Rhapsody (2001) pioneered subscription streaming. Spotify, launched in 2008, popularised the freemium model and grew into the largest dedicated music streaming service, followed by competitors including Apple Music, Amazon Music, YouTube Music, and Tidal. By the late 2010s streaming accounted for the majority of global recorded-music revenue, reviving industry growth after years of decline caused by piracy.

Economics

Services typically pay rights holders a pooled share of revenue distributed pro rata by streams, leading to ongoing debate about per-stream payouts — fractions of a cent per play — and their fairness to artists and songwriters. Playlists and recommendation algorithms have become central to music discovery and marketing.
