Raj of Sarawak

Raj of Sarawak
Capital: Kuching
Established: 1841
Ended: 1946
Rulers: James Brooke, Charles Brooke, Charles Vyner Brooke

The Raj of Sarawak, also spelled Raj of Sarawak and commonly called the Kingdom of Sarawak, was a state in Borneo established by Sir James Brooke in 1841 by receiving independent territory from the Sultanate of Brunei as a reward for helping fight piracy and insurgency among the indigenous peoples of the region. It was ruled by Brooke and his descendants, known as the White Rajahs, for just over a century.

History

James Brooke, a former soldier of the British East India Company, arrived in Sarawak in 1839 and helped the Sultan of Brunei suppress a rebellion. As a reward he was made Rajah of Sarawak in 1841, with the territory later confirmed by treaty. Brooke and his successors expanded the state substantially at Brunei's expense, fighting piracy, suppressing headhunting among the Dayak peoples, and encouraging Chinese and Malay immigration.

His nephew Charles Brooke succeeded him in 1868 and ruled until 1917, consolidating administration, building infrastructure and codifying law. Charles Vyner Brooke, the third Rajah, ruled until the Japanese occupation of Borneo in December 1941 during the Second World War. After the war, in 1946, Vyner Brooke ceded Sarawak to Britain as a Crown Colony, a decision that provoked the anti-cession movement among many Sarawakians. Sarawak later joined the Federation of Malaysia in 1963.

Government and society

The Brooke regime governed through a supreme council and a system of Residents and district officers, relying heavily on local Malay and Dayak chiefs. The administration was famously lean and paternalistic; the Rajahs styled themselves as protectors of the native peoples against exploitation. The economy rested on sago, rubber, pepper, gold and later oil, with revenues administered personally by the Rajah as both sovereign and head of government.

Legacy

The Brooke century remains a distinctive chapter in Southeast Asian history: a dynastic state founded by an English adventurer, recognised by Britain and the United States, which issued its own stamps, currency and flag. Kuching's Astana palace and Fort Margherita survive as landmarks of the era.
