Grand Tourer

A grand tourer (from Italian gran turismo), often abbreviated to GT, is a type of automobile designed for high speed and long-distance driving, combining luxury and performance features. Grand tourers are typically front-engined, rear-wheel-drive two-door coupes with either a two-seat or a 2+2 seating arrangement.

Characteristics

The ideal grand tourer is able to cruise at high speed for long distances in comfort, carrying its occupants and their luggage in refined surroundings. Compared with a pure sports car, a grand tourer places greater emphasis on ride comfort, interior appointments, interior space and straight-line performance, and is usually larger and heavier. Engines are typically powerful, often V8 or V12 units in classic examples, or large six-cylinder engines.

Well-known grand tourers include the Aston Martin DB series, the Bentley Continental GT, the Ferrari 250 GT and later front-engined Ferraris, the Jaguar E-Type and XK series, the Maserati GranTurismo, and the Mercedes-Benz SL.

History

The term derives from the Grand Tour, the traditional trip of Europe undertaken by young aristocrats, and from the Italian tradition of gran turismo racing cars developed in the 1950s, such as the Alfa Romeo and Ferrari berlinettas built for events like the Mille Miglia. Post-war manufacturers in Britain, Italy, Germany and later Japan developed the concept into a distinct market segment of luxurious high-performance coupes.

In motorsport

"GT" is also used as a racing classification. GT racing categories (such as GT3 and GT4) are based on production-derived cars, and endurance events including the 24 Hours of Le Mans feature grand touring classes alongside prototypes.
