Subcomandante Marcos

Subcomandante Marcos, later known as Subcomandante Galeano, is a Mexican spokesman and military commander of the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN), the Indigenous insurgent group that rose up in Chiapas on 1 January 1994. Recognizable by his ski mask and pipe, Marcos became the public voice of the movement through poetic communiqués mixing politics, humour, and storytelling.

Background

The Mexican government identified Marcos as Rafael Sebastián Guillén Vicente, a former university lecturer, though he never confirmed the identification. He joined the guerrilla movement in the mountains of Chiapas in the 1980s.

Zapatista uprising

The EZLN rebellion protested the North American Free Trade Agreement and centuries of marginalization of Indigenous peoples. After twelve days of fighting, a ceasefire led to negotiations, and Marcos's writings drew international attention and solidarity networks.

Later years

In 2014 Marcos announced that the persona of Subcomandante Marcos would cease to exist, re-emerging under the name Subcomandante Galeano in honour of a murdered Zapatista teacher.
