The Argentine Army (Ejército Argentino) is the land warfare branch of the Armed Forces of the Argentine Republic. It was formally created on 29 May 1810, days after the May Revolution, and counts among its heroes General José de San Martín, whose Army of the Andes liberated Chile and Peru from Spanish rule.

History

The Army fought in the wars of independence, the Cisplatine War, the Paraguayan War, and the Conquest of the Desert. In the 20th century it intervened repeatedly in politics, most notoriously in the 1976–1983 military dictatorship. Its last major war was the Falklands War of 1982, fought mainly with conscript troops.

Modern force

Conscription was abolished in 1994 after the Carrasco case, making the Army an all-volunteer force. It contributes to United Nations peacekeeping missions and is organized into divisions and specialized mountain, jungle, and rapid-deployment forces.
