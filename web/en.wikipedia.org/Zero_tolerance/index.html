Zero tolerance

A zero tolerance policy is one which imposes a penalty for every infraction of a rule, without discretion or exceptions. People in positions of authority are not allowed to exercise judgement in individual cases; penalties, such as suspension or expulsion in schools or fines and arrest in public order enforcement, are predetermined and imposed regardless of mitigating circumstances.

History

The term originated in United States drug enforcement in the 1980s, during the war on drugs, and spread in the 1990s to policing strategies associated with the broken windows theory and to school discipline policies, under which students were suspended or expelled for possessing weapons, drugs, or sometimes ordinary objects and medications deemed to fall within a banned category.

Criticism

Critics argue that zero tolerance policies can produce disproportionate and absurd outcomes, remove professional discretion, and contribute to a "school-to-prison pipeline", with enforcement falling unevenly on minority groups. Research reviews, including by the American Psychological Association, have found little evidence that such policies improve school safety or student behaviour. Supporters argue that consistent, predictable enforcement deters misconduct and removes bias from individual decisions.
