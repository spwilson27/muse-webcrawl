Matriarchs (Bible)

The Matriarchs (Hebrew: אמהות, Imahot) are the four women regarded in Judaism as the ancestral mothers of the Jewish people, counterparts to the three Patriarchs. They are Sarah, wife of Abraham; Rebekah, wife of Isaac; and Leah and Rachel, wives of Jacob. The matriarchs are central figures in the Book of Genesis.

The four matriarchs

Sarah, Abraham's wife, is promised a son in old age and gives birth to Isaac. Rebekah, chosen as Isaac's wife, bears the twins Jacob and Esau and helps secure the blessing for Jacob. Leah, Jacob's first wife, is the mother of six tribes' ancestors including Judah and Levi. Rachel, Jacob's beloved wife, is the mother of Joseph and Benjamin; she died in childbirth on the road to Bethlehem, where her tomb is traditionally venerated.

Burial and significance

According to Genesis, Sarah, Rebekah and Leah were buried with their husbands in the Cave of Machpelah in Hebron, the Cave of the Patriarchs. In Jewish prayer the matriarchs are invoked alongside the patriarchs, for example in blessings referring to "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob", and in modern liturgy the matriarchs are often named explicitly. Rabbinic tradition credits the matriarchs with prophecy and regards them as models of faith and kindness.
