Frosted glass

Frosted glass
MaterialGlass, surface-treated
MethodsAcid etching, sandblasting, films, coatings
UsesPrivacy glazing, light diffusion, decoration

Frosted glass is glass whose surface has been roughened or treated so that it becomes translucent: it transmits light but scatters it, blurring the view through the pane. It is used where privacy or soft, diffused light is wanted without blocking illumination entirely.

Production

Traditional frosting is done by acid etching — historically with hydrofluoric acid — or by sandblasting (abrasive blasting) the surface. Modern alternatives include translucent interlayers in laminated glass, applied frost-effect films, and ceramic frit coatings fired onto the glass. The treated surface scatters light because its microscopic roughness redirects rays in many directions.

Applications

Frosted glass is common in bathroom windows and shower screens, office partitions, doors, and light fittings, where it softens glare and diffuses light. It appears in decorative ware and in picture framing glass. "Smart glass" technologies can switch a pane between clear and frosted states electrically, but are a distinct product.
