Naomi (biblical figure)

Naomi (Hebrew: נָעֳמִי, "pleasant") is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the mother-in-law of Ruth in the Book of Ruth. Her story is set in the period of the judges and centres on loyalty, bereavement and redemption.

Story

Naomi, her husband Elimelech and their sons Mahlon and Chilion leave famine-stricken Bethlehem for Moab. Elimelech dies, the sons marry Moabite women — Ruth and Orpah — and then also die, leaving Naomi widowed and childless. Hearing that the famine has ended, Naomi sets out for home. She urges her daughters-in-law to remain in Moab; Orpah turns back, but Ruth famously refuses: "Where you go, I will go... your people shall be my people, and your God my God."

On arriving in Bethlehem, Naomi asks to be called Mara ("bitter"), saying she went away full and returned empty. Through Ruth's gleaning in the fields of Boaz, a kinsman-redeemer, Naomi's family fortunes are restored; Ruth marries Boaz and bears Obed, Naomi's grandson and an ancestor of King David.
