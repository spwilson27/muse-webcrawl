Tuscany (Italian: Toscana) is a region in central Italy. Its capital, Florence, is home to some of the world's most recognizable Renaissance art and architecture, including Michelangelo's David, Botticelli's works in the Uffizi, and the cathedral with its terracotta-tiled dome engineered by Brunelleschi.

History

The region was the heartland of the Etruscan civilization before Roman rule. In the Middle Ages and Renaissance, Tuscany's city-states — above all Florence under the Medici — became the cradle of the Renaissance. The Tuscan dialect of Florence became the basis of standard Italian through the writings of Dante, Petrarch, and Boccaccio.

Geography and economy

The region has an area of about 22,985 square kilometres. Surrounded and crossed by major mountain chains, and with few (but fertile) plains, the region has a relief that is dominated by hilly country. The rolling hills of Chianti and the Val d'Orcia produce some of Italy's most famous wines — Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino, and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano — and the province of Massa-Carrara is home to the famous white marble quarries.

Culture

Besides Florence, the region's cities include Pisa with its leaning tower, Siena with its shell-shaped Piazza del Campo and the Palio horse race, Lucca, Arezzo, and the medieval hill towns of San Gimignano and Cortona. Tourism, agriculture, and wine are the pillars of the economy.
