'Ndrangheta

The 'Ndrangheta is a prominent Italian Mafia-type criminal syndicate based in the Calabria region of southern Italy. Originating in the 19th century among rural communities in Calabria, it is organized around closely knit family clans known as 'ndrine, bound by blood ties and marriage alliances that have historically made the organization highly resistant to infiltration and to defections by informants.

Structure

The basic unit is the 'ndrina, a family clan controlling a territory, usually a town or neighbourhood. Clans are coordinated through collegial bodies. In the province of Reggio Calabria, the mandamenti and the Provincia (also called Crimine) have served as coordinating structures. Rituals, ranks, and codes of honour modelled in part on older secret societies have long been part of its internal culture.

Activities and reach

Historically involved in kidnapping for ransom during the 1970s and 1980s, the 'Ndrangheta later expanded into cocaine trafficking — becoming a major broker in the European cocaine market through links with South American producers — as well as extortion, money laundering, public-contract fraud, and waste trafficking. By the early 21st century it had established cells and investments across northern Italy, the rest of Europe, North America, and Australia, and is widely regarded by law enforcement and researchers as one of the wealthiest and most powerful organized crime groups in the world.

Law enforcement response

Major operations include the 2010 Crimine-Infinito operation linking Calabrian and Lombard clans, the 2020–2023 Rinascimento-Scott maxi-trial in Vibo Valentia, and numerous international operations coordinated with Europol and foreign police forces. Despite these efforts, its family-based structure continues to pose distinctive challenges to investigators.
