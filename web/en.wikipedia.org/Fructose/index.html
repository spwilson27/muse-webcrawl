Fructose

Simple sugar (monosaccharide), "fruit sugar"

FormulaC6H12O6
TypeKetose monosaccharide
Found inFruit, honey, root vegetables, sucrose
Relative sweetness~1.2–1.8 × sucrose

Fructose, or fruit sugar, is a simple ketonic monosaccharide with the chemical formula C6H12O6, the same formula as glucose but with a different structure. It is found in many plants, often bonded with glucose to form the disaccharide sucrose, and is one of the three dietary monosaccharides (with glucose and galactose) absorbed directly into the blood.

Properties and sources

Fructose is the sweetest of the naturally occurring sugars and is highly soluble in water. It occurs free in fruits, berries, melons, honey (where it makes up roughly half the sugar content) and some root vegetables, and is produced commercially from corn starch as high-fructose corn syrup, widely used to sweeten soft drinks and processed foods. In plants it may be stored as the polymer inulin and other fructans.

Metabolism

Unlike glucose, fructose is metabolised almost entirely in the liver, where fructokinase converts it to intermediates that can enter glycolysis, be stored as glycogen, or be converted to fat. Because its initial metabolism bypasses the main regulatory step of glycolysis, high fructose intakes have been studied in connection with insulin resistance, fatty liver disease and gout — fructose breakdown also accelerates production of uric acid. Hereditary fructose intolerance, a rare genetic disorder of the enzyme aldolase B, makes fructose ingestion dangerous.
