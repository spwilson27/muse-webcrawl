Kitchen

Kitchen

TypeRoom
PurposeCooking and food preparation

A kitchen is a room or part of a room used for cooking and food preparation in a dwelling or in a commercial establishment. A typical modern middle-class kitchen is equipped with a stove, a sink with hot and cold running water, a refrigerator, worktops and fitted cabinets arranged according to ergonomic principles, plus a microwave oven, dishwasher and other electric appliances.

History

For most of history, cooking was done over an open fire, and dedicated kitchens emerged slowly: in medieval great houses a separate kitchen served the hall, while ordinary homes cooked in a single multi-purpose room. Chimneys, iron ranges and piped water transformed kitchens in the nineteenth century, and the fitted kitchen was rationalised in the 1920s by Christine Frederick’s scientific management and Margarete Schütte-Lihotzky’s Frankfurt kitchen, designed for efficient work in a small space.

Design and types

Kitchen layouts are commonly described by the "work triangle" between sink, stove and refrigerator, and by plan shapes such as single-wall, galley, L-shaped, U-shaped and island kitchens. Commercial kitchens in restaurants are organised by stations along the brigade system, while specialised forms include the kitchenette, the outdoor kitchen, the galley kitchen of ships and aircraft, and the ceremonial kitchens of temples and palaces.
