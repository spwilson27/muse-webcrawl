Drag racing

Top Fuel dragsters at the start line.

Drag racing is a type of motor racing in which two automobiles or motorcycles compete side by side to be the first to cross a finish line over a short, straight course, usually an eighth or quarter mile, from a standing start. Reaction time and acceleration are decisive, and races are organised in elimination brackets.

Classes

The fastest class, Top Fuel dragsters, are over 10 metres long and produce more than 11,000 horsepower from supercharged nitromethane-burning engines, accelerating to over 540 km/h in under four seconds over 1,000 feet (the competition distance for the fastest classes since 2008). Other professional classes include Funny Car, Pro Stock and Pro Stock Motorcycle; countless sportsman classes allow amateurs to race street-based cars.

Organisation

The National Hot Rod Association (NHRA), founded by Wally Parks in 1951, is the principal sanctioning body in the United States. A race begins with a burnout to heat the tyres, staging at the "Christmas tree" starting lights, and a parachute deployment to help slow the fastest cars after the finish.
