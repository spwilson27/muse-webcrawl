Reginald Blomfield

Sir Reginald Theodore Blomfield RA (20 December 1856 – 27 December 1942) was a British architect, garden designer, and author. A leading figure of the late Arts and Crafts movement who later championed a renewed English classicism, he is best remembered today for his work with the Imperial War Graves Commission after the First World War, including the Menin Gate at Ypres and the Cross of Sacrifice found in Commonwealth war cemeteries.

Born: 20 December 1856, Bow, Devon
Died: 27 December 1942 (aged 86)
Nationality: British
Occupation: Architect, author
Notable works: Menin Gate; Cross of Sacrifice; Lambeth Bridge (with others)

Career

Blomfield was educated at Haileybury and Exeter College, Oxford, and articled to his uncle, the architect and church-restorer Arthur Blomfield. He travelled in Europe before setting up his own practice in 1884, initially designing country houses in the Arts and Crafts spirit and writing influential books on formal garden design — The Formal Garden in England (1892), written with the artist F. Inigo Thomas, argued for structured, architectural gardens at a time when naturalistic planting was fashionable.

He was professor of fine art at the Royal Academy and wrote widely on architectural history, including A History of French Architecture and studies of Renaissance architecture in England. Blomfield was knighted in 1919.

War memorials

As one of the principal architects of the Imperial War Graves Commission, alongside Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker, Blomfield designed the Cross of Sacrifice — the simple stone cross with a bronze sword that stands in Commonwealth war cemeteries with forty or more graves. His Menin Gate memorial in Ypres, Belgium (unveiled 1927), commemorates more than 54,000 Commonwealth soldiers who died in the Ypres Salient and have no known grave. He also designed numerous civic war memorials across Britain.
