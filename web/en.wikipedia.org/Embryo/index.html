An embryo is the initial stage of development of a multicellular organism. In organisms that reproduce sexually, embryonic development is the part of the life cycle that begins just after fertilisation of the female egg cell by the male sperm cell. The resulting fusion of these two cells produces a single-celled zygote that undergoes many cell divisions that produce cells known as blastomeres.

Development

After fertilisation, the zygote divides to form a morula and then a blastocyst, which implants in the uterus in mammals. During gastrulation the cells organise into three germ layers — ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm — from which all tissues and organs arise. In human development the embryonic period extends to the end of the eighth week, after which the developing organism is called a fetus.

Embryos in other organisms

In plants, the embryo is part of the seed, consisting of precursor tissues for leaves, stem, and root. In animals such as fish, amphibians, and birds, embryonic development occurs in eggs outside the mother's body, nourished by yolk. Embryogenesis is studied in model organisms including the fruit fly Drosophila, the nematode C. elegans, zebrafish, and the mouse.

Research and ethics

Embryos are central to developmental biology and to assisted reproduction (IVF). Human embryonic stem cells, derived from early embryos, can differentiate into many cell types and are the subject of scientific research and ethical debate in many countries.
