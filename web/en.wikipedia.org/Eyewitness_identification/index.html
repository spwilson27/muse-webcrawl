Eyewitness identification

In eyewitness identification, in criminal law, evidence is received from a witness who identified a person as someone they had seen in connection with a crime. Methods include police lineups, photo arrays, and show-ups.

Reliability

Psychological research has shown that eyewitness memory is fallible and susceptible to suggestion, stress, weapon focus, and cross-racial identification difficulties. Mistaken identifications have been a leading factor in wrongful convictions later overturned by DNA evidence.

Reforms

Recommended practices include double-blind administration, sequential presentation of lineup members, unbiased instructions, and recording witness confidence at the time of first identification.
