A kilowatt-hour (unit symbol: kW⋅h or kWh; commonly abbreviated kWh) is a non-SI unit of energy equal to 3.6 megajoules (MJ). It is the product of a power of one kilowatt (1 kW) sustained for one hour: if energy is transmitted or used at a constant rate of one kilowatt for one hour, the total energy is one kilowatt-hour.

Usage

The kilowatt-hour is most familiar as the billing unit for electrical energy delivered to consumers by electric utilities. A household appliance rated at 1,000 watts (1 kW) running for one hour, or a 100-watt device running for ten hours, consumes one kilowatt-hour. Average household electricity consumption ranges from roughly 10 kWh per day in modest households to around 30 kWh per day in the United States. Battery capacity for electric vehicles is also usually quoted in kilowatt-hours (for example, 40–100 kWh).

Confusion with kilowatts

The kilowatt-hour is a unit of energy, whereas the kilowatt is a unit of power (energy per unit time). Multiplying power by time gives energy, so "kilowatts per hour" is a different (and rarely used) concept; news reports sometimes confuse the two. The symbol is properly written kWh — a dot (kW⋅h) may be used to emphasise that it is a product.

Conversions

  • 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ = 3,600 kJ
  • 1 kWh ≈ 3412 BTU
  • 1 megawatt-hour (MWh) = 1,000 kWh; 1 gigawatt-hour (GWh) = 1,000,000 kWh
