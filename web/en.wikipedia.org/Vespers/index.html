Vespers

Vespers is the evening prayer service in the Liturgy of the Hours (canonical hours) of the Catholic Church and in the daily office of Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Lutheran, and Anglican traditions (where it is often called Evensong in its choral Anglican form). The name derives from the Latin vesper, "evening" — hence also the star Venus as the "evening star."

Structure

In the Roman Rite, Vespers (Evening Prayer) consists of an introduction ("O God, come to my assistance"), a hymn, psalmody (two psalms and a New Testament canticle), a short reading, a responsory, the Magnificat (the canticle of Mary from Luke 1:46–55) sung with its antiphon, intercessions, the Lord's Prayer, and a concluding prayer. In the Byzantine Rite, Great Vespers on Saturday evenings and feasts includes the singing of Psalm 103, the hymn "O Gladsome Light" (Phos Hilaron) — one of the oldest Christian hymns — the prokeimenon, and, on great feasts, readings and a litia procession.

History and music

Evening prayer descends from Jewish evening devotions and the early church's prayer at the lighting of the lamps. Monastic rules, above all that of St Benedict, fixed its place among the daily hours. Vespers has inspired major musical settings, most famously Claudio Monteverdi's Vespro della Beata Vergine (1610), as well as settings by Mozart, Rachmaninoff (in his All-Night Vigil), and many others. The Sicilian Vespers (1282), a revolt that began at the hour of evening prayer in Palermo, took its name from the service.
