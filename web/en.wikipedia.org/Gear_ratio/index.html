Gear ratio

The gear ratio of a gear train is the ratio of the angular speeds (or rotational speeds) of the input gear to the output gear, equivalently the ratio of the number of teeth on the driven gear to the number of teeth on the driving gear. It determines how a mechanism trades speed against torque.

Definition

gear ratio = (teeth on output gear) / (teeth on input gear) = (input speed) / (output speed)

For example, if a 10-tooth pinion drives a 40-tooth gear, the gear ratio is 4:1 (or 4.0). The output gear then turns at one quarter of the input speed but delivers roughly four times the torque (neglecting friction losses). A ratio greater than 1 is a reduction drive (or "underdrive"); a ratio less than 1 is an overdrive, increasing speed at the cost of torque.

Applications

Gear ratios are central to the design of transmissions in automobiles, where each selectable gear offers a different ratio suited to starting, cruising or climbing; to bicycle drivetrains, where the ratio between chainring and sprocket determines pedalling effort; and to clocks, industrial machinery and robotics. Overall ratios of multi-stage trains are found by multiplying the ratios of the individual stages, and idler gears change direction without affecting the ratio's magnitude.
