The Water Music (HWV 348–350) is a collection of orchestral movements, often published as three suites, composed by George Frideric Handel. It premiered on 17 July 1717 during a royal barge trip on the River Thames by King George I.

Premiere

According to contemporary reports, the king travelled from Whitehall to Chelsea with musicians performing on a neighbouring barge, and liked the music so much that it was played three times during the outing. The large outdoor performance used a substantial orchestra including horns and trumpets.

Music

The suites are in F major, D major and G major, comprising dances and movements such as minuets, bourrées, hornpipes and the famous "Alla Hornpipe". The Water Music, with Handel's Music for the Royal Fireworks, remains among his most popular orchestral works.
