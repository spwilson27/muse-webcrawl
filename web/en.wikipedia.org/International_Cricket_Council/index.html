International Cricket Council (ICC)
Founded: 15 June 1909 (as Imperial Cricket Conference)
Headquarters: Dubai, United Arab Emirates
Members: 108 (12 full members)
Tournaments: Cricket World Cup, T20 World Cup

The International Cricket Council (ICC) is the world governing body of cricket. It organises world championship events, most notably the Cricket World Cup, and coordinates action on playing rules, match officials, and anti-corruption in the international game.

History

The body was founded in 1909 as the Imperial Cricket Conference by representatives of England, Australia, and South Africa. It was renamed the International Cricket Conference in 1965 and took its current name in 1987. Membership gradually expanded beyond the Commonwealth, and headquarters moved from Lord's in London to Dubai in 2005.

Membership

The ICC has 108 member nations: 12 full members, whose teams play Test cricket, and 96 associate members. Full members include England, Australia, India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, New Zealand, South Africa, the West Indies, Zimbabwe, Afghanistan, and Ireland.

Activities

The ICC stages the World Cup (50 overs), the T20 World Cup, the Champions Trophy, and the World Test Championship, appoints elite umpires and referees, maintains team rankings, and runs development programmes. Its revenues derive mainly from global events, above all World Cups hosted in major markets. See also Cricket and Cricket World Cup.
