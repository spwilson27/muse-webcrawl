Laminitis

Laminitis
FieldEquine veterinary medicine
AffectedHorses and ponies
Risk factorsObesity, EMS, PPID, grain overload, sepsis

Laminitis is a painful and potentially crippling disease of the horse's foot involving inflammation and damage to the laminae — the interlocking tissues that suspend the pedal (coffin) bone within the hoof capsule. When the laminae fail, the bone can rotate or sink, a process often called "founder".

Causes

Laminitis is now understood to arise through several pathways: endocrinopathic laminitis linked to equine metabolic syndrome and pituitary pars intermedia dysfunction (PPID) accounts for most cases; sepsis-related laminitis can follow grain overload, colitis, or retained placenta; and supporting-limb laminitis can develop when a horse overloads one leg because of injury to the opposite limb.

Signs and treatment

Affected horses show reluctance to move, a "sawhorse" stance rocking weight back onto the heels, a strong digital pulse, and heat in the feet. Management combines treating the underlying cause, cryotherapy in the acute stage, analgesia, dietary control of sugar and starch, and therapeutic shoeing; severe cases with pedal bone penetration have a poor prognosis and may require euthanasia.
