Panavision is an American motion picture equipment company specialising in cameras and lenses, based in Woodland Hills, California. Founded in 1954 by Robert Gottschalk, it became famous for its anamorphic widescreen lenses and its proprietary 35 mm camera systems, which it rents — rather than sells — to film productions. The credit "Filmed in Panavision" appears on thousands of films.

History

Panavision began by making anamorphic projection lenses to serve the CinemaScope boom. It then developed superior camera lenses — the Auto Panatar lenses used from the late 1950s — that corrected anamorphic distortions ("mumps") of earlier systems. Its Super Panavision 70 format used 65 mm film for epics such as Lawrence of Arabia. The Panaflex camera (1972), designed with Albert Mayer, was a lightweight, quiet, reflex 35 mm camera that transformed location shooting, and the Genesis HD camera (2004), developed with Sony, was a landmark digital cinema camera. The Millennium DXL2 (2018), built around a RED 8K sensor, continues the line in the digital era.

Business model

Unlike most manufacturers, Panavision owns and maintains its equipment, renting complete camera packages through a worldwide network of offices. It also supplies lighting and grip equipment through subsidiaries. Many celebrated cinematographers have long-standing relationships with the company, and Panavision lenses are often chosen for their distinctive optical character.
