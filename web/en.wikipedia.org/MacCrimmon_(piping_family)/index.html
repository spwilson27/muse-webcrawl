MacCrimmon (piping family)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The MacCrimmons (Gaelic: MacCruimein) were a Scottish family celebrated as hereditary pipers to the chiefs of Clan MacLeod of Dunvegan on the Isle of Skye, and as the foremost composers and teachers of piobaireachd, the classical music of the Great Highland bagpipe. For roughly three centuries, from about 1500 into the early nineteenth century, successive generations of the family maintained a celebrated piping college at Borreraig, near Dunvegan, to which pupils travelled from across the Highlands.

Origins

Family tradition and clan lore hold that the MacCrimmons came to Skye from Ireland or, in some accounts, that an early MacCrimmon served the MacLeods of Harris and Dunvegan in the sixteenth century. The family's lands at Borreraig were held rent-free in return for their services as hereditary pipers. Tradition names Iain Odhar MacCrimmon (c. 1550–c. 1625) among the earliest of the line, and credits his descendants with shaping piobaireachd into its classical form.

Famous members

Donald Mor MacCrimmon (c. 1570–1640) is associated with some of the earliest attributed piobaireachd compositions. Patrick Mor MacCrimmon (c. 1645–1730) is credited with many of the great classical tunes, including laments and salutes still played in competition today. His brother Patrick Og (c. 1645–1730s), and later Donald Ruadh MacCrimmon (d. 1825), who served in the Peninsular War era and is linked with the tune "The MacCrimmons will never return" tradition, continued the line. John MacCrimmon, the last hereditary piper, gave up the office in the early nineteenth century as the old clan order declined.

Legacy

At Borreraig a memorial cairn, erected in 1933, commemorates the MacCrimmons near the site of their college. A substantial part of the classical piobaireachd repertoire is attributed to the family, including "Lament for the Children", "The Earl of Ross's March" and "Lament for Donald Ban MacCrimmon". The MacCrimmon name remains one of the most prestigious in piping, and a piping centre at Dunvegan continues to teach the art they codified.
