Philadelphia Museum of Art

The Philadelphia Museum of Art (renamed the Philadelphia Art Museum in 2025) is an art museum originally chartered in 1876 for the Centennial Exposition in Philadelphia. The main museum building on Fairmount, a hill at the northwest end of the Benjamin Franklin Parkway, was completed in 1928. The museum administers collections containing more than 240,000 objects, spanning European, American, and Asian art, and is among the largest art museums in the United States by gallery space.

Collections

Holdings include the world's largest collection of works by Marcel Duchamp, including Nude Descending a Staircase, No. 2 and Étant donnés; major works by the Brancusi and Picasso bequests; Thomas Eakins's The Gross Clinic; and extensive holdings of American decorative arts, arms and armour, and East Asian art. The museum also administers the Rodin Museum and historic houses in Fairmount Park.

The "Rocky Steps"

The museum's east entrance steps became internationally famous after the 1976 film Rocky, in which Sylvester Stallone's character runs up them during a training montage. Visitors frequently re-enact the run, and a bronze statue of Rocky stands near the bottom of the steps. A major renovation led by architect Frank Gehry, the "Core Project", reopened interior spaces in 2021.
