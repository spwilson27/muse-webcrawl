Share capital

Share capital is the portion of a company's equity that has been obtained by trading stock to a shareholder for cash or other items of value. It represents the funds raised by issuing shares, as distinct from retained earnings accumulated from profitable trading.

Components

Share capital appears on the balance sheet and typically comprises the nominal (par) value of issued shares plus share premium — the amount paid by subscribers above nominal value. Legal systems distinguish authorised share capital (the maximum a company is permitted to issue), issued share capital (shares actually allotted), and paid-up capital (amounts shareholders have actually paid).

Legal functions

Historically, share capital rules were intended to protect creditors: capital maintenance doctrines restrict returning capital to shareholders, for example by limiting dividends to distributable profits and regulating share buybacks and reductions of capital. Modern reforms in several jurisdictions have relaxed nominal-value and authorised-capital requirements, particularly for private companies.

Changes

Share capital can be increased by new issues, rights issues, and bonus (scrip) issues capitalising reserves, and reduced through buybacks, cancellations, or formal capital reductions approved as required by company law.
