The Visuddhimagga (Pali for "The Path of Purification") is the 'great treatise' on Theravada Buddhist doctrine and meditation written by the scholar-monk Buddhaghosa in the 5th century CE in Sri Lanka. It is a comprehensive manual systematizing the interpretation of the Mahavihara tradition.

Contents

The work is organised around the threefold training of virtue (sīla), concentration (samādhi) and wisdom (paññā), corresponding to the seven stages of purification. Its twenty-three chapters describe monastic discipline, forty meditation subjects, the development of the jhānas, the five aggregates, dependent origination, and the progressive stages of insight culminating in nibbāna.

Influence

The Visuddhimagga became the authoritative summary of Theravada orthodoxy and shaped Buddhist practice in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia and Laos. Its detailed meditation instructions underpin modern vipassanā movements, and its lists and categories were used by later Abhidhamma manuals. Like the devotional systems of Vaishnavism, it represents a great commentarial consolidation of an older tradition.

Translations

The standard English translation is Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli's The Path of Purification (1956), published by the Buddhist Publication Society.
