The Cambridge University Boat Club (CUBC) is the rowing club of the University of Cambridge, England. Founded in 1828, it is responsible for selecting and training the Cambridge crews that compete against Oxford University in the annual Boat Race on the River Thames. Since 2020 the club has united the previously separate men's, women's, and lightweight rowing clubs under a single organisation.

History

Cambridge first challenged Oxford to a race in 1829, at Henley-on-Thames, in a contest that became The Boat Race; the universities have raced most years since 1856 over the Championship Course between Putney and Mortlake in London. Cambridge crews wear light blue, traditionally said to derive from the colours of Eton or of the university's colleges. The women's Boat Race joined the men's on the Tideway in 2015, and the unified CUBC was formed in 2020 by merging the Cambridge University Boat Club, the Cambridge University Women's Boat Club (founded 1941), and the lightweight club.

Facilities

The club's historic Goldie Boathouse on the River Cam is named after former president John Goldie; the reserve crew for the Boat Race, Goldie, also bears his name. Because the Cam is narrow and busy, much of the squad's training takes place at the club's Ely boathouse on the River Great Ouse, opened in 2016, which provides a long, straight stretch of water suited to high-performance rowing.

The Boat Race

Cambridge and Oxford have contested the Boat Race since 1829, and the overall tally of wins has remained close throughout the event's long history, with Cambridge holding a narrow lead in the men's race in recent decades. The event draws hundreds of thousands of spectators to the banks of the Thames and a television audience of millions.
