Ivan Franko

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ivan Yakovych Franko (Ukrainian: Іван Якович Франко; 27 August 1856 – 28 May 1916) was a Ukrainian poet, writer, social and literary critic, journalist, interpreter, economist, political activist, and doctor of philosophy. He was a co-founder of the socialist and nationalist movement in western Ukraine and one of the most important figures in Ukrainian literature.

Life

Franko was born in the village of Nahuievychi in Galicia, then part of the Austrian Empire. He studied at the University of Lviv and later at Chernivtsi and Vienna, earning a doctorate in 1893. Repeatedly arrested for his political activities, he edited numerous journals and helped found the Ukrainian Radical Party in 1890.

Works

His enormous output includes the philosophical poem Moses, the novel Boryslav Laughs, the historical novella Zakhar Berkut, and the children's tale Lys Mykyta ("Mykyta the Fox"). Franko wrote in Ukrainian, Polish, and German, translated world literature into Ukrainian, and made major contributions to Ukrainian literary criticism and ethnography. The city of Ivano-Frankivsk is named in his honor.
