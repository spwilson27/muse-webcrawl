House of Lords

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The House of Lords is the upper house of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. Membership is by appointment, heredity or official function. The Lords shares the task of making and shaping laws with the elected House of Commons, and both houses meet in the Palace of Westminster.

Type: Upper house of Parliament
Members: Lords Spiritual and Lords Temporal
Presiding officer: Lord Speaker
Meeting place: Palace of Westminster, London

Composition

Members are called peers. The Lords Spiritual are 26 senior bishops of the Church of England. The Lords Temporal consist mainly of life peers appointed under the Life Peerages Act 1958, together with 92 hereditary peers who remain under the House of Lords Act 1999, which removed most hereditary members. The house has no fixed size and usually numbers around 800 members, making it one of the largest parliamentary chambers in the world.

Functions

The Lords scrutinises and revises legislation, holds the government to account through questions and committees, and provides a forum of expertise. Under the Parliament Acts, it cannot ultimately block most legislation passed by the Commons, and by the Salisbury Convention it does not block bills implementing the governing party’s manifesto commitments.

History

Originating in the councils of magnates and senior clergy that advised Anglo-Norman kings, the Lords emerged as a separate house in the 14th century. It was abolished during the Commonwealth (1649–1660) and restored with the monarchy. Until 2009 its senior members, the Law Lords, formed the United Kingdom’s highest court; that judicial role passed to the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom.
