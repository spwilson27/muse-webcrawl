Expo 86, officially the 1986 World Exposition on Transportation and Communication, was a world's fair held in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada, from May 2 to October 13, 1986. The fair, the theme of which was "Transportation and Communication: World in Motion – World in Touch", coincided with Vancouver's centennial and was opened by the Prince and Princess of Wales.

History

The idea for the fair originated with provincial minister Grace McCarthy and Patrick Reid, Canada's Commissioner General for the event. Initially planned for a site at the former Pier B-C near Canada Place, the exposition was ultimately built on the north shore of False Creek, on former railway lands. The fair was a major catalyst for downtown development in Vancouver.

Attractions

The site featured pavilions from 54 nations, provinces, and corporations. Notable landmarks built for the fair include Canada Place, the SkyTrain rapid transit system, BC Place Stadium, and the Plaza of Nations. The geodesic dome built as the Expo Centre later became Science World. The Canadian Pacific Railway exhibited a restored steam locomotive, and the fair's mascot was Expo Ernie, a robot.

Legacy

Expo 86 drew more than 22 million visitors and is widely credited with transforming Vancouver's international image and spurring redevelopment of the False Creek area. It remains the last world's fair held in North America to date.
