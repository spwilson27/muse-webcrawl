Tesseract

In geometry, the tesseract is the four-dimensional analogue of the cube. Just as the surface of the cube consists of six square faces, the hypersurface of the tesseract consists of eight cubic cells. The tesseract is one of the six convex regular 4-polytopes. It is also called an 8-cell, C8, (regular) octachoron, or four-dimensional hypercube.

Geometry

The tesseract can be constructed by extending the pattern of lower-dimensional cubes: a point is swept along a line to make a segment, a segment swept perpendicular to itself makes a square, a square swept makes a cube, and a cube swept in the fourth direction makes a tesseract. Its elements are:

ElementCount
Cells (cubes)8
Faces (squares)24
Edges32
Vertices16

With vertices at coordinates (±1, ±1, ±1, ±1), a tesseract centred at the origin has edge length 2 and four-dimensional hypervolume 16 for edge length s generally s4. Its Schläfli symbol is {4,3,3}. The dual polytope is the 16-cell. Like the cube, the tesseract can be unfolded into a net in three dimensions — most famously the arrangement of eight cubes in a cross shape used by Salvador Dalí in Crucifixion (Corpus Hypercubus) (1954).

Projections

Because four-dimensional objects cannot be viewed directly, tesseracts are studied through projections and sections, in analogy with the shadows of three-dimensional objects. Famous depictions include the cube-within-a-cube projection (showing the inner cube as the "far" cell), the rhombic-dodecahedron-like cell-first projection, and three-dimensional cross-sections that pass through tetrahedra, octahedra, and other polyhedra as a tesseract moves through our space.

In popular culture

The tesseract entered popular culture through Charles Howard Hinton's writings, Madeleine L'Engle's novel A Wrinkle in Time (1962), where "tessering" is a means of travelling through a fifth dimension, and Robert A. Heinlein's story "—And He Built a Crooked House—". The name was used for the cosmic artefact in the Marvel Cinematic Universe films, and for the climactic setting inside a black hole in the film Interstellar (2014).
