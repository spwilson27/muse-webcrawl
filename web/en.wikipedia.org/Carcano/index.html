Carcano is the frequently used name for a series of Italian bolt-action, internal box magazine fed, repeating military rifles and carbines. Introduced in 1891, the rifles were chambered for the rimless 6.5×52mm Carcano round and were developed by chief technician Salvatore Carcano at the Turin Army Arsenal.

History

The Carcano replaced the earlier Vetterli-Vitali rifles as the standard arm of the Italian military. The design combined a Mauser-style bolt with an en-bloc clip feeding system derived from the Mannlicher pattern. Variants included long rifles, short rifles, cavalry carbines, and special troops carbines, recognizable by their folding bayonets on some models.

World War II and later use

Italy issued a 7.35×51mm version, the Modello 1938, shortly before the Second World War, but supply difficulties led to a return to the 6.5mm chambering. Carcanos remained in Italian service after the war and were widely exported and surplused.

Assassination of John F. Kennedy

A surplus Carcano M91/38 rifle, purchased by mail order, was used by Lee Harvey Oswald in the assassination of U.S. President John F. Kennedy in Dallas on 22 November 1963, which brought the type worldwide notoriety.
