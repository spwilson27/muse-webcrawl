Superior laryngeal nerve

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The superior laryngeal nerve is a branch of the vagus nerve (tenth cranial nerve). It arises from the inferior ganglion of the vagus near the base of the skull and descends in the neck alongside the pharynx before dividing into two branches that supply structures of the larynx.

Branches and function

The internal laryngeal nerve pierces the thyrohyoid membrane and provides sensory innervation to the mucous membrane of the larynx above the vocal folds; stimulation triggers the protective cough and laryngeal closure reflexes. The external laryngeal nerve descends on the inferior constrictor of the pharynx, running near the superior thyroid artery, and supplies the cricothyroid muscle, which tenses the vocal folds and raises vocal pitch.

Clinical significance

The external branch is at risk during thyroidectomy because of its close relation to the superior thyroid vessels; injury impairs the ability to produce high-pitched sounds and causes voice fatigue — a noted hazard for professional singers. Damage to the internal branch can blunt the cough reflex and predispose to aspiration. The nerve's relationship to the superior thyroid artery is classified by systems such as that of Cernea to guide safe dissection.
