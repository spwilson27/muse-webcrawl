Mary was a first-century Jewish woman of Nazareth, the wife of Joseph and the mother of Jesus. In Christian theology she is venerated as the Virgin Mary because of the doctrine of the virgin birth — the belief that she conceived Jesus miraculously by the Holy Spirit while remaining a virgin. She is a central figure of Christianity, honoured especially in Catholic, Orthodox and Anglican traditions, and is also revered in Islam as Maryam, the mother of the prophet Isa (Jesus).

In the New Testament

Mary appears principally in the Gospels of Matthew and Luke, which describe the Annunciation by the angel Gabriel, the journey to Bethlehem and the Nativity, and in episodes such as the finding of the boy Jesus in the Temple, the wedding at Cana and the Crucifixion, where she stands at the foot of the cross. The Acts of the Apostles places her with the disciples at Pentecost.

Veneration and doctrines

The Council of Ephesus (431) affirmed her title Theotokos, "God-bearer". Catholic doctrine teaches her Immaculate Conception, perpetual virginity and Assumption into heaven; Marian devotions include the Rosary, pilgrimages to shrines such as Lourdes, Fátima and Guadalupe, and reported apparitions. Orthodox Christianity honours her as the Theotokos with feasts throughout the liturgical year, while most Protestant traditions honour her as the mother of Jesus but reject much Marian devotion.

In Islam

Maryam is the only woman named in the Qur'an, where an entire chapter (Surah Maryam) bears her name. Islam affirms the virgin birth of Jesus and regards Mary as one of the most righteous of women, chosen above the women of the worlds.

In art

The Madonna and Child is one of the most common subjects in Western art, depicted by artists from medieval icon painters to Michelangelo, Raphael and Caravaggio; Mary also appears in countless sculptures, hymns and musical settings such as the many versions of the Ave Maria and the Magnificat.
