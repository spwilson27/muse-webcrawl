PageMaker

PageMaker is a discontinued desktop publishing computer program, introduced in 1985 by the Aldus Corporation on the Apple Macintosh. Its graphical user interface, combined with the Apple LaserWriter laser printer and Adobe PostScript, effectively created the desktop publishing (DTP) industry, allowing users to produce professional-quality documents without traditional typesetting equipment.

History

PageMaker was developed by Aldus, founded by Paul Brainerd, who coined the term "desktop publishing". Version 1.0 shipped in July 1985 for the Macintosh; a version for the IBM PC followed in 1986, running under Windows 1.0. The program's pasteboard metaphor let users place text and graphics blocks on a page and arrange them freely. PageMaker won numerous awards and made Aldus one of the most successful early Macintosh software companies.

Aldus was acquired by Adobe Systems in 1994. Adobe continued PageMaker through version 7.0 (2001), but encouraged users to migrate to InDesign, which Adobe developed as its successor; PageMaker's feature set was increasingly aimed at small business rather than professional publishers. Adobe ended development of PageMaker in 2004.

Features

PageMaker supported long documents with master pages, style sheets, text threading between frames, and pre-press features such as colour separations and trapping in later versions. Its "Place" command imported text (in formats such as Microsoft Word) and graphics (TIFF, EPS, PICT). The program's emphasis was on quick assembly of documents from elements created elsewhere rather than on text composition itself.

Legacy

PageMaker is credited, along with the Macintosh and the LaserWriter, with democratising publishing in the mid-1980s and transforming graphic design workflows. Many of its interface conventions survived into InDesign and other layout applications.
