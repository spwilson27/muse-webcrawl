Montreal Metro

The Montreal Metro (French: Métro de Montréal) is a rubber-tyred underground rapid transit system serving Greater Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Operated by the Société de transport de Montréal (STM), it opened in 1966 and comprises four lines and 68 stations.

History

Construction began in 1962, inspired by the Paris Métro and timed to support Montreal's hosting of Expo 67. The initial network comprised the Green and Orange lines and the Yellow line to the Expo site on Île Sainte-Hélène and Longueuil. The Blue line opened in stages between 1986 and 1988, and the Orange line was extended to Laval in 2007.

Technology

Trains run on rubber tyres over concrete roll ways, a technology adopted from Paris that allows steep gradients and quiet operation. The entire system is underground, an adaptation to Montreal's severe winters.

Architecture and art

Each station was designed by a different architect and decorated with public art, making the network notable for its varied design.
