Mitanni (Akkadian: Ḫanigalbat) was a Hurrian-speaking state of northern Mesopotamia and southeastern Anatolia that flourished from about 1600 to 1260 BC. At its height in the fifteenth century BC it was one of the great powers of the Near East, rivalling Egypt, the Hittite Empire, Babylonia and Assyria, and controlling territory from the Mediterranean coast of Syria to the Zagros foothills.

History

The Hurrians had settled in northern Mesopotamia from at least the third millennium BC. By about 1600 BC a unified kingdom had emerged under a dynasty whose kings bore Indo-Aryan throne names and whose aristocracy — the maryannu chariot warriors — preserved a small Indo-Aryan linguistic element, visible in divine names (Mitra, Varuna, Indra, the Nasatyas) invoked in a treaty with the Hittites, and in the horse-training terminology of the Kikkuli text.

Under kings such as Barattarna and Shaushtatar, Mitanni expanded westward, taking Aleppo and Alalakh and reducing Assyria to vassalage; Shaushtatar is said to have looted the Assyrian capital Assur. Conflict with Egypt over Syria under Thutmose III ended in accommodation: by the reign of Artatama I the two powers exchanged royal marriages, and the Amarna letters preserve cordial correspondence and dowry negotiations between Amenhotep III and Tushratta.

Decline

A succession crisis after Tushratta's assassination split the kingdom. The Hittite king Suppiluliuma I exploited the division, installing a client king in the west, while Assyria under Ashur-uballit I and his successors reasserted independence and gradually annexed Mitanni's heartland. By about 1260 BC the remnant, known as Hanigalbat, had been absorbed into the Middle Assyrian Empire.

Society and sources

Mitanni society combined a Hurrian-speaking population with a royal court that used Akkadian for diplomacy. Archaeological knowledge is limited — the capital Washukanni has never been securely located — so the state is known mainly from foreign archives: the Egyptian Amarna letters, Hittite treaties and the provincial archives of Nuzi and Alalakh, which illuminate law, land tenure and chariot warfare in its realm.
