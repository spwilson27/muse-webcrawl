Window function

In signal processing and statistics, a window function (also known as an apodization function or tapering function) is a mathematical function that is zero-valued outside of some chosen interval. Typically, windows are symmetric around the middle of the interval, approach a maximum in the middle, and taper away from the middle.

When another function or waveform/data-sequence is "multiplied" by a window function, the product is also zero-valued outside the interval: all that is left is the part where they overlap. In effect, the data inside the interval is "viewed through the window" rather than the entire extent. Window functions are above all used in spectral analysis, in the design of filters, and in beamforming.

Applications

Window functions are used in spectral analysis/modification/resynthesis, the design of finite impulse response filters, as well as beamforming and antenna design. In spectral analysis, multiplying a finite-length segment of a signal by a suitable window reduces spectral leakage, the artefact in which energy from a sinusoid at one frequency appears spread across neighbouring frequencies because the segment does not contain a whole number of cycles.

Typical window functions

Common window functions include the rectangular window, the triangular (Bartlett) window, the Hann window, the Hamming window, the Blackman window, the Kaiser window, the Gaussian window, and the flat-top window. Each trades off main-lobe width against side-lobe attenuation: narrower main lobes give better frequency resolution, while lower side lobes give better suppression of leakage from strong neighbouring components.

Hann window

The Hann window, named after Julius von Hann, is defined by w(n) = 0.5(1 − cos(2πn/N)) over the interval. It is one of the most widely used windows because of its good balance of resolution and leakage suppression, and it is the basis of the "raised cosine" family that also includes the Hamming window.
