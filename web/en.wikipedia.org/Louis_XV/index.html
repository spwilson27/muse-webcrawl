Louis XV
Reign: 1 September 1715 – 10 May 1774
Born: 15 February 1710, Palace of Versailles

Predecessor: Louis XIV

Louis XV (15 February 1710 – 10 May 1774), known as Louis the Beloved (Louis le Bien-Aimé), was King of France from 1 September 1715 until his death in 1774. He succeeded his great-grandfather Louis XIV at the age of five and reigned for nearly 59 years, one of the longest reigns in European history.

Early reign

During his minority the kingdom was governed by the Regent, Philippe II, Duke of Orléans, whose Regency saw the disastrous financial experiments of John Law. After the Regent's death, the Duke of Bourbon and then Cardinal Fleury guided the kingdom; Fleury's ministry (1726–1743) restored stability and prosperity. Louis married Maria Leszczyńska of Poland in 1725, with whom he had ten children.

Wars and diplomacy

France fought the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748), gaining little despite victories such as Fontenoy. The Diplomatic Revolution of 1756 allied France with its old enemy Austria against Prussia and Britain. The Seven Years' War (1756–1763) ended disastrously: by the Treaty of Paris (1763) France ceded Canada and its claims east of the Mississippi to Britain, though it recovered its sugar islands in the Caribbean. Lorraine (1766) and Corsica (1768) were incorporated into France during his reign.

Late reign and legacy

Louis's court was famed for his mistresses — Madame de Pompadour, a major political and cultural influence until her death in 1764, and later Madame du Barry. Late in the reign, Chancellor Maupeou's coup of 1771 exiled the parlements and reformed the judiciary, a measure reversed by Louis XVI. Deeply in debt and widely criticised by the end, Louis died of smallpox in 1774; the fiscal crisis he left behind helped precipitate the French Revolution under his grandson.
