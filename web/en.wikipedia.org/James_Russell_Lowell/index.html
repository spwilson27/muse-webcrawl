James Russell Lowell (February 22, 1819 – August 12, 1891) was an American Romantic poet, critic, editor and diplomat. He belongs to the Fireside Poets, the group of nineteenth-century New England writers — including Longfellow, Whittier, Holmes and Bryant — whose work dominated American letters in their lifetimes. An ardent abolitionist, Lowell used his writing, especially the satirical Biglow Papers, in the service of reform.

James Russell Lowell (1819–1891)

Early life

Lowell was born at Elmwood, his family's estate in Cambridge, Massachusetts, into a distinguished New England family. He graduated from Harvard College in 1838 and, after an unhappy turn toward law, devoted himself to literature. His first book of poems appeared in 1841. Marriage to Maria White, herself a poet and abolitionist, deepened his commitment to anti-slavery causes.

Literary career

Lowell won fame in 1848 with two contrasting works: A Fable for Critics, a witty verse survey of American writers, and The Biglow Papers, dialect satires spoken by the Yankee farmer Hosea Biglow attacking the Mexican-American War and slavery; a second series during the Civil War supported the Union cause. Poems such as "The First Snowfall" (written after the death of his infant daughter) and "The Present Crisis" — from which the hymn "Once to Every Man and Nation" derives — became staples of American anthologies. In 1855 he succeeded Longfellow as Smith Professor of French and Spanish at Harvard, and in 1857 he became the first editor of The Atlantic Monthly, later co-editing the North American Review with Charles Eliot Norton. His critical essays, collected in Among My Books, are among the finest American literary criticism of the century.

Diplomatic career and death

President Rutherford B. Hayes appointed Lowell minister to Spain (1877–1880), and he then served as minister to Great Britain (1880–1885), where he became a popular public speaker and a friend of British literary society. He died at Elmwood in 1891 and is buried in Mount Auburn Cemetery, Cambridge.
