Seattle City Light

Seattle City Light is the publicly owned electric utility serving Seattle, Washington and parts of neighbouring communities. Founded in 1905, it is one of the largest municipal utilities in the United States and is a department of the City of Seattle.

History

City Light grew out of a municipal water and lighting plant on Cedar River. Under superintendent J. D. Ross in the early 20th century it built major hydroelectric projects on the Skagit River — Gorge, Diablo and Ross dams — which long supplied most of Seattle's power and became famous for the night-time illuminated tours of the Skagit.

Operations

The utility generates most of its electricity from hydroelectric sources, including the Skagit River Hydroelectric Project and Boundary Dam on the Pend Oreille River, supplemented by purchases from the Bonneville Power Administration and wind and other resources. It has described its retail supply as carbon neutral since 2005, achieved through a mix of low-carbon generation and offsets. City Light serves roughly 900,000 people in its service territory.
