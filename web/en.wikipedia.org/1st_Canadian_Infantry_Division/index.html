1st Canadian Infantry Division

Active1939–1945; 1954–1958; 1988–1999
CountryCanada
BranchCanadian Army
TypeInfantry
NicknameThe Old Red Patch

The 1st Canadian Infantry Division was an infantry division of the Canadian Army active during the First and Second World Wars. During the Second World War it was first commanded by Major-General Andrew McNaughton and fought in the Allied invasion of Sicily in 1943 and the Italian Campaign.

Second World War

Mobilised in September 1939, the division sailed for Britain in December of that year. After years of training and garrison duty, it landed in Sicily on 10 July 1943 as part of Operation Husky. It then fought up the Italian peninsula, taking part in the Moro River Campaign and the Battle of Ortona in December 1943, and the breaking of the Hitler Line in May 1944.

North-West Europe

In early 1945 the division was transferred from Italy to North-West Europe in Operation Goldflake, joining the First Canadian Army for the liberation of the Netherlands, where it ended the war.

Insignia

The division's formation sign was a plain red rectangle, a distinction dating to the First World War that earned its soldiers the nickname "The Old Red Patch".
