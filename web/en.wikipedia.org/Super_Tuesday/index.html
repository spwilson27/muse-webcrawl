Super Tuesday

Super Tuesday is the United States presidential primary election day in February or March when the greatest number of U.S. states hold primary elections and caucuses. Approximately one-third of all delegates to the presidential nominating conventions can be won on Super Tuesday, more than on any other single day. The results on Super Tuesday therefore often determine each party's eventual presidential nominee.

History

The term was used as early as 1976 for the final Tuesday of primaries, but it acquired its modern meaning in the 1980s, when a group of Southern states moved their primaries to the same early date in an effort to increase the region's influence in selecting nominees. The 1988 Super Tuesday, in which around twenty states voted, was designed to help a moderate Southern Democrat; instead the delegates split among several candidates. Since then, state legislatures have repeatedly shifted primary dates in a process known as "front-loading".

Importance

Because so many delegates are at stake across demographically and geographically diverse states, Super Tuesday tests a candidate's national appeal and organisational strength. Campaigns need substantial funds to advertise in many media markets simultaneously, and candidates who perform poorly often withdraw shortly afterwards. Strong showings can create momentum that proves decisive for the rest of the primary season.

Recent cycles

In 2008, twenty-four states participated in what was dubbed "Super Duper Tuesday" on 5 February. In 2016 Super Tuesday fell on 1 March and included the so-called "SEC primary" of Southern states. In 2020, fourteen states voted on 3 March, awarding about a third of pledged Democratic delegates; Joe Biden's victories that day revived his campaign and propelled him to the nomination. In 2024, fifteen states held Republican and Democratic contests on 5 March.
