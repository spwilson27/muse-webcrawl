Pafnuty Chebyshev

Pafnuty Lvovich Chebyshev
Born: May 16, 1821, Okatovo, Kaluga Governorate, Russian Empire
Died: December 8, 1894 (aged 73), Saint Petersburg, Russian Empire
Fields: Mathematics
Known for: Chebyshev polynomials, Chebyshev's inequality, law of large numbers

Pafnuty Lvovich Chebyshev (Russian: Пафну́тий Льво́вич Чебышёв, IPA: [pɐfˈnutʲɪj ˈlʲvovʲɪtɕ tɕɪbɨˈʂof]) (16 May 1821 – 8 December 1894) was a Russian mathematician and considered to be the founding father of Russian mathematics. Chebyshev is known for his fundamental achievements in the fields of probability, statistics, mechanics, and number theory.

Contributions

Chebyshev's inequality states that the probability that a random variable deviates from its mean by more than a specified number of standard deviations is bounded by the inverse square of that number; he used it to give a rigorous proof of the weak law of large numbers. In number theory he worked on the distribution of prime numbers, proving Bertrand's postulate (that for every n > 1 there is a prime between n and 2n) and bounding the prime-counting function. The Chebyshev polynomials — a sequence of orthogonal polynomials arising in approximation theory — and the Chebyshev linkage, a mechanical linkage that converts rotational motion into approximate straight-line motion (his "plantigrade machine"), also bear his name.

Legacy

Chebyshev taught at Saint Petersburg University for decades and founded the St. Petersburg mathematical school; his students included Andrey Markov and Aleksandr Lyapunov. The lunar crater Chebyshev and the asteroid 2010 Chebyshev are named in his honour.
