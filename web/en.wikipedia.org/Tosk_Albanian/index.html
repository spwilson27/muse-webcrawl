Tosk Albanian is the southern of the two principal dialect groups of the Albanian language, the other being Gheg spoken in the north. The traditional dividing line between the two is the Shkumbin River in central Albania.

Features

Tosk is characterised by the rhotacism of intervocalic n to r (for example, Gheg venë versus Tosk verë, "wine"), the retention of certain older features, and differences in verb morphology and vocabulary. Tosk varieties include the Arbëresh of Italy, the Arvanitika of Greece, and the dialects of Labëria, Çamëria and Korçë.

Standard language

Modern Standard Albanian, adopted in its unified form in 1972, is based predominantly on Tosk, a choice that remains a subject of discussion among Gheg speakers. Written Tosk has a literary tradition reaching back to early Albanian texts printed in the Arbëresh diaspora.
