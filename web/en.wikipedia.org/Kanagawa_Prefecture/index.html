Kanagawa Prefecture

Kanagawa Prefecture
Country: Japan
Region: Kantō
Capital: Yokohama
Population: about 9.2 million

Kanagawa Prefecture (神奈川県) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Kantō region of Honshu, southwest of Tokyo. Its capital and largest city is Yokohama, Japan's second most populous city. With over nine million residents, Kanagawa is the second most populous prefecture in the country, forming part of the Greater Tokyo Area.

Geography and cities

The prefecture faces Tokyo Bay and Sagami Bay and includes the Miura Peninsula, the resort mountains of Hakone, and Mount Tanzawa. Major cities besides Yokohama include Kawasaki, Sagamihara, Fujisawa, and the historic cities of Kamakura — seat of Japan's first shogunate — and Odawara. Enoshima island and the Shōnan coast are popular leisure destinations.

History

Kanagawa played a pivotal role in Japan's opening to the world: Commodore Perry's ships anchored off Uraga in 1853, the Convention of Kanagawa followed in 1854, and Yokohama opened as a treaty port in 1859, growing into a great centre of foreign trade. The prefecture later became a heartland of heavy industry and remains a hub for manufacturing, research, and port logistics.

Culture

The prefecture gives its name to Hokusai's celebrated woodblock print "The Great Wave off Kanagawa", one of the most famous images in Japanese art.
