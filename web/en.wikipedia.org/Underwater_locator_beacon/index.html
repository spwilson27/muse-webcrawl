Underwater locator beacon
SignalAcoustic pulse at 37.5 kHz, once per second
ActivationWater immersion

Used onFlight recorders; also submersibles and equipment

An underwater locator beacon (ULB), also called an underwater acoustic beacon or pinger, is a device fitted to aviation flight recorders (the cockpit voice recorder and flight data recorder) to assist in locating them after a crash over water. Once submerged, it is automatically activated and emits an ultrasonic pulse at 37.5 kHz once per second.

Operation and search

Search teams detect the pulses with hydrophones, towed pinger locators or autonomous underwater vehicles, typically within a range of a few kilometres depending on conditions. Regulations long required a battery life of at least 30 days; following the prolonged searches for Air France Flight 447 (2009) and Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 (2014), standards were strengthened, including 90-day beacons and requirements for recorders on new large aircraft to be fitted with low-frequency (8.8 kHz) beacons detectable at greater range.

Other uses

Similar pingers mark submersibles, remotely operated vehicles, oceanographic instruments and other equipment that might need recovery from the seabed.
