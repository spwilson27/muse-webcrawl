Cappadocia is a historical region in Central Anatolia, Turkey, famous for its distinctive "fairy chimney" rock formations, cave dwellings, rock-cut churches, and underground cities. The name derives from the Old Persian Katpatuka, often interpreted as "land of beautiful horses".

Geology and history

The landscape was formed by erosion of volcanic ash (tuff) deposited by eruptions of Mount Erciyes and neighbouring volcanoes. The soft rock was carved from ancient times into houses, monasteries, and refuges, including the multi-level underground cities of Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı. Cappadocia was successively part of the Hittite, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, and Byzantine worlds; the Cappadocian Fathers were important early Christian theologians. Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.

Tourism

Today Cappadocia is one of Turkey's most visited regions, noted for cave hotels and dawn hot-air balloon flights over the valleys around Göreme and Uçhisar.
