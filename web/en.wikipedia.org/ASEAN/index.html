The Association of Southeast Asian Nations, commonly abbreviated as ASEAN, is a regional intergovernmental organization comprising ten Southeast Asian countries: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam. Timor-Leste was admitted in principle as an observer and prospective eleventh member.

History

ASEAN was founded on 8 August 1967 in Bangkok by Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. Brunei joined in 1984, Vietnam in 1995, Laos and Myanmar in 1997, and Cambodia in 1999. The ASEAN Charter, adopted in 2007, gave the organization legal personality and a rules-based framework. The ASEAN Community — with its Political-Security, Economic and Socio-Cultural pillars — was formally launched on 31 December 2015.

Aims and the "ASEAN Way"

ASEAN promotes economic growth, regional peace and stability, and cooperation in trade, education and culture. Its decision-making style, often called the "ASEAN Way", emphasizes consensus, non-interference in members' internal affairs, and informal diplomacy. The ASEAN Free Trade Area (AFTA) and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership link its combined population of roughly 680 million people and an economy of several trillion US dollars.
