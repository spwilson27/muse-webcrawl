Sudbury Basin

The Sudbury Basin, an ancient impact structure in Ontario, Canada.

The Sudbury Basin, also known as the Sudbury Structure or the Sudbury Nickel Irruptive, is a major geological structure in Ontario, Canada. It is the third-largest known impact structure on Earth, as well as one of the oldest; the crater formed about 1.849 billion years ago in the Paleoproterozoic era by the impact of a bolide estimated at 10–15 km in diameter.

Geology and mining

The basin is an oval roughly 62 km long, 30 km wide and 15 km deep, subsequently deformed by orogenic compression. Debris from the impact was scattered over a vast area, with impact ejecta found as far away as Minnesota. The impact melted the crust and concentrated magmatic sulphide ores rich in nickel, copper, platinum, palladium, gold and other metals around the basin's margins. The region around the city of Greater Sudbury became one of the world's great mining camps; companies such as Inco (now Vale) and Falconbridge (now Glencore) built their fortunes on Sudbury ore.

Research and environment

Because its rocks resemble lunar highland breccias, Apollo astronauts trained at Sudbury. A century of smelting caused severe local environmental damage — acid rain from sulphur dioxide emissions denuded hillsides — but reclamation and regreening programmes since the 1970s, along with the reduction in emissions and the 381-metre Inco Superstack, have greatly improved the landscape. The deep Creighton Mine hosts the SNOLAB neutrino and dark-matter observatory.
