Joseph Leonard Goldstein (born 18 April 1940) is an American biochemist and geneticist. Working with Michael S. Brown at the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center in Dallas, he discovered the low-density lipoprotein (LDL) receptor and elucidated how cells regulate cholesterol metabolism — work that earned them the 1985 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Research

In the 1970s Goldstein and Brown studied patients with familial hypercholesterolemia, an inherited condition causing very high blood cholesterol and early heart attacks. They showed that normal cells take up LDL cholesterol through specific surface receptors, and that in familial hypercholesterolemia these receptors are absent or defective. Their work explained how cholesterol is cleared from the blood and laid the scientific foundation for the statin class of cholesterol-lowering drugs. Later, the pair discovered the SREBP pathway, the transcription-factor system by which cells sense and regulate their cholesterol and fatty-acid synthesis.

Career

Goldstein received his medical degree from UT Southwestern in 1966, trained at Massachusetts General Hospital, and spent two years at the National Institutes of Health with Nobel laureate Marshall Nirenberg. He returned to Dallas in 1972 to head the medical school's new division of medical genetics. With Brown, he has mentored generations of scientists and received numerous honours, including the Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research and the National Medal of Science.
