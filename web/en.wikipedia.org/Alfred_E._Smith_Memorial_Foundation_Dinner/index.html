Alfred E. Smith Memorial Foundation Dinner

The Alfred E. Smith Memorial Foundation Dinner, commonly called the Al Smith Dinner, is an annual white-tie charity dinner in New York City held to raise funds for Catholic Charities of the Archdiocese of New York. It is named for Al Smith, the four-term governor of New York and the Democratic nominee for president in 1928.

History

The dinner was first held in 1945, the year after Smith's death, under the sponsorship of Cardinal Francis Spellman. Since 1960, when John F. Kennedy and Richard Nixon attended, it has been a traditional stop for the major-party presidential nominees in election years, who deliver humorous, self-deprecating speeches roasting themselves and each other.

Tradition

The keynote speeches offer a brief truce in campaign hostilities and have produced many memorable lines. Notable appearances include those by Winston Churchill (by recording), Ronald Reagan, Barack Obama, Mitt Romney, Hillary Clinton and Donald Trump. The 2020 dinner was held virtually because of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Proceeds support charitable programs serving children and families in the Archdiocese of New York, raising millions of dollars at each event.
