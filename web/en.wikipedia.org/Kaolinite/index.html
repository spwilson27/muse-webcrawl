Kaolinite is a clay mineral with the chemical composition Al2Si2O5(OH)4. It is a layered silicate mineral, with one tetrahedral sheet of silica linked through oxygen atoms to one octahedral sheet of alumina. Kaolinite is the principal component of kaolin (china clay), the white clay essential to the manufacture of porcelain, and takes its name from Gaoling ("high ridge"), a village near Jingdezhen in China where the clay was first described.

Formation and occurrence

Kaolinite forms by the chemical weathering or hydrothermal alteration of aluminium silicate minerals such as feldspar, typically in warm, moist, acidic environments. Major deposits occur in China, the United States (Georgia), Brazil, the United Kingdom (Cornwall), and Ukraine.

Uses

The mineral's softness, whiteness, fine particle size, and chemical inertness make it a major industrial raw material: as the basis of porcelain and other ceramics, as a filler and coating pigment in paper, in paints, rubber, and plastics, in cosmetics, and historically in medicine as an adsorbent. On heating, kaolinite loses its structural water to form metakaolin and, at higher temperatures, mullite — transformations central to ceramic firing.
