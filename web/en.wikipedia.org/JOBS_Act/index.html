Jumpstart Our Business Startups Act

The Jumpstart Our Business Startups Act (JOBS Act) is a law enacted in the United States on April 5, 2012, intended to encourage funding of small businesses by easing various securities regulations.

Provisions

Title I created the category of "emerging growth company", allowing smaller companies simplified reporting and confidential filing during initial public offerings. Title II lifted the ban on general solicitation for certain private offerings to accredited investors. Title III, implemented by the SEC as Regulation Crowdfunding, permitted companies to raise limited amounts from non-accredited investors through registered funding portals.

Reception

Supporters credited the Act with reviving IPO activity and enabling equity crowdfunding, while critics warned of reduced investor protection and increased fraud risk. Subsequent legislation, including proposed "JOBS Act 3.0" packages, has sought to extend its provisions.
