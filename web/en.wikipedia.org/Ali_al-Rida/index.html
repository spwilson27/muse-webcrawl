Ali al-Rida

Ali ibn Musa al-Rida (Arabic: علي بن موسى الرضا; c. 1 January 766 – c. 6 June 819), also known as Imam Reza, was a descendant of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the eighth Imam of Twelver Shia Islam. Renowned for his piety and scholarship, he was designated heir apparent by the Abbasid caliph al-Ma'mun in a controversial attempt at reconciliation between the Abbasid court and the Alid family.

The Imam Reza Shrine in Mashhad, Iran, built over his tomb.

Life

Born in Medina, Ali al-Rida succeeded his father Musa al-Kazim as Imam in 799. In 816 the caliph al-Ma'mun summoned him to Merv in Khorasan and named him crown prince, even changing the Abbasid black banners to Alid green and minting coins in his name. The appointment provoked unrest in Baghdad, where a rival caliph was proclaimed.

Death and shrine

Ali al-Rida died suddenly in 818 or 819 at Tus, while travelling with al-Ma'mun toward Baghdad; Shia tradition holds that he was poisoned by the caliph. He was buried in the village of Sanabad, which grew into the city of Mashhad ("place of martyrdom"). The Imam Reza Shrine there is one of the largest mosque complexes in the world and a major pilgrimage destination, drawing millions of visitors annually.
