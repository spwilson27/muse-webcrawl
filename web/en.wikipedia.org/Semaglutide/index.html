Semaglutide


An Ozempic pen

Semaglutide is an antidiabetic medication used for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and an anti-obesity medication used for long-term weight management. It is a peptide similar to the hormone glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), modified with a side chain, and is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Developed by the Danish company Novo Nordisk, it is sold under the brand names Ozempic (injection) and Rybelsus (oral) for diabetes, and Wegovy for weight management.

Medical uses

Semaglutide is approved for improving blood sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes, reducing the risk of major cardiovascular events in people with diabetes and established heart disease, and for chronic weight management in adults and adolescents with obesity, or overweight with a weight-related condition. In clinical trials for obesity, weekly injections produced average weight loss around 15% of body weight over 68 weeks.

Mechanism of action

As a GLP-1 receptor agonist, semaglutide increases insulin secretion in response to meals, suppresses glucagon, slows gastric emptying, and acts on appetite centers in the brain to increase satiety. Its structure resists degradation by the enzyme DPP-4 and binds albumin, giving it a half-life of about one week, allowing once-weekly dosing.

Adverse effects

Common side effects are gastrointestinal, including nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and constipation. Less common risks include gastroparesis, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, and a possible association with thyroid C-cell tumors, which led to a boxed warning; it is contraindicated in people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 syndrome.

Society and culture

High demand for weight loss led to global shortages in the early 2020s, widespread off-label use, compounded and counterfeit products, and a major effect on Novo Nordisk's valuation, which at its peak made it one of Europe's most valuable companies.
