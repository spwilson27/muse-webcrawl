Downtown Eastside

East Hastings Street, the main artery of the Downtown Eastside

The Downtown Eastside (abbreviated DTES) is a neighbourhood in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. One of the city's oldest neighbourhoods, the DTES is the site of a complex set of social issues, including disproportionately high levels of drug use, homelessness, poverty, crime, mental illness and sex work. It is also known for its strong community resilience, history of social activism, and artistic contributions.

Geography and history

The neighbourhood is centred on East Hastings Street and includes parts of Chinatown, Japantown, Gastown's eastern edge and the industrial waterfront. It was the original centre of Vancouver: the city's first hotels, theatres, shops and city hall stood there, and the area housed successive waves of immigrants and resource workers. From the mid-20th century, as the retail and business core shifted west, the DTES declined economically; single-room occupancy (SRO) hotels became housing of last resort for low-income and transient residents.

Social issues

The neighbourhood has among the highest rates of HIV infection and overdose deaths in Canada, driven by injection drug use and, since the 2010s, a toxic drug supply dominated by fentanyl. British Columbia declared a public health emergency over overdose deaths in 2016. The area became notorious for the case of serial killer Robert Pickton, whose victims included many women from the DTES, and for the wider issue of missing and murdered Indigenous women, who are overrepresented among residents.

Responses and community

The DTES hosts pioneering harm-reduction services, including Insite, North America's first legal supervised injection site, opened in 2003, needle exchanges and overdose prevention sites. Community organisations such as the Portland Hotel Society, VANDU and the Downtown Eastside Residents Association campaign for social housing and against displacement by gentrification, which intensified ahead of the 2010 Winter Olympics and with new condominium development.
