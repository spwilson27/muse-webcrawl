The economy of Norway is a highly developed mixed economy with state ownership in strategic areas. Norway has the fourth-highest per-capita income in the world according to the World Bank and IMF, and one of the highest standards of living globally. Since the discovery of oil in the North Sea in the late 1960s, petroleum and natural gas have played a dominant role; Norway is one of the world's largest exporters of crude oil and natural gas outside the Middle East.

Petroleum and the sovereign wealth fund

The petroleum sector accounts for a large share of export revenues, GDP, and government income. To manage this wealth and avoid "Dutch disease", Norway established the Government Pension Fund Global in 1990, investing petroleum revenues abroad in equities, bonds, and real estate. The fund — managed by Norges Bank Investment Management — has grown to be the largest sovereign wealth fund in the world, valued at well over US$1 trillion, roughly $250,000 per Norwegian citizen. A fiscal rule limits annual government use of the fund to its expected real return, historically estimated at 3–4%.

Other sectors

Outside petroleum, Norway has major industries in hydropower (which supplies nearly all domestic electricity), shipping and maritime services, fisheries and aquaculture (Norway is the world's largest exporter of farmed salmon), aluminium and other metals, and chemicals. The public sector is large, with generous welfare provisions, high taxes, and low unemployment by international standards.
