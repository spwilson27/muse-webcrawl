Gambeson

A gambeson (also known as an aketon, padded jack or arming doublet) is a padded defensive jacket, worn as armour separately, combined with mail or plate armour, or underneath both. Gambesons were produced with a sewing technique called quilting, often with linen or wool stuffed with scrap cloth, tow, or horsehair, and were worn throughout medieval Europe.

History

Padded textile armour has ancient precedents, including the Greek linothorax. In the medieval period the gambeson served both as a complete armour for common soldiers — for whom it might be their only protection — and as the foundation garment of the knight's harness, cushioning blows and supporting the weight of mail and plate. Arming doublets of the later Middle Ages were fitted with laces and points to attach plate defences and mail.

Effectiveness

Multiple quilted layers of linen provide significant resistance to cutting and can blunt arrows and thrusts, especially when combined with a mail shirt. Surviving examples and written accounts show gambesons could be many layers thick and tailored like civilian doublets, sometimes richly decorated for wealthy wearers.
