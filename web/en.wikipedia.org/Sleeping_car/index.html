Sleeping car

A sleeping car or sleeper (often called a wagon-lit in Europe) is a railway passenger car that can accommodate all passengers in beds at night, to allow them to sleep. George Pullman created the American Pullman sleeping car, which gave rise to the Pullman Company, while in Europe Georges Nagelmackers founded the Compagnie Internationale des Wagons-Lits, operator of the Orient Express.

Types

Configurations range from open-section berths with curtains (as in classic Pullman cars, attended by Pullman porters who converted seats to beds), through semi-private roomettes and slumbercoaches, to fully private compartments with en-suite facilities. The couchette car, common in Europe, offers simpler shared compartments with fold-down bunks and no bedding service of hotel standard.

History and decline

Sleeping cars first appeared in the 1830s–1860s on American railroads. They reached their height in the early 20th century on named luxury trains. Air travel and motorways shrank night-train networks from the 1960s onward, but sleeper trains persist on long routes in Russia, China, India and elsewhere, and several European operators have revived night trains in recent years.
