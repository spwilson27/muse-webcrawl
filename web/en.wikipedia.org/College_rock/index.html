College rock

College rock is the alternative rock music that was played on student-run university and college campus radio stations located in the United States and Canada in the 1980s. The stations' playlists were often created by students who avoided the mainstream rock played on commercial radio stations. The term also came to describe the bands featured on those playlists, which drew on post-punk, new wave, jangle pop, and early indie rock.

Characteristics and artists

College rock bands combined the experimentation of post-punk and new wave with a more melodic pop style, and were typically signed to independent labels. Key acts include R.E.M. (whose 1983 album Murmur topped college charts), the Smiths, the Cure, Depeche Mode, the Replacements, Hüsker Dü, 10,000 Maniacs, the Violent Femmes, and Camper Van Beethoven. The trade publication CMJ New Music Report tracked college radio airplay and helped define the genre's canon.

Legacy

As many college rock acts crossed over commercially in the late 1980s and early 1990s — R.E.M. becoming arena headliners — the term "alternative rock" displaced "college rock" as the industry label. The college-radio ecosystem nonetheless shaped American indie music culture and foreshadowed the alternative boom of the 1990s.
