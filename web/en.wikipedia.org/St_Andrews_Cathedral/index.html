St Andrews Cathedral

The Cathedral of St Andrew is a ruined Roman Catholic cathedral in St Andrews, Fife, Scotland. Built in 1158, it was the largest church in Scotland and for centuries the centre of the medieval Scottish Church, housing the shrine of Saint Andrew, the nation's patron saint. The cathedral fell into ruin after the Scottish Reformation and is now in the care of Historic Environment Scotland.

History

Construction began in 1158 and the cathedral was consecrated in 1318 in the presence of King Robert the Bruce. At over 119 metres long, it dominated the town and drew pilgrims to the relics of St Andrew. It was damaged by fire, storms and war, and finally stripped of its treasures and altars during the Reformation in 1559–60, after which it was abandoned and used as a source of building stone.

Remains

The surviving fragments include the east and west gables of the nave, part of the south nave wall and the south transept, together with St Rule's Tower, which predates the cathedral and belonged to an earlier church on the site. The cathedral grounds contain a museum of early medieval carved stones and a large graveyard.
