Futon

A futon (Japanese: 布団) is the traditional style of Japanese bedding. A complete futon set consists of a mattress (shikibuton) and a duvet or thick blanket (kakebuton), both usually filled with cotton or synthetic fibre. The futon is laid directly on tatami mats or the floor for sleeping and is folded and stored away during the day, freeing the room for other uses.

Traditional use

Futons are relatively thin — typically 5–10 cm — and flexible enough to be aired, beaten, and folded. Regular airing in sunlight is an important part of futon care in Japan, and professional futon cleaning and re-carding services exist to renew the cotton filling.

Western futons

In the West, the term "futon" was adopted from the 1970s onward for a thicker mattress, often filled with cotton, foam, or innersprings, placed on a slatted wooden frame that converts between a sofa and a bed. Western futons differ substantially from the Japanese original in thickness, size, and use as dual-purpose furniture.

Materials

Traditional fillings are cotton batting; modern futons may use polyester, wool, latex, or memory foam layers, and some Japanese futons are made from buckwheat husks for the pillow (sobagara makura) that often accompanies the set.
