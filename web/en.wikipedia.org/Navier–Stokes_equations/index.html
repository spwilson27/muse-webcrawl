Navier–Stokes equations

The Navier–Stokes equations are partial differential equations describing the motion of viscous fluids. Named after Claude-Louis Navier and George Gabriel Stokes, they express conservation of momentum for a Newtonian fluid, together with the incompressibility (or mass conservation) condition.

Formρ(∂u/∂t + u·∇u) = −∇p + μ∇²u + f; ∇·u = 0
DerivedNavier (1822); Stokes (1845)
FieldsFluid mechanics, aerodynamics, meteorology, oceanography

Derivation and terms

The equations balance fluid inertia (including the nonlinear convective term u·∇u), pressure gradients, viscous diffusion proportional to dynamic viscosity μ, and external forces such as gravity. In dimensionless form the Reynolds number measures the relative importance of inertia and viscosity; high Reynolds numbers correspond to turbulence.

Solutions and the Millennium problem

Exact solutions exist only for simple geometries (e.g., Poiseuille and Couette flow); engineering relies on computational fluid dynamics. Whether smooth solutions always exist in three dimensions, or can develop singularities, is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems, carrying a $1 million prize — among the most famous open problems in mathematics.
