Carrozzeria Ghia S.p.A. was one of the most famous Italian automobile design and coachbuilding firms, founded in Turin in 1916 by Giacinto Ghia. Ghia produced elegant bodies for Alfa Romeo, Fiat, Lancia and many others, and its name later became the flagship trim designation — and eventually a design studio — of the Ford Motor Company.

Early years

Giacinto Ghia, who had worked as a test driver for Itala and Diatto, began building light sporting bodies in Turin after the First World War. The firm gained a reputation in the 1920s and 1930s for sleek coachwork on Alfa Romeo chassis, and Ghia cars won acclaim at concours d'élégance. After the Turin works were bombed in 1942 and Ghia's death in 1944, the company was rebuilt under new management.

Postwar golden age

In the 1950s Ghia, under stylists including Mario Boano and (briefly) a young Giorgetto Giugiaro, produced celebrated designs such as the Volkswagen Karmann Ghia (styled under Luigi Segre's direction), the Chrysler K-310 and other Chrysler "idea cars", the Fiat 2300 S Coupé and bodies for De Tomaso and Iso. In the United States, the Dual-Ghia cars combined Chrysler drivetrains with Ghia bodies, and the Karmann Ghia — produced from 1955 to 1974 — became one of the firm's most commercially successful designs, selling over 440,000 units.

Ford era

After passing through Alejandro de Tomaso's ownership, Ghia was acquired by Ford in 1970. It became Ford's Ghia Operations, the company's flagship design studio in Europe, producing numerous concept cars at Turin. The Ghia badge was applied to the highest trim level of European Fords — the Granada Ghia, Sierra Ghia, Mondeo Ghia and others — a role later taken by the "Titanium" designation, and the name survived into the 2010s on the luxury Vignale line's predecessors. The Turin studio was wound down around 2001, ending Ghia's existence as an operating design house.
