Apportionment (politics)

Apportionment is the process by which seats in a legislative body are distributed among administrative divisions, such as states or provinces, entitled to representation. Because populations change, apportionment is typically revised after each census, and the method used can significantly affect political outcomes.

United States

In the United States, the 435 seats of the House of Representatives are apportioned among the 50 states after each decennial census. Since 1941 the method of equal proportions (Huntington–Hill method) has been used. Reapportionment is followed by redistricting — the redrawing of congressional district boundaries within each state — which is politically contentious and can lead to gerrymandering.

Methods

Various divisor methods (such as D'Hondt, Sainte-Laguë and largest-remainder methods) are used around the world to allocate seats proportionally while keeping the total fixed. Mathematics shows that no method satisfies all desirable fairness criteria simultaneously — a result related to the Alabama paradox and Balinski and Young's impossibility theorem.
