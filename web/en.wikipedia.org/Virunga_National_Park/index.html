Virunga National Park is a national park in the Albertine Rift Valley in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. Created in 1925, it is Africa's oldest national park and among the most biologically diverse protected areas on the continent, spanning lowland rainforest, savanna, lava plains, swamps, and the glaciated Rwenzori Mountains, as well as the active volcanoes Nyiragongo and Nyamulagira. It is famed as a refuge of the endangered mountain gorilla.

Wildlife

The park protects roughly a third of the world's ~1,000 mountain gorillas, as well as eastern lowland gorillas, chimpanzees, okapis, forest elephants, lions, and one of Africa's largest hippo populations in Lake Edward. Its bird list exceeds 700 species.

Threats and conservation

Decades of regional conflict, armed groups, poaching, charcoal production, and oil-exploration pressure have made Virunga one of the world's most dangerous parks for rangers — more than 200 rangers have been killed in its protection. It has been on UNESCO's List of World Heritage in Danger since 1994. Conservation efforts, led for years by director Emmanuel de Merode (who survived an assassination attempt in 2014), combine ranger patrols, community development, and hydroelectric projects. The park was the subject of the 2014 documentary Virunga.
