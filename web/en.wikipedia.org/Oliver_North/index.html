Oliver Laurence North (born October 7, 1943) is a retired United States Marine Corps lieutenant colonel, political commentator, author, and former president of the National Rifle Association. He became a central figure in the Iran–Contra affair of the 1980s.

Military career

North graduated from the United States Naval Academy in 1968 and served in the Vietnam War as a platoon commander, earning the Silver Star, Bronze Star, and two Purple Hearts. He later served as an instructor and on the National Security Council staff under President Ronald Reagan, where he worked on counterterrorism and covert operations.

Iran–Contra affair

As an NSC staff member, North helped coordinate the secret sale of arms to Iran — intended in part to secure the release of American hostages in Lebanon — and the diversion of proceeds to the Contra rebels fighting Nicaragua's Sandinista government, in apparent violation of congressional restrictions. When the scheme became public in 1986, North was fired and testified before Congress in televised hearings in 1987, famously appearing in uniform. He was convicted in 1989 on three felony counts, but the convictions were vacated on appeal in 1991 on the grounds that witnesses may have been influenced by his immunized congressional testimony.

Later career

North ran unsuccessfully for the United States Senate from Virginia in 1994. He became a syndicated columnist, television and radio host, war correspondent for Fox News, and author of several novels and memoirs. In 2018 he was named president of the National Rifle Association, resigning in 2019 amid a dispute over the organization's finances and leadership.
