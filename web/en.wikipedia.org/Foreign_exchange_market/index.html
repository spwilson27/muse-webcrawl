Foreign exchange market

The foreign exchange market (forex, FX, or currency market) is a global decentralized market for the trading of currencies. This market determines foreign exchange rates for every currency. It includes all aspects of buying, selling and exchanging currencies at current or determined prices. In terms of trading volume, it is by far the largest market in the world, with average daily turnover measured in the trillions of US dollars.

Characteristics

The forex market operates through financial institutions and functions on several levels. Unlike stock markets, it has no central exchange; trading takes place over the counter between banks, dealers, brokers, and other market participants linked by electronic networks. The market is open 24 hours a day on weekdays, moving through the major financial centres of London, New York, Singapore, and Tokyo.

Participants

Participants include central banks, commercial and investment banks, currency speculation firms, corporations engaged in international trade, pension and hedge funds, and retail traders. Currencies are traded in pairs, such as the euro against the US dollar (EUR/USD), and the US dollar is involved in the great majority of transactions.

History

Modern foreign exchange trading developed after the collapse of the Bretton Woods system in the early 1970s, when major currencies moved to floating exchange rates. The Bank for International Settlements surveys the market every three years; its 2022 survey put global daily turnover at about .5 trillion.
