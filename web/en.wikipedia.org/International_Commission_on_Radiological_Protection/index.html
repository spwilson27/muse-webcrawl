International Commission on Radiological Protection

The International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) is an independent, international organisation that advances the science of radiological protection for the public good. It provides recommendations and guidance on all aspects of protection against ionising radiation. The ICRP is a registered charity in the United Kingdom and has its scientific secretariat in Ottawa, Canada.

The ICRP was founded in 1928, at the second International Congress of Radiology in Stockholm, as the International X-ray and Radium Protection Committee (IXRPC). It was restructured and given its present name in 1950. Its recommendations form the basis of radiological protection standards, legislation and guidance in most countries, and it works closely with its sister body, the International Commission on Radiation Units and Measurements (ICRU), founded in 1925.

System of radiological protection

The ICRP's system rests on three fundamental principles:

  • Justification — any decision that alters a radiation exposure situation should do more good than harm;
  • Optimisation — exposures should be kept as low as reasonably achievable (ALARA), taking economic and social factors into account;
  • Dose limitation — the total dose to any individual from regulated sources in planned exposure situations should not exceed the limits recommended by the Commission.

The ICRP distinguishes three types of exposure situation — planned, emergency and existing — and three categories of exposure: occupational, public and medical exposure of patients. Its 2007 Recommendations, published as ICRP Publication 103, are the most recent general statement of the system. They recommend an occupational effective dose limit of 20 millisieverts per year averaged over five years, and a public limit of 1 millisievert per year.

Structure

The ICRP consists of a Main Commission and five standing committees covering radiation effects, doses from radiation exposure, protection in medicine, application of the Commission's recommendations, and protection of the environment. Its work is carried out by committees and task groups made up of several hundred scientists serving in a voluntary capacity. The organisation's reports are published in the Annals of the ICRP.
