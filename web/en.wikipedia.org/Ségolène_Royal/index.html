Marie-Ségolène Royal (French: [se.ɡɔ.lɛn ʁwa.jal]; born 22 September 1953) is a French politician. A leading member of the Socialist Party, she was the party's candidate in the 2007 French presidential election, in which she lost in the second round to Nicolas Sarkozy — the first woman in France to reach the run-off as the candidate of a major party and to win the nomination of a major party for the presidency.

Career

Royal served as an adviser to President François Mitterrand before becoming Minister of the Environment (1992–1993) and later Minister Delegate for School Education and for Family and Childhood under Prime Minister Lionel Jospin. As president of the Poitou-Charentes regional council from 2004, she built a national profile with an emphasis on "participatory democracy".

After defeating Dominique Strauss-Kahn and Laurent Fabius for the 2006 Socialist nomination, she won 46.94% in the 2007 presidential run-off. She subsequently lost the 2008 party leadership contest to Martine Aubry by a narrow margin, failed to retain her parliamentary seat in 2012, and returned to government under her former partner President François Hollande as Minister of Ecology (2014–2017), in which role she presided over the 2015 Paris COP21 climate conference diplomacy. From 2017 to 2020 she served as France's Ambassador for the Arctic and Antarctic Poles.

Personal life

Born in Dakar into a military family, Royal was in a long-term relationship with François Hollande, with whom she has four children; the couple announced their separation on the night of her 2007 election defeat.
