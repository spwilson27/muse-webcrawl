IPCC Sixth Assessment Report

The Sixth Assessment Report (AR6) of the United Nations (UN) Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) is the sixth in a series of reports which assess scientific, technical, and socio-economic information concerning climate change. Three Working Group reports and a Synthesis Report were completed between 2021 and 2023, along with special reports on global warming of 1.5 °C, oceans and the cryosphere, and land, prepared during the same assessment cycle.

AR6 assessed that human activity has unequivocally warmed the climate.

Structure

Working Group I, The Physical Science Basis, concluded it is "unequivocal that human influence has warmed the atmosphere, ocean and land", finding global surface temperature had risen 1.07 °C between 1850–1900 and 2010–2019, and that every increment of warming intensifies hazards. Working Group II, Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability, assessed that climate change has caused widespread adverse impacts and losses, with about 3.3–3.6 billion people living in contexts highly vulnerable to climate change. Working Group III, Mitigation of Climate Change, found global emissions continued to rise, that limiting warming to 1.5 °C requires emissions to peak before 2025 at the latest and fall by roughly 43% by 2030, and that feasible, low-cost options exist across all sectors.

Scenarios and findings

AR6 used five illustrative Shared Socioeconomic Pathway (SSP) scenarios, from SSP1-1.9 (very low emissions, ~1.4 °C by 2100) to SSP5-8.5 (very high emissions, ~4.4 °C). The report stated that unless there are immediate, rapid and large-scale reductions in greenhouse gas emissions, limiting warming to 1.5 °C or even 2 °C will be beyond reach, that some impacts such as sea-level rise are irreversible for centuries to millennia, and that deep emissions cuts also bring large air-quality and health co-benefits. Extreme events such as those affecting ice shelves and ice caps were examined in detail.

Production and reception

More than 700 authors from over 90 countries contributed, drawing on tens of thousands of studies; governments approved the Summaries for Policymakers line by line. UN Secretary-General António Guterres described the reports as a "code red for humanity" and an "atlas of human suffering", and the Synthesis Report's March 2023 launch stressed that options to halve emissions by 2030 exist in every sector.
