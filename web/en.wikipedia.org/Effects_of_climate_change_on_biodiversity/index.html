Effects of climate change on biodiversity

The effects of climate change on biodiversity are increasingly severe as human-caused climate change accelerates species' range shifts, phenological changes, and extinction risks. Climate change is recognised as one of the major drivers of biodiversity loss, interacting with habitat destruction, overexploitation, pollution and invasive species.

Range and phenology shifts

Many species are shifting their ranges poleward and to higher elevations as temperatures rise, and the timing of seasonal events (phenology) — flowering, migration, breeding — is changing, sometimes desynchronising interactions between predators and prey or pollinators and plants. Marine species have generally shifted faster than terrestrial ones.

Extinction risk

The IPCC and IPBES assess that climate change substantially increases extinction risk, particularly for species with narrow ranges, specialised habitats, or limited dispersal — such as those on mountaintops, islands, coral reefs and in polar regions. Ocean warming and acidification drive coral bleaching and reef degradation. Under high-emissions scenarios, a substantial fraction of assessed species face elevated risk of extinction by 2100.

Responses

Conservation responses include expanding and connecting protected areas, assisted migration debates, ecosystem restoration, and mitigation of emissions to limit warming, alongside adaptation planning for ecosystems and the services they provide.
