Hindu priest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A pujari performing pūjā at a temple

A Hindu priest (Sanskrit: purohita, pujari or archaka) is a person who performs worship services, rituals and ceremonies on behalf of devotees in Hinduism. Priests conduct pūjā in temples and homes, officiate at life-cycle rites (saṃskāras) such as weddings and funerals, and recite mantras and scriptures. Traditionally most priests come from the Brahmin varṇa, though traditions vary by region and sect, and some temples and movements train priests from other communities.

Types of priests

A purohita is traditionally a family or court priest who advises patrons and officiates at their domestic rites. Temple priests, called pujari or, in South Indian Agamic temples, archaka or gurukkal, perform the daily cycle of services to the deity — waking, bathing, dressing, feeding and putting the image to rest. Vedic sacrifices are conducted by specialist ritualists such as the hotṛ, adhvaryu and udgātṛ.

Training and duties

Priestly training traditionally takes place in a gurukula or pāṭhaśālā, where students memorise Vedic recitation, Sanskrit, ritual manuals and the Āgamas of their sect. Daily duties centre on maintaining the purity of the shrine, performing ārati with lamps and offerings of flowers, food and incense, and distributing prasāda (blessed food) to worshippers. Priests also read temple calendars (pañcāṅga) to fix festival dates and auspicious times for rites.
