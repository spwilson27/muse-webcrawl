Australian Senate

Senate
Type: Upper house of the Parliament of Australia
Seats: 76
Meeting place: Parliament House, Canberra

The Australian Senate is the upper house of the bicameral Parliament of Australia, the lower house being the House of Representatives. The powers of the Senate, and its composition under which each state is represented equally regardless of population, derive from the Constitution of Australia.

Composition

The Senate consists of 76 senators: 12 for each of the six states, elected for six-year terms, and two for each of the mainland self-governing territories, elected for the term of the House of Representatives. Senators are chosen by a system of proportional representation using the single transferable vote.

Powers

The Senate has nearly equal legislative power to the House, except that it may not originate or amend money bills, though it may request amendments and can reject such bills. Half-Senate elections normally coincide with House elections, and a double dissolution of both houses may be called to resolve deadlocks between them, as occurred in 1975 and 2016.
