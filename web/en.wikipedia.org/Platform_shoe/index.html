Platform shoe

A platform shoe is a shoe with a noticeably thick sole, typically between 3 and 10 centimetres, raising the whole foot rather than only the heel. Platforms have appeared in many cultures and have repeatedly returned to fashion.

History

Precursors include the chopines worn in Renaissance Venice, Japanese geta, and the raised pattens used to keep feet above muddy streets. Platforms surged in the 1930s with designs by Salvatore Ferragamo, again in the glam rock and disco era of the 1970s, and in the 1990s with pop acts such as the Spice Girls; designers such as Vivienne Westwood made them a runway staple.

Design

The thick sole may be made of cork, wood or synthetic materials and is combined with sandals, boots, trainers and formal shoes alike.
