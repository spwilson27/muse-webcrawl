Mark Oliver Saville, Baron Saville of Newdigate (born 20 March 1936) is a British judge and former Justice of the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom. He is best known for chairing the Bloody Sunday Inquiry, established in 1998 to re-examine the events of 30 January 1972 in Derry, Northern Ireland.

Career

Saville was educated at Rye Grammar School and Brasenose College, Oxford, where he read law. He was called to the Bar by the Middle Temple in 1962 and took silk in 1975. He became a High Court judge in 1985, sitting in the Queen's Bench Division, and was appointed a Lord Justice of Appeal in 1994. In 1997 he became a Lord of Appeal in Ordinary.

Bloody Sunday Inquiry

In January 1998 Prime Minister Tony Blair announced a new inquiry into Bloody Sunday, with Saville as its chairman. The inquiry sat for many years, hearing evidence from hundreds of witnesses, and its report was published in June 2010. The report concluded that the killings were unjustified and unjustifiable, prompting a formal apology from Prime Minister David Cameron in the House of Commons.

Supreme Court

On 1 October 2009 Saville became one of the first Justices of the newly created Supreme Court of the United Kingdom. He retired from the court in 2010.
