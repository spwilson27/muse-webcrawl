Perverse incentive

A perverse incentive is an incentive that has an unintended and undesirable result that is contrary to the intentions of its designers. The classic example is the "cobra effect", named after an apocryphal story from British India in which a bounty on dead cobras led people to breed cobras for the reward, ultimately increasing the cobra population.

Examples

Historical examples include the Hanoi rat massacre of 1902, where a bounty on rat tails led to the release of tail-less rats; Soviet factories producing useless output when quotas were set by weight or quantity; and healthcare or sales targets that encourage gaming rather than genuine performance improvements.

Related concepts

Perverse incentives are related to Goodhart's law ("when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"), moral hazard, and the broader study of unintended consequences in economics and policy design.
