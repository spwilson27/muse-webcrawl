Fiordland National Park

Fiordland National Park
Location: Southland, New Zealand
Area: 12,607 km²
Established: 1952
Part of: Te Wahipounamu World Heritage Site
Milford Sound, the best-known fiord in the park.

Fiordland National Park is a national park in the south-west corner of the South Island of New Zealand. It is the largest of the 13 national parks in New Zealand, with an area of 12,607 square kilometres, and a major part of the Te Wahipounamu site inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. The park is administered by the Department of Conservation.

Geography

The park's landscape was carved by glaciers during the ice ages, leaving a coastline deeply indented by fourteen fiords, of which Milford Sound and Doubtful Sound are the most famous. Steep granite peaks rise directly from the sea, and numerous waterfalls — including Sutherland Falls, among the tallest in New Zealand — plunge from hanging valleys. The region is one of the wettest in the world, with annual rainfall of up to 7 metres in places, sustaining dense temperate rainforest of southern beech and podocarps.

Wildlife

Fiordland is a refuge for rare native birds, including the flightless takahē, rediscovered in the Murchison Mountains in 1948, the kākāpō, and the mohua. Introduced predators such as stoats, rats, and possums are the subject of intensive conservation control programmes, and several offshore islands within the park have been cleared of pests as sanctuaries. Bottlenose dolphins, fur seals, and Fiordland crested penguins inhabit the fiords.

Recreation

The park contains three of New Zealand's Great Walks: the Milford Track, the Kepler Track, and the Routeburn Track. Milford Sound, reachable by road through the Homer Tunnel, receives hundreds of thousands of visitors a year, while the more remote Doubtful Sound is reached by boat and bus from Manapouri.
