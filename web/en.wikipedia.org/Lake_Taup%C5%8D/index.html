Lake Taupō (Māori: Taupō-nui-a-Tia) is a large crater lake in New Zealand's North Island, centred on the caldera of the Taupō Volcano. With a surface area of 616 km² it is the largest lake by surface area in New Zealand, and the largest freshwater lake in Australasia.

Geology

The lake occupies the caldera created by the Oruanui eruption about 25,500 years ago, the largest volcanic eruption in the last 70,000 years, and was enlarged by the Hatepe eruption of about 232 CE, one of the most violent eruptions of the last 5,000 years, which emptied the lake and devastated the surrounding region. The volcano remains active and is classified as a supervolcano.

Recreation and ecology

The town of Taupō sits on the northeastern shore. The lake is famed for rainbow and brown trout fishing, introduced in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and hosts the Lake Taupo Cycle Challenge and Ironman New Zealand. Its only outflow is the Waikato River, New Zealand's longest river, leaving at the northern end past the Huka Falls.
