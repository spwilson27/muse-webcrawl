Barotropic fluid

In fluid dynamics, a barotropic fluid is a fluid whose density is a function of pressure only: ρ = ρ(p). A barotropic flow is a generalization of this concept in which the pressure is also a function of density only. In such flows, surfaces of constant pressure coincide with surfaces of constant density, and the vorticity equation has no baroclinic torque term.

Applications

Barotropic models are widely used in meteorology and oceanography as simplifications of atmospheric and oceanic circulation. A barotropic atmosphere model assumes temperature is uniform on pressure surfaces; it captures many features of mid-latitude flow with a single vertical layer. In astrophysics, the barotropic equation of state is used in models of stellar interiors, and incompressible flow (constant density) is the simplest barotropic case.

Contrast with baroclinic fluids

In a baroclinic fluid, density depends on both pressure and temperature, so density and pressure surfaces intersect, generating vorticity through the baroclinic term. Weather systems such as extratropical cyclones are fundamentally baroclinic phenomena.
