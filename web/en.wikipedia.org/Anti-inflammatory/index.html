Anti-inflammatory

NSAIDs are the most widely used anti-inflammatory drugs

Anti-inflammatory refers to the property of a substance or treatment that reduces inflammation — the body's response to injury or infection characterised by redness, swelling, heat and pain. The principal classes of anti-inflammatory agents are the nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and the corticosteroids.

NSAIDs

NSAIDs — including ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac and aspirin — act by inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes, reducing the synthesis of prostaglandins that mediate inflammation, pain and fever. They are used for arthritis, musculoskeletal injury, headache and many other conditions. Side effects include gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding, kidney impairment, and increased cardiovascular risk; selective COX-2 inhibitors (coxibs) were developed to reduce gastrointestinal toxicity.

Corticosteroids

Glucocorticoids such as prednisone and hydrocortisone are powerful anti-inflammatories and immunosuppressants used in autoimmune diseases, allergies, asthma and organ transplantation, but long-term use causes serious adverse effects including osteoporosis, diabetes and adrenal suppression.

Other agents and diet

Biologic therapies (e.g. anti-TNF antibodies) target specific inflammatory cytokines in diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis. Ice, compression and rest are physical anti-inflammatory measures, and some dietary components — omega-3 fatty acids, curcumin and polyphenols — show modest anti-inflammatory effects.
