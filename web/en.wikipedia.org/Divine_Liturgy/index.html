Divine Liturgy

Divine Liturgy
TraditionByzantine Rite: Eastern Orthodox, Byzantine Catholic
Principal formsLiturgy of St John Chrysostom; Liturgy of St Basil the Great; Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts
CentreCelebration of the Eucharist
Liturgical languageOriginally Greek; also Church Slavonic and many vernaculars

The Divine Liturgy (Greek: Θεία Λειτουργία, Theia Leitourgia) is the primary worship service of the Byzantine Rite, celebrated by the Eastern Orthodox Church and the Eastern Catholic Churches that follow that rite. It is the service at which the Eucharist is consecrated and distributed, normally on Sundays and feast days. The term is also applied, more broadly, to the eucharistic services of the Oriental Orthodox churches, though these belong to other liturgical families.

Principal forms

The Liturgy of St John Chrysostom is celebrated on most days of the year and is the best known form. The Liturgy of St Basil the Great, with a longer and more theologically elaborate anaphora, is celebrated on ten days, including the Sundays of Great Lent and the feasts of Christmas and Theophany. During Lent on weekdays the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is served, in which the faithful commune from the consecrated Lamb reserved from the previous Sunday; it has no consecration of its own.

Structure

The service follows a classical pattern. After the preparatory rites (prothesis, at which the bread and wine are prepared), the Liturgy of the Word ("of the Catechumens") comprises antiphons, the Little Entrance, hymns, readings from the Epistle and Gospel, and litanies. The Liturgy of the Faithful follows with the Great Entrance, in which the gifts are carried to the altar, the Creed, the anaphora — the great eucharistic prayer invoking the Holy Spirit upon the gifts (the epiclesis) — the Lord's Prayer, communion, and dismissal. Singing by choir and congregation pervades the whole service.

Theology

Orthodox theology understands the Liturgy as a participation in the worship of heaven, "on earth as it is in heaven": the congregation joins the angels around God's throne. The consecrated bread and wine are confessed to be the true Body and Blood of Christ, the change effected by the Holy Spirit invoked in the epiclesis. Icons, incense, and chant all serve this vision of the kingdom made present.
