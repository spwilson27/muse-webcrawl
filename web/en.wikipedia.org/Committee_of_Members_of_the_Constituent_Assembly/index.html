Committee of Members of the Constituent Assembly

The Committee of Members of the Constituent Assembly (Russian: Комитет членов Учредительного собрания, Komuch) was an anti-Bolshevik government formed in Samara, Russia, on 8 June 1918 during the Russian Civil War. Composed mainly of Socialist Revolutionaries (SRs) who had been elected to the All-Russian Constituent Assembly, it claimed to be the legitimate government of Russia after the Bolsheviks dispersed the Assembly in January 1918.

History

After the Czechoslovak Legion captured Samara, SR deputies established the Committee under the chairmanship of Vladimir Volsky. Komuch declared the restoration of democratic freedoms, an eight-hour working day, and its intention to reconvene the Constituent Assembly. It formed its own armed force, the People's Army, commanded by Vladimir Kappel, which achieved early successes against the Red Army, taking Kazan in August 1918 and capturing the Imperial gold reserve held there.

Komuch was weakened by conflict with more conservative officers and by dependence on the Czechoslovak Legion. Following the Ufa State Conference in September 1918, it transferred its authority to the Provisional All-Russian Government (the Directory). After Admiral Alexander Kolchak's coup in November 1918, members of the Committee were arrested; some were executed.
