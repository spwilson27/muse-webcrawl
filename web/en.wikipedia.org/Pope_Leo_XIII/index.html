Pope Leo XIII (born Vincenzo Gioacchino Raffaele Luigi Pecci; 2 March 1810 – 20 July 1903) was head of the Catholic Church from 20 February 1878 until his death in July 1903. His 25-year pontificate was the fourth longest in history, and he was the oldest pope at death.

Pontificate

Leo XIII is best known for his 1891 encyclical Rerum novarum, which addressed the conditions of the working classes, defended private property and workers' rights to organize, and founded modern Catholic social teaching. His encyclical Aeterni Patris (1879) revived Thomism as the Church's preferred philosophy. He promoted the rosary in eleven encyclicals, earning the title "the Rosary Pope", encouraged Catholic engagement with modern scholarship, and opened the Vatican Archives to researchers.

Legacy

Leo sought to position the papacy as an intellectual and diplomatic force after the loss of the Papal States. At his death in 1903, aged 93, he was succeeded by Pius X.
