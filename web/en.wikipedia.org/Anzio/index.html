Anzio is a town and comune on the coast of the Lazio region of Italy, about 51 kilometres south of Rome. It is a seaport and resort town, known in antiquity as Antium, and famous in modern times as the site of the Allied landings of 1944 during the Second World War.

History

Ancient Antium was a Volscian and later Latin town, captured by Rome in 338 BC. It was a favoured seaside resort of wealthy Romans and the birthplace of the emperors Caligula and Nero; ruins of Roman villas survive along the coast. In the Middle Ages the town declined, and nearby Nettuno became the main settlement.

Battle of Anzio

On 22 January 1944, Allied forces under Operation Shingle landed at Anzio and Nettuno, intending to outflank the German Gustav Line. The beachhead was contained by German counterattacks for months before the Allies broke out in May 1944, contributing to the fall of Rome on 4 June 1944. The Anzio War Cemetery and Beach Head War Cemetery commemorate the fallen.

Modern town

Modern Anzio grew around its port, developed under Pope Innocent XII in the late 17th century. Today it is known for fishing, ferry services to the Pontine Islands, beaches, and seafood.
