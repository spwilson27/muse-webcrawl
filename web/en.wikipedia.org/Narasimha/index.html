Narasimha

Narasimha (Sanskrit: नरसिंह, "man-lion") is the fourth avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu, who incarnated in the form of a part-lion, part-man being to destroy the demon king Hiranyakashipu and restore dharma. He is worshipped across Vaishnava traditions, and his iconography — typically bursting from a pillar disembowelling the demon on his lap — is among the most dramatic in Hindu art.

The legend

Hiranyakashipu had obtained a boon from Brahma that he could not be killed by man or beast, indoors or outdoors, by day or night, on earth or in the sky, or by any weapon. His young son Prahlada was a devoted worshipper of Vishnu, which enraged the king. When Hiranyakashipu demanded to know whether Vishnu was in a palace pillar and struck it, Narasimha emerged from within — at twilight (neither day nor night), on the threshold (neither in nor out), placed the demon on his lap (neither earth nor sky) and slew him with his claws (no weapon), being neither man nor beast. Lakshmi, in some tellings, then pacified his furious form.

Worship

Major Narasimha temples include Ahobilam and Simhachalam in Andhra Pradesh and Hampi in Karnataka. He is invoked as a fierce protector against evil, and Narasimha mantras are recited for courage and defence. The festival Narasimha Jayanti marks his appearance.
