Gurmukhi

The Gurmukhi alphabet, used to write Punjabi in India.

Gurmukhi (Punjabi: ਗੁਰਮੁਖੀ, "from the mouth of the Guru") is an abugida script developed from the Laṇḍā and Śāradā scripts of the Brahmic family. It is the standard script for writing the Punjabi language in India and is the script of the Sikh holy scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib.

History

Tradition attributes the standardization of Gurmukhi to Guru Angad, the second Sikh Guru, in the 16th century, who promoted it for recording the hymns of Guru Nanak. The name is generally interpreted as "from the mouth of the Guru," though it may also relate to the script's association with the Gurmukhs, the followers of the Guru.

Characteristics

Gurmukhi has 35 primary letters (pentī akkhar, "thirty-five letters"), with additional letters formed using diacritics such as the bindī (dot). Like other Brahmic scripts, it is an abugida: consonant letters carry an inherent vowel that is modified or suppressed by vowel signs (matras). Characters hang from a horizontal headline, and words are not joined together in cursive fashion. Punjabi is tonal, and Gurmukhi uses certain consonant letters (notably the voiced aspirates) to mark tone.

Usage

In India, Gurmukhi is the official script of Punjab and is used for government, education, and media in Punjabi. In Pakistan, Punjabi is usually written in the Shahmukhi script, a form of the Perso-Arabic alphabet. Gurmukhi is also used liturgically by Sikhs worldwide, and in India it occasionally writes other languages such as Hindi, Sindhi, and Braj Bhasha in religious contexts.
