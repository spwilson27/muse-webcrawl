Irish Defence Forces

Irish: Óglaigh na hÉireann

Founded: 1 October 1924

Branches: Army, Naval Service, Air Corps

Headquarters: McKee Barracks, Dublin

Commander-in-Chief: President of Ireland

The Defence Forces (Irish: Óglaigh na hÉireann) are the armed forces of Ireland. They encompass the Army, Air Corps, Naval Service and Reserve Defence Forces. The Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces is the President of Ireland, while day-to-day military command runs through the Chief of Staff under the Minister for Defence.

History

The Defence Forces trace their origins to the Irish Volunteers and the Irish Citizen Army of the revolutionary period, and were formally established in 1924 following Irish independence. Ireland remained neutral during the Second World War (officially "The Emergency"). Since 1958, Ireland has maintained a continuous presence in United Nations peacekeeping operations, beginning with the Congo, and Irish troops have served in Lebanon (UNIFIL), Chad, Liberia, Syria and elsewhere — one of the longest unbroken peacekeeping records of any country.

Organisation and roles

The Army is organised into two brigades; the Naval Service, based at Haulbowline in Cork Harbour, patrols Ireland's territorial waters and large exclusive economic zone; and the Air Corps, based at Casement Aerodrome, Baldonnel, provides air support, maritime patrol and air ambulance services. Primary roles include defending the state against armed aggression, aiding the civil power (including support to An Garda Síochána), fishery protection, and participation in international peace support operations. Ireland is not a member of NATO and maintains a policy of military neutrality, though it participates in the EU's Common Security and Defence Policy and NATO's Partnership for Peace.
