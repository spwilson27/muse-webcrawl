World's Columbian Exposition

The World's Columbian Exposition, also known as the Chicago World's Fair, was a world's fair held in Chicago, Illinois, from 1 May to 30 October 1893, celebrating the 400th anniversary of Christopher Columbus's arrival in the New World in 1492. It attracted more than 27 million visitors.

The White City

Most of the fair's buildings were designed in the Beaux-Arts style by architects including Daniel Burnham and Frederick Law Olmsted, and were covered in white stucco, giving the fairgrounds the name the White City. The Court of Honor centred on a large reflecting basin. The fair strongly influenced American architecture, city planning and the City Beautiful movement.

Attractions

The fair introduced the first Ferris wheel, built by George Washington Gale Ferris Jr., the Midway Plaisance with its ethnographic displays and entertainments, early motion-picture and electric-light displays, and products such as Pabst Blue Ribbon beer and Juicy Fruit gum. The Manufactures and Liberal Arts Building was then the largest building in the world.

Legacy

Nearly all the fair's structures were temporary; the Palace of Fine Arts, built more durably, survives as the Museum of Science and Industry. The fair is also remembered in Erik Larson's book The Devil in the White City, which interweaves it with the crimes of H. H. Holmes.
