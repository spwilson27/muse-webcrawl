Ossicles

Part ofMiddle ear
BonesMalleus, incus, stapes
SystemAuditory system

The ossicles (also called the auditory ossicles) are three small bones in the middle ear: the malleus (hammer), incus (anvil), and stapes (stirrup). They are among the smallest bones in the human body and together form a short chain that connects the eardrum (tympanic membrane) to the oval window of the inner ear.

Function

Sound vibrations striking the eardrum are transmitted through the ossicles to the cochlea. Because the eardrum is much larger than the oval window, and because the ossicles act as a lever, the chain amplifies the pressure of the vibrations, overcoming the impedance mismatch between air and the fluid of the inner ear.

Development

The malleus and incus develop from the first pharyngeal arch, while the stapes develops largely from the second arch. The ossicles are essentially fully formed at birth and change little in size thereafter.

Clinical significance

Disorders of the ossicles include otosclerosis, in which the stapes becomes fixed, and ossicular discontinuity following infection or trauma, both of which cause conductive hearing loss. Surgical repair is known as ossiculoplasty.
