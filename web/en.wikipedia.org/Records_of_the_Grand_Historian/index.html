The Records of the Grand Historian (史記, Shǐjì) is a monumental history of China completed around 91 BC by the Han dynasty historian Sima Qian, building on work begun by his father Sima Tan. It covers roughly 2,500 years, from the age of the legendary Yellow Emperor to the reign of Emperor Wu of Han, and is the foundational text of Chinese historiography.

The Shiji established the standard form for Chinese dynastic histories.

Structure

The work comprises 130 chapters (some 526,500 characters) arranged in five sections: 12 Basic Annals (本紀) recording the deeds of rulers; 10 Tables (表) of chronology and genealogy; 8 Treatises (書) on subjects such as rites, music, astronomy, the calendar, and economics; 30 Hereditary Houses (世家) on aristocratic families and states; and 70 Ranked Biographies (列傳) of remarkable individuals, from assassins and merchants to generals and scholars. This "annals–biography" (jizhuanti) format became the template for all subsequent official dynastic histories, the Twenty-Four Histories.

Sima Qian

Sima Qian (c. 145 – c. 86 BC) inherited the post of Grand Historian (Taishiling) from his father. After he defended the surrendered general Li Ling, Emperor Wu had him castrated in 98 BC. Sima chose to endure the humiliation rather than commit suicide so that he could finish the history, a decision he explained in his famous Letter to Ren An.

Significance

The Shiji is admired not only as history but as literature: its vivid character portraits and dramatic narrative shaped Chinese prose for two millennia. Its treatment of figures from all levels of society, and Sima's willingness to include critical judgements of rulers, set a standard for historical writing in East Asia.
