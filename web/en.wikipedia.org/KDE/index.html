KDE is an international free software community that develops free and open-source software, best known for the KDE Plasma desktop environment, a large suite of applications, and the libraries and frameworks on which they are built. Its products run mainly on Linux and other Unix-like systems, with many applications also available on Windows and macOS.

History

KDE was founded in 1996 by Matthias Ettrich, a student at the University of Tübingen, who wanted a consistent, easy-to-use desktop for Unix. The name originally stood for "Kool Desktop Environment" and later simply "K Desktop Environment"; since a 2009 rebranding, "KDE" refers to the community itself rather than any single product. The first stable desktop release, KDE 1.0, appeared in 1998. Because KDE was built on the then-proprietary Qt toolkit, the project prompted the creation of the rival GNOME desktop; Qt was later released under free licences, resolving the controversy. Successive generations — KDE 2, KDE 3, the KDE Software Compilation 4, and from 2014 the separately versioned Plasma 5, Frameworks, and Applications — led to Plasma 6 in 2024.

Software and organisation

Well-known KDE software includes the Plasma desktop, the Dolphin file manager, the Konsole terminal, the KWrite and Kate text editors, the Okular document viewer, the Krita painting program, and the Kdenlive video editor. The community's non-profit legal and financial arm is KDE e.V., based in Germany, and its mascot is the small green dragon Konqi. KDE software is used by default in distributions such as Kubuntu and is the desktop of devices including Valve's Steam Deck (in desktop mode).
