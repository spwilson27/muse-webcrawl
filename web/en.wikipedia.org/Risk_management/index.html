Risk management
Process stepsIdentify, assess, treat, monitor, communicate
Treatment optionsAvoid, reduce, transfer/share, retain/accept
Key standardsISO 31000; COSO ERM Framework

Risk management is the identification, evaluation and prioritisation of risks — the effect of uncertainty on objectives — followed by the coordinated application of resources to minimise, monitor and control the probability or impact of unfortunate events, or to maximise the realisation of opportunities. It is practised in finance, engineering, healthcare, project management, information security and public policy.

Process

Standard frameworks such as ISO 31000 describe a repeating cycle: establish the context and risk criteria; identify risks; analyse their likelihood and consequences (often with a risk matrix or quantitative models such as Monte Carlo simulation); evaluate them against the organisation's risk appetite; treat them; and monitor and communicate throughout. A risk register documents identified risks, owners and planned responses.

Treatment strategies

Common strategies are avoidance (not undertaking the activity), reduction (controls that lower likelihood or impact), transfer or sharing (insurance, hedging, outsourcing — see reinsurance in the insurance context), and retention (accepting the risk within appetite, sometimes with reserves). In financial risk management, measures such as value at risk (VaR), stress testing and diversification are used to quantify and control market, credit and liquidity risks.

Enterprise risk management

Enterprise risk management (ERM) integrates risk considerations across an entire organisation rather than in departmental silos — an approach promoted by the COSO framework and made near-mandatory for banks and insurers by regimes such as Basel III and Solvency II. Critics note that formal risk management can create an illusion of control, underweighting rare "tail" events, a weakness exposed by the 2008 financial crisis.
