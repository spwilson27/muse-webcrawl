Bhangra (dance)

Bhangra is a lively form of folk dance and music that originated in the Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent, traditionally performed by farmers during the harvest festival of Vaisakhi. Dancers move energetically to the beat of the dhol drum, accompanied by singing of boliyan (short verses), the algoza flute, chimta, and other folk instruments.

History and development

Rooted in the harvest celebrations of the Majha area of Punjab, bhangra was codified as a staged folk dance in the mid-20th century. With the Punjabi diaspora, it travelled to the United Kingdom, Canada, and the United States, where in the 1980s British Asian musicians fused it with Western pop, reggae, and later hip hop to create modern bhangra music — a commercial genre distinct from the traditional dance. Artists such as Malkit Singh and later Panjabi MC (whose "Mundian To Bach Ke" became a global hit in 2002) brought the sound worldwide.

The dance today

Performers wear a turban (pagh), a long shirt (kurta) with a waistcoat, and a loose lungi or trousers, often swinging colourful handkerchiefs. Collegiate bhangra competitions, especially in North America and the UK, feature acrobatic, highly synchronised routines by university teams. Bhangra has also been incorporated into fitness classes and Bollywood film choreography.
