Texas sharpshooter fallacy
TypeInformal fallacy; cognitive bias
Related conceptsMultiple comparisons problem, data dredging, clustering illusion

The Texas sharpshooter fallacy is an informal fallacy in which differences in data are ignored while similarities are emphasized, leading to false conclusions. The name comes from a joke about a Texan who fires a gun at the side of a barn, then paints a target around the tightest cluster of bullet holes to make it appear he is an excellent shot.

Explanation

The fallacy arises when a hypothesis is formed after examining data — cherry-picking a pattern from random noise — and the same data is then treated as if it confirmed the hypothesis. In statistics it is closely related to the multiple comparisons problem: with enough comparisons, striking-looking "clusters" or correlations will occur by chance, as when apparent cancer clusters near power lines turn out to be consistent with random variation.

Examples

The fallacy commonly appears in claims based on coincidences, prophecies "fulfilled" by selecting matching events afterward, and studies that test many hypotheses but report only significant results. Scientists guard against it by pre-registering hypotheses, correcting significance levels for multiple comparisons, and testing post-hoc patterns on fresh data.
