Perpetuity

A perpetuity is an annuity that has no end, or a stream of cash payments that continues forever. In finance, a perpetuity is a security that pays a fixed coupon indefinitely with no redemption date. Although a true perpetuity never ends, its present value is finite because payments far in the future contribute vanishingly little after discounting.

Valuation

The present value of a level perpetuity paying an amount C each period, discounted at rate r per period, is PV = C / r. For a growing perpetuity whose payments grow at a constant rate g, the value is PV = C1 / (r − g), provided r > g — the Gordon growth model used to value dividend-paying stocks.

Examples

The classic historical example is the British government consol ("consolidated annuities"), perpetual bonds first issued in the 18th century and notable during the Napoleonic Wars and both World Wars; the last undated British gilts were redeemed in 2015. Perpetuities also arise in real-estate valuation, scholarship and prize endowments, and in the valuation formula for perpetual preferred stock. A "perpetuity due" pays at the beginning of each period, while an ordinary perpetuity pays at the end.
