Double star

A double star observed through a telescope.

In observational astronomy, a double star or visual double is a pair of stars that appear close to each other when viewed from Earth, particularly with the aid of optical telescopes. A double star may be a genuine binary star system in which the two stars are gravitationally bound and orbit a common centre of mass, or it may be an optical double, a chance alignment of stars at very different distances that merely appear near one another.

Observation

Double stars are characterised by their angular separation, usually measured in arcseconds, and their position angle. Famous examples include Albireo in Cygnus, prized for its colour contrast between an amber primary and a blue-green companion, and the naked-eye pair Mizar and Alcor in Ursa Major. Long-term observation of visual doubles allows astronomers to determine orbital elements and hence stellar masses, one of the few direct ways of measuring mass.

Classification

Visual binaries are distinguished from spectroscopic binaries, in which the components are too close to resolve and are detected through periodic Doppler shifts in their spectra, and from eclipsing binaries. Catalogues such as the Washington Double Star Catalog record hundreds of thousands of pairs.
