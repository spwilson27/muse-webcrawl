Bilge Qaghan (Old Turkic: 𐰋𐰃𐰠𐰏𐰀 𐰴𐰍𐰣, Bilgä Qaghan; 683/684 – 734) was the khagan of the Second Turkic Khaganate. His reign, together with the counsel of his brother Kül Tigin and the statesman Tonyukuk, marked the final flourishing of the Göktürk empire in Mongolia.

Background

Bilge was the son of Ilterish Qaghan, founder of the restored khaganate. After the death of his uncle Qapaghan Qaghan in 716, Bilge seized power with the help of his brother Kül Tigin, executing the previous regime's heirs.

Reign

Bilge consolidated the empire, campaigned against rebellious tribes such as the Karluks and the Oghuz, and negotiated relations with Tang China, securing a marriage alliance and border trade. Advised by Tonyukuk, he resisted pressure to settle the Turks in walled towns or adopt foreign religions wholesale, a policy he recorded on his memorial stele.

The Orkhon inscriptions

Following the deaths of Kül Tigin (731) and Bilge himself (734), monumental stelae were erected in the Orkhon Valley bearing long texts in Old Turkic runic script. The Bilge Khagan inscription recounts his reign and warns future generations of Turks. Deciphered in 1893 by Vilhelm Thomsen, the inscriptions are the oldest extensive texts in any Turkic language.

Death

Bilge was poisoned by a courtier in 734; he survived long enough to execute the poisoner and his family before dying. He was succeeded briefly by his son Yollıg Khagan.
