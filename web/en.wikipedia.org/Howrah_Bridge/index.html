Howrah Bridge

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Howrah Bridge, officially renamed Rabindra Setu in 1965 after the Bengali poet Rabindranath Tagore, is a balanced cantilever bridge over the Hooghly River in West Bengal, India, linking the cities of Kolkata and Howrah. Commissioned in 1943, it is one of the busiest cantilever bridges in the world and an iconic symbol of Kolkata.

Official name: Rabindra Setu
Opened: 3 February 1943
Type: Cantilever truss bridge
Total length: 705 m (2,313 ft)
Main span: 457.2 m (1,500 ft)
Carries: Vehicles and pedestrians

Construction

The bridge replaced a pontoon bridge of 1874. Designed by Rendel, Palmer and Tritton and built by the Cleveland Bridge & Engineering Company with steel supplied largely by Tata Steel, it consumed some 26,500 tons of steel. Remarkably, the structure was erected without nuts and bolts, using rivets throughout. It was opened during the Second World War without a formal ceremony, for fear of attracting Japanese air attack.

Traffic

The bridge carries a daily traffic of roughly 100,000 vehicles and 150,000 pedestrians, making it among the busiest bridges of its type anywhere. Its eight lanes and wide footpaths connect Howrah railway station — one of India’s busiest — with central Kolkata.
