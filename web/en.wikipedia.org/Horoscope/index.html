A horoscope is an astrological chart representing the positions of the Sun, Moon and planets, the zodiac signs and angular relationships (aspects) at a chosen moment, most commonly the moment of a person's birth. In popular usage, "horoscope" also refers to an astrologer's interpretation or a newspaper forecast based on the chart. The word derives from the Greek hōroskopos, "time observer".

History

Horoscopic astrology developed in Hellenistic Egypt from Babylonian and Greek traditions by the late 2nd or early 1st century BCE. It spread through the Roman world, India and the medieval Islamic world, and became a standard feature of European learned culture until the scientific revolution.

Construction

A chart divides the ecliptic into twelve signs and twelve houses, determined by the ascendant — the sign rising on the eastern horizon. Planetary placements and aspects are then interpreted according to the tradition followed. Newspaper "sun-sign" columns use only the Sun's sign and are a 20th-century simplification popularised in the 1930s.

Criticism

Scientists regard astrology as a pseudoscience; controlled tests, such as Shawn Carlson's 1985 double-blind study published in Nature, found astrologers' chart interpretations performed no better than chance.
