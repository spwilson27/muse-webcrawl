Sakhalin (Russian: Сахали́н) is an island in Northeast Asia, part of Russia's Sakhalin Oblast. Roughly 948 km (589 mi) long and narrow in shape, it lies between the Sea of Okhotsk to the east and the Sea of Japan to the west, separated from the Asian mainland by the Strait of Tartary and from Japan's Hokkaido by La Pérouse Strait. Its name derives from the Manchu Sahaliyan Ula, "Black River", referring to the Amur.

History

Sakhalin was historically inhabited by the Nivkh, Ainu, and Orok peoples and was claimed in turn by Qing China, Japan, and Russia. The 1855 Treaty of Shimoda left the island under joint Russo-Japanese occupation; in 1875 Japan ceded its claims to Russia in exchange for the Kuril Islands, and Sakhalin became a notorious penal colony, described by Anton Chekhov in his 1895 book Sakhalin Island. After the Russo-Japanese War, the 1905 Treaty of Portsmouth divided the island at the 50th parallel: the south (Karafuto Prefecture) went to Japan, the north to Russia. In August 1945 the Soviet Union retook the south, and the Japanese population was repatriated; the whole island has since been administered by Russia (and the USSR), though Japan does not formally recognize the northern part dispute and once maintained claims.

Geography and economy

The island is mountainous in the south and centre, with a low-lying marshy plain in the north. Its climate is cold and humid, with heavy snow. The economy rests on oil and natural gas extraction from fields off the northeast coast (the Sakhalin-1 and Sakhalin-2 projects, including one of the world's largest liquefied natural gas plants), fishing, coal, and timber. A fixed link to the mainland has been proposed repeatedly but never built; transport depends on ferries, rail, and air. See also Kuril Islands and Sea of Okhotsk.
