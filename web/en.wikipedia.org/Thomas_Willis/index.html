Thomas Willis

Born27 January 1621, Great Bedwyn, Wiltshire
Died11 November 1675 (aged 54), London
Known forCircle of Willis; founding neurology
FieldsAnatomy, neurology, physiology

Thomas Willis (27 January 1621 – 11 November 1675) was an English physician who played a central part in the history of anatomy, neurology and psychiatry, and was a co-founder of the Royal Society. His Cerebri Anatome (1664), illustrated by Christopher Wren, gave the most complete account yet of the brain and nervous system and introduced the word "neurology" into usage.

Contributions

Willis described the ring of arteries at the base of the brain that still bears his name — the circle of Willis — and explained its function in providing collateral blood flow. He classified the cranial nerves, described numerous disorders including myasthenia gravis-like symptoms and restless legs syndrome (once called Willis–Ekbom disease), and noted the sweet taste of diabetic urine, adding "mellitus" to diabetes. A Sedleian professor of natural philosophy at Oxford and later a fashionable London physician, he treated the brain as the seat of the soul's rational functions and helped shift medicine towards anatomical, mechanistic explanation.
