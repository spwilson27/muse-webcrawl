Escrow

TypeLegal/financial arrangement
PartiesTwo transacting parties and a neutral third-party escrow agent
Common usesReal estate, mergers and acquisitions, online sales, software source code
In escrow, assets are held by a neutral party until agreed conditions are met.

Escrow is a contractual arrangement in which a third party (the escrow agent) receives and disburses money or property for the primary transacting parties, with the disbursement dependent on conditions agreed to by those parties. The arrangement reduces risk for both sides: the buyer is assured of receiving what was promised, and the seller is assured of payment.

Real estate

In property transactions, particularly in the United States, an escrow account may be opened when a buyer makes an earnest-money deposit, and closing funds are held by an escrow or title company until all conditions of the sale are satisfied. Lenders also commonly maintain escrow accounts for borrowers, collecting monthly amounts toward property taxes and homeowners insurance and paying those bills when due.

Other uses

Escrow is used in mergers and acquisitions (a portion of the purchase price held back against representations and warranties), in internet commerce where a service holds payment until goods are delivered, and in software licensing, where source code is deposited with an agent so a licensee can access it if the vendor ceases business. Escrow agents are typically regulated and must act as neutral fiduciaries for both parties. See also trust law and conveyancing.
