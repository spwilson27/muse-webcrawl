Windermere is a lake in Cumbria, England, and the largest natural lake in England by volume. It lies within the Lake District National Park and is fed by a number of rivers. The lake has been a popular tourist destination since the arrival of the Kendal and Windermere Railway in 1847, and the town of Windermere and the village of Bowness-on-Windermere grew up around its shore.

Geography

Windermere is a ribbon lake formed in a glacial trough after the retreat of ice at the end of the last ice age. It is approximately 18 kilometres (11 miles) long and up to 1.5 kilometres (0.9 miles) wide, and contains 18 islands, the largest of which is Belle Isle. The lake drains into the River Leven at its southern end.

Tourism

Steamer services have operated on the lake since 1845. The lake is also used for sailing, rowing, and speed-record attempts; the speed record on Windermere was set by Norman Buckley in 1956. A speed limit of 10 knots was introduced on the lake in 2005.
