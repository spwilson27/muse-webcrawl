Hyderabadi biryani

Hyderabadi biryani is a celebrated style of biryani originating in Hyderabad, in the Deccan region of India, closely identified with the cuisine of the Nizams of Hyderabad. It is made with basmati rice, meat (most traditionally goat/mutton or chicken), yogurt, onions, and a complex blend of spices, and is cooked by the dum pukht method — slow steaming in a sealed vessel.

Varieties

Two principal forms are recognised. In kachchi biryani, raw marinated meat is layered with partially soaked rice and cooked together so the meat and rice finish simultaneously — the most demanding and prized version. In pakki biryani, the meat is cooked separately before layering with the rice for the final dum. The dish is typically coloured with saffron, garnished with caramelised onions (birista), mint, and coriander, and served with mirchi ka salan (a peanut and chili curry) and raita.

History

Biryani reached the Deccan with Mughal influence under Aurangzeb's campaigns and was refined in the kitchens of the Nizams, blending Mughlai technique with local Telugu and Marathwada ingredients and a greater emphasis on chili heat. Hyderabad's biryani restaurants — institutions such as Paradise, founded in 1953 — made the dish a symbol of the city's identity, and it is a staple at weddings and festivals across Telangana.
