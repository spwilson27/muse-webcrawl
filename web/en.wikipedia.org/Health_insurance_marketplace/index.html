Health insurance marketplace

A health insurance marketplace, also called a health insurance exchange, is a service in the United States that helps individuals, families and small businesses shop for and enrol in health insurance. The marketplaces were established by the Affordable Care Act (ACA) of 2010, with the first open enrolment beginning in October 2013 for coverage starting in 2014.

Operation

The federal government operates the marketplace for most states through the website HealthCare.gov, while some states run their own exchanges, such as Covered California and New York State of Health. Plans are grouped into metal tiers (bronze, silver, gold and platinum) according to how costs are shared, and all must cover a set of essential health benefits without denying coverage for pre-existing conditions.

Subsidies

Many enrolees qualify for premium tax credits that lower monthly premiums on a sliding scale with income, and for cost-sharing reductions on silver plans. Enrolment normally happens during an annual open-enrolment period, with special enrolment available after life events such as losing other coverage or having a child.
