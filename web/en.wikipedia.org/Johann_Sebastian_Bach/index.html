Johann Sebastian Bach

Born: 31 March 1685, Eisenach
Died: 28 July 1750 (aged 65), Leipzig
Era: Baroque
Works: Over 1,100 compositions (BWV catalogue)

Johann Sebastian Bach (31 March 1685 – 28 July 1750) was a German composer and musician of the late Baroque period. He is known for his prolific output encompassing orchestral music such as the Brandenburg Concertos; solo instrumental works such as the cello suites and the sonatas and partitas for solo violin; keyboard works such as the Goldberg Variations and The Well-Tempered Clavier; organ works such as the Schubler Chorales and the Toccata and Fugue in D minor; and choral works such as the St Matthew Passion and the Mass in B minor.

Since the 19th-century Bach Revival, he has been generally regarded as one of the greatest composers in the history of Western music.

Life

Bach was born in Eisenach, in the duchy of Saxe-Eisenach, into an extraordinary musical family: his father, Johann Ambrosius Bach, was the director of the town musicians, and all of his uncles were professional musicians. Orphaned at ten, he lived with his elder brother Johann Christoph, from whom he received his first formal keyboard lessons. He held successive posts as a church organist at Arnstadt (1703–1707) and Mühlhausen (1707–1708), as court organist and concertmaster at Weimar (1708–1717), and as Kapellmeister at Köthen (1717–1723). In 1723 he was appointed Thomaskantor in Leipzig, directing music at four churches and teaching at the St. Thomas School, a post he held until his death in 1750.

Music and style

Bach's music is admired for its intellectual depth, contrapuntal mastery, and harmonic inventiveness. He absorbed and synthesised the styles of his German predecessors with Italian and French influences. His compositions often employ rigorous fugue, canon, and motivic development. In his later years he explored increasingly abstract contrapuntal projects in The Musical Offering and The Art of Fugue.

Legacy

Neglected after his death in favour of newer styles, Bach's reputation was revived in the 19th century, notably through Felix Mendelssohn's 1829 performance of the St Matthew Passion. His works are catalogued by BWV (Bach-Werke-Verzeichnis) numbers. Today his music is performed and recorded worldwide and remains foundational to Western art music.
