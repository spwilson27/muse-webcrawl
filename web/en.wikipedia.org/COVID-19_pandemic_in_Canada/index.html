COVID-19 pandemic in Canada

The COVID-19 pandemic in Canada began with the first confirmed case on 25 January 2020, involving a traveller who had returned to Toronto from Wuhan, China. The pandemic caused several waves of infection, tens of thousands of deaths, and major social and economic disruption.

Course of the pandemic

A state of emergency was declared in most provinces in March 2020, with closures of schools, businesses and the Canada–US border to non-essential travel. Long-term care homes were severely affected in the first wave, prompting military assistance in Ontario and Quebec. Successive waves were driven by the Alpha, Delta and Omicron variants, with the Omicron wave beginning in late 2021 producing the highest case counts.

Vaccination

Canada's vaccination campaign began in December 2020 with the Pfizer–BioNTech and Moderna vaccines. Despite a slow start amid supply constraints, Canada achieved one of the world's highest vaccination rates by late 2021, with more than 80 percent of the eligible population fully vaccinated.

Impact

Federal support programs such as the Canada Emergency Response Benefit (CERB) cushioned an economic contraction, while public health measures and vaccine mandates prompted protests, most prominently the 2022 "Freedom Convoy" occupation of Ottawa, which led to the first-ever invocation of the Emergencies Act.
