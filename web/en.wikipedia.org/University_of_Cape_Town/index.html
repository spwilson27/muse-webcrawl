The University of Cape Town (UCT) is a public research university in Cape Town, South Africa. Established in 1829 as the South African College, it is the oldest university in the country and is consistently ranked the top university in Africa. It received full university status in 1918 and moved to its present site on Cecil Rhodes's Groote Schuur estate below Devil's Peak in the 1920s.

History

The South African College began as a school for boys in 1829 and developed university-level teaching in the later 19th century. UCT admitted its first women students in the 1880s and remained formally non-racial in admissions for much of its history, though government policy progressively restricted it under apartheid; the university was a centre of liberal opposition to apartheid. In 2015 the Rhodes Must Fall movement, beginning with protests over the statue of Cecil Rhodes on campus, sparked wider debates about decolonisation in South African higher education.

Campuses and faculties

UCT's main teaching campus in Rondebosch is divided into Upper, Middle, and Lower Campus; the Faculty of Health Sciences is at Groote Schuur Hospital in Observatory, where Christiaan Barnard performed the first human heart transplant in 1967. The university has six faculties: Commerce, Engineering and the Built Environment, Health Sciences, Humanities, Law, and Science, plus the Graduate School of Business.

Notable people

Five Nobel laureates are associated with UCT, including Max Theiler, Allan MacLeod Cormack, Aaron Klug, and J. M. Coetzee, who also won the Booker Prize twice.
