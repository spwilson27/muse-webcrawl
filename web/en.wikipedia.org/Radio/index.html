Radio

A mid-20th-century tabletop radio receiver.

Radio is the technology of communicating using radio waves — electromagnetic waves with frequencies between about 3 hertz and 3 terahertz (usually considered in practice up to 300 gigahertz). Radio waves are generated by a transmitter connected to an antenna, which radiates them through space; they are received by a receiving antenna connected to a receiver. Radio is widely used for broadcasting, two-way communication, radar, navigation, remote control, mobile telephony and wireless networking.

History

Electromagnetic waves were predicted by James Clerk Maxwell in the 1860s and demonstrated by Heinrich Hertz in 1887. In the 1890s Guglielmo Marconi developed the first practical wireless telegraphy systems, achieving transatlantic signalling in 1901. Voice broadcasting began in the 1920s (stations such as KDKA in Pittsburgh), using amplitude modulation (AM) and later frequency modulation (FM, developed by Edwin Armstrong). Vacuum-tube and then transistor receivers made radio a mass medium.

Uses

Broadcast radio (AM, FM and digital services such as DAB), television, mobile phones, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, satellite communications, air-traffic control, emergency services and radio astronomy all depend on the regulated radio spectrum. Signals are encoded by modulating a carrier wave's amplitude, frequency, phase or pulses.
