The 2. Bundesliga (German pronunciation: [ˈtsvaɪ̯tə ˈbʊndəsˌliːɡa]; lit. "2nd Federal League") is the second division of professional football in Germany. It sits below the Bundesliga and above the 3. Liga in the German football league system. All of the 2. Bundesliga clubs qualify for the DFB-Pokal, the annual German Cup competition.

History

The league was founded in 1974, initially with two regional divisions of twenty clubs each, North and South, to bridge the financial and competitive gap between the Bundesliga and the amateur Regionalligas. In 1981 the two groups were merged into a single nationwide division of twenty teams. Following German reunification, clubs from the former East Germany were integrated in the early 1990s. The league was reduced to 18 teams in 1994.

Format

Eighteen clubs play a double round-robin schedule of 34 matches. The top two clubs are promoted automatically to the Bundesliga; the third-placed club plays a two-legged promotion/relegation play-off against the 16th-placed Bundesliga club. The bottom two clubs are relegated to the 3. Liga, while the 16th-placed club contests a play-off against the third-placed team of the 3. Liga.

Attendance and significance

The 2. Bundesliga is notable for the size of its clubs and crowds: fallen giants of German football frequently play in it, and its average attendance ranks among the highest of any second division in the world. Former champions who have spent seasons in the second tier include Schalke 04, Hamburger SV, Hertha BSC, and 1. FC Köln.
