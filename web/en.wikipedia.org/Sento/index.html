Sentō

A sentō (銭湯) is a type of Japanese communal bathhouse where patrons pay an admission fee to bathe in large indoor tubs of heated water. Bathers wash thoroughly before entering the communal baths, which are segregated by sex. Sentō have been a fixture of Japanese neighbourhood life since the Edo period.

Features

A traditional sentō has a changing room, washing stations with stools, taps and showers, and one or more deep soaking tubs. Many feature a painted mural, often of Mount Fuji, above the baths. Etiquette requires entering the water clean and keeping towels out of the tub.

Decline and revival

Numbers fell sharply as private bathrooms became standard in Japanese homes — from over 18,000 in Tokyo in the late 1960s to only a few hundred today — though some bathhouses have been revived as design attractions, cafés and community spaces.
