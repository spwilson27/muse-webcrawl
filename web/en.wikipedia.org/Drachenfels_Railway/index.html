Drachenfels Railway

The Drachenfels Railway (German: Drachenfelsbahn) is a rack railway in the Siebengebirge mountains of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, running from Königswinter on the Rhine up to the summit of the Drachenfels mountain (321 m). Opened in 1883, it is Germany's oldest cog/rack railway still in operation and carries visitors to the ruined Drachenfels castle, the Nibelungenhalle, and one of the most famous viewpoints over the Rhine.

History

The line was built to carry tourists up the Drachenfels, already celebrated through Byron's poem and Wagnerian associations (the mountain is linked in legend with Siegfried's slaying of the dragon Fafnir). Steam rack locomotives hauled trains from the opening in 1883; the line was electrified in 1953 with purpose-built railcars that remain characteristic of the railway. In September 1958 a derailment of an overcrowded steam-era remnant working resulted in the railway's worst accident. A passing loop at the mid-station (near Schloss Drachenburg, added after that castle became a popular attraction) allows two trains to operate. Modern railcars introduced in the 2000s supplemented the historic fleet.

Route

From the valley station in Königswinter (near the Rhine promenade and the Siebengebirgsmuseum) the line climbs steeply — gradients reach about 1 in 5 (20%) — through vineyards and woodland, with the intermediate station serving the fairy-tale Schloss Drachenburg (built 1882–84) and the Nibelungenhalle with its reptile zoo. The summit station stands below the ruins of Burg Drachenfels, destroyed in the Thirty Years' War, with panoramic views along the Rhine toward Bonn and Cologne.
