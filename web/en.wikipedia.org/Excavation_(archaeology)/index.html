Excavation (archaeology)

Excavation
FieldArchaeology
Key methodsStratigraphic excavation, recording, sieving

In archaeology, excavation is the exposure, processing and recording of archaeological remains. It involves the systematic removal of soil and deposits to reveal buried features, structures and artefacts, and is often described as a destructive process, since the excavation of a deposit destroys the context it records.

Principles

Modern excavation follows the principle of stratigraphy: deposits are removed in reverse order to that in which they were laid down, one context at a time. Each context is documented with plans, photographs and written records, and relationships between contexts are summarised in a Harris matrix. Because the evidence is destroyed as it is dug, meticulous recording is essential.

Techniques

Excavations range from small test pits and evaluation trenches to large open-area digs. Tools include trowels, shovels and mechanical excavators for removing topsoil; finds are recovered by hand and through sieving and flotation, which retrieves seeds and other environmental evidence.

Types

Research excavations address academic questions, while rescue or salvage excavations take place in advance of development. In many countries, developer-funded commercial archaeology now accounts for most fieldwork.
