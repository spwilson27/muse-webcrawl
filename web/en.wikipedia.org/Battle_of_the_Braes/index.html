Battle of the Braes

Battle of the Braes
DateApril 1882
LocationThe Braes, near Portree, Isle of Skye, Scotland
ContextHighland Land Wars; crofters' resistance to eviction
OutcomeCrofters resisted eviction; five men tried and fined; public sympathy contributed to the Napier Commission and the Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886

The Battle of the Braes was a confrontation between crofters and police in April 1882 in the Braes district south of Portree on the Isle of Skye, Scotland. It became one of the most famous episodes of the Highland Land Wars and a landmark in the struggle for crofters' rights.

Background

Crofters on Lord Macdonald's estate at the Braes had lost access to grazing land on Ben Lee, which had been let to a sheep farmer. In protest they withheld rent and threatened to drive sheep onto the disputed grazing. The landlord sought to evict some of the tenants and served summonses on them.

The confrontation

When a sheriff officer arrived to serve the summonses, the crowd burned the documents. A force of about fifty Glasgow policemen was sent to arrest the ringleaders. On the road at the Braes they were met by a large crowd of men, women and children armed with sticks and stones. After a scuffle in which several police were injured, five crofters were arrested, but the police were forced to retreat, and the episode drew national press attention.

Aftermath

The five arrested men were tried in Edinburgh, fined, and returned home as heroes. Public outrage over the treatment of crofters helped prompt the government to appoint the Napier Commission in 1883, whose findings led to the Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886, granting crofters security of tenure and fair rents.
