Samara (fruit)

Winged samaras of a maple spin as they fall, dispersing the seed.

A samara is a type of dry, winged fruit (an achene) in which a flattened wing of fibrous tissue develops from the ovary wall, allowing the seed to be carried by the wind (anemochory). As a samara falls, the wing causes it to autorotate like a helicopter rotor, slowing its descent and increasing dispersal distance.

Examples

The classic samaras are those of maples (Acer), which produce paired double samaras often called "helicopters" or "whirlybirds". Other producers include ash, elm, tree of heaven, and the tropical genus Combretum; the extinct seed fern Diplopteridium bore similar structures in deep time.

Function and engineering

The aerodynamic design of the samara — a heavy seed at one end and a single wing sweeping back — creates a stable leading-edge vortex that maximises lift. This has inspired biomimetic designs for miniature air vehicles and sensor-delivery "microfliers".

Cultural notes

Children in many countries play with fallen maple samaras, splitting the seed case and sticking it on the nose, or throwing them to watch them spin.
