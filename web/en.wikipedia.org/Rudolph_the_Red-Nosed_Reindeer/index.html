Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer is a fictional reindeer created by Robert L. May in 1939, when the Chicago-based retailer Montgomery Ward assigned May to write a Christmas story booklet to give away to shoppers. Rudolph, shunned by the other reindeer for his glowing red nose, is chosen by Santa Claus to lead his sleigh on a foggy Christmas Eve, the light of his nose guiding the team.

The song and later adaptations

May's brother-in-law, songwriter Johnny Marks, adapted the story into the song "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer", recorded by Gene Autry in 1949; it reached No. 1 on the U.S. charts and became one of the best-selling singles of all time. The 1964 Rankin/Bass animated television special, narrated by Burl Ives, has been broadcast annually ever since and fixed Rudolph's image in popular culture.

Cultural impact

The story is often read as a fable about difference becoming a gift: the trait that made Rudolph an outcast is precisely what makes him indispensable. Rudolph is sometimes called Santa's ninth reindeer, joining the eight named in Clement Clarke Moore's 1823 poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas".
