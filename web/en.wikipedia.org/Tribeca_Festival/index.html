Tribeca Festival

LocationNew York City, United States
Founded2002
FoundersJane Rosenthal, Robert De Niro, Craig Hatkoff

The Tribeca Festival is an annual film festival organized by Tribeca Productions. It takes place each spring in New York City, showcasing a diverse selection of film, episodic, talks, music, games, art, and immersive programming. Tribeca was founded by actor Robert De Niro, producer Jane Rosenthal, and Craig Hatkoff in 2002 to spur the economic and cultural revitalization of Lower Manhattan following the September 11, 2001 attacks on the World Trade Center. Until 2020, it was known as the Tribeca Film Festival.

Programming

The festival screens narrative features, documentaries, and shorts in competition and out of competition, awarding prizes including Best Narrative Feature and Best Documentary Feature. Over the years it has expanded beyond film to include television premieres, video games, podcasts, immersive virtual-reality works, and live music and talk events.

History

The inaugural festival in 2002 attracted an estimated 150,000 attendees and generated about $10.4 million in revenue for New York City. The festival has grown to include venues across the city and now routinely draws hundreds of thousands of attendees. In 2021 it was rebranded from the Tribeca Film Festival to the Tribeca Festival to reflect its multi-disciplinary scope.
