Video game preservation

Video game preservation is the effort to conserve video games and their associated hardware, source code, and cultural materials against loss through media degradation, hardware obsolescence, server shutdowns, and copyright restrictions. Preservationists include museums, libraries, archives, fan communities, and organizations such as the Video Game History Foundation and The Strong National Museum of Play.

Challenges

  • Bit rot and media decay — magnetic disks, optical discs, and cartridge ROMs deteriorate over time.
  • Hardware dependence — games written for obsolete consoles and computers cannot run without the original hardware or emulation.
  • Online components — games requiring remote servers may become wholly unplayable when servers are shut down.
  • Lost source code — many developers discarded code and assets, making faithful ports or remasters impossible.
  • Legal barriers — anti-circumvention rules such as Section 1201 of the DMCA restrict copying even for archival purposes, though libraries and archives have won limited exemptions.

Methods

Preservation relies on emulation, ROM dumping, source-code archiving, and documentation of development materials and oral histories. A 2023 study by the Video Game History Foundation and the Software Preservation Network found that about 87% of classic games released in the United States are not available through any legal commercial channel, a figure frequently cited in debates over expanded archival exemptions.
