Daniel Libeskind (born 12 May 1946) is a Polish-born American architect, artist, professor and set designer. Known for an angular, fragmented style associated with deconstructivism, he first gained international attention with the Jewish Museum in Berlin, opened in 2001.

Life and career

Libeskind was born in Łódź, Poland, to Holocaust survivors, emigrated to Israel as a child — where he studied music — and moved to New York in 1959. He studied architecture at Cooper Union and at the University of Essex. For years he worked mainly as an academic and theorist before winning the Jewish Museum competition in 1989. In 2003 his firm won the competition for the master plan of the rebuilt World Trade Center site in New York.

Selected works

Major buildings include the Imperial War Museum North in Manchester, the Denver Art Museum's Hamilton Building, the Royal Ontario Museum's Michael Lee-Chin Crystal in Toronto, the Danish Jewish Museum in Copenhagen, the Military History Museum in Dresden, and One World Trade Center's site plan with its 1,776-foot symbolic height.
