Guanajuato

Guanajuato is a Mexican state in the Bajío region of central Mexico, and also the name of its capital city, Guanajuato City. The state is historically important as the birthplace of Mexican independence, and the city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site famed for its steep alleys, colourful houses and underground streets.

History

The region was colonised by Spain in the 1520s and became one of the world's richest silver-mining districts; the Valenciana mine near the capital was among the most productive silver mines in the world in the eighteenth century. In 1810, the priest Miguel Hidalgo issued the Cry of Dolores in the town of Dolores, Guanajuato, launching the War of Independence, and the first major battle of the war was fought at the Alhóndiga de Granaditas in the state capital.

Geography and economy

The state of Guanajuato has roughly six million inhabitants; its largest city is León, a major centre of the leather and footwear industries. The state is also an important agricultural and automotive manufacturing region. Other notable cities include San Miguel de Allende, Celaya, Irapuato and Salamanca.

Culture

Guanajuato City hosts the Festival Internacional Cervantino, one of Latin America's most important performing-arts festivals, held each October. The city is the birthplace of the muralist Diego Rivera and is known for its mummy museum and its network of subterranean roadways built in former riverbeds.
