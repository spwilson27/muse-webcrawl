John D. Rockefeller Jr.

John Davison Rockefeller Jr. (29 January 1874 – 11 May 1960) was an American financier and philanthropist, the only son of Standard Oil co-founder John D. Rockefeller. He devoted his life to managing and giving away the family fortune, funding Rockefeller Center, the restoration of Colonial Williamsburg, the Museum of Modern Art, and the land for the United Nations headquarters in New York.

Early life

Rockefeller was born in Cleveland, Ohio, and educated at Brown University, graduating in 1897. He joined his father's office in New York, became a director of Standard Oil and of US Steel, but found his vocation in organised philanthropy, helping to professionalise the family's giving through bodies such as the Rockefeller Foundation (founded 1913). His public reputation suffered from the Ludlow Massacre of 1914 at a Rockefeller-controlled Colorado coal company, after which he worked to reform the family's labour relations and public image.

Projects

During the Great Depression he built Rockefeller Center in midtown Manhattan, an Art Deco complex that employed tens of thousands. He financed the restoration of Colonial Williamsburg in Virginia from 1926, the reconstruction of Versailles and Reims Cathedral after the First World War, and the Cloisters museum in New York. A committed conservationist, he bought and donated land for Grand Teton, Acadia, Shenandoah and other national parks, often anonymously. In 1946 he purchased and donated the East River site for the United Nations headquarters.

Personal life and legacy

With his first wife, Abby Aldrich Rockefeller, a co-founder of the Museum of Modern Art, he had six children, five sons — John III, Nelson, Laurance, Winthrop and David — who became prominent in philanthropy, politics, conservation and banking. He is estimated to have given away more than $500 million in his lifetime. He died in Tucson, Arizona, in 1960.
