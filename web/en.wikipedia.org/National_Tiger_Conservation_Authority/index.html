National Tiger Conservation Authority

National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)
Formed: 2005
Headquarters: New Delhi, India
Parent agency: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change
Type: Statutory body

The National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) is a statutory body of the Government of India under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change. It was established in December 2005 following the recommendations of the Tiger Task Force, and it received statutory status through amendments to the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972, which came into force in 2006.

Functions

The NTCA is responsible for strengthening tiger conservation in India. Its functions include approving tiger conservation plans prepared by state governments, evaluating and assessing various aspects of sustainable ecology and disallowing ecologically unsustainable land use in tiger reserves, laying down normative standards for tourism activities and guidelines for tiger conservation in buffer and core areas, and providing information on protection measures, tiger estimation, and disease surveillance. The authority also supports states in anti-poaching operations and habitat management.

Project Tiger

The NTCA oversees Project Tiger, the centrally sponsored scheme launched in 1973 for in-situ conservation of tigers in designated tiger reserves. The number of tiger reserves has grown from nine at the program's inception to more than fifty, covering a significant portion of India's forested area. India conducts a national tiger estimation exercise every four years; the 2022 estimation recorded a minimum population of over 3,100 tigers, making India home to the majority of the world's wild tigers.

Structure

The authority is chaired by the Minister for Environment, Forest and Climate Change and includes members of Parliament, officials, and wildlife experts. A Member Secretary, drawn from the Indian Forest Service, heads its secretariat in New Delhi.
