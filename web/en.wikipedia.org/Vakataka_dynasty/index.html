Vakataka dynasty

Periodc. 250 – c. 500 CE
RegionDeccan, central India (Vidarbha)
FounderVindhyashakti
Notable rulersPravarasena I, Rudrasena II, Harishena
ReligionHinduism (Shaivism and Vaishnavism); patronage of Buddhism

The Vakataka dynasty was an ancient Indian dynasty that ruled parts of the Deccan and central India from about the mid-3rd century to the end of the 5th century CE. Emerging after the decline of the Satavahanas, the Vakatakas were contemporaries and allies of the Gupta Empire and are remembered for their patronage of art, architecture and Sanskrit learning.

Origins and expansion

The dynasty was founded by Vindhyashakti (c. 250–270 CE), probably a feudatory of the Satavahanas in the Vidarbha region. His son Pravarasena I expanded Vakataka power considerably and was the only ruler of the dynasty to take the imperial title Samrat, performing Vedic sacrifices including the Ashvamedha. After his death the kingdom divided into branches, principally the Pravarapura–Nandivardhana branch and the Vatsagulma branch.

Relations with the Guptas

Prabhavatigupta, daughter of the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II, married the Vakataka king Rudrasena II and, after his early death, ruled as regent for her sons for about two decades. This alliance made the Vakatakas close partners of the Guptas and helped secure Gupta influence in the Deccan without direct annexation.

Culture and legacy

The Vakatakas were great patrons of the arts. The later phase of the celebrated Buddhist cave paintings and sculptures at Ajanta was executed under the Vatsagulma branch, especially during the reign of Harishena (c. 475–500 CE), in whose time the dynasty reached its greatest extent before rapidly declining. The court poet Kalidasa is traditionally associated with the Vakataka milieu, and his poem Meghaduta is linked to the court of Pravarasena II, himself credited with the Prakrit poem Setubandha. Vakataka rule gave way in the 6th century to successor powers including the Kalachuris, Vishnukundinas and the rising Chalukyas.
