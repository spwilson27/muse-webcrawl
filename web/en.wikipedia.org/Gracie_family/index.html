Gracie family


The Gracie family, founders of Brazilian jiu-jitsu (Gracie jiu-jitsu).

The Gracie family (Portuguese: [ˈɡɾejsi]) is a family of martial artists originally from Belém, state of Pará, Brazil, and now settled throughout Brazil, the United States, and other countries. They are known for promoting the self-defence martial art system of Gracie Jiu-Jitsu, commonly known as Brazilian jiu-jitsu, which they are credited with originating in the 1910s–1920s. The family also co-founded the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) in 1993 and helped ignite the global popularity of mixed martial arts (MMA).

Origins

The Gracies descend from Scottish immigrant George Gracie, who settled in Brazil in the 19th century. His descendant Gastão Gracie, a businessman in Pará, befriended the Japanese judoka Mitsuyo Maeda (known as Conde Koma), who had arrived in Brazil in 1914 demonstrating judo. Gastão's eldest son, Carlos Gracie (1902–1994), learned Maeda's art and began teaching it in Rio de Janeiro in the 1920s, opening the first Gracie academy in 1925. Carlos's younger brother Hélio Gracie (1913–2009), smaller and frailer, is credited with adapting the techniques to rely on leverage and timing rather than strength, becoming the family's pre-eminent fighter-teacher and patriarch of its most famous line.

The Gracie Challenge and vale tudo

To prove their art's effectiveness, the Gracies issued open challenges to practitioners of other styles — the "Gracie Challenge" — and competed in vale tudo (no-holds-barred) matches for decades. Hélio fought famous bouts, including a marathon match against former student Waldemar Santana in 1955 and a loss to judoka Masahiko Kimura in 1951 (the reverse ude-garami with which Kimura broke his arm is still called the "Kimura" in Brazilian jiu-jitsu). The rivalry with the Luta Livre school in the 1980s–90s brought the family further notoriety in Brazil.

Global expansion and the UFC

Hélio's sons — among them Rickson, Royler, and Royce — and Carlos's sons — including Carlson and Rolls — spread the art internationally. Rorion Gracie, who moved to California in 1978, co-founded the UFC in 1993; his younger brother Royce Gracie, chosen as the family champion, won three of the first four UFC tournaments against larger opponents, demonstrating jiu-jitsu's effectiveness and revolutionising martial arts training worldwide. Gracie academies and affiliated schools now operate globally, and family members continue to compete in MMA, submission grappling, and gi jiu-jitsu across multiple generations.

Diet and culture

Carlos Gracie developed the "Gracie diet", a food-combining regimen long promoted within the family. The family's size, internal rivalries, and business disputes have been extensively documented in books and films about the history of MMA.
