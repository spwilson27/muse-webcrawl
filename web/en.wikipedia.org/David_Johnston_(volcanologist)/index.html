David Johnston (volcanologist)

David Alexander Johnston (18 December 1949 – 18 May 1980) was an American volcanologist with the United States Geological Survey (USGS). He was killed in the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens in Washington, where he was manning an observation post six miles from the volcano when its north flank collapsed. His final radio transmission — "Vancouver! Vancouver! This is it!" — became one of the most famous messages in the history of volcanology.

Career

Johnston studied geology at the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign and earned his PhD from the University of Washington in 1978, researching volcanic gases and the 1970 eruption of Mount Augustine in Alaska. At the USGS he specialized in monitoring volcanic gases, and in the spring of 1980 he was among the first scientists on the scene as Mount St. Helens reawoke, arguing forcefully for keeping the area around the volcano closed to the public.

Death at Mount St. Helens

On the morning of 18 May 1980, Johnston was alone at the Coldwater II observation post, on a ridge north of the volcano. At 8:32 a.m. a magnitude 5.1 earthquake triggered the largest landslide in recorded history; the ensuing lateral blast swept over the ridge at several hundred miles per hour. Johnston's body was never found. His warnings and the evacuation zone he championed are credited with saving many lives.

Legacy

The Johnston Ridge Observatory, overlooking the crater of Mount St. Helens within the Mount St. Helens National Volcanic Monument, is named in his honor, as is the USGS's David A. Johnston Cascades Volcano Observatory in Vancouver, Washington.
