Galleria Borghese

The Galleria Borghese (Borghese Gallery) is an art gallery in Rome, Italy, housed in the former Villa Borghese Pinciana. At the outset, the gallery building was integrated with its gardens, but nowadays the Villa Borghese gardens are considered a separate tourist attraction. The Galleria Borghese houses a substantial part of the Borghese collection of paintings, sculpture and antiquities, begun by Cardinal Scipione Borghese, the nephew of Pope Paul V.

History

The villa was built by the architect Flaminio Ponzio, developing sketches by Scipione Borghese himself, who used it as a villa suburbana, a party villa at the edge of Rome. Scipione was an early patron of the sculptor Gian Lorenzo Bernini and an avid collector of works by Caravaggio, whose paintings form a celebrated part of the collection. In 1808, Prince Camillo Borghese was forced to sell many classical sculptures to his brother-in-law Napoleon; they are now in the Louvre. The Italian state purchased the villa and its collection in 1902.

Collection

The museum displays masterpieces by Bernini, including Apollo and Daphne, The Rape of Proserpina and David, as well as Antonio Canova's reclining Paolina Borghese as Venus Victrix. Paintings include works by Caravaggio, Raphael (including The Deposition), Titian, Rubens, Dosso Dossi and Correggio. The rooms are richly decorated with frescoes, stuccoes and polychrome marble inlay.
