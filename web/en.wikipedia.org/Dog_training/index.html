Dog training

Dog training is a type of animal training, the application of behavior analysis which uses the environmental events of antecedents (trigger for a behavior) and consequences to modify the dog behavior, either for it to assist in specific activities or undertake particular tasks, or for it to participate effectively in contemporary domestic life.

Methods

Training approaches are generally described in terms of operant conditioning: positive reinforcement rewards desired behavior with food, praise, or play; negative punishment removes something desirable; and aversive methods apply corrections. Modern science-based trainers predominantly favor reward-based methods, and professional organizations discourage the use of pain or intimidation.

History

Dogs have been trained for herding, hunting, guarding, and warfare for centuries. Formal obedience training developed in the early 20th century, influenced by military and police dog programs, and popularized by figures such as Konrad Most and later B. F. Skinner's work on operant conditioning. Clicker training, adapted from dolphin training, became widely used from the 1990s.

Applications

Beyond basic obedience — sit, stay, recall, and loose-leash walking — trained dogs serve as service dogs, detection dogs, police and military dogs, search-and-rescue dogs, and competitors in sports such as agility and obedience trials.
