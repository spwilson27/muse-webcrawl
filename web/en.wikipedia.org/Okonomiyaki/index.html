Okonomiyaki

Okonomiyaki (お好み焼き) is a Japanese teppanyaki savoury pancake dish consisting of wheat flour batter and other ingredients (mixed, or as toppings) cooked on a teppan (flat griddle). Common additions include cabbage, meat, and seafood, and toppings include okonomiyaki sauce (made with Worcestershire sauce), aonori (dried seaweed flakes), katsuobushi (bonito flakes), Japanese mayonnaise, and pickled ginger.

Name

The name is derived from the word okonomi, meaning "how you like" or "what you like", and yaki, meaning "grilled". It is sometimes called "Japanese pizza" or "Japanese pancake" in English, though it is neither.

Regional styles

The two best-known styles are the Kansai (Osaka) style, in which all ingredients are mixed into the batter before cooking, and the Hiroshima style, in which the ingredients are layered and yakisoba or udon noodles are included. Both are finished with sauce, mayonnaise, aonori, and katsuobushi. In Hiroshima, the dish became popular after World War II as an inexpensive food made from rationed flour, and the city is famous for its multi-storey "Okonomimura" food building devoted to the dish.

History

Precursors include funoyaki, a thin crepe-like sweet associated with the tea ceremony, and Edo-period snacks such as monjayaki in Tokyo. The modern dish took shape in the early 20th century and spread widely after the Second World War.
