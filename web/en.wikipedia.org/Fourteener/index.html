Fourteener

DefinitionPeak with elevation ≥ 14,000 ft (4,267 m)

A fourteener is a mountain peak with an elevation of at least 14,000 ft (4,267 m). The term is used mainly in the United States by mountaineers, where all the world's fourteen eight-thousanders lie elsewhere but nearly a hundred peaks exceed 14,000 feet. Fourteeners are popular climbing objectives, and "bagging" all of them in a state is a recognised mountaineering goal.

Distribution

Colorado has the most fourteeners of any state — 53 by the common criterion requiring at least 300 feet of topographic prominence, 58 by elevation alone — including Mount Elbert (14,440 ft), the highest peak in the Rocky Mountains. Alaska's Denali (20,310 ft) is the highest fourteener and the highest mountain in North America. California has 12, including Mount Whitney, the highest point in the contiguous United States, and Washington has Mount Rainier, the most topographically prominent peak in the contiguous states.

Climbing

Most fourteeners have at least one non-technical "standard route" achievable by fit hikers in summer, though altitude sickness, lightning and rapidly changing weather are serious hazards. Records for climbing all the Colorado fourteeners have fallen below ten days. In winter, and on peaks such as Capitol Peak with its exposed Knife Edge, fourteeners demand full mountaineering skills.

Other uses of the term

"Fourteener" also denotes a poem of fourteen syllables per line (heptameter), and in prosody a line of fourteen syllables — though this article concerns the mountains.
