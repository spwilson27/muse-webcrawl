Duchy

A duchy, also called a dukedom, is a territory, fief, or region ruled by a duke or duchess. Historically, duchies were among the most powerful subdivisions of medieval kingdoms and empires in Europe, and some, such as Normandy, Burgundy, and Bavaria, were effectively sovereign states.

History

The title of duke derives from the Latin dux, a military commander. In the Frankish kingdoms, dukes governed large frontier provinces. In the Holy Roman Empire, the so-called stem duchies — Saxony, Franconia, Swabia, Bavaria, and Lotharingia — formed the core of the kingdom. Over time many duchies were inherited, partitioned, elevated to kingdoms, or absorbed into larger states.

Modern survivals

The Grand Duchy of Luxembourg is the world's only remaining sovereign grand duchy. In the United Kingdom, the Duchy of Lancaster and the Duchy of Cornwall survive as landed estates associated with the monarch and the Prince of Wales respectively.
