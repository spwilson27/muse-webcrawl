Anatomical plane

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The principal anatomical planes of the human body

An anatomical plane is a hypothetical plane used to transect the body, in order to describe the location of structures or the direction of movements. In human and animal anatomy, three principal planes are used:

  • The sagittal plane or lateral plane divides the body into left and right parts. The median (midsagittal) plane passes through the midline; parasagittal planes run parallel to it.
  • The coronal plane or frontal plane divides the body into dorsal and ventral (back and front, or posterior and anterior) portions.
  • The transverse plane, also known as the axial or horizontal plane, divides the body into cranial and caudal (head and tail) portions, and is perpendicular to the other two planes.

Terminology

Anatomical planes are defined relative to the standard anatomical position. In humans, the transverse plane is horizontal, but in quadrupeds it runs differently relative to the ground. Additional terms such as oblique planes describe cuts at other angles, and planes of specific organs may be named for them, such as the transpyloric plane in abdominal anatomy.

Uses

Planes provide a shared reference framework for describing anatomy, directing surgery, and interpreting images. Medical imaging modalities such as CT and MRI conventionally display the body in axial (transverse), coronal, and sagittal slices, and three-dimensional reconstructions allow arbitrary planes to be viewed.
