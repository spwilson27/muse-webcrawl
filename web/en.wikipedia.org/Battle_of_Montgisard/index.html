The Battle of Montgisard was fought between the Kingdom of Jerusalem and the Ayyubid Sultanate on 25 November 1177 at Montgisard, in the Levant between Ramla and Ibelin. The 16-year-old Baldwin IV, seriously afflicted by leprosy, led outnumbered Christian forces against Saladin's army and obtained a decisive victory.

Background

Saladin invaded the kingdom from Egypt with a large army, expecting Baldwin to be unable to resist; the king had been bottled up in Ascalon with only a few hundred knights. Baldwin slipped out and, joined by the Templars under Odo of St Amand and Reynald of Châtillon, marched to intercept the invaders, who had dispersed to plunder the countryside around Ramla and Lydda.

The battle

The Crusaders caught Saladin's army strung out and unprepared near the hill of Montgisard. Baldwin's charge broke the Ayyubid forces, and Saladin barely escaped on a racing camel. His army suffered heavy losses in the rout and during its retreat to Egypt.

Aftermath

The victory secured the kingdom for another decade and made Baldwin IV's reputation, though it did not permanently remove the Ayyubid threat: Saladin returned to crush the Crusader states at Hattin in 1187.
