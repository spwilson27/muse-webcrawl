Traffic management is the organisation, arrangement, guidance and control of both stationary and moving traffic, including pedestrians, bicyclists and all types of vehicles. Its aims are to improve safety, reduce congestion and delay, and make efficient use of existing road capacity — most often through operational measures rather than new construction.

Methods

Core techniques include traffic signal coordination and adaptive signal control, ramp metering on motorways, lane management (including reversible and high-occupancy vehicle lanes), parking management, and incident management to clear crashes and breakdowns quickly. Traffic control centres monitor networks with detectors and cameras and can post variable message signs and speed advisories.

Demand management

Authorities also manage demand through congestion pricing, parking charges, travel information, and promotion of public transport, walking, and cycling. Major events and roadworks are planned with temporary traffic management schemes using signs, barriers, and detours.

Intelligent systems

Intelligent transportation systems (ITS) add real-time data from sensors, connected vehicles, and navigation platforms, enabling predictive control and traveller information. Air and rail networks run analogous flow-management systems.
