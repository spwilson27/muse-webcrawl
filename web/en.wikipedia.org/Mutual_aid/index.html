Mutual aid

A community mutual aid food distribution

Mutual aid is a form of social organisation in which people cooperate voluntarily and reciprocally for mutual benefit, exchanging resources and services outside of market exchange or state provision. The concept spans evolutionary biology, anarchist political theory, and everyday community practice.

Kropotkin's theory

The term was popularised by the Russian naturalist and anarchist Peter Kropotkin in Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution (1902), a collection of essays arguing — against readings of Darwin that stressed competition — that cooperation within species is a major driver of evolution and that mutual aid institutions, from medieval guilds to village communes, were central to human social progress.

Practice

Historically, mutual aid has taken institutional form in friendly societies, cooperatives, and mutual insurance. In the 21st century, informal mutual aid networks expanded during disasters and during the COVID-19 pandemic, organising grocery deliveries, funds, and neighbourhood support on a solidarity rather than charity model — summarised in the slogan "solidarity not charity".
