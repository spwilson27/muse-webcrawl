Finland at the Winter Olympics

Cross-country skiing has historically been Finland's strongest Winter Olympic sport.

Finland has competed at every edition of the Winter Olympic Games since the first Games in Chamonix in 1924. The National Olympic Committee for Finland is the Finnish Olympic Committee, founded in 1907. Despite a population of only about 5.5 million, Finland is one of the most successful nations in Winter Olympic history, traditionally strong in cross-country skiing, ski jumping, and ice hockey.

History

Finnish athletes dominated early Olympic cross-country skiing and ski jumping. At the first Winter Games in 1924, Finland placed second in the medal table. Speed skater Clas Thunberg and the "Flying Finns" of endurance sport established Finland's winter reputation, and jumper Matti Nykänen became one of the most successful ski jumpers of all time in the 1980s.

Finland's men's ice hockey team won its first Olympic gold medal at the Beijing 2022 Games, defeating the Russian Olympic Committee team in the final; the women's team has won several bronze medals. Finland hosted the 1952 Summer Olympics in Helsinki but has never hosted the Winter Games.
