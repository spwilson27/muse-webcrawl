Presidential election

A presidential election is a method by which the voters of a country choose its president, the head of state (and often head of government) in a presidential or semi-presidential republic. Presidential elections are held in many countries, including the United States, France, Brazil, and the Philippines.

Systems

Presidents may be elected directly by popular vote or indirectly, as in the United States, where voters choose members of the Electoral College who formally elect the president. Many countries use a two-round system: if no candidate wins a majority in the first round, the top two candidates compete in a runoff.

Campaigns

Presidential campaigns typically involve party primaries or nominations, debates, advertising, and fundraising, culminating in a fixed election day and a transfer of power on a set inauguration date. Term lengths commonly range from four to seven years, and many constitutions limit presidents to two terms.
