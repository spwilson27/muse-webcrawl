Antoine Galland (4 April 1646 – 17 February 1715) was a French orientalist and archaeologist, best known as the first European translator of One Thousand and One Nights (the Arabian Nights). His version, Les mille et une nuits, published in twelve volumes between 1704 and 1717, shaped the European reception of Arabic literature.

Life and career

Galland studied oriental languages in Paris and travelled extensively in the Levant as an antiquarian and secretary, collecting coins, manuscripts, and inscriptions for French patrons. On his return he became an assistant to the keeper of the royal library, and in 1709 he was appointed professor of Arabic at the Collège Royal. He also published translations of the Qur'an's commentary literature and a work on the origin of coffee.

The Nights

Galland translated the Nights from a fourteenth- or fifteenth-century Syrian manuscript. His later volumes included the famous "orphan tales" — Aladdin and Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves — which have no known Arabic manuscript source predating Galland; he heard them from the Syrian storyteller Hanna Diyab in 1709. Galland adapted the tales to French classical taste, smoothing elements he judged crude, but his translation introduced the collection to world literature and inspired countless adaptations.
