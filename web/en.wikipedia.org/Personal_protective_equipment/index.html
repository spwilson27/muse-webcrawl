Personal protective equipment (PPE) is equipment worn to minimise exposure to hazards that cause serious workplace injuries and illnesses, including chemical, radiological, physical, electrical, mechanical and biological hazards. Common items include gloves, safety glasses and shoes, earplugs and earmuffs, hard hats, respirators, coveralls, high-visibility clothing and full body suits.

Use and limitations

In the hierarchy of hazard controls, PPE ranks last because it protects only the individual wearer and depends on correct selection, fit, maintenance and consistent use. Employers in many jurisdictions are legally required to assess hazards, provide suitable PPE free of charge and train workers in its use. Respiratory protection ranges from disposable filtering facepieces (such as N95/FFP2 masks) to supplied-air respirators for highly toxic environments.

Healthcare PPE

In healthcare, PPE — gloves, gowns, masks and eye protection — is central to infection prevention and control, guarding against contact, droplet and airborne transmission. The COVID-19 pandemic from 2020 caused worldwide shortages and a surge in demand, prompting stockpiling policies and renewed attention to domestic manufacturing capacity. Medical PPE must meet standards for fluid resistance and filtration, and safe donning and doffing procedures are essential to avoid self-contamination.
