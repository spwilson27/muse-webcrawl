Charles the Bald

Charles the Bald (13 June 823 – 6 October 877) was a Carolingian ruler: King of West Francia from 843, King of Italy from 875, and Holy Roman Emperor from 875 until his death. The youngest son of Louis the Pious, he received the western Frankish kingdom under the Treaty of Verdun in 843.

Reign

Charles's rule was marked by the challenge of Viking raids, aristocratic resistance and conflict with his brothers Louis the German and Lothair I. He confirmed the division of the empire with his brothers at Verdun in 843 and later partitioned Lotharingia with Louis the German at the Treaty of Meerssen in 870. To resist the Vikings he ordered fortified bridges built on the great rivers under the Edict of Pîtres (864), and paid tribute on several occasions when defence failed.

Imperial crown and culture

Crowned emperor by Pope John VIII in 875, Charles also claimed Italy after the death of Louis II. His court was a centre of the Carolingian Renaissance: he patronised the scholar John Scotus Eriugena, and lavish illuminated manuscripts such as the Vivian Bible and his own psalter were produced for him. His epithet "the Bald" may have been ironic, referring to abundant hair, or may reflect having been tonsured as a child.

Death

Charles died in October 877 while crossing the Alps at Avrieux in Savoy, possibly of malaria, as he hurried back from an unsuccessful campaign in Italy. He was succeeded in West Francia by his son Louis the Stammerer.
