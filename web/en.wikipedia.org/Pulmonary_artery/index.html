Pulmonary artery

The pulmonary arteries are the blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood from the right side of the heart to the lungs, where carbon dioxide is exchanged for oxygen. They are unusual among arteries in transporting oxygen-poor blood; conversely, the pulmonary veins return oxygenated blood to the heart. The pulmonary circulation operates at much lower pressure than the systemic circulation.

Anatomy

The pulmonary trunk arises from the right ventricle at the pulmonary valve and, after a short course of about 5 cm, divides beneath the aortic arch into the left and right pulmonary arteries. The right pulmonary artery, longer than the left, passes under the carina to the right lung; the left pulmonary artery runs to the left lung and is connected to the descending aorta by the ligamentum arteriosum, the remnant of the fetal ductus arteriosus. Within the lungs, the arteries branch alongside the bronchi, dividing into lobar, segmental, and subsegmental branches before forming the capillary networks around the alveoli.

Embryology

The pulmonary arteries develop from the sixth pharyngeal arch arteries. In fetal life most right-ventricular output bypasses the fluid-filled lungs through the ductus arteriosus into the aorta; after birth the ductus closes and the full cardiac output passes through the lungs.

Clinical significance

Blockage of the pulmonary arteries by a blood clot, a pulmonary embolism, can be life-threatening and is a major cause of cardiovascular death. Persistently elevated pressure in the pulmonary arteries defines pulmonary hypertension, which strains the right ventricle. The pulmonary arteries are imaged by CT pulmonary angiography, and pressures can be measured during right-heart catheterization; a Swan-Ganz catheter is used to estimate left-heart filling pressures in critical care.
