High availability

High availability (HA) is a characteristic of a system that aims to ensure an agreed level of operational performance, usually uptime, for a higher-than-normal period. Availability is commonly expressed as a percentage of uptime in a given year, with targets such as "five nines" (99.999%), allowing only a few minutes of downtime annually.

Principles

Highly available systems are designed to eliminate single points of failure through redundancy: duplicate components take over automatically when one fails (failover), and data is replicated so that no single failure causes loss. Other principles include load balancing, health monitoring, and the ability to perform maintenance without downtime. Designers also plan for failure detection and recovery, and for correlated failures affecting supposedly independent components.

Measurement

Availability is the ratio of mean time between failures to the sum of mean time between failures and mean time to repair. Service-level agreements often specify availability targets and remedies. Related concepts include reliability, fault tolerance and disaster recovery; a system may be highly available yet still lose data, so availability is often considered together with durability and consistency.
