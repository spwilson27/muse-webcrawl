Bagad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A bagad (Breton pronunciation roughly "bah-GAHD"; plural bagadoù) is a Breton band, composed of bagpipes (Breton: binioù kozh or binioù bras, the latter closely related to the Great Highland bagpipe), bombards (Breton shawms, bombard), and a drum section including snare drums, tenor drums, and a bass drum. It is the modern ensemble form of traditional Breton music, originating in Brittany, in western France.

History

The bagad was created in the years following the Second World War, inspired in part by the Scottish pipe bands that Breton musicians encountered during the conflict. Breton cultural activist Polig Monjarret was instrumental in organizing the first ensembles and founding Bodadeg ar Sonerion (the Assembly of Breton Pipers and Bombard players) in 1943–46 era organizing efforts, which standardized teaching and repertoire. The first bagad, Bagad Koad-Serc'h, formed in the late 1940s, and the model spread rapidly across Brittany's towns and villages.

Instrumentation and performance

A typical bagad performs in concentric ranks: bombard players at the front, bagpipes behind them, and percussion at the rear. The bombard, a small double-reed instrument with a piercing tone, carries much of the melodic character of Breton dance tunes, while the pipes provide a powerful drone-based sound. Repertoire includes traditional Breton dance suites — gavottes, an dro, hanter dro, plinn — as well as marches and composed pieces written for competitions.

Competitions

Bagadoù compete in an elaborate national championship organized by Sonerion, with ensembles ranked in categories from fifth up to first category. The championship unfolds in two rounds: a winter contest (held in Brest) emphasizing traditional repertoire, and a summer contest at the Interceltic Festival of Lorient emphasizing free, creative programs. Renowned first-category ensembles include Bagad Kemper (Quimper), Bagad Roñsed-Mor (Locoal-Mendon), Bagad Cap Caval, and Bagad Brieg.

Cultural role

The bagad is a central institution of the Breton cultural revival, teaching music, the Breton language, and dance to generations of young musicians. Bagad music has crossed over into popular music through artists such as Alan Stivell, Dan Ar Braz, and the rock band Tri Yann, and bagadoù regularly perform at festivals throughout the Celtic world.
