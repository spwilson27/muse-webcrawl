Joseph I of Portugal

Joseph I (Portuguese: José I; 6 June 1714 – 24 February 1777), known as the Reformer (Portuguese: o Reformador), was King of Portugal from 1750 until his death. He was the third child of King John V and Maria Anna of Austria.

Reign

Joseph's reign was dominated by his chief minister, Sebastião José de Carvalho e Melo, the Marquis of Pombal, to whom the king largely delegated government. Pombal pursued sweeping Enlightenment-inspired reforms of the economy, education, and administration, and curtailed the power of the nobility and the Church.

On 1 November 1755, Lisbon was devastated by the great 1755 Lisbon earthquake, followed by a tsunami and fires. Joseph, who had been outside the city, developed a lifelong fear of living within stone walls and thereafter resided in a complex of tents and pavilions at Ajuda. Pombal directed the reconstruction of Lisbon's downtown, the Baixa, on a rational grid plan with anti-seismic building techniques.

Távora affair

In 1758 an assassination attempt on the king led to the Távora affair, in which members of the powerful Távora family were executed and the Jesuits were implicated; the order was expelled from Portugal in 1759. Joseph was succeeded by his daughter, Maria I.
