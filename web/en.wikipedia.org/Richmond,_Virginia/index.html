Richmond, Virginia


Downtown Richmond and the James River

Richmond is the capital city of the Commonwealth of Virginia, in the United States. An independent city on the James River at the fall line, it has a population of about 230,000 within the city limits and roughly 1.3 million in its metropolitan area. Richmond was the capital of the Confederate States of America during most of the Civil War and remains a centre of government, law, finance and higher education.

History

The site, near the falls of the James and the centre of the Powhatan chiefdom, was visited by English colonists from Jamestown as early as 1607. The town was founded in 1737 by William Byrd II, who named it for the English town of Richmond (now part of London) because of the similar river view, and it was laid out by William Mayo. Richmond became Virginia's capital in 1780, during the Revolutionary War; Patrick Henry had delivered his "Give me liberty or give me death!" speech at St. John's Church in the city in 1775, and Benedict Arnold raided and burned the town in 1781. The Virginia State Capitol, designed by Thomas Jefferson, dates from 1788.

As the Confederacy's capital from 1861, Richmond was the prime target of Union campaigns; the Tredegar Iron Works made it the South's industrial heart. After the fall of Petersburg, Confederate forces evacuated on 2–3 April 1865, setting fires that destroyed much of the city; President Abraham Lincoln visited the captured capital days before his assassination.

Economy and culture

Modern Richmond hosts the Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond, major law and advertising firms, Fortune 500 companies, and Virginia Commonwealth University. Tobacco, historically dominant, has declined in relative importance. The city's Monument Avenue was long lined with Confederate statues; following protests in 2020, the city removed them, including the monumental Robert E. Lee statue in 2021. Attractions include the Virginia Museum of Fine Arts, the American Civil War Museum, and whitewater rapids on the James through the city centre.

Geography

Richmond straddles the fall line between the Piedmont and the coastal plain, built, like its English namesake, on a series of hills above its river. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers and mild winters.
