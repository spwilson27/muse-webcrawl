Hell's Gate (British Columbia)

LocationFraser Canyon, British Columbia, Canada
Coordinates49°47′N 121°27′W
RiverFraser River

Hell's Gate is an abrupt narrowing of British Columbia's Fraser River, located immediately downstream of Boston Bar in the southern Fraser Canyon. The towering rock walls of the canyon plunge toward the river, which at this point narrows dramatically to about 35 metres (115 feet) wide. Before the construction of fishways, the constriction created a torrent so powerful that it was a major obstacle to migrating salmon and to early explorers.

History

The name derives from explorer Simon Fraser's 1808 journal entry describing his harrowing passage through the canyon: "We had to pass where no human being should venture for surely we have encountered the gates of hell." Fraser and his men were forced to climb along the cliffs and use ladders and ropes fashioned with the help of local Indigenous people to pass the gorge.

Rockslide and fishways

In 1913, blasting during construction of the Canadian Northern Railway triggered a massive rockslide at Hell's Gate that partially blocked the river and devastated the Fraser River sockeye salmon runs, most famously the great run of that year. The slide created a barrier that many migrating salmon could not pass, and the runs did not fully recover for decades. Between 1944 and 1946, fishways — stepped pools built at the sides of the gorge — were constructed by the International Pacific Salmon Fisheries Commission to allow salmon to bypass the obstruction, substantially restoring passage.

Tourism

Hell's Gate is the site of the Hell's Gate Airtram, a gondola that descends across the river to a viewing platform, restaurant, and fisheries exhibit on the opposite bank. It is a popular stop on the Trans-Canada Highway through the Fraser Canyon.
