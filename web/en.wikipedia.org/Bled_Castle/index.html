Bled Castle

Bled Castle above Lake Bled, Slovenia.

Bled Castle (Slovene: Blejski grad) is a medieval castle built on a precipice above the town of Bled and Lake Bled in northwestern Slovenia. Perched roughly 130 metres above the glacial lake, it is regarded as the oldest Slovenian castle and is one of the country's most visited tourist attractions, offering panoramic views of the lake, its island church, and the Julian Alps and Karawanks.

History

The castle was first mentioned in a deed of donation in 1011, when the German emperor Henry II granted the estate of Bled to Bishop Albuin of Brixen; the oldest part of the castle, a Romanesque tower, dates from around this period. It was administered for centuries by the bishops of Brixen, usually through castellans and lessees, and was rebuilt after an earthquake in 1511 in largely the form it retains today. After secularization in the early 19th century the castle passed through private hands; following the Second World War it was converted into a museum.

The castle today

The complex consists of upper and lower courtyards with defensive walls, a Gothic chapel dedicated to St. Albuin and St. Ingenuin (rebuilt in the 16th century and decorated with frescoes), a printing workshop demonstrating the work of a Gutenberg-style press, a wine cellar, a smithy, and a museum presenting the history of the Bled region from prehistory onward. The castle hosts weddings, cultural events, and state protocol functions for Slovenia.
