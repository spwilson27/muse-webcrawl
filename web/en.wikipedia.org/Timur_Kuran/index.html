Timur Kuran (born 1954) is a Turkish-American economist and political scientist, professor at Duke University. He is best known for developing the theory of preference falsification, which explains how individuals can conceal their true preferences under social pressure, and why political revolutions therefore often take observers by surprise.

Work

Kuran's book Private Truths, Public Lies (1995) formalised preference falsification and its role in the sudden collapse of East European communism and the Iranian Revolution. In The Long Divergence (2011) he argued that institutions of Islamic law, including inheritance rules, the waqf and the absence of corporations, contributed to the economic divergence between the Middle East and Western Europe.

Background

Kuran studied at Princeton and Stanford, taught at the University of Southern California, and has been at Duke since 2007. His research ranges over institutional economics, Islamic economic history and the dynamics of public opinion.
