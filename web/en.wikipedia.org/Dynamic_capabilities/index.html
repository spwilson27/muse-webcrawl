Dynamic capabilities

In strategic management, dynamic capabilities are a firm's ability to integrate, build and reconfigure internal and external competences in order to address rapidly changing environments. The framework extends the resource-based view of the firm.

Framework

The concept was developed principally by David Teece, Gary Pisano and Amy Shuen in a seminal 1997 paper, "Dynamic Capabilities and Strategic Management." Teece later described the capabilities as resting on three activities: sensing opportunities and threats, seizing opportunities through new products and business models, and transforming the organization to maintain competitiveness.

Relation to ordinary capabilities

Dynamic capabilities are distinguished from ordinary (operational) capabilities, which concern a firm's current operations. Dynamic capabilities are higher-order routines that modify the resource base itself — through innovation, alliances, acquisitions and asset reallocation.

Criticism

Scholars have debated whether the concept is tautological — successful firms being labelled as having dynamic capabilities after the fact — and how such capabilities can be measured and built deliberately.
