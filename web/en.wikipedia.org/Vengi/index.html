Vengi

Vengi was an ancient region and later a kingdom in India, located between the Godavari and Krishna rivers in what is today the state of Andhra Pradesh. Its capital was the city of Vengi, near modern Pedavegi in the Eluru district. The region was an important centre of political power, trade, and culture in the Deccan for more than a millennium.

History

The earliest rulers of Vengi known from inscriptions were the Salankayana dynasty, who governed from roughly the 4th to the 5th century CE. They were succeeded by the Vishnukundina dynasty, under whom the region prospered and who are credited with excavating early rock-cut cave temples. In the 7th century, the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II conquered the region and installed his brother Kubja Vishnuvardhana as its ruler, founding the Eastern Chalukya line, which ruled Vengi for about five centuries.

The Eastern Chalukyas were frequently drawn into the wars between the Rashtrakutas of the Deccan and the Cholas of the Tamil country. In the late 10th and 11th centuries, close dynastic ties bound Vengi to the Chola empire; the Chalukya-Chola emperor Kulottunga I, originally a prince of Vengi, united the two realms. The region later passed through the hands of the Kakatiyas, the Eastern Gangas, the Reddi kingdom, and the Vijayanagara Empire.

Culture and language

Vengi was an important early centre of Telugu literature. The Eastern Chalukya court patronised poets, and the court poet Nannaya began the Telugu translation of the Mahabharata in the 11th century under Rajaraja Narendra. Buddhist and Jain communities also flourished in the region in earlier centuries, and the nearby sites of Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda preserve some of the finest early Indian sculpture.
