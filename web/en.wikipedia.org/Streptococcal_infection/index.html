Streptococcal infection

Streptococcal infection
ICD-10: A49.1, B95
Caused by: Streptococcus bacteria
Types: Group A, Group B

Streptococcal infections are diseases caused by bacteria of the genus Streptococcus, spherical Gram-positive organisms that often grow in chains. The most clinically important groups are Group A Streptococcus pyogenes and Group B Streptococcus agalactiae.

Group A infections

Group A streptococcus causes streptococcal pharyngitis (strep throat), scarlet fever, impetigo, cellulitis and erysipelas, as well as severe invasive diseases such as necrotising fasciitis and streptococcal toxic shock syndrome. Untreated pharyngitis can lead to post-streptococcal complications including acute rheumatic fever and glomerulonephritis.

Group B infections

Group B streptococcus colonises the gastrointestinal and genital tracts and is a leading cause of sepsis, pneumonia and meningitis in newborns, as well as infections in pregnant women and older adults.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis uses throat swabs, rapid antigen tests and culture. Most streptococcal infections remain sensitive to penicillin, the treatment of choice, with macrolides and other antibiotics used for allergic patients.
