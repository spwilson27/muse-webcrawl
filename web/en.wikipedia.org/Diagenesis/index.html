Diagenesis

Diagenesis is the set of physical and chemical processes that transform sediment into sedimentary rock after deposition and during and after burial, at temperatures and pressures below those of metamorphism. Diagenesis turns loose sand into sandstone, mud into shale, and calcareous ooze into limestone.

Processes

The main diagenetic processes are compaction, in which overlying sediment squeezes out pore water and packs grains more tightly; cementation, in which minerals such as quartz, calcite, or clays precipitate from pore fluids and bind grains together; dissolution, which can create secondary porosity; and recrystallisation and mineral replacement, including the conversion of aragonite to calcite and the dolomitisation of limestone.

Importance

Diagenesis determines the porosity and permeability of sedimentary rocks and hence their value as reservoirs for groundwater, oil, and natural gas. It also controls the maturation of organic matter into kerogen and hydrocarbons, and affects the preservation of fossils — the field of taphonomy studies the related processes affecting organisms between death and fossilisation.
