Power chord

Power chord

Notation: e.g. E5

Notes: Root and perfect fifth (often plus octave)

Instruments: Electric guitar (also bass, keyboards)

Genres: Rock, punk, heavy metal

A power chord is a colloquial name for a chord in guitar music, especially electric guitar, consisting principally of the root note and the fifth, sometimes doubled at the octave. Power chords are written with the suffix "5", as in E5. Because they omit the third, power chords are neither major nor minor, giving them an ambiguous, open sound that stays consonant even under heavy distortion.

Why distortion works

When a full major or minor chord is played through a distorting amplifier, intermodulation between its notes produces dissonant combination tones. The root and fifth of a power chord, by contrast, generate combination tones that reinforce the chord, producing the thick, aggressive sound characteristic of rock and metal.

Technique and history

Power chords are usually played in movable shapes on the low strings of the guitar, shifted up and down the neck, often with one or two fingers, and are frequently combined with palm muting. Their roots lie in blues and early rock (Link Wray's "Rumble", 1958, is often cited as a landmark), and they became foundational through the work of The Who, Black Sabbath, AC/DC, the Ramones and countless punk and metal bands.
