Michael Jeffrey Jordan (born February 17, 1963), also known by his initials MJ, is an American businessman and former professional basketball player, widely regarded as the greatest basketball player of all time. He played fifteen seasons in the National Basketball Association (NBA), winning six NBA championships with the Chicago Bulls.

Career

Jordan starred at the University of North Carolina before being drafted third overall by Chicago in 1984. His scoring, athleticism and competitiveness drove the Bulls to two championship "three-peats" (1991–1993 and 1996–1998). He briefly retired in 1993 to play minor-league baseball, returned in 1995, and finally retired after two seasons with the Washington Wizards. He also won two Olympic gold medals, in 1984 and with the 1992 "Dream Team".

Legacy and business

Jordan's endorsements, especially the Nike Air Jordan line, made him a global cultural icon and the first billionaire athlete. He was majority owner of the Charlotte Hornets from 2010 to 2023 and co-owns the 23XI Racing NASCAR team.
