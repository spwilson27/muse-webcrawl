Premiership Rugby, officially known under sponsored names including Gallagher Premiership Rugby, is an English professional rugby union competition, consisting of 10 clubs, and is the top division of the English rugby union system. Clubs qualify for Europe's two club competitions, the European Rugby Champions Cup and the EPCR Challenge Cup. The winner of the league championship is the top team after a play-off series following the regular season.

History

League rugby in England began in 1987 with the Courage League. The competition became fully professional after rugby union opened the game to professionalism in 1995; the top flight was rebranded as the Allied Dunbar Premiership and then the Zurich Premiership, and later sponsored by Guinness, Aviva and Gallagher. The league expanded to 13 clubs before financial failures — Wasps, Worcester Warriors and London Irish all collapsed during the 2022–23 season — reduced it to 10, with promotion and relegation suspended and later conditional.

Format

Each club plays the others home and away during the regular season. Four points are awarded for a win, two for a draw, with bonus points for scoring four or more tries or losing by seven points or fewer. The top four clubs qualify for semi-finals, and the winners meet in a final traditionally held at Twickenham Stadium. The league operates under a salary cap — reduced to £5 million from 2024–25 — with marquee-player exclusions.

Champions

Leicester Tigers are the most successful club with 11 titles, followed by Bath and Saracens. Other champions include Wasps, Harlequins, Northampton Saints, Exeter Chiefs and Sale Sharks. Saracens' 2019 title was followed by relegation for salary-cap breaches, from which they returned as champions in 2023.
