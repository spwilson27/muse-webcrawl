Condé Nast is a global mass media company founded in 1909 by Condé Montrose Nast and owned by Advance Publications. Its headquarters are at One World Trade Center in the Financial District of New York City. The company publishes some of the world's best-known magazine brands, including Vogue, The New Yorker, Vanity Fair, GQ, Wired, Bon Appétit, Architectural Digest, Allure, Glamour, Condé Nast Traveler, and Tatler, in numerous international editions.

History

Condé Nast built the business by purchasing Vogue in 1909 and developing it into a leading fashion authority, then acquiring and launching titles aimed at affluent readers, an audience attractive to advertisers. The Newhouse family purchased the company in 1959, and under S. I. Newhouse Jr. it expanded aggressively, buying The New Yorker in 1985 and reviving Vanity Fair. In the 2010s the company consolidated operations at One World Trade Center, folded or digitised some print titles such as Teen Vogue's print edition, and restructured its international licensing and editorial operations under global leadership, including Anna Wintour's role as global chief content officer.

Business

Condé Nast operates in more than 30 markets, reaching consumers through print magazines, websites, video, podcasts, and events. Like other legacy publishers it has shifted toward digital subscriptions, e-commerce, and video entertainment, including Condé Nast Entertainment, which develops film and television projects based on its brands and journalism.
