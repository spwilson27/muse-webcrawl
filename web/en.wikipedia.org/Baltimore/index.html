Baltimore is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Maryland. With a population of 585,708 at the 2020 census, it is the 30th-most populous city in the United States. Baltimore is an independent city, not part of any county. The city was founded in 1729 and named for the proprietary governor of the Province of Maryland, Cecilius Calvert, 2nd Baron Baltimore.

History

Baltimore grew as a major seaport on the Patapsco River, an arm of Chesapeake Bay. During the War of 1812, the British attack on Fort McHenry in 1814 inspired Francis Scott Key to write "The Star-Spangled Banner," which became the national anthem of the United States. The Baltimore and Ohio Railroad, the first common-carrier railroad in the United States, was chartered in 1827 and built westward from the city. The Great Baltimore Fire of 1904 destroyed much of the downtown.

Economy and culture

Today the economy centres on services, health care, and education; Johns Hopkins University and its hospital are the city's largest employers. The Port of Baltimore is one of the busiest on the U.S. East Coast. Attractions include the Inner Harbor redevelopment, the National Aquarium, Fort McHenry National Monument, and Oriole Park at Camden Yards, home of the Baltimore Orioles; the Baltimore Ravens play in the NFL. The city has also faced long-standing challenges with deindustrialisation, population decline, and crime.
