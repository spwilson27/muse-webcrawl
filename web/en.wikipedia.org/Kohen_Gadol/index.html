Kohen Gadol

The Kohen Gadol (Hebrew: כֹּהֵן גָּדוֹל, "High Priest") was the chief priest of ancient Israel, the supreme religious figure of the Tabernacle and later the Temple in Jerusalem, and the only person permitted to enter the Holy of Holies, on Yom Kippur.

Role and duties

The High Priest, a descendant of Aaron, conducted the Temple service, offered the daily and festival sacrifices, and on the Day of Atonement performed the atonement rites for the nation, pronouncing the Tetragrammaton in the Holy of Holies. He wore distinctive vestments, including the breastplate (hoshen) bearing twelve stones for the tribes of Israel and the golden frontlet (tzitz).

History

The office was hereditary in the line of Zadok for much of the First Temple period; in the Second Temple period appointment became politicized under Hellenistic and Roman rulers. The office ended with the destruction of the Temple in 70 CE.
