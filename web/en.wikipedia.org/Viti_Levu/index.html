Viti Levu

Viti Levu (pronounced [ˈβitʃi ˈleβu]; "Great Fiji") is the largest island in the Republic of Fiji. It is the site of the country's capital and largest city, Suva, and home to a large majority of Fiji's population — roughly 600,000 of the national total. The island measures about 146 kilometres east to west and 106 kilometres north to south, with an area of 10,388 km², making it Fiji's dominant landmass.

Geography

The island is of volcanic origin and is divided by a mountain range running north to south; its highest point is Mount Tomanivi (formerly Mount Victoria) at 1,324 metres. The eastern side receives heavy rainfall and is lush, while the western side lies in the rain shadow and has a markedly drier climate suited to sugarcane. Major rivers include the Rewa, Navua, Sigatoka, and Ba. The island is divided between the Central and Western divisions and contains the cities of Suva, Lautoka, and Nadi, site of the main international airport.

Economy

Sugarcane farming and sugar milling, centred on Lautoka and Ba, are major industries, along with tourism on the western "sun coast" and Coral Coast, gold mining at Vatukoula, and bottling of spring water. Most of Fiji's government, commerce, and transport infrastructure is on Viti Levu.

History

Archaeological evidence shows human settlement of the island for more than three thousand years. The 19th century saw powerful chiefdoms, notably Bau, whose chief Seru Epenisa Cakobau declared himself king of Fiji before ceding the islands to Britain in 1874. Suva replaced Levuka as capital in 1882.
