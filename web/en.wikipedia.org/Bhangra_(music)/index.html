Bhangra (music)

Stylistic originsPunjabi folk music; later pop, hip hop, reggae
Cultural originsPunjab; developed in Britain in the 1970s–80s
Typical instrumentsDhol, tumbi, vocals, synthesisers

Bhangra is a genre of upbeat popular music associated with the Punjabi diaspora, taking its name from the traditional harvest dance of the Punjab region of South Asia. The modern form developed in Britain in the 1970s and 1980s among immigrant communities.

Development

Traditional bhangra accompaniment centres on the double-headed dhol drum and the single-stringed tumbi. UK-based bands such as Alaap and Heera fused these with rock and disco, and in the 1990s producers blended bhangra with hip hop, house and reggae, producing international hits including "Mundian To Bach Ke" by Panjabi MC.

Cultural significance

Bhangra became a prominent expression of British Asian identity and remains popular at weddings and celebrations across the Punjabi diaspora, while the folk dance itself is recognised on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
