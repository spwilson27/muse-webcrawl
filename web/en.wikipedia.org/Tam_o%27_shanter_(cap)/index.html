Tam o' shanter (cap)

A tam o' shanter (often abbreviated to tam) is a flat woolen cap traditionally worn in Scotland, named after Tam o' Shanter, the hero of Robert Burns's 1790 poem of that name. The cap is made of wool, sits flat on the head, and is usually topped with a toorie (pom-pom) in the centre.

Description

The tam is typically wider than a beret and has a snug headband. It is associated with Scottish Highland dress and with regiments of the British Army, which adopted the Tam O'Shanter as military headgear during the First World War.

The poem

Burns's poem tells of Tam, a farmer who, riding home drunk from Ayr, encounters a witches' sabbath at Alloway Kirk and barely escapes, his mare losing her tail to the witch Nannie.
