Strict settlement
Field: English land law
Period: c. 1650–1925

A strict settlement was a form of legal arrangement for landed estates in England and Wales, developed in the mid-17th century, under which land was held in trust so that it descended intact to a single heir — typically the eldest son — for successive generations.

Mechanism

On a marriage or an heir's coming of age, the estate was resettled: the current owner took a life interest, with remainder to his unborn eldest son in tail, while younger children and widows received portions charged on the land. This prevented any one generation from selling or mortgaging the family estate outright.

Effects and abolition

Strict settlements concentrated great estates in aristocratic families and shaped English landownership for centuries, though they also encouraged debt and rigidity. The Settled Land Acts and the 1925 property legislation effectively ended the system.
