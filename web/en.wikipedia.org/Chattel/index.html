Chattel

Movable personal property — furniture, goods, livestock — constitutes chattels.
FieldProperty law
SynonymPersonal property, personalty
OppositeReal property (land and fixtures)
EtymologyOld French chatel, from Latin capitale; cognate with "cattle"

In common law, a chattel is any tangible movable property — personal property that is not land or attached to land. The term (pronounced "chat-el") derives from the Old French chatel, property, ultimately from Latin capitale, and is cognate with "cattle," reflecting the era when livestock was a principal form of movable wealth.

Categories

The law historically divides property into real property (land and interests in land, "realty") and personal property or "personalty," of which chattels are the tangible part. Lawyers distinguish:

  • Chattels real — interests in land that are nonetheless treated as personalty, classically leasehold estates (a term of years in land). The oxymoronic name reflects their hybrid nature: real in subject matter, personal in the remedies and devolution rules applied to them.
  • Chattels personal — movable tangible things: furniture, vehicles, clothing, livestock, and other goods, as distinct from choses in action (intangible rights such as debts and shares, enforceable only by action).

Legal significance

The classification matters for wills (a bequest of "chattels" or "household chattels" has a settled construction), taxation, seizure in execution of judgments, secured lending (chattel mortgages and chattel paper under the U.S. Uniform Commercial Code), and the distinction between fixtures — chattels so annexed to land (by attachment and purpose) that they become part of the realty — and items that remain chattels, such as a tenant's trade fixtures.

Chattel slavery

The word acquired its darkest association in chattel slavery, the system — most fully developed in the Atlantic slave trade and the Americas — under which enslaved persons were legally classified as chattel property: movable, saleable, and heritable, with their children automatically enslaved. The term is used to distinguish this absolute property regime from other forms of slavery and servitude in history.
