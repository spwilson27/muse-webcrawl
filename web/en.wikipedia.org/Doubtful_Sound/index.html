Doubtful Sound

Waterfalls in Doubtful Sound, Fiordland.

Doubtful Sound / Patea is a fiord in Fiordland, in the far south-west of New Zealand's South Island. It is the second largest and among the deepest of the fiords in Fiordland National Park, after Dusky Sound. Despite its name it is technically a fiord rather than a sound, having been carved by glaciers.

History

Captain James Cook named it "Doubtful Harbour" in 1770, being doubtful whether it was navigable under sail. Whalers and sealers later called it Doubtful Sound. The fiord's Māori name, Patea, was formally added in a dual naming. In the late 18th and early 19th centuries Spanish explorers under Alessandro Malaspina visited and named features such as Bauza Island.

Geography and wildlife

Doubtful Sound is about 40 kilometres long and is fed by several arms, including Deep Cove, Hall Arm and Crooked Arm. Heavy rainfall—among the highest in New Zealand—creates hundreds of temporary waterfalls and a distinctive brackish surface layer that supports black coral trees and a population of bottlenose dolphins. Access is limited, typically via Lake Manapouri and the Wilmot Pass road, making it quieter than the more famous Milford Sound.
