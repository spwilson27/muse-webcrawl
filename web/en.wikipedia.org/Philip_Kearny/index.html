Philip Kearny

Philip Kearny Jr. (June 2, 1815 – September 1, 1862) was a United States Army officer, notable for his leadership in the Mexican–American War and the American Civil War, in which he was killed in action.

Early career

Born in New York City into a wealthy family, Kearny studied cavalry tactics in France with the Chasseurs d'Afrique and fought in Algeria. In the Mexican–American War he lost his left arm at the Battle of Churubusco in 1847.

Civil War

Appointed a Union brigadier general and later major general, he commanded a division in the Army of the Potomac during the Peninsula Campaign and the Second Battle of Bull Run. He introduced the "Kearny patch", a red cloth insignia that was a precursor of Union corps badges. Kearny was shot and killed at the Battle of Chantilly on September 1, 1862.
