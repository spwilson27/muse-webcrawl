Hugo Award for Best Novel

The Hugo Award for Best Novel is one of the Hugo Awards given each year by the World Science Fiction Society for the best science fiction or fantasy novel of the previous year published in English. First awarded in 1953, it is considered one of the most prestigious honours in the genre, alongside the Nebula Award. Winners are selected by members of the annual Worldcon convention.

History

The first winner was The Demolished Man by Alfred Bester (1953). Subsequent winners include Fritz Leiber's The Big Time, Walter M. Miller Jr.'s A Canticle for Leibowitz, Frank Herbert's Dune, Ursula K. Le Guin's The Left Hand of Darkness and The Dispossessed, and William Gibson's Neuromancer. Some years produced ties, and no award was given in 1954 and 1957 (when the category was not included).

Multiple winners

N. K. Jemisin won three consecutive awards (2016–2018) for the Broken Earth trilogy, the first author to win for all three books of a trilogy. Other repeat winners include Lois McMaster Bujold, Connie Willis, Vernor Vinge, Kim Stanley Robinson, and Arkady Martine. "Retro Hugo" awards have also been presented for years before 1953 in which no Hugos were given.
