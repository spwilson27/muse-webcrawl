Chrétien de Troyes

Bornc. 1135, Troyes, France
Diedc. 1185
OccupationPoet
Known forArthurian romances

Chrétien de Troyes (c. 1135 – c. 1185) was a French poet and trouvère who flourished in the late 12th century and is regarded as the inventor of the Arthurian romance. Writing in Old French for the courts of Marie de Champagne and Philip of Flanders, he introduced enduring elements of the Arthurian legend, including Lancelot, the Holy Grail, and Camelot.

Works

His five surviving romances are Erec and Enide, Cligès, Yvain, the Knight of the Lion, Lancelot, the Knight of the Cart, and Perceval, the Story of the Grail — the last two left unfinished. Lancelot first presents the adulterous love of Lancelot and Guinevere, while Perceval contains the earliest known mention of the Grail in literature.

Influence

Chrétien's blending of chivalric adventure, courtly love, and moral and psychological conflict shaped medieval romance across Europe, influencing German, Welsh, English, and Norse retellings of Arthurian material for centuries.
