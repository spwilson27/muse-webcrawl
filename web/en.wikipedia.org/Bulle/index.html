Bulle is a municipality in the district of Gruyère in the canton of Fribourg in Switzerland. In January 2006 Bulle incorporated the formerly independent municipality of La Tour-de-Trême. It is the second-largest town in the canton after Fribourg and the economic center of the Gruyère region.

History

Bulle is first mentioned in the 9th century as Bulo. From the 13th century it belonged to the bishops of Lausanne, whose bailiffs resided in the castle, and it developed as the market town of the Gruyère valley. In 1536 it passed with the region under the rule of Fribourg. A great fire in 1805 destroyed much of the old town, which was rebuilt in its present form.

Economy

Historically known for cattle markets and the trade in Gruyère cheese, Bulle today hosts food processing, wood, pharmaceutical, and machinery industries, and serves as a commercial hub for the southern part of the canton.

Sights and transport

The Musée Gruérien presents the region's history and folk art. The town is a rail junction on the metre-gauge lines of the Transports publics Fribourgeois and has an interchange on the A12 motorway linking Bern and Vevey. Nearby Gruyères, with its medieval castle, is one of Switzerland's most visited villages.
