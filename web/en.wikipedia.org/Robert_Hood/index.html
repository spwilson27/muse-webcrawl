Robert Hood (born 1965) is an American electronic music producer and DJ from Detroit, Michigan. He is a founding member of the influential techno collective Underground Resistance, together with Jeff Mills and "Mad" Mike Banks, and is widely credited as the pioneer of minimal techno.

Career

Hood began his career in the early 1990s, initially as the MC of Underground Resistance before turning to production. After leaving the group, he founded the M-Plant label in 1994 to release his own work. His album Minimal Nation (1994, Axis) and the single "Minus" defined a stripped-down, repetitive aesthetic built from a handful of machine elements—basslines, hi-hats, and chords—that transformed techno and influenced a generation of producers in Detroit, Berlin, and beyond. The follow-up Internal Empire (1994, Tresor) remains a landmark.

Under his alias Floorplan, initially launched in the late 1990s and revived with his daughter Lyric Hood, Hood has produced gospel- and disco-inflected house and techno, including the widely played track "Never Grow Old" and the album Paradise (2013). His later solo work includes the dystopia-inspired trilogy for Dekmantel and the LP Mirror Man (2020). An ordained minister, Hood often speaks about the role of faith in his music.
