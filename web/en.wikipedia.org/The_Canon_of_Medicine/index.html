The Canon of Medicine (Arabic: Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb, "The Law in Medicine") is an encyclopedia of medicine in five books written by the Persian physician-philosopher Avicenna (Ibn Sina) and completed in 1025. It remained a standard medical textbook in Europe and the Islamic world until the 18th century.

Contents

The work systematises the medical knowledge of its time, drawing on Greek, Roman, Persian, and Indian sources. Its five books cover general principles of medicine, materia medica (some 800 drugs), diseases of individual organs, systemic conditions such as fevers, and compound remedies. It describes the contagious nature of tuberculosis, the spread of disease through water and soil, and includes early discussions of quarantine and clinical trials.

Influence

Translated into Latin in the 12th century, the Canon was used in European universities including Montpellier and Leuven for centuries, and is regarded as one of the most famous books in the history of medicine.
