Mexican muralism

Mexican muralism was the promotion of mural painting in Mexico, generally with social and political messages, as part of efforts to reunify the country under the post-revolutionary government. From the 1920s to about 1970, large public murals were painted on government buildings and schools, depicting Mexican history, indigenous heritage and revolutionary ideals.

Leading artists

The movement's central figures, known as Los Tres Grandes ("the Big Three"), were Diego Rivera, David Alfaro Siqueiros and José Clemente Orozco. Rivera's cycles at the National Palace and the Secretariat of Public Education, Orozco's murals in Guadalajara, and Siqueiros's experimental techniques influenced public art worldwide, including the United States' WPA murals.

Legacy

Mexican muralism established the modern tradition of politically engaged public art and inspired later movements such as the Chicano mural movement in the southwestern United States.
