Franklin Knight Lane

Franklin Knight Lane (1864–1921), US Secretary of the Interior under Woodrow Wilson.

Franklin Knight Lane (15 July 1864 – 18 May 1921) was an American progressive politician from California who served as United States Secretary of the Interior from 1913 to 1920 in the administration of President Woodrow Wilson.

Life and career

Lane was born on Prince Edward Island, Canada, and raised in California, where he worked as a journalist and lawyer in San Francisco. He ran unsuccessfully for mayor of San Francisco and for governor of California, but built a national reputation as a reformer. In 1906 President Theodore Roosevelt appointed him to the Interstate Commerce Commission, where he served until Wilson made him Secretary of the Interior in 1913.

As secretary, Lane oversaw the public lands, reclamation projects, and the nation's expanding national parks; the National Park Service was created in 1916 during his tenure, and Lane's 1918 policy letter to the Service's first director, Stephen Mather, set out the basic principles that still govern the parks. He supported conservation and development in balance, and after leaving office in 1920 he wrote letters, posthumously published as The Letters of Franklin K. Lane, that became a classic statement of American progressive ideals. The letters include his famous "Makers of the Flag" address, delivered each Flag Day.
