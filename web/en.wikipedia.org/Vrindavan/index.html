Vrindavan

Vrindavan is a historic city in the Mathura district of Uttar Pradesh, India. Located on the banks of the Yamuna River about 15 km north of Mathura, it is one of the most important pilgrimage centres in the Braj region and is sacred in Hinduism as the place where the deity Krishna spent his childhood.

Religious significance

Vrindavan is associated with Krishna's youthful pastimes (lila) with Radha and the gopis. It contains thousands of temples, including the Banke Bihari Temple, ISKCON's Krishna Balaram Mandir, Radha Raman Temple, Prem Mandir and the historic Govind Dev Temple built in 1590. The 16th-century saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is credited with rediscovering many of the lost holy sites of the area.

Festivals

The city draws millions of pilgrims, especially during Janmashtami and Holi, which is celebrated in distinctive local forms such as Lathmar Holi in nearby Barsana and the Widows' Holi. Vrindavan is also known as a refuge for widows, thousands of whom live there in ashrams.
