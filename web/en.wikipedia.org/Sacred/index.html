Sacred

Sacred describes something that is dedicated or set apart for the service or worship of a deity, or considered worthy of spiritual respect or devotion. The concept of the sacred has been extensively studied in religious studies and sociology, often in contrast with the profane (mundane, ordinary).

Etymology and concepts

The English word derives from the Latin sacer, meaning "set apart" or "holy". French sociologist Émile Durkheim, in The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life (1912), treated the sacred/profane dichotomy as the central characteristic of religion: sacred things are those protected and isolated by prohibitions, while profane things are those to which the prohibitions apply in ordinary life.

In various traditions

Different traditions designate sacred places (temples, churches, mosques, shrines, sacred groves), sacred texts (scriptures), sacred times (festivals and holy days), sacred objects (relics, icons) and sacred persons (priests, saints). Anthropologist Rudolf Otto described the experience of the sacred as the numinous — a mystery that is both terrifying and fascinating (mysterium tremendum et fascinans).
