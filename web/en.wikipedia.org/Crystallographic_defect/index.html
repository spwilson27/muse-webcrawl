Crystallographic defect

A crystallographic defect is an interruption of the regular patterns of arrangement of atoms or molecules in a crystalline solid. Defects profoundly influence the mechanical, electrical, optical and thermal properties of materials, and are deliberately engineered in semiconductors and alloys.

Classification

Point defects (zero-dimensional) include vacancies, interstitial atoms and substitutional impurities, as well as colour centres in ionic crystals. Line defects (one-dimensional) are dislocations — edge, screw and mixed — whose motion enables plastic deformation at stresses far below the theoretical strength. Planar defects (two-dimensional) include grain boundaries, twin boundaries, stacking faults and surfaces. Volume defects include voids, precipitates and inclusions.

Significance

Doping silicon with parts-per-million impurities creates the electronic behaviour exploited in all modern electronics, while controlling dislocations and grain structure is the basis of metallurgy and materials strengthening.
