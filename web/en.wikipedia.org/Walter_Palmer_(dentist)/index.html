Walter J. Palmer (born 1955) is an American dentist and recreational big-game hunter from Bloomington, Minnesota. He gained international notoriety in July 2015 after it was reported that he had killed Cecil, a well-known male lion in Hwange National Park, Zimbabwe, during a guided hunt.

The killing of Cecil

Cecil was a 13-year-old lion fitted with a GPS collar by a University of Oxford research team studying lions in the park. According to Zimbabwean authorities, Cecil was lured from the protected area to adjoining private land, where Palmer wounded him with an arrow; the lion was tracked and killed with a rifle some 40 hours later. Palmer had paid approximately US$50,000 for the hunt.

Reaction and aftermath

The killing provoked a global outcry, intense media coverage, and a large social-media backlash; Palmer's dental practice was temporarily closed amid protests and vandalism. Zimbabwean authorities charged his professional guide, Theo Bronkhorst, but charges against Palmer were dropped in October 2015 after officials determined his hunting papers were in order. Palmer stated he had relied on his guides to ensure a legal hunt and expressed regret. The incident spurred changes to trophy-import rules and airline policies on transporting hunting trophies, and is frequently cited as a turning point in public debate over trophy hunting.
