Vin santo

Vin santo ("holy wine") is a style of Italian dessert wine traditionally made in Tuscany from dried (passito) white grapes, principally Trebbiano and Malvasia. Grapes are harvested and left to dry on straw mats or hung racks for several weeks or months to concentrate their sugars before pressing.

Production

The must is fermented and aged in small wooden barrels called caratelli, traditionally sealed and stored under the roof of a vinsantaia, where seasonal temperature variation influences slow fermentation and ageing over several years. The resulting wine ranges from very dry to extremely sweet, with amber colour and flavours of nuts, honey, dried apricot and spice.

Serving

Vin santo is famously paired with cantucci, almond biscuits from Prato, which are dipped into the wine. Recognised wines include Vin Santo del Chianti DOC and Vin Santo di Montepulciano DOC. Similar passito holy wines are made elsewhere in Italy and on the Greek island of Santorini (Vinsanto).
