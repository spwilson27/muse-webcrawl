Adjoint functors

In mathematics, specifically category theory, adjunction is a relationship that two functors may have between categories. Two functors that form an adjunction are known as adjoint functors. Adjunctions are ubiquitous in mathematics, as they capture the idea of an optimal or most efficient construction in one category solving a universal problem posed in another.

Definition

A functor F from category C to D is a left adjoint to a functor G from D to C if there is a natural bijection between morphisms F(X) → Y in D and morphisms X → G(Y) in C, for all objects X in C and Y in D. Equivalently, the adjunction can be specified by a unit natural transformation η : Id → GF and a counit ε : FG → Id satisfying the triangle identities.

Examples

The free group functor is left adjoint to the forgetful functor from groups to sets. The tensor product functor (– ⊗ A) is left adjoint to the internal hom functor hom(A, –), giving an adjunction underlying currying. In topology, the suspension functor is adjoint to the loop space functor. Galois connections between partially ordered sets are adjunctions between posetal categories.

Properties

Left adjoints preserve colimits and right adjoints preserve limits. Adjunctions induce monads, and every monad arises from an adjunction. The notion was introduced by Daniel Kan in 1958 and popularised by Saunders Mac Lane, who remarked that adjoint functors "occur everywhere".
