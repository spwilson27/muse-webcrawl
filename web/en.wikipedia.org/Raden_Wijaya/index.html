Raden Wijaya (also spelled Raden Vijaya), who reigned under the name Kertarajasa Jayawardhani, was the founder and first king of the Majapahit empire in eastern Java, reigning from 1293 until his death in 1309. His rise is one of the celebrated episodes of Javanese history, recounted in the Pararaton and the Nagarakretagama.

Founding of Majapahit

Wijaya was a prince of the Singhasari kingdom and son-in-law of its last king, Kertanagara. When Jayakatwang of Kediri rebelled and killed Kertanagara in 1292, Wijaya fled to Madura, gathered support, and was granted land in the Tarik forest on the Brantas delta, where he founded a settlement said to be named after the bitter maja fruit — Majapahit.

In 1293 a Mongol expedition sent by Kublai Khan landed in Java to punish Kertanagara's earlier defiance. Wijaya allied with the Mongol forces to destroy Jayakatwang, then turned on his allies, ambushing and driving them from Java before they could install him as a vassal. On 10 November 1293 — commemorated as the founding date of Majapahit — he was crowned king.

Reign

Wijaya's reign was troubled by revolts of his own comrades-in-arms, including Rangga Lawe (1295), Lembu Sora (1296/1300) and Nambi (1316, after his death), as dramatised in later chronicles. He married four daughters of Kertanagara, including the chief queen Gayatri (Rajapatni). He died in 1309, was cremated and enshrined, and was succeeded by his son Jayanegara. Under his grandson-in-law Gajah Mada and King Hayam Wuruk, Majapahit grew into the largest empire in Southeast Asian history.
