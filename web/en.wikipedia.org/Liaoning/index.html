Liaoning

Liaoning is a coastal province in Northeast China that is also known as Fengtian and is the smallest, southernmost and most populous of the region's three provinces. Its capital and largest city is Shenyang, and other major cities include Dalian, Anshan and Fushun. Liaoning borders the Yellow Sea (Korea Bay) and Bohai Sea to the south, North Korea to the southeast, Jilin to the northeast, Hebei to the southwest, and Inner Mongolia to the northwest.

Capital: Shenyang
Area: 148,600 km²
Population: c. 42 million (2020)
Country: China

History

The province's name derives from the Liao River, its main waterway. Liaoning was a contested frontier between Chinese dynasties and peoples of Manchuria; the Manchu Qing dynasty originated in the region. In the early twentieth century the area, as Fengtian province, was a focus of rivalry between Russia and Japan and the setting of the Mukden Incident of 1931. It became one of China's first industrial bases under the People's Republic, centred on steel, machinery and petrochemicals, and in recent decades has been part of the "Revitalize the Northeast" programme.

Geography and economy

The terrain is higher in the east and west with the Liao River plain in the middle. Liaoning remains a major centre of heavy industry, shipbuilding (at Dalian) and equipment manufacturing, with a large port complex on the Bohai rim.
