National Collegiate Athletic Association

The National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) is a nonprofit organization that regulates student athletics among about 1,100 schools in the United States, Canada, and Puerto Rico. It organizes the athletic programs of colleges and helps over 500,000 college student-athletes compete annually.

History

The NCAA was founded in 1906 as the Intercollegiate Athletic Association of the United States, partly in response to injuries and deaths in college football. It adopted its current name in 1910. Walter Byers served as its first full-time executive director from 1951 to 1988, a period of rapid growth of televised sports.

Structure

Member schools are divided into three divisions based on size and resources. Division I schools, the largest, award athletic scholarships and generate most revenue, especially through the men's basketball tournament known as March Madness and football bowl games (though top-level football championships are run outside the NCAA).

Eligibility and controversy

For decades the NCAA enforced strict amateurism rules. Court cases including O'Bannon v. NCAA and NCAA v. Alston (2021), and state name, image, and likeness (NIL) laws, have transformed compensation for athletes in the 2020s.
