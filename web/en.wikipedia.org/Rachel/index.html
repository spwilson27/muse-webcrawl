Rachel

Rachel
Hebrew: רָחֵל ("ewe")
Spouse: Jacob
Children: Joseph and Benjamin
Father: Laban
Burial: Rachel's Tomb, near Bethlehem

Rachel (Hebrew: רָחֵל, "ewe") is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the beloved wife of the patriarch Jacob and the mother of Joseph and Benjamin, two of the twelve tribes of Israel. She was the younger daughter of Laban, and the sister of Jacob's other wife, Leah.

Biblical narrative

According to Genesis, Jacob fell in love with Rachel at a well in Haran and agreed to work seven years for Laban for her hand — years that "seemed unto him but a few days, for the love he had to her". Laban deceived him by substituting the veiled Leah at the wedding, and Jacob worked seven further years for Rachel. Long barren while Leah bore children, Rachel finally gave birth to Joseph. She later stole her father's household gods (teraphim) during the family's flight, hiding them by sitting on them.

Death and tomb

Rachel died giving birth to Benjamin on the road near Bethlehem, where Jacob buried her and set up a pillar. Rachel's Tomb, just north of Bethlehem, has been venerated by Jews, Christians and Muslims for centuries. In the Book of Jeremiah, Rachel is pictured weeping for her exiled descendants — an image quoted in the Gospel of Matthew in connection with the Massacre of the Innocents — and in Jewish tradition she is one of the four matriarchs.
