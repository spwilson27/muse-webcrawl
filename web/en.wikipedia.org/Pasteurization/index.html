Pasteurization

InventorLouis Pasteur (1864)
PurposeKill pathogenic microorganisms in food and drink
Common methodsHTST (flash), UHT, vat (batch)
Typical productsMilk, juice, eggs, beer, wine, canned food

Pasteurization (or pasteurisation) is a process of food preservation in which packaged and unpacked foods (e.g., milk and fruit juices) are treated with mild heat, usually to less than 100 °C (212 °F), to eliminate pathogens and extend shelf life. It is named after the French microbiologist Louis Pasteur, who developed the method in the 1860s to prevent wine and beer from souring.

History

Pasteur, commissioned to investigate wine spoilage, showed that heating wine briefly to around 55–60 °C killed the microbes responsible for unwanted fermentation. The process was subsequently applied to beer and vinegar and, from the late 19th century, to milk, where it dramatically reduced transmission of tuberculosis, brucellosis, typhoid fever, and other milk-borne diseases. Commercial pasteurization of milk became widespread and in many jurisdictions legally required in the early 20th century.

Methods

In high-temperature short-time (HTST) or flash pasteurization, milk is heated to about 72 °C (162 °F) for at least 15 seconds. Ultra-high-temperature (UHT) processing heats milk to around 135 °C (275 °F) for a few seconds, permitting months of unrefrigerated storage in aseptic packaging. Older vat (batch) pasteurization holds milk at 63 °C (145 °F) for 30 minutes. Pasteurization is not sterilization: it kills most vegetative pathogens but not bacterial spores, so pasteurized products generally still require refrigeration.

Regulation and debate

Most countries require commercial milk to be pasteurized, though raw milk is legally sold in some places, provoking public-health debate; health authorities warn that raw milk carries a risk of listeriosis, E. coli, and other infections. Beyond milk, pasteurization is applied to juices, liquid eggs, almonds, and many other products, sometimes by non-thermal means such as high-pressure processing or irradiation.
