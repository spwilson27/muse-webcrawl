County of Flanders

The County of Flanders was a historic territory in the Low Countries, one of the most powerful political entities in medieval Europe, centred on Bruges, Ghent and Ypres and roughly corresponding to modern Belgian East and West Flanders, French Nord, and Dutch Zeelandic Flanders.

History

Established in the 9th century under Baldwin I as a march of West Francia, its counts built a wealthy cloth-trading region. Flanders passed through Burgundian, Habsburg Spanish and Austrian rule, saw the Battle of the Golden Spurs in 1302, and was divided after the Peace of Utrecht and the French Revolutionary Wars. The countship was abolished in 1795.
