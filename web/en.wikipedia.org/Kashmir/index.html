Kashmir

Kashmir is the northernmost geographical region of the Indian subcontinent, centred on the Kashmir Valley in the western Himalayas. Since the partition of British India in 1947, the territory has been disputed between India, Pakistan, and China, which administer different portions of the former princely state of Jammu and Kashmir.

Geography

The region encompasses the fertile, densely populated Kashmir Valley, ringed by the Pir Panjal and Great Himalayan ranges; the mountainous areas of Gilgit-Baltistan; the Jammu plains; the high-altitude desert of Ladakh; and the Aksai Chin plateau. Major rivers include the Indus, Jhelum, and Chenab, whose waters are shared under the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960.

History

Kashmir was a centre of Hindu and Buddhist scholarship in ancient times and later of Persianate Islamic culture under sultans and the Mughals. In 1846, after the First Anglo-Sikh War, the British sold the region to Gulab Singh, founder of the Dogra dynasty of Jammu and Kashmir. At partition in 1947, the Hindu maharaja's decision to accede to India amid invasion by tribal forces triggered the first Indo-Pakistani war; a UN-brokered ceasefire left the territory divided along what became the Line of Control.

The dispute

India and Pakistan have fought further wars over Kashmir, in 1965 and 1999, and an insurgency in Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir began in the late 1980s. In August 2019 the Indian government revoked the region's special constitutional status and reorganised it into the union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. Pakistan administers Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan, while China controls Aksai Chin and the Trans-Karakoram Tract. The dispute remains one of the most militarised in the world.
