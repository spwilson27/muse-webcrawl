Drop kick

A drop kick is a type of kick in various codes of football in which a player drops the ball and kicks it as it touches the ground, or just after it bounces. It contrasts with the punt, in which the ball is kicked before it touches the ground, and the place kick, in which the ball is held stationary on the ground or a tee.

Rugby

In rugby union, the drop kick is used for drop goals (worth three points), for kick-offs and restarts, and for certain conversions in the past. A drop goal — kicking the ball through the posts from open play — has decided famous matches, including World Cup finals. In rugby league, drop goals are worth one point (two points from beyond the 40-metre line in the NRL) and drop kicks are used for goal-line drop-outs.

American and Canadian football

The drop kick was once a standard method of scoring field goals and extra points in gridiron football, but it was largely displaced by the place kick once the ball became more pointed and harder to bounce predictably. It remains legal: Doug Flutie famously converted a drop-kick extra point for the New England Patriots in 2006, the first in the NFL since 1941.

Other codes

In Australian rules football the drop kick was a common kicking style for much of the game's history before being supplanted by the drop punt. In association football, the technique survives in the half-volley and in the goalkeeper's drop-kick distribution from the hands.
