Canada Place is a landmark building situated on the Burrard Inlet waterfront of Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. It is home to the Vancouver Convention Centre's East Building, the Pan Pacific Vancouver Hotel, the World Trade Centre Vancouver, and Vancouver's main cruise ship terminal. Its distinctive roof, a series of five white fabric sails, has become one of the most recognizable features of the Vancouver skyline.

History

Canada Place was constructed on the site of the former Pier B–C of the Canadian Pacific Railway. It was built as the Canada Pavilion for Expo 86, the world's fair held in Vancouver in 1986, and opened on May 2, 1986, in a ceremony attended by Prince Charles and Diana, Princess of Wales. The structure extends into Burrard Inlet on a foundation of concrete piles.

The building's sails are made of fibreglass Teflon-coated fabric stretched over steel frames. At night the sails are illuminated, with colours changed to mark special occasions and events.

Uses

The cruise ship terminal at Canada Place is one of Canada's busiest, serving as the home port for Alaska cruises and handling hundreds of thousands of passengers annually. The complex also hosts the FlyOver Canada flight-simulation attraction, and its outdoor promenade, the Canadian Trail, displays the names of Canadian places along a walkway styled with the provinces and territories.

Every Canada Day, Canada Place hosts the city's principal celebrations, and a pair of heritage horns atop the roof—the "Heritage Horns"—sound the first four notes of "O Canada" at noon daily.
