Tympanum (architecture)

The Last Judgment tympanum at Conques Abbey
Architectural elementDecorative panel above a doorway
Common inRomanesque and Gothic churches, classical temples

In architecture, a tympanum (plural: tympana or tympanums) is the semi-circular or triangular decorative wall surface over an entrance, door or window, which is bounded by a lintel and an arch. It often contains pedimental sculpture or other ornamentation.

In ancient Greek and Roman architecture, the tympanum is the triangular area within a pediment, often filled with sculpture. In medieval Romanesque and Gothic churches, the tympanum above the main portal became one of the principal fields for stone sculpture, frequently depicting Christ in Majesty or the Last Judgment, as in the celebrated tympana at Vézelay, Conques, and Autun.

The tympanum may also simply be the surface enclosed by a double door, or in modern architecture the space between an arch and the rectangular opening below it.
