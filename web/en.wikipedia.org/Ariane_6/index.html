Ariane 6 is a European expendable launch system developed and manufactured by ArianeGroup for the European Space Agency (ESA) and operated by Arianespace. It succeeded the Ariane 5 as Europe's heavy-lift launcher, with a design intended to lower launch costs and allow greater flexibility for institutional and commercial missions.

Design

Ariane 6 uses a cryogenic core stage powered by the Vulcain 2.1 engine (a simplified derivative of Ariane 5's Vulcain 2) burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen, an upper stage powered by the reignitable Vinci engine, and solid rocket boosters (P120C, shared with Vega C) — two for the Ariane 62 version and four for the Ariane 64. The P120C is among the largest single-piece solid rocket motors ever flown.

Development and first flight

Approved by ESA member states in 2014, the programme suffered several years of delays. The inaugural flight took place on 9 July 2024 from the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou, French Guiana, launching the Ariane 62 variant; the main objectives were achieved, though the upper stage's final auxiliary propulsion unit anomaly prevented a planned controlled deorbit demonstration. The first Ariane 64 flight and regular commercial service (including launches for Amazon's Project Kuiper constellation) followed.
