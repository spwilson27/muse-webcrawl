Soufrière Hills


Pyroclastic flows from Soufrière Hills

The Soufrière Hills are an active complex of volcanic lava domes forming an andesitic stratovolcano on the Caribbean island of Montserrat, a British Overseas Territory. After centuries of dormancy, the volcano reawakened in 1995, and its eruption devastated the southern half of the island, destroying the capital city, Plymouth, and causing the evacuation of most of the population.

The 1995 eruption

Activity began on 18 July 1995 with phreatic explosions and earthquakes. A lava dome grew in the summit crater, periodically collapsing to produce pyroclastic flows. Plymouth was evacuated in 1995 and 1996, and on 25 June 1997 pyroclastic flows killed 19 people in abandoned farming settlements. In August 1997 flows destroyed central Plymouth, burying much of the city under metres of ash and debris; the island's airport, W. H. Bramble Airport, was also destroyed.

Consequences

An exclusion zone covering the southern two-thirds of Montserrat remains in force. The island's population fell from about 12,000 before the eruption to around 5,000, with many residents resettling in the United Kingdom and elsewhere. Government operations moved to Brades in the north, and a new capital is under development at Little Bay. The Montserrat Volcano Observatory monitors the volcano continuously.

Later activity

Dome growth continued in episodes through the 2000s, with major dome collapses in 2003 and 2006 and an explosive event in February 2010 that generated flows reaching the sea. Activity has since declined, though the volcano remains active and closely monitored.
