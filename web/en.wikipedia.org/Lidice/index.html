Lidice

Lidice is a village in the Central Bohemian Region of the Czech Republic, northwest of Prague. It became an international symbol of Nazi brutality after it was completely destroyed in June 1942 in reprisal for the assassination of Reinhard Heydrich, the acting Reich Protector of Bohemia and Moravia.

The massacre

Heydrich was attacked in Prague on 27 May 1942 by Czechoslovak agents trained in Britain (Operation Anthropoid) and died of his wounds on 4 June. Hitler ordered savage reprisals. On flimsy pretexts linking Lidice to the assassins, German forces surrounded the village on the night of 9–10 June 1942. All 173 men and boys over 15 were shot. The women were deported to Ravensbrück concentration camp, where many died; most of the village's 98 children were murdered by gassing at Chełmno, while a few deemed suitable for "Germanisation" were given to SS families. The buildings were burned and dynamited and the site levelled; even the cemetery was desecrated.

Memory and rebuilding

News of the destruction provoked worldwide outrage. Towns in several countries adopted the name Lidice, including communities in the United States, Mexico, and Brazil, and "Lidice Shall Live" campaigns raised funds so the name would not be erased. After the war, surviving women and children returned, and a new village was built beside the old site from 1947. The original grounds are preserved as a national memorial, including a celebrated 1980s bronze monument depicting the 82 local children who were murdered.
