Perception

Perception is the organisation, identification, and interpretation of sensory information in order to represent and understand the environment. It involves signals from the sense organs that are actively processed by the nervous system, shaped both by the incoming (bottom-up) data and by the perceiver's prior knowledge, expectations, and attention (top-down processing).

Process

Perception begins with transduction of physical energy — light, sound waves, pressure, chemicals — into neural signals. The brain then groups and interprets these signals; Gestalt psychology identified principles such as proximity, similarity, and figure–ground segregation by which the mind organises scenes into objects. Perceptual constancies allow objects to be recognised as stable despite changing retinal images, and illusions reveal the inferential shortcuts the system takes.

Modalities and study

Beyond the five classical senses, perception includes proprioception, balance (vestibular sense), pain, temperature, time, and internal states. Perception is studied across psychology, neuroscience, philosophy (where direct and indirect realism debate how perception relates to the external world), and artificial intelligence and robotics (machine perception).
