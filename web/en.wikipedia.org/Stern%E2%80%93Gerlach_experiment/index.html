Stern–Gerlach experiment

Stern–Gerlach experiment
Performed: 1922, Frankfurt
By: Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach
Demonstrated: Space quantisation of angular momentum; existence of spin

The Stern–Gerlach experiment, performed in 1922 by Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach, demonstrated that the angular momentum of atoms is quantised. It is one of the foundational experiments of quantum mechanics.

Setup

A beam of silver atoms was passed through an inhomogeneous magnetic field and deposited on a detector plate. If the atoms' magnetic moments could point in any direction, the beam would spread into a continuous smear. Instead it split into two discrete spots, showing that the magnetic moment — and hence angular momentum — can take only quantised values.

Significance

The result was later understood as evidence for electron spin: the two spots correspond to the two possible spin projections of the silver atom's outer electron. The experiment remains the standard illustration of measurement and quantum state selection, and sequential Stern–Gerlach devices are used in textbooks to discuss superposition and the measurement problem.
