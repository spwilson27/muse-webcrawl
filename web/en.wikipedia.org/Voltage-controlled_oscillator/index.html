Voltage-controlled oscillator

A voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO) is an electronic oscillator whose oscillation frequency is controlled by a voltage input. The applied input voltage determines the instantaneous oscillation frequency, so a varying control voltage can produce frequency modulation (FM) or phase modulation. VCOs are fundamental building blocks in radios, synthesisers, function generators, and phase-locked loops.

TypeElectronic oscillator
Control inputAnalog voltage
OutputPeriodic waveform (sine, square, or triangle)
ApplicationsPLLs, FM synthesis, radio tuning, clock generation

A VCO's output frequency varies with its control voltage.

Operation

Most VCOs vary the frequency of an LC resonant circuit using a varactor diode, whose capacitance changes with the applied reverse voltage. At microwave frequencies, YIG (yttrium iron garnet) spheres tuned by a magnetic field are sometimes used. Other designs vary the charging current of a capacitor in a relaxation oscillator, producing triangle and square waves. Important performance measures include tuning range, tuning linearity, phase noise, and pushing (sensitivity of frequency to supply voltage).

Applications

In a phase-locked loop, the VCO is driven by a feedback signal so that its output locks to a reference frequency, enabling stable frequency synthesis in radios and microprocessors. In music synthesisers, the VCO is the primary sound source: its pitch follows a control voltage, conventionally one volt per octave in analog synthesisers. VCOs are also used in frequency modulation transmitters, metal detectors, and voltage-to-frequency converters.
