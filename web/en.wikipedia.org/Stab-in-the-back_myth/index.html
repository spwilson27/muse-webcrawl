Stab-in-the-back myth
German: Dolchstoßlegende
Origin: Germany after World War I
Claim: Army was betrayed by civilians at home

The stab-in-the-back myth (German: Dolchstoßlegende) was a widely believed conspiracy theory in post-World War I Germany holding that the German Army did not lose the war on the battlefield but was betrayed by civilians on the home front — socialists, republicans and Jews.

Origins

The idea spread after the November 1918 armistice, amplified by remarks attributed to commanders such as Paul von Hindenburg, who testified in 1919 that the army had been "stabbed in the back". In reality, the army's leadership had itself pressed for an armistice amid military collapse.

Consequences

The myth poisoned Weimar Republic politics, delegitimised the democratic government that signed the peace, and was exploited heavily by the Nazi Party.
