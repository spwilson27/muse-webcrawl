Sequence

In mathematics, a sequence is an enumerated collection of objects in which repetitions are allowed and order matters. Formally, a sequence is a function whose domain is an interval of the integers — usually the natural numbers. The elements of a sequence are called its terms, and the number of terms (possibly infinite) is its length.

Examples and notation

Famous sequences include the sequence of prime numbers (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, …), the Fibonacci sequence (0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, …), arithmetic sequences with a constant difference between terms, and geometric sequences with a constant ratio. A sequence is commonly denoted (an), with an its n-th term; an explicit formula, such as an = n², or a recurrence relation may define it.

Convergence

A sequence is said to converge to a limit L if its terms eventually become and remain arbitrarily close to L; otherwise it diverges. The limit of a sequence is the central notion of mathematical analysis: real numbers can themselves be defined as equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences of rationals. Important special cases include monotone sequences, which converge if bounded, and subsequences, governed by the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem.

Other fields

  • In computer science, sequences appear as strings, lists, and streams of data.
  • In biology, a sequence refers to the ordered residues of DNA, RNA, or protein.
  • In music and film, a sequence is an ordered series of related passages or shots.
