The European Gravitational Observatory (EGO) is an international consortium founded in 2000 by the French National Centre for Scientific Research (CNRS) and the Italian National Institute for Nuclear Physics (INFN), later joined by Nikhef of the Netherlands and institutions from Poland and Hungary. EGO operates the Virgo interferometer near Cascina, in the province of Pisa, Italy, one of the world's principal detectors of gravitational waves.

Virgo

Virgo is a laser interferometer with two perpendicular arms, each 3 kilometres long, capable of measuring changes in length smaller than a thousandth of a proton's diameter. It works in collaboration with the two LIGO detectors in the United States and the KAGRA detector in Japan (the LVK collaboration). In August 2017 Virgo joined the second observing run and contributed to the first three-detector detection of gravitational waves, GW170814, and to GW170817, the first observed merger of two neutron stars, whose precise sky localisation enabled a landmark multi-messenger astronomy campaign.

Activities

Beyond operating and upgrading Virgo — including the Advanced Virgo upgrades and planned Virgo_nEXT — EGO promotes gravitational-wave research in Europe, supports outreach, and hosts scientific events at its Cascina site.
