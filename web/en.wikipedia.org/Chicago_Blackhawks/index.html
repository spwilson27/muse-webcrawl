Chicago Blackhawks

The Chicago Blackhawks are a professional ice hockey team based in Chicago. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Central Division of the Western Conference. Founded in 1926, they are one of the league's "Original Six" teams and have won the Stanley Cup six times, most recently in 2015.

History

The club was founded by Chicago businessman Frederic McLaughlin, who named it after the Black Hawk Division in which he had served during World War I, itself named for the Sauk leader Black Hawk. The Blackhawks won Stanley Cups in 1934, 1938, and 1961, the last led by stars Bobby Hull, Stan Mikita, Glenn Hall, and Pierre Pilote. A long championship drought followed, marked by declining attendance under owner William Wirtz.

Revival and dynasty

After Rocky Wirtz took over in 2007, the team rebuilt around Jonathan Toews and Patrick Kane, drafted in 2006 and 2007. Coached by Joel Quenneville, Chicago won the Stanley Cup in 2010, 2013, and 2015, establishing the closest thing to a modern NHL dynasty. The team plays its home games at the United Center, which it shares with the Chicago Bulls.

Name and identity

The team's name and Native American head logo have drawn criticism and debate, though the franchise has retained them while pledging to raise awareness of Native American history. The club shortened its written name from "Black Hawks" to the single word "Blackhawks" in 1986.
