Guru Hargobind (19 June 1595 – 3 March 1644) was the sixth of the ten Sikh Gurus. He became Guru at the age of eleven, following the execution of his father, Guru Arjan, by the Mughal emperor Jahangir. Guru Hargobind introduced the process of militarisation into Sikhism to resist persecution, symbolised by his wearing two swords — Miri and Piri — representing temporal and spiritual sovereignty.

Miri and Piri

At his succession ceremony in 1606, Guru Hargobind asked for two swords instead of the traditional wool sash, declaring that one represented spiritual authority (Piri) and the other temporal power (Miri). He also built the Akal Takht ("Throne of the Timeless One") facing the Golden Temple in Amritsar, where he held court, and maintained a standing army, horses and weaponry.

Relations with the Mughals

Guru Hargobind was imprisoned for a time in Gwalior Fort under Jahangir; his release, along with 52 Hindu princes, is commemorated by Sikhs at Bandi Chhor Divas, coinciding with Diwali. Under Shah Jahan, relations deteriorated and Sikh forces fought several battles, including Amritsar (1634), Lahira and Kartarpur, in which the Guru's troops prevailed.

Later life and succession

He spent his final years at Kiratpur Sahib, a town he founded in the Shivalik foothills. Before his death in 1644 he passed the Guruship to his grandson, Guru Har Rai.
