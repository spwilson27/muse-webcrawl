The Nasdaq (originally an acronym for the National Association of Securities Dealers Automated Quotations) is an American stock exchange based in New York City. It is the world's first electronic stock market and ranks among the largest exchanges globally by market capitalisation of its listed companies, trading more shares daily than any other U.S. exchange.

History

Nasdaq began operations in 1971 as an automated quotation system for over-the-counter stocks, replacing telephone-based price reporting. Its electronic model attracted young growth companies, particularly in technology, and it became the listing home of firms such as Apple, Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet. The exchange was spun off from the National Association of Securities Dealers in 2000–2001 and formally recognised as a national securities exchange in 2006. Operator Nasdaq, Inc. (formerly Nasdaq OMX Group) also runs Nordic and Baltic exchanges acquired through its 2008 takeover of OMX.

Market structure

Unlike the New York Stock Exchange, Nasdaq has always operated as a fully electronic, dealer-based market with no physical trading floor; its public face is the Nasdaq MarketSite at Times Square. Listings are tiered into the Global Select, Global and Capital markets. Its best-known benchmarks are the Nasdaq Composite Index and the Nasdaq-100.
