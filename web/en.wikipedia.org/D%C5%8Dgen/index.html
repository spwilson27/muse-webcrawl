Dōgen Zenji (道元禅師; 19 January 1200 – 22 September 1253), also known as Eihei Dōgen, was a Japanese Buddhist priest, philosopher, and poet who founded the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan. He is regarded as one of the most important figures in Japanese Buddhism and one of Japan's great original philosophers.

Life

Born into an aristocratic family in Kyoto and orphaned young, Dōgen was ordained on Mount Hiei in the Tendai school but became dissatisfied with its teachings, particularly over the question of how practice relates to original enlightenment. He studied with Eisai's successor Myōzen and travelled with him to China in 1223, where he studied Caodong (Sōtō) Chan under the master Rujing at Mount Tiantong. There Dōgen attained awakening — described as "dropping off body and mind" — and returned to Japan in 1227 determined to transmit "just sitting" (shikantaza) meditation. In 1243 he moved to remote Echizen Province and founded Eihei-ji, which remains one of the two head temples of Sōtō Zen.

Teachings and writings

Dōgen's masterpiece is the Shōbōgenzō, a collection of essays written over more than two decades, including famous fascicles such as "Genjōkōan", "Uji" (on being-time), and "Sansuigyō" (mountains and waters). He taught the oneness of practice and enlightenment (shushō ittō): zazen is not a means to become enlightened but the very expression of Buddha-nature. Other works include the Eihei Kōroku, the Shōbōgenzō Zuimonki recorded by his disciple Ejō, and the Tenzo Kyōkun (Instructions for the Cook), a classic of Zen monastic discipline.
