Elastic net regularization

Constraint regions: ridge (circle), lasso (diamond), and elastic net (rounded diamond)

In statistics and machine learning, the elastic net is a regularized regression method that linearly combines the L1 and L2 penalties of the lasso and ridge methods. It was introduced by Hui Zou and Trevor Hastie in 2005.

Motivation

The lasso (L1 penalty) performs variable selection by shrinking some coefficients exactly to zero, but it has drawbacks: it can select at most as many variables as there are observations, and among a group of highly correlated predictors it tends to pick one arbitrarily. Ridge regression (L2 penalty) shrinks coefficients of correlated variables toward each other but performs no selection. The elastic net was designed to overcome these limitations: it encourages a grouping effect, selecting or discarding correlated predictors together, and can select more variables than there are observations.

Formulation

The elastic net estimator minimises the residual sum of squares plus a penalty λ[α‖β‖1 + (1−α)‖β‖2²/2], where α ∈ [0, 1] controls the balance between the lasso and ridge penalties. Pure lasso corresponds to α = 1 and pure ridge to α = 0. The method is widely used for high-dimensional data such as genomic studies, and can be fitted efficiently with coordinate descent algorithms.
