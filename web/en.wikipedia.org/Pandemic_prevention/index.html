Pandemic prevention

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pandemic prevention comprises the measures taken to reduce the likelihood that an infectious disease outbreak escalates into a pandemic, or to prevent such outbreaks altogether. It is distinguished from pandemic preparedness and response, which concern managing a pandemic once it has begun, though the areas overlap. Most known pandemic threats are zoonotic — pathogens that spill over from animals to humans — so much of prevention focuses on the human–animal–environment interface, an approach known as One Health.

Strategies

  • Surveillance and early detection: monitoring of human and animal populations for unusual illness, laboratory networks, genomic sequencing of pathogens, and wastewater monitoring to detect outbreaks early, when containment is still possible.
  • Reducing spillover risk: regulating wildlife trade and live animal markets, improving biosecurity on farms, reducing deforestation and other land-use changes that bring humans into contact with wildlife reservoirs, and controlling vectors such as mosquitoes.
  • Vaccination: immunisation of human populations against epidemic-prone diseases, vaccination of livestock and, in some cases, wildlife; and maintenance of vaccine platform technologies that can be adapted rapidly to new pathogens.
  • Infection control: sanitation, clean water, hygiene, ventilation, and hospital infection-control practices that reduce transmission generally.
  • Research: cataloguing animal viruses with pandemic potential, developing broad-spectrum antivirals, and prototype vaccines for families of high-risk pathogens.

Governance

International cooperation is organised largely through the World Health Organization and the International Health Regulations (2005), which require countries to report events that may constitute a public health emergency of international concern. Bodies such as CEPI (the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations, founded in 2017) finance vaccine development for emerging diseases. The COVID-19 pandemic prompted renewed negotiation over international agreements on pandemic prevention, preparedness and response, and debate about the comparatively low cost of prevention relative to the enormous economic and human costs of a pandemic.
