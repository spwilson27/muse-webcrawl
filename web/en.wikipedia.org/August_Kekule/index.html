August Kekulé

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Friedrich August Kekulé (1829–1896)

Friedrich August Kekulé, later Friedrich August Kekule von Stradonitz (7 September 1829 – 13 July 1896), was a German organic chemist. From the 1850s until his death, Kekulé was one of the most prominent chemists in Europe, especially in the field of theoretical chemistry. He was the principal founder of the theory of chemical structure, in particular the Kekulé structure of benzene.

Life and career

Kekulé studied at the University of Giessen under Justus von Liebig, and later worked in Paris, London — where he was influenced by Alexander Williamson and Charles Gerhardt — and Heidelberg. He became professor at Ghent in 1858 and at Bonn in 1867, remaining there for the rest of his career.

Structural theory

In 1857–58 Kekulé proposed that carbon is tetravalent and can link to itself in chains, the basis of structural theory (independently proposed by Archibald Scott Couper). In 1865 he published the ring structure of benzene, with alternating double bonds. He later famously claimed the idea had come to him in a dream of a snake seizing its own tail (the ouroboros), an anecdote from his 1890 Benzolfest speech whose historicity has been debated.

Legacy

Kekulé's structural theory transformed organic chemistry, enabling the systematic design of dyes and pharmaceuticals in the German chemical industry. He was ennobled in 1895, adding "von Stradonitz" to his name.
