Throbbing Gristle

Throbbing Gristle were an English music and visual arts group formed in 1975 in Kingston upon Hull by Genesis P-Orridge, Cosey Fanni Tutti, Peter "Sleazy" Christopherson and Chris Carter. They are widely regarded as the founders of industrial music, having popularised the term through their own record label, Industrial Records, whose slogan was "industrial music for industrial people".

The group emerged from the performance art collective COUM Transmissions, whose confrontational exhibitions — most notoriously the 1976 "Prostitution" show at London's Institute of Contemporary Arts — scandalised the British press and Parliament. Throbbing Gristle carried that confrontational ethos into music, combining tape loops, custom-built electronic instruments (including Carter's sampler-like "Gristleism" devices and the later Gristleizer effects unit), distorted bass, and P-Orridge's declamatory vocals.

History

Throbbing Gristle's debut album, The Second Annual Report (1977), was followed by D.o.A: The Third and Final Report (1978) and 20 Jazz Funk Greats (1979), the latter ironically packaged with a wholesome cover photograph taken at Beachy Head, a notorious suicide spot. Heathen Earth (1980) documented a live performance before an invited audience. The group's live shows were documented on numerous cassette releases.

In 1981 the group announced that their "mission is terminated" and split. Members went on to form Psychic TV, Chris & Cosey, and Coil. Throbbing Gristle re-formed in 2004 for a series of performances and new recordings, including Part Two: The Endless Not (2007). After the death of Christopherson in 2010 and P-Orridge in 2020, the group's remaining archival work was curated by Carter and Tutti.

Legacy

Throbbing Gristle's influence extends across industrial, noise, electronic body music and experimental rock. Their do-it-yourself production and distribution methods, confrontational stagecraft, and use of found sound and cut-up techniques helped define the aesthetic of industrial culture.
