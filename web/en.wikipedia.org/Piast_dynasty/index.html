Piast dynasty

The Piast dynasty was the first historical ruling dynasty of Poland. Beginning with the semi-legendary Piast the Wheelwright and his son Siemowit, the Piasts ruled the emerging Polish state from the 10th century until 1370, when Casimir III the Great died without a male heir. Cadet branches in Silesia survived until 1675.

Origins and rise

The first documented ruler, Mieszko I (c. 960–992), accepted baptism in 966, bringing Poland into Latin Christendom. His son Bolesław I the Brave was crowned the first king of Poland in 1025. The dynasty's realm waxed and waned through invasions, succession crises, and a long period of fragmentation after 1138, when Bolesław III Wrymouth divided the kingdom among his sons.

Reunification and end

Władysław I the Elbow-high reunified much of Poland and was crowned in 1320. His son Casimir III the Great (r. 1333–1370), the last Piast king, strengthened the monarchy, codified law, founded the University of Kraków, and expanded eastward. On his death the throne passed to his nephew Louis I of Hungary of the Angevin dynasty. Silesian Piast dukes continued to rule their duchies until the death of George William of Legnica in 1675.
