Weddell Sea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Weddell Sea is part of the Southern Ocean and contains the Weddell Gyre. It is a large embayment in the coast of Antarctica between the Antarctic Peninsula and Coats Land, bounded to the south by the Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf, the second largest ice shelf in the world. The sea is named after the Scottish sailor James Weddell, who entered it in 1823 and originally named it for King George IV.

Oceanography

The Weddell Sea is a major site of deep- and bottom-water formation: cold, dense Antarctic Bottom Water produced here under and near the ice shelves spreads northward to ventilate the abyssal world ocean. The clockwise Weddell Gyre circulates water along the Antarctic margin. Much of the sea is covered by sea ice year-round, and in 2016–2017 a vast open-water polynya reappeared over Maud Rise, attracting scientific attention.

Exploration

After Weddell's voyage of 1823, which reached 74°15′S — the farthest south attained at the time — the sea was explored by expeditions including Otto Nordenskjöld's Swedish Antarctic Expedition (1901–1904), Wilhelm Filchner's expedition that discovered the Filchner Ice Shelf (1911–1912), and Ernest Shackleton's Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition, whose ship Endurance was trapped and crushed by Weddell Sea ice in 1915; the wreck was located on the seabed in 2022.

Wildlife

The sea supports abundant wildlife, including Weddell seals, emperor penguins — notably a large colony near Snow Hill Island — orcas, and rich krill-based food webs.
