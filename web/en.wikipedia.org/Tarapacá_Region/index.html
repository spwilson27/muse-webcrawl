Tarapacá Region

The Tarapacá Region (Spanish: Región de Tarapacá) is one of Chile's sixteen administrative regions. It is located in the far north of the country, bordering Bolivia to the east, the Arica y Parinacota Region to the north, the Antofagasta Region to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Its capital and largest city is the port of Iquique.

History

Tarapacá was formerly part of Peru. Chile occupied the territory during the War of the Pacific (1879–1884), and it was formally ceded to Chile by the Treaty of Ancón in 1883. The region's nitrate (saltpeter) deposits in the Atacama Desert made it immensely valuable in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; the abandoned nitrate towns of Humberstone and Santa Laura are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The region also saw the 1907 Santa María School massacre of striking nitrate workers in Iquique, a landmark event in Chilean labour history.

Geography and economy

The region spans the coastal plain, the Chilean Coast Range, the intermediate depression (pampa), and the Andes altiplano. It is divided into the provinces of Iquique and Tamarugal. The economy is based on copper mining (including the large Collahuasi mine), fishing, port activity, and the Iquique Free Trade Zone (ZOFRI), one of South America's largest duty-free commercial centres. Tourism draws visitors to desert geoglyphs such as the Atacama Giant and to the city of Pica's oasis.

Demographics

The population is concentrated in Iquique and the adjacent city of Alto Hospicio, with smaller Aymara communities in the Andean highlands.
