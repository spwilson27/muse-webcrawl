The Chautauqua Institution is a non-profit education centre and summer resort in Chautauqua County, New York, on the shores of Chautauqua Lake. Founded in 1874, it hosts a nine-week summer season of lectures, concerts, opera, theatre, religious services and recreational activities, organised around programming in the arts, education, religion and recreation.

History

The institution began in 1874 as the Chautauqua Lake Sunday School Assembly, founded by inventor Lewis Miller and Methodist bishop John Heyl Vincent to train Sunday-school teachers. It quickly broadened into a general adult-education movement, inspiring the nationwide Chautauqua movement of travelling "tent Chautauquas" and correspondence courses that brought lectures and culture to small-town America. U.S. presidents from Ulysses S. Grant onward have visited; Franklin D. Roosevelt delivered his "I hate war" speech there in 1936. The grounds are a National Historic Landmark District.

Programmes and notable events

The season features daily lectures in the open-air Amphitheater, performances by the Chautauqua Symphony Orchestra and resident opera and theatre companies, and Schools of Music, Art, Dance and Writing. In 1985 the institution hosted Mikhail Gorbachev-era U.S.–Soviet exchanges. In August 2022 the novelist Salman Rushdie was attacked and gravely wounded moments before lecturing at the Amphitheater, an event that drew worldwide attention.
