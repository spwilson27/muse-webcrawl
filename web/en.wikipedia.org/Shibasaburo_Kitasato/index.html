Kitasato Shibasaburō

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: portrait of Kitasato Shibasaburō]

Kitasato Shibasaburō (Japanese: 北里 柴三郎; 29 January 1853 – 13 June 1931) was a Japanese physician and bacteriologist. He is remembered as the co-discoverer of the infectious agent of bubonic plague in Hong Kong during an outbreak in 1894, almost simultaneously with Alexandre Yersin. He had previously been internationally recognised for work with Emil von Behring in Berlin on the development of antitoxins for diphtheria and tetanus.

Born in Kumamoto Prefecture, Kitasato studied medicine at Tokyo Imperial University and then worked in Robert Koch's laboratory in Germany from 1885 to 1891. With Behring, he showed in 1890 that immunity to tetanus could be transferred by serum from immunised animals — the foundation of serum therapy.

Career in Japan

Returning to Japan in 1891, Kitasato founded the Institute for the Study of Infectious Diseases, the first institute of its kind in Japan. When the government moved the institute under Tokyo Imperial University in 1914, he resigned in protest and founded the Kitasato Institute, which he headed for the rest of his life. In 1917 he became the first dean of the medical school at Keio University.

Kitasato was nominated for the first Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1901 for his antitoxin work with Behring, though the prize went to Behring alone — an omission long regarded as controversial. His portrait appears on the Japanese 1,000-yen note issued in 2024.
