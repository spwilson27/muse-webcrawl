Brewing

Brewing is the production of beer through steeping a starch source (commonly cereal grains, most often malted barley) in water and fermenting the resulting sweet liquid with yeast. It is practised at scales from homebrewing to large industrial breweries.

The process

The main steps are malting, milling, mashing (converting starches to sugars), lautering, boiling with hops, cooling, fermentation, conditioning, and packaging. The sweet liquid before fermentation is called wort; hops contribute bitterness, flavour, and preservative qualities.

History

Brewing is among humanity's oldest food technologies, with evidence of fermented grain beverages in Mesopotamia and Egypt over 5,000 years ago. Medieval monasteries refined techniques; the Bavarian Reinheitsgebot of 1516 restricted ingredients to water, barley, and hops. Industrialization brought steam power, refrigeration, and Louis Pasteur's studies of fermentation.

Ingredients

Beer is typically made from water, malted barley or other grains, hops, and yeast. Ale yeasts ferment warm and fast; lager yeasts ferment cold and condition for longer. Adjuncts such as wheat, maize, rice, and oats are common in many styles.
