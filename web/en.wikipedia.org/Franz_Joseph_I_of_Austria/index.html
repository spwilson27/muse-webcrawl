Franz Joseph I of Austria

Franz Joseph I (1830–1916) reigned for nearly 68 years, one of the longest reigns in European history.

Franz Joseph I (German: Franz Joseph I.; 18 August 1830 – 21 November 1916) was Emperor of Austria, King of Hungary, and ruler of the lands of the Habsburg monarchy from 1848 until his death in 1916. His reign of almost 68 years was the third longest in European history and encompassed the transformation of the Austrian Empire into the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary.

Reign

Franz Joseph ascended the throne at eighteen during the revolutions of 1848, after the abdication of his uncle Ferdinand I. With the help of Russian troops he crushed the Hungarian revolution, and he ruled as a neo-absolutist monarch through the 1850s. Defeats by France and Piedmont (1859) and by Prussia (1866) cost Austria its Italian possessions and its leadership in Germany, and led to the Ausgleich (Compromise) of 1867, which made Hungary a co-equal partner in a dual monarchy.

The later decades of his reign saw rapid industrialisation, the flowering of Viennese culture, and mounting tension among the empire's nationalities. His personal life was marked by tragedy: his brother Maximilian was executed in Mexico (1867), his only son Crown Prince Rudolf died at Mayerling (1889), and his wife, the Empress Elisabeth ("Sisi"), was assassinated in 1898. The assassination of his heir, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, at Sarajevo in June 1914 triggered the July Crisis and the First World War, in the middle of which Franz Joseph died in 1916. He was succeeded by his grandnephew Charles I, the last Habsburg emperor.
