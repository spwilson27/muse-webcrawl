Kama River

Kama River
Country: Russia
Length: 1,805 km
Mouth: Volga River
Basin size: about 507,000 km²

The Kama (Russian: Кама) is a major river in European Russia and the largest left tributary of the Volga. Rising in the Udmurt Republic, it flows 1,805 kilometres generally south and west through Perm Krai and Tatarstan before joining the Volga at the Kuybyshev Reservoir south of Kazan. By discharge, the Kama is actually larger than the Volga at their confluence.

Course and tributaries

The Kama drains a basin of about 507,000 square kilometres. Its principal tributaries include the Vishera, Chusovaya, Belaya, and Vyatka rivers. Major cities on its banks include Solikamsk, Berezniki, Perm, and Naberezhnye Chelny, the last home to the giant KamAZ truck works, named after the river.

Navigation and dams

The river has been an important transport artery since the Middle Ages, linking the Urals with central Russia; fur traders and the Stroganov family built fortunes along its upper course. In the Soviet period it was dammed for hydroelectric power, notably at Kama (Perm), Votkinsk, and Nizhnekamsk, creating large reservoirs that regulate flow and support navigation deep into the Ural region.
