uMkhonto we Sizwe (abbreviated MK; Zulu for "Spear of the Nation") was the paramilitary wing of the African National Congress (ANC), founded on 16 December 1961 to fight against the apartheid government of South Africa. Its formation, announced in a manifesto and marked by a campaign of sabotage attacks on government installations, signalled the ANC's turn from exclusively non-violent resistance after the Sharpeville massacre and the banning of the movement.

History

Nelson Mandela, who had pressed the ANC leadership to sanction armed action, became MK's first commander-in-chief and travelled abroad to raise support and undergo military training. In July 1963 police raided the movement's secret headquarters at Liliesleaf Farm in Rivonia, Johannesburg, arresting most of MK's high command; the ensuing Rivonia Trial sent Mandela and others to prison for life.

Rebuilt in exile, MK operated from bases in countries including Angola, Zambia and Tanzania, working alongside movements such as ZAPU in Rhodesia and FRELIMO in Mozambique, and receiving training and materiel from the Soviet bloc, Cuba and other allies. Within South Africa, MK carried out sabotage and attacks on military, police and infrastructure targets through the 1980s; some operations caused civilian deaths, which the Truth and Reconciliation Commission later examined. MK suspended armed action in August 1990 as negotiations began, and in 1994 its roughly 28,000 personnel were integrated into the new South African National Defence Force.

Legacy

MK veterans retain a formal association, and the name has remained politically resonant: in December 2023 former president Jacob Zuma launched a new political party named uMkhonto weSizwe (MK Party), prompting a trademark dispute with the ANC.
