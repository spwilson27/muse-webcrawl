The UEFA European Championship, commonly known as the Euros, is the primary association football tournament for men's senior national teams of UEFA member associations. Held every four years since 1960, in the even-numbered year between World Cups, it determines the continental champion of Europe. The winners receive the Henri Delaunay Trophy, named after the French administrator who conceived the competition.

History

The first tournament, held in France in 1960 as the European Nations' Cup, featured just four teams in the finals and was won by the Soviet Union. The finals expanded to eight teams in 1980, sixteen in 1996, and twenty-four from Euro 2016 onward, preceded by a qualifying competition open to all UEFA members. Famous editions include Denmark's surprise victory in 1992 after late admission, Greece's triumph in 2004, and Spain's back-to-back titles in 2008 and 2012, the latter completing a unique Euro–World Cup–Euro treble. Euro 2020, postponed to 2021 because of the COVID-19 pandemic, was staged across eleven host cities and won by Italy. Spain won a record fourth title at Euro 2024 in Germany, beating England in the final.

Format

Since 2016, the finals feature six groups of four teams, with the top two from each group and the four best third-placed teams advancing to a 16-team knockout stage culminating in the final. Hosts qualify automatically; all other entrants go through qualifying groups and, since 2020, play-offs linked to the UEFA Nations League.

Records

Spain is the most successful team with four titles (1964, 2008, 2012, 2024), followed by Germany with three. Cristiano Ronaldo of Portugal holds the records for most goals at the finals (14) and most tournaments played (six, 2004–2024).
