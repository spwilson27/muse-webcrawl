Beauty

Beauty is commonly described as a property of entities — objects, persons, places, landscapes, and ideas — that provides a perceptual experience of pleasure or satisfaction. It is a central subject of aesthetics, one of the main branches of philosophy, and is closely related to concepts such as harmony, proportion, and the sublime.

History of thought

Ancient Greek philosophers, including Plato and Aristotle, tied beauty to order, symmetry, and form; Plato associated it with the eternal Forms and with goodness and truth. Medieval thinkers treated beauty as a transcendental property of being. In the eighteenth century, David Hume argued that beauty is a sentiment in the perceiver, while Immanuel Kant analysed judgements of beauty as both subjective and claimed with universal validity.

Modern perspectives

Contemporary research spans philosophical aesthetics, evolutionary psychology — which studies preferences for symmetry and averageness — and cultural criticism examining how beauty standards vary across societies and eras and are shaped by media and commerce.
