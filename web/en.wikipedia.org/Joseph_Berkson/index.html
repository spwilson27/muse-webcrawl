Joseph Berkson

Joseph Berkson
Born: 1899, New York City
Died: 1982
Fields: Biostatistics

Joseph Berkson (1899 – 1982) was an American physician and statistician, head of the Division of Biometry and Medical Statistics at the Mayo Clinic. He is known for Berkson's paradox and for his work on the logit model in bioassay.

Contributions

In 1944 Berkson proposed the logit function for dose-response analysis as an alternative to the probit. His 1946 paper on hospital-based case-control studies described the selection bias now called Berkson's paradox, in which conditioning on a common effect induces a spurious association.

Views on smoking

Berkson was a prominent sceptic of the causal interpretation of the association between smoking and lung cancer in the 1950s, arguing that statistical association alone did not prove causation — a position that placed him in public debate with other statisticians of the era.

Career

Trained in medicine and physics at Johns Hopkins, Berkson joined the Mayo Clinic in 1933 and built its medical statistics programme.
