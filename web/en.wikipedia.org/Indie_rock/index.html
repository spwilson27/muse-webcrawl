Indie rock

Indie rock grew out of the independent-label underground of the 1980s.

Indie rock is a subgenre of rock music that originated in the United States and the United Kingdom in the 1970s and 1980s. Originally used to describe independent record labels, the term became associated with the music they produced and was initially interchangeable with alternative rock. Indie rock artists typically retain creative control, releasing music on small labels or independently and favouring artistic expression over commercial success.

History

The indie underground of the 1980s was built on college radio, fanzines, independent distribution and labels such as SST, Dischord, Touch and Go, Rough Trade and Creation. Bands like R.E.M., The Smiths, Sonic Youth, Hüsker Dü, Pixies and Dinosaur Jr. defined the sound. After Nirvana's breakthrough in 1991, many indie acts signed to major labels, blurring the term's meaning. In the 2000s, internet distribution and acts such as The Strokes, Interpol, Arcade Fire, The White Stripes and Franz Ferdinand drove a mainstream indie rock revival, followed by a further wave including Arctic Monkeys and Vampire Weekend.

Characteristics

Indie rock is noted for proportionally more female artists than other rock genres in some surveys, a DIY ethic, and a wide stylistic range — from the noise experiments of Sonic Youth to the lo-fi recordings of Guided by Voices and Pavement, who became emblematic of the genre's deliberately unpolished aesthetic.
