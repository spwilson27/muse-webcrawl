Deposition (geology)

Deposition is the geological process in which sediments, soil and rocks are added to a landform or landmass. It is the laying down of material carried by wind, water, ice or gravity after the transporting agent loses the energy needed to move it. Deposition is the constructive counterpart of erosion: material eroded in one place is deposited in another, building up new landforms and, over geological time, the sedimentary rock record.

Mechanisms

Particles are deposited when the velocity of the transporting medium falls below the settling velocity of the grains. In rivers, this occurs on the inside of meander bends, in floodplains during overbank flooding, and where a river enters standing water to form a delta. Wind deposits sand as dunes when airflow is obstructed or slows, and fine dust as loess far downwind of deserts and glacial outwash plains. Glaciers deposit unsorted debris called till when the ice melts, forming moraines, drumlins and eskers. In the sea, sediments accumulate on continental shelves and in deep basins, where the remains of plankton rain down to form oozes that may lithify into chalk, limestone or shale.

Sedimentary environments

The characteristics of a deposit — its grain size, sorting, bedding and fossil content — record the environment in which it formed. Geologists distinguish continental environments (rivers, lakes, deserts, glaciers), transitional environments (deltas, beaches, tidal flats) and marine environments (reefs, continental slopes, abyssal plains). Successions of deposits obey principles such as Walther's law, under which vertically stacked facies represent environments that once lay side by side. Over time, burial and compaction turn loose sediment into sedimentary rock through lithification.

Landforms

Depositional landforms include alluvial fans, deltas such as those of the Nile and Mississippi, sand dunes, beaches and barrier islands, drumlins, moraines, floodplains and natural levees. Rates of deposition range from fractions of a millimetre per year in the deep ocean to metres in a single flood event.
