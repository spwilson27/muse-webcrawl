Pan bagnat

A pan bagnat: a round loaf filled with salade niçoise ingredients.

TypeSandwich
Place of originFrance
RegionNice, Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur
Main ingredientsRound bread, raw vegetables, tuna or anchovies, hard-boiled egg, olive oil

Pan bagnat (pronounced [pɑ̃ baɲa]) is a sandwich from the region of Nice, in southern France. The name comes from the Provençal (Niçard) phrase pan banhat, meaning "wet bread", referring to the bread soaked in olive oil — and traditionally also the juices of the filling. It is essentially a salade niçoise served in a hollowed-out round loaf.

Composition

A classic pan bagnat uses a round, crusty country loaf (about 15–20 cm across), rubbed with garlic and moistened with olive oil, filled with the ingredients of a salade niçoise: tomatoes, radishes, spring onions, green pepper, artichoke hearts, broad beans in season, tuna (or anchovies), hard-boiled egg, black Niçoise olives, basil, salt and pepper. The sandwich is traditionally pressed and left to rest — ideally for hours — so the oil and tomato juices soak into the crumb, which is why it is said to improve by the next day.

Customs and protection

Originally a way for fishermen and workers to use stale bread softened with oil and tomato, the pan bagnat became the emblematic picnic and beach food of Nice. Recipes adding mayonnaise, chicken, ham or lettuce are considered inauthentic by local purists; a Nice association, the Commune Libre du Pan Bagnat, promotes the traditional recipe.
