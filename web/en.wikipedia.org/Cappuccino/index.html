TypeCoffee drink
OriginItaly

A cappuccino is an Italian coffee drink traditionally prepared with equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and milk foam. It is named for the Capuchin friars, whose brown robes resemble the drink's colour. The cappuccino became established in Italian cafés in the early 20th century as espresso machines spread.

Preparation

A shot of espresso is poured into a ceramic cup, followed by steamed milk, and topped with a thick layer of frothed milk, sometimes dusted with cocoa or cinnamon. A "wet" cappuccino has more steamed milk and less foam; a "dry" one the reverse. In Italy it is considered a morning drink, rarely ordered after about 11 a.m.

Popularity

With the global spread of coffeehouse chains in the late 20th century, the cappuccino became one of the most popular espresso-based drinks worldwide, and the canvas for latte art.
