Dies irae

The Dies irae ("Day of Wrath") is a Latin sequence attributed to Thomas of Celano (c. 1200–1265), describing the Last Judgment, when the saved will be gathered and the damned cast into eternal fire. It formed part of the Roman Catholic Requiem Mass from the fourteenth century until the liturgical reforms following the Second Vatican Council.

Text

The poem, in rhymed accentual verse, opens: Dies irae, dies illa / Solvet saeclum in favilla / Teste David cum Sibylla ("That day of wrath, that dreadful day, / shall heaven and earth in ashes lay, / as David and the Sibyl say"). Its dramatic vision of judgment made it one of the most famous poems of the Middle Ages.

Musical settings

The chant melody of the Dies irae has been quoted by many composers, including Berlioz in the Symphonie fantastique, Saint-Saëns in Danse macabre, Rachmaninoff throughout his works, and in numerous film scores. The sequence has been set in full in Requiems by Mozart, Verdi, Berlioz, and many others.
