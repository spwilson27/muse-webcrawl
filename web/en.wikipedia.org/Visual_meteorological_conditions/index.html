In aviation, visual meteorological conditions (VMC) is an aviation flight category for meteorological conditions that allow pilots to fly the aircraft while maintaining visual separation from the terrain and other aircraft and staying clear of clouds. It is the opposite of instrument meteorological conditions (IMC). The boundary criteria between IMC and VMC are called VMC minima and are defined by visibility and a minimum distance from clouds.

Definition and minima

With reasonable safety margins, VMC minima are defined by each national authority, broadly following ICAO standards. Typical requirements are a flight visibility of 5 km (or 8 km at and above flight level 100 in some jurisdictions) and minimum distances from cloud of 1,500 m horizontally and 1,000 ft vertically; lower minima apply in certain classes of uncontrolled airspace at low altitude, where pilots must simply remain clear of cloud with the surface in sight. In United States regulation (14 CFR 91.155), basic VFR weather minimums vary by airspace class and altitude — for example, 3 statute miles visibility and defined cloud clearances (500 ft below, 1,000 ft above, 2,000 ft horizontal) in most controlled airspace below 10,000 ft MSL.

Significance

Flight under visual flight rules (VFR) is permitted only in VMC, and the pilot is responsible for seeing and avoiding other traffic and terrain. When conditions deteriorate below VMC minima, pilots must fly under instrument flight rules (IFR) using instruments and air traffic control separation. Inadvertent flight by a VFR pilot into IMC is a major cause of fatal general-aviation accidents, because of the risk of spatial disorientation and controlled flight into terrain.

A related category, special VFR, allows flight in controlled airspace near an airport with reduced visibility provided the pilot remains clear of clouds and the visibility is at least one statute mile (in U.S. practice). In aviation weather reports (METARs), conditions at or above VMC minima at U.S. airports are commonly summarised with the term "CAVOK" in ICAO-format reports — ceiling and visibility OK.
