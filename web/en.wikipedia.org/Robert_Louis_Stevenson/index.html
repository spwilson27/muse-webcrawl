Robert Louis Stevenson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Robert Louis Stevenson (born Robert Lewis Balfour Stevenson; 13 November 1850 – 3 December 1894) was a Scottish novelist, essayist, poet, and travel writer. He is best known for the adventure novel Treasure Island (1883), the Gothic novella Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (1886), and the historical novels Kidnapped (1886) and The Master of Ballantrae (1889). Plagued by ill health throughout his life, he travelled widely in search of a suitable climate and died in Samoa, where he had settled.

Life

Stevenson was born in Edinburgh into a family of lighthouse engineers. He studied engineering and then law at the University of Edinburgh but devoted himself to writing. His travels — a canoe trip through Belgium and France recorded in An Inland Voyage (1878) and a walk through the Cévennes with a donkey in Travels with a Donkey in the Cévennes (1879) — established his reputation as a travel writer. In 1880 he married Fanny Van de Grift Osbourne. In 1888 the family sailed the Pacific, eventually settling at Vailima, Samoa, where Stevenson, known locally as Tusitala ("teller of tales"), died of a cerebral haemorrhage in 1894.

Works and legacy

Treasure Island, written for his stepson Lloyd Osbourne, popularized the modern pirate mythos of treasure maps, parrots, and "X marks the spot". Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde became a definitive exploration of the duality of human nature. Stevenson also wrote poetry (A Child's Garden of Verses, 1885), essays, and Pacific tales. Once dismissed by modernist critics as a mere entertainer, he has been re-evaluated as a major stylist and a precursor of modern fiction.
