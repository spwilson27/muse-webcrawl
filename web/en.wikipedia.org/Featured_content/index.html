Wikipedia: Featured content

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Featured content represents the best of what Wikipedia has to offer. These are articles, pictures, and lists that the community has assessed through formal review processes and found to meet strict quality standards for accuracy, completeness, prose, and presentation. Featured content is identified by a small gold star (★) in the upper-right corner of the page.

Types of featured content

Featured articles — articles considered to be among the best Wikipedia produces. They must be well-written, comprehensive, well-researched, neutral, and stable, and follow the manual of style. A selection appears each day as the Today's featured article on the Main Page.
Featured lists — lists that meet the same professional standard, useful and complete, with a clear structure and consistent formatting.
Featured pictures — images that add significant encyclopedic value and are technically strong. One is shown daily as the Picture of the day.
Featured portals and featured topics — portals and coherent sets of articles on a single subject that are individually of high quality and collectively comprehensive.

Review process

Content is nominated at dedicated review pages, where editors scrutinise it against published criteria. Articles that succeed become featured; those that fall short receive detailed feedback. Featured status is not permanent: a featured article review can be opened for articles that no longer meet the standards, and they may be improved or delisted. Below the featured tier, good articles form a broader class of well-developed content that has passed a lighter review.

On the Main Page

Featured content is showcased to readers through the Main Page: the featured article of the day, the picture of the day, and rotating selections from featured lists. The system exists both to recognise editors' best work and to give readers a dependable way to find Wikipedia at its most reliable and engaging.
