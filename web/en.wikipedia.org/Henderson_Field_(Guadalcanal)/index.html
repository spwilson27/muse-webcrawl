Henderson Field (Guadalcanal)

LocationGuadalcanal, Solomon Islands
Coordinates9°26′S 160°03′E
Current useHoniara International Airport
Named forMajor Lofton R. Henderson, USMC

Henderson Field is a former military airfield on Guadalcanal, Solomon Islands, during World War II. Originally built by the Japanese Empire, the airfield was captured by Allied forces and renamed for United States Marine Corps Major Lofton R. Henderson, a squadron commander killed at the Battle of Midway. The airfield, and the aircraft based there — the so-called "Cactus Air Force" — were the focal point of the Guadalcanal campaign, and control of the field was the decisive strategic prize of the six-month battle. Today the site is Honiara International Airport, the main international airport of the Solomon Islands.

Construction and capture

Japanese forces landed on Guadalcanal in mid-1942 and began building an airfield at Lunga Point, from which aircraft could threaten the Allied supply lines between the United States, Australia, and New Zealand. Before it was complete, the US 1st Marine Division landed on 7 August 1942 and captured the unfinished field. American engineers — Navy Seabees and Marine aviation engineers — completed the runway using captured Japanese equipment.

The Guadalcanal campaign

The first Marine aircraft landed on 20 August 1942, beginning operations by the hastily assembled Cactus Air Force ("Cactus" being the Allied code name for Guadalcanal). Henderson Field endured near-daily Japanese air raids, naval bombardments — most devastatingly by battleships on the night of 13–14 October 1942 — and repeated ground assaults, including the battles of the Tenaru, Edson's Ridge, and the Matanikau. Fighter strips (Fighter One and Fighter Two) were built nearby to disperse operations. Despite the damage, the field was repeatedly repaired, and by early 1943 it supported large-scale Allied operations advancing up the Solomon Islands chain.

Legacy

After the war the site was developed into the principal civil airport for the Solomon Islands capital, Honiara. Wrecks, memorials, and the preserved runway trace remain important sites of remembrance for the Guadalcanal campaign.
