Battle of Red Cliffs

The Battle of Red Cliffs, also known as the Battle of Chibi, was a decisive naval battle in Chinese history fought at the end of the Han dynasty, in the winter of AD 208–209. The allied forces of the warlords Sun Quan and Liu Bei defeated the vastly larger fleet of the northern warlord Cao Cao, halting his bid to conquer southern China.

Background

Having unified the north, the chancellor Cao Cao marched south in 208 with a huge army, seizing Jing Province almost without a fight. Against the advice of counsellors who urged surrender, Sun Quan chose resistance and allied with the fugitive warlord Liu Bei. Their combined force of about 50,000 men, with the fleet commanded by Sun Quan's general Zhou Yu, met Cao Cao on the Yangtze near the Red Cliffs.

The battle

Cao Cao's northern troops were exhausted, disease-ridden and untrained for naval combat; to steady his ships in the river current he had them chained together bow to stern. Exploiting a rare winter south-east wind, Zhou Yu's subordinate Huang Gai feigned surrender and sailed a squadron of fire ships, packed with kindling and oil, into the packed northern fleet. The flames spread through the chained vessels and into the shore camps, and the allies' follow-up attack completed the rout. Cao Cao escaped north overland along the muddy Huarong road, harassed and half-starved.

Consequences

Red Cliffs was one of the great turning points of Chinese history. It secured the survival of independent regimes in the south and fixed the division of China that crystallised into the Three Kingdoms period: Cao Cao's Wei in the north, Liu Bei's Shu Han in the west and Sun Quan's Wu in the south-east. The battle, embroidered with the stratagems of Zhou Yu and Zhuge Liang, became a centrepiece of the classic novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms and of Chinese literature generally.
