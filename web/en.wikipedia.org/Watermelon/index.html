Watermelon

Scientific nameCitrullus lanatus
FamilyCucurbitaceae
OriginAfrica

Watermelon (Citrullus lanatus) is a flowering plant species of the Cucurbitaceae family and the name of its edible fruit. A scrambling and trailing vine-like plant, it is a highly cultivated fruit worldwide, with more than 1,000 varieties.

Description

The fruit has a smooth green rind, often with darker stripes, and a juicy, sweet interior flesh that is usually deep red to pink but can be orange, yellow, or white. The flesh is about 91% water and contains many small black seeds, although seedless varieties have been developed. Watermelon is rich in vitamin C, vitamin A, and the antioxidant lycopene.

History and cultivation

Watermelon originated in Africa, where wild forms grow in the Kalahari region; seeds have been found in ancient Egyptian tombs, and the fruit was cultivated in Egypt by 2000 BCE. It spread to the Mediterranean, Asia, and eventually the Americas. China is by far the world's largest producer. The plant thrives in warm climates with a long growing season and sandy, well-drained soil.

Uses

Watermelon is eaten fresh, juiced, and used in salads and desserts. The rind is pickled in some cuisines, and roasted seeds are eaten as a snack in parts of Asia and the Middle East.
