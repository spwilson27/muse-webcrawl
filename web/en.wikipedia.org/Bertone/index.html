Bertone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gruppo Bertone, commonly known simply as Bertone, was an Italian industrial design company which specialized in car styling, coachbuilding and manufacturing. It was based in Grugliasco, northern Italy, and was founded in Turin in 1912 by Giovanni Bertone as a workshop for repairing carriages and wagons.

History

Under Giovanni's son Giuseppe "Nuccio" Bertone, who took over after the Second World War, the company became one of the great Italian design houses. Bertone designed cars for Alfa Romeo, Fiat, Lancia, Lamborghini, Ferrari, Volvo and many others. Landmarks attributed to its designers — including Giorgetto Giugiaro and Marcello Gandini early in their careers — include the Lamborghini Miura, Countach and Espada, the Lancia Stratos, the Fiat X1/9 and the Alfa Romeo BAT concept cars and Giulietta Sprint.

Decline and revival

After Nuccio Bertone's death in 1997 the firm struggled amid the contraction of the independent coachbuilding industry. The company went through bankruptcy in 2014, and the Bertone name and trademarks were subsequently acquired and revived for design and limited-run hypercar projects, including the GB110 announced in the 2020s.
