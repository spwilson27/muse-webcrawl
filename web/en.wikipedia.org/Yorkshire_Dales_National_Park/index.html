The Yorkshire Dales National Park is a national park in northern England, covering 2,178 square kilometres (841 sq mi) mostly in North Yorkshire with a smaller part in Cumbria. Designated in 1954, it protects a landscape of deep river valleys (the "dales"), limestone pavements, waterfalls, and rolling fells, and is one of the largest protected areas in England.

Geography

The park is centred on the Pennines and takes in a series of parallel valleys running roughly west to east, including Swaledale, Wensleydale, Wharfedale and Airedale. Its highest summits are the Yorkshire Three Peaks: Whernside (736 m), Ingleborough (723 m) and Pen-y-ghent (694 m). Notable features include the limestone pavement at Malham Cove, the cave systems around Ingleborough, Aysgarth Falls on the River Ure, and Hardraw Force, England's tallest single-drop waterfall above ground.

Geology

Much of the bedrock is Carboniferous limestone, which weathers into distinctive clints and grykes, dry valleys and cave systems. Millstone grit caps many of the higher fells, giving the landscape its contrasting dark moorland tops.

History and culture

The Dales have been farmed for millennia; the pattern of stone barns and drystone walls in small meadows is a hallmark of the area. Lead mining flourished in the 18th and 19th centuries, especially in Swaledale and Arkengarthdale. Today the economy rests on hill farming and tourism, with visitors drawn by walking, caving, and villages such as Grassington, Hawes and Kettlewell. The park was extended in 2016 to include parts of the Orton Fells and the northern Howgill Fells.
