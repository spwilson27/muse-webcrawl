Home equity

Home equity is the difference between the market value of a home and the outstanding balance of all liens on the property, such as a mortgage. It represents the portion of the home the owner truly owns and typically grows as the mortgage is paid down or the property appreciates.

Calculation

Equity equals the property's current market value minus remaining loan balances. A down payment creates initial equity; amortization and price changes increase or decrease it. If debts exceed value, the owner has negative equity, sometimes called being underwater.

Borrowing against equity

Homeowners can borrow against equity through home equity loans, home equity lines of credit (HELOCs), or cash-out refinancing. Because the home secures such borrowing, default risks foreclosure, and lenders typically limit combined loan-to-value ratios.
