LocationPortchester, Hampshire, England
BuiltLate 3rd century AD (Roman fort); medieval castle within

Portchester Castle is a medieval fortress built within the walls of a Roman shore fort at Portchester, on the northern shore of Portsmouth Harbour in Hampshire. The Roman fort, one of the Saxon Shore forts, was constructed in the late 3rd century and its walls are the most complete Roman fort walls in northern Europe.

History

Within the Roman enclosure, Norman builders erected a castle with a great keep in the 11th–12th centuries, creating a fortified bailey in one corner. The castle served as a royal residence and a staging point for campaigns in France; Henry V assembled here before Agincourt in 1415. In the 18th and 19th centuries it housed prisoners of war, including thousands of French prisoners during the Napoleonic Wars.

Today

The site is cared for by English Heritage. The parish church of St Mary stands within the Roman walls, and the keep houses exhibitions on the castle's history.
