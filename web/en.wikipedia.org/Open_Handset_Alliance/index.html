Open Handset Alliance

The Open Handset Alliance (OHA) is a consortium of 84 firms to develop open standards for mobile devices. It was founded on 5 November 2007, led by Google with major handset manufacturers, mobile carriers, semiconductor companies and software firms, and simultaneously announced Android, a mobile operating system based on the Linux kernel.

Founding members included HTC, Motorola, Samsung Electronics, LG Electronics, T-Mobile, Sprint Nextel, Intel, Nvidia, Qualcomm, Texas Instruments and Telefónica. The first commercial Android phone, the HTC Dream (T-Mobile G1), was released in October 2008.

Purpose

The alliance aimed to create an open, complete and free mobile platform to counter proprietary systems, with members committing not to produce phones using incompatible forks of Android. Its activity diminished as Android governance centred on Google's Android Open Source Project.
