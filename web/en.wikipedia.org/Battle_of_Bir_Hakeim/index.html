Battle of Bir Hakeim

The Battle of Bir Hakeim took place from 26 May to 11 June 1942 during the Battle of Gazala in the North African campaign of World War II. The 1st Free French Brigade under General Marie-Pierre Kœnig held the fortified box at Bir Hakeim, the southern anchor of the British Gazala Line in Libya, against repeated attacks by German and Italian forces under Erwin Rommel.

Background

Bir Hakeim was an old Ottoman fort at a desert crossroads. When Rommel launched his offensive on 26 May 1942, swinging around the southern end of the Gazala Line, the Free French position blocked his supply routes and threatened his flank. The Italian Ariete Division's first attack on 27 May was repulsed with heavy tank losses.

The siege

For sixteen days the roughly 3,700 Free French troops — including Foreign Legionnaires, colonial troops from across the French empire, and a battalion of Jewish volunteers from Palestine in British service — endured artillery bombardment, Stuka dive-bombing and armoured assaults, supplied precariously by air and by night convoys. Their resistance delayed Rommel and bought time for the British Eighth Army.

Breakout and legacy

On the night of 10–11 June, Kœnig's force broke out through the encirclement and reached British lines, bringing out most of its surviving men and wounded. The stand at Bir Hakeim became a founding legend of the Free French and greatly raised their standing with the Allies.
