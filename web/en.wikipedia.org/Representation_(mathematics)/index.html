Representation (mathematics)

In mathematics, a representation is a very general relationship that expresses similarities (or equivalences) between mathematical objects or structures. Roughly speaking, a collection of mathematical objects may be said to represent another collection of objects if the representing objects display the properties and structure of the represented ones in some way. More precisely, given a set of objects with some structure, a representation is a homomorphism from the abstract structure to a more concrete one — most commonly, a group of matrices.

A representation realizes an abstract structure concretely, e.g. as a group of matrices.

Group representations

The best-known example is representation theory of groups: a representation of a group G on a vector space V assigns to each group element an invertible linear operator on V in such a way that the group operation corresponds to composition of operators. Finite groups, Lie groups, and algebraic groups all have rich representation theories, with applications ranging from crystallography and particle physics to number theory. Key concepts include irreducible representations, characters, and the decomposition of representations into direct sums.

Other senses

The term also appears across mathematics: a representation of an algebra turns its elements into operators; representation theorems, such as Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras or Cayley's theorem for groups, show that every abstract structure of a given kind can be represented by a concrete one; and in category theory, a representation of a category is a functor into another category. In a looser sense, coordinate systems, graphs, and notation systems all serve as representations of mathematical content.
