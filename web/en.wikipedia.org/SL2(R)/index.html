SL2(R)

In mathematics, the special linear group SL2(ℝ) is the group of 2 × 2 real matrices with determinant 1:

SL2(ℝ) = { (a b; c d) : a, b, c, d ∈ ℝ and ad − bc = 1 }.

It is a real Lie group with important applications in geometry, group theory and mathematical physics. SL2(ℝ) is connected but not simply connected; its fundamental group is isomorphic to the integers ℤ.

Möbius transformations of SL2(ℝ) act as isometries of the hyperbolic plane.

Properties

SL2(ℝ) is a three-dimensional noncompact simple Lie group; its Lie algebra sl2(ℝ) consists of the 2 × 2 traceless real matrices. Its maximal compact subgroup is the rotation group SO(2). The centre of SL2(ℝ) is {±I}, and the quotient PSL2(ℝ) = SL2(ℝ)/{±I} is the group of orientation-preserving isometries of the hyperbolic plane.

Action on the upper half-plane

SL2(ℝ) acts on the complex upper half-plane ℍ = {z : Im z > 0} by fractional linear (Möbius) transformations, g·z = (az + b)/(cz + d). This action preserves the Poincaré metric and makes ℍ a model of hyperbolic geometry. The discrete subgroup SL2(ℤ), the modular group, gives rise to modular forms and modular curves.

Iwasawa decomposition

Every element of SL2(ℝ) can be written uniquely as a product kan with k in SO(2), a a positive diagonal matrix, and n an upper-triangular matrix with ones on the diagonal — the Iwasawa decomposition, a special case of the general theory of semisimple Lie groups. The representation theory of SL2(ℝ), developed in part by Valentine Bargmann, is foundational to harmonic analysis on Lie groups.
