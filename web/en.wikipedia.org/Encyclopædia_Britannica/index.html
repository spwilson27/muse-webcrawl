Encyclopædia Britannica

The Encyclopædia Britannica is a general-knowledge English-language encyclopaedia, long regarded as a scholarly reference work. First published in Edinburgh in three volumes between 1768 and 1771, it grew through fifteen official editions.

History

Editors and contributors included prominent scholars; the ninth (1875–1889) and eleventh (1911) editions are especially celebrated. The work moved to American ownership and Chicago headquarters in the 20th century. The 15th edition (1974) introduced a three-part structure of Micropædia, Macropædia, and Propædia.

Digital era

Britannica published CD-ROM and online versions from the 1990s. In 2012 it ended print publication after 244 years, continuing as a digital encyclopedia and educational publisher at Britannica.com.
