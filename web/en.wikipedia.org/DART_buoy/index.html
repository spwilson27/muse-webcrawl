DART buoy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A DART buoy (Deep-ocean Assessment and Reporting of Tsunamis) is the surface component of a tsunameter system used to detect tsunamis in the open ocean and relay real-time data to warning centers. The DART network is operated primarily by the United States National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), with partner stations maintained by other nations around the Pacific and Indian Oceans.

System design

Each DART station consists of two parts: a bottom pressure recorder (BPR) anchored to the seafloor at depths of up to about 6,000 metres, and a moored surface buoy. The BPR measures changes in water pressure with a resolution of less than a millimetre of sea-surface height, sampling every 15 seconds. Data travel from the BPR to the buoy by an acoustic telemetry link, and from the buoy to shore by Iridium satellite communications.

Operating modes

In standard mode the system reports averaged water-column height every hour. When the onboard algorithm detects a deviation consistent with a passing tsunami wave, the station switches to event mode, transmitting data at one-minute intervals (and later fifteen-second data) so that forecasters can confirm the wave and estimate its height at distant coasts.

History

Development began at NOAA's Pacific Marine Environmental Laboratory in the 1980s, and the first array of six stations was deployed in the North Pacific and equatorial Pacific by 2001. The devastating 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami spurred major expansion; by the late 2000s the United States array had grown to 39 stations, and additional DART-type systems were installed in the Indian Ocean by Australia, India, Thailand, Chile, and other nations.

Role in warning

DART data feed numerical forecast models, such as NOAA's Method of Splitting Tsunami (MOST) model, allowing warning centers to confirm whether a tsunami has actually been generated by a submarine earthquake and to refine evacuation guidance — or to cancel warnings that coastal tide gauges alone might not resolve for hours.
