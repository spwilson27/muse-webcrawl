South Bass Island

South Bass Island
Location: Lake Erie, Ohio, United States
Area: about 6.4 km² (1,588 acres)
Village: Put-in-Bay
County: Ottawa County

South Bass Island is an island in the western basin of Lake Erie, in Ottawa County, Ohio, United States. It is the southernmost of the three Bass Islands and is best known for the village of Put-in-Bay, a popular summer resort. The island is reached by ferry from Port Clinton, Sandusky and Catawba Island, and has a small airport.

History

The island entered American history during the War of 1812: Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry's fleet sailed from Put-in-Bay to defeat a British squadron at the Battle of Lake Erie on 10 September 1813, prompting Perry's famous report, "We have met the enemy and they are ours." The victory is commemorated by Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial, a 107-metre Doric column completed in 1915 that remains one of the tallest monuments in the United States. In the later 19th century the island became known for vineyards and wine making, and for resort hotels such as the grand Hotel Victory.

Tourism

Today the island's economy is dominated by seasonal tourism: bars, restaurants, marinas, campgrounds and attractions including Perry's Cave, the Crystal Cave (a large celestite geode), a butterfly house, and the Lake Erie Islands Historical Society museum. Golf carts are the most common means of transport for visitors.

Geography

The island is about 5.6 km long and 2.4 km wide, formed of Silurian dolomite. Its sheltered bay and state park shoreline attract boaters, anglers and birdwatchers, particularly during spring and autumn migrations.
