Morphosyntactic alignment

In linguistics, morphosyntactic alignment is the grammatical relationship between arguments — specifically, between the two arguments of transitive verbs such as "the hunter" and "the deer" in "The hunter killed the deer", and the single argument of intransitive verbs such as "the deer" in "The deer ran away". The way a language groups these three roles — conventionally labelled S (intransitive subject), A (transitive agent), and P (transitive patient) — defines its alignment type.

The core arguments

Languages differ in which roles receive the same case marking or verb agreement. The question of alignment is independent of word order; it concerns case, agreement, and other grammatical coding.

The two commonest alignments: nominative–accusative (S = A) and ergative–absolutive (S = P).

Marking strategies

Alignment may be expressed by case marking on nouns, by agreement on the verb, or by both ("head marking" vs "dependent marking"). Many languages show split ergativity, using ergative alignment only in certain tenses, aspects, or person categories — for instance Hindi uses ergative marking in the perfective aspect only.
