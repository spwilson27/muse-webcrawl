Dwarka is a town and municipality in Devbhumi Dwarka district in the state of Gujarat, India, on the western shore of the Okhamandal Peninsula on the Arabian Sea. It is one of the four sacred Hindu pilgrimage sites of the Char Dham and one of the seven most ancient religious cities (Sapta Puri) in India. Dwarka is traditionally identified as the capital city of the god Krishna, founded, according to legend, after he left Mathura.

Legend and history

In the Mahabharata and the Puranas, Krishna's Dwarka is a fabulous city built on land reclaimed from the sea, said to have submerged after his death. Marine archaeological explorations off modern Dwarka and at Bet Dwarka have found stone structures and anchors, debated as evidence of ancient habitation. The present town's great Dwarkadhish Temple (Jagat Mandir), dedicated to Krishna as the "King of Dwarka", is a five-storey Chalukya-style temple whose main shrine is traditionally dated to antiquity and rebuilt over the centuries; the philosopher Adi Shankara is said to have established one of his four peethas (monasteries) here in the 8th century.

Geography and economy

Dwarka lies at the mouth of the Gulf of Kutch. Its economy rests on pilgrimage tourism, agriculture and nearby cement and chemical industries. Major festivals include Janmashtami, celebrating Krishna's birth. The nearby island of Bet Dwarka, reached by ferry, contains further Krishna temples.
