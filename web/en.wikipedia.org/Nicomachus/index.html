Nicomachus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Numbers in Nicomachus's Introduction to Arithmetic

Nicomachus of Gerasa (Greek: Νικόμαχος; c. 60 – c. 120 AD) was an ancient Greek Neopythagorean philosopher and mathematician from Gerasa, in the Roman province of Syria (modern Jerash, Jordan). He is best known for his works Introduction to Arithmetic and Manual of Harmonics, which became standard textbooks for well over a thousand years.

Works

The Introduction to Arithmetic (Arithmetike eisagoge) was the first work to treat arithmetic as a subject separate from geometry. It classifies numbers as even and odd, prime and composite, perfect, abundant and deficient, and contains the earliest surviving Greek multiplication table as well as what is now known as Nicomachus's theorem, relating sums of cubes to square numbers. Boethius translated and adapted it into Latin, making it the basis of arithmetic teaching in medieval Europe.

The Manual of Harmonics is an important source for Pythagorean music theory, retelling legends about Pythagoras and describing consonances in terms of numerical ratios. Nicomachus also promised, and may have written, larger works on arithmetic, music, geometry and astronomy — the four subjects later grouped as the quadrivium — most of which are lost.

Influence

As a Neopythagorean, Nicomachus viewed numbers as the key to the structure of the universe, and his mystical as well as mathematical treatment of number shaped Greek, Arabic and Latin scholarship through late antiquity and the Middle Ages.
