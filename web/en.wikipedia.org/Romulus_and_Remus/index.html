Romulus and Remus

The Capitoline Wolf with Romulus and Remus

Romulus and Remus are twin brothers whose legendary story gives the founding myth of the city of Rome and the Roman Kingdom. According to tradition, they were the children of Rhea Silvia and the god Mars. After being abandoned as infants, they were suckled by a she-wolf, raised by a shepherd, and went on to found a city on the banks of the Tiber in 753 BC — a project that ended in a quarrel in which Romulus killed Remus.

The legend

Numitor, king of Alba Longa, was deposed by his brother Amulius, who forced Numitor's daughter Rhea Silvia to become a Vestal Virgin. When she bore twins by Mars, Amulius ordered the infants drowned in the Tiber. The basket carrying them washed ashore at the foot of the Palatine Hill, where a she-wolf (the Lupa Capitolina) suckled them and a woodpecker fed them, until the shepherd Faustulus and his wife Acca Larentia took them in.

As young men the twins restored their grandfather Numitor to his throne, then resolved to found a city of their own. They quarrelled over the site and over the interpretation of omens: Remus, on the Aventine, saw six vultures; Romulus, on the Palatine, saw twelve. In the ensuing dispute — in the best-known version, Remus mockingly leapt over Romulus's newly built wall — Romulus killed his brother, declaring, "So perish every one that shall hereafter leap over my walls."

Foundation of Rome

Romulus then founded Rome on the Palatine Hill, traditionally dated to 21 April 753 BC, became its first king, populated it by offering asylum to outlaws and fugitives, and arranged the abduction of the Sabine women to provide wives for his followers.

Historicity

The tale is regarded as myth rather than history, though it may preserve memories of early settlement on the Palatine and of conflict between neighbouring hill communities. The she-wolf became a lasting emblem of Rome.
