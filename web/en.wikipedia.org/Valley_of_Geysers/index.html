Valley of Geysers

The Valley of Geysers (Russian: Долина гейзеров, Dolina geizerov) is a geyser field on the Kamchatka Peninsula, Russia, and has the second largest concentration of geysers in the world. This 6 km (3.7 mi) long basin with approximately ninety geysers and many hot springs is situated on the left bank of the deep Geysernaya River valley, into which geothermal waters from a relatively young stratovolcano, Kikhpinych, flow. It is part of the Kronotsky Nature Reserve, which, in turn, is incorporated into the World Heritage Site "Volcanoes of Kamchatka". The valley is difficult to reach, with helicopters providing the only feasible means of transport.

History

The valley was discovered in 1941 by the local scientist Tatyana Ustinova, who published her findings fourteen years later. Biannual scientific expeditions came to the valley since 1975, and the site became a popular tourist attraction in the 1990s after the collapse of the Soviet Union.

The 2007 mudslide

On 3 June 2007, a massive mudflow inundated two thirds of the valley. Oleg Mitvol of Russia's Ministry of Natural Resources said "we witnessed a unique natural event, but the consequences of such a natural catastrophe are irreversible". A large natural dam formed over the Geysernaya River, creating Geysernoye Lake. Several of the largest geysers were buried or damaged, though others were later partially uncovered as the water level receded.

Geology

The area is notable for its concentration of geysing phenomena in a relatively young volcanic landscape. The famous Velikan (Giant) geyser erupts roughly every six to eight hours, throwing a jet of water more than 25 metres into the air. Temperatures in parts of the basin are extreme, and the valley supports a unique microclimate with lush vegetation, including rare species of plants dependent on the warm soil.
