Affective neuroscience

Affective neuroscience is the study of how the brain creates emotional responses. It integrates psychology and neuroscience to explore how brain structures and chemicals influence emotions and behavior in animals and humans. The term was coined by neuroscientist Jaak Panksepp in the early 1990s.

Core emotional systems

Panksepp proposed seven primary emotional systems, labeled SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, LUST, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, and PLAY, arising from subcortical brain circuits shared across mammals. Other researchers emphasize the amygdala's role in fear and salience, the insula in interoception and disgust, and prefrontal regions in emotion regulation.

Methods

The field uses functional neuroimaging (fMRI, PET), lesion studies, animal models, and pharmacological manipulation. Its findings inform research on depression, anxiety disorders, and addiction, and have influenced affective computing.
