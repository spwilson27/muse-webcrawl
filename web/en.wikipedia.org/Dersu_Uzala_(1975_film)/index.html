Dersu Uzala (Russian: Дерсу Узала) is a 1975 Soviet-Japanese biographical adventure film directed by Akira Kurosawa. Based on the memoirs of Russian explorer Vladimir Arsenyev, it recounts his expeditions in the Sikhote-Alin region of the Russian Far East with the Nanai hunter Dersu Uzala, played by Maxim Munzuk, with Yury Solomin as Arsenyev.

Production

The film was Kurosawa's only work in Russian and his only film made outside Japan; it was produced by Mosfilm after a difficult period in the director's career. It was shot in 70mm in the Siberian wilderness over about two years, in punishing weather conditions.

Reception

The film won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film in 1976 (for the Soviet Union) and the Golden Prize at the Moscow International Film Festival. It is widely praised for its epic cinematography of the taiga and its meditation on friendship, civilisation and the natural world.
