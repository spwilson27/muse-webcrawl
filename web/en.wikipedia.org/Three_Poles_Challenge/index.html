The three "poles": North, South, and the summit of Everest

The Three Poles Challenge is an adventurer's challenge to reach the three extreme points sometimes called the Earth's poles: the North Pole, the South Pole, and the summit of Mount Everest, regarded as the "third pole" because of its altitude.

History

Norwegian explorer Erling Kagge became the first person to complete all three: he reached the North Pole in 1990, summited Everest in 1994 and skied solo and unsupported to the South Pole in 1992–93, completing the set in 1994. Kagge also accomplished the feat without supplementary oxygen on Everest.

Variations

Participants debate what counts as reaching the geographic poles: full-length journeys begin from the coast of Antarctica or from land in the Arctic, while "last degree" trips cover only the final 60 nautical miles. Some adventurers combine the challenge with the Seven Summits in the Explorers Grand Slam. Fastest and youngest completions, and completions on foot versus by other means, are tracked by the polar guiding community, though only a few dozen people have achieved all three goals.

Notable completers

Besides Kagge, completers include British adventurer Adrian Hayes, who made the fastest recognised completion in 2006–2007, and Park Young-seok of South Korea, the first Asian to complete the challenge together with the Seven Summits.
