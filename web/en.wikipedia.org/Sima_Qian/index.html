Sima Qian

Bornc. 145 BC, Xia Yang (near modern Hancheng, Shaanxi)
Diedc. 86 BC
OccupationHistorian, astrologer, court official
Notable workRecords of the Grand Historian (Shiji)

Sima Qian (c. 145 – c. 86 BC) was a Chinese historian of the Western Han dynasty. He is considered the father of Chinese historiography for his Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji), a monumental history of China spanning from the age of the legendary Yellow Emperor to his own time, covering some two thousand years.

Life

Sima Qian inherited the post of court astrologer-historian (taishi ling) from his father, Sima Tan, who had begun the great history; on his deathbed the elder Sima charged his son to complete it. Sima Qian travelled widely through China in his youth, visiting historical sites and collecting traditions. In 99 BC he incurred the wrath of Emperor Wu by defending the defeated general Li Ling; given the choice between execution and castration, he chose castration so that he might live to finish his father's work — a decision he explained in his famous Letter to Ren An.

The Shiji

Completed around 91 BC, the Shiji comprises 130 chapters organised into basic annals, chronological tables, treatises, hereditary houses, and ranked biographies. Its biographical format became the model for all subsequent Chinese dynastic histories, and its vivid narrative style has made it a classic of Chinese literature as well as history.
