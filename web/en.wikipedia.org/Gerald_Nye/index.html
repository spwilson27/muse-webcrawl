Gerald Prentice Nye (19 December 1892 – 17 July 1971) was an American politician who represented North Dakota in the United States Senate from 1925 to 1945. A progressive Republican and leading isolationist, he is best remembered as chairman of the Special Committee on Investigation of the Munitions Industry, the "Nye Committee", in the 1930s.

Early life and Senate career

Nye was born in Hortonville, Wisconsin, and worked as a newspaper editor in Wisconsin, Iowa and North Dakota. Appointed to the Senate in 1925 to fill a vacancy, he won election in his own right and became known for supporting farm relief and for helping expose the Teapot Dome scandal era corruption.

Nye Committee

From 1934 to 1936 Nye chaired the Senate investigation into the profits and political influence of American arms manufacturers during the First World War. The committee's hearings popularised the "merchants of death" thesis — the idea that munitions makers had pushed the United States into the war for profit — and helped inspire the Neutrality Acts of the 1930s restricting American involvement in foreign conflicts.

Isolationism and later life

Before and during the Second World War Nye was a prominent supporter of the America First Committee and opposed aid to Britain and American intervention. His association with anti-interventionist causes, and remarks criticised as antisemitic, damaged his standing; he lost his Senate seat in the 1944 election. He later worked for the Federal Housing Administration and in private business, and died in Washington, D.C. in 1971.
