Arthur Robert Ashe Jr. (July 10, 1943 – February 6, 1993) was an American professional tennis player. He won three Grand Slam singles titles — the first at the inaugural US Open of the Open Era in 1968, when he was still an amateur — and remains the only black man ever to win the singles title at Wimbledon, the US Open, and the Australian Open. He was ranked world No. 1 by several sources and won 76 career titles in singles and doubles.

Career

Born and raised in segregated Richmond, Virginia, Ashe was mentored by Dr. Robert Walter Johnson, who had also coached Althea Gibson. He starred at UCLA, won the NCAA singles and doubles titles in 1965, and after his 1968 US Open triumph turned professional in 1969. His most famous victory came at Wimbledon in 1975, when he out-thought defending champion Jimmy Connors in the final. Ashe also captained the United States Davis Cup team and won the competition as a player in 1968, 1969, and 1970.

Activism and later life

Ashe was a prominent civil rights and anti-apartheid campaigner, co-founding the Association of Tennis Professionals and later the Arthur Ashe Foundation for the Defeat of AIDS. Heart problems forced his retirement in 1980; he contracted HIV from a blood transfusion during heart surgery and announced his illness in 1992. He died of AIDS-related pneumonia in February 1993. The main stadium of the US Open, Arthur Ashe Stadium, is named in his honour.
