Gulf of Riga

The Gulf of Riga, bordered by Latvia and Estonia.

The Gulf of Riga (Latvian: Rīgas līcis; Estonian: Liivi laht, "Livonian Gulf") is a bay of the Baltic Sea between Latvia and Estonia. It covers about 18,000 km², with a maximum depth of about 67 metres. The Estonian islands of Saaremaa and Muhu partially separate it from the rest of the Baltic, with the Irbe Strait as its main western outlet.

Geography

The principal river flowing into the gulf is the Daugava (Western Dvina), reaching the sea at the Latvian capital, Riga, which sits at the gulf's southern shore. Other rivers include the Lielupe, Gauja, Salaca and Pärnu. The shores are mostly low and sandy; important ports include Riga, Jūrmala, Pärnu and Ventspils (via the Irbe Strait). The gulf freezes in most winters.

Ecology

The brackish waters support herring, sprat and flounder fisheries. The island of Ruhnu, in the middle of the gulf, belongs to Estonia.
