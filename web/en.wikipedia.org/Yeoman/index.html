Yeoman

ContextLate medieval and early modern England
MeaningFreeholder farmer below the gentry; also a household or naval rank

A yeoman was a member of a social class in England ranking below the gentry: a freeholder who owned and cultivated his own land. The term later broadened to denote small independent farmers generally, and also specific offices, including yeomen of the guard and the naval rating of yeoman.

Historical role

Yeomen emerged as a distinct class in the late Middle Ages, typically holding land worth above the threshold that qualified them for jury service and, later, the county franchise. They formed a backbone of English rural society, supplied archers to medieval armies, and were celebrated in literature as sturdy, independent figures.

Later uses

In the United States, "yeoman farmer" described independent family farmers, an ideal invoked by Thomas Jefferson. In the Royal Navy and US Navy, a yeoman is a rating responsible for clerical and administrative work.
