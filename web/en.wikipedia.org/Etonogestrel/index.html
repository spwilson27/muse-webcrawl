Etonogestrel is a progestin medication used in hormonal contraception. It is the active metabolite of desogestrel and is best known as the hormone contained in the single-rod subdermal contraceptive implant marketed as Nexplanon (and formerly Implanon), and, combined with ethinylestradiol, in the vaginal ring NuvaRing.

Contraceptive implant

The etonogestrel implant is a small, flexible plastic rod about 4 cm long that is inserted under the skin of the upper arm by a healthcare professional. It releases a low, steady dose of etonogestrel — initially around 60–70 micrograms per day, declining over time — and provides contraception for at least three years. It is among the most effective reversible methods of birth control, with a failure rate below 0.1 per cent per year, because it does not depend on user action after insertion.

Mechanism and side effects

Etonogestrel works primarily by suppressing ovulation, and secondarily by thickening cervical mucus. The most common side effect is irregular menstrual bleeding, including amenorrhoea or unpredictable spotting, which is also the most frequent reason for removal. Other reported effects include headache, acne, breast tenderness and mood changes. The rod is radiopaque in the Nexplanon version, allowing localisation by X-ray if it cannot be felt.
