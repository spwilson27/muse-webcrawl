Flensburg

Flensburg (Danish and Low Saxon: Flensborg) is an independent town in the north of the German state of Schleswig-Holstein, on the border with Denmark. With a population of around 90,000, it lies at the head of the Flensburg Firth (Flensburger Förde), the westernmost inlet of the Baltic Sea, and is the northernmost major town in Germany. Flensburg is known as a former rum-trading port, the seat of the German federal traffic-offender register (the "points in Flensburg"), and the last seat of government of Nazi Germany in May 1945.

History

Flensburg was founded in the thirteenth century by Danish settlers and received its town charter in 1284. For centuries it belonged to the Duchy of Schleswig under the Danish crown and grew wealthy as a shipping town: in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries its merchants traded extensively with the Danish West Indies (now the U.S. Virgin Islands), importing raw rum, sugar, and spices, and Flensburg's rum houses blended and sold rum across northern Europe — a heritage preserved by the Maritime Museum and surviving rum firms such as Johannsen (est. 1878). After the Second Schleswig War of 1864, the town passed to Prussia and then the German Empire. In the 1920 Schleswig plebiscites, Flensburg voted to remain with Germany while the zone to its north joined Denmark, fixing the modern border just north of the town.

The Flensburg Government

In May 1945, after Adolf Hitler's suicide, his successor Grand Admiral Karl Dönitz established his government at the naval academy at Mürwik in Flensburg. This "Flensburg Government" attempted to negotiate a partial surrender to the Western Allies and administered Germany's unconditional surrender on 8 May, but was dissolved by the Allies on 23 May 1945 when Dönitz and his ministers were arrested — making Flensburg, briefly, the final capital of the Third Reich.

Modern town

Today Flensburg is home to the Europa-Universität Flensburg and a university of applied sciences, the Naval Academy Mürwik of the German Navy, shipbuilding, and the Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt (Federal Motor Transport Authority), whose traffic penalty point register gives German drivers their "Punkte in Flensburg". The town has a significant Danish minority with Danish schools, a library, and the Danish-language newspaper Flensborg Avis; the handball club SG Flensburg-Handewitt is one of Germany's most successful, winning the EHF Champions League in 2014.
