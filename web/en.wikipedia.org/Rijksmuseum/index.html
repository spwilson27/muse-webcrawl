The Rijksmuseum (Dutch: [ˈrɛiksmyˌzeːjʏm]) is the national museum of the Netherlands dedicated to Dutch arts and history, located in Amsterdam. It is situated at the Museum Square (Museumplein) in the borough of Amsterdam South, close to the Van Gogh Museum, the Stedelijk Museum Amsterdam, and the Concertgebouw.

The Rijksmuseum was founded in The Hague on 19 November 1798 and moved to Amsterdam in 1808, where it was first located in the Royal Palace and later in the Trippenhuis. The current main building was designed by Pierre Cuypers and opened in 1885. On 13 April 2013, after a ten-year renovation which cost €375 million, the main building was reopened by Queen Beatrix.

Collection

The museum has on display 8,000 objects of art and history from a total collection of 1 million objects from the years 1200–2000, among which are some masterpieces by Rembrandt, Frans Hals, and Johannes Vermeer. The museum also has a small Asian collection, which is on display in the Asian pavilion.

The best-known work in the collection is Rembrandt's The Night Watch (De Nachtwacht, 1642), which occupies the central Gallery of Honour. Other highlights include Vermeer's The Milkmaid, Jan Steen's genre scenes, and a large collection of Delftware. The Rijksmuseum Research Library is the largest public art history research library in the Netherlands.

Building

The building, designed by Pierre Cuypers in a mixture of Gothic and Renaissance elements, is constructed largely of red brick. A distinctive feature is the passageway (now known as the Rijksmuseum passage) running through the building, restored to public use during the 2003–2013 renovation by Spanish architects Cruz y Ortiz.
