The University of Sussex is a public research university located in Falmer, East Sussex, England. It received its Royal Charter in August 1961, the first of the plate glass university generation, and was a founding member of the 1994 Group of research-intensive universities.

History

Proposals for a university in Brighton date to the 19th century, but the scheme was blocked by the First World War. The idea was revived in the 1950s, and in 1959 Brighton County Borough Council approved the construction of a campus at Falmer. The university opened to its first students in October 1961 and received its Royal Charter the same year. It grew rapidly in the 1960s and became known for an interdisciplinary approach, organising teaching into schools of studies rather than traditional departments.

Campus

The campus, designed principally by architect Sir Basil Spence, lies at Falmer, about 4 miles (6.4 km) from Brighton city centre, at the edge of the South Downs National Park. Several campus buildings are listed, including Falmer House, which is Grade I listed. The campus is noted for its population of wildlife and extensive parkland.

Academics and research

Sussex counts five Nobel Prize winners among its faculty, including Sir Anthony Leggett, Sir Paul Nurse and Sir Harry Kroto, whose team discovered the buckminsterfullerene molecule at Sussex. The university is highly ranked for development studies, and its Institute of Development Studies (IDS), founded in 1966, is based on campus. Brighton and Sussex Medical School, run jointly with the University of Brighton, opened in 2003.

Student life

The University of Sussex Students' Union runs societies, media outlets including the student newspaper The Badger, and the campus bars and venues. The university's teams compete in British Universities and Colleges Sport under the name Sussex.
