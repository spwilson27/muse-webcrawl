The Apu Trilogy

The Apu Trilogy comprises three Bengali-language films directed by Satyajit Ray: Pather Panchali (1955), Aparajito (1956) and Apur Sansar (The World of Apu, 1959). They follow the life of Apu Roy, a boy growing up in rural Bengal, from childhood through adolescence to marriage and fatherhood. Widely regarded among the greatest films ever made, the trilogy was foundational for India's parallel cinema movement and brought Indian filmmaking to international attention.

The films

Pather Panchali ("Song of the Little Road") depicts the impoverished childhood of Apu and his sister Durga in a Bengal village, with their parents Harihar and Sarbajaya and an aged aunt. It won a special prize at Cannes and launched Ray's career. Aparajito ("The Unvanquished") follows the family's move to Varanasi, the father's death, and Apu's education in Calcutta, straining his bond with his mother; it won the Golden Lion at Venice. Apur Sansar ("The World of Apu") shows the adult Apu, an aspiring writer, entering an improvised marriage with Aparna, losing her in childbirth, and finally reconciling with his young son.

Production and legacy

Ray, an advertising artist, was inspired by Italian neorealism, especially Bicycle Thieves, and shot the first film with non-professional actors and cinematographer Subrata Mitra on a shoestring budget. Ravi Shankar's sitar scores became celebrated in their own right. The trilogy regularly appears in critics' polls of the greatest films of all time and influenced directors worldwide; it was fully restored by the Criterion Collection and Janus Films in 2015 after the original negatives, damaged by fire, were reconstructed.
