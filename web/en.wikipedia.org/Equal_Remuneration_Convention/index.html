Equal Remuneration Convention

ILO Convention C100

Adopted
29 June 1951, Geneva
In force
23 May 1953
Subject
Equal pay for work of equal value

The Equal Remuneration Convention, formally the Convention Concerning Equal Remuneration for Men and Women Workers for Work of Equal Value, is an International Labour Organization convention adopted in 1951 as ILO Convention No. 100. It is one of the eight ILO fundamental conventions.

Provisions

States ratifying the convention undertake to promote and ensure the application of the principle of equal remuneration for men and women workers for work of equal value, through legislation, wage-setting machinery, or collective agreements. "Remuneration" is defined broadly to include wages and any additional emoluments, in cash or kind. The convention encourages objective appraisal of jobs to compare their value.

Ratification and influence

The convention has been ratified by the large majority of ILO member states — more than 170. Its principle of "equal pay for work of equal value" (broader than "equal pay for equal work") influenced subsequent instruments, including Article 141 of the Treaty of Rome (1957) in European law and the Equal Pay Act 1970 in the United Kingdom.
