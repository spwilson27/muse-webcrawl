House of Commons of England

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The House of Commons of England was the lower house of the Parliament of England (which also included the monarch and the House of Lords) from its development in the 14th century until the union of England and Scotland in 1707, when it was replaced by the House of Commons of Great Britain.

Type: Lower house
Established: 14th century
Disbanded: 1 May 1707
Meeting place: Palace of Westminster, London
Succeeded by: House of Commons of Great Britain

Origins

The Parliament of England developed out of the magnum concilium, the great council that advised the king in the High Middle Ages. In 1265 Simon de Montfort summoned a parliament that included two knights from each county and two burgesses from each borough — the first time the “commons” (representatives of communities, from the Norman French communes) were present. Edward I’s Model Parliament of 1295 regularised this representation.

Development

By the mid-14th century the knights and burgesses were meeting separately from the nobility and clergy, forming their own house. The Commons gradually acquired a Speaker (the first holder of the title being Sir Thomas Hungerford in 1377), control over taxation grants, and, after the Glorious Revolution of 1688, a dominant position in the constitution as the house that determined the ministry’s survival through supply.

Membership

Members were returned for counties (knights of the shire) and parliamentary boroughs. The franchise was uneven: county voters were generally the forty-shilling freeholders (from a statute of 1430), while borough franchises varied widely, and many small “rotten boroughs” were controlled by patrons. At the Union there were 513 members for England and Wales.

Abolition

The Acts of Union 1707 merged the English and Scottish parliaments. The last English Commons simply carried over, with 45 Scottish members added, to form the first House of Commons of Great Britain; the same body, after 1801, became the House of Commons of the United Kingdom.
