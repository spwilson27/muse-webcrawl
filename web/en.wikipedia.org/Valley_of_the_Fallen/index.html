Valley of the Fallen

The Valley of the Fallen (Spanish: Valle de los Caídos) is a monumental complex near Madrid, Spain, built between 1940 and 1958 under the dictatorship of Francisco Franco. Located in the Sierra de Guadarrama, in the municipality of San Lorenzo de El Escorial, it comprises a Catholic basilica excavated into a granite mountain, a large abbey, and a 150-metre (490 ft) high cross that remains the tallest memorial cross in the world.

History

Franco ordered the construction shortly after the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) as a memorial to those who had died in the conflict. Much of the construction work was carried out by Republican prisoners of war under a programme of forced labour ("redención de penas por el trabajo"), a fact that has made the site one of the most controversial monuments in modern Spain.

The basilica, dedicated to Our Lady of the Pillar, was consecrated in 1960. Franco himself was buried behind its high altar upon his death in 1975. José Antonio Primo de Rivera, founder of the Falange, was also interred there until his remains were exhumed in 2023. The crypts contain the remains of more than 33,000 Civil War dead from both sides, many transferred without the consent of their families.

Exhumation of Franco

After years of debate, and following Spain's 2007 Historical Memory Law, the Spanish government removed Franco's remains from the basilica on 24 October 2019 and reinterred them in the Mingorrubio cemetery in El Pardo, Madrid. The site was officially renamed Valle de Cuelgamuros under the 2022 Democratic Memory Law.
