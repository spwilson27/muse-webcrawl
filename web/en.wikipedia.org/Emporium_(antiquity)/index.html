Emporium (antiquity)

An emporium (plural emporia), in ancient Greece and Rome, was a trading post, market town, or district set aside for foreign merchants to conduct commerce, often at a port and under special legal arrangements.

Characteristics

Derived from the Greek emporion, emporia facilitated exchange between different peoples and legal systems. Examples include Naukratis in Egypt, the sole Greek trading post permitted there, Piraeus for Athens, and Ostia and later Portus for Rome. Emporia frequently had warehouses, temples serving foreign cults, and officials overseeing trade disputes.

Later use

The term was reused for early medieval trading settlements in northwestern Europe, such as Dorestad and Hedeby, and survives in general use for a large retail store.
