Academy Award for Best International Feature Film

Academy Award for Best International Feature Film
Formerly: Best Foreign Language Film
Presented by: Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences
First awarded: 1947 (honorary) / 1956 (competitive)

The Academy Award for Best International Feature Film (known as Best Foreign Language Film prior to 2020) is one of the Academy Awards handed out annually by the U.S.-based Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences. It is given to a feature-length motion picture produced outside the United States with a predominantly non-English dialogue track.

History

Before the category became competitive in 1956, the Academy presented honorary awards to notable foreign films, including Shoeshine (1947) and Bicycle Thieves (1949). The competitive award debuted at the 29th Academy Awards, won by Italy's La Strada.

Eligibility and process

Each country is invited to submit one film, selected by a designated national organization. A shortlist is announced before the final five nominees. The award is officially accepted by the film's director on behalf of the submitting country.

Records

Italy has won the most awards in the category, followed by France and Japan. Notable winners include 8½, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon, Amour, and Parasite.
