Percopsiformes

OrderPercopsiformes
Families3 (Percopsidae, Aphredoderidae, Amblyopsidae)
RangeFresh waters of North America

The Percopsiformes are a small order of freshwater ray-finned fishes found in North America, comprising about ten living species. The order includes the trout-perches (family Percopsidae), the pirate perch (Aphredoderidae), and the cavefishes (Amblyopsidae).

Characteristics

Percopsiforms are small fishes with features combining those of soft-rayed and spiny-rayed groups, which historically made their classification puzzling. The trout-perches (Percopsis) have an adipose fin like a trout but spines in the dorsal and anal fins like a perch. Amblyopsid cavefishes — such as the northern cavefish and the Ozark cavefish — live in subterranean waters and show various degrees of eye loss and depigmentation.

Evolution

Fossil percopsiforms, including forms from the Eocene Green River Formation and Cretaceous deposits, show the group was formerly more widespread and diverse. Molecular studies place the order among the paracanthopterygian fishes, related to cods and dories.
