Douglas Hyde, scholar and first President of Ireland

Douglas Hyde (Irish: Dubhghlas de hÍde; 17 January 1860 – 12 July 1949), who published poetry under the pseudonym An Craoibhín Aoibhinn, was an Irish academic, linguist, scholar of the Irish language and politician. A key figure in the Gaelic revival, he founded the Gaelic League (Conradh na Gaeilge) in 1893 to promote the Irish language, and he served as the first President of Ireland from June 1938 to June 1945.

Early life and scholarship

Hyde was born in Castlerea, County Roscommon, the son of a Church of Ireland rector, and grew up at Frenchpark near Lough Gara, where he learned Irish from local speakers. He studied at Trinity College Dublin, where he took degrees in modern languages, law and divinity. He became a noted collector of Irish folklore and songs, publishing collections such as Beside the Fire (1890) and the landmark Love Songs of Connacht (1893), and wrote the first modern history of Irish literature, A Literary History of Ireland (1899).

Gaelic League

In his 1892 lecture "The Necessity for De-Anglicising Ireland", Hyde argued that Ireland should preserve its own language and customs. The following year he co-founded the Gaelic League and served as its president until 1915, when he resigned as the movement became increasingly politicised; Hyde insisted the League remain non-political and non-sectarian. He became the first professor of Irish at University College Dublin in 1909 under the new National University of Ireland.

President of Ireland

Hyde was appointed to the Seanad in 1938 and then chosen, by agreement of all parties, as the first President of Ireland under the 1937 Constitution. He took the oath of office in Irish and moved into Áras an Uachtaráin in Phoenix Park. Despite a severe stroke in 1940, he remained in office until 1945.

Death and legacy

Hyde died in Dublin in 1949 and was buried at Portahard cemetery, County Roscommon, after a state funeral. His former home, Ratra House in Frenchpark, is now the Douglas Hyde Interpretive Centre. He appears on the Irish 50-pound note series and is remembered as a unifying, non-partisan head of state and a central figure of the Irish cultural revival.
