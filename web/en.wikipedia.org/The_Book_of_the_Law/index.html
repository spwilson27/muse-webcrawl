The Book of the Law

The Book of the Law, also known by its Latin title Liber AL vel Legis, is the central sacred text of Thelema, the philosophical and religious system founded by the English occultist Aleister Crowley. Crowley said the book was dictated to him in Cairo, Egypt, over three days in April 1904 by a praeterhuman intelligence named Aiwass, whom he later identified as his Holy Guardian Angel.

Content

The book consists of three chapters, each said to have been written in one hour at noon on 8, 9 and 10 April 1904. Each chapter is spoken by a deity: the first by Nuit, Egyptian goddess of the stars; the second by Hadit, her consort; and the third by Ra-Hoor-Khuit, a form of Horus, god of war and vengeance.

Its best-known pronouncements state the Law of Thelema: "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law" and "Love is the law, love under will." The text prophesies the Aeon of Horus, a new age of humanity, and contains mystical passages, number ciphers and injunctions that Crowley and his followers regarded as divinely inspired and not to be altered — Crowley directed that the text be printed exactly and warned against discussing its contents in certain ways.

Reception and influence
