Acts of Union 1800

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Acts of Union 1800 were two complementary Acts of Parliament—one passed by the Parliament of Great Britain and one by the Parliament of Ireland—that united the Kingdom of Great Britain and the Kingdom of Ireland to create the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland, with effect from 1 January 1801.

Background

Great Britain and Ireland had shared a monarch since 1541, but Ireland retained its own parliament in Dublin. The Irish Rebellion of 1798, in which French troops landed to support the United Irishmen, convinced the British government of William Pitt the Younger that a legislative union was essential for security and to resolve the question of Catholic emancipation, which could not safely be passed by the Protestant Ascendancy parliament alone.

Passage

A first union proposal was defeated in the Irish House of Commons in 1799. The government of Lord Cornwallis and Lord Castlereagh then undertook an extensive campaign of patronage—peerages, pensions, offices and compensation for the owners of disfranchised boroughs—to secure a majority. The measures passed both parliaments in 1800. The terms gave Ireland 100 MPs in the Commons and 32 members of the Lords (28 temporal and 4 spiritual) at Westminster, merged customs and trade, and united the Churches of England and Ireland. Pitt's expectation that Catholic emancipation would follow was blocked by George III, prompting Pitt's resignation in 1801; emancipation was not achieved until 1829.

Aftermath

The Union lasted until the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 and the creation of the Irish Free State in 1922, after which Northern Ireland remained in the United Kingdom and the state's name was changed in 1927 to the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. The Union flag adopted in 1801, combining the crosses of St George, St Andrew and St Patrick, remains the flag of the United Kingdom.
