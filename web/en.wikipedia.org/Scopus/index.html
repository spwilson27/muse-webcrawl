Scopus

Scopus is a scientific abstract and citation database launched by the academic publisher Elsevier in 2004. It covers scientific journals, books, and conference proceedings across the life sciences, social sciences, physical sciences, and health sciences.

Coverage

Scopus indexes content from more than 25,000 active titles published by over 7,000 publishers, amounting to tens of millions of records, many with references dating back to 1970 and earlier. Titles are selected by an independent Content Selection and Advisory Board according to criteria that include peer review, publishing regularity, and citation activity.

Features

The database provides citation tracking, author profiles with automatic disambiguation, affiliation profiles, and analytical tools. Journal-level metrics associated with Scopus data include CiteScore, SJR (SCImago Journal Rank), and SNIP (Source Normalized Impact per Paper). Scopus data underpins several university ranking and research evaluation systems.

Comparison with other databases

Scopus is a principal competitor of the Web of Science. Comparative studies generally find that Scopus has broader coverage of recent conference proceedings and international journals, while Web of Science has deeper historical coverage; both are widely used in scientometrics and bibliometrics.
