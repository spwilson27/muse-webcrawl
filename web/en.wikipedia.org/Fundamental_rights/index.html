Fundamental rights

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fundamental rights are a group of rights that have been recognized by a high degree of protection from encroachment. These rights are specifically identified in a constitution, or have been found under the principles of due process of law. They typically include civil and political rights such as freedom of speech, religion, and assembly, equality before the law, and the right to a fair trial.

In constitutional systems

In India, Part III of the Constitution guarantees Fundamental Rights — including equality, freedom, protection against exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural and educational rights, and constitutional remedies — which are enforceable by the courts, with citizens able to petition the Supreme Court directly under Article 32. In the European Union, the Charter of Fundamental Rights, given binding force by the Treaty of Lisbon in 2009, catalogues rights concerning dignity, freedoms, equality, solidarity, citizens' rights, and justice.

In the United States, courts distinguish "fundamental rights" protected by substantive due process under the Fourteenth Amendment, to which restrictions are subject to strict scrutiny; these have included rights to marry, to direct the upbringing of children, and to privacy in certain intimate and reproductive decisions, though the scope of unenumerated rights remains contested.

Relationship to human rights

Fundamental rights overlap with, but are not identical to, human rights as proclaimed in international instruments such as the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948). Fundamental rights are usually those portions of human rights that a particular legal system has entrenched in its constitution and made judicially enforceable.
