Generalized coordinates

Generalized coordinates are a set of parameters that completely describe the configuration of a system relative to a reference configuration, chosen so that the number of parameters equals the system's degrees of freedom. They are a central device in analytical mechanics, especially Lagrangian mechanics, where they simplify problems with constraints.

Definition and examples

Any convenient quantities may serve as generalized coordinates — angles, distances, areas — provided they uniquely fix the positions of all parts of the system. For a simple pendulum, a single angle θ from the vertical suffices, automatically enforcing the constraint that the bob stays on its arc. For a double pendulum, two angles are needed. Cartesian coordinates are a special case.

Generalized velocities and momenta

The time derivatives of the generalized coordinates are the generalized velocities. In Lagrangian mechanics, equations of motion take the Euler–Lagrange form in these coordinates. In Hamiltonian mechanics, each generalized coordinate has a conjugate generalized momentum, and coordinates whose absence from the Lagrangian implies a conserved momentum are called cyclic or ignorable.

Advantages

Generalized coordinates build holonomic constraints into the description, reducing the number of variables and eliminating constraint forces from the equations — a decisive advantage for complex systems such as linkages, rigid bodies, and robots.
