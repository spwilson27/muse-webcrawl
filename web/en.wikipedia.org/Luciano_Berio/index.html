Luciano Berio (24 October 1925 – 27 May 2003) was an Italian composer widely known for his experimental work, in particular his composition Sinfonia (1968) and his series of virtuoso solo pieces titled Sequenza, and for his pioneering work in electronic music.

Life

Berio was born in Oneglia, Liguria. He learned piano from his father and grandfather, both organists and composers. After injuring his hand while conscripted at the end of the Second World War, he turned from performance to composition, studying at the Milan Conservatory under Giulio Cesare Paribeni and Giorgio Federico Ghedini, and later with Luigi Dallapiccola at Tanglewood in the United States, where he also met and began his studies of serialism. He became one of the leading figures of the European post-war avant-garde, and taught in the United States, including at the Juilliard School and Harvard University.

Music

With Bruno Maderna, Berio founded the Studio di fonologia in Milan in 1955, one of the first electronic music studios. His electronic works include Thema (Omaggio a Joyce) (1958), based on the voice of his first wife, the singer Cathy Berberian.

Sinfonia (1968), written for the New York Philharmonic and the Swingle Singers, collages quotations from Mahler and many other composers with spoken texts from Lévi-Strauss, Beckett and other sources; it is often cited as a landmark of postmodern music. The fourteen Sequenzas (1958–2002) explore the virtuoso possibilities of individual instruments. Berio also became known for his arrangements and "transcriptions" of music by earlier composers, and for the Folk Songs cycle (1964) written for Berberian.
