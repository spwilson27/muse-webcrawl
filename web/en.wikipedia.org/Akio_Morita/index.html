Akio Morita (26 January 1921 – 3 October 1999) was a Japanese businessman and co-founder of Sony Corporation alongside Masaru Ibuka. Born into a family of sake brewers in Nagoya, he studied physics at Osaka Imperial University and served as a naval officer during World War II, where he met Ibuka.

Sony

In 1946 Morita and Ibuka founded Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo, renamed Sony in 1958. Morita handled marketing and international business while Ibuka led engineering. He championed products such as transistor radios, the Trinitron television, and the Walkman (1979), which he personally promoted. Morita established Sony's American operations and made Sony one of the first Japanese companies listed on the New York Stock Exchange.

Later life

Morita co-authored the controversial essay "The Japan That Can Say No" (1989) and the memoir Made in Japan (1986). He suffered a stroke in 1993 and died in Tokyo in 1999.
