Giorgio Vasari

Giorgio Vasari (30 July 1511 – 27 June 1574) was an Italian Renaissance painter, architect, writer, and historian, best known for his Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects (Le Vite), first published in 1550 and expanded in 1568 — a work regarded as the foundation of Western art history.

Born in Arezzo, Tuscany, Vasari trained in Florence and became a protégé of the Medici family, for whom he worked throughout his career. As an architect he designed the Uffizi building in Florence (begun 1560), now one of the world's great art museums, and the famous elevated Vasari Corridor linking the Palazzo Vecchio to the Palazzo Pitti across the Ponte Vecchio. As a painter he decorated, among other projects, the Sala dei Cento Giorni in the Palazzo della Cancelleria in Rome and rooms in the Palazzo Vecchio, working in the Mannerist style.

The Lives

Le Vite de' più eccellenti pittori, scultori, e architettori presents biographies of Italian artists from Cimabue and Giotto to Vasari's own time, culminating in Michelangelo, the only living artist included in the first edition. The work established a model of art-historical narrative — progress from primitive beginnings to a peak in the High Renaissance — that shaped the discipline for centuries, and coined or popularised terms such as "Renaissance" (rinascita, "rebirth") and "Gothic" as a term of disparagement. Although later scholarship has corrected many of Vasari's anecdotes and judgements, the Lives remains an indispensable source.

Vasari was also a co-founder of Florence's Accademia delle Arti del Disegno in 1563. He died in Florence in 1574.
