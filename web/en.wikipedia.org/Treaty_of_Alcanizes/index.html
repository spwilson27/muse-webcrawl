Treaty of Alcanizes

Signed12 September 1297
LocationAlcañices, Castile
PartiesKingdom of Castile; Kingdom of Portugal

The Treaty of Alcanizes (Portuguese: Tratado de Alcanizes; Spanish: Tratado de Alcañices) was signed on 12 September 1297 between Ferdinand IV of Castile and Denis of Portugal. It established the border between the Kingdom of Portugal and the Crown of Castile, much of which survives as the modern frontier between Portugal and Spain, making it one of the oldest borders in Europe.

Terms

The treaty confirmed Portuguese possession of the Algarve and settled disputed territories along the frontier. Portugal ceded some towns east of the Guadiana while retaining others, and the agreement fixed the line along the Guadiana river in the south. The treaty was reinforced by marriage arrangements between the two royal houses.

Legacy

Although later conflicts, including the dispute over Olivenza in the 19th century, modified parts of the frontier, the 1297 settlement remains the foundation of the Portuguese-Spanish border, frequently cited as one of the oldest continuously defined international borders in the world.
