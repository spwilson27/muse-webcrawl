Skagerrak

The Skagerrak is a strait running between the Jutland peninsula of Denmark, the southeast coast of Norway, and the southwest coast of Sweden, connecting the North Sea and the Kattegat sea area, which leads to the Baltic Sea.

Geography

The strait is about 240 km (150 mi) long and between 80 and 140 km (50–87 mi) wide. It deepens toward the Norwegian coast, reaching over 700 metres in the Norwegian Trench. Important ports include Oslo, Kristiansand, and Arendal in Norway; Skagen and Hirtshals in Denmark; and Strömstad and Gothenburg (via the Kattegat approach) in Sweden. Strategically, the Skagerrak is the only natural sea connection, apart from the Kiel Canal, between the North Sea and the Baltic.

History

The name is Dutch in origin, from Skagen (the Danish town at the tip of Jutland) and rak, a straight waterway. During both world wars the strait was strategically vital. The Battle of Jutland, the largest naval battle of the First World War, was fought in and near the Skagerrak in 1916. In the Second World War the strait figured in the German invasion of Denmark and Norway in 1940. The Skagerrak is also a busy shipping route and an important fishing area, and oil and gas installations operate in its waters.
