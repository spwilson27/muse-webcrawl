The Rumaila oil field is a supergiant oil field located in southern Iraq, approximately 50 km west of Basra, near the border with Kuwait. Discovered in 1953 by the Basra Petroleum Company, it is the largest oil field in Iraq and the third-largest in the world, containing an estimated 17 billion barrels of recoverable reserves.

History

Rumaila was nationalised in 1972 and later operated by the state-owned South Oil Company (now Basra Oil Company). During the Gulf War of 1990–91, disputed production from the field — Iraq accused Kuwait of slant drilling into Rumaila — was among the grievances cited before Iraq's invasion of Kuwait. In 2009, Iraq's first post-invasion licensing round awarded a technical service contract for Rumaila to a consortium of BP and PetroChina, which took over operations the following year through the Rumaila Operating Organization.

Production

Production, which had fallen below 1 million barrels per day by the late 2000s, was raised to roughly 1.4–1.5 million barrels per day in the 2010s, making Rumaila responsible for around a third of Iraq's total oil output. The field produces mainly Basra Light and Basra Heavy crude grades exported through terminals in the Persian Gulf.

Geology

Oil is held chiefly in Cretaceous sandstone reservoirs, notably the Mishrif limestone and the Zubair (Third Pay) sandstone, at depths of around 3,000 metres. The structure straddles the Iraq–Kuwait border; the Kuwaiti portion is known as the Ratqa field.
