WAVES

WAVES (Women Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Service) was the women's reserve of the United States Naval Reserve during the Second World War. Authorised by Congress in July 1942 and directed by Mildred H. McAfee, the WAVES enabled women to serve in the Navy in non-combat roles — as clerks, radiomen, air traffic controllers, instructors, pharmacists' mates, and in many other ratings — releasing men for sea duty. At its peak in 1945 the force numbered about 86,000 women, roughly 2.5 percent of the Navy's total strength.

WAVES served in more than 900 shore establishments across the United States.

History

The WAVES were created amid intense debate over women in uniform, and unlike their Army counterpart (initially an auxiliary corps), WAVES were full members of the Naval Reserve from the start. Recruits trained at colleges and naval training stations including Hunter College in New York, Smith College, and Mount Holyoke College. Although initially restricted to service within the continental United States, from late 1944 they could volunteer for Hawaii and Alaska.

After the war most WAVES were demobilised, but the Women's Armed Services Integration Act of 1948 gave women a permanent place in the regular Navy. The WAVES designation persisted as the administrative label for women in the Navy until 1978, when separate women's organisations were disbanded and men and women were fully integrated into the same units and career paths.
