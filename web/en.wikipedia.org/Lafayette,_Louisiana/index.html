Lafayette is a city in south-central Louisiana and the parish seat of Lafayette Parish. It is the centre of Acadiana, the French Louisiana region, and is known for its Cajun and Creole culture, music and cuisine.

History

The town was founded in 1821 as Vermilionville on land donated by Jean Mouton, a French-speaking Acadian. It was renamed Lafayette in 1884 for the Marquis de Lafayette, the French hero of the American Revolution. The arrival of the railroad in 1880 and the founding of the University of Louisiana at Lafayette (1900) spurred growth; oil and gas discoveries in the mid-20th century made it a hub of the petroleum industry.

Culture

Lafayette hosts Festival International de Louisiane, one of the largest free Francophone festivals in the United States, and Festivals Acadiens et Créoles celebrating Cajun and Zydeco music. Vermilionville and Acadian Village preserve Acadian heritage. The city is a centre of Cajun food culture, including gumbo, boudin and crawfish.

Geography and economy

The city lies on the Vermilion River in the Gulf Coastal Plain, about 50 miles southwest of Baton Rouge. Major employers include the university, healthcare, energy services and technology.
