Kew Gardens is a botanic garden in south-west London that houses the world's largest and most diverse botanical and mycological collections, with more than 50,000 living plants. Formally the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, it is a non-departmental public body sponsored by the UK government and a major centre for plant science, conservation, and research.

History

The gardens originated in the exotic garden laid out at Kew Park by Lord Capel John Tewkesbury, and were enlarged by Augusta, Princess of Wales, who founded a garden of about nine acres in 1759 with the help of the botanist Lord Bute. Under Joseph Banks and the first official director, William Hooker, appointed in 1841, Kew became a national institution. The Palm House (built 1844–1848) and Temperate House (designed by Decimus Burton) are among its most famous structures.

Collections and science

Kew's herbarium holds around seven million preserved specimens, and its Millennium Seed Bank at Wakehurst preserves seeds of tens of thousands of species. The gardens include the Princess of Wales Conservatory, the Alpine House, a treetop walkway, Kew Palace, and the ten-storey Great Pagoda completed in 1762. The site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003.
