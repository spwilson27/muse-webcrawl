The Band

The Band was a Canadian-American rock band formed in Toronto, Ontario, in 1967. The group originally consisted of Rick Danko (bass, vocals), Levon Helm (drums, vocals), Garth Hudson (keyboards), Richard Manuel (piano, vocals) and Robbie Robertson (guitar, vocals). They first gained fame as Bob Dylan's backing band — as "the band" behind Dylan on his 1965–66 world tour and the legendary Basement Tapes sessions of 1967 — before emerging under their own name.

History

The members had played together since the early 1960s as the Hawks, the backing group of rockabilly singer Ronnie Hawkins. After separating from Hawkins, they toured as the Canadian Squires and Levon and the Hawks before Dylan recruited them. Retreating to Woodstock, New York, after Dylan's 1966 motorcycle accident, they recorded the informal sessions with Dylan later released in part as The Basement Tapes (1975).

Their debut album, Music from Big Pink (1968), named for the house they rented in West Saugerties, defined a rootsy, ensemble-first alternative to psychedelic rock and contained their signature song "The Weight". The follow-up, The Band (1969), included "Up on Cripple Creek", "The Night They Drove Old Dixie Down" and "Rag Mama Rag". Subsequent albums included Stage Fright (1970) and Cahoots (1971).

The Last Waltz

The original group ended with a farewell concert on Thanksgiving Day, 1976, at Winterland in San Francisco, featuring guests including Dylan, Van Morrison, Neil Young, Joni Mitchell, Eric Clapton and Muddy Waters. Filmed by Martin Scorsese, The Last Waltz (1978) is regarded as one of the greatest concert films ever made. The Band re-formed without Robertson in 1983; Manuel died in 1986, Danko in 1999, Helm in 2012, Robertson in 2023, and Hudson in 2025. The Band was inducted into the Canadian Music Hall of Fame in 1989 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1994, and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2008.
