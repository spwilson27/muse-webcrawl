Spiegel affair

The Spiegel affair of 1962 was a political scandal in the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) involving the news magazine Der Spiegel and Defence Minister Franz Josef Strauss. After the magazine published an article critical of the readiness of the West German armed forces, its offices were raided and its publisher Rudolf Augstein and several editors were arrested on suspicion of treason.

Course of events

The raid, ordered with Strauss's involvement, provoked widespread protests in defence of press freedom. The affair led to a government crisis: FDP ministers resigned, Chancellor Konrad Adenauer reshuffled his cabinet, and Strauss was forced to resign. Charges against Augstein were eventually dropped.

Significance

The affair is regarded as a landmark in the development of West German democracy, strengthening freedom of the press and public scrutiny of government.
