Runestone

A runestone is a stone with a runic inscription, typically raised as a memorial. The tradition began in the 4th century and lasted into the 12th, with most stones dating to the late Viking Age (c. 950–1100). Runestones are found predominantly in Scandinavia, with the greatest concentration — about half of the roughly 3,000 known stones — in Sweden.

Inscriptions

Most Viking Age inscriptions follow a formula: "X raised this stone in memory of Y", often adding that the sponsor built a bridge or cleared a road "for the soul" of the deceased, and frequently ending with a Christian prayer, "God help his soul". Many are signed by professional runemasters such as Öpir. Famous examples include the Jelling stones in Denmark (often called Denmark's birth certificate), the Rök stone with its legendary verses, and the Ingvar runestones commemorating a disastrous expedition to Serkland (the Caspian region).

Modern fate

Many stones were reused in churches and walls, or were lost. Today they are catalogued in national databases such as Sweden's Rundata, and form an irreplaceable primary source for Viking Age language, religion, and society.
