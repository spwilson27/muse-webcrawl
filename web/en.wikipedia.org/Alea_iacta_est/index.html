Alea iacta est

Alea iacta est ("the die is cast") is a Latin phrase attributed by the Roman historian Suetonius to Julius Caesar, said as he crossed the Rubicon River with his army on 10 January 49 BC, an act that began the civil war against Pompey and the Senate. The phrase signifies passing a point of no return — taking an irrevocable step.

Caesar crossing the Rubicon, the act of no return the phrase commemorates.

Origin

Suetonius writes that Caesar, hesitating at the boundary of his province, said "iacta alea est". Plutarch reports that Caesar actually quoted in Greek a line from the comic playwright Menander — "let the die be cast" — suggesting a gambler's resolve rather than a boast. In Latin, alea refers both to a die and to a game of chance; Caesar himself was famously fond of dice imagery.

Usage

The phrase remains in common use in many languages to mark a decisive, irreversible commitment. Variants include "alea iacta esto" and the French "les jeux sont faits".
