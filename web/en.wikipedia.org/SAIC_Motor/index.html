SAIC Motor

TypeState-owned public company
IndustryAutomotive
HeadquartersShanghai, China
BrandsMG, Roewe, Maxus, IM Motors

SAIC Motor Corporation Limited is a Chinese state-owned automobile manufacturer headquartered in Shanghai. It is the largest of the traditional "Big Four" state-owned Chinese carmakers and has for many years sold more vehicles than any other Chinese manufacturer, largely through its joint ventures.

Operations

SAIC's joint ventures include SAIC-Volkswagen and SAIC-General Motors (with Buick, Chevrolet and Cadillac), which long made it the biggest seller in the Chinese market. Its wholly owned brands include MG (the formerly British marque it acquired the rights to through Nanjing Automobile), Roewe, Maxus (formerly LDV), and the premium electric brand IM Motors. SAIC also produces Wuling vehicles through the SAIC-GM-Wuling joint venture, maker of the best-selling Hongguang Mini EV.

History

The company's roots lie in Shanghai's early automotive industry; it assembled China's first domestically produced cars and formed its Volkswagen joint venture, initially building the Santana, in the 1980s. In the 2000s it acquired MG Rover intellectual property and SsangYong (later sold), and expanded exports, with MG becoming a significant brand in Europe, Australia and other markets.
