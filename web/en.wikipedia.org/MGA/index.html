The MGA is a sports car produced by MG, a division of the British Motor Corporation (BMC), from 1955 to 1962. It replaced the MG TF 1500 and represented a complete styling break with the pre-war-style MG cars that preceded it. A total of 101,081 units were built, the vast majority exported, chiefly to the United States.

Design

The MGA's sleek, ponton-style body derived from a streamlined design MG had built for a privateer Le Mans entry in 1951. Unlike its separate-chassis predecessors, it used unitary construction with a low seating position. Early cars used the 1,489 cc BMC B-series engine; the range grew to include the 1588 cc MGA 1600 and 1600 Mk II, a fixed-head coupé, and the high-performance Twin-Cam (1958–1960), whose dual-overhead-camshaft engine suffered reliability problems that hurt its reputation despite strong performance. A rare "DeLuxe" version combined Twin-Cam running gear with the standard engine.

Competition and succession

MGAs competed in rallies and endurance races and set class speed records at Bonneville. In 1962 the MGA was replaced by the MGB, which would go on to a nearly two-decade production run.
