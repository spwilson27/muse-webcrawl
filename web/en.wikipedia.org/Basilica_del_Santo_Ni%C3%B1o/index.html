The Basilica Minore del Santo Niño de Cebú is a minor basilica in Cebu City, Philippines, founded in 1565 by Augustinian friars. It is the oldest Roman Catholic church in the country and houses the image of the Santo Niño de Cebú, a statue of the Child Jesus regarded as the oldest Christian relic in the Philippines.

History

The image of the Santo Niño was originally given by Ferdinand Magellan to the wife of Rajah Humabon at their baptism in 1521. In 1565, soldiers of Miguel López de Legazpi found the statue preserved in a wooden box amid the ashes of a burned village, an event interpreted as miraculous. An Augustinian church was built on the spot, and the present stone church was constructed in the 1730s in a blend of Muslim, Romanesque, and neoclassical features. Pope Paul VI elevated the church to a minor basilica in 1965, on the 400th anniversary of the Christianization of the Philippines.

Devotion and Sinulog

The basilica is a major pilgrimage site. Each January, millions of devotees attend the feast of the Santo Niño, celebrated with the Sinulog festival — novena masses, a fluvial procession, and street dancing in honor of the Child Jesus. The original image is kept behind bullet-proof glass in a chapel within the basilica.

Architecture

The church is built of coral stone with a three-level facade, a bell tower, and the adjoining convent and pilgrim center. The 2013 Bohol earthquake damaged the bell tower and facade, which were subsequently restored.
