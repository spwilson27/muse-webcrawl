Microtubule

Microtubules are polymers of tubulin that form part of the cytoskeleton and provide structure and shape to eukaryotic cells. They have a diameter of about 25 nm and lengths from 200 nm to 25 micrometres. Microtubules are assembled from dimers of α-tubulin and β-tubulin that stack into protofilaments; typically 13 protofilaments associate laterally to form a hollow tube.

Dynamic instability

Microtubules grow and shrink stochastically at their ends, a behaviour called dynamic instability. Growth is favoured when a cap of GTP-bound tubulin is present; loss of the cap triggers rapid depolymerization ("catastrophe"), while regaining it ("rescue") restores growth. This dynamic behaviour lets the cell rapidly reorganize its microtubule network.

Organisation

In animal cells microtubules are nucleated from the centrosome, which contains a pair of centrioles and γ-tubulin ring complexes. Their minus ends are anchored at the centrosome while plus ends explore the cytoplasm. Stable arrays of microtubules also form the axonemes of cilia and flagella (in a "9+2" arrangement) and the mitotic spindle.

Functions

  • Intracellular transport by kinesin and dynein motor proteins
  • Separation of chromosomes during mitosis and meiosis
  • Beating of cilia and flagella
  • Maintaining cell shape and polarity

Drugs

Several important drugs target microtubules: colchicine and vinblastine promote depolymerization, while paclitaxel (Taxol) stabilizes them; both strategies disrupt the mitotic spindle and are used in chemotherapy.
