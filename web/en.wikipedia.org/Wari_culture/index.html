Wari culture

Wari (Huari)
Periodc. 500 – 1000 CE (Middle Horizon)
CapitalHuari, near modern Ayacucho, Peru
RegionCentral Andes, modern Peru
Known forFirst Andean empire; administrative centres, roads, textiles, ceramics

The Wari (Spanish: Huari) were a Middle Horizon civilization that flourished in the south-central Andes and coastal area of modern-day Peru, from about 500 to 1000 CE. Their capital, also called Huari, near present-day Ayacucho, grew into one of the largest cities of the early Andes. The Wari state is generally regarded as the first empire of the Andes, centuries before the Inca, and its expansion spread administrative architecture, road networks, terrace agriculture, and craft traditions across much of Peru.

Expansion and administration

From the Ayacucho basin, Wari influence extended from the Cajamarca region in the north to the Sicuani area in the south. The state founded planned provincial centres — including Pikillaqta near Cusco and Viracochapampa in the north — laid out on orthogonal grids with enclosed patios and storage facilities, through which goods and labour were administered. Wari elites famously consumed maize beer (chicha) in feasting rituals that bound subjects to the state.

Art

Wari art is noted for brilliantly coloured polychrome ceramics and some of the finest textiles ever woven in the Andes, including tunics with abstract, nearly geometric designs derived from earlier south-coast traditions and imagery related to the staff-bearing deity also seen at Tiwanaku, with which Wari shared a Middle Horizon style and religious iconography.

Decline

By about 1000 CE the Wari state had collapsed, possibly under the stress of prolonged drought; Huari was abandoned and its great compounds deliberately sealed and burned in places. The political template it created — roads, storage, and centralized administration — nonetheless foreshadowed the later Inca Empire.
