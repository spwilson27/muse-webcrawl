Paranasal sinuses

The paranasal sinuses are a group of four paired air-filled cavities in the bones of the skull that surround and open into the nasal cavity. They are lined with respiratory epithelium and are named after the bones in which they lie: the maxillary, frontal, sphenoid, and ethmoid sinuses.

Anatomy

The maxillary sinuses, in the maxilla below the orbits, are the largest. The frontal sinuses lie in the frontal bone above the eyes and vary greatly in size. The ethmoid sinuses consist of numerous small air cells between the eyes, and the two sphenoid sinuses sit deep in the sphenoid bone near the pituitary gland. All drain into the nasal cavity through openings called ostia, chiefly in the middle meatus.

Function

Proposed functions include lightening the skull, humidifying and warming inspired air, adding resonance to the voice, and producing mucus and nitric oxide that aid nasal defenses.

Clinical significance

Inflammation of the sinuses (sinusitis) is a common condition causing facial pain, congestion, and discharge, and may follow viral infections or arise from anatomical obstruction of drainage.
