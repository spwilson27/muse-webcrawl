High Court of Australia

High Court of Australia
Established: 1903
Location: Canberra, Australian Capital Territory
Composition: Chief Justice and six Justices
Authorised by: Constitution of Australia, s 71

The High Court of Australia is the apex court of Australia and the final court of appeal in the Australian legal system. It is also the country's constitutional court, exercising original jurisdiction in constitutional interpretation and serving as the highest appellate court for both federal and state courts. The Court was established by the Judiciary Act 1903 and first sat in Melbourne in October 1903; since 1980 it has been housed in its own building on the shore of Lake Burley Griffin in Canberra.

Composition and jurisdiction

The Court consists of the Chief Justice and six other Justices, appointed by the Governor-General in Council on the advice of the federal government; justices must retire at age 70 following a 1977 constitutional referendum. The first bench included Chief Justice Sir Samuel Griffith and Justices Edmund Barton (Australia's first Prime Minister) and Richard O'Connor. Appeals generally require a grant of special leave; the Court also hears cases between states, challenges to Commonwealth legislation, and questions referred under its original jurisdiction.

Landmark decisions

Notable rulings include the Engineers' Case (1920), which transformed federal–state relations; the Tasmanian Dam case (1983); Mabo v Queensland (No 2) (1992), recognising native title; and Wik (1996). Under Chief Justices such as Sir Owen Dixon, the Court developed a strong tradition of legalism.
