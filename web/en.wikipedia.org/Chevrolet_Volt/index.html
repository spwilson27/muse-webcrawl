The Chevrolet Volt is a plug-in hybrid compact car manufactured by General Motors under the Chevrolet brand from 2010 to 2019. Unveiled as a concept at the 2007 Detroit auto show and launched in December 2010, it was the first mass-produced plug-in hybrid sold by a major automaker in the United States.

Powertrain

The Volt's Voltec system drives the wheels primarily with an electric motor powered by a lithium-ion battery pack (16 kWh in the first generation). When the battery is depleted, a small petrol engine acts mainly as a generator to sustain driving — a configuration GM called an "extended-range electric vehicle" (EREV). The first generation offered about 35 miles of electric range and the second (2016–2019) about 53 miles.

Reception and legacy

The Volt won numerous awards, including North American Car of the Year 2011 and European Car of the Year 2012 (as the Opel/Vauxhall Ampera, its rebadged sibling). It was among the best-selling plug-in cars in the US for much of the 2010s. GM ended Volt production in February 2019 as it shifted toward all-electric vehicles like the Bolt EV; its Voltec architecture influenced later GM electrification programmes.
