Dunfermline (Scottish Gaelic: Dùn Phàrlain) is a city and former royal burgh in Fife, Scotland, about 3 miles from the northern shore of the Firth of Forth and 13 miles northwest of Edinburgh. It was one of the historic capitals of Scotland and is the burial place of several Scottish monarchs.

History

The royal court of Malcolm III and Saint Margaret was based at Dunfermline in the 11th century. Margaret founded a priory that became Dunfermline Abbey in 1128 under David I; Robert the Bruce was buried in the abbey choir in 1329. The town later became famous for linen weaving and damask production, and a royal palace adjoining the abbey hosted the birth of Charles I in 1600.

Carnegie

The industrialist Andrew Carnegie was born in Dunfermline in 1838 and became its great benefactor, donating the world's first Carnegie library in 1883, the Pittencrieff Park estate, and public baths. His birthplace cottage is now a museum.

Modern Dunfermline

Granted city status in 2022 as part of the Platinum Jubilee honours, Dunfermline is a major commuter town for Edinburgh, with a large Amazon fulfilment centre at its edge and the football club Dunfermline Athletic based at East End Park.
