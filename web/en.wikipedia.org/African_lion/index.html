African lion

Scientific namePanthera leo (populations)
FamilyFelidae
RangeSub-Saharan Africa
DietCarnivore
StatusVulnerable (species level)

The African lion refers to the lion (Panthera leo) populations of Africa. Lions are large, social cats living in groups called prides, mainly in the savannas and grasslands of sub-Saharan Africa. Two subspecies are widely recognized following the Cat Classification Task Force revision: Panthera leo leo (Northern lion, including West and Central African and Asiatic populations) and Panthera leo melanochaita (Southern lion, in East and Southern Africa).

Description

Males weigh typically 150–250 kg and females 120–182 kg. Adult males bear the characteristic mane, which varies in color and extent with age, genetics, and climate. Lions are apex predators hunting mainly medium-to-large ungulates such as wildebeest, zebra, and buffalo, often cooperatively and at night.

Social behavior

Uniquely among cats, lions are highly social. A pride consists of related females, their offspring, and a coalition of one or more adult males. Females do most of the hunting and communal cub-rearing; males defend the territory and the pride against rival coalitions. Takeovers by new males commonly lead to infanticide of cubs, which brings females back into estrus.

Conservation

African lion numbers have fallen sharply over the past century due to habitat loss, prey depletion, retaliatory killing over livestock depredation, and trophy hunting. Estimates put the wild population at roughly 20,000–25,000, and West African lions are critically endangered. Lions survive in notable strongholds in the Serengeti, Okavango Delta, and Kruger ecosystems.
