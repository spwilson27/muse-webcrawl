Granada plc was a British conglomerate best known as the parent of Granada Television, the ITV franchise holder for North West England and one of the most influential British television producers. It ceased to exist as an independent company in 2004, when it merged with Carlton Communications to create ITV plc.

History

The business was founded by Sidney Bernstein, who opened the first Granada cinema in Dover in 1930 and named the chain after the Spanish city of Granada. The company expanded into theatres and, later, television rentals. In 1954 Granada won the ITV franchise for the north of England and began broadcasting from Manchester in 1956. Granada Television produced landmark programmes including Coronation Street (from 1960), the documentary strand World in Action, Brideshead Revisited (1981), and The Jewel in the Crown (1984).

Expansion and diversification

Under later leadership the group diversified into motorway services (through its catering and hospitality arm), computer services, and publishing, and acquired other ITV franchisees, including LWT (London Weekend Television) in 1994, Yorkshire Television and Tyne Tees Television. Charles Allen became chief executive in the 1990s. In 2000–2001 Granada demerged its catering business (which became part of Compass Group) to focus on media.

Merger into ITV plc

Following consolidation permitted by the Communications Act 2003, Granada merged with Carlton in February 2004 to form ITV plc, uniting almost all of the ITV network in England and Wales under one company. The Granada name survived for a time in production branding (Granada Productions) and in the regional service's identity.
