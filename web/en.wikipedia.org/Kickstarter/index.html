Kickstarter, PBC is an American public benefit corporation based in Brooklyn, New York, that operates a global crowdfunding platform focused on creativity. Launched in 2009, it allows creators to raise money for projects — films, games, music, art, design products, and technology — through pledges from the public.

Model

Kickstarter uses an all-or-nothing model: a project must reach its funding goal by its deadline or no money changes hands. Backers typically receive rewards rather than equity. Kickstarter charges a 5 percent fee on funds raised by successful projects, in addition to payment processing fees. Over its history the platform has channelled several billion dollars to hundreds of thousands of projects.

Notable projects

Some of the largest campaigns raised tens of millions of dollars, including the Pebble smartwatch (2012 and successors), the Coolest Cooler, the Veronica Mars film, the Exploding Kittens card game, and the video game Shenmue III. The platform has also funded Oscar-winning films and Pulitzer-recognised journalism, though high-profile failures and undelivered projects have drawn criticism and scrutiny of backer risk.

Company history

Kickstarter was founded by Perry Chen, Yancey Strickler, and Charles Adler; the idea originated in Chen's attempt to fund a concert. In 2015 the company reincorporated as a public benefit corporation, committing to social goals in its charter. Its employees unionised in 2020, a first among major American tech companies. In 2021 the company announced — and later quietly shelved — a plan to build a blockchain-based crowdfunding protocol.
