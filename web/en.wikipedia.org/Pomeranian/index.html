Pomeranian

The Pomeranian (often known as a Pom) is a breed of dog of the Spitz type that is named for the Pomerania region in north-west Poland and north-east Germany in Central Europe. Classed as a toy dog breed because of its small size, the Pomeranian is descended from larger Spitz-type dogs, specifically the German Spitz.

Appearance

Pomeranians are small dogs weighing 1.35–3.2 kilograms (3.0–7.0 lb) and standing 20–30 centimetres (8–12 in) high at the withers. They are compact but sturdy dogs with an abundant textured coat with a highly plumed tail set high and flat. The top coat forms a ruff of fur on the neck. Recognised coat colours include white, black, brown, red, orange, cream, blue, sable, and combinations of these.

History

The breed's ancestors were large sled and sheep-herding Spitz dogs of the Arctic regions, weighing as much as 13–23 kg. Breeders in Pomerania reduced the size, and in the 19th century the breed became fashionable in Britain after Queen Victoria acquired a notably small red Pomeranian in 1888, prompting further breeding for small size. The Pomeranian has since remained one of the most popular toy breeds in the world; famous owners and notable dogs have included Michelangelo's and Isaac Newton's pets in earlier centuries, and in recent decades celebrity-owned Pomeranians with large social-media followings.

Temperament and health

Pomeranians are typically friendly, lively, and playful, but can be assertive and prone to excessive barking. They are generally intelligent and trainable but may be stubborn. Common health concerns include luxating patella, tracheal collapse, alopecia X ("black skin disease"), and dental problems; with proper care the breed commonly lives 12 to 16 years.
