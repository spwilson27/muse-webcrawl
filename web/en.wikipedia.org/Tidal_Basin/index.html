The Tidal Basin is a man-made reservoir located between the Potomac River and the Washington Channel in Washington, D.C. It is part of West Potomac Park, near the National Mall, and is surrounded by the Jefferson Memorial, the Martin Luther King Jr. Memorial, and the Franklin Delano Roosevelt Memorial. The basin is famous for the Japanese cherry trees that ring its shores, a celebrated attraction during the annual National Cherry Blossom Festival.

History and function

The basin was created in the 1880s and 1890s, when the Army Corps of Engineers dredged the Potomac flats to create Hains Point and the surrounding parkland. It was designed to flush the Washington Channel: at high tide, gates admit water from the Potomac; at low tide, the impounded water is released to scour silt from the channel. The inlet bridge and outlet works were completed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Cherry trees

In 1912, the city of Tokyo donated some 3,000 cherry trees to Washington, which were planted around the Tidal Basin; the first two trees were planted by First Lady Helen Herron Taft and the wife of the Japanese ambassador. The surviving trees and their descendants bloom each spring, drawing over a million visitors to the National Cherry Blossom Festival. In 2024, about 140 trees were removed as part of a multi-year project to repair the basin's sinking seawalls, with replacement trees to be planted on completion.

Recreation

Paddle boats can be rented on the basin seasonally, and a 2.1-mile path encircles the water. The land around it has periodically suffered from flooding and subsidence, prompting major seawall reconstruction in the 2020s.
