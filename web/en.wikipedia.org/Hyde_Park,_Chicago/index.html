Hyde Park, Chicago

Summary

Location: South Side of Chicago, Illinois, on Lake Michigan

Founded: 1853 by Paul Cornell

Annexed to Chicago: 1889

Anchors: University of Chicago; Museum of Science and Industry

Population: About 29,000 (2020)

Hyde Park is a community area on the South Side of Chicago, Illinois, located on the shore of Lake Michigan about seven miles south of the Loop. It is best known as the home of the University of Chicago and the Museum of Science and Industry, and for its role in hosting the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893.

History

Hyde Park was founded in 1853 by Paul Cornell, a lawyer and cousin of Cornell University founder Ezra Cornell, who bought land around 51st Street and promoted it as a suburban resort community, naming it after Hyde Park in New York and London. The Illinois Central Railroad made it accessible from downtown Chicago. Hyde Park was incorporated as an independent township in 1861 and annexed to Chicago in 1889. The World's Columbian Exposition of 1893, held largely in adjacent Jackson Park and along the Midway Plaisance, transformed the area.

University and institutions

The University of Chicago, founded in 1890 with Rockefeller funding, dominates the neighbourhood physically and economically. Other institutions include the Museum of Science and Industry — housed in the Palace of Fine Arts from the 1893 fair — the Robie House by Frank Lloyd Wright, the Oriental Institute, and the future site of the Obama Presidential Center in Jackson Park. Hyde Park was the longtime home of Barack Obama, who taught law at the university.

Character

The neighbourhood is known for its racial and economic diversity, tree-lined streets, bookstores, and cultural life, including the Hyde Park Jazz Festival and the 57th Street Art Fair, the oldest juried art fair in the Midwest.
