Estonian language

Estonian is a Finnic language spoken mainly in Estonia, where it is the official language. It belongs to the southern branch of the Finnic languages of the Uralic family and is closely related to Finnish. Around 1.1 million people speak Estonian, including about 920,000 in Estonia.

Features

Estonian is agglutinative, with 14 noun cases, no grammatical gender, and a distinctive three-way phonemic quantity distinction in both consonants and vowels. Its orthography, based on the Latin alphabet, uses letters such as õ, ä, ö, and ü; õ represents a unique unrounded close-mid back vowel.

Vocabulary

The language has many old loanwords from Germanic, Low German, and High German, as well as from Russian and Swedish. In the 20th century language reformer Johannes Aavik introduced many coined words.
