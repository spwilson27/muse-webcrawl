Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife

The Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife is a double-edged fighting knife resembling a dagger or poignard, designed by William Ewart Fairbairn and Eric Anthony Sykes in Shanghai during the 1930s and adopted by British Commandos and other special forces during the Second World War. Its slender blade and distinctive silhouette made it one of the most famous fighting knives of the war, and it remains in use and widely reproduced today.

Design and history

Fairbairn and Sykes, officers of the Shanghai Municipal Police, developed the design from their experience of close combat and earlier experiments with fighting knives. In 1940 they brought the design to Britain, where it was manufactured by Wilkinson Sword of London. Issued from 1941 to British Commandos, the SOE, airborne forces and later to allied units — including the US Office of Strategic Services, whose similar "OSS stiletto" was derived from it — the knife went through three main patterns, varying in blade profile, grip material and pommel. The blade, about 18 cm (7 inches) long, was designed primarily for thrusting, with a foil-like point and a narrow cross-section.

Legacy

The Fairbairn–Sykes knife is commemorated in the insignia of several commando and special forces units, and its image forms part of the badge of the British Royal Marines' dagger symbol and of the US Army Special Forces tradition derived from the First Special Service Force. Original wartime examples are highly collectible, and the pattern continues to be made by Sheffield cutlers.
