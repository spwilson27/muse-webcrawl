Oymyakon (Russian: Оймяко́н; Yakut: Өймөкөөн) is a rural locality in Oymyakonsky District of the Sakha Republic, Russia, in the Russian Far East. It is generally considered the coldest permanently inhabited settlement on Earth and, together with Verkhoyansk, one of the Northern Hemisphere's two "Poles of Cold".

Climate

Oymyakon has an extreme subarctic climate. The weather station recorded −67.7 °C on 6 February 1933, the lowest temperature ever reliably measured in the Northern Hemisphere; an unofficial reading of −71.2 °C from 1924 is often cited locally. Average January temperatures are around −46 °C. The extreme cold results from the village's position in a valley between mountain ranges, where dense cold air pools under the Siberian High and temperature inversions trap it. Summers, by contrast, are mild and can exceed 30 °C, giving the region one of the greatest seasonal temperature ranges on Earth.

Life in the village

Fewer than 500 people live in Oymyakon, historically herders of reindeer and horses; the name, "unfrozen water", refers to a nearby thermal spring that allows livestock to drink in winter. Daily life is adapted to the cold: cars are kept running or stored in heated garages, plumbing is rare because pipes freeze and burst, the school closes only below about −52 °C, and the short two-hour flight to Yakutsk is a lifeline. In recent years the village has become a destination for extreme tourism, with visitors posing at its Pole of Cold monument.
