Dubin Inquiry

The Dubin Inquiry, formally the Commission of Inquiry into the Use of Drugs and Banned Practices Intended to Increase Athletic Performance, was a Canadian government inquiry established in 1988 following the Ben Johnson doping scandal at the Seoul Olympic Games. It was chaired by Ontario Court of Appeal justice Charles Leonard Dubin.

Background

On 24 September 1988, Canadian sprinter Ben Johnson won the Olympic 100 metres final in a world-record 9.79 seconds. Three days later he was stripped of the gold medal after testing positive for the anabolic steroid stanozolol. The Canadian government of Prime Minister Brian Mulroney immediately ordered a public inquiry into drug use in Canadian sport.

Proceedings and findings

The inquiry heard testimony over more than a year from athletes, coaches, physicians and officials. Johnson admitted long-term steroid use, and his coach Charlie Francis testified that elite sprinting was saturated with doping and described a systematic programme. Other witnesses included sprinter Angella Issajenko and physician Jamie Astaphan, who supplied athletes with performance-enhancing drugs.

Dubin's 1990 report concluded that the failure of many sports bodies to confront doping represented a crisis of values in sport, placed responsibility broadly — on athletes, coaches, medical advisers, sports organisations and government agencies — and recommended stronger independent drug testing, education, and a restructuring of Canada's sport system. The inquiry led to the creation of a more rigorous national anti-doping framework, with the Canadian Centre for Ethics in Sport eventually becoming the central anti-doping body.
