Raoult's law

Raoult's law is a law of physical chemistry concerning the vapor pressure of solutions, established by French chemist François-Marie Raoult in the 1880s. It states that the partial vapor pressure of each component of an ideal mixture of liquids equals the vapor pressure of the pure component multiplied by its mole fraction in the mixture.

Consequences

For a solution with a non-volatile solute, Raoult's law implies that the vapor pressure of the solvent is lowered in proportion to the solute's mole fraction. This underlies the colligative properties: boiling-point elevation and freezing-point depression.

Deviations

Real solutions deviate from the law when intermolecular forces between unlike molecules differ from those between like molecules. Positive deviations (higher pressure than predicted) occur in mixtures such as ethanol–water components at some compositions, while negative deviations occur where unlike molecules attract strongly, as in chloroform–acetone. Raoult's law is exact in the limit of dilute solutions for the solvent, complementing Henry's law for the solute.
