Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India is a landmark decision of the Supreme Court of India, delivered on 6 September 2018, which decriminalised consensual homosexual sex between adults. A five-judge constitution bench unanimously held that Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code was unconstitutional insofar as it criminalised consensual sexual conduct between adults, finding the provision violated the rights to equality, freedom of expression, and privacy guaranteed by the Constitution.

Background

Section 377, a colonial-era law of 1860, punished "carnal intercourse against the order of nature." In 2009 the Delhi High Court in Naz Foundation v. Govt. of NCT of Delhi read the section down to exclude consensual adult acts, but in 2013 a two-judge bench of the Supreme Court reversed that ruling in Suresh Kumar Koushal v. Naz Foundation. Petitions by dancer Navtej Singh Johar and others, together with the 2017 privacy judgment in Puttaswamy, led to reconsideration by a larger bench.

Judgment

The Court held that LGBT persons are entitled to equal citizenship, that sexual orientation is an inherent part of identity protected against discrimination, and that the Constitution does not permit a minority's rights to be held hostage to majoritarian morality. Chief Justice Dipak Misra's opinion famously observed that the Court must "punch above" social prejudice in favour of constitutional morality. Section 377 continued to apply to non-consensual acts and acts with animals. See also Section 377.
