Permadeath

In roguelikes such as NetHack, a character's death permanently ends the game.

Permadeath or permanent death is a game mechanic in both tabletop and video games in which player characters who die are permanently removed from the game and may not be revived. Games with permadeath are typically those in which the player completes the game by carefully choosing the right course of action; errors have lasting consequences.

History

Permadeath is a defining convention of the roguelike genre, from Rogue (1980) through NetHack and Angband: when the character dies, the save file is deleted and the player must start over with a newly generated character and dungeon. The term itself came into wide use in discussions of massively multiplayer online games in the late 1990s and early 2000s, where designers debated whether permanent character death would enrich or destroy an online world.

Modern use

Optional "hardcore" or "ironman" modes implementing permadeath appear in games such as Diablo II, Minecraft and XCOM, and survival games like DayZ build tension around the permanent loss of a character and its equipment. Permadeath also appears for individual characters in strategy and role-playing franchises such as Fire Emblem (where later entries made it optional) and in the "roguelite" genre, where some progress or unlocks persist between runs.
