Tetralogy of Fallot

Tetralogy of Fallot is a congenital heart defect consisting of four related anatomical abnormalities: a ventricular septal defect, pulmonary valve stenosis, an overriding aorta, and right ventricular hypertrophy. It is the most common cause of blue baby syndrome and accounts for roughly 5 to 10 percent of congenital heart defects.

SpecialtyCardiology, cardiac surgery
SymptomsCyanosis, heart murmur, "tet spells"
Named afterÉtienne-Louis Arthur Fallot
TreatmentOpen-heart surgery

Signs and symptoms

Obstruction of blood flow to the lungs causes deoxygenated blood to bypass them through the septal defect, producing cyanosis — a bluish tint to the skin. Children may have episodes of rapid, deep breathing and worsening cyanosis known as "tet spells", during which they instinctively squat to increase blood flow to the lungs.

Treatment

Definitive treatment is surgical repair, usually performed in the first year of life: the septal defect is closed with a patch and the pulmonary outflow is widened. The first successful palliative procedure was the Blalock–Taussig shunt of 1944. With modern repair, most patients survive into adulthood, though many need later pulmonary valve replacement.
