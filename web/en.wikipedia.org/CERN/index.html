CERN

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The European Organization for Nuclear Research, known as CERN (from the French Conseil Européen pour la Recherche Nucléaire), is an intergovernmental organisation operating the largest particle physics laboratory in the world. Founded in 1954, it is based in Meyrin, on the Franco-Swiss border near Geneva, and has 23 member states.

Facilities

CERN's flagship instrument is the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), a 27-kilometre ring of superconducting magnets and particle detectors buried about 100 metres underground, which began operation in 2008. Its experiments include ATLAS, CMS, ALICE and LHCb. A chain of smaller accelerators, including the Proton Synchrotron and Super Proton Synchrotron, feeds particles into the LHC.

Discoveries and achievements

On 4 July 2012 CERN announced the discovery of a new particle consistent with the Higgs boson, confirming a key prediction of the Standard Model; François Englert and Peter Higgs received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2013. Earlier CERN milestones include the discovery of the W and Z bosons (1983) and the first creation of antihydrogen atoms (1995).

Other contributions

CERN is also famous as the birthplace of the World Wide Web, invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989 to share information among physicists, and it has contributed to grid computing, medical imaging and cancer therapy technologies. More than 12,000 scientists from over 100 countries use its facilities.
