Regalia

Regalia are the emblems, symbols, and ceremonial objects of royalty or high office — typically crowns, sceptres, orbs, swords, robes, and thrones used at coronations and state occasions. The term derives from the Latin regalia, "the king's things."

Examples

The Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom, kept in the Tower of London, include St Edward's Crown and the Imperial State Crown. Japan's Imperial Regalia — the sword Kusanagi, the mirror Yata no Kagami, and the jewel Yasakani no Magatama — are legendary objects said to have been given by the sun goddess Amaterasu. The Holy Roman Empire's Imperial Regalia, including the Imperial Crown and Holy Lance, are preserved in Vienna.

Wider uses

The word extends beyond monarchy: academic regalia denotes the gowns, hoods, and mortarboards worn at graduations; fraternal and civic orders speak of the regalia of office; and in many Indigenous cultures "regalia" denotes the ceremonial dress worn for dances and powwows, which is culturally specific and not a costume.
