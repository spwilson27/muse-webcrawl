Dog show

A dog show is an event where dogs are exhibited. The most popular form in many countries is the conformation show, in which a judge familiar with a specific dog breed evaluates individual purebred dogs against the breed standard, rewarding those that most closely conform to the ideal type of their breed.

Judging

Handlers, professional or amateur, present dogs to the judge, moving them around the ring ("gaiting") and standing them for examination. Dogs compete first against others of their breed, then against winners within their group, and group winners compete for Best in Show.

Famous shows

Major shows include Crufts in the United Kingdom, the world's largest dog show, founded in 1891; the Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show in the United States, held since 1877; and the World Dog Show organized by the FCI.

Criticism

Conformation showing has been criticized for encouraging breeding for appearance over health, contributing to exaggerated features and inherited disorders in some breeds — a controversy highlighted by the 2008 documentary Pedigree Dogs Exposed.
