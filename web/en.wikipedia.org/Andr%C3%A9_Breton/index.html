André Breton

Born19 February 1896, Tinchebray, France
Died28 September 1966, Paris, France
OccupationPoet, writer
MovementSurrealism

André Breton (1896–1966) was a French poet and writer, the co-founder and principal theorist of Surrealism. His Manifesto of Surrealism (1924) defined the movement as "pure psychic automatism" and launched one of the most influential avant-garde currents of the twentieth century.

Life and work

Breton trained in medicine and psychiatry and worked in neurological wards during the First World War, where he encountered the ideas of Freud and the case of shell-shocked patients. After involvement with Paris Dada, he broke with Tristan Tzara and, with Louis Aragon and Philippe Soupault, developed automatic writing, publishing the experimental text Les Champs magnétiques (1920). His novel Nadja (1928) and poetry collections such as Clair de terre are central surrealist works.

Later years

Breton spent the Second World War in exile in the United States before returning to Paris in 1946. He remained the movement's often authoritarian leader, guiding surrealism until his death in 1966, while quarrelling with and expelling many former allies. His influence extends across modern poetry, art, and political thought.
