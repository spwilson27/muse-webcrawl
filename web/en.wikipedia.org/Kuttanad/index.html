Kuttanad is a region of the Alappuzha, Kottayam and Pathanamthitta districts in the Indian state of Kerala, lying around Vembanad Lake. It is one of the few places in the world where farming is carried on around 1.2 to 3 metres (4 to 10 ft) below sea level, earning it the nickname "the rice bowl of Kerala."

Geography

The region is a network of reclaimed deltaic land, polders (kayal nilangal), canals, rivers and backwaters formed by the Pamba, Meenachil, Achankovil and Manimala rivers flowing into Vembanad Lake. Earthen bunds and dykes keep the saline lake water out of the paddy fields, and cultivation relies on pumping out water during the puncha (summer) crop season.

History

Massive reclamation of the kayal lands took place in the 19th and early 20th centuries, notably under the Travancore administration and private entrepreneurs. The Thanneermukkom saltwater barrier, completed in 1975, was built to prevent salt intrusion into the paddy fields, though it has had debated ecological effects on the lake.

Tourism

Kuttanad is a major backwater tourism destination, noted for houseboats (kettuvallams), the Nehru Trophy Boat Race held on Punnamada Lake, duck curry, toddy, and scenic paddy-field vistas. The Government of India has recognised the Kuttanad below-sea-level farming system as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System (GIAHS) site through the FAO.
