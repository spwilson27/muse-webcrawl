Processual archaeology

Processual archaeology, also called the New Archaeology, is an approach that emerged in the 1960s emphasizing the scientific method, systems theory, and the study of cultural processes — how societies adapt to environments and change over time — rather than merely describing culture history.

Development

Lewis Binford's 1962 article "Archaeology as Anthropology" is a founding text; David Clarke in Britain developed parallel ideas. Processualists advocated hypothesis testing, explicit research design, quantitative methods, ethnoarchaeology, and middle-range theory linking static remains to past behaviour.

Criticism

From the 1980s, post-processual archaeologists such as Ian Hodder criticized its functionalism and neglect of symbolism, agency, and historical particularity. Processual methods remain foundational in much of world archaeology, especially in North America.
