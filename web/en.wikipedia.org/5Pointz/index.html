5 Pointz: The Institute of Higher Burnin' was an American mural space at 45-46 Davis Street in Long Island City, Queens, New York City. The 200,000-square-foot factory complex was covered in graffiti art and was considered the world's premier "graffiti mecca," where aerosol artists from around the world painted under the curation of Jonathan Cohen, known as Meres One.

History

The site began as the Phun Phactory in 1993, a program intended to redirect graffiti writers to legal walls. Renamed 5 Pointz in 2002 — for the five boroughs of New York coming together — the complex became a major tourist attraction and backdrop for films and music videos.

Destruction and lawsuit

In November 2013 the building's owner, Jerry Wolkoff, had the murals whitewashed overnight in preparation for demolition and redevelopment into residential towers. The artists sued under the Visual Artists Rights Act. In 2018 a federal judge awarded the artists $6.75 million in statutory damages for the destruction of 45 works, a landmark ruling for street art; the U.S. Supreme Court declined to review the case in 2020.
