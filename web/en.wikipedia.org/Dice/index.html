Dice

Dice (singular die) are small, throwable objects with marked sides used to generate random numbers or symbols. Dice are used for gambling, tabletop games, and divination. The most familiar are six-sided cubes numbered 1 through 6 with pips, with opposite faces summing to seven, but polyhedral dice with 4, 8, 10, 12, and 20 faces are common in role-playing games.

History

Dice are among the oldest gaming implements known. Knucklebones of animals served as proto-dice in the ancient world, and cube dice have been found in Egyptian tombs and in excavations of the Indus Valley civilization dating back thousands of years. Dice were used in ancient Rome, in medieval Europe, and throughout Asia, often despite periodic bans on gambling.

Construction and fairness

Precision dice are manufactured so that each face is equally likely to land face up; casino dice have sharp edges and flush-filled pips to avoid bias. Loaded or "crooked" dice are altered to favor certain outcomes and are used for cheating. The probability of any outcome of a fair die roll is uniform, and rolls of multiple dice follow distributions studied in elementary probability theory.
