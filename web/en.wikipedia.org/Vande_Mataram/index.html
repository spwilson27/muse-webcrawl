Vande Mataram

Vande Mataram (also rendered Bande Mataram; "I bow to thee, Mother") is a poem written in Sanskritised Bengali by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee in the 1870s. It was included in his 1882 novel Anandamath and personifies India as a goddess, becoming a rallying cry of the Indian independence movement.

History

The song was first sung publicly by Rabindranath Tagore at the 1896 session of the Indian National Congress. During the Swadeshi movement following the 1905 partition of Bengal, "Vande Mataram" became a popular slogan of protest, and British authorities at times banned its utterance. Aurobindo Ghosh and others promoted it as a national anthem of the freedom struggle.

Status

On 24 January 1950, the Constituent Assembly of India adopted the first two stanzas of Vande Mataram as the national song of India, giving it equal status with the national anthem, "Jana Gana Mana". Only the first two stanzas are officially used, as later stanzas explicitly reference Hindu goddess imagery, a point of longstanding debate.

Translations and versions

The poem has been translated many times, including prose renderings by Aurobindo Ghosh, and set to music by numerous composers; a well-known version was composed by A. R. Rahman for the 1997 album Vande Mataram.
