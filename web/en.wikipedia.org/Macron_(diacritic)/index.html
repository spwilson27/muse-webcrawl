Macron (diacritic)

A macron is a diacritical mark consisting of a straight bar placed above a letter, usually a vowel. It is used in many writing systems and transliteration schemes to indicate a long vowel, and in some languages it has been adopted into the standard orthography.

Uses

In Latin orthography and in the International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration, a macron marks vowel length, as in ā, ē, ī, ō, ū. Latvian and Lithuanian use macrons in standard spelling, as does Māori, where the macron (tohutō) distinguishes long vowels. In Japanese rōmaji under the Hepburn system, macrons show long vowels (Tōkyō). In medicine and older English typesetting, a macron over a c once indicated the Latin word cum, "with".

Encoding

Precomposed macron letters occupy many Unicode code points (e.g. U+0100 Ā), and a combining macron (U+0304) can be placed over arbitrary characters. It should not be confused with the breve, which marks short vowels, or the overline used in mathematics.
