The Southeastern Anatolia Project (Turkish: Güneydoğu Anadolu Projesi, GAP) is a multi-sector regional development project in southeastern Turkey centred on the construction of dams and hydroelectric power plants on the Euphrates and Tigris rivers and extensive irrigation networks in their basins.

Scope

The project envisages 22 dams and 19 hydroelectric power plants, together with irrigation of around 1.8 million hectares of land. Its centrepiece is the Atatürk Dam on the Euphrates, one of the largest earth-and-rock fill dams in the world, completed in the early 1990s. Other major works include the Keban, Karakaya, Birecik, Karkamış and Ilısu dams.

Effects and controversies

GAP transformed the regional economy, expanding cotton and other irrigated agriculture, but it has been controversial. Reservoirs flooded archaeological sites, most famously Zeugma and the ancient town of Hasankeyf, and displaced tens of thousands of people, many of them Kurds. Downstream, Syria and Iraq have raised concerns about reduced river flows, making GAP a longstanding issue in regional water politics.
