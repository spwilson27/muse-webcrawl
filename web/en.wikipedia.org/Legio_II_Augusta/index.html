Legio II Augusta

Active43 BC – at least AD 255
CountryRoman Republic, then Roman Empire
TypeRoman legion
NicknameAugusta; later Antoniniana
MascotCapricorn

Legio II Augusta was a legion of the Imperial Roman army, originally levied around 43 BC, possibly by the consul Gaius Vibius Pansa Caetronianus or by Octavian (the future Augustus), from whom it took its title "Augusta". Its emblem was the Capricorn, Augustus's own birth sign.

History

The legion fought in the civil wars of the late Republic, including at Philippi and Perugia, and later in the Cantabrian Wars in Hispania. It served for long periods on the Rhine frontier in Germania, suffering in the aftermath of the Teutoburg Forest disaster era, before being transferred to Britain in AD 43 as one of the four legions of the Claudian invasion force under Aulus Plautius; its legate was the future emperor Vespasian, who won numerous engagements in the southwest.

In Britain

Legio II Augusta campaigned in Wales against the Silures and was famously slow to respond during Boudica's revolt in AD 60/61, its acting commander Poenius Postumus later falling on his sword. The legion built and occupied the fortress at Isca Augusta (Caerleon) in Wales from about AD 74/75, which remained its base for two centuries. Detachments helped build Hadrian's Wall and the Antonine Wall, and the legion is still attested at Caerleon into the later third century.
