Gatorade is an American brand of sports-themed beverage and food products, built around its signature line of sports drinks. The beverage was first developed in 1965 by a team of researchers at the University of Florida to assist student athletes of the Florida Gators in exercising in hot, humid conditions. It is now manufactured by PepsiCo and distributed in over 80 countries; Gatorade is the leading sports drink brand in the United States.

History

In 1965, University of Florida physician Robert Cade and colleagues Dana Shires, Harry James Free, and Alejandro de Quesada analysed why Gators football players were suffering from heat-related illness, and formulated a drink of water, sodium, potassium, phosphate, and sugar to replace fluids and electrolytes lost in sweat. The drink was credited by coach Ray Graves as helping the team — nicknamed "the Gator-Aid team" — to victory, including in the 1967 Orange Bowl, and the brand was commercialised by Stokely-Van Camp. Quaker Oats acquired the brand in 1983, and PepsiCo obtained it through its 2001 acquisition of Quaker.

Composition and products

Gatorade Thirst Quencher contains water, sugar, citric acid, and electrolytes (sodium and potassium salts) in proportions intended to promote rehydration. The product line has expanded to include low-calorie G2 and zero-sugar variants, protein shakes, energy chews, and powders. The traditional ritual of a coach being doused with a cooler of Gatorade after a victory — the "Gatorade shower" — began in American football in the mid-1980s.

Science and criticism

The Gatorade Sports Science Institute, founded in 1985, funds research on exercise physiology and hydration. Health commentators have criticised sugary sports drinks for contributing calories when consumed by non-athletes, pointing out that for most casual exercisers water is sufficient.
