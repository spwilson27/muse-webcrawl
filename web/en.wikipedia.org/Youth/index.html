Youth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Youth is the time of life when one is young: the stage between childhood and adulthood (maturity). It is an experience that may shape an individual's level of dependency, which can be marked in various ways according to different cultural perspectives. Personal experience is marked by an individual's cultural norms or traditions, while a youth's level of dependency means the extent to which they still rely on their family emotionally and economically.

Definitions

Definitions of youth vary by institution and culture. The United Nations defines "youth" as persons between the ages of 15 and 24 years for statistical purposes, while the African Youth Charter uses 15–35. The World Health Organization distinguishes adolescents (10–19), youth (15–24), and "young people" (10–24). In many countries, legal milestones associated with youth include the age of majority, voting age, and school-leaving age, which typically fall between 16 and 21.

Development

Youth encompasses adolescence and early adulthood, periods of rapid physical change (puberty) and of cognitive and psychosocial development: the formation of identity, abstract reasoning, peer relationships, and increasing independence. Sociologists note that in industrialised societies the transition to full adult roles — completing education, entering work, leaving the parental home, forming partnerships — has lengthened, giving rise to the concept of "emerging adulthood".

Youth in society

Young people make up a large share of the population in many developing countries, and youth issues — education, employment, health, and civic participation — are central to public policy. Distinct youth cultures and subcultures, from music and fashion to digital media, have been a feature of modern societies since the mid-twentieth century. International Youth Day is observed on 12 August.
