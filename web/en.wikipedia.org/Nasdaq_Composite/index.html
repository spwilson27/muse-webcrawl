The Nasdaq Composite (ticker symbol ^IXIC) is a stock market index that includes almost all stocks listed on the Nasdaq stock exchange. It is one of the three most widely followed stock market indices in the United States, alongside the Dow Jones Industrial Average and the S&P 500. Because the Nasdaq exchange lists a large proportion of technology companies, the Composite is heavily weighted toward the information technology sector and is often treated as a proxy for the performance of technology stocks.

The Nasdaq Composite since its introduction in 1971.

Calculation

The index is calculated using a market-capitalization weighting method, meaning each stock's influence on the index is proportional to its market value. It includes more than 3,000 securities — common stocks, ADRs, REITs, and tracking stocks — but excludes ETFs, preferred shares, and most derivatives. The base level of 100 was set at its introduction on February 5, 1971.

History

The Composite rose rapidly during the dot-com bubble of the late 1990s, closing above 5,000 for the first time in March 2000 before losing nearly 80 percent of its value in the subsequent crash. It did not regain its 2000 peak on a closing basis until 2015. The index passed 10,000 in June 2020 and continued to new highs in the following decade.

Related indices

The Nasdaq-100 includes the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on the exchange and is the basis for the widely traded Invesco QQQ exchange-traded fund.
