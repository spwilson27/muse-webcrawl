Black Lives Matter

Black Lives Matter (BLM) is a decentralized political and social movement that seeks to highlight racism, discrimination, and racial inequality experienced by Black people in the United States, and to promote anti-racism. Its primary concerns are police brutality and racially motivated violence against Black people. The movement began in 2013 with the use of the hashtag #BlackLivesMatter on social media.

Origins

The phrase originated after the 2013 acquittal of George Zimmerman in the shooting of Trayvon Martin. Activists Alicia Garza, Patrisse Cullors, and Opal Tometi created the hashtag and built a network that expanded nationally after the 2014 deaths of Michael Brown in Ferguson, Missouri, and Eric Garner in New York City, which sparked widespread protests over police conduct.

George Floyd protests

The movement reached unprecedented scale in 2020 following the murder of George Floyd by Minneapolis police officer Derek Chauvin. Protests spread to thousands of locations in the United States and around the world, with estimates of 15–26 million participants in the US alone, making them among the largest protests in American history. The demonstrations prompted international debate about policing, monuments to historical figures associated with slavery and colonialism, and systemic racism.

Organization and aims

Black Lives Matter is deliberately decentralized: the Black Lives Matter Global Network Foundation, founded as the movement grew, coexists with many unaffiliated local chapters and activists using the name. Stated goals have included police reform and accountability, criminal-justice reform, and investment in Black communities. The movement has influenced electoral politics, corporate and institutional diversity policies, and public opinion about race and policing, while also attracting significant political criticism and counter-movements.
