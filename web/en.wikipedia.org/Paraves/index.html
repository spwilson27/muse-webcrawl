Paraves

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
CladeDinosauria; Theropoda; Maniraptora
Fossil rangeMiddle Jurassic – Present

Paraves is a clade of theropod dinosaurs that contains birds and the non-avian dinosaurs most closely related to them. The name, meaning "near birds", was coined by Paul Sereno in 1997. Paraves includes the Avialae (birds and their extinct relatives such as Archaeopteryx) together with the Deinonychosauria, which comprises the dromaeosaurids ("raptors" such as Deinonychus and Velociraptor) and the troodontids.

Characteristics

Paravians share features including a long, mobile arm with a semi-lunate carpal bone permitting a folding wrist, enlarged brains relative to other theropods, and plumage: many had fully developed pennaceous feathers forming wings on the arms and sometimes on the legs. Most early paravians were small — generally under 2 metres long — and many were capable of gliding or powered flight. The four-winged condition seen in forms such as Microraptor suggests flight evolved in a context of small, feathered, possibly arboreal animals.

Evolution

The group originated by the Middle–Late Jurassic; Anchiornis and Archaeopteryx date to about 160–150 million years ago. During the Cretaceous, dromaeosaurids and troodontids diversified across Laurasia while birds radiated globally. Only one paravian lineage — crown birds — survived the Cretaceous–Palaeogene extinction event 66 million years ago.

Classification

Within Paraves, the exact relationships of dromaeosaurids, troodontids, and avialans remain debated: troodontids and dromaeosaurids are usually united as Deinonychosauria, the sister group to Avialae, though some analyses place troodontids closer to birds. The discovery of numerous small feathered paravians from the Jehol Biota and other Chinese deposits has blurred the boundary between "birds" and "non-avian dinosaurs".
