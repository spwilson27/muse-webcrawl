Easement

Field: Property law

Related concepts: Servitude, right of way, profit à prendre

An easement is a nonpossessory right to use or enter onto the real property of another without possessing it. It is "best typified in the right of way which one landowner, A, may enjoy over the land of another, B". An easement is a property right and a type of incorporeal property in itself at common law in England, and in most common law jurisdictions.

Types

An easement may be affirmative, allowing the holder to do something on the servient land, such as travel across it, or negative, preventing the servient owner from doing something, such as building in a way that blocks light. Easements are also classified as appurtenant, benefiting a particular parcel of land (the dominant tenement), or in gross, benefiting a person or entity regardless of land ownership, as with utility easements.

Creation

Easements can be created by express grant or reservation in a deed, by implication from prior use or necessity, or by prescription through long, open, and continuous use. An easement by necessity arises where a parcel would otherwise be landlocked and unusable without access across another's land.

Termination

An easement may end by release, abandonment, merger of the dominant and servient estates, expiration of its term, or a change in conditions that makes its purpose impossible. Mere non-use, without more, is generally insufficient to extinguish an easement.
