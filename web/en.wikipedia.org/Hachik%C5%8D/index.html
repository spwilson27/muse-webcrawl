Hachikō

Hachikō (ハチ公, November 10, 1923 – March 8, 1935) was a Japanese Akita dog remembered for his remarkable loyalty to his owner, Hidesaburō Ueno, for whom he continued to wait for over nine years following Ueno's death. Hachikō was born on a farm near the city of Ōdate, Akita Prefecture, and was brought to Shibuya, Tokyo, where he accompanied his owner to Shibuya Station each morning and returned each evening to greet him.

Life

In May 1925, Professor Ueno suffered a cerebral hemorrhage while delivering a lecture and died without ever returning to the station where Hachikō waited. For the next nine years, nine months and fifteen days, Hachikō returned to the station every day at the time the train was due, waiting in vain for his master. Fellow commuters and station staff, initially wary, came to feed and care for him.

Legacy

Hachikō became a national symbol of loyalty in Japan. A bronze statue was erected at Shibuya Station in 1934, with Hachikō himself present at its unveiling; the statue was later re-cast after being recycled during the Second World War. His stuffed remains are displayed at the National Museum of Nature and Science in Ueno, Tokyo, and his story has been retold in books and films, including the 1987 Japanese film Hachikō Monogatari and the 2009 American film Hachi: A Dog's Tale.
