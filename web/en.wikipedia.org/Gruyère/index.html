Gruyère

Gruyère (French: La Gruyère) is a region of Switzerland, in the south of the canton of Fribourg, named after the town of Gruyères. It comprises the district of Gruyère, an area of Alpine foothills drained by the upper Saane (Sarine) river, between the plain around Bulle and the high mountains of the Bernese and Vaud Alps.

Geography and history

The region is centred on the town of Gruyères, with its medieval hilltop castle, the seat of the counts of Gruyères who ruled the area for centuries until the last count, Michael, fled his creditors in 1554–55, after which the county was divided between Fribourg and Bern. The administrative district of Gruyère today has its capital at Bulle, the largest town in the region.

Economy and culture

Gruyère is famous for the hard cheese of the same name, produced in village dairies and Alpine pastures throughout the region, as well as for double cream (crème double de la Gruyère), traditionally served with meringues. The landscape of green pre-Alpine hills, dairy farms, and chalets is an emblematic image of rural Switzerland. Tourism is important: visitors come for the castle of Gruyères, the H. R. Giger Museum, the demonstration cheese dairy (La Maison du Gruyère) at Pringy, the Cailler chocolate factory at Broc, and the Moléson mountain above Gruyères village.
