The seven trumpets are sounded by seven angels in the Book of Revelation (chapters 8–11), the last book of the New Testament. When the Lamb opens the seventh seal of the scroll, seven angels are given seven trumpets; as each angel sounds his trumpet, a new plague or cataclysm is unleashed upon the earth, in a sequence parallel to the seven seals before them and the seven bowls that follow. The trumpet series is announced as "three woes" accompanying the last three trumpets.

The seven blasts

  • First trumpet — Hail and fire mingled with blood are thrown to the earth; a third of the earth, trees, and grass is burned.
  • Second trumpet — A great mountain burning with fire is cast into the sea; a third of the sea becomes blood and a third of sea creatures and ships are destroyed.
  • Third trumpet — A great star called Wormwood falls on the rivers and springs, making a third of the waters bitter and deadly.
  • Fourth trumpet — A third of the sun, moon, and stars is struck and darkened.
  • Fifth trumpet (first woe) — The bottomless pit is opened; locust-like creatures with scorpion tails torment unbelievers for five months under their king Abaddon (Apollyon).
  • Sixth trumpet (second woe) — Four angels bound at the Euphrates are released with an army of two hundred million horsemen; a third of mankind is killed.
  • Seventh trumpet (third woe) — Loud voices in heaven proclaim "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ"; the temple of God is opened and the ark of the covenant revealed.

Interpretation

Interpreters differ sharply over the trumpets: preterists link them to the fall of Jerusalem and the Roman Empire; historicists to successive invasions and calamities across church history; futurists to a coming tribulation; and idealists read them symbolically as partial, recurring judgements that warn rather than finally destroy — each trumpet affects only "a third" of its target. Between the sixth and seventh trumpets, Revelation interposes the episode of the little book and the two witnesses (chapters 10–11).
