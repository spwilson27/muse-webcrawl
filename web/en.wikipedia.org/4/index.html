4 (four) is a number, numeral and glyph. It is the natural number following 3 and preceding 5. It is the smallest squared prime (22) and the only even number in this form. Four is the smallest composite number, its proper divisors being 1 and 2.

In mathematics

Four is the smallest composite number and the smallest number that is the sum of two primes in two different ways counted with repetition restricted to 2 + 2; it is a highly composite building block of elementary arithmetic. A number is divisible by 4 if its last two digits form a number divisible by 4.

A four-sided polygon is a quadrilateral, and the simplest polyhedron is the tetrahedron, which has four triangular faces and four vertices. By the four colour theorem, four colours suffice to colour any map drawn in the plane so that no two adjacent regions share a colour. Lagrange's four-square theorem states that every natural number can be expressed as the sum of at most four squares. Four is also the order of the smallest non-cyclic group, the Klein four-group.

Evolution of the glyph

The modern glyph descends, through the Brahmi numerals of ancient India, from a sign resembling a cross or plus sign; the Gupta form added a curved line, and the Nagari form developed a more rounded shape. The open triangular form common in digital displays and some typefaces is a later typographic variant.

In science

  • Four is the atomic number of beryllium.
  • There are four classical states of matter commonly listed in everyday terms — solid, liquid, gas and plasma.
  • DNA is built from four nucleobases: adenine, cytosine, guanine and thymine.
  • Spacetime in relativity is described with four dimensions: three of space and one of time.

In culture and religion

Many traditions group things in fours: the four seasons, the four cardinal directions, the four classical elements (earth, water, air and fire), the Four Noble Truths of Buddhism, and the four Gospels of the New Testament. In East Asian cultures the number four is often considered unlucky because its pronunciation resembles the word for "death" in Chinese, Japanese and Korean — a superstition known as tetraphobia.
