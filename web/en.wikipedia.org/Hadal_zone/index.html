Hadal zone

The hadal zone, also called the hadopelagic zone, is the deepest region of the ocean, lying within oceanic trenches at depths from about 6,000 metres (20,000 ft) down to nearly 11,000 metres (36,000 ft) at the bottom of the Mariana Trench. It is named after Hades, the ancient Greek god of the underworld, and is the least explored environment on Earth.

Conditions

The hadal zone is characterised by complete darkness, near-freezing temperatures of 1–4 °C, and immense hydrostatic pressure exceeding 1,000 atmospheres at the greatest depths. Less than about 0.3% of the ocean floor lies this deep, concentrated in 27 major trenches, mostly around the Pacific Rim. Food is scarce and derives mainly from marine snow sinking from surface waters and occasional falls of carrion.

Life

Despite the extreme conditions, specialised fauna thrive, including amphipods, holothurians (sea cucumbers), xenophyophores, cusk-eels, and snailfish — the Mariana snailfish lives at depths beyond 8,000 metres. Hadal organisms show adaptations such as piezolytes like trimethylamine N-oxide that stabilise proteins against pressure. Human exploration began with the bathyscaphe Trieste in 1960 and continues with vehicles such as Limiting Factor.
