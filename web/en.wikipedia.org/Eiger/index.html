Eiger

The Eiger is a 3,967-metre (13,015 ft) mountain of the Bernese Alps, overlooking Grindelwald and Lauterbrunnen in the Bernese Oberland of Switzerland. It is the easternmost peak of a ridge crest that includes the Mönch and the Jungfrau. The Eiger is famous in mountaineering for its 1,800-metre-high north face, the Nordwand, one of the great north faces of the Alps, whose forbidding reputation earned it the nickname 'Mordwand' (murder wall).

Climbing history

The summit was first reached on 11 August 1858 by Charles Barrington with guides Christian Almer and Peter Bohren, via the west flank. The north face resisted attempts that cost several lives in the 1930s before the first ascent in 1938 by an Austro-German team of Anderl Heckmair, Ludwig Vörg, Heinrich Harrer and Fritz Kasparek, whose four-day climb is recounted in Harrer's book The White Spider.

Geography and access

The Jungfrau railway tunnels through the Eiger, with windows cut into the north face at the Eigerwand station. Kleine Scheidegg, at the mountain's foot, is a major viewpoint and ski area. The Eiger forms part of the Jungfrau-Aletsch UNESCO World Heritage Site.
