Root nodule

A sectioned root nodule; the pink colour is leghemoglobin

Root nodules are specialised structures found on the roots of certain plants, most famously the legumes (family Fabaceae), that house symbiotic nitrogen-fixing bacteria. In legumes these bacteria are collectively called rhizobia (genera such as Rhizobium, Bradyrhizobium and Sinorhizobium), which convert inert atmospheric nitrogen (N2) into ammonia that the plant can use, in exchange for carbohydrates produced by photosynthesis.

Formation

Nodule formation, or nodulation, begins with an exchange of chemical signals between plant and bacterium. The plant root releases flavonoids that attract compatible rhizobia and switch on their nod genes. The bacteria respond by producing Nod factors (lipochitooligosaccharides), which trigger root hair curling and the division of cortical cells to form the nodule primordium. Bacteria enter through an infection thread and are eventually enclosed within plant cells in membrane-bound compartments called symbiosomes, where they differentiate into nitrogen-fixing bacteroids.

Inside the nodule, the oxygen-carrying protein leghemoglobin—structurally similar to animal hemoglobin—gives active nodules their characteristic pink colour and maintains the low free-oxygen environment required by the oxygen-sensitive nitrogenase enzyme complex, while still delivering enough oxygen for bacterial respiration.

Types

Legume nodules are broadly of two kinds. Determinate nodules, found in many tropical legumes such as soybean and common bean, are roughly spherical and lose their meristem early. Indeterminate nodules, found in temperate legumes such as pea, clover and alfalfa, retain a persistent meristem and become elongated, with distinct developmental zones.

Other nodulating plants

Beyond the legumes, a group of plants called actinorhizal plants—including alder, bayberry and Casuarina—form nodules with the filamentous bacterium Frankia. The non-legume Parasponia is a notable exception that nodulates with rhizobia. Root nodules are of enormous agricultural and ecological importance because they reduce dependence on synthetic nitrogen fertilisers.
