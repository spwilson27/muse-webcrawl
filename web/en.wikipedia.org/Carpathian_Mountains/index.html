Carpathian Mountains

Length: about 1,500 km
Highest peak: Gerlachovský štít, 2,655 m (Slovakia)
Countries: Austria, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Poland, Hungary, Ukraine, Romania, Serbia

The Carpathian Mountains or Carpathians form an arc of mountain ranges in Central and Eastern Europe, roughly 1,500 km long — the third-longest European range after the Urals and the Scandinavian Mountains. Stretching from Austria and the Czech Republic in the west through Slovakia, Poland, Hungary, and Ukraine to Romania and Serbia in the south and east, the Carpathians enclose the Pannonian Basin and the Transylvanian Plateau.

Geography and geology

The range is conventionally divided into the Western Carpathians (including the High Tatras, with the highest summit, Gerlachovský štít at 2,655 m), the Eastern Carpathians, and the Southern Carpathians in Romania (with Moldoveanu Peak, 2,544 m). The mountains arose during the Alpine orogeny mainly in the Cretaceous and Tertiary, from the collision of the Alcapa and Tisza-Dacia microplates with the European margin; unlike the Alps, much of the outer Carpathians consists of flysch — alternating sandstone and shale — and the inner ranges include volcanic rocks.

Nature

The Carpathians hold some of Europe's largest remaining tracts of virgin forest and its largest populations of brown bears, wolves, and Eurasian lynx outside Russia. Primeval beech forests of the range are inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list. Major rivers, including the Vistula, Dniester, Tisza, and the Danube (which cuts the range at the Iron Gates), rise in or cross the Carpathians.

People and culture

The mountains are home to diverse peoples, including Slovaks, Poles (with the Górale highlanders of Podhale), Ukrainians and Hutsuls, Romanians, Hungarians and Székelys, and others. Traditional pastoral culture, wooden churches (many UNESCO-listed), and spa and ski resorts such as Zakopane mark the region, which also figures in legend as the home of Vlad the Impaler and the fictional Count Dracula, associated with Bran Castle in Transylvania.
