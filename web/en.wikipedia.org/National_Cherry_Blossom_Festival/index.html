National Cherry Blossom Festival

LocationWashington, D.C., USA
WhenLate March – mid April
First held1935 (roots in 1912 gift of trees)
Attendance~1.5 million

The National Cherry Blossom Festival is an annual spring celebration in Washington, D.C., commemorating the gift of 3,020 Japanese cherry trees from Tokyo mayor Yukio Ozaki to the city on 27 March 1912. The festival marks the blossoming of the trees around the Tidal Basin and celebrates U.S.–Japanese friendship.

History

The 1912 gift — an earlier 1910 shipment had to be destroyed because of insect infestation — was planted around the Tidal Basin and on the White House grounds; First Lady Helen Herron Taft and the wife of the Japanese ambassador planted the first two trees. A small festival began in 1935 and grew after the Second World War into a multi-week event. Japan has continued the exchange, and in 2024 announced a further gift of 250 trees for the U.S. semiquincentennial.

Events

The festival typically runs for about three weeks around the predicted peak bloom, which the National Park Service forecasts each year. Events include the opening ceremony, the Blossom Kite Festival on the Washington Monument grounds, the National Cherry Blossom Festival Parade along Constitution Avenue, fireworks over the Southwest waterfront (Petalpalooza), and ranger-led walks around the Tidal Basin.
