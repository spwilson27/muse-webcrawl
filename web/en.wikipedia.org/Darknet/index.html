A darknet or dark net is an overlay network within the Internet that can only be accessed with specific software, configurations, or authorisation, and often uses a unique customised communication protocol. Two typical darknet types are social networks (usually used for file hosting with a peer-to-peer connection), and anonymity proxy networks such as Tor via an anonymised series of connections.

Terminology

The term "darknet" was popularised in the 1970s to denote networks isolated from ARPANET for security reasons, and gained its modern sense in the 2000s. Content hosted on darknets forms the dark web, distinguished from the deep web — ordinary web content not indexed by search engines, such as webmail and online banking.

Technology and uses

Darknets route traffic through multiple relays with layered encryption (onion routing in Tor's case), concealing users' locations and usage from network surveillance and traffic analysis. Legitimate uses include circumventing censorship, protecting dissidents, journalists and whistleblowers, and secure communication; major organisations such as the BBC and The New York Times operate Tor onion services. Darknets have also hosted illicit commerce, notably the Silk Road marketplace shut down by the FBI in 2013, and various successors seized in later operations.

Software

The best-known darknet software is Tor, originally developed by the United States Naval Research Laboratory. Alternatives include I2P (the Invisible Internet Project), Freenet, and ZeroNet, each with different models of anonymity and decentralisation.
