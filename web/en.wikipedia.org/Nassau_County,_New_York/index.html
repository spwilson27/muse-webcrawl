Nassau County is a suburban county on Long Island in the U.S. state of New York, immediately east of New York City. With about 1.4 million residents, it is one of the most densely populated and affluent counties in the United States, and together with Suffolk County it forms the part of Long Island outside the city.

Geography and communities

The county comprises three towns — Hempstead, North Hempstead and Oyster Bay — two cities (Glen Cove and Long Beach) and 64 incorporated villages. Its North Shore, the "Gold Coast," was famed for Gilded Age estates, while the South Shore faces the Atlantic Ocean barrier beaches of Long Beach and Jones Beach State Park.

History

Nassau County was created in 1899 from the eastern part of Queens County after Queens' western portion joined New York City. After the Second World War it became a symbol of American suburbanisation: Levittown, begun in 1947 in the Town of Hempstead, was the archetypal mass-produced suburb. Aviation history is also prominent — Charles Lindbergh departed on his 1927 transatlantic flight from Roosevelt Field.

Economy and transport

The county is largely residential, served by the Long Island Rail Road and parkways such as the Long Island Expressway. Major institutions include Hofstra University, the Nassau Coliseum in Uniondale, and Belmont Park racetrack on the Queens border.
