The International Air Transport Association (IATA) is a trade association of the world's airlines, founded in 1945. Its member airlines carry most of the world's scheduled air traffic. IATA supports airline activity and helps formulate industry policy and standards, including the widely used two-letter airline designators and three-letter airport codes.

Activities

IATA operates the Billing and Settlement Plan used to distribute ticket revenue among airlines, runs the IATA Operational Safety Audit (IOSA), and coordinates scheduling and slot conferences. It also publishes standards for baggage handling, dangerous goods, and passenger processes such as the bar-coded boarding pass.

History

IATA succeeded the International Air Traffic Association founded at The Hague in 1919. Until the late 1970s it also coordinated international fares under government approval, a role curtailed by deregulation, especially in the United States and Europe.
