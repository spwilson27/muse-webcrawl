Cross of Sacrifice

The Cross of Sacrifice is a Commonwealth war memorial designed in 1918 by Sir Reginald Blomfield for the Imperial War Graves Commission (now the Commonwealth War Graves Commission, CWGC). It is erected in Commonwealth war cemeteries containing forty or more graves, and is instantly recognisable: a tall, plain cross of white stone on an octagonal base, with a bronze longsword, blade down, fixed to its front.

Design

Blomfield designed the cross in four sizes, ranging from about 4.4 to 9 metres in height, so that its scale suits the size of the cemetery. The downward-pointing sword symbolises both the military character of the dead and the idea of sacrifice without triumphalism. In larger cemeteries the Cross is paired with Sir Edwin Lutyens's Stone of Remembrance. Blomfield deliberately chose a cross — a widely recognised symbol — while the Commission's cemeteries also accommodate individual headstones of many faiths.

Distribution

Over a thousand Crosses of Sacrifice stand in CWGC cemeteries and plots around the world, from the large cemeteries of the Western Front in France and Belgium to sites in Africa, Asia, and the Pacific commemorating the dead of both World Wars.
