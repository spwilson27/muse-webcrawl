High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy

The High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy (HR/VP) is the chief co-ordinator and representative of the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) of the European Union. The position was created by the Treaty of Amsterdam (1999) and greatly strengthened by the Treaty of Lisbon (2009), which merged the role with that of European Commissioner for External Relations, making the holder a Vice-President of the European Commission (hence "HR/VP"). The High Representative heads the European External Action Service (EEAS), the EU's diplomatic service.

Role

The High Representative conducts the CFSP, contributes proposals to its development, chairs the Foreign Affairs Council of EU foreign ministers, and represents the Union in international organizations and conferences. As a Vice-President of the Commission, the holder co-ordinates the external dimensions of other EU policies such as trade, development, and neighbourhood policy. The EEAS, established in 2010, brings together officials from the Council, the Commission, and member states' diplomatic services, and runs the EU's delegations (embassies) around the world.

History

Javier Solana, a former NATO Secretary General, became the first High Representative for the CFSP in 1999. The Lisbon Treaty was designed to answer the (probably apocryphal) question attributed to Henry Kissinger — "Who do I call if I want to call Europe?" — by creating a single figure combining the Council's foreign-policy chief and the Commission's external relations commissioner. Catherine Ashton was the first holder of the expanded post and set up the EEAS. Josep Borrell's tenure covered the EU's response to Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, including coordinated sanctions and military assistance financing. Kaja Kallas, former prime minister of Estonia, took office on 1 December 2024.
