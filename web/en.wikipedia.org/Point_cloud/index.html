A point cloud is a discrete set of data points in space. The points may represent a 3D shape or object, each point having its set of Cartesian coordinates (X, Y, Z). Point clouds are generally produced by 3D scanners or by photogrammetry software, which measure many points on the external surfaces of objects around them. They are used for many purposes, including creating 3D computer-aided design models, metrology and quality inspection, and visualization, animation, rendering, and mass customization.

Generation

Point clouds are most commonly generated using lidar (time-of-flight laser scanning, terrestrial or airborne), structured-light and laser triangulation scanners, stereo vision, structure-from-motion photogrammetry from photographs, and depth cameras. A single scan may contain millions to billions of points; multiple scans are aligned (registered) using targets or iterative closest point (ICP) algorithms.

Processing

Raw point clouds are typically cleaned (removing noise and outliers), downsampled (e.g. voxel-grid filtering), and segmented into objects or surfaces. Surface reconstruction converts points into meshes via methods such as Poisson reconstruction or Delaunay triangulation; normal estimation and feature detection support recognition and modelling. Large datasets are stored in spatial structures such as octrees and in compressed formats (notably LAZ for lidar).

Applications

Point clouds underpin topographic and bathymetric mapping, autonomous-vehicle perception, robotics (SLAM), construction and building information modelling (scan-to-BIM), heritage documentation and archaeology, forestry inventory, film and game production, reverse engineering, and medical imaging. In machine learning, architectures such as PointNet process point clouds directly for classification and segmentation.

Standards and software

Important standards include LAS (and its compressed form LAZ) for airborne lidar, the ASTM E57 format for 3D imaging data, and the Point Cloud Library (PCL), an open-source framework for point cloud processing; formats such as PLY and XYZ are also widely used for exchange.
