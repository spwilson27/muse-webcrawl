Katharevousa
Language: Greek
Type: Puristic, archaizing variety
Official in Greece: until 1976
Replaced by: Demotic Greek (Standard Modern Greek)

Katharevousa (Greek: Καθαρεύουσα, "purifying") is a form of the Greek language that was devised in the early nineteenth century as a compromise between Ancient Greek and the spoken vernacular, Demotic. Associated with the scholar Adamantios Korais, it purged foreign loanwords and restored archaic grammar and vocabulary.

The language question

For over a century Katharevousa was the official language of the Greek state, education and press, while Demotic was the language of everyday life — a diglossia at the centre of the Greek "language question" that provoked riots (notably over Demotic translations of the Gospels in 1901 and of Oresteia in 1903).

Resolution

After the fall of the military junta, Demotic was made the official language in 1976, and remains the basis of Standard Modern Greek, though Katharevousa has left a lasting imprint on its vocabulary. See also Greek language question.
