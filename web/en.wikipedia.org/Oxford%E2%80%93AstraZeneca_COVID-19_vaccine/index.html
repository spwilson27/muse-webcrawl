Oxford–AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine

Brand names: Vaxzevria, Covishield

Type: Viral vector vaccine (ChAdOx1)

Developers: University of Oxford (Jenner Institute), AstraZeneca

First authorised: United Kingdom, 30 December 2020

The Oxford–AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand names Vaxzevria and Covishield, is a viral-vector vaccine against COVID-19 developed by the University of Oxford's Jenner Institute and the British-Swedish pharmaceutical company AstraZeneca. It uses a modified chimpanzee adenovirus, ChAdOx1, engineered to carry the gene for the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. It was one of the first COVID-19 vaccines authorised and, supplied at cost during the pandemic under a not-for-profit pledge, became the most widely distributed vaccine in low- and middle-income countries in 2021.

Development and authorisation

Work began at Oxford in January 2020, building on the Jenner Institute's earlier ChAdOx1 platform trialled against MERS. AstraZeneca partnered with the university in April 2020 to manufacture and distribute the vaccine globally. Phase III results reported in late 2020 showed an average efficacy of about 70 percent against symptomatic disease, with higher figures in some dosing regimens. The UK's MHRA authorised it on 30 December 2020 — vaccination began on 4 January 2021 — followed by the European Medicines Agency in January 2021 and an emergency-use listing from the World Health Organization in February 2021, enabling supply through COVAX. In India the Serum Institute produced it as Covishield, becoming the world's largest supplier by volume.

Safety concerns

In March 2021, reports emerged of a very rare syndrome of thrombosis with thrombocytopenia (blood clots with low platelets, also called VITT) occurring after vaccination, on the order of a few cases per million doses. Many countries briefly suspended use, and then resumed with age restrictions, preferring alternative vaccines for younger adults because their risk from COVID-19 was lower. Regulators consistently assessed the benefits as outweighing the risks for older populations. The vaccine was never authorised in the United States.

Withdrawal

Demand fell as mRNA vaccines and variant-adapted boosters predominated. In 2024 AstraZeneca withdrew Vaxzevria's marketing authorisations worldwide, citing a surplus of updated vaccines and declining demand; the company acknowledged in UK court proceedings that the vaccine could, in very rare cases, cause thrombosis with thrombocytopenia syndrome. Estimates credited the vaccine with preventing millions of deaths in its first year of use.
