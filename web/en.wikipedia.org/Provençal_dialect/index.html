Provençal (Occitan: Provençau or Prouvençau) is a variety of Occitan spoken by people in Provence and parts of Drôme and Gard. Historically, the term has also been used loosely to refer to the whole Occitan language, the literary language of the troubadours in the Middle Ages; in modern linguistic usage it denotes the dialect group of southeastern Occitania, alongside Languedocien, Limousin, Auvergnat, Gascon and Vivaro-Alpine.

History

Provençal descends from the Latin spoken in Roman Provence. In the 12th and 13th centuries a closely related literary form of Occitan became the great lyric language of the troubadours, whose poetry influenced literature across Europe. After Provence was incorporated into the Kingdom of France in the late 15th century, French progressively displaced Occitan in administration and writing. In the 19th century the poet Frédéric Mistral led a revival of Provençal literature through the Félibrige movement, which he founded in 1854; Mistral won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904.

Subdialects

Provençal proper divides into several local varieties, including Maritime Provençal (around Marseille, Toulon and Aix), Rhodanian Provençal (around Avignon, Nîmes and Arles) and Niçard, spoken around Nice, which some classify separately. Maritime and Rhodanian varieties differ notably in the treatment of final vowels and in the outcome of Latin palatalised consonants.

Orthography

Two spelling systems are in use. The classical norm, developed in the 20th century and used for Occitan generally, aims at unity across dialects and continuity with medieval spelling. The Mistralian norm, devised by Joseph Roumanille and used by Mistral, is based more closely on French orthographic conventions and is still used by some Provençal associations.

Status

Like the other Occitan varieties, Provençal is severely endangered; intergenerational transmission largely ceased during the 20th century, though it is taught optionally in some schools and supported by regional cultural associations and the Occitan-language immersion schools known as calandretas.
