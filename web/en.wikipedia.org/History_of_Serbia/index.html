History of Serbia

The history of Serbia covers the period from the settlement of the Serbs in the Balkans in the early Middle Ages, through the medieval Nemanjić kingdom and empire, five centuries of Ottoman rule, the 19th-century revival of the Serbian state, the Yugoslav period, and the modern Republic of Serbia.

Medieval monasteries such as Studenica anchor Serbia's cultural heritage.

Medieval Serbia

Slavic tribes, including the Serbs, settled the Balkans in the 6th and 7th centuries. The medieval Serbian state grew under the Nemanjić dynasty: Stefan the First-Crowned was crowned king in 1217, and his brother Saint Sava established the autocephalous Serbian Orthodox Church in 1219. Under Stefan Dušan, crowned emperor in 1346, Serbia reached its greatest territorial extent, ruling much of the southern Balkans, and Dušan promulgated a famous law code.

Ottoman period

After Dušan's death the empire fragmented. The Battle of Kosovo in 1389, in which both Prince Lazar and Sultan Murad I died, became central to Serbian national memory. The Serbian Despotate fell to the Ottomans in 1459, and Serbia remained under Ottoman rule for nearly five centuries, though the Habsburgs held northern parts intermittently.

The Serbian state revived

The First Serbian Uprising (1804), led by Karađorđe, and the Second (1815), led by Miloš Obrenović, won autonomy and then recognised independence at the Congress of Berlin in 1878. Rivalry between the Obrenović and Karađorđević dynasties marked the new kingdom, which expanded in the Balkan Wars of 1912–13.

Yugoslavia

Serbia's victory in the First World War — at great cost — was followed in 1918 by the creation of the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, later Yugoslavia. After the Second World War, in which Yugoslavia was occupied and divided, Serbia was a republic within Tito's socialist federation. Following Yugoslavia's break-up in the wars of the 1990s, Serbia remained in a federation with Montenegro until 2006, when it again became a fully independent state.
