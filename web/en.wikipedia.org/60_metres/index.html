60 metres

The 60 metres is a sprint event in track and field athletics. It is the championship distance for indoor competition, where the standard 200-metre indoor track cannot accommodate a straight 100 metres, and it is run at the World Athletics Indoor Championships and most national indoor championships. Outdoors it is sometimes contested as an early-season or exhibition event, and it was run at the 1900 and 1904 Olympic Games.

The race

Because the race is so short, it disproportionately rewards explosive starting and acceleration rather than maximum top speed; elite sprinters typically reach their fastest speeds shortly after the 60-metre mark. Reaction time and the drive phase out of the starting blocks are therefore decisive, and races are frequently separated by hundredths of a second.

Records

The men's world record of 6.34 seconds was set by Christian Coleman of the United States in 2018, breaking the long-standing mark of Maurice Greene. The women's world record of 6.92 seconds, set by Irina Privalova in 1993, has stood for decades. The event's specialists are often also elite 100 metres runners, using the indoor season to sharpen their starts before the outdoor season begins.
