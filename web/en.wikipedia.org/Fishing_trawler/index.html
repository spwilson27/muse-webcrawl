Fishing trawler

TypeCommercial fishing vessel
MethodTrawling (dragging nets)

A fishing trawler is a commercial fishing vessel designed to operate fishing trawls — large nets towed through the water at various depths or along the seabed.

Types

Trawlers are classified by how the net is deployed. Stern trawlers haul the net up a ramp or over the stern; side trawlers shoot and haul over the side; and outrigger trawlers tow nets from outrigger booms. Large factory trawlers process and freeze the catch at sea, while smaller vessels land fresh fish iced in the hold. Beam trawlers drag a net held open by a rigid beam, and pair trawlers tow a single net between two boats.

History

Trawling developed with sail in the nineteenth century, notably the Brixham trawlers of Devon, and was transformed by steam and then diesel power, echo-sounding, and synthetic-fibre nets. The advent of stern factory trawlers after the Second World War greatly expanded distant-water fishing.

Environmental concerns

Bottom trawling in particular is criticised for damaging seabed habitats and for bycatch of non-target species; regulators respond with gear restrictions, closed areas, and quotas.
