Eighteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution

Proposed: 18 December 1917

Ratified: 16 January 1919

Took effect: 17 January 1920

Repealed: 5 December 1933 (by the Twenty-first Amendment)

Subject: Prohibition of alcohol

The Eighteenth Amendment (Amendment XVIII) to the United States Constitution established the prohibition of alcohol in the United States. The amendment was proposed by Congress on 18 December 1917 and ratified by the requisite number of states on 16 January 1919. It was repealed by the Twenty-first Amendment on 5 December 1933 — the only amendment to the Constitution ever repealed, and the only one to limit the activities of ordinary citizens (rather than the powers of government).

Text

Section 1. After one year from the ratification of this article the manufacture, sale, or transportation of intoxicating liquors within, the importation thereof into, or the exportation thereof from the United States and all territory subject to the jurisdiction thereof for beverage purposes is hereby prohibited.

Section 2. The Congress and the several States shall have concurrent power to enforce this article by appropriate legislation.

Section 3. This article shall be inoperative unless it shall have been ratified as an amendment to the Constitution by the legislatures of the several States, as provided in the Constitution, within seven years from the date of the submission hereof to the States by the Congress.

Background

The amendment was the culmination of the decades-long temperance movement, led by organisations such as the Woman's Christian Temperance Union and the Anti-Saloon League, which blamed alcohol for crime, poverty, and domestic violence. Wartime grain conservation, anti-German sentiment directed at brewers, and the new federal income tax (which freed the government from dependence on liquor excise revenue) all helped the dry cause. Many states and counties had already gone dry before 1919.

Prohibition era and repeal

The Volstead Act (National Prohibition Act) provided for enforcement from 17 January 1920. Prohibition reduced overall alcohol consumption and cirrhosis deaths, especially in its early years, but it also fuelled a vast illegal trade: speakeasies, bootlegging, rum-running, and organised crime syndicates such as Al Capone's Chicago Outfit flourished. Enforcement was underfunded and corruptible, and public support collapsed amid the Great Depression. The Twenty-first Amendment repealed the Eighteenth in 1933, returning control of alcohol to the states — the only amendment ratified by state conventions rather than legislatures.
