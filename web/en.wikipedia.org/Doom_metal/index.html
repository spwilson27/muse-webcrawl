Doom metal

Doom metal emphasises slow tempos and a thick, heavy sound.

Doom metal is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music that typically employs slower tempos, low-tuned guitars, and a "thick" or "heavy" sound, creating music intended to evoke a sense of impending doom, dread, and despair. It is strongly influenced by the early work of Black Sabbath, whose songs such as "Black Sabbath" and "Iron Man" set the template; the genre's name derives from Black Sabbath vocalist Ozzy Osbourne's song "Hand of Doom".

History

Proto-doom acts of the 1970s include Black Sabbath, Pentagram, and Bedemon. The genre took shape in the early–mid 1980s with bands such as Saint Vitus, Candlemass, Trouble, and Witchfinder General. Candlemass's Epicus Doomicus Metallicus (1986) gave its name to the "epic doom" style.

Subgenres

Traditional doom retains a blues-influenced, retro sound. Epic doom adds grandiose, operatic vocals. Death-doom (Paradise Lost, My Dying Bride, Anathema) fuses doom with death metal and fed into gothic metal; funeral doom pushes tempos and atmosphere to extremes. Sludge metal (Eyehategod, Crowbar) mixes doom with hardcore punk, while stoner metal (Sleep, Electric Wizard) emphasises groove and psychedelia. Drone doom, exemplified by Sunn O))), centres on sustained, massively amplified tones.

Characteristics

Tempos are slow, guitars are heavily distorted and often tuned very low, and lyrics dwell on suffering, loss, occult themes, and pessimism. Vocals range from clean, mournful singing to growls.
