Pain au chocolat

Pain au chocolat (French pronunciation: [pɛ̃ o ʃɔkɔla], literally "bread with chocolate"), also known as chocolatine in the south-west part of France and in Quebec, is a type of viennoiserie sweet roll consisting of a cuboid-shaped piece of yeast-leavened laminated dough, similar in texture to a puff pastry, with one or two pieces of dark chocolate in the center, surrounded by the pastry.

Description

Pain au chocolat is made of the same layered dough as a croissant, but is formed into a rectangle rather than a crescent and contains batons of dark chocolate. It is a staple of French bakeries (boulangeries) and is typically eaten at breakfast or as an afternoon snack (goûter), often alongside coffee.

Name controversy

The name used for the pastry is a longstanding point of regional identity in France: most of the country says pain au chocolat, while the south-west (and Quebec) says chocolatine. Surveys, press coverage, and even a proposed parliamentary bill in 2018 (which sought to recognise "chocolatine" officially) have kept the debate in the public eye. The word chocolatine is believed to derive from the Gascon language, while some accounts link early usage to English workers in Bordeaux bringing "chocolate in bread" (chocolatines) in the 19th century.

History

The pastry descends from Viennese baking traditions brought to Paris in the 1830s by August Zang's Boulangerie Viennoise, where a chocolate version of the kipferl-based pastries was sold. By the early 20th century, pain au chocolat had become a standard item across France.
