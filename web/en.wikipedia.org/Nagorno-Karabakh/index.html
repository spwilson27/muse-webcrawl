Nagorno-Karabakh (Russian: Нагорный Карабах, "mountainous Karabakh"; Armenian: Լեռնային Ղարաբաղ; Azerbaijani: Dağlıq Qarabağ) is a mountainous region in the South Caucasus, internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan. Historically inhabited by both Armenians and Azerbaijanis, it was the centre of a decades-long conflict between Armenia and Azerbaijan.

History

In 1923 the Soviet government established the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast within the Azerbaijan SSR, with an ethnic Armenian majority. As the Soviet Union weakened, the region's Armenians demanded transfer to the Armenian SSR; the dispute escalated into the First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1988–1994), which ended with Armenian forces controlling the oblast and surrounding districts. The resulting self-proclaimed Republic of Artsakh was recognised by no UN member state.

Azerbaijan regained large areas in the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War of 2020, brokered by a Russian-ceasefire agreement with Russian peacekeepers deployed. In September 2023, after a months-long blockade of the Lachin corridor, Azerbaijan launched a brief offensive; the Artsakh authorities capitulated, the republic announced its dissolution effective 1 January 2024, and nearly the entire ethnic Armenian population — over 100,000 people — fled to Armenia in an exodus widely described as ethnic cleansing by international observers, a characterisation Azerbaijan disputes.

Geography

The region is rugged and mountainous, averaging about 1,100 metres above sea level, with the Murovdag and Karabakh ranges, deep river gorges, and forests. Its principal city is Stepanakert (Azerbaijani: Khankendi); other towns include Shusha (Shushi), a cultural centre for both peoples.
