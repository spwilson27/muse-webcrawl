Robert Rubin

Robert Edward Rubin (born August 29, 1938) is an American banker and former government official who served as the 70th United States Secretary of the Treasury from 1995 to 1999 under President Bill Clinton.

Career

Rubin spent 26 years at Goldman Sachs, rising to co-chairman, before joining the Clinton administration as director of the National Economic Council. As Treasury Secretary he was associated with deficit reduction, a strong-dollar policy, and responses to the Mexican peso crisis of 1994–95 and the Asian financial crisis of 1997–98. After leaving government he became a senior executive at Citigroup, a role that later drew scrutiny during the 2008 financial crisis.

Later work

Rubin co-founded the Hamilton Project at the Brookings Institution and served as co-chair of the Council on Foreign Relations. His memoir In an Uncertain World was published in 2003.
