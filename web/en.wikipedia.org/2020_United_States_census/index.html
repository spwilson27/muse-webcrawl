The United States census of 2020 was the twenty-fourth decennial United States census. Census Day, the reference day used for the count, was April 1, 2020. It was the first U.S. census in which most households were invited to respond online, alongside options to respond by phone or by mail.

The census counted a resident population of 331,449,281 in the fifty states and the District of Columbia, an increase of 7.4 percent over the 308,745,538 counted in the 2010 census. The growth rate was the second-slowest in U.S. history, after the 1930s.

Apportionment

As required by the Constitution, the census results were used to reapportion seats in the U.S. House of Representatives among the states. Texas gained two seats; Colorado, Florida, Montana, North Carolina, and Oregon each gained one. California, Illinois, Michigan, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and West Virginia each lost one seat. It was the first time in census history that California lost a congressional seat.

Controversies and challenges

The census was conducted amid the COVID-19 pandemic, which disrupted field operations and door-to-door follow-up. The Trump administration's attempt to add a question on citizenship status was blocked by the Supreme Court in 2019 on procedural grounds. Legal disputes over the counting deadline and the treatment of undocumented immigrants in apportionment also reached the courts, and apportionment results were delivered to President Joe Biden in April 2021.
