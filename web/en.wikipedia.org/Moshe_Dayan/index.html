Moshe Dayan (Hebrew: משה דיין‎; 20 May 1915 – 16 October 1981) was an Israeli military leader and politician. As commander of the Jerusalem front in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Chief of Staff of the Israel Defense Forces (1953–1958) during the 1956 Sinai campaign, and Minister of Defense during the Six-Day War of 1967, he became a worldwide symbol of the new state of Israel. His black eyepatch, worn after he lost his left eye to a sniper's bullet in 1941, became his trademark.

Early life and military career

Dayan was born on Kibbutz Degania Alef, one of the first children born on a kibbutz. He joined the Haganah at 14 and served in Orde Wingate's Special Night Squads and in the Palmach. In 1941, serving with Allied forces in Vichy-held Lebanon, he was shot while using binoculars as a lookout, losing his left eye.

Commander and minister

As Chief of Staff, Dayan shaped the IDF's offensive doctrine and led the 1956 Sinai Campaign. Recalled to government on the eve of the Six-Day War in June 1967, he oversaw Israel's victory and the capture of the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Sinai and Golan Heights. Blamed for Israel's unpreparedness in the Yom Kippur War of October 1973, he resigned in 1974. As Foreign Minister under Menachem Begin from 1977, he played a key role in negotiating the Camp David Accords and the Egypt–Israel peace treaty of 1979.

Dayan was also a keen amateur archaeologist, whose private collecting of antiquities was controversial in Israel. His daughter Yael Dayan became a noted writer and politician.
