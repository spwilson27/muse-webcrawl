Concepción volcano

Concepción is an active stratovolcano in Nicaragua, forming the northwestern part of the island of Ometepe in Lake Nicaragua. Rising to about 1,610 metres, it is one of the country's tallest and most active volcanoes, and its near-perfect cone makes it a prominent landmark.

Geology and activity

Concepción is connected by a narrow isthmus to the neighbouring Maderas volcano, together forming the island of Ometepe. The volcano has erupted frequently in historical times, with recorded eruptions since the late nineteenth century consisting mostly of moderate explosive activity and ash emissions that periodically affect the island's towns, including Moyogalpa and Altagracia. Notable eruptive episodes occurred in 1957 and 2005–2010, producing ash plumes and evacuations but limited damage.

Setting

Unlike the heavily modified cones of many Nicaraguan volcanoes, Concepción retains a symmetrical profile mantled in ash and lava flows. Its slopes support farms and, at higher elevations, protected cloud forest habitat within the Ometepe Biosphere Reserve, designated by UNESCO in 2010.
