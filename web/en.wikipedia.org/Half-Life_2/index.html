Half-Life 2

Developer: Valve

Publisher: Valve; Sierra Entertainment

Release: November 16, 2004

Genre: First-person shooter

Engine: Source

Half-Life 2 is a 2004 first-person shooter game developed and published by Valve. Like the original Half-Life (1998), it follows theoretical physicist Gordon Freeman, who awakens about twenty years after the events of the first game to find Earth conquered by the alien Combine empire. With the help of resistance fighter Alyx Vance, Freeman fights to free humanity in and around the dystopian City 17.

Half-Life 2 introduced the Source game engine, with advanced physics simulation and facial animation for its time, and the Gravity Gun, a weapon that lets players manipulate objects in the environment. It was the first game distributed through Valve's Steam platform, a requirement that was controversial at launch.

Gameplay

As in the original, the story unfolds entirely from Freeman's perspective without cutscenes. Players progress through linear levels combining combat against Combine soldiers and alien creatures with environmental puzzles that exploit the physics engine. Vehicle sections include an airboat and a dune buggy. The Gravity Gun (formally the Zero Point Energy Field Manipulator) became the game's signature, turning saw blades, radiators, and explosive barrels into weapons.

Development

Development lasted roughly five years and cost an estimated $40 million. In 2003, the game's unfinished source code was stolen and leaked online by a hacker, and the release was delayed from September 2003 to November 2004. Valve founder Gabe Newell directed a large team; writer Marc Laidlaw developed the story, and Viktor Antonov designed City 17, inspired by Eastern European cities such as Sofia and Belgrade.

Reception

Half-Life 2 was a critical and commercial success, winning 39 Game of the Year awards and selling over 12 million copies by 2011. Critics praised its physics, animation, sound, narrative, and graphics. It is frequently cited among the greatest video games ever made. Two episodic sequels, Episode One (2006) and Episode Two (2007), followed, and the series returned in 2020 with the virtual reality game Half-Life: Alyx.
