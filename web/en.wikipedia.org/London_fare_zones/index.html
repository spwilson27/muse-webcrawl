London fare zones

London fare zones are concentric zones radiating from central London used by Transport for London (TfL) and National Rail to calculate fares on London's public transport network, including the London Underground, London Overground, the Elizabeth line, Docklands Light Railway, trams and most National Rail services within the capital. Zone 1 covers the central area, and the zones extend outward to Zone 9 at the edge of the Oyster and contactless payment area.

History

London Transport introduced fare zones in the early 1980s as part of a simplification of its fares structure. The zones underpinned the Travelcard, launched in 1983, which offered unlimited travel within chosen zones. The system was later adopted for the Oyster smartcard from 2003 and for contactless payment capping, in which the fare charged depends on the zones passed through, subject to daily and weekly caps.

Operation

Every station is assigned to one zone, with some stations on a boundary priced as being in either of the adjoining zones, whichever is more favourable for the journey. Fares rise with the number of zones traversed, and special rules apply to journeys avoiding Zone 1 on designated routes, which are charged at a lower rate when passengers touch in on pink route validators. Extensions of the contactless area have progressively added stations beyond Greater London into surrounding counties, and a small number of stations lie in zones 10–15 for National Rail fares purposes outside the TfL zonal system.
