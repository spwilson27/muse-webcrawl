Logo (programming language)

Logo is an educational programming language, designed in 1967 by Wally Feurzeig, Seymour Papert, and Cynthia Solomon. It is a multi-paradigm adaptation and dialect of Lisp, created at Bolt, Beranek and Newman in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

Turtle graphics

Logo is best known for its turtle graphics, in which commands such as FORWARD and RIGHT move a "turtle" — originally a floor robot and later a cursor on screen — that leaves a drawn trail. The approach let children explore geometry and programming by "body-syntonic" reasoning, imagining themselves as the turtle, as advocated in Papert's book Mindstorms (1980).

Language features

Logo has no standard, but implementations such as UCBLogo (Berkeley Logo), MSWLogo, FMSLogo and MicroWorlds offer lists, recursion, procedures and file handling. It emphasises a low floor and no ceiling: easy for beginners yet powerful enough for sophisticated projects.

Influence

Widely used in schools in the 1980s on microcomputers, Logo influenced later educational environments including Scratch and the constructionist approach to learning, and its turtle concept survives in Python's turtle module and many other systems.
