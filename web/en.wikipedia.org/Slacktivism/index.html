Slacktivism
TypePortmanteau / pejorative term
Blends"Slacker" + "activism"
Coined1990s; popularised 2000s
Related termsClicktivism, hashtag activism, virtue signalling

Slacktivism is a pejorative portmanteau of slacker and activism, describing forms of political or charitable support that require little effort, time or personal commitment — such as liking or sharing a social media post, signing an online petition, wearing an awareness ribbon or changing a profile picture — and that are suspected of serving mainly to make the supporter feel good rather than to produce change.

History of the term

The term circulated in the 1990s among youth subcultures, initially without strongly negative connotation, describing small acts of personal protest. After the rise of social media, journalists and commentators increasingly used it critically; related terms include clicktivism and hashtag activism. The 2012 Kony 2012 video campaign and the 2014 ALS Ice Bucket Challenge are frequently discussed examples — the latter, notably, raised over a hundred million dollars for research, complicating simple dismissal.

Debate

Critics argue that token online support can substitute for, and thereby crowd out, meaningful action, while costing organisations little to collect and little for supporters to give. Defenders respond that low-barrier engagement raises awareness, builds mailing lists and can act as a gateway to deeper involvement; research findings are mixed, with some studies suggesting publicly visible token support may reduce later contributions while private token support may increase them through consistency effects.
