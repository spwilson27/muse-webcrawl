Warren McCulloch

Warren Sturgis McCulloch (November 16, 1898 – September 24, 1969) was an American neurophysiologist and cybernetician. He is remembered for foundational work in neural networks and cybernetics.

McCulloch–Pitts neuron

In 1943, with logician Walter Pitts, McCulloch published "A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity", which introduced the McCulloch–Pitts neuron — a simplified mathematical model of a neuron that could compute logical functions. The paper laid groundwork for artificial neural networks and theoretical computer science.

Career

McCulloch worked at Yale, the University of Illinois College of Medicine, and from 1952 at MIT's Research Laboratory of Electronics. He helped organise the Macy conferences that launched cybernetics, and published the influential essay "What Is a Number, that a Man May Know It, and a Man, that He May Know a Number?"
