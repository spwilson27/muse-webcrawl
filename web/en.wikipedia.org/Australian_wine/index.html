Australian wine

The Australian wine industry is one of the world's largest exporters of wine, exporting approximately 800 million litres annually in recent decades to a large international market, with major destinations historically including the United Kingdom, the United States and China. Australia has no native grape varieties; viticulture began with cuttings brought by the First Fleet in 1788 and expanded with nineteenth-century settlers, including Silesian Lutherans in the Barossa Valley.

Regions

Wine is produced in every state, with the bulk in the south-east. Major regions include the Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale and Clare Valley in South Australia; the Hunter Valley in New South Wales; the Yarra Valley and Rutherglen in Victoria; Margaret River in Western Australia; and the cool-climate island state of Tasmania. Australia has over 60 designated wine regions and a system of Geographical Indications established in the 1990s.

Styles and varieties

Shiraz (Syrah) is the most planted variety and Australia's signature red, alongside Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Merlot, Sémillon and Riesling. The country is noted both for premium single-vineyard wines — exemplified by Penfolds Grange — and for technologically innovative, fruit-forward commercial wines. Sparkling wines, fortified wines from Rutherglen, and emerging alternative varieties also feature. Because of its phylloxera-free status in most states, South Australia retains some of the world's oldest producing vineyards.
