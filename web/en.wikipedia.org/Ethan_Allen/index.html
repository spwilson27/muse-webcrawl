Ethan Allen

Statue of Ethan Allen outside the Vermont State House

Ethan Allen (1738–1789) was an American farmer, businessman, land speculator, philosopher, writer, and patriot in the American Revolutionary War. He is best known as one of the founders of the state of Vermont and for the capture of Fort Ticonderoga early in the war.

Early life and the Green Mountain Boys

Born in Litchfield, Connecticut, Allen moved to the New Hampshire Grants (present-day Vermont), where land titles were disputed between New York and New Hampshire. He helped organise the Green Mountain Boys, a militia formed to resist New York's attempts to assert jurisdiction, and was declared an outlaw by New York's governor.

Revolutionary War

On 10 May 1775, Allen and the Green Mountain Boys, together with Benedict Arnold, surprised and captured the British garrison at Fort Ticonderoga, securing artillery later used at Boston. Later that year Allen was captured during an ill-fated attempt to take Montreal and was held prisoner, much of it in England, until exchanged in 1778. His 1779 narrative of his captivity became a bestseller and a staple of American patriotic literature.

Vermont and later life

Allen championed Vermont's independence, declared in 1777, and took part in its politics and in negotiations (the Haldimand negotiations) intended to secure its position. His book Reason: The Only Oracle of Man (1784) was one of the first American works advocating deism and attacking orthodox Christianity. He died in Burlington in 1789, two years before Vermont entered the Union as the fourteenth state. Two U.S. Navy submarines and numerous places bear his name, as does the furniture company Ethan Allen Inc., founded in Vermont in 1932.
