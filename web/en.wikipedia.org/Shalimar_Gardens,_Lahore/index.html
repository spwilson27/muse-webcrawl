The Shalimar Gardens (Punjabi/Urdu: شالامار باغ) are a Mughal garden complex located in Lahore, Pakistan. Construction began in 1641 under the emperor Shah Jahan and was completed the following year. They are among the finest surviving examples of Mughal garden design at the height of the empire's artistic development.

Design

The gardens are laid out as a charbagh (paradise garden) on three descending terraces — named Farah Baksh ("Bestower of Pleasure"), Faiz Baksh ("Bestower of Goodness"), and Hayat Baksh ("Bestower of Life") — running along an axis from south to north. The complex covers about 16 hectares (40 acres) and contains more than 400 fountains, water channels and pools, pavilions, marble cascades, and a great reservoir. A high brick wall with cusped merlons surrounds the garden.

Heritage status

In 1981 the Shalimar Gardens were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List together with the Lahore Fort. The gardens later appeared on the list of World Heritage in Danger (2000–2012) because of damage from a road-widening project and water-system deterioration, before restoration work removed the threat designation.
