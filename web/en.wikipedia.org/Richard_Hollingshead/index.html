Richard Hollingshead

Richard Milton Hollingshead Jr. (May 25, 1900 – May 13, 1975) was an American inventor credited with creating the drive-in theatre. He opened the first patented drive-in, the Automobile Movie Theatre, in Camden, New Jersey, in June 1933.

Invention

Hollingshead, who worked in his father's auto parts business, experimented in his own driveway by mounting a projector on the hood of his car and projecting onto a screen nailed to a tree, testing sight lines and sound arrangements to accommodate viewers seated in their cars. He received U.S. Patent 1,909,537 on May 16, 1933, for the design.

First drive-in

His theatre on Crescent Boulevard in Camden opened on June 6, 1933, charging 25 cents per car and 25 cents per person. It showed the British comedy Wives Beware. The concept spread rapidly, and after patent disputes, drive-ins proliferated across the United States, peaking in the 1950s.

Legacy

Although Hollingshead sold his theatre after a few years and his patent was later declared invalid, he is remembered as the founder of an enduring form of American popular entertainment and car culture.
