Umbanda

Umbanda is a syncretic Brazilian religion that blends African (especially Yoruba and Bantu) traditions with Roman Catholicism, Kardecist Spiritism and Indigenous Brazilian spirituality. It emerged in Rio de Janeiro in the 1920s and is practised throughout Brazil and in neighbouring countries. Umbanda centres on charity, humility and communion with benevolent spirits who are believed to guide and heal the living.

History

According to tradition, Umbanda traces its founding to 15 November 1908, when Zélio Fernandino de Moraes, a young man from Niterói who, at a Kardecist session, is said to have incorporated the spirit of the Caboclo das Sete Encruzilhadas ("Caboclo of the Seven Crossroads"), who announced a new religion in which Indigenous and African spirits — rejected by both orthodox Spiritism and the Catholic Church — would work for charity. The first Umbanda temple, the Tenda Espírita Nossa Senhora da Piedade, followed. Umbanda grew rapidly among Brazil's urban working class, suffered periods of state suspicion and police registration, and received increasing public recognition from the later 20th century.

Beliefs and practice

Umbandists worship a supreme God (Olorum) and venerate the orixás, divine forces of nature (Oxalá, Iemanjá, Ogum, Xangô, Oxóssi and others) often identified with Catholic saints. Worship takes place in temples called terreiros, tendas or centros, led by a pai or mãe de santo. In ceremonies (giras), mediums incorporate spirit guides — caboclos (Indigenous spirits), pretos velhos (spirits of elderly enslaved Africans, exemplifying humility), crianças (child spirits) and others — who give counsel and healing to attendees without charge, charity being the religion's cardinal virtue.

Variants

Umbanda is highly diverse: Umbanda Branca ("White Umbanda") emphasises its Kardecist and Catholic strands, while Umbanda Omolokô and Umbanda de Angola lie closer to Candomblé. It is often contrasted with Quimbanda, which works with Exu and Pomba Gira spirits. Estimates of adherents in Brazil vary widely, from roughly 400,000 declaring Umbanda in the census to millions of sympathisers and occasional participants.
