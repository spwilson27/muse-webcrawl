The reindeer (Rangifer tarandus), known as the caribou in North America when wild, is a species of deer native to Arctic, subarctic, tundra, boreal, and mountainous regions of Northern Europe, Siberia, and North America. It is the only representative of the genus Rangifer.

Description

Reindeer vary considerably in size and colour among subspecies. Uniquely among deer, females of most populations grow antlers as well as males, though female antlers are smaller. Males shed their antlers after the autumn rut, while females retain theirs through the winter. Their noses contain specialised structures that warm cold incoming air, and their hooves adapt seasonally — soft and spongy in summer for traction on wet tundra, hard and sharp-edged in winter for cutting through ice and digging through snow to reach lichens, their principal winter food (often called "reindeer moss", Cladonia rangiferina).

Migration

Some North American caribou herds undertake the longest terrestrial migrations of any land mammal, travelling up to 5,000 km (3,100 mi) a year between wintering grounds in the boreal forest and calving grounds on the tundra. Herds such as the Western Arctic and Porcupine herds number in the hundreds of thousands, though many populations worldwide have declined sharply in recent decades, leading the IUCN to list the species as Vulnerable.

Domestication and human use

Reindeer have been herded for centuries by Indigenous peoples of Eurasia, including the Sámi of Fennoscandia, the Nenets, Evenki, Chukchi, and others of Siberia. Semi-domesticated reindeer provide meat, hides, antlers, and milk, and are used for transport, pulling sledges. In North America, caribou were never fully domesticated, though reindeer introduced to Alaska from Siberia in the late 19th century are herded by Alaska Native communities.

In culture

Reindeer figure prominently in the folklore of northern peoples. In modern Western popular culture, flying reindeer pull Santa Claus's sleigh on Christmas Eve, a tradition popularised by the 1823 poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas" and the 1939 story of Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer.
