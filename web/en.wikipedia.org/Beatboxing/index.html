Beatboxing is a form of vocal percussion primarily involving the art of producing drum beats, rhythm, and musical sounds using one's mouth, lips, tongue, and voice. It may also involve singing, vocal imitation of turntablism, and the simulation of horns, strings, and other musical instruments. Beatboxing is closely associated with hip hop culture, where it is regarded as one of the elements of the movement, but it is not limited to hip hop music.

History

Vocal imitation of percussion has antecedents in many traditions, from scat singing to Indian konnakol and the "eefing" of rural America. Modern beatboxing emerged in early-1980s New York, partly as a practical substitute for a drum machine when performing on the street. Doug E. Fresh, billed as the "Human Beat Box", Darren "Buffy" Robinson of the Fat Boys, and Biz Markie brought the technique to records and film. Rahzel, of the Roots, popularised simultaneous singing and beatboxing in the 1990s with tracks such as "If Your Mother Only Knew".

Technique

The basic sounds are the kick drum (a plosive "b"), the hi-hat (a short "ts"), and the snare (a breathy "pff" or inward "K" snare). Advanced performers layer basslines, scratches imitated with the voice, inward breathing sounds that allow continuous rhythms, and polyphonic techniques combining a beat with humming. Notation systems have been developed to transcribe beats.

Contemporary scene

Since the 2000s, beatboxing has developed a global competitive culture, with world championships and battle leagues, prominent artists such as Beardyman and Reeps One, and a large online community sharing tutorials. It is also used in a cappella groups, where a vocal percussionist anchors the ensemble.
