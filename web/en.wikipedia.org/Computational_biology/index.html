Computational biology

Computational biology refers to data analysis and mathematical modelling and computational simulation techniques applied to biological, ecological, behavioural, and social systems. It is an interdisciplinary field drawing on computer science, mathematics, statistics, and the life sciences.

The field is closely related to bioinformatics, which focuses on the analysis of biological data — particularly DNA, RNA, and protein sequences — using algorithms, databases, and statistical methods.

Subfields

Major areas include sequence alignment and genome assembly, gene prediction, protein structure prediction, modelling of gene regulatory networks, phylogenetic analysis, and the simulation of cellular processes. The advent of high-throughput sequencing generated enormous datasets that made computational methods indispensable to modern biology.

Methods

Computational biologists use dynamic programming, hidden Markov models, machine learning, molecular dynamics simulation, and systems of differential equations to model and analyse biological systems. Landmark achievements include the assembly of the human genome and the solution of the protein folding problem by machine-learning systems such as AlphaFold.
