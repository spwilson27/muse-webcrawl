Burning bush

The burning bush is an object described in the Book of Exodus in the Hebrew Bible. According to the narrative, the bush was on fire but was not consumed by the flames — hence the name — and it was on the ground of this bush that Moses was appointed by God to lead the Israelites out of Egypt.

The narrative

In Exodus 3, Moses, tending the flock of his father-in-law Jethro on Mount Horeb, sees a bush burning without being destroyed. From the bush the angel of the Lord and then God call to him; Moses removes his sandals because the ground is holy. God reveals the divine name, traditionally rendered "I Am that I Am", and commissions Moses to confront Pharaoh.

Interpretations

Jewish and Christian traditions have read the episode as a theophany — a manifestation of God — and as a symbol of divine presence, zeal and endurance. Saint Catherine's Monastery at Mount Sinai preserves a bush traditionally identified with the biblical one.
