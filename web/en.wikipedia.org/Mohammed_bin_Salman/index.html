Mohammed bin Salman, Crown Prince and Prime Minister of Saudi Arabia

Mohammed bin Salman Al Saud (born 31 August 1985), often known by his initials MBS, is the Crown Prince and Prime Minister of Saudi Arabia and the kingdom's de facto ruler. The son of King Salman, he rose rapidly after his father's accession in 2015, becoming defence minister, then deputy crown prince, and in June 2017 Crown Prince after the removal of his cousin Muhammad bin Nayef. He became prime minister in September 2022.

Reforms and Vision 2030

Mohammed bin Salman is the architect of Vision 2030, a programme to diversify the Saudi economy away from oil, expand the Public Investment Fund, and develop sectors such as tourism, entertainment, and technology — symbolised by the planned futuristic city of Neom. Social reforms under his rule have included lifting the ban on women driving (2018), curbing the religious police, reopening cinemas, and reducing the role of the Wahhabi religious establishment.

Consolidation of power and criticism

His tenure has also seen an assertive foreign policy — the intervention in Yemen's civil war from 2015, the 2017–2021 blockade of Qatar — and crackdowns at home, including the November 2017 detention of hundreds of princes and businessmen at the Ritz-Carlton in Riyadh in an anti-corruption campaign. The 2018 assassination of journalist Jamal Khashoggi in the Saudi consulate in Istanbul drew global condemnation; a US intelligence assessment released in 2021 concluded that the crown prince had approved the operation, which Saudi Arabia denies. Human rights groups criticise continuing executions and imprisonment of dissidents alongside the reform programme.
