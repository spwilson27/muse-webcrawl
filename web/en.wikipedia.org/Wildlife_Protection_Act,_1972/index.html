Wildlife Protection Act, 1972

The Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 is an Act of the Parliament of India enacted for the protection of plants and animal species. Before 1972, India had only five designated national parks. The Act established schedules of protected plant and animal species, outlawed hunting of many wild animals, and provided the legal framework for national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, conservation reserves, and community reserves across the country.

Provisions

The Act prohibits the hunting of wild animals listed in its schedules, regulates trade in wild animals, animal articles, and trophies, and empowers state governments to declare protected areas. It created statutory bodies including the National Board for Wildlife and, later, the National Tiger Conservation Authority (2005 amendment) and the Wildlife Crime Control Bureau. Amendments in 2002 and 2006 strengthened penalties and recognised conservation and community reserves.

Schedules

Species are listed in schedules graded by the level of protection; those in the highest schedules — including the Bengal tiger, Asiatic lion, Indian elephant, and one-horned rhinoceros — receive absolute protection and offences against them carry the highest penalties. A 2022 amendment rationalised the schedules and incorporated species listed under the CITES convention on international trade in endangered species.

Significance

Enacted on 9 September 1972 and extended across India, the Act underpins conservation programmes such as Project Tiger (launched 1973) and remains the central statute of Indian wildlife law.
