Refreshable braille display

A refreshable braille display, or braille terminal, is an electro-mechanical device for displaying braille characters, usually by means of round-tipped pins raised through holes in a flat surface. Blind computer users who cannot use a monitor use it together with a screen reader to read text output by touch.

Most displays show 20 to 80 cells of eight dots each (six for Braille characters plus two for status). Piezoelectric actuators raise the pins. Prices historically ran to thousands of dollars, though low-cost designs such as the Orbit Reader have reduced them. The displays connect by USB or Bluetooth to computers and phones.
