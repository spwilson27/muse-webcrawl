Robert Cecil, 1st Earl of Salisbury

Robert Cecil, chief minister to Elizabeth I and James I

Robert Cecil, 1st Earl of Salisbury, KG, PC (1 June 1563 – 24 May 1612), was an English statesman noted for his direction of the government during the Union of the Crowns, as Tudor England gave way to Stuart rule (1603). He served as Secretary of State, Lord High Treasurer and Lord Privy Seal.

Career

The younger son of William Cecil, 1st Baron Burghley, Elizabeth I's chief adviser, Robert Cecil was groomed for office and succeeded his father as the Queen's principal minister. He ran an extensive intelligence network and managed the secret correspondence with James VI of Scotland that ensured a smooth succession in 1603.

Under James I

James created him Earl of Salisbury in 1605. Cecil was closely associated with the discovery of the Gunpowder Plot of 1605. As Lord Treasurer from 1608 he attempted to reform royal finances, negotiating the Great Contract of 1610, which Parliament rejected. He built Hatfield House in Hertfordshire, where he died in 1612, exhausted by overwork.
