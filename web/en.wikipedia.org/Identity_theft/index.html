Identity theft

Identity theft is the deliberate misuse of another person's identifying information.

Identity theft occurs when someone deliberately uses another person's identifying information, usually as a method to gain a financial advantage or obtain credit and other benefits in the other person's name, and perhaps to the other person's disadvantage or loss. Victims are usually left with debts they did not incur and damaged credit records. Personal data such as names, dates of birth, government identification numbers, and bank details are obtained through methods including phishing, data breaches, theft of mail and wallets, and skimming devices on payment terminals.

Types

Common forms include financial identity theft, in which accounts are opened or drained in the victim's name; criminal identity theft, in which an offender gives a victim's name to police; medical identity theft, used to obtain healthcare; and tax identity theft, used to claim fraudulent refunds. "Synthetic" identity theft combines real and fabricated data to create an entirely new identity.

Prevalence and prevention

In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission has reported identity theft as its most received category of consumer complaint for many years. Recommended protections include monitoring credit reports, placing credit freezes, using unique passwords and multi-factor authentication, and promptly reporting fraud. Laws such as the Identity Theft and Assumption Deterrence Act of 1998 criminalized the offense at the federal level in the United States.
