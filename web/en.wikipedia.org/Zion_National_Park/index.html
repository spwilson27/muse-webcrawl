Zion National Park

Zion National Park is an American national park located in southwestern Utah near the town of Springdale. A prominent feature of the 229-square-mile (593 km2) park is Zion Canyon, which is 15 miles (24 km) long and up to 2,640 ft (800 m) deep. The canyon walls are reddish and tan-coloured Navajo Sandstone eroded by the North Fork of the Virgin River.

The park sits at the junction of the Colorado Plateau, Great Basin, and Mojave Desert, giving it an unusual diversity of plants and animals across four life zones. Human habitation of the area dates back some 8,000 years; Mormon pioneers named the canyon in the 1860s, and it was first protected as Mukuntuweap National Monument in 1909 before becoming a national park in 1919.

Attractions

Popular features include Angels Landing, a notorious narrow ridge trail with chain-assisted sections; the Narrows, a hike through the Virgin River gorge; the Emerald Pools; Checkerboard Mesa; and Kolob Canyons in the park's northwest section. To manage congestion, private vehicles have been restricted in the main canyon since 2000, with visitors using a free shuttle system for much of the year.

Geology

Zion's rock layers record about 150 million years of sedimentation, uplift and erosion. Nine exposed formations, mostly sandstone, were lifted more than 10,000 feet by the uplift of the Colorado Plateau, and the Virgin River has since cut the canyon at one of the fastest erosion rates in the region.
