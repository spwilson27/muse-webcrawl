The Environment Agency (EA) is a non-departmental public body of the UK government, sponsored by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs, responsible for the protection and enhancement of the environment in England. It was established on 1 April 1996 under the Environment Act 1995.

Responsibilities

The Agency's work includes flood and coastal risk management, water quality and water resources regulation, waste management and the regulation of waste sites, fisheries, navigation on some rivers, land quality, and the regulation of industrial pollution and radioactive substances. It operates flood warning services and maintains flood defences on main rivers and the coast.

History and structure

The EA brought together the National Rivers Authority, Her Majesty's Inspectorate of Pollution, and the waste regulation functions of local authorities. Environmental functions in Wales were transferred to Natural Resources Wales in 2013; Scotland is covered by the Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA). The Agency employs roughly ten thousand staff and is one of the largest environmental regulators in the world.
