Ian Brown

Ian George Brown (born 20 February 1963) is an English singer and musician. He is the lead singer and co-founder of the alternative rock band the Stone Roses, formed in Manchester in 1983. The band's self-titled debut album (1989) is widely regarded as one of the greatest British albums of all time and a landmark of the "Madchester" and baggy scenes. Following the band's breakup in 1996, Brown launched a solo career; the Stone Roses reunited in 2011, released two new singles in 2016, and disbanded again in 2017.

The Stone Roses

Brown co-founded the Stone Roses with guitarist John Squire; the classic line-up included bassist Mani (Gary Mounfield) and drummer Reni (Alan Wren). Songs such as "I Wanna Be Adored", "She Bangs the Drums", "Fools Gold" and "I Am the Resurrection" defined their sound — jangling psychedelic guitar over danceable grooves. Their 1989 Spike Island and Alexandra Palace concerts became legendary; legal battles with their label Silvertone delayed the second album, Second Coming (1994), and internal tensions ended the band in 1996. The 2011 reunion drew record crowds at Manchester's Heaton Park.

Solo career

Brown's solo albums include Unfinished Monkey Business (1998, a No. 4 hit in the UK), Golden Greats (1999), Music of the Spheres (2001), Solarized (2004), The World Is Yours (2007), My Star (2009) and Ripples (2019). His best-known solo singles include "My Star", "Dolphins Were Monkeys", "F.E.A.R." — built on an acrostic lyric — and "Stellify". He has collaborated with UNKLE and appeared in a cameo as a Slytherin-affiliated student extra scene in Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban (reading A Brief History of Time in the Leaky Cauldron).

Public image

Nicknamed "King Monkey", Brown is known for his simian stage swagger, laconic interview style, and interest in religion, conspiracy theories and scootering culture. His public scepticism about COVID-19 vaccines and lockdowns in 2020–21 drew significant attention and led to festival cancellations.
