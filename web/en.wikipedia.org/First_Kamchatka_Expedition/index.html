First Kamchatka Expedition

The First Kamchatka Expedition was a Russian exploring expedition (1725–1731) led by the Danish-born navigator Vitus Bering, commissioned by Peter the Great to determine whether Asia and America were connected by land or separated by water, and to map Russia's far north-eastern frontier.

The voyage

Bering's party travelled overland across Siberia to the Kamchatka Peninsula, an enormous logistical undertaking, and built the vessel St. Gabriel at Nizhnekamchatsk. In the summer of 1728 Bering sailed north through the strait that now bears his name and into the Arctic Ocean, demonstrating that Asia and America were not joined — although fog prevented him from actually sighting the American coast, leaving some doubt in St Petersburg. In 1729 he made a second short voyage eastward from Kamchatka, and in 1730 he returned to the capital with charts of the region.

Consequences

The expedition's incomplete answers prompted the far larger Great Northern (Second Kamchatka) Expedition of 1733–1743, during which Bering reached Alaska in 1741 and died on Bering Island. The Bering Strait and Bering Sea commemorate his name.
