Business rates in England

Business rates in England, formally National Non-Domestic Rates, are a tax on the occupation of non-domestic property, paid by businesses and other organisations occupying commercial premises such as shops, offices, pubs, and warehouses. The system descends from the local rates levied under the Poor Law and was nationalised in its modern form by the Local Government Finance Act 1988.

How they work

Each property is assigned a rateable value by the Valuation Office Agency, representing its estimated annual rental value at a set valuation date. The bill is calculated by multiplying the rateable value by a multiplier (the "poundage") set by central government. Periodic revaluations update values; transitional arrangements phase in large changes. Various reliefs exist, including small business rate relief, charitable relief, and exemptions for certain properties.

Debate

Business rates are a significant source of local government revenue, raising on the order of £25 billion a year. The tax is frequently criticised by retailers for burdening high-street shops relative to online competitors, prompting repeated government reviews and temporary relief schemes, including during the COVID-19 pandemic.
