Mekong

The Mekong or Mekong River is a trans-boundary river in East Asia and Southeast Asia. It is the world's twelfth-longest river and the third-longest in Asia, with an estimated length of 4,880 km (3,030 mi) and a drainage area of 795,000 km², discharging 475 km³ of water annually.

Geography

From its headwaters in the Tibetan Plateau, the river runs through Southwest China (as the Lancang River), Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia, and southern Vietnam. In Cambodia it splits and forms the vast Mekong Delta, one of the world's most productive rice-growing regions.

Ecology

The basin is exceptionally biodiverse, home to species such as the Irrawaddy dolphin, giant freshwater stingray, and Mekong giant catfish. Seasonal flood pulses reverse the flow of the Tonlé Sap in Cambodia.

Economy and dams

The river supports fisheries, agriculture, transport and hydropower for tens of millions of people. A cascade of dams in China and tributary dams in Laos have raised concerns about sediment, fisheries and downstream water security.

Navigation and history

Rapids and waterfalls such as Khone Phapheng limit through-navigation. European exploration in the 1860s, led by Francis Garnier, sought a trade route to China.
