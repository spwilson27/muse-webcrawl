North Platte River

[Image placeholder: North Platte River]

The North Platte River is a major tributary of the Platte River in the United States, about 716 miles (1,152 km) long. It rises in north-central Colorado, flows north through Wyoming — past Casper — then turns southeast into Nebraska, where it joins the South Platte River near the city of North Platte to form the Platte River.

Course and history

The river's headwaters are in the Park Range and Medicine Bow Mountains of Colorado. In Wyoming it is impounded by a series of reservoirs including Seminoe, Pathfinder, Alcova, Glendo and Guernsey, built for irrigation and hydroelectric power. The valley of the North Platte was a corridor of westward migration, followed by the Oregon, California and Mormon trails in the nineteenth century.

Water use

The river is heavily used for irrigation in Wyoming and the Nebraska panhandle. Its waters have long been disputed between Colorado, Wyoming and Nebraska, and are apportioned under interstate decrees and compacts.
