Raleigh is the capital city of the U.S. state of North Carolina and the seat of Wake County. It is the second-most populous city in North Carolina, after Charlotte. Raleigh is nicknamed the "City of Oaks" for its many oak trees lining the streets of the heart of the city.

History

Raleigh was chosen as the site of the state capital in 1788 and formally established in 1792, making it one of the few American capital cities planned and built from the start for that purpose. It was named after Sir Walter Raleigh, sponsor of the Roanoke Colony. The North Carolina State Capitol, completed in 1840, still stands at the centre of the original grid plan. The city was largely spared destruction during the American Civil War.

Geography

Raleigh lies in the northeast central region of North Carolina, where the Piedmont plateau meets the Atlantic Coastal Plain — a "fall line" geography that gives the city gently rolling hills transitioning to flat coastal terrain to the east.

Economy and education

Together with Durham and Chapel Hill, Raleigh forms the Research Triangle, anchored by North Carolina State University in Raleigh and the Research Triangle Park, one of the largest research parks in the world. Major employers include state government, technology and pharmaceutical firms, and healthcare systems. The city is also home to Shaw University and Saint Augustine's University, among the oldest historically Black universities in the South.

Culture

Raleigh hosts the North Carolina Museum of Art, the North Carolina Museum of Natural Sciences, and the annual World of Bluegrass festival. Its NHL team, the Carolina Hurricanes, plays at the Lenovo Center.
