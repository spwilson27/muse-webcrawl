Ramjet

TypeAirbreathing jet engine
CompressorNone (ram compression)
Useful speed rangeRoughly Mach 2.5 to Mach 6
ApplicationsMissiles, target drones, experimental aircraft

A ramjet is a form of airbreathing jet engine that relies on the forward motion of the engine to compress incoming air, rather than a rotating compressor. Because it has no compressor, a ramjet produces no thrust at zero airspeed and must first be accelerated to a sufficient speed — typically by a rocket booster or a carrier aircraft — before it can operate.

Principle of operation

At supersonic flight speeds, air entering the intake is decelerated through shock waves, raising its pressure and temperature. Fuel injected into the combustion chamber burns in this high-pressure air stream at subsonic local flow speeds, and the hot gases expand through a converging-diverging nozzle to produce thrust. Ramjets operate most efficiently around Mach 3 and remain useful up to about Mach 6.

History

The concept was described by French inventor René Lorin in 1913. Practical development followed in the 1930s and 1940s in the Soviet Union, Germany and the United States. Notable ramjet-powered vehicles include the Lockheed D-21 reconnaissance drone and numerous surface-to-air and anti-ship missiles such as the British Sea Dart and the Franco-Indian BrahMos's peers in its class of supersonic cruise weapons.

Related engines

A scramjet (supersonic combustion ramjet) keeps the airflow through the combustor supersonic, allowing operation at hypersonic speeds above Mach 5. Combined-cycle designs such as the turbo-ramjet powered the SR-71 Blackbird.
