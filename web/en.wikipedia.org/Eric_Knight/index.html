Eric Knight, author of Lassie Come-Home

Eric Mowbray Knight (10 April 1897 – 15 January 1943) was an English novelist and screenwriter who is mainly known for Lassie Come-Home, the novel that introduced the fictional collie Lassie. Born in Leeds in the West Riding of Yorkshire, he grew up in the countryside his books describe and emigrated to the United States as a young man.

Life and career

Knight served in the Canadian Army in the First World War and later worked as a journalist, film critic, and Hollywood screenwriter. He wrote a number of novels and short stories, including This Above All (1941), a bestselling wartime romance later filmed by Anatole Litvak. His short story "Lassie Come-Home" appeared in The Saturday Evening Post in 1938 and was expanded into a novel in 1940; the 1943 MGM film adaptation made Lassie one of the most famous animal characters in cinema.

War service and death

Having become an American citizen, Knight returned to military service in the Second World War as a major in the U.S. Army, producing films for Frank Capra's unit, including work associated with the Why We Fight series. He was killed on 15 January 1943 when the transport plane carrying him crashed in the jungle of Dutch Guiana (now Suriname).
