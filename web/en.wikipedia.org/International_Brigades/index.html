The International Brigades (Spanish: Brigadas Internacionales) were military units set up by the Communist International to assist the Popular Front government of the Second Spanish Republic during the Spanish Civil War. Composed of foreign volunteers from more than 50 countries, they fought from late 1936 until their withdrawal in the autumn of 1938.

The Brigades drew volunteers — largely workers, communists, socialists, and anti-fascists — from across Europe and the Americas.

Formation

After the military rising of July 1936, the Comintern organised recruitment of foreign volunteers, who were funnelled through Paris and across the Pyrenees to a base at Albacete. The first brigades — the XI, XII, and XIII — were formed in October–November 1936 and thrown into the defence of Madrid. Famous national units included the German Thälmann Battalion, the Polish Dąbrowski Battalion, the French Commune de Paris Battalion, and the predominantly American Abraham Lincoln Battalion, which first saw action at the Battle of Jarama in February 1937.

Composition

Estimates of total membership range from about 32,000 to 40,000, of whom perhaps 10,000–15,000 were killed. The largest contingents came from France, Germany, Italy, Poland, the United States, and Britain. Volunteers were predominantly communists, though many were socialists, anarchists, trade unionists, or unaffiliated anti-fascists. Political commissars accompanied the units, and discipline could be severe.

Withdrawal and aftermath

In September 1938 Prime Minister Juan Negrín announced the unilateral withdrawal of the Brigades in a bid to pressure Germany and Italy to remove their forces from Franco's side. The volunteers were given a farewell parade in Barcelona on 28 October 1938, addressed by Dolores Ibárruri ("La Pasionaria"). Many veterans later fought in the Second World War resistance movements; others, particularly returnees to the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe, suffered in Stalinist purges. In 1996 Spain granted surviving Brigaders honorary Spanish citizenship.
