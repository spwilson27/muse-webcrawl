USS Maine (1889)

USS Maine was a United States Navy ship launched in 1889, classified first as an armored cruiser and later as a second-class battleship. Her sinking by explosion in Havana Harbor, Cuba, on 15 February 1898 killed 268 of her crew and became a catalyst for the Spanish–American War, popularised by the slogan "Remember the Maine, to hell with Spain!"

Service

Maine was one of the first modern capital ships built for the "New Navy" of the 1880s. Because construction took nearly nine years, she was already partly obsolete at commissioning in 1895. She served with the North Atlantic Squadron on exercises and goodwill visits along the US east coast.

Sinking

Sent to Havana in January 1898 to protect American interests during the Cuban War of Independence, Maine exploded at her moorings on the night of 15 February. The blast destroyed the forward third of the ship; 260 men died that night and eight more later of wounds. A US naval court of inquiry concluded that a naval mine had detonated her magazines, and American newspapers blamed Spain, fuelling public pressure that led to war in April 1898.

Cause debated

Spain's own inquiry concluded the explosion was internal. A 1911 re-examination, when the wreck was raised by cofferdam in Havana Harbor, reaffirmed the external-mine finding, but a 1976 study commissioned by Admiral Hyman G. Rickover concluded the likely cause was a fire in a coal bunker igniting adjacent magazines. The wreck was towed out to sea and scuttled with honours in 1912; her main mast is preserved at Arlington National Cemetery.
