Souvlaki (Greek: σουβλάκι, plural souvlakia) is a popular Greek fast food consisting of small pieces of meat — most commonly pork, but also chicken, lamb or beef — grilled on a skewer. It is eaten straight from the skewer, served on a plate with sides, or wrapped in a pita with tomato, onion, fried potatoes and tzatziki. The word derives from souvla (σούβλα), meaning skewer.

History

Grilling meat on skewers in Greece goes back to antiquity: excavations at Akrotiri on Santorini uncovered stone barbecue supports from the 17th century BC, and Homer mentions meat roasted on spits. In the modern era, souvlaki became a street food in the late 19th and early 20th centuries and spread widely after the Second World War, becoming a staple of Greek tavernas and takeaway shops worldwide through the Greek diaspora.

Regional terminology

In Thessaloniki and northern Greece, souvlaki is the standard term for the skewered meat, while in Athens the same item is often called kalamaki ("little reed", after the bamboo skewer), and "souvlaki" there commonly refers to the pita wrap as a whole. The wrapped version with gyros meat is a close cousin and sometimes rival in naming.

Serving

Souvlaki is typically seasoned with salt, pepper, oregano and lemon. As a wrap it is served in an oiled, lightly grilled pita with tzatziki; as a portion (merida) it comes with bread, salad and chips. Chicken and pork predominate in Greece, where lamb souvlaki is rarer than abroad.
