Frederick McKay

Frederick S. McKay
Born: April 13, 1874, Lawrence, Massachusetts, U.S.
Died: August 21, 1959 (aged 85), Colorado Springs, Colorado, U.S.
Occupation: Dentist
Known for: Investigating the cause of Colorado brown stain; linking fluoride to mottled enamel and reduced tooth decay

Frederick Sumner McKay (April 13, 1874 – August 21, 1959) was an American dentist whose decades-long investigation of stained and mottled teeth in Colorado led to the discovery that naturally occurring fluoride in drinking water both caused dental fluorosis and protected against tooth decay. His work laid the scientific foundation for community water fluoridation.

Investigation of Colorado brown stain

McKay graduated from the University of Pennsylvania School of Dental Medicine in 1900 and soon afterward opened a practice in Colorado Springs, Colorado. There he encountered large numbers of patients whose teeth were permanently stained brown and mottled, a condition locals called "Colorado brown stain," yet who seemed to suffer unusually little tooth decay. Determined to find the cause, McKay spent more than thirty years studying the condition, surveying communities across the western United States and corresponding with researchers around the world.

In 1909 McKay enlisted the help of G. V. Black, a prominent dental researcher, and the two documented that the mottling occurred in people who had lived in affected areas during childhood while their teeth were forming. The breakthrough came in 1931, when chemist H. V. Churchill of the Aluminum Company of America, testing water from Bauxite, Arkansas, identified high levels of fluoride as the common factor. Working with U.S. Public Health Service researcher H. Trendley Dean, McKay helped establish that fluoride concentrations around one part per million prevented cavities without causing noticeable mottling.

Legacy

McKay's findings led directly to the first artificial fluoridation of a public water supply, in Grand Rapids, Michigan, in 1945, and to widespread adoption of water fluoridation in the United States and abroad. He is remembered as a pioneer of preventive dentistry and dental epidemiology.
