Milarepa

Jetsun Milarepa (c. 1052–1135) was a Tibetan siddha and yogi, one of the most famous practitioners of Tibetan Buddhism and a central figure of the Kagyu school. His life story — from murderer by black magic to enlightened hermit-poet within a single lifetime — is Tibet's best-loved spiritual biography, and his spontaneous songs, collected as the Hundred Thousand Songs of Milarepa, remain classics of Tibetan literature.

Life

Born Mila Thöpaga ("delightful to hear"), he lost his father young and saw the family's property seized by an aunt and uncle. His mother sent him to learn sorcery, and he took revenge by causing the collapse of a house that killed many wedding guests, and by destroying crops with hailstorms. Remorseful, he sought out the translator Marpa, who subjected him to years of gruelling trials — repeatedly building and demolishing stone towers — to purify his karma before finally granting him the teachings.

Milarepa then spent years meditating in mountain caves, subsisting on nettles that reputedly turned his skin green, attaining realization through the practice of tummo (inner heat) while wearing only a single cotton cloth (repa, hence his name). Among his disciples, Gampopa became the principal lineage holder who systematised the Kagyu school.

Legacy

The standard biography, The Life of Milarepa, was composed by Tsangnyön Heruka in the 15th century. Milarepa's cave sites across the Himalayas remain major pilgrimage destinations.
