Theodore Roosevelt

Theodore Roosevelt Jr. (October 27, 1858 – January 6, 1919), often referred to as Teddy or T. R., was the 26th president of the United States, serving from 1901 to 1909. A leader of the Republican Party and the Progressive movement, he championed his "Square Deal" domestic policies.

Presidency

Roosevelt became president after the assassination of William McKinley in September 1901. He prosecuted monopolistic trusts, regulated railroads and food and drugs, and conserved vast public lands, creating national parks, forests, and monuments. Abroad he mediated the end of the Russo-Japanese War — earning the Nobel Peace Prize — and pushed the construction of the Panama Canal.

Later life

After leaving office he split the Republican vote in 1912 by running for the Progressive "Bull Moose" Party. He wrote dozens of books, led the Rough Riders in the Spanish–American War, and his face is carved on Mount Rushmore alongside Washington, Jefferson, and Lincoln.
