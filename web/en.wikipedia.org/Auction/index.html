Auction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


An auction in progress

An auction is usually a process of buying and selling goods or services by offering them up for bids, taking bids, and then selling the item to the highest bidder or buying the item from the lowest bidder. Some exceptions to this definition exist and are described in the section about different types.

Types

The most common form is the English auction, an open ascending-price auction in which the price rises until only one bidder remains. A Dutch auction begins at a high price that falls until a bidder accepts it. In a first-price sealed-bid auction, bidders submit bids privately and the highest bidder pays their own bid; in a Vickrey (second-price) auction, the highest bidder pays the second-highest bid, a format designed to encourage truthful bidding.

History

Auctions have been recorded since antiquity — Herodotus described bride auctions in Babylon, and the sale of the Roman Empire to Didius Julianus in 193 CE is a famous example. Modern auction theory, developed by William Vickrey and later scholars, underpins the design of spectrum auctions and online advertising markets.

Applications

Auctions are used for art and antiques, real estate, livestock, government securities, radio spectrum licences, electricity markets, and online marketplaces. Phenomena studied in auction theory include the winner's curse, bid shading, and revenue equivalence.
