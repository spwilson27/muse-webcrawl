Monarch

A monarch is a head of state for life or until abdication, whose office — a monarchy — is typically hereditary. Monarchs bear titles such as king, queen, emperor, empress, sultan, emir, or prince, and their authority ranges from near-absolute rule to a purely ceremonial and symbolic role in constitutional monarchies.

Forms

In an absolute monarchy the monarch holds extensive executive, legislative, and judicial power; in a constitutional monarchy, power is limited by a constitution and exercised mainly by elected governments, with the monarch as a non-partisan symbol of national continuity. Succession usually follows primogeniture within a royal dynasty, though elective monarchies exist, as in Malaysia and the Vatican City (whose pope is an elected absolute monarch).

Modern monarchies

More than forty sovereign states retain monarchs, including the United Kingdom and the other Commonwealth realms sharing King Charles III, Japan (whose emperor is the only reigning head of state styled "emperor"), Spain, the Netherlands, Saudi Arabia, and Thailand. Monarchs also head many constituent and traditional polities below the sovereign level.
