The Indian Armed Forces are the military forces of the Republic of India, comprising the Indian Army, the Indian Navy and the Indian Air Force, supported by paramilitary and strategic forces. With more than 1.4 million active personnel, they form one of the largest standing military forces in the world, and one of the largest volunteer forces anywhere. The President of India is the supreme commander, while administration rests with the Ministry of Defence.

Organisation

The three services each have their own chief of staff and service headquarters in New Delhi. Joint operations are coordinated through integrated commands — including the Andaman and Nicobar Command and the Strategic Forces Command, which administers India's nuclear arsenal — and, since 2020, through the office of the Chief of Defence Staff (CDS), created to promote "jointness" and theatre commands after the recommendations of successive reform committees. India's nuclear doctrine professes no first use, and its triad spans land-based missiles, aircraft and a growing fleet of nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines.

History

The armed forces descend from the armies of the East India Company and the British Indian Army, which served in both world wars; at independence in 1947 the forces were divided between India and Pakistan. Since then India has fought wars with Pakistan in 1947–48, 1965, 1971 and the Kargil conflict of 1999, and a border war with China in 1962. The 1971 war, which led to the creation of Bangladesh, is regarded as the services' defining joint victory. Indian forces are also among the largest contributors to United Nations peacekeeping operations, and they perform extensive disaster-relief and aid-to-civil-authority roles at home.

Personnel and budget

Recruitment is voluntary and intensely competitive; short-service entry schemes and a large reserve and territorial component supplement the regular forces. India has consistently ranked among the world's top military spenders and among the largest importers of arms, while pursuing "Make in India" programmes to build an indigenous defence industry — from aircraft carriers and submarines to missiles and fighter aircraft. The officer corps is trained at institutions such as the National Defence Academy, India's tri-service academy at Khadakwasla.
