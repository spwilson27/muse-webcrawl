Pauline epistles


Early papyrus manuscript of Paul's letters

The Pauline epistles are the thirteen books of the New Testament attributed to Paul the Apostle, written as letters to early Christian communities and individuals in the first century AD. They form the largest single block of the New Testament and are among its earliest documents, predating the Gospels.

The letters

The epistles traditionally attributed to Paul are: Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 and 2 Thessalonians, 1 and 2 Timothy, Titus, and Philemon. The Epistle to the Hebrews was also included in early collections of Paul's letters, though most modern scholars deny his authorship.

Authorship

Mainstream scholarship regards seven letters as undisputedly authentic: Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, and Philemon. The remaining six are debated; many scholars consider them pseudepigraphal, written by followers in Paul's name after his death. The Pastoral Epistles (1 and 2 Timothy and Titus) are the most widely questioned.

Content and influence

Paul's letters address the theology of justification by faith, the relationship between Judaism and the Gentile mission, church order, ethics, and the expected return of Christ. They have been foundational for Christian doctrine — centrally for Martin Luther and the Protestant Reformation — and remain among the most studied texts of antiquity.
