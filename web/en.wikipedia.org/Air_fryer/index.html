Air fryer

An air fryer is a small countertop convection oven designed to simulate deep frying without submerging the food in oil. A fan circulates hot air at high speed around food held in a perforated basket, producing a crisp browned exterior through the Maillard reaction while using far less oil than a deep fryer.

History

Convection ovens have existed since the 1940s. The modern air fryer was developed by Dutch inventor Fred van der Weij, who sought a healthier way to prepare fried snacks and patented his rapid-air technology in 2005. Philips introduced the first appliance under the name "Airfryer" at the IFA consumer electronics fair in Berlin in 2010. The devices surged in popularity in the late 2010s and 2020s, becoming among the best-selling kitchen appliances in many countries; manufacturers now offer basket, oven-style and dual-basket models, and multi-cookers with air-frying functions.

Operation and use

Air fryers typically operate up to about 200–230 °C (400–450 °F). They are used for foods traditionally fried — chips (fries), chicken wings, nuggets — and for roasting vegetables, reheating leftovers and baking in small portions. Because the cooking chamber is small, food cooks quickly but must often be shaken or turned and cooked in single layers for even crisping.

Health and criticism

Air-fried foods generally contain less fat than deep-fried equivalents, and studies have found reduced formation of acrylamide in potatoes compared with deep frying. Critics note that "air fryer" is partly a marketing term for a compact convection oven, that capacities are limited, and that results differ from true frying for battered foods.
