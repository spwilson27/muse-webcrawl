Pabellón criollo

Place of originVenezuela
Main ingredientsShredded beef, black beans, white rice, fried plantains
TypeNational dish

Pabellón criollo is a traditional Venezuelan dish widely regarded as the country's national dish. It consists of shredded beef (carne mechada), black beans, white rice, and slices of fried ripe plantain (tajadas), arranged side by side on the plate.

Composition and symbolism

The dish is often said to symbolise Venezuela's cultural mixture: the dark beans, brown beef, white rice and golden plantains representing the country's diverse ethnic heritage. It developed during the colonial era, combining European, African and Indigenous ingredients and cooking methods.

Variations

Regional variations include pabellón con barandas ("with railings"), in which the plantain slices surround the other components; pabellón a caballo, topped with a fried egg; and versions substituting chigüire (capybara), fish, or llanero cheese. In the Margarita Island version, shredded fish or baby shark may replace the beef.
