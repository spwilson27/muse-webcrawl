Boxing

Boxing is a combat sport and martial art in which two participants, wearing protective gloves and other protective equipment such as hand wraps and mouthguards, throw punches at each other for a predetermined amount of time in a boxing ring. Fights are supervised by a referee and scored by judges; a boxer wins by knockout, technical knockout, decision, or the opponent's disqualification or retirement.

History

Fist-fighting contests appear in ancient Egyptian, Sumerian and Greek art, and boxing was an Olympic sport in the ancient world. Bare-knuckle prizefighting flourished in 18th- and 19th-century England before the Marquess of Queensberry Rules (1867) introduced gloves, three-minute rounds and the ten-count, shaping the modern sport. The 20th century produced global icons including Jack Dempsey, Joe Louis, Muhammad Ali, Sugar Ray Robinson and Manny Pacquiao.

Technique and rules

Basic punches are the jab, cross, hook and uppercut, combined with footwork, head movement and defensive skills such as blocking, slipping and clinching. Amateur (Olympic-style) boxing emphasizes scoring clean blows, while professional bouts are contested over up to twelve rounds across weight divisions from minimumweight to heavyweight, under sanctioning bodies including the WBA, WBC, IBF and WBO.

Health and culture

Boxing is celebrated for its discipline and accessibility but has long been debated because of the risk of head injury, including chronic traumatic encephalopathy. It remains a staple of popular culture and a pathway out of poverty for many athletes worldwide.
