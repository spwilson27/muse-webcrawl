Stevia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Leaves of Stevia rebaudiana, the source of stevia sweeteners.

Stevia is a genus of about 240 species of herbs and shrubs in the sunflower family (Asteraceae), native to subtropical and tropical regions from western North America to South America. The species Stevia rebaudiana, commonly known as sweetleaf, is widely grown for its sweet-tasting leaves, the source of the natural sweeteners known as stevia glycosides.

History and use

The Guaraní people of Paraguay and Brazil used the leaves for centuries to sweeten drinks such as yerba mate. The plant was scientifically described by the botanist Moisés Santiago Bertoni around 1900, and its sweet compounds, the diterpene glycosides stevioside and rebaudioside A, were isolated in the 1930s. Stevia extracts are 200–300 times sweeter than sucrose with negligible calories.

Regulation and cultivation

Japan adopted stevia widely from the 1970s. High-purity steviol glycosides were approved as food additives in the United States (GRAS status from 2008) and the European Union (2011), and stevia-sweetened products are now sold globally. Commercial cultivation occurs in Paraguay, China, and many other countries.

Chemistry

The sweetness resides in steviol glycosides concentrated in the leaves. After ingestion they are hydrolysed by gut bacteria to steviol, which is absorbed and excreted; regulatory reviews have set an acceptable daily intake of 4 mg/kg body weight expressed as steviol equivalents.
