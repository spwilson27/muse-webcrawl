Chequers is the country house residence of the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. The 16th-century manor house stands near Ellesborough at the foot of the Chiltern Hills in Buckinghamshire, about 40 miles (64 km) north-west of central London.

History

The house was built around 1565 by William Hawtrey; its name may come from the chequer tree or from an early owner's link to the Exchequer. Lady Mary Grey was confined here in the 1560s. The estate passed by marriage to the Russell and Lee families; in 1917 Viscount Lee of Fareham gave it to the nation under the Chequers Estate Act as a country retreat for prime ministers, the first of whom to use it was David Lloyd George in 1921. During the Second World War the aircraft recognition training school was nearby, and Churchill famously paced its Long Gallery.

Use

Chequers hosts cabinet away-days, summits and foreign leaders; the 2018 cabinet meeting there produced the controversial "Chequers plan" for Brexit. It is not open to the public, and is staffed by personnel from the Royal Navy and Royal Air Force. Its counterpart official residences include 10 Downing Street and the Dorneywood estate.
