The Independent Games Festival (IGF) is an annual festival at the Game Developers Conference, the largest annual gathering of the video game industry. Founded in 1998, it was modelled on the Sundance Film Festival as a way to showcase and honour innovation by independent game developers, and its awards — above all the Seumas McNally Grand Prize — are the most prestigious in indie gaming.

History and format

The IGF was established by the CMP Game Group (now part of the GDC's organisers) with the first competition held in 1999. Any independent developer may enter, finished or unfinished, for a modest fee; volunteer judges and category juries narrow hundreds of entries to finalists, who exhibit their games in the IGF pavilion at GDC in San Francisco, where the winners are announced at an awards ceremony held jointly with the Game Developers Choice Awards.

Awards

The main competition awards include the Seumas McNally Grand Prize (named for the young developer of the 2000 winner Tread Marks, who died shortly after), Excellence in Visual Art, Excellence in Audio, Excellence in Design, Excellence in Narrative, the Nuovo Award for experimental and unconventional games, Best Student Game, and the Audience Award. A parallel Student Showcase has run since the festival's early years. Grand Prize winners have included Gish, Darwinia, Aquaria, Blueberry Garden, Monaco, Papers, Please, Outer Wilds, Night in the Woods, Return of the Obra Dinn, Inscryption and Balatro; Minecraft and Fez won top honours on their way to mainstream success.

Significance

An IGF nomination can transform a small team's prospects, bringing press attention, platform deals and distribution offers. The festival is credited with helping to define "indie games" as a recognised creative movement within the industry, championing personal, experimental and artistically ambitious work alongside commercial titles.
