Republic of Florence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Republic of Florence (Italian: Repubblica di Firenze) was a medieval and early modern Italian city-state centred on the city of Florence in Tuscany. It existed, with interruptions, from 1115 — when the Florentines formed a commune after the death of Matilda of Tuscany — until 1532, when Pope Clement VII and Emperor Charles V installed Alessandro de' Medici as hereditary Duke of Florence, formally ending the republic.

Florence became one of the wealthiest and most influential cities in Europe through banking, the wool trade, and finance. Its gold florin was a standard currency across the continent. The republic is also famed as the cradle of the Renaissance, nurturing artists and thinkers such as Dante Alighieri, Giotto, Brunelleschi, Leonardo da Vinci, and Michelangelo.

Government

Florence was governed through the Signoria, a council of nine priors chosen by lot from the major guilds for terms of only two months, headed by the Gonfaloniere of Justice. Real power frequently rested with wealthy merchant families and the guilds. Factional conflict between Guelphs and Ghibellines, and later between the Medici and their rivals, repeatedly destabilised the constitution.

The Medici

From 1434, Cosimo de' Medici and his descendants dominated the republic while preserving its outward forms. Lorenzo the Magnificent (r. 1469–1492) made Florence a centre of art and learning. The Medici were expelled in 1494, restored in 1512, expelled again in 1527, and finally installed as hereditary dukes after the Siege of Florence (1529–1530).

Culture

Florentine bankers financed popes and kings across Europe, and their patronage funded works such as Brunelleschi's dome of Florence Cathedral and Botticelli's paintings. Writers including Dante, Petrarch, Boccaccio, Machiavelli, and Guicciardini shaped Italian literature and political thought.
