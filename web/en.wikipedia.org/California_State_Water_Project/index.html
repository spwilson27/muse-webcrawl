California State Water Project

The California State Water Project (SWP) is a state water management project in California under the supervision of the California Department of Water Resources. It is one of the largest public water and power utilities in the world, providing drinking water for more than 27 million people and irrigation for hundreds of thousands of acres.

Components

The system collects water from the Feather River at Lake Oroville — behind Oroville Dam, the tallest earthfill dam in the US — and moves it through the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta, then south via the California Aqueduct. Pumping plants lift water over the Tehachapi Mountains at the Edmonston Pumping Plant, the largest water lift of its kind, to Southern California reservoirs such as Castaic and Perris.

History

Voters approved the Burns–Porter Act bond in 1960 during the governorship of Pat Brown. Deliveries began in the late 1960s and 1970s to 29 contracting agencies.

Issues

Allocations vary sharply with hydrology, and pumping from the Delta is restricted to protect endangered fish such as Delta smelt, making the project central to California's water politics and proposals for a Delta conveyance tunnel.
