Polarization (waves)

Applies toTransverse waves, esp. electromagnetic
StatesLinear, circular, elliptical, unpolarized
Described byStokes parameters, Jones calculus

Polarization (also polarisation) is a property of transverse waves which specifies the geometrical orientation of the oscillations. In an electromagnetic wave such as light, polarization describes the direction in which the electric field oscillates, perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

Types of polarization

In linear polarization the electric field oscillates along a single fixed line. In circular polarization the field vector rotates at a constant rate, tracing a helix, and may be right- or left-handed. The general case is elliptical polarization. Light that is a mixture of many polarization states is called unpolarized, as is most natural sunlight before scattering or reflection.

Production and measurement

Polarized light is produced by polarizing filters, by reflection at Brewster's angle, by birefringent crystals such as calcite, and by scattering in the atmosphere — which is why the sky is partially polarized. Polarization is quantified by the Stokes parameters and manipulated with wave plates and polarizers described by Jones calculus.

Applications

Polarization is exploited in sunglasses that block glare, liquid-crystal displays, 3D cinema, photography, stress analysis of transparent materials, radio antenna design, and astronomy, where polarized light reveals magnetic fields and scattering geometry.
