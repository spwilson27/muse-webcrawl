Brown rice

TypeWhole grain rice
ProcessingHusk removed; bran and germ retained
Cooking timeLonger than white rice

Brown rice is a whole grain rice with the inedible outer hull removed. This is in contrast to white rice, which is the same grain further milled to remove the bran and germ layers as well, and then polished. Retaining the bran and germ gives brown rice its tan colour, chewier texture, slightly nutty flavour, and higher content of fibre, magnesium, and other nutrients.

Nutrition

Brown rice is a good source of manganese, selenium, magnesium, and dietary fibre, and contains more B vitamins than unenriched white rice. Its glycaemic index is generally lower than that of white rice. Because the bran contains oils, brown rice can become rancid and has a shorter shelf life—typically about six months at room temperature, longer if refrigerated or frozen.

Cooking

Brown rice takes substantially longer to cook than white rice (often 35–45 minutes) because water penetrates the bran layer slowly. It is commonly cooked with a water ratio of about 1:1.5 to 1:2, and soaking beforehand can reduce cooking time.

Varieties

Any rice cultivar can be sold as brown rice, including long-grain, short-grain, basmati, and jasmine types. Germinated brown rice (GABA rice) is produced by soaking the grain until it just begins to sprout, increasing its content of gamma-aminobutyric acid.
