The Wow! signal was a strong narrowband radio signal detected on August 15, 1977, by Ohio State University's Big Ear radio telescope in the United States, which was being used to support the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI). The signal appeared to come from the direction of the constellation Sagittarius and bore expected hallmarks of a potential artificial origin.

The famous printout bearing the alphanumeric sequence "6EQUJ5" and Jerry Ehman's handwritten "Wow!"

Detection

Astronomer Jerry R. Ehman discovered the anomaly while reviewing recorded data and wrote "Wow!" beside the alphanumeric sequence 6EQUJ5, which encoded the signal's intensity over 72 seconds — the full length of the observation window as the telescope's beam swept past the source. The signal's frequency was close to the 1420 MHz hydrogen line, a frequency considered a natural "water hole" for interstellar communication.

Investigations

Despite many follow-up observations, the signal was never detected again, and its origin remains unexplained. Proposed natural explanations have included a passing comet's hydrogen cloud, though that hypothesis was disputed. It remains one of the most famous candidate technosignatures.
