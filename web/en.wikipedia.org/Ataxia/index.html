Ataxia

Ataxia (from Greek ἀταξία, "lack of order") is a neurological sign consisting of lack of voluntary coordination of muscle movements that can include gait abnormality, speech changes and eye movement abnormalities. Ataxia is a clinical manifestation indicating dysfunction of parts of the nervous system that coordinate movement, principally the cerebellum and its connections, rather than a specific disease.

Types

Cerebellar ataxia arises from lesions of the cerebellum and produces a wide-based, unsteady gait, intention tremor, dysarthria and nystagmus. Sensory ataxia results from loss of proprioception (dorsal column or peripheral nerve disease) and worsens dramatically when vision is removed — a positive Romberg sign. Vestibular ataxia stems from dysfunction of the vestibular system and is accompanied by vertigo.

Causes

Acquired causes include stroke, multiple sclerosis, tumours, alcohol toxicity, vitamin B12 or E deficiency, hypothyroidism, infections and certain medications. Acute ataxia in children commonly follows viral illness.

Hereditary ataxias

The inherited ataxias include the autosomal dominant spinocerebellar ataxias (SCAs), many caused by CAG repeat expansions; Friedreich's ataxia, the most common recessive ataxia, caused by a GAA expansion in the FXN gene; and ataxia-telangiectasia, associated with immune deficiency and cancer risk.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation uses clinical examination, MRI, laboratory tests and genetic testing. There is no cure for most ataxias; management targets treatable causes and emphasises physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy and mobility aids. The antisense and gene-therapy era has brought the first disease-targeted trials for some genetic forms.
