Binder clip

A binder clip is a simple device for binding together sheets of paper. It leaves the sheets intact and is quicker to apply and remove than staples or paper clips, and can bind thicker stacks. It consists of a rectangular sheet of springy steel formed into a loop, painted black (or sometimes silver), with two wire handles that can be folded up to open the jaws and folded down flat against the clipped stack.

History

The binder clip was invented in 1910 by Washington, DC inventor Louis E. Baltzley, who wanted a better way to bind his father's manuscripts. His company, the Baltzley Manufacturing Company, began production, and the design was later acquired and sold widely; the clips are manufactured today in many countries and in sizes from about 9 mm to 50 mm in width.

Uses

Beyond holding paper, binder clips are used for sealing food bags, organising cables, holding tablecloths, and countless improvised purposes; the removable wire handles can also serve as hooks or stands. Unlike a bulldog clip, whose handles are fixed, a binder clip's handles can be removed entirely after applying the clip.
