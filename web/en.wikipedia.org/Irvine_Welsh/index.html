Irvine Welsh

Born: 27 September 1958, Leith, Edinburgh, Scotland

Occupation: Novelist, playwright, screenwriter

Notable works: Trainspotting (1993), Filth (1998), Porno (2002)

Irvine Welsh (born 27 September 1958) is a Scottish novelist, playwright and short story writer. His novel Trainspotting was made into a film of the same name. His work is characterised by a raw Scots dialect and a brutal depiction of Edinburgh life. He has also written plays and screenplays, and has published books on the homeless.

Career

Welsh was born in Leith, the port area of Edinburgh, and grew up in Muirhouse. After various jobs — television repairman, punk musician in London, council housing officer in Edinburgh — he began writing in the late 1980s. Trainspotting (1993), his first novel, follows a group of heroin addicts in Leith, including Mark Renton and Francis Begbie, and was written largely in phonetic Edinburgh Scots. Its 1996 film adaptation, directed by Danny Boyle, became a defining work of 1990s British cinema.

Other works

Subsequent novels include Marabou Stork Nightmares (1995), Filth (1998), Glue (2001), Porno (2002), a sequel to Trainspotting, The Bedroom Secrets of the Master Chefs (2006), Skagboys (2012), a prequel, and The Blade Artist (2016), centred on Begbie. His short story collection The Acid House (1994) was also filmed. Welsh divides his time between Ireland, the United States, and Scotland.
