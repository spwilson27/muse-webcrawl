Stained glass

Medieval stained glass in a Gothic church window.

Stained glass is coloured glass as a material or works created from it. Throughout its thousand-year history, the term has been applied almost exclusively to the windows of churches and other significant religious buildings. Although traditionally made in flat panels and used as windows, the creations of modern stained glass artists also include three-dimensional structures and sculpture. Modern vernacular usage has often extended the term "stained glass" to include domestic leadlight and objects created from glass foil, typified by the famous lamps of Louis Comfort Tiffany.

Manufacture

Coloured glass is made by adding metallic salts during manufacture — cobalt for blue, gold for ruby red, manganese for purple. Pieces are cut to shape following a full-size design (cartoon), details are painted on with vitreous enamel or silver stain (which produces yellow tones and gives the craft its name), the pieces are fired, then assembled in H-section lead cames and soldered into panels, which are set in an iron framework in the window opening.

History

Coloured glass was made in ancient Egypt and Rome, but stained glass windows as an art form flourished in medieval Europe. The oldest surviving complete windows in situ are those of Augsburg Cathedral (c. 1130s). The Gothic era saw vast windows at Chartres, Bourges, and the Sainte-Chapelle in Paris, where walls became screens of glowing colour telling biblical stories to a largely illiterate congregation. The Reformation and later the Dissolution of the Monasteries destroyed much English glass; the craft revived in the 19th century with the Gothic Revival and the Arts and Crafts movement (William Morris), and was transformed in America by John La Farge and Louis Comfort Tiffany, who developed opalescent glass and copper-foil construction.

Modern use

Contemporary stained glass appears in secular buildings, homes, and public art, using techniques such as fusing, slumping, acid etching, and dalle de verre (thick slab glass set in concrete or resin). Major modern commissions include Marc Chagall's windows for Reims Cathedral and Jerusalem, and the progressively abstract windows of German post-war churches.
