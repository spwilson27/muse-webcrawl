Calais

Calais is a French port city in the Pas-de-Calais department in northern France. Situated on the Strait of Dover at the narrowest point of the English Channel, it is the closest French town to England, with the White Cliffs of Dover visible on clear days.

History

After the Battle of Crécy, Edward III besieged Calais in 1346–47; six burghers famously offered themselves to save the town, commemorated by Rodin's sculpture The Burghers of Calais. Calais remained the last English possession on mainland France until 1558, when it was retaken by the Duke of Guise — prompting Mary I's lament that "Calais" would be found written on her heart.

Port and transport

The Port of Calais is among France's busiest passenger ports, with ferries to Dover. Nearby Coquelles is the French terminal of the Channel Tunnel, opened in 1994.

Lace and war

In the 19th century Calais became a centre of machine lace-making with Nottingham workers. The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War and briefly the target of a German feint during Operation Fortitude before D-Day.
