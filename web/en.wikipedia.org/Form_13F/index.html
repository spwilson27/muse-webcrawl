Form 13F

Form 13F is a quarterly report filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) by institutional investment managers that exercise investment discretion over 00 million or more in Section 13(f) securities, principally exchange-traded equities. The reports disclose the managers' holdings of those securities.

Requirements

The filing obligation arises under Section 13(f) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, added by Congress in 1975 to promote public disclosure of institutional holdings. Managers must file within 45 days of the end of each calendar quarter, listing holdings by issuer, class, CUSIP, number of shares, and market value.

Use and limitations

Investors, journalists, and researchers use 13F filings to track the positions of prominent funds and managers. The data have significant limitations: they are published with a lag, cover only long positions in designated securities (not short positions or most derivatives), and are reported on a quarterly snapshot basis. The SEC has periodically proposed raising the 00 million reporting threshold, most notably in 2020, though the proposal was not adopted.
