Xeroderma pigmentosum
SpecialtyMedical genetics, dermatology
CauseDefects in nucleotide excision repair genes

Frequency~1 in 1,000,000 (US/Europe); higher in Japan and North Africa

Xeroderma pigmentosum (XP) is a rare autosomal recessive genetic disorder of DNA repair in which the ability to repair damage caused by ultraviolet (UV) light is deficient. In extreme cases, all exposure to sunlight must be forbidden, leading affected people to be called "children of the night."

Characteristic freckling and skin changes in a child with XP.

Symptoms

Signs typically appear in infancy or early childhood: severe sunburn after minimal exposure, pronounced freckling before age two, dry skin (xeroderma) and pigment changes (pigmentosum), eye involvement, and a greatly elevated risk of skin cancers including melanoma and squamous cell carcinoma. About a quarter of patients develop progressive neurological degeneration.

Genetics

XP is caused by mutations in genes of the nucleotide excision repair pathway (complementation groups XPA through XPG) or, in the variant form, in the POLH gene encoding polymerase eta, which normally bypasses UV-induced DNA lesions.

Management

There is no cure; management centers on rigorous UV avoidance, protective clothing and films, vitamin D supplementation, surveillance and early removal of skin tumors.
