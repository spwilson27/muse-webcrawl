Replicant

A replicant is a fictional bioengineered humanoid featured in the 1982 film Blade Runner and its 2017 sequel Blade Runner 2049. Created by the Tyrell Corporation (and later the Wallace Corporation), replicants are virtually indistinguishable from adult humans; a fictional test, the Voigt-Kampff empathy test, is used to identify them by measuring emotional responses.

Origin of the term

The word was coined for the film by screenwriter David Peoples; Philip K. Dick's source novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? (1968) called such beings "androids" or "andys". In the film's future Los Angeles of 2019, the Nexus-6 replicants are stronger and more agile than humans, with at least equal intelligence, but were given a deliberately short four-year lifespan to limit the risk posed by their developing emotions and memories.

In the films

The plot follows blade runner Rick Deckard, tasked with "retiring" six escaped Nexus-6 replicants led by Roy Batty. The replicant Rachael, who initially believes herself human, raises the central question of what distinguishes humans from their manufactured doubles; Batty's dying "tears in rain" monologue has become one of cinema's most quoted speeches. In Blade Runner 2049, the replicant blade runner K discovers evidence that a replicant has given birth, overturning the supposed boundary between born and made.

Legacy

"Replicant" has entered wider usage as a term for synthetic humans and as a touchstone in debates about artificial intelligence, personhood and slavery. The concept influenced later works across science fiction film, anime and video games.
