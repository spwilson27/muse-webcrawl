American Law Institute

The American Law Institute (ALI) is a research and advocacy group of judges, lawyers, and legal scholars established in 1923 to promote the clarification and simplification of United States common law and its adaptation to changing social needs. It is based in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, and is best known for its Restatements of the Law, the Model Penal Code, and its work on the Uniform Commercial Code.

Projects

The ALI's Restatements summarise the principles of areas of common law such as contracts, torts, property, agency, and conflict of laws, and are frequently cited by courts. The Model Penal Code, completed in 1962, influenced the revision of criminal codes in most US states. Together with the Uniform Law Commission, the ALI drafted the Uniform Commercial Code, the comprehensive set of rules governing commercial transactions adopted in nearly every state. More recent projects include Principles of the Law and restatements on consumer contracts, liability insurance, and children and the law.

Membership

Membership is limited to about 3,000 elected members, drawn from judges, law professors, and practising lawyers, supplemented by ex officio members such as chief judges, law school deans, and bar association presidents. Drafts are prepared by specialist reporters, reviewed by advisers and the ALI Council, and approved at the annual meeting.
