Nickel

Symbol: Ni
Atomic number: 28
Melting point: 1,455 °C
Density: 8.9 g/cm³

Nickel is a chemical element with the symbol Ni and atomic number 28. It is a silvery-white, hard, ductile transition metal, resistant to corrosion even at high temperatures. Pure native nickel is rare on Earth's surface, though the element is abundant in the Earth's core and in meteoric iron.

History

German miners who sought copper in Erzgebirge ore but got none blamed a mischievous spirit, "Nickel," giving the metal its name (Kupfernickel, "devil's copper"). Axel Fredrik Cronstedt isolated the metal in 1751. The United States five-cent coin, the "nickel," is 75% copper and 25% nickel.

Uses

The great majority of nickel production goes into stainless steel and other corrosion-resistant alloys such as Inconel and Monel. Nickel is also used in rechargeable batteries (including lithium-ion and nickel-metal hydride), electroplating, and catalysts. Major producers include Indonesia, the Philippines, Russia, and Canada (Sudbury Basin).
