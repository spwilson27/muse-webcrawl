John Koukouzelis

Born: c. 1280, Dyrrhachium (probably)
Died: c. 1360s, Mount Athos
Venerated in: Eastern Orthodox Church
Feast: 1 October
Known for: Reform of Byzantine chant notation; kalophonic chant

John Koukouzelis (Greek: Ιωάννης Κουκουζέλης; c. 1280 – c. 1360s) was a Byzantine composer, singer and musical theoretician, venerated as a saint by the Eastern Orthodox Church. He is regarded as the most illustrious musician of the Palaiologan era and one of the greatest figures in the history of Byzantine chant, a reformer of its musical notation comparable in importance to the earlier John of Damascus.

Life

Koukouzelis was probably born in Dyrrhachium (modern Durrës) to a Bulgarian mother, which earned him the nickname "the Angel-voiced" as well as his surname, said to derive from a childhood joke mixing the Greek word for beans (koukia) with the Slavic word for cabbage (zelis) — the food of his schoolboy diet. Recognised for his beautiful voice, he was educated at the imperial school in Constantinople and became a leading singer at the court. According to his Life, he later withdrew to the monastery of the Great Lavra on Mount Athos, where he lived as a monk until his death.

Works

Koukouzelis is credited with a reform and elaboration of Byzantine musical notation, codified in his treatise on the chant signs and in the didactic chant "Mega ison". He composed extensively in the new ornate kalophonic ("beautiful-sounding") style, including elaborate settings of psalm verses and a widely transmitted All-Night Vigil repertory. His compositions, preserved in numerous manuscripts, shaped the chant traditions of Greece and the Balkans for centuries, and his fame spread through the Slavic world, where he was honoured as a Bulgarian-born saint of music.

Legacy

Koukouzelis Nunatak on Livingston Island in Antarctica is named after him, and he is depicted in frescoes across the Orthodox world, usually holding a scroll of musical notation.
