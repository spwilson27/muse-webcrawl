European Green Party

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The European Green Party (EGP), also known as European Greens, is a transnational, European political party representing national parties from across Europe that share Green values. It was founded on 21–22 February 2004 at a congress in Rome by the merger of the European Federation of Green Parties with earlier green coordination structures.

Ideology and platform

The EGP is committed to environmental responsibility, individual freedom, inclusivity, diversity, equality, gender equality, global sustainable development, peace and non-violence. It advocates ambitious climate action, a just transition to renewable energy, protection of civil liberties and the rule of law, and a more democratic and federal European Union. Its guiding charter was adopted at its founding congress.

Organisation

The party comprises green parties from EU member states and other European countries, including associate members and candidates. Its highest decision-making body is the Congress; between congresses a Council meets, and day-to-day work is led by a Committee (executive board) with two co-chairs, traditionally one woman and one man following the green principle of co-leadership.

European Parliament

Members of the European Parliament from EGP member parties sit in the Greens/European Free Alliance group, formed with the European Free Alliance. The group became the fourth largest in the Parliament after the 2019 European elections during the so-called "Green Wave", and its members have included parties such as Germany's Bündnis 90/Die Grünen, France's Europe Écologie, and the Netherlands' GroenLinks. The EGP runs joint European election campaigns with common lead candidates (Spitzenkandidaten).
