Artistic gymnastics

Artistic gymnastics is a discipline of gymnastics in which athletes perform short routines on different apparatuses. The sport is governed by the Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique (FIG), which designs the Code of Points and regulates all aspects of international elite competition. Artistic gymnastics is a popular spectator sport at the Summer Olympic Games and other major competitions.

History

Gymnastics developed from exercises used by ancient Greeks, including skills for mounting and dismounting a horse. The modern system was shaped in the early 19th century by Friedrich Ludwig Jahn of Germany, known as the "father of gymnastics", who introduced apparatus such as the horizontal bar, parallel bars, rings and pommel horse. The FIG was founded in 1881, and men's gymnastics was included in the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896. Women's events were added at the 1928 Amsterdam Games.

The sport's popularity surged in the 1970s with stars such as Olga Korbut and Nadia Comăneci, who scored the first perfect 10.0 at the 1976 Montreal Olympics. In 2006 the FIG replaced the perfect-10 system with an open-ended Code of Points combining a difficulty (D) score and an execution (E) score.

Apparatus

Women's events

Women compete on four apparatus: the vault, uneven bars, balance beam, and floor exercise (performed to music). Routines combine acrobatic elements, dance, leaps and turns.

Men's events

Men compete on six apparatus: floor exercise, pommel horse, still rings, vault, parallel bars, and horizontal bar. Strength elements, particularly on rings and pommel horse, are distinctive features of the men's programme.

Competition format

Major competitions include qualification rounds, team finals, individual all-around finals, and apparatus finals for the top eight gymnasts on each event. Gymnasts are judged on difficulty and execution; falls and steps on landing incur deductions. Famous champions include Simone Biles of the United States, the most decorated gymnast in history, Japan's Kōhei Uchimura, and Sawao Kato.

Scoring

Under the current Code of Points, a gymnast's score is the sum of the D-score (the value of the skills performed) and the E-score (starting from 10.0, with deductions for errors in technique and artistry). This open-ended system rewards difficulty while still penalising poor execution.
