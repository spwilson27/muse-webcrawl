Army Public School massacre
Date: 16 December 2014
Location: Peshawar, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan
Deaths: 149 (including 132 schoolchildren)
Perpetrators: Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP)

The Army Public School massacre, also called the Peshawar school massacre, was a terrorist attack on 16 December 2014 in which seven gunmen of the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) attacked the Army Public School in the northwestern Pakistani city of Peshawar. They killed 149 people, including 132 schoolchildren, making it one of the deadliest school attacks in history.

Attack

The gunmen, wearing army uniforms, entered the school — attended mainly by children of military personnel — and opened fire in classrooms and the auditorium, where an assembly was under way. Pakistani security forces stormed the building and killed all seven attackers after a siege lasting several hours. More than 100 people were wounded.

Aftermath

The massacre provoked outrage across Pakistan and internationally. The government lifted its moratorium on the death penalty for terrorism cases, launched the National Action Plan against militancy, and intensified military operations in the tribal areas. The TTP said the attack was retaliation for the army's Operation Zarb-e-Azb in North Waziristan. 16 December is commemorated annually in Pakistan.
