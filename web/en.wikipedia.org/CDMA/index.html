Code-division multiple access

Code-division multiple access (CDMA) is a channel access method used by various radio communication technologies. It is an example of multiple access, where several transmitters can send information simultaneously over a single communication channel, distinguished by the use of different spreading codes.

Principle

In CDMA, each user's signal is multiplied by a unique pseudo-random code sequence, spreading it over a wide bandwidth. The receiver, knowing the code, correlates the received signal to recover the desired data while other signals appear as noise. This is a form of spread spectrum communication.

Applications

CDMA was the basis of the IS-95 (cdmaOne) and CDMA2000 mobile phone standards developed by Qualcomm, and of the UMTS/W-CDMA air interface used in many 3G networks. It is also used in GPS satellite navigation, where each satellite transmits with a distinct code. As carriers moved to 4G LTE and 5G, CDMA networks were progressively shut down in the 2020s.

Advantages

Advantages include resistance to interference and jamming, soft capacity limits, and the ability to reuse the same frequency in every cell.
