Boaty McBoatface

Boaty McBoatface is the name given to an autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) operated by the United Kingdom's National Oceanography Centre. The name became famous in 2016 when it overwhelmingly won an online poll by the Natural Environment Research Council (NERC) to name a new polar research ship; NERC instead named the ship RRS Sir David Attenborough and gave the runaway name to the vessel's lead AUV as a compromise.

The poll

The #NameOurShip campaign invited public suggestions. The name “Boaty McBoatface”, suggested by former BBC Radio Jersey presenter James Hand, attracted worldwide attention and won with about 124,000 votes, far ahead of the runner-up. Although the poll was advisory, the decision to override it drew criticism and parliamentary scrutiny; NERC's chief executive defended naming the ship after Sir David Attenborough while assigning the popular name to the submersible.

The vehicle

Boaty McBoatface is a long-range Autosub capable of travelling up to about 6,000 metres deep and thousands of kilometres under ice and in open ocean, carrying sensors for temperature, salinity and currents. It undertook its maiden science voyage in 2017 aboard RRS James Clark Ross in the Orkney Passage, gathering data on abyssal water mixing relevant to climate models, and later carried out missions beneath Antarctic ice shelves.

Cultural impact

The episode spawned a family of imitative names, including a Swedish train named Trainy McTrainface and Australian racehorse Horsey McHorseface, and “McBoatfacing” entered discussions of public consultations and internet polls.
