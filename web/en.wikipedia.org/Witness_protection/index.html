Witness protection

Witness protection is security provided to a threatened person providing testimonial evidence to the criminal justice system, including defendants and other immediate family members. It often includes relocation to a new place and the issuing of a new identity, so that the witness can live beyond the reach of those who might retaliate.

United States

The best-known programme is the United States Federal Witness Protection Program, known as WITSEC, established by the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 and administered by the U.S. Marshals Service. It was created largely through the efforts of Justice Department official Gerald Shur to protect witnesses testifying against the Mafia. Participants receive new identity documents, housing, subsistence payments, and help finding employment in an undisclosed location. Since its inception the programme has protected tens of thousands of witnesses and their families; the Marshals Service states that no participant who followed programme guidelines has been harmed while under its protection.

Around the world

Many other countries operate witness protection schemes, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Germany, Italy — where protection of pentiti (Mafia defectors) proved crucial to prosecutions — and the Philippines. International tribunals also maintain protection units for witnesses in war-crimes trials. Programmes vary in the level of support offered and in whether relocation is domestic or abroad.
