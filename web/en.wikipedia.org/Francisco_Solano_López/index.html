Francisco Solano López Carrillo (1827–1870) was the president of Paraguay from 1862 until his death in 1870. He led Paraguay into the War of the Triple Alliance (1864–1870) against Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay — the most devastating war in South American history, which destroyed much of Paraguay's population and economy.

Rise to power

The son of president Carlos Antonio López, Francisco was groomed as heir, commanding the army and undertaking a diplomatic mission to Europe in 1853–1854, where he met his Irish-born partner Eliza Lynch. He succeeded his father in 1862 and continued Paraguay's state-led modernisation while concentrating power in his own hands.

War of the Triple Alliance

In 1864 López intervened in the Uruguayan civil war, seizing a Brazilian steamer and invading Brazilian and then Argentine territory, which brought the three neighbours into alliance against him. Despite remarkable Paraguayan resistance, the war became a catastrophe: Asunción was occupied in 1869 and López was killed at the Battle of Cerro Corá on 1 March 1870, refusing to surrender.

Legacy

López is one of the most debated figures in Paraguayan history — condemned as a reckless tyrant by traditional historiography and glorified as a national hero by revisionist writers and the Paraguayan state, which honoured him as a marshal and national hero in the 20th century.
