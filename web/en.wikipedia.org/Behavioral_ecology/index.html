Behavioral ecology

Behavioral ecology, also spelled behavioural ecology, is the study of the evolutionary basis for animal behavior due to ecological pressures. It emerged from ethology, ecology and evolutionary biology.

Topics

The field examines foraging, mating systems, parental care, territoriality, communication, cooperation and altruism, often using optimality models, game theory and kin selection theory.

History

Key figures include Niko Tinbergen, W. D. Hamilton, John Maynard Smith and Richard Dawkins, whose work explained behaviours such as altruism in terms of gene-level selection.
