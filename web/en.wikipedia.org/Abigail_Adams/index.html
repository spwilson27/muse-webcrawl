Abigail Adams (née Smith; 1744–1818) was the wife and closest advisor of John Adams, the second president of the United States, and the mother of John Quincy Adams, the sixth president. She was the first lady of the United States from 1797 to 1801 and is remembered for her extensive correspondence, which offers a vivid first-hand account of the American Revolutionary era and an early voice on women's rights.

Life

Born in Weymouth, Massachusetts, to the minister William Smith, Abigail received no formal schooling but read widely in her father's library. She married John Adams in 1764 and managed their farm in Braintree (later Quincy) and raised their children during his long absences on legal and diplomatic business. Her letters to him — more than a thousand survive — are celebrated for their wit and political insight. In her most famous letter of March 1776 she urged him to "remember the ladies" when drafting the new nation's laws, warning that women would "foment a rebellion" if given no voice.

First lady and legacy

As first lady she was an active political partner to her husband, so influential that critics called her "Mrs. President"; she supported the Alien and Sedition Acts and opposed slavery and the denial of education to women and girls. She was the first first lady to live in the White House in Washington, D.C., in 1800. She died of typhoid fever in 1818. Abigail Adams is the only woman to have been both the wife of one U.S. president and the mother of another until Barbara Bush.
