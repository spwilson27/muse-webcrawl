Targeted advertising

Targeted advertising is a form of advertising in which marketers direct their messages to audiences with specific traits, interests, and preferences, rather than to a general audience. Online, it relies on collecting data about users — such as demographics, browsing history, search behaviour, location, and purchases — and using algorithms to select which advertisements each user sees.

Methods

Common methods include behavioural targeting, which profiles users from their online activity; contextual advertising, which matches ads to the content of the page being viewed; retargeting, which shows ads for products a user previously viewed; geotargeting, based on location; and demographic and psychographic targeting. Social media platforms allow advertisers to target combinations of attributes with great precision, and programmatic advertising automates the buying of impressions in real time.

History

Advertisers traditionally targeted audiences through the choice of magazine, newspaper section, or television programme. The web introduced far finer targeting in the 1990s and 2000s, with search advertising, cookies, and later social-media profiles enabling individual-level selection. Mobile devices added location data and app-based identifiers.

Criticism and regulation

Targeted advertising has been criticised for invading privacy, enabling discrimination (for example in housing or employment ads), facilitating manipulation in political campaigns, and funding an extensive data-broker industry. Regulators have responded with measures such as the European Union's GDPR and ePrivacy rules, California's consumer privacy laws, restrictions on tracking by browser and platform makers, and bans or limits on targeting minors and on the use of sensitive categories.
