Verizon

Verizon Communications Inc. is an American multinational telecommunications conglomerate headquartered in New York City. It is one of the largest telecommunications companies in the world and one of the largest providers of wireless services in the United States, competing principally with AT&T and T-Mobile US.

History

Verizon was formed in 2000 from the merger of Bell Atlantic and GTE, two companies descended in part from the breakup of the Bell System. The name combines veritas (Latin for truth) and horizon. Its wireless division, Verizon Wireless, was originally a joint venture with Vodafone; Verizon bought out Vodafone's stake for $130 billion in 2014, one of the largest corporate deals in history. The company also expanded into media with the acquisitions of AOL (2015) and Yahoo (2017), later sold off in 2021.

Operations

Verizon offers mobile phone service over its nationwide 4G LTE and 5G networks, fixed broadband under the Fios brand (fibre to the home), and enterprise and wholesale network services. It has around 140 million US retail connections and annual revenues exceeding $130 billion.
