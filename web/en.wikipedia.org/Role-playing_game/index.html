Role-playing game

A role-playing game (sometimes spelled roleplaying game and abbreviated to RPG) is a game in which players assume the roles of characters in a fictional setting. Players take responsibility for acting out these roles within a narrative, either through literal acting or through a process of structured decision-making regarding character development. Actions taken within many games succeed or fail according to a formal system of rules and guidelines.

Forms

There are several forms of role-playing games. The original form, sometimes called the tabletop role-playing game (TRPG or TTRPG), is conducted through discussion, whereas in live action role-playing (LARP), players physically perform their characters' actions. In both of these forms, an arranger called a game master (GM) usually decides on the rules and setting, and acts as referee; each of the other players takes on the role of a single character. Video games have adopted the term for genres including computer role-playing games (CRPGs) and massively multiplayer online role-playing games (MMORPGs), in which software enforces the rules and adjudicates outcomes.

Tabletop games

The hobby began with Dungeons & Dragons (1974), created by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson out of miniature wargaming. Players create characters defined by statistics and abilities, and resolve uncertain actions with dice. Campaigns may run for years. Other influential systems include Call of Cthulhu, Traveller, GURPS, Vampire: The Masquerade, and Pathfinder. The tabletop hobby saw a major resurgence in the 2010s with the fifth edition of Dungeons & Dragons, actual-play streaming shows, and online virtual tabletops.

Purpose and social role

Advocates emphasise collaborative storytelling, improvisation, problem-solving, and social interaction. RPGs are used in education, therapy, and military training simulations. Critics periodically raised moral panics — notably in the 1980s — but research generally finds the hobby benign and often beneficial.
