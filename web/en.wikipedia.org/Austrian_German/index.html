Austrian German

Austrian German (German: Österreichisches Deutsch) is the variety of Standard German written and spoken in Austria and South Tyrol. It has its own codified norms, described in the Österreichisches Wörterbuch, published by the Austrian Federal Ministry of Education since 1951, and its official status was recognised in Austria's accession to the European Union, which also secured protection for a list of specifically Austrian food terms.

Vocabulary

Austrian German contains numerous distinctive words, many of them borrowings from the languages of the former Habsburg Empire — Czech, Hungarian, Italian, Croatian, Serbian and Yiddish. Examples include Jänner for January (versus German Januar), Erdapfel for potato, Paradeiser for tomato (in the east), Semmel for a bread roll, heuer for "this year", and Feber for February in legal usage. Kitchen and administrative vocabulary shows especially strong regional character.

Pronunciation and grammar

Standard pronunciation in Austria tends to be softer than northern German norms, with less forceful aspiration and a distinctive intonation; many Austrians speak Bavarian dialects in everyday life and shift along a continuum toward the standard. Minor grammatical differences include uses of auxiliary verbs (for example, sein with verbs of standing, sitting and lying) shared with southern German varieties.
