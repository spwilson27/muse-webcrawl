Pashto (پښتو, Pəx̌tó) is an Eastern Iranian language in the Indo-European family, and one of the two official languages of Afghanistan alongside Dari. It is the native language of the Pashtun people and is spoken by tens of millions in Afghanistan and in Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province and northern Balochistan, as well as by a large diaspora.

Dialects and grammar

Pashto divides broadly into northern and southern dialect groups, distinguished among other features by the pronunciation of the letters ښ and ږ (the classic "soft" southern versus "hard" northern variants). The literary standard draws on both. Grammatically Pashto is a split-ergative language: in past-tense transitive clauses the subject is marked in the oblique case and the verb agrees with the object. It has two genders, a rich system of verb conjugations, and a vocabulary layered with loanwords from Persian, Arabic, Turkic, and Indic languages.

Literature

Pashto literature flourished from the 16th century. Bayazid Pir Roshan, founder of the Roshani movement, is credited as an early prose writer and inventor of an early Pashto script. The 17th-century warrior-poet Khushal Khan Khattak is regarded as the national poet of the Pashtuns, while the Sufi poet Rahman Baba remains beloved for his lyrical Diwan. Court poetry in Pashto was patronised under the Afghan empires, and the language is central to the oral tradition of the landay, a two-line folk couplet often composed by women.

Status

In Afghanistan, Pashto is promoted by the state and used in government, media and education, though Dari dominates in Kabul and in much administration. In Pakistan it is a major regional language with a vigorous press, film and music culture centred on Peshawar.
