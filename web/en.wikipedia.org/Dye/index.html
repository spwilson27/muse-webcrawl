Dye

A dye is a coloured substance that chemically bonds to the material to which it is applied, distinguishing it from a pigment, which does not chemically bind. Dyes are generally applied in aqueous solution and may require a mordant to improve fastness on fibres.

History

Natural dyes such as indigo, madder, weld and Tyrian purple were used from antiquity to colour textiles. The industry was transformed in 1856 when William Henry Perkin accidentally synthesized mauveine, the first synthetic dye, launching the modern chemical dye industry.

Types

Major classes include acid dyes, basic dyes, direct dyes, reactive dyes, vat dyes and disperse dyes, each suited to particular fibres. Natural dyes were largely displaced by synthetic ones because of cost, consistency and fastness, though they persist in artisanal and eco-conscious production.

Applications

Beyond textiles, dyes are used in food colouring, hair colouring, biological staining, printing inks, lasers and optical media.
