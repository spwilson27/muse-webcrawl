Metabolic adaptation

Metabolic adaptation, also called adaptive thermogenesis, is the process by which the body alters its efficiency in response to changes in energy intake, most notably during periods of caloric restriction and weight loss. During dieting, total daily energy expenditure often falls by more than would be predicted from the loss of body mass alone.

Mechanism

When energy intake decreases, the body responds by reducing resting metabolic rate, non-exercise activity thermogenesis, and the thermic effect of food. Hormonal changes, including reduced leptin and thyroid hormone levels, contribute to increased hunger and decreased energy expenditure. These adaptations are thought to be an evolutionary defence against starvation.

Implications

Metabolic adaptation is a major factor in weight-loss plateaus and in the high rate of weight regain after dieting, because a reduced body maintains itself on fewer calories than a never-reduced body of the same size.
