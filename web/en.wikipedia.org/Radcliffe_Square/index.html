Radcliffe Square

Radcliffe Square, Oxford, looking toward the Radcliffe Camera.

Radcliffe Square is a square in central Oxford, England, widely regarded as one of the most beautiful in the city. It is named after John Radcliffe, the royal physician whose bequest funded several of the buildings surrounding it. The square is cobbled and largely pedestrianised, situated between the High Street to the south and Broad Street to the north.

Surrounding buildings

At the centre stands the Radcliffe Camera (built 1737–1749, designed by James Gibbs), a circular Palladian reading room of the Bodleian Library and an iconic symbol of Oxford. Around the square are the University Church of St Mary the Virgin to the south, All Souls College to the east, Brasenose College to the west, and the Old Bodleian Library buildings and the Divinity School to the north.

Views

The view from the tower of St Mary's offers a celebrated panorama of the square and Oxford's "dreaming spires". The square appears frequently as a filming location, including in the television series Inspector Morse and Lewis.
