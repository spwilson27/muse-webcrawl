Food processing

Food processing is the transformation of agricultural products into food, or of one form of food into other forms. Food processing includes many forms of processing foods, from grinding grain to make raw flour to home cooking to complex industrial methods used to make convenience foods. Some food processing methods play important roles in reducing food waste and improving food preservation, reducing the overall negative impact of agriculture, and improving food security.

An industrial food-processing plant.

History

Processing is as old as cooking, drying, salting, fermenting, and milling. Canning (Appert, early 1800s), pasteurisation (Pasteur, 1860s), refrigeration, and freeze-drying industrialised preservation in the 19th and 20th centuries, enabling urban food supply at scale and the modern packaged-food industry.

Methods and classification

Primary processing turns raw commodities into ingredients (milling wheat into flour, butchering animals); secondary processing turns ingredients into edible products (bread, sausages); tertiary processing produces ready-to-eat convenience foods. Techniques include thermal processing, fermentation, dehydration, extrusion, irradiation, high-pressure processing, and modified-atmosphere packaging. The NOVA classification groups foods by degree of processing, with "ultra-processed foods" (formulations of industrial ingredients) attracting public-health scrutiny for links with obesity and chronic disease.

Regulation and safety

Food processing is regulated for hygiene and safety, using systems such as HACCP, good manufacturing practice, labelling and additive approvals administered by bodies including the US FDA and USDA and the European Food Safety Authority. Benefits of processing include safety, shelf life, affordability, and availability; criticisms focus on nutrient loss, additives, and the health profile of highly processed products.
