Vector bundle

In mathematics, a vector bundle is a topological construction that makes precise the idea of a family of vector spaces parameterised by another space X: to every point x of the space X we associate (or "attach") a vector space V(x) in such a way that these vector spaces fit together to form another space of the same kind as X (e.g. a topological space, manifold, or algebraic variety), which is then called the total space of the vector bundle. Each vector space V(x) is called the fibre over x.

Definition

A real vector bundle consists of a total space E, a base space X, and a continuous surjection π : E → X, such that each fibre π−1({x}) carries the structure of a finite-dimensional real vector space, and every point of X has a neighbourhood over which the bundle is isomorphic to a product U × ℝn (local triviality). The dimension n is the rank of the bundle. The Möbius strip is the classic example of a nontrivial line bundle over the circle.

Examples and sections

The tangent bundle of a smooth manifold, whose fibre at each point is the tangent space, is a central example; its sections are vector fields. Other examples include normal bundles, cotangent bundles, and line bundles in algebraic geometry. A continuous map from the base into the total space choosing a point in each fibre is called a section; whether a bundle admits nowhere-vanishing sections is a fundamental question answered by characteristic classes.

Operations and classification

Vector bundles admit Whitney sums, tensor products, duals, and pullbacks. Over a fixed base, isomorphism classes of bundles are studied through K-theory and classified by characteristic classes such as Stiefel–Whitney, Chern, and Pontryagin classes, which measure the obstruction to triviality.
