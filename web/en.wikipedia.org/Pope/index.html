The pope (Latin: papa, from Greek πάππας, pappas, "father") is the bishop of Rome and the head of the worldwide Catholic Church. Catholics regard him as the successor of Saint Peter, to whom they hold Christ gave primacy among the apostles, and as Christ's vicar on earth. The pope is also ex officio head of state of Vatican City, the sovereign city-state entirely enclosed by Rome. The office — the papacy — is one of the most enduring institutions in the world, with a claimed unbroken line of some 266 popes.

Authority and doctrine

The pope exercises supreme, full and universal jurisdiction over the Catholic Church under canon law. The First Vatican Council (1870) defined the dogma of papal infallibility: when the pope speaks ex cathedra — solemnly defining a doctrine of faith or morals to be held by the whole Church — he is preserved from error. Popes govern through the Roman Curia, appoint bishops and cardinals, canonise saints, issue encyclicals, bulls and apostolic constitutions, and conduct diplomacy through the Holy See, which maintains relations with most states and permanent observers at the United Nations.

Election

Since 1059 the pope has been elected by the College of Cardinals in a secret conclave held in the Sistine Chapel, requiring a two-thirds majority; white smoke signals a successful election. Popes serve for life, although Benedict XVI's resignation in 2013 — the first voluntary papal resignation in nearly 600 years — showed the office can be relinquished.

History

The papacy's influence grew through late antiquity and the Middle Ages, when popes such as Gregory IX and Innocent III asserted authority over kings and emperors, launched crusades, and reformed canon law. The Western Schism (1378–1417), with rival popes at Rome and Avignon, and the Reformation of the 16th century gravely divided Western Christendom. The popes ruled the Papal States of central Italy until 1870, when Rome was annexed by the Kingdom of Italy; the Lateran Treaty of 1929 created Vatican City. In the modern era, popes such as Paul VI, who closed the Second Vatican Council, and John Paul II, have been global public figures.
