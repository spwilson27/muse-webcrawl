Theory of everything

A theory of everything (TOE) is a hypothetical framework in theoretical physics that would fully explain and link together all physical phenomena, unifying the fundamental forces of nature — gravity, electromagnetism, the weak nuclear force and the strong nuclear force — within a single coherent theory.

Background

Modern physics rests on two pillars that are mutually incompatible in certain regimes: general relativity, describing gravity and large-scale structure, and quantum mechanics, describing the subatomic world in the form of the Standard Model of particle physics. A theory of everything would reconcile them, resolving problems such as the singularities inside black holes and at the Big Bang.

Candidate theories

The leading approaches include string theory and its extension M-theory, which posit that fundamental particles are vibrations of one-dimensional strings and require additional spatial dimensions, and loop quantum gravity, which quantizes spacetime itself. No candidate has yet produced testable predictions distinguishing it from existing theory, and the search remains one of the great open problems of physics.
