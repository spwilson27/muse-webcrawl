Howard Devoto

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Howard Devoto (born Howard Andrew Trafford, 15 March 1952) is an English singer and songwriter. He co-founded the punk rock band Buzzcocks with Pete Shelley in 1976, then left to form the post-punk band Magazine, whose work was influential on later alternative rock.

Born: 15 March 1952, Scunthorpe, England
Genres: Punk rock, post-punk, new wave
Instruments: Vocals
Associated acts: Buzzcocks, Magazine, Luxuria

Buzzcocks

Devoto met Pete Shelley at the Bolton Institute of Technology, and together they organised the Sex Pistols’ famous June 1976 Lesser Free Trade Hall concert in Manchester and formed Buzzcocks. Devoto sang on the band’s self-released debut EP Spiral Scratch (1977), one of the first independently released punk records, before leaving a few weeks after its release.

Magazine and later work

With guitarist John McGeoch and others he formed Magazine in 1977. Their debut single “Shot by Both Sides” and albums including Real Life (1978) and The Correct Use of Soap (1980) combined literary lyrics with angular, keyboard-rich arrangements and helped define post-punk. After Magazine split in 1981, Devoto released the solo album Jerky Versions of the Dream (1983) and formed Luxuria. Magazine reunited in 2009, releasing the album No Thyself in 2011.
