Hutt River (New Zealand)

Hutt River / Te Awa Kairangi
LocationWellington Region, New Zealand

SourceTararua Range

The Hutt River (Māori: Te Awa Kairangi) is a river in the Wellington Region of New Zealand's North Island. It flows southwest for about 56 kilometres from the southern Tararua Range through the Hutt Valley — past Upper Hutt and Lower Hutt — to enter Wellington Harbour at Petone.

Course and settlement

The river gives its name to the Hutt Valley and its two cities. Early European settlement at Britannia (Petone) in 1840 was plagued by flooding from the river, prompting settlers to move. The river has shifted course historically, and its floodplain, lying along the active Wellington Fault, is protected by stopbanks; major floods occurred in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Recreation and water supply

The Hutt River Trail runs along its banks for walking and cycling. The river and its aquifer supply a significant share of the Wellington region's drinking water, and its pools are popular for swimming and trout fishing. In 2017 its dual name, Te Awa Kairangi / Hutt River, was made official.
