Time

Time is the continuous progression of existence that occurs in an apparently irreversible succession from the past, through the present, and into the future. It is a component quantity of various measurements used to sequence events, compare the duration of events, and quantify rates of change of quantities in material reality or in conscious experience.

Measurement

Time has long been measured by astronomical cycles, sundials, water clocks and mechanical clocks. The modern SI unit of time, the second, is defined by the caesium standard: the duration of 9,192,631,770 periods of the radiation corresponding to the transition between two hyperfine levels of the caesium-133 atom. Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) provides the basis for civil timekeeping worldwide.

Physics

In classical mechanics time was treated as absolute and universal. Einstein's theory of relativity showed that time is relative: the rate at which it passes depends on the observer's motion and gravitational field, and time combines with space into a four-dimensional spacetime. General relativity and quantum mechanics still leave open questions about the fundamental nature of time.

Philosophy

Philosophers debate whether time is an objective structure of the universe or a feature of human cognition, and whether past and future events exist on an equal footing with the present (eternalism) or only the present is real (presentism).
