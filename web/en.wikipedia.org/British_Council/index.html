The British Council is a British organisation specialising in international cultural and educational opportunities. Founded in 1934, it works in more than 100 countries promoting a wider knowledge of the United Kingdom and the English language, encouraging cultural, scientific, technological and educational cooperation, and building connections between people in the UK and other countries.

History

The Council was founded in 1934 as the British Committee for Relations with Other Countries, at a time when the British government sought to counter the cultural propaganda of fascist regimes in Europe. It was renamed the British Council in 1936 and received a Royal Charter in 1940. During the Second World War it expanded its network of institutes and libraries overseas. In the postwar decades it became one of the world's largest providers of English language teaching and examinations.

Activities

The British Council teaches English, administers examinations such as IELTS (jointly with partner organisations), runs schools and teaching centres, supports international education partnerships, and delivers arts and cultural programmes including festivals, exhibitions and exchanges. It also provides study-abroad advice and operates libraries and digital learning platforms in many countries.

Organisation and funding

The Council is a registered charity and an executive non-departmental public body sponsored by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office. It receives a government grant-in-aid but earns the majority of its income from teaching, examinations and contracted services. Its head office is in London, and it is governed by a board of trustees under its Royal Charter.
