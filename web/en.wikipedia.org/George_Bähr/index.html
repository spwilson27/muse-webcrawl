George Bähr (15 March 1666 – 16 March 1738) was a German architect and master carpenter, chiefly remembered as the designer of the Frauenkirche (Church of Our Lady) in Dresden, one of the masterpieces of European Baroque architecture and a landmark of Protestant church building.

Bähr's Frauenkirche in Dresden, with its distinctive stone dome, the "Steinerne Glocke" (stone bell).

Life and career

Born into a family of weavers in Fürstenwalde in the Ore Mountains, Bähr was trained as a carpenter and worked his way up through the building trades. In 1705 he was appointed council master carpenter (Ratszimmermeister) of Dresden, although he was not formally trained as an architect. He designed several Dresden churches, including work on the Dreikönigskirche, and a number of town houses.

The Frauenkirche

In 1722 the Dresden city council resolved to replace the old Frauenkirche with a grand new building; Bähr's design was chosen and the foundation stone laid in 1726. Over the objections of sceptics — who doubted his daring stone dome, 26 metres across, could stand — Bähr, who called it the Steinerne Glocke ("stone bell"), carried the work forward until his death in 1738. The church was consecrated in 1734 and the dome completed in 1743 under his successor. The Frauenkirche was destroyed in the firebombing of Dresden in February 1945 and its ruins kept as a memorial; it was reconstructed after German reunification and reconsecrated in 2005.
