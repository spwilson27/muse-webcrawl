Rap metal

Rap metal is a fusion genre that combines the vocal and instrumental elements of hip hop with heavy metal. It is usually composed of heavy metal guitar riffs, funk metal elements, rapped vocals and sometimes turntables. The genre emerged in the mid-1980s and achieved major commercial success in the late 1990s as a core component of nu metal.

History

Early milestones included Run-DMC's collaboration with Aerosmith on "Walk This Way" (1986), Anthrax's "I'm the Man" (1987) and the Beastie Boys' licensed-to-ill era crossovers. Rage Against the Machine, formed in 1991, fused rap vocals with heavy, funk-influenced riffing and political lyrics, becoming the genre's defining act. In the late 1990s, bands such as Limp Bizkit, Korn and Linkin Park brought rap metal and nu metal to the top of the charts.

Characteristics

Rap metal typically features a rapper fronting a live band rather than a DJ, down-tuned guitars, aggressive shouted or rapped verses, and choruses that may be sung. Lyrics often emphasise aggression, alienation or social protest.

Criticism and legacy

The genre was often derided by purists of both parent genres, and its mainstream popularity collapsed in the early 2000s. Nevertheless its influence persists in modern metal and in periodic revivals of nu metal aesthetics.
