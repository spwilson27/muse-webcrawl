Ali Farka Touré

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Ali Farka Touré (1939–2006)

Ali Ibrahim "Farka" Touré (31 October 1939 – 6 March 2006) was a Malian singer and multi-instrumentalist, and one of the African continent's most internationally renowned musicians. His music is widely regarded as representing a point of intersection of traditional Malian music and its North American counterpart, the blues, which he believed had originated in West Africa.

Touré was ranked number 76 on Rolling Stone's list of "The 100 Greatest Guitarists of All Time" and number 37 on Spin magazine's "100 Greatest Guitarists of All Time".

Early life

Touré was born in the village of Kanau, on the banks of the Niger River in the cercle of Gourma-Rharous in north-western Mali. His family belonged to the Songhai community and moved to Niafunké, in the Timbuktu region, when he was still young. He took up the guitar in the 1950s, inspired by Guinean guitarist Kanté Facély and by the ngoni-playing of his region.

Career

After independence, Touré joined the national cultural troupe of Mali and recorded for the Son Afric label in the 1970s. His international breakthrough came in the late 1980s with albums for World Circuit Records.

His 1994 album Talking Timbuktu, recorded with American guitarist Ry Cooder, won the Grammy Award for Best World Music Album. He won a second Grammy for In the Heart of the Moon (2005), a collaboration with kora player Toumani Diabaté, and a posthumous third for Ali and Toumani (2010).

Later life and farming

Touré was also a farmer and served as mayor of Niafunké, investing his earnings in irrigation and development for his village. He died of bone cancer in Bamako on 6 March 2006. His son Vieux Farka Touré is also a guitarist.
