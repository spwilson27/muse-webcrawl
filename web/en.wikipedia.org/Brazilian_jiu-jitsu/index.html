Brazilian jiu-jitsu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Practitioners sparring (rolling)

Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ) is a self-defence martial art and combat sport based on grappling, ground fighting, and submission holds. It focuses on taking an opponent to the ground, controlling them, and applying joint locks or chokeholds to force a submission. BJJ emphasises technique and leverage so that a smaller, weaker person can defend against a bigger, stronger assailant.

History

The art grew out of Japanese judo and jujutsu brought to Brazil in the early 20th century. Mitsuyo Maeda, a Japanese judoka, immigrated to Brazil and taught Carlos Gracie, whose family — most notably Hélio Gracie — refined the techniques with an emphasis on ground fighting. Members of the Gracie family issued open challenges (the "Gracie Challenge") to fighters of other styles for decades. In 1993, Royce Gracie's victories in the first Ultimate Fighting Championship events brought worldwide attention to the art and helped launch modern mixed martial arts.

Techniques

Training centres on positions such as guard, mount, side control and back control, and on transitions, escapes, sweeps and submissions including the rear naked choke, triangle choke, armbar and kimura. Live sparring, known as "rolling", is a core part of practice. Practitioners wear a heavy cotton uniform called a gi, though no-gi training is also common.

Competition

Sport BJJ tournaments, such as the World Jiu-Jitsu Championship organised by the IBJJF, award points for dominant positions and advantages, with an immediate win by submission. BJJ has become a foundational discipline in mixed martial arts.
