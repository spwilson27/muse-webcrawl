PyCon is an annual convention for the discussion and promotion of the Python programming language. The original PyCon, held in the United States, was founded in 2003 and is operated under the auspices of the Python Software Foundation (PSF), which took over organisation in 2008. It is notable among developer conferences for being community-driven, relatively inexpensive, and governed by a strong code of conduct.

PyCon US

The first PyCon US took place in Washington, D.C., in 2003, modelled partly on earlier Python conferences. It grew from a few hundred attendees to several thousand, moving among cities including Chicago, Atlanta, Portland, Cleveland, and Pittsburgh, with virtual editions during the COVID-19 pandemic. The programme includes talks, tutorials, lightning talks, poster sessions, a job fair, and development sprints, often including sprints on CPython itself. Python creator Guido van Rossum traditionally delivered a keynote during his tenure as benevolent dictator for life.

Other PyCons

Regional and national PyCons are held worldwide under licence from the PSF, including EuroPython (which predates the PyCon name), PyCon UK, PyCon Australia, PyCon India, and dozens of others. The name is also echoed by community events such as PyData conferences, which began as part of the PyCon ecosystem.
