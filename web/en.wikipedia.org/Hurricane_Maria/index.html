Hurricane Maria

Hurricane Maria
Formed: 16 September 2017; Dissipated: 3 October 2017
Peak: Category 5, 175 mph (280 km/h), 908 mbar
Areas: Lesser Antilles, Puerto Rico, Hispaniola, Bahamas, US East Coast
Damage: About $91.6 billion (2017 USD)
Fatalities: 3,059 estimated (mainly Puerto Rico)

Hurricane Maria was a deadly Category 5 Atlantic hurricane that devastated the northeastern Caribbean in September 2017, particularly Dominica, Saint Croix and Puerto Rico. It is regarded as the worst natural disaster in recorded history to affect those islands and was the deadliest storm of the hyperactive 2017 Atlantic hurricane season.

Meteorological history

Maria formed from a tropical wave east of the Lesser Antilles on 16 September 2017 and rapidly intensified, reaching Category 5 before making landfall on Dominica on 18 September. After weakening slightly it regained strength and struck Puerto Rico near Yabucoa on 20 September as a high-end Category 4 with 155 mph winds — the strongest to hit the island since 1928.

Impact

Dominica lost housing stock and infrastructure on a massive scale, with damage exceeding 200% of GDP. In Puerto Rico Maria destroyed the electrical grid, causing the largest blackout in US history; some areas lacked power for nearly a year. Flooding, landslides and the collapse of communications and healthcare contributed to a large excess death toll. A George Washington University study estimated 2,975 excess deaths in Puerto Rico, a figure the territorial government adopted.

Response and aftermath

The federal and territorial response was widely criticised as slow and inadequate, sparking political controversy in the United States. Recovery and grid reconstruction continued for years, and Maria accelerated out-migration from Puerto Rico to the mainland.

Retirement

The name Maria was retired by the World Meteorological Organization and replaced with Margot for future seasons.
