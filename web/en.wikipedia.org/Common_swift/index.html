Common swift

Common swift
Scientific name: Apus apus
Family: Apodidae
Wingspan: 38–40 cm
Range: breeds Europe and Asia; winters in Africa

The common swift (Apus apus) is a medium-sized bird superficially similar to a swallow but belonging to the order Apodiformes. It is among the most aerial of birds, spending months continuously airborne, and is famous for its screaming parties over summer rooftops.

Description

Common swifts are 16–17 cm long with sickle-shaped wings and an entirely sooty-brown plumage apart from a pale throat patch. Their tiny legs are used only for clinging to vertical surfaces; they cannot take off from flat ground easily and rarely land except at the nest.

Life in the air

Young swifts may not land at all for two or three years after fledging. They eat, drink, mate and even sleep on the wing, ascending at dusk to sleep in slow glides. Studies with geolocators have recorded individuals airborne for ten consecutive months.

Breeding and migration

Pairs nest in crevices in buildings and cliffs, laying two or three eggs. Birds breed across Europe and temperate Asia and migrate to sub-Saharan Africa for the northern winter.
