In Greek mythology, Persephone (Greek: Περσεφόνη), also called Kore ("the maiden"), is the daughter of Zeus and the harvest goddess Demeter, and the queen of the underworld as wife of Hades. Her myth explains the cycle of the seasons and stood at the heart of the Eleusinian Mysteries.

The abduction myth

While gathering flowers with her companions, Persephone was seized by Hades, who carried her in his chariot to the underworld to be his bride, with Zeus's tacit consent. The grieving Demeter searched the earth and, withholding her gifts, caused famine until Zeus intervened. Before her release, Persephone ate pomegranate seeds in the underworld — in many versions six seeds — binding her to return there for a portion of each year. Her annual descent and return were linked to winter and spring: when Persephone is below, Demeter mourns and nothing grows; when she returns, the earth blooms.

Cult and character

At Eleusis, Persephone and Demeter were worshipped in secret rites promising initiates a happier afterlife. As queen of the dead, Persephone appears in funerary contexts and in myths of descent such as those of Orpheus and Heracles; despite her fearsome role she could be merciful, and she is invoked in curse tablets and the Orphic gold leaves that guided souls in the afterlife.
