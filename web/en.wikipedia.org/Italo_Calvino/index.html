Italo Calvino (15 October 1923 – 19 September 1985) was an Italian writer and journalist. His best-known works include the Our Ancestors trilogy (1952–1959), the short story collection Cosmicomics (1965), and the novels Invisible Cities (1972) and If on a winter's night a traveler (1979).

Life

Calvino was born in Cuba to Italian parents and raised in Sanremo, Italy. He fought with the Italian Resistance during the Second World War, an experience reflected in his first novel, The Path to the Nest of Spiders (1947). He worked for many years for the Turin publishing house Einaudi and lived in Paris from 1967, where he joined the Oulipo group of experimental writers.

Style and legacy

Calvino's writing combines fable-like fantasy with philosophical reflection and playful formal experimentation, often described as postmodern. His posthumous Six Memos for the Next Millennium, based on lectures prepared for Harvard, is a celebrated statement of literary values. He is one of the most widely translated Italian authors of the twentieth century.
