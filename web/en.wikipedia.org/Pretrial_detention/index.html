Pretrial detention

Pretrial detention, also known as preventive detention or remand, is the process of detaining a person who has been arrested and charged with a crime but not yet tried or convicted. It is intended to ensure the accused appears at trial and to protect public safety.

Practice

Detention may follow a court hearing at which a judge sets conditions of release, which can include cash bail, electronic monitoring, or travel restrictions, or orders the defendant held without bail. Worldwide, roughly a third of all prisoners are pretrial detainees who have not been convicted of the charges against them.

Criticism and reform

Critics argue that cash-based systems detain people for poverty rather than risk, cause job and housing loss, and pressure defendants to plead guilty. Reform efforts in several jurisdictions have reduced reliance on money bail and expanded risk-assessment and supervised-release programs.
