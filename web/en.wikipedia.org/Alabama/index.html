Alabama

Alabama is a state in the Southeastern region of the United States, bordered by Tennessee, Georgia, Florida, Mississippi, and a short coastline on the Gulf of Mexico. It is the 30th largest state by area and the 24th most populous, with about 5.1 million residents. The capital is Montgomery, and the largest city by population is Huntsville; Birmingham is the largest metropolitan area. Alabama admitted to the Union in 1819 as the 22nd state.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Alabama, Choctaw, Chickasaw and Creek, inhabited the region for thousands of years. Spanish explorer Hernando de Soto traversed it in 1540; French colonists founded Mobile in 1702. Alabama became a centre of the cotton economy and slavery, joined the Confederacy in 1861, and played a central role in the American civil rights movement, including the Montgomery bus boycott of 1955–56 and the Selma to Montgomery marches of 1965.

Geography and economy

The terrain descends from the Appalachian foothills of the north to the coastal plain along the Gulf. Major rivers include the Alabama, Tennessee and Mobile. Historically agricultural, the state's economy now includes automotive manufacturing, aerospace (centred on NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville), steel, banking and healthcare.

Demographics and culture

Alabama is known as the "Heart of Dixie" and the Yellowhammer State. It has a rich musical heritage spanning blues, country and Southern rock, and its college football teams—the Alabama Crimson Tide and Auburn Tigers—command intense statewide loyalty.
