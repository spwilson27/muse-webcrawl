The Yokosuka E14Y (Allied reporting name "Glen") was a Japanese submarine-borne reconnaissance seaplane. It was designed to be carried aboard large Imperial Japanese Navy submarines and launched by catapult for scouting missions. The E14Y is notable as the only Axis aircraft to drop bombs on the continental United States during the Second World War.

Design and development

The E14Y was a low-wing monoplane floatplane with twin floats, powered by a Hitachi Tempu 12 nine-cylinder radial engine of about 340 horsepower. It could be disassembled and stored in a watertight hangar on the deck of a submarine, then assembled and launched in a matter of minutes. Its crew consisted of a pilot and an observer-gunner.

Operational history

E14Ys flew reconnaissance missions over Allied ports and coastlines, including Sydney, Melbourne, Wellington, and Pearl Harbor in preparation for follow-up attacks. In September 1942, an E14Y launched from the submarine I-25 and flown by Nobuo Fujita dropped incendiary bombs on forests near Brookings, Oregon, in the so-called Lookout Air Raids, the only aerial bombing of the continental United States by a foreign power during the war.

Specifications

The aircraft had a maximum speed of about 246 km/h, a range of roughly 880 km, and was armed with a single flexible 7.7 mm machine gun in the rear cockpit, with provision for small bombs.
