Multipole expansion

A multipole expansion is a mathematical series that represents a function depending on angles — usually the two angles of a sphere — and is used to approximate fields such as electric and gravitational potentials at distances large compared with the source.

The terms of the expansion are labelled by their order: the first (zero-order) term is the monopole, followed by the dipole, quadrupole, octupole, and higher terms, each falling off faster with distance than the last.

Electrostatics

For a charge distribution confined near the origin, the potential at a distant point can be expanded in powers of 1/r. The monopole term depends on the total charge, the dipole term on the electric dipole moment, and the quadrupole term on the distribution's quadrupole moment tensor. If the total charge vanishes, the leading behaviour of the field is dipolar; if the dipole moment also vanishes (as for a neutral atom with certain symmetries), the quadrupole dominates.

Applications

Multipole expansions underlie the description of molecular charge distributions in chemistry, antenna radiation patterns, geopotential models of the Earth, and the fast multipole method used to accelerate N-body calculations. Expansions in spherical harmonics give the angular form of the same idea and are used throughout physics, including the analysis of the cosmic microwave background.
