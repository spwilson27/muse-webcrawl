The New York City Police Department (NYPD), officially the City of New York Police Department, is the primary law enforcement agency within New York City. Established on May 23, 1845, the NYPD is one of the oldest municipal police departments in the United States and, by most measures, the largest local law enforcement agency in the country, with roughly 36,000 uniformed officers and about 19,000 civilian employees.

History

The NYPD was founded in 1845, replacing an older night-watch system, and was modelled in part on London's Metropolitan Police. Its early decades were marked by events such as the Draft Riots of 1863 and rivalry with the competing Metropolitan Police in the "police war" of 1857. Under commissioners such as Theodore Roosevelt (1895–1897) the department underwent reform. In the 1990s, under Mayor Rudolph Giuliani and Commissioner William Bratton, the department adopted the CompStat system of data-driven accountability and "broken windows" policing during a period of sharply falling citywide crime.

Organisation

The department is headed by the Police Commissioner, a civilian appointed by the Mayor, while the highest-ranking uniformed officer is the Chief of Department. The city is divided into precincts — 77 patrol precincts grouped into borough commands — plus specialised units including the Emergency Service Unit, the Harbor Unit, an aviation unit, highway patrol, transit and housing bureaus, detective squads and counterterrorism and intelligence divisions. Police officers are commonly called "New York's Finest".

Rank structure

Uniformed ranks ascend from police officer through detective grades, sergeant, lieutenant, captain, deputy inspector, inspector, and chief grades up to the Chief of Department. Detectives are investigators rather than supervisors, holding grades equivalent in pay to officers and sergeants.

In popular culture

The NYPD is among the most depicted police forces in the world, featured in television series such as Law & Order, NYPD Blue, Blue Bloods and Brooklyn Nine-Nine, and in countless films set in New York.
