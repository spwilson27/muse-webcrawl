Ideology

An ideology is a set of beliefs and values held by an individual, group, or society, especially a system of ideas that forms the basis of economic or political theory and policy. The term was coined in the 1790s by Antoine Destutt de Tracy for a "science of ideas".

History and usage

Under Napoleon, "ideologue" became a term of abuse, and in Marxist thought ideology came to denote ideas that obscure or legitimise relations of domination — a "false consciousness". In neutral social-scientific usage, an ideology is simply a patterned set of political beliefs, such as liberalism, conservatism, socialism, or nationalism.

Analysis

Scholars study how ideologies structure political competition, justify institutions, and shape identity. Political scientists measure ideological positions along spectra such as left–right, while critics debate whether ostensibly post-ideological or technocratic politics merely conceals ideological commitments.
