The University of Rhode Island (URI) is a public land-grant research university with its main campus in Kingston, Rhode Island, United States. It is the flagship public research university of the state of Rhode Island and the state's only public research university.

History

The university was founded in 1892 as the Rhode Island College of Agriculture and the Mechanic Arts, established under the Morrill Land-Grant Acts with funds from the sale of federal land. Its first class consisted of 17 students. In 1909 the institution was renamed Rhode Island State College, and in 1951 it took its current name, the University of Rhode Island.

Campuses

The main Kingston Campus occupies about 1,200 acres in the village of Kingston in South Kingstown. Its historic central quadrangle is surrounded by granite buildings in the Colonial Revival style. The university also operates the Narragansett Bay Campus, home to the Graduate School of Oceanography, the Feinstein Campus in Providence, and the W. Alton Jones Campus in West Greenwich.

Academics

URI offers bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees through colleges including Arts and Sciences, Engineering, Business, Health Sciences, Pharmacy, Nursing, Environment and Life Sciences, and Education and Professional Studies. The Graduate School of Oceanography, founded in 1961, is one of the leading oceanographic institutions in the United States.

Athletics

The university's athletic teams are the Rhode Island Rams, competing in NCAA Division I, primarily in the Atlantic 10 Conference, with football in the Coastal Athletic Association. The men's basketball team plays at the Ryan Center.
