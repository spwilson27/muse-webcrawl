Celje (pronounced [ˈtsɛ̀ːljɛ]; German: Cilli) is the fourth-largest city in Slovenia, located in the Styria region at the confluence of the Savinja and Voglajna rivers, beneath the medieval Celje Castle.

History

The site was the Celtic and Roman town of Celeia, a prosperous municipium under the Romans. In the Middle Ages the town became the seat of the Counts of Celje, the most powerful noble dynasty on Slovenian territory; in 1451 the counts received princely status, but the male line died out with the murder of Ulrich II in 1456, after which the town passed to the Habsburgs.

Landmarks

Celje Castle, first built in the 12th–13th centuries and expanded by the Counts, overlooks the old town, which preserves a medieval street plan, the Celje Cathedral (St. Daniel's), and the Water Tower. The Regional Museum holds the rich Roman remains of Celeia, displayed in part beneath the modern city.

Modern city

Celje is an industrial and trade-fair centre, Slovenia's principal fair city, and home to the handball club RK Celje and the football club NK Celje.
