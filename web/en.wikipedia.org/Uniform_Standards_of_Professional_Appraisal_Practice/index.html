Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice

Appraisers in the United States must comply with USPAP for most federally related transactions.

The Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP) is the generally recognized ethical and performance standard for the appraisal profession in the United States. First adopted in 1989 by the Appraisal Foundation, USPAP is updated periodically (generally every two years) by the Foundation's Appraisal Standards Board.

Background

USPAP was created in response to the savings and loan crisis of the 1980s. Under Title XI of the Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act of 1989 (FIRREA), state-certified and state-licensed appraisers must comply with USPAP when performing appraisals for federally related transactions.

Contents

USPAP includes an Ethics Rule, a Record Keeping Rule, a Competency Rule, and a Scope of Work Rule, together with standards for real property appraisal and reporting (Standards 1 and 2), appraisal review (Standard 3), and other appraisal disciplines including mass appraisal, personal property, and business valuation.

Enforcement

Compliance is enforced by state appraisal licensing boards, which may discipline appraisers for violations. Federal banking regulators also require compliance for appraisals used in federally related transactions.
