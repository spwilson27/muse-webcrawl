Weddell Gyre

The Weddell Gyre is one of the two major gyres of the Southern Ocean, a large clockwise-rotating (cyclonic) circulation in the Weddell Sea between the Antarctic Peninsula and Coats Land, Antarctica. It is bounded to the north by the Antarctic Circumpolar Current and interacts with the smaller Ross Gyre to the east of the Ross Sea.

Circulation

The gyre is driven primarily by wind stress curl over the southwestern Atlantic sector of the Southern Ocean, combined with the region's topography. Its western boundary current flows northward along the eastern side of the Antarctic Peninsula, while the return flow brings relatively warm Circumpolar Deep Water southward at the gyre's eastern side. Volume transport is estimated in the tens of Sverdrups, varying seasonally.

Role in the global ocean

The Weddell Gyre is a key site of deep and bottom water formation: on the continental shelf of the southern Weddell Sea, brine rejected during sea-ice formation produces dense High Salinity Shelf Water that mixes and descends to form Antarctic Bottom Water, the densest water mass in the world ocean. The gyre stores and releases heat and carbon, transports sea ice, and influences ice-shelf melting—notably through warm water reaching the Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf cavity—making it an intensively studied component of the climate system.
