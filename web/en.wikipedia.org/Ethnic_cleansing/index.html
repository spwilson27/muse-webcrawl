Ethnic cleansing

Ethnic cleansing is the systematic forced removal of ethnic, racial or religious groups from a given area, with the intent of making the region ethnically homogeneous. It is accomplished through deportation, displacement, mass killing, intimidation and destruction of cultural and religious sites. The term gained widespread use during the Yugoslav Wars of the 1990s.

Definition and law

Ethnic cleansing is not defined as a distinct crime in international law, but its constituent acts — deportation, persecution, murder — are crimes against humanity and war crimes, and it may overlap with genocide when the intent is to destroy a group in whole or in part. The United Nations Commission of Experts on the former Yugoslavia defined it as rendering an area ethnically homogeneous by using force or intimidation to remove persons of given groups.

Historical examples

Examples frequently cited by historians include the expulsion of Germans from Eastern Europe after the Second World War, the population exchanges and expulsions surrounding the Partition of India in 1947, the Armenian genocide in the Ottoman Empire, the displacement of Palestinians in 1948, the Bosnian War (1992–1995) including the Srebrenica massacre, and the expulsion of Rohingya from Myanmar in 2017. Scholars debate the boundaries of the term for each case.

Methods and consequences

Methods range from discriminatory laws and property confiscation to terror and massacres designed to make return impossible. Consequences include refugee crises, intergenerational trauma and long-running territorial disputes.
