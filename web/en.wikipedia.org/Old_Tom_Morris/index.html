Thomas Mitchell Morris (16 June 1821 – 24 May 1908), otherwise known as Old Tom Morris, was a Scottish golfer and a pioneer of the modern game. Born in St Andrews, he was a four-time winner of The Open Championship and served as greenkeeper and professional at St Andrews Links for decades.

Career

Morris apprenticed as a ball maker under Allan Robertson, generally regarded as the first professional golfer, before moving to Prestwick, where he laid out the course and won the first of his Open titles in 1861. In 1867, at age 46, he became the oldest winner of the Open, a record that still stands. His son, Young Tom Morris, won the championship four consecutive times.

Course design and legacy

Morris designed or remodelled many famous links, including Muirfield, Royal Dornoch, and Lahinch. He introduced concepts such as separate teeing areas and managed greens with topdressing. He was inducted into the World Golf Hall of Fame in 1976.
