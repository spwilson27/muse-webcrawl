Usedom

The pier at Heringsdorf on Usedom.

Usedom (Polish: Uznam) is a Baltic Sea island in Pomerania, shared since 1945 between Germany and Poland. It is separated from the mainland by the Peenestrom and the Szczecin Lagoon, and borders the island of Wolin to the east. With an area of 445 square kilometres (of which 373 are in Germany), it is the second-largest German island after Rügen.

History

The island takes its name from the town of Usedom, derived from a Slavic word meaning "at the mouth (of a river)." During the Second World War the island was the site of the Peenemünde Army Research Center, where the V-2 rocket was developed under Wernher von Braun; the site is now a museum. After 1945 the eastern part, including the town of Świnoujście, was assigned to Poland, while the west remained German.

Tourism

Usedom is one of Germany's sunniest regions and a major Baltic resort destination, famed for the "Kaiserbäder" (Imperial spas) of Ahlbeck, Heringsdorf and Bansin, with their long piers and white resort architecture, as well as the resorts of Zinnowitz and Trassenheide.
