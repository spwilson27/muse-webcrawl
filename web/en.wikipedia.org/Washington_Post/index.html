The Washington Post, locally known as the Post and abbreviated WaPo, is an American daily newspaper published in Washington, D.C. Founded in 1877, it is the most widely circulated newspaper in the Washington metropolitan area and one of the country's leading national newspapers. It has won dozens of Pulitzer Prizes, including for its investigation of the Watergate scandal.

History

The paper was founded by Stilson Hutchins in 1877. It passed through several owners before the financier Eugene Meyer bought it at a bankruptcy auction in 1933. Under Meyer and his daughter Katharine Graham, who became publisher in 1963, the Post rose to national prominence. In 1971 it co-published the Pentagon Papers, leading to a landmark Supreme Court decision on press freedom.

In 1972–74, reporters Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein, guided by an anonymous source known as "Deep Throat" (later revealed to be FBI official Mark Felt) and backed by editor Ben Bradlee and Graham, uncovered the Watergate scandal, contributing to President Richard Nixon's resignation. The episode, dramatised in All the President's Men, cemented the paper's reputation for investigative reporting.

Bezos era

In 2013 Jeff Bezos, founder of Amazon, purchased the paper for $250 million through Nash Holdings, ending 80 years of Graham family ownership. The Post expanded its digital operation and national and international readership, adopting a subscription-focused model and, in 2017, the slogan "Democracy Dies in Darkness".
