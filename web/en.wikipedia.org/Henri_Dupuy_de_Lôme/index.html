Henri Dupuy de Lôme

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stanislas Charles Henri Laurent Dupuy de Lôme (15 October 1816 – 1 February 1885) was a French naval architect. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern naval engineering, responsible for the first steam-powered ship of the line, the first seagoing ironclad warship, a pioneering dirigible airship, and early work on submarines and gyrocompasses.

Early life and career

Dupuy de Lôme was born at Ploemeur near Lorient in Brittany, the son of a naval officer. Educated at the École Polytechnique, he joined the corps of naval engineers and after study in England — where he observed iron shipbuilding and screw propulsion — published an influential 1844 report advocating iron hulls and steam power for the French navy.

Major designs

His first triumph was the Napoléon (launched 1850), the first warship designed from the keel up with a steam engine and screw propeller; although wooden-hulled and fully rigged, she made 14 knots and set the pattern for steam ships of the line worldwide. In 1857 he became director of construction and produced the Gloire (launched 1859), the world's first seagoing ironclad: a wooden hull sheathed in 120 mm wrought-iron armour, which at a stroke rendered existing wooden navies obsolete and triggered the ironclad building race with Britain.

During the Franco-Prussian War he worked on aerial projects and in 1872 flew a human-powered navigable balloon (dirigible). Late in life he collaborated on the electric submarine Gymnote and an experimental gyrocompass.

Political career and honours

Dupuy de Lôme was elected to the Chamber of Deputies in 1869 and became a life senator in 1877. He was a member of the French Academy of Sciences. A French armoured cruiser and later vessels bore his name; the French Navy's intelligence-collection ship Dupuy-de-Lôme (A 759), commissioned in 2006, continues the honour.
