Mann–Whitney U test

The Mann–Whitney U test (also called the Mann–Whitney–Wilcoxon test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test) is a nonparametric test of the null hypothesis that, for randomly selected values X and Y from two populations, the probability that X exceeds Y equals the probability that Y exceeds X. It is the nonparametric alternative to the independent samples t-test and does not assume a normal distribution.

Procedure

All observations from both samples are ranked together, ties receiving average ranks. The U statistic is computed from the sum of the ranks for one sample: U = R − n(n+1)/2, where R is that sample's rank sum and n its size. Large and small values of U lead to rejection of the null hypothesis. The test is widely used for ordinal data and for small or non-normal samples in medicine, psychology, and the social sciences.

Assumptions and interpretation

The observations must be independent and at least ordinal. Strictly, the test detects stochastic dominance; it is a test of equal medians only under the additional assumption that the two distributions have the same shape. Its asymptotic relative efficiency compared with the t-test is 3/π (about 0.955) for normal data.
