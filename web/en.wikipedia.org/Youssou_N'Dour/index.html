Youssou N'Dour (born 1 October 1959) is a Senegalese singer, songwriter, composer, occasional actor, businessman, and politician. He is widely regarded as one of the most celebrated African musicians in history and is credited with popularising mbalax, the vibrant Senegalese dance music that blends traditional Wolof percussion (especially the sabar) with influences from Afro-Cuban music, jazz, and rock.

Career

Born in Dakar into a family of griots on his mother's side, N'Dour began singing as a child and by his teens was performing with the Star Band. In 1979 he formed Étoile de Dakar, and then Super Étoile, developing the mbalax sound. International recognition followed in the 1980s through collaborations with Peter Gabriel, whose WOMAD festival and Real World label brought him to global audiences. His 1994 duet with Neneh Cherry, "7 Seconds", was an international hit, and his album Egypt (2004), celebrating his Sufi Muslim faith, won the Grammy Award for Best Contemporary World Music Album.

Business and politics

In Senegal, N'Dour owns a recording studio, a record label, a nightclub, and the media group Futurs Médias, which includes the newspaper L'Observateur and television and radio outlets. After campaigning against a third term for President Abdoulaye Wade, he sought the presidency himself in 2012 but was barred from the ballot; he then backed Macky Sall and was appointed Minister of Tourism and Culture (later Tourism and Leisure) in Sall's government from 2012 to 2013.
