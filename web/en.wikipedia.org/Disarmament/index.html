Disarmament

Disarmament is the act of reducing, limiting, or abolishing weapons. It can refer to a unilateral act by one state or to negotiated agreements between states restricting the numbers, types, or deployment of arms. The term is most often used in connection with weapons of mass destruction, but it also covers conventional forces, landmines, and small arms.

History

Early efforts included the Hague Conferences of 1899 and 1907, the Washington Naval Treaty of 1922 limiting capital ship tonnage, and League of Nations discussions in the 1920s and 1930s. After the Second World War, disarmament became a central theme of the United Nations; landmark treaties included the Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963), the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968), the Biological Weapons Convention (1972), the Chemical Weapons Convention (1993), and strategic arms agreements between the United States and Russia such as START.

Categories and challenges

Disarmament is divided into nuclear, chemical, biological, and conventional categories, together with regional measures such as nuclear-weapon-free zones. Negotiated reductions face the verification problem — ensuring compliance without compromising national security — addressed through inspections by bodies such as the IAEA and the OPCW. Unilateral disarmament, by contrast, is sometimes advocated on moral or economic grounds but remains rare in practice.
