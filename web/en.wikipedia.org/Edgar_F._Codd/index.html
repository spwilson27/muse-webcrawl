Edgar F. Codd

Edgar Frank "Ted" Codd (19 August 1923 – 18 April 2003) was an English computer scientist who, while working for IBM, invented the relational model for database management, the theoretical basis of relational databases and the query language SQL. His 1970 paper "A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks" is regarded as one of the foundational works of computer science.

Life and career

Codd studied mathematics and chemistry at Exeter College, Oxford, served as an RAF pilot during the Second World War, and moved to the United States in 1948. He joined IBM in 1949 and later earned his PhD from the University of Michigan. Working at IBM's San Jose Research Laboratory, he published the relational model in 1970, partly out of frustration with the navigational database systems of the day.

The relational model

Codd proposed that data be organised into relations (tables) of tuples, manipulated by a relational algebra with a firm basis in predicate logic. He defined the concept of normal forms to eliminate redundancy and formulated Codd's twelve rules defining a fully relational database management system. IBM was slow to commercialise the model, and rival products based on his ideas appeared first, but IBM's DB2 and the SQL standard ultimately made relational databases dominant.

Honours

Codd received the Turing Award in 1981 for his fundamental and continuing contributions to the theory and practice of database management systems.
