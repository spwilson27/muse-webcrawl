The Women's Boat Race

The Women's Boat Race is an annual set of rowing races between the Cambridge University Women's Boat Club and the Oxford University Women's Boat Club. It was first held in 1927, nearly a century after the first men's Boat Race. Since 2015 the race has been held on the Championship Course on the River Thames in London, on the same day and over the same course as the men's race.

History

The first women's race took place in Oxford in 1927, though for its early decades the event was sporadic and, at first, judged partly on style rather than solely on finishing order. Between the 1930s and 1970s the race alternated between the River Cam in Cambridge and the Isis in Oxford. In 1977 the race moved to Henley-on-Thames, where it formed part of the Henley Boat Races for nearly four decades.

Move to the Tideway

Following a sponsorship deal with Newton Investment Management, it was announced that the Women's Boat Race would move to the Championship Course in London from 2015, racing on the same day as the men's event. The move greatly increased the race's profile and television audience. Cambridge dominated the early Tideway years, including a record-breaking victory in 2017 when Oxford's crew suffered a disastrous start.

Overall record

As of the mid-2020s, Cambridge leads the overall series by a comfortable margin, having won the large majority of races since the move to London, though Oxford has won multiple titles including in the pre-Tideway era.
