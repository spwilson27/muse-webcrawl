Champaran Satyagraha

The Champaran Satyagraha of 1917 was a peasant uprising and civil disobedience movement in the Champaran district of Bihar, India, and Mahatma Gandhi's first satyagraha movement in India.

Background

Under the tinkathia system, tenant farmers were forced to grow indigo on 15% of their land for European planters at fixed, low prices, despite the collapse of indigo demand after synthetic dyes appeared. Local leader Raj Kumar Shukla persuaded Gandhi to come to Champaran.

The movement and outcome

Gandhi and a team including Rajendra Prasad documented thousands of peasant grievances. Ordered to leave, Gandhi refused and pleaded guilty, turning the trial into a moral victory. The government appointed an inquiry committee including Gandhi, and the resulting Champaran Agrarian Act abolished tinkathia and granted partial refunds.
