Uintah County is a county in the U.S. state of Utah, located in the state's northeastern corner in the Uinta Basin, bordering Colorado to the east. As of the 2020 census, the population was 35,620. The county seat and largest city is Vernal. The county is named for the Uintah band of the Ute people who inhabited the region.

History

The area was home to the Ute people for centuries. In 1861 President Abraham Lincoln established the Uintah Valley Reservation for the Ute by executive order, and in 1868 a treaty confirmed the reservation; the reservation's successor, the Uintah and Ouray Indian Reservation, occupies much of the basin and is the second-largest Indian reservation in the United States. The reservation was opened to non-Native settlement in the early 1900s after allotment.

Uintah County was created by the Utah territorial legislature in 1880. Ashley Valley, where Vernal now stands, was settled by Mormon pioneers beginning in the late 1870s. The county's early economy was based on farming, ranching and freighting to the mines and military posts of the region.

Economy

The Uinta Basin is rich in energy resources, and oil and natural gas production, along with mining of gilsonite (a form of asphaltite unique to the region, first commercialised here in the 1880s), phosphate and oil shale deposits, has driven repeated boom-and-bust cycles in the county. Agriculture, government services and tourism are also important.

Uintah County is also known for its paleontological significance: the eastern part of the county contains portions of Dinosaur National Monument, famous for its quarry of Jurassic dinosaur fossils near Jensen. The region around Vernal markets itself as "Dinosaurland".

Geography

The county covers about 4,501 square miles of high desert basin country between the Uinta Mountains to the north and the Tavaputs Plateau (Book Cliffs) to the south. The Green River flows through the eastern part of the county. Communities include Vernal, Maeser, Naples, Ballard, Fort Duchesne (the headquarters of the Ute Indian Tribe), Jensen, Lapoint and Tridell.

Demographics

As of recent censuses the county's population is roughly four-fifths non-Hispanic white, with a significant Native American population (largely members of the Ute Indian Tribe) and a growing Hispanic population.
