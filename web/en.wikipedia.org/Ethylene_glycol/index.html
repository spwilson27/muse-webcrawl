Ethylene glycol

Ethylene glycol (IUPAC name ethane-1,2-diol) is an organic compound with formula (CH2OH)2. It is a colourless, viscous, sweet-tasting liquid, mainly used as a raw material for polyester fibres and as an antifreeze in vehicles.

Production

Ethylene glycol is produced from ethylene via the intermediate ethylene oxide, which is hydrated thermally or catalytically. Global production is tens of millions of tonnes per year. Alternative routes from synthesis gas and, experimentally, from biomass are also used.

Uses

Roughly half of production goes to antifreeze and coolant formulations, where it lowers the freezing point and raises the boiling point of water. The other major use is polymerisation with terephthalic acid to make polyethylene terephthalate (PET) for bottles and polyester textiles. Minor uses include de-icing fluids, hydraulic fluids and solvents.

Toxicity

Ethylene glycol is poisonous: metabolism by alcohol dehydrogenase produces glycolic and oxalic acids, causing metabolic acidosis and kidney failure. Treatment uses fomepizole or ethanol and haemodialysis. Its sweet taste makes accidental poisoning of children and animals a recognised hazard, leading some manufacturers to add bittering agents.
