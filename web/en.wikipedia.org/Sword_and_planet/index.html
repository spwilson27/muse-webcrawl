Sword and planet

Image placeholder: classic sword-and-planet pulp artwork.

Sword and planet is a subgenre of science fiction and science fantasy in which the hero, usually transported from Earth to an alien world, has sword-and-sorcery-style adventures in a setting with advanced elements such as airships, ray guns or psychic powers mixed with primitive weapons and societies. The stories are essentially planetary romances with an emphasis on swashbuckling action.

Origins

The genre was founded by Edgar Rice Burroughs with A Princess of Mars (1912), in which Civil War veteran John Carter is transported to a dying, heroic Mars ("Barsoom") and wins fame with sword and courage. Burroughs's success spawned decades of imitations in pulp magazines and paperback series.

Examples and influence

Notable practitioners include Leigh Brackett (the Eric John Stark stories set on Mars and Venus), Michael Moorcock (writing as Edward P. Bradbury, the Kane of Old Mars trilogy), Lin Carter, and Marion Zimmer Bradley, whose Darkover books blend psi powers with a feudal planet. The genre influenced later space opera and works such as Star Wars. It is closely related to its sister genre sword and sorcery.
