The Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) is an American professional association and standards organisation for electrical engineering, electronics engineering, computer science, and related disciplines. It describes itself as "the world's largest technical professional organization dedicated to advancing technology for the benefit of humanity," with more than 400,000 members in over 160 countries.

History

The IEEE was formed in 1963 by the merger of the American Institute of Electrical Engineers (AIEE, founded 1884) and the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE, founded 1912). Its emblem combines the IRE's triangle and the AIEE's kite-shaped emblem of Benjamin Franklin's electricity experiments.

Activities

The IEEE publishes a large share of the world's technical literature in its fields—millions of documents in the IEEE Xplore digital library, including journals such as Proceedings of the IEEE and the trade magazine IEEE Spectrum. It sponsors more than a thousand conferences annually and, through the IEEE Standards Association, develops widely used standards, most famously the IEEE 802 family for local area networks (including Ethernet 802.3 and Wi-Fi 802.11) and the IEEE 754 standard for floating-point arithmetic. The organisation is structured into ten geographic regions, dozens of technical societies (such as the Computer Society and Power & Energy Society), and student branches at universities worldwide. It confers honours including the IEEE Medal of Honor.
