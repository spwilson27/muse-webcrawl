Peace of Travendal

The Peace of Travendal was a peace treaty concluded at Traventhal (Travendal) in Holstein on 18 August 1700 between Denmark–Norway and Holstein-Gottorp, under pressure from Sweden and the Maritime Powers, at the opening of the Great Northern War.

Background

Denmark attacked Holstein-Gottorp, whose duke was allied by marriage to Charles XII of Sweden, while Swedish forces were engaged against Saxony and Russia. A Swedish army landed on Zealand with English and Dutch naval support and threatened Copenhagen, compelling Denmark to negotiate.

Terms and consequences

Denmark recognised the sovereignty of Holstein-Gottorp, agreed to pay compensation, and withdrew from the anti-Swedish coalition. The settlement freed Charles XII to turn east against Russia, whom he crushed at Narva that November. Denmark remained out of the war until 1709, rejoining after Sweden's defeat at Poltava.
