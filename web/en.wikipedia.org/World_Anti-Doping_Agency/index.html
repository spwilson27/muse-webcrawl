The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) is a foundation initiated by the International Olympic Committee to promote, coordinate and monitor the fight against drugs in sports. Established in Lausanne in 1999 following the Festina scandal and the first World Conference on Doping in Sport, it is funded equally by the Olympic movement and governments of the world, and is headquartered in Montreal, Canada.

World Anti-Doping Code

WADA's central instrument is the World Anti-Doping Code, first adopted in 2003 and subsequently revised, which harmonises anti-doping rules across sports and countries. Under the Code, WADA publishes the annual Prohibited List of banned substances and methods, accredits testing laboratories, and oversees systems such as the Athlete Biological Passport and whereabouts reporting for out-of-competition testing.

Governance and controversies

WADA is governed by a Foundation Board and Executive Committee drawn equally from sports bodies and governments. The agency has been central to major doping cases, including the investigation of state-sponsored doping in Russia, which led to sanctions against Russian sport from 2016 onward, and the BALCO and Armstrong affairs in the United States.
