Morihei Ueshiba

Morihei Ueshiba (植芝 盛平)

Born: 14 December 1883, Tanabe, Wakayama, Japan
Died: 26 April 1969, Iwama, Ibaraki, Japan
Known for: Founder of Aikido
Style: Aikido (from Daitō-ryū Aiki-jūjutsu)

Morihei Ueshiba (植芝 盛平, December 14, 1883 – April 26, 1969) was a Japanese martial artist and the founder of the martial art of aikido. He is often referred to by aikido practitioners as "the Founder" (Kaiso) or Ōsensei ("Great Teacher").

Early life and training

Ueshiba was born in Tanabe, Wakayama Prefecture. A sickly child, he took up sumo and swimming, and later studied several martial arts, including jujutsu and swordsmanship, and served in the army during the Russo-Japanese War. In 1912 he moved to Hokkaido as part of a settlement group, where he met the Daitō-ryū Aiki-jūjutsu master Takeda Sōkaku, whose art became the principal technical foundation of aikido.

Spiritual development and aikido

From 1920 Ueshiba became deeply involved with the Ōmoto-kyō religion under Deguchi Onisaburō, accompanying him on an ill-fated 1924 expedition to Mongolia. Ueshiba's martial teaching gradually shifted from the hard techniques of Daitō-ryū toward a philosophy of harmony and reconciliation; he named the art "aikido" (the way of harmonising energy) around 1942. He established his headquarters dojo in Tokyo (the Kobukan, later the Aikikai Hombu Dojo) and a retreat and shrine complex at Iwama.

Legacy

After World War II, Ueshiba's students spread aikido worldwide. He taught that "the way of the warrior is to manifest divine love" and that aikido was a means of resolving conflict without destruction. He died in 1969 and was succeeded as head of the Aikikai by his son Kisshomaru Ueshiba. The Japanese government decorated him with the Medal of Honor with Purple Ribbon and posthumously with the Order of the Sacred Treasure.
