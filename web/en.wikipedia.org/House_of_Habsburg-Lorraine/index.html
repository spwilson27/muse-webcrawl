House of Habsburg-Lorraine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The House of Habsburg-Lorraine (German: Haus Habsburg-Lothringen) is the name of the dynastic house established by the marriage in 1736 of Maria Theresa of Austria, heiress of the House of Habsburg, to Francis Stephen, Duke of Lorraine. It ruled the Habsburg monarchy until its dissolution in 1918.

Founded: 1736
Founder: Francis I and Maria Theresa
Titles: Holy Roman Emperor (to 1806); Emperor of Austria; King of Hungary; King of Bohemia
Last ruler: Charles I of Austria
Deposition: 1918

Origins

When Emperor Charles VI died in 1740 without a male heir, his daughter Maria Theresa succeeded to the Habsburg hereditary lands under the Pragmatic Sanction of 1713. Her husband, Francis Stephen of Lorraine, was elected Holy Roman Emperor as Francis I in 1745, and their descendants bore the double name Habsburg-Lorraine, joining the two houses.

Rule

The house produced the emperors Joseph II, Leopold II, Francis II (later Francis I of Austria), Ferdinand I, Franz Joseph I and Charles I. Its members also ruled as grand dukes of Tuscany (a secundogeniture line), dukes of Modena (the Austria-Este branch), and, briefly, as Emperor Maximilian I of Mexico (executed 1867) and as Empress Marie Louise, consort of Napoleon.

End of the monarchy

Defeat in the First World War dissolved the Austro-Hungarian Empire. Charles I renounced participation in government in November 1918, and the Austrian republic’s Habsburg Law of 1919 exiled the family and confiscated its property. The current head of the house is Karl von Habsburg, grandson of Charles I.
