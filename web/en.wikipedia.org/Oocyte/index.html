Oocyte

An oocyte (UK: /ˈoʊəsaɪt/, US: /ˈoʊsaɪt/) is a female germ cell or gametocyte involved in reproduction. In other words, it is an immature ovum, or egg cell. An oocyte is produced in the ovary during female gametogenesis (oogenesis). The female germ cells produce a primordial germ cell (PGC), which then undergoes mitosis, forming oogonia. During oogenesis, the oogonia become primary oocytes.

Development in mammals

In humans, oogonia proliferate before birth, so that a female is born with essentially all the primary oocytes she will ever have — on the order of one to two million, declining to a few hundred thousand by puberty. Each primary oocyte is arrested in prophase of meiosis I (the dictyate stage) within a primordial follicle, and may remain arrested for decades. At each menstrual cycle after puberty, a small cohort of follicles resumes growth; typically one oocyte completes meiosis I, producing a large secondary oocyte and a small first polar body. The secondary oocyte begins meiosis II but arrests at metaphase II, and is released at ovulation; it completes meiosis only if fertilised by a sperm cell.

Structure

The oocyte is among the largest cells in the human body, about 0.1 mm in diameter and visible to the naked eye. It is surrounded by a glycoprotein coat, the zona pellucida, and by layers of supporting granulosa (cumulus) cells. Its cytoplasm — the ooplasm — contains yolk granules (modest in humans), mitochondria (inherited exclusively from the mother by offspring), ribosomes, and maternal messenger RNAs and proteins that direct the earliest stages of embryonic development before the embryonic genome activates.

Clinical significance

Oocyte quality and quantity decline with age, in part because errors in meiosis accumulate, increasing the risk of aneuploidy such as trisomy 21. In assisted reproduction, oocytes are retrieved after ovarian stimulation for in vitro fertilisation (IVF) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI); mature oocytes can also be cryopreserved by vitrification for fertility preservation or egg donation.
