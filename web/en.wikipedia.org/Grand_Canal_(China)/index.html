Grand Canal (China)

LengthAbout 1,776 km (1,104 mi) — the world's longest artificial waterway
TerminiBeijing (north) to Hangzhou (south)
Begun5th century BC (earliest sections)

The Grand Canal of China (Chinese: 大运河, Dà Yùnhé; the Beijing–Hangzhou Grand Canal is 京杭大运河) is the longest canal in the world, running roughly 1,776 kilometres from Beijing in the north to Hangzhou in the south. By linking the Yellow River and Yangtze River basins, it bound the agricultural south to the political capitals of the north and served for over a millennium as the empire's economic lifeline, carrying tax grain, salt and commerce.

History

The earliest sections were dug in the late Spring and Autumn period — the Hangou canal of 486 BC connected the Yangtze to the Huai. The Sui dynasty (581–618) unified and massively extended the network under Emperor Yang, mobilising millions of labourers to create the system that supplied Luoyang and Chang'an; the cost in lives contributed to the dynasty's fall. The Yuan dynasty (1271–1368) straightened the route to its new capital at Beijing across the Shandong summit, while the Ming and Qing maintained it through a vast administration of dredging, dikes and granaries.

Engineering

The canal crosses five major river systems and climbs over the Shandong hills by a staircase of locks. China pioneered the pound lock here: a lock with gates at both ends was built in AD 984 by the transport commissioner Qiao Weiyue, replacing dangerous slipways and double slipways. Flash locks, spillways and feeder lakes regulated water levels along the summit section.

Decline and revival

The canal declined in the 19th century as the Yellow River shifted course catastrophically in 1855, sea transport and railways undercut barge traffic, and silt went undredged; the northern sections dried up. The People's Republic restored navigation on the southern sections for coal and bulk cargo, and the canal forms the central route of the eastern line of the South–North Water Transfer Project. UNESCO inscribed it as a World Heritage Site in 2014.
