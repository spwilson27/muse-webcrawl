Weimar Constitution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Weimar Constitution (German: Weimarer Verfassung) was the constitution that governed Germany during the Weimar Republic era (1919–1933). It was signed by President Friedrich Ebert on 11 August 1919 and took effect on 14 August. Drafted principally by the liberal jurist Hugo Preuss and adopted by the National Assembly meeting in Weimar, it established Germany as a democratic, federal, parliamentary republic with universal suffrage — including, for the first time, women's suffrage.

Structure and provisions

The constitution created a bicameral legislature — the Reichstag, elected by proportional representation, and the Reichsrat representing the states (Länder) — and a directly elected Reich President serving seven-year terms. Its second part contained an extensive catalogue of fundamental rights, including equality before the law, freedom of speech and religion, and social rights to work and education. It also provided for referendums and popular initiatives.

Article 48 and weaknesses

The notorious Article 48 allowed the President, "if public security and order are seriously disturbed or endangered", to take necessary measures including rule by emergency decree and suspension of basic rights. Intended as a safeguard, it was used repeatedly by Ebert and, after 1930, enabled Presidents Hindenburg and Chancellor Brüning to govern without parliament. Pure proportional representation encouraged a fragmented party system, and the constitution lacked defences against anti-democratic parties.

End and legacy

The constitution was never formally abolished by the Nazi regime; instead it was hollowed out by the Reichstag Fire Decree (1933) and the Enabling Act, which allowed Hitler's government to legislate contrary to it. After 1945, the Basic Law of 1949 retained many Weimar features — federalism, strong basic rights, a constitutional court — while correcting its weaknesses with devices such as the constructive vote of no confidence and the five-percent electoral threshold.
