A fouled anchor is an anchor that has become entangled ("fouled") with its own rope or chain. As a symbol, the fouled anchor — with a length of rope or cable wrapped around the shank and flukes — is one of the best-known emblems of naval service, appearing on cap devices, badges, and insignia in several navies, most prominently that of the United States Navy chief petty officer.

Symbolism

In heraldry and naval tradition, the fouled anchor dates back centuries; a fouled anchor appears on the seal of the Lord High Admiral of Great Britain. The motif is often interpreted as representing the trials and tribulations of naval life, seamanship under adversity, or simply an unmistakable piece of nautical imagery. In practical seamanship, a fouled anchor is a genuine hazard: an anchor fouled by its own cable may fail to set properly or may be difficult to weigh.

Use in insignia

The United States Navy's chief petty officer anchor device, adopted in the 1890s, features a fouled anchor with the letters "USN". In the Royal Navy and Commonwealth navies, fouled anchors also appear in the insignia of senior rates and on the flags of naval officers.
