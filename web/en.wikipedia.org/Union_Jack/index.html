Union Jack

Union Jack
Use: National flag of the United Kingdom
Adopted: 1 January 1801 (current version)
Design: Crosses of St George, St Andrew, and St Patrick superimposed

The Union Jack, officially the Union Flag, is the national flag of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. It combines the red cross of St George (England), the white saltire of St Andrew (Scotland), and the red saltire of St Patrick (Ireland), reflecting the successive unions of the kingdoms.

History

The first Union Flag was created in 1606 after James VI and I united the crowns of England and Scotland, superimposing the cross of St George on the saltire of St Andrew. When Ireland joined the Union in 1801, St Patrick's saltire was added, producing the present design. Wales is not represented, having been part of the Kingdom of England when the first flag was designed.

Usage

The flag appears in the canton of several other national flags, including those of Australia and New Zealand, and of British Overseas Territories. Technically, "Union Jack" properly describes the flag when flown from a ship's jack staff, though the term is used universally.
