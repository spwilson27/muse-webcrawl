Canadian honours system

The Canadian honours system comprises the orders, decorations, and medals by which the Canadian Crown recognises outstanding achievement, bravery, and service to Canada. The monarch is the fount of honour and sovereign of the orders; the governor general acts as chancellor of the principal orders and administers the system through the Chancellery of Honours at Rideau Hall. A distinctly Canadian system was established in 1967, the centennial year, with the creation of the Order of Canada.

Principal orders

The highest honour is the Order of Merit, a personal gift of the sovereign. The Order of Canada, motto Desiderantes meliorem patriam ("They desire a better country"), is the cornerstone of the system, with three grades: Companion, Officer, and Member. It is followed by the Order of Military Merit, the Order of Merit of the Police Forces, and the Royal Victorian Order, as well as provincial and territorial orders such as the Order of Ontario and the Ordre national du Québec.

Decorations and medals

Bravery decorations are headed by the Cross of Valour, Canada's highest civilian bravery award, followed by the Star of Courage and the Medal of Bravery; military valour decorations include the Canadian Victoria Cross, created in 1993, which has yet to be awarded. The system also includes meritorious service decorations, the Sovereign's Medal for Volunteers, commemorative medals for royal jubilees and anniversaries, and long-service medals for the Canadian Armed Forces, police, and emergency services. All awards are worn in a prescribed order of precedence published by the government.

History

Before 1967 Canadians received British imperial honours, a practice interrupted by the Nickle Resolution of 1919 and finally ended with the creation of the Canadian system under Prime Minister Lester B. Pearson. Nominations are open to the public and are considered by independent advisory councils.
