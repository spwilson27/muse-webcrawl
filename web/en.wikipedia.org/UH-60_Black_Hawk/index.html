UH-60 Black Hawk

The Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk is a four-blade, twin-engine, medium-lift utility military helicopter manufactured by Sikorsky Aircraft. It has served as the United States Army's primary utility helicopter since the late 1970s, and variants are operated by armed forces around the world. Named after the Native American war leader Black Hawk, the helicopter is designed for air assault, medical evacuation, troop transport, and general utility missions.

Development

The UH-60 emerged from the Army's Utility Tactical Transport Aircraft System (UTTAS) competition of the early 1970s, intended to replace the Bell UH-1 Iroquois. Sikorsky's YUH-60A first flew on 17 October 1974 and won the competition in 1976. The design emphasised survivability: crashworthy seats and landing gear, redundant systems, and a cabin able to withstand ballistic impacts. It entered service in 1979.

Design

The Black Hawk carries a crew of four and up to eleven equipped troops. Two General Electric T700 turboshaft engines drive a four-blade main rotor; later models introduced more powerful engines, improved rotor blades, and digital avionics. The airframe can be transported inside a C-130 Hercules with minimal disassembly, a key UTTAS requirement.

Variants and operators

The basic design spawned a large family: the SH-60 Seahawk for the US Navy, the HH-60 Pave Hawk for combat search and rescue, the MH-60 special-operations variants, and the VH-60N used for presidential transport. Civilian and export versions are marketed as the S-70. More than 4,000 aircraft have been built, and the type saw prominent service in Grenada, Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia — where two were shot down in the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu — Iraq, and Afghanistan.
