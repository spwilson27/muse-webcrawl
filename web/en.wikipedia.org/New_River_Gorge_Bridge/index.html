New River Gorge Bridge

The New River Gorge Bridge is a steel arch bridge near Fayetteville, West Virginia, carrying U.S. Route 19 over the New River Gorge. With an arch 1,700 feet (518 m) long, it was for many years the world's longest single-span arch bridge, and at 876 feet (267 m) above the river it is among the highest vehicular bridges in the world.

Construction and history

Completed in 1977 at a cost of $37 million, the bridge cut the crossing of the gorge from a 40-minute drive down winding roads to about one minute. It is built of weathering steel and is depicted on the West Virginia state quarter.

Bridge Day

Each October, the bridge closes to traffic for Bridge Day, a festival during which BASE jumpers and rappellers are permitted to descend from the deck, drawing tens of thousands of spectators.
