Pennsylvania Station (1910–1963)

LocationMidtown Manhattan, New York City
Opened27 November 1910
Demolished1963–1966
ArchitectMcKim, Mead & White
StyleBeaux-Arts
OwnerPennsylvania Railroad

Pennsylvania Station was a monumental railroad station in New York City and one of the great architectural landmarks of the Beaux-Arts style. Built by the Pennsylvania Railroad and opened in 1910, it occupied two city blocks between Seventh and Eighth Avenues and 31st and 33rd Streets in Midtown Manhattan. Its demolition, begun in 1963, is widely regarded as a galvanising moment for the historic preservation movement in the United States.

Architecture

Designed by the firm of McKim, Mead & White, the station was clad in pink granite and modelled in part on the ancient Baths of Caracalla in Rome. Its vast waiting room, 300 feet long, featured Corinthian columns of travertine and a coffered ceiling 150 feet high; a steel-and-glass train concourse admitted daylight to the platforms below. The exterior was fronted by a colonnade of Doric columns and adorned above the entrances by stone eagles sculpted by Adolph Weinman, several of which survive in public places today.

History

The station was the centrepiece of the Pennsylvania Railroad's New York tunnel extension, which first brought its trains into Manhattan under the Hudson River. At its peak during the Second World War it served over 100 million passengers a year. Post-war declines in rail travel and the railroad's financial difficulties led the Pennsylvania Railroad to sell the air rights; the head house was demolished between 1963 and 1966 and replaced by the Pennsylvania Plaza office tower and the fourth Madison Square Garden. Trains have since used a cramped underground station on the same site.

Legacy

Public outrage at the demolition helped secure the passage of New York's Landmarks Law in 1965, which soon after saved Grand Central Terminal. Architectural historian Vincent Scully famously wrote that through the old Penn Station "one entered the city like a god", whereas now "one scuttles in like a rat". In 2021, Moynihan Train Hall opened across Eighth Avenue in the adapted Farley Post Office building, restoring a grand waiting room to the station complex.
