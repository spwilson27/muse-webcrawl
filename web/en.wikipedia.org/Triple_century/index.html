Triple century

A triple century is among the rarest feats for a batsman

A triple century (or triple hundred) is a score of 300 or more runs by a single batsman in one innings of cricket. It is a rare and celebrated individual achievement, comparable in rarity to a bowler taking a ten-wicket haul.

Test cricket

In Test cricket, the first triple century was scored by Andy Sandham of England against the West Indies at Kingston, Jamaica, in 1930 (325 runs). The highest individual score in Tests is Brian Lara's 400 not out for the West Indies against England at St John's, Antigua, in 2004 — the only quadruple century in Test history. Lara also held the previous record of 375.

Only four batsmen have scored two Test triple centuries: Don Bradman (334 and 304), Brian Lara (375 and 400*), Virender Sehwag (309 and 319), and Chris Gayle (317 and 333). Sehwag's 319 against South Africa at Chennai in 2008 is the fastest Test triple century, scored off 278 balls. The first triple century in a day/night Test was scored by David Warner (335 not out) against Pakistan at Adelaide in 2019.

First-class cricket

In first-class cricket generally, the highest score is Brian Lara's 501 not out for Warwickshire against Durham in 1994, the only quintuple century in first-class history. The first first-class triple century was made by W. G. Grace in 1876. Several batsmen, including Bill Ponsford and Don Bradman, have scored more than one first-class triple century.
