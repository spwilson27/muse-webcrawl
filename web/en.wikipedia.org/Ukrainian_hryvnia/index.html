The hryvnia (Ukrainian: гривня, abbr. грн, symbol ₴, code UAH) is the national currency of Ukraine. It was introduced on 2 September 1996, replacing the karbovanets at a rate of 100,000 to 1 hryvnia, following a period of hyperinflation after independence from the Soviet Union. A hryvnia is subdivided into 100 kopiyky. The name derives from a measure of weight and neck ornament used in Kyivan Rus'.

Coins and banknotes

Banknotes circulate in denominations of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, and 1,000 hryvnias, featuring figures from Ukrainian history and culture such as Taras Shevchenko, Lesya Ukrainka, Ivan Franko, and Hryhorii Skovoroda. Lower-value notes have progressively been replaced by coins.

Exchange rate history

The hryvnia was pegged within bands for much of its early history; it lost substantial value after the 2014 annexation of Crimea and war in Donbas, and again following Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, when the National Bank fixed and later managed the rate with international support.
