Note (perfumery)

Perfume notes
Top notesImmediate, volatile (citrus, herbs)
Heart notesMiddle character (florals, spices)
Base notesLong-lasting (woods, resins, musk)

In perfumery, a note is a descriptor of a scent perceived in a fragrance, and by extension the ingredients that produce it. Perfumes are conventionally described as having three sets of notes that unfold over time, like the notes of a musical composition.

Top notes

Top (or head) notes are perceived immediately on application. They are light, volatile molecules that evaporate within minutes — citrus oils such as bergamot and lemon, and aromatic herbs, are typical. They form the first impression of a fragrance.

Heart notes

The heart (or middle) notes emerge as the top notes fade and form the main character of the perfume, lasting for several hours. Floral notes such as rose and jasmine, green notes and warm spices are common.

Base notes

Base notes are heavy, slow-evaporating materials — woods, resins, vanilla, amber and musks — that support the composition and can linger on skin for many hours, mingling with the heart notes in the "dry down".
