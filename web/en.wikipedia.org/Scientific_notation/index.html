Scientific notation

Scientific notation is a way of expressing numbers that are too large or too small to be conveniently written in decimal form. It may be referred to as scientific form or standard index form, or standard form in the United Kingdom. It is commonly used by scientists, mathematicians, and engineers, in part because it can simplify certain arithmetic operations. In scientific notation, nonzero numbers are written in the form m × 10n, where n is an integer and the coefficient m is a nonzero real number between 1 and 10 in absolute value.

Examples

The mass of the Earth is about 5.972 × 1024 kilograms; the mass of an electron is about 9.109 × 10−31 kilograms. The number 123,000,000,000 is written 1.23 × 1011, and 0.00045 is written 4.5 × 10−4. Scientific notation also conveys significant figures clearly: the notation 1.2300 × 105 indicates five significant figures.

E notation and calculators

On calculators and in computing, where superscript notation is awkward, E notation is often used: 1.23E11 or 1.23e+11 means 1.23 × 1011. Most programming languages use this format for displaying floating-point numbers. Related conventions include engineering notation, in which the exponent of ten must be a multiple of three so that it matches an SI prefix such as kilo- or milli-.

Operations

To multiply numbers in scientific notation, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents; to divide, divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents. Addition and subtraction require the exponents to be made equal first. These rules make calculations with quantities spanning many orders of magnitude manageable.
