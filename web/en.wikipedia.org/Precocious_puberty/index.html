Precocious puberty

Precocious puberty
Specialty: Endocrinology, paediatrics
Symptoms: Early secondary sexual development
Causes: Central (GnRH-dependent) or peripheral (GnRH-independent)

Early activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis causes most cases of precocious puberty.

Precocious puberty is the unusually early onset of puberty, conventionally defined as the appearance of secondary sexual characteristics before age 8 in girls and before age 9 in boys. It is considerably more common in girls than in boys.

Types and causes

Central precocious puberty (gonadotropin-dependent) results from early activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis and pulsatile secretion of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH). Most cases in girls are idiopathic; in boys, central nervous system lesions such as hypothalamic hamartomas are more frequently responsible. Activating mutations in the kisspeptin system (KISS1/KISS1R) and loss-of-function mutations in MKRN3 are recognised genetic causes. Peripheral precocious puberty (gonadotropin-independent) arises from sex-steroid production independent of GnRH — for example from ovarian or adrenal tumours, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, exogenous hormone exposure, or McCune–Albright syndrome.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation includes growth charts, bone-age radiographs, basal and GnRH-stimulated LH/FSH measurements, and imaging when indicated. Central precocious puberty is treated with GnRH analogues (such as leuprolide or histrelin implants), which suppress the axis and halt progression; peripheral causes are treated according to the underlying source of hormone production. Untreated, early epiphyseal fusion can compromise adult height, and early maturation may cause psychosocial difficulties.
