Rudolf E. Kálmán

Rudolf Emil Kálmán (Hungarian: Kálmán Rudolf Emil; May 19, 1930 – July 2, 2016) was a Hungarian-American electrical engineer, mathematician, and inventor, best known for his development of the Kalman filter, a mathematical technique that is used extensively in control systems and avionics.

The Kalman filter

In a landmark 1960 paper, Kálmán presented a recursive solution to the discrete-data linear filtering problem: an algorithm that uses a series of noisy measurements over time to produce optimal estimates of unknown variables. Stanley Schmidt championed its use at NASA, and the filter became essential to the navigation and guidance computers of the Apollo program. It is now ubiquitous in navigation, robotics, econometrics, and signal processing.

Career and honours

Born in Budapest, Kálmán emigrated to the United States during World War II, studied at MIT and Columbia, and worked at the Research Institute for Advanced Studies in Baltimore and Stanford University before becoming a professor at the University of Florida. He received the IEEE Medal of Honor, the Kyoto Prize, the Charles Stark Draper Prize, and the U.S. National Medal of Science, presented in 2009.
