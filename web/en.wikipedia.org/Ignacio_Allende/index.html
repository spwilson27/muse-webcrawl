Ignacio Allende

Born 21 January 1769, San Miguel el Grande
Died 26 June 1811, Chihuahua
Role Leader in Mexican War of Independence

Ignacio José de Allende y Unzaga (21 January 1769 – 26 June 1811) was a Mexican Spanish Army officer who became a leader of the independence movement against Spain. Alongside Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla, he launched the struggle that began the Mexican War of Independence.

Early life

Allende was born in San Miguel el Grande (now San Miguel de Allende, named in his honour) into a wealthy criollo family. He joined the colonial army and rose to the rank of captain, but grew sympathetic to independence conspiracies, including the Querétaro conspiracy of 1810.

Independence war

When the conspiracy was discovered, Hidalgo issued the Grito de Dolores on 16 September 1810, and Allende served as one of the principal commanders of the insurgent army, capturing Guanajuato and other cities. After defeats and disputes over strategy, he was captured at the Wells of Baján in March 1811, tried at Chihuahua, and executed by firing squad on 26 June 1811. He is honoured as a national hero of Mexico.
