Scattering parameters

Scattering parameters or S-parameters (the elements of a scattering matrix or S-matrix) describe the electrical behaviour of linear electrical networks when undergoing various steady state stimuli by electrical signals. The parameters are useful for several branches of electrical engineering, including electronics, communication systems design, and especially for microwave engineering. The S-parameters are members of a family of similar parameters, other examples being Y-parameters, Z-parameters, H-parameters, T-parameters, and ABCD-parameters.

Definition

For a network with N ports, the S-parameters relate the waves incident on each port to the waves reflected or transmitted from each port. Using ai for the incident wave and bi for the outgoing wave at port , the relation is b = Sa. For a two-port network the matrix is

b1 = S11a1 + S12a2;   b2 = S21a1 + S22a2

Here S11 is the input reflection coefficient with the output port terminated in a matched load, S21 the forward transmission coefficient (gain or loss), S12 the reverse transmission coefficient, and S22 the output reflection coefficient. Each parameter is a complex number, conveniently expressed in magnitude (often decibels) and phase, and each is measured with all other ports terminated in the system characteristic impedance, usually 50 Ω.

Measurement and use

S-parameters are measured with a vector network analyzer, which sweeps a signal across frequency and records reflected and transmitted waves at every port. Unlike Y- or Z-parameters, S-parameters do not require open- or short-circuit terminations, which are impractical and even hazardous at microwave frequencies, and the travelling waves involved remain well behaved for distributed structures such as transmission lines. Results are conventionally stored in Touchstone files (.s2p and similar) and plotted on a Smith chart or as rectangular magnitude/phase traces. Amplifiers, filters, antennas, cables, and connectors are all routinely specified and simulated in terms of their S-parameters; quantities such as return loss, insertion loss, gain, isolation, and VSWR derive directly from them.

Properties

A passive, lossless network has a unitary S-matrix; reciprocity implies a symmetric matrix (Sij = Sji); and a perfectly matched network has zero diagonal elements. S-parameters may be converted to and from impedance, admittance, and ABCD representations by standard algebraic transformations when the port impedances are known.
