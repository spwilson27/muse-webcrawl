Shelf life

Date labels such as "best before" and "use by" communicate a product's shelf life.

Shelf life is the length of time that a commodity may be stored without becoming unfit for use, consumption, or sale. It applies to foods, beverages, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, cosmetics, and many manufactured products. During shelf life, a properly stored product is expected to retain its safety, quality, and stated characteristics.

Shelf life is commonly communicated through date marks: "use by" dates relate to safety (particularly for perishable foods), "best before" dates to quality, and "sell by" dates to stock rotation. Manufacturers determine shelf life through stability and challenge testing under expected storage conditions, taking into account temperature, humidity, light exposure, packaging, and microbial growth. In the United States, expiration dating is federally required mainly for drugs and some infant formula, while most food date labels are voluntary and vary by state—a patchwork that officials estimate contributes to consumer confusion and food waste.

Extending shelf life

Techniques to extend shelf life include refrigeration and freezing, pasteurisation and sterilisation, drying, curing, preservatives, vacuum and modified-atmosphere packaging, and aseptic processing, which allows products such as UHT milk to remain shelf-stable for months. Shelf life limits for pharmaceuticals and medical supplies also govern stockpiling for emergencies, with government Shelf-Life Extension Programs testing whether properly stored drugs remain potent beyond labelled dates.
