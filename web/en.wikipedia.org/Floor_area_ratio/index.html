Floor area ratio

Floor area ratio (FAR), also called floor space ratio (FSR) or plot ratio, is the ratio of a building's total floor area to the size of the land parcel (lot) upon which it is built. It is a key control in zoning and urban planning used to regulate building bulk and density.

Calculation

FAR is computed as total gross floor area divided by lot area. For example, a FAR of 2.0 on a 1,000 m² lot permits 2,000 m² of floor space — which could be a two-storey building covering the entire lot or a taller building covering part of it. Certain spaces, such as parking or mechanical floors, are often excluded from the calculation.

Use and debate

Zoning codes assign maximum (and occasionally minimum) FARs by district. Planners debate FAR limits because restricting them can constrain housing supply and raise prices, while higher FARs support transit-oriented density; FAR is also combined with height limits, setbacks, and lot coverage rules to shape a building's envelope.
