Ivan Meštrović

Ivan Meštrović (pronounced [ǐʋan mɛ̌ʃtrɔʋitɕ]; 15 August 1883 – 16 January 1962) was a Croatian sculptor, architect, and writer, widely regarded as one of the most important sculptors of the 20th century and the most prominent Croatian artist of the modern era. Life

Born into a peasant family of stone-cutters in Slavonia and raised in Dalmatia, Meštrović was apprenticed in Split and studied sculpture in Vienna, where the Secession influenced his early work. His monumental, archaic figures expressing South Slav national themes made him famous at the 1911 Rome exhibition. A supporter of Yugoslav unification, he lived in exile during the First World War; afterwards he settled in Zagreb, designing his atelier-home and the family mausoleum at Otavice.

Later career and emigration

Meštrović's major public monuments include the Victor of Belgrade (1928), the statue of Gregory of Nin in Split (1929), the Mausoleum of Njegoš on Lovćen, and the two mounted Native American figures (The Bowman and The Spearman, 1928) in Chicago's Grant Park. Imprisoned briefly by the Ustaše in 1941, he emigrated in 1947 to the United States, teaching at Syracuse University and the University of Notre Dame, and became a U.S. citizen. He died in South Bend in 1962; the Meštrović Gallery in Split preserves his house and works.
