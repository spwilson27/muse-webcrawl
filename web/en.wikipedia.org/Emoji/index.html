Emoji are pictograms, logograms, ideograms, or smileys embedded in text and used in electronic messages and web pages. The word comes from Japanese e ("picture") and moji ("character"); its similarity to the English word "emoticon" is coincidental. Emoji exist in various genres, including facial expressions, common objects, places, types of weather, food, and animals, and function as a visual supplement — or replacement — for written language.

History

The first emoji set was created in 1999 by Japanese designer Shigetaka Kurita for NTT DoCoMo's i-mode mobile internet platform; the 176 original designs, drawn on 12×12-pixel grids, are now held by the Museum of Modern Art in New York. Rival Japanese carriers developed their own sets, and emoji became a fixture of Japanese mobile culture. International adoption began when Apple included an emoji keyboard for Japanese iPhone users in 2008 and expanded worldwide in 2011; Android followed soon after.

Standardization

The Unicode Consortium encoded the first core emoji set in Unicode 6.0 (2010), assigning each emoji a code point while leaving visual design to platform vendors — which is why the same emoji can look noticeably different on Apple, Google, Samsung, and Microsoft devices, sometimes causing miscommunication. The Consortium's emoji subcommittee accepts public proposals for new emoji annually; additions have included diverse skin-tone modifiers (2015), gender-inclusive designs, and professions. Sequences using the zero-width joiner (ZWJ) combine base characters into new forms, such as family and profession emoji.

Cultural impact

"Face with Tears of Joy" 😂 was named Word of the Year by Oxford Dictionaries in 2015 — the first pictograph so honoured. Emoji have been studied by linguists as a form of paralanguage, used in marketing, literature experiments (the novel Emoji Dick, a translation of Moby-Dick into emoji), and legal proceedings, where courts interpret emoji in messages as evidence of intent. World Emoji Day is celebrated on 17 July, the date shown on the calendar emoji 📅.
