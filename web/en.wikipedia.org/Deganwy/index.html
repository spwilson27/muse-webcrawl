Deganwy is a small town and electoral ward in Conwy County Borough in Wales, lying on the Creuddyn peninsula on the north coast, midway between Conwy and Llandudno. Historically part of Caernarfonshire, the town grew around its railway station and marina on the estuary of the River Conwy.

History

The name Deganwy derives from the Welsh Din Gonwy, "fort on the River Conwy". The hill above the town, the Vardre, was the site of Deganwy Castle, an early stronghold associated with Maelgwn Gwynedd in the 6th century. The castle was later rebuilt by the English Crown and destroyed in the 13th century during the campaigns of Llywelyn ap Gruffudd. The modern settlement developed in the Victorian era as a suburb of Llandudno, aided by the opening of Deganwy railway station on the Conwy Valley line in 1866.

Geography

Deganwy overlooks the Conwy estuary to the west and has a sheltered marina, Deganwy Quay, which includes residential development, a hotel and berthing for leisure craft. The Wales Coast Path passes through the town. The twin volcanic hills of the Vardre rise behind the town and carry the remains of the medieval castle.

Governance

Deganwy forms part of the community of Conwy and returns councillors to Conwy County Borough Council. It lies within the Aberconwy parliamentary constituency.
