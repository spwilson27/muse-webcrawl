Diplomacy

Diplomats negotiating at an international conference.

Diplomacy is the practice of conducting negotiations between representatives of states or other international actors, and the art of dealing with people in a sensitive and tactful way. It entails influencing the decisions and conduct of foreign governments and officials through dialogue, negotiation, and other non-violent means. Diplomacy is the chief instrument of foreign policy and the primary means by which states pursue their interests short of war.

History

Diplomatic practice dates to the ancient world: the Amarna letters of the 14th century BC record correspondence between Egyptian pharaohs and other Near Eastern rulers, and ancient Greece and India developed systems of envoys with recognized immunities. The modern diplomatic system emerged in Renaissance Italy, where city-states such as Venice and Milan maintained resident ambassadors. The Congress of Vienna (1815) codified diplomatic ranks, and the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) remains the foundational treaty governing diplomatic immunity, missions, and protocol.

Functions and methods

The Vienna Convention lists the functions of a diplomatic mission: representing the sending state, protecting its interests and nationals, negotiating with the host government, reporting on conditions, and promoting friendly relations. Methods range from quiet bilateral negotiation and multilateral conference diplomacy — exemplified by the United Nations — to summit diplomacy between heads of government, public diplomacy aimed at foreign publics, and specialized forms such as economic, cultural, and science diplomacy. "Shuttle diplomacy," crisis diplomacy, and track-two (unofficial) diplomacy are widely used variants.

Diplomatic immunity

Diplomats enjoy legal immunities codified in the 1961 Convention: inviolability of the person and of embassy premises, immunity from criminal jurisdiction, and various exemptions from taxation and customs duties. These protections enable diplomats to perform their functions without coercion by the host state; a diplomat who abuses the position may be declared persona non grata and expelled.
