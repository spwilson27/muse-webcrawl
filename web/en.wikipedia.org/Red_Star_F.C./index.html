Red Star Football Club (French: Red Star Football Club), commonly known as Red Star FC, is a French professional football club founded in Paris in 1897 by Jules Rimet, the future president of FIFA and creator of the World Cup. Based since 1910 in Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine, just north of Paris, it is one of the oldest football clubs in France and a founding member of the French professional league.

The Stade Bauer in Saint-Ouen, home ground since 1909.

History

Red Star was a dominant force in early French football, winning the Coupe de France five times: in 1921, 1922, 1923 (three consecutive titles), 1928, and 1942. The club moved to the Stade Bauer in Saint-Ouen in 1909. After the Second World War it declined, spending most of its history in the second and third divisions, and suffered financial crises and relegations to amateur football in the 2000s before climbing back to Ligue 2.

Identity

Red Star is known for a working-class, community-oriented identity distinct from Paris Saint-Germain, emphasising affordable tickets, local youth development, and cultural programmes through its Red Star Lab initiative. Its home colours are green and white striped shirts; the club badge features a red star, from which it takes its name.

Honours

  • Coupe de France: 1921, 1922, 1923, 1928, 1942
  • Championnat National champions: 2014–15, 2023–24
  • Ligue 2 / Division 2 champions: 1933–34, 1938–39
