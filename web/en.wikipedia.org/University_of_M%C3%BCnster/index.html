University of Münster

Type: Public research university
Established: 1780
Location: Münster, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany
Students: ~45,000

The University of Münster (German: Universität Münster, until 2023 formally the Westphalian Wilhelms University) is a public research university in Münster, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. Founded in 1780 and developed into a full university in the early twentieth century, it is one of Germany's largest universities.

Profile

The university has 15 faculties covering theology (both Catholic and Protestant), law, economics, medicine, mathematics and computer science, natural sciences and humanities. It hosts major research centres in religious studies (the Cluster of Excellence "Religion and Politics"), mathematics (the Cluster "Mathematics Münster") and cell dynamics. Its main building is the baroque Schloss, the former prince-bishop's palace.

City and university

Münster is a quintessential German university city — students make up a large share of the population and bicycles dominate its streets. The university is a member of the U15 group of leading German research universities.
