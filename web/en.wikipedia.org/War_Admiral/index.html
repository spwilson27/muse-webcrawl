War Admiral (1934–1959) was an American Thoroughbred racehorse, best known as the fourth winner of the American Triple Crown and for his famous match race against Seabiscuit in 1938. A son of the great Man o' War, War Admiral was bred and owned by Samuel D. Riddle's Glen Riddle Farm and trained by George Conway.

Racing career

War Admiral inherited little of his sire's size — he stood a modest 15.2 hands — but had his speed and fiery temperament, including a notorious habit of resisting the starting gate. Undefeated as a three-year-old in 1937, he won the Kentucky Derby, the Preakness Stakes, and the Belmont Stakes to complete the Triple Crown, despite injuring himself at the start of the Belmont and racing with a bloody hoof wound. He was named American Horse of the Year for 1937.

The match race

Through 1938, public clamour grew for a meeting between War Admiral, the aristocrat of the East, and Seabiscuit, the rags-to-riches champion of the West. The two met at Pimlico on November 1, 1938, in the "Match of the Century", before some 40,000 spectators and a radio audience estimated at 40 million. In a race immortalized in books and the 2003 film Seabiscuit, Seabiscuit upset War Admiral, pulling away in the stretch to win by four lengths.

Later career and stud

War Admiral retired with 21 wins from 26 starts and earnings of $273,240. At stud he was the leading sire in North America in 1945 and sired the champion filly Busher and numerous stakes winners; his daughters made him an influential broodmare sire, and he appears in the pedigrees of later greats including Dr. Fager, Buckpasser, and Seattle Slew. He died in 1959 and was buried at Faraway Farm in Kentucky, near his sire. War Admiral was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1958 and ranked 13th in the Blood-Horse magazine list of the top 100 racehorses of the 20th century.
