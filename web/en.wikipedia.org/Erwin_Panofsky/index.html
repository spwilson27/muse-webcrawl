Erwin Panofsky, founder of modern iconology.

Erwin Panofsky (March 30, 1892 – March 14, 1968) was a German art historian, one of the most influential scholars of art history in the twentieth century. A leading figure of the Hamburg school associated with Aby Warburg's library, he emigrated to the United States in 1934 after the Nazi rise to power and spent the rest of his career at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.

Life and career

Panofsky studied at the universities of Berlin, Munich, and Freiburg, receiving his doctorate in 1914 with a dissertation on Albrecht Dürer's theory of art. He taught at the University of Hamburg from 1921, where he worked alongside Ernst Cassirer and in the orbit of the Warburg Library. Dismissed under Nazi racial laws in 1933, he settled in Princeton, becoming a professor at the Institute for Advanced Study in 1935, a position he held until his death.

Iconology

Panofsky systematized the study of subject matter in art into three levels: pre-iconographical description (primary or natural subject matter), iconographical analysis (secondary or conventional subject matter, such as allegories and stories), and iconological interpretation, which seeks the underlying cultural attitudes a work reveals. His method was set out in Studies in Iconology (1939) and Meaning in the Visual Arts (1955).

Major works

His books include Perspective as Symbolic Form (1927), Gothic Architecture and Scholasticism (1951), Early Netherlandish Painting (1953), and Renaissance and Renascences in Western Art (1960). His analysis of Jan van Eyck's Arnolfini Portrait, reading its details as "disguised symbolism," remains a landmark of the field. See also iconography and Aby Warburg.
