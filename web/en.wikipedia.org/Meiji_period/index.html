The Meiji era (Japanese: 明治時代, Meiji jidai) was an era of Japanese history that extended from October 23, 1868, to July 30, 1912. The era corresponds to the reign of Emperor Meiji and saw Japan's rapid transformation from an isolated feudal society into a modern, industrialised world power. It was preceded by the Keiō era and succeeded by the Taishō era, upon the death of Emperor Meiji.

Meiji Restoration and reforms

The era began with the Meiji Restoration of 1868, in which the Tokugawa shogunate was overthrown and political power nominally restored to the emperor. The oligarchs who dominated the new government abolished the feudal domains (1871) and the samurai class privileges, introduced conscription (1873) and compulsory primary education (1872), built railways and telegraph networks, adopted the Gregorian calendar, and undertook the Iwakura Mission to study Western institutions. Japan promulgated the Meiji Constitution in 1889 and opened the Imperial Diet in 1890.

Foreign relations and wars

Meiji Japan revised the unequal treaties with Western powers, defeated Qing China in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895), gaining Taiwan, and defeated Russia in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905) — the first modern victory of an Asian power over a European great power. Korea was annexed in 1910. By the emperor's death in 1912, Japan had become one of the great powers of the world.
