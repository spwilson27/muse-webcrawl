Emerson, Lake & Palmer

Emerson, Lake & Palmer (ELP) were an English progressive rock supergroup formed in London in 1970. The band consisted of Keith Emerson (keyboards, formerly of the Nice), Greg Lake (vocals, bass, guitar, formerly of King Crimson), and Carl Palmer (drums, percussion, formerly of Atomic Rooster). With nine RIAA-certified gold record albums in the US and an estimated 48 million albums sold worldwide, they were one of the most popular and commercially successful progressive rock groups of the 1970s.

Music and career

ELP were known for their adaptations of classical music pieces, including Mussorgsky's Pictures at an Exhibition, and for elaborate live shows featuring Emerson's theatrical keyboard playing. Albums such as Tarkus (1971), Trilogy (1972) and Brain Salad Surgery (1973) established their reputation. Their rendition of Aaron Copland's "Fanfare for the Common Man" became a hit single in 1977. The group disbanded in 1979, reformed in the 1990s, and performed a final reunion concert in 2010. Emerson and Lake both died in 2016.
