List of track gauges

Track gauge is the distance between the inner sides of the two rails of a railway track. This page lists track gauges used around the world, from miniature railways to the broadest industrial gauges. By far the most common is standard gauge (1,435 mm / 4 ft 8½ in), used on roughly 55% of the world's railways.

Break of gauge

Where railways of different gauges meet, a break of gauge occurs. Passengers and freight must be transferred, or vehicles must use variable-gauge axles or bogie exchange. Notable break-of-gauge points include the borders between France and Spain (historically), Poland and Ukraine, and China and Mongolia.

History

In the early 19th century gauges varied widely by region. George Stephenson's 4 ft 8½ in gauge spread with British railway building, and the UK Gauge Act of 1846 standardised it in Britain. Other countries adopted gauges for strategic, economic or terrain reasons, and many networks have since converted to standard gauge.
