The UCI World Tour (2009–2010: UCI World Ranking) is the premier annual male elite road cycling tour, sitting at the top of the UCI's competition hierarchy. It comprises a calendar of races — the three Grand Tours, the five Monuments, other one-week stage races and one-day classics — in which the 18 licensed UCI WorldTeams are guaranteed, and obliged, to compete.

History

The World Tour grew out of the UCI ProTour, launched in 2005, after the UCI settled its long dispute with the Grand Tour organizers ASO, RCS Sport and Unipublic. In 2009 and 2010 the events were combined into a single world calendar with a unified points ranking, and from 2011 the series took the name UCI World Tour. In 2017 the calendar was expanded with additional events, and from 2020 a promotion and relegation system linked to three-year team rankings was introduced, deciding which teams hold WorldTeam licences.

Races

The calendar traditionally opens with the Tour Down Under in Australia and includes, among others, Paris–Nice, Tirreno–Adriatico, Milan–San Remo, the Tour of Flanders, Paris–Roubaix, Liège–Bastogne–Liège, the Tour de Romandie, the Giro d'Italia, the Critérium du Dauphiné, the Tour de Suisse, the Tour de France, the Vuelta a España, the Grand Prix races in Québec and Montréal, and Il Lombardia, which closes the European season.

Points and rankings

Riders score points according to their finishing positions, with the Grand Tours and Monuments carrying the highest values. The UCI publishes individual, team and national world rankings; at the end of each three-year cycle, rankings determine promotion and relegation between the WorldTeam and ProTeam tiers.

Teams

Eighteen teams hold WorldTeam licences. ProTeams may receive wild-card invitations, and at least one slot in each race is reserved for them. WorldTeams must enter every World Tour event, giving organizers a guaranteed field of the world's leading riders.
