Ringo Starr

Sir Richard Starkey (born 7 July 1940), known professionally as Ringo Starr, is an English musician, songwriter and actor who achieved international fame as the drummer for the Beatles. Starr occasionally sang lead vocals with the group, usually for one song on each album, including "Yellow Submarine" and "With a Little Help from My Friends". He also wrote and sang the Beatles songs "Don't Pass Me By" and "Octopus's Garden", and is credited as a co-writer of four others.

Career

Starr was born in Liverpool and played in the skiffle group the Eddie Clayton Skiffle Group and then with Rory Storm and the Hurricanes before joining the Beatles in August 1962, replacing Pete Best. His steady, distinctive drumming — including his left-handed playing on a right-handed kit — influenced generations of drummers.

After the Beatles broke up in 1970, Starr enjoyed a successful solo career with hits including "It Don't Come Easy", "Photograph" and "You're Sixteen", and the albums Ringo (1973) and Goodnight Vienna (1974). Since 1989 he has toured with Ringo Starr & His All-Starr Band. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice — as a Beatle in 1988 and as a solo artist in 2015 — and was knighted in 2018 for services to music.
