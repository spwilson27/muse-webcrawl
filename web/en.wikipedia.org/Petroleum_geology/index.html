Petroleum geology

A structural trap: oil and gas accumulate beneath an impermeable cap rock.

Petroleum geology is the study of the origin, occurrence, movement, and accumulation of hydrocarbon fuels. It applies principles of geology, geophysics, and geochemistry to locate subsurface accumulations of crude oil and natural gas. Petroleum geology is a major branch of economic geology and underlies the exploration side of the petroleum industry.

The petroleum system

The discipline is organized around the concept of the petroleum system, the set of elements and processes required for hydrocarbons to accumulate:

  • Source rock — organic-rich rock (commonly shale) that generates oil and gas when buried and heated
  • Maturation and migration — hydrocarbons are expelled from the source rock and migrate upward through permeable strata and fractures
  • Reservoir rock — porous and permeable rock, typically sandstone or carbonate, that stores the hydrocarbons
  • Seal (cap rock) — an impermeable layer, such as shale or salt, that prevents further migration
  • Trap — a geometry (structural, such as anticlines and fault traps, or stratigraphic) that concentrates hydrocarbons in one place
  • Timing — traps must exist before or during migration for an accumulation to form

Exploration methods

Petroleum geologists use geological mapping, seismic reflection surveys, well logging, core analysis, and geochemical fingerprinting to identify prospects and estimate the size and risk of undiscovered accumulations. Basin analysis reconstructs the burial and thermal history of sedimentary basins to predict where source rocks have matured. Risking weighs the chance that each element of the petroleum system is present and effective.

Unconventional resources

In unconventional plays such as shale oil, shale gas, and oil sands, the source rock itself acts as reservoir and seal, and petroleum geology focuses on identifying "sweet spots" of favorable mineralogy, organic richness, and natural fracturing where hydraulic fracturing can release the hydrocarbons.
