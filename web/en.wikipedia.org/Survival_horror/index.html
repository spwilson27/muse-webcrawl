Survival horror

Survival horror is a subgenre of horror video games that emphasizes survival and resource management over combat power: the player character is typically under-armed and vulnerable, ammunition and healing items are scarce, and progress involves exploration, puzzle-solving, and evasion within threatening environments.

History

Precursors include Alone in the Dark (1992), but the genre was named and popularized by Capcom's Resident Evil (1996), which combined fixed camera angles, limited inventory, and a zombie-infested mansion. Konami's Silent Hill (1999) added psychological horror and fog-shrouded exploration. Later entries shifted toward action before a revival of purer survival horror in the 2010s with titles such as Amnesia: The Dark Descent and Outlast, and successful remakes of the classic series.

Design elements

Common features include sparse save points, inventory puzzles, environmental storytelling through documents, and sound design used to signal unseen danger.
