Metronidazole

Metronidazole, sold under the brand name Flagyl among others, is an antibiotic and antiprotozoal medication. It is used to treat infections caused by anaerobic bacteria and certain parasites, including trichomoniasis, giardiasis, amoebiasis, bacterial vaginosis, pelvic inflammatory disease, and infections with Clostridioides difficile when other options are unavailable or ineffective.

Medical use

Metronidazole is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. It is available by mouth, as a cream or gel, and by injection into a vein. It has been in clinical use since 1960, having been developed from azomycin, a natural product isolated from a Streptomyces species.

Mechanism of action

Metronidazole is a prodrug: inside anaerobic organisms, its nitro group is reduced by low-redox-potential electron-transfer proteins, generating cytotoxic intermediates that cause DNA strand breakage and inhibit nucleic acid synthesis, killing the organism.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, a metallic taste, loss of appetite, and headaches. Consuming alcohol during treatment is traditionally warned against because of a possible disulfiram-like reaction, though the evidence for this interaction is debated. Rare but serious effects include peripheral neuropathy and central nervous system toxicity with prolonged use.
