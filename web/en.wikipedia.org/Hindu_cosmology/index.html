Hindu cosmology

Hindu cosmology is the description of the universe and its states of matter, cycles within time, physical structure and effects on living entities according to Hindu texts. Hindu cosmology is intertwined with the concept of the Trimurti — Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver, and Shiva the destroyer — and with cyclical conceptions of time in which the universe is repeatedly created and destroyed.

Time cycles

Time in Hindu cosmology is divided into immense cycles. A kalpa, or day of Brahma, lasts 4.32 billion years and comprises 1,000 cycles of the four yugas (ages): Satya Yuga, Treta Yuga, Dvapara Yuga, and Kali Yuga, in the ratio 4:3:2:1. The current age is traditionally held to be the Kali Yuga, said to have begun in 3102 BCE. Fourteen manvantaras, each presided over by a Manu, make up a kalpa, and between them come periods of dissolution (pralaya).

Structure of the universe

The physical universe is described as an egg-shaped cosmos (Brahmanda) containing fourteen worlds (lokas): seven higher realms including Bhuloka (earth) and Svarga (heaven), and seven lower realms. At the centre stands Mount Meru, the axis of the world, around which the continents and oceans are arranged in concentric rings. The earthly continent of Jambudvipa is described in the Puranas with Mount Meru at its centre and the landmass of Bharata-varsha to its south.

Creation and dissolution

Creation accounts vary across texts. The Rigveda's Nasadiya Sukta famously questions whether even the gods know how creation arose. In Puranic accounts, Vishnu sleeps upon the serpent Ananta Shesha in the cosmic ocean, and a lotus springs from his navel bearing Brahma, who then fashions the universe. At the end of each kalpa the universe is dissolved back into a state of potentiality before being created anew.
