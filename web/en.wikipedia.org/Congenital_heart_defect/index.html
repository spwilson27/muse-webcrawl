Congenital heart defect

A congenital heart defect (CHD), also known as a congenital heart anomaly, is a defect in the structure of the heart or great vessels that is present at birth. Signs and symptoms depend on the specific type of defect and can include rapid breathing, bluish skin (cyanosis), poor weight gain, and fatigue. Congenital heart defects do not generally cause chest pain.

Causes and diagnosis

The cause of a congenital heart defect is often unknown. Risk factors include certain infections during pregnancy such as rubella, use of certain medications or drugs such as alcohol or tobacco, parents being closely related, poor nutritional status, and maternal obesity or diabetes. Diagnosis may be suspected based on symptoms and confirmed with echocardiography; some defects are detected by prenatal ultrasound or pulse oximetry screening of newborns.

Types and treatment

Defects are commonly divided into cyanotic and acyanotic groups. Examples include ventricular septal defect (the most common), atrial septal defect, tetralogy of Fallot, patent ductus arteriosus, pulmonary stenosis, and transposition of the great arteries. Treatment varies: some defects need no treatment, others are managed with catheter-based procedures or open-heart surgery, and heart transplantation is occasionally required. Congenital heart defects are the most common birth defect, affecting roughly 1% of live births worldwide.
