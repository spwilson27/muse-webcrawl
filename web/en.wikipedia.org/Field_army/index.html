Field army

A field army (or numbered army) is a military formation in many armed forces, composed of two or more corps and typically commanded by a general or field marshal. Field armies are among the largest conventional land formations, with strengths commonly in the hundreds of thousands.

Organisation

A field army consists of a headquarters, two to five corps, and supporting army-level troops such as artillery, engineers, and logistics units. Armies are in turn grouped into army groups or theatres. Armies are often designated by number (e.g., First Army) or by name.

History

The modern field army emerged in the Napoleonic era as armies grew too large for a single commander to direct in the older style. Corps and armies became permanent headquarters structures during the nineteenth century and were widely used in both World Wars.
