Castile and León (Spanish: Castilla y León) is an autonomous community in north-western Spain, created in 1983 by the union of the historic regions of Castile and León. It is the largest autonomous community in Spain by area, and is bordered by Portugal to the west and by ten other Spanish regions. Valladolid is its largest city and the seat of the regional government, the Junta of Castile and León.

Geography

The region is dominated by the northern Meseta, a high plateau drained by the Douro (Duero) river, and ringed by mountain systems such as the Cantabrian Mountains, the Iberian System, and the Sierra de Gredos. Its climate is continental, with cold winters and hot, dry summers.

History

The medieval Kingdoms of León (founded 910) and Castile grew out of the Christian reconquest of the peninsula and were definitively united in 1230 under Ferdinand III. The region contains major Romanesque and Gothic monuments, including the cathedrals of Burgos, León, and Salamanca.

Culture

Castile and León is considered the heartland of the Castilian Spanish language; its universities at Salamanca (founded 1218) and Valladolid are among the oldest in Europe. The region is known for wine areas such as Ribera del Duero and for cured hams and roast suckling pig.
