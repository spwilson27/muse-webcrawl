Sustainable Development Goal 6

Sustainable Development Goal 6 (SDG 6 or Global Goal 6) is one of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals established by the United Nations in 2015. Its official wording is: "Ensure availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all." Progress is overseen by UN-Water, with the WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme tracking drinking-water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services.

Targets

The goal has eight targets to be achieved by 2030. They include: safe and affordable drinking water for all (6.1); sanitation and hygiene for all, ending open defecation, with attention to women, girls and people in vulnerable situations (6.2); improved water quality by reducing pollution and increasing wastewater treatment and reuse (6.3); increased water-use efficiency and sustainable withdrawals to address water scarcity (6.4); integrated water resources management at all levels, including transboundary cooperation (6.5); protection and restoration of water-related ecosystems (6.6); and means of implementation covering international cooperation, capacity building and local community participation (6.a, 6.b).

Progress

UN assessments report that billions of people still lack safely managed drinking water, sanitation or basic hygiene — in 2022 roughly 2.2 billion lacked safely managed drinking water and about 2 billion lacked safely managed sanitation. Progress is well short of the rate needed to reach the 2030 targets, and water stress affects more than two billion people. SDG 6 is closely linked to health (SDG 3), food security (SDG 2) and climate action (SDG 13).
