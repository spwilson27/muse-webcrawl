The Rikken Seiyūkai (Japanese: 立憲政友会, "Constitutional Association of Political Friendship"), often shortened to Seiyūkai, was one of the main political parties in the Empire of Japan. It was founded in 1900 by Itō Hirobumi, the architect of the Meiji Constitution, as an alliance of bureaucrats and former members of the Liberal Party, and it became the dominant force in the lower house of the Imperial Diet for much of the pre-war period.

History

Itō founded the party to create a governing bloc in the Diet that would support his cabinets and counter the opposition parties. Under its second president, Saionji Kinmochi, the party alternated in power with cabinets led by Katsura Tarō in the so-called "Keien" era. Hara Takashi, president from 1914, built the party into a powerful machine rooted in local elites, railway expansion and public works, and in 1918 became the first commoner to serve as prime minister of Japan — the start of an era of party government during the Taishō democracy.

The Seiyūkai alternated in office with its rival, the Rikken Minseitō. Seiyūkai prime ministers included Takahashi Korekiyo, Tanaka Giichi and Inukai Tsuyoshi, whose assassination in the May 15 Incident of 1932 ended party cabinets. In 1940 the party, like all others, dissolved into the Imperial Rule Assistance Association as Japan mobilised for war. A postwar party of the same name was briefly revived after 1945, and many of its politicians later joined the Liberal Democratic Party.

Ideology

The Seiyūkai was a broadly conservative party advocating constitutional government, balanced budgets (in Hara's and Takahashi's versions offset by activist public spending), and a strong but parliamentary state. It enjoyed close ties to the Mitsui zaibatsu, while the Minseitō was linked to Mitsubishi.
