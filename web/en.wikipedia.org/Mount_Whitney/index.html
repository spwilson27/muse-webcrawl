Mount Whitney

Mount Whitney is the highest mountain in the contiguous United States, with an elevation of 14,505 feet (4,421 m). It is in the Sierra Nevada range of east-central California, on the boundary between Inyo and Tulare counties, and lies just 84.6 miles (136.2 km) west-northwest of Badwater Basin in Death Valley — the lowest point in North America — making for the greatest topographic contrast over such a short distance in the contiguous US.

History

The peak was named by members of the California Geological Survey in 1864 for their chief, Josiah Whitney. Several survey climbers failed to reach the summit in the 1870s — Clarence King famously climbed nearby Mount Langley by mistake — before three fishermen from Lone Pine made the first recorded ascent on 18 August 1873. The Smithsonian Institution Shelter, a stone hut built in 1909, still stands at the summit.

Climbing routes

The Mount Whitney Trail from Whitney Portal is 22 miles (35 km) round trip with about 6,100 feet (1,859 m) of gain, and is among the most popular big-mountain hikes in the US. More technical routes include the East Face (first climbed in 1931) and the Mountaineer's Route, a steeper gully used by John Muir in 1873.

Permits

Demand far exceeds capacity: all overnight use and day hikes in the Whitney Zone during the May–November season require permits allocated by lottery, administered by the Inyo National Forest.
