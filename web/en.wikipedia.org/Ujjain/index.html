Ujjain

Ujjain is a city in the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh, situated on the eastern bank of the Shipra River. One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in India, it is counted among the Sapta Puri, the seven sacred cities of Hinduism, and is a major pilgrimage centre.

Ujjain was the capital of the ancient kingdom of Avanti and a major centre of learning and trade; tradition associates it with King Vikramaditya and the poet Kalidasa. The city passed through the hands of the Maurya, Gupta, Paramara, Delhi Sultanate, Mughal, and Maratha powers, and was the capital of the Scindia state before Gwalior.

The Mahakaleshwar Temple in Ujjain houses one of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines dedicated to Shiva. Every twelve years the city hosts the Simhastha Kumbh Mela on the Shipra, one of the largest religious gatherings in the world. The Jantar Mantar observatory here was built by Maharaja Jai Singh II in the 18th century, and Ujjain long served as the prime meridian of Indian astronomy and geography. Its population is roughly 500,000–600,000.
