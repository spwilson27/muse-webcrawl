Yaroslav the Wise

Yaroslav I "the Wise"
Grand Prince of Kyiv
Reign: 1019 – 1054
Born: c. 978
Died: 20 February 1054, Kyiv
Spouse: Ingegerd of Sweden

Yaroslav I Vladimirovich (c. 978 – 1054), called Yaroslav the Wise, was Grand Prince of Kyiv from 1019 until his death, and earlier prince of Novgorod and Rostov. His reign marks the cultural and political zenith of Kievan Rus'.

Rise to power

A son of Vladimir the Great, Yaroslav governed Novgorod and withheld tribute from his father. After Vladimir's death in 1015, a fratricidal struggle followed: Yaroslav's brother Sviatopolk "the Accursed" seized Kyiv and was implicated in the murders of Boris and Gleb, later canonised. With Novgorodian and Varangian support Yaroslav defeated Sviatopolk at the Alta River in 1019 and took the throne, though conflict with his brother Mstislav continued until a partition of Rus' along the Dnieper in 1026.

Achievements

Yaroslav promulgated the earliest East Slavic legal code, the Russkaya Pravda, founded St Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv (1037), built the Golden Gate, and promoted literacy, book copying, and church schools. In 1051 he appointed the native-born Hilarion as Metropolitan of Kyiv without Constantinople's prior approval — a landmark in church history. His diplomacy bound Rus' to Europe's royal houses: his daughters married kings of France (Anna of Kyiv became queen consort of Henry I), Norway, and Hungary, earning him the epithet "father-in-law of Europe."

Death and legacy

Yaroslav died in 1054 and was buried in St Sophia's marble sarcophagus, which still survives. His testament dividing Rus' among his sons failed to prevent later fragmentation. He is venerated as a saint in the Orthodox Church, and his portrait appears on Ukraine's two-hryvnia note.
