Marmolada is a mountain in north-eastern Italy and the highest peak of the Dolomites. Its summit, Punta Penia, rises to 3,343 metres above sea level. The mountain consists of a ridge running west to east, and carries the largest glacier in the Dolomites on its northern face.

Geography

The Marmolada glacier (Ghiacciaio della Marmolada) descends the northern slope toward the Fedaia Pass and its artificial lake. The steep south face, over 1,000 metres high, is a celebrated challenge in Alpine climbing. The mountain lies on the border between the provinces of Belluno and Trentino.

History

The summit was first climbed in 1864 by the Austrian mountaineer Paul Grohmann. During the First World War the front line between Austria-Hungary and Italy ran across the glacier; soldiers tunnelled an "ice city" into the glacier itself. On 3 July 2022 a large serac collapse on the glacier killed eleven people, an event widely linked to exceptional summer heat.
