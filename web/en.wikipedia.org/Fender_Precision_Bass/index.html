Fender Precision Bass

The Fender Precision Bass (often shortened to "P-Bass") is a model of electric bass guitar manufactured by Fender Musical Instruments Corporation. Introduced in 1951, it was the first mass-produced electric bass guitar to earn widespread attention and use, and it remains among the best-selling and most imitated electric basses of all time.

History

In the late 1940s, Leo Fender sought to create an amplified alternative to the large, quiet double bass, whose players struggled to be heard in big bands and were inconvenient to transport. Working from the design language of his Telecaster guitar, Fender produced a fretted, solid-body instrument played horizontally like a guitar. The name "Precision" referred to its fretted neck, which allowed notes to be played with precision, unlike the fretless double bass.

Design

The original 1951 model had a slab ash body, a single-coil pickup, and a 34-inch scale length — a scale that became the industry standard. In 1957 the design was substantially revised: the body gained contoured edges and a larger Stratocaster-style headstock, and the single coil was replaced by a split-coil pickup wired as a humbucker, which became the model's signature pickup configuration. Typical controls are a single volume and a single tone knob.

Legacy and users

The Precision Bass defined the sound of rock and roll, Motown, and countless other genres. Noted players include James Jamerson of Motown's Funk Brothers, whose melodic lines on a 1962 Precision ("The Funk Machine") set a standard for session playing; Donald "Duck" Dunn; Carol Kaye; John Paul Jones; Steve Harris of Iron Maiden; and Sting. Its design has been copied by nearly every guitar manufacturer, and Fender continues to produce many variants under both the Fender and Squier brands.
