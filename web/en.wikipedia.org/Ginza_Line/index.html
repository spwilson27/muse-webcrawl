The Ginza Line (銀座線) is a subway line in Tokyo, Japan, operated by Tokyo Metro. It runs between Shibuya in the southwest and Asakusa in the northeast, passing through the Ginza shopping district from which it takes its name. On maps and signage the line is coloured orange and its stations are numbered with the prefix "G".

History

The Ginza Line began as a project of businessman Noritsugu Hayakawa, who, inspired by the London Underground, founded the Tokyo Underground Railway. The first section, between Ueno and Asakusa, opened on 30 December 1927, making it the oldest subway line in Japan and in Asia. Queues of passengers eager to ride the novelty stretched around the block. The line was extended westward in stages, reaching Shibuya by 1939 after a merger with a rival company completed the route of today.

Route and operation

The 14.3-kilometre line serves 19 stations, connecting major centres including Shibuya, Omotesandō, Akasaka-mitsuke, Toranomon, Ginza, Kyōbashi, Kanda, Ueno, and Asakusa. Unlike most Tokyo Metro lines it uses standard gauge (1,435 mm) track and third-rail power at 600 V DC, a legacy of its early origins. Trains run as six-car sets, and the line is among the most heavily used in the network.
