Jerry Alan Fodor (April 22, 1935 – November 29, 2017) was an American philosopher and cognitive scientist, one of the founders of the modern philosophy of mind and of computationalism. He taught for many years at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and later at Rutgers University.

Main ideas

Fodor is best known for the language of thought hypothesis, set out in The Language of Thought (1975): thinking takes place in an internal symbolic system ("Mentalese") with compositional structure, and mental processes are computations over those representations. In The Modularity of Mind (1983) he argued that perceptual input systems are specialised, fast, mandatory, and informationally encapsulated modules, while central cognition is holistic—a position he later explored with pessimism about its tractability in The Mind Doesn't Work That Way (2000). With Ernest Lepore he criticised semantic holism, and with Massimo Piattelli-Palmarini he co-authored What Darwin Got Wrong (2010), a controversial critique of natural selection.

Influence

Fodor's representational theory of mind helped define the classical computational paradigm in cognitive science, and his arguments shaped subsequent debates about connectionism, innateness, and the architecture of cognition.
