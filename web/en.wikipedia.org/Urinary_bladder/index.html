The urinary bladder is a hollow muscular organ in humans and other vertebrates that stores urine from the kidneys before disposal by urination. Urine enters the bladder through the two ureters and exits via the urethra. An average adult bladder holds roughly 400–600 millilitres, its walls stretching as it fills, and signals of fullness are relayed through the pelvic nerves to trigger the micturition reflex.

Structure

The bladder is a roughly spherical organ situated in the lesser pelvis, behind the pubic symphysis. Its wall comprises the detrusor muscle — interlacing smooth muscle fibres — lined by transitional epithelium (urothelium) that can stretch without tearing. The trigone is a smooth triangular region at the base bounded by the two ureteric orifices and the internal urethral orifice; in males the prostate gland surrounds the bladder neck.

Function

As the bladder fills, stretch receptors signal the spinal cord and brain. Voluntary control of the external urethral sphincter allows continence; during voiding, parasympathetic impulses contract the detrusor while the sphincters relax. Disorders of this coordination produce retention or incontinence.

Clinical significance

Common conditions include cystitis (bladder inflammation, usually from bacterial infection), overactive bladder, bladder stones, and bladder cancer, which is strongly associated with smoking and occupational exposure to aromatic amines. Investigation includes urine testing, ultrasound, cystoscopy and urodynamic studies. Surgical removal (cystectomy) requires urinary diversion such as an ileal conduit or a constructed neobladder.
