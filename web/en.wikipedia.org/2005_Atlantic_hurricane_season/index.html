The 2005 Atlantic hurricane season was the most active Atlantic hurricane season in recorded history at the time, producing a record 28 named storms, 15 hurricanes and seven major hurricanes. It was the first season to exhaust the standard name list, forcing the use of Greek letter names, and it remained the costliest hurricane season on record until 2017.

Major storms

Five Category 4 or 5 hurricanes struck the United States or its neighbouring coasts. Hurricane Katrina devastated the US Gulf Coast in August, breaching the levees of New Orleans and killing more than 1,800 people; Hurricane Rita followed in September, and Hurricane Wilma in October became the most intense Atlantic hurricane ever recorded, with a minimum pressure of 882 mbar. Hurricanes Dennis, Emily and Stan also caused extensive death and destruction in the Caribbean, Florida and Central America.

Records and retirement

The names Dennis, Katrina, Rita, Stan and Wilma were retired after the season — a record five retirements. The season's activity was fuelled by exceptionally warm sea surface temperatures and favourable atmospheric conditions, and it prompted extensive debate about the relationship between hurricanes and climate change.
