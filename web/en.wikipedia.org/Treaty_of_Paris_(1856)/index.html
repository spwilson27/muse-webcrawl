Treaty of Paris (1856)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaty of Paris of 1856 brought an end to the Crimean War. It was signed on 30 March 1856 at the Congress of Paris by Russia on one side and France, the United Kingdom, the Ottoman Empire, Sardinia-Piedmont, Prussia and Austria on the other. Not to be confused with the Treaty of Paris (1763) that ended the Seven Years' War.

Terms

The treaty neutralised the Black Sea: its waters and ports were opened to merchant shipping but closed to warships, and Russia and the Ottoman Empire agreed not to maintain naval arsenals on its coasts. Russia returned southern Bessarabia's Danube mouth territory to Moldavia, gave up its claim to a protectorate over Ottoman Christians, and accepted free navigation of the Danube. The Ottoman Empire was admitted into the Concert of Europe, with its independence and territorial integrity guaranteed; the Åland Islands were demilitarised.

Aftermath

The settlement curtailed Russian influence in the Near East for a generation, but Russia repudiated the Black Sea clauses in 1870 during the Franco-Prussian War, securing their cancellation at the London Conference of 1871. The treaty is also noted for its declaration on maritime law, abolishing privateering among the signatories.
