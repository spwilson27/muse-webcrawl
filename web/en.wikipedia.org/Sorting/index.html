Sorting

FieldComputer science, operations
DefinitionArranging items in an ordered sequence

For the Harry Potter concept, see Sorting Hat. For other uses, see Sort (disambiguation).

Sorting is any process of arranging items systematically, and has two common, yet distinct meanings: ordering — arranging items in a sequence ordered by some criterion — and categorizing — grouping items with similar properties.

In computing

Sorting is one of the most fundamental operations in computer science. A sorting algorithm puts the elements of a list into an order, most commonly numerical or lexicographical. Famous algorithms include quicksort, mergesort, heapsort, insertion sort, and bubble sort. Efficient sorting matters because it optimises algorithms that require sorted input, such as searching and merging, and because sorted output is more useful to humans. Comparison-based sorting has a lower bound of Ω(n log n) comparisons in the worst case; non-comparison methods such as radix sort and counting sort can beat this bound under suitable assumptions.

In industry and logistics

Physical sorting systems separate objects by size, weight, colour, or destination — from mail and parcels travelling through automated sorting centres, to ore and recycling streams separated by mechanical and optical sorters, and agricultural produce graded by quality.

By hand

Everyday sorting — laundry, coins, playing cards — illustrates the same principles of classification and ordering that formal algorithms implement at scale.
