Turin (Italian: Torino) is a city and an important business and cultural centre in northern Italy. It is the capital city of Piedmont and of the Metropolitan City of Turin, and was the first capital of the unified Kingdom of Italy from 1861 to 1865.

History

Founded as a Roman colony, Augusta Taurinorum, at the confluence of the Po and Dora Riparia rivers, Turin became the principal seat of the House of Savoy, which made it capital of the Duchy of Savoy and later of the Kingdom of Sardinia. From Turin the Savoy kings directed the unification of Italy, and Vittorio Emanuele II became the first king of Italy in 1861. The city's grand boulevards, arcades and baroque palaces — including the Venaria Reale and other Savoy residences, now UNESCO World Heritage sites — date largely from this period.

Industry and culture

In the twentieth century Turin became Italy's automotive capital as the home of Fiat (founded 1899), and later of brands within the Stellantis group. Its landmarks include the Mole Antonelliana, symbol of the city and now housing the National Museum of Cinema, and the Turin Cathedral, which houses the Shroud of Turin. The city hosts two famous football clubs, Juventus and Torino, and hosted the 2006 Winter Olympics. Turin is also associated with vermouth, the chocolate-hazelnut spread tradition of gianduia, and the Slow Food movement founded in nearby Bra.
