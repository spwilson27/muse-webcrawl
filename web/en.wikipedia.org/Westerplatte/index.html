Westerplatte
Location: Gdańsk, Poland
Known for: Battle of Westerplatte, 1–7 September 1939

Westerplatte is a peninsula in Gdańsk, Poland, at the mouth of the Dead Vistula on the Baltic coast. It is famous as the site of the first battle of the Second World War in Europe: the defence of the Polish Military Transit Depot against German forces in September 1939.

Battle of Westerplatte

At 04:47 on 1 September 1939, the German battleship Schleswig-Holstein opened fire on the depot, beginning the war. The garrison of about 180–210 Polish soldiers under Major Henryk Sucharski and Captain Franciszek Dąbrowski held out for seven days against some 3,400 German troops supported by dive bombers, naval gunfire, and artillery, surrendering only on 7 September when ammunition and medical supplies were exhausted. The defence became a symbol of Polish resistance; the phrase "Westerplatte still defends itself" was broadcast to encourage the nation.

Memorial

The site is preserved as a museum and memorial, dominated by the 25-metre Monument of the Coast Defenders, unveiled in 1966. A small military cemetery holds the defenders' remains, including Sucharski's, transferred there in 1971.
