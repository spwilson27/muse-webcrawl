Roger Linn

Roger Linn (born August 24, 1955) is an American designer of electronic musical instruments and equipment. He is known as the designer of the LM-1, the first drum machine to use samples of acoustic drums, and the LinnDrum, as well as the co-designer of the Akai MPC60, an iconic instrument in the development of hip hop music.

Career

Linn founded Linn Electronics and in 1979 introduced the LM-1 Drum Computer, which played digitally sampled sounds of real drums and introduced features such as swing and shuffle quantization that shaped popular music production. Its successor, the LinnDrum (1982), was used on countless hit records of the 1980s. Linn later collaborated with Akai Professional on the MPC60 (1988) and MPC3000 (1994) sampling workstations designed with musician and producer Roger Linn's input on groove-oriented sequencing.

Through his design company Roger Linn Design, he later released products such as the AdrenaLinn effects processor for guitar and, with Dave Smith, the Tempest analog drum machine. In 2014 he released the LinnStrument, an expressive MIDI controller with a grid of pressure- and movement-sensitive pads.

Influence

Linn's instruments are credited with helping define the sound of pop, rock, and hip hop from the 1980s onward; artists such as Prince made extensive use of the LM-1 and LinnDrum.
