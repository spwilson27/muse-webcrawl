Mime artist

A mime artist (from Greek mimos, "imitator") is a performer who uses silent, exaggerated physical movement and gesture to tell stories or portray characters, a theatrical technique known as mime or pantomime.

History

Mime dates to ancient Greek and Roman theatre and flourished in the Italian commedia dell'arte and the 19th-century Parisian pantomime of Jean-Gaspard Deburau. In the 20th century, Étienne Decroux developed "corporeal mime", and his student Marcel Marceau became the world's most famous mime with his character Bip.

Technique

Mimes create the illusion of interacting with invisible objects — walls, ropes, wind, staircases — through precise body control, isolation of movements, and fixed facial expression. Street mime and statue mime are common forms of busking performance.
