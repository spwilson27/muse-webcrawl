X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) is a surface-sensitive quantitative spectroscopic technique in chemistry that measures the elemental composition, empirical formula, chemical state and electronic state of elements within a material.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy

Also known as: ESCA
Developed by: Kai Siegbahn (Nobel Prize in Physics 1981)

Principle

The sample is irradiated with X-rays; photoelectrons ejected by the photoelectric effect are measured by kinetic energy. Binding energy = hν − KE − work function. Chemical shifts reveal oxidation states and bonding. Only electrons from the top ~10 nm escape without energy loss, making XPS surface-specific.

Applications

Catalysts, polymers, corrosion, semiconductors and thin films. Kai Siegbahn received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1981 for high-resolution electron spectroscopy.
