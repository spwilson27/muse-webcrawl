Strings of Life

"Strings of Life" is a 1987 electronic dance music track by Rhythim Is Rhythim, the project of Detroit producer Derrick May, with pianist Michael James. Built around a driving, percussive piano riff played by James over pounding house rhythms, it became one of the most celebrated and influential records of early Detroit techno and a defining anthem of the UK's late-1980s acid house and rave explosion.

Background and release

May, one of the "Belleville Three" founders of Detroit techno, recorded the track with James, whose classically inflected piano improvisation supplied its signature staccato piano motif. Released on May's Transmat label in 1987, the record's title came from a remark attributed to a friend of May about the music's big, emotional sound. Its dramatic strings-like piano stabs — actually a piano sequence processed and layered — gave the track its name and its euphoric character.

Reception and legacy

"Strings of Life" was a major hit in British clubs during the 1988 "Second Summer of Love", and it has repeatedly been ranked among the greatest dance tracks of all time in polls by publications such as Mixmag and DJ Magazine. It has been remixed and re-released many times, including a 2004 version by Soul Central as "Strings of Life (Stronger on My Own)" that reached the UK top ten. The track is credited with helping to launch techno's move from Detroit's underground to international dance culture.
