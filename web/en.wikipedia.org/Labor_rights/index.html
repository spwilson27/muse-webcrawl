Labor rights

Labor rights
Core standardsFreedom of association; collective bargaining; abolition of forced and child labor; non-discrimination
Key bodyInternational Labour Organization (ILO)

Labor rights are a group of legal rights and claimed human rights having to do with labor relations between workers and their employers, usually obtained under labor and employment law. They include the right to organize in trade unions, to bargain collectively, to a minimum wage, to safe working conditions, and to be free from forced labor, child labor and employment discrimination.

Historical development

Labor rights emerged with the industrial revolution, as reformers and unions campaigned against child labor, excessive hours and dangerous factories. Nineteenth-century factory acts in Britain, the eight-hour-day movement, and the growth of mass unions gradually established the principle that workers are entitled to legal protections beyond the contract of employment.

International standards

The International Labour Organization, founded in 1919, sets international labor standards through conventions. Its 1998 Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work identifies core standards: freedom of association and collective bargaining, elimination of forced labor, abolition of child labor, elimination of discrimination, and — added in 2022 — a safe and healthy working environment. Labor rights also appear in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Article 23) and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights.

Core topics

  • Freedom of association and the right to strike
  • Minimum wage and working time
  • Occupational safety and health
  • Protection against unfair dismissal
  • Equality and non-discrimination at work

Criticism and debate

Labor rights remain contested: employers' groups argue some regulations burden growth and informal employment, while unions and rights advocates point to enforcement gaps, especially in global supply chains and the gig economy.
