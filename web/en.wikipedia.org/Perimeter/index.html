Perimeter

The perimeter of a polygon is the sum of the lengths of its sides.

A perimeter is a closed path that encompasses, surrounds, or outlines either a two-dimensional shape or a one-dimensional length. The perimeter of a circle or an ellipse is called its circumference. Calculating the perimeter has several practical applications, such as determining the length of fence required to enclose a field or garden.

Formulas

For a polygon, the perimeter is the sum of the lengths of its sides. A rectangle with length l and width w has perimeter 2(l + w); a square of side s has perimeter 4s; a regular polygon with n sides of length s has perimeter ns. The circumference of a circle of radius r is 2πr. Curves more generally are measured by arc length.

Properties

The isoperimetric inequality states that among all closed plane curves of a given perimeter, the circle encloses the largest area. Perimeter is measured in units of length, unlike area, which is measured in square units. In geography and everyday usage, "perimeter" can also denote a boundary itself, as in a security perimeter.
