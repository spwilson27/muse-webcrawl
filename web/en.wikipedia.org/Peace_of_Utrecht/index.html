Peace of Utrecht

The Peace of Utrecht was a series of treaties signed in the Dutch city of Utrecht in 1713–1714 (with related settlements at Rastatt and Baden) that ended the War of the Spanish Succession.

Terms

Philip V, grandson of Louis XIV, was recognised as King of Spain but renounced any union of the French and Spanish crowns. Spain ceded Gibraltar and Minorca to Britain, the Spanish Netherlands and Italian possessions to Austria, and Sicily to Savoy. Britain also gained Newfoundland, Acadia (Nova Scotia), and Hudson Bay territories from France, recognition of the Protestant succession, and the asiento contract to supply enslaved Africans to Spanish America.

Significance

Utrecht established the balance of power as an explicit principle of European diplomacy, curbed French expansion, and confirmed Britain's rise as the leading maritime and colonial power. The treaties shaped European politics until the mid-18th century.
