Spätburgunder is the German name for the red wine grape variety known internationally as Pinot noir. The name means "late Burgundian", referring to its origin in Burgundy and its relatively late ripening. Germany is one of the world's largest producers of Pinot noir, and the variety is the country's most planted red grape.

Viticulture in Germany

Spätburgunder thrives in Germany's cooler continental climate, particularly on the limestone and volcanic soils of Baden, the steep slate slopes of the Ahr — a region famed almost exclusively for its red wines — and in the Pfalz and Rheinhessen. Historical plantings used high-yielding clones, but since the 1990s quality-minded growers have turned to low-yield French clones, producing wines of much greater depth.

Wine styles

Traditional German Spätburgunder was pale and light, but modern examples are riper, deeper and often aged in small oak barrels (Barrique). The grape is also used for Weißherbst rosé and, increasingly, for blanc de noirs white wines and the base of Sekt sparkling wines. Dry styles predominate, and top estates in the VDP association market their best wines as Grosses Gewächs.
