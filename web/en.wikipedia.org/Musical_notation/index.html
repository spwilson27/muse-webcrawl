Modern staff notation: notes placed on a five-line staff indicate pitch and duration.

Musical notation is any system used to visually represent aurally perceived music, played with instruments or sung by the human voice, through the use of written, printed, or otherwise-produced symbols. Modern Western notation places noteheads on a five-line staff; vertical position indicates pitch and the shape of the note indicates its rhythmic duration.

History

Notational systems existed in ancient Mesopotamia, Greece, and China. In medieval Europe, neumes indicated melodic contour for chant; Guido d'Arezzo (c. 991–after 1033) is credited with the four-line staff and solmisation syllables that led to modern practice. Mensural notation developed rhythmic precision in the 13th–16th centuries, and by the Baroque era the essentials of modern notation — five-line staff, bar lines, key signatures — were in place.

Elements of modern notation

  • Clefs (treble, bass, alto, tenor) fix the pitches of the staff lines.
  • Notes and rests use filled/unfilled heads, stems, flags and beams to show duration.
  • Accidentals (sharps, flats, naturals) modify pitch; key signatures state the prevailing sharps or flats.
  • Time signatures specify metre; tempo marks, dynamics (p, f, crescendo) and articulation marks guide performance.

Other systems

Other traditions and purposes use tablature (fingering diagrams for lute and guitar), figured bass, graphic scores, Braille music notation, and Chinese jianpu numbered notation.
