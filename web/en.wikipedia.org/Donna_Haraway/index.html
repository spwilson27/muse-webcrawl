Donna Haraway


Donna J. Haraway, scholar of science and technology studies.

Donna J. Haraway (born September 6, 1944) is an American scholar and professor emerita in the History of Consciousness and Feminist Studies departments at the University of California, Santa Cruz. She is a leading figure in science and technology studies, feminist theory, and the philosophy of science, best known for her 1985 essay "A Cyborg Manifesto."

Life and career

Haraway was born in Denver, Colorado. She studied zoology, philosophy, and English literature at Colorado College, and completed a Fulbright fellowship in Paris before earning a PhD in biology from Yale University in 1972. Her dissertation examined the role of metaphors of organicism in twentieth-century developmental biology. She taught at the University of Hawaii and Johns Hopkins University before joining UC Santa Cruz in 1984.

Work

Her book Primate Visions (1989) examined the history of primatology through feminist and postcolonial lenses. In "A Cyborg Manifesto," published in Socialist Review, she proposed the cyborg as a figure dissolving boundaries between human and machine, organism and artifact, and physical and non-physical. Later works including Modest_Witness (1997), The Companion Species Manifesto (2003), When Species Meet (2008), and Staying with the Trouble (2016) developed themes of multispecies kinship, the Chthulucene, and "making kin" in response to the Anthropocene.
