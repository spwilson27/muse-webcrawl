Telenet

Telenet was an American commercial packet-switched computer network which went into service in 1975. It was established by Bolt Beranek and Newman (BBN), the company that had built the ARPANET, and was one of the first public data networks in the world, offering dial-up access from hundreds of cities to time-sharing computers and databases.

History

Telenet was conceived by BBN executive Larry Roberts, often called the father of the ARPANET, who set out to commercialise packet switching. The network used X.25 protocols and dedicated lines leased from telephone carriers. Subscribers could connect with a local telephone call and then reach host computers across the United States and, through international gateways, in dozens of other countries. Sprint acquired Telenet in 1979, renaming it SprintNet, and the service became a backbone for early online services, corporate networks and e-mail systems before the rise of the public Internet.

The Belgian company

The name Telenet is also used by an unrelated Belgian telecommunications company, founded in 1996, which provides cable television, broadband internet and mobile services in Flanders and Brussels. It is a subsidiary of Liberty Global.
