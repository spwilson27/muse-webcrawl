Yenisei River

The Yenisei River (Russian: Енисе́й, Yenisey) is the largest river system flowing to the Arctic Ocean and the fifth-longest river system in the world. Rising in Mongolia and Tuva, it flows northward through Siberia for about 3,487 km (2,167 mi) — 5,539 km (3,442 mi) including its longest headstream, the Angara–Selenga system — and empties into the Kara Sea.

Length3,487 km (2,167 mi); 5,539 km with Angara–Selenga
SourceConfluence of the Great and Little Yenisei at Kyzyl, Tuva
MouthYenisei Gulf, Kara Sea (Arctic Ocean)
DischargeAbout 19,800 m³/s — the largest of any Russian river
Major tributariesAngara, Stony Tunguska, Lower Tunguska

Course

The Yenisei forms at Kyzyl — near the geographical centre of Asia — and cuts through the Sayan Mountains in deep gorges now dammed by the Sayano-Shushensk (Russia's largest power plant) and Krasnoyarsk hydroelectric stations. At Krasnoyarsk, a city of over a million, it turns across the West Siberian Plain, receiving the Angara (the outflow of Lake Baikal, joining near Strelka) and the two Tunguska rivers, then flows past the port of Igarka and Dudinka before widening into the 600-km Yenisei Gulf estuary.

History and significance

The river valley was home to Ket and other Yeniseian-speaking peoples and to the Yenisei Kyrgyz khaganate. Russian Cossacks reached the river in the early 1600s, founding Krasnoyarsk in 1628; the river became Siberia's great north–south highway, used by Arctic convoys and, historically, by the "tea road" trade from Kyakhta. The catastrophic 1908 Tunguska event occurred in a tributary basin. In the Soviet era the river was central to Norilsk's nickel industry (shipped via Dudinka) and to grand hydroelectric development; the lower river forms part of the Northern Sea Route's hinterland.
