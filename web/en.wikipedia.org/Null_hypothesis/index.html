Null hypothesis

The null hypothesis (often denoted H0) is the claim in scientific research that the effect being studied does not exist — that no relationship or difference is present between specified populations. It is the default position assumed true unless statistical evidence indicates otherwise.

Statistical hypothesis testing

In significance testing, data are collected and the probability (the p-value) of observing results at least as extreme as those obtained, assuming the null hypothesis is true, is computed. If the p-value falls below a chosen significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected in favour of the alternative hypothesis.

History

The concept was developed in the 1920s and 1930s through the work of Ronald Fisher, who introduced significance testing, and Jerzy Neyman and Egon Pearson, who formalised the alternative hypothesis and the notions of Type I and Type II errors.

Criticism

The null hypothesis framework has been criticised for encouraging dichotomous thinking and for the common misinterpretation of p-values; alternatives include confidence intervals, effect sizes, and Bayesian methods.
