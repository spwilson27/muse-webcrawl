Kuwait, officially the State of Kuwait, is a country in West Asia at the northern edge of the Arabian Peninsula, bordered by Iraq to the north and Saudi Arabia to the south, with a coastline on the Persian Gulf. Its capital is Kuwait City.

History

The Al Sabah family has ruled since the 18th century; Kuwait was a British protectorate from 1899 until independence in 1961. Oil, discovered in 1938 at Burgan, transformed the small trading and pearling port into one of the world's richest states per capita. In August 1990 Iraq under Saddam Hussein invaded and annexed Kuwait, prompting the Gulf War, in which a US-led coalition liberated the country in early 1991.

Economy

Kuwait holds about 6% of world oil reserves; petroleum accounts for roughly half of GDP and most government revenue. The Kuwaiti dinar is the world's highest-valued currency unit. The sovereign wealth fund, the Kuwait Investment Authority (founded 1953), is among the oldest and largest.
