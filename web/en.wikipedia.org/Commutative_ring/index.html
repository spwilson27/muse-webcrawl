Commutative ring

Commutative ring
Field: abstract algebra
Examples: integers, polynomial rings, fields
Study: commutative algebra; algebraic geometry

In mathematics, a commutative ring is a ring in which multiplication is commutative: for all elements a and b, ab = ba. The study of commutative rings, called commutative algebra, is a foundation of modern algebraic geometry and number theory.

Definition

A ring is a set equipped with addition and multiplication satisfying the field axioms except that multiplicative inverses need not exist. Commutative rings are usually assumed to have a multiplicative identity 1 ≠ 0. Examples include the integers ℤ, the rational, real and complex numbers, and polynomial rings R[x].

Ideals and prime ideals

Subsets called ideals, closed under absorption by arbitrary ring elements, play the role that normal subgroups play for groups. Prime and maximal ideals generalise prime numbers; the set of prime ideals forms the spectrum of the ring, the basic object of algebraic geometry.

Important classes

Noetherian rings, in which every ideal is finitely generated, were systematised by Emmy Noether. Other key classes include principal ideal domains, unique factorisation domains, local rings and Artinian rings.
