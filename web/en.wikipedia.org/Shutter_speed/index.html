Shutter speed

In photography, shutter speed or exposure time is the length of time that the film or digital sensor inside a camera is exposed to light when taking a photograph. The quantity of light that reaches the film or sensor is proportional to the exposure time. Shutter speed is one of the three elements of the exposure triangle, along with aperture and ISO sensitivity.

Measurement

Shutter speeds are measured in seconds or fractions of a second. Conventional steps include 1/1000, 1/500, 1/250, 1/125, 1/60, 1/30, 1/15, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, and 1 second; each step halves or doubles the amount of light. Cameras also offer "bulb" mode, in which the shutter remains open as long as the release is held, for long exposures.

Effects on image

Fast shutter speeds freeze motion, making them suitable for sports and wildlife photography. Slow shutter speeds allow moving subjects to blur, which can convey motion — for example, silky water in landscape photography — but they also increase the risk of blur from camera shake, often requiring a tripod. For handheld shooting, a common rule of thumb is to use a shutter speed no slower than the reciprocal of the focal length.

Creative use

Photographers use long exposures for night photography, light trails, and star trails, and techniques such as panning, in which the camera follows a moving subject at a moderate shutter speed so the subject remains sharp against a blurred background.
