Jean-Paul Charles Belmondo (1933–2021) was a French actor, one of the biggest stars of French cinema of the 20th century. He rose to fame as the face of the French New Wave with Jean-Luc Godard's Breathless (1960), and later became a hugely popular action and comedy star, famous for performing his own stunts.

Career

The son of sculptor Paul Belmondo, he trained at the Paris Conservatoire. After Breathless he worked with Godard (Pierrot le Fou), Jean-Pierre Melville (Léon Morin, Priest) and François Truffaut. From the 1970s his commercial hits — Borsalino, The Professional (1981), Ace of Aces — made him a box-office rival to Alain Delon. He won the César Award for Best Actor for Itinéraire d'un enfant gâté (1988).

Later life and honours

After a stroke in 2001, Belmondo largely retired. He received an honorary Palme d'Or at Cannes in 2011 and a national homage at Les Invalides following his death in 2021.
