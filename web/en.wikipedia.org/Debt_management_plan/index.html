Debt management plan


A household budget used to build a debt management plan

A debt management plan (DMP) is an informal arrangement between a debtor and their creditors to repay unsecured debts, usually at a reduced monthly rate, often with interest or charges frozen or reduced. Plans are commonly administered by credit counseling organizations, which negotiate with creditors on the debtor's behalf and distribute a single monthly payment among them.

How it works

A credit counselor reviews the debtor's income, expenses, and debts, and proposes an affordable monthly payment. If creditors agree, the debtor makes one payment to the agency, which then pays each creditor. A typical plan lasts three to five years. Because a DMP is informal in many jurisdictions, creditors are generally not legally bound to accept it, though participation is widespread among major lenders.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include a single regular payment, reduced interest, and an end to most creditor contact. Disadvantages include possible fees, the requirement to close credit accounts, the multi-year commitment, and the fact that secured debts and most student loans are not covered. In the United States, nonprofit agencies offering DMPs are often members of the National Foundation for Credit Counseling.
