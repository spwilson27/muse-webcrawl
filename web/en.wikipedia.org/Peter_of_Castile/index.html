Peter of Castile

Peter (Spanish: Pedro; August 30, 1334 – March 23, 1369), called Peter the Cruel (el Cruel) or Peter the Just (el Justo), was King of Castile and León from 1350 until his death.

Reign

The son of Alfonso XI and Maria of Portugal, Peter's reign was marked by conflict with the nobility and a prolonged civil war against his illegitimate half-brother Henry of Trastámara, who was supported by France. Peter in turn received English support, including Edward the Black Prince's victory at Nájera in 1367.

Death and reputation

Peter was defeated at the Battle of Montiel in 1369 and killed shortly afterward by Henry, who succeeded him as Henry II. Later chroniclers and propaganda under the Trastámaras cemented the epithet "the Cruel", though others have remembered him as a defender of royal justice and of minorities, including the courtier Samuel ha-Levi.
