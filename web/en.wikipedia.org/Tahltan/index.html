Tahltan

The Tahltan are a First Nations people of the Athabaskan-speaking ethnolinguistic group who live in the Stikine River country of northwestern British Columbia, Canada. Their traditional territory, centred on the upper Stikine River and including the communities of Telegraph Creek, Dease Lake, and Iskut, spans roughly 95,000 square kilometres and is known to them as the Sacred Headwaters—the source of the Stikine, Nass, and Skeena rivers.

Culture and society

Tahltan society is organized into matrilineal clans of the Raven (Tsesk'iya) and Wolf (Chiyone) phratries, with hereditary chiefs and clan ownership of hunting and fishing territories. Historically the Tahltan were renowned traders, controlling the obsidian from the Mount Edziza complex—prized for tool-making—and exchanging it widely, while trading coastal goods such as eulachon oil from the Tlingit to interior peoples. Salmon from the Stikine, caribou, moose, and mountain sheep formed the basis of the subsistence economy.

History

Contact with Europeans intensified in the 19th century through the fur trade and the Stikine and Klondike gold rushes, which brought epidemics that severely reduced the Tahltan population. The Tahltan asserted their sovereignty in the Declaration of the Tahltan Tribe of 1910, addressed to the government of British Columbia, which affirmed that their land had never been ceded. The modern Tahltan Central Government represents the Tahltan Nation in negotiations with provincial and federal governments.

Contemporary issues

The Tahltan have been prominent in debates over resource development in the Sacred Headwaters (Klappan), successfully opposing coalbed methane development there, while also entering agreements over major mines in their territory. The Tahltan language (Tałtan Ẕāke), an endangered Northern Athabaskan language, is the subject of revitalization efforts, including immersion programming and documentation projects.
