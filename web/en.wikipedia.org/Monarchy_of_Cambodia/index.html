The monarchy of Cambodia is the constitutional monarchy of the Kingdom of Cambodia. The King of Cambodia is the head of state, a symbol of national unity who "reigns but does not govern" under the constitution of 1993. Unusually, the Cambodian monarchy is elective: the king is chosen for life by the Royal Council of the Throne from among male descendants of King Ang Duong aged at least 30. The reigning king is Norodom Sihamoni, who succeeded his father Norodom Sihanouk in 2004.

History

Cambodian kingship traces its origins to the early kingdoms of Funan and Chenla and to the Khmer Empire founded by Jayavarman II in 802, whose god-kings (devaraja) built the temples of Angkor. After the decline of Angkor, the kingdom passed through capitals at Longvek and Oudong under pressure from Siam and Vietnam, until Cambodia became a French protectorate in 1863. King Norodom moved the capital to Phnom Penh in 1866.

The modern monarchy was reshaped by Norodom Sihanouk, crowned in 1941, who led Cambodia to independence from France in 1953, abdicated in 1955 to enter politics, and ruled as head of state until overthrown in 1970. The monarchy was abolished during the Khmer Republic (1970–1975) and the Khmer Rouge period (1975–1979) and remained in abeyance under the Vietnamese-backed regime. The 1991 Paris Peace Agreements and the 1993 constitution restored the monarchy, with Sihanouk again as king. Citing ill health, he abdicated in October 2004; the Royal Council of the Throne selected his son Sihamoni, a former ballet dancer and cultural ambassador, who was crowned on 29 October 2004.

Constitutional role

Under the 1993 constitution the king is head of state for life, inviolable, and commander-in-chief of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces. He appoints the prime minister and Council of Ministers upon the recommendation of the National Assembly, promulgates laws, grants pardons and amnesties, accredits ambassadors, and confers national decorations, but exercises no governing power. The constitution declares that "the King of Cambodia shall reign but shall not govern."

Royal Council of the Throne

The council, chaired by the prime minister, includes the presidents of the Senate and National Assembly, senior officials, and the heads of the Mahanikay and Dhammayuttika Buddhist orders. Within seven days of a king's death or abdication it selects a successor from the eligible royal lineages descended from Ang Duong.
