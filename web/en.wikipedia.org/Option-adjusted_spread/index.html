Option-adjusted spread

The option-adjusted spread (OAS) is a measure of the spread of a fixed-income security's yield over a benchmark yield curve, adjusted to account for the value of embedded options such as call or prepayment options. It is widely used to compare mortgage-backed securities and callable bonds with option-free benchmarks.

Definition

The OAS is the constant spread added to each rate along a set of simulated interest-rate paths such that the average present value of the bond's cash flows across those paths equals its market price. Unlike the Z-spread, the OAS removes the portion of the spread attributable to optionality: for callable bonds the OAS is lower than the Z-spread, because investors are compensated for the option they have sold to the issuer.

Use

Analysts use the OAS to judge relative value: a higher OAS than comparable securities may indicate undervaluation, though comparisons depend on the interest-rate model and volatility assumptions used.
