Energy conversion efficiency

Energy conversion efficiency (η) is the ratio between the useful output of an energy conversion machine and the input, in energy terms. It is a dimensionless number between 0 and 1 (or expressed as a percentage) for any single conversion step.

Definition

Formally, η = Pout/Pin, where Pout is the useful power output and Pin is the total input power. Because of the second law of thermodynamics, no heat engine can exceed the Carnot efficiency, and real devices lose energy through friction, waste heat, and other irreversibilities.

Examples

Typical efficiencies include roughly 20–30% for internal combustion car engines, up to about 60% for modern combined-cycle gas turbines, over 90% for electric motors, and 15–23% for commercial photovoltaic panels. Efficiency chains multiply: converting fuel to electricity to light, for example, compounds the losses of each step.
