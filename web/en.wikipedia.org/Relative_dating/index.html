Relative dating

Relative dating is the science of determining the relative order of past events — that is, the age of an object in comparison to another — without necessarily determining their absolute age. It is a foundational technique in geology, palaeontology and archaeology.

Principles

Geologists use principles codified from the work of Nicolas Steno and others: the principle of superposition (younger strata lie above older ones in undisturbed sequences), original horizontality, cross-cutting relationships, inclusions, and faunal succession, the observation that fossil assemblages succeed one another in a predictable order. Together these allow rock units and events to be placed in sequence. Relative dating contrasts with absolute dating methods such as radiometric dating, which assign numerical ages.
