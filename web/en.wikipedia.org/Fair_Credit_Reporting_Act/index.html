Fair Credit Reporting Act

EnactedOctober 26, 1970
Citation15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.
Administered byFederal Trade Commission; Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) is a United States federal law enacted in 1970 that regulates the collection, dissemination, and use of consumer information, including consumer credit information.

Provisions

The Act promotes the accuracy, fairness, and privacy of information in the files of consumer reporting agencies. Consumers have the right to know what is in their file, to dispute incomplete or inaccurate information, and to limit access to their reports to parties with a permissible purpose, such as creditors, insurers, and employers. Consumer reporting agencies must follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy and must remove or correct inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable information.

Amendments and enforcement

The FCRA was substantially amended by the Fair and Accurate Credit Transactions Act of 2003, which gave consumers the right to free annual credit reports. Enforcement is shared by the Federal Trade Commission and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, and consumers may sue for negligent or wilful noncompliance.
