Wilpena Pound is a natural amphitheatre of mountains in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia, within the Ikara–Flinders Ranges National Park. From the air and from surrounding peaks, its encircling ranges form a vast, roughly elliptical bowl, entered only through the narrow Wilpena Gorge.

Geology

The Pound is formed by two synclines of hard quartzite of the Pound Subgroup, tilted and eroded so that the resistant rock rims a flat-floored valley of softer sediments. The surrounding ranges include St Mary Peak (Ngarri Mudlanha), at 1,171 metres the highest point in the Flinders Ranges. The rocks were laid down in the Adelaide Geosyncline in the late Precambrian and folded during the Delamerian Orogeny around 500 million years ago.

History

The Adnyamathanha people are the traditional owners; in their language the place is Ikara, "meeting place", and the peaks feature in Yura Muda (Dreaming) stories. The name "Wilpena" derives from a nearby pastoral station, and the Pound's floor was farmed and grazed by Europeans from the 1850s until the early 20th century, when flooding and the difficulty of access ended cultivation; a homestead and the grave of the Hill family remain. The area is now a major tourist destination, with a resort at its entrance and walking tracks to lookouts including Wangarra and to St Mary Peak, access to which is managed in consultation with the Adnyamathanha.
