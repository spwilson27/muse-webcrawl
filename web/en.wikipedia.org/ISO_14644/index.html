ISO 14644


A semiconductor fabrication cleanroom.

ISO 14644 is an international standard, "Cleanrooms and associated controlled environments", published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO). It specifies classes of air cleanliness in terms of the number of particles per cubic metre at specified particle sizes, and the standard test methods and requirements for the design, operation, and monitoring of cleanrooms and clean zones.

Classification

ISO 14644-1 classifies cleanrooms from ISO Class 1 (cleanest) to ISO Class 9 (equivalent to ordinary room air). The class is determined by the maximum permitted concentration of airborne particles at sizes from 0.1 µm to 5 µm. For example, ISO Class 5 (comparable to the former US FED STD 209E Class 100) allows at most 3,520 particles per cubic metre at 0.5 µm. Classes are certified in "as-built", "at-rest", or "operational" occupancy states.

Parts of the standard

  • ISO 14644-1 — Classification of air cleanliness by particle concentration (1999; revised 2015).
  • ISO 14644-2 — Monitoring to provide evidence of cleanroom performance.
  • ISO 14644-3 — Test methods.
  • ISO 14644-4 — Design, construction and start-up; further parts address operations, separative devices, surface cleanliness, and energy efficiency.

History

ISO 14644-1 was published in 1999 and largely superseded the U.S. Federal Standard 209E, which was cancelled in 2001. It underpins cleanroom practice in semiconductor fabrication, pharmaceuticals (together with EU GMP Annex 1), biotechnology, medical devices, and aerospace.
