The Royal Society of Chemistry (RSC) is a learned society and professional body for chemists in the United Kingdom and internationally. Founded in its present form in 1980 through the merger of the Chemical Society, the Royal Institute of Chemistry, the Faraday Society and the Society for Analytical Chemistry, it traces its origins to the Chemical Society of London founded in 1841, making it one of the oldest chemical societies in the world.

Activities

The RSC is one of the world's largest publishers of chemistry research, producing around 50 peer-reviewed journals — including Chemical Science, Chemical Communications and Chemical Society Reviews — together with books, databases such as ChemSpider, and the magazine Chemistry World. As a professional body it accredits university chemistry degrees, maintains registers of Chartered Chemists (CChem) and Chartered Scientists, and sets standards for professional practice.

The society campaigns on science policy, education and the public understanding of chemistry, convenes conferences and interest groups across all branches of the science, and administers prizes including the Harrison-Meldola Memorial Prizes and its annual portfolio of over 100 awards.

History

The Chemical Society was founded in 1841 by 77 scientists including Thomas Graham, its first president. Professional qualifications developed through the separate Institute of Chemistry (1877). The 1980 amalgamation united the learned-society and professional functions under a new royal charter, and the society established its publishing headquarters at Thomas Graham House on the Cambridge Science Park in 1989.
