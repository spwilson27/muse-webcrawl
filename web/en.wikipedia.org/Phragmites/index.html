Phragmites

Phragmites is a genus of four species of large perennial reed grasses found in wetlands throughout temperate and tropical regions of the world. The best known species is the common reed, Phragmites australis, one of the most widely distributed flowering plants on Earth.

Description

Common reed grows 2–6 metres (6.5–20 ft) tall, with long, broad leaves and hollow stems. In late summer it produces large, feathery purple-brown flower plumes that fade to silvery white. It spreads aggressively by rhizomes and by wind-dispersed seed, forming dense stands in marshes, lake shores, ditches and estuaries.

Ecology and invasiveness

Native stands provide habitat and water purification, but an introduced Eurasian lineage in North America is considered highly invasive, crowding out native wetland plants and altering marsh hydrology. Management by cutting, flooding, herbicide and prescribed burning is widespread but difficult.

Uses

Reeds have been used for thatching, mats, pens, arrows and musical instruments, and the young shoots and rhizomes are edible. In constructed wetlands, Phragmites is planted for wastewater treatment.
