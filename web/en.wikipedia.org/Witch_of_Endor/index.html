Witch of Endor

The Witch of Endor is a woman described in the First Book of Samuel (chapter 28) of the Hebrew Bible, who, at the request of King Saul, summoned the spirit of the dead prophet Samuel on the eve of Saul's final battle. In the King James Version she is called "a woman that hath a familiar spirit at Endor"; in Hebrew she is described as a mistress of an ʾov, a spirit or ghost.

The biblical account

Saul, facing the Philistine army at Mount Gilboa and receiving no answer from God by dreams, Urim, or prophets, ordered his servants to find a medium — despite having earlier expelled mediums and necromancers from Israel. Disguised, he visited the woman at Endor by night. After reassuring her she would not be punished, she conjured up an old man in a mantle, whom Saul perceived to be Samuel. The spirit reproached Saul for disturbing him and foretold that Israel would be delivered into Philistine hands and that Saul and his sons would be with Samuel the next day — a prophecy fulfilled in the battle where Saul and Jonathan died.

Interpretation

Theologians have long debated the passage: whether the apparition was truly Samuel, a demon impersonating him, or a fraud by the woman — who, notably, is portrayed sympathetically, comforting the fainting king and feeding him his last meal. The narrative is one of the few biblical passages describing necromancy and has been cited throughout debates about witchcraft, spiritualism, and the state of the dead.

In culture

The episode inspired art, Handel's oratorio Saul, and numerous literary retellings, and "the Witch of Endor" remains the archetype of the necromancer in Western culture.
