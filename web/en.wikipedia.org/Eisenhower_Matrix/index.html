Eisenhower Matrix

The Eisenhower Matrix: tasks sorted by urgency and importance

The Eisenhower Matrix, also known as the Eisenhower Box or Urgent-Important Matrix, is a time management and decision-making framework that helps prioritise tasks by urgency and importance. It is named after Dwight D. Eisenhower, the 34th president of the United States, who was quoted as saying, "I have two kinds of problems, the urgent and the important. The urgent are not important, and the important are never urgent." Stephen Covey popularised the method in his book The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People.

The four quadrants

UrgentNot urgent
ImportantQuadrant I: Do first — crises, deadlines, pressing problemsQuadrant II: Schedule — planning, prevention, personal development
Not importantQuadrant III: Delegate — interruptions, some meetings and callsQuadrant IV: Eliminate — time-wasting activities

The framework encourages spending more time in Quadrant II — important but not urgent activities — since these contribute most to long-term goals, while reducing time spent on tasks that are neither urgent nor important.
