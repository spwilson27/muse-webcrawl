Swedish Academy

The Swedish Academy (Swedish: Svenska Akademien), founded in 1786 by King Gustav III, is one of the Royal Academies of Sweden. Modelled on the Académie française, it has 18 members — "the Eighteen" (De Aderton) — and is best known internationally for deciding who will be the laureate of the Nobel Prize in Literature.

Purpose

The Academy's statutes charge it with cultivating the purity, vigour and sublimity of the Swedish language. It publishes the authoritative dictionary Svenska Akademiens ordlista (SAOL) and the monumental historical dictionary SAOB, and awards numerous literary prizes.

Nobel Prize in Literature

Under Alfred Nobel's will, the Academy elects the recipient of the Nobel Prize in Literature each year, announced in October. A five-member Nobel Committee prepares the choice, which the full Academy ratifies by vote. The prize ceremony is held in Stockholm on 10 December.

History and controversies

The Academy was dormant in the 1790s after Gustav III's assassination but revived under later monarchs. In 2018 a crisis over conflicts of interest and a sexual-assault scandal linked to a member's spouse led to several resignations, the postponement of that year's Nobel Prize in Literature (awarded in 2019 alongside Olga Tokarczuk's), and reforms allowing members to resign — previously membership had been for life with no exit.
