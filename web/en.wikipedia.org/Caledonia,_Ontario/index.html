Caledonia is an unincorporated community and former town in Haldimand County, Ontario, Canada, situated on the Grand River. It lies within the lands of the Haldimand Tract, granted to the Six Nations in 1784, and has grown substantially in recent decades as a commuter community for Hamilton and the Greater Toronto Area.

History

Caledonia began as a mill settlement in the 1830s, taking advantage of water power from a dam on the Grand River. The Grand River Navigation Company built locks and dams that made the community a local shipping and milling centre; a toll house and swing bridge were among its early structures. The nine-span Caledonia Bridge, built in 1927, is a notable reinforced-concrete structure and one of the few bridges of its kind in Canada.

The 2006 land dispute

Caledonia drew national attention beginning in February 2006, when members of the Six Nations of the Grand River occupied a residential subdivision under construction known as Douglas Creek Estates, asserting that the land remained part of the Haldimand Tract. The occupation, named Kanonhstaton ("the protected place") by protesters, led to road blockades, a provincial purchase of the disputed lands, and years of tension and litigation that became a flashpoint in Canadian debates over Indigenous land rights and the duty to consult.

Community

Caledonia hosts the annual Caledonia Fair, held each fall since the nineteenth century, and is home to historic sites such as Edinburgh Square Heritage and Cultural Centre, a former town hall and jail. The community is served by Highway 6 and Argyle Street, its historic main street.
