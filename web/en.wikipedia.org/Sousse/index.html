Sousse

Sousse or Susa (Arabic: سوسة) is a city in Tunisia, the capital of the Sousse Governorate. Located 140 km (87 mi) south of the capital Tunis, on the Gulf of Hammamet in the Mediterranean Sea, the city has a population of about 270,000 (2014 census) and is the country's third-largest city. It is a major tourist resort, known for its beaches, and its medina is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The city was founded by the Phoenicians in the 9th century BCE as Hadrumetum, making it one of the oldest cities in Tunisia. It later came under Roman, Vandal, Byzantine, and Arab rule; under the Aghlabids in the 9th century CE it became an important port and shipyard for the conquest of Sicily, and its great Ribat fortress and Great Mosque date from this period. The medina, enclosed by walls built in 859 CE, preserves many monuments of early Islamic architecture.

Economy and landmarks

Modern Sousse's economy rests on tourism, textiles, olive oil, and transport equipment. Landmarks include the Ribat of Sousse, the Great Mosque, the Kasbah (housing the archaeological museum with its notable Roman mosaics), and the nearby resort of Port El Kantaoui, built in 1979.
