Hip replacement
Also: total hip arthroplasty
Specialty: Orthopaedic surgery
Indications: Osteoarthritis, hip fracture, rheumatoid arthritis

Hip replacement is a surgical procedure in which the hip joint is replaced by a prosthetic implant, that is, a hip prosthesis. Such surgery is generally conducted to relieve arthritis pain or fix severe physical joint damage as part of treatment of a hip fracture.

Procedure

The damaged femoral head is removed and replaced with a metal stem and ball; the socket (acetabulum) is fitted with a cup, often with a polyethylene or ceramic liner. Modern total hip arthroplasty was pioneered by Sir John Charnley in the 1960s.

Outcomes and risks

Most patients gain large pain relief and mobility; implants commonly last 20+ years. Risks include infection, dislocation, loosening, leg-length difference and blood clots.

Alternatives

Hip resurfacing, partial replacement (hemiarthroplasty) and non-surgical management suit selected patients.
