Lake County is a county in the northeastern corner of Illinois, along the shore of Lake Michigan, for which it is named. Its county seat and largest city is Waukegan. With about 714,000 residents at the 2020 census, it is the third-most populous county in the state and forms part of the Chicago metropolitan area, bordering Wisconsin to the north.

Geography

The county comprises roughly 1,150 km² of land plus a large expanse of Lake Michigan waters. Its interior is dotted with glacial lakes — the Chain O'Lakes in the northwest is a major recreational boating area — and forest preserves managed by the Lake County Forest Preserve District, one of the largest such districts in Illinois. Communities range from old industrial port Waukegan and Naval Station Great Lakes in North Chicago to affluent North Shore suburbs such as Lake Forest, Highland Park and Lake Bluff, and fast-growing exurban towns to the west.

History and economy

Lake County was formed in 1839 from McHenry County. Its economy features major corporate employers in pharmaceuticals and biotechnology — AbbVie and Abbott Laboratories are headquartered near North Chicago — along with healthcare, logistics and retail. The county is served by Metra commuter rail lines to Chicago, Interstate 94, and the Six Flags Great America theme park in Gurnee. It is known for high incomes and strong public schools, though also for some of the highest property taxes in the United States.
