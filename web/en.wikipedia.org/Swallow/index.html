Swallow

Swallows are passerine birds of the family Hirundinidae, adapted to aerial insect hunting. The family, which also includes martins and saw-wings, comprises around 90 species found on all continents except Antarctica.

Description

Swallows have streamlined bodies, long pointed wings and, in many species, deeply forked tails. Their plumage is often glossy blue-black above and pale below. They take insects on the wing with wide gapes and agile flight.

Behaviour

Many species build cup nests of mud pellets on cliffs or buildings; others burrow in banks. Northern species such as the barn swallow are long-distance migrants, wintering in the tropics or the Southern Hemisphere. Swallows have long been welcomed as harbingers of spring and symbols of safe return, notably in sailors' tattoo traditions.
