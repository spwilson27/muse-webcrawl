Marine ecology

Marine ecology is the scientific study of interactions among organisms and their environment in the ocean, including marine habitats such as estuaries, salt marshes, mangrove forests, coral reefs, the open ocean and the deep sea. It examines how marine populations and communities are distributed and how they respond to abiotic factors (light, temperature, salinity, currents, nutrients) and to one another through predation, competition and symbiosis.

Habitats and processes

Marine ecologists study the intertidal zone, where organisms endure alternating immersion and exposure; continental shelves and kelp forests, which are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth; and the pelagic realm, where phytoplankton photosynthesis drives food webs that support fish, seabirds and marine mammals. The deep sea depends largely on marine snow falling from above, except at hydrothermal vents and cold seeps fuelled by chemosynthesis. Key processes include primary production, nutrient cycling, larval dispersal by currents, and trophic cascades such as the sea otter–urchin–kelp interaction.

Human impacts

The field increasingly focuses on anthropogenic pressures: overfishing and bycatch, eutrophication and coastal dead zones, plastic and chemical pollution, ocean warming and acidification, habitat destruction and invasive species. Conservation tools informed by marine ecology include marine protected areas, ecosystem-based fisheries management and restoration ecology for reefs, seagrasses and wetlands.
