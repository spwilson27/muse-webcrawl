Artemisia I of Caria

Reign: c. 480 BC

Capital: Halicarnassus

Allegiance: Achaemenid Empire

Artemisia I of Caria was a queen of Halicarnassus in the 5th century BC who ruled as a client of the Achaemenid Empire and commanded her own ships in Xerxes I's invasion of Greece in 480 BC.

Battle of Salamis

Herodotus, himself from Halicarnassus, portrays Artemisia as a shrewd adviser who counselled Xerxes against fighting at Salamis. In the battle she distinguished herself; pursued by an Athenian ship, she rammed and sank a friendly vessel, convincing her pursuer she was an ally and winning Xerxes' admiration. After the Persian defeat, she advised Xerxes to withdraw to Asia, and escorted his sons to Ephesus.

Legacy

Artemisia remains one of the few female military commanders of the ancient world known by name, and a prize of ten thousand drachmas reportedly offered for her capture went unclaimed.
