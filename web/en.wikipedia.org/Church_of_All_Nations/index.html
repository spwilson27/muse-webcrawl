Church of All Nations

The Church of All Nations, also known as the Church of the Agony, is a Roman Catholic church located on the Mount of Olives, next to the Garden of Gethsemane in Jerusalem. It enshrines a section of bedrock where, according to tradition, Jesus prayed before his arrest on the night before the crucifixion.

History

The present church, designed by Italian architect Antonio Barluzzi, was built between 1919 and 1924 on the foundations of earlier churches: a 4th-century Byzantine basilica destroyed by an earthquake in 746, and a small Crusader chapel abandoned in 1345. Funds for the construction came from donations by Catholic communities in many countries, hence the name "All Nations". The church is administered by the Franciscan Custody of the Holy Land.

Architecture

The facade is supported by a row of Corinthian columns and crowned by a large mosaic depicting Christ as the link between heaven and earth, flanked by the coats of arms of the contributing nations. The interior is deliberately kept dim, with alabaster windows tinted violet, evoking the night of the Agony. The exposed bedrock lies before the altar, surrounded by a crown of thorns in wrought iron, and the ceilings bear the coats of arms of the donor nations.
