Child protection

Child protection is the safeguarding of children from violence, exploitation, abuse, abandonment, and neglect. It involves government agencies, laws, and social services that aim to ensure children grow up in safe environments, as articulated internationally in the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989).

Systems and practice

Child protection systems typically include reporting mechanisms, investigation and assessment by social workers, family support and preservation services, foster care and adoption, and criminal prosecution of offenders. Mandatory reporting laws in many countries require designated professionals to report suspected maltreatment.

History

Organised child protection began in the 19th century with societies for the prevention of cruelty to children, and expanded greatly after cases such as that of Mary Ellen Wilson in 1874 and, in Britain, the death of Victoria Climbié in 2000, which prompted major reforms.
