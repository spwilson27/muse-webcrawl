Canadian Football League

The Canadian Football League (CFL; French: Ligue canadienne de football) is a professional sports league in Canada. It is the highest level of play in Canadian football, a distinct gridiron code played on a larger field with twelve players per side and three downs. The league's nine teams are divided into East and West divisions, and its championship game, the Grey Cup, is one of Canada's largest annual sporting events, first contested in 1909.

The Canadian game

Canadian football differs from the American game in several ways: a 110-yard field (plus 20-yard end zones), twelve players, three downs to gain ten yards, a one-yard neutral zone, the single point ("rouge") for balls kicked into or through the end zone not returned, and unlimited backfield motion. These rules produce a faster, more pass-oriented game.

History

The CFL was formed in 1958 from the Interprovincial Rugby Football Union and the Western Interprovincial Football Union, though its roots run to rugby football played in Canada from the 1860s. The league survived financial crises and a failed expansion into the United States in the 1990s; the Baltimore Stallions won the 1995 Grey Cup, the only American-based team to do so. The modern league has nine stable franchises.

Teams

East Division: Hamilton Tiger-Cats, Montreal Alouettes, Ottawa Redblacks, Toronto Argonauts. West Division: BC Lions, Calgary Stampeders, Edmonton Elks, Saskatchewan Roughriders, Winnipeg Blue Bombers. The Toronto Argonauts are the most successful club in Grey Cup history.

Season and playoffs

Teams play an 18-game regular season from June to late October, followed by division playoffs leading to the Grey Cup, usually held in late November. The league operates with a salary cap and a roster ratio requiring a minimum number of Canadian ("National") players.
