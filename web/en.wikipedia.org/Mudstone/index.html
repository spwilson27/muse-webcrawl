Mudstone

Mudstone
TypeSedimentary rock
CompositionSilt- and clay-sized particles
Grain sizeLess than 0.0625 mm

Mudstone, a type of mudrock, is a fine-grained sedimentary rock whose original constituents were clays or muds. Mudstone is distinguished from shale by its lack of fissility — it does not readily split into thin layers along bedding. Instead, mudstone tends to break into blocky fragments. The grain size is up to 0.0625 millimetres (0.00246 in), so individual particles are too small to be seen with the naked eye.

Mudstone forms where very fine sediment settles out of quiet water — in deep oceans, lake bottoms, floodplains and tidal flats — and is later compacted and cemented (lithified). In terms of the Dunham classification of limestones, a carbonate mudstone is one containing less than 10% grains. When heated and compressed with burial, some mudstones can begin to develop the fissility of shale, and further metamorphism produces slate.

Distinguishing features

Because its particles are clay- and silt-sized, mudstone feels smooth to the fingers, and silty varieties feel slightly gritty when chewed or scratched. Colours range from grey, brown and black (from organic matter) to red and green, reflecting the oxidation state of iron during deposition. Mudstones are important source rocks for petroleum and can act as seals (caprocks) over reservoirs.
