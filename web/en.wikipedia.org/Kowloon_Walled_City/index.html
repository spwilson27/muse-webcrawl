The Kowloon Walled City was an extremely densely populated, largely ungoverned settlement in Kowloon City, Hong Kong. Originally a Chinese military fort, it became an enclave whose legal status was ambiguous after Britain leased the New Territories in 1898, and it grew after the Second World War into a labyrinth of some 300 interconnected high-rise buildings built almost entirely without architects or regulation.

History

A walled garrison town was built in 1847 under the Qing dynasty. After 1899 the site was left in limbo: claimed by China but administered by neither China nor Britain. Refugees arrived after the Chinese Civil War, and in the 1950s–1970s triad gangs controlled much of the city, which became notorious for unlicensed dentists and doctors, small factories, drug dens and gambling.

Life in the city

At its peak, tens of thousands of residents lived in about 2.6 hectares, making it one of the most densely populated places ever recorded. Despite crime and poor sanitation, residents formed close-knit communities with schools, temples, workshops and rooftop kindergartens; sunlight rarely reached its lower alleyways.

Demolition and park

Following a 1987 agreement between the British and Chinese governments, residents were rehoused and the city demolished in 1993–94. The site opened in 1995 as the Kowloon Walled City Park, preserving the old yamen building and remnants of the south gate. The city remains a powerful cultural image, inspiring films, video games and fiction.
