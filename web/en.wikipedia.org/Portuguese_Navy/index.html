The Portuguese Navy (Portuguese: Marinha Portuguesa) is the naval branch of the Portuguese Armed Forces. One of the oldest navies in the world, it played a central role in the Age of Discovery, when Portuguese fleets pioneered maritime routes to India, Brazil, Africa, and the Far East.

History

Although Portuguese naval forces existed from the 12th century, King Dinis formally organised the navy in 1317, appointing Manuel Pessanha of Genoa as its first admiral. In the 15th and 16th centuries, under the patronage of Prince Henry the Navigator and the Portuguese Crown, naval expeditions rounded the Cape of Good Hope (Bartolomeu Dias, 1488), reached India (Vasco da Gama, 1498), and Brazil (Pedro Álvares Cabral, 1500). The navy later fought in the colonial wars in Africa (1961–1974) and was involved in the Carnation Revolution of 1974.

Modern force

Today the navy, with roughly 8,000–10,000 personnel, operates frigates (including the Vasco da Gama and Bartolomeu Dias classes), the Tridente-class submarines, corvettes, patrol vessels, and the replenishment ship Bérrio, as well as the Fuzileiros marine corps and naval aviation helicopters. Its tasks include NATO commitments, maritime patrol of Portugal's extensive exclusive economic zone (including the Azores and Madeira), search and rescue, and fisheries protection. The main base is the Lisbon Naval Base at Alfeite, across the Tagus from Lisbon.

Heritage

The navy preserves the sailing training ship NRP Sagres and the museum ship Dom Fernando II e Glória. Its motto draws on the seafaring tradition celebrated in Camões's epic Os Lusíadas.
