Yemeni civil war (2014–present)

The Yemeni civil war is an ongoing conflict that began in 2014 when Houthi forces (Ansar Allah), allied with former president Ali Abdullah Saleh, seized the capital Sanaa and ousted President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi's government. In March 2015 a Saudi-led coalition intervened militarily to restore Hadi, turning the war into a major regional and humanitarian crisis.

Background

Yemen, unified in 1990, suffered corruption, a southern separatist movement, al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula, and six Saada wars against the Zaydi Houthi movement (2004–2010). The 2011 Arab Spring uprising forced Saleh out under a Gulf-brokered deal; Hadi's transitional government and a National Dialogue failed to resolve grievances, and in September 2014 the Houthis took Sanaa.

Course of the war

Houthi advances on Aden in 2015 triggered Operation Decisive Storm. Years of fighting, coalition airstrikes, a blockade of Hodeidah port, and battles for Taiz and Marib followed. Saleh broke with the Houthis and was killed by them in December 2017. The UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council seized Aden in 2019. The 2018 Stockholm Agreement reduced fighting around Hodeidah. A UN-brokered truce from April 2022 lapsed formally but fighting stayed low; in 2022 Hadi transferred power to a Presidential Leadership Council. From late 2023 Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping, in declared solidarity with Gaza, drew US and UK strikes.

Humanitarian impact

The UN has called Yemen one of the world's worst humanitarian crises: hundreds of thousands dead directly and indirectly by some estimates, millions displaced, widespread food insecurity, cholera outbreaks, and a devastated health system. War-crimes allegations have been made against all parties.
