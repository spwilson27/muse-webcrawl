The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) is a United Nations agency tasked with helping countries eliminate poverty and achieve sustainable economic growth and human development. Headquartered in New York City, it is the largest UN development aid agency, with offices in 177 countries. The UNDP emphasizes developing local capacity towards long-term self-sufficiency and prosperity.

It administers the Human Development Index, published annually in the Human Development Report, and funds projects in areas such as democratic governance, poverty reduction, crisis prevention and recovery, energy and environment, and health. The UNDP relies entirely on voluntary contributions from UN member states.

History

The UNDP was founded on 22 November 1965 by General Assembly Resolution 2029 (XX), through the merger of two earlier programmes: the Expanded Programme of Technical Assistance (EPTA), established in 1949, and the United Nations Special Fund, created in 1958. The merged agency began operations on 1 January 1966.

The programme's first administrator was Paul G. Hoffman, former head of the Marshall Plan's Economic Cooperation Administration. In 1970, the UNDP introduced the concept of "country programming", under which assistance was organized around multi-year country programmes agreed with recipient governments.

In 1990, the UNDP published the first Human Development Report, introducing the Human Development Index (HDI) developed by economists Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen. The reports reframed development in terms of people's capabilities and choices rather than income alone.

Mandate and work

The UNDP's work is guided by the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), adopted in 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda. Its stated priorities include:

  • Poverty reduction and inclusive growth
  • Democratic governance and institution building
  • Climate change adaptation and disaster risk reduction
  • Gender equality
  • Crisis prevention and recovery
  • Access to HIV/AIDS treatment and health systems strengthening

The agency serves as the operational arm of the UN at the country level and coordinates the UN Resident Coordinator system, which leads UN country teams in more than 130 countries.

Funding

UNDP is funded entirely by voluntary contributions from member states of the United Nations, multilateral partners, the private sector, and other sources. Its regular (core) resources are supplemented by earmarked contributions for specific programmes and projects. The largest donors have traditionally included the United States, the United Kingdom, Japan, Germany, and the European Union.

Administrators

The Administrator of the UNDP is the third-ranking official of the United Nations after the Secretary-General and Deputy Secretary-General. Past administrators include William Draper III, James Gustave Speth, Mark Malloch Brown, Kemal Derviş, Helen Clark, and Achim Steiner.
