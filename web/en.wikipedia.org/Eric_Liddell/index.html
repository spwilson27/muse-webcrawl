Eric Henry Liddell (16 January 1902 – 21 February 1945) was a Scottish sprinter, rugby union international and Christian missionary. He won the 400 metres at the 1924 Paris Olympics after famously refusing to run in his favoured 100 metres because the heats were held on a Sunday, which conflicted with his Christian observance.

Early life and sport

Liddell was born in Tientsin (Tianjin), China, to Scottish missionary parents working for the London Missionary Society. Educated at Eltham College in London and the University of Edinburgh, he excelled at athletics and was capped seven times for Scotland at rugby union in 1922–23. His distinctive running style, with head thrown back and arms flailing, became well known.

Paris 1924

Knowing the Olympic schedule in advance, Liddell trained for the 400 metres instead of the Sunday 100 metres. In Paris he won bronze in the 200 metres and then won the 400 metres final in an Olympic and world-record-equalling time of 47.6 seconds, running the first 200 m in 22.2 seconds.

Missionary work and death

In 1925 Liddell returned to China as a missionary teacher, working in Tianjin and later in rural Xiaozhang. During the Second Sino-Japanese War he was interned by the Japanese at the Weihsien (Weifang) internment camp, where he organised sports and taught children; he died there of a brain tumour in February 1945, months before liberation. His story was dramatised in the film Chariots of Fire (1981).
