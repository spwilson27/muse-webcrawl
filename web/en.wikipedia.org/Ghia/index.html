Carrozzeria Ghia S.p.A. is an Italian automobile design and coachbuilding firm established in Turin in 1916 by Giacinto Ghia (1887–1944). It became one of the most famous Italian design houses of the postwar era, styling cars for Alfa Romeo, Fiat, Lancia, Chrysler, and Volkswagen, before being acquired by the Ford Motor Company, which used the Ghia name for decades as its top trim level.

History

Giacinto Ghia began as a test driver and mechanic before setting up his own workshop. Early Ghia special-bodied Alfa Romeos found success in events such as the Mille Miglia. After Ghia's death and the wartime destruction of the Turin works, the firm was revived under Mario Boano, producing elegant bodies for Fiat, Alfa Romeo, and Lancia and, from 1950, a series of dream cars for Chrysler under the direction of Virgil Exner, including the Chrysler K-310 and the Norseman — the latter lost when the SS Andrea Doria sank in 1956. Ghia also styled production cars such as the dual-cowl Dual-Ghia for an American consortium, the Volkswagen Karmann Ghia (Type 14, 1955), whose bodywork Ghia designed and Karmann built, and the Fiat 2300 S Coupé.

Ford ownership and the Ghia badge

After passing through Alejandro de Tomaso's hands, Ghia was sold to Ford in 1970 (with full control by 1973). Under Ford, Ghia became a styling studio producing concept cars such as the Ford Granada-based designs and later the 1992 Focus concept that previewed Ford's "New Edge" look. More famously, from the 1973 Granada onward, "Ghia" became the badge of the most luxurious trim level across Ford's European and global ranges — applied to the Fiesta, Escort, Sierra, Mondeo, and many other models — a usage that continued into the 2010s before being phased out in favour of names such as Titanium and Vignale.
