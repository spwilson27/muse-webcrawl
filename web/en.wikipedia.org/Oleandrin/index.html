Oleandrin

Oleandrin is a toxic cardiac glycoside found in the oleander plant (Nerium oleander). Along with neandrin, it is responsible for the toxicity of oleander sap and leaves. Ingestion of oleandrin can cause gastrointestinal and cardiac effects, including nausea, vomiting, arrhythmias, and, in severe cases, death.

Pharmacology and toxicity

Like other cardiac glycosides, oleandrin inhibits the membrane sodium–potassium pump (Na+/K+-ATPase), raising intracellular calcium in cardiac muscle and increasing contractility at low doses, while producing dangerous conduction disturbances at toxic doses. Symptoms of oleander poisoning resemble those of digoxin poisoning, and digoxin-specific antibody fragments (Fab) have been used in treatment, along with supportive care. Oleander poisoning is a significant problem in South Asia and in veterinary medicine, where livestock may eat clippings.

Investigational uses

Oleandrin and oleander extracts (such as the investigational preparation PBI-05204) have been studied in preclinical and early clinical research for anticancer activity, since cardiac glycosides can induce apoptosis in some cancer cell lines. No oleandrin product is an approved medicine. During the COVID-19 pandemic, oleandrin was promoted in some quarters as a possible treatment; health authorities warned there was no evidence of efficacy and emphasised its toxicity.
