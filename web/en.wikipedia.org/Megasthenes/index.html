Megasthenes (c. 350 – c. 290 BCE) was an ancient Greek historian, diplomat and Indian ethnographer. He served as an ambassador of Seleucus I Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya, founder of the Mauryan Empire, at Pataliputra in northern India.

Indica

Megasthenes wrote an account of India, the Indica, describing its geography, rivers, fauna, government, social divisions and customs. The original work is lost, but fragments survive in quotations by later authors such as Strabo, Arrian and Diodorus Siculus. His description of seven classes of Indian society and of the administration of Pataliputra is an important source for early Indian history, though some details are doubted by modern scholars.

Legacy

Through the fragments of the Indica, Megasthenes shaped the Greek and Roman world's image of India for centuries.
