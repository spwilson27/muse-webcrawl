King William's War

King William's War (1688–1697) was the North American theatre of the Nine Years' War between France and the Grand Alliance, and the first of the French and Indian Wars fought between English and French colonies and their Native American allies.

Course

Fighting centred on New England, Acadia and New France: French and Indigenous raids struck frontier towns such as Schenectady, Salmon Falls and Deerfield, while English expeditions under Sir William Phips briefly captured Port-Royal and failed before Quebec in 1690. Privateering and raids disrupted both sides without decisive territorial result.

Outcome

The Treaty of Ryswick (1697) ended the war in Europe and restored the status quo ante bellum in America, leaving the colonial contest unresolved until Queen Anne's War.
