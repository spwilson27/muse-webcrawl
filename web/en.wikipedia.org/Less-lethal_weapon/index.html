Less-lethal weapon

A less-lethal weapon (also called a non-lethal or less-than-lethal weapon) is a weapon intended to be less likely to kill a living target than conventional weapons such as knives and firearms. Such weapons are used by police and military forces to limit the escalation of conflict in situations where employment of lethal force is prohibited or undesirable, in riot control, prisoner control, and crowd control, and in self-defence.

Types

Common less-lethal weapons include kinetic impact projectiles such as rubber bullets, plastic bullets, bean bag rounds and sponge grenades; chemical agents such as tear gas and pepper spray; conducted energy weapons such as Tasers; water cannon; long-range acoustic devices; and blunt instruments such as batons. The term "less-lethal" is preferred over "non-lethal" because all such weapons retain some risk of serious injury or death, particularly if misused or used at short range.

Policy and criticism

Use-of-force policies generally require officers to employ the least force reasonably necessary and to treat less-lethal weapons as one step in a continuum of force. Critics note that such weapons have caused deaths and permanent injuries, and argue that their availability may encourage use of force in situations where none would otherwise be used.
