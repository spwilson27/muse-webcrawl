Muhal Richard Abrams (born Richard Louis Abrams; September 19, 1930 – October 29, 2017) was an American educator, administrator, composer, arranger, clarinetist, cellist, and jazz pianist in the free jazz medium. He co-founded the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM) in Chicago in 1965 and served as its first president, shaping the course of avant-garde jazz for decades.

Largely self-taught after studies at Governors State University and briefly at Chicago Musical College, Abrams founded the Experimental Band in 1961, which became the seed of the AACM. The association provided concerts, training and solidarity for black experimental musicians outside the commercial mainstream, launching figures including Anthony Braxton, Henry Threadgill, and the Art Ensemble of Chicago. His debut album, Levels and Degrees of Light (1968), appeared on Delmark Records.

Later career and honours

Abrams moved to New York in the late 1970s and recorded extensively for labels including Black Saint/Soul Note and New World, composing orchestral and chamber works alongside small-group jazz. He received the NEA Jazz Masters Fellowship in 2010. He died in Manhattan in 2017 at the age of 87.
