Discrete mathematics
ScopeDiscrete (separable) mathematical structures
Main topicsLogic, combinatorics, graph theory, set theory, number theory, algorithms
ApplicationsComputer science, cryptography, operations research

Discrete mathematics is the study of mathematical structures that are fundamentally discrete rather than continuous: objects that can be counted and are typically associated with the integers, as opposed to the "smoothly varying" objects of calculus and analysis. It includes logic, set theory, combinatorics, graph theory, elementary number theory, and the theory of algorithms and computability.

Topics

Major subdivisions include mathematical logic (propositional and predicate logic), combinatorics (the art of counting), graph theory (networks of vertices and edges, originating with Euler's 1736 solution of the Königsberg bridges problem), discrete probability, finite algebra, and recurrence relations. Discrete structures are the mathematical foundation of computer science: digital computers operate on discrete states, and discrete mathematics supplies the language of algorithms, data structures, formal languages, and cryptography.
