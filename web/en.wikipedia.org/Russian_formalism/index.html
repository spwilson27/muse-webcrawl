Russian formalism

Leading formalist Viktor Shklovsky.

Russian formalism was an influential school of literary criticism in Russia from the 1910s to the 1930s. It arose in two centres: the Moscow Linguistic Circle (founded 1915) and the Petrograd Society for the Study of Poetic Language, known as OPOYAZ (founded 1916). Key figures included Viktor Shklovsky, Boris Eichenbaum, Roman Jakobson, Yuri Tynyanov and Vladimir Propp.

Key concepts

The formalists sought to make literary study scientific by focusing on the specific devices of literature itself — its "literariness" (literaturnost). Shklovsky's essay "Art as Technique" (1917) introduced the concept of defamiliarization (ostranenie), the idea that art makes the familiar strange to renew perception. Formalists also distinguished fabula (the chronological story) from syuzhet (its arrangement in the work), and analysed plot, metre, sound patterning and narrative structure.

Suppression and legacy

Under Stalinist cultural policy, formalism was denounced as incompatible with Marxist criticism and socialist realism, and the movement was suppressed by the early 1930s. Jakobson carried formalist ideas to Prague, influencing the Prague Linguistic Circle and, through structuralism and narratology, much of 20th-century literary theory worldwide.
