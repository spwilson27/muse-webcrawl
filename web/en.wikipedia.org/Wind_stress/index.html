Wind stress

Wind stress is the shear stress exerted by the wind on the surface of large bodies of water such as oceans, seas and lakes. It is the tangential force per unit area that transfers momentum from the atmosphere to the ocean, driving surface currents, waves and mixing.

Formulation

Wind stress is usually estimated with the bulk formula τ = ρa Cd U², where ρa is air density, U is wind speed at a reference height (usually 10 m), and Cd is a dimensionless drag coefficient that increases with wind speed and sea state. Typical values are around 0.1 N/m².

Role in oceanography

Spatial variation in wind stress, expressed as its curl, drives Ekman transport and Ekman pumping, which set up the great subtropical gyres and zones of upwelling and downwelling. Wind stress is central to theories of Sverdrup balance, storm surge, and air–sea interaction in climate models.
