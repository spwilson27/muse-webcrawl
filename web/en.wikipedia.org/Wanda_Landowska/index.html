Wanda Landowska

Wanda Aleksandra Landowska (5 July 1879 – 16 August 1959) was a Polish harpsichordist and pianist whose performances, teaching, writings and especially her many recordings played a large role in reviving the popularity of the harpsichord in the early 20th century. She was the first person to record Bach's Goldberg Variations on the harpsichord in 1933. She became a naturalized French citizen in 1938.

Life and career

Landowska was born in Warsaw to Polish parents and began playing piano at the age of four, studying at the Warsaw Conservatory. She studied composition in Berlin and, after moving to Paris in 1900, became fascinated by early music, touring Europe playing the harpsichord. Intrigued by the instrument's possibilities, she had the piano manufacturer Pleyel build her a large, iron-framed harpsichord, which she used for the rest of her career.

Legacy

In 1925 she founded the École de Musique Ancienne at her home in Saint-Leu-la-Forêt, which became a center for early music performance. Forced to flee France in 1940, she settled in the United States, teaching and recording extensively, including a monumental series of Bach recordings. Composers such as Manuel de Falla and Francis Poulenc wrote harpsichord works for her. Her performances of Bach, delivered with the famous (probably apocryphal) remark, "You play Bach your way, and I'll play Bach his way," defined an era of the early music revival.
