Gunpla

Gunpla (ガンプラ, Ganpura) is a portmanteau of "Gundam plastic model", referring to the line of plastic model kits produced by Bandai depicting the mecha and other vehicles of the Gundam franchise. First released in 1980 following the debut of Mobile Suit Gundam in 1979, Gunpla kits became one of the most successful model kit lines in the world and a cornerstone of Bandai's business.

History

The original 1979 television series performed modestly on broadcast, but its popularity surged through reruns and the sale of model kits. Bandai acquired the merchandising rights and released the first Gunpla kits in July 1980; the 1/144 scale RX-78-2 Gundam kit sold millions of units and helped transform Gundam into a multimedia franchise.

Grades and scales

Kits are commonly organised into grades reflecting complexity and detail. High Grade (HG) kits, usually 1/144 scale, balance affordability and poseability. Real Grade (RG) kits add intricate inner frames at the same scale. Master Grade (MG) kits at 1/100 scale feature full inner frames, while Perfect Grade (PG) kits at 1/60 scale represent the most detailed mainstream releases. Entry Grade and SD (super deformed) lines target beginners and collectors of stylised designs.

Construction

Modern Gunpla uses snap-fit construction with parts moulded in multiple colours on runners (sprues), generally requiring no glue or paint. Builders often customise kits through panel lining, decals, topcoats and full repainting, a hobby culture supported by competitions such as the Gunpla Builders World Cup.
