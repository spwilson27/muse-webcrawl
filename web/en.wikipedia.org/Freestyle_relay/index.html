Freestyle relay

A freestyle relay is a swimming race in which four swimmers each swim a leg of the race in turn, all using freestyle (in practice, the front crawl), with the next swimmer starting only when the previous swimmer touches the wall. Freestyle relays are a standard part of Olympic and World Championship programmes.

Events

The principal international freestyle relays are the 4 × 100 metres and 4 × 200 metres events for men and women. The 4 × 100 m freestyle relay has been on the Olympic programme since 1904 for men (with a discontinuity) and 1912 for women; the 4 × 200 m freestyle relay dates from 1908 for men and was added for women in 1996. A mixed 4 × 100 m freestyle relay, with two men and two women, is contested at World Championships. In short-course (25 m pool) competition, a 4 × 50 m freestyle relay is also held.

Technique and rules

Relay takeovers are a key skill: the outgoing swimmer aims to leave the block just as the incoming swimmer touches, gaining time without false-starting; modern rules permit the outgoing swimmer to be in motion but their feet must remain in contact with the block until the touch. Teams are disqualified for takeovers completed too early (judged by electronic timing tolerances) or for swimming out of declared order. Relay strategy — placing the fastest swimmer as "anchor" on the final leg or leading off with a strong swimmer — is a significant tactical element, and the lead-off leg may count as an individual time for records.
