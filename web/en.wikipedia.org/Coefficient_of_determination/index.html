Coefficient of determination

In statistics, the coefficient of determination, denoted R2 or r2 and pronounced "R squared", is the proportion of the variance in the dependent variable that is predictable from the independent variable(s). It is a statistic used in the context of statistical models whose main purpose is either the prediction of future outcomes or the testing of hypotheses, on the basis of related information.

Definition

For a set of observed values with mean ȳ, fitted values ŷ, total sum of squares SStot and residual sum of squares SSres, the coefficient of determination is defined as R2 = 1 − SSres/SStot. It provides a measure of how well observed outcomes are replicated by the model, based on the proportion of total variation of outcomes explained by the model.

Interpretation

Values of R2 typically range from 0 to 1 in ordinary least squares regression with an intercept: a value of 1 indicates that the model explains all the variability of the data around its mean, and 0 indicates that it explains none. In simple linear regression, r2 is the square of the Pearson correlation coefficient between the observed and predicted values. However, R2 does not indicate whether a model is appropriate, whether the independent variables are a cause of changes in the dependent variable, or whether omitted-variable bias exists; a high R2 can accompany a badly specified model, and adding more predictors never decreases R2, which motivates the adjusted R2 that penalizes extra variables.

Generalizations

The concept has been generalized beyond linear regression to generalized linear models and other settings, where several competing definitions exist and values can fall outside the [0, 1] interval, including negative values when the model fits worse than a horizontal line at the mean.
