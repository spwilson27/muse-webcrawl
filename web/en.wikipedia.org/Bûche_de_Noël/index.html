Bûche de Noël

A bûche de Noël (French for "Yule log") is a traditional Christmas cake, especially popular in France, Belgium, Switzerland and other francophone countries. It is generally made of sponge cake rolled with cream to form a cylinder, and frosted with chocolate buttercream scored to resemble the bark of a log.

History

The cake commemorates the ancient custom of burning a large Yule log in the hearth over the Christmas season. When domestic fireplaces declined in the 19th century, the log was replaced symbolically by a cake served at the Réveillon feast.

Preparation

A thin Genoise sponge is baked, spread with chocolate, coffee or chestnut cream, and rolled into a log. The exterior is coated with ganache or buttercream textured with a fork, and decorations such as meringue mushrooms, marzipan holly and a dusting of powdered sugar "snow" complete the effect.

Variations

Modern versions include ice-cream logs and flavours such as praline, red fruit and salted caramel, and the cake is also traditional in Quebec and other parts of the francophone world.
