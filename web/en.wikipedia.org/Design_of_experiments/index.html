The design of experiments (DOE) is a branch of applied statistics concerned with planning, conducting, analyzing, and interpreting controlled tests to evaluate the factors that control the value of a parameter or group of parameters. A designed experiment is one in which the investigator deliberately changes one or more factors in order to observe the effect those changes have on one or more response variables.

History

The foundations of experimental design were laid by Ronald Fisher in the 1920s and 1930s through his work at the Rothamsted Experimental Station on agricultural field trials. Fisher introduced the principles of randomization, replication, and blocking, and developed the analysis of variance (ANOVA) as the principal method for analyzing experimental results.

Principles

Fisher's three basic principles remain central. Randomization assigns treatments to experimental units by chance, protecting against bias and validating statistical inference. Replication repeats the experiment on multiple units so that experimental error can be estimated. Blocking groups similar experimental units together so that known sources of variation can be separated from treatment effects.

Common designs

Widely used designs include completely randomized designs, randomized block designs, Latin squares, factorial and fractional factorial designs, and response surface designs used for optimization. Factorial designs are especially efficient because they estimate the effects of several factors, and their interactions, simultaneously.

Applications

Experimental design is used in agriculture, medicine (including randomized clinical trials), manufacturing quality improvement, engineering, marketing, and the social sciences—any setting in which controlled manipulation of inputs is used to learn about their effects on outputs.
