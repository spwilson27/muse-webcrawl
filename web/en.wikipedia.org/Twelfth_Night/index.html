Twelfth Night

Twelfth Night, or What You Will is a romantic comedy by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written around 1601–1602 as a Twelfth Night's entertainment for the close of the Christmas season. It was first published in the First Folio of 1623. The play centres on the twins Viola and Sebastian, who are separated in a shipwreck, and features mistaken identity, cross-dressing and tangled love.

Plot

Viola, shipwrecked on the coast of Illyria and believing her twin brother Sebastian drowned, disguises herself as a young man, Cesario, and enters the service of Duke Orsino. Orsino sends Cesario to woo the Countess Olivia on his behalf, but Olivia falls in love with Cesario, while Viola falls in love with Orsino. Meanwhile, Olivia's steward Malvolio is gulled by Sir Toby Belch, Maria, Sir Andrew Aguecheek and Feste the fool into believing Olivia loves him, via a forged letter. Sebastian, who has survived, arrives and is mistaken for Cesario; he marries Olivia. The twins' reunion untangles the plot: Orsino marries Viola, and Toby marries Maria.

Themes and reception

The play is named for the Feast of Epiphany, traditionally a festival of inversion and misrule, reflected in its subplot of revelry and the humbling of the puritanical Malvolio. Its famous lines include "If music be the food of love, play on" and "Some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon them." Twelfth Night remains one of Shakespeare's most performed comedies and has inspired musicals, films and adaptations such as She's the Man.
