Uintah and Ouray Reservation

The Uintah and Ouray Reservation is a Native American reservation in northeastern Utah, United States, and the homeland of the Ute Indian Tribe of the Uintah and Ouray Reservation, a federally recognized tribe. It is the second-largest reservation in the United States by area, covering roughly 4.5 million acres (about 6,800 square miles, though much of it is held in trust or allotted parcels interspersed with other ownership) across parts of seven counties in the Uintah Basin.

The reservation was formed from the Uintah Valley Reservation, created by executive order in 1861 and confirmed by Congress in 1864 for the Uintah band, and the Uncompahgre (Ouray) Reservation established in 1882 for Utes removed from Colorado after the Meeker incident of 1879. The two were administratively combined in the 20th century. The tribe's three bands are the Uintah, Uncompahgre, and White River bands.

Tribal headquarters are in Fort Duchesne. The tribe operates enterprises in energy—the basin is a major oil and natural gas producing region—agriculture, and other fields, and its jurisdiction has been the subject of major litigation, including the US Supreme Court's involvement in boundary disputes with the State of Utah.
