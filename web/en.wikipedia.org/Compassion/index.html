Compassion

NatureEmotion and virtue
EtymologyLatin compati — "to suffer with"
Related conceptsEmpathy, sympathy, altruism, karuṇā, mercy
In BuddhismKaruṇā, one of the four immeasurables

Compassion is a social feeling that motivates people to go out of their way to relieve the physical, mental, or emotional pains of others and themselves. It involves sensitivity to another's suffering combined with a desire to alleviate it — distinguishing it from empathy (feeling with another) and sympathy (feeling concern for another), in that compassion adds a motivation to act.

In religious and ethical traditions

Compassion is a central virtue in most major ethical and religious traditions. In Buddhism, karuṇā is one of the four immeasurables (brahmavihāras) cultivated in meditation, and the bodhisattva ideal is defined by compassion for all sentient beings. In Jainism, compassion toward all living things underlies the principle of ahiṃsā (non-violence). Judaism emphasizes God's compassion and the imitation of divine mercy; Christianity presents Jesus's ministry as one of compassion for the suffering; Islam begins nearly every chapter of the Qur'an by invoking God as "the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful." Philosophers from Aristotle, who analyzed pity in the Rhetoric, to Schopenhauer, who made compassion the foundation of morality, have debated its nature and scope; Martha Nussbaum has defended compassion as an essential, though fallible, component of a just society.

Psychology and neuroscience

Psychological research links compassion to prosocial behavior, well-being, and resilience. Compassion can be cultivated through training such as loving-kindness (mettā) meditation, and programs like Compassion-Focused Therapy use it to treat shame and self-criticism. Scholars distinguish compassion from "empathic distress," which can lead to burnout in caregivers, whereas compassion — associated with warmth and approach motivation — appears protective. Researchers including Tania Singer and Paul Gilbert have mapped distinct neural correlates of empathy and compassion.

Self-compassion

Psychologist Kristin Neff's work popularized self-compassion: treating oneself with kindness, recognizing one's common humanity, and holding painful experiences in mindful awareness rather than over-identifying with them — a trait associated with lower anxiety and depression.
