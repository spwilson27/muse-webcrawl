Universal Primary Education

Universal Primary Education (UPE) is the goal that all children of primary school age, everywhere in the world, should be able to complete a full course of primary schooling. It was the second of the United Nations Millennium Development Goals (MDG 2), targeted for achievement by 2015, and it continues under Sustainable Development Goal 4, which aims for free, quality primary and secondary education for all.

Background

The right to free primary education was affirmed in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948). The 1990 World Conference on Education for All at Jomtien and the 2000 Dakar Framework for Action set enrolment targets, and many countries abolished primary school fees in response.

Progress and challenges

Global primary net enrolment rose from roughly 83 per cent in 2000 to about 91 per cent by 2015. Nevertheless, tens of millions of children remained out of school, concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa and in conflict-affected states. Barriers include poverty, child labour, distance to school, gender norms, disability and shortages of trained teachers. Enrolling children has proved easier than ensuring they complete school and learn basic literacy and numeracy.
