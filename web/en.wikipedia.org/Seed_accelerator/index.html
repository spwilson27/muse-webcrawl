Seed accelerator

A seed accelerator is a fixed-term, cohort-based program that provides early-stage companies with seed investment, mentorship, education and workspace, culminating in a public pitch event or "demo day" before investors. In exchange, accelerators typically take a small equity stake, often around 5–10 per cent, or invest through a convertible instrument such as a SAFE.

History

The model is usually traced to Y Combinator, founded by Paul Graham and partners in Cambridge, Massachusetts in 2005 and later based in Silicon Valley. Its alumni include Airbnb, Dropbox, Stripe and Reddit. Techstars, founded in Boulder, Colorado in 2006, developed a franchised network of city-based programs. The format spread worldwide in the 2010s, with corporate, university and government-backed accelerators proliferating alongside independent ones; the Seed Accelerator Rankings Project and directories such as GAN tracked the growing field.

How programs work

Accelerators recruit a batch or cohort of startups through a competitive application process, invest a standard amount of seed capital, and run an intensive program of roughly three months. Founders receive mentoring from experienced entrepreneurs and investors, attend workshops, and refine their product and pitch. Programs end with a demo day at which the cohort presents to an audience of venture capitalists and angel investors in hopes of raising a priced or convertible seed round.

Distinctions

Accelerators differ from incubators, which typically offer longer, open-ended support and workspace without a fixed cohort or demo day, and from angel investors and venture capitalists, who provide capital without a structured curriculum. Critics note wide variation in quality: the value of a program depends heavily on its network and track record, and many newer accelerators add little beyond their small cheque.
