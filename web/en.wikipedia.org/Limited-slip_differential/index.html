Limited-slip differential

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A limited-slip differential resists large speed differences between the driven wheels.

A limited-slip differential (LSD) is a type of differential gear arrangement that allows its two output shafts to rotate at different speeds but limits the maximum difference between them. Unlike an open differential—which sends most power to whichever wheel has the least grip, spinning it uselessly on ice or mud—an LSD redistributes torque toward the wheel with more traction, improving acceleration and stability.

How it works

Mechanical LSDs resist relative motion between the outputs using friction. In clutch-type LSDs, spring-loaded clutch packs are squeezed harder as input torque rises or as the wheels begin to rotate at different speeds. Viscous units use silicone fluid that thickens when sheared by a speed difference. Geared (helical/Torsen-type) units use worm gears whose friction depends on the torque ratio. Electronic LSDs instead brake a spinning wheel using the ABS system, or use electronically controlled clutches, simulating the effect.

Behaviour

LSDs are characterized by their locking behaviour under power ("1-way"), on deceleration ("1.5-way"), or both ("2-way"), important in motorsport and drifting. Excessive locking can cause understeer or tyre wear on tarmac, so road cars use milder settings. LSDs are widely fitted to sports cars, off-road vehicles and performance models; famous trade names include Positraction and Torsen.
