Western African Ebola virus epidemic

Ebola epidemic in West Africa
PeriodDecember 2013 – June 2016
Countries most affectedGuinea, Liberia, Sierra Leone
Cases~28,600
Deaths~11,300

The Western African Ebola virus epidemic (2013–2016) was the most widespread outbreak of Ebola virus disease (EVD) in history, causing major loss of life and socioeconomic disruption in the region, mainly in Guinea, Liberia, and Sierra Leone. The first cases were recorded in Guinea in December 2013; the disease spread to neighbouring Liberia and Sierra Leone and, on a smaller scale, to other countries in Africa, Europe and the United States.

The World Health Organization (WHO) declared the outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern in August 2014. By the time the WHO declared the end of the emergency in March 2016, more than 28,600 cases and over 11,300 deaths had been reported, making it far larger than all previous Ebola outbreaks combined.

Course of the epidemic

The outbreak is thought to have begun with the infection of a young child in the village of Meliandou, Guinea, possibly through contact with fruit bats. Weak health systems, porous borders, burial practices involving washing of bodies, and initial delays in recognition allowed the virus to spread through the Forest Region and into the capital cities of all three countries.

Response

The response involved national governments, the WHO, Médecins Sans Frontières, the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and foreign military medical missions. Treatment centres were built, contact tracing and safe-burial programmes were expanded, and experimental vaccines were trialled; the rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine showed high efficacy in a 2015 ring vaccination trial in Guinea and was later deployed in subsequent outbreaks.

Impact

The epidemic devastated fragile health systems, killed hundreds of health workers, orphaned thousands of children, and caused severe economic damage through closed borders, collapsed trade and suspended mining and agriculture. It also spurred reforms in global health emergency response.
