Edo Castle

Edo Castle (江戸城, Edo-jō) is a flatland castle that was built in 1457 by Ōta Dōkan in Edo, Toshima District, Musashi Province — present-day Tokyo. It became the residence of the Tokugawa shogunate and the seat of the government of Japan during the Edo period (1603–1868). The grounds today form part of the Tokyo Imperial Palace.

History

After Tokugawa Ieyasu was granted the Kantō provinces in 1590, he made Edo his headquarters and vastly expanded the modest fortifications. Following the establishment of the shogunate in 1603, a massive programme of construction, carried out largely at the expense of the daimyo, transformed the castle into the largest in Japan, surrounded by concentric moats, massive stone walls, and dozens of gates and watchtowers (yagura).

The five-storey main keep (tenshu), completed in 1607 and rebuilt even larger under Ieyasu's grandson Iemitsu, burned down in the Great Fire of Meireki in 1657 and was never rebuilt; its stone base still stands. Fires repeatedly damaged the palace buildings, but the castle remained the political centre of Japan until the Meiji Restoration.

Meiji era to present

In 1868 the last shogun, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, surrendered the castle to imperial forces without a battle, and the Emperor Meiji took up residence, renaming Edo Tokyo. Most surviving palace buildings were lost to fire in 1873, and the wartime air raids of 1945 destroyed much of what remained. Surviving structures include several gates, guardhouses, the Fujimi-yagura turret, and extensive moats and stone ramparts. The East Gardens, opened to the public in 1968, occupy the former honmaru and ninomaru grounds.
