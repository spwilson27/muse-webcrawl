Hanna Diyab (c. 1688 – after 1763) was a Syrian Maronite merchant and storyteller from Aleppo. He is credited as the oral source for some of the most famous stories in the One Thousand and One Nights, including "Aladdin" and "Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves", which he told to the French translator Antoine Galland in Paris in 1709.

Life and travels

Diyab travelled from Aleppo with the French merchant and antiquarian Paul Lucas, journeying through Egypt, Tripoli, Tunis, and across the Mediterranean to France. In Paris he met Galland, then working on his translation of the Nights, and narrated a series of tales which Galland incorporated into the final volumes of Les mille et une nuits.

Autobiography

Late in life Diyab wrote an autobiographical travel narrative in colloquial Arabic, a manuscript rediscovered in the Vatican Library in the twentieth century. It provides a vivid account of Aleppo, his travels, and his time in Paris, including his audiences at the court of Louis XIV.

Legacy

Scholars such as Paulo Lemos Horta have argued that Diyab should be recognised as a co-creator of the modern Nights, since no Arabic manuscript sources have ever been found for "Aladdin" and several other "orphan tales" attributed to his narration.
