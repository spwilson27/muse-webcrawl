False Memory Syndrome Foundation

Founded1992
Dissolved2019
HeadquartersPhiladelphia, Pennsylvania, United States

The False Memory Syndrome Foundation (FMSF) was a nonprofit organisation founded in 1992 in Philadelphia by Peter J. and Pamela Freyd. It advocated for people who claimed to have been falsely accused of child sexual abuse based on recovered memories.

History and positions

The foundation was formed amid controversy over recovered-memory therapy, during which some patients reported memories of abuse that they had not previously remembered. The FMSF argued that such memories could be created through suggestive therapeutic techniques and coined the term "false memory syndrome." Its scientific advisory board included psychologists and memory researchers such as Elizabeth Loftus.

Criticism and closure

Critics accused the foundation of shielding abusers and of downplaying the reality of child abuse and dissociative disorders. The FMSF announced its dissolution at the end of 2019, stating that its goals had largely been achieved as scientific understanding of memory malleability had become mainstream.
