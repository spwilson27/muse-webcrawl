All Blacks

The New Zealand national rugby union team, commonly known as the All Blacks, represents New Zealand in men's international rugby union. Administered by New Zealand Rugby, the team is one of the most successful in international sport, having won the Rugby World Cup three times (1987, 2011, and 2015) and holding a winning record against every other national team.

History

Rugby was introduced to New Zealand in 1870, and the first national team toured New South Wales in 1884. The famous 1905–06 "Originals" tour of the British Isles, France, and North America lost only one match and popularized the All Blacks name, traditionally explained by their all-black uniform. New Zealand won the inaugural Rugby World Cup on home soil in 1987, and repeated the feat in 2011 and in England in 2015, becoming the first team to retain the trophy.

The haka

Before matches the team performs the haka, a Māori posture dance, most famously "Ka Mate," composed by the Ngāti Toa chief Te Rauparaha around 1820. Since 2005 the team has occasionally performed "Kapa o Pango," written specifically for the All Blacks. The ritual is intended to honour Māori culture and challenge opponents.

Records

The All Blacks have been ranked number one in the world for more years than any other team and recorded an 18-match winning streak in 2015–16. Notable players include Richie McCaw, the most-capped All Black, Dan Carter, the leading points scorer in test history, and Jonah Lomu, whose performances at the 1995 World Cup made him rugby's first global superstar.
