Isle of Lewis

The Isle of Lewis (Scottish Gaelic: Eilean Leòdhais) is the northern part of Lewis and Harris, the largest island of the Outer Hebrides in Scotland. Its principal town is Stornoway, the largest settlement in the Hebrides and the ferry port for Ullapool on the mainland.

History and culture

Lewis has been inhabited for millennia; the Callanish Stones, erected around 2900 BC, are among Britain's finest prehistoric monuments. The famous Lewis chessmen — 12th-century walrus-ivory chess pieces found at Uig in 1831 — attest to the island's Norse heritage, Lewis having been part of the Norse Kingdom of the Isles until 1266. Scottish Gaelic remains widely spoken, and the island is known for strict Presbyterian observance of the Sabbath in many communities.

Geography

Much of Lewis is a low peat-covered plateau dotted with lochs, contrasting with the mountains of Harris to the south. Lambing, crofting, fishing and Harris Tweed weaving are traditional industries; the island's northern tip is marked by the Butt of Lewis lighthouse.
