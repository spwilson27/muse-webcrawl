The Hebrew Bible or Tanakh is the canonical collection of Hebrew scriptures, comprising the Torah ("Teaching"), the Nevi'im ("Prophets"), and the Ketuvim ("Writings") — the acronym "Tanakh" being formed from the initial Hebrew letters of these three divisions. It is the primary source for the Old Testament of the Christian Bible, although the order, number, and division of books differ between traditions.

The texts were composed over many centuries, by multiple authors and editors, largely in Biblical Hebrew with some portions in Biblical Aramaic. Rabbinic Judaism traditionally holds that the canon was closed by the early centuries of the Common Era, with the Masoretic Text — preserved most completely in the Leningrad Codex (1008 CE) — becoming the authoritative version.

Terminology

"Hebrew Bible" is a term used by scholars to avoid confessional implications of names such as "Old Testament" (Christian) or "Tanakh" (Jewish), while referring to essentially the same corpus of texts. Jewish tradition counts 24 books; Protestant Old Testaments contain the same material arranged as 39 books; Catholic and Orthodox canons add deuterocanonical books preserved in the Greek Septuagint tradition.

Structure

The Torah consists of the five books of Moses: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy. The Nevi'im comprise the Former Prophets (Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Kings) and the Latter Prophets (Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the Twelve Minor Prophets). The Ketuvim include Psalms, Proverbs, Job, the Five Scrolls (Song of Songs, Ruth, Lamentations, Ecclesiastes, Esther), Daniel, Ezra–Nehemiah, and Chronicles.

Textual history

The oldest surviving fragments are the Dead Sea Scrolls (c. 250 BCE – 68 CE), which revealed both remarkable continuity and significant textual diversity. The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Greek Septuagint, and the medieval Masoretic manuscripts represent the major surviving textual witnesses. Modern critical editions, such as the Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia, are based chiefly on the Leningrad Codex.
