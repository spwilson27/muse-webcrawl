Clan Ogilvy is a Scottish clan originating in Angus. The Ogilvys were hereditary sheriffs of Angus and rose to become Earls of Airlie, one of the senior peerage families of Scotland. The clan name derives from the lands of Ogilvy in the parish of Glamis, and the family descends from Gillebride, Earl of Angus, in the 12th century.

History

The Ogilvys held the office of sheriff of Angus and the constableship of Dundee. Sir Walter Ogilvy was killed at the Battle of Harlaw in 1411. The family received the lands of Lintrathen and Airlie in the 15th century, and James Ogilvy was created Lord Ogilvy of Airlie in 1491. The Ogilvys of Airlie were prominent royalists: James Ogilvy, 1st Earl of Airlie (created 1639), supported Charles I during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms, and his son fought alongside the Marquis of Montrose.

The clan took part in the Jacobite risings of 1715 and 1745; David Ogilvy, styled Lord Ogilvy, commanded a regiment at Culloden in 1746 and escaped into exile in France, after which the earldom was attainted. The honours were restored in the 19th century. The Ogilvys of Inverquharity, a cadet branch, were noted soldiers, and Lady Ogilvy of that line is remembered as a Jacobite heroine.

Castles

The clan's seats include Cortachy Castle, the present seat of the Earl of Airlie, Airlie Castle, Forter Castle in Glenisla (built by the Earl of Airlie in 1560 and later restored), and Auchterhouse. The earls of Airlie have long been associated with the royal household; David Ogilvy, 12th Earl of Airlie, served as Lord Chamberlain, and his wife Virginia was a lady-in-waiting to Queen Elizabeth II. Angus Ogilvy, a younger son, married Princess Alexandra of Kent.
