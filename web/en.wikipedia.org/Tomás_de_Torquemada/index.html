Tomás de Torquemada (1420 – 1498) was a Castilian Dominican friar and confessor to Queen Isabella I of Castile. He is best known as the first Grand Inquisitor of the Spanish Inquisition, which he organised and led from 1483 until his death, and his name has become synonymous with religious persecution and fanaticism.

Early life

Torquemada was born in 1420, probably in Valladolid, into a family of converso (converted Jewish) background according to some historians. He entered the Dominican order, became prior of the monastery of Santa Cruz at Segovia, and gained the confidence of the Infanta Isabella, serving as her confessor. He supported her marriage to Ferdinand of Aragon and the union of the Spanish crowns.

Grand Inquisitor

Following the papal bull of 1478 authorising Ferdinand and Isabella to appoint inquisitors, Torquemada was appointed Inquisitor General for Castile and Aragon in 1483. He drew up the Instrucciones, the procedural code of the Inquisition, established tribunals across Spain, and directed its campaign against judaizantes — conversos accused of secretly practising Judaism — as well as alleged heretics, blasphemers and others. Trials used secret denunciations and torture, and those convicted and unrepentant were "relaxed" to the secular arm for execution at autos-da-fé. Estimates of executions during his tenure vary widely, from several hundred to around two thousand.

Expulsion of the Jews

Torquemada was a leading advocate of the Alhambra Decree of 1492, which expelled all practising Jews from Spain. A famous (possibly apocryphal) story relates that when Jewish representatives offered Ferdinand a large sum to revoke the decree, Torquemada threw a crucifix on the table, likening the monarchs to Judas selling Christ.

Death and legacy

Torquemada retired to the monastery of St Thomas Aquinas in Ávila, which he had founded, and died there in 1498. His historical reputation is overwhelmingly negative; he appears as a villain in works by Dostoevsky (the Grand Inquisitor of The Brothers Karamazov is partly inspired by his type), in The Pit and the Pendulum adaptations, and in Mel Brooks's comic portrayal in History of the World, Part I.
