Crest badge

A crest badge is a heraldic badge worn to show allegiance to an individual or membership in a family or clan. In Scottish heraldry, the crest badge consists of the chief's crest encircled by a strap and buckle bearing the chief's motto or slogan. Clan members wear the badge to indicate their allegiance to their clan chief.

Usage

Crest badges are commonly worn as bonnet badges, kilt pins, and brooches, and are frequently made of silver or pewter. Under the conventions of the Court of the Lord Lyon, a clansman's badge must show the strap and buckle, distinguishing it from the chief's own crest, which may be worn plain. Armigers have their own form of the badge with a circlet.

History

The wearing of plant badges and crests to identify clan and lordly followings in Scotland dates back centuries; the formalized strap-and-buckle crest badge became standard in the 19th and 20th centuries as clan societies popularized Highland dress.
