Mycoplasma

Mycoplasma is a genus of bacteria that lack a cell wall around their cell membrane. Because they have no rigid wall, they are pleomorphic — able to assume varied shapes — and are resistant to antibiotics that target cell wall synthesis, such as penicillins. They are among the smallest known free-living organisms, with cells typically 0.2–0.3 micrometres in diameter, and have very small genomes.

Several species are pathogens of humans and animals. Mycoplasma pneumoniae causes a form of "walking pneumonia," a relatively mild atypical pneumonia, as well as tracheobronchitis. Mycoplasma genitalium is a sexually transmitted organism associated with urethritis and pelvic inflammatory disease, and has one of the smallest known genomes of any free-living organism, about 580 kilobases, making it a model in minimal-genome and synthetic biology research.

Significance

Mycoplasmas are also notorious contaminants of laboratory cell cultures, where they can pass through filters and alter experimental results undetected. Their stripped-down metabolism — they depend on their host for many nutrients, including sterols — reflects an evolution toward parasitic life.
