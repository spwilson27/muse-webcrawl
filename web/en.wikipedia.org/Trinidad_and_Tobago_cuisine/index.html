Trinidad and Tobago cuisine
Influences: Indian, African, Creole, Chinese, European, Amerindian

The cuisine of Trinidad and Tobago reflects the country's diverse population, drawing on Indian, African, Creole, European, Chinese and Indigenous traditions. It is known for boldly seasoned curries, street food and pepper sauces.

Notable dishes

Signature foods include doubles — curried chickpeas in fried flatbread, sold by street vendors; roti with curried chicken, goat or vegetables; callaloo, a soup of dasheen leaves; pelau, a rice dish with pigeon peas and meat; bake and shark, a fried bread sandwich with shark meat sold at Maracas Beach; and crab and dumplings in Tobago.

Influences

East Indian indentured labourers arriving from 1845 brought curries, roti and sweets such as barfi; African, Chinese and Middle Eastern communities added further dishes and techniques, producing a distinctive fusion cuisine.
