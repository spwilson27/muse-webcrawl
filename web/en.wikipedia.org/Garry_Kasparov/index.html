Garry Kimovich Kasparov is a Russian chess grandmaster, former World Chess Champion, writer and political activist. Born in Baku, he became the youngest undisputed world champion in 1985 by defeating Anatoly Karpov and remained the world No. 1 for much of the next two decades.

Chess career

Kasparov's matches with Karpov defined an era of championship chess. After a split from FIDE in 1993, he defended a separate classical title until losing to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000. He retired from regular professional chess in 2005 while still ranked first.

Computers

Kasparov played the IBM supercomputer Deep Blue in 1996 and 1997; he won the first match and lost the rematch, a landmark in public discussion of artificial intelligence. He later promoted human–computer “advanced chess” and wrote about decision-making and technology.

Politics and writing

After retirement Kasparov became a prominent critic of Vladimir Putin, helped found opposition movements in Russia, and later lived outside Russia while campaigning for democracy and support for Ukraine. His books include Winter Is Coming and Deep Thinking.
