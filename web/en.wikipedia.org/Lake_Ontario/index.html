Lake Ontario

Location: Canada (Ontario); United States (New York)
Type: Great Lake
Surface area: 19,000 km²
Average depth: 86 m
Max. depth: 244 m
Surface elevation: 75 m

Lake Ontario is one of the five Great Lakes of North America. It is bounded on the north by the Canadian province of Ontario and on the south by New York state. It is the smallest Great Lake by surface area, but the second-deepest by average depth, and the easternmost and lowest in elevation of the five.

The lake's name comes from a Huron word meaning "great lake". Its main inflow is the Niagara River, draining Lake Erie; its outflow is the St. Lawrence River, which carries its waters to the Atlantic Ocean. Major cities on its shores include Toronto and Hamilton in Ontario and Rochester in New York.

Geography

Because of its depth, Lake Ontario rarely freezes completely. At its eastern end, the Thousand Islands region marks the transition to the St. Lawrence River. The Welland Canal, part of the St. Lawrence Seaway system, bypasses Niagara Falls to connect the lake with Lake Erie.

History and environment

The lake was an important theatre in the French and Indian War and the War of 1812. Like the other Great Lakes, it suffered serious industrial pollution in the twentieth century, followed by substantial recovery under binational agreements; concerns remain over invasive species, shoreline flooding and toxic contaminants in fish.
