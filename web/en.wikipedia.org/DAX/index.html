DAX

The DAX (Deutscher Aktienindex, "German stock index") is Germany's principal stock market index, tracking the performance of the largest and most liquid companies listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange. It is widely used as a benchmark for the German equity market and for the health of Europe's largest economy.

Composition

From its launch on 1 July 1988 with 30 constituents, the DAX was expanded to 40 companies in September 2021. Membership is determined mainly by free-float market capitalisation and trading volume, reviewed regularly by Deutsche Börse. Constituents have included industrial and financial heavyweights such as Siemens, SAP, Allianz, Deutsche Bank, BASF and the major German carmakers.

Calculation

The DAX is unusual among major indices in being calculated as a performance (total return) index: it assumes dividends are reinvested, so its long-term rise partly reflects dividend income as well as price changes. A price-only version is also published. The index is calculated every second during trading hours from prices on the Xetra electronic trading system.

History

The DAX began with a base value of 1,000 as of 30 December 1987. It passed 10,000 points in 2014 and has since climbed well beyond that level. Following the Wirecard accounting scandal of 2020, in which a DAX constituent collapsed, Deutsche Börse tightened the index's membership rules, adding profitability requirements and faster expulsion for rule breaches.
