Min'yō
OriginJapan
Meaning"Folk song"
Typical instrumentsShamisen, shakuhachi, taiko, fue

Min'yō (民謡) is a genre of traditional Japanese folk music and song. The term combines the characters for "people" or "folk" and "song". Min'yō songs are closely tied to local regions, occupations and festivals across Japan.

Styles and function

Traditional min'yō includes work songs of farmers and fishermen (such as the herring-fishing songs of Hokkaidō), rice-planting songs, lullabies, and festival music for Bon dances. Performances typically feature a singer accompanied by shamisen, with shakuhachi flute, taiko drums and fue, along with hand-clapping and shouted interjections (kakegoe). Singing is noted for ornamented melisma and the characteristic strained vocal technique called kobushi.

Modern practice

Today min'yō is performed by amateur and professional associations, preserved through competitions and instruction, and fused with contemporary styles in crossover genres sometimes called neo-folk or, in Tsugaru shamisen performance, virtuosic solo concert music.
