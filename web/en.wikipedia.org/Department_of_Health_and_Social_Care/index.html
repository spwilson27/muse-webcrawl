The Department of Health and Social Care (DHSC) is a department of His Majesty's Government responsible for government policy on health and adult social care matters in England, along with a few elements of the same matters which are not otherwise devolved to the Scottish Government, Welsh Government or Northern Ireland Executive.

History

The department traces its origins to the Ministry of Health (1919). In 1968 it merged into the Department of Health and Social Security; in 1988 the health functions were separated again as the Department of Health. "and Social Care" was added to its name in January 2018 to reflect its responsibility for social care policy.

Role

DHSC sets strategy and funding for the National Health Service in England — by far the largest part of its budget — and oversees public health, including through the UK Health Security Agency and the Office for Health Improvement and Disparities. It is accountable to Parliament for the performance of the health and care system, while day-to-day commissioning is handled by NHS England and integrated care boards.

Ministers and structure

The department is headed by the Secretary of State for Health and Social Care, supported by ministers of state and parliamentary under-secretaries, and by a permanent secretary and the Chief Medical Officer. Arm's-length bodies include the Care Quality Commission, NICE, and NHS Blood and Transplant.
