Tonnetz

In musical tuning and harmony, the Tonnetz (German: "tone-network") is a conceptual lattice diagram representing tonal pitch space, first described by Leonhard Euler in 1739. Various visual representations of the Tonnetz can be used to show traditional harmonic relationships in European classical music.

History and use

Euler's Tonnetz organizes pitches in a two-dimensional grid: one axis follows lines of perfect fifths, and the other follows lines of major thirds. Hugo Riemann later developed the diagram into a tool for harmonic analysis in his theory of harmonic functions, and it was taken up in the 20th century by neo-Riemannian theory, which uses the Tonnetz to model triadic transformations such as the parallel (P), leading-tone exchange (L), and relative (R) operations.

In the neo-Riemannian Tonnetz, major and minor triads appear as triangles, and moving to an adjacent triangle across a shared edge corresponds to a transformation that keeps two common tones while moving the third by step. Because the lattice of fifths and thirds can be wrapped around a torus, the diagram also illustrates cycles of triadic progression.

Geometry

The Tonnetz can be realized as a triangulation of the plane (or, when identifications are made, of a torus) in which each triangle is a consonant triad and each vertex is a pitch class. Voice-leading distances between chords can be measured by paths across the diagram, a feature exploited in geometrical music theory.
