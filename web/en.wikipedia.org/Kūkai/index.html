Kūkai (空海; 774–835), also known posthumously as Kōbō Daishi (弘法大師, "The Grand Master Who Propagated the Dharma"), was a Japanese Buddhist monk, calligrapher, scholar and engineer who founded the esoteric Shingon school of Buddhism.

Life

Born into the Saeki family in Sanuki Province (modern Kagawa Prefecture) on Shikoku, Kūkai travelled to Tang-dynasty China in 804, where he studied esoteric Buddhism (Mikkyō) under the master Huiguo in Chang'an and was initiated as a successor in the lineage. Returning to Japan in 806, he gained imperial favour, was entrusted with the temple Tō-ji in Kyoto, and in 816 founded the monastic complex on Mount Kōya, which remains the headquarters of Shingon Buddhism.

Legacy

Kūkai systematised esoteric doctrine in works such as the Jūjūshinron ("Ten Stages of the Development of Mind"). Tradition credits him with devising the kana syllabary and composing the Iroha poem, though these attributions are debated. He was also famed as one of the Three Brushes (Sanpitsu) of calligraphy and as an engineer credited with rebuilding the Mannō reservoir. In 921 Emperor Daigo granted him the posthumous title Kōbō Daishi. Followers believe he did not die but remains in eternal meditation at Okunoin on Mount Kōya.
