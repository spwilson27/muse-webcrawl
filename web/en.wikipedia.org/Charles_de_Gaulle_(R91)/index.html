Charles de Gaulle is the flagship of the French Navy (Marine Nationale) and the largest warship ever built in Western Europe. She is the tenth French aircraft carrier and the first French surface vessel — and the only non-American carrier — powered by nuclear propulsion. The ship, named for French statesman and general Charles de Gaulle, carries a pennant number R91 and replaced the conventional carrier Foch.

Design and construction

Ordered in 1986 and laid down at Brest in 1989, the carrier was built by DCNS (now Naval Group). Her two K15 pressurized water reactors, derived from those of French ballistic missile submarines, drive two shafts for a speed of about 27 knots and effectively unlimited range, though she is slower than the steam-powered ship she replaced. She displaces 42,500 tonnes at full load, is 261.5 metres long, and operates two 75-metre C13-3 steam catapults of the type used on US Nimitz-class carriers, launching aircraft including the Dassault Rafale M, E-2C Hawkeye, and NH90 and Dauphin helicopters; her air group numbers up to 40 aircraft.

Service history

Construction was troubled by budget pauses, a propeller failure during trials, and concerns over radiation shielding and the length of the flight deck, which had to be extended for the Hawkeye. After commissioning in 2001, she deployed in support of Operation Enduring Freedom in the Indian Ocean and Afghanistan, operated over Libya in 2011, and in 2015 deployed to the Persian Gulf and then the eastern Mediterranean for strikes against Islamic State targets in Iraq and Syria, including in response to the November 2015 Paris attacks. Her successor under the PANG programme is planned for the late 2030s.
