Powerball

Powerball is an American lottery game offered by 45 states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, coordinated by the Multi-State Lottery Association (MUSL), a nonprofit, government-benefit association owned and operated by its member lotteries. Each Powerball play costs $2, with an optional "Power Play" multiplier available for an additional $1.

Gameplay

Players select five numbers from 1 to 69 (white balls) and one number from 1 to 26 (the red Powerball). Drawings are held three times a week—on Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday evenings—at the Florida Lottery's drawing studio in Tallahassee. The jackpot is won by matching all five white balls in any order plus the red Powerball. The annuity jackpot starts at $20 million and grows with each drawing without a winner; winners may instead choose a lower one-time lump-sum cash payment. The odds of winning the jackpot are 1 in 292,201,338.

History and records

Powerball began in April 1992, succeeding the MUSL's earlier Lotto*America game, and was designed by Steve Caputo. Its two-drum format—later widely imitated, including by rival Mega Millions—allowed both high jackpots and a variety of smaller prizes. Rule changes in 2015 lengthened the jackpot odds and accelerated jackpot growth. On 7 November 2022, a single ticket sold in Altadena, California, won a jackpot of $2.04 billion, the largest lottery prize in world history. Earlier record jackpots included the $1.586 billion prize of January 2016, split among tickets in California, Florida, and Tennessee.

Profits are distributed to the jurisdictions where tickets are sold and generally support public programs such as education, infrastructure, and services for seniors, as designated by each member lottery.
