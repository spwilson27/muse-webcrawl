The Lewis and Clark Expedition, also known as the Corps of Discovery Expedition, was a United States expedition to cross the newly acquired western portion of the country after the Louisiana Purchase. Led by Captain Meriwether Lewis and Second Lieutenant William Clark, the expedition travelled from May 1804 to September 1806, journeying from St. Louis to the Pacific coast and back.

President Thomas Jefferson commissioned the expedition shortly after the Louisiana Purchase in 1803 to explore and map the new territory, find a practical route across the western half of the continent, establish an American presence before European powers could claim the region, and study the area's plants, animal life and geography, as well as the Native American tribes along the route.

Journey

The Corps ascended the Missouri River in the spring of 1804, wintering near the Mandan villages in present-day North Dakota, where they hired the French-Canadian trader Toussaint Charbonneau and his Shoshone wife Sacagawea as interpreters. In 1805 they crossed the Great Plains, portaged the Great Falls of the Missouri, and traversed the Rocky Mountains via Lemhi Pass. After crossing the Bitterroot Range with Shoshone and Nez Perce guides, they descended the Clearwater, Snake and Columbia rivers, reaching the Pacific Ocean in November 1805. They wintered at Fort Clatsop near present-day Astoria, Oregon, and returned to St. Louis in September 1806.

Outcomes

The expedition mapped the transcontinental route, documented some 178 plants and 122 species and subspecies of animals previously unknown to science, and established diplomatic relations with dozens of Native American nations. Remarkably, only one member of the party, Sergeant Charles Floyd, died during the journey. The detailed journals kept by Lewis and Clark remain a foundational record of the early American West.

Key figures

  • Meriwether Lewis — expedition commander and Jefferson's former private secretary.
  • William Clark — co-leader, mapmaker and brother of George Rogers Clark.
  • Sacagawea — Shoshone woman whose presence aided negotiations with Native nations.
  • York — Clark's enslaved manservant, a full working member of the party.
