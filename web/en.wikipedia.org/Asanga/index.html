Asanga (Sanskrit: असङ्ग) was a 4th–5th century Indian Buddhist scholar and a founder, with his half-brother Vasubandhu, of the Yogachara ("yoga practice") school of Mahayana Buddhism, also known as the consciousness-only school.

Life

Born in Purushapura in Gandhara, Asanga was first ordained in an early Buddhist school before converting to Mahayana. Tradition holds that he spent twelve years in retreat and received teachings from the bodhisattva Maitreya, under whose inspiration he is said to have written the "Five Treatises of Maitreya". He later persuaded Vasubandhu to embrace Mahayana.

Philosophy

Asanga systematized Yogachara doctrines including the storehouse consciousness (ālaya-vijñāna), the three natures (trisvabhāva), and the doctrine that external objects are representations of consciousness. His Abhidharma-samuccaya is a comprehensive Mahayana compendium of Abhidharma categories.

Legacy

His works were translated into Chinese by Paramartha and Xuanzang, shaping East Asian Buddhism, and into Tibetan, where they remain central to scholastic study.
