The Three Mile Island accident was a partial nuclear meltdown of the Unit 2 reactor at the Three Mile Island Nuclear Generating Station near Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, on 28 March 1979. It was the most significant accident in US commercial nuclear power plant history.

Accident sequence

A failure in the secondary cooling circuit stopped the feedwater pumps, triggering a shutdown; a pilot-operated relief valve then opened to release pressure but stuck open, draining coolant. Misleading panel indications led operators to believe the reactor was overfilled, and they reduced emergency cooling. The exposed core overheated and partially melted before the situation was recognised and stabilised hours later.

Consequences

Small amounts of radioactive gases were released, but studies by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission and health agencies found average doses to nearby residents very low and no detectable increase in cancers attributable to the accident. Governor Thornburgh advised evacuation of pregnant women and young children within five miles, and about 140,000 people temporarily left the area. Cleanup of Unit 2 took over a decade and cost roughly $1 billion. The accident galvanised the anti-nuclear movement, led to sweeping reforms in operator training, regulation and emergency planning, and effectively halted new reactor orders in the United States for decades.
