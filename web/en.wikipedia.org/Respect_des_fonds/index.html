Respect des fonds

Respect des fonds is a principle in archival science — in French, "respect for the [archival] fonds" — holding that the records of a single creator (a person, family, or organisation) must be kept together as one fonds and not intermingled with the records of other creators.

History

The principle was articulated in France in 1841, when the archivist Natalis de Wailly issued instructions that archives be grouped by their creating administration rather than by subject. It was later developed in the Netherlands and Prussia and, together with the related principle of original order, became a foundation of modern archival arrangement and description.

Application

Under respect des fonds, provenance determines arrangement: records are preserved in the order given them by their creator, because that order is itself evidence of how the records were created and used. The principle underlies the multilevel description used in modern archival catalogues and standards such as ISAD(G).
