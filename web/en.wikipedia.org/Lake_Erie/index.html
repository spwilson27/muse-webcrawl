Lake Erie

Location: Canada (Ontario); United States (Michigan, Ohio, Pennsylvania, New York)
Type: Great Lake
Surface area: 25,700 km²
Average depth: 19 m
Max. depth: 64 m
Surface elevation: 173 m

Lake Erie is the fourth-largest lake by surface area of the five Great Lakes of North America and the eleventh-largest globally. It is the shallowest and southernmost of the Great Lakes, bounded by Ontario to the north, and by Ohio, Pennsylvania, New York and Michigan to the south and west.

The lake's name derives from the Erie people, an Iroquoian tribe who lived along its southern shore. Water flows in from Lake Huron via the Detroit River and out through the Niagara River and Niagara Falls into Lake Ontario.

Geology and climate

Because it is the shallowest of the Great Lakes, Erie warms rapidly in spring and summer and often freezes over in winter. Its shallowness also makes it prone to sudden, violent storms; many shipwrecks rest on its bed.

Environmental issues

By the 1960s Lake Erie had become heavily polluted by industrial waste and phosphorus runoff, and was popularly described as "dead". Cleanup efforts under the Clean Water Act and the Great Lakes Water Quality Agreement revived its fisheries, though harmful algal blooms, fed by agricultural runoff, have recurred since the 2000s — notably the 2014 bloom that contaminated Toledo's drinking water.

Economy

The lake supports a major commercial and sport fishery (walleye, yellow perch, lake whitefish), and its shores host ports such as Cleveland, Toledo, Erie and Buffalo.
