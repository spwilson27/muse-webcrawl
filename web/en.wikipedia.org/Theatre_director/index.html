Theatre director

A theatre director is a practitioner in the theatre field who is responsible for the overall artistic vision and practical realization of a production. The director collaborates with actors, designers and technicians to unify the elements of a play — text, performance, staging, set, costume, lighting and sound — into a coherent whole for an audience.

Role

The director typically interprets the script, casts the actors, leads rehearsals, and shapes blocking, pacing and tone. In many companies the director also selects the repertoire, though this may fall to an artistic director.

History

Although functions resembling direction existed earlier — playwrights such as Shakespeare and Molière supervised their own productions — the modern director emerged in the late 19th century with figures such as the Duke of Saxe-Meiningen and André Antoine. Konstantin Stanislavski, Bertolt Brecht and later innovators such as Peter Brook made the director the central creative figure of modern theatre. See also Theatre games and Directing.
