Puttable bond

A puttable bond (also called a put bond or retractable bond) is a bond that grants the holder the right, but not the obligation, to demand early repayment of the principal at a predetermined date or dates before maturity, typically at par. The put option protects the investor if interest rates rise or the issuer's credit quality deteriorates.

Characteristics

Because the put feature benefits the holder, puttable bonds are issued with lower coupons than otherwise comparable straight bonds. They are the opposite of callable bonds, which give the issuer the right to redeem early. Some issues combine features or set put dates tied to events such as a change of control ("poison put") or the death of the holder ("survivor's option"). Embedded options like puts are valued in fixed-income analysis using option-adjusted spread methods.
