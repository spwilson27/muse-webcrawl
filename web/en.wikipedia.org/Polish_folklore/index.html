Polish folklore

Polish folklore encompasses the folk traditions, myths, legends, music, dance and customs of Poland, rooted in Slavic mythology and later Christian influences. Pre-Christian Slavic beliefs featured deities such as Perun, Świętowit and Mokosz, along with household spirits, water nymphs (rusałki) and forest beings.

Well-known legends include the Wawel Dragon of Kraków, the tale of Lech, Czech and Rus explaining the founding of Slavic nations, and the mermaid Syrenka, symbol of Warsaw. Seasonal customs mark the calendar: drowning Marzanna in spring, Kupala Night at midsummer, harvest festivals (dożynki), and Christmas traditions centered on Wigilia.

Folk arts

Regional folk costumes, embroidery, pottery from Bolesławiec, amber jewellery from the Baltic coast, and intricate lace from Koniaków are celebrated elements of material folklore.
