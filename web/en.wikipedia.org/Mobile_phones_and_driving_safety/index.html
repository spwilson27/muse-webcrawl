Mobile phones and driving safety

The use of mobile phones while driving is a well-documented road safety hazard. Distraction from talking, texting, or interacting with a phone impairs reaction time, lane keeping, and hazard awareness, and is estimated to multiply crash risk several-fold; texting, which combines visual, manual, and cognitive distraction, is considered especially dangerous.

Research findings

Simulator and naturalistic driving studies have repeatedly found that phone conversation impairs driving performance comparably to moderate alcohol intoxication, and that hands-free conversation reduces but does not eliminate the cognitive distraction — drivers show "inattention blindness" to objects in plain view. Large studies using in-vehicle cameras, such as the SHRP 2 naturalistic study, found that visually demanding secondary tasks substantially raise crash odds for both novice and experienced drivers.

Law and enforcement

Many jurisdictions ban hand-held phone use while driving, and a growing number prohibit all phone interaction except through mounted, voice-operated systems; texting bans are nearly universal in developed countries. In the United States, regulation is at state level: most states ban hand-held use and texting for all drivers, with stricter rules for novices and school bus drivers. Penalties typically combine fines and license points, though enforcement is complicated by the difficulty of observing violations.

Countermeasures

Beyond legislation, countermeasures include phone "do not disturb while driving" modes, employer policies, insurance telematics, public awareness campaigns, and vehicle systems that limit device functions while in motion.
