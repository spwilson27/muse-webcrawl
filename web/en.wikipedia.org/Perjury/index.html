Perjury

Perjury (also called forswearing) is the intentional act of swearing a false oath or falsifying an affirmation to tell the truth, whether spoken or in writing, concerning matters material to an official proceeding. It is a criminal offence in most legal systems.

Elements

Perjury generally requires that a statement was made under oath or affirmation, that it was false, that the maker knew it was false (or did not believe it true), and that the statement was material — capable of influencing the proceeding. Honest mistakes and genuine failures of memory are not perjury. Related offences include subornation of perjury (persuading another to perjure themselves) and perjury traps in investigative practice.

Penalties

In the United States, federal perjury carries up to five years' imprisonment. In England and Wales, the Perjury Act 1911 provides a maximum of seven years. High-profile cases, from the trial of Alger Hiss to perjury convictions of public officials, illustrate the offence's role in protecting the integrity of judicial proceedings.
