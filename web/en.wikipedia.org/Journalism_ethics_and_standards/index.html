Journalism ethics and standards

Journalistic ethics and standards comprise principles of ethics and good practice applicable to the specific challenges faced by journalists. Historically and currently, this subset of media ethics is widely known to journalists as their professional "code of ethics."

Core principles

Common elements of codes of ethics include truthfulness, accuracy, objectivity, impartiality, fairness, and public accountability — as these apply to the acquisition of newsworthy information and its subsequent dissemination to the public. The Society of Professional Journalists' code, first adopted in 1973 and repeatedly revised, organises its principles under four headings: Seek Truth and Report It, Minimize Harm, Act Independently, and Be Accountable and Transparent.

Standards in practice

Professional standards include verifying information before publication, corroborating claims with multiple sources, distinguishing news from opinion and advertising, correcting errors promptly, avoiding conflicts of interest, protecting confidential sources, and refusing gifts or favours that could compromise independence. Particular ethical issues include the use of anonymous sources, undercover reporting, chequebook journalism, coverage of suicide and of victims of crime, and the publication of graphic images.

Self-regulation

Enforcement is typically by self-regulation: press councils, ombudsmen, and editors' codes (such as the UK Editors' Code of Practice) rather than law, reflecting the value placed on freedom of the press. Critics note that commercial pressures, the 24-hour news cycle, and social media have strained traditional standards.
