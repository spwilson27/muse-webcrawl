Saying

A saying is any concisely written or spoken expression that is especially memorable because of its meaning or style. Sayings include proverbs, aphorisms, maxims, adages, and other short, pithy expressions whose meaning is widely understood and which express a general truth, piece of advice, or observation about the world. Unlike idioms, whose meaning is figurative and not deducible from the words themselves, a saying is usually intelligible on its face while gaining force from tradition and repetition.

Types of sayings

Scholars of paremiology distinguish several overlapping forms. A proverb is a traditional saying that expresses a perceived truth based on common sense or experience—"Actions speak louder than words." An aphorism is a concise statement of a principle, often with a named author, such as Hippocrates' "Life is short, art long." A maxim states a rule of conduct; an adage is a proverb that has gained credibility through long use; an epigram adds wit or paradox to the form. A cliché, by contrast, is a saying whose frequent repetition has drained it of force.

Function and transmission

Sayings condense communal experience into portable form. They teach, warn, console, and argue by invoking shared wisdom rather than proof; folklorists note that proverbs are often used in matched opposing pairs—"Look before you leap" against "He who hesitates is lost"—so that a saying fits almost any occasion. Collections of sayings are among the oldest written literature, from Sumerian proverb tablets and the biblical Book of Proverbs to Erasmus's Adagia (1500), a Renaissance bestseller that gathered thousands of Greek and Latin sayings with commentary.

In modern use

Sayings persist in everyday speech, advertising slogans, and political rhetoric, and new ones continue to be coined and spread—"Garbage in, garbage out" from computing, for example. Dictionaries of quotations and proverbs, such as the Oxford Dictionary of Proverbs, catalogue their origins and variants across languages, many sayings having close equivalents in dozens of cultures.
