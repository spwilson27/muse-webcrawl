Sublime Porte

The Sublime Porte (Ottoman Turkish: Bāb-ı ʿĀlī, "High Gate"; Turkish: Bab-ı Âli) was the name given in diplomatic practice to the central government of the Ottoman Empire, and by extension a metonym for the Ottoman state itself. The name derives from the gate giving entrance to the block of buildings in Istanbul (formerly Constantinople) that housed the principal state departments, including the offices of the Grand Vizier, the chief executive of the empire under the sultan.

Usage

European diplomats and journalists routinely wrote of decisions of "the Porte" in the same way that later writers refer to "the Kremlin" or "the White House". The term was especially current in the 18th and 19th centuries, when the foreign embassies in Constantinople negotiated with the Grand Vizier's government — the Imperial Council having earlier met at the Topkapı Palace.

The building

The Bab-ı Âli complex stood near the Topkapı Palace and the Alay Köşkü. After fires in the 19th century the buildings were reconstructed in stone. Following the establishment of the Republic of Turkey in 1923, the complex came to house the Istanbul governorship, a role it retains.
