Nighthawks

Nighthawks is a 1942 oil painting by the American artist Edward Hopper. It depicts three customers and a counterman in a brightly lit downtown diner late at night, viewed through the diner's glass windows from a darkened street. It is one of the most famous and widely imitated works of American art.

Description and creation

Hopper said the scene was suggested by a restaurant on Greenwich Avenue in New York, simplified and universalised. The fluourescent-lit interior, the absence of a visible entrance, and the figures' isolation evoke modern urban loneliness and unease, heightened by its completion shortly after the attack on Pearl Harbor.

Reception and legacy

Acquired by the Art Institute of Chicago in 1942 for $3,000, the painting remains in its collection. It has inspired countless parodies and homages in film, literature, music and advertising, and influenced the look of film noir and works such as Blade Runner.
