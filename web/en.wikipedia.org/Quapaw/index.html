Quapaw

RegionsArkansas, Oklahoma (historically also Illinois, Missouri)
LanguageQuapaw (Dhegiha Siouan), English
Self-nameUgákhpa ("those going against the current")

The Quapaw (also called Arkansas) are a federally recognized tribe of Native American people, the Quapaw Nation, headquartered in Quapaw, Oklahoma. Their historical territory lay at the confluence of the Arkansas and Mississippi rivers; the state of Arkansas takes its name from the French rendering of a Quapaw-related term.

History

The Quapaw, speakers of a Dhegiha Siouan language closely related to those of the Omaha, Ponca, Kansa and Osage, were encountered by French explorers Marquette and Jolliet in 1673. Allies of France during the colonial era, they ceded their lands in treaties of 1818 and 1824 and were removed to Indian Territory (present-day Oklahoma) in the 1830s.

Culture and government

The Quapaw Nation is governed by an elected business committee and operates enterprises in northeastern Oklahoma, including the Downstream Casino Resort. Efforts continue to revitalize the Quapaw language, which is taught in tribal programmes.
