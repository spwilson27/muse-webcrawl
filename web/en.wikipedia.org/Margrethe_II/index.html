Margrethe II (born 16 April 1940) is a member of the Danish royal family who reigned as Queen of Denmark from 14 January 1972 until her abdication on 14 January 2024. She is the eldest daughter of Frederik IX and Ingrid of Sweden.

Early life and accession

Margrethe was born during the first week of the German occupation of Denmark in the Second World War. At her birth the Danish throne passed only through the male line, but a 1953 constitutional change allowed female succession, making her heir presumptive. She studied at Copenhagen, Cambridge, Aarhus, the Sorbonne and the London School of Economics. In 1967 she married the French diplomat Henri de Laborde de Monpezat, later Prince Henrik, with whom she had two sons. She succeeded her father on his death in 1972, becoming Denmark's first female sovereign since Margrethe I in the early 15th century.

Reign

As monarch, Margrethe was known for her weekly audiences, her New Year's addresses, and her insistence on remaining politically neutral while engaging widely with Danish society. Her reign saw Denmark join the European Community in 1973 and a steady modernisation of the monarchy's public role. She celebrated her Golden Jubilee in 2022.

Abdication and later life

In her New Year's address on 31 December 2023, Margrethe announced she would abdicate on the 52nd anniversary of her accession. On 14 January 2024 she signed the instrument of abdication and her elder son succeeded as Frederik X; she retained the style and title of Queen.

Interests

Margrethe is an accomplished artist: she has illustrated Danish editions of The Lord of the Rings (her drawings reportedly encouraged Tolkien's publisher), designed costumes and sets for theatre and film, and exhibited paintings and découpage works. She also has a serious interest in archaeology.
