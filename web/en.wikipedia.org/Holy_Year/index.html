Holy Year

A Holy Year, or Jubilee Year, is a special year of remission of sins and universal pardon in the Roman Catholic Church. Ordinary jubilees are normally celebrated every 25 years; extraordinary Holy Years may be proclaimed by the pope for special occasions.

Origins

The concept derives from the biblical Jubilee described in Leviticus, in which slaves were freed and debts forgiven every fiftieth year. Pope Boniface VIII proclaimed the first Christian Holy Year in 1300, granting a plenary indulgence to pilgrims who visited the Roman basilicas; vast crowds, including Dante, came to Rome.

Development

After experiments with 50- and 33-year intervals, Paul II fixed the ordinary jubilee at every 25 years in the 15th century, so that each generation might experience one. The ritual centrepiece is the opening of the Holy Door of St Peter's Basilica, walled up between jubilees, and of the other major basilicas.

Extraordinary jubilees

Popes also proclaim extraordinary Holy Years, for example for anniversaries of the Redemption (1933, 1983) or, under Pope Francis, the Jubilee of Mercy (2015–16), when Holy Doors were opened in cathedrals worldwide for the first time.
