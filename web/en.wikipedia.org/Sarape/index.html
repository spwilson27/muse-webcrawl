Sarape

A sarape (also spelled serape) is a long blanket-like shawl or cloak traditionally worn by men in Mexico. It is usually woven from wool or cotton and features brightly coloured stripes and patterns, often with fringes at the ends.

History and production

The sarape is particularly associated with the city of Saltillo in Coahuila, whose fine "Saltillo sarapes" of the colonial and 19th-century periods are prized for their intricate diamond-centred designs. Similar garments are produced in Tlaxcala, Puebla, and other weaving centres. The garment evolved from a blending of indigenous weaving traditions and Spanish colonial influences.

Use

Traditionally worn draped over the shoulders or over one shoulder, the sarape serves as a cloak, blanket, or saddle covering, and is an iconic element of charro dress. Today sarapes are widely sold as souvenirs and used as decorative textiles.
