Malbork Castle

The Castle of the Teutonic Order in Malbork is a 13th-century fortified monastery and castle in Malbork, Poland. It is the largest castle in the world measured by land area and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The Teutonic Knights began building the castle (Marienburg) in the 1270s beside the Nogat River. It became the Order's headquarters in 1309 and grew into a vast complex of three castles — High, Middle and Low — enclosed by defensive walls. After the Thirteen Years' War it passed to Poland in 1466 and served as a royal residence. Heavily damaged in the Second World War, it was extensively restored and now houses a museum.

Architecture

Built largely of red brick in Gothic style, the complex includes the Grand Master's palace, the great refectory, St Mary's Church, chapter house, dormitories and extensive fortifications, exemplifying the power of the crusading order.
