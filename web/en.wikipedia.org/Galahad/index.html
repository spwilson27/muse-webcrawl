Galahad

Sir Galahad is a knight of King Arthur's Round Table and one of the three achievers of the Holy Grail in Arthurian legend. He is the son of Sir Lancelot and Elaine of Corbenic, and is renowned for his purity and piety, which make him the only knight worthy of completing the Grail Quest.

Legend

Galahad first appears in the Lancelot-Grail (Vulgate) cycle of the thirteenth century. Conceived when Elaine tricks Lancelot into believing she is Guinevere, Galahad is raised in a convent and knighted by his father. At Camelot he takes the Siege Perilous, a seat reserved for the Grail knight, and draws a sword from a floating stone.

The Grail Quest

Together with Percival and Bors, Galahad reaches the Grail chapel at Sarras, where he beholds the Grail openly and achieves the quest. Having attained this spiritual perfection, he asks to die and ascends to heaven, sometimes taking the Grail with him. Later retellings, including Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur and Tennyson's poems, cemented his image as the ideal of the Christian knight.
