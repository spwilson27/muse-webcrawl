Electronics

Electronics is a scientific and engineering discipline that studies and applies the principles of physics to design, create, and operate devices that manipulate electrons and other electrically charged particles. It is a subfield of physics and electrical engineering which uses active devices such as transistors and diodes to control and amplify electric currents and to convert them from one form to another.

History

Electronics began with the invention of the vacuum tube in the early 1900s, enabling radio, television, and early computers. The transistor (1947) and the integrated circuit (1958) miniaturised electronics, leading to the modern digital revolution.

Branches

Analogue electronics processes continuous signals, while digital electronics uses discrete levels to represent information. Other branches include microelectronics, power electronics, optoelectronics, embedded systems, and radio-frequency electronics.

Components

Circuits are built from passive components (resistors, capacitors, inductors) and active components (diodes, transistors, integrated circuits), mounted on printed circuit boards.

Applications

Electronic devices are ubiquitous: computers, smartphones, medical equipment, vehicles, industrial controls, and household appliances all depend on them.
