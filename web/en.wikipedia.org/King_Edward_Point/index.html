King Edward Point

King Edward Point is a research station and the capital of South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands, a British Overseas Territory in the South Atlantic. It lies on the northeastern coast of South Georgia at Cumberland East Bay, near the former whaling station of Grytviken.

History and role

The site was named after King Edward VII. A magistrate's residence and administrative post were established in the early 20th century to regulate the whaling industry. During the 1982 Falklands War, Argentine forces occupied the point before it was retaken by British forces in Operation Paraquet. Since 2001 the British Antarctic Survey has operated the applied fisheries research station there on behalf of the territory's government.

The small resident population consists of government officers and scientists; the station supports fisheries management, including the toothfish fishery around the islands, and provides harbour and customs services for visiting vessels.
