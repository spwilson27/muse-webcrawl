United Overseas Bank Limited (UOB) is a Singaporean multinational banking corporation headquartered in Singapore. Founded in 1935, it is one of the three largest banks in Southeast Asia by total assets, alongside DBS Bank and OCBC Bank, with a network of more than 500 branches and offices across 19 countries and territories, principally in Asia.

History

The bank was founded on 6 August 1935 by businessman Wee Kheng Chiang together with six partners, as the United Chinese Bank. It was established to serve the Hokkien Chinese community in Singapore. The bank was renamed United Overseas Bank in 1965, when it opened its first overseas branch in Hong Kong.

Under Wee's son, Wee Cho Yaw, who became chairman in 1974, UOB grew through a series of acquisitions, including Chung Khiaw Bank in 1971, Lee Wah Bank in 1973, and Industrial & Commercial Bank in 1987. In 2001, after a contested contest with DBS, UOB acquired Overseas Union Bank (OUB), then Singapore's fourth-largest bank, in a deal valued at about S$10 billion, greatly expanding its local market share.

UOB expanded regionally, acquiring banks in Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, and China. In 2022, it agreed to acquire Citigroup's consumer banking businesses in Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam for approximately S$4.9 billion, roughly doubling its retail customer base in those markets.

Operations

UOB provides a wide range of financial services: personal financial services, private banking, commercial and corporate banking, transaction banking, investment banking, treasury services, asset management, and venture capital. Its subsidiaries include United Overseas Insurance and the asset manager UOB Asset Management. The bank operates in China through UOB (China), headquartered in Shanghai, and holds a stake in Bank of Jiangsu-linked ventures.

The bank's headquarters, UOB Plaza at Raffles Place, is a prominent Singapore skyscraper complex designed by Kenzo Tange, completed in 1986 and extended in 1995.

Shareholding

UOB is listed on the Singapore Exchange (SGX: U11) and is a constituent of the Straits Times Index. The Wee family remains a significant shareholder, and the bank is widely regarded as one of the best-capitalized banks in Asia, with high credit ratings from Moody's, S&P, and Fitch.
