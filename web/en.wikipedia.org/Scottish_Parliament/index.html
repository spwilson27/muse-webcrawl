Scottish Parliament

The Scottish Parliament (Scottish Gaelic: Pàrlamaid na h-Alba; Scots: Scots Pairlament) is the devolved, unicameral legislature of Scotland. Located in the Holyrood area of Edinburgh, it is often referred to by the metonym Holyrood. The Parliament is a democratically elected body comprising 129 Members of the Scottish Parliament (MSPs), who debate and pass legislation on devolved matters including health, education, justice, policing, local government, agriculture, the environment and some taxation.

History

Scotland's original parliament, the Parliament of Scotland, sat from the early thirteenth century until 1707, when the Acts of Union merged it with the Parliament of England to form the Parliament of Great Britain. Calls for a restored Scottish legislature grew through the twentieth century. A 1979 referendum failed to deliver devolution, but after the 1997 referendum voted decisively in favour, the UK Parliament passed the Scotland Act 1998. The new Parliament was convened on 12 May 1999, with the words of Winnie Ewing: "The Scottish Parliament, adjourned on the 25th day of March in the year 1707 is hereby reconvened." Donald Dewar became the first First Minister.

Elections and composition

MSPs are elected by the additional member system, a form of proportional representation: 73 represent individual constituencies under first-past-the-post, and 56 are elected from eight regional lists. Elections are normally held every five years. The Parliament elects a Presiding Officer and nominates a First Minister, who heads the Scottish Government. Its powers were expanded by the Scotland Acts of 2012 and 2016, the latter following the 2014 independence referendum, giving Holyrood control over income tax rates and bands in Scotland and other fiscal and welfare powers.

The building

The Scottish Parliament Building at Holyrood, designed by the Spanish architect Enric Miralles, opened in 2004. Its cost rose from initial estimates of £10–40 million to around £414 million, provoking controversy and a public inquiry, though the building has also won architectural acclaim, including the Stirling Prize. Its debating chamber features a semi-circular layout intended to encourage consensus rather than confrontation.
