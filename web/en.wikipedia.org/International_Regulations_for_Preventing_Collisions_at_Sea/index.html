The International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea 1972 (COLREGs) are published by the International Maritime Organization (IMO) and set out, among other things, the "rules of the road" or navigation rules to be followed by ships and other vessels at sea to prevent collisions between two or more vessels. COLREGs can also refer to the specific political line that divides inland waterways, which are subject to their own navigation rules, and coastal waterways which are subject to COLREGs.

Content

The regulations consist of 41 rules divided into six sections: general provisions; steering and sailing rules; lights and shapes; sound and light signals; exemptions; and verification of compliance. Key rules include maintaining a proper lookout (Rule 5), proceeding at a safe speed (Rule 6), assessing risk of collision (Rule 7), action to avoid collision (Rule 8), the obligations of give-way and stand-on vessels in crossing and head-on situations (Rules 15–17), and the hierarchy of responsibilities between vessel types—for example, power-driven vessels must generally keep out of the way of vessels under sail, fishing, restricted in their ability to manoeuvre or not under command.

History

Earlier collision regulations emerged in the 19th century from British steam navigation acts and international maritime conferences, including the 1889 Washington conference rules and the 1948 Safety of Life at Sea conference regulations. The 1972 convention modernised and unified them and is kept up to date by IMO amendments.

Application

The COLREGs apply to all vessels on the high seas and in all waters connected with them navigable by seagoing vessels. Many states adopt corresponding inland rules; in the United States, for example, the Inland Navigation Rules apply on inland waters west of designated demarcation lines.
