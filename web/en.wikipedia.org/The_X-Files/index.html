The X-Files is an American science fiction drama television series created by Chris Carter. The original series aired from September 10, 1993, to May 19, 2002, on Fox, spanning nine seasons and 202 episodes, and was revived for two further seasons in 2016 and 2018. The series revolves around Federal Bureau of Investigation special agents Fox Mulder (David Duchovny) and Dana Scully (Gillian Anderson), who investigate X-Files: marginalized, unsolved cases involving paranormal phenomena.

Premise

Mulder, a profiler convinced that his sister was abducted by aliens, is a true believer in the existence of extraterrestrials and a government conspiracy to hide the truth. Scully, a medical doctor and scientist, is assigned to debunk his work, but her reliance on evidence gradually brings her to question her own skepticism. Their investigations alternate between self-contained "monster of the week" episodes and a serialized "mythology" arc concerning alien colonization and the shadowy Syndicate, personified by the Cigarette Smoking Man. The show's taglines — "The truth is out there," "Trust no one," and "I want to believe" — entered popular culture.

Production and reception

Filmed in Vancouver for its first five seasons and in Los Angeles thereafter, the series became Fox's signature drama and a defining show of the 1990s, drawing over 19 million American viewers at its peak. It won numerous awards, including Emmy Awards for Anderson and for its writing, makeup, and cinematography, and Golden Globe Awards for best drama series and for both leads. Two feature films were released: The X-Files (1998) and The X-Files: I Want to Believe (2008).

Legacy

The series had an enduring cultural influence, popularizing conspiracy-themed television, shifting expectations for female leads in science fiction — the so-called "Scully effect" encouraging women into STEM fields — and spawning the spin-offs Millennium and The Lone Gunmen.
