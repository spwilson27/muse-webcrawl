Northrop Grumman EA-6B Prowler

The Northrop Grumman (formerly Grumman) EA-6B Prowler is a twin-engine, four-seat, mid-wing electronic-warfare aircraft derived from the A-6 Intruder airframe. Operated by the United States Navy and, until 2019, the United States Marine Corps, it was designed for carrier-based and advanced-base operations in support of strike aircraft and ground troops by jamming enemy radar systems and gathering radio intelligence, and it could also attack radar sites with AGM-88 HARM anti-radiation missiles.

Design and development

The Prowler was developed from the A-6 Intruder attack aircraft, with a lengthened fuselage to accommodate a four-person cockpit arranged side-by-side in two rows, and a distinctive gold-tinted canopy to shield the crew from radio-frequency emissions. Its AN/ALQ-99 tactical jamming system, carried in external pods, could monitor and jam enemy radar and communications across a wide frequency range. The aircraft is recognisable by the large fin-tip pod housing receiver antennas.

Operational history

The EA-6B entered service in 1971 and flew in the later stages of the Vietnam War. It supported virtually every U.S. combat operation thereafter, including in Libya, the Gulf War, the Balkans, Afghanistan, and Iraq. Marine Corps Prowlers also flew long-range electronic-attack missions from land bases. From 1995, following the retirement of the Air Force's EF-111A Raven, the Prowler was the only dedicated radar-jamming aircraft in U.S. service until the EA-18G Growler entered service.

Retirement

The Navy completed its transition to the EA-18G Growler in 2015. The Marine Corps' last Prowler squadron, VMAQ-2, was deactivated on 8 March 2019, ending 48 years of Prowler operations. Several aircraft are preserved in museums, including at the National Air and Space Museum.
