Eight Masters of the Tang and Song

Classical prose in the guwen tradition

The Eight Masters of the Tang and Song (Chinese: 唐宋八大家) were a group of eight Chinese prose writers of the Tang and Song dynasties who were renowned for their mastery of the essay in the classical guwen ("ancient prose") style. The designation was popularised during the Ming dynasty, when anthologists such as Mao Kun collected and promoted their works as models of classical prose.

The eight masters

From the Tang dynasty: Han Yu (768–824) and Liu Zongyuan (773–819), who led the Classical Prose Movement advocating a return to the direct, unadorned style of ancient texts over the ornate parallel prose then in fashion. From the Song dynasty: Ouyang Xiu (1007–1072), Su Xun (1009–1066), Su Shi (1037–1101), Su Che (1039–1112) — Su Xun and his two sons — Zeng Gong (1019–1083), and Wang Anshi (1021–1086).

Influence

The eight masters' essays became canonical models for later Chinese prose writing and were staples of classical education and the imperial examination system. Their works range over political memorials, letters, prefaces, travel records, and philosophical argument, and remain widely read and studied in China today.
