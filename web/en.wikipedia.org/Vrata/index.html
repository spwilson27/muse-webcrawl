Vrata


Sanskrit: व्रत
Meaning: Vow, resolve, religious observance
Religions: Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism

Vrata (Sanskrit: व्रत) is a Sanskrit word meaning a vow, resolve or solemn religious observance. In Hinduism it refers to ritual practices — most commonly fasting, but also abstinence, prayer, charity and pilgrimage — undertaken voluntarily for a fixed period in devotion to a deity or in pursuit of a goal, such as health, prosperity, the well-being of family members, or spiritual merit.

Practice

Vratas are described in the Puranas and in dedicated manuals (vrata literature). The practitioner typically makes a formal resolve (sankalpa), observes dietary rules that range from a single simple meal to complete fasting, recites the origin story of the vow (vrata katha), and concludes with offerings and gifts. Well-known vratas include Ekadashi, observed on the eleventh day of each lunar fortnight in honour of Vishnu; Karva Chauth, kept by married women in North India for the longevity of their husbands; Satyanarayana Puja; and the sixteen Mondays (Solah Somvar) devoted to Shiva.

In Jainism and other traditions

In Jainism, vrata has a more technical meaning: the vows that define the religious life. The five great vows (mahavratas) of monks — non-violence (ahimsa), truth (satya), non-stealing (asteya), celibacy (brahmacharya) and non-possession (aparigraha) — are observed in a lesser form (anuvratas, "small vows") by laypeople. Related concepts of disciplined observance appear in Buddhism and Sikhism as well.
