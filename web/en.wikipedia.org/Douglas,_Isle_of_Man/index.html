Douglas (Manx: Doolish) is the capital city and largest town of the Isle of Man, a self-governing British Crown Dependency in the Irish Sea. It is located at the mouth of the River Douglas and lies on a sweeping bay about two miles long. Around a third of the island's population lives in Douglas and its suburbs, and the town is the centre of the island's government, finance, shipping and transport.

History

The town takes its name from the Dhoo and Glass, the two rivers which join there (from Manx dubh "black" and gless "green"). A settlement grew around the harbour and St Mary's Isle (Conister Rock) in the bay. Douglas developed as a trading port in the 17th and 18th centuries, partly through the "running trade" (smuggling) with Britain. It was made the island's capital in 1863, when the Lieutenant Governor moved his residence from Castletown. In the Victorian era Douglas became a major holiday resort, served by steamers from Liverpool and Fleetwood, and the famous horse-drawn trams along its promenade date from 1876.

Government

The High Court of Tynwald, the island's parliament, sits in Douglas, as do the Council of Ministers and government departments in the Legislative Buildings on Bucks Road. The Lieutenant Governor's official residence, Government House, is in Onchan, adjoining the town.

Economy

Douglas is the hub of the Isle of Man's offshore financial-services sector, including banking, insurance, fund management, e-gaming and shipping registries. The Sea Terminal at the harbour serves ferry routes to Heysham and Liverpool operated by the Isle of Man Steam Packet Company.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Tower of Refuge (1832) on Conister Rock, built after the wreck of the St George; the Manx Museum; the Gaiety Theatre; the Villa Marina; the Jubilee Clock; and the horse tramway along the two-mile promenade. Douglas Head offers views over the bay. The town is also the start/finish of the TT motorcycle races' Grandstand on Glencrutchery Road.
