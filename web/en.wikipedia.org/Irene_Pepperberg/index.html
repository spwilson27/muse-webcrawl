Irene Maxine Pepperberg (born 1949) is an American scientist noted for her studies of cognition in parrots. Her work with the African grey parrot Alex challenged the assumption that birds are incapable of language-like communication and advanced understanding of animal intelligence.

Work with Alex

Trained as a chemist at Harvard, Pepperberg bought Alex (1976–2007) in 1977 and taught him using her model/rival technique, in which two trainers model interactions for the bird. Over three decades Alex learned labels for dozens of objects, colours, and shapes; concepts of same/different and bigger/smaller; quantities up to six; and could report the material, colour, and shape of items — performance suggesting comprehension rather than mere mimicry. His final words to Pepperberg, "You be good. I love you," drew worldwide attention.

Later work

Pepperberg has continued research with other grey parrots, including Griffin, through The Alex Foundation, and held research positions at Harvard and Brandeis. Her memoir Alex & Me (2008) became a bestseller. Her research is widely cited in debates over whether non-human animals can acquire referential communication, and in comparative studies of cognition in birds and mammals.
