The GSMA (GSM Association) is a global industry organisation representing the interests of mobile network operators and the broader mobile ecosystem. Its members include more than 750 mobile operators and hundreds of companies in related industries, including handset makers, software firms, and equipment providers. The association traces its origins to the 1982 Groupe Spécial Mobile that developed the GSM standard in Europe, and the association itself was formed in 1995.

Activities

The GSMA works on behalf of the industry in areas such as spectrum policy advocacy, roaming and interoperability standards, fraud and security, and the development of services including eSIM specifications and the allocation of IMEI numbers for devices. It runs development programmes such as Mobile for Development, focused on mobile money and digital inclusion in low- and middle-income countries, and publishes the annual State of the Industry Report on Mobile Money and Mobile Economy series.

MWC

The GSMA organises Mobile World Congress (MWC), one of the world's largest technology trade shows, held annually in Barcelona since 2006 (as the successor to the 3GSM World Congress), with regional editions in Shanghai and Las Vegas. MWC Barcelona typically draws around 100,000 attendees and serves as a launch venue for major devices and network technology announcements.

Governance

The GSMA is governed by a board of senior executives from major operator groups, with a director general leading its staff. Its head office is in London, with regional offices worldwide.
