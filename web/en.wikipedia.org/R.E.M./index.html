R.E.M. was an American rock band from Athens, Georgia, formed in 1980 by singer Michael Stipe, guitarist Peter Buck, bassist Mike Mills and drummer Bill Berry. One of the first alternative rock bands to achieve mainstream success, R.E.M. pioneered the jangle-pop sound of the 1980s college-rock scene and became one of the world's best-selling music acts, with estimated sales of more than 90 million albums.

History

After the single "Radio Free Europe" (1981) and the EP Chronic Town (1982), the band's debut album Murmur (1983) was named Rolling Stone's album of the year. A run of acclaimed I.R.S. Records albums — Reckoning (1984), Fables of the Reconstruction (1985), Lifes Rich Pageant (1986) and Document (1987), the last producing the top-ten hit "The One I Love" — carried them to arena status.

Signing to Warner Bros., R.E.M. reached global stardom with Out of Time (1991), containing "Losing My Religion" and "Shiny Happy People," and Automatic for the People (1992), widely regarded as their masterpiece. Drummer Bill Berry retired in 1997 after suffering a brain aneurysm on tour in 1995; the remaining trio continued until announcing an amicable split in September 2011, after the album Collapse into Now.

Legacy

R.E.M. were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2007, their first year of eligibility. Their chiming guitars, mumbled-then-declarative vocals and literate songwriting shaped alternative rock acts from Nirvana and Radiohead to The National.
