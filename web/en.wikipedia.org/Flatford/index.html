Flatford

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Flatford is a small hamlet in the parish of East Bergholt in Suffolk, England, on the River Stour near the Essex border. It is famous as the home country of the painter John Constable (1776–1837), whose depictions of the Stour valley — known as "Constable Country" — made Flatford's mill, bridge, and lock among the most recognisable images in English art.

Constable's Flatford

Flatford Mill, a red-brick watermill on the Stour, was owned by Constable's father Golding Constable, a wealthy corn merchant. The mill and its surroundings appear in many of the artist's works, including The Hay Wain (1821), whose view looks across the millpond toward the mill, and Boat Building near Flatford Mill. Willy Lott's House, the white cottage beside the mill named for Constable's lifelong friend and tenant farmer, is the building seen at the left of The Hay Wain. Nearby stands Valley Farm, a timber-framed house that also appears in his paintings.

Present day

Flatford Mill is now owned by the National Trust and leased to the Field Studies Council as a residential field centre. Bridge Cottage, a 16th-century thatched cottage by Flatford Bridge, houses an exhibition about Constable. A visitor centre and tea room serve the many tourists who walk the Stour valley paths between East Bergholt, Flatford, and Dedham — themselves subjects of Constable's art.
