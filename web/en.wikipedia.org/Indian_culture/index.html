Culture of India

Indian culture is the heritage of social norms and technologies that originated in or are associated with the ethno-linguistically diverse India, a civilization with roots reaching back more than 4,500 years to the Indus Valley civilisation. India is the birthplace of four major world religions — Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism — and its culture has been shaped by millennia of trade, conquest, and synthesis with Persian, Central Asian, and European influences.

Language and literature

India has 22 officially recognized languages and hundreds of others. Sanskrit literature includes the Vedas, the epics Mahabharata and Ramayana, and the works of Kalidasa; Tamil Sangam literature is comparably ancient. Modern Indian literature in English and the regional languages, and the world's largest film output — including Bollywood and regional cinemas — carry Indian stories worldwide.

Arts and festivals

Classical dance forms such as Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, and Odissi, and the classical music systems of Hindustani and Carnatic traditions, remain living arts. The festival calendar includes Diwali, Holi, Eid, Durga Puja, Pongal, Onam, and Christmas, celebrated across regions and religions. Indian cuisine, renowned for its use of spices, varies strikingly by region, from northern tandoori and Mughlai dishes to southern dosas and coastal seafood.

Society

Family — often in joint households — and community remain central social institutions, alongside enduring debates over caste, gender, and modernity. Concepts such as namaste greeting, respect for elders, and the philosophy of "unity in diversity" are often cited as hallmarks of the culture.
