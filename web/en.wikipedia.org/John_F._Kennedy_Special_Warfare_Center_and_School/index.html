John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School

LocationFort Liberty (formerly Fort Bragg), North Carolina
BranchUnited States Army
RoleSpecial operations training
Named forPresident John F. Kennedy

The U.S. Army John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School (SWCS), at Fort Liberty, North Carolina, is the U.S. Army's principal institution for training special operations forces, including Special Forces (the "Green Berets"), Civil Affairs and Psychological Operations personnel.

History

The center traces its origins to the Psychological Warfare School established at Fort Riley, Kansas, in 1950 and moved to Fort Bragg in 1952, where the Psychological Warfare Center combined psywar and the newly created Special Forces. President John F. Kennedy championed the Green Berets, visiting Fort Bragg in 1961 and authorising the green beret as distinctive headgear; after his assassination the institution was renamed in his honour in 1964. It was reorganised as a center and school, and since 1987 has operated under the U.S. Army Special Operations Command.

Training

SWCS runs the Special Forces Qualification Course (the "Q Course"), comprising selection (SFAS), language and culture training, military occupational specialty training, collective training (including the Robin Sage unconventional warfare exercise in the North Carolina Piedmont), survival (SERE) and graduation (Regimental First Formation). It also trains Civil Affairs and Military Information Support Operations soldiers, and includes the Noncommissioned Officer Academy and sniper and military freefall courses. Graduates earn the Special Forces tab and green beret.

Organisation

The center includes several training groups and the 1st Special Warfare Training Group (Airborne). Its motto is associated with the Special Forces maxim De oppresso liber — "to free the oppressed".
