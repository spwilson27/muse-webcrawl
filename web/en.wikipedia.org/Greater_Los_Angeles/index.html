Greater Los Angeles

The urbanized area of Greater Los Angeles, centred on the Los Angeles basin.

Greater Los Angeles is the most populous metropolitan area in the U.S. state of California and the second-most populous in the United States after New York. In its broadest definition — the Los Angeles–Long Beach combined statistical area — it covers five counties (Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, and Ventura), spans about 33,954 square miles (87,940 km²), and had a population of roughly 18.5 million in the 2020 census, of whom about 12.5 million live in the two-county metropolitan statistical area.

Geography

The region occupies a broad coastal plain and inland valleys bounded by the Pacific Ocean and by mountain ranges including the San Gabriels, San Bernardinos, and Santa Anas. Major urban centres besides the city of Los Angeles itself include Long Beach, Anaheim, Santa Ana, Riverside, San Bernardino, Glendale, Pasadena, and Oxnard. The Inland Empire, comprising Riverside and San Bernardino counties, is a major logistics hub, while the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach form the busiest container port complex in the Americas.

Economy and culture

Greater Los Angeles is a global centre of entertainment — Hollywood anchors the world's film and television industries — as well as international trade, aerospace, technology, petroleum, fashion, and tourism. It is one of the most ethnically diverse regions in the United States, with large Latino, Asian American, and other communities, and home to major universities including UCLA, USC, and Caltech.
