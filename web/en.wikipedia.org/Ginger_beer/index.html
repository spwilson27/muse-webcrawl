Ginger beer is a carbonated beverage flavoured with ginger, sweetened and typically served cold. It originated in Yorkshire, England, in the mid-18th century and became popular throughout Britain, its colonies and the United States, reaching peak popularity in the early 20th century.

History and production

Traditional ginger beer was brewed: a starter culture known as a "ginger beer plant" — a symbiotic colony of yeast and bacteria — fermented a mixture of ginger, sugar, water and lemon juice for several days, producing a mildly alcoholic drink. Commercial recipes were constrained by British excise law from 1855, which limited alcohol content to 2%. Traditional Jamaican-style ginger beer remains notably fiery thanks to a high proportion of fresh ginger.

Most modern ginger beer is not brewed but manufactured: a ginger-flavoured, carbonated soft drink containing little or no alcohol (generally under 0.5%, allowing it to be sold as a soft drink). It differs from ginger ale, a milder, clearer drink developed later in Ireland and North America.

Uses

Ginger beer is the essential mixer in the Moscow mule (with vodka and lime, served in a copper mug) and in the Dark 'n' Stormy (with dark rum). It is also drunk on its own and used in alcohol-free "mocktail" versions of those drinks.
