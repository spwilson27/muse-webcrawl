Middle lamella

The middle lamella is a layer rich in pectin that cements together the primary walls of adjacent plant cells. It is the first layer formed during cell division, laid down at the cell plate during cytokinesis, and it is upon this layer that each daughter cell deposits its own primary wall.

Composed largely of calcium and magnesium pectates, the middle lamella glues neighbouring cells into tissues. In mature woody tissue the middle lamella is most concentrated at the corners of the cell junctions. Controlled dissolution of the middle lamella by pectinase enzymes is important in fruit ripening and softening, leaf and fruit abscission, and in industrial maceration processes such as flax retting and wood pulping, where lignified compound middle lamellae must be separated.
