Gucci is an Italian luxury fashion house based in Florence, Italy, and one of the world's best-known luxury brands. Founded by Guccio Gucci in 1921, the company began as a shop for fine leather luggage and equestrian equipment and grew into a global seller of ready-to-wear clothing, handbags, shoes, accessories, fragrances, and home decoration. Gucci is the largest brand of the French luxury group Kering.

History

Guccio Gucci, who had worked as a porter at London's Savoy Hotel and admired the luggage of wealthy guests, opened his first shop in Florence in 1921. Under his sons, especially Aldo Gucci, the firm expanded internationally after the Second World War, opening in New York in 1953. Signature products such as the bamboo-handled bag (1947), the horsebit loafer (1953), and the Flora scarf created for Grace Kelly (1966) established its identity. The interlocking double-G logo and the green-red-green web stripe became globally recognized trademarks.

Family feuding in the 1980s, dramatized in the 2021 film House of Gucci, ended family control; the murder of Maurizio Gucci, Guccio's grandson, in 1995 marked its nadir as a scandal but the brand was simultaneously being revived under chief executive Domenico De Sole and designer Tom Ford, whose provocative collections from 1994 restored Gucci's fashion authority. After Ford's departure in 2004, Frida Giannini led design until 2015, when Alessandro Michele's eclectic, maximalist aesthetic drove a period of rapid growth. Michele left in 2022; Sabato De Sarno became creative director in 2023 and was succeeded in 2025 by Demna, formerly of Balenciaga.

Business

Gucci sells through hundreds of directly operated stores worldwide and is consistently ranked among the most valuable luxury brands, with annual revenues in the vicinity of €10 billion in its strongest recent years before a downturn in demand in the mid-2020s prompted strategic changes at Kering.
