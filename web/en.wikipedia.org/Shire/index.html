Shire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: English shire countryside]

A shire is a traditional term for an administrative division of land, found in Great Britain and some other English-speaking countries. It is generally synonymous with county. The shires were first established in Wessex in the early Anglo-Saxon period and spread across England by the tenth century, each governed by a shire reeve — the origin of the office of sheriff.

Many English county names retain the suffix "-shire": Yorkshire, Lancashire, Cheshire, Shropshire (formerly Salopshire), and Hampshire among them. In Scotland, shires were the basis of county administration until local government reorganisation in 1975. In Australia, "shire" remains the designation of many rural local government areas, such as the Shire of Mornington Peninsula in Victoria.

Fiction

The term was borrowed by J. R. R. Tolkien for the Shire, the homeland of the hobbits in The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, modelled on the rural English counties of his childhood.
