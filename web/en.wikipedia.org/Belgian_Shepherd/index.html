Belgian Shepherd

The Belgian Shepherd (also known as the Belgian Sheepdog or Chien de Berger Belge) is a Belgian breed of herding dog. In its home country and under the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI) it is a single breed with four varieties distinguished by coat type and colour: the long-haired black Groenendael, the long-haired fawn/mahogany Tervuren, the short-haired Malinois, and the rough-haired Laekenois. Some registries, such as the American Kennel Club, register the varieties as separate breeds.

History

In the late nineteenth century Belgium, like other European countries, moved to standardize its native shepherd dogs. Professor Adolphe Reul of the Belgian veterinary school gathered examples of the type in 1891, and the Club du Chien de Berger Belge was founded the same year. The varieties take their names from localities around Brussels: Groenendael, Tervuren, Malines (Malinois), and Laeken.

Characteristics

Belgian Shepherds are medium-sized, square-built, athletic dogs, typically 56–66 cm at the withers and 25–30 kg in weight. They are intelligent, alert, and energetic, with a strong work drive. The Malinois in particular has become one of the world's most popular military and police working dogs, used for detection, tracking, and protection work, while the Groenendael and Tervuren are common in herding, obedience, agility, and as companion dogs.

Temperament and care

The breed is loyal and trainable but requires substantial exercise, mental stimulation, and experienced handling; under-stimulated dogs may develop destructive or anxious behaviours. Health concerns include hip and elbow dysplasia, epilepsy, and eye conditions such as cataracts and progressive retinal atrophy.
