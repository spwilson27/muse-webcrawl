Warner Bros. Entertainment Inc. (commonly known as Warner Bros., or abbreviated as WB) is an American film and entertainment studio headquartered at the Warner Bros. Studios complex in Burbank, California, and a subsidiary of Warner Bros. Discovery. Founded in 1923 by four brothers, Harry, Albert, Sam, and Jack Warner, the company established itself as a leader in the American film industry before diversifying into animation, television, and video games. It is one of the "Big Five" major American film studios.

History

The Warner brothers, sons of Polish-Jewish immigrants, began in film exhibition and distribution before incorporating Warner Bros. Pictures in 1923. The studio gambled on sound technology, releasing The Jazz Singer (1927), the first successful "talkie", which hastened the end of the silent era. In the 1930s Warner Bros. became known for gritty gangster films and musicals, and its animation unit produced the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies shorts that introduced Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, and Porky Pig.

Major successes across later decades include Casablanca (1942), the Harry Potter and The Lord of the Rings franchises, the DC Comics superhero films, and television series from Friends to The Big Bang Theory produced by its television arm. The studio passed through ownership by Warner Communications, Time Warner, and AT&T's WarnerMedia before becoming part of Warner Bros. Discovery in 2022.
