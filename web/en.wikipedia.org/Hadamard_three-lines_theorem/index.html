Hadamard three-lines theorem

In complex analysis, the Hadamard three-lines theorem is a result about the behaviour of bounded holomorphic functions defined in a strip in the complex plane. It gives an interpolation estimate: the supremum of a function on an interior line of the strip is controlled by its suprema on the two boundary lines, raised to powers that depend logarithmically on the position of the interior line.

Statement

Let f be a holomorphic function on the interior of the strip {z : a < Re(z) < b}, continuous and bounded on the closed strip. Define M(x) = supy |f(x + iy)|. Then log M(x) is a convex function of x; equivalently, for a < x < b,

M(x) ≤ M(a)(b−x)/(b−a) · M(b)(x−a)/(b−a).

Applications

The theorem is a key tool in interpolation theory for operators, underlying the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem for Lp spaces and Phragmén–Lindelöf type estimates.
