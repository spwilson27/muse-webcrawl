Multiplexing


Field: Telecommunications, electronics
Device: Multiplexer (MUX)
Inverse operation: Demultiplexing

In telecommunications and computer networks, multiplexing (sometimes contracted to muxing) is a method by which multiple analog or digital signals are combined into one signal over a shared medium, so that scarce channel resources are used efficiently. The device performing the combining is a multiplexer; the reverse process is demultiplexing.

Types

  • Frequency-division multiplexing (FDM) assigns each signal a distinct frequency band; used in radio, television, and wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM) on optical fibre.
  • Time-division multiplexing (TDM) interleaves signals in successive time slots, as in classic digital telephony trunks.
  • Code-division multiplexing separates signals by spreading codes (CDMA).
  • Statistical multiplexing allocates capacity dynamically, as in packet-switched networks.
  • Space-division multiplexing uses separate physical paths, such as multiple wires or antennas (MIMO).

Applications

Multiplexing is fundamental to telephone networks, cable television, fibre-optic backbones, and satellite links. In electronics, multiplexers also select among data inputs and drive displays and sensor arrays; in computing, the term covers techniques such as terminal multiplexing.
