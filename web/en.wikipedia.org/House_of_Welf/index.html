House of Welf

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The House of Welf (also Guelf or Guelph) is a European dynasty that has included many German and British monarchs from the 11th to the 20th century. Its name derives from the Frankish magnate Welf I, Duke of Bavaria (died 1101), and its partisans in medieval Italy, the Guelphs, opposed the Ghibellines who supported the Hohenstaufen emperors.

Founded: 9th century (Older Welfs); 11th century (Younger Welfs)
Titles: Duke of Bavaria; Duke of Saxony; Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg; Elector and King of Hanover; King of Great Britain (House of Hanover)
Current head: Ernst August, Prince of Hanover

History

The Younger House of Welf rose through Welf IV, who became Duke of Bavaria in 1070. His descendants acquired Saxony and vast north German lands. Henry the Lion (1129–1195), Duke of Saxony and Bavaria, was the most powerful prince of his age before being stripped of his duchies by Frederick Barbarossa in 1180. His son Otto IV became Holy Roman Emperor. In 1235 the family received the new Duchy of Brunswick-Lüneburg.

House of Hanover

A Welf cadet branch, the House of Hanover, gained the electoral dignity in 1692 and inherited the British throne in 1714 under George I, beginning the Georgian era. Hanoverian Welfs ruled Britain until 1901 and the Kingdom of Hanover until its annexation by Prussia in 1866; the senior line ruled the Duchy of Brunswick until 1918. The house persists today, headed by the Prince of Hanover.
