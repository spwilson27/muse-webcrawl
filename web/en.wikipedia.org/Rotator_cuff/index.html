Rotator cuff

The rotator cuff is a group of four muscles and their tendons that surround the shoulder joint, stabilising the head of the humerus within the shallow glenoid cavity of the scapula and enabling the arm's wide range of motion. The four muscles are remembered by the mnemonic SITS: supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, and subscapularis.

LocationShoulder (glenohumeral joint)
FunctionStabilisation; abduction and rotation of the arm

The four muscles

The supraspinatus, arising above the scapular spine, initiates abduction of the arm; the infraspinatus and teres minor, on the posterior scapula, externally rotate the arm; and the subscapularis, on the anterior scapula, internally rotates it. Together their tendons blend into the joint capsule, forming the cuff.

Injuries

Rotator cuff tears and tendinopathy are among the most common causes of shoulder pain, arising from trauma, repetitive overhead activity, and age-related degeneration; the supraspinatus tendon is most often affected. Diagnosis uses clinical tests and imaging such as ultrasound or MRI. Treatment ranges from physiotherapy and anti-inflammatory medication to arthroscopic surgical repair for full-thickness tears.
