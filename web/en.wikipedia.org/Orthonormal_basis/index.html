Orthonormal basis

In linear algebra, an orthonormal basis for an inner product space V is a basis whose vectors are all unit vectors (norm 1) and mutually orthogonal: for basis vectors ei, ej, the inner product ⟨ei, ej⟩ equals 1 if i = j and 0 otherwise (the Kronecker delta).

Properties

Orthonormal bases make computations especially simple. The coordinates of any vector v are simply the inner products v = Σ ⟨v, ei⟩ ei; the norm satisfies Parseval's identity, ‖v‖² = Σ |⟨v, ei⟩|²; and the matrix changing between two orthonormal bases is an orthogonal (or unitary) matrix. The standard basis of Rn is the prototypical example.

Construction and generalisation

The Gram–Schmidt process converts any finite basis into an orthonormal one. In infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, an orthonormal basis (Hilbert basis) need not be a basis in the algebraic sense; expansions converge in norm, as with Fourier series, where the trigonometric functions form an orthonormal basis of L² on an interval. A complete orthonormal system is one whose span is dense in the space.
