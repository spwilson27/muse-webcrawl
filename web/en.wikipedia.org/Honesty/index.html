Honesty

HonestyCategory: virtue
Related: truthfulness, integrity, sincerity
Opposite: dishonesty, deception

Honesty is a facet of moral character that connotes truthfulness and the absence of lying, cheating, theft or other forms of deception. The honest person not only refrains from false statements but avoids misleading others through omission, manipulation or pretence, and is typically also regarded as straightforward and trustworthy.

In philosophy

Immanuel Kant held that lying is always wrong, treating truthfulness as a perfect duty arising from the categorical imperative. Utilitarians, by contrast, generally evaluate honesty by its consequences, though many argue that a general practice of truth-telling is socially indispensable. Virtue ethicists since Aristotle have treated honesty as part of the broader virtue of truthfulness, the mean between boastfulness and self-deprecation.

In psychology

Personality research places honesty within the Honesty–Humility factor of the HEXACO model, which predicts lower tendencies toward manipulation, rule-breaking and greed. Developmental studies examine how children acquire norms of truth-telling and how "white lies" are judged across cultures.

In society

Honesty underpins trust in commerce, science and public life: fraud, perjury and academic dishonesty are sanctioned precisely because institutions depend on reliable communication. Many cultures encode the value in proverbs such as "honesty is the best policy".
