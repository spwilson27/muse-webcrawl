Manx Electric Railway

Location:Isle of Man
Route:Douglas – Laxey – Ramsey
Length:17 miles (27 km)
Gauge:3 ft (914 mm)

The Manx Electric Railway is an electric interurban tramway on the Isle of Man in the Irish Sea. It connects the island's capital, Douglas, with Laxey and the town of Ramsey in the north along a scenic coastal route of about 17 miles (27 km). Opened in stages between 1893 and 1899, it is notable for operating some of the oldest tramcars in regular service anywhere in the world.

History

The first section, from Douglas to Groudle Glen, opened on 7 September 1893, built by the Douglas & Laxey Coast Electric Tramway company. The line reached Laxey in 1894 and Ramsey in 1899. The company was later renamed the Manx Electric Railway Company. After the company's liquidation in the 1950s, the railway was nationalised in 1957 and is today operated as part of the Isle of Man's public heritage railways.

Rolling stock

The fleet of enclosed winter saloons and open crossbench "toastrack" cars is largely original Victorian and Edwardian stock. Cars 1 and 2, built in 1893, are the oldest tramcars in the world still operating on their original line. Power is supplied at 550 volts DC by overhead wire.

The route

From Derby Castle terminus in Douglas, the line climbs along the cliffs past Groudle Glen (where it connects with the Groudle Glen Railway), Onchan, and Baldrine to Laxey, where passengers can change for the Snaefell Mountain Railway. It then continues through Maughold and Ballajora to Ramsey, offering sweeping views across the Irish Sea to Scotland and Cumbria on clear days.
