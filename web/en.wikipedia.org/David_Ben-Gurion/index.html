David Ben-Gurion (1886–1973) was the primary national founder of the State of Israel and its first prime minister. As head of the Jewish Agency during the British Mandate, he led the Zionist movement's political and military preparations for statehood, read out Israel's Declaration of Independence on 14 May 1948, and directed the new state through the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.

Early life

Born David Grün in Płońsk, Poland, he became a committed Zionist as a youth and immigrated to Ottoman Palestine in 1906, working as an agricultural labourer. Expelled by the Ottomans during the First World War, he helped organise the Jewish Legion in the United States and served in the British Army. Returning to Palestine, he founded the Histadrut trade union federation in 1920 and led the Mapai party from 1930.

Path to statehood

As chairman of the Jewish Agency Executive from 1935, Ben-Gurion balanced cooperation with Britain against clandestine immigration and defence through the Haganah. After the Second World War and the Holocaust, he pressed for a Jewish state, accepted the UN Partition Plan of 1947, and unified Jewish armed groups into the Israel Defense Forces.

Prime minister

Ben-Gurion served as prime minister and defence minister 1948–1953 and again 1955–1963. He oversaw mass immigration that doubled Israel's Jewish population, the austerity programme, reparations from West Germany, the 1956 Suez campaign, and the development of the Negev, where he retired to Kibbutz Sde Boker. He resigned definitively in 1963 and died in 1973; he is regarded as Israel's founding father.
