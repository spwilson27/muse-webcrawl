Patrick Henry

Died6 June 1799 (aged 63), Red Hill, Virginia
Office1st and 6th Governor of Virginia

Patrick Henry (29 May 1736 – 6 June 1799) was an American statesman, lawyer, orator, and Founding Father who served as the first and sixth Governor of Virginia. He is best remembered for his declaration "Give me liberty, or give me death!" in a speech of March 1775 that helped persuade Virginia to arm itself for the coming conflict with Britain.

Early career

Born in Hanover County, Virginia, Henry failed as a storekeeper and planter before turning to law, gaining fame in the Parsons' Cause case of 1763, where he argued against the established clergy salaries and denounced royal interference — an early landmark of colonial resistance. Elected to the House of Burgesses in 1765, he introduced the radical Virginia Resolves against the Stamp Act.

Revolutionary leader

Henry served in the First and Second Continental Congresses and, in March 1775, addressed the Second Virginia Convention at St. John's Church in Richmond with the speech that made him famous. After the Gunpowder Incident of April 1775 he led a volunteer company toward Williamsburg. In 1776 he became the first governor of the independent Commonwealth of Virginia, serving three one-year terms and supporting George Rogers Clark's western campaigns.

Later life

A leading Anti-Federalist, Henry opposed the US Constitution at the Virginia Ratifying Convention of 1788, fearing a too-powerful central government; his campaign helped spur the adoption of the Bill of Rights. He returned as governor (1784–1786), declined several federal posts, and died at his Red Hill plantation in 1799.
