Complete blood count

An automated hematology analyzer

A complete blood count (CBC), also known as a full blood count (FBC), is a set of medical laboratory tests that provide information about the cells in a person's blood. The CBC indicates the counts of white blood cells, red blood cells and platelets, the concentration of hemoglobin, and the hematocrit (the proportion of blood volume occupied by red blood cells). Red blood cell indices — MCV, MCH, and MCHC — are also reported.

Measurements

A CBC typically reports:

  • White blood cell count (WBC), often with a differential counting neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils.
  • Red blood cell count (RBC), hemoglobin (Hb), and hematocrit (Hct).
  • Red cell indices: mean corpuscular volume (MCV), mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH), and mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC); red cell distribution width (RDW) may also be given.
  • Platelet count and mean platelet volume (MPV).

Method

Modern CBCs are performed by automated hematology analyzers that count and size cells using electrical impedance (the Coulter principle) and light scattering or fluorescence flow cytometry. Abnormal results may be flagged for manual review of a blood smear under a microscope.

Interpretation

The CBC is one of the most common blood tests in medicine. It aids in diagnosing anemia, infection, inflammation, leukemia and other hematological disorders, and in monitoring responses to treatment such as chemotherapy. Interpretation requires comparing values to reference ranges that vary with age, sex, and laboratory.
