Angioplasty

Angioplasty, also known as balloon angioplasty or percutaneous transluminal angioplasty (PTA), is a minimally invasive procedure to widen narrowed or obstructed blood vessels, most commonly arteries narrowed by atherosclerosis.

Procedure

A catheter with a deflated balloon at its tip is threaded through a blood vessel — usually via the femoral or radial artery — to the site of narrowing under X-ray guidance. The balloon is inflated to compress the plaque against the vessel wall and stretch the artery, then deflated and withdrawn. In coronary angioplasty (PCI), a stent — a small wire-mesh tube, often drug-eluting — is usually left in place to keep the artery open, followed by antiplatelet therapy to prevent clotting.

History and uses

Charles Dotter performed the first transluminal angioplasty in 1964, and Andreas Grüntzig developed the balloon catheter and performed the first coronary angioplasty in 1977. Angioplasty is the standard emergency treatment for heart attack (ST-elevation myocardial infarction) when available promptly, and is also used for stable angina, peripheral artery disease, renal artery stenosis, and carotid disease. Alternatives include coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG).
