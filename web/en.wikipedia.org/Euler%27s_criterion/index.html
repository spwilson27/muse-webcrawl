Euler's criterion

Euler's criterion is a formula in number theory for determining whether an integer is a quadratic residue modulo a prime. It is named after Leonhard Euler, who proved it in 1748.

Statement

Let p be an odd prime and a an integer coprime to p. Then a is a quadratic residue modulo p if and only if a(p−1)/2 ≡ 1 (mod p), and a is a quadratic non-residue if and only if a(p−1)/2 ≡ −1 (mod p). Equivalently, a(p−1)/2 ≡ (a|p) (mod p), where (a|p) denotes the Legendre symbol.

Use

The criterion follows from Fermat's little theorem and the fact that exactly half of the non-zero residues modulo p are quadratic residues. It gives an effective test for quadratic residuosity, though for large numbers it is slower than tests based on quadratic reciprocity, and it forms a theoretical basis for primality tests such as the Euler–Jacobi pseudoprime test and the Solovay–Strassen test.
