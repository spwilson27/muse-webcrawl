Chairlift

A chairlift is a type of aerial lift which consists of a continuously moving steel cable loop strung between two end terminals and usually over intermediate towers, carrying a series of chairs. They are the primary on-mountain transport at ski resorts, carrying skiers and snowboarders uphill with their equipment attached.

History and design

The first chairlift was installed at Sun Valley, Idaho, in 1936, adapted from banana-loading conveyor systems used on fruit docks. Early chairs carried one person; modern lifts carry two, three, four, six, or eight passengers. Detachable-grip lifts allow chairs to slow in the station for easier loading while the cable runs at full line speed.

Safety

Passengers lower a restraint bar after loading. Lifts have multiple braking and monitoring systems, and evacuation procedures exist for stoppages. Chairlifts are also used in summer for sightseeing and mountain biking at many resorts.
