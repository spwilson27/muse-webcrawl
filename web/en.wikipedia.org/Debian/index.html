Debian


Debian logo

Debian (pronounced /ˈdɛbiən/) is a free and open-source operating system (OS) composed of software developed by a community-supported project called the Debian Project. The project was announced by Ian Murdock on 16 August 1993; the first stable version, Debian 1.1 (code named "Buzz"), was released in June 1996.

Debian is one of the oldest operating systems based on the Linux kernel, and is best known for its package management system, APT, and the huge number of software packages available for it. The Debian Project's work is guided by the Debian Social Contract, its constitution, and the Debian Free Software Guidelines. Releases are code-named after Toy Story characters, and the unstable development branch is permanently named "Sid."

History

Ian Murdock, a student at Purdue University, announced Debian in a Usenet newsgroup posting in 1993. The name is a portmanteau of his girlfriend's name, Debra, and his own first name, Ian. After Murdock's departure, Bruce Perens became project leader and helped write the Debian Social Contract. The project is run by volunteers worldwide, electing a Debian Project Leader annually.

Releases and derivatives

At any time Debian maintains three branches: stable, testing, and unstable. Many other Linux distributions, including Ubuntu, Linux Mint, Raspberry Pi OS, and Kali Linux, are derived from Debian. The latest stable release is version 13, code-named "Trixie," released in August 2025.

Organization

The Debian Project comprises more than a thousand developers. Its decision-making relies heavily on consensus-building, votes conducted according to the Condorcet method, and a strong culture of doing the work ("do-ocracy"). Debian is funded through donations handled by Software in the Public Interest, Inc.
