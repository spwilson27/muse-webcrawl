Church (building)

A church, church house, or church building is a building used for Christian worship services and other Christian religious activities. The term is usually used in reference to the physical buildings where Christians worship, and less often for the community of believers itself, for which the phrase "the Church" is also used. Church architecture ranges from simple meeting houses to vast cathedrals and basilicas.

History

The earliest Christians met in homes and in adapted house churches such as the Dura-Europos church (c. 233 AD). After the legalisation of Christianity under Constantine in the 4th century, Christians adopted the Roman basilica as a model for large congregational buildings. Over the centuries, regional styles developed: Byzantine domed churches, Romanesque cruciform churches, Gothic cathedrals with pointed arches and stained glass, Renaissance and Baroque churches, and the plain chapels and meeting houses of the Protestant Reformation.

Architecture and layout

A typical church is oriented with its altar at the eastern end and contains a nave for the congregation, often flanked by aisles, with a chancel, sanctuary and altar at the liturgical east end. Other common elements include the narthex (entrance hall), transepts forming a cross-shaped plan, a baptistery or font, a bell tower or steeple, stained-glass windows and a pulpit. In many modern churches, especially in evangelical traditions, the layout resembles an auditorium centred on a stage.
