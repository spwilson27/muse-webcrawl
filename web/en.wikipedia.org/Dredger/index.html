Dredger

A dredger is a vessel or machine equipped to excavate material from beneath water, a process known as dredging. Dredgers are used to deepen and maintain navigable waterways, ports and harbours, reclaim land, construct foundations, and extract minerals such as sand, gravel, and gold from the seabed or riverbed.

Types

Mechanical dredgers

Mechanical dredgers remove material with buckets or grabs. The bucket ladder dredger uses a continuous chain of buckets on a ladder; grab dredgers use a crane-mounted clamshell; backhoe and dipper dredgers use excavator-type arms mounted on pontoons or barges.

Hydraulic dredgers

Hydraulic dredgers use pumps to move a slurry of water and sediment through pipelines. The cutter suction dredger loosens material with a rotating cutter head and pumps it ashore or to a placement site. The trailing suction hopper dredger drags suction pipes along the bottom while underway, filling its own hopper, and is widely used for maintaining shipping channels and for land reclamation projects such as artificial islands.

Uses

Capital dredging creates new ports, channels, and reclaimed land, while maintenance dredging removes accumulated silt to keep existing waterways at their designed depth. Environmental dredging removes contaminated sediments from harbours and industrial waterways. Dredgers also support offshore construction, pipeline trenching, and beach nourishment, in which sand is pumped onto eroding beaches.

Environmental impact

Dredging can disturb aquatic habitats, increase turbidity, and release contaminants bound in sediments. Modern projects typically require environmental assessment and monitoring, and dredged material is increasingly treated as a resource for construction and habitat creation rather than waste.
