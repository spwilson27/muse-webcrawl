Origin of birds

Origin of birds
Question: The evolutionary ancestry of birds
Consensus: Birds are living theropod dinosaurs (Avialae within Dinosauria)
Key fossil: Archaeopteryx (1861)

The scientific question of the origin of birds — where birds come from in evolution — was settled for most scientists by the late 20th century: birds are a group of maniraptoran theropod dinosaurs that originated in the Mesozoic era. In the years since the 1861 discovery of Archaeopteryx, a fossil with feathers but a dinosaurian skeleton, evidence accumulated that birds descend from small carnivorous dinosaurs, making them, in cladistic terms, living dinosaurs.

Evidence

Shared anatomical features between birds and theropods include hollow pneumatic bones, a furcula (wishbone, formed from fused clavicles), three-toed limbs, and brooding behaviour. Since the 1990s, discoveries in China's Liaoning deposits have yielded thousands of specimens of feathered non-avian dinosaurs — such as Sinosauropteryx, Microraptor and Anchiornis — showing feathers evolving before flight, probably for insulation and display.

History of the debate

Thomas Henry Huxley championed the dinosaur-bird link in the 1860s; Gerhard Heilmann's contrary arguments (notably the supposed absence of clavicles in dinosaurs) delayed acceptance until John Ostrom's work on Deinonychus revived the theory in the 1970s. A minority of researchers long argued for descent from earlier archosaurs ("thecodonts"), but the fossil and phylogenetic evidence for the theropod origin is now overwhelming.
