Kaziranga National Park

Kaziranga National Park is a national park in the Golaghat and Nagaon districts of Assam, India, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It hosts two-thirds of the world's Indian rhinoceroses (greater one-horned rhinoceroses).

Established in 1905 as a reserve forest at the urging of Mary Curzon, wife of Viceroy Lord Curzon, after she failed to see a rhino, the park covers about 430 square kilometres along the Brahmaputra River, whose annual floods renew its tall elephant grass, marshland and tropical forest.

Fauna

Besides rhinos, Kaziranga holds large breeding populations of tigers, Asian elephants, wild water buffalo and swamp deer, and is recognized as an Important Bird Area. Conservation successes raised rhino numbers from about a dozen in 1905 to over 2,600 in recent censuses, though poaching and flooding remain threats.
