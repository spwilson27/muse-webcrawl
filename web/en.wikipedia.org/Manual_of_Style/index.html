Manual of Style

Notable published style manuals

A style guide or manual of style is a set of standards for the writing and design of documents, intended to ensure consistency of language, formatting, and presentation across a publication, organization, or field. This article discusses style manuals in general; Wikipedia's own house style is documented in its internal Manual of Style.

Contents of a style manual

Style manuals typically prescribe conventions for spelling (including which dictionary to follow), punctuation, capitalization, citation of sources, numbers and dates, abbreviations, headings, lists, tables, and images. Many also address grammar and usage questions on which authorities differ, and guidance on inclusive or bias-free language.

Notable manuals

Widely used examples include The Chicago Manual of Style (first published 1906 by the University of Chicago Press), the Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association (APA style), the MLA Handbook of the Modern Language Association, The Associated Press Stylebook for journalism, The Elements of Style by Strunk and White, Fowler's Modern English Usage, and the New Hart's Rules of Oxford University Press. Governments publish their own, such as the U.S. Government Publishing Office Style Manual.

House styles

Newspapers, journals, universities, and companies maintain in-house style guides that supplement or override a published manual on points such as the Oxford comma, date formats, or whether to italicize foreign terms. Scholarly journals enforce their manuals strictly through copy-editing; in book publishing, the copy editor typically records decisions on a style sheet for each title.
