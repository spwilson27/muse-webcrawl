Cabrini–Green Homes

The Cabrini–Green Homes were a Chicago Housing Authority (CHA) public housing project on the Near North Side of Chicago, Illinois. Built between 1942 and 1962, the complex comprised rowhouses and mid- and high-rise apartment buildings that at their peak housed more than 15,000 residents. The last high-rise was demolished in 2011.

History

The site was named for Saint Frances Cabrini and the Chicago priest and housing advocate William Green. The Frances Cabrini Rowhouses opened in 1942, followed by the Cabrini Extension high-rises in the 1950s and the William Green Homes in 1962. Initially racially mixed, the development became almost entirely African American as white residents left.

Decline and notoriety

By the 1970s and 1980s the project had become nationally known for gang violence, poverty, and deteriorating conditions. A widely publicised 1981 episode saw Mayor Jane Byrne move temporarily into a Cabrini–Green apartment to draw attention to the violence. High-profile crimes at the complex in the 1990s reinforced its reputation and the case for demolition.

Demolition and redevelopment

Under the CHA's Plan for Transformation, announced in 1999, the high-rises were progressively demolished between 1995 and 2011 and residents relocated with housing vouchers or into mixed-income developments built on and near the site. The redevelopment of the area, adjacent to some of Chicago's most valuable real estate, remains a prominent example in debates about public housing policy in the United States.
