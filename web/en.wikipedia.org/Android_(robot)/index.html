Android (robot)

TypeHumanoid robot
DistinctionDesigned to closely resemble a human in appearance and behaviour
Female formSometimes called a gynoid

An android is a humanoid robot designed to resemble a human being, especially in body shape, skin, facial features and movement. The word derives from the Greek andr- ("man") and the suffix -eides ("of the species; alike"). Androids designed to resemble women are sometimes termed gynoids.

History

Artificial humans appear in myth and literature for centuries, and mechanical automata imitating people were built from the eighteenth century onward. The word "android" gained currency in science fiction, where androids became a staple for exploring the boundary between human and machine — notably in the works of Philip K. Dick, whose novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? inspired the film Blade Runner.

Modern androids

Contemporary research androids, such as the Geminoid series developed by Hiroshi Ishiguro's laboratory in Japan and Hanson's Sophia, aim for lifelike faces, expressions and conversation, and are used to study human–robot interaction. A well-known psychological obstacle is the "uncanny valley": as a robot's resemblance to a human approaches, but does not reach, perfection, observers may feel eeriness or revulsion.

In popular culture

Androids feature prominently in films and television such as Star Trek's Data, the Alien franchise's synthetic crew members, and Westworld, typically raising questions of consciousness, rights and identity.
