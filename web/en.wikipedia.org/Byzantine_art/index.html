Byzantine art

Byzantine art comprises the art of the Byzantine Empire from the 4th or 5th century until the fall of Constantinople in 1453. Centred on the Orthodox Church, it is characterised by icons, gold-ground mosaics, frescoes and illuminated manuscripts of a stylised, symbolic character.

Periods

Early Byzantine art, under Justinian I, produced the mosaics of Ravenna and Hagia Sophia. The Iconoclasm controversy (8th–9th centuries) destroyed many images before icons were restored and a canonical system of church decoration developed. The Macedonian and Komnenian periods saw refined manuscript illumination and enamelwork; after 1261, Palaiologan art brought a renewed emotional naturalism.

Influence

Byzantine styles spread to the Balkans, Rus', Georgia and Norman Sicily and strongly influenced medieval art in the West, especially in Venice; its legacy continues in Orthodox icon painting today.
