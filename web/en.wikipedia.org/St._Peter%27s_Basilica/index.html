The Papal Basilica of Saint Peter in the Vatican, commonly known as St. Peter's Basilica, is a church in Vatican City, designed principally by Donato Bramante, Michelangelo, Carlo Maderno and Gian Lorenzo Bernini. It is among the largest churches in the world and one of the holiest sites of Catholicism, traditionally the burial site of Saint Peter.

History

The present basilica replaced the 4th-century Old St. Peter's Basilica built under Constantine over the site believed to be Peter's tomb. Construction began in 1506 under Pope Julius II and was completed in 1626. Michelangelo designed the great dome, which dominates the Rome skyline, and his Pietà stands inside.

Architecture

The Latin-cross interior is richly decorated in Renaissance and Baroque style, with Bernini's bronze baldachin over the papal altar and his colonnaded St. Peter's Square outside. The façade by Maderno is surmounted by statues of Christ and the apostles. The basilica is the pope's principal church but is not a cathedral — the pope's cathedra is at St. John Lateran.
