Voice leading

Voice leading (or part writing) is the linear progression of individual melodic lines (voices) and their interaction with one another to create harmonies, typically in accordance with a set of principles derived from the practice of Western tonal music. Smooth voice leading generally moves each voice by the smallest possible distance, keeps common tones between chords, and avoids forbidden parallels such as consecutive perfect fifths and octaves.

Principles

In species counterpoint and in the part-writing exercises of music theory courses, voices are expected to be melodically independent, to avoid voice crossing and excessive overlap, and to resolve tendency tones such as the leading tone. Analysts from Heinrich Schenker onward have treated voice leading as fundamental to tonal structure at all levels, a view developed in neo-Riemannian theory, where chord progressions are modelled as transformations between voices.
