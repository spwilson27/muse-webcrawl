Basic English


Ogden's Basic English word list concept.

Basic English (British American Scientific International and Commercial English) is a controlled language based on standard English, but with a greatly simplified vocabulary and grammar. It was created by the linguist and philosopher Charles Kay Ogden as an international auxiliary language, and as an aid for teaching English as a second language. It was given its classic exposition in Ogden's book Basic English: A General Introduction with Rules and Grammar (1930).

The system

The vocabulary of Basic English consists of 850 core words: 600 nouns ("things"), 150 adjectives ("qualities"), and 100 "operations" including verbs and function words. Only 18 verbs are used, their functions expanded through combinations with prepositions and adverbs (e.g., "put off" or "go up"). Derivatives can be formed with a small set of endings, and international words (such as scientific terms) are added as needed. Ogden claimed that learning Basic English took seven weeks, compared to seven years for full English.

Reception and influence

The system was promoted in the 1930s and 1940s with support from figures including I. A. Richards and (for a time) Winston Churchill, who endorsed it in speeches. Critics, among them George Orwell in his early advocacy before he turned against controlled language, debated its expressive limits; the simplified English of Orwell's Newspeak is sometimes linked, ironically, to Basic English. Descendants and analogues include Voice of America's Special English (now Learning English) and Simplified Technical English for aerospace documentation.
