Cramér–Rao bound

FieldEstimation theory, mathematical statistics
Named afterHarald Cramér, C. R. Rao
Key conceptFisher information

In estimation theory and statistics, the Cramér–Rao bound (CRB) expresses a lower bound on the variance of unbiased estimators of a deterministic parameter. It states that the variance of any unbiased estimator is at least the reciprocal of the Fisher information: Var(θ̂) ≥ 1 / I(θ). The bound is named after Harald Cramér and Calyampudi Radhakrishna Rao, who derived it independently in the 1940s, building on earlier work by Fréchet, Darmois, and others; it is therefore also called the Cramér–Rao lower bound or the Fréchet–Darmois–Cramér–Rao bound.

Statement

Under regularity conditions on the probability distribution of the observations, if θ̂ is an unbiased estimator of θ, then Var(θ̂) ≥ 1 / [n I1(θ)] for n independent observations. In the multiparameter case, the covariance matrix of the estimator is bounded below (in the positive-semidefinite order) by the inverse of the Fisher information matrix.

Efficiency

An unbiased estimator that achieves the bound is called efficient; maximum likelihood estimators are asymptotically efficient under broad conditions, so the bound is a benchmark for estimator performance. Generalisations include the Barankin bound and Bayesian versions such as the Van Trees inequality, which apply to biased estimators or incorporate prior information.

Applications

The bound is fundamental in signal processing (limits on estimating arrival times, frequencies, and directions), radar, communications, and experimental design, where it quantifies the best precision any measurement scheme can achieve.
