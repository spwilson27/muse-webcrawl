Deutsche Welle

Deutsche Welle (DW)
Founded: 3 May 1953
Headquarters: Bonn and Berlin, Germany
Languages: 30+
Owner: Federal Government of Germany (public broadcaster)

Deutsche Welle (DW, literally "German Wave") is Germany's international broadcaster, funded by the federal government. Tasked by law with conveying Germany as a liberal democratic state rooted in European culture, it broadcasts television, radio and online content in more than 30 languages, comparable in role to the BBC World Service.

History

DW began shortwave radio broadcasting on 3 May 1953, with an address by Federal President Theodor Heuss. During the Cold War its services targeted listeners behind the Iron Curtain, and after reunification it absorbed staff and frequencies from former East German international broadcasting. Television services began in 1992, and DW progressively shifted resources from shortwave radio to satellite television and digital media.

Services

The flagship television channel DW (English) broadcasts news and magazine programming worldwide, with additional channels in German, Spanish and Arabic. The website dw.com publishes in 30 languages, and DW Akademie trains media professionals from developing countries. DW is a member of the ARD consortium's broader public broadcasting landscape but is financed directly from federal tax revenue rather than the licence fee.

Editorial status

The Deutsche Welle Act guarantees the broadcaster's journalistic independence from government. In February 2022 Russia banned DW and closed its Moscow bureau in retaliation for Germany's action against RT DE; DW has also faced office closures and blocks in several other states, which it and German officials have criticised as censorship.
