De Tomaso Pantera

The De Tomaso Pantera is a mid-engine sports car produced by Italian automobile manufacturer De Tomaso between 1971 and 1993. It combined Italian styling — a steel monocoque body designed by Tom Tjaarda at Ghia — with a Ford 351 Cleveland V8 engine and a ZF five-speed transaxle.

History

Shown at the New York Motor Show in 1970, the Pantera ("panther") replaced the Mangusta. Ford imported the cars to the United States and sold them through Lincoln-Mercury dealers until 1975; Elvis Presley famously owned one and once shot it in frustration when it failed to start. After Ford withdrew, De Tomaso continued production in progressively updated forms — the wide-arched GT5 and GT5-S of the 1980s and the final Si of the early 1990s — bringing total production to roughly 7,260 cars.

The Pantera's long production life, American powertrain, and relatively attainable price made it one of the most successful Italian-American hybrid sports cars. Early cars were criticized for overheating and build quality, issues progressively addressed in later versions.
