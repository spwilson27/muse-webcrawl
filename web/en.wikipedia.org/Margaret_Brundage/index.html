Margaret Brundage

Margaret Brundage (née Johnson; 9 December 1900 – 9 April 1976) was an American illustrator and painter best remembered for her covers for the pulp magazine Weird Tales. Working in pastels, she produced most of the magazine's covers between 1932 and 1938, becoming its best-known cover artist during the editorship of Farnsworth Wright.

Career

Brundage studied at the Academy of Fine Arts in Chicago — where a classmate was Walt Disney — and worked as a fashion illustrator before selling her first cover to Weird Tales in 1932. Her covers frequently illustrated scenes from stories by Robert E. Howard, Clark Ashton Smith, Seabury Quinn and H. P. Lovecraft, and often featured semi-nude women in scenes of peril, bondage or supernatural menace. The covers were commercially successful but controversial; complaints from readers and civic groups contributed to pressure for tamer artwork.

Style and later work

Brundage's pastel technique produced soft, glowing images whose originals are fragile and rarely survive in good condition. Because she signed much of her early work "M. Brundage", many readers were unaware the artist was a woman. After her regular work for Weird Tales ended in the late 1930s, she continued painting, and her original covers became highly collectable in later decades.

Legacy

Brundage is regarded as one of the most important pulp magazine artists and a pioneering woman in science fiction and fantasy art. Original Brundage pastels have sold for large sums at auction, and her work is regularly exhibited in histories of pulp illustration.
