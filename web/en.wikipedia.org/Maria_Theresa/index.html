Maria Theresa Walburga Amalia Christina (13 May 1717 – 29 November 1780) was ruler of the Habsburg dominions from 1740 until her death in 1780, and the only woman to hold the position suo jure (in her own right). She was the sovereign of Austria, Hungary, Croatia, Bohemia, Transylvania, Mantua, Milan, Galicia and Lodomeria, the Austrian Netherlands, and Parma. By marriage, she was Duchess of Lorraine, Grand Duchess of Tuscany, and Holy Roman Empress.

Early life and accession

Maria Theresa was the eldest daughter of Emperor Charles VI, who had issued the Pragmatic Sanction of 1713 to allow a daughter to inherit the Habsburg lands. When Charles died in October 1740, she succeeded him, but several European powers repudiated their recognition of the sanction. Frederick II of Prussia invaded Silesia, triggering the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748). Although she lost most of Silesia, Maria Theresa retained the bulk of her inheritance and was triumphantly crowned Queen of Hungary in 1741 and Queen of Bohemia in 1743.

Reforms

Over her forty-year reign Maria Theresa carried out sweeping administrative, fiscal, military, and educational reforms. She centralised government, created a unified standing army, reformed taxation so that nobles and clergy no longer enjoyed blanket exemptions, and founded the Theresian Military Academy. The Ratio Educationis of 1774 made schooling compulsory for children of both sexes between six and twelve, one of the earliest such laws in Europe. She also promoted inoculation against smallpox, having her own children inoculated, and restricted the use of judicial torture.

Later reign and co-regency

After the death of her husband Francis I in 1765, her son Joseph II became co-regent. Their relationship was often strained by Joseph's more radical reforming impulses, which Maria Theresa, a devout Catholic conservative, frequently restrained. She participated in the First Partition of Poland in 1772, acquiring Galicia, and fought the brief War of the Bavarian Succession in 1778–79.

Family and legacy

Maria Theresa had sixteen children, thirteen of whom survived infancy. Her daughters included Marie Antoinette, Queen of France, and Maria Carolina, Queen of Naples; her sons Joseph II and Leopold II both became Holy Roman Emperors. Her dynastic marriages substantially extended Habsburg influence. She is remembered as one of the most successful Habsburg rulers and a key figure of enlightened absolutism.
