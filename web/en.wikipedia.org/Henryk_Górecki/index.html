Henryk Mikołaj Górecki (6 December 1933 – 12 November 2010) was a Polish composer of contemporary classical music. He achieved international fame with his Symphony No. 3, the "Symphony of Sorrowful Songs" (1976), whose 1992 recording with soprano Dawn Upshaw and conductor David Zinman became one of the best-selling classical recordings of all time.

Life and career

Górecki was born in Czernica in Silesia. Music played only a small part in his early life — his mother, an amateur pianist, died when he was two — and he taught himself before studying composition with Bolesław Szabelski in Katowice in the 1950s. He came to prominence at the Warsaw Autumn festival with works of astringent modernism such as Scontri (1960) and his Symphony No. 1.

Style

From the late 1960s Górecki's style turned toward an increasingly spare, modal idiom drawing on Polish folk music, medieval chant and Renaissance polyphony. The Symphony No. 2 "Copernican" (1973) foreshadowed the Third Symphony, written in 1976: its three slow movements set texts of lamentation — a fifteenth-century Polish prayer, words scratched on a Gestapo cell wall by an imprisoned teenager, and a Silesian folk song of a mother mourning her son. The symphony's ethos has been associated with the "holy minimalism" of composers such as Arvo Pärt.

Later years

The immense success of the Third Symphony in the 1990s surprised Górecki, who remained a private figure living in Katowice, where he had taught composition for many years. Later works include the string quartets written for the Kronos Quartet and the Symphony No. 4 "Tansman Episodes", left substantially complete at his death and premiered posthumously in 2014. He died in Katowice in November 2010 after a long illness.
