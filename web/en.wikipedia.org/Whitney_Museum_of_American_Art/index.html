Whitney Museum of American Art

The Whitney Museum of American Art, known informally as "the Whitney", is a modern and contemporary art museum in the Meatpacking District of Manhattan, New York City. Founded in 1930 by Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney, it is devoted to American art of the 20th and 21st centuries and to living artists.

History

Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney, a sculptor and patron, began collecting the work of living American artists after the Metropolitan Museum of Art declined her donation. She founded the Whitney Studio and then the museum, which opened in 1931 on West 8th Street in Greenwich Village. In 1966 the museum moved to a distinctive granite building designed by Marcel Breuer on Madison Avenue at East 75th Street; that building later housed the Met Breuer and then became the headquarters of Frick Madison during renovation works.

Current home and collection

In 2015 the Whitney moved downtown to a building designed by Renzo Piano between the Hudson River and the High Line. The collection includes more than 25,000 works by over 3,500 artists, including Edward Hopper, Alexander Calder, Georgia O'Keeffe, Jasper Johns, and many contemporary artists.

Whitney Biennial

The museum's signature exhibition, the Whitney Biennial, is the longest-running survey of contemporary American art, begun in 1932 as an annual and held every two years since 1973. It is often considered a career-making showcase and a bellwether of trends in American art.
