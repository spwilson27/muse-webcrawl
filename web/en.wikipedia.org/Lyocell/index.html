Lyocell

Lyocell is a form of regenerated cellulose fibre made from dissolving wood pulp using an organic solvent, N-methylmorpholine N-oxide (NMMO), in a closed-loop process. The best-known brand is Tencel, produced by the Austrian company Lenzing AG.

Production

Wood pulp, often from eucalyptus, is dissolved in hot NMMO, extruded through spinnerets into a dilute solvent bath, and precipitated as fibres. Over 99% of the solvent is recovered and reused, giving the process a lower environmental impact than viscose rayon production, which uses carbon disulfide.

Properties and uses

Lyocell fibres are strong (even when wet), soft, absorbent, breathable, and resistant to wrinkling. They are used in clothing, denim, bedding, towels, and nonwoven products. The generic name "lyocell" was assigned by the US Federal Trade Commission in 1996.

History

The solvent-spinning process was developed in the 1970s–80s; commercial production began in 1992 at a plant in Mobile, Alabama, under Courtaulds, whose fibres business Lenzing later acquired.
