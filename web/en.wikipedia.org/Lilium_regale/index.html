Lilium regale

Lilium regale, the regal lily, is a species of lily native to the Min River valley of Sichuan in southwestern China. It bears large, trumpet-shaped, intensely fragrant white flowers flushed with purple on the outside and a yellow throat, on stems 1.2–1.8 m tall, flowering in midsummer.

Discovery and cultivation

The species was introduced to Western gardens by the plant hunter Ernest Henry Wilson, who collected it for the Arnold Arboretum in 1910; on his first expedition for the bulbs he was badly injured by a rockslide that shattered his leg — his "lily leg". Lilium regale proved vigorous and easy in cultivation, seeding prolifically, tolerating lime, and resisting many of the virus problems of other lilies. It transformed lily breeding and remains one of the most widely grown garden lilies, holding the Royal Horticultural Society's Award of Garden Merit. It is a parent of many trumpet lily hybrids.

Description

Plants produce up to 25 flowers per stem under good conditions, with narrow scattered leaves and bulbs that multiply by offsets and seed. The flowers open in the evening and are pollinated by hawk-moths, releasing their strongest scent at night.
