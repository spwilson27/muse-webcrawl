White Rajahs

White Rajahs of Sarawak
StateKingdom of Sarawak
Period1841–1946
RulersJames Brooke; Charles Brooke; Vyner Brooke

The White Rajahs were a dynastic monarchy of the British Brooke family, who ruled the Kingdom of Sarawak on the island of Borneo from 1841 until 1946. The dynasty began when the adventurer James Brooke helped suppress a rebellion for the Sultan of Brunei and was rewarded in 1841 with governorship — soon confirmed as Rajah — of the territory around Kuching.

The three Rajahs

James Brooke (r. 1841–1868) suppressed piracy and headhunting, issued a legal code, and expanded Sarawak's territory at Brunei's expense. His nephew Charles Brooke (r. 1868–1917), born Charles Johnson, consolidated the state, built its economy on rubber, pepper, and oil, and governed for nearly fifty years. Charles's son Vyner Brooke (r. 1917–1946) modernized the administration and in 1941 promulgated a constitution ending absolute rule, but the Japanese occupation of 1941–1945 broke the dynasty's power.

End of the dynasty

In 1946 Vyner Brooke ceded Sarawak to Britain as a Crown Colony, a controversial decision opposed by many Sarawakians and by his heir Anthony Brooke, sparking an anti-cession movement. Sarawak became part of Malaysia in 1963.
