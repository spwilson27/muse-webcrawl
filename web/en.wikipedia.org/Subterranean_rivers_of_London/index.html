Subterranean rivers of London

The subterranean rivers of London are the tributaries of the River Thames and the River Lea that were culverted and built over as London grew. Once open streams — many of them important to the city's early settlement, mills, and place-names — they were progressively covered from the medieval period onward and, in the Victorian era, largely incorporated into London's combined sewer system under Joseph Bazalgette's interceptor sewers.

Principal rivers

The best known is the River Fleet, London's largest subterranean river, which rises on Hampstead Heath and reaches the Thames at Blackfriars; its valley is followed by Farringdon Street. Others include the Tyburn (through Mayfair), the Westbourne, the Effra (through Brixton to Vauxhall), the Wandle (which remains largely open), the Ravensbourne, the Neckinger, the Walbrook (through the City), and the Hackney Brook.

History

As London expanded, the streams became fouled drains and were arched over. Their courses survive in street alignments, parish boundaries, and names such as Fleet Street, Westbourne Grove, and Effra Road. Interest in "lost rivers" has grown, with proposals to daylight some stretches, though most remain integral parts of the drainage network and can flood streets when surcharged.
