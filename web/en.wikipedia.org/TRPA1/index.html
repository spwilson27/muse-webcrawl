TRPA1

Transient receptor potential cation channel, subfamily A, member 1 (TRPA1) — an ion channel protein encoded in humans by the TRPA1 gene. A sensor for pain, cold and environmental irritants; also called the "wasabi receptor".

Transient receptor potential cation channel, subfamily A, member 1 (abbreviated TRPA1) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the TRPA1 (and in mice and rats by the Trpa1) gene.

TRPA1 is an ion channel located on the plasma membrane of many human and animal cells. This ion channel is best known as a sensor for pain, cold and environmental irritants. In humans, this channel is thought to play a role in many conditions, including familial episodic pain syndrome, some types of chronic pain, and inflammation. In animals, TRPA1 is involved in detecting potentially harmful environmental chemicals, mechanical stimuli, and noxious cold.

Function

TRPA1 is a member of the transient receptor potential channel family. TRPA1 contains 14 N-terminal ankyrin repeats, and is believed to function as a mechanical and chemical stress sensor. One of its specific functions is to sense pain and possibly cold. It is activated by a wide variety of pungent and irritating compounds, including allyl isothiocyanate (the active component of mustard oil and wasabi), cinnamaldehyde in cinnamon, acrolein in tear gas and vehicle exhaust, and allicin in garlic. Because of its sensitivity to wasabi and related compounds, TRPA1 is sometimes nicknamed the "wasabi receptor".

Clinical significance

A gain-of-function mutation in TRPA1 causes familial episodic pain syndrome, a rare disorder of severe pain triggered by fatigue, cold, or physical stress. TRPA1 is considered an attractive target for the development of novel analgesic drugs.
