Young Italy

Young Italy (Italian: La Giovine Italia) was a political movement founded in 1831 by Giuseppe Mazzini in Marseille. Its goal was to create a united Italian republic through promoting a general insurrection in the Italian states and in the lands occupied by the Austrian Empire. Its motto was "God and the People" (Dio e Popolo).

Origins

Mazzini, a former Carbonaro exiled from Piedmont, concluded that the secret societies of the Carbonari had failed because of their vague aims and elite character. Young Italy would instead educate and mobilise the people — particularly youth — around a clear programme: Italy was to become "one, independent, free and republican". Members (nominally under forty) swore an oath before God and the nation, and the movement published a journal, La Giovine Italia, smuggled into Italy.

Activity and failures

Young Italy's planned uprisings — including the invasion of Savoy in 1834 and revolts in the Two Sicilies, such as the Bandiera brothers' doomed Calabrian expedition of 1844 — all failed militarily. Yet Mazzini's propaganda profoundly shaped the Risorgimento: a generation of patriots, including Giuseppe Garibaldi (who joined in 1833), was formed in its ranks.

Legacy

Mazzini went on to found Young Europe (1834), an international association of national movements, and briefly led the Roman Republic of 1849. Though Italian unification was ultimately achieved (1861) under the Piedmontese monarchy rather than Mazzini's republic, Young Italy is credited with keeping the ideal of a united, democratic Italy alive.
