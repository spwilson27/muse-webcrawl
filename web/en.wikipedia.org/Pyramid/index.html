Pyramid

A pyramid (from Greek: pyramís) is a structure whose outer surfaces are triangular and converge to a single step at the top, making the shape roughly a pyramid in the geometric sense. The base of a pyramid can be trilateral, quadrilateral, or of any polygon shape. Monumental pyramids were built by many ancient civilizations, most famously the Egyptian pyramids at Giza and the Mesoamerican pyramids of Mexico and Central America.

Egyptian pyramids

The Egyptian pyramids were built as royal tombs during the Old and Middle Kingdoms (c. 2700–1700 BC). The earliest is the Step Pyramid of Djoser at Saqqara, designed by the architect Imhotep. The largest and best known are the three pyramids of the Giza plateau, built for the pharaohs Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure; the Great Pyramid of Khufu was the tallest human-made structure in the world for more than 3,800 years.

Pyramids elsewhere

In Nubia, the kingdoms of Kush built more pyramids than Egypt itself, though smaller and steeper, at sites such as Meroë. In the Americas, cultures including the Maya, Aztecs, and Teotihuacanos raised great tiered pyramids topped by temples. Pyramid-shaped structures also appear in Mesopotamia (ziggurats), China (imperial mausolea), and Southeast Asia.

Geometry

A pyramid is a polyhedron formed by connecting a polygonal base to an apex by triangular faces. The volume of a pyramid is one third the product of the base area and the height.
