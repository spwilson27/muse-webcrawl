Swithun

Swithun (or Swithin; Old English: Swīþhūn; c. 800 – 2 July 863) was an Anglo-Saxon bishop of Winchester, later revered as a saint. He is chiefly remembered in English folklore for the weather proverb associated with his feast day, 15 July: if it rains on St Swithun's Day, rain is said to continue for forty days.

Life

Little is reliably known of Swithun's life. He was educated at the Old Minster, Winchester, became a priest, and served as chaplain and adviser to King Egbert of Wessex and tutor to his son Æthelwulf. He was consecrated Bishop of Winchester in 852 or 853. Contemporary records mention few notable acts, though he was remembered for charitable works, church building, and humility — including the legend that he restored, miraculously, a basket of eggs broken by a market woman.

Cult and the weather legend

Swithun asked to be buried outdoors, where rain would fall on his grave. In 971 his remains were translated into Winchester Cathedral, a move said to have been accompanied by violent storms, giving rise to the proverb: "St Swithun's day if thou dost rain / For forty days it will remain." His cult flourished, his shrine at Winchester became a major pilgrimage site, and numerous churches in England and Scandinavia were dedicated to him before the shrine was destroyed in the Reformation.
