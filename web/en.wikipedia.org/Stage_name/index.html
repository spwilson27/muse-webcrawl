Stage name

A stage name is a pseudonym used by actors, comedians, musicians, and other performers. Professional wrestlers, magicians, and exotic dancers also commonly adopt stage names, sometimes called ring names or nom de scène.

Reasons for adoption

Performers adopt stage names for many reasons: their birth name may be difficult to pronounce, spell, or remember; it may be very common, or already used by another performer; it may carry unwanted ethnic or social associations in a prejudiced era; or a catchier or more distinctive name may simply serve the performer's image and marketing. Unions such as SAG-AFTRA historically required members not to duplicate another member's professional name, prompting many actors to change theirs.

Examples

Famous examples include Marilyn Monroe (born Norma Jeane Mortenson), John Wayne (Marion Morrison), Lady Gaga (Stefani Germanotta), Elton John (Reginald Dwight), and Mark Twain (Samuel Clemens, a pen name rather than a stage name). In music, stage names are especially prevalent in hip hop, electronic music, and popular music generally.

Related concepts

Related pseudonyms include pen names for authors, ring names for wrestlers and boxers, religious names taken on joining an order, and noms de guerre. Some performers legally change their names to their stage names; others use the stage name only professionally.
