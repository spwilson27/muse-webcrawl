Body neutrality

Body neutrality is a movement and mindset that emphasizes accepting one's body as it is and appreciating what it can do, rather than focusing on its appearance. It emerged in the 2010s partly as a response to the body positivity movement, offering an alternative for people who find the demand to love their appearance difficult or unattainable.

Principles

Body neutrality encourages shifting attention away from how the body looks and toward respectful, functional care: eating intuitively, exercising for enjoyment and health rather than punishment, resting when needed, and dressing comfortably. Its proponents argue that a neutral stance reduces the emotional labour of constant self-evaluation and is compatible with the realities of chronic illness, disability, ageing, and recovery from eating disorders.

History

The term gained currency around 2015, and was popularized by writers and clinicians such as Anne Poirier, whose Body Neutrality work grew out of eating disorder treatment, and later by social media creators and popular books on intuitive eating and self-compassion. Wellness and fitness communities increasingly adopted neutral language, for example praising strength gains or endurance rather than changes in weight or shape.

Comparison with body positivity

Whereas body positivity advocates celebrating all bodies as beautiful, body neutrality holds that beauty need not be the metric at all: a person's worth is not tied to appearance, and one does not have to feel positive about one's body to treat it well. Critics note the movements overlap considerably in practice, and that neutrality can still be difficult for people whose bodies are stigmatized by others.
