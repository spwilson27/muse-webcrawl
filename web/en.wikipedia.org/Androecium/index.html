Androecium

DefinitionThe collective male reproductive organs of a flower
ComponentsStamens (anther + filament)
Female counterpartGynoecium

The androecium is the set of stamens in a flower — the male reproductive structures that produce pollen. The term derives from the Greek andros (man) and oikia (house), and collectively designates all the microsporophylls of the flower.

Structure

Each stamen typically consists of a slender stalk, the filament, bearing an anther in which pollen grains develop within pollen sacs (microsporangia). The number, arrangement, fusion and length of stamens vary widely among plant families and are key characters in plant taxonomy and floral formulae.

Variation

Stamens may be free or fused: fused filaments form groups in the mallow family, while fused anthers characterize the aster family. In some flowers some stamens are sterile, forming staminodes that may serve to attract pollinators or guide them to nectar. Androecial arrangement — such as the four long and two short stamens (tetradynamous) of the cabbage family — is a classic diagnostic feature.

Function

The androecium's role is to produce and present pollen so that it can be transferred, by wind, water or animal pollinators, to the receptive stigma of the gynoecium of a compatible flower, achieving fertilization.
