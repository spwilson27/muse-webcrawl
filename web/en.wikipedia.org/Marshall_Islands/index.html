Marshall Islands

The Marshall Islands, officially the Republic of the Marshall Islands, is an island country west of the International Date Line and north of the equator in the Micronesia region of the northwestern Pacific Ocean. The country consists of 29 coral atolls and five isolated islands, spread over 1.9 million km² of ocean. The capital and largest city is Majuro.

History

The islands were settled by Micronesian navigators around 2000 years ago. Sighted by Spanish explorers in the 16th century, they were later named for the British captain John Marshall, who explored them in 1788. Germany annexed the islands in 1885; Japan seized them in 1914 and administered them under a League of Nations mandate. In World War II the United States captured the atolls of Kwajalein and Enewetak in 1944.

Between 1946 and 1958 the United States conducted 67 nuclear weapons tests in the Marshall Islands, including the 15-megaton Castle Bravo shot at Bikini Atoll in 1954, the largest American thermonuclear test, whose fallout contaminated inhabited atolls. The legacy of testing, including health effects and displaced communities, remains a central issue in Marshallese politics.

Government and relations

The Marshall Islands is a parliamentary republic in free association with the United States under the Compact of Free Association, by which the US provides defence, funding and access to services, and uses the Ronald Reagan Ballistic Missile Defense Test Site at Kwajalein Atoll. The country faces severe risks from sea-level rise due to climate change, since most land lies less than two metres above sea level.
