New York City Housing Authority

The New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA) is a public development corporation that provides public housing in New York City. Founded in 1934 under Mayor Fiorello La Guardia, it is the largest public housing authority in North America, housing roughly 360,000 residents in about 177,000 apartments across some 335 developments in the five boroughs, with hundreds of thousands more on waiting lists or assisted through Section 8 vouchers.

History

NYCHA's first development, First Houses on the Lower East Side, opened in 1935. Postwar construction produced large tower-in-the-park developments such as the Queensbridge Houses (the largest public housing complex in the United States) and the Robert Fulton Houses. Federal funding declines from the 1970s onward left the authority with an enormous backlog of repairs; by the 2010s NYCHA faced scandals over lead paint inspections, heating failures, and a federal monitorship imposed in 2019.

Operations

The authority has pursued conversion of developments to public-private management under the federal RAD program (PACT in New York) to fund repairs. NYCHA is also one of the city's largest landlords and employers.
