Futures exchange

A futures exchange or futures market is a central financial exchange where people can trade standardized futures contracts defined by the exchange. Futures contracts are derivatives contracts to buy or sell specific quantities of a commodity or financial instrument at a specified price with delivery set at a specified time in the future.

History

One of the earliest futures markets was the Dojima Rice Exchange in Osaka, Japan, in the 1730s, trading rice bills. In the United States, the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT), founded in 1848, developed standardized forward contracts — the direct ancestors of modern futures — for grain. The Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME), founded in 1898 as the Chicago Butter and Egg Board, later pioneered financial futures, including currency futures launched in 1972 under economist Leo Melamed.

Operation

Futures exchanges provide a regulated marketplace with standardized contract specifications: quantity, quality, delivery months, and tick size. A clearing house interposes itself between buyers and sellers, guaranteeing performance and requiring margin deposits that are marked to market daily. Most trading is now electronic, though some exchanges historically used open outcry pit trading.

Major exchanges

Major futures exchanges include CME Group (which absorbed the CBOT, NYMEX, and COMEX), Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), Eurex, the Shanghai Futures Exchange, the Dalian Commodity Exchange, and the Multi Commodity Exchange of India.
