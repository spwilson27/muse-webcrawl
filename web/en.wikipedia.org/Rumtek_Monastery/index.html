Rumtek Monastery, also called the Dharmachakra Centre, is a Tibetan Buddhist monastery located in the Indian state of Sikkim, near the capital Gangtok. It is the largest monastery in Sikkim and the seat in exile of the Gyalwang Karmapa, head of the Karma Kagyu school of Tibetan Buddhism.

Rumtek Monastery, seat in exile of the Karmapa.

History

The original Rumtek monastery was built in the mid-18th century under the direction of the 9th Karmapa, Wangchuk Dorje, and served as the main seat of the Karma Kagyu lineage in Sikkim. After fleeing Tibet in 1959 following the Chinese occupation, the 16th Karmapa, Rangjung Rigpe Dorje, settled in Sikkim and rebuilt Rumtek as his seat in exile; the new monastery, modelled on the Karmapa's Tsurphu Monastery in Tibet, was inaugurated in 1966.

Architecture and treasures

The monastery is built in traditional Tibetan style with vivid murals, thangka paintings, and a renowned collection of religious art and relics, including works associated with the Black Crown of the Karmapas kept in a nearby institute. Behind the main hall stands the Karma Shri Nalanda Institute for Buddhist studies, founded in 1981. A major annual event is the performance of sacred cham dances during the Losar (Tibetan New Year) festivities.

Karmapa controversy

Since the death of the 16th Karmapa in 1981, a dispute over the identity of his successor has divided the Karma Kagyu school, with rival claimants and litigation over control of Rumtek, which has at times been guarded by Indian security forces.
