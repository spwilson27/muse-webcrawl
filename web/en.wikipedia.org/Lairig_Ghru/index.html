Lairig Ghru

The Lairig Ghru is a mountain pass in the Cairngorms of Scotland, traditionally used as a drovers' route between Deeside and Speyside, running from near Braemar through the heart of the Cairngorm plateau to Aviemore. The name is Gaelic, commonly interpreted as the "gloomy" or "forbidding pass".

The route rises to about 835 metres at the Pools of Dee, in a deep U-shaped glacial valley between Ben Macdui and Braeriach, two of Britain's highest mountains, and is notorious for its boulder fields and rapid, severe weather changes. Once used to drive cattle to markets in the south, the Lairig Ghru is now a classic, demanding hillwalking route of about 28–30 km, and it has featured in the writing of Nan Shepherd's The Living Mountain.
