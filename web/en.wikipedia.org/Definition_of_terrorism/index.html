Definition of terrorism

There is no universal agreement on the definition of terrorism. Numerous definitions have been proposed by governments, international organisations, academics and legal instruments, and scholars have catalogued well over a hundred of them. The difficulty of defining the term has hampered the adoption of a comprehensive United Nations convention against terrorism.

Common elements

Most definitions share several features: the use or threat of violence; a political, ideological or religious motive; the intention to intimidate or coerce a population or government; and targets that are often civilians or non-combatants. Alex Schmid's revised academic consensus definition, built from surveys of experts, emphasises terrorism as a doctrine and practice of deliberately alarming political violence directed at symbolic victims to influence an audience.

Points of dispute

Debates centre on whether state violence can be terrorism, whether attacks on military targets in occupied territory qualify, and how to distinguish terrorism from guerrilla warfare or national liberation struggles — encapsulated in the cliché that "one man's terrorist is another man's freedom fighter". National laws also differ in breadth, with some criticised for sweeping in protest or insurgency.
