Cleveland

Cleveland is a city in the U.S. state of Ohio and the county seat of Cuyahoga County. Located along the southern shore of Lake Erie at the mouth of the Cuyahoga River, it lies across the lake from Canada. Founded in 1796 and named for surveyor Moses Cleaveland, the city became a major manufacturing and transportation centre and remains the anchor of Northeast Ohio's largest metropolitan area.

History

Cleveland grew rapidly after the opening of the Ohio and Erie Canal and the arrival of railroads, becoming a centre for iron, steel, oil refining — John D. Rockefeller founded Standard Oil there in 1870 — and automobile manufacturing. Its population peaked at over 900,000 in 1950. Deindustrialisation, suburbanisation and the notorious 1969 Cuyahoga River fire marked its later 20th-century decline, but the city has since invested in healthcare, education and downtown redevelopment.

Economy and culture

The city's economy is anchored by the Cleveland Clinic, one of the world's leading hospital systems, along with banking, insurance and manufacturing. Cultural institutions include the Cleveland Orchestra, the Cleveland Museum of Art, and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, which opened on the lakefront in 1995. Professional sports teams include the Guardians (baseball), Browns (football) and Cavaliers (basketball).

Geography and climate

Cleveland sits on the glaciated Appalachian Plateau with a humid continental climate; its position on Lake Erie produces significant lake-effect snow in winter.
