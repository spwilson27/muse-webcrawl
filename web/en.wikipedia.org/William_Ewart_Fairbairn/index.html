William Ewart Fairbairn

William Ewart Fairbairn (28 February 1885 – 20 June 1960) was a British soldier, police officer and pioneer of close-combat training. Serving with the Shanghai Municipal Police, he developed the hand-to-hand system known as Defendu and co-designed the Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife used by British commandos in the Second World War.

Shanghai policing

After service in the Royal Marines, Fairbairn joined the Shanghai Municipal Police in 1907. Patrolling one of the world's most violent ports, he survived hundreds of street fights — by his own account over 600 — and systematically studied Asian and Western martial arts, including judo (in which he earned a black belt), jujutsu, kung fu and boxing. He reorganised the force's training and created its riot squad (the Reserve Unit), developing practical methods of shooting, baton work and unarmed combat out of hard operational experience.

Defendu and wartime work

Fairbairn published his system as Defendu (1926), later revised for wartime as Get Tough! (1942). With Eric Sykes he designed the slim, double-edged Fairbairn–Sykes commando dagger and the "Shanghai knife". Recalled to Britain in 1940, he trained British commandos, the Special Operations Executive and American OSS officers (including in Canada at Camp X), earning the nickname "Dangerous Dan". His methods influenced modern military combatives.
