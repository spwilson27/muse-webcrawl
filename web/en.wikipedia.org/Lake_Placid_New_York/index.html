Lake Placid is a village in the Adirondack Mountains in Essex County, New York, United States. As of the 2020 census, its population was 2,205. The village is named after the nearby Lake Placid, although its main street runs along Mirror Lake. It is famous as the site of the 1932 and 1980 Winter Olympics.

History

Lake Placid developed in the late 19th century as a resort for the wealthy; the Lake Placid Club, founded by Melvil Dewey in 1895, popularised winter sports in the United States. The village hosted the Winter Olympics in 1932 and again in 1980, the latter famous for the "Miracle on Ice", in which the young United States hockey team defeated the heavily favoured Soviet team.

Winter sports

The village remains a major winter sports centre, with the Olympic Center, the Herb Brooks Arena, the Olympic ski jumps at the MacKenzie Intervale complex, the bobsled and luge track at Mount Van Hoevenberg, and the nearby Whiteface Mountain ski area. It hosts Ironman Lake Placid, one of the longest-running Ironman triathlons in North America.

Geography

Lake Placid sits at an elevation of about 1,800 feet (550 m) in the High Peaks region of the Adirondacks, in the town of North Elba, which also contains the John Brown Farm State Historic Site, the abolitionist's home and grave.
