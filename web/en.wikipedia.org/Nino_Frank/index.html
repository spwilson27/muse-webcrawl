Nino Frank

Nino Frank (27 June 1904 – 17 August 1988) was an Italian-born French film critic and writer. He is credited with coining the term film noir in 1946, in an article published in the French magazine L'Écran français analysing the cycle of American crime films released in France after the Second World War.

Life and career

Frank was born in Barletta, in Apulia, Italy, and moved to Paris in the 1920s, where he worked as a journalist, translator and screenwriter. During the interwar years he collaborated with Irish writer James Joyce on the French translation of the "Anna Livia Plurabelle" section of Finnegans Wake. He also worked in Italian cinema during the 1930s before returning to France.

Film noir

In August 1946, Frank published "Un nouveau genre policier: l'aventure criminelle" ("A new kind of police drama: the criminal adventure") in L'Écran français. Writing about American films such as The Maltese Falcon, Double Indemnity, Laura and Murder, My Sweet, he grouped them under the label film noir ("black film"), borrowing the term from the Série noire crime novels published by Gallimard. The term was later adopted internationally by critics and scholars and came to define one of the most studied movements in film history.

Legacy

Frank continued to write on cinema for decades and remained an influential voice in French film criticism. His 1946 article is regarded as a foundational text of film studies.
