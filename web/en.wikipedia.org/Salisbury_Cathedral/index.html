Salisbury Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of the Blessed Virgin Mary, is an Anglican cathedral in Salisbury, Wiltshire, England. Built between 1220 and 1258 in the Early English Gothic style, it is renowned for the unity of its architecture, its soaring spire — the tallest church spire in the United Kingdom at 123 m — and for preserving the best surviving of the four original copies of Magna Carta.

History

The cathedral was founded when Bishop Richard Poore moved the see from Old Sarum to a new site in the Avon valley (New Sarum). Remarkably, the main building was completed within 38 years, giving it a stylistic coherence unusual among medieval cathedrals. The cloisters and chapter house followed, and around 1320 the great spire was added atop the crossing tower, straining the structure so much that the piers visibly bend; internal iron ties and later Sir Christopher Wren's survey helped stabilise it.

Architecture and treasures

The west front is richly decorated with statues, and the interior features slender Purbeck marble columns and the elegant scissor arches (added in the 15th century) in the transepts. The chapter house displays the cathedral's 1215 Magna Carta, written in iron-gall ink on vellum. The cathedral also houses the world's oldest working mechanical clock (c. 1386) and the largest cathedral close in Britain.

Cultural presence

John Constable painted the cathedral repeatedly from the Bishop's garden and the water meadows; it later inspired William Golding's novel The Spire (1964) and features in Ken Follett's The Pillars of the Earth as partial inspiration.
