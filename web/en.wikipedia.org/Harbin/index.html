Harbin is the capital and largest city of Heilongjiang province in northeastern China. It is a sub-provincial city and a major industrial, commercial and cultural centre of Northeast China (historical Manchuria), nicknamed the "Ice City" for its bitterly cold winters and the "Oriental Moscow" for its Russian-influenced architecture.

History

Harbin grew from a small fishing settlement into a boomtown founded in 1898 as the construction headquarters of the Russian-built Chinese Eastern Railway. It became a cosmopolitan refuge for Russians, especially after the 1917 Revolution, and for Jewish and other European communities; landmarks such as the onion-domed Saint Sophia Cathedral (1907) survive from this era. The city was occupied by Japan from 1932 as part of Manchukuo, and the notorious Unit 731 biological-warfare facility operated in its Pingfang district. After 1949 Harbin became one of China's first major industrial bases, specialising in power-generation equipment, aircraft and machinery.

Climate and culture

Harbin has a monsoon-influenced humid continental climate with long, frigid, dry winters — January averages around −18 °C — and warm, rainy summers. Since 1985 it has hosted the Harbin International Ice and Snow Festival, one of the world's largest winter festivals, featuring full-size illuminated ice buildings at Sun Island and Ice and Snow World. The city's cuisine, beer culture, and summer music festival also reflect its mixed Chinese and Russian heritage.
