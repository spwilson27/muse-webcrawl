The Space Race was the Cold War competition between the United States and the Soviet Union, roughly 1955 to 1975, to achieve superior spaceflight capability — a proxy contest in technology, military potential, and ideology.

Origins and Firsts

Rooted in wartime rocketry and the intercontinental ballistic missile arms race, the contest burst into public view when the USSR launched Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite, on 4 October 1957, prompting the creation of NASA in 1958. The Soviets achieved many early firsts: first animal (Laika), first human — Yuri Gagarin, 12 April 1961 — first woman (Valentina Tereshkova), and first spacewalk (Alexei Leonov).

Moon Race

President John F. Kennedy in 1961 committed the US to landing a man on the Moon before decade's end. The Apollo programme achieved this on 20 July 1969 with Apollo 11's Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin, while Soviet crewed lunar efforts were cancelled after repeated N1 rocket failures.

Conclusion and Legacy

Competition gave way to cooperation with the joint Apollo–Soyuz Test Project in 1975. The race drove rapid advances in rockets, satellites, computing, and science education, and framed later international and commercial spaceflight.
