Toll road

A toll road, also known as a turnpike or tollway, is a public or private road for which a fee (toll) is assessed for passage. Tolls are used to finance construction and maintenance, to repay borrowing, or, in congested urban areas, to manage demand through congestion pricing.

History

Tolls have been levied since antiquity on roads, bridges and ferries. In 18th- and 19th-century Britain, turnpike trusts maintained thousands of miles of road funded by tolls collected at tollhouses, and similar turnpikes spread in the United States. The modern era began with motorway-age projects such as the Pennsylvania Turnpike (1940) and the Italian autostrade.

Collection methods

Traditional collection uses staffed or automatic booths at toll plazas. Increasingly, roads use electronic toll collection — transponders such as E-ZPass in the United States or overhead gantries reading licence plates ("all-electronic tolling") — which removes the need to stop. Critics of tolling point to diversion of traffic onto untolled routes and to the regressive incidence of flat fees.
