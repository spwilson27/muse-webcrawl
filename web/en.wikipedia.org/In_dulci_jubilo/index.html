In dulci jubilo

"In dulci jubilo" ("In sweet rejoicing") is a traditional Christmas carol dating from the fourteenth century. The original text, attributed in legend to the German mystic Heinrich Seuse (Suso), is a macaronic mixture of Latin and German, in which the singer describes a vision of singing angels around the Christ child.

Text and tune

The carol first appeared in manuscript around 1400, and the melody — one of the best-known of all medieval tunes — was printed in collections such as the Piae Cantiones (1582). The first verse begins "In dulci jubilo, nun singet und seid froh!" ("In sweet rejoicing, now sing and be glad!").

Arrangements and translations

Composers including Michael Praetorius, Dietrich Buxtehude, and Johann Sebastian Bach set the carol — Bach used it in chorale preludes (BWV 608 and BWV 729) and in his Christmas Oratorio. English translations include "Good Christian Men, Rejoice" by John Mason Neale (1853) and Robert Lucas de Pearsall's arrangement, a staple of the Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols at King's College, Cambridge. Mike Oldfield's instrumental version was a UK hit single in 1975.
