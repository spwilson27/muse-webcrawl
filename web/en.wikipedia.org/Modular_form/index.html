Modular form

In mathematics, a modular form is a complex analytic function on the upper half-plane that satisfies a certain kind of functional equation with respect to the modular group SL(2, Z) and also satisfies a growth condition. The theory of modular forms therefore belongs to complex analysis but the main importance of the theory has traditionally been in its connections with number theory. Modular forms appear in other areas, such as algebraic topology, sphere packing, and string theory.

Definition

A modular form of weight k for the group SL(2, Z) is a holomorphic function f on the upper half-plane ℍ such that for every matrix with integer entries and determinant 1,

f((aτ + b)/(cτ + d)) = (cτ + d)k f(τ),

and f is holomorphic as τ approaches the cusp at infinity. The second condition gives a Fourier expansion f(τ) = Σ an qn with q = e2πiτ. If a0 = 0, the form is called a cusp form.

Examples

The simplest examples are the Eisenstein series, whose Fourier coefficients involve divisor sums, and the discriminant cusp form Δ of weight 12, whose Fourier coefficients are given by Ramanujan's tau function. The ratio of suitable forms yields the j-invariant, which classifies elliptic curves over the complex numbers. The Dedekind eta function and theta functions are further fundamental examples.

Modular functions and curves

A modular function is a modular form of weight 0; meromorphic modular functions are the function field of a modular curve, a quotient of the upper half-plane by a congruence subgroup such as Γ0(N). These curves have rich arithmetic structure and are central objects in modern number theory.

Applications in number theory

Modular forms encode arithmetic information in their Fourier coefficients. Hecke operators act on spaces of modular forms, and the associated L-functions satisfy functional equations. The modularity theorem (formerly the Taniyama–Shimura conjecture) states that every elliptic curve over the rationals is modular — that its L-function is that of a modular form of weight 2. Andrew Wiles's proof of this theorem for semistable curves in the 1990s also proved Fermat's Last Theorem. Modular forms also underlie the monstrous moonshine conjectures connecting them to the Monster group, and in 2016 Maryna Viazovska used modular forms to solve the sphere-packing problem in dimension 8.

History

The theory grew out of the nineteenth-century study of elliptic functions and elliptic integrals by Abel, Jacobi, Weierstrass and Klein, and the arithmetic theory developed by Erich Hecke and others in the twentieth century. Srinivasa Ramanujan discovered many deep properties of the tau function and mock theta functions, stimulating much later work.
