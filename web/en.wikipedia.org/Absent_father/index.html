Absent father

An absent father is a father who is not present in a child's upbringing, whether through death, divorce or separation, desertion, work obligations, incarceration, or never having formed part of the household. "Father absence" is widely studied in developmental psychology, sociology, and public policy because a substantial share of children in many countries grow up in mother-only households.

Research on effects

A large body of research associates father absence with statistically higher risks of poorer educational attainment, behavioral problems, early sexual activity, and poverty for children. Researchers debate how much of the association is causal: much of it is entangled with factors such as household income loss, parental conflict, reduced supervision, and neighborhood effects, and outcomes vary with the reason for and timing of the absence, the quality of the remaining caregiving, and contact maintained with the father.

Public debate

Father absence is a recurring theme in political debates about family structure, welfare policy, and child support enforcement. In the United States it has been linked in public discourse to the Moynihan Report of 1965 and to later campaigns promoting marriage and responsible fatherhood; critics argue that such debates can stigmatize single mothers and overlook economic causes. Programs responding to the issue include fatherhood initiatives, child support enforcement, and mentoring.
