The Battle of Sentium was fought in 295 BC near Sentium (now Sassoferrato in the Marche region of Italy) between the Roman Republic and an alliance of Samnites, Etruscans, Umbrians and Senone Gauls. It was the decisive battle of the Third Samnite War, and one of the largest battles of early Roman history.

Background

Rome faced a grand coalition of its Italian enemies. The consuls Quintus Fabius Maximus Rullianus and Publius Decius Mus marched into Umbria to confront the combined Samnite-Gallic army near Sentium, while the Etruscan and Umbrian contingents were drawn away by a Roman raid on their homelands.

The battle

Decius, whose wing was being driven back by the Gauls and their chariots, repeated the famous devotio of his father — ritually consecrating himself and the enemy to the gods of the underworld before charging to his death. Decius's troops rallied, and Fabius crushed the Samnites under Gellius Egnatius, who was killed. Roman casualties were heavy, but the coalition army was destroyed.

Aftermath

Sentium broke the coalition and left Rome the dominant power in Italy; the Samnites fought on until 290 BC before submitting, and the Senones were annihilated a decade later.
