Bone density

Also called: Bone mineral density (BMD)

Units: g/cm² (areal), g/cm³ (volumetric)

Measured by: Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA)

Clinical use: Diagnosis of osteoporosis

Bone density, or bone mineral density (BMD), is the amount of bone mineral in bone tissue, usually expressed as grams of mineral per square centimetre of bone scanned. It is a proxy for bone strength: denser bone is generally stronger and less likely to fracture. Clinically, bone density measurement is the standard method for diagnosing osteoporosis and estimating fracture risk.

Measurement

The most widely used technique is dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA or DXA), which scans the hip and lumbar spine with two X-ray energies and calculates an areal density. Results are reported as T-scores, comparing the patient with a healthy young adult reference population. By World Health Organization criteria, a T-score of −1.0 or above is normal, between −1.0 and −2.5 indicates osteopenia (low bone mass), and −2.5 or below defines osteoporosis. Other methods include quantitative computed tomography, which measures true volumetric density, and heel ultrasound.

Factors affecting bone density

Bone mass peaks in early adulthood and declines with age, especially in women after the menopause when oestrogen levels fall. Adequate calcium and vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise, and avoidance of smoking and heavy alcohol use support bone density, while glucocorticoid medicines, hyperthyroidism, and prolonged immobility reduce it. Heredity accounts for much of the variation between individuals.

Treatment of low bone density

Management combines lifestyle measures with medicines that slow bone resorption or stimulate formation, such as bisphosphonates, denosumab, and teriparatide; statistical corrections such as the Bonferroni correction are used when interpreting the many comparisons in bone-density research.
