Sallie Martin

Sallie Martin, known as the "Mother of Gospel Music".

Sallie Martin (November 20, 1895 – June 18, 1988) was an American gospel singer and businesswoman, widely known as the "Mother of Gospel Music". Working closely with Thomas A. Dorsey in Chicago, she helped organise, promote and professionalise gospel music in the United States.

Life and career

Born in Pittfield, Georgia, Martin moved to Chicago in 1927 and joined Ebenezer Baptist Church. She became a soloist and business partner of Dorsey, travelling to churches across the country to sell his sheet music and demonstrate his songs. In 1933 she co-founded the National Convention of Gospel Choirs and Choruses.

The Sallie Martin Singers

After parting with Dorsey over business disputes, Martin formed the Sallie Martin Singers, one of the first all-female professional gospel groups, which included her adopted daughter Cora Martin-Moore. She also ran her own gospel music publishing company in Los Angeles and recorded for the Specialty label.

Legacy

Martin was a shrewd entrepreneur who insisted gospel singers be paid fairly, and she mentored generations of performers. She was inducted into the Gospel Music Hall of Fame in 1991, and her annual Gospel Singers Convention in Los Angeles drew performers from across the country.
