Schiltron

A schiltron (also written sheltron, sceld-trome, or schiltrom) is a compact body of troops forming a battle array, shield wall or phalanx. The term is associated with Scottish troops, particularly pikemen in densely packed formations, during the Wars of Scottish Independence in the late 13th and early 14th centuries.

Description

The schiltron was a formation of spearmen carrying long spears — later formalized as pikes of 12 to 14 feet — arranged in tight ranks so that the spearpoints of several rows projected in front of the formation, presenting an impenetrable hedge to charging cavalry. Formations could be circular or rectangular. The word may derive from Old English roots meaning "shield-troop".

Battles

Schiltrons of Scots spearmen under William Wallace were defeated at the Battle of Falkirk (1298), where static circular schiltrons were broken up by English archers before being ridden down. Under Robert the Bruce at the Battle of Bannockburn (1314), schiltrons were used offensively, advancing downhill against the English and driving them into the Bannock Burn. Scottish schiltrons appeared again at battles such as Old Byland (1322) and Halidon Hill (1333), where they suffered heavily from longbow fire.

Tactics

The formation demanded high discipline and morale: if the ranks broke, the spearmen were highly vulnerable. Its success depended on terrain, on protection from missile fire, and on timing — used aggressively, as at Bannockburn, it could overwhelm disorganized opponents; used statically, as at Falkirk and Halidon Hill, it presented a large target for archers.
