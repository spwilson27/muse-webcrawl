Side effect

In medicine, a side effect is an effect, whether therapeutic or adverse, that is unintended and secondary to the one intended. Although the term is predominantly employed to describe adverse effects, it can also apply to beneficial but unintended consequences of a drug or treatment.

Adverse effects

Adverse side effects range from mild symptoms such as nausea, headache and drowsiness to severe reactions including organ damage or anaphylaxis. The frequency and severity of side effects are documented during clinical trials and listed in patient information leaflets, allowing clinicians to weigh benefits against risks.

Beneficial side effects

Some side effects prove useful and can lead to drug repurposing: minoxidil, developed for hypertension, was found to stimulate hair growth, and sildenafil was originally investigated for angina before its better-known use.

Terminology

The term is distinct from "adverse event", which need not be causally linked to the treatment, and from "contraindication", a condition that makes a treatment inadvisable.
