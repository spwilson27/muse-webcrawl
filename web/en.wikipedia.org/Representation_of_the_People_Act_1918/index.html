Representation of the People Act 1918

Hero image (placeholder):

The Representation of the People Act 1918 was an Act of Parliament passed to reform the electoral system in Great Britain and Ireland. It is sometimes known as the Fourth Reform Act.

Provisions

The Act greatly extended the franchise in parliamentary elections to all men aged 21 or over (and servicemen aged 19 and over), and gave the vote to women aged 30 or over who met minimum property qualifications. It nearly tripled the electorate, from about 7.7 million to about 21.4 million, and for the first time women could also stand for Parliament (under the separate Parliament (Qualification of Women) Act 1918). The Act also introduced redistribution of seats and made provisions for absent voting by servicemen. Equal franchise for women at age 21 followed with the Representation of the People (Equal Franchise) Act 1928.
