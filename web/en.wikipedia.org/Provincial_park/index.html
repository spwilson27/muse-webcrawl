A provincial park is a protected natural area established and managed by a provincial government. The term is used most prominently in Canada, where provincial park systems complement the federal national park system and together protect a large share of the country's landscapes. Comparable designations exist elsewhere under names such as "state park" in the United States.

Canada

Ontario created Algonquin Provincial Park in 1893, the first provincial park in Canada; at over 7,600 km² it remains one of the most visited. British Columbia established its first park, Strathcona, in 1911, and its system, managed by BC Parks, now includes more than a thousand parks and other protected areas covering roughly 14% of the province. Quebec labels its provincial parks "national parks" (parcs nationaux) under provincial law, administered by Sépaq. Park classifications typically range from wilderness parks with minimal facilities to recreation and natural-environment parks offering campgrounds, beaches, and trails.

Purposes

Provincial parks serve conservation of ecosystems and wildlife, outdoor recreation and tourism, protection of cultural heritage, and scientific research. Rules generally restrict resource extraction and development more strictly than in surrounding Crown land, although the balance between preservation and recreation varies by province and park class.
