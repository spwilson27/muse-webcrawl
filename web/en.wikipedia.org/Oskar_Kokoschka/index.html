Oskar Kokoschka


Born: 1 March 1886, Pöchlarn, Austria-Hungary
Died: 22 February 1980 (aged 93), Montreux, Switzerland
Nationality: Austrian
Movement: Expressionism

Oskar Kokoschka (1 March 1886 – 22 February 1980) was an Austrian artist, poet, playwright and teacher best known for his intense expressionistic portraits and landscapes, characterised by swirling brushwork and psychological depth. Along with Gustav Klimt and Egon Schiele, he was a leading figure of Viennese modernism.

Early work in Vienna

Born in Pöchlarn in Lower Austria, Kokoschka studied at Vienna's School of Arts and Crafts, where Klimt encouraged him. His early works caused scandal: his 1909 play Murderer, the Hope of Women is considered one of the first expressionist dramas. His penetrating portraits of Viennese intellectuals sought, in his words, to paint the soul visible through the skin. A passionate affair with Alma Mahler, widow of composer Gustav Mahler, inspired some of his most famous works, including The Bride of the Wind (1913); after their separation he commissioned a life-sized doll of her, which he later destroyed.

War, exile and later life

Kokoschka volunteered in the First World War and was seriously wounded in 1915. He taught at the Dresden Academy from 1919 to 1924, then travelled widely, painting city views from elevated viewpoints. The Nazis condemned his work as degenerate, and he fled to Prague in 1934 and to London in 1938, becoming a British citizen. After the war he settled in Villeneuve, Switzerland, in 1953, founded the "School of Seeing" summer academy in Salzburg, and produced panoramic landscapes, stage designs and political works. He died in Montreux in 1980.
