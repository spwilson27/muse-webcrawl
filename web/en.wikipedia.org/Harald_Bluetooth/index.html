Harald Bluetooth

Reign: c. 958 – c. 986

Predecessor: Gorm the Old

Successor: Sweyn Forkbeard

Died: c. 985/986, Jomsborg (legend)

Harald "Bluetooth" Gormsson (Old Norse: Haraldr Blátǫnn; died c. 985/86) was King of Denmark from about 958 and briefly King of Norway. Son of Gorm the Old, he is credited with consolidating Danish royal power, converting the Danes to Christianity, and raising the famous Jelling stones, on which he proclaimed that he "won all of Denmark and Norway and made the Danes Christian".

Reign

Harald fortified his kingdom with ring fortresses (Trelleborg-type forts) and the Danevirke, converted to Christianity around the 960s — a move that both unified the realm and reduced pressure from the Christian Holy Roman Empire to the south — and extended Danish influence into Norway. Late in his reign he faced rebellion led by his son Sweyn Forkbeard, during which he died, according to tradition, at the fortress of Jomsborg.

The nickname and the technology

The epithet "Bluetooth" (Blátǫnn, "blue tooth") may refer to a prominent dead, dark-coloured tooth. In 1997 the wireless communication technology Bluetooth was named after him: just as Harald united Danish tribes, the technology unites communication protocols, and its logo combines the runes for his initials, H (ᚼ) and B (ᛒ).
