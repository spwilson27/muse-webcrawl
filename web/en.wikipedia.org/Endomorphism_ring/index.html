Endomorphism ring

In mathematics, the endomorphism ring of an abelian group, module, or other object is the set of all its endomorphisms (maps from the object to itself), equipped with addition and composition, making it a ring.

Definition

For an abelian group A, End(A) consists of all group homomorphisms f: A → A. Addition is pointwise and multiplication is composition of functions. The result is generally a noncommutative ring, though for A = Z^n it is the ring of n×n integer matrices.

Properties

Automorphisms are the units of the endomorphism ring. Studying a module through its endomorphism ring is central in representation theory and Morita theory. The endomorphism ring of an elliptic curve, for instance, can be Z, an order in an imaginary quadratic field, or a quaternion order, corresponding to ordinary and supersingular cases.
