In ancient Roman religion and myth, Luna is the divine embodiment of the Moon (Latin: Lūna [ˈluːna]). She is often presented as the female complement of the Sun, Sol, conceived of as a god. Luna was the Roman equivalent of the Greek moon goddess Selene, from whom she inherited mythology such as the tale of the sleeping shepherd Endymion, though native Italic and Sabine roots for her cult have also been proposed.

Cult

Varro listed Luna among the twelve principal agricultural deities (di indigetes) and among the twenty chief gods of Rome. Her temple on the Aventine Hill was traditionally said to have been dedicated in the 6th century BC by Servius Tullius and was destroyed in the Great Fire of Rome under Nero (AD 64). Another temple stood on the Capitoline. Her festival was celebrated on 31 March, and as Luna Noctiluca ("Luna that shines by night") she had a shrine on the Palatine whose lamps lit the night. The Roman calendar was originally lunar, and the word mensis ("month") is linked to the moon's cycle; silver, the moon's metal, underlies her association with the crescent-shaped symbol still used for the Moon in astronomy and astrology (☾).

Iconography

In Roman art Luna is shown as a beautiful woman with a crescent moon in her hair or on her brow, driving a two-horse chariot (biga) across the night sky, often accompanied by stars, the winds, or the sleeping Endymion. She appears, paired with Sol, framing scenes on sarcophagi and on the Arch of Constantine, where the two celestial deities mark the cosmic scope of imperial rule.

Distinction from Diana

Although Diana, like the Greek Artemis, took on lunar aspects — and the triple goddess Diana–Luna–Hecate was invoked in poetry and magic — Luna remained a distinct deity with her own cult and temples. Over time, popular religion tended to merge the moon goddesses, and "Luna" became the ordinary Latin word for the moon itself, giving modern Romance languages their words for it (e.g. French lune, Spanish and Italian luna) and terms such as "lunar" and "lunatic."
