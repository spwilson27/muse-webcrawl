Thomas Alva Edison (February 11, 1847 – October 18, 1931) was an American inventor and businessman. He developed many devices in fields such as electric power generation, mass communication, sound recording, and motion pictures. These inventions, which include the phonograph, the motion picture camera, and early versions of the electric light bulb, have had a widespread impact on the modern industrialized world.

Early life and career

Born in Milan, Ohio, and raised in Port Huron, Michigan, Edison had little formal schooling and was largely self-taught, becoming a telegraph operator as a teenager — work that taught him chemistry and electricity despite progressive hearing loss. His first patented invention was an electric vote recorder (1869); his improved stock ticker and quadruplex telegraph (which transmitted multiple messages on one wire) brought him the money to set up his laboratory at Menlo Park, New Jersey, in 1876 — the first industrial research laboratory.

Major inventions

At Menlo Park, the "Wizard of Menlo Park" and his team produced the phonograph (1877), the first machine to record and reproduce sound, and in 1879 a high-resistance carbon-filament lamp that burned for over 13 hours, followed by a complete system of electric generation and distribution, first demonstrated at Pearl Street Station in New York in 1882. Edison later established a larger laboratory at West Orange, New Jersey, and developed the kinetoscope motion-picture viewer, alkaline storage batteries, and a vast mining and cement business. He held 1,093 U.S. patents, a record for an individual. His promotion of direct current in the "war of the currents" against George Westinghouse's and Nikola Tesla's alternating current ended in defeat, and his companies were merged into General Electric in 1892.

Legacy

Edison is credited with creating the model of the modern industrial research laboratory and with systematizing invention as a commercial enterprise. His name survives in companies such as Consolidated Edison and in the Edison effect and numerous place names.
