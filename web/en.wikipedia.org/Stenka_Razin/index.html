Stenka Razin

Bornc. 1630, Zimoveyskaya, Don Host Oblast
Died16 June 1671, Moscow
Known forLeading the Razin rebellion (1670–1671)

Stepan Timofeyevich Razin (Russian: Степан Тимофеевич Разин; c. 1630 – 16 June 1671), known as Stenka Razin, was a Don Cossack leader who led a major uprising against the nobility and tsarist bureaucracy in southern Russia in 1670–1671. He became one of the most celebrated figures of Russian folklore, celebrated in songs, legends, and the first Russian feature film (1908).

Background

Razin was born in the Cossack stanitsa of Zimoveyskaya on the Don River. By the 1660s he was an experienced ataman (Cossack leader), had served on diplomatic missions to the Kalmyks, and had a reputation from campaigns against the Crimean Tatars and Turks. His elder brother Ivan was executed in 1665 by a tsarist commander, an event said to have hardened Razin's hatred of the boyar nobility.

The rebellion

In 1667 Razin led a band of Cossacks on a raiding expedition to the Caspian Sea, plundering Persian towns and defeating a Persian fleet — the largest Russian naval victory on the Caspian of the era. In 1670 the campaign became open rebellion: Razin's forces seized Tsaritsyn and Astrakhan and moved up the Volga, taking Saratov and Samara, while serfs, Old Believers, and non-Russian peoples of the Volga region flocked to his banner. The revolt spread over a vast area of southern Russia before tsarist troops under Prince Yury Baryatinsky defeated Razin at Simbirsk in October 1670.

Capture and execution

Wounded, Razin retreated to the Don, where wealthy Cossack elders captured him and handed him over to the tsar. He was tortured and publicly quartered in Red Square, Moscow, on 16 June 1671.

Legacy

Razin became the hero of the famous folk song "Volga, Volga, Mat' Rodnaya" and the ballad about the Persian princess; he was celebrated by Pushkin and glorified in Soviet historiography as a leader of a "peasant war" against feudalism.
