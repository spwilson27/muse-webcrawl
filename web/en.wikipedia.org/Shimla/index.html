Shimla

Shimla, also known as Simla, is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Himachal Pradesh.

Shimla, also known as Simla (the official name until 1972), is the capital and the largest city of the northern Indian state of Himachal Pradesh. In 1864, Shimla was declared the summer capital of British India, and it remained the summer seat of the colonial government until independence in 1947. Situated in the foothills of the Himalayas at an elevation of about 2,276 metres (7,467 ft), the city is famous for its Victorian-era architecture, its cool climate and its heritage as a hill station.

History

The area came under British control after the Anglo-Gurkha War of 1814–16. As the summer capital, Shimla was the setting for much of the social and political life of the Raj; the Viceregal Lodge (now Rashtrapati Niwas, home of the Indian Institute of Advanced Study) was completed in 1888. In 1945 the Simla Conference on India's future was held there, and in July 1972 the city hosted the signing of the Simla Agreement between India and Pakistan.

Geography and landmarks

The city is built across a series of ridges and spurs. Its pedestrianised Mall Road and the Ridge are its social heart, lined with landmarks such as Christ Church (1857), the Gaiety Theatre, and Scandal Point. Shimla is connected to the plains by the UNESCO-listed Kalka–Shimla Railway, a narrow-gauge mountain railway opened in 1903. It remains a popular tourist destination and the administrative centre of the state.
