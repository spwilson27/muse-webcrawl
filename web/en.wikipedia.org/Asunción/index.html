Asunción

Asunción (Spanish: [asunˈsjon]) is the capital and largest city of Paraguay, located on the left (eastern) bank of the Paraguay River opposite the mouth of the Pilcomayo River. Founded on 15 August 1537 by the Spanish explorer Juan de Salazar y Espinosa, it is one of the oldest cities in South America and is known as the "Mother of Cities", because expeditions departing from it founded Buenos Aires, Santa Fe, Corrientes and numerous other colonial settlements.

History

Asunción began as a fortified outpost, Nuestra Señora Santa María de la Asunción, established on the Feast of the Assumption in 1537. In 1541 its cabildo made it the first Spanish American city to establish self-governing municipal institutions. The city suffered in the War of the Triple Alliance (1864–1870), occupied by Brazilian forces from 1869; Paraguay lost much of its population. In the 20th century Asunción grew rapidly, especially after the Chaco War (1932–1935) and during the long Stroessner dictatorship (1954–1989).

Geography and climate

The city occupies rolling terrain on the eastern bank of the Paraguay River, near its confluence with the Paraná. Its metropolitan area, Gran Asunción, includes San Lorenzo, Fernando de la Mora, Luque, Lambaré and others, housing over two million people — roughly a third of Paraguay's population. Asunción has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild winters.

Economy

Asunción is Paraguay's principal port, industrial and financial centre. Government, commerce, banking and services dominate; the Ciudad del Este trade and hydroelectric revenues underpin the national economy, while the costanera waterfront and Villa Morra district host much modern development.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Palacio de los López (presidential palace), the Panteón Nacional de los Héroes, the Metropolitan Cathedral, the Casa de la Independencia museum (1811), and the historic Manzana de la Rivera cultural centre.
