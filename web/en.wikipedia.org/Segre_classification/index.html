Segre classification

The Segre classification sorts stress–energy tensors by the algebraic structure of their eigenvectors.

The Segre classification (sometimes called the Segre–Plebanski classification) is an algebraic classification of rank-two symmetric tensors in four-dimensional Lorentzian manifolds, used in general relativity to classify the stress–energy tensor — and by extension the possible forms of matter and energy — at each point of a spacetime. It is named after the Italian mathematician Corrado Segre, whose classification of symmetric bilinear forms underlies it, and was adapted to relativity by Jerzy Plebanski.

Classification

The classification examines the eigenvalue problem for the tensor regarded as a linear operator. Because the metric has Lorentzian signature, the tensor need not be diagonalizable; the possible types are denoted by symbols such as [111,1], [11,11], [11,2], [1,111], [(11),11], [z z̄, 11] and [31], in which digits list the sizes of Jordan blocks and parentheses group degenerate eigenvalues. The most physically common cases include type [111,1], a diagonalizable tensor with one timelike eigenvector — the form taken by a perfect fluid — and type [11,11] or [(11),11], characteristic of electromagnetic fields and null radiation.

Relation to other classifications

For the electromagnetic field regarded as a stress–energy tensor, the Segre type distinguishes null fields (such as plane waves) from non-null fields. The classification parallels the Petrov classification of the Weyl tensor. In cosmology and black-hole physics, the Segre type constrains which matter models — dust, perfect fluids, scalar fields, null dust — can source a given solution of the Einstein field equations.
