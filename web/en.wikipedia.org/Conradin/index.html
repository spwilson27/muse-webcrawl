Conrad III (25 March 1252 – 29 October 1268), called Conradin (diminutive of Conrad), was the last direct heir of the Hohenstaufen dynasty of emperors and kings. Son of Conrad IV and grandson of the Emperor Frederick II, he grew up in Bavaria after his father's death in 1254, while his uncle Manfred seized the crown of Sicily.

Italian expedition

After Manfred fell at Benevento in 1266 against Charles of Anjou — the papal champion who conquered Sicily for the Capetian House of Anjou — Italian Ghibellines appealed to the young Conradin to reclaim his inheritance. In 1267, aged fifteen, he crossed the Alps with his friend Frederick of Baden. Welcomed in Verona, Pavia and Rome, his small army advanced south but was defeated by Charles at the Battle of Tagliacozzo on 23 August 1268.

Execution and legacy

Captured while fleeing near Astura, Conradin was handed to Charles and, after a show trial in Naples, beheaded in the Piazza del Mercato on 29 October 1268 together with Frederick of Baden. Legend relates that he threw his glove into the crowd as a challenge to avenge him. His death at sixteen ended the Hohenstaufen line and extinguished, for a generation, imperial power in Italy; the Swabian duchy fragmented. Conradin became a romantic symbol of doomed youth — celebrated in German poetry and drama — and the Angevin regime that executed him faced the Sicilian Vespers revolt fourteen years later. He was buried in the church of Santa Maria del Carmine in Naples. See also Castel del Monte, his grandfather's monument in Apulia.
