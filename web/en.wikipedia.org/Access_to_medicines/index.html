Access to medicines

Access to medicines refers to the reasonable ability for people to get needed medicines required to achieve health. Such access is considered part of the right to health as established by international law, including the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights. The World Health Organization (WHO) states that essential medicines should be available in the context of functioning health systems at all times, in adequate amounts, in the appropriate dosage forms, with assured quality, and at a price the individual and the community can afford.

Barriers

Barriers to access include high prices, intellectual property protections such as patents, lack of research and development for diseases that predominantly affect people in low- and middle-income countries (so-called neglected diseases), weak health infrastructure, shortages of trained health workers, taxes and tariffs on medicines, and inadequate financing. Intellectual property has been associated with higher drug prices in some markets, limiting affordability for patients and public health programmes.

Essential medicines

The WHO Model List of Essential Medicines, first published in 1977 and updated every two years, identifies medicines that satisfy the priority health care needs of a population. The concept of essential medicines is intended to guide countries in selecting medicines for public procurement and reimbursement, and to promote rational use.

International agreements

The Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS) sets minimum standards for patent protection, including for pharmaceuticals, but also includes flexibilities such as compulsory licensing. The 2001 Doha Declaration on the TRIPS Agreement and Public Health affirmed that TRIPS should be interpreted in a manner supportive of the right of WTO members to protect public health and promote access to medicines for all. Campaigns by organisations such as Médecins Sans Frontières (Access Campaign, launched 1999) have advocated for lower prices, generic competition, and increased research into neglected diseases.

Initiatives

International initiatives include Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance; the Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria; UNITAID; the Medicines Patent Pool, which negotiates voluntary licences to speed generic production; and various differential pricing and donation programmes. Generic medicines, which are considerably cheaper than originator products, are a major mechanism for improving access, particularly following patent expiry or through licensed production.
