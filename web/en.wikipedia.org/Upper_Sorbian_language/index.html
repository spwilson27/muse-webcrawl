Upper Sorbian (Upper Sorbian: hornjoserbšćina) is a West Slavic minority language spoken by the Sorbs in the historical province of Upper Lusatia, today part of the German state of Saxony, centered on the city of Bautzen (Upper Sorbian: Budyšin). Together with Lower Sorbian, spoken farther north in Brandenburg, it forms the Sorbian group, most closely related to Polish, Kashubian, Czech, and Slovak.

Status and use

Upper Sorbian is officially recognized in Germany, and in the "core settlement area" public signage is bilingual and residents may use Sorbian with authorities and in schools. Estimates of active speakers range from about 13,000 to 20,000, and the language is classified as endangered, as German dominates most public life. Catholic parishes northwest of Bautzen, where Sorbian remained the everyday language longest, are considered the stronghold of the language.

Features

Upper Sorbian retains the dual number alongside singular and plural, has seven cases, and a verbal system related to Czech. Its phonology includes distinctive consonants written with letters such as ć, č, š, ž, ř, and ł. The literary standard was codified in the 19th-century Sorbian national revival, led by figures such as Handrij Zejler; the newspaper Serbske Nowiny and institutions like the Domowina federation (founded 1912) sustain Sorbian culture. An Upper Sorbian Wikipedia exists (Hornjoserbska Wikipedija).
