The Autobahn (formally Bundesautobahn, "federal motorway") is the federal controlled-access highway system of Germany. It is famous for sections without a mandatory general speed limit for cars and motorcycles, where an advisory speed of 130 km/h applies.

History

The first purpose-built motor road, the AVUS in Berlin, opened in 1921; the Cologne–Bonn autobahn followed in 1932. The Nazi regime greatly expanded the network as the Reichsautobahn from 1933, and after 1949 both West and East Germany extended the system; reunification in 1990 brought extensive modernisation of the eastern network.

Characteristics

Autobahns are dual carriageways with grade-separated junctions, hard shoulders, and design standards supporting high speeds. Speed limits are imposed near cities, at junctions and through roadworks, and vehicles such as trucks are limited to 80–90 km/h. A toll system applies to heavy goods vehicles.
