Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership

The CPTPP was signed in Santiago, Chile on 8 March 2018

The Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), also known as TPP11, is a trade agreement between Australia, Brunei, Canada, Chile, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, New Zealand, Peru, Singapore, the United Kingdom, and Vietnam. It evolved from the Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP), which was never ratified after the United States withdrew in January 2017.

History

The remaining eleven TPP members, led by Japan, negotiated a revised agreement incorporating most of the original TPP text while suspending 22 provisions, many concerning intellectual property that had been inserted at U.S. insistence. The CPTPP was signed on 8 March 2018 in Santiago, Chile, and entered into force on 30 December 2018 for the first six countries to ratify it.

Provisions

The agreement eliminates or reduces tariffs on most goods among members, and sets rules on services, investment, government procurement, state-owned enterprises, labour, and the environment. Together the members account for roughly 13–15% of global GDP — considerably less than the original TPP would have covered with the United States included.

Expansion

The United Kingdom applied to join in 2021 and signed its accession protocol in July 2023, becoming the first non-founding and first European member; the agreement entered into force for the UK in December 2024. Other economies, including China and Taiwan, have also lodged applications, which require consensus among members.
