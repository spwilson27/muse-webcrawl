Demand response

Demand response reduces or shifts electricity consumption during peak periods in return for financial incentives or time-based rates.

Demand response (DR) is a set of technologies and programs in electric power systems that encourage end-use customers to reduce or shift their electricity usage during peak periods, or in response to grid conditions, in exchange for financial incentives or lower time-based rates. Historically, electricity systems have been built to meet supply to demand; demand response treats demand itself as a controllable resource that can be increased, decreased or time-shifted, improving reliability and reducing the need for expensive peaking generation.

Mechanisms

Demand response takes two broad forms. Price-based programs expose customers to time-of-use pricing, critical peak pricing, or real-time prices, letting them respond to price signals. Incentive-based programs pay customers — directly or through aggregators — to curtail load on command: examples include direct load control of air conditioners and water heaters, interruptible tariffs, emergency demand response, and capacity-market participation. Smart meters, smart thermostats and automated building systems increasingly automate responses.

Role in modern grids

Regulators such as the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission in the United States (notably FERC Order 745, upheld by the Supreme Court in 2016) enabled demand response to compete in wholesale energy markets. As variable renewables grow, flexible demand helps absorb midday solar surpluses ("load shifting") and shave evening peaks, complementing energy storage. Typical responsive loads include industrial processes, commercial HVAC, refrigeration, water pumping, electric-vehicle charging and residential appliances.
