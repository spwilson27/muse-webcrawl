Tethys (mythology)

In Greek mythology, Tethys (/ˈtiːθɪs, ˈtɛθɪs/; Ancient Greek: Τηθύς, romanized: Tēthýs) was a Titan daughter of Uranus and Gaia, a sister and the wife of the Titan Oceanus, and the mother of the river gods and the Oceanids. As the wife of Oceanus and mother of the world's great rivers and springs, Tethys was an archaic sea goddess associated with the fresh waters of the world.

Mythology

Although Tethys plays no active part in surviving myths, her importance is reflected in her position in the earliest Greek cosmogony: in the Iliad, Hera speaks of "Oceanus, from whom the gods are sprung, and mother Tethys," suggesting an older tradition in which Oceanus and Tethys were the primordial parents of all the gods. In Hesiod's Theogony she is one of the twelve Titans. She is credited with bearing Oceanus some three thousand rivers and three thousand Oceanid nymphs, among them Styx and Metis.

During the Titanomachy — the ten-year war between the Titans and the Olympians — Tethys remained neutral, and in some accounts she raised Hera, who had been given to her by Rhea for safekeeping. Tethys appears frequently in ancient art, especially in Roman-era mosaics from the eastern provinces, where she is shown as a serene woman rising from the sea, sometimes with a rudder and accompanied by fish and sea creatures.

Legacy

The prehistoric Tethys Ocean, which once separated the ancient continents of Gondwana and Laurasia, was named after the goddess, as is Tethys, a mid-sized moon of Saturn discovered in 1684 by Giovanni Domenico Cassini.
