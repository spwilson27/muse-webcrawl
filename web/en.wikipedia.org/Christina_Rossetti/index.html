Christina Georgina Rossetti (5 December 1830 – 29 December 1894) was an English poet who wrote romantic, devotional and children's poems, including "Goblin Market" and "Remember". She was a sister of the artist and poet Dante Gabriel Rossetti and a central figure in the circle of the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood, for whose paintings she modelled.

Life

Born in London to Italian émigré parents — her father Gabriele Rossetti was a poet and scholar of Dante — Christina was educated at home and began writing verse in childhood. Deeply committed to Anglo-Catholic Christianity, she twice refused marriage proposals on religious grounds and lived a quiet, increasingly reclusive life devoted to family and devotion.

Works

Her first collection, Goblin Market and Other Poems (1862), established her reputation. The title poem, a richly sensuous fairy tale about two sisters and sinister goblin fruit-sellers, has been read as a fable of temptation, sisterhood and redemption. Her shorter lyrics — "Remember", "When I am dead, my dearest" and the carol "In the Bleak Midwinter", later set to music by Gustav Holst and Harold Darke — remain widely anthologised. She also wrote children's verse in Sing-Song (1872) and extensive devotional prose in later life.

Reputation

Though overlooked for much of the twentieth century, Rossetti is now recognised as one of the major Victorian poets, admired for her formal control, melancholy intensity and the tension in her work between renunciation and desire.
