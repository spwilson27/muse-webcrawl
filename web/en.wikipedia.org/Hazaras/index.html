Hazaras

Hazaras
Regions: Hazarajat (central Afghanistan); Quetta, Pakistan; Iran; large diaspora
Language: Hazaragi and Dari (Persian dialects)
Religion: Predominantly Twelver Shia Islam; minorities of Ismaili Shia and Sunni Islam

The Hazaras (Persian: هزاره, Hazāra) are a Persian-speaking ethnic group native to the Hazarajat region of central Afghanistan, with large communities in Pakistan (especially Quetta) and Iran and a worldwide diaspora. They speak Hazaragi and Dari, dialects of Persian, and are predominantly Twelver Shia Muslims, which has historically set them apart from Afghanistan's Sunni majority.

Origins

The name Hazara derives from the Persian word hazār, "thousand", traditionally linked to the military units (ming) of the Mongol armies. Hazaras are generally regarded as descending in part from Mongol and Turkic soldiers who settled in the region during and after the Mongol invasions of the thirteenth century, mixing with the local Iranian population; genetic studies show a blend of East Asian (Mongolic) and West Eurasian ancestry, and Hazaragi retains many Mongolian loanwords.

History

Hazara tribes were largely autonomous until the late nineteenth century, when the Afghan emir Abdur Rahman Khan conquered the Hazarajat in brutal campaigns (c. 1888–1893) involving massacres, enslavement, and land confiscation, which killed or displaced a large share of the population and pushed many Hazaras to flee to British India (Quetta) and Persia (Mashhad). Hazaras remained marginalised through much of the twentieth century. During the Afghan civil war of the 1990s they were targeted by the Taliban, including the 1998 Mazar-i-Sharif massacre. After 2001 Hazaras gained political representation and invested heavily in education, but Hazara civilians in Afghanistan and Pakistan have continued to suffer sectarian attacks by the Taliban, Islamic State – Khorasan, and Lashkar-e-Jhangvi.

Culture

Hazara culture is noted for music centred on the dambura (a two-stringed lute), distinctive embroidery and dress, and foods shared with wider Afghan cuisine. Hazara communities place a strong emphasis on education, including for girls, both in Afghanistan and in the diaspora in Australia, Europe, and North America.
