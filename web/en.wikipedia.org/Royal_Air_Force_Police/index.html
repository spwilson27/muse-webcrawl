The Royal Air Force Police (RAFP) is the service police branch of the Royal Air Force, headed by the Provost Marshal of the Royal Air Force. Its members are responsible for law enforcement and security on RAF stations and wherever the RAF deploys around the world.

History

The RAF Police was formed on 1 April 1918, the same day as the Royal Air Force itself, when the RAF took over policing duties from the Royal Military Police personnel previously attached to the Royal Flying Corps. During the Second World War the branch expanded greatly, providing airfield defence and security for airfields at home and overseas.

Role

RAF Police personnel provide a wide range of policing and security functions, including criminal investigations, counter-intelligence, close protection, military working dogs, and the protection of aircraft and airfields. They deploy on operations alongside other RAF units and are trained at the Defence School of Policing and Guarding at Southwick Park, Hampshire.

Organisation

The branch is commanded by the Provost Marshal (RAF), an Air Commodore. RAF Police squadrons and flights are stationed at major RAF bases, and RAF Police dog units provide patrol and detection capabilities.
