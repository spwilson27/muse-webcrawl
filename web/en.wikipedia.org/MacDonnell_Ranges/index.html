MacDonnell Ranges
Location: Northern Territory, Australia
Highest point: Mount Zeil, 1,531 m
Length: approx. 644 km

The MacDonnell Ranges (Arrernte: Tyurretye) are a series of mountain ranges in the Northern Territory of Australia, consisting of parallel ridges running east and west of Alice Springs. They extend some 644 kilometres and include Mount Zeil, the highest point in mainland Australia west of the Great Dividing Range.

Geology and features

The ranges are composed of ancient quartzite and feature dramatic gorges and waterholes such as Simpsons Gap, Standley Chasm and Ormiston Gorge, protected within the West MacDonnell National Park.

Indigenous heritage

The ranges are central to Arrernte culture and Dreaming stories, and the Larapinta Trail traverses their western section for 223 kilometres.
