Organon

The Organon (Greek: Ὄργανον, meaning "instrument" or "tool") is the standard collection of Aristotle's six works on logic. The name was given by later commentators of the Peripatetic school, reflecting the view that logic is not a part of philosophy but its instrument or propaedeutic.

The six treatises

  1. Categories — classifies the kinds of things that can be subjects or predicates of propositions.
  2. On Interpretation — deals with propositions and their relations of contradiction.
  3. Prior Analytics — develops the syllogism, the core of Aristotle's deductive logic.
  4. Posterior Analytics — concerns scientific knowledge and demonstration.
  5. Topics — a handbook of dialectical argument from accepted opinions.
  6. On Sophistical Refutations — analyses logical fallacies.

Transmission and influence

The texts were edited and arranged, probably by Andronicus of Rhodes in the 1st century BC. Boethius translated most of them into Latin, and through his versions the Organon shaped medieval scholasticism. In the Islamic world, logicians such as al-Farabi and Avicenna wrote extensive commentaries and adaptations; al-Farabi was called "the second teacher" after Aristotle. The corpus remained the foundation of logical education in Europe until the rise of modern symbolic logic in the 19th century.
