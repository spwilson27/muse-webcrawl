Fall of Kabul (2021)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The fall of Kabul was the capture of the capital city of Afghanistan by the Taliban on 15 August 2021, the culmination of a military offensive that began in May 2021 against the internationally recognised Islamic Republic of Afghanistan. The collapse of the Afghan government and its security forces, which occurred far faster than most foreign assessments predicted, effectively ended the War in Afghanistan (2001–2021).

Background

Under the February 2020 Doha Agreement, the United States agreed to withdraw its forces from Afghanistan, and in April 2021 President Joe Biden confirmed a full withdrawal by the end of August 2021. Through the summer of 2021, the Taliban seized rural districts and then, from 6 August, captured provincial capitals in rapid succession — Zaranj, Kunduz, Herat, Kandahar and Mazar-i-Sharif fell within days — while Afghan National Security Forces often surrendered or dissolved without a fight.

Capture of the city

On 15 August 2021, Taliban fighters reached the outskirts of Kabul. President Ashraf Ghani fled the country, later resurfacing in the United Arab Emirates, and the government collapsed. Taliban forces entered the city, stating they wished to prevent looting and ensure security, and occupied the presidential palace. The Taliban subsequently declared the restoration of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan.

Evacuation

Chaotic evacuation operations centred on Hamid Karzai International Airport, where thousands of Afghans and foreign nationals sought to leave. Images of crowds on the runway and of people clinging to departing aircraft were broadcast worldwide. On 26 August, an ISIS–K suicide bombing at Abbey Gate killed 13 US service members and around 170 Afghan civilians. By 30 August 2021, the final US military aircraft departed, completing the withdrawal and ending America's longest war, with more than 120,000 people airlifted from Kabul in the preceding weeks.
