Labours of Hercules

The Labours of Hercules (Greek: ἆθλοι Ἡρακλέους) are a series of twelve tasks performed by the Greek hero Heracles (Roman: Hercules) as a penance, in service to King Eurystheus of Mycenae. Driven mad by Hera, Heracles had killed his own wife and children; the Delphic oracle ordered him to serve Eurystheus for twelve years, during which the king imposed a sequence of seemingly impossible labours — originally ten, but two were disallowed, making twelve.

The twelve labours

  1. Nemean lion — slay the invulnerable lion of Nemea; Heracles strangled it and wore its pelt thereafter.
  2. Lernaean Hydra — kill the many-headed serpent whose heads regrew; his nephew Iolaus cauterised the stumps (this aid caused Eurystheus to disallow the labour).
  3. Ceryneian Hind — capture the golden-horned deer sacred to Artemis.
  4. Erymanthian Boar — capture the giant boar of Mount Erymanthos alive.
  5. Augean stables — clean the vast, filthy stables of King Augeas in one day by diverting two rivers (disallowed because Heracles demanded payment).
  6. Stymphalian birds — drive off the man-eating, bronze-beaked birds of Lake Stymphalia.
  7. Cretan Bull — capture the bull that ravaged Crete.
  8. Mares of Diomedes — steal the man-eating horses of the Thracian king Diomedes.
  9. Belt of Hippolyta — obtain the girdle of the Amazon queen.
  10. Cattle of Geryon — steal the red cattle of the three-bodied giant Geryon in the far west.
  11. Apples of the Hesperides — fetch the golden apples, with the help of Atlas.
  12. Cerberus — descend into Hades and bring back the three-headed guard dog.

In art and culture

The labours were a favourite subject of ancient vase painting and sculpture — notably the metopes of the Temple of Zeus at Olympia — and remain a common motif in Western art and literature, from Rubens to modern film.
