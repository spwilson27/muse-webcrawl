National Film Preservation Board

The National Film Preservation Board is the board selecting films each year for preservation in the Library of Congress's National Film Registry. It was established by the National Film Preservation Act of 1988.

Role

The board advises the Librarian of Congress, who makes the final selection of up to 25 films annually that are deemed "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant" and at least ten years old. Members represent the film industry, archives, scholars, and filmmakers, including representatives of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, the Directors Guild, and university film programs.

National Film Registry

By the mid-2020s the registry held over 900 titles, from early actualities such as Newark Athlete (1891) to major features, documentaries, animation, and independent films. Selection does not itself preserve a film, but it directs attention and resources toward preservation.

Related work

The related National Film Preservation Foundation, created in 1996, funds preservation by archives and libraries of orphan films not protected by commercial interests.
