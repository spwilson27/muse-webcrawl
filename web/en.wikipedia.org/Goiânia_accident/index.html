Goiânia accident

The Goiânia accident was a radioactive contamination incident that occurred on September 13, 1987, in Goiânia, Goiás, Brazil. Scavengers broke into an abandoned radiotherapy clinic and removed a teletherapy source containing caesium-137; fascinated by the glowing blue powder inside, they spread it among family and neighbours. The accident is considered one of the worst radiological incidents in history, rated level 5 on the International Nuclear Event Scale.

Sequence of events

When the Instituto Goiano de Radioterapia moved premises, a caesium-137 teletherapy unit was left behind in the derelict clinic amid a legal dispute. Two scavengers entered the ruins and dismantled the source assembly for scrap. One of them punctured the source capsule, exposing a powder — caesium chloride — that glowed a striking blue in the dark (an effect associated with the material absorbing moisture and fluorescence). The source passed through junkyards and family homes; children and adults rubbed the powder on their skin, and some ingested it. It was only on September 28, after many had fallen ill, that a medical physicist confirmed the material was radioactive.

Consequences

Four people died from acute radiation syndrome, including a six-year-old girl who had eaten food with contaminated hands; about 250 people showed significant contamination, and over 100,000 residents were monitored. The cleanup demolished several houses and removed thousands of cubic metres of contaminated soil and waste, which was stored in a purpose-built repository at Abadia de Goiás. The accident led to tighter international controls on medical radioactive sources and remains a standard case study in the dangers of "orphan sources". Images of the glowing powder have made Goiânia a reference point in discussions of radiological terrorism preparedness.
