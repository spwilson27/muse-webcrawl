Genoa (Italian: Genova) is the capital of the Italian region of Liguria and the sixth-largest city in Italy, forming the southern corner of the Milan–Turin–Genoa industrial triangle. Its port is Italy's busiest by cargo tonnage and one of the largest in the Mediterranean. Nicknamed la Superba ("the Proud"), Genoa was the centre of the maritime Republic of Genoa, a great trading and banking power from the eleventh to the eighteenth century that rivalled Venice and financed the Spanish Empire.

History

Settled by Ligurians and trading since antiquity, Genoa rose as a commune around 1096 and joined the First Crusade, gaining commercial privileges across the Levant. It defeated Pisa at Meloria (1284) and fought Venice in the War of Chioggia (1380), controlling colonies and fondachi from Corsica and Sardinia to Crimea (Caffa) and Chios. Genoese bankers — through institutions such as the Bank of Saint George (1407), among Europe's earliest public banks — financed Spain's kings and the voyages of Genoa's most famous son, Christopher Columbus, born in the city in 1451. The Republic, reformed by Andrea Doria in 1528, survived until Napoleon extinguished it in 1797; the city was annexed to Piedmont-Sardinia in 1815 and became a great industrial and emigrant port of united Italy.

Geography and sights

The city stretches along a narrow strip between the Ligurian Sea and the Apennines, climbing steeply from its amphitheatre-like old port. Landmarks include the Lanterna lighthouse (1543), the Cathedral of San Lorenzo, the medieval old town's alleyways (caruggi), the Via Garibaldi palaces of the Strade Nuove — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and the Porto Antico, redeveloped by Renzo Piano for 1992 with one of Europe's largest aquariums. The collapse of the Morandi Bridge in August 2018, killing 43 people, led to its replacement by the Genoa Saint George Bridge, designed by Piano and opened in 2020.

Economy and culture

Shipping, shipbuilding, steel, finance and tourism anchor the economy; the port handles over 50 million tonnes of cargo annually. Genoa is famous culinarily for pesto alla genovese, focaccia and farinata. Its football clubs, Genoa CFC (founded 1893, Italy's oldest) and Sampdoria, share the Stadio Luigi Ferraris.
