Swabian German

Map of the Alemannic dialect area; Swabian is spoken in the north-east, around Stuttgart, Ulm and Augsburg.

Swabian (German: Schwäbisch) is a dialect of Alemannic German spoken in the Swabia region of south-western Germany, principally in the central and eastern parts of Baden-Württemberg (around Stuttgart, the Swabian Alb, and Ulm) and in the south-west of Bavaria around Augsburg. It is one of the most recognisable regional dialects of German.

Features

Swabian differs from Standard German in pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammar. Characteristic features include the pronunciation of Standard German ei in certain words, the distinctive realization of st and sp as [ʃt] and [ʃp] in all positions (e.g. Fescht for Fest), the use of the diminutive suffix -le (e.g. Häusle, Mädle), and vocabulary items such as Grüß Gott as a greeting. Unlike most German dialects, Swabian retains a distinction in verb forms reminiscent of older stages of the language, and it lacks the simple past tense, using the perfect instead.

Varieties

Swabian shades gradually into neighbouring Alemannic (Low Alemannic and Swabian-Franconian transitional) and East Franconian varieties. There is no standardized written form; dialect literature, comedy, and advertising make heavy use of it as a marker of regional identity.

Status

Although spoken by millions, Swabian — like other German dialects — has retreated in favour of regional colloquial varieties closer to Standard German, especially among younger speakers in urban areas. It remains a strong identity symbol and is frequently imitated (often inaccurately) in German media.
