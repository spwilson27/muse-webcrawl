Bülowplatz murders

The Bülowplatz murders were the killings of two Berlin police officers, Captains Paul Anlauf and Franz Lenck, on 9 August 1931 at Bülowplatz (now Rosa-Luxemburg-Platz) in Berlin. The assassinations were carried out by members of the Communist Party of Germany (KPD) during the political violence of the late Weimar Republic.

Background

In the summer of 1931 Berlin was gripped by street fighting between communists, Nazis and the police. After several communists were shot by police, the KPD leadership planned retaliation against police officers in the Scheunenviertel district.

The attack

Anlauf and Lenck were ambushed near the Babylon cinema and shot at close range by a KPD squad that included Erich Mielke and Erich Ziemer. Both officers died of their wounds. The gunmen escaped, and Mielke fled to the Soviet Union.

Aftermath

Several accomplices were tried and convicted, some receiving death sentences. The case gained renewed attention after German reunification, when Mielke — by then the former East German Minister for State Security — was tried in Berlin and in 1993 convicted of the murders, receiving a six-year prison sentence.
