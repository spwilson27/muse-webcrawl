Ceremony of the Keys (London)

LocationTower of London
TimeNightly, shortly before 22:00
Performed byChief Yeoman Warder and Tower guard

The Ceremony of the Keys is the nightly locking-up ritual of the Tower of London, performed every evening for at least 700 years. It is one of the oldest surviving military ceremonies in the world.

The ritual

At 21:52 the Chief Yeoman Warder, carrying a candle lantern and the King's Keys, locks the outer gates and proceeds to the Bloody Tower, escorted by members of the Tower guard. Challenged by a sentry — "Halt! Who comes there?" — he replies, "The Keys." Asked "Whose Keys?", he answers, "King Charles's Keys." The guard then presents arms and the cry "God preserve King Charles" is given before the keys are taken to the King's House.

History

The ceremony is said to have continued unbroken even during the Blitz, when it was delayed by bombing. A small number of public tickets, booked in advance, allow visitors to watch. A separate Ceremony of the Keys is also held at Holyroodhouse in Edinburgh.
