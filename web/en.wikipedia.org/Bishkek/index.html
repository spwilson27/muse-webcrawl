Bishkek

Bishkek is the capital and largest city of Kyrgyzstan. Located in the Chüy Valley near the northern foothills of the Kyrgyz Ala-Too range, it has a population of over one million. The city was founded in 1825 as the Kokand fortress of Bishkek, was developed under the Russian Empire and the Soviet Union under the name Frunze (1926–1991), and became the capital of independent Kyrgyzstan in 1991.

History

The site began as a fortress of the Khanate of Kokand controlling caravan routes through the Tien Shan. Russian forces destroyed and then rebuilt the fort, and the settlement grew as a Russian colonial town, receiving city status in 1878. In 1926 it was renamed Frunze after the Soviet military commander Mikhail Frunze, who was born there. During the Second World War, factories and institutes evacuated from western Soviet cities spurred industrial growth. On independence in 1991 the city reverted to the name Bishkek — popularly explained as deriving from the name of a wooden churn used to make kumis (fermented mare's milk).

Geography and layout

Bishkek lies at about 800 metres elevation with the snow-capped Ala-Too mountains rising to over 4,800 metres to the south; Ala Archa National Park is a popular excursion. The Soviet-era city centre follows a grid plan with wide, tree-lined boulevards, irrigation channels, and large squares, including Ala-Too Square with the State History Museum and a prominent statue (successively of Lenin, then of the national hero Manas).

Economy and transport

The city concentrates most of Kyrgyzstan's government, universities, banking, and light industry, and is known for large markets such as Dordoy Bazaar, a major wholesale hub sending goods across Central Asia. Manas International Airport, about 25 km northwest, is the country's main gateway.
