Citroën Traction Avant

Manufacturer: Citroën
Production: 1934–1957
Class: Executive car
Layout: Front-engine, front-wheel drive
Designer: Flaminio Bertoni; André Lefèbvre (engineering)

The Traction Avant pioneered front-wheel drive in mass production.

The Citroën Traction Avant (French for "front traction", i.e. front-wheel drive) is a range of cars produced by Citroën from 1934 to 1957. Designed by André Lefèbvre and Flaminio Bertoni in a remarkably short 18 months, it was among the first mass-produced cars with front-wheel drive and a unitary (monocoque) body, and it featured independent front suspension and hydraulic brakes — a combination that made it revolutionary for its time.

Development

André Citroën gambled the company on the new car, building a new factory and developing an automatic transmission that proved unworkable and was replaced at the last moment by a conventional three-speed manual gearbox. The first model, the 7A, was launched in April 1934 with a 1,303 cc engine; larger 11CV and six-cylinder 15CV versions followed. The development costs, combined with the failure of the automatic transmission, pushed Citroën into bankruptcy in December 1934; its largest creditor, Michelin, took control and continued production.

Design

The Traction Avant's low, sleek bodywork — styled by sculptor Flaminio Bertoni — dispensed with running boards and sat far lower than contemporary cars thanks to the absence of a separate chassis and the compact front-drive layout. Engines drove the front wheels through a gearbox mounted ahead of the engine. Later models included the long-wheelbase 11 Familiale, the elegant factory cabriolets and coupés, and the 15H of 1954, which introduced hydropneumatic self-levelling rear suspension that foreshadowed the DS.

War and post-war years

Production was interrupted by the Second World War, during which Traction Avants were used by the Wehrmacht, the French Resistance and the Gestapo alike — an association that fixed the car in French popular culture. Production resumed after 1945, and the car remained in manufacture until July 1957, by which time about 760,000 had been built in France, Belgium, the United Kingdom and elsewhere.
