Retinopathy

Retinopathy is any damage to the retina of the eyes, which may cause vision impairment. Retinopathy often refers to retinal vascular disease, or damage to the retina caused by abnormal blood flow. Age-related macular degeneration is technically included under the umbrella term retinopathy but is often discussed as a separate entity. In general, retinopathy is an ocular manifestation of systemic disease such as diabetes or hypertension.

Causes

Retinopathy is a common condition and a leading cause of blindness in adults. Its causes include:

  • Diabetic retinopathy – caused by damage to the blood vessels of the retina in people with diabetes mellitus; it is the most common cause of retinopathy in working-age adults.
  • Hypertensive retinopathy – caused by chronically elevated blood pressure.
  • Retinopathy of prematurity – abnormal development of retinal blood vessels in premature infants.
  • Radiation retinopathy – following exposure of the eye to radiation.
  • Solar retinopathy – damage from looking directly at the sun.
  • Sickle cell retinopathy – associated with sickle cell disease.
  • Retinal vascular occlusion – blockage of the retinal veins or arteries.

Pathophysiology

Most forms of retinopathy involve disease of the retinal microvasculature. In diabetes, chronic hyperglycaemia damages capillary pericytes and basement membranes, producing microaneurysms, haemorrhages, and capillary closure. Retinal ischaemia then stimulates the release of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), which can drive the growth of fragile new blood vessels (proliferative retinopathy) and leakage causing macular oedema.

Diagnosis

Retinopathy is diagnosed by examination of the retina, usually after pupil dilation, using ophthalmoscopy or fundus photography. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) is used to detect and quantify macular oedema, and fluorescein angiography can reveal areas of capillary non-perfusion and abnormal vessel growth. Regular screening of people with diabetes is recommended in many countries.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause and stage. Good control of blood glucose and blood pressure slows progression of diabetic and hypertensive retinopathy. Laser photocoagulation has long been used to treat proliferative disease and macular oedema. Intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF agents such as ranibizumab, aflibercept and bevacizumab are now a mainstay for diabetic macular oedema and proliferative disease. Vitrectomy surgery may be required for advanced complications such as vitreous haemorrhage or tractional retinal detachment.
