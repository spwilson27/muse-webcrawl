Ignatius of Antioch

Born c. 50 AD
Died c. 108–140 AD, Rome
Feast 17 October

Ignatius of Antioch (c. 50 – c. 108/140 AD), also known as Ignatius Theophorus, was an early Christian writer and the bishop of Antioch. He is counted among the Apostolic Fathers and is venerated as a saint and martyr.

Letters

On his journey under guard to Rome, where he expected martyrdom, Ignatius wrote seven letters to churches in Asia Minor and to Polycarp of Smyrna. They are among the most important documents of early Christianity, witnessing to early church order, the role of bishops, and Eucharistic teaching; in them the term "catholic church" appears for the first time in surviving Christian literature.

Martyrdom

Tradition holds that Ignatius was executed in Rome, thrown to wild beasts, during the reign of Trajan, though details and dates are debated by scholars.
