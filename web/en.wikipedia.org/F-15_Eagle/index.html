The McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle is an American twin-engine, all-weather tactical fighter aircraft designed by McDonnell Douglas (now part of Boeing). Following reviews of proposals, the United States Air Force selected McDonnell Douglas's design in 1969 to meet the service's need for a dedicated air superiority fighter. The Eagle first flew in July 1972 and entered service in 1976. It is among the most successful modern fighters, with over 100 victories in air-to-air combat and no losses.

Design

The F-15 was designed around the "not a pound for air-to-ground" philosophy, with a large, lightweight wing, two Pratt & Whitney F100 engines giving a thrust-to-weight ratio greater than one, and advanced avionics including the APG-63 (later APG-70) radar and a head-up display that allowed the pilot to fight without looking down at instruments. Its armament includes a 20 mm M61 Vulcan cannon and AIM-7 Sparrow, AIM-120 AMRAAM and AIM-9 Sidewinder missiles.

Variants and service

Single-seat F-15A/C and two-seat F-15B/D models serve air superiority roles, while the F-15E Strike Eagle, introduced in 1988, added a dedicated strike capability. Israel, Japan and Saudi Arabia are major foreign operators; Israeli F-15s achieved many of the type's combat victories beginning in 1979. The newest variant, the F-15EX Eagle II, entered US service in the 2020s to supplement the fleet. In 1985, an F-15 famously destroyed the P78-1 satellite in the ASM-135 ASAT anti-satellite test.

Specifications (F-15C)

Maximum speed: Mach 2.5+ (1,650 mph) at altitude. Range: 3,450 miles with conformal fuel tanks and drop tanks. Service ceiling: 65,000 ft. Rate of climb: over 50,000 ft/min.
