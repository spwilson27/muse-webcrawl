The Five (composers)

Also known asThe Mighty Handful
OriginSaint Petersburg, Russian Empire
Years active1856–1870s
MembersMily Balakirev, César Cui, Modest Mussorgsky, Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov, Alexander Borodin

The Five, also known as the Mighty Handful (Russian: Могучая кучка), were five prominent 19th-century Russian composers who worked together to create a distinctly Russian style of classical music. The group consisted of Mily Balakirev (the leader), César Cui, Modest Mussorgsky, Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov, and Alexander Borodin.

History

The group began to coalesce in 1856, when the young Balakirev met Cui; Mussorgsky joined in 1857, Rimsky-Korsakov in 1861, and Borodin in 1862. The name "Mighty Handful" was coined by the critic Vladimir Stasov in an 1867 review. Unlike the conservatory-trained musicians of the day, most members of The Five were self-taught or pursued other professions: Cui was a fortifications engineer, Borodin a chemist, Mussorgsky a guards officer and civil servant, and Rimsky-Korsakov a naval officer.

Musical aims

The Five sought a nationalist music free of what they saw as the academic conventions of Western European conservatories. Drawing on the example of Mikhail Glinka, they incorporated Russian folk melodies, church modes, and oriental scales into their works, and favoured programme music and opera on Russian historical and folk subjects.

Legacy

Among their best-known works are Mussorgsky's Boris Godunov and Pictures at an Exhibition, Borodin's Prince Igor, and Rimsky-Korsakov's Scheherazade. By the 1870s the group had largely drifted apart as its members matured and developed individual styles.
