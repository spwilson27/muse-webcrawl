Zaya Pandita (1599–1662) was an Oirat Mongol Buddhist monk and scholar credited with creating the Clear Script (Todo Bichig) in 1648, a writing system designed specifically for the Oirat language, and with translating a large body of Tibetan Buddhist literature into Oirat.

Life

Born into the Khoshut nobility of the Oirat Mongols, Zaya Pandita was adopted as a child by Baibagas Khan and sent to Tibet at age seventeen to study Buddhism, spending about two decades there and earning the title pandita for his learning. Returning to the Oirats in the late 1630s, he travelled among the Oirat groups — Khoshut, Torghut, Dzungar, and Dörbet — teaching Buddhism, mediating conflicts, and promoting literacy.

Clear Script and translations

The traditional Mongolian script, written vertically, represented Oirat sounds imperfectly. Zaya Pandita's Clear Script modified the letters to match spoken Oirat precisely, enabling a flourishing written culture; it remained in use among the Kalmyks of the Volga into the twentieth century. Together with his disciples he translated some 186 works from Tibetan, including sutras, medical and astronomical treatises, and the biography of Milarepa.

Legacy

Zaya Pandita is a central cultural figure for the Oirat peoples, especially the Kalmyks of Kalmykia, where monuments and Kalmyk State University institutions bear his name.
