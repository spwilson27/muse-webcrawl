Perseus (constellation)

Genitive: Persei
Abbreviation: Per
Brightest star: Mirfak (α Per)
Area: 615 sq. deg. (28th)
Bordering: Cassiopeia, Andromeda, Triangulum, Aries, Taurus, Auriga, Camelopardalis

Perseus is a constellation in the northern sky, named after the Greek mythological hero Perseus, who slew the Gorgon Medusa. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. Perseus lies in the Milky Way and is best seen in northern winter skies, bordered by Cassiopeia and Andromeda to the north.

Stars

The brightest star is Mirfak (Alpha Persei), a yellow-white supergiant of magnitude 1.8 surrounded by the Alpha Persei Cluster. The best known star is Algol (Beta Persei), the prototype of the Algol variable stars: an eclipsing binary whose brightness drops every 2.87 days, historically called the "Demon Star" and associated in the constellation figure with the severed head of Medusa. Rho Persei also varies, and in 1901 the bright nova GK Persei flared in the constellation.

Deep-sky objects and meteors

Perseus contains the famous Double Cluster (NGC 869 and NGC 884), two open clusters visible to the naked eye; the California Nebula (NGC 1499); and the Little Dumbbell Nebula (M76). The massive Perseus Cluster of galaxies (Abell 426), some 240 million light-years away, hosts the active galaxy NGC 1275. The radiant of the Perseid meteor shower, peaking each August, lies in Perseus.

Mythology

In Greek myth, Perseus was the son of Zeus and Danaë who beheaded Medusa and rescued Andromeda from the sea monster Cetus; the neighbouring constellations Andromeda, Cassiopeia, Cepheus and Cetus depict the other figures of the legend.
