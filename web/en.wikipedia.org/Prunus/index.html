Prunus

Prunus
Family: Rosaceae
Subfamily: Amygdaloideae
Notable species: plum, cherry, peach, nectarine, apricot, almond

Prunus is a genus of trees and shrubs in the rose family, Rosaceae, that includes plums, cherries, peaches, nectarines, apricots, and almonds. The genus has a cosmopolitan distribution, being native to the temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere, and is commonly referred to as the stone fruit or drupe genus because its fruit is a drupe — a fleshy fruit with a single hard stone enclosing the seed. Depending on the classification, the genus contains several hundred species; many are of major economic importance as fruit crops and ornamentals.

Description

Members of Prunus are deciduous or evergreen trees and shrubs with alternate, simple leaves, often with glands on the petiole. The flowers, usually white or pink with five petals and numerous stamens, are often produced in early spring before or with the leaves — the celebrated cherry blossom (sakura) of Japan belongs to this genus. The fruit is a drupe with a fleshy mesocarp surrounding a hard, pitted stone (endocarp) containing the seed. Seeds and other parts of many species contain amygdalin, a cyanogenic glycoside that releases hydrogen cyanide when metabolized.

Classification and uses

The genus is divided into several subgenera, often treated historically as separate genera: Prunus (plums), Cerasus (cherries), Persica (peach), Armeniaca (apricots), and Amygdalus (almonds), along with the evergreen cherry laurels (Laurocerasus) and bird cherries (Padus). Molecular studies support a single broad genus.

Stone fruits are among the most important temperate fruit crops worldwide: sweet and sour cherries, European and Japanese plums, peaches and nectarines, apricots, and almonds (grown for the seed). Many species and hybrids are planted as ornamentals for their blossom, including flowering cherries, purple-leaf plums, and cherry laurel hedges. Prunus timber, such as cherry wood, is prized in furniture making. Species can suffer from diseases such as bacterial canker, silver leaf, and plum pox virus.
