Abba Kovner (Hebrew: אבא קובנר; 14 March 1918 – 25 September 1987) was a Jewish partisan leader, poet and writer. He led the resistance movement in the Vilna Ghetto during the Holocaust and later became one of Israel's most significant Hebrew poets.

Resistance in Vilna

Kovner grew up in Vilnius (Vilna) and was active in the socialist-Zionist youth movement Hashomer Hatzair. After Nazi Germany occupied the city in 1941 and massacres began at Ponary, Kovner was among the first to conclude that the murders were part of a systematic extermination plan. His manifesto of late December 1941 — "Let us not go like sheep to the slaughter!" — was the first public call for Jewish armed resistance. In January 1942 he co-founded and commanded the United Partisan Organization (FPO). When the ghetto was liquidated in September 1943, Kovner escaped through the sewers and fought as a partisan commander in the Rudniki forest.

After the war

Kovner helped organize the Bricha, the clandestine emigration of Holocaust survivors to Mandatory Palestine, and was a leader of Nakam ("Revenge"), a group that plotted retribution against Germans, including a plan to poison water supplies and a 1946 poisoning of bread at a prisoner-of-war camp near Nuremberg. He testified at the Eichmann trial in 1961.

Literary work

Kovner settled at Kibbutz Ein HaHoresh and became a central figure in Israeli cultural life, writing acclaimed Hebrew poetry and prose about the Holocaust and helping design museum exhibitions at Beit Hatfutsot and Yad Vashem's circle. He won the Israel Prize for literature in 1970.
