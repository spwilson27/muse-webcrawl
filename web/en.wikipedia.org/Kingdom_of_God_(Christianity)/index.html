Kingdom of God (Christianity)

The Kingdom of God (also Kingdom of Heaven in Matthew's Gospel) is a central concept in Christianity, forming the core of Jesus's preaching in the Synoptic Gospels. It refers to God's sovereign rule, understood variously as a present spiritual reality, a future eschatological order, or both.

In the New Testament

Jesus proclaimed that "the kingdom of God has come near" and taught about it in parables such as the mustard seed, the leaven and the sower. The Lord's Prayer petitions "your kingdom come". Interpretations differ over whether the kingdom is primarily future and apocalyptic, realised in the ministry of Jesus, or inaugurated but awaiting consummation.

Theological interpretations

Augustine identified the kingdom in part with the Church. Modern scholarship, including the "already and not yet" view associated with scholars such as George Eldon Ladd, holds that the kingdom is present in Christ's work while awaiting a future fulfilment. Liberation and social-gospel traditions emphasise its implications for justice in the present world.
