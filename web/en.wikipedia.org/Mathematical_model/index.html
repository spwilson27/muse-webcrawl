Mathematical model

A mathematical model is an abstract description of a system using mathematical concepts and language. The process of developing such a model is mathematical modelling. Models are used throughout the natural sciences, engineering, economics, and social sciences to explain behaviour, predict outcomes, and design systems, trading fidelity for tractability.

Classification

Models may be linear or nonlinear; static or dynamic; deterministic or stochastic; discrete or continuous; and deductive, inductive or "floating". Examples include differential equations for population growth and epidemics (such as the SIR model), optimisation models in operations research, game-theoretic models of strategic interaction, statistical models fitted to data, and large simulation models such as numerical weather prediction and climate models.

Building and assessment

Modelling involves identifying variables and assumptions, formulating relationships, estimating parameters, and validating the model against observations. Models are judged by fit, predictive accuracy, simplicity and usefulness rather than literal truth; as statistician George Box remarked, "all models are wrong, but some are useful." Sensitivity and uncertainty analysis examine how conclusions depend on assumptions and inputs.
