Quentin Tarantino

BornQuentin Jerome Tarantino, March 27, 1963, Knoxville, Tennessee, U.S.
OccupationsFilm director, screenwriter, producer, actor, author
Years active1987–present

Quentin Jerome Tarantino (born March 27, 1963) is an American filmmaker, actor, and author. His films are known for stylized violence, non-linear storytelling, extended dialogue, eclectic soundtracks, and extensive references to popular culture and film history.

Career

Tarantino worked at a video rental store in Manhattan Beach, California, while writing screenplays. His directorial debut, Reservoir Dogs (1992), gained critical acclaim, and Pulp Fiction (1994) won the Palme d'Or at Cannes and the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. He followed with Jackie Brown (1997), the two-part Kill Bill (2003–2004), Death Proof (2007), Inglourious Basterds (2009), Django Unchained (2012) — which won him a second Oscar for original screenplay — The Hateful Eight (2015), and Once Upon a Time in Hollywood (2019).

Style and influence

Tarantino is a leading figure of 1990s independent cinema. His trademarks include chaptered narratives, ensemble casts, long conversational scenes, and homages to genres such as blaxploitation, spaghetti westerns, and martial arts films. He has frequently collaborated with actors such as Samuel L. Jackson and cinematographer Robert Richardson.

Other work

He owns the New Beverly Cinema in Los Angeles, champions film projection on celluloid, and published a novelization of Once Upon a Time in Hollywood (2021) and the non-fiction book Cinema Speculation (2022). He has long stated an intention to retire from directing after ten films.
