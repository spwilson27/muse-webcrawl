Double Asteroid Redirection Test

The Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) was a NASA space mission aimed at testing a method of planetary defense against near-Earth objects. It was designed to assess the feasibility of the kinetic impactor technique: deliberately crashing a spacecraft into an asteroid to change its orbit. On 26 September 2022 the DART spacecraft successfully impacted Dimorphos, the smaller companion of the binary asteroid 65803 Didymos, shortening its orbital period by about 33 minutes — the first time humanity deliberately altered the motion of a celestial object.

Mission profile

DART was launched on a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Vandenberg Space Force Base on 24 November 2021. The roughly 610 kg spacecraft was mostly a simple box with a single instrument, the DRACO camera, used with the SMART Nav autonomous guidance system to identify and strike the 160-metre Dimorphos, whose existence as a binary companion made the deflection measurable from Earth through changes in the moonlet's orbital period. DART also carried the Italian Space Agency's LICIACube cubesat, released 15 days before impact to photograph the collision and the resulting ejecta plume.

Impact and results

At 23:14 UTC on 26 September 2022, DART struck Dimorphos at about 6.15 km/s (roughly 22,000 km/h). Telescopes worldwide, and the Hubble and James Webb space telescopes, observed a plume and a comet-like tail of debris extending thousands of kilometres. Within two weeks NASA announced that Dimorphos's orbital period around Didymos had decreased from 11 hours 55 minutes to about 11 hours 22 minutes — a change of some 32–33 minutes, far exceeding the 73-second threshold for success. The momentum enhancement factor (β) was estimated at roughly 3–5, showing that recoil from ejected material multiplied the effect of the impact itself.

Follow-up

The European Space Agency's Hera mission, launched in October 2024, is travelling to the Didymos system to survey the impact crater and measure Dimorphos's mass and structure in detail, completing the validation of the deflection technique. Neither Didymos nor Dimorphos posed any threat to Earth; the system was chosen purely as a measurable test target.
