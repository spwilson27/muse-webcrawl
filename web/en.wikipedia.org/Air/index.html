Air

Air is the invisible mixture of gases that forms Earth's atmosphere. Dry air consists of about 78.08% nitrogen, 20.95% oxygen, 0.93% argon, 0.04% carbon dioxide, and trace amounts of other gases, along with variable water vapour averaging around 1% at sea level. Air protects life on Earth by absorbing ultraviolet radiation, warming the surface through heat retention, and reducing temperature extremes.

Composition

The composition of air is remarkably uniform up to about 10 kilometres altitude. Carbon dioxide levels, historically near 280 parts per million, have risen above 420 ppm due to human activity. Near the ground, humidity varies from near zero in deserts to saturation in tropical regions.

Properties

At sea level and 15 °C, air has a density of about 1.225 kg/m³ and exerts a standard pressure of 101.325 kPa. Air is compressible, transmits sound at roughly 343 m/s at 20 °C, and its refractive index causes the blue colour of the sky through Rayleigh scattering.

Uses

Compressed air powers pneumatic tools and brakes; liquefied air is fractionally distilled to produce industrial oxygen, nitrogen, and argon. Air is essential for combustion and for aerobic respiration in animals and plants.
