The Guatemalan Civil War was a civil conflict fought from 1960 to 1996 between Guatemala's military-led governments and leftist guerrilla movements. Rooted in the 1954 CIA-backed coup that overthrew the democratically elected president Jacobo Árbenz, the war became one of Latin America's bloodiest, with the state responsible for the great majority of atrocities against civilians, particularly the indigenous Maya population.

Course of the war

The conflict began with a failed military revolt in November 1960. Guerrilla groups such as the Rebel Armed Forces (FAR) operated in the east, while by the 1970s new movements — the Guerrilla Army of the Poor (EGP) and the Organization of the People in Arms (ORPA) — organised in the western highlands. Under generals Fernando Romeo Lucas García and Efraín Ríos Montt (1982–83), the army pursued scorched-earth "beans and bullets" campaigns that destroyed hundreds of Maya villages. In 1982 the guerrilla groups united as the URNG.

Peace and legacy

UN-brokered negotiations culminated in the Agreement on a Firm and Lasting Peace, signed on 29 December 1996. The UN-sponsored Historical Clarification Commission concluded that some 200,000 people were killed or disappeared, that state forces committed 93% of violations, and that acts of genocide were committed against Maya groups in 1981–83. Ríos Montt was convicted of genocide in 2013, though the verdict was annulled on procedural grounds.
