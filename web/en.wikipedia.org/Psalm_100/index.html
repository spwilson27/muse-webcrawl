Psalm 100

Psalm 100, the "Jubilate Deo".

Psalm 100 is the 100th psalm in the Book of Psalms of the Hebrew Bible. In the slightly different numbering of the Greek Septuagint and Latin Vulgate it is Psalm 99. Its superscription calls it "A psalm of thanksgiving" (Hebrew: Mizmor l'todah), and in Latin it is known by its opening words, Jubilate Deo ("Shout to the Lord" / "Make a joyful noise to the Lord"). It is a short, jubilant hymn inviting all the earth to worship God with gladness.

Text

In the King James Version the psalm reads:

Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands.
Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing.
Know ye that the LORD he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.
Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.
For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations.

The psalm belongs to the group of enthronement or kingship psalms and, as a thanksgiving psalm, is associated with the todah (thank offering) in the worship of the Jerusalem Temple. In Jewish liturgy it is recited as part of the morning service (Pesukei dezimra) on most days, though traditionally omitted on Shabbat and festivals.

Uses and musical settings

In the Anglican tradition the psalm, as "Jubilate Deo", is a canticle appointed for Morning Prayer in the Book of Common Prayer, as an alternative to the Benedictus. William Kethe's 1561 metrical version, sung to the tune "Old 100th", begins "All people that on earth do dwell" and became one of the most famous hymns in English. The same tune carries the Doxology "Praise God, from whom all blessings flow". Composers who have set Psalm 100 include William Byrd, Heinrich Schütz, Henry Purcell (whose setting for the Chapel Royal is notable), George Frideric Handel (the "Utrecht Jubilate"), Felix Mendelssohn, Max Reger, and Ralph Vaughan Williams, whose 100th Psalm Tune setting was written for the coronation of Elizabeth II in 1953.
