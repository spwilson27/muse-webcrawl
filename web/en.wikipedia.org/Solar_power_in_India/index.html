Solar power in India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A solar park in India

Solar power in India is a fast-developing industry. India receives high solar insolation, with about 300 clear and sunny days a year in much of the country, and has built some of the world's largest solar parks. The National Solar Mission, launched in 2010 under the National Action Plan on Climate Change, set ambitious targets for grid-connected and off-grid solar capacity.

Growth

Installed solar capacity grew from a few tens of megawatts in 2010 to tens of gigawatts within a decade, driven by falling module prices, competitive auctions, and large projects such as the Bhadla Solar Park in Rajasthan, among the largest in the world. Major programmes include solar parks, rooftop solar subsidies, solar pumps for agriculture (PM-KUSUM), and the International Solar Alliance, headquartered in India.

Challenges

Challenges include land acquisition, grid integration and storage, financing costs, duties on imported modules, and building domestic manufacturing capacity.
