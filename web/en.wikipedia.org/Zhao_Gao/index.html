Zhao Gao

Zhao Gao (Chinese: 趙高; died 207 BCE) was a eunuch and close advisor to Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of China, and later the dominant power behind the throne of the Second Emperor, Qin Er Shi. He is remembered in Chinese tradition as one of the most notorious villains of antiquity, whose intrigues are blamed for the swift downfall of the Qin dynasty.

Career

Zhao Gao served as an official in charge of law enforcement and as tutor to the emperor's younger son Huhai. When Qin Shi Huang died suddenly in 210 BCE during an imperial tour, Zhao Gao conspired with the chancellor Li Si to conceal the death, forge an edict ordering the heir Fusu to commit suicide, and install Huhai as emperor. Under the new reign, Zhao Gao engineered the disgrace and execution of Li Si in 208 BCE and purged officials and imperial princes.

"Calling a deer a horse"

According to Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian, Zhao Gao once presented a deer at court and called it a horse; when officials disagreed, he had them punished, thereby testing who would submit to his authority. The episode gave Chinese the idiom zhǐ lù wéi mǎ ("calling a deer a horse"), meaning a deliberate distortion of the truth.

Downfall

As rebellions swept the empire, Zhao Gao forced Qin Er Shi to commit suicide in 207 BCE and installed Ziying as king of Qin. Ziying, fearing the same fate, lured Zhao Gao into the palace and had him executed along with his family. The Qin dynasty itself fell the following year.
