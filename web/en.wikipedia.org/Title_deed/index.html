Title deed

A title deed is a legal document that evidences ownership of, or an interest in, a property or asset. In real property law, a deed is the written instrument by which title to land is transferred from a grantor to a grantee; the collection of deeds tracing ownership back through time forms the "chain of title".

Types

Common forms include the general warranty deed, in which the grantor guarantees clear title against all claims; the special warranty deed, which covers only claims arising during the grantor's ownership; the bargain and sale deed; and the quitclaim deed, which transfers whatever interest the grantor may have without any guarantee. In England and Wales and other Torrens-system jurisdictions, registered title at a land registry has largely replaced unregistered deeds as proof of ownership.

Recording

In many jurisdictions deeds are lodged with a public recorder's office, giving constructive notice to purchasers and protecting the grantee against later conflicting transfers. Title insurance is commonly purchased in the United States to guard against defects in the chain of title that a deed search may miss.
