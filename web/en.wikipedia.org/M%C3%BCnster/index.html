Münster is a city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, in the Westphalian region north of the Ruhr. It is a major university city and administrative centre, historically the seat of a powerful prince-bishopric.

History

The settlement began with a monastery (monasterium) founded around 793 by the missionary Ludger, who became its first bishop. In 1534–35 the city was seized by radical Anabaptists under Jan van Leiden, whose rule ended in a siege and brutal reprisals; the cages in which the leaders' bodies were displayed still hang from St Lambert's Church.

In 1648 the Peace of Westphalia was negotiated in Münster and Osnabrück, ending the Thirty Years' War and reshaping the European state system. The historic Rathaus houses the Friedenssaal (Hall of Peace) where the treaty between Spain and the Dutch Republic was ratified.

Modern city

Heavily damaged in the Second World War, Münster's old town was rebuilt in its historic style. The University of Münster is one of Germany's largest, and the city is famed as Germany's cycling capital, with bicycles outnumbering residents. The Aasee lake and the Promenade ring of parks define its green centre.
