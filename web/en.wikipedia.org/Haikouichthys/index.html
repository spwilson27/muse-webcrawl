Haikouichthys

Haikouichthys is an extinct genus of craniate from the early Cambrian, about 518 million years ago.

Haikouichthys is an extinct genus of craniate (animals with a head and distinct sense organs) believed to have lived 518 million years ago, during the Cambrian explosion of multicellular life. Haikouichthys had a defined skull and other characteristics that have led paleontologists to label it a true craniate, and it has even been popularly characterised as one of the earliest fish. Cladistic analysis indicates that the animal is probably a basal chordate or a basal craniate, but it does not possess a skeleton sufficient to be classified as a true vertebrate with complete certainty.

Discovery

The fossil comes from the Chengjiang Fauna of the Maotianshan Shales of Yunnan Province, China. It was first described in 1999 by Hou Xianguang and colleagues. The name derives from Haikou, the location near Kunming where the fossil was found, and the Greek ichthys, "fish". Hundreds of specimens have since been recovered, including clusters of individuals that may indicate schooling behaviour.

Description

The animal is about 2.5 cm (1 inch) long and is narrower than Myllokunmingia, another putative early craniate from the same beds. The holotype preserves a series of six or seven gill arches, a possible skull of cartilage, a notochord, and what appear to be myomeres (segmented muscle blocks) arranged in a zigzag pattern along the body. There are indications of paired eyes and possibly paired nasal sacs, features that place Haikouichthys closer to vertebrates than more primitive chordates such as the lancelet. A prominent dorsal fin with fin radials ran along the back, and the tail appears to have had a fin fold.

Significance

Haikouichthys is one of the earliest known animals to possess the basic vertebrate body plan: a head with sensory organs, a segmented muscular trunk, and a post-anal tail. Together with Myllokunmingia and Zhongjianichthys, it documents the rapid emergence of craniate-grade organisation in the early Cambrian, only a few million years after the first appearance of complex animal fossils in the Chengjiang biota.
