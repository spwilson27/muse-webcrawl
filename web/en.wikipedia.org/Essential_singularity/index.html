Essential singularity

In complex analysis, an essential singularity of a function is a "severe" singularity near which the function exhibits wild behaviour. It is an isolated singularity that is neither a removable singularity nor a pole.

Characterisation

In the Laurent series expansion around an essential singularity there are infinitely many terms with negative powers. The Casorati–Weierstrass theorem states that the image of any punctured neighbourhood is dense in the complex plane; Picard's Great Theorem strengthens this: the function takes every complex value infinitely often, with at most one exception. A classic example is exp(1/z) at z = 0.
