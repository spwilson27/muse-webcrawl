Car platform

A car platform is a shared set of common design, engineering, and production efforts, as well as major components, over a number of outwardly distinct models and even types of cars, often from different, but somewhat related, marques.

Definition and benefits

Platform sharing allows manufacturers to reduce the costs associated with the development of products by basing those products on a fewer number of platforms. This further allows the manufacturer to create distinct models from a design perspective on similar underpinnings. A platform strategy helps spread development costs across many vehicles and increases economies of scale in purchasing components.

Components

A platform typically includes the floorpan and the basic dimensions of the vehicle, its suspension geometry and hard points, and often a shared set of powertrain mounting provisions. Elements that vary between models built on one platform include body styling, interior design, and features.

Examples

Well-known examples include the Volkswagen Group's MQB platform, used across brands such as Volkswagen, Audi, Škoda and SEAT, and Toyota's TNGA architecture. General Motors, Ford, and Stellantis have similarly consolidated their model ranges onto a small number of global architectures.

Modular architectures

Modern "modular" platforms, such as MQB and Hyundai-Kia's architectures, allow a wide range of wheelbases, track widths, and powertrains — including battery-electric drivetrains — to be derived from a single toolkit of components.
