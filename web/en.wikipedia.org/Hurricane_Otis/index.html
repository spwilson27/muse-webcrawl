Hurricane Otis

Summary

Formed: 22 October 2023

Dissipated: 26 October 2023

Peak intensity: Category 5; winds 165 mph (270 km/h); 923 mbar

Landfall: Near Acapulco, Guerrero, Mexico

Fatalities: At least 52

Damage: Over US$12 billion

Hurricane Otis was a devastating tropical cyclone that struck the Mexican state of Guerrero in October 2023. Otis underwent explosive rapid intensification in the hours before landfall, strengthening from a tropical storm to a Category 5 hurricane far faster than forecast models predicted, and it made landfall near Acapulco as the strongest hurricane ever recorded to hit Mexico's Pacific coast.

Meteorological history

Otis formed from a disturbance south of Mexico on 22 October 2023. Forecasters expected a modest tropical storm, but on 24 October the storm intensified at an extraordinary rate, gaining about 105 mph (170 km/h) of wind speed in 24 hours — among the fastest intensification rates on record. It reached peak winds of 165 mph (270 km/h) and made landfall near Acapulco shortly after midnight on 25 October at near-peak strength, before rapidly weakening over the mountains of Guerrero.

Impact

Acapulco, a resort city of about one million people, suffered catastrophic damage: hotels, high-rise buildings, homes, and the airport were heavily damaged, and power, water, and communications were knocked out across the region. At least 52 people were confirmed dead, with others reported missing. Total damage exceeded US$12 billion, making Otis one of Mexico's costliest natural disasters. The poor forecasts and the storm's nighttime landfall left residents with little warning, prompting criticism and renewed study of rapid intensification in a warming climate.
