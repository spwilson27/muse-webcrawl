Pie

A pie is a baked dish made of a pastry dough casing containing a filling of sweet or savoury ingredients. Pies may be filled with fruit, custard, meat, vegetables or cheese, and range from open-faced tarts to double-crusted pies sealed with a pastry lid.

History

Pastry-wrapped foods date to ancient Egypt and Rome; in medieval England inedible pastry "coffyns" served as cooking vessels for meat fillings. Sweet fruit pies developed as pastry became refined. British and colonial traditions spread pies worldwide: apple pie became an American icon, meat pies a staple in Australia and New Zealand, and savoury pies remain central to British pub food.

Varieties

Sweet examples include apple, pumpkin, pecan, lemon meringue and key lime pie; savoury examples include steak and kidney pie, chicken pot pie, shepherd's pie (topped with potato rather than pastry) and quiche-like custard pies. Hand pies and pasties are portable single servings.
