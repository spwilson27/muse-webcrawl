Autism, also known as autism spectrum disorder (ASD), is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by difficulties with social interaction and communication, and by restricted and repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests, or activities. Symptoms generally become apparent in the first two to three years of life, and the condition persists into adulthood, though its expression varies widely — hence "spectrum".

History and classification

The word "autism" derives from the Greek autos ("self") and was coined by the Swiss psychiatrist Eugen Bleuler in 1910. Leo Kanner's 1943 paper on "early infantile autism" and Hans Asperger's near-contemporaneous work laid the foundations of the modern concept. The DSM-5 (2013) unified the former separate diagnoses of autistic disorder, Asperger syndrome, and pervasive developmental disorder not otherwise specified into the single category of autism spectrum disorder, a change mirrored in the ICD-11.

Causes

ASD is highly heritable; twin studies suggest genetic factors account for the majority of risk, with hundreds of genes implicated, alongside a smaller contribution from prenatal environmental factors. There is no credible evidence linking autism to vaccines; the 1998 study that suggested such a link was retracted as fraudulent.

Support and outcomes

There is no cure, and much contemporary discussion — including the neurodiversity movement — frames autism as a difference to be accommodated as well as a disability to be supported. Early behavioural and educational interventions, speech and occupational therapy, and treatment of co-occurring conditions such as epilepsy, ADHD, anxiety, and sleep disorders form the mainstays of support. Outcomes range from independent living to requiring lifelong care.
