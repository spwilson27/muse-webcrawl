Osteomalacia


Meaning: Softening of the bones in adults
Main cause: Vitamin D deficiency
Childhood counterpart: Rickets

Osteomalacia is the softening of the bones caused by impaired bone metabolism, principally inadequate mineralisation of newly formed bone matrix (osteoid). It is most often due to severe vitamin D deficiency, which reduces intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate. The corresponding condition in children, where the growing skeleton is affected, is called rickets.

Signs and symptoms

Adults with osteomalacia may develop diffuse bone pain and tenderness, muscle weakness especially of the proximal muscles causing a waddling gait, and an increased risk of fractures from minor trauma. Radiographs may show characteristic Looser zones (pseudofractures), thin translucent bands in bones such as the ribs, pelvis and femur. Blood tests typically reveal low or low-normal calcium, low phosphate, raised alkaline phosphatase and raised parathyroid hormone secondary to the vitamin D deficiency.

Causes and treatment

Causes include insufficient sunlight exposure or dietary vitamin D, malabsorption (for example coeliac disease or after gastric surgery), chronic kidney disease, certain medications, and rare inherited or tumour-induced disorders of phosphate wasting. Treatment addresses the underlying cause, usually with vitamin D and calcium supplementation, after which pain and weakness improve over months and bone density recovers.
