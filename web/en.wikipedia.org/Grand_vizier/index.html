Grand vizier (Ottoman Turkish: صدر اعظم, Sadr-ı Azam) was the title of the effective head of government of many sovereign states in the Islamic world, above all the Ottoman Empire. The grand vizier was the sultan's absolute deputy, held the imperial seal, and presided over the imperial council (Divan); he could be appointed and dismissed only by the sultan.

Ottoman office

The office developed in the 14th century as the vizierate grew in power. Grand viziers directed the administration, commanded armies in the field as serdar-ı ekrem, and managed foreign relations through the Sublime Porte — a name derived from the gate of the grand vizier's offices, which became a metonym for the Ottoman government itself. The Köprülü family supplied a dynasty of grand viziers in the 17th century who restored Ottoman fortunes.

Other states

The title, from the Arabic wazīr (minister), was used across the Abbasid Caliphate, Mughal India, Safavid Persia and other states; in some, the grand vizier (Wazir-e-Azam) remains the formal title of a prime minister, as in Pakistan.
