Château Frontenac

Location1 Rue des Carrières, Quebec City, Canada
Opened1893
ArchitectBruce Price
OperatorFairmont Hotels

The Fairmont Le Château Frontenac, commonly known as the Château Frontenac, is a grand railway hotel in Quebec City, Canada, overlooking the Saint Lawrence River from Cape Diamond. It is one of Canada's most recognisable landmarks and is often described as one of the most photographed hotels in the world.

History

The hotel was built by the Canadian Pacific Railway to promote luxury tourism along its network, opening in 1893. Designed by architect Bruce Price in the Châteauesque style, with steep copper roofs and turrets, it was named after Louis de Buade, Count of Frontenac, governor of New France in the 17th century. It hosted the Quebec Conferences of 1943 and 1944, where Churchill, Roosevelt and Mackenzie King planned Allied strategy.

Recognition

The hotel is a National Historic Site of Canada and stands within the UNESCO-listed Historic District of Old Québec.
