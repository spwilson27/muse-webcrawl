Isfahan is a major city in central Iran, about 406 kilometres (252 mi) south of Tehran, situated on the Zayandeh River at the foot of the Zagros mountains. It is one of Iran's oldest and most celebrated cities, renowned for its Persian-Islamic architecture, grand boulevards, covered bridges, palaces, and minarets. A Persian proverb calls it Esfahān nesf-e-jahān—"Isfahan is half the world."

History

The site has been inhabited since ancient times and grew under successive Persian dynasties. Isfahan reached its golden age under the Safavid Shah Abbas I, who made it his capital in 1598 and rebuilt it on a monumental scale. He laid out the vast Naqsh-e Jahan Square, flanked by the Shah Mosque, the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque, the Ali Qapu Palace, and the entrance to the grand bazaar. The Armenian quarter of New Julfa, home to the Vank Cathedral, was established across the river for deported Armenian merchants and craftsmen.

Architecture and culture

Landmarks include the Jameh Mosque, a record of a thousand years of mosque architecture; the Khaju Bridge and Si-o-se-pol spanning the Zayandeh River; and the Chehel Sotun palace. Isfahan remains a centre of traditional crafts—Persian carpets, miniature painting, metalwork, and tilework—and a major industrial and university city.
