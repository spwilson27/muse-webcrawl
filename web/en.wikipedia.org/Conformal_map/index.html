Conformal map

In mathematics, a conformal map is a function that locally preserves angles, but not necessarily lengths. More formally, a map is conformal if it preserves oriented angles between curves through a point, as well as orientation. Conformal maps are central to complex analysis: a holomorphic function with non-zero derivative is conformal wherever it is defined.

Complex analysis

The Riemann mapping theorem states that any non-empty open simply connected proper subset of the complex plane can be conformally mapped onto the open unit disk. Conformal maps are used to solve problems in two-dimensional potential theory by transforming complicated domains into simple ones, such as the upper half-plane or disk. Famous examples include Möbius transformations, the exponential map, and the Joukowsky transform used in aerodynamics to model flow around airfoils.

Cartography

In mapmaking, a conformal projection preserves local angles and shapes of small features. The Mercator projection and the stereographic projection are conformal. Because no flat map can preserve both angles and areas, conformal world maps greatly distort areas at large scales — the Mercator projection famously enlarges polar regions.
