Condyle

For other uses, see Condyle (disambiguation). This article is about the anatomical structure.

A condyle (/ˈkɒndaɪl/; Latin: condylus, from Greek: κόνδυλος, kondylos; knuckle) is the round prominence at the end of a bone, most often part of a joint — an articulation with another bone. It is one of the markings of bones, used as landmarks in anatomy. Condyles are typically covered with articular cartilage and fit into a corresponding depression of an opposing bone.

Examples

In the knee

The distal end of the femur bears medial and lateral condyles, which articulate with the tibial plateau to form the knee joint. The corresponding proximal tibial condyles receive them. Injuries and arthritis of the femoral condyles are common causes of knee pain.

In the skull

The occipital condyles, two kidney-shaped projections on the underside of the occipital bone, articulate with the atlas (first cervical vertebra), permitting nodding movements of the head.

In the jaw

The mandibular condyle (condylar process of the mandible) articulates with the temporal bone at the temporomandibular joint, enabling opening, closing and lateral movement of the jaw.

Epicondyle

An epicondyle is a smaller projection situated upon or above a condyle, serving as an attachment point for muscles and ligaments — for example, the medial and lateral epicondyles of the humerus, involved in "tennis elbow" and "golfer's elbow".
