Battle of Bautzen (1945)

The Battle of Bautzen was one of the last major armoured engagements on the Eastern Front of World War II, fought from 21 to 30 April 1945 around the town of Bautzen in Upper Lusatia, Germany. It pitted elements of the German Army Group Centre against the Soviet 52nd Army and the Polish 2nd Army, and is often described as one of the last German tactical victories of the war.

Background

During the Soviet Berlin offensive, Marshal Ivan Konev's 1st Ukrainian Front pushed westward. The Polish 2nd Army under Karol Świerczewski advanced toward Dresden, exposing a long flank. German forces under Ferdinand Schörner counterattacked with the Hermann Göring Panzer Division, the Brandenburg Panzergrenadier Division and other formations, striking the Poles near Bautzen.

The battle

The German counterstroke cut off and mauled several Polish divisions; the Polish 5th Infantry Division and 16th Tank Brigade were nearly annihilated in the fighting around Bautzen and Weißenberg. Soviet reinforcements were rushed in, Bautzen itself changed hands amid heavy destruction, and the Germans ultimately withdrew as the overall situation collapsed. Polish casualties were severe — roughly 18,000 men, including some 5,000 killed — making Bautzen one of the bloodiest battles in Polish military history.

Aftermath

The battle delayed but could not prevent the Soviet advance; within days Germany surrendered. In Poland, the battle is commemorated as a symbol of sacrifice, though Świerczewski's leadership has been criticised for costly errors.
