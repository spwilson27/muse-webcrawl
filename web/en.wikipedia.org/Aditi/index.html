Aditi

Aditi (Sanskrit: अदिति, "boundless" or "limitless") is a goddess in Hinduism, personifying the infinite and the vast expanse of the universe. In the Vedas she is celebrated as the mother of the gods — above all of the group of solar deities known as the Adityas — and as a protective, liberating mother goddess who frees her worshippers from sin, sickness and constraint.

In the Vedas

Aditi is invoked in numerous hymns of the Rigveda, usually together with her sons. She is the wife of the sage Kashyapa and mother of the twelve Adityas, who include Varuna, Mitra, Aryaman, Indra, Surya and, in later tradition, Vishnu in his dwarf avatar Vamana — Aditi's son by her prayers for a child who could defeat the demon king Bali. Her name, from a-diti ("not bound"), reflects her nature as the unbounded sky and universal mother; she is sometimes identified with the earth, with speech (Vak), and with the cow.

Later tradition

In post-Vedic texts Aditi also appears among the wives of Kashyapa and mothers of the devas, in contrast to her sister Diti, mother of the daityas (demons). She is given a role in the churning of the ocean of milk and in Krishna's birth cycle, where she is said to be reborn as Devaki. Temples dedicated specifically to Aditi are rare, but she remains a figure of cosmic motherhood in Hindu thought.
