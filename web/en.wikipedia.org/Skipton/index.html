Skipton

Skipton (also known as Skipton-in-Craven) is a market town and civil parish in North Yorkshire, England. Historically in the East Division of the wapentake of Staincliffe in the West Riding of Yorkshire, it is on the River Aire and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal to the south of the Yorkshire Dales. It is often described as the "Gateway to the Dales".

The town's name derives from Old English meaning "sheep farm" (sceap + tun). It is recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086.

Landmarks and history

Skipton Castle, founded around 1090 by Robert de Romille and rebuilt in stone in the following century, is one of the best-preserved medieval castles in England; it was the seat of the Clifford family and was held for the Royalists during the Civil War, surrendering in 1645. The town's broad High Street hosts a market four days a week. The parish church of Holy Trinity dates in part from the 13th century. The Leeds and Liverpool Canal, completed through Skipton in the early 19th century, brought textile and quarrying trade; Skipton Building Society, founded in 1853, grew from the town into one of the UK's largest building societies.
