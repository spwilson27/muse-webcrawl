List of horse breeds

This list of horse breeds includes the many distinct populations of domestic horses developed through selective breeding for particular traits — speed, strength, endurance, gait, size or temperament — as well as feral populations of free-roaming horses. Breeds are usually maintained by registries (studbooks) that record pedigrees; the total number of recognised breeds worldwide runs into the hundreds.

Light and riding breeds

  • Arabian — one of the oldest and most influential breeds, foundation of many others; noted for endurance and a distinctive dished head.
  • Thoroughbred — the premier racehorse breed, developed in England from Arabian, Barb and Turkoman stallions.
  • American Quarter Horse — the world's most numerous breed, bred for sprinting and cattle work; Morgan, American Saddlebred, Tennessee Walking Horse and Missouri Fox Trotter (gaited breeds); Appaloosa and American Paint Horse (colour breeds).
  • Andalusian and Lusitano (Iberian), Lipizzaner (of the Spanish Riding School), Hanoverian, Holsteiner, Trakehner, Dutch Warmblood and other warmbloods bred for dressage and show jumping; Akhal-Teke of Turkmenistan; Marwari of India; Friesian of the Netherlands.

Draft breeds

  • Shire — among the tallest and heaviest; Clydesdale (Scotland, feathered feet), Percheron (France), Belgian Draught, Suffolk Punch, Irish Draught, Ardennais and Brabant.

Ponies and small breeds

  • Welsh Pony and Cob, Shetland pony, Connemara, Dartmoor and Exmoor (British native ponies), Haflinger (Austria), Fjord horse (Norway), Icelandic horse (a gaited small horse, never called a pony in Iceland), Fell and Dales ponies, and miniature horses.

Feral and other populations

Free-roaming populations include American mustangs, Australian brumbies, and the wild Przewalski's horse, a distinct subspecies reintroduced to Mongolia. Donkey and mule breeding, and extinct breeds such as the Norfolk Trotter, are treated in related articles.
