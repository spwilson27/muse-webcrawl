Ruan

The ruan (Chinese: 阮; pinyin: ruǎn) is a traditional Chinese plucked string instrument. It is a lute with a fretted neck, a circular body, and four strings, played with a plectrum or the fingernails. One of the principal instruments of the modern Chinese orchestra, it belongs to the same family as the pipa and is sometimes called the "Chinese guitar" in older Western sources; its moon-shaped round body is distinctive.

History

The instrument is named after Ruan Xian, one of the Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove (3rd century CE), who was depicted playing such a round-bodied lute; instruments of this type appear in tomb art from the Han and Jin dynasties. Historically a solo instrument of modest size, the ruan declined in prominence for centuries before being revived and redesigned in the 20th century.

Modern family

In the 1950s Chinese luthiers, notably Zhang Ziliang, developed a full family of ruan in different sizes for orchestral use: the small high-pitched gaoyinruan, the soprano xiaoruan, the tenor zhongruan (the most commonly used as a solo instrument) and the bass daruan. The modern instrument has 24 frets arranged in a chromatic scale over two octaves and typically metal strings, giving it a mellow, rounded tone suited to both accompaniment and solo repertoire. Well-known performers include Liu Xing and the ruan master Lin Jiliang, who composed much of its modern repertoire.
