Lusophony

The Portuguese-speaking world

Total speakers: ~290 million
Official in: 9 countries and territories

Lusophony (Portuguese: Lusofonia) is the collection of people and countries that speak the Portuguese language, as a native, official, or cultural language, together with the network of cultural and political ties among them. The term derives from Lusus, the mythical founder of Lusitania, the Roman province corresponding roughly to modern Portugal.

Geographical extent

Portuguese is official in Portugal, Brazil — by far the largest Lusophone country, with over 200 million speakers — Angola, Mozambique, Cape Verde, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé and Príncipe, and Equatorial Guinea, and is co-official in Macau and East Timor. Substantial Portuguese-speaking communities also exist in Goa, Malacca, and the diaspora in France, the United States, Canada, and elsewhere.

Institutions

The Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CPLP), founded in 1996 and headquartered in Lisbon, promotes cooperation among Lusophone states in diplomacy, culture, and language policy. The International Portuguese Language Institute supports the teaching and study of the language worldwide.

Language

Brazilian and European Portuguese differ in pronunciation, vocabulary, spelling conventions, and some grammar, though a 1990 orthographic agreement has worked to standardise written forms. Portuguese is the sixth most widely spoken language in the world and the most spoken language in the Southern Hemisphere, largely because of Brazil.
