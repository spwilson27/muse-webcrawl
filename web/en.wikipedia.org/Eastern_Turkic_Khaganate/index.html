The Eastern Turkic Khaganate was the eastern portion of the First Turkic Khaganate after its division in 581 AD following a succession war. Centred on the sacred Ötüken forest region of Mongolia, it controlled the eastern Eurasian steppe and frequently warred with, and extracted tribute from, Sui and Tang China.

First Khaganate

After the split of the united khaganate founded by Bumin Qaghan in 552, the eastern realm was ruled by khagans including Ishbara and Qilibi. In 630 the Tang emperor Taizong's campaigns, exploiting internal revolts of subject peoples such as the Xueyantuo, destroyed the Eastern Khaganate and captured Illig Qaghan, bringing the Turks under Tang suzerainty for half a century.

Second Khaganate

In 682 Ilterish Qaghan (Kutlug) led a successful revolt and restored the khaganate, advised by the strategist Tonyukuk. Under Bilge Qaghan and his brother Kul Tigin, commemorated in the Orkhon inscriptions — the oldest known texts in a Turkic language, written in Old Turkic runes — the state again dominated the steppe and negotiated with Tang China as an equal. After Bilge's death in 734, succession struggles weakened the state, and in 744 a coalition of Uyghurs, Karluks and Basmyls overthrew the last khagan and founded the Uyghur Khaganate.
