Samyutta Nikaya

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Saṃyutta Nikāya ("Connected Discourses" or "Grouped Discourses") is a Buddhist scripture, the third of the five nikāyas (collections) in the Sutta Pitaka of the Pali Canon, the scriptures of Theravada Buddhism. It contains about 2,889 short discourses (suttas) attributed to the Buddha and his chief disciples, grouped into 56 collections (saṃyuttas) organised by subject.

Contents

The collection is divided into five books (vaggas): the Sagāthā Vagga (verses), Nidāna Vagga (causality, including the classic expositions of dependent origination, paṭicca-samuppāda), Khandha Vagga (the five aggregates), Saḷāyatana Vagga (the six sense bases), and Mahā Vagga (the great book, covering the Noble Eightfold Path, the factors of awakening, and the Four Noble Truths). Its Chinese counterpart is the Saṃyukta Āgama of the Sarvāstivāda school.

Significance

Because its groupings cluster discourses on a single doctrinal theme, the Samyutta is a primary source for core early Buddhist teachings — dependent origination, the aggregates, not-self, and the path to awakening — and its verses include encounters between the Buddha and figures such as Māra and the devas.
