FIDE

FIDE — Fédération Internationale des Échecs
Founded: 20 July 1924, Paris, France
Headquarters: Lausanne, Switzerland
Motto: Gens una sumus ("We are one family")
Membership: 201 national federations

The International Chess Federation, known by its French acronym FIDE (Fédération Internationale des Échecs), is an international organization based in Switzerland that connects the various national chess federations and acts as the governing body of international chess competition.

FIDE was founded in Paris on 20 July 1924, during the first unofficial Chess Olympiad. It is one of the oldest international sports federations and is recognised by the International Olympic Committee as the supreme body responsible for chess.

Role

FIDE organises the World Chess Championship and the Women's World Chess Championship, the Chess Olympiad, and other international tournaments. It maintains the official rating system (FIDE ratings, based on the Elo system), awards titles such as Grandmaster (GM), International Master (IM), FIDE Master (FM) and Candidate Master (CM), and codifies the Laws of Chess applied in competition worldwide.

History

After its founding, FIDE initially had little influence; in 1948, following the death of champion Alexander Alekhine, it took control of the World Championship and organised the 1948 tournament won by Mikhail Botvinnik. A schism occurred in 1993 when Garry Kasparov and Nigel Short broke away to form the Professional Chess Association; the world title was reunified in 2006.

Presidents

Presidents have included Alexander Rueb (1924–1949), Folke Rogard, Max Euwe (1970–1978), Friðrik Ólafsson, Florencio Campomanes, Kirsan Ilyumzhinov (1995–2018) and Arkady Dvorkovich (from 2018).
