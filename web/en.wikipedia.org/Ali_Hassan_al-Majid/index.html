Ali Hassan Abd al-Majid al-Tikriti (30 November 1941 – 25 January 2010), commonly known as Ali Hassan al-Majid and by the nickname "Chemical Ali", was an Iraqi politician, military commander and first cousin of President Saddam Hussein. He was a leading figure of the Ba'athist regime and was responsible for some of its gravest crimes, including the Anfal campaign against the Kurds.

Career

Born near Tikrit, al-Majid rose through the Ba'ath Party's security apparatus after the 1968 coup. He served as Minister of Defence (1991–1995) and Minister of the Interior, directed the occupation administration of Kuwait in 1990, and was a member of the Revolutionary Command Council. As secretary of the party's Northern Bureau from 1987, he held sweeping powers over Iraqi Kurdistan.

The Anfal campaign and Halabja

In 1987–88 al-Majid directed the Anfal campaign against Kurdish rebels and civilians, in which villages were destroyed, populations deported and tens of thousands killed. On 16 March 1988 Iraqi forces attacked the town of Halabja with chemical weapons, including mustard gas and nerve agents, killing an estimated 3,200 to 5,000 people — the largest chemical weapons attack on a civilian population. His recorded orders boasting of exterminating the Kurds earned him the name "Chemical Ali". He later oversaw the suppression of the 1991 Shia uprising in southern Iraq.

Trial and execution

Captured in August 2003 after the U.S.-led invasion, al-Majid was tried by the Iraqi High Tribunal. He was sentenced to death four times — for the Anfal genocide (2007), the suppression of the 1991 uprising, the 1999 crackdown in Sadr City, and the Halabja attack — and was hanged on 25 January 2010.
