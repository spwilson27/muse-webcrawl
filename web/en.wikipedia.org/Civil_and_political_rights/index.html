Civil and political rights

Civil and political rights are a class of rights that protect individuals' freedom from infringement by governments, social organizations, and private individuals. They ensure one's entitlement to participate in the civil and political life of society and the state.

Scope

Civil rights include the right to life, freedom from torture and slavery, liberty and security of person, fair trial, privacy, and freedoms of thought, conscience, religion, expression, assembly, and association. Political rights include the right to vote, to stand for election, to participate in government, and to equal access to public service. They also include protection against discrimination on grounds such as race, sex, language, or religion.

International law

The principal international instrument is the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), adopted by the United Nations General Assembly in 1966 and in force since 1976. Together with the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, it forms part of the International Bill of Human Rights. Regional instruments include the European Convention on Human Rights and the American Convention on Human Rights.

First-generation rights

Civil and political rights are often called "first-generation" rights in Karel Vasak's classification, rooted in the Enlightenment and the American and French revolutions. Unlike economic and social rights, they are typically framed as negative obligations—requiring the state to refrain from interference—although effective protection also demands positive state action, such as courts and electoral administration.
