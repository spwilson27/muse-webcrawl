Antonia Maury

Antonia Maury, a pioneering stellar spectroscopist

Antonia Caetana de Paiva Pereira Maury (21 March 1866 – 8 January 1952) was an American astronomer who was among the first to detect and calculate the orbit of a spectroscopic binary star. Working at the Harvard College Observatory, she developed an influential system for classifying stellar spectra.

Early life and education

Maury was born in Cold Spring, New York, into a scientifically distinguished family: her grandfather John William Draper and uncle Henry Draper were noted astronomers. She graduated from Vassar College in 1887 with honours in physics, astronomy, and philosophy, studying under Maria Mitchell.

Career

At Harvard, Maury was hired by Edward Charles Pickering as one of the "Harvard Computers." She found the observatory's classification scheme inadequate and devised her own, published in 1897 in the Spectra of Bright Stars, which introduced subdivisions based on the sharpness and width of spectral lines—effectively distinguishing giant from dwarf stars before those categories were understood. Her scheme was initially resisted but was later vindicated when Ejnar Hertzsprung recognized its significance and incorporated its ideas into what became the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram framework. In 1889 she discovered the second spectroscopic binary, Beta Aurigae, and calculated its orbit. She later worked at the Draper family's Hastings-on-Hudson observatory.

Recognition

Maury received the Annie J. Cannon Award in Astronomy in 1943. The lunar crater Maury is named in honour of her family.
