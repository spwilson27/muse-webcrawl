DualShock 3

The DualShock 3 wireless controller (placeholder image)

The DualShock 3 is a game controller developed by Sony Computer Entertainment for the PlayStation 3 video game console. Announced at the 2007 Tokyo Game Show, it was released in Japan in November 2007 and in other regions in 2008. It succeeded the Sixaxis controller, restoring the vibration feedback (rumble) that the Sixaxis had omitted while retaining its motion-sensing capabilities.

Background

The original PlayStation 3 controller, the Sixaxis, lacked rumble because of a patent dispute with Immersion Corporation and Sony's stated concern that vibration would interfere with motion sensing. After the legal dispute was settled in 2007, Sony introduced the DualShock 3, which added two vibration motors — one large and one small, as in earlier DualShock controllers — to the Sixaxis design.

Design and features

The DualShock 3 keeps the familiar DualShock layout: two analog sticks, a directional pad, four face buttons (triangle, circle, cross, square), four shoulder buttons with analog L2/R2 triggers, Start and Select buttons, and a central PS button. It connects wirelessly via Bluetooth 2.0 and can be charged and used wired through a mini-USB port. Its internal lithium-ion battery provides roughly 30 hours of play. Six-axis motion sensing detects tilt and acceleration, and the L2/R2 triggers and face buttons are pressure-sensitive.

Release

The controller was sold separately and later bundled with all PlayStation 3 models, in colours including black, satin silver, white, red, blue and camouflage patterns. It was succeeded by the DualShock 4, released with the PlayStation 4 in 2013.
