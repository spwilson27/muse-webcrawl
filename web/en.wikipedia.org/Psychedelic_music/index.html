Psychedelic music

Stylistic origins: rock, folk, blues, electronic, raga
Cultural origins: mid-1960s, United States and United Kingdom
Subgenres include: psychedelic rock, psychedelic folk, psychedelic soul, psychedelic trance, acid house

Psychedelic music (sometimes called psychedelia) is a wide range of popular music styles and genres influenced by 1960s psychedelia, a subculture of people who used psychedelic drugs such as LSD, psilocybin mushrooms, mescaline, and DMT to experience visual and auditory hallucinations, synesthesia, and altered states of consciousness. The music aims to replicate or enhance such experiences.

Characteristics

Psychedelic music often incorporates exotic instrumentation such as sitars and tablas, studio effects including tape loops, phasing, flanging, backwards recording, and long delay, as well as extended improvisation, surreal or whimsical lyrics, and elaborate album artwork. Key recordings include the Beatles' Revolver (1966) and Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967), Pink Floyd's early work, the 13th Floor Elevators, Jefferson Airplane, and the Jimi Hendrix Experience.

Development

The style emerged in the mid-1960s among folk rock and blues rock bands in the United States and Britain, and was central to the counterculture and the 1967 Summer of Love. In the following decades the psychedelic impulse fed into progressive rock, krautrock, funk, electronic music, and later revivals including the Paisley Underground, shoegaze, acid house, and psychedelic trance (psytrance).
