Bernard Pyne Grenfell

Bernard Pyne Grenfell (16 December 1869 – 18 May 1926) was an English scientist, scholar, and Egyptologist. Together with his friend and colleague Arthur Surridge Hunt, he is best known for the discovery and publication of the Oxyrhynchus Papyri, one of the largest groups of manuscripts discovered by archaeologists.

Oxyrhynchus

Grenfell and Hunt began excavating at Oxyrhynchus, in Middle Egypt, in the winter of 1896–97 for the Egypt Exploration Fund. Ancient rubbish dumps preserved in the dry sand yielded hundreds of thousands of papyrus fragments — literary texts, private letters, tax receipts, and official documents from the Ptolemaic and Roman periods. Among their finds were lost works of Sappho, Pindar, and Menander, fragments of early Christian texts including the so-called "Sayings of Jesus", and an extensive archive of everyday life in Roman Egypt.

Career

Educated at The Queen's College, Oxford, Grenfell spent much of his career excavating in Egypt, also working at sites in the Faiyum where he and Hunt uncovered Ptolemaic papyri and mummy portraits. He became Professor of Papyrology at Oxford in 1908. The long series of Oxyrhynchus Papyri volumes that Grenfell and Hunt edited transformed the study of the ancient world and continues to be published today.
