Decia gens

The gens Decia was a plebeian family at ancient Rome, which became celebrated in the early centuries of the Republic for the self-sacrifice of two of its members, father and son, who devoted themselves to death in battle to secure victory for the Roman army — an act known as devotio.

Origin

The nomen Decius is a patronymic surname derived from the praenomen Decimus, meaning "tenth". The family is first known from the fourth century BC, though it may have been of considerable antiquity.

Members

The most famous members were Publius Decius Mus, consul in 340 BC, who devoted himself at the Battle of the Veseris during the Latin War, and his son of the same name, consul in 295 BC, who repeated the act at the Battle of Sentinum during the Samnite Wars. A third Decius Mus, grandson of the first, is said to have devoted himself at Asculum in 279 BC against Pyrrhus of Epirus, though some historians doubted the story. Other members include Decius, Roman emperor from AD 249 to 251, notorious for the Decian persecution of Christians.

Branches

The principal cognomen borne by the family was Mus, meaning "mouse". Other surnames found among the Decii include Subulo.
