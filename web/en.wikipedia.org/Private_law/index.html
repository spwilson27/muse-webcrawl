Private law

Private law is the branch of law that deals with the relations between individuals or institutions, as distinct from public law, which governs the relationship between individuals and the state, and the structure of government itself. In private-law disputes the parties are, in principle, legal equals, and enforcement typically depends on the initiative of the injured party rather than on state prosecution.

Branches

Private law conventionally includes:

  • Contract law — agreements and their enforcement.
  • Tort law (in civil-law systems, the law of delict and extracontractual obligations) — compensation for wrongs such as negligence and nuisance.
  • Property law — ownership and rights in things, including land law.
  • Family law — marriage, divorce, and the custody of children.
  • Succession law — wills and inheritance.
  • Commercial and company law — business organisations and transactions.
  • Labour law in many classifications, in its contractual aspects.

Civil and common law traditions

The public/private distinction is most developed in civil-law systems, descending from the Roman jurist Ulpian's division of law; it structures the great civil codes, such as the French Code civil and the German Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch, which are largely codifications of private law. In common-law jurisdictions the distinction is less architecturally important but still used academically and in fields such as private international law (conflict of laws), which determines which jurisdiction's private law applies in cross-border disputes.
