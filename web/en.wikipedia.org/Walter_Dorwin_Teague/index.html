Walter Dorwin Teague (December 18, 1883 – December 5, 1960) was an American industrial designer, architect and commercial artist, often called the "dean of industrial design" and regarded, alongside Norman Bel Geddes, Raymond Loewy and Henry Dreyfuss, as one of the founding figures of the profession in the United States.

Career

Teague studied at the Art Students League of New York and began his career in advertising typography and illustration. In 1926 he founded his own design consultancy, one of the first dedicated industrial design firms. A 1927 commission from Eastman Kodak led to a decades-long relationship in which he designed many of the company's cameras and packaging, including the Art Deco Beau Brownie and Bantam Special.

Major work

Teague's firm designed streamlined Texaco petrol stations rolled out across the United States in the 1930s, interiors and livery for the Boeing 707 airliner, Steinway pianos, Pringles packaging (later), and exhibitions including Ford's pavilion at the 1939 New York World's Fair. His 1940 book Design This Day set out his philosophy that good design was a business asset.

Legacy

The firm he founded, Teague, continues to operate as a design consultancy. Teague's restrained, classical approach to streamlining contrasted with the more flamboyant styling of some contemporaries and helped establish industrial design as a strategic corporate function.
