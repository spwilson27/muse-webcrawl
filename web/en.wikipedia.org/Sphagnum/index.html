Sphagnum is a genus of approximately 380 accepted species of mosses, commonly known as sphagnum moss, also bog moss and quacker moss (although that term is also sometimes used for peat moss). Accumulations of Sphagnum can store water, since both living and dead plants can hold large quantities of water inside their cells; plants may hold 16 to 26 times as much water as their dry weight. The empty cells help retain water in drier conditions.

Description

Individual sphagnum plants consist of a main stem with tightly arranged clusters of branches (fascicles) and a dense head (capitulum) of young branches at the top. The leaves are of two cell types: small, green, living photosynthetic cells, and large, clear, dead hyaline cells with pores and spiral thickenings, which absorb and store water and give the plant its spongy texture and pale colour.

Peat formation and ecology

As sphagnum grows it slowly acidifies its environment and releases compounds that inhibit decay, so dead material accumulates as peat. Sphagnum-dominated wetlands — bogs, mires and muskeg — are major carbon stores; peatlands cover only about 3% of the world's land surface but hold a disproportionate share of soil carbon. In the process of bog succession, sphagnum can gradually convert open water or fen into raised bog.

Distribution

The genus is most abundant in the boreal and temperate zones of the Northern Hemisphere, with further centres of diversity in South America, New Zealand and Tasmania. Important species include Sphagnum magellanicum, S. capillifolium, S. cuspidatum and S. fallax.

Uses

Dried sphagnum ("peat moss") is widely used as a soil conditioner and growing medium in horticulture. Living sphagnum is used in orchid culture, terrariums and green roofs. Because of its absorbency and mild antiseptic properties, sphagnum was used extensively as a wound dressing, notably during the First World War. Harvesting of peat, however, damages carbon-storing bogs, and many countries now encourage peat-free composts; sphagnum farming (paludiculture) is being developed as a more sustainable alternative.
