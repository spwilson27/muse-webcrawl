Shutout

In team sports, a shutout (often abbreviated SO) is a game in which one team prevents the other from scoring any points at all. The term is used principally in North American sports such as baseball, ice hockey, and American and Canadian football, as well as in association football, where the equivalent term is a "clean sheet".

Baseball

In baseball, a shutout is credited to a pitcher who pitches a complete game and allows no runs. When two or more pitchers combine to allow no runs, it is called a combined shutout. Walter Johnson holds the major league career record with 110 shutouts.

Ice hockey

In ice hockey, a shutout is credited to a goaltender who stops every shot and finishes the game without conceding a goal. Martin Brodeur holds the National Hockey League record with 125 regular-season shutouts.

Association football

In association football a shutout is known as a clean sheet, credited principally to the goalkeeper and defence. Shutouts are considerably less frequent than in hockey because of football's lower scoring.
