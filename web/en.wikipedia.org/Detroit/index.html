Detroit

Detroit is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Michigan and the seat of Wayne County. Located on the Detroit River across from Windsor, Ontario, it is a major port on the Great Lakes system and the historic centre of the American automobile industry, earning it the nickname "the Motor City". Founded in 1701 by French explorer Antoine Laumet de La Mothe, sieur de Cadillac, Detroit grew into an industrial giant in the twentieth century; by 1950 it was the fifth-largest city in the United States, with about 1.85 million residents. Population loss to the suburbs and deindustrialization reduced the city to roughly 620,000–640,000 residents by the 2020 census (633,218), and in 2013 Detroit filed the largest municipal bankruptcy in U.S. history.

History

French settlers established Fort Pontchartrain du Détroit in 1701 on the strait (French: détroit) between Lake Erie and Lake St. Clair. The settlement passed to Britain after the French and Indian War and to the United States in 1796; a devastating fire in 1805 destroyed nearly the whole town, leaving the motto on its flag: Speramus Meliora; Resurget Cineribus — "We hope for better things; it will rise from the ashes". In the early 1900s entrepreneurs including Henry Ford, the Dodge brothers and Walter Chrysler made Detroit the world capital of automobile manufacturing. The industry powered the Great Migration of African Americans to the city, whose Black population built the Paradise Valley and Black Bottom neighbourhoods and later Motown Records. Post-war suburbanization, the 1967 uprising, plant closures and the decline of the Big Three automakers drove decades of population loss, fiscal crisis and, ultimately, the 2013 Chapter 9 bankruptcy, from which Detroit emerged in December 2014.

Economy and culture

The Detroit metropolitan area remains headquarters to General Motors and Ford (in Dearborn), with Stellantis's North American operations in Auburn Hills. Downtown and Midtown have seen substantial reinvestment since the 2010s, alongside universities, hospitals and the Detroit Medical Center. Culturally the city is synonymous with Motown — the record label founded by Berry Gordy in 1959 that launched Diana Ross, Stevie Wonder and Marvin Gaye — as well as Detroit techno, the Detroit Institute of Arts, and teams including the Lions (NFL), Tigers (MLB), Pistons (NBA) and Red Wings (NHL), all now playing downtown.

Geography

Detroit lies on the north bank of the Detroit River, one of the busiest waterways in the world and the only place where Canada (Windsor) lies south of a U.S. city. The city covers about 139 square miles of land, entirely within Wayne County, and encloses the separate cities of Hamtramck and Highland Park.
