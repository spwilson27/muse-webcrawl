Uruapan

Uruapan
Country: Mexico
State: Michoacán
Founded: 1533
Known for: Avocado production

Uruapan is a city in the Mexican state of Michoacán, the second largest in the state after the capital Morelia. Situated on the western edge of the Purépecha highlands at the Tierra Caliente transition, it is often described as the world capital of the avocado, lying at the heart of Mexico's avocado-exporting region.

History and sights

The city was founded as a planned town by the Franciscan friar Juan de San Miguel in 1533. The Barranca del Cupatitzio National Park, a lush ravine park around the young Cupatitzio River, is a noted green space within the city. Uruapan is also close to the Parícutin volcano, which erupted in 1943.

Economy

Avocado packing and export dominate the local economy, alongside other agriculture and lacquered-wood crafts (maque) inherited from the colonial era.
