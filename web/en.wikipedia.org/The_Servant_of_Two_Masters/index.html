The Servant of Two Masters (Italian: Il servitore di due padroni) is a comedy by the Italian playwright Carlo Goldoni, written in 1746 at the request of the actor Antonio Sacco (Sacchetti), who played the title role. A landmark of the commedia dell'arte tradition, it centers on Truffaldino, a perpetually hungry servant who takes employment with two masters at once — neither aware of the other — in the hope of double wages and double dinners.

Plot

In Venice, the merchant Pantalone's daughter Clarice is to marry Silvio, now that her original fiancé, Federigo Rasponi of Turin, has reportedly been killed. But "Federigo" arrives — actually his sister Beatrice in disguise, seeking her lover Florindo, who fled Turin after killing Federigo in a duel and is, unknown to her, lodging in the same inn. Both Beatrice and Florindo hire the same servant, Truffaldino. His frantic efforts to serve two masters simultaneously — delivering letters to the wrong recipients, inventing a fictitious fellow-servant "Pasquale," and desperately serving a banquet to both at once — drive the comedy's famous lazzi, until the confusions resolve and the lovers are united.

Goldoni's reform

Goldoni first supplied only a scenario for improvised performance, then progressively wrote out the dialogue, making the play a key document of his reform of Italian comedy from masked improvisation toward scripted, character-based drama. The published text of 1753 preserves the improvisational energy of the commedia while fixing it in literature.

Legacy

The play remains in the international repertoire, most famously in Giorgio Strehler's Piccolo Teatro production (1947), which toured for decades, and it was loosely adapted as the hit British play One Man, Two Guvnors (2011) by Richard Bean, which transferred the action to 1960s Brighton.
