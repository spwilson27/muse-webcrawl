Against Method: Outline of an Anarchistic Theory of Knowledge
Author: Paul Feyerabend
Published: 1975
Subject: Philosophy of science

Against Method: Outline of an Anarchistic Theory of Knowledge is a 1975 book by the Austrian philosopher of science Paul Feyerabend, in which he argues that there is no single scientific method that applies to all periods and all fields of science, and that science has progressed precisely because scientists broke methodological rules. His slogan — "anything goes" — summarises this position of epistemological anarchism.

Argument

Feyerabend developed his case largely through a detailed study of Galileo's defence of Copernican astronomy, arguing that Galileo succeeded through propaganda, rhetoric and counterinduction rather than adherence to empirical method. He contended that rules such as falsificationism or consistency with established theory would have blocked many celebrated advances, and that science should be separated from the state as church is, allowing free competition among traditions including non-scientific ones.

Reception

The book was highly controversial: many scientists and philosophers denounced it as an attack on reason, while others praised it as a liberating critique of rigid methodologies. It remains one of the most widely read and debated works in the philosophy of science, and grew out of Feyerabend's debates with his friend Imre Lakatos, whose sudden death in 1974 ended their planned joint volume.
