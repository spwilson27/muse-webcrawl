Neurovascular unit

The neurovascular unit (NVU) is a conceptual framework in neuroscience describing the close functional relationship between brain cells and the cerebral blood vessels that supply them. It comprises neurons, astrocytes, endothelial cells, pericytes, smooth muscle cells, microglia and the extracellular matrix.

Function

The components of the NVU work together to maintain the blood–brain barrier, regulate cerebral blood flow in response to neural activity (neurovascular coupling), and support the brain's metabolic needs. Endothelial tight junctions form the physical barrier, while astrocyte end-feet and pericytes regulate its properties and vessel tone.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of the neurovascular unit is implicated in stroke, Alzheimer's disease and other dementias, multiple sclerosis and migraine, and the concept has shifted stroke research from a purely neuron-centred view toward protecting the whole unit.
