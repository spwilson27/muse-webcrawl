Pear


The European pear, Pyrus communis.

Pears are fruits produced and consumed around the world, growing on trees of the genus Pyrus in the rose family (Rosaceae). The common or European pear (Pyrus communis) is native to temperate Europe and western Asia; the East Asian or nashi pear (Pyrus pyrifolia) is a separate species with crisp, round fruit.

Cultivation

Pears have been cultivated since antiquity and were prized by the Romans, who developed dozens of varieties. Modern commercial cultivars include Bartlett (Williams), Conference, Bosc, Anjou, and Comice. Unlike most fruit, European pears are picked unripe and ripen best off the tree; they are grown in temperate climates worldwide, with China the largest producer. Fire blight, a bacterial disease, is a serious threat to orchards.

Uses

Pears are eaten fresh, canned, dried, and as juice; fermented pear juice is perry. The wood is fine-grained and used for woodwind instruments and carving. Pears are a good source of dietary fibre, vitamin C, and copper, and are often among the first fruits given to infants because they are rarely allergenic.
