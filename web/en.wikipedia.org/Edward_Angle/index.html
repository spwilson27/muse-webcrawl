Edward Angle

Edward Hartley Angle (June 1, 1855 – August 11, 1930) was an American dentist widely regarded as the founder of modern orthodontics. He developed influential classifications of malocclusion and a series of orthodontic appliances, culminating in the edgewise appliance, and founded the first school and society devoted to orthodontics.

Career

Angle received his dental degree from the Pennsylvania College of Dental Surgery in 1878 and taught at several dental schools before concentrating on the speciality he named orthodontics. In 1900 he founded the Angle School of Orthodontia in St. Louis, the first institution devoted exclusively to orthodontic training, and in 1901 he helped found the organisation that became the American Association of Orthodontists.

Classification and appliances

Angle's classification, based on the relationship of the first molars, divides malocclusions into Class I (neutrocclusion), Class II (distocclusion) and Class III (mesiocclusion) and remains in everyday clinical use. His appliances progressed from the E-arch through the pin and tube and ribbon arch appliances to the edgewise bracket (1928), in which a rectangular wire engaged in a rectangular slot gives three-dimensional control of tooth movement; the edgewise system underlies most modern fixed orthodontic treatment.
