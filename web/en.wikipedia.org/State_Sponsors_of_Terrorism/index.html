State Sponsors of Terrorism

Countries designated by the U.S. Department of State as State Sponsors of Terrorism.

State Sponsors of Terrorism is a designation applied by the United States Department of State to countries that it determines have repeatedly provided support for acts of international terrorism. The designation, first established in 1979, triggers a range of unilateral sanctions, including bans on defense exports and sales, controls on exports of dual-use items, prohibitions on economic assistance, and various financial restrictions.

Designated countries

Countries that have been placed on the list include Cuba, Iran, Iraq, Libya, North Korea, South Yemen, Sudan, and Syria. As of the mid-2020s the list consists of Cuba (reinstated in 2021), Iran (since 1984), North Korea (reinstated in 2017), and Syria (since 1979). Removal requires a certification that the country has not supported terrorism for a preceding period and has provided assurances it will not do so in the future.

Effects and criticism

Designation restricts U.S. foreign assistance, requires the U.S. to oppose loans from international financial institutions such as the IMF and World Bank, and exposes the country's government to lawsuits by victims of terrorism in U.S. courts. The list is sometimes criticized as politically influenced, and designations have been used as bargaining instruments in wider diplomatic negotiations.
