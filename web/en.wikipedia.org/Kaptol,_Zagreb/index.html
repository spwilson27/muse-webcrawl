Kaptol, Zagreb

Kaptol is a part of the Upper Town of Zagreb, Croatia, and the historical seat of the city's Roman Catholic archdiocese. It centres on Zagreb Cathedral, the tallest building in Croatia, whose twin Gothic spires dominate the capital's skyline. Together with the neighbouring fortified town of Gradec, Kaptol forms the medieval core of modern Zagreb.

History

The Diocese of Zagreb was founded in 1094 by King Ladislaus I of Hungary, and the cathedral chapter (Croatian: kaptol, from Latin capitulum) around which the settlement grew gave the district its name. Kaptol developed as an ecclesiastical town of canons' curiae and the archbishop's palace, fortified in the 15th–16th centuries against Ottoman attack. For centuries it feuded — sometimes violently, over tolls and jurisdiction — with the free royal city of Gradec on the neighbouring hill, until the two were united into a single city of Zagreb in 1850. The cathedral, begun in the 13th century and rebuilt after the 1880 earthquake in neo-Gothic style by Hermann Bollé, suffered damage again in the earthquake of 2020.

Today

Kaptol's principal features include the cathedral, the Archbishop's Palace, the Dolac market adjacent to the west, and the curve of grand 19th-century canons' residences along Kaptol street, now home to restaurants, shops and the Zagreb City Museum nearby.
