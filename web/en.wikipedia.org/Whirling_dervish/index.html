Whirling dervish

[Image: Whirling dervish]

Whirling dervishes are members of the Mevlevi order of Sufism, founded by the followers of Jalal al-Din Muhammad Rumi, the 13th-century Persian poet and mystic, in Konya, Turkey. They are known for the sema ceremony, a form of physically active meditation in which dervishes whirl in a sustained spinning dance as a form of dhikr, or remembrance of God.

The sema ceremony

In the ceremony, the dervishes wear long white skirts (tennure), tall felt hats (sikke) symbolizing the tombstone of the ego, and black cloaks that are removed as the whirling begins, symbolizing rebirth into the truth. The spinning, performed to music featuring the ney (reed flute), is understood to represent the orbits of planets and the soul's journey toward union with the divine. The right palm is turned upward to receive God's blessing and the left downward to channel it to the earth.

History and recognition

The Mevlevi order was influential in Ottoman cultural and political life for centuries. In 1925, Sufi orders were banned in the Turkish Republic, and the sema survived largely as a cultural performance. In 2005, UNESCO proclaimed the Mevlevi Sema Ceremony a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity. The tomb and museum of Rumi in Konya remain a major pilgrimage and tourist site.
