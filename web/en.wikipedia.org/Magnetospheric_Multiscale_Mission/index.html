Magnetospheric Multiscale Mission

Operator: NASA
Launched: 13 March 2015 (UTC)
Launch vehicle: Atlas V 421
Spacecraft: Four identical observatories flying in a tetrahedral formation

The Magnetospheric Multiscale Mission (MMS) is a NASA robotic space mission to study Earth's magnetosphere, using four identical spacecraft flying in a tetrahedral (pyramid) formation in highly elliptical orbits around Earth.

Objectives

MMS investigates magnetic reconnection, the fundamental process in which magnetic field lines in plasma break and explosively reconnect, converting magnetic energy into particle energy. Reconnection drives geomagnetic storms, auroras, and solar flares, but its microphysics occurs on electron scales that earlier missions could not resolve.

Instruments and results

Each spacecraft carries 25 instruments measuring fields and plasmas at record time resolution — up to 8,190 samples per second for some sensors. Soon after launch, MMS made the first direct measurements of an electron diffusion region and observed the electron physics at the heart of reconnection, first on Earth's dayside magnetopause and later in the magnetotail. The formation's separations, as small as about 7 km, set a Guinness record for the closest flying formation of satellites.
