Naturalism (art)

Naturalism in art is the depiction of realistic objects in a natural setting. As a movement of the late 19th century, Naturalism developed out of Realism, seeking an almost photographic transcription of nature and everyday life, including its harsh and humble aspects, informed by contemporary science and direct observation. The term is also used more loosely for any art that aims at faithful representation rather than stylization.

Development

French painters such as Jules Bastien-Lepage became leading figures of Naturalism in the 1870s and 1880s, painting large-scale scenes of peasant life with meticulous detail. The movement spread internationally, influencing artists in Britain, Scandinavia, Russia, and the United States. It overlapped with plein air painting and, in literature, with the naturalist novels of Émile Zola, which applied quasi-scientific determinism to fiction.

Naturalism and Realism

Scholars debate how sharply Naturalism can be distinguished from Realism; generally, Naturalism is treated as an extension or intensification of Realist aims, with a stronger emphasis on observation, environment, and the unidealized depiction of the lower classes.
