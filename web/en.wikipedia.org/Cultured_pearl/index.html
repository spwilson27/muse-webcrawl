Cultured pearl

A cultured pearl is a pearl created by a pearl farmer under controlled conditions, by implanting a nucleus or graft tissue into a pearl oyster or mussel, which then deposits layers of nacre around it. Cultured pearls account for nearly all pearls sold in jewellery today, natural pearls having become exceedingly rare.

History

Experimentation with pearl culturing dates to early 20th-century Japan, where Mikimoto Kōkichi, with the work of Mise and Nishikawa, developed and patented the spherical pearl culturing technique. Akoya pearl farming in Japan made round cultured pearls commercially available from the 1920s, transforming the pearl market.

Types

Saltwater cultured pearls include Japanese and Chinese Akoya pearls, white and golden South Sea pearls from Australia, Indonesia and the Philippines, and black Tahitian pearls from French Polynesia. Freshwater cultured pearls, produced mainly in China in mussels such as Hyriopsis cumingii, are grown without a bead nucleus in most cases and come in a wide range of shapes and colours.

Production

In bead-nucleated farming, a technician inserts a shell bead together with a piece of donor mantle tissue into the gonad of an oyster. The mollusc coats the nucleus with nacre over a cultivation period of months to several years before harvest. Quality is judged by lustre, surface, shape, colour and size.
