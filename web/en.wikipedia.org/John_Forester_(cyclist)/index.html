John Forester (cyclist)

Born: 7 October 1929, London, England
Died: 14 April 2020 (aged 90), San Diego, California, US
Occupation: Engineer, cycling activist, author
Known for: Vehicular cycling; Effective Cycling (1976)

John Forester (7 October 1929 – 14 April 2020) was an English-American engineer, cycling activist, and author of Effective Cycling (1976). He was one of the best-known advocates of vehicular cycling — the practice of riding a bicycle on roads in accordance with the principles for driving vehicles — and a prominent critic of separated cycling infrastructure such as bike lanes and cycle tracks.

Early life

Forester was born in London, the son of the novelist C. S. Forester. He emigrated to the United States in the 1940s and trained as an engineer, working in industrial engineering and human factors. He settled in California, where his professional work included research on industrial lighting and human performance.

Cycling advocacy

Beginning in the 1970s, Forester became a leading figure in American cycling politics. Influenced by John Franklin and his own analysis of crash statistics, he argued that cyclists fare best when they act and are treated as drivers of vehicles, and that the majority of car-bicycle collisions arise from improper cyclist behaviour such as riding on pavements (sidewalks) or against traffic, rather than from overtaking motorists. His book Effective Cycling, first published by MIT Press in 1976, codified techniques of confident road cycling and went through many editions.

Forester served as a president of the League of American Wheelmen (now the League of American Bicyclists) and was involved in drafting California cycling legislation. His opposition to bikeways made him a controversial figure: supporters credited him with defending cyclists' right to the road, while critics charged that his stance impeded the growth of utility cycling by rejecting facilities that attract less confident riders.

Death

Forester died in San Diego, California, on 14 April 2020, aged 90.
