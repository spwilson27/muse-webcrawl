Aviation call signs

Aviation call signs are the identifiers used in radio communication between aircraft and air traffic control. They must be unambiguous, pronounceable and distinct from other traffic on the frequency.

Civil aviation

Airlines use a telephony designator assigned by ICAO followed by a flight number — for example "Speedbird" for British Airways, "Shamrock" for Aer Lingus, "Clipper" historically for Pan Am, and "Cactus" for the former America West/US Airways. General aviation aircraft usually use their registration marks, pronounced in the phonetic alphabet. If call signs are confusingly similar, controllers may change them temporarily, and some flights — such as those carrying the U.S. president (Air Force One) — use special designators.

Military aviation

Military call signs include mission-oriented tactical call signs assigned per sortie and personal nicknames given to aviators, a tradition especially strong in U.S. naval aviation. Units also use fixed call signs for ground stations and controllers; distress and urgency prefixes (Mayday and Pan-pan) take priority over ordinary call signs.
