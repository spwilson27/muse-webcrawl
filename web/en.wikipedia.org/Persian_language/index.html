Persian language

Persian (endonym: فارسی, Fārsī) is a Western Iranian language belonging to the Indo-Iranian branch of the Indo-European languages. It is the official language of Iran, where it is called Farsi; its standardised varieties in Afghanistan (Dari) and Tajikistan (Tajik) are mutually intelligible with it. Persian has well over 70 million native speakers and is a pluricentric language.

History

Persian descends from Old Persian, the language of the Achaemenid Empire, written in cuneiform. Middle Persian (Pahlavi) was the language of the Sasanian Empire. After the Muslim conquest of Persia in the 7th century, New Persian emerged by the 9th century, written in a modified Arabic script and enriched with a large Arabic vocabulary, while remaining grammatically Iranian. Classical Persian literature flourished under the Samanids and later dynasties, producing poets such as Rudaki, Ferdowsi, Rumi, Saadi and Hafez.

Grammar

Persian is a subject–object–verb language with no grammatical gender and no case marking on nouns. It uses the ezafe construction to link nouns and modifiers, and verbs are conjugated for tense, aspect, mood and person. Formal and informal registers differ mainly in vocabulary and some verb forms.

Vocabulary

Persian vocabulary is predominantly Iranian in origin, with a very large stratum of Arabic loanwords (especially in formal, religious and scientific registers), a smaller set of Turkic loans, and modern borrowings from French and English. Conversely, Persian has contributed many words to Arabic, Turkish, Urdu, Hindi and, through them and directly, to English — including "paradise", "bazaar", "caravan", "pajamas" and "shah".

Varieties

The three national standards — Iranian Persian, Dari and Tajik — differ modestly in pronunciation, vocabulary and script. Regional dialects include Tehrani (the basis of the Iranian standard), Isfahani, Shirazi and Mashhadi, alongside related languages such as Luri and Gilaki.
