History of mobile phones

The history of mobile phones covers mobile communication devices that connect to a telephone network by radio link. Early mobile telephony began with bulky car-mounted radiotelephones in the 1940s, progressed through the first handheld cellular phone demonstrated by Motorola in 1973, and expanded rapidly after the launch of the first commercial cellular networks in the early 1980s. By the 2000s the mobile phone had become the most widely adopted consumer electronic device in history, and the arrival of the smartphone after 2007 merged telephony with general-purpose computing.

Early mobile telephony

Mobile radio telephone systems predate cellular technology by decades. Bell System's Mobile Telephone Service began in St. Louis in 1946, offering a handful of radio channels shared across a city; calls were placed with the help of an operator and the equipment filled much of a car's trunk. Improved services such as IMTS (Improved Mobile Telephone Service, 1964) allowed direct dialling but still supported very few simultaneous users. Inventors at Bell Labs had described the cellular concept — dividing a service area into small "cells" that reuse the same frequencies — as early as 1947, but the electronics needed to hand calls between cells were not yet practical.

The first cellular phones

On 3 April 1973, Motorola engineer Martin Cooper placed the first handheld mobile phone call, to his rival Joel Engel at Bell Labs, using a prototype of the DynaTAC. Motorola invested roughly $100 million over a decade to develop the product. The commercial DynaTAC 8000X, approved by the FCC in 1983, cost $3,995, weighed about 1.1 kg, offered roughly 30 minutes of talk time and took around 10 hours to recharge. The first commercial cellular networks launched in Japan (NTT, 1979) and in the Nordic countries (NMT, 1981), followed by the AMPS system in the United States in 1983.

Second generation and mass adoption

Second-generation (2G) digital networks, led by the European GSM standard launched in Finland in 1991, brought better voice quality, encryption, and the Short Message Service (SMS); the first text message, "Merry Christmas", was sent in December 1992. Phones shrank dramatically: the Nokia 1011 (1992) was among the first mass-market GSM handsets, and models such as the Nokia 6110 and 3210 defined the late 1990s. Prepaid plans, interchangeable covers, and built-in games like Snake turned the mobile into a youth and mass-market product. By 2002, global subscriptions passed one billion.

Smartphones

Devices combining telephony with computing appeared in the 1990s — IBM's Simon (1994) is often called the first smartphone, and Nokia's Communicator series, BlackBerry email devices (from 1999), and Palm Treos built a business market. The landscape changed on 9 January 2007, when Steve Jobs announced the iPhone, whose multitouch interface and full web browser set the template for modern handsets; the App Store followed in 2008. Google's Android, first shipped on the HTC Dream in 2008, became the dominant platform by unit sales. Third- and fourth-generation networks (3G from 2001, LTE from 2009) made mobile internet access routine, and 5G networks began rolling out in 2019.

Impact

Mobile phones reshaped communication, commerce, and media: mobile money services such as M-Pesa (2007) brought banking to hundreds of millions, camera phones transformed photography and journalism, and by the mid-2010s most web traffic came from phones. Global mobile subscriptions exceeded the world's population in the 2010s, making the mobile phone the fastest-spreading technology in history.
