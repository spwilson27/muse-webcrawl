North Downs Way

[Image placeholder: North Downs Way]

The North Downs Way is a long-distance footpath in southern England, opened in 1978. It runs 153 miles (246 km) from Farnham in Surrey to Dover in Kent, along the ridge of chalk hills known as the North Downs, and is one of the fifteen National Trails in England and Wales.

Route

From Farnham the trail passes through Guildford, Dorking, Reigate, and the Surrey Hills, offering views over the Weald. In Kent it runs above Wye and Canterbury — where an alternative loop visits the cathedral city — before rejoining near Folkestone and finishing at Dover. Much of the route follows the ancient trackway associated with pilgrims travelling to the shrine of Thomas Becket at Canterbury, overlapping in places with the Pilgrims' Way.

Geology and landscape

The North Downs are a chalk escarpment formed from the remains of marine organisms in a prehistoric sea. The trail crosses chalk grassland rich in orchids and butterflies, ancient yew and box woodland, and several Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty (now National Landscapes).
