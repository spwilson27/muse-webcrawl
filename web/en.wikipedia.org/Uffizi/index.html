Uffizi

The Uffizi Galleries (Italian: Gallerie degli Uffizi) is a prominent art museum in the historic centre of Florence, Italy, and one of the most visited art museums in the world. The palace was begun in 1560 by Giorgio Vasari for Cosimo I de' Medici as offices (uffizi) for the Florentine magistrates, and its upper floor was progressively turned into a gallery for the Medici collections.

The collection contains masterpieces of the Italian Renaissance, including works by Giotto, Botticelli (such as The Birth of Venus and Primavera), Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, Raphael, Titian, and Caravaggio, alongside major holdings of ancient sculpture and works by Flemish, Dutch, and German masters.

Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici, the last of the Medici line, bequeathed the family collections to the city of Florence in 1737 on condition they never leave the city. The gallery opened to the public in 1769. The museum underwent a major expansion project, the Nuovi Uffizi, begun in the 1980s and largely completed in the 2010s–2020s, greatly increasing its exhibition space.
