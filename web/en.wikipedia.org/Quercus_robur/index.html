Quercus robur

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Quercus robur, the pedunculate oak, common oak, or English oak, is a species of flowering plant in the beech and oak family Fagaceae. It is native to most of Europe and western Asia, from the British Isles and Scandinavia east to the Caucasus. It is a large deciduous tree, commonly 20–40 metres tall, famous for its longevity: many specimens live for many centuries, and some over a thousand years.

Description

The leaves are lobed with very short stalks, while the acorns are borne on long peduncles — the feature that distinguishes it from the sessile oak (Quercus petraea). The tree is monoecious, flowering in spring with wind-pollinated catkins. Oaks support an exceptionally rich associated fauna: in Britain alone, several hundred insect species feed on Q. robur, and its acorns feed birds and mammals in autumn "mast" years.

Uses and significance

Oak timber is hard, strong, and durable, historically prized for shipbuilding, construction, furniture, and barrels for wine and spirits. The English oak is a national symbol of England, and famous individual trees — such as the Major Oak of Sherwood Forest — are landmarks. In forestry, Q. robur is planted across temperate regions worldwide, though it is affected by diseases including acute oak decline and oak processionary moth in parts of its range.
