Anxi Protectorate

Established640 AD (Tang dynasty)
SeatTurfan (Gaochang), later Kucha
RegionTarim Basin, Central Asia

The Anxi Protectorate, also known as the Protectorate General to Pacify the West, was a protectorate established by the Tang dynasty of China in 640 to administer and control the Tarim Basin region of Central Asia, in what is now Xinjiang.

History

After Tang forces conquered the oasis kingdom of Gaochang in 640, Emperor Taizong created the Anxi Protectorate to govern the Western Regions. Its seat was initially at Gaochang (Turfan) and was later moved to Kucha. The protectorate supervised the oasis city-states of the Tarim Basin and Tang garrisons along the northern Silk Road.

Administration

The protectorate general oversaw military colonies and local rulers who retained much of their internal authority while acknowledging Tang suzerainty. Garrisons known as the Four Garrisons of Anxi were stationed at Kucha, Kashgar, Khotan, and Karasahr (later Tokmak/Suyab) to secure the region.

Decline

Tang control weakened after the An Lushan Rebellion (755–763), when troops were recalled east. Tibetan forces progressively occupied the Tarim Basin in the late 8th century, and the protectorate effectively ceased to exist by about 790.
