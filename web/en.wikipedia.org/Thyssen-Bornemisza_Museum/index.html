The Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum (Spanish: Museo Nacional Thyssen-Bornemisza) is an art museum in Madrid, Spain, located near the Prado Museum on the city's main boulevard, the Paseo del Prado. Together with the Prado and the Museo Reina Sofía, it forms Madrid's “Golden Triangle of Art”.

History

The collection was begun in the 1920s and 1930s by Baron Heinrich Thyssen-Bornemisza (1875–1947) and greatly expanded by his son, Baron Hans Heinrich Thyssen-Bornemisza (1921–2002), who assembled one of the largest private art collections in the world. After years of negotiation, the collection was moved from the Villa Favorita in Lugano, Switzerland, to Madrid, and the museum opened in the renovated Villahermosa Palace in 1992. In 1993 the Spanish government purchased the core collection of 775 works for the nation. In 2004 an extension opened housing the Carmen Thyssen-Bornemisza collection on loan, and in 2017 the museum adopted the name Museo Nacional Thyssen-Bornemisza.

Collection

The museum is noted for the breadth of its holdings, filling gaps in the collections of its neighbours: Italian primitives, early Netherlandish painting, English, Dutch and German art, and twentieth-century movements including Impressionism, Post-Impressionism, German Expressionism, Russian avant-garde, and American painting of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Highlights include works by Jan van Eyck, Hans Holbein the Younger (Portrait of Henry VIII), Caravaggio (Saint Catherine of Alexandria), Van Gogh, Gauguin, Kandinsky, and Edward Hopper (Hotel Room).

The building

The Villahermosa Palace, a neoclassical building dating from the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, was remodelled by architect Rafael Moneo for the museum's opening. The permanent collection is arranged chronologically from the top floor downward, allowing visitors to follow the history of Western painting from the thirteenth century to the present.
