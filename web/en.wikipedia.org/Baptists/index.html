Baptists

Believer's baptism by immersion is a defining Baptist practice.

Baptists are a denomination of Protestant Christianity distinguished by baptizing only professing Christian believers (believer's baptism) and doing so by complete immersion. Baptists emerged from the English Separatist movement in the early seventeenth century, with the first Baptist church founded in Amsterdam in 1609 under John Smyth, and the movement was established in England by Thomas Helwys and others from 1611–1612.

Beliefs and practices

Key Baptist distinctives include the authority of the Bible alone (sola scriptura), salvation by faith alone, the priesthood of all believers, congregational church government with the autonomy of the local church, and religious liberty with separation of church and state. Baptist churches are generally not governed by any hierarchy above the local congregation, though congregations cooperate in associations and conventions such as the Southern Baptist Convention — the largest Baptist body and the largest Protestant denomination in the United States — and the Baptist World Alliance, founded in 1905.

History and distribution

Baptists spread in colonial America under figures such as Roger Williams, who founded the first Baptist church in America at Providence, Rhode Island, in 1638. Missionary work carried the movement worldwide; today there are tens of millions of Baptists, with the largest populations in the United States, and significant communities in India, Nigeria, Brazil, and Myanmar.
