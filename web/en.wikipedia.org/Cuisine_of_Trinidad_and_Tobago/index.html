Cuisine of Trinidad and Tobago

The cuisine of Trinidad and Tobago is a fusion of African, Indian, European, Chinese, Amerindian, and Middle Eastern influences, reflecting the islands' diverse population and colonial history. It is known for bold seasoning, curries, and street food.

Signature dishes

Well-known dishes include doubles — two pieces of fried flatbread (bara) filled with curried chickpeas (channa) and chutneys; roti served with curried goat, chicken, or vegetables; callaloo, a soup of dasheen leaves and okra; pelau, a one-pot rice dish with pigeon peas and meat caramelized in burnt sugar; and bake and shark, a fried shark sandwich sold at Maracas Bay. Crab and dumplings is a Tobago speciality.

Influences

East Indian indentured labourers brought curries, roti, and sweets such as jalebi and kurma. African traditions contributed ground provision cookery and callaloo, while Chinese, Syrian-Lebanese, and Creole French communities added their own dishes and techniques. Hot pepper sauce, especially made with Scotch bonnet and the local Moruga scorpion pepper, accompanies most meals.

Street food and festivals

Street vendors sell aloo pies, pholourie, souse, and corn soup, especially during Carnival. Christmas brings pastelles (steamed cornmeal parcels similar to tamales), black cake, and sorrel drink.
