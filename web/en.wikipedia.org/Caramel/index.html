Caramel

Caramel is a medium to dark-orange confectionery product made by heating a variety of sugars. The process of caramelization involves slowly heating sugar to around 170 °C (340 °F), breaking down the molecules and forming new compounds with a characteristic nutty flavor and brown color.

Production and uses

Caramel can be cooked as a dry sugar melt or as a wet syrup with water. Adding cream, butter, and salt produces soft caramel candies, sauces, and fillings such as those in caramel apples, crème caramel, crème brûlée toppings, pralines, and dulce de leche (made by slowly heating sweetened milk). Caramel color, produced by controlled heat treatment of sugars, is one of the most widely used food colorings, found in cola and many other products.

Chemistry

Caramelization is a form of pyrolysis distinct from the Maillard reaction, since it involves sugars alone. It generates hundreds of flavor compounds, including diacetyl (buttery) and furans (nutty), and brown polymers called caramelans, caramelens, and caramelins.
