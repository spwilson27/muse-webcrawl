Twin Earth

Twin Earth is a thought experiment proposed by the philosopher Hilary Putnam in his 1975 paper "The Meaning of 'Meaning'". It is the best-known argument for semantic externalism: the view that the meanings of words and the contents of thoughts are not determined solely by what is in a speaker's head, but depend partly on the speaker's environment and linguistic community.

The thought experiment

Suppose there is a planet, Twin Earth, identical to Earth in every observable respect except that the liquid called "water" there is not H2O but a chemically different substance, XYZ, which behaves like water at normal temperatures and pressures. An Earth speaker, Oscar, and his molecule-for-molecule duplicate on Twin Earth both use the word "water". When the Earth Oscar says "water", he refers to H2O; when Twin Oscar says "water", he refers to XYZ. Since the two are internally identical yet mean different things, Putnam concludes that "meanings just ain't in the head."

Significance

Twin Earth is central to debates in the philosophy of language and philosophy of mind about natural-kind terms, reference, and mental content. Putnam combined externalism with a "division of linguistic labor": ordinary speakers refer successfully to substances like gold or water partly by deferring to experts who can identify them.
