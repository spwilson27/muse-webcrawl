Nonprofit organization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A nonprofit organization (NPO), also known as a non-business entity or not-for-profit organization, is an organization dedicated to furthering a particular social cause or advocating for a shared point of view, in which any surplus revenue is used to further the organization's mission rather than being distributed to owners or shareholders as profit. Nonprofits include charities, foundations, trade unions, professional associations, religious organizations, universities, hospitals, museums, and community groups, and they form a major part of the "third sector" alongside government and business.

Legal structure and taxation

The legal forms vary by country: in the United States, nonprofits are usually corporations formed under state law, and many qualify for exemption from federal income tax under section 501(c) of the Internal Revenue Code — most famously 501(c)(3) organizations (charitable, educational, religious, and scientific), whose donors may deduct contributions. In the United Kingdom, common forms are the charitable company, the charitable incorporated organisation (CIO), and the community interest company. Nonprofits are governed by a board of directors or trustees who owe fiduciary duties, and in most jurisdictions the organization may employ staff and earn income, provided profits are not privately distributed (the "non-distribution constraint").

Management and funding

Funding comes from donations, grants from foundations and governments, membership dues, fees for services, and endowment income. Nonprofits rely heavily on volunteers and on public trust, and are subject to reporting requirements, public disclosure of finances (such as Form 990 in the US), and oversight by charity regulators and attorneys general. Challenges in the sector include fundraising costs, measurement of social impact, governance failures, and competition for donor attention.

Scale

Millions of nonprofit organizations exist worldwide; in the United States alone there are roughly 1.5–2 million registered nonprofits, employing about a tenth of the private workforce, with hospitals and universities among the largest. International NGOs such as the Red Cross, Médecins Sans Frontières, and Oxfam operate across borders in relief and development.
