Ashmolean Museum

The Ashmolean Museum of Art and Archaeology on Beaumont Street in Oxford, England, is Britain's first public museum. Its first building was constructed from 1678 to 1683 to house the cabinet of curiosities that Elias Ashmole gave to the University of Oxford in 1677. It is one of the four GLAM museums of the University, alongside the Bodleian Libraries, the History of Science Museum, and the Museum of Natural History.

History

Ashmole's collection was based substantially on the "Ark" assembled by John Tradescant the elder and the younger, and included coins, books, geological specimens, and curiosities such as the stuffed dodo. The present neoclassical building on Beaumont Street was designed by Charles Cockerell and opened in 1845; the original building now houses the History of Science Museum.

Collections

The museum holds extensive collections of Greek, Roman, Egyptian and Near Eastern antiquities, including the Parian Marble, Minoan pottery, and the Alfred Jewel, as well as European art with works by Raphael, Michelangelo, Turner, and Picasso, and a major collection of Pre-Raphaelite works.

Redevelopment

A major redevelopment designed by Rick Mather Architects, completed in 2009, doubled the display space, adding six new floors within the existing site and new conservation studios and an education centre.
