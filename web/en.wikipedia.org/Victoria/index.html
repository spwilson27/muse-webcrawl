Victoria

Victoria most commonly refers to Queen Victoria (1819–1901), Queen of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland from 1837 to 1901 and Empress of India, whose reign — the Victorian era — saw the British Empire reach its greatest extent. The name, from the Latin word for "victory", has been given to many places, people and things around the world.

Places

Notable places named Victoria include the Australian state of Victoria, whose capital is Melbourne; Victoria, British Columbia, the capital of the Canadian province; Victoria, Seychelles, the capital of the island republic; Victoria Falls on the Zambezi River between Zambia and Zimbabwe; and Lake Victoria, the largest lake in Africa, bordered by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania. Hong Kong's Victoria Harbour and Victoria Peak also bear the queen's name.

Other uses

Victoria is also a female given name, the Roman goddess of victory (identified with the Greek Nike), a genus of water lilies (Victoria amazonica), and the name of numerous ships, railway stations, theatres and businesses worldwide.
