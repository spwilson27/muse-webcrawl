Ave Regina caelorum ("Hail, Queen of the heavens") is one of the four seasonal Marian antiphons of the Roman Catholic Church, sung at the end of Compline (Night Prayer). It is traditionally sung from the Feast of the Presentation (2 February or the preceding Sunday) until Holy Week.

Text and use

The antiphon greets Mary as Queen of heaven and mistress of the angels, as the root and gate from whom light came into the world, and asks her to rejoice and intercede with Christ. Like the other Marian antiphons—the Salve Regina, Alma Redemptoris Mater, and Regina caeli—it closes the day's final office.

Musical settings

The antiphon has been set to music by many composers across the centuries, including Guillaume Du Fay, Tomás Luis de Victoria, and Marc-Antoine Charpentier, and it exists in a beloved chant form sung in monasteries and parish churches during Lent.
