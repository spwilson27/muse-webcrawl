Bertrand's box paradox

Bertrand's box paradox is a paradox of elementary probability theory, first posed by Joseph Bertrand in his 1889 work Calcul des probabilités. It illustrates how intuition about conditional probability can fail when the sampling procedure is not taken into account.

The problem

There are three boxes: one containing two gold coins, one containing two silver coins, and one containing one gold and one silver coin. A box is chosen at random and a coin drawn from it proves to be gold. What is the probability that the other coin in the same box is also gold?

Solution

The intuitively tempting answer of 1/2 is wrong. Because there are three equally likely ways to draw a gold coin and two of them belong to the gold–gold box, the correct probability is 2/3. The paradox is closely related to the Monty Hall problem.
