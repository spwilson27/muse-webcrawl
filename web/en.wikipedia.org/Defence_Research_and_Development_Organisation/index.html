The Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) is the premier agency under the Department of Defence Research and Development in the Ministry of Defence of the Government of India, charged with military research and development. Headquartered in New Delhi, it was formed in 1958 by merging the Technical Development Establishment and the Directorate of Technical Development and Production with the Defence Science Organisation.

Organisation

The DRDO operates a network of around fifty laboratories organised into technology clusters covering aeronautics, armaments, electronics, combat vehicles, missiles, naval systems, life sciences, and advanced computing. It employs roughly 30,000 people, of whom about 5,000 are scientists. The organisation is headed by the Secretary, Department of Defence R&D, who also serves as scientific adviser to the Raksha Mantri (Defence Minister).

Programmes

The DRDO's best-known programme is the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, launched in 1983 under A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, which produced the Prithvi and Agni ballistic missiles, the Akash surface-to-air missile, the Nag anti-tank missile, and the Trishul system. Other major projects include the Arjun main battle tank, the Tejas light combat aircraft (developed with the Aeronautical Development Agency), the BrahMos supersonic cruise missile (a joint venture with Russia), the INS Arihant nuclear submarine's systems, airborne early-warning aircraft, and the Pinaka multi-barrel rocket launcher. The agency also develops radars, electronic warfare suites, unmanned aerial vehicles, and life-support systems for soldiers.

Criticism and reform

The DRDO has been criticised by parliamentary committees and the Comptroller and Auditor General for cost and time overruns on projects such as the Tejas and the Kaveri engine, and India continues to import a large share of its defence equipment. Successive governments have announced reforms, including greater participation by private industry and restructuring of laboratories, to raise indigenous content.
