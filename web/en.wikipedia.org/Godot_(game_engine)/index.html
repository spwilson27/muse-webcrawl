Godot (game engine)

The Godot editor organizes games as trees of scenes and nodes.

Godot (pronounced guh-DOH) is a cross-platform, free and open-source game engine released under the permissive MIT license. It provides a comprehensive set of tools for creating 2D and 3D video games from a unified editor, and can export projects to desktop, mobile, web, and console platforms. The engine's development is community-driven and coordinated by the Godot Foundation and the Software Freedom Conservancy.

History

Godot was developed by Argentine programmers Juan Linietsky and Ariel Manzur beginning in 2007 for use by game studios in Latin America, and was released as open source in 2014. Version 3.0 (2018) rewrote much of the 3D renderer, and Godot 4.0 (2023) introduced a new Vulkan-based rendering engine, a substantially improved scripting experience, and many other changes. The engine's popularity surged in 2023 when many developers, protesting pricing changes at the commercial engine Unity, migrated to or adopted Godot.

Design

Games in Godot are built from scenes — trees of nodes with attached behaviors — that can be saved, instanced, and nested. Scripting is done primarily in GDScript, a Python-like language designed for the engine, with official support also for C# and, through the GDExtension API, C++ and other languages. The editor runs on the engine itself and is available for Windows, macOS, and Linux.
