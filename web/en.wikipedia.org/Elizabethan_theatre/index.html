Elizabethan theatre

Elizabethan theatre refers to the theatre of England between the Reformation and the closing of the theatres in 1642. It is sometimes called English Renaissance theatre. Its most famous exponent is William Shakespeare, but the period also produced Christopher Marlowe, Ben Jonson, Thomas Kyd and John Webster.

Public playhouses were open-air amphitheatres, such as the Theatre, the Curtain and the Globe, where audiences stood in the yard or sat in covered galleries. Plays were performed in the afternoon by daylight, with minimal scenery and elaborate costumes. All roles, including female roles, were played by men and boys.

The theatre flourished under royal patronage through acting companies such as the Lord Chamberlain's Men, later the King's Men, and the Admiral's Men. Its repertoire included histories, comedies, tragedies and the revenge tragedy, a genre pioneered by Kyd's The Spanish Tragedy.
