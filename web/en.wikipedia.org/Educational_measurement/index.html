Educational measurement

Educational measurement refers to the use of educational assessments and the analysis of data (such as scores obtained from educational assessments) to infer the abilities and proficiencies of students. Approaches overlap with those in psychometrics. Educational measurement involves assigning numbers to traits such as achievement, interest, attitudes, aptitudes, intelligence, and performance.

Aims and scope

The aim of theory and practice in educational measurement is typically to measure abilities and levels of attainment of students in areas such as reading, writing, mathematics, science, and other curriculum subjects. Traditionally, attention has focused on whether assessments are reliable and valid — that is, whether they measure consistently and whether they measure what they claim to measure — and on the inferences drawn from scores for placement, certification, admissions, and accountability.

Theoretical approaches

The classical approach, classical test theory, models an observed score as a true score plus error, and underlies concepts such as reliability coefficients and the standard error of measurement. Modern test theory, particularly item response theory (IRT), models the probability of a response to an item as a function of a latent trait and item parameters, enabling item banking, adaptive testing, test equating, and differential item functioning analyses. The Rasch model is a widely used one-parameter IRT model with a distinct measurement philosophy emphasizing invariant comparison.

Principles and practice

Measurement specialists develop test specifications, write and review items, assemble test forms, set performance standards (standard setting, e.g., the Angoff method), scale and equate scores across administrations, and evaluate validity arguments in the tradition of Samuel Messick, who treated validity as a unified judgment about the interpretation and social consequences of test use. The Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing, issued jointly by AERA, APA, and NCME, codify professional practice.

Applications

Educational measurement underpins large-scale programmes such as NAEP in the United States, international assessments like PISA and TIMSS, university admissions tests, licensure examinations, and classroom assessment. Contemporary developments include computer-adaptive testing, automated scoring, learning analytics, and evidence-centered design.
