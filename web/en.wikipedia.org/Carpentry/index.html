Carpentry

Carpentry is a skilled trade in which the primary work performed is the cutting, shaping, and installation of building materials during the construction of buildings, ships, timber bridges, and concrete formwork. Carpenters traditionally work with wood but also use other materials, and the trade is among the oldest in the world.

Types

Rough carpentry covers structural framing — studs, joists, rafters, and formwork — where strength matters more than appearance. Finish carpentry covers the visible final elements: trim, mouldings, doors, stairs, and fitted furniture, demanding precise work. Other specialties include cabinetmaking, ship's carpentry, and scenic carpentry for stage and film sets.

Tools

Traditional hand tools include the hammer, saw, chisel, plane, square, and tape measure; modern carpentry adds power tools such as circular saws, nail guns, routers, and laser levels.

Training

Carpenters commonly train through apprenticeships combining on-the-job work with technical instruction, often lasting three to four years. In many countries the trade is organised through unions and guilds with roots in medieval craft guilds; St Joseph, the foster father of Jesus, is traditionally depicted as a carpenter.
