Max Fleischer (born Majer Fleischer; July 19, 1883 – September 11, 1972) was an American animator, inventor, film director and producer, and a pioneer in the development of the animated cartoon. With his brother Dave Fleischer he founded Fleischer Studios, which produced such characters as Betty Boop, Koko the Clown and Popeye the Sailor, and made the first Superman animated films.

Early life and inventions

Fleischer was born in Kraków, then in Austria-Hungary, and emigrated with his family to New York City as a small child. While working as an editor at Popular Science magazine, he devised the rotoscope (patented in 1917), a device that projected live-action film frame by frame onto a drawing board so animators could trace realistic motion. His test film of his brother Dave dressed as a clown became Koko the Clown, star of the Out of the Inkwell series.

Fleischer Studios

The Fleischer studio became Walt Disney's chief rival in the 1930s, distributing through Paramount Pictures. Its output included Betty Boop, the Popeye cartoons (which out-grossed Disney's Mickey Mouse shorts in some years), the feature films Gulliver's Travels (1939) and Mr. Bug Goes to Town (1941), and the technically acclaimed Superman cartoons of 1941–42. Financial difficulties and disputes with Paramount led to the studio's takeover in 1942; it was reorganized as Famous Studios, and Max Fleischer spent his later years working for other producers, including the Jam Handy Organization and Bray Studios.

Legacy

Fleischer's studio pioneered or popularized numerous techniques — rotoscoping, "follow the bouncing ball" sing-along films, and three-dimensional setback backgrounds — and its urban, surreal style remains a major influence on animation. His son Richard Fleischer became a noted film director.
