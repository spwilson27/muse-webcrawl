Dnieper–Carpathian Offensive

The Dnieper–Carpathian Offensive was a major strategic offensive operation of the Red Army against German forces in Ukraine during the Second World War, fought from 24 December 1943 to 17 April 1944. It was one of the largest operations of the war, involving around 2.6 million Soviet troops of the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th Ukrainian Fronts.

Course

The offensive liberated the Right-Bank Ukraine west of the Dnieper River. Its phases included the Zhitomir–Berdichev and Korsun–Shevchenkovsky operations — the latter trapping and largely destroying a German pocket in February 1944 — and advances through the spring mud (rasputitsa) toward the Dniester and the Carpathian foothills.

Outcome

By April 1944 Soviet forces had reached the borders of Romania and pre-war Poland and cut off German forces in Crimea's overland supply, setting the stage for the offensives of summer 1944. German Army Group South suffered heavy losses and was split, part of it being redesignated Army Group North Ukraine.
