Zeebrugge

Zeebrugge (Dutch: Zeebrugge, French: Zeebruges) is a village on the Belgian North Sea coast and a seaport subdivision of the city of Bruges, in the province of West Flanders. It was built at the end of the 19th century as the modern sea port of Bruges, connected to the historic inland city by the Baudouin Canal (Boudewijnkanaal), about 10 km long. Zeebrugge is one of Europe's most important roll-on/roll-off and liquefied natural gas (LNG) ports and a major fishing and ferry harbour; in 2022 its port authority merged with that of Antwerp to form the Port of Antwerp-Bruges.

History

The artificial harbour was inaugurated in 1907. During the First World War, German forces used Zeebrugge and Bruges as a base for U-boats and torpedo boats threatening the English Channel. On 23 April 1918 the Royal Navy mounted the famous Zeebrugge Raid, an audacious attempt to block the canal by sinking concrete-filled ships in its mouth; the raid partially succeeded and became celebrated in Britain, with eight Victoria Crosses awarded. The port was fought over again in 1944 during the liberation of Belgium.

The port today

Zeebrugge handles tens of millions of tonnes of cargo a year: it is one of the world's leading ports for the import and export of new cars, a major hub for roll-on/roll-off freight to the United Kingdom, and hosts an LNG terminal that is a significant entry point for gas into Europe. Ferry services have historically linked Zeebrugge with Hull and other British ports. The fishing port is among Belgium's largest, with an important fish auction. The village also has a seaside resort district with a wide sandy beach.

Herald of Free Enterprise

On 6 March 1987, the roll-on/roll-off ferry Herald of Free Enterprise capsized just outside the harbour after sailing with her bow doors open; 193 passengers and crew died, in one of the worst peacetime British maritime disasters.
