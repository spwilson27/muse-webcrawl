Lancelot

Sir Lancelot du Lac
Occupation: Knight of the Round Table
Parents: King Ban, Queen Elaine
Children: Galahad

Sir Lancelot du Lac is a character in the Arthurian legend, first appearing in the works of the 12th-century French poet Chrétien de Troyes. He is typically depicted as King Arthur's closest companion and one of the greatest Knights of the Round Table, and also as the lover of Queen Guinevere, an adulterous affair that ultimately helps bring about the fall of Camelot.

Origins

Lancelot first appears in Chrétien's poem Erec and Enide and takes a leading role in The Knight of the Cart (Lancelot, le Chevalier de la Charrette), where he rescues Guinevere from the villain Meleagant. The prose Lancelot, part of the Vulgate Cycle, greatly expanded his story: the son of King Ban of Benoic, he is raised by the Lady of the Lake — hence "du Lac".

The Grail quest

Lancelot's sin of adultery renders him unworthy to achieve the Holy Grail; in the Queste del Saint Graal the Grail is won instead by his son Galahad, together with Perceval and Bors. Lancelot is granted only a partial vision of the Grail.

Downfall of the Round Table

After the affair with Guinevere is exposed by Mordred and Agravain, Lancelot's rescue of the queen from execution leads to civil war. Returning from exile, he arrives too late to save Arthur at the Battle of Camlann, and he retires to a hermitage, dying in penitence. His story was retold in Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur and in many modern works.
