Muddle Instead of Music

"Muddle Instead of Music: On the Opera Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District" (Russian: Сумбур вместо музыки — Об опере «Леди Макбет Мценского уезда») is an editorial denouncing Dmitri Shostakovich's opera Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk that appeared in the Soviet newspaper Pravda on 28 January 1936. The anonymous article, widely believed to reflect or involve the views of Joseph Stalin, is commonly taken to mark the beginning of the campaign of Socialist realism enforcement against "formalism" in Soviet music.

The Pravda editorial of 28 January 1936

Background

Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk, premiered in 1934, was initially a great success in the Soviet Union and abroad, and was performed nearly 200 times in two seasons. Stalin attended a performance at a branch of the Bolshoi Theatre on 26 January 1936 and left before the end, reportedly displeased.

The article

The editorial condemned the opera as deliberately dissonant, "muddle instead of music", bourgeois and formalist, with a "ticking, squealing, growling" score and crude naturalism. The opera was immediately withdrawn. Shostakovich's subsequent works came under intense scrutiny, and his Fourth Symphony was withdrawn before its premiere; his Fifth Symphony (1937), subtitled in the press "A Soviet artist's creative response to just criticism", restored him to official favor.
