Agroforestry

Alley cropping: annual crops grown between rows of trees, a classic agroforestry arrangement.

Agroforestry (or agro-sylviculture) is a land use management system in which combinations of trees and shrubs are grown around or among crops or pastureland. It combines agricultural and forestry techniques to create more diverse, productive, profitable, healthy, and sustainable land-use systems. By integrating woody perennials into farmland, agroforestry can increase biodiversity, reduce erosion, improve water cycling and store carbon in biomass and soils.

Classification

Agroforestry systems are commonly classified by the components involved: silvoarable systems combine trees with crops; silvopastoral systems combine trees with livestock grazing; and agrosilvopastoral systems combine all three. Structural arrangements include alley cropping, windbreaks and shelterbelts, riparian buffer strips, forest farming (cultivating high-value crops under a forest canopy), and homegardens — multi-storey plantings of trees, shrubs and herbs around dwellings that are widespread in the tropics.

Benefits

Trees in farm systems provide shade, fodder, fruit, timber and fuelwood; their roots can capture nutrients leached below the crop rooting zone and return them as leaf litter. Nitrogen-fixing trees such as Faidherbia albida in Africa and Leucaena species can fertilise associated crops. Agroforestry is promoted as a climate adaptation and mitigation strategy: it sequesters carbon, moderates microclimates, and contributes to carbon sequestration goals while diversifying farm income. It can also reduce pressure on natural forests and help counter air pollution locally by trapping particulates.

History

Tree–crop combinations are ancient: shifting cultivation systems such as the taungya method (named in 19th-century Burma) planted trees with food crops, and Indigenous peoples worldwide managed forest gardens for millennia. The modern scientific discipline was formalised in the 1970s, with the founding of the International Council for Research in Agroforestry (ICRAF, now World Agroforestry) in 1978 in Nairobi, Kenya.
