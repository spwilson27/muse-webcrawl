Effie Gray

Born: Euphemia Chalmers Gray, 7 May 1828, Perth, Scotland

Died: 23 December 1897 (aged 69), Perth, Scotland

Spouses: John Ruskin (m. 1848; annulled 1854); John Everett Millais (m. 1855; died 1896)

Children: 8, including John Guille Millais

Euphemia "Effie" Gray (7 May 1828 – 23 December 1897) was a Scottish artist and social figure of the Victorian era. She is best known as the wife successively of the art critic John Ruskin and of his protégé, the Pre-Raphaelite painter John Everett Millais. Her first marriage was annulled on the grounds that it was never consummated — one of the great scandals of nineteenth-century Britain — and the story of the Ruskin–Gray–Millais love triangle has been retold in plays, operas, and films.

Early life

Effie Gray was born in Perth, the daughter of George Gray, a lawyer and businessman, and Sophia Margaret Jameson. She grew up at Bowerswell House, which had also been the childhood home of John Ruskin's father. She first met Ruskin as a girl; he wrote the fairy tale The King of the Golden River for her when she was twelve.

Marriage to Ruskin

She married Ruskin in 1848 and the couple lived in London, travelling in Britain and Italy. The marriage was unhappy from the start: Ruskin repeatedly postponed consummation, and Effie felt isolated and neglected. In 1853, while the couple stayed in Scotland with the young painter John Everett Millais — who used Effie as the model for his painting The Order of Release, 1746 — she and Millais fell in love. With her parents' support, Effie sued for annulment, granted in 1854 on the ground of Ruskin's "incurable impotency", an allegation Ruskin disputed.

Marriage to Millais and later life

Effie married Millais in 1855 and the marriage was happy and productive; they had eight children. She managed the household and her husband's career effectively, sat for several of his paintings, and returned to society, though Queen Victoria would receive her only after Millais's deathbed baronetcy prompted royal relenting. She published a book on art, The Choice of a Model. Effie died in Perth on 23 December 1897, a year after her husband, and was buried in Kinnoull churchyard.

In popular culture

The story has been dramatised repeatedly, notably in the play The Countess (1995) by Gregory Murphy, Emma Thompson's film Effie Gray (2014), and the BBC radio and television dramas about the Pre-Raphaelites, including Desperate Romantics (2009).
