Keystone (architecture)

A decorated keystone at the crown of a masonry arch.

A keystone (also capstone) is the wedge-shaped stone at the apex of a masonry arch or, typically, ribbed vault. It is the final piece placed during construction and locks all the stones into position, allowing the arch or vault to bear weight. The term is also used metaphorically for the central or most important element of something.

Function

An arch is built over temporary timber centering. The keystone is driven into the crown last; its wedge shape converts the outward thrust of the arch into compression among the voussoirs (the arch stones), so the finished arch is self-supporting and often stronger under load. Strictly, all the voussoirs share the load, but the keystone's position made it symbolically central, and it is frequently carved or enlarged as a decorative feature.

History and ornament

True arches with keystones appear in ancient Roman engineering and in earlier Mesopotamian and Egyptian vaulting. In classical and Renaissance architecture, keystones were often embellished with scrolls, masks, or coats of arms. Pennsylvania is nicknamed the "Keystone State" in part because of its central position among the original colonies, with the keystone adopted as a state emblem.
