Bandi Chhor Divas

Bandi Chhor Divas (Punjabi: ਬੰਦੀ ਛੋੜ ਦਿਵਸ, "Day of Liberation") is a Sikh festival commemorating the release of Guru Hargobind, the sixth Sikh Guru, from imprisonment in Gwalior Fort by the Mughal emperor Jahangir in 1619. According to tradition, the Guru refused freedom unless the 52 Hindu princes imprisoned with him were also released; the emperor agreed that as many princes as could hold onto the Guru's cloak could leave, and a special cloak with 52 tassels allowed all of them to walk free.

History

Guru Hargobind had been detained in Gwalior Fort on the orders of Jahangir. When the emperor, influenced by courtiers and Sufi saints such as Mian Mir, ordered his release on the occasion of Diwali in 1619, the Guru insisted on the release of the 52 rajas held with him. His return to Amritsar was celebrated with lamps lit across the city, and the Golden Temple (Harmandir Sahib) is illuminated for the festival to this day.

Celebration

Bandi Chhor Divas falls on the same day as the Hindu festival of Diwali, the new-moon night of Kartik. Sikhs light divas (oil lamps), decorate gurdwaras, share sweets and watch fireworks, particularly at Amritsar, where the Golden Temple complex is spectacularly lit. The festival emphasises freedom, justice and standing up for the oppressed.
