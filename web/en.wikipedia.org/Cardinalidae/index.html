Cardinalidae

A male northern cardinal (Cardinalis cardinalis) (placeholder)

The Cardinalidae are a family of New World passerine birds, sometimes called the cardinal-grosbeaks or cardinal-buntings. It includes the cardinals, several grosbeaks, buntings, and allies — around fifty species in roughly a dozen genera, distributed throughout North and South America, from southern Canada to northern Argentina. Members are robust, seed-eating songbirds with stout, often conical bills adapted for cracking seeds, strong legs, and frequently bright plumage and crests.

Characteristics

Cardinalids range from about 12 cm (the small buntings) to over 25 cm (the larger grosbeaks and saltators formerly placed here). Many show strong sexual dimorphism: the male northern cardinal is brilliant red while the female is olive-brown. They are noted songbirds, and in cardinals both sexes sing — unusual among temperate songbirds. Diets centre on seeds, supplemented by fruit and insects, especially when feeding nestlings.

Taxonomy

The family's limits have shifted repeatedly with molecular studies. Genera now commonly included include Cardinalis (cardinals), Piranga (the "tanagers" such as the scarlet and summer tanagers, transferred from Thraupidae), Passerina (the North American buntings, including the indigo and painted buntings), Pheucticus (grosbeaks), Spiza (dickcissel), and Granatellus (chats). The northern cardinal (Cardinalis cardinalis) gives the family its name and is the state bird of seven US states.
