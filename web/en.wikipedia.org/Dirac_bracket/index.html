Dirac bracket

The Dirac bracket is a generalisation of the Poisson bracket developed by Paul Dirac to treat classical systems with constraints, as a prelude to canonical quantisation. It modifies the Poisson bracket so that it is consistent with the constraint equations holding at all times.

Motivation

In Hamiltonian mechanics, systems described by singular Lagrangians — including gauge theories — possess constraints linking coordinates and momenta. Simply imposing the constraints inside ordinary Poisson brackets leads to inconsistencies, because the constraints themselves may not have vanishing brackets with each other.

Definition

For second-class constraints, Dirac defined a new bracket by subtracting terms involving the Poisson brackets of the observables with the constraints, weighted by the inverse of the constraint bracket matrix. With the Dirac bracket, constraints may be set to zero before evaluating brackets, and the bracket satisfies antisymmetry, linearity, the Leibniz rule and the Jacobi identity.

Applications

Dirac brackets are standard tools in the quantisation of gauge theories, string theory and constrained mechanical systems.
