Sailendra dynasty

The Sailendra dynasty (Sanskrit: शैलेन्द्र, "Lord of the Mountain") was a prominent Indonesian dynasty that emerged in 8th-century central Java. Renowned patrons of Mahayana Buddhism, the Sailendras built some of Southeast Asia's greatest monuments, most famously the great stupa of Borobudur.

History

The Sailendras rose alongside — and at times intermarried and competed with — the Hindu Sanjaya dynasty within the Mataram Kingdom. Inscriptions such as the Kalasan (778 CE) and Kelurak (782 CE) inscriptions record their temple foundations. The dynasty's maritime connections extended across Southeast Asia; Sailendra-linked rulers are associated with the thalassocratic empire of Srivijaya in Sumatra, and Balaputradewa, a Sailendra prince, became a king of Srivijaya in the 9th century.

Decline and legacy

By the mid-9th century, Sailendra power in Java waned, particularly after the marriage alliance and dynastic struggles involving the Sanjaya king Pikatan and the Sailendra princess Pramodhawardhani, who nevertheless sponsored Buddhist temples including Plaosan. The dynasty's enduring legacy is its architecture: Borobudur remains the world's largest Buddhist temple. See also Borobudur and Mataram Kingdom.
