Tretinoin

Tretinoin
Also: all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA)
Use: acne, acute promyelocytic leukaemia

Tretinoin, also known as all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA), is a medication used for the treatment of acne and acute promyelocytic leukaemia. For acne, it is applied to the skin as a cream, gel or ointment. For leukaemia, it is taken by mouth for up to three months.

Tretinoin is a naturally occurring derivative of vitamin A (retinol). It works by binding to retinoic acid receptors, altering gene expression, normalising keratinisation in the skin and, in leukaemia, inducing differentiation of promyelocytes into mature neutrophils.

Medical uses

Topical tretinoin is a first-line treatment for comedonal and inflammatory acne and is also used off-label for photoaging. Oral tretinoin, combined with arsenic trioxide or chemotherapy, produces high remission rates in acute promyelocytic leukaemia.

Side effects

Common side effects when applied to the skin include irritation, redness and peeling. Oral use can cause differentiation syndrome, headache and raised lipids. Use in pregnancy is not recommended due to the risk of birth defects.
