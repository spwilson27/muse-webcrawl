Air superiority fighter

An air superiority fighter (also called an air dominance fighter) is a fighter aircraft designed to seize control of enemy airspace by engaging and destroying enemy forces, usually in aerial combat. Air superiority fighters are designed to undertake a variety of roles such as air-to-air combat against adversary aircraft, and are generally among the most capable and expensive combat aircraft.

History

The concept emerged during World War II and the Korean War, when gaining control of the skies over a battlefield proved decisive. Dedicated designs became prominent in the 1970s with aircraft such as the American F-14 Tomcat and F-15 Eagle, built in response to Soviet fighters like the MiG-25.

Modern examples

Contemporary air superiority fighters include the F-22 Raptor, which combined stealth, supercruise and advanced sensors, the F-15EX, Russia's Su-35 and Su-57, and China's J-20. Many modern designs are multirole, but air superiority remains a distinct primary mission for these top-end aircraft.
