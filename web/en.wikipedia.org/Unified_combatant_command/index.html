Unified combatant command

The geographic combatant commands divide responsibility for regions of the world.

A unified combatant command, also referred to as a combatant command (COCOM), is the highest joint military command of the United States Department of Defense. Each command is composed of forces from two or more military departments and is headed by a four-star officer who reports directly to the Secretary of Defense and, through the Secretary, to the President. Combatant commanders exercise combatant command (command authority) over assigned forces.

List of commands

There are eleven combatant commands. Seven have geographic areas of responsibility:

  • United States Africa Command (AFRICOM)
  • United States Central Command (CENTCOM)
  • United States European Command (EUCOM)
  • United States Indo-Pacific Command (INDOPACOM)
  • United States Northern Command (NORTHCOM)
  • United States Southern Command (SOUTHCOM)
  • United States Space Command (SPACECOM)

Four have worldwide functional responsibilities:

  • United States Cyber Command (CYBERCOM)
  • United States Special Operations Command (SOCOM)
  • United States Strategic Command (STRATCOM)
  • United States Transportation Command (TRANSCOM)

History

The system of unified commands originated during the Second World War and was formalized by the National Security Act of 1947. The Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986 greatly strengthened the authority of combatant commanders relative to the individual services, making joint operations the norm.
