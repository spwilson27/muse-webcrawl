Monte Brasil

Monte Brasil is a peninsula and volcanic cone on the island of Terceira in the Azores, Portugal, forming the southern shore of the city of Angra do Heroísmo. It rises to about 205 metres above sea level and shelters the city's harbour.

The massif is the remnant of a tuff cone volcano formed by an underwater eruption; its crater, a broad caldera partly open to the sea, is now occupied by woods and pasture.

Nature and recreation

Monte Brasil is classified as a nature reserve and recreational forest reserve. It is covered largely by introduced woodland, including Japanese cedar, and contains picnic areas, viewpoints (miradouros) and a small zoo-like enclosure with deer. Paths cross the caldera and climb to the summit cross and the Pico do Facho lookout.

The surrounding waters are also protected, and the bay was historically the anchorage for fleets returning from India and Brazil, from which the hill is said to take its name.

Fortifications

Because of Angra's strategic importance, Monte Brasil was heavily fortified. The Fortress of São João Baptista, built under Spanish rule from the 1590s, encloses the isthmus with a long curtain wall and is one of the largest fortresses built by Spain overseas. The smaller Fort of São Sebastião guards the eastern side of the bay.

In 1983 the area featured in the events surrounding the city's classification as a UNESCO World Heritage site.
