Veterans Day

Veterans Day (originally known as Armistice Day) is a federal holiday in the United States observed annually on November 11, honouring military veterans of the United States Armed Forces — all persons who have served, living or dead. It coincides with Armistice Day and Remembrance Day commemorations in other countries marking the end of the First World War.

History

Major hostilities of the First World War formally ended at the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month of 1918, when the Armistice with Germany took effect. President Woodrow Wilson proclaimed the first Armistice Day in 1919, and Congress made it a federal holiday in 1938. After veterans' organisations campaigned to honour veterans of all wars, Congress amended the law in 1954, replacing "Armistice" with "Veterans", and President Dwight D. Eisenhower issued the first Veterans Day proclamation. The Uniform Monday Holiday Act briefly moved the observance to the fourth Monday in October (1971–1977) before it was restored to November 11.

Observance

Federal government offices close, and ceremonies are held at the Tomb of the Unknowns at Arlington National Cemetery, including a wreath-laying at 11 a.m. Parades and services take place nationwide. Veterans Day differs from Memorial Day (the last Monday in May), which specifically honours those who died in military service. The holiday's official spelling omits the apostrophe, as the day is not "possessed" by veterans but dedicated to them.
