The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque (Arabic: جامع الشيخ زايد الكبير) is the largest mosque in the United Arab Emirates and the country's principal place of worship, located in Abu Dhabi. Commissioned by Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the founding president of the UAE — who is buried in its courtyard — it was built between 1994 and 2007 at a cost of roughly US$545 million, involving thousands of artisans and materials from around the world. It is celebrated for its white marble architecture and is one of the most visited landmarks in the Emirates.

Design and construction

The mosque complex covers more than 30 acres and blends architectural traditions from across the Islamic world: Mughal and Indo-Islamic forms in its onion domes and arches, Moorish details, and a layout recalling Persian mosques. More than 30,000 tonnes of white Macedonian marble were used, inlaid with semi-precious stones in floral designs. Four minarets of about 107 metres stand at the corners, and 82 domes of seven sizes crown the prayer halls. Some 3,000 workers and 38 contracting companies took part in the construction, and natural materials — marble, gold, crystals, ceramics — were chosen for their durability.

Interior and features

The main prayer hall accommodates over 7,000 worshippers beneath three great Swarovski-crystal chandeliers; the largest, about 12 tonnes and 15 metres high, was among the biggest chandeliers in the world when installed. The hall's carpet, hand-knotted in Iran by some 1,200 artisans from about 2.3 billion knots, is the largest hand-knotted carpet in the world, covering roughly 5,600 m² and weighing about 35 tonnes. The qibla wall displays the 99 names of Allah in traditional calligraphy, and the courtyard (sahan), paved with floral marble inlay, can hold tens of thousands more worshippers; reflecting pools mirror the colonnades.

Cultural role

Beyond daily and Friday prayers and the great Eid gatherings, the mosque operates a cultural centre and library and runs guided tours; it is unusual among major mosques for welcoming non-Muslim visitors as a matter of policy, reflecting Sheikh Zayed's stated aim of a monument uniting the cultural diversity of the Islamic world. It consistently ranks among the world's top landmarks in traveller polls.
