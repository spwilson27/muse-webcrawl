The Hepworth Wakefield

The Hepworth Wakefield is an art museum in Wakefield, West Yorkshire, England, which opened on 21 May 2011. The gallery is situated on the south side of the River Calder and takes its name from the artist and sculptor Barbara Hepworth, who was born in Wakefield in 1903. Designed by David Chipperfield Architects, its cluster of trapezoidal concrete blocks rises from the riverbank near the medieval chantry chapel on Wakefield Bridge.

Collections and exhibitions

The museum houses the Wakefield Permanent Art Collection — several thousand works of modern British art, with major holdings of Hepworth and of Henry Moore, who was born in nearby Castleford — together with a changing programme of exhibitions of modern and contemporary art. The Hepworth family gifted a large group of Hepworth's working models and plasters to the gallery. In 2017 the Hepworth Wakefield won the Art Fund's Museum of the Year award, then the largest museum prize in the world.

Regeneration context

The gallery forms part of the Yorkshire Sculpture Triangle, with the Yorkshire Sculpture Park, Leeds Art Gallery and the Henry Moore Institute, and its waterfront development was intended to anchor the regeneration of Wakefield's historic riverfront warehouses district.
