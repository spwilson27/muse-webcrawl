The February strike (Dutch: Februaristaking) was a general strike in the German-occupied Netherlands during the Second World War, held on 25 and 26 February 1941. It was the first public protest in occupied Europe against the Nazi persecution of the Jews, and one of the few mass protests against the deportations anywhere in Europe.

Background and course

In February 1941, German forces and Dutch Nazi collaborators staged raids in Amsterdam's Jewish quarter; on 22–23 February, 425 Jewish men were rounded up and deported (most were later murdered, chiefly at Mauthausen). In response, the banned Communist Party of the Netherlands called a general strike. On 25 February tram drivers, dock workers and municipal employees stopped work, and the strike spread to factories, offices and schools across Amsterdam and to nearby towns including Zaandam, Haarlem and Hilversum.

Suppression and legacy

The German authorities suppressed the strike by 27 February with mass arrests, fines and lethal force; nine people were killed and many were wounded or imprisoned. Despite its suppression, the strike became a central symbol of Dutch resistance. It is commemorated annually on 25 February at the Dokwerker statue on the Jonas Daniël Meijerplein, unveiled by Queen Juliana in 1952.
