Bikejoring

Bikejoring is a dog-powered sport in which a dog, or a team of dogs, pulls a person riding a bicycle. It is a dryland activity related to mushing and skijoring, and is widely used as off-season training for sled dog teams as well as a recreational and competitive sport in its own right.

Equipment and technique

The dog wears a sled-dog harness and is attached to the bicycle by a towline with a shock-absorbing section, typically connected via an antenna-like arm that keeps the line clear of the front wheel. The rider pedals to assist the dog and controls pace and braking. Common commands are those used in mushing: "hike" to go, "gee" and "haw" for right and left turns, and "whoa" to stop.

Racing

Bikejoring races, usually over courses of a few kilometres on forest trails, are organised under bodies such as the International Federation of Sleddog Sports, with classes for one or two dogs. Speeds can exceed 30 km/h. Because dogs overheat easily, races are typically held in cool weather and cancelled above set temperature limits.

Related sports

Bikejoring belongs to the family of dryland mushing sports alongside canicross (running with a dog), dog scootering, and carting. Many participants begin with a single pet dog, and the sport has grown as a way to exercise high-energy breeds such as pointers, huskies, and purpose-bred Eurohounds.
