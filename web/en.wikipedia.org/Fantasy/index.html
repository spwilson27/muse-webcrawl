Fantasy

Fantasy is a genre of speculative fiction involving magical elements, typically set in a fictional universe and usually inspired by mythology and folklore. Fantasy is distinguished from the genres of science fiction and horror by the absence of scientific or macabre themes, respectively, though these genres overlap.

In popular culture, the fantasy genre predominantly features swords and magic, and the settings often resemble medieval Europe. In recent decades, however, fantasy has come to include works set in modern, historical or entirely invented worlds, and in many cultures around the globe.

History

Fantastic elements appear in the earliest recorded literature, such as the Epic of Gilgamesh and Homer's Odyssey. Modern fantasy literature took shape in the 19th and early 20th centuries through the fairy tales of Hans Christian Andersen, the romances of William Morris and Lord Dunsany, and the works of writers such as E. R. Eddison and Robert E. Howard.

The Tolkien era

The publication of J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit (1937) and The Lord of the Rings (1954–55) transformed fantasy into a major publishing category. Tolkien's "high fantasy", set in the fully realised secondary world of Middle-earth, defined the template of elves, dwarves, quests and Dark Lords that successors have imitated and reacted against ever since. C. S. Lewis's Chronicles of Narnia appeared in the same period.

Subgenres

Fantasy includes many subgenres: high or epic fantasy, low fantasy set in the real world, sword and sorcery, urban fantasy, dark fantasy, historical fantasy, and portal fantasy. Writers such as Ursula K. Le Guin, George R. R. Martin, Brandon Sanderson and N. K. Jemisin have each reshaped the genre for contemporary readers.

In other media

Fantasy is a major genre in film, television, tabletop role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, and video games, where magic systems, fantastical races and imagined worlds are central features.
