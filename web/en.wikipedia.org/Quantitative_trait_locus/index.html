Quantitative trait locus

A quantitative trait locus (QTL) is a region of DNA associated with a particular quantitative trait — a phenotype that varies in degree, such as height or yield, and is usually attributable to the cumulative effects of many genes plus the environment.

QTL mapping

QTL mapping statistically links phenotypic variation in a mapping population (such as recombinant inbred lines) to molecular markers such as SNPs or microsatellites. The strength of association is often expressed as a LOD score. Fine mapping and candidate-gene analysis can then narrow a QTL interval to the causative gene.

Applications

QTL analysis is widely used in plant and animal breeding to select for yield, disease resistance and quality traits, and in human genetics it complements genome-wide association studies in dissecting complex diseases.
