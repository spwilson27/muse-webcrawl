Maya peoples

The Maya are an ethnolinguistic group of Indigenous peoples of Mesoamerica. The ancient Maya civilization was formed by members of this group, and today's Maya maintain a distinctive set of traditions and beliefs resulting from the merger of pre-Columbian and Spanish colonial ideas and cultures.

Population and languages

Millions of Maya live today in southern Mexico, Guatemala, Belize, El Salvador, and Honduras, speaking some thirty Mayan languages including Kʼicheʼ, Yucatec Maya, Qʼeqchiʼ, Mam, and Kaqchikel. Guatemala has the largest Maya population.

History

Maya civilization flourished from the Preclassic period through the Classic period (c. 250–900 CE), building great cities such as Tikal, Palenque, and Copán. The Spanish conquest in the 16th and 17th centuries subjugated the Maya kingdoms, but Maya culture, language, and identity have endured to the present day.
