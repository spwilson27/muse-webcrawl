Open reading frame

In molecular genetics, an open reading frame (ORF) is the part of a reading frame that has the ability to be translated. An ORF is a continuous stretch of codons that do not contain a stop codon, typically beginning with a start codon (usually AUG) and ending with a stop codon (UAA, UAG, or UGA).

ORFs are used in gene prediction: long ORFs in a DNA sequence are likely to be part of a protein-coding gene. Bioinformatics tools scan all six reading frames (three forward, three reverse complement) for ORFs. Not all ORFs are translated; upstream ORFs (uORFs) in 5' untranslated regions can regulate translation of the main coding sequence.

Significance

In sequenced genomes, novel short ORFs and long non-coding RNAs complicate annotation. Alternative ORFs overlapping known genes can produce alternative proteins, demonstrated by ribosome profiling.
