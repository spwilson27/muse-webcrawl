Mexicali

Mexicali is the capital city of the Mexican state of Baja California, seat of the Mexicali Municipality, and the second-largest city in the state after Tijuana. It lies on the Mexico-United States border, directly across from Calexico, California, in the Mexicali Valley, an irrigated agricultural region fed by Colorado River water. The city's name is a portmanteau of "México" and "California". The population is around one million.

History and economy

Mexicali was founded in 1903 as irrigation works developed the valley, and grew with cotton farming, which drew a notable Chinese community whose influence survives in the city's well-known Chinese-Mexican restaurants. Today the economy includes agriculture and food processing in the surrounding valley, maquiladora manufacturing (aerospace, electronics, automotive), services and cross-border commerce.

Climate and geography

The city sits below 10 metres elevation in the Sonoran Desert and has an extremely hot arid climate: summer temperatures routinely exceed 40 °C and have reached about 50 °C, among the highest recorded in Mexico, with mild winters and very low rainfall. The area is seismically active, notably affected by the 2010 El Mayor-Cucapah earthquake. Mexicali is served by an international airport, federal highways to Tijuana and mainland Mexico, and rail.
