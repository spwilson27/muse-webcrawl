Field Marshal William Joseph Slim, 1st Viscount Slim (6 August 1891 – 14 December 1970) was a British Army officer who commanded the Fourteenth Army in the Burma campaign of the Second World War and is widely regarded as one of Britain's finest generals of the war.

Career

Slim rose from the ranks of the Territorial Army through service at Gallipoli and in Mesopotamia in the First World War, then in the British Indian Army. In 1942 he took over the retreat from Burma, the longest in British military history, rebuilt the shattered forces into the Fourteenth Army, and in 1944–45 defeated the Japanese at Imphal and Kohima and reconquered Burma. His memoir Defeat into Victory is a classic of military literature. After the war he served as Chief of the Imperial General Staff (1948–1952) and as Governor-General of Australia (1953–1960).
