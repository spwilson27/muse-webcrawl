NFE2L2

Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2), also known as nuclear factor erythroid-derived 2-like 2, is a transcription factor that in humans is encoded by the NFE2L2 gene. NRF2 is a master regulator of the cellular response to oxidative stress, controlling the expression of antioxidant proteins that protect against oxidative damage triggered by injury and inflammation.

Gene: NFE2L2 (chromosome 2q31.2)

Protein: NRF2

Regulator: KEAP1

Function: Antioxidant response

Regulation

Under normal conditions, NRF2 is bound in the cytoplasm by KEAP1, which targets it for ubiquitination and rapid proteasomal degradation. When cells encounter electrophiles or reactive oxygen species, KEAP1's reactive cysteine residues are modified, NRF2 is stabilised, and it accumulates in the nucleus. There it binds antioxidant response elements (AREs) and activates genes encoding enzymes such as heme oxygenase-1, NQO1, and glutathione-synthesising enzymes.

Clinical relevance

Dysregulation of the KEAP1–NRF2 pathway is implicated in cancer — mutations that permanently activate NRF2 are common in lung tumours — as well as neurodegenerative and metabolic disease. The drug dimethyl fumarate, an NRF2 activator, is approved for multiple sclerosis and psoriasis.
