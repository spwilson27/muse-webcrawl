Amenhotep III

Amenhotep III (Ancient Egyptian: jmn-ḥtp, "Amun is satisfied"), also known as Amenhotep the Magnificent, was the ninth pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty of ancient Egypt. He ruled from June 1386 to 1349 BC, or from June 1388 BC to December 1351/1350 BC, after his father Thutmose IV died. His reign was a period of unprecedented prosperity and grandeur, when Egypt reached the peak of its artistic and international power.

Reign

Amenhotep III inherited an empire stretching from Syria to Nubia and maintained it largely through diplomacy rather than war; the Amarna letters preserve his correspondence with the rulers of Babylon, Assyria, Mitanni and Hatti, including diplomatic marriages. He married Tiye, a non-royal woman, who became his Great Royal Wife and a prominent figure at court. He celebrated three sed jubilee festivals in years 30, 34 and 37 of his reign.

Building works

Amenhotep III was one of the most prolific builders in Egyptian history. His works include much of the Luxor Temple, a vast mortuary temple at Kom el-Hetan on the west bank of Thebes—fronted by the Colossi of Memnon—and the pleasure palace at Malkata. Hundreds of statues of the king survive, along with enormous scarabs commemorating events of his reign.

Death and succession

Amenhotep III died around the age of fifty, suffering from dental disease and obesity; his mummy was later reburied in the royal cache in Amenhotep II's tomb. He was succeeded by his son Amenhotep IV, who changed his name to Akhenaten and revolutionised Egyptian religion around the sun-disc Aten.
