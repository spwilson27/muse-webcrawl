Gallipoli campaign

Gallipoli campaign
Date: 19 February 1915 – 9 January 1916
Location: Gallipoli Peninsula, Ottoman Empire
Result: Ottoman victory
Belligerents: British Empire and France vs. Ottoman Empire

The Gallipoli campaign, also known as the Dardanelles campaign, was an unsuccessful Allied operation of the First World War intended to seize the Dardanelles strait, knock the Ottoman Empire out of the war, and open a supply route to Russia.

Course of the campaign

A naval attack in February–March 1915 failed when Allied warships struck mines in the strait. Landings on the Gallipoli Peninsula followed on 25 April 1915, including the Australian and New Zealand Army Corps (ANZAC) at what became Anzac Cove. Ottoman defenders, aided by German advisers and commanded in part by Mustafa Kemal, contained the beachheads, and months of trench stalemate followed. A fresh offensive at Suvla Bay in August also failed.

Evacuation and legacy

The Allies evacuated in December 1915 and January 1916 — the campaign's most successful phase. Casualties were heavy on both sides, each suffering roughly a quarter of a million men killed, wounded, or sick. The campaign is commemorated annually on Anzac Day in Australia and New Zealand, and it enhanced Mustafa Kemal's reputation on the road to his founding of the Turkish Republic.
