Gingivitis is a non-destructive disease that causes inflammation of the gingiva (gums). The most common form results from the accumulation of bacterial plaque at and below the gum line, producing gums that are red, swollen and prone to bleeding when brushed or flossed. It is the mildest and most prevalent form of periodontal disease.

Causes and risk factors

Plaque-induced gingivitis develops when plaque is not removed by regular brushing and flossing and hardens into calculus (tartar), which shelters bacteria. Contributing factors include poor oral hygiene, smoking, hormonal changes (such as in puberty and pregnancy), diabetes, certain medications, dry mouth and vitamin C deficiency. Non-plaque-induced forms are rarer and may be caused by specific infections, allergic reactions or trauma.

Course and treatment

Unlike periodontitis, gingivitis is fully reversible: the inflammation resolves with thorough plaque removal and does not involve loss of the bone and connective tissue that hold teeth in place. However, untreated gingivitis can progress to periodontitis in susceptible individuals, which can lead to tooth loss and has been associated with systemic conditions. Treatment consists of professional scaling and polishing to remove calculus, followed by effective daily oral hygiene; antiseptic mouthwashes may be used as an adjunct.
