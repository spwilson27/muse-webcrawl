Protagoras

Protagoras (c. 490 – c. 420 BC) of Abdera was a pre-Socratic philosopher and the most famous of the sophists, teaching rhetoric and virtue for fees across Greece, especially in Athens.

Doctrine

He is known for the dictum "Man is the measure of all things: of the things that are, that they are, and of the things that are not, that they are not" — interpreted as relativism about truth and perception. His agnostic opening on the gods ("Concerning the gods I cannot know either that they exist or that they do not") reportedly led to prosecution for impiety. Plato's dialogues Protagoras and Theaetetus are principal sources, since his own writings survive only in fragments.
