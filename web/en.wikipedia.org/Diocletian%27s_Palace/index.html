Diocletian's Palace

LocationSplit, Croatia
BuiltAD 295–305
Built forEmperor Diocletian
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site

Diocletian's Palace (Croatian: Dioklecijanova palača) is an ancient palace built for the Roman emperor Diocletian at the end of the third century AD, which today forms about half of the old town of Split, Croatia. It was constructed as Diocletian's retirement residence after his abdication in AD 305 — the first voluntary abdication of a Roman emperor.

Layout

The palace rectangle, roughly 215 by 180 metres, combines features of a luxury villa and a fortified military camp, with walls and towers, two intersecting main streets (the cardo and decumanus), an imperial residential quarter along the sea front, a peristyle courtyard, temples, and Diocletian's mausoleum, which was later converted into Split's Cathedral of Saint Domnius.

Later history

After the fall of the nearby city of Salona in the 7th century, refugees settled inside the palace walls, and the structure was continuously inhabited and remodelled through the Middle Ages and beyond; some 3,000 people still live within it today.

Preservation

The palace is one of the best-preserved monuments of Roman architecture in the world. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979, together with the historical core of Split.
