Ezekiel

Ezekiel (Hebrew: יְחֶזְקֵאל, Yəḥezqēʾl) was a Jewish priest and prophet who lived in the 6th century BC and is the traditional author and central figure of the Book of Ezekiel in the Hebrew Bible. He was among the exiles deported to Babylon after Jerusalem's first fall to Nebuchadnezzar II in 597 BC.

Life and ministry

Ezekiel received his prophetic call in the fifth year of the exile (593 BC) by the Kebar River in Babylonia. His ministry combines stark oracles of judgement on Jerusalem with promises of national restoration. Famous episodes include the vision of the divine chariot with the four living creatures (the tetramorph), the sign-acts (including lying on his side to "bear the sin" of Israel), and the vision of the valley of dry bones that come to life (Ezekiel 37).

The Book of Ezekiel

The book's final chapters (40–48) describe a visionary new temple and a restored holy land. Ezekiel's imagery profoundly influenced later apocalyptic literature, including the Book of Revelation.
