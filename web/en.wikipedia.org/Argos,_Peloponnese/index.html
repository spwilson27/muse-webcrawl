Argos is a city in Argolis, in the eastern Peloponnese, Greece, and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with settlement dating back some 7,000 years. In Greek mythology it was a kingdom of Diomedes and a major setting of the epics.

Ancient history

Argos was a major Mycenaean centre and, in the Archaic period, a rival of Sparta for dominance of the Peloponnese; its hoplites won the Battle of Hysiae in 669 BC. It generally remained neutral or pro-Macedonian in later conflicts and flourished under Roman rule. The city was famous for its sanctuary of Hera, the Heraion, and for sculptors such as Polykleitos.

Modern city

Today Argos is a market town for the surrounding agricultural plain of citrus and olives, overlooked by the medieval Larissa castle. Its ancient theatre, one of the largest in Greece, still hosts performances.
