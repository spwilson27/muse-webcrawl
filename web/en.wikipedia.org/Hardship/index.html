Hardship

ConceptAdversity, suffering, deprivation
RelatedPoverty, resilience, suffering

Hardship refers to severe suffering, deprivation, or adversity in life circumstances — a condition of difficulty that a person or community must endure, such as poverty, illness, hunger, displacement, or loss. The word derives from "hard" in the sense of difficult or severe, combined with the suffix "-ship" denoting a state or condition.

Forms

Hardship may be economic (unemployment, debt, homelessness), physical (disease, disability, famine), or social (persecution, bereavement, exile). Events such as wars, natural disasters, and economic depressions impose hardship on whole populations at once.

Responses and significance

Religions and philosophies have long treated hardship as a test of character, a spur to compassion, or a path to spiritual growth. In law and public policy, "hardship" is a formal concept: undue hardship standards appear in employment accommodation law, hardship exemptions in tax and immigration law, and hardship withdrawals from retirement plans.

Resilience

Psychological research studies why some people emerge from hardship with post-traumatic growth while others suffer lasting harm, pointing to social support, meaning-making, and prior resources as key factors.
