The Yellow House (Het gele huis), also titled The Street, is an 1888 oil painting by the Dutch Post-Impressionist artist Vincent van Gogh. It depicts the house at 2 Place Lamartine in Arles, France, which Van Gogh rented in May 1888 and decorated in preparation for the visit of Paul Gauguin.

Background

Van Gogh rented four rooms in the right-hand wing of the building, painting its exterior a vivid yellow. He furnished the house as a "Studio of the South", imagining a colony of artists, and filled it with his own works, including versions of the Sunflowers painted to decorate Gauguin's bedroom. Gauguin arrived in October 1888; the two artists' nine-week collaboration ended in December with the crisis in which Van Gogh mutilated his own ear.

Fate of the house

The building was badly damaged by bombing in the Second World War and was subsequently demolished. The painting itself is held by the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam. A watercolour version and related drawings of the house and its interior, including Van Gogh's Bedroom in Arles, survive as companion works.
