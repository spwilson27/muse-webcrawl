Drive-in

A drive-in is a facility, most famously a drive-in theater, at which customers can receive service or entertainment while remaining in their automobiles. The term is most closely associated with the United States, where drive-in theaters, restaurants, and other businesses flourished in the mid-20th century alongside the growth of car culture.

Drive-in theaters

The drive-in theater (or drive-in cinema) features a large outdoor movie screen, a projection booth, a concession stand, and parking for automobiles, whose occupants watch the film from their cars. Sound was originally provided by speakers on poles beside each parking space and later by low-power radio broadcasts received on the car radio. The first patented drive-in opened in Camden, New Jersey, in 1933; the format peaked in the late 1950s and early 1960s with more than 4,000 theaters in the United States, before declining due to rising land values, daylight saving time, and home video. A few hundred remain in operation, and the format saw a revival of interest during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Drive-in restaurants

Drive-in restaurants serve customers who eat in their cars, historically attended by carhops, sometimes on roller skates. The format is distinct from the drive-through, in which customers order at a window and leave. Chains such as Sonic Drive-In continue the tradition in the United States.

Other drive-ins

Other businesses adopting the drive-in model have included drive-in churches, banks, pharmacies, and wedding chapels, particularly in Las Vegas.
