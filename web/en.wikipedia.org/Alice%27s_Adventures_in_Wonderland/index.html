Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (also known as Alice in Wonderland) is an 1865 English children's novel by Lewis Carroll, the pen name of Charles Lutwidge Dodgson. It tells of a young girl named Alice who falls through a rabbit hole into a fantasy world of anthropomorphic creatures.

Plot

Sitting with her sister on a riverbank, Alice follows a waistcoated White Rabbit down a rabbit hole. In Wonderland she repeatedly grows and shrinks, meets the Cheshire Cat, attends a mad tea party with the Mad Hatter and the March Hare, plays croquet with the Queen of Hearts, hears the Gryphon and the Mock Turtle, and finally testifies at the trial of the Knave of Hearts before waking to find it was a dream.

Origins

The story grew from a tale Dodgson improvised on a boat trip on the Thames on 4 July 1862 for Alice Liddell and her sisters. A hand-written version, Alice's Adventures Under Ground, was later expanded and published with illustrations by John Tenniel.

Influence

The book has never been out of print, has been translated into nearly 200 languages, and is a founding classic of the literary nonsense genre, famed for its wordplay, logic games, and poems such as "Jabberwocky" (in the sequel) and "The Mouse's Tale".
