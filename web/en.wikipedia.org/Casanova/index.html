Giacomo Girolamo Casanova (2 April 1725 – 4 June 1798) was an Italian adventurer and author from the Republic of Venice. His autobiography, Histoire de ma vie (Story of My Life), is regarded as one of the most authentic and vivid sources of the customs and norms of European social life in the 18th century. His name has become synonymous with the art of seduction, and "Casanova" remains a common term for a charming womanizer.

Giacomo Casanova, adventurer and memoirist of 18th-century Europe.

Life

Born in Venice to a family of actors, Casanova studied law at Padua and sampled careers in the church, the military, and music before embarking on decades of travel across Europe. He moved in the highest circles — meeting Voltaire, Frederick the Great, Catherine the Great, and Mozart's librettist Da Ponte — while supporting himself through gambling, diplomacy, espionage for the Venetian Inquisition, and various schemes, including helping to establish the French national lottery.

Imprisonment and escape

In 1755 the Venetian Inquisitors arrested Casanova for offences against religion and common decency and imprisoned him in the Piombi, the notorious cells under the lead roofs of the Doge's Palace, without trial. On the night of 31 October 1756 he escaped across the rooftops — the only person ever to escape the Piombi — and recounted the adventure in his Histoire de ma fuite (1788), which made him a celebrity across Europe.

Later years and memoirs

Wandering and impoverished in old age, Casanova settled in 1785 as librarian to Count Waldstein at Dux Castle in Bohemia. There he wrote his great memoir in French, some 3,700 pages covering his life to 1774. He died at Dux in 1798. The memoir, long published only in expurgated form, appeared complete in 1960–62; the original manuscript was purchased by the Bibliothèque nationale de France in 2010.
