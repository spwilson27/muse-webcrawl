Nataraja

Nataraja (Sanskrit: Lord of Dance) is a depiction of the Hindu god Shiva as the divine cosmic dancer. His dance, the Tandava, symbolises creation, preservation and destruction of the universe. Classic bronze Nataraja sculptures were created under the Chola dynasty (9th–13th centuries).

DeityShiva
SymbolismCosmic dance; creation and destruction
Noted shrineChidambaram Temple, Tamil Nadu

Symbolism

Shiva dances within an arch of flames, holding a damaru (drum of creation) and fire (destruction), with one hand in abhaya mudra and another pointing to his raised foot, while trampling the dwarf Apasmara, representing ignorance.

Chidambaram

The Thillai Nataraja Temple at Chidambaram is the foremost shrine of this form, where Shiva is also worshipped in an empty-space (akasha) form.

In modern culture

A Nataraja statue stands at CERN, gifted by India in 2004, as a metaphor for the cosmic dance of subatomic particles, echoing Fritjof Capra's The Tao of Physics.
