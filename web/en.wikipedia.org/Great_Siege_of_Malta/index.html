Great Siege of Malta

Date18 May – 11 September 1565
LocationMalta
ResultVictory for the Knights Hospitaller
Defenders~6,100 (Knights, soldiers, Maltese)
Attackers~35,000–40,000 Ottoman troops

The Great Siege of Malta occurred in 1565 when the Ottoman Empire attempted to conquer the strategic island of Malta, then held by the Knights Hospitaller (the Knights of St John). The siege, from 18 May to 11 September 1565, is considered one of the most celebrated and desperate sieges in military history.

Background

The Knights, expelled from Rhodes in 1522, had been granted Malta by Charles V in 1530. Their corsairing threatened Ottoman shipping, and Suleiman the Magnificent resolved to destroy them and gain a base for further expansion in the western Mediterranean. The Grand Master, Jean Parisot de Valette, prepared the fortified harbours of Birgu and Senglea and the star fort of St Elmo guarding Grand Harbour.

The siege

An Ottoman fleet landed some 35,000–40,000 men. Fort St Elmo, expected to fall in days, held out for over a month against overwhelming bombardment, buying time at the cost of its entire garrison. Furious assaults on Birgu and Senglea in July and August were repelled with heavy losses on both sides; Valette, aged 70, personally fought in the breaches. Disease and supply problems ravaged the attackers. When a relief force from Sicily finally landed in September, the Ottomans re-embarked and withdrew.

Aftermath

The victory was celebrated across Christian Europe; Voltaire later wrote that "nothing is better known than the siege of Malta". The Knights rebuilt their defences and founded the new capital, Valletta, on Mount Sciberras behind St Elmo. Ottoman prestige suffered, though the empire remained formidable until the Battle of Lepanto in 1571.
