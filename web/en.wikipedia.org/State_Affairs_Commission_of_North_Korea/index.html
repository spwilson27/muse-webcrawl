State Affairs Commission of North Korea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Mansudae Assembly Hall in Pyongyang, seat of North Korea's state institutions.

The State Affairs Commission of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (SAC) is the supreme leadership institution of North Korea, defined by the country's constitution as the highest organ of state power. It was established on 29 June 2016, replacing the National Defence Commission.

History

The commission was created by an amendment to the Socialist Constitution adopted at the fourth session of the 13th Supreme People's Assembly. Its predecessor, the National Defence Commission, had been the centre of the military-first (songun) system under Kim Jong-il. The new body broadened the top institution's remit to cover the economy and foreign affairs as well as defence.

Composition

The President of the State Affairs Commission is Kim Jong Un, who holds the office as the country's supreme leader. The commission includes vice-presidents and members drawn from the senior ranks of the Workers' Party of Korea, the Korean People's Army and the government cabinet, including figures responsible for foreign policy such as the foreign minister.

Role

According to the constitution, the commission deliberates and decides on major state policies, supervises the armed forces, and its president appoints or removes key officials, concludes treaties, and grants pardons. In practice it functions as the apex executive body of the North Korean state.
