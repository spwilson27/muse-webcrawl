Tarsus (skeleton)

Tarsus
Bones7: talus, calcaneus, navicular, cuboid, 3 cuneiforms
LocationHindfoot and midfoot
Corresponds toCarpus (wrist) of the hand

The tarsus is a cluster of seven articulating bones in each foot, situated between the lower end of the tibia and fibula of the lower leg and the metatarsals of the foot. Together the tarsals form the posterior half of the foot skeleton and the longitudinal and transverse arches of the foot.

The tarsal bones

The seven tarsals are the talus, the calcaneus (heel bone, the largest tarsal), the navicular, the cuboid, and the three cuneiform bones (medial, intermediate, and lateral). The talus and calcaneus make up the hindfoot; the navicular, cuboid, and cuneiforms form the midfoot and articulate with the five metatarsals at the tarsometatarsal (Lisfranc) joints.

Function

The tarsals transmit body weight from the leg to the ground and, through the arches they form, provide both rigidity for propulsion and flexibility for shock absorption. The arrangement is homologous to the carpal bones of the wrist. In some animals the tarsals are modified dramatically: in birds several are fused into the tarsometatarsus, and in horses the tarsus forms the hock joint.

Clinical notes

Tarsal coalition — an abnormal fusion of two tarsals — is a cause of painful flatfoot in adolescents. Fractures of the calcaneus are common after falls from height, and injuries to the Lisfranc joint complex can destabilise the midfoot.
