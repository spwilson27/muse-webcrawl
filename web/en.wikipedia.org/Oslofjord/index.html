Oslofjord

The Oslofjord (Norwegian: Oslofjorden) is an inlet of the Skagerrak in southeastern Norway, extending about 100 kilometres north from an imaginary line between the Torbjørnskjær and Færder lighthouses to the capital, Oslo, at its head.

Geography

Despite its name, the Oslofjord is not a fjord in the geological sense but a rift valley or graben. The fjord is dotted with islands and narrows at Drøbak, where the Oscarsborg Fortress guarded the approach to Oslo and famously sank the German cruiser Blücher on 9 April 1940.

Recreation

The inner fjord is heavily used for boating, swimming, and summer cottages. Towns along its shores include Drøbak, Moss, Horten, and Tønsberg.
