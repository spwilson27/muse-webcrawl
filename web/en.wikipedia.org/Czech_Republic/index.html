Czech Republic

Prague, the capital, with Prague Castle above the Vltava River.

The Czech Republic, also known by its short-form name Czechia (Czech: Česko), is a landlocked country in Central Europe bordered by Austria, Germany, Poland and Slovakia. It comprises the historical lands of Bohemia, Moravia and Czech Silesia. With a population of about 10.9 million and a capital and largest city at Prague, it is a member of the European Union, NATO, the OECD and the Visegrád Group.

History

The Czech lands were settled by Slavic tribes in the sixth century and unified under the Přemyslid dynasty; the Kingdom of Bohemia became a major power of the Holy Roman Empire under Charles IV, who founded Charles University in 1348. The reforms of Jan Hus sparked the Hussite Wars of the fifteenth century. Habsburg rule followed the Battle of White Mountain (1620) and lasted until 1918, when Czechoslovakia was founded under Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk. The interwar republic was destroyed by the Munich Agreement (1938) and Nazi occupation; after 1945, the expulsion of the German population and the 1948 communist coup brought four decades of one-party rule, interrupted by the Prague Spring of 1968, crushed by a Warsaw Pact invasion. The Velvet Revolution of 1989 restored democracy under Václav Havel, and on 1 January 1993 Czechoslovakia peacefully dissolved into the Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Geography and economy

The country is mostly hilly: Bohemia is ringed by low mountains including the Krkonoše, whose Sněžka (1,603 m) is the highest point, while Moravia drains toward the Danube via the Morava River. The Elbe and Vltava are the principal western rivers. The Czech economy is among the most industrialised in Europe, anchored by automotive manufacturing (Škoda Auto), engineering, electronics, brewing and tourism; it has one of the EU's lowest unemployment rates and a high standard of living, though it retains its own currency rather than the euro.

Culture

Czech culture has produced composers Bedřich Smetana, Antonín Dvořák and Leoš Janáček; writers Franz Kafka, Karel Čapek (who popularised the word "robot"), Jaroslav Hašek and Milan Kundera; and filmmaker Miloš Forman. Prague's historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the country has one of the world's highest densities of castles and of beer consumption per capita.

Government

The Czech Republic is a parliamentary republic: the president is head of state and the prime minister heads the government, accountable to the Chamber of Deputies. It joined NATO in 1999 and the European Union in 2004.
