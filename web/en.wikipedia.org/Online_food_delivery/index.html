Online food delivery

Online food delivery is a service in which food is ordered from a restaurant or retailer through a website or mobile application and delivered to the customer. Orders may be fulfilled by the restaurant itself or by third-party platforms that aggregate many restaurants and provide their own courier networks.

History

Early services in the 1990s, such as Pizza Hut's online ordering experiments, let customers order from individual chains. Around 2000, aggregator sites such as Grubhub (founded 2004) and Just Eat (2001) built marketplaces listing many independent restaurants. Smartphone apps transformed the industry in the 2010s: platforms including DoorDash (2013), Uber Eats (2014) and Deliveroo (2013) added real-time tracking and venture-funded courier fleets. In China, Meituan and Ele.me grew into very large delivery businesses extending into groceries and other goods.

Business models

Platforms typically charge restaurants a commission on each order — often a substantial percentage — plus delivery and service fees to customers. Couriers are frequently engaged as independent contractors, making the industry central to debates over gig-economy labour classification. "Ghost kitchens" or dark kitchens prepare food solely for delivery, with no storefront. The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 sharply accelerated adoption as dine-in service closed.

Criticism

The sector has faced criticism over restaurant margins, courier pay and safety, road congestion, packaging waste, and the accuracy of tipping practices on some apps.
