The 82nd Airborne Division is an airborne infantry division of the United States Army specialising in parachute assault operations into denied areas. Based at Fort Liberty (formerly Fort Bragg), North Carolina, it is the largest paratrooper formation in the world and serves as the core of the US Immediate Response Force, able to deploy anywhere in the world within 18 hours.

History

Constituted in 1917 as an infantry division and nicknamed "All American" because its original ranks drew men from all 48 states, the 82nd fought in the First World War. Converted to airborne in 1942 under Major General Matthew Ridgway, it made combat jumps in Sicily, Salerno and Normandy — where its paratroopers landed around Sainte-Mère-Église on D-Day — and fought in Operation Market Garden and the Battle of the Bulge.

Post-war service

The division has since deployed to the Dominican Republic, Vietnam, Grenada, Panama, the Gulf War, the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq, and regularly responds to humanitarian crises, including the 2021 Kabul airlift. Its shoulder patch bears the letters "AA" for its "All American" nickname.
