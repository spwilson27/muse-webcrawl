The Ford Foundation is an American private foundation with the stated mission of advancing human welfare. Created in 1936 by Edsel Ford and his father Henry Ford, it was originally funded by a US$25,000 gift. After the deaths of the Fords in the 1940s, the foundation inherited the bulk of their Ford Motor Company stock and became the largest philanthropy in the world. It sold its Ford stock holdings over the following decades and is now an independent institution with no connection to the car company.

The Ford Foundation Building (1967), noted for its indoor atrium garden.

History and programmes

From the 1950s the foundation became a major funder of education, the arts, public broadcasting (including support for National Educational Television and the Children's Television Workshop, creators of Sesame Street), and international development, notably the Green Revolution in agriculture. It supported the American civil rights movement and public-interest law, funded area-studies programmes at universities, and supported microfinance pioneers such as the Grameen Bank. The foundation's headquarters building in Manhattan, designed by Kevin Roche, is a landmark of modern architecture.

Focus on inequality

Under president Darren Walker (from 2013), the foundation reorganised its work entirely around addressing inequality in its many forms—economic, racial, gender, and civic—and in 2020 issued US$1 billion in social bonds to increase grant-making during the COVID-19 pandemic.
