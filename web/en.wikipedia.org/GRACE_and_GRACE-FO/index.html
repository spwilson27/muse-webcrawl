GRACE and GRACE-FO

GRACE: 2002–2017
GRACE-FO: launched 22 May 2018
Operators: NASA / DLR (German Aerospace Center)
Orbit: Polar, ~490 km altitude
Instruments: Microwave ranging system, accelerometers, GPS

The Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE) was a joint mission of NASA and the German Aerospace Center (DLR). Twin satellites took detailed measurements of Earth's gravity field anomalies from their launch in March 2002 until the end of their science mission in October 2017. GRACE Follow-On (GRACE-FO), a successor mission launched in May 2018, continues the record, tracking Earth's water movement and changes in ice sheets and aquifers.

Method

The two satellites fly about 220 km apart in the same orbit. As they pass over regions of stronger or weaker gravity, their separation changes slightly; microwave ranging measures these changes to micrometre precision. Combined with accelerometer and GPS data, the measurements are converted into monthly maps of Earth's gravity field, which reveal how mass — mostly water — is moving around the planet, with a spatial resolution of a few hundred kilometres.

Findings

GRACE data transformed the study of the water cycle and cryosphere. The mission measured ice-mass loss from Greenland and Antarctica (each losing hundreds of gigatonnes per year during parts of the record), groundwater depletion in major aquifers such as California's Central Valley and northern India, sea-level rise contributions, drought, and post-glacial rebound. Its record is a key line of evidence in assessments of climate change.

GRACE Follow-On

GRACE-FO launched on 22 May 2018 aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Vandenberg Air Force Base. In addition to the microwave ranging instrument, it carries an experimental laser ranging interferometer — a technology demonstrator for future missions such as LISA — which measures inter-satellite distance even more precisely. The mission was designed for five years and continues the monthly gravity-field series with a gap of about a year between the two missions.
