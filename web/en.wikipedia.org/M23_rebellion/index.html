M23 rebellion

The M23 rebellion was an armed conflict in North Kivu province in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) between the Congolese government and the March 23 Movement (M23), a rebel group composed largely of former soldiers who had mutinied from the Congolese army in April 2012. The rebellion began in April 2012 and ended in November 2013 with a Congolese military victory.

Background

M23 took its name from a peace agreement signed on 23 March 2009 between the government and the National Congress for the Defence of the People (CNDP), whose fighters were integrated into the national army. The mutineers accused the government of failing to honour the deal, including provisions on pay, rank, and the return of refugees. Many of the rebels were ethnic Tutsis who said they needed protection against the FDLR, a Hutu militia.

Course of the conflict

Under commanders including Bosco Ntaganda and Sultani Makenga, M23 seized territory in North Kivu and briefly captured the provincial capital Goma in November 2012 before withdrawing under international pressure. United Nations experts reported that Rwanda provided support to the rebels, a charge Rwanda denied. In 2013, the UN Force Intervention Brigade — authorised to carry out offensive operations — fought alongside Congolese forces, and by late October 2013 M23's positions had collapsed. On 5 November 2013 the group declared an end to its rebellion.

Aftermath

The group's defeat was followed by peace declarations signed in Nairobi in December 2013. M23 later re-emerged as a fighting force in late 2021 and 2022, launching a renewed offensive in North Kivu that drew in regional forces and became a major regional crisis.
