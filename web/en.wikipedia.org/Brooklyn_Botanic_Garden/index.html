Brooklyn Botanic Garden (BBG) is a botanical garden in the borough of Brooklyn in New York City. The 52-acre garden is located in the Prospect Heights neighbourhood, adjacent to Prospect Park. It holds over 14,000 taxa of plants and has nearly a million visitors each year. It includes the Steinhardt Conservatory, which contains the C. V. Starr Bonsai Museum, three climate-themed plant pavilions, and an aquatic plant house.

History

The garden was founded in 1910 and opened in 1911 on land set aside as part of the Brooklyn Institute of Arts and Sciences. Its landscape design was led by Harold Caparn and later McKim, Mead & White, with gardens built over successive decades. It has long served as a centre for horticultural education and urban gardening, including the pioneering Children's Garden (1914), among the first in an American botanic garden.

Gardens and collections

Highlights include the Japanese Hill-and-Pond Garden (1915), one of the oldest Japanese gardens in an American public garden; the Cherry Esplanade and Cherry Walk, famous for the annual Sakura Matsuri cherry blossom festival; the Cranford Rose Garden (1928); the Shakespeare Garden; the Native Flora Garden; and the Fragrance Garden (1955), designed for the blind. The Steinhardt Conservatory (1988) houses desert, tropical and temperate pavilions.

Programs

BBG runs extensive education programs for children and adults, community greening initiatives, and scientific research in plant conservation and urban ecology.
