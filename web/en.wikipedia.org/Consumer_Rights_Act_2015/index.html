Consumer Rights Act 2015

The Consumer Rights Act 2015 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that consolidates and reforms consumer protection law. It received royal assent on 26 March 2015 and its main provisions came into force on 1 October 2015. The Act replaced and simplified rules previously spread across legislation including the Sale of Goods Act 1979, the Unfair Terms in Consumer Contracts Regulations 1999 and the Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982.

Goods

For goods, the Act requires that they be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described. Consumers have a short-term right to reject faulty goods within 30 days and obtain a full refund. After that period, the trader must be given an opportunity to repair or replace; if that fails or is impossible, the consumer may claim a price reduction or exercise a final right to reject.

Digital content

The Act was notable for extending consumer rights expressly to digital content, such as downloads, software and streaming media. Digital content must likewise be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described, with remedies of repair, replacement or price reduction where it is faulty, and compensation where it damages a consumer's device or other content.

Services and unfair terms

Services must be performed with reasonable care and skill, and information given by the trader may be binding. The Act also consolidated the law on unfair contract terms, providing that terms must be fair and transparent to bind consumers, and introduced reforms on consumer enforcement and competition class actions.
