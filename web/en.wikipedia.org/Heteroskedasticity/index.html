Heteroskedasticity

FieldStatistics, econometrics
DefinitionNon-constant variance of a variable or error term
OppositeHomoskedasticity
TestsBreusch–Pagan, White, Goldfeld–Quandt

In statistics, a sequence of random variables is heteroskedastic (or heteroscedastic) if its variability is unequal across observations — that is, the standard deviations or variances differ. The term derives from Greek hetero ("different") and skedastikos ("dispersion"). Its opposite, constant variance, is homoskedasticity.

In regression

Classical linear regression assumes the error term has constant variance. When heteroskedasticity is present, ordinary least squares estimates remain unbiased but are no longer efficient, and the usual standard errors are biased, invalidating hypothesis tests and confidence intervals. Residual plots that fan out as fitted values grow are a classic visual sign.

Detection and correction

Common diagnostic tests include the Breusch–Pagan test, the White test, and the Goldfeld–Quandt test. Remedies include transforming variables (for example, taking logarithms), weighted least squares, and the use of heteroskedasticity-consistent ("robust") standard errors, notably the Huber–White sandwich estimator, now standard practice in econometrics.

Occurrence

Heteroskedasticity arises naturally in cross-sectional data spanning different scales — income and spending across households, for instance — and in time series, where it is modelled by ARCH and GARCH processes describing volatility clustering in financial markets.
