Livonian Brothers of the Sword

The Livonian Brothers of the Sword was a Catholic military order of German warrior monks founded in 1202 in Livonia (in present-day Latvia and Estonia) during the Livonian Crusade.

Foundation

Bishop Albert of Riga founded the order to conquer and convert the pagan peoples of the eastern Baltic. Its knights wore white mantles bearing a red cross and sword, and took vows of poverty, chastity and obedience.

Conquests

The Brothers built castles, founded towns and subdued much of modern Latvia and southern Estonia, often in conflict with Danes, Lithuanians, Russians and the local peoples they fought to convert.

Defeat and merger

In 1236 the order was crushed by Samogitians and Semigallians at the Battle of Saule, where its master Volkwin was killed. The survivors merged in 1237 into the Teutonic Order as its autonomous Livonian branch, the Livonian Order.
