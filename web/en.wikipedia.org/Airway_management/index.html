Airway management

Airway management includes a set of maneuvers and medical procedures performed to prevent and relieve an airway obstruction. Airway management is commonly divided into two categories: basic and advanced. Basic techniques include head and neck maneuvers to optimize ventilation, abdominal thrusts and back blows to clear foreign bodies, and the use of simple adjuncts such as oropharyngeal and nasopharyngeal airways, as well as bag-valve-mask ventilation.

Basic airway management

The head tilt–chin lift and jaw thrust maneuvers open the airway by moving the tongue away from the posterior pharynx. In an unconscious patient, adjuncts such as the oropharyngeal airway or nasopharyngeal airway help maintain patency. Obstruction by a foreign body may be treated with back blows and abdominal thrusts (the Heimlich maneuver) in a conscious adult.

Advanced airway management

Advanced techniques include the placement of supraglottic airway devices such as the laryngeal mask airway, and tracheal intubation, typically performed with a laryngoscope or video laryngoscope and confirmed with capnography. When intubation and ventilation both fail, a surgical airway — emergency cricothyrotomy — may be required. Rapid sequence induction is used to secure the airway quickly in patients at risk of aspiration.

Assessment

Predictors of difficult airway management include limited mouth opening, a short thyromental distance, reduced neck mobility, and a high Mallampati classification. Structured approaches such as the Vortex approach and the Difficult Airway Society guidelines help clinicians plan escalating interventions.
