The National University of Ireland (NUI) is a federal university system in Ireland, established under the Irish Universities Act 1908. It serves as the degree-awarding body for its constituent universities and recognised colleges, and represents their interests nationally and internationally.

Constituent universities

The constituent universities of the NUI are University College Dublin, University College Cork, the University of Galway, and Maynooth University. Each is autonomous in its teaching and research, while degrees are conferred under the NUI charter. The system's history traces back to the Queen's Colleges founded in 1845 and the Royal University of Ireland, which the NUI replaced in 1908.

Governance

The head of the NUI is its Chancellor, elected by the graduates of the university; the first chancellor was Éamon de Valera, who held the office for decades. The NUI Senate is the governing body of the federal university. Graduates of the NUI elect four members of Seanad Éireann, the upper house of the Irish parliament, through the National University constituency.

Functions

Beyond degree conferral, the NUI promotes scholarship through awards and publications, supports the Irish language, and administers matriculation requirements for entry to its constituent universities.
