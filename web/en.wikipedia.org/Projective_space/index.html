Projective space

In mathematics, a projective space is the set of lines through the origin of a vector space, equivalently the set of one-dimensional subspaces. The real projective plane ℝP² models points at infinity so that parallel lines meet, formalizing perspective drawing.

Definitions

Given a vector space V over a field K, the projective space P(V) consists of equivalence classes of nonzero vectors under scaling: points are written in homogeneous coordinates [x₀ : … : xₙ]. The standard n-dimensional space is KPn. Quotients by polynomials give projective varieties, central to algebraic geometry; Desargues's and Pappus's theorems characterize its incidence structure. Projective spaces also underpin computer graphics and vision through homogeneous coordinate transforms.
