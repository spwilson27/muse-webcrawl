New START (Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty; Russian: СНВ-III) is a nuclear arms reduction treaty between the United States and the Russian Federation with the formal name of Measures for the Further Reduction and Limitation of Strategic Offensive Arms. It was signed on 8 April 2010 in Prague, and, after ratification, entered into force on 5 February 2011. It is expected to last until 5 February 2026, following an agreement in 2021 between the two parties to extend it by five years.

New START replaced the Treaty of Moscow (SORT), which was due to expire in December 2012. It follows START I (expired in December 2009), the proposed START II (never in force) and START III (never negotiated) treaties, and lowers the limit on deployed strategic nuclear warheads. Under the terms of the treaty, the number of strategic nuclear missile launchers is halved.

Limits

The treaty sets three central limits on each side's deployed strategic forces:

  • 1,550 deployed strategic nuclear warheads;
  • 700 deployed intercontinental ballistic missiles (ICBMs), submarine-launched ballistic missiles (SLBMs) and heavy bombers equipped for nuclear armaments;
  • 800 deployed and non-deployed ICBM launchers, SLBM launchers and heavy bombers.

The treaty's verification regime includes on-site inspections, data exchanges and notifications. Russia has argued that United States missile defence plans could undermine the treaty's effectiveness. In February 2023, Russian President Vladimir Putin announced that Russia was suspending its participation in the treaty, though not fully withdrawing from it.
