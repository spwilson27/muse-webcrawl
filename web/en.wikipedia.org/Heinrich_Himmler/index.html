Heinrich Luitpold Himmler (7 October 1900 – 23 May 1945) was a German Nazi politician and military leader, Reichsführer of the Schutzstaffel (SS) from 1929, and one of the most powerful men in Nazi Germany. As head of the SS, the police and the concentration-camp system, he was among the chief architects and overseers of the Holocaust — the genocide of six million Jews — and of the regime's wider crimes, which murdered millions of Roma, Poles, Soviet prisoners of war and others.

Early life

Himmler was born in Munich into a middle-class Catholic family; his father was a schoolteacher and deputy headmaster. Too young and physically unfit to see combat in the First World War, he trained as an officer cadet and then studied agronomy at the Technical University of Munich, joining the Nazi Party in 1923 and taking part in the failed Beer Hall Putsch. He worked as a propaganda organiser before Gregor Strasser appointed him deputy Reich propaganda leader; he married Margarete Boden in 1928 and ran a chicken farm while rising in the movement.

Rise of the SS

Appointed Reichsführer-SS in January 1929, Himmler expanded the SS from a small bodyguard formation of about 280 men into a vast state-within-a-state, eventually numbering millions including the Waffen-SS. He modelled the order on racial-elite principles, with marriage controls and pseudo-historical research (the Ahnenerbe). After the Nazis took power in 1933 he consolidated control of Germany's police forces, founded the first official concentration camp at Dachau, and, following the Night of the Long Knives (1934), the SS became an independent instrument of terror. In 1936 he was named Chief of the German Police; Reinhard Heydrich ran the security apparatus (Gestapo, SD, later the RSHA) under him.

War and genocide

During the Second World War, Himmler's SS administered the concentration and extermination camps, organised the Einsatzgruppen mass shootings, and implemented the "Final Solution" through agencies under his authority; in October 1943 his Posen speeches to SS leaders explicitly acknowledged the extermination of the Jews. He directed the occupation and racial "Germanisation" policies in Eastern Europe as Reich Commissioner for the Strengthening of Germandom, overseeing vast programmes of expulsion and mass murder. In 1943 he also became Reich Minister of the Interior, and in 1944, after the July plot, commander of the Replacement Army.

Downfall and death

In April 1945, Himmler's secret attempts to negotiate a separate peace with the Western Allies (via Count Folke Bernadotte) were exposed; Hitler expelled him from the party and stripped him of office. Disguised as a discharged sergeant, Himmler was captured by British forces near Lüneburg and, on being identified and searched, bit into a cyanide capsule concealed in his mouth, dying on 23 May 1945. He was buried in an unmarked grave on the Lüneburg Heath.
