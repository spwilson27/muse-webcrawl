Norman Wilkinson

Norman Wilkinson CBE RI (24 November 1878 - 30 May 1971) was a British artist who usually worked in oils, watercolour and drypoint. Best known as a marine painter and poster artist, he is credited with inventing dazzle camouflage for ships during the First World War.

A ship painted in dazzle camouflage

Career

Wilkinson studied at the Portsmouth and Southsea School of Art and in Paris, and became a prolific illustrator and poster designer for railway and shipping companies, notably the London and North Western Railway and the London Midland and Scottish Railway. As president of the Royal Institute of Painters in Water Colours, he championed poster art as a serious medium.

Dazzle camouflage

Serving in the Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve in 1917, Wilkinson proposed painting ships in bold, contradictory geometric patterns — not to hide them, but to break up their outlines and confuse enemy range-finders about their course and speed. The Admiralty adopted the scheme, putting Wilkinson in charge of a camouflage unit at the Royal Academy; hundreds of British and later American vessels were dazzle-painted.

Later work

Wilkinson was an official war artist in both world wars and painted the well-known series of images of the D-Day landings. His marine paintings are held by the National Maritime Museum and other collections.
