Air ambulance

An air ambulance is an aircraft — usually a helicopter or fixed-wing aeroplane — specially equipped to transport sick or injured patients to medical facilities, particularly where ground transport would be too slow or impossible. Air ambulances carry medical equipment such as ventilators, monitors, and defibrillators, and are staffed by flight medics, nurses, and sometimes physicians.

Operations

Rotor-wing services typically respond to accident scenes and inter-hospital transfers within a few hundred kilometres, landing at scenes or hospital helipads. Fixed-wing air ambulances handle long-distance and international repatriation flights. The speed advantage is often framed by the "golden hour" concept in trauma care, in which rapid delivery to definitive treatment improves survival.

History

Military aeromedical evacuation began with balloons and early aeroplanes in the First World War and expanded greatly with helicopters in the Korean and Vietnam Wars, where rapid evacuation of wounded soldiers cut mortality. Civilian helicopter EMS programmes began in the late 1960s and 1970s; in the United Kingdom many services are provided by charities such as London's Air Ambulance.

Criticism and cost

Air ambulance use has been debated over cost and medical necessity, particularly in the United States, where surprise billing by private operators prompted legislative attention. Safety has also been a concern, as emergency medical helicopters operate at low altitude in poor weather and at night.
