Phase velocity

Symbolvp
Formulavp = ω / k
Contrasted withGroup velocity

The phase velocity of a wave is the rate at which the phase of the wave propagates through space — that is, the speed at which a point of constant phase, such as a wave crest, travels. For a sinusoidal wave with angular frequency ω and wavenumber k, the phase velocity is

vp = ω / k = λ f

Crests move at the phase velocity; the envelope moves at the group velocity

Phase velocity must be distinguished from group velocity, the speed at which the overall envelope of a wave packet — and with it, in ordinary circumstances, information and energy — propagates. In a non-dispersive medium the two are equal; in a dispersive medium they differ, and the phase velocity may exceed the speed of light, as it does for electromagnetic waves in a waveguide or for X-rays in matter. This does not violate relativity, because no signal travels at the phase velocity.

Matter waves

In quantum mechanics, particles are described by wave packets whose group velocity equals the particle velocity, while the phase velocity of the de Broglie waves is half that value for a non-relativistic particle. The concept also underlies the refractive index of a medium, defined as the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to the phase velocity in the medium.
