Caroline Herschel

Caroline Lucretia Herschel
Born: 16 March 1750, Hanover
Died: 9 January 1848, Hanover
Occupation: Astronomer, singer
Known for: Discovering eight comets

Caroline Lucretia Herschel (16 March 1750 – 9 January 1848) was a German astronomer, whose most significant contributions to astronomy were the discoveries of several comets, including the periodic comet 35P/Herschel–Rigollet. She was the younger sister of astronomer William Herschel, with whom she worked throughout her career.

Work with William Herschel

Herschel followed her brother to Bath, England, first training as a singer and performing as a soprano soloist. As William turned to astronomy, she became his indispensable assistant — grinding and polishing mirrors, recording observations, and performing the calculations for his catalogues of nebulae and star clusters. She reorganised and indexed Flamsteed's catalogue, work published by the Royal Society in 1798.

Discoveries

Using a small Newtonian sweeper of her own, she discovered eight comets between 1786 and 1797, as well as the galaxy NGC 253-adjacent object M110's companion discoveries and several nebulae. In 1787 George III granted her an annual salary of £50 as William's assistant — making her arguably the first woman in Britain to hold a salaried scientific post.

Honours

She received the Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society in 1828 and was elected an honorary member of the Society in 1835, alongside Mary Somerville — the first women so honoured. The lunar crater C. Herschel bears her name.
