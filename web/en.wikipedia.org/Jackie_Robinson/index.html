Jackie Robinson

Jack Roosevelt Robinson (1919 – 1972) was an American professional baseball player who became the first African American to play in Major League Baseball in the modern era, breaking the colour line when he debuted for the Brooklyn Dodgers on 15 April 1947.

Born31 January 1919, Cairo, Georgia
Died24 October 1972, Stamford, Connecticut
PositionSecond baseman
TeamsBrooklyn Dodgers (1947–1956)
Number42 (retired league-wide)

Career

Signed by executive Branch Rickey, Robinson endured racial abuse with public restraint while winning the inaugural Rookie of the Year award in 1947 and the National League MVP in 1949. He helped the Dodgers to six pennants and the 1955 World Series championship and was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1962.

Legacy

Robinson was a civil rights advocate before and after baseball, and a businessman and columnist after retiring. Major League Baseball retired his number 42 across all teams in 1997, and 15 April is celebrated annually as Jackie Robinson Day, when all players wear 42.
