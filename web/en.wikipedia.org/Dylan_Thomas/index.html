Dylan Marlais Thomas (27 October 1914 – 9 November 1953) was a Welsh poet and writer whose works include the poems "Do not go gentle into that good night" and "And death shall have no dominion", the "play for voices" Under Milk Wood, and stories and radio broadcasts such as A Child's Christmas in Wales and Portrait of the Artist as a Young Dog. He became widely popular in his lifetime and remained so after his premature death at the age of 39 in New York City.

Life

Born in Swansea, Thomas left school at sixteen to become a reporter for the South Wales Daily Post. His first collection, 18 Poems (1934), published when he was twenty, established his reputation for dense, musical, image-rich verse. He married Caitlin Macnamara in 1937; they had three children and lived a famously turbulent, impoverished life, eventually settling at the Boathouse in Laugharne, Carmarthenshire.

Work

During the Second World War Thomas wrote film scripts and BBC broadcasts; his celebrated radio readings later made him a sensation on American lecture tours in the early 1950s, where his booming voice and heavy drinking became legendary. Under Milk Wood, a portrait of a fictional Welsh seaside town, was first performed in New York in 1953 and broadcast by the BBC after his death.

Death and legacy

Thomas collapsed at the Chelsea Hotel during his fourth American tour and died in hospital on 9 November 1953; pneumonia and the effects of alcohol were implicated, and the exact causes remain debated. He influenced generations of poets and musicians; Bob Dylan adopted his stage surname partly in homage, and the Dylan Thomas Prize and the Dylan Thomas Centre in Swansea commemorate his work.
