New Glenn is a heavy-lift orbital launch vehicle developed by the American company Blue Origin. Named after John Glenn, the first American to orbit the Earth, the two-stage rocket stands about 98 metres tall with a 7-metre-diameter fairing, and its first stage is designed to be reusable for at least 25 flights, landing on a ship downrange.

Design

The first stage is powered by seven BE-4 engines burning liquefied natural gas and liquid oxygen, while the second stage uses two BE-3U engines burning liquid hydrogen and oxygen. New Glenn is designed to deliver up to 45 metric tons to low Earth orbit and 13 metric tons to geostationary transfer orbit.

History

Development began in 2012 and the vehicle was publicly announced in 2016. Its maiden flight, NG-1, launched from Cape Canaveral's Launch Complex 36 in January 2025, successfully reaching orbit with the Blue Ring prototype payload, although the first-stage booster was lost during its landing attempt.
