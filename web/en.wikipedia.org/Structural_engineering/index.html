Structural engineering

Structural engineering is a sub-discipline of civil engineering in which structural engineers are trained to design the 'bones and joints' that create the form and shape of human-made structures. Structural engineers must understand and calculate the stability, strength, rigidity and earthquake susceptibility of built structures for buildings and nonbuilding structures.

Scope

The structural engineer designs load-bearing elements — beams, columns, slabs, trusses, foundations, shells, and cables — in materials including steel, reinforced and prestressed concrete, timber, and masonry. The design must resist dead loads, live loads, wind, snow, seismic forces, and other actions, while satisfying requirements for safety, serviceability (deflection, vibration, cracking), durability, and economy.

History

Structural design is as old as monumental building, from Egyptian pyramids and Roman concrete to Gothic cathedrals. The scientific theory of structures developed from the 17th century onward with Galileo's and Hooke's work on strength of materials, Euler's column formula, and Navier's beam theory. The 19th and 20th centuries brought structural steel, reinforced concrete, and finally computer analysis, including the finite element method.

Analysis and codes

Modern practice uses structural analysis — statics, matrix methods, and finite element modelling — to predict stresses and deformations. Designs are checked against building codes such as Eurocode, ASCE 7, and the International Building Code, which specify load combinations and safety factors. In most jurisdictions structural engineers must be licensed professional engineers.
