World War II in Yugoslavia

World War II in Yugoslavia began on 6 April 1941, when the Kingdom of Yugoslavia was swiftly conquered by the Axis powers and partitioned among Germany, Italy, Hungary, and Bulgaria, with puppet states established including the Independent State of Croatia. The ensuing conflict combined a war of national liberation against the occupiers with a civil war between the communist-led Partisans under Josip Broz Tito, the royalist Chetniks, and collaborationist forces.

6 April 1941 – 25 May 1945
Location
Kingdom of Yugoslavia

Invasion and partition

After Yugoslavia's government signed the Tripartite Pact in March 1941 and was promptly overthrown in a coup, Hitler ordered an invasion. Belgrade was bombed, the Yugoslav army collapsed within eleven days, and the country was dismembered. The Ustaše-led Independent State of Croatia pursued genocidal policies against Serbs, Jews, and Roma.

Resistance

Two resistance movements emerged: the Chetniks of Draža Mihailović, loyal to the government-in-exile, and the Partisans, organised by the Communist Party of Yugoslavia. The Partisans built a mass liberation movement, proclaimed liberated territories such as the Republic of Užice and the Bihać Republic, and by 1943 had won Allied recognition as the principal anti-Axis force at the Tehran Conference.

Liberation

In October 1944 the Partisans and the Red Army liberated Belgrade. By May 1945 the Partisans controlled the whole country. Yugoslavia was unique in Eastern Europe in liberating itself largely by its own efforts, and Tito emerged as head of a new federal socialist state.

Casualties

Yugoslavia suffered among the highest proportional losses in Europe. Official postwar Yugoslav figures claimed 1.7 million deaths; later demographic research has generally estimated around one million actual war-related deaths.
