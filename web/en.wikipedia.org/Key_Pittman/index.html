Key Pittman

Key Pittman (September 12, 1872 – November 10, 1940) was an American politician and lawyer who represented Nevada in the United States Senate from 1913 until his death in 1940. A Democrat, he chaired the Senate Foreign Relations Committee from 1933 to 1940.

Early life

Pittman was born in Vicksburg, Mississippi. After studying law he travelled widely, joining the Klondike Gold Rush in Alaska, where he practised law, before settling in Tonopah, Nevada, during its silver boom.

Senate career

Elected to the Senate in 1912, Pittman became an influential advocate for the silver mining industry, authoring the Pittman Act of 1918 and supporting silver purchase legislation. As chairman of the Foreign Relations Committee during Franklin D. Roosevelt's presidency, he helped steer the Neutrality Acts through Congress. He died in Reno, Nevada, shortly after winning re-election in 1940.
