The 2023 SAG-AFTRA strike was a labour strike by the Screen Actors Guild – American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (SAG-AFTRA) against the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP). Beginning on 14 July 2023 and ending on 9 November 2023 after 118 days, it was the longest actors' strike in Hollywood history. Together with the concurrent Writers Guild of America strike, it shut down most American film and scripted television production.

Causes

The union, led by president Fran Drescher and chief negotiator Duncan Crabtree-Ireland, sought higher base pay, improved residuals for streaming content, and protections against the unconsented use of artificial intelligence. The AI dispute centred on proposals to scan background performers and reuse their digital likenesses in perpetuity — described by the union as an existential threat to performers' livelihoods. SAG-AFTRA's members authorised the strike by 98 percent after talks and a federal mediation failed.

Course and effects

Under strike rules, the union's roughly 160,000 members ceased acting work and, unusually, stopped all promotional activity, emptying red carpets and film festivals of stars. Premieres were postponed, blockbusters such as Dune: Part Two were delayed, and the Emmy Awards moved from September to January. Studio executives' confrontational remarks drew public criticism, while striking writers and actors drew broad public support. Interim agreements allowed some independent productions to continue.

Settlement

A tentative agreement was reached on 8 November 2023, and the strike ended at 12:01 a.m. on 9 November. The deal, valued by the union at over $1 billion, included the first-ever streaming participation bonus, wage increases, and a framework requiring informed consent and compensation for the creation and use of digital replicas of performers, living or deceased. Members ratified the contract in December with 78 percent voting in favour.
