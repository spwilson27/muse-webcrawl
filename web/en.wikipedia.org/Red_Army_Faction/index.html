The Red Army Faction (German: Rote Armee Fraktion, RAF), also known popularly as the Baader–Meinhof Group or Gang, was a far-left militant organisation founded in West Germany in 1970. It described itself as a communist urban guerrilla movement engaged in armed resistance against what it called the imperialism of the West German state and the United States. The group was responsible for bombings, assassinations, kidnappings, and bank robberies over nearly three decades, and is blamed for 34 deaths.

The RAF emblem: a red star superimposed with an MP5 submachine gun.

Origins

The RAF emerged from the West German student movement of 1968 and protests against the Vietnam War, the Springer press, and the perceived continuity of former Nazis in the West German establishment. Its founding followed the 1970 armed freeing of Andreas Baader from custody, organised with journalist Ulrike Meinhof. Members trained with the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine in Jordan.

The German Autumn

The first generation's leaders were arrested in 1972. In 1977, the "German Autumn", a second generation escalated violence to free them: industrialist Hanns Martin Schleyer was kidnapped and murdered, and the Lufthansa Flight 181 (Landshut) was hijacked by allied Palestinian militants. After German GSG 9 commandos stormed the aircraft in Mogadishu on 18 October 1977, Baader, Ensslin, and Jan-Carl Raspe died in Stammheim Prison that night; Irmgard Möller survived. The state maintains they committed suicide, a conclusion long disputed by sympathisers.

Later years and dissolution

A third generation continued assassinations into the 1980s and early 1990s, killing banker Alfred Herrhausen (1989) and Treuhand chief Detlev Rohwedder (1991). After German reunification, evidence emerged that some members had been sheltered by the East German Stasi. In April 1998 the group sent a communiqué to Reuters announcing its dissolution: "Almost 28 years ago, on 14 May 1970, the RAF was born in an act of liberation. Today we end this project."
