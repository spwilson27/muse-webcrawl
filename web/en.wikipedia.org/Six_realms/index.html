Six realms

The six realms, also known as the six modes of existence, are the six categories of rebirth in Buddhist cosmology within the cycle of samsara. Sentient beings are reborn into one of these realms according to their karma. The six realms are conventionally depicted in the Bhavacakra, or Wheel of Life, popular in Tibetan Buddhism.

The six realms

  • Gods (deva) — the realm of the gods, characterized by pleasure and long life, but still subject to death and rebirth; beings here may become complacent and neglect the path to liberation.
  • Demigods (asura) — powerful beings consumed by jealousy, envy, and conflict, forever fighting the gods.
  • Humans (manuṣya) — the human realm, considered the most favorable for attaining enlightenment because it offers a balance of suffering and opportunity.
  • Animals (tiryagyoni) — the realm of animals, dominated by instinct, ignorance, and being preyed upon.
  • Hungry ghosts (preta) — beings tormented by insatiable hunger and thirst, depicted with huge bellies and tiny mouths.
  • Hell (naraka) — realms of intense suffering, hot and cold hells, where beings endure torment until their negative karma is exhausted.

Interpretation

The realms are understood both as literal places of rebirth and, in many modern and traditional readings, as psychological states that beings may experience within a single lifetime. In some earlier schemes five realms were taught, with gods and demigods counted together. Liberation from all six realms — nirvana — is the goal of Buddhist practice.
