Melodic minor scale

In music theory, the melodic minor scale is a variant of the natural minor scale in which the sixth and seventh degrees are raised when ascending, while the traditional natural minor form is used when descending. Thus in A melodic minor, the ascending scale is A–B–C–D–E–F♯–G♯–A, and the descending scale is A–G♮–F♮–E–D–C–B–A.

Rationale

The raised seventh creates a leading tone that pulls strongly back to the tonic, as in the harmonic minor scale, while raising the sixth as well avoids the awkward augmented second between the flattened sixth and raised seventh found in harmonic minor, producing a smoother melodic line for stepwise motion. Composers of the Baroque and Classical periods used the form flexibly according to melodic direction rather than as a rigid rule.

Jazz melodic minor

In jazz theory, "melodic minor" usually refers to the ascending form used both ascending and descending (sometimes called "jazz minor"), together with its modes, such as the Lydian dominant scale (fourth mode) and the altered scale (seventh mode), which are widely used in improvisation.
