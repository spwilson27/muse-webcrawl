Vacuum permeability

Vacuum permeability (μ0)
Value1.25663706127(20) × 10−6 H/m (approx.)
Unithenry per metre (H/m), or newton per ampere squared (N/A²)
StatusMeasured constant since the 2019 SI redefinition

The vacuum permeability, also called the permeability of free space, the magnetic constant or μ0 (pronounced "mu nought" or "mu zero"), is the proportionality constant between magnetic induction and magnetic field strength in classical vacuum. It relates the magnetic field produced by an electric current to the current producing it in free space, and together with the vacuum permittivity ε0 it determines the speed of light in vacuum.

The force between two current-carrying wires historically defined μ0 through the ampere.

Value and the SI redefinition

Before 2019, μ0 had the exact defined value 4π × 10−7 H/m, a consequence of the former definition of the ampere as the current that produces a force of 2 × 10−7 newtons per metre between two infinitely long parallel conductors one metre apart in vacuum. In the 2019 redefinition of the SI base units, the ampere was instead defined by fixing the elementary charge, so μ0 became a quantity determined experimentally. Its current recommended value is approximately 1.25663706127 × 10−6 H/m, with a small relative uncertainty of about 1.6 × 10−10. The measured value deviates from the old exact value by only about one part in ten billion, so practical electromagnetism is unaffected, but conceptually μ0 is now tied to the fine-structure constant through the relation μ0 = 2αh/(e²c).

Role in electromagnetism

In Ampère's law and the Biot–Savart law, μ0 sets the scale of magnetic fields from currents: for example, the field around a long straight wire is B = μ0I/(2πr). In materials, the permeability μ = μrμ0 generalises the constant, with μr the relative permeability. The combination of μ0 and ε0 gives the speed of light, c = 1/√(ε0μ0), a cornerstone of Maxwell's theory predicting electromagnetic waves. μ0 also appears in the definition of the characteristic impedance of free space, about 376.73 Ω, and in the magnetic energy density u = B²/(2μ0).

Terminology

Standards organisations prefer "magnetic constant" for μ0, reserving "permeability" for material properties, but "vacuum permeability" and "permeability of free space" remain in widespread use in textbooks.
