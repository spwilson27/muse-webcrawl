The Second French Empire (Second Empire) was the regime of Napoleon III from 1852 to 1870, between the Second and Third Republics. Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte, nephew of Napoleon I, was elected President of the Republic in 1848; blocked from re-election, he seized power in the coup of 2 December 1851 and, after a plebiscite, proclaimed himself Emperor a year later.

The authoritarian and liberal empires

The 1850s "Authoritarian Empire" combined universal male suffrage plebiscites with press controls and police repression, while delivering rapid modernisation: railways, the Crédit Mobilier, free-trade treaties, and Baron Haussmann's rebuilding of Paris with its boulevards, parks and sewers. From 1860 the regime liberalised — freer trade, legalised strikes (1864), a stronger parliament — evolving toward the "Liberal Empire" confirmed by the plebiscite of May 1870.

Foreign policy and fall

Abroad, France fought alongside Britain in the Crimean War, helped Piedmont against Austria in 1859 (gaining Nice and Savoy), expanded in Indochina, Senegal and Algeria — and disastrously intervened in Mexico (1862–67), installing Maximilian as emperor before withdrawing under U.S. pressure. The rise of Prussia under Bismarck outmanoeuvred Napoleon III; in the Franco-Prussian War the emperor was captured at Sedan on 2 September 1870, and the Empire collapsed two days later with the proclamation of the Republic in Paris.
