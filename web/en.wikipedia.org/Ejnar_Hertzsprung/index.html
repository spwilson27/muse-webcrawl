Ejnar Hertzsprung

Ejnar Hertzsprung (Danish: [ˈɑjnɐ ˈhɛɐ̯tsˌpʰʁɔŋ]; 8 October 1873 – 21 October 1967) was a Danish chemist and astronomer. He is best known for discovering that stars of the same colour can have very different luminosities — distinguishing the giant and dwarf stars — and for the diagram plotting stellar luminosity against temperature that he devised independently of Henry Norris Russell, known as the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram.

Work

Trained as a chemical engineer, Hertzsprung turned to astronomy and worked at observatories in Copenhagen, Potsdam, Göttingen and Leiden, where he became director of Leiden Observatory in 1935. In papers of 1905 and 1907 he showed from proper motions and spectra that red stars fall into two luminosity classes, and his 1911 diagram for stars in the Pleiades and Hyades clusters was among the first colour–magnitude diagrams.

Legacy

The Hertzsprung–Russell diagram remains fundamental to the study of stellar evolution. Hertzsprung also contributed to stellar photometry, discovered two asteroids, and measured the distance to the Small Magellanic Cloud using Cepheid variables. The lunar crater Hertzsprung and the asteroid 1693 Hertzsprung are named for him.
