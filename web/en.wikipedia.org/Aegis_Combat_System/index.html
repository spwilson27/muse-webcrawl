Aegis Combat System

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Aegis Combat System is an American integrated naval weapons system developed by the Missile and Surface Radar Division of RCA, and now produced by Lockheed Martin. It uses powerful computer and radar technology to track and guide weapons to destroy enemy targets.

Overview

The system is designed as a total weapon system, from detection to kill. The heart of Aegis is the AN/SPY-1, an advanced automatic detect-and-track, multi-function, three-dimensional phased array radar. The radar can perform search, track, and missile guidance functions simultaneously, tracking well over 100 targets at a time.

The name "Aegis" refers to the shield of the Greek god Zeus. Initially used by the United States Navy, Aegis is now also used by the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force, Spanish Navy, Royal Norwegian Navy, Republic of Korea Navy, and Royal Australian Navy. Aegis-equipped ships form the core of many navies' air and missile defense capability.

Components

The principal elements of the Aegis Weapon System are the AN/SPY-1 radar, the command and decision system, the weapon control system, the fire control system, the vertical launching system (Mk 41 VLS), and the missiles themselves — principally the Standard Missile family (SM-2, SM-3, and SM-6).

Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense

The Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System is a program of the United States Missile Defense Agency to use Aegis-equipped ships, with the SM-3 interceptor, to engage short- to intermediate-range ballistic missiles during their midcourse phase. A land-based derivative, Aegis Ashore, has been deployed in Romania and Poland.

History

The system was first deployed operationally aboard the cruiser USS Ticonderoga in 1983, the lead ship of the Ticonderoga-class cruisers. It was later installed on the Arleigh Burke-class destroyers, which were designed around the system. Successive software "baselines" have added capabilities including cooperative engagement and ballistic missile defense.
