Taunus

The Taunus is a low mountain range in the German state of Hesse, lying north of Frankfurt am Main between the Rhine, Main, and Lahn rivers. Part of the Rhenish Massif, it stretches about 75 km from southwest to northeast. Its highest peak is the Großer Feldberg at 878 metres (some surveys say 881 m), topped by a telecommunications and observation tower and popular with hikers.

Geography and settlement

The range falls in three broad parts: the Vordertaunus facing the Rhine-Main plain, the wooded High Taunus ridge (Hoher Taunus), and the more rural Hintertaunus to the north. Wealthy suburbs of Frankfurt — including Königstein, Kronberg, Oberursel, and Bad Homburg — cluster on its southern slopes, and the area is noted for spa towns and mineral springs. Much of the range is forested, chiefly with beech and spruce, and protected within nature parks (Naturpark Taunus and Naturpark Rhein-Taunus).

History

The Taunus was crossed by the Roman frontier works, the Limes, and the reconstructed Roman fort of the Saalburg near Bad Homburg is a major museum site and part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire. Medieval castles, the resort culture of the 19th century, and the region's role as a retreat for Frankfurt's merchants have all left marks on its landscape.
