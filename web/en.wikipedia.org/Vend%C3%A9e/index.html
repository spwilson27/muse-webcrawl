Vendée

Vendée is a department in the Pays de la Loire region of western France, on the Atlantic coast, named after the river Vendée. Its prefecture is La Roche-sur-Yon. The name is most widely known historically for the counter-revolutionary War in the Vendée (1793–1796) during the French Revolution.

Geography

The department includes coastal plains and marshes (the Marais Poitevin in the south and Marais Breton in the north), the bocage countryside of hedgerows inland, and the offshore islands of Noirmoutier and Yeu. The seaside resort of Les Sables-d'Olonne is the start and finish of the Vendée Globe solo round-the-world yacht race.

The War in the Vendée

In March 1793, resentment at conscription, the Civil Constitution of the Clergy, and the execution of Louis XVI ignited a royalist and Catholic uprising. The rebels formed the Catholic and Royal Army under leaders such as Jacques Cathelineau, Maurice d'Elbée, and François de Charette. After initial victories they were defeated at Cholet and Savenay; republican "infernal columns" then devastated the region, causing very large numbers of deaths and a long historical controversy over the scale and character of the repression.

Modern department

Today the Vendée is known for its beaches, the Puy du Fou historical theme park, agriculture, and maritime industries. Its population has grown steadily, making it one of France's more dynamic rural departments.
