Run batted in

AbbreviationRBI
PluralRBIs (also "runs batted in")
Career MLB leaderHank Aaron (2,297)
Single-season MLB leaderHack Wilson (191, 1930)

A run batted in (RBI) is a statistic in baseball and softball that credits a batter for making a play that allows a run to be scored, unless the run scores on an error or on a ground-ball double play. For example, a batter is credited with an RBI for a home run he hits himself, for a base hit that drives in a runner, for a bases-loaded walk or hit-by-pitch, and for a sacrifice fly or sacrifice bunt.

Rules

Under Major League Baseball rules, a batter receives an RBI when a run scores as the direct result of his plate appearance, except when the run scores because the batter grounded into a double play or because of an error on a play where an out was expected. Pitchers in leagues without the designated hitter are credited under the same rules.

Records

Hank Aaron holds the Major League career record with 2,297 RBIs, followed by Babe Ruth and Albert Pujols. The single-season record of 191 was set by Hack Wilson of the Chicago Cubs in 1930. Japanese professional baseball's career leader is Sadaharu Oh.

Importance

The RBI has traditionally been regarded as a measure of "clutch" hitting and was one of the three components of the Triple Crown. Modern sabermetric analysis considers raw RBI totals heavily dependent on opportunities — how many runners are on base when a batter comes to the plate — and therefore prefers rate-based and context-adjusted measures for evaluating hitters.
