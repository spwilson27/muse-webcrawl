Pressure

Pressure is force divided by the area over which it acts.

Pressure (symbol: p or P) is the force applied perpendicular to the surface of an object per unit area over which that force is distributed:

p = F / A

The SI unit for pressure is the pascal (Pa), equal to one newton per square metre (N/m²). Other common units include the bar, the standard atmosphere (atm ≈ 101,325 Pa) and millimetres of mercury (mmHg), still used for blood pressure. Pressure is a scalar quantity related to the energy density of a medium; in gases it arises from molecular collisions with container walls, as described by the ideal gas law.

Types and applications

Absolute pressure is measured relative to a vacuum, while gauge pressure is relative to the surrounding atmospheric pressure. In fluids at rest, pressure increases with depth according to the hydrostatic relation p = ρgh, a principle exploited in barometers, manometers and hydraulics (Pascal's law). Partial pressure in gas mixtures follows Dalton's law. Pressure is central to weather systems, thermodynamics (where it is a conjugate variable to volume), acoustics (sound pressure), and physiology (blood pressure, intraocular pressure).
