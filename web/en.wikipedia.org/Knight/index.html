Knight

Mounted warrior and member of the lower nobility in medieval Europe, bound by the code of chivalry and usually holding land in return for military service.

A knight is a person granted an honorary title of knighthood by a monarch or other political leader for service to the sovereign or country, especially in a military capacity. Historically, in Europe, knighthood was conferred upon mounted warriors: during the High Middle Ages the knight was a member of the lower nobility who fought as heavy cavalry, typically having been raised through the stages of page and squire.

Origins

The institution developed in the early Middle Ages from the mounted retainers of Frankish rulers; the Latin miles and the rise of the stirrup-equipped armoured horseman gave mounted warriors growing social importance. By the 11th and 12th centuries, knighthood had become a hereditary social rank associated with landholding (the knight's fee), heraldry, and the feudal obligation of military service.

Chivalry

From the 12th century a code of conduct — chivalry — bound knights to ideals of prowess, loyalty, courtesy, generosity, and protection of the weak, coloured by Christian teaching and by the romances of courtly literature. The accolade (dubbing) ceremony, often preceded by a vigil of prayer, formally made a squire a knight. Military orders such as the Knights Templar, Hospitallers, and Teutonic Knights combined monastic vows with warfare during the Crusades.

Tournaments and warfare

Knights trained and displayed their skills in tournaments — melees, jousts, and pas d'armes — while in battle they charged with couched lance in close formation. Improvements in infantry tactics, the longbow and pike, and finally gunpowder weapons eroded the knight's battlefield dominance between the 14th and 16th centuries.

Later and modern knighthood

As feudal armies declined, knighthood survived as an honour. Modern orders of knighthood (such as the British orders of chivalry) are awarded for public service, arts, and science; recipients of knighthoods bachelor use the title "Sir" (or "Dame" for women). The concept also endures in popular culture, from Arthurian legend to film and games.
