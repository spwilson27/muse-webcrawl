A gharana (from the Urdu/Hindi word for "house" or "family") is a system of organising musicians by stylistic lineage in Hindustani classical music of northern India, Pakistan and Nepal. Gharanas are distinguished by distinctive approaches to raga interpretation, voice production or instrumental technique, repertoire, and ornamentation, and are usually named after the place where the founding family or teacher lived.

Origins and transmission

The gharana system crystallised in the 19th and early 20th centuries, when court musicians settled in particular towns and their styles were passed down through hereditary families and devoted disciples. Training traditionally follows the guru–shishya parampara, in which a student lives with and serves a master for years while absorbing the style through imitation. Instruments such as the tabla, sitar and sarod, and dance forms such as Kathak, also developed gharanas.

Well-known gharanas

Among vocal khyal gharanas, Gwalior is regarded as the oldest; others include Agra, Kirana (associated with Abdul Karim Khan and Bhimsen Joshi), Jaipur-Atrauli (associated with Kesarbai Kerkar and Kishori Amonkar), Patiala (associated with Bade Ghulam Ali Khan) and Rampur-Sahaswan. In the modern era, recording, concert travel and music institutions have blurred gharana boundaries, and many performers combine elements from several schools, though gharana identity remains a marker of pedigree and style.
