Iranian Kurdistan

Iranian Kurdistan or Eastern Kurdistan (Kurdish: ڕۆژھەڵاتی کوردستان, Rojhilatê Kurdistanê) is the unofficial name for the parts of western and northwestern Iran inhabited by Kurds which borders Iraq and Turkey. It includes the provinces of Kurdistan, Kermanshah, West Azerbaijan, Ilam, and Lorestan, and is one of the four parts of Greater Kurdistan, alongside southeastern Turkey (Northern Kurdistan), northern Iraq (Southern Kurdistan), and northern Syria (Western Kurdistan).

Demographics

Iranian Kurdistan covers roughly 111,000 km2 and is the second largest Kurdistan region. The majority of Kurds in Iran speak Sorani, Kurmanji, Southern Kurdish, or Laki, and most are Sunni Muslims, though there are significant Shia Kurdish communities in the southern provinces.

History

In 1946 the short-lived Republic of Mahabad was proclaimed in the northwest with Soviet support, before being crushed by Iranian forces after the Soviet withdrawal. Kurdish political movements in Iran, including the KDPI and Komala, have periodically campaigned for autonomy. The region saw armed conflict after the 1979 revolution and again during the Iran–Iraq War.
