Beaufort scale

The Beaufort scale is an empirical measure that relates wind speed to observed conditions at sea or on land. The scale was devised in 1805 by Irish Royal Navy officer Francis Beaufort on HMS Woolwich and originally described the effect of wind on a full-rigged man-of-war's sails. It was standardised in the 19th century and later extended to describe sea state and land conditions, and remains in use in marine weather forecasts.

History and use

Beaufort's scale was adopted by the Royal Navy in the 1830s and by international agreement in the following decades. In 1946 the scale was extended to force 17 for extreme tropical cyclones, an extension used mainly in China and Taiwan. The United Kingdom's Met Office and the BBC's Shipping Forecast still report winds in Beaufort terms. Modern wind speeds are measured by anemometer, but the scale's descriptive terms — calm, breeze, gale, storm — remain everyday vocabulary.
