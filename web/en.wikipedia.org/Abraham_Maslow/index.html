Abraham Harold Maslow (1908–1970) was an American psychologist best known for creating Maslow's hierarchy of needs, a theory of human motivation. A leading figure in humanistic psychology — the "third force" alongside behaviourism and psychoanalysis — he emphasised the study of healthy, self-fulfilled people rather than mental illness.

Hierarchy of needs

In his 1943 paper "A Theory of Human Motivation", Maslow proposed that human needs form a hierarchy: physiological needs and safety needs must largely be satisfied before belongingness and love, esteem, and finally self-actualisation — the fulfilment of one's potential. The hierarchy is commonly drawn as a pyramid, although Maslow himself never depicted it that way. He later added cognitive, aesthetic and transcendence needs to the model.

Career

Maslow earned his doctorate at the University of Wisconsin, where he studied dominance behaviour in primates. He taught at Brooklyn College (1937–1951) and then chaired the psychology department at Brandeis University. His books include Motivation and Personality (1954) and Toward a Psychology of Being (1962). He also popularised the observation that "if the only tool you have is a hammer, you tend to treat everything as if it were a nail".
