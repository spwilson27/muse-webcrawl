Ivan Danilovich Chernyakhovsky (29 June 1907 – 18 February 1945) was a Soviet military commander and twice Hero of the Soviet Union. He was the youngest front commander in the Red Army during the Second World War and one of its most highly regarded operational commanders.

Career

Born in Ukraine, Chernyakhovsky joined the Red Army in 1924 and rose rapidly through the armoured forces. At the outbreak of the German invasion in 1941 he commanded a tank division, and he later led the 60th Army at Voronezh and Kursk. In April 1944 he took command of the 3rd Belorussian Front, which played a central role in Operation Bagration, the destruction of German Army Group Centre, and in the liberation of Vilnius and the advance into East Prussia.

Death and legacy

Chernyakhovsky was mortally wounded by artillery fire near Mehlsack (now Pieniężno, Poland) in February 1945, during the East Prussian offensive. After the war, the East Prussian city of Insterburg was renamed Chernyakhovsk in his honour, and he remains the namesake of streets and monuments in several countries.
