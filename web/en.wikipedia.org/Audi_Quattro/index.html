The Audi Quattro is a road and rally car produced by the German automobile manufacturer Audi from 1980 to 1991. It was the first rally car to successfully use all-wheel drive, and it revolutionized rallying. The name has also been used for Audi's all-wheel-drive system itself (spelled quattro, lowercase).

History

Unveiled at the 1980 Geneva Motor Show, the Quattro combined a turbocharged five-cylinder engine with permanent four-wheel drive — a concept inspired by the Volkswagen Iltis military vehicle. Its success prompted the FIA to permit all-wheel drive in rallying, after which four-wheel drive became standard in the sport.

Rally success

The Quattro and its short-wheelbase Sport Quattro evolution won the World Rally Championship manufacturers' titles in 1982 and 1984, and drivers' titles with Hannu Mikkola (1983) and Stig Blomqvist (1984). Michèle Mouton became the first woman to win a WRC event driving a Quattro at the 1981 San Remo Rally. The final Group B S1 E2 version produced over 500 horsepower.

Legacy

Audi has applied the quattro badge to its all-wheel-drive road cars ever since, making it a central part of the brand's identity.
