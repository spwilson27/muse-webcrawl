Meseta Central
Location: central Iberian Peninsula, Spain
Average elevation: 610–760 m
Area: approx. 210,000 km²

The Meseta Central (literally "central tableland") is a vast high plateau in the centre of the Iberian Peninsula, the geographic heart of Spain. Covering roughly two-fifths of the country at an average elevation of 600–800 metres, it is divided by the Sistema Central mountain range into a northern sub-plateau (Castile and León) and a southern one (Castile-La Mancha, Extremadura and Madrid).

Geography and climate

The Meseta is ringed by mountain ranges — the Cantabrian Mountains to the north, the Iberian System to the east and the Sierra Morena to the south — and drains westward through the Duero, Tagus and Guadiana rivers to the Atlantic. Its continental Mediterranean climate brings hot, dry summers, cold winters with frost and snow in the north, and low rainfall concentrated in spring and autumn. The landscape of plains, low hills and dehesa supports dry farming of cereals, vineyards, olives and sheep grazing.

Significance

The plateau is Spain's demographic and political core: Madrid, the capital, stands near its centre, alongside historic cities such as Toledo, Salamanca, Valladolid, Burgos, León and Segovia. The Meseta's openness shaped the history of Castile — from the Reconquista frontier to the sheep-herding Mesta — and its dialects formed standard Castilian Spanish.
