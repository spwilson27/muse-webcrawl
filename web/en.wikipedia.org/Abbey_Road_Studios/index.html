Abbey Road Studios (formerly EMI Recording Studios) is a recording studio at 3 Abbey Road, St John's Wood, City of Westminster, London. Established in November 1931 by the Gramophone Company, a predecessor of British music company EMI, it is best known as the recording venue of the Beatles, who used it extensively in the 1960s and named their 1969 album Abbey Road after the street, featuring the famous zebra crossing photograph taken outside the studio.

History

The nine-bedroom Georgian townhouse was converted into the world's first purpose-built recording studios, opened with a performance of Elgar's Land of Hope and Glory conducted by Sir Edward Elgar. Studio Two became the Beatles' main room, where producer George Martin and engineers including Geoff Emerick and Ken Scott recorded nearly all of the band's catalogue. Studio One, one of the largest purpose-built recording spaces in the world, is renowned for orchestral and film-score sessions.

Notable recordings

Besides the Beatles, artists who recorded landmark albums at Abbey Road include Pink Floyd (The Dark Side of the Moon), Cliff Richard, Shirley Bassey, Oasis, Radiohead, Adele, Ed Sheeran and Lady Gaga. The studios' innovation department developed recording equipment and techniques, and the site remains a working studio as well as a tourist landmark; the zebra crossing outside was given Grade II listed status.
