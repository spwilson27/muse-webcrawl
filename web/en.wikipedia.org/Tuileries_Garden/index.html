Tuileries Garden

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tuileries Garden (French: Jardin des Tuileries) is a public garden located between the Louvre and the Place de la Concorde in the 1st arrondissement of Paris, France. Created by Catherine de' Medici as the garden of the Tuileries Palace in 1564, it was eventually opened to the public in 1667 and became a public park after the French Revolution.

History

The garden takes its name from the tile factories (tuileries) that previously stood on the site. Queen Catherine de' Medici, widow of Henry II, began the palace and garden in 1564 in the Italian style. Between 1660 and 1664 the garden was redesigned by André Le Nôtre, the celebrated gardener of Louis XIV, who gave it the formal French layout of straight avenues, parterres, ponds and terraces that it largely retains today.

The Tuileries Palace was burned by the Paris Commune in 1871 and demolished in 1883, but the garden survived. It hosted events of the 1900 Summer Olympics and served as a gathering place through both World Wars.

Description

Covering about 22.4 hectares (55 acres), the garden is aligned on the historical axis of Paris running from the Louvre through the Place de la Concorde and the Champs-Élysées to the Arc de Triomphe. Its features include the Grand Carré with its round and horseshoe ponds, the Grand Couvert wooded area, the Orangerie (now a museum of Impressionist and post-Impressionist painting, famed for Monet's Water Lilies), and the Jeu de Paume gallery.

Art and sculpture

The garden is an open-air museum of sculpture, with works by Aristide Maillol, Auguste Rodin, Antoine Coysevox and others, alongside contemporary installations. Cafés, fountains and the annual Fête des Tuileries funfair make it one of the most visited gardens in Paris.
