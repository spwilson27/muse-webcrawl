Voriconazole

Voriconazole
Trade names: Vfend, others
Class: Triazole antifungal
Routes: Intravenous, oral
ATC code: J02AC03

Voriconazole, sold under the brand name Vfend among others, is an antifungal medication used to treat a number of serious fungal infections. It is a second-generation triazole with a broad spectrum of activity against yeasts and moulds, including Aspergillus and Candida species.

Medical uses

Voriconazole is a first-line treatment for invasive aspergillosis and is also used for candidemia in non-neutropenic patients, oesophageal candidiasis and infections caused by Scedosporium and Fusarium. It is available both intravenously and orally, with high oral bioavailability.

Side effects and monitoring

Common adverse effects include transient visual disturbances, rash, photosensitivity, and elevations of liver enzymes. Voriconazole shows nonlinear pharmacokinetics and wide interpatient variability, and it is a potent inhibitor of cytochrome P450 enzymes, so blood-level monitoring and attention to drug interactions are important.

History

The drug was developed by Pfizer and approved for medical use in 2002. It appears on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
