Aristotelian logic

Aristotelian logic, also called term logic, is the system of reasoning developed by Aristotle and elaborated by his followers and later commentators. For more than two thousand years it was regarded in the Western tradition as the foundation of all logical inquiry.

The Organon

Aristotle's logical writings were collected by later editors into the Organon, comprising the Categories, On Interpretation, Prior Analytics, Posterior Analytics, Topics, and Sophistical Refutations. The Prior Analytics presents the theory of the syllogism, the core of the system.

The syllogism

A syllogism is a deductive argument consisting of two premises and a conclusion, each statement relating two terms through a copula. Statements are classified by quantity (universal or particular) and quality (affirmative or negative), giving the four categorical forms A, E, I, and O. Classic examples include: All men are mortal; Socrates is a man; therefore Socrates is mortal.

Square of opposition

The relations of contradiction, contrariety, subcontrariety, and subalternation among the four categorical forms are traditionally displayed in the square of opposition.

Influence

Aristotelian logic dominated Islamic and medieval European philosophy and remained the standard university curriculum until the rise of modern predicate logic in the 19th century with the work of Gottlob Frege and others.
