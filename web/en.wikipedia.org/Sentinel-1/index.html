Sentinel-1

Sentinel-1 is a pair of European radar-imaging satellites launched under the European Space Agency's Copernicus Programme. The mission carries a C-band synthetic-aperture radar (SAR) instrument that provides imagery in all weather conditions, day or night.

Overview

Sentinel-1A was launched on 3 April 2014 and Sentinel-1B on 25 April 2016, both aboard Soyuz rockets from Kourou, French Guiana. Operating in a polar, sun-synchronous orbit, the constellation was designed to revisit the same area every six days. Sentinel-1B suffered a power failure in December 2021 and its mission was declared ended in 2022; Sentinel-1C was launched in December 2024 to restore the two-satellite constellation.

Applications

Sentinel-1 data support monitoring of sea ice, icebergs and glaciers, oil spills and ship detection, ground deformation from earthquakes and subsidence via radar interferometry, flood mapping, and land-use change. All data are distributed free of charge under Copernicus's open data policy.

Instruments

The SAR instrument operates at 5.405 GHz and supports several modes, including Interferometric Wide Swath mode, the default over land, with a 250 km swath and 5 × 20 m resolution.
