Lake Forest, Illinois

Lake Forest is a city located in Lake County, Illinois, United States, on the shore of Lake Michigan. The population was 19,367 at the 2020 census. It is a suburb of Chicago and is noted for its historic estates, tree-lined streets, and downtown Market Square.

History

The Potawatomi people originally inhabited the area. Lake Forest was founded in 1857, the same year Lake Forest Academy was established, when Presbyterian clergy and business leaders sought a location for a university, a religious colony and a town. The city plan by landscape architect Almerin Hotchkiss preserved ravines and woodland. Lake Forest College opened in 1857.

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries wealthy Chicago families built large country estates, many designed by architects such as Howard Van Doren Shaw and landscape architects such as Jens Jensen. Market Square, completed in 1916 and designed by Shaw, is often cited as an early American shopping centre designed for the automobile.

Geography

Lake Forest lies along the North Shore of Lake Michigan, about 28 miles north of downtown Chicago. According to the U.S. Census Bureau it has a total area of about 17.3 square miles, of which a small portion is water. Ravines drain eastward to the lake.

Demographics

The city is predominantly residential and comparatively affluent. The 2020 census recorded 19,367 residents. Households are largely family households, and median household income is among the highest in Illinois.

Education

Public schools include Lake Forest High School, Deer Path Middle School, and elementary schools Cherokee, Everett and Sheridan. Private schools include Lake Forest Academy, Woodlands Academy of the Sacred Heart, and the School of St. Mary. Lake Forest College, a private liberal arts college, is located in the city.

Transportation

Metra's Milwaukee District North Line and Union Pacific North Line both serve stations in Lake Forest. U.S. Route 41 passes through the western part of the city.

Notable people

Residents have included author Dave Eggers, who set part of his memoir A Heartbreaking Work of Staggering Genius in Lake Forest, and many Chicago business and civic leaders.
