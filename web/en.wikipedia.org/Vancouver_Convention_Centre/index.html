The Vancouver Convention Centre (formerly known as the Vancouver Convention & Exhibition Centre) is a convention centre in downtown Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada, situated on the city's waterfront on Burrard Inlet. It consists of two buildings: the East building, located inside Canada Place (opened in 1987), and the West building, which opened on April 3, 2009.

West building

The West building is noted for its six-acre (2.4 hectare) living roof, the largest in Canada and the largest non-industrial living roof in North America, planted with more than 400,000 indigenous plants and grasses. Four beehives house European honey bees that pollinate the roof plants. The building's foundation includes an artificial reef habitat designed to support marine life, and its seawater heating and cooling system uses the inlet's water for temperature regulation. The building was a major venue precinct during the 2010 Winter Olympics, serving as the international broadcast centre.

Events

The centre hosts conferences, trade shows, and exhibitions throughout the year, including TED conference events and fan conventions. The Olympic Cauldron for the 2010 Winter Games stands on Jack Poole Plaza adjacent to the West building and is relit for special occasions.
