The Torres Strait Islands are a group of at least 274 small islands in the Torres Strait, the waterway separating far northern continental Australia's Cape York Peninsula from the island of New Guinea. The islands are part of the Australian state of Queensland and are home to the Torres Strait Islander peoples, a Melanesian people culturally distinct from mainland Aboriginal Australians.

Geography

The islands are grouped into five clusters: the Top Western, Western, Near Western, Central and Eastern island groups. They range from sandy cays to volcanic and continental islands. Thursday Island (Waiben) is the administrative and commercial centre of the strait.

People and culture

Torres Strait Islanders traditionally spoke two unrelated languages, Kala Lagaw Ya in the west and centre and Meriam Mir in the east, and today widely speak Torres Strait Creole. Islander culture is centred on the sea, gardening, dance and elaborate headdresses such as the dhari (dari). The Torres Strait Islander Flag, designed by Bernard Namok, was officially recognised in 1995.

Governance

Most of the islands are governed by the Torres Strait Regional Authority and local island councils. The region was central to the landmark Mabo case, brought by Eddie Mabo of Mer (Murray Island), which overturned the doctrine of terra nullius in Australian law in 1992.
