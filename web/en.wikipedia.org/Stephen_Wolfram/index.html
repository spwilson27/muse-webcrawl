Stephen Wolfram (born 1959) is a British-American computer scientist, physicist and businessman. A child prodigy who earned his PhD in particle physics from Caltech at age 20, he founded Wolfram Research in 1987.

Work

Wolfram created Mathematica, first released in 1988, and its underlying Wolfram Language. His 2002 book A New Kind of Science argued that simple computational systems such as cellular automata can generate great complexity and might underlie natural phenomena. In 2009 he launched WolframAlpha, a computational knowledge engine. His later Wolfram Physics Project seeks a fundamental theory of physics based on hypergraph rewriting.

Cellular automata

Wolfram systematized the study of one-dimensional cellular automata, classifying them into four classes by behaviour, and popularized rules such as Rule 30 and Rule 110, the latter proven computationally universal.
