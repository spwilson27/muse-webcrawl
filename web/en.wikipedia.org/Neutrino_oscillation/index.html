Neutrino oscillation

Neutrino oscillation is a quantum mechanical phenomenon in which a neutrino created with a specific lepton flavour (electron, muon, or tau) later measures as having a different flavour. It implies that neutrinos have nonzero mass, contrary to the original Standard Model of particle physics.

History

The idea was proposed by Bruno Pontecorvo in the 1950s and 1960s. The solar neutrino problem — detectors observed far fewer electron neutrinos from the Sun than predicted — was explained when the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory showed in 2001–2002 that the "missing" neutrinos had oscillated into other flavours. Atmospheric neutrino results from Super-Kamiokande in 1998 provided further evidence.

Theory

Oscillation arises because flavour states are superpositions of three mass states, described by the PMNS mixing matrix. The probability of flavour change depends on the mass-squared differences, mixing angles, and the distance travelled relative to the neutrino's energy.

Recognition

Takaaki Kajita and Arthur B. McDonald received the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics for the discovery of neutrino oscillations, which showed that neutrinos have mass.
