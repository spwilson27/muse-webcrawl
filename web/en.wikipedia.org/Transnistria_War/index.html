The Transnistria War (Romanian: Războiul din Transnistria) was an armed conflict that broke out on 2 March 1992 between the Republic of Moldova and separatist forces in the breakaway region of Transnistria, a narrow strip of territory on the east bank of the Dniester River with a largely Russian- and Ukrainian-speaking population. It was the violent culmination of tensions that had been building since Moldova's moves toward independence from the Soviet Union.

Background

As the USSR dissolved, Moldova's parliament adopted Romanian as the state language and raised the possibility of unification with Romania. Leaders in Tiraspol, fearing marginalisation and the loss of the region's heavy industry, proclaimed a separate Pridnestrovian Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic in 1990. Skirmishes between Moldovan police and separatist militias escalated through 1991, notably around Dubăsari.

Course of the war

Full-scale fighting began in March 1992 and intensified in May and June, with the heaviest battles around Dubăsari, Coşniţa and the city of Bender (Tighina) on the west bank. The intervention of units of the Russian 14th Guards Army, under General Alexander Lebed, on the separatist side proved decisive in halting the Moldovan advance on Bender in June 1992.

Ceasefire and aftermath

On 21 July 1992 Presidents Mircea Snegur of Moldova and Boris Yeltsin of Russia signed a ceasefire agreement in Moscow. A tripartite peacekeeping force of Moldovan, Russian and Transnistrian troops has since patrolled a security zone along the Dniester. The conflict remains "frozen": Transnistria functions as a de facto state with its own government, currency and army, but no UN member recognises it, and Russian troops remain stationed in the region.
