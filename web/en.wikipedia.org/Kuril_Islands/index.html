The Kuril Islands or Kuril Archipelago (Russian: Кури́льские острова́) is a volcanic archipelago in Russia's Sakhalin Oblast that stretches approximately 1,300 km (810 mi) northeast from Hokkaido in Japan to the Kamchatka Peninsula in Russia, separating the Sea of Okhotsk from the north Pacific Ocean.

Geography

There are 56 islands in the chain, most of them volcanic; the archipelago contains around 100 volcanoes, of which some 40 are active. Major islands include Iturup, Kunashir, Shikotan and the Habomai group in the south, Paramushir and Onekotan in the north, and Simushir and Urup in the centre. The climate is severe, with long, cold, stormy winters and short, foggy summers.

History

The islands were originally inhabited by the Ainu people. Russia and Japan first delineated their border in the Kurils by the Treaty of Shimoda (1855); by the Treaty of Saint Petersburg (1875), Japan gained the whole chain in exchange for Sakhalin. After the Second World War the Soviet Union occupied the islands, and they were incorporated into the USSR. The sovereignty of the four southernmost islands remains disputed by Japan, which calls them the Northern Territories.
