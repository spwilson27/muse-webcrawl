Theory of Forms

The theory of Forms (or theory of Ideas) is a philosophical doctrine developed by Plato: material objects in the sensible world are imperfect copies or participations of eternal, unchanging, abstract Forms — such as Beauty itself, Equality itself, or the Good.

Key dialogues

The theory appears in the Phaedo, Republic, and Symposium. In the allegory of the cave, prisoners mistake shadows for reality, just as people mistake sensible things for the Forms known by reason. The Form of the Good is the highest principle, the source of truth and being.

Criticism

Plato himself pressed objections in the Parmenides, including the "Third Man" regress: if a man resembles the Form of Man, a further Form seems needed to explain their common likeness. Aristotle rejected separately existing Forms, holding that form exists within things.
