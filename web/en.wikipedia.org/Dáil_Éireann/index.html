Dáil Éireann (Irish: [ˌd̪ˠaːlʲ ˈeːɾʲən̪ˠ], "Assembly of Ireland") is the lower house, and principal chamber, of the Oireachtas, the legislature of Ireland, which also comprises the president of Ireland and Seanad Éireann (the Senate). Its members, known as Teachtaí Dála (TDs), are directly elected at least once every five years under proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote (PR-STV).

History

The first Dáil was convened on 21 January 1919 in the Mansion House, Dublin, by Sinn Féin MPs elected in the 1918 general election, who refused to take their seats at Westminster and instead constituted themselves as the parliament of the declared Irish Republic, adopting a Declaration of Independence the same day. The Dáil was suppressed by the British authorities but persisted through the War of Independence. Under the 1922 Irish Free State constitution and the current Constitution of Ireland (1937), the Dáil has been the dominant chamber of the legislature, and it has sat at Leinster House since 1922.

Powers

The Dáil is by far the more powerful of the two houses. It nominates the Taoiseach (prime minister), who is then appointed by the president; it can pass a motion of no confidence forcing the government's resignation or a dissolution. Money bills originate in the Dáil, and the Seanad may only recommend amendments to legislation and delay most bills by at most 90 days. The government is responsible to the Dáil alone.

Elections and composition

Following boundary revisions under legislation passed in 2023, the Dáil has 174 members elected from 43 multi-seat constituencies. Every citizen over 21 may stand, and Irish and British citizens resident in the state may vote in general elections. Sessions are presided over by the Ceann Comhairle, elected by secret ballot at the first sitting after a general election.
