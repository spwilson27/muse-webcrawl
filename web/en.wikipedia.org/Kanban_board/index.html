Kanban board

A kanban board is a work-visualisation tool used to implement the kanban method for managing work. Work items are represented as cards that move through columns representing stages of a workflow — in the simplest layout, "To do," "Doing," and "Done" — allowing a team to see the state of every piece of work at a glance, limit work in progress, and identify bottlenecks.

Origins

The board derives from the kanban signalling system developed by Taiichi Ohno at Toyota in the late 1940s as part of the Toyota Production System, in which cards authorised the production or movement of parts in a just-in-time flow. In the 2000s, David J. Anderson and others adapted the idea to knowledge work, particularly software development, formalising practices such as visualising the workflow, setting explicit work-in-progress (WIP) limits per column, and managing flow.

Practice

Boards may be physical (cards on a whiteboard) or digital (as in tools such as Trello, Jira, and Azure Boards). Common refinements include swimlanes for different work types or priorities, color-coded card types, and pull policies defining when work may enter a column. In agile software development, kanban boards complement or contrast with time-boxed methods such as Scrum by emphasising continuous flow and cycle time.
