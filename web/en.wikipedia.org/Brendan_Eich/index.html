Roles: CTO and briefly CEO of Mozilla; CEO of Brave Software
Brendan Eich, creator of JavaScript

Brendan Eich (born 4 July 1961) is an American computer programmer and technology executive. He created the JavaScript programming language in 1995 while at Netscape, and co-founded the Mozilla project, serving as chief technical officer of the Mozilla Corporation and briefly as its chief executive in 2014. He is the founder and CEO of Brave Software, which develops the Brave web browser.

JavaScript

Eich joined Netscape in 1995 and, working under pressure to add a scripting language to the Netscape Navigator browser, created the first version of JavaScript in about ten days in May 1995. The language (initially called Mocha, then LiveScript) was standardised as ECMAScript, and became one of the core technologies of the web.

Mozilla and after

After AOL shuttered Netscape in 2003, Eich helped spin out the Mozilla Foundation. Appointed CEO of Mozilla Corporation in March 2014, he resigned after eleven days amid public controversy over a 2008 donation supporting California's Proposition 8. In 2015 he founded Brave Software; the Brave browser, which blocks ads and trackers by default and includes the Basic Attention Token (BAT) cryptocurrency system, launched in 2016.
