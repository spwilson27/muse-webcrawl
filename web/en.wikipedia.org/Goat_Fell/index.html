Goat Fell

Goat Fell (Scottish Gaelic: Gaoda Bheinn) is the highest point on the Isle of Arran, Scotland, at 874 metres (2,867 ft). It is one of four Corbetts on the island, and its distinctive granite summit ridge dominates the view from the Firth of Clyde and from the ferry approach to Brodick.

Geography and geology

The mountain lies near the village of Brodick on Arran's east coast, within the grounds of Brodick Castle and under the care of the National Trust for Scotland. It is composed of granite intruded into older schists around 60 million years ago during the opening of the North Atlantic, and its rough crags were sharpened by glaciation. The name may derive from the Gaelic gad (harsh wind) or from a Norse word for a steep peak; "fell" is the northern British term for a hill or mountain.

Ascent

The most popular route starts at Brodick Castle and climbs through woodland and open moorland beside the Cnocan Burn to the summit ridge, taking roughly 4–5 hours return. A longer horseshoe route continues over North Goatfell and down the ridge of Stacach to Corrie. On clear days the summit view extends to Ben Lomond, the Paps of Jura, Ailsa Craig and the coast of Ireland.

History

Goat Fell was the scene of one of Scotland's notorious Victorian crimes: in July 1889 Edwin Rose was murdered on the mountain by John Watson Laurie, who was convicted largely on circumstantial evidence and whose case remains debated.
