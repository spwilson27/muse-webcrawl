Twenty-fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution

Twenty-fourth Amendment

The Twenty-fourth Amendment prohibits poll taxes in federal elections.

Proposed: August 27, 1962
Ratified: January 23, 1964

The Twenty-fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution prohibits both Congress and the states from conditioning the right to vote in federal elections on payment of a poll tax or other types of tax. The amendment was proposed by Congress to the states on August 27, 1962, and was ratified by the requisite number of states on January 23, 1964.

Southern states of the former Confederacy adopted poll taxes in the late 19th century as a measure to prevent African Americans from voting following the Reconstruction Era and passage of the Fifteenth Amendment. After the introduction of poll taxes, voter turnout among African Americans and poor whites declined dramatically. The amendment addressed this practice, which survived legal challenges over the years, most notably in the Supreme Court case Breedlove v. Suttles (1937).

Background

The poll tax was one of several devices, alongside literacy tests and white-only primaries, used to disenfranchise Black voters in the Jim Crow South. By the time of the amendment, five states still retained poll taxes: Alabama, Arkansas, Mississippi, Texas, and Virginia. A proposed amendment to eliminate the poll tax had been debated in Congress for years; President John F. Kennedy supported the effort.

Proposal and ratification

Congress proposed the amendment on August 27, 1962. South Dakota became the 38th state to ratify on January 23, 1964, completing the three-fourths requirement. Mississippi was the last state to ratify, doing so in 1995, although the amendment had long since taken effect.

Aftermath

The amendment applied only to federal elections. In Harper v. Virginia State Board of Elections (1966), the Supreme Court ruled that poll taxes in state elections also violated the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment, thereby eliminating the poll tax in all American elections.
