P-Funk

P-Funk (from "Parliament-Funkadelic", also "Pure Funk") is the repertoire, mythology and collective of musicians led by George Clinton, centred on the bands Parliament and Funkadelic. Active from the late 1960s, P-Funk blended funk, psychedelic rock, soul and science-fiction spectacle into one of the most influential bodies of work in popular music.

The collective

Clinton's doo-wop group the Parliaments became the harder, psychedelic Funkadelic in Detroit while the same musicians recorded as the smoother Parliament for the Casablanca label. Key members included bassist Bootsy Collins, keyboardist Bernie Worrell, guitarists Eddie Hazel and Garry Shider, and drummer Jerome "Bigfoot" Brailey. Parliament's albums Mothership Connection (1975) and Funkentelechy vs. the Placebo Syndrome (1977) developed an elaborate Afrofuturist mythology, staged with the famous Mothership landing on tour.

Influence

P-Funk became the foundational sample source for West Coast hip hop and G-funk (Dr. Dre, Snoop Dogg), and influenced Prince, the Red Hot Chili Peppers and Outkast. Parliament-Funkadelic was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, and Clinton received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award with the collective in 2019.
