101st Airborne Division

The 101st Airborne Division (Air Assault) ("Screaming Eagles") is a light infantry division of the United States Army trained for air assault operations. Headquartered at Fort Campbell, Kentucky, it is one of the most decorated divisions in the Army and is famous for its role in the Second World War.

Second World War

Activated in 1942, the division first saw combat on D-Day, 6 June 1944, when its paratroopers jumped into Normandy ahead of the seaborne landings. In September 1944 it dropped into the Netherlands during Operation Market Garden. In December 1944 the division was rushed to hold the Belgian crossroads town of Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge; surrounded by German forces, its acting commander, Brigadier General Anthony McAuliffe, replied to a surrender demand with the famous one-word message "NUTS!" The division later advanced into Germany and ended the war in the Bavarian Alps.

Later service

Elements of the division enforced school desegregation at Little Rock, Arkansas, in 1957. The division deployed to Vietnam in 1965 and fought there until 1972, including the battle for Hamburger Hill in 1969. Reorganised as an air assault division using helicopters, it took part in the Gulf War of 1991, conducting a deep air assault into Iraq, and later served repeated tours in Iraq and Afghanistan following the attacks of 11 September 2001.

Legacy

The division's 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment was the subject of Stephen Ambrose's book Band of Brothers and its television adaptation. Its soldiers wear the Screaming Eagle patch, featuring the head of a bald eagle, on the left shoulder.
