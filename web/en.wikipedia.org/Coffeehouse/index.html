Coffeehouse

A coffeehouse, coffee shop, or café is an establishment that primarily serves coffee and related beverages, together with light food. Coffeehouses have historically served as centres of social interaction, conversation, business, and the exchange of news and ideas.

History

Coffeehouses appeared in the Ottoman world in the sixteenth century, in cities such as Mecca, Cairo, and Constantinople (Istanbul), where they became venues for conversation, games, music, and political discussion. Coffee reached Europe in the seventeenth century; the first coffeehouses opened in Venice, Oxford, and London in the 1650s, and in Vienna, Paris, and the Dutch ports soon after.

The English coffeehouse

In Restoration and Augustan London, coffeehouses — nicknamed "penny universities" because a penny bought admission and coffee — were hubs of commerce, science, literature, and politics. Lloyd's of London began as Edward Lloyd's coffeehouse frequented by shipowners and insurers; others gave rise to the London Stock Exchange and to famous clubs and periodicals. Distinct houses attracted distinct clienteles of merchants, politicians, scientists, and wits.

Modern coffeehouses

Continental café culture flourished in Vienna and Paris, and espresso bars spread from Italy after the Second World War. In the late twentieth century, chains such as Starbucks popularised a model of the coffee shop as a "third place" between home and work, offering speciality coffee drinks, seating, and wireless internet; independent specialty coffee shops have also grown worldwide.
