Shaivism
DeityShiva as Supreme Being
TextsVedas, Shaiva Agamas, Puranas
RegionsIndia, Nepal, Sri Lanka, diaspora

Shaivism is one of the major traditions within Hinduism that worships Shiva as the Supreme Being. Shaivites hold that Shiva is all and everything — creator, preserver, destroyer, revealer, and concealer — and the tradition is among the oldest living religions, with roots in pre-Vedic India.

History

Elements are traced to the Indus Valley, Vedic Rudra hymns, and the Shvetashvatara Upanishad. From the early centuries CE, schools such as Pashupata, Shaiva Siddhanta, Kashmir Shaivism (Trika), Lingayatism, and Nath traditions developed distinct philosophies, temple rituals, and yogic practices, spreading to Southeast Asia including Khmer and Javanese kingdoms.

Beliefs and Practice

Shaivism spans monistic and dualistic theologies. Common marks are the tripundra (three ash lines), rudraksha beads, the lingam as Shiva's symbol, and festivals such as Maha Shivaratri. Pilgrimage centres include Varanasi, Kedarnath, and the twelve Jyotirlingas.
