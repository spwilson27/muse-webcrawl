Fokker Dr.I

The Fokker Dr.I (Dreidecker, "triplane") was a World War I fighter aircraft built by Fokker-Flugzeugwerke in Germany. Designed in response to the British Sopwith Triplane, it became famous as the aircraft flown in the last months of Manfred von Richthofen, the "Red Baron," who scored his final 19 victories in the type and was killed in one on 21 April 1918.

Design and development

Chief designer Reinhold Platz adapted the experimental V.4 prototype into the production Dr.I: three short-span cantilever wings, a boxy fuselage of welded steel tube with fabric covering, and twin synchronized 7.92 mm Spandau machine guns. The thick airfoil sections gave exceptional climb and maneuverability, though level speed was modest compared with contemporary biplanes.

Service history

The Dr.I entered service in autumn 1917 but was briefly grounded after wing failures killed several pilots, including Heinrich Gontermann; strengthened wings returned it to the front. In the hands of aces such as Richthofen, Werner Voss, and Kurt Wolff it was a formidable dogfighter, but it was progressively superseded by the faster Fokker D.VII during 1918.
