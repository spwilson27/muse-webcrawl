Eilat (Hebrew: אֵילַת) is Israel's southernmost city, a busy port and resort at the northern tip of the Red Sea, on the Gulf of Aqaba. It lies in the southern Negev desert, close to the Egyptian town of Taba to the south and the Jordanian port city of Aqaba to the east.

History

The area has been inhabited since antiquity and is associated with the biblical port of Ezion-Geber. The modern city grew from the police station of Umm Rashrash, captured by Israeli forces in March 1949 during Operation Uvda, the final operation of the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. Eilat was declared a city in 1959.

Economy and tourism

Eilat is a free trade zone exempt from value-added tax, which supports its retail and hotel economy. Its warm, dry climate, coral reefs and desert mountains make it a year-round resort destination. The Coral Beach Nature Reserve and the Underwater Observatory are major attractions, and the nearby Timna Valley contains ancient copper mines.

Port and transport

The Port of Eilat gives Israel access to the Indian Ocean without passage through the Suez Canal and handles bulk cargoes such as phosphates, potash and vehicle imports. The city is served by Ramon Airport, opened in 2019 north of the city, replacing the older Eilat and Ovda airports.
