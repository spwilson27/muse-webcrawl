Cold soup

Cold soups are soups served at or below room temperature, often as refreshing first courses in summer. Many are traditional national dishes that originated as peasant food, made from raw or lightly cooked seasonal vegetables and served chilled, sometimes with bread, yogurt, or sour cream.

Notable varieties

Gazpacho from Andalusia, Spain, blends raw tomatoes, cucumber, peppers, garlic, olive oil, and bread; its smoother cousin salmorejo comes from Córdoba. Okroshka, a Russian summer soup, is made on a base of kvass or kefir with chopped vegetables, sausage, and herbs. Lithuanian šaltibarščiai is a vivid pink beet soup with kefir, served with hot potatoes. Other examples include Bulgarian tarator (yogurt, cucumber, walnuts, dill), French vichyssoise (leek and potato, served cold), Korean naengmyeon broth and chilled oi naengguk (cucumber), and sweet fruit soups such as Hungarian sour cherry soup and Scandinavian berry soups served as dessert.

Preparation and serving

Cold soups are typically prepared ahead so flavours meld during chilling; some are cooked first (beet and potato soups) and then refrigerated, while others, like gazpacho and tarator, are entirely raw. Garnishes — diced vegetables, herbs, croutons, a swirl of cream or oil — are added at serving. Food safety requires prompt refrigeration, since chilled soups are eaten without reheating.
