Glastonbury

Glastonbury is a town and civil parish in Somerset, England, situated on the Somerset Levels about 37 miles (60 km) south of Bristol. It is renowned for its associations with early Christianity, Arthurian legend, and the ruined Glastonbury Abbey, and for the nearby Glastonbury Festival of contemporary performing arts.

History and legend

Glastonbury Abbey, founded in the early medieval period, was one of the richest and most powerful monasteries in England before its dissolution in 1539. Legend connects the site with Joseph of Arimathea and the Holy Grail, and in 1191 the monks announced the discovery of the graves of King Arthur and Guinevere. The conical Glastonbury Tor, topped by the roofless St Michael's Tower, is a prominent landmark linked with the mythical Isle of Avalon.

The town today

The town has a population of around 9,000 and an economy shaped by tourism, pilgrimage and alternative spirituality; its high street is known for shops catering to New Age and pagan interests. The Glastonbury Festival, founded in 1970, is held at Worthy Farm in the nearby village of Pilton.
