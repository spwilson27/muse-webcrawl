FIFA World Cup

The FIFA World Cup Trophy, awarded since 1974.

The FIFA World Cup is an international association football competition contested by the senior men's national teams of the members of FIFA, the sport's global governing body. It has been held every four years since the inaugural tournament in 1930, except in 1942 and 1946 because of the Second World War, and is the most widely viewed sporting event in the world.

Format

Qualification rounds held over the preceding three years determine which teams join the host nation(s) in the finals tournament. The 2022 edition in Qatar featured 32 teams; the 2026 tournament in Canada, Mexico, and the United States expands to 48 teams. The finals consist of a group stage followed by knockout rounds culminating in the final.

History and winners

Uruguay hosted and won the first tournament in 1930. Brazil is the most successful nation with five titles (1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, 2002) and is the only team to have played in every tournament. Germany and Italy have four titles each, Argentina three (1978, 1986, 2022), and France and Uruguay two each; England and Spain have one each. Argentina, captained by Lionel Messi, won the 2022 final against France on penalties after a 3–3 draw widely considered among the greatest finals ever played.

Trophy

The original Jules Rimet Trophy was awarded permanently to Brazil after its third victory in 1970 (and later stolen and never recovered). The current FIFA World Cup Trophy, introduced in 1974, is made of solid gold and remains in FIFA's possession, with winners receiving a replica.

Cultural impact

The tournament commands television audiences in the billions and generates enormous economic and political attention for hosts; a separate Women's World Cup has been held since 1991.
