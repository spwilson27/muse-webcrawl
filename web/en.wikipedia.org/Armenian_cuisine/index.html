Armenian cuisine

Armenian cuisine is the traditional food of Armenia and the Armenian diaspora, one of the oldest cuisines of Asia and the South Caucasus. It reflects the geography of the Armenian Highlands — a mountainous region with harsh winters — and centuries of exchange with neighbouring Persian, Arab, Georgian, and Ottoman Turkish culinary traditions.

Characteristics

Staples include wheat (often bulgur), barley, rice, lamb, eggplant, lentils, chickpeas, yogurt (matsun), and a rich variety of herbs. Meals are commonly accompanied by lavash, the thin flatbread inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Notable dishes

Signature dishes include khorovats (grilled meats), harisa (wheat and meat porridge), dolma (stuffed vegetables and vine leaves), ghapama (pumpkin stuffed with rice, dried fruit, and honey), khachapuri-like cheese breads in some regions, and lahmajun (thin meat-topped flatbread). Soups such as khash (slow-cooked cow's feet, eaten communally in winter) hold ritual significance.

Desserts and drinks

Desserts include gata (sweet pastry), pakhlava, and dried-fruit preparations. Armenia is also an ancient centre of viticulture; the Areni-1 winery, dated to about 4100 BC, is the oldest known winery in the world.
