Molybdenum

SymbolMo
Atomic number42
GroupGroup 6 (transition metal)
Melting point2,623 °C
Main oreMolybdenite (MoS₂)

Molybdenum is a chemical element with the symbol Mo and atomic number 42. It is a silvery-grey refractory metal with the sixth-highest melting point of any element (2,623 °C) and great strength at high temperatures. Its name derives from the Greek molybdos, meaning lead, because its ores were once confused with lead ores.

Occurrence and production

Molybdenum occurs chiefly as molybdenite and is mined in the United States, China, Chile, and Peru, often as a by-product of copper mining. Carl Wilhelm Scheele recognised it as a new element in 1778, and Peter Jacob Hjelm first isolated the metal in 1781.

Uses

Most molybdenum is used in metallurgy: small additions greatly increase the strength, hardness, and corrosion resistance of steels, including stainless and tool steels. Its compounds serve as catalysts in petroleum refining, as lubricants (molybdenum disulfide), and as pigments. Molybdenum is also an essential trace nutrient: it is a cofactor in enzymes such as nitrate reductase and nitrogenase, making it vital to plants and animals.
