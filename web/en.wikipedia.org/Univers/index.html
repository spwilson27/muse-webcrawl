Univers

Univers (pronounced [ynivɛʁ]) is a Swiss neo-grotesque sans-serif typeface designed by Adrian Frutiger and released by his employer Deberny & Peignot in 1957. Classified as a neo-grotesque sans-serif, it was one of the first typeface families to fulfill the idea that a typeface should form a system of related designs, using a two-digit numerical system to identify weight, width, and position instead of names.

Univers weights are identified by numbers, e.g. Univers 55 (regular) and Univers 65 (bold).

Design

Univers was created in the postwar Swiss Style alongside Neue Haas Grotesk (later renamed Helvetica) and Folio. Whereas Helvetica is based on older grotesque models, Univers was designed as a wholly new system: Frutiger's numbering scheme runs from Univers 39 (thin ultra-condensed) to Univers 83 (extra black extended), with the first digit indicating weight and width and the second indicating position and obliqueness. The regular weight is Univers 55. The design avoids superfluous decoration, with strokes cut horizontally and vertically to give an even, rational appearance.

Usage

Univers was widely adopted for corporate identity and signage in the 1960s and 1970s. It has been used by companies such as Swiss International Air Lines and in the visual identity of the 1972 Munich Olympics designed by Otl Aicher; modified forms have been used for the Montreal Metro and other transit systems. Frutiger later expanded the concept in the Linotype Univers family (1997), extending the numbering to four digits.
