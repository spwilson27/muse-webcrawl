Star Wars Day is an informal commemorative day observed annually on May 4 to celebrate the Star Wars media franchise created by George Lucas. The date originates from the pun "May the Fourth be with you", a play on the famous film line "May the Force be with you."

History

One of the earliest recorded uses of the phrase was on May 4, 1979, when an advertisement in the London Evening News congratulated Margaret Thatcher on becoming prime minister: "May the Fourth Be with You, Maggie. Congratulations." The pun was later popularized by fans, and by the early 2010s May 4 had become widely recognized as Star Wars Day, marked by fan events, costume gatherings, film screenings, and themed celebrations around the world.

Observance

After Disney acquired Lucasfilm in 2012, the day was officially embraced by the franchise's owners, with Disney parks, retailers, and streaming services marking it with merchandise releases, announcements, and premieres; new Star Wars series and specials have frequently debuted on the date. Fans also observe "Revenge of the Fifth" (May 5) and "Revenge of the Sixth" (May 6) as tongue-in-cheek counterparts referencing the Sith.

May 25

May 25 also holds significance for fans, as the original Star Wars film premiered on that date in 1977; the city of Los Angeles declared May 25 "Star Wars Day" in 2007.
