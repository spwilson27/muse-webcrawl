The Delta Works (Dutch: Deltawerken) is a series of construction projects in the southwest of the Netherlands to protect a large area of land around the Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta from the sea. Constructed between 1954 and 1997, the works consist of dams, sluices, locks, dykes, levees, and storm surge barriers located in the provinces of South Holland and Zeeland. The aim of the dams, sluices, and barriers was to shorten the Dutch coastline, thus reducing the number of dykes that had to be raised. Along with the Zuiderzee Works, the Delta Works have been declared one of the Seven Wonders of the Modern World by the American Society of Civil Engineers.

History

The estuaries of the Rhine, Meuse, and Scheldt had flooded repeatedly over the centuries. After the devastating North Sea flood of 1953 (the Watersnoodramp), which killed 1,836 people in the Netherlands and flooded large areas of Zeeland and South Holland, a Delta Commission was installed and the Delta Plan was drawn up under engineers including Johan van Veen. The Delta Act of 1958 authorized closing the sea inlets — except the Westerschelde (access to Antwerp) and the Nieuwe Waterweg (access to Rotterdam), which were instead protected by raised dykes and, later, movable barriers.

Principal structures

The first completed work was the storm surge barrier in the Hollandse IJssel (1958). Major dams — the Veerse Gatdam, Grevelingendam, Volkerakdam, Haringvlietdam, and Brouwersdam — closed successive inlets through the 1960s and early 1970s. The capstone was the Oosterscheldekering (Eastern Scheldt storm surge barrier): originally planned as a closed dam, environmental protests over the loss of the estuary's tidal ecosystem and oyster fisheries led to a redesign as a 9 km barrier with 62 enormous sluice gates, normally open to the tide but closed during storms; it was inaugurated by Queen Beatrix on 4 October 1986. The final elements, the Maeslantkering — two gigantic swinging arms protecting Rotterdam, among the largest moving structures on Earth — and the Hartelkering, were completed in 1997.

Operation and future

The barriers are operated by Rijkswaterstaat; the Oosterscheldekering is closed when water levels are forecast to exceed 3 metres above NAP, on average about once a year, and the Maeslantkering closes automatically based on computer forecasts (first operational closure in a storm in 2007). With sea levels rising due to climate change, the Netherlands' ongoing Delta Programme (with an annually appointed Delta Commissioner since 2010) plans reinforcements and adaptive strategies for the coming century, and Dutch delta engineering expertise is exported worldwide.
