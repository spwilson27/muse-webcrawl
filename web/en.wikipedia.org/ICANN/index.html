ICANN

The Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN, pronounced "I can") is an American nonprofit corporation responsible for coordinating the maintenance and procedures of several databases related to the namespaces and numerical spaces of the Internet, ensuring the network's stable and secure operation. Founded on September 18, 1998, its headquarters are in the Playa Vista neighbourhood of Los Angeles, California.

ICANN performs the technical maintenance work of the Central Internet Address pools and DNS root zone registries pursuant to the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA) functions contract. Much of its work concerns the Internet's global Domain Name System (DNS), including the introduction of new generic top-level domains (gTLDs) and the operation of root name servers.

History

Before ICANN's creation, the IANA functions were administered, largely single-handedly, by computer scientist Jon Postel under contracts with the United States government. In 1998 the U.S. Department of Commerce issued a policy statement ("White Paper") that called for the creation of a private, nonprofit corporation to take over responsibility for IP address allocation, protocol parameter assignment, domain name system management, and root server system management. ICANN was incorporated that year, with Esther Dyson as its founding chairwoman and Michael Roberts as its first president and CEO.

On October 1, 2016, the IANA functions contract between ICANN and the U.S. National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) expired and was not renewed, completing the transition of Internet technical coordination to the global multistakeholder community. The IANA functions are now performed by Public Technical Identifiers (PTI), an affiliate of ICANN.

Structure

ICANN is governed by a 16-member Board of Directors and develops policy through three Supporting Organizations: the Generic Names Supporting Organization (GNSO), the Country Code Names Supporting Organization (ccNSO), and the Address Supporting Organization (ASO). Advisory committees — including the Governmental Advisory Committee (GAC), the At-Large Advisory Committee (ALAC), the Root Server System Advisory Committee (RSSAC), and the Security and Stability Advisory Committee (SSAC) — provide advice to the Board.

Activities

ICANN accredits domain name registrars, coordinates the delegation of country code top-level domains (ccTLDs), and administers the programme that introduced over a thousand new generic top-level domains beginning in 2013. It also convenes three public meetings each year in different regions of the world.
