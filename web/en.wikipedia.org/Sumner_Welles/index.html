Sumner Welles

Benjamin Sumner Welles (October 14, 1892 – September 24, 1961) was an American government official, diplomat, and leading foreign policy adviser to President Franklin D. Roosevelt. As Under Secretary of State from 1937 to 1943, he was one of the most influential American diplomats of the era, a key architect of Roosevelt's hemispheric "Good Neighbor" policy and an early advocate of a postwar international organisation that became the United Nations.

Career

Born into a wealthy New York family and educated at Groton and Harvard, Welles entered the Foreign Service in 1915 and specialised in Latin American affairs. He served as ambassador to Cuba in 1933, where he mediated during the fall of the Machado government. As Under Secretary under Secretary of State Cordell Hull — with whom he feuded bitterly — Welles effectively ran much of the State Department.

Diplomacy

In February–March 1940, Welles undertook a famous fact-finding mission to Rome, Berlin, Paris, and London to explore a negotiated peace in Europe; the mission confirmed to Roosevelt that no acceptable settlement with Nazi Germany was possible. In July 1940 he issued the Welles Declaration, by which the United States refused to recognise the Soviet annexation of the Baltic states — a policy maintained until those states regained independence in 1991. He helped draft the Atlantic Charter and presided over the committees that planned the United Nations.

Fall and later life

Welles's career ended in scandal in 1943, when details of a 1940 incident — in which he had allegedly made sexual advances to railroad porters — were used by his enemies, including Hull and Senator William Bullitt, to force his resignation. He spent his remaining years writing and lecturing on foreign policy, producing works such as The Time for Decision (1944).
