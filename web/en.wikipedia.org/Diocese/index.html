Diocese

A cathedral church, the seat of a diocesan bishop

In church governance, a diocese or bishopric is the ecclesiastical district under the jurisdiction of a bishop. The word derives from the Latin dioecesis, itself from the Greek dioikesis (“administration”), originally denoting a grouping of provinces in the late Roman Empire.

History

As Christianity spread, bishops settled in cities and their territories of pastoral oversight took shape as dioceses, often following Roman administrative boundaries. By the Middle Ages the diocese was the fundamental territorial unit of the Western Church, subdivided into parishes and grouped into provinces under archbishops. The structure persists in the Roman Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, and many Anglican and Lutheran churches.

Organization

A diocese is typically divided into parishes, each served by a priest or pastor. The bishop's seat (cathedra) is located in the cathedral church. In the Catholic Church, an especially large or historically important diocese headed by an archbishop is called an archdiocese. Groups of dioceses form an ecclesiastical province.

Other uses

Some churches, such as the Church of Sweden and the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, retain dioceses or synods with similar functions, while many Reformed and free churches assign the term different or no significance.
