The Game Boy Advance SP (SP for "Special") is a 32-bit handheld game console developed, released, and marketed by Nintendo as an upgraded version of the Game Boy Advance. It was first released in Japan on February 14, 2003, followed by North America in March and Europe in April of the same year.

Design

The SP introduced a clamshell ("flip") design protecting the screen, a built-in rechargeable lithium-ion battery replacing AA batteries, and — most notably — a front-lit screen (later back-lit in the AGS-101 revision of 2005), addressing the original Advance's most criticised shortcoming, its unlit display. It omitted the headphone jack, requiring an adapter for the proprietary charging port. Hardware was otherwise compatible with the full Game Boy Advance library and most Game Boy and Game Boy Color games.

Game Boy Micro

In 2005 Nintendo released the Game Boy Micro, a smaller redesign, as the final system in the Game Boy line. The SP nevertheless remained the best-selling revision, with the Advance family selling over 81 million units in total.
