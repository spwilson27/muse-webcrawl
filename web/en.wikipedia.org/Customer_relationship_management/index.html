Customer relationship management

Customer relationship management (CRM) is a process and supporting technology for managing a company's interactions with current and potential customers. CRM systems compile data from sales, marketing, and customer service channels — calls, emails, chats, purchases — into a unified record used to improve service, retention, and sales growth.

History

CRM grew out of 1980s database marketing and contact management software, and developed into integrated suites in the 1990s with vendors such as Siebel Systems. Salesforce, founded in 1999, popularized delivery as cloud software-as-a-service. Today the CRM software market is among the largest enterprise software categories.

Functions

Typical components include contact and lead management, sales pipeline tracking, marketing automation (campaigns and email), customer service ticketing, analytics and reporting, and, increasingly, AI assistants that summarize interactions and suggest next steps. CRM strategies distinguish operational, analytical, and collaborative CRM.

Criticism and challenges

Implementations often fail because of poor adoption by sales staff, low-quality data, and misalignment between the tool and business processes. Privacy regulation, such as the GDPR, also constrains how customer data may be collected and used.
