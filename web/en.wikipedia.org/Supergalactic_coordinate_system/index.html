Supergalactic coordinate system

The supergalactic coordinate system is a celestial coordinate system designed to follow the plane of the Local Supercluster (Virgo Supercluster). The system was introduced by Gérard de Vaucouleurs in the 1950s after he recognized that the distribution of nearby galaxies is flattened along a plane.

Definition

Coordinates are given as supergalactic longitude (SGL) and supergalactic latitude (SGB). By definition, the supergalactic equator lies at galactic latitude 6.32° in its north pole direction near galactic coordinates l = 47.37°, b = +6.32°. The zero point of supergalactic longitude is at the intersection of the supergalactic plane and the galactic plane, at galactic longitude 137.37°.

Use

The system is used in extragalactic astronomy to describe the large-scale structure of the nearby universe: the thin distribution of galaxies near the supergalactic plane, and structures such as the Great Attractor and galaxy filaments, which stand out clearly in supergalactic coordinates.
