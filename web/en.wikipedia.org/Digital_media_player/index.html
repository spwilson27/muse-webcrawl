Digital media player

A digital media player (also known as a streaming device or streaming box) is a type of consumer electronics device designed for the storage, playback, or viewing of digital media content. Players are often integrated into home cinema configurations and connect to a television or AV receiver.

Types

Portable media players store files locally on flash memory or a hard drive and play audio and video on the go; the Apple iPod was the best-known example. Home streaming players such as Roku, Apple TV, Amazon Fire TV and Chromecast instead fetch content over a home network or the internet.

Functionality

Modern players support streaming services such as Netflix, YouTube and Spotify, local network media via DLNA, and screen mirroring from phones and computers. They typically connect via HDMI and are controlled by remote or voice assistant.

History

The category grew out of late-1990s MP3 players and network media adapters, and expanded greatly in the 2010s as broadband streaming displaced physical media and downloads.
