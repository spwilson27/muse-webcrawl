Steering

Steering is a system of components and linkages that allows a vehicle to follow a desired course. In most road vehicles the driver turns a steering wheel, whose rotation is transmitted through a steering column and gearbox to steer the front wheels; vessels, aircraft, and tracked vehicles use analogous mechanisms.

Automobile steering

The dominant design is rack and pinion steering, in which a pinion on the steering shaft moves a toothed rack connected by tie rods to the wheels. Older recirculating-ball systems survive in trucks. Steering geometry follows the Ackermann principle, angling the inner wheel more sharply than the outer wheel so both roll around a common centre.

Power steering

Power assistance—long provided hydraulically and now usually electrically ("EPS")—reduces driver effort and enables features such as variable ratio, lane-keeping assistance, and steer-by-wire, in which the mechanical connection is replaced by electronic control, first offered in production by Infiniti in 2013.

Other vehicles

Ships are steered by a rudder controlled from a wheel or autopilot; bicycles and motorcycles are steered largely by leaning combined with handlebar input; tracked vehicles steer by driving their tracks at different speeds; and rockets and aircraft use aerodynamic surfaces or thrust vectoring.
