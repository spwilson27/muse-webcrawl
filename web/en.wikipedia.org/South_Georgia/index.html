South Georgia

South Georgia is an island in the South Atlantic Ocean that is part of the British Overseas Territory of South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands. It lies about 1,400 km east-southeast of the Falkland Islands. There is no permanent native population; a small number of government officers, museum staff, and scientists live at Grytviken and the research station at King Edward Point.

Geography and wildlife

The main island is about 170 km long, rugged and largely barren, with the Allardyce Range culminating in Mount Paget (2,935 m). More than half the island is covered by glaciers. South Georgia is one of the world's great wildlife refuges, with huge colonies of king penguins, elephant and fur seals, macaroni penguins, and breeding wandering albatrosses.

History

Captain James Cook made the first landing in 1775 and claimed the island for Britain. In the 19th and 20th centuries it was a centre of sealing and then industrial whaling, with stations such as Grytviken (founded 1904 by Carl Anton Larsen) and Leith Harbour; whaling ended in the 1960s. Ernest Shackleton is buried at Grytviken, where he died in 1922, having first crossed the island on foot in 1916 after the loss of the Endurance. Argentine forces occupied the island briefly during the 1982 Falklands War before British forces retook it in Operation Paraquet.

Conservation

A rat eradication programme completed in 2015, the largest of its kind, removed invasive rodents that preyed on ground-nesting birds, and the surrounding marine protected area is among the world's largest.
