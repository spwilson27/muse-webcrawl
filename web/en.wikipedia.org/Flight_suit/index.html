Flight suit

A flight suit is a full-body garment worn while flying aircraft such as military airplanes, gliders and helicopters. These suits are generally made to keep the wearer warm, as well as being practical (having multiple pockets), and durable (including fire retardant). Its appearance is usually similar to a jumpsuit. A military flight suit may also show rank insignia and unit patches.

History and materials

Early aviators wore leather flying jackets, helmets and heavy clothing for warmth in open cockpits. Electrically heated suits appeared during the Second World War for high-altitude bomber crews. The modern one-piece suit evolved in the post-war decades; the standard United States military flight suit, the CWU-27/P, is made of Nomex, an aramid fibre that is inherently flame resistant, since protection in the event of a cockpit fire is one of the garment's principal purposes. Nomex suits were introduced to US service in the late 1960s and 1970s after a series of fatal fires.

Features

Flight suits typically have multiple zippered pockets positioned for access while seated and strapped in — on the thighs, calves, chest and sleeves — including a pencil pocket on the left forearm. They are designed with few loose parts that could snag on cockpit equipment during ejection. Colours are usually sage green, tan or orange; high-visibility orange suits are worn for some test, rescue and carrier operations.

Related garments include the anti-G suit, whose inflatable bladders squeeze the legs and abdomen to prevent blood pooling under high acceleration, and the full and partial pressure suits worn for very high-altitude and space flight.
