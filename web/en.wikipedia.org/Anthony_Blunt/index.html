Anthony Blunt

Born: 26 September 1907, Bournemouth, England
Died: 26 March 1983, London
Occupation: Art historian, spy
Known for: Surveyor of the King's Pictures; member of the Cambridge Five

Anthony Frederick Blunt (26 September 1907 – 26 March 1983) was a British art historian and Soviet spy. Director of the Courtauld Institute of Art and Surveyor of the King's (later Queen's) Pictures, he was one of the most respected art historians of his generation — and, as was publicly revealed in 1979, the "Fourth Man" of the Cambridge Five spy ring.

Early life and recruitment

Blunt was educated at Marlborough College and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he joined the Apostles society and fell in with Guy Burgess, who recruited him to Soviet intelligence in the mid-1930s. During the Second World War Blunt served in MI5, passing information to the NKVD while rising within British counter-intelligence.

Art history career

After the war Blunt became director of the Courtauld Institute (1947–1974) and Surveyor of the King's Pictures, overseeing the Royal Collection. His scholarship on Nicolas Poussin and on Baroque and French art was widely admired, and he was knighted in 1956.

Exposure

Blunt confessed to MI5 in 1964 in exchange for immunity, a secret kept until Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher exposed him in the House of Commons in November 1979. He was stripped of his knighthood and died in London in 1983. See also Kim Philby and Guy Burgess.
