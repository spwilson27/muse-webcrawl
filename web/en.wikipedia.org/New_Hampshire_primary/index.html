The New Hampshire primary is a general term for primary elections held in the U.S. state of New Hampshire, in which voters choose each political party's nominees for federal, state, and local offices. The term is most commonly associated with the New Hampshire presidential primary, the first primary of the U.S. presidential nominating season, but New Hampshire also holds a state primary each September before the November general election.

Presidential primary

The presidential primary, held in January or February of presidential election years, is the most prominent of New Hampshire's primaries. State law requires it to precede any similar contest by at least seven days, preserving its first-in-the-nation status among primaries. Undeclared voters—those not registered with a party, a large share of the New Hampshire electorate—may vote in either party's primary by requesting that party's ballot on election day.

State primary

The state primary is held on the second Tuesday in September of even-numbered years. Voters select nominees for governor, the Executive Council, the state Senate and House of Representatives, Congress, and county offices. Because New Hampshire's governorship carries a two-year term, gubernatorial primaries occur every two years, more frequently than in most states.

Characteristics

New Hampshire primaries are noted for high voter turnout, which is among the highest in the nation, and for the tradition that results from small towns such as Dixville Notch and Hart's Location are reported almost immediately after midnight voting. Ballot access is comparatively easy, and write-in votes have occasionally played a role, as when Lyndon B. Johnson won the 1964 Democratic presidential primary as a write-in candidate.
