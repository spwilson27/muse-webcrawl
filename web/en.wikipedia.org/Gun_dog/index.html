Gun dog

Gun dogs, or bird dogs, are types of hunting dogs developed to assist hunters in finding and retrieving game, usually birds. The category is traditionally divided into three groups: retrievers, which locate and bring back shot game; flushing dogs (spaniels), which drive game from cover within range of the guns; and pointing breeds, which locate game by scent and indicate it by standing rigidly "on point".

Groups and breeds

Retrievers such as the Labrador Retriever, Golden Retriever and Chesapeake Bay Retriever specialise in marking fallen birds and retrieving them, especially from water, carrying game with a "soft mouth" that does not damage it. Spaniels, including the English Springer Spaniel and Cocker Spaniel, quarter the ground ahead of the hunter and flush game into the air. Pointing breeds — the (English) Pointer, English, Gordon and Irish setters, and the versatile continental "hunt, point, retrieve" breeds such as the German Shorthaired Pointer and Brittany — range widely, freeze on scent, and may also retrieve.

Training and work

Gun dogs are trained to be steady to shot, to respond to whistle and hand signals at distance, and to retrieve on command. Field trials and working tests assess these skills competitively. Many gun dog breeds are also popular as family pets, though their energy and working drive require exercise and mental stimulation.

History

Specialised bird dogs developed alongside the spread of shotguns in the 18th and 19th centuries, with British breeders refining most of the classic breeds for driven and walked-up shooting, while continental Europe favoured all-round HPR dogs.
