Ngaruawahia

Ngaruawahia (Maori: Ngaruawahia) is a town in the Waikato region of New Zealand's North Island, about 20 kilometres northwest of Hamilton. It stands at the confluence of the Waikato and Waipa rivers and is significant as the home of Turangawaewae Marae, the seat of the Maori King movement.

History

The area was an important Waikato Tainui settlement. During the Invasion of the Waikato in 1863-64 the town was occupied and briefly considered as a possible capital of the colony; its name was at one point changed to Newcastle before reverting. In the 20th century it became closely associated with the Kingitanga under Princess Te Puea Herangi, who established Turangawaewae Marae from 1921.

Today

Turangawaewae hosts major gatherings including the annual Koroneihana celebrating the Maori monarch's coronation. The town is served by State Highway 1C and the North Island Main Trunk railway, and sits near the Hakarimata Range, a popular walking area.
