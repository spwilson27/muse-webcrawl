Fort William, Ontario

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort William was a city in Ontario, Canada, located on the Kaministiquia River at the western shore of Lake Superior. Founded as a fur-trade post and incorporated as a town in 1892 and a city in 1907, it amalgamated with the neighbouring city of Port Arthur on 1 January 1970 to form the city of Thunder Bay.

History

The name derives from Fort William, the North West Company's inland headquarters established in 1803 at the mouth of the Kaministiquia and named for William III. The great fur-trade rendezvous there ended after the company merged with the Hudson's Bay Company in 1821, though the historic post has been reconstructed as the Fort William Historical Park. The modern town grew with the Canadian Pacific Railway and the lakehead grain trade in the 1880s, and with its twin city Port Arthur became a major transshipment point for Prairie grain, with some of the largest grain elevators in the world.

Amalgamation

After decades of rivalry and failed attempts, Fort William and Port Arthur voted in a 1969 referendum to amalgamate; the name "Thunder Bay" narrowly defeated "Lakehead" and "The Lakehead" in a plebiscite. The neighbourhood names Fort William and Port Arthur persist within Thunder Bay, and "Fort William" remains the name of a federal and provincial electoral district centred on the south end of the city.
