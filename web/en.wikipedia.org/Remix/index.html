Remix

Remixing developed alongside DJ culture and electronic dance music.

A remix (or reorchestration) is a piece of media which has been altered or contorted from its original state by adding, removing, or changing pieces of the item. A song, piece of artwork, book, poem, or photograph can all be remixes. The only characteristic of a remix is that it appropriates and changes other materials to create something new — most commonly a song, created by blending or reconstructing other musical works in a new composition.

Remixing emerged from the disco and club scenes of the 1970s, when DJs and producers such as Tom Moulton created extended versions of songs for dancefloors, and from Jamaican dub music, in which engineers reworked existing recordings into experimental new tracks. With the rise of hip hop and electronic dance music, the remix became a standard commercial practice: labels commission remixes to promote singles across different radio formats and club audiences.

Techniques

Remixers typically work from the original multitrack recordings or "stems", rearranging sections, changing tempo and key, adding new beats, basslines, or instrumentation, and restructuring the song. In popular usage, a remix that radically transforms the source material may be credited to the remixer as a featured artist, while a lighter-touch alteration may be called an edit, rework, or refix.

Copyright

Because a remix is a derivative work, it generally requires permission from the owners of the original recording and composition. Unofficial remixes, known as bootlegs, circulate widely despite their uncertain legal status, and debates about sampling and remixing have shaped modern copyright law and the philosophy of remix culture.
