Led Zeppelin

Led Zeppelin were an English rock band formed in London in 1968, consisting of guitarist Jimmy Page, singer Robert Plant, bassist and keyboardist John Paul Jones, and drummer John Bonham. Their heavy, guitar-driven sound, drawing on blues and folk, made them pioneers of hard rock and heavy metal.

Career

Their untitled fourth album (1971), featuring "Stairway to Heaven", is among the best-selling albums of all time. Other celebrated records include Led Zeppelin II, Led Zeppelin IV, Houses of the Holy and Physical Graffiti. The band disbanded after Bonham's death in 1980, with occasional reunions, notably the 2007 Ahmet Ertegun Tribute Concert at London's O2 Arena with Jason Bonham on drums.

Legacy

Led Zeppelin have sold an estimated 200–300 million records worldwide and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1995.
