Cirac–Zoller controlled-NOT gate

The Cirac–Zoller controlled-NOT gate is a scheme for implementing a controlled-NOT (CNOT) quantum logic gate between two qubits stored in trapped ions. It was proposed in 1995 by J. Ignacio Cirac and Peter Zoller in the first concrete design for a quantum computer based on ion traps, and it founded the field of trapped-ion quantum computing.

Scheme

In the proposal, a string of ions is held in a linear Paul trap and cooled to the motional ground state. Each ion's qubit is stored in two long-lived internal electronic states. A shared collective vibrational mode of the ion string serves as a "bus": a laser pulse maps the state of a control ion onto the common motion, a second pulse on the target ion flips its state conditional on that motion, and a third pulse restores the motion, completing the CNOT while leaving the ions in the Lamb–Dicke regime.

Significance

The Cirac–Zoller paper showed that the elementary operations needed for universal quantum computation could be performed with existing atomic-physics techniques. The gate was demonstrated experimentally in 2003 at Innsbruck, and trapped-ion systems — although many now use the related Mølmer–Sørensen gate — remain among the most precise quantum computing platforms.
