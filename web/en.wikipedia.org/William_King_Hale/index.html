William King Hale

[Image: William King Hale]

William King Hale (December 24, 1874 – August 15, 1962) was an American cattleman and convicted murderer in Osage County, Oklahoma, who was a central figure in the Osage Indian murders of the 1920s. A powerful local political boss, Hale orchestrated a scheme to gain control of Osage headrights — shares in the tribe's mineral wealth — by arranging marriages and then murders.

Osage murders

Members of the Osage Nation had become immensely wealthy from oil royalties. Hale's nephew Ernest Burkhart married Mollie Kyle, an Osage woman, while Hale arranged the murders of members of her family, including her sisters and mother, so that the headrights would pass to Mollie and then to Burkhart. The investigation by the young Bureau of Investigation, under J. Edgar Hoover, became one of the agency's first major homicide cases.

Conviction

Hale was convicted in 1929 of ordering the murder of Henry Roan and sentenced to life imprisonment. He was paroled in 1947. The events were recounted in David Grann's 2017 book Killers of the Flower Moon and Martin Scorsese's 2023 film adaptation, in which Hale was played by Robert De Niro.
