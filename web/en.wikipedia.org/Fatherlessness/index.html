Fatherlessness

FieldSociology, family studies, developmental psychology
Related conceptsSingle-parent family, absent father, shared parenting

Fatherlessness refers to the condition or experience of a child growing up without a father present in the home, whether due to death, divorce, separation, incarceration, abandonment, or a father who never resided with the child. It is a subject of study in sociology and developmental psychology, and a recurring topic in public policy debates about family structure.

Prevalence

In the United States, roughly one in four children lives in a single-parent household, most commonly with their mother, and the proportion of children born to unmarried parents has risen over recent decades. Rates vary widely by country and community. Comparable patterns appear in many industrialised nations, though the causes and social contexts differ.

Research findings

A large body of research finds statistical associations between father absence and outcomes such as lower educational attainment, higher rates of poverty, and increased risk of behavioural problems and early parenthood. Researchers caution that these correlations are strongly confounded by household income, parental conflict, and neighbourhood conditions, and that many children raised without a resident father thrive. Other scholarship emphasises the quality of parenting and the involvement of non-resident fathers and other caregivers over household form alone.
