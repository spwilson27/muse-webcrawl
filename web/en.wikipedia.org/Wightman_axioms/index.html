Wightman axioms

The Wightman axioms (also called Gårding–Wightman axioms), named after Arthur Wightman and Lars Gårding, are an attempt at a mathematically rigorous formulation of quantum field theory. Arthur Wightman formulated the axioms in the early 1950s, but they were first published only in 1964, after Haag and Kastler proposed an alternative, conceptually important algebraic approach.

The axioms

The framework assumes a Hilbert space carrying a unitary representation of the Poincaré group, with a unique invariant vacuum state. Quantum fields are operator-valued distributions: smeared with test functions, they give (generally unbounded) operators defined on a common dense domain containing the vacuum. The axioms require:

  • Relativistic invariance — states transform under a unitary representation of the Poincaré group, and the spectrum of the energy-momentum operator lies in the forward light cone.
  • Locality (microscopic causality) — fields at spacelike-separated points commute or anticommute.
  • Cyclicity of the vacuum — polynomial combinations of fields applied to the vacuum span a dense set of states.

Wightman functions and reconstruction

The vacuum expectation values of products of fields, called Wightman functions, encode the whole theory. The Wightman reconstruction theorem states that any set of distributions satisfying the corresponding positivity, invariance, spectral and locality conditions arises from a quantum field theory obeying the axioms.

Consequences and related frameworks

From the axioms one can prove celebrated structural results of quantum field theory, including the CPT theorem and the connection between spin and statistics. The Wightman approach generated the programme of constructive quantum field theory, which produced rigorous interacting models in two and three spacetime dimensions. It is complemented by the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms for Euclidean field theory and by algebraic quantum field theory, and is closely related to the framework of Haag and Kastler.
