The War of the Portuguese Succession (1580–1583) was the result of the near-extinction of the Portuguese royal line after the death of King Sebastian at the Battle of Alcácer Quibir in 1578 and the short reign of his great-uncle, Cardinal-King Henry, who died in January 1580 without an heir. The principal claimants to the throne were Philip II of Spain, who descended from Manuel I through his mother, and António, Prior of Crato, the illegitimate son of Infante Luís, who was acclaimed king by popular support in Lisbon.

Course of the war

Philip backed his hereditary claim with an army under the Duke of Alba, which defeated António's forces at the Battle of Alcântara on 25 August 1580 and occupied Lisbon. António fled and continued the struggle from the Azores, with French support; a Franco-Portuguese fleet under Filippo Strozzi was defeated by the Spanish under the Marquis of Santa Cruz at the Battle of Ponta Delgada (1582), and the last Azorean resistance on Terceira Island was overcome in 1583.

Consequences

Philip was recognized as Filipe I of Portugal by the Cortes of Tomar (1581), which preserved Portugal's separate laws, currency, and administration while uniting the two crowns in the Iberian Union. The union lasted until the Portuguese Restoration War of 1640 restored an independent Portuguese monarchy under the House of Braganza.
