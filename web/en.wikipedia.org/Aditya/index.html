Aditya

In Hinduism, Adityas (Sanskrit: आदित्य, lit. "offspring of Aditi") are a group of solar deities, the sons of the goddess Aditi. The name Āditya, in the singular, is taken to refer to the sun god Surya. Generally, the Adityas are twelve in number, corresponding to the twelve months of the year.

In the Vedas

In the Rigveda, the Adityas are initially a smaller group — commonly seven or eight — including Varuna, Mitra, Aryaman, Bhaga, Daksha, and Amsha, presided over by Varuna as guardians of ṛta, the cosmic and moral order. A hymn recounts that Aditi bore eight sons but cast away the eighth, Martanda, associated with the sun.

The twelve Adityas

In the Puranas and epics the list is standardized at twelve, with names varying by source: typically Dhata, Mitra, Aryaman, Sakra (Indra), Varuna, Amsha, Bhaga, Vivasvan, Pushan, Savitr, Tvastr, and Vishnu — the last being the dwarf avatar Vamana among his incarnations. Each presides over a month of the solar year, and the twelvefold group appears prominently in temple iconography and calendrical tradition.

"Aditya" is also a common Indian given name, and the term is used more broadly as a synonym for the sun itself.
