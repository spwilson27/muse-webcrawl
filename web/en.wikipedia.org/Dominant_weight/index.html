Dominant weight

A dominant weight in the weight lattice of a Lie algebra.

In mathematics, in the representation theory of Lie groups and Lie algebras, a dominant weight is a weight of a representation that lies in a particular closed cone — the dominant Weyl chamber — determined by a choice of positive roots. Every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of a semisimple Lie algebra (over the complex numbers) is uniquely determined by its highest weight, and the highest weights of such representations are exactly the dominant integral weights. The study of dominant weights therefore organises the whole classification of finite-dimensional representations of semisimple Lie algebras.

Definition

Let 𝒥 be a semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra and a chosen system of simple roots. A weight is an element of the dual space of the Cartan subalgebra. A weight is called dominant if its pairings with the simple coroots are all nonnegative integers. Equivalently, a weight is dominant if it can be written as a nonnegative integer combination of the fundamental weights.

Highest weight theory

The theorem of the highest weight states that for each dominant integral weight there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) finite-dimensional irreducible representation of the Lie algebra having that weight as its highest weight, and that every finite-dimensional irreducible representation arises this way. The set of dominant weights thus parametrises the representation theory completely.

Dominant weights and the Weyl chamber

The Weyl group acts on the weight lattice, and each orbit contains exactly one dominant weight. The closed dominant chamber is a fundamental domain for this action. This fact underlies the construction of character formulas, including the Weyl character formula, which expresses the character of the irreducible representation attached to a dominant weight as a ratio of alternating sums over the Weyl group.
