Bullwhip effect

The bullwhip effect is a phenomenon in supply chains whereby small fluctuations in consumer demand cause progressively larger fluctuations in orders at the wholesale, distributor, manufacturer, and supplier levels — like the crack of a whip.

Causes identified by researchers include demand forecast updating, order batching, price fluctuations and promotions, and rationing games during shortages. Jay Forrester described the effect in the 1960s, and the MIT 'beer game' demonstrates it experientially.

Mitigation strategies include sharing point-of-sale data with all partners (as in vendor-managed inventory), everyday low pricing, smaller and more frequent orders, and collaborative planning (CPFR).
