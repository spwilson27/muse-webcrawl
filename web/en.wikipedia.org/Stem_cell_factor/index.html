Stem cell factor

Stem cell factor (SCF) binds the c-KIT receptor tyrosine kinase on the surface of stem and progenitor cells.

Stem cell factor (SCF), also known as KIT ligand, steel factor and mast cell growth factor, is a cytokine that binds to the c-KIT receptor (CD117). It plays an essential role in the survival, proliferation and differentiation of haematopoietic stem cells, melanocytes, germ cells and mast cells. In humans it is encoded by the KITLG gene.

Function

SCF exists in both membrane-bound and soluble forms, produced by alternative splicing and proteolytic cleavage. Together with other growth factors it maintains haematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow niche, and it is required for the development of melanocytes (skin pigmentation), spermatogenesis and oogenesis, and the maturation of mast cells and interstitial cells of Cajal in the gut.

Genetics and disease

The gene's name derives from the mouse Steel locus, whose mutations cause anaemia, sterility and white spotting — mirroring mutations in the c-KIT receptor itself (the White spotting locus). In humans, loss-of-function variants can cause piebaldism-like pigmentation defects and familial progressive hyperpigmentation, while activating mutations in its receptor KIT are implicated in gastrointestinal stromal tumours and mastocytosis.

Clinical use

Recombinant SCF (ancestim) has been used in combination with other cytokines to mobilise haematopoietic stem cells into the blood for collection, although its use is limited by mast-cell-mediated side effects.
