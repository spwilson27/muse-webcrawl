Saṃsāra

Saṃsāra
Sanskritसंसार (saṃsāra)
Meaning"wandering", "world"
TraditionsHinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism
Related conceptsKarma, rebirth, moksha, nirvana

Saṃsāra (Sanskrit: संसार) is a central concept in Indian religions denoting the continuous cycle of birth, death and rebirth to which all beings in the material world are subject. In Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism, existence within saṃsāra is characterised by suffering or unsatisfactoriness (duḥkha), and liberation from the cycle is the ultimate spiritual goal.

In Hinduism

Within Hindu thought, the individual self (ātman) transmigrates from body to body according to its karma — the accumulated consequences of past actions. Release from saṃsāra, called moksha, is attained through knowledge, devotion and disciplined action, and is realised as union with or recognition of the ultimate reality, Brahman.

In Buddhism

Buddhism teaches that saṃsāra consists of six realms of rebirth — gods, demigods, humans, animals, hungry ghosts and hells — depicted in the bhavacakra, the wheel of life. Because Buddhism denies a permanent soul (anattā), rebirth is understood as the continuation of a causal stream of consciousness. Escape from the cycle, nirvana, follows the cessation of craving and ignorance through the Noble Eightfold Path.

In Jainism

In Jainism, the soul (jīva) is bound to the cycle by karmic particles attracted through action; through right faith, knowledge and conduct, culminating in non-violence (ahiṃsā), the soul sheds its karma and attains liberation (kevala).
