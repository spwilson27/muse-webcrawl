His Majesty's Naval Base, Clyde (HMNB Clyde), commonly known as Faslane, is one of three operating bases of the Royal Navy (alongside HMNB Devonport and HMNB Portsmouth). Situated on the Gare Loch in Argyll and Bute, Scotland, the base is the home of the United Kingdom's nuclear deterrent: the Vanguard-class ballistic missile submarines armed with Trident missiles, whose warheads are stored at the nearby Royal Naval Armaments Depot Coulport on Loch Long.

Role

Faslane hosts the Faslane Patrol Boat Squadron, mine-countermeasure vessels of the Clyde-based squadron, and the Royal Navy's entire submarine service: since the 2020s all British submarines — Vanguard class and the Astute-class attack submarines — are based at Clyde, which is being developed as a single-purpose submarine base. Continuous At-Sea Deterrence has been maintained from the base since 1969, first with Polaris and, since the 1990s, with Trident.

History

The site was used during the Second World War and expanded in the 1960s to host the Resolution-class Polaris submarines. The decision to base the Trident force at Faslane in the 1980s brought major construction, including the ship lift and covered facilities at the base.

Protests

The presence of nuclear weapons has made Faslane the focus of long-running peace protests, including the Faslane Peace Camp (established 1982) and large blockades by anti-nuclear campaigners. The base's future was a central issue in debates about Scottish independence.
