Wester Ross (Scottish Gaelic: Ros an Iar) is an area of the Northwest Highlands of Scotland, forming the western portion of the historic county of Ross and Cromarty. It is now part of the Highland council area. The region is renowned for its dramatic mountain and coastal scenery, including the Torridon hills, the Applecross peninsula and the sea lochs of Loch Maree and Loch Torridon.

Wester Ross is one of the most sparsely populated areas of Britain. Ullapool, founded as a herring port in 1788, is the largest settlement and a ferry port for Stornoway in the Outer Hebrides. Fishing, fish farming, tourism and crofting are the mainstays of the local economy.

Geography and nature

The landscape is dominated by ancient Torridonian sandstone mountains such as Liathach, Beinn Eighe and Slioch, rising above glaciated valleys. Beinn Eighe became Britain's first National Nature Reserve in 1951. Loch Maree contains numerous wooded islands with remnants of Caledonian pinewood. In 2016 Wester Ross was designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.

Places of interest

  • Inverewe Garden, a celebrated subtropical garden created by Osgood Mackenzie
  • The Bealach na Bà pass to Applecross, one of Britain's steepest roads
  • Corrieshalloch Gorge near Ullapool
  • The Falls of Measach
