Rope ladder

A rope ladder is a ladder made of rope side rails with rungs of rope, wood or metal, valued for being light, flexible and easy to stow. Unlike rigid ladders it must be anchored at the top (and ideally tensioned) and swings as it is climbed, demanding a distinctive climbing technique.

Uses

Rope ladders have long been used aboard ships — the pilot ladder and Jacob's ladder for boarding — in caving and potholing (wire-cored ladders), in assault and escape equipment, as fire escapes, and in children's play equipment and obstacle courses. On sailing ships, rope ladders formed the ratlines of the standing rigging, allowing crew to climb aloft.

Climbing technique

Because the ladder hangs free, climbers keep the body close and often place a foot on the side rather than the centre of each rung, or climb with the ladder against the body, to stop it swinging away. Modern caving ladders use steel wire with aluminium rungs and are used with a safety line. See also ladder and pilot ladder.
