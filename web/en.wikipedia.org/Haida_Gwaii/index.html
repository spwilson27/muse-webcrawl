Haida Gwaii

Haida Gwaii (Haida: X̱aayda Gwaay, "Islands of the Haida People") is an archipelago off the northern Pacific coast of British Columbia, Canada. Formerly known as the Queen Charlotte Islands, it was renamed in 2010 under a reconciliation agreement between British Columbia and the Haida Nation. The archipelago consists principally of Graham Island in the north and Moresby Island in the south, separated by the Skidegate Channel, along with some 150 smaller islands.

Geography and ecology

The islands cover about 10,180 km² and are known for unique endemic species—often called the "Galápagos of the North"—that evolved in isolation. Lush temperate rainforest covers much of the land, and the southern part of the archipelago is protected as Gwaii Haanas National Park Reserve and Haida Heritage Site, co-managed by Canada and the Haida Nation.

People

The Haida have lived on the islands for millennia and are renowned for monumental totem poles and longhouses; the abandoned village of SGang Gwaay is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The population of roughly 4,800 is concentrated in communities such as Daajing Giids, Skidegate, Masset, and Old Massett.
