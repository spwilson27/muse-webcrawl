A selection of earring styles.

An earring is a piece of jewellery attached to the ear, usually through a piercing in the earlobe, though clip-on and magnetic types exist for unpierced ears. Earrings are worn by both women and men in many cultures and may be made of metal, plastic, glass, beads, wood or precious stones.

History

Ear piercing is one of the oldest known forms of body modification: earrings appear in ancient Egyptian, Persian, Greek and Roman art, and Ötzi the Iceman, dating to about 3300 BCE, had pierced ears. In some eras earrings signalled wealth or status; in others they fell out of fashion in Western Europe, especially when hairstyles and collars covered the ears. They returned to prominence in the 20th century, and ear piercing for men broadened from the 1960s onwards.

Types

Common types include studs, which sit on the lobe; hoops, rings passing through the piercing; drop and chandelier earrings, which hang below the lobe; and ear cuffs, which clip to the outer ear. Ears may also be pierced through the cartilage — helix, tragus and conch piercings — or stretched with gauges. Materials for sensitive skin are typically surgical steel, titanium or high-carat gold to avoid nickel allergy.
