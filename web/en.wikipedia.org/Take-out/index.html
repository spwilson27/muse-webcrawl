Take-out

Take-out (also called takeaway, takeout, or carry-out) is prepared food purchased at a restaurant or fast-food outlet to be eaten elsewhere, typically at home or at work. The term is most common in North American English ("takeout"), while "takeaway" predominates in British, Australian, and New Zealand English.

History

The concept of food sold for consumption off the premises is ancient: thermopolia in Roman cities such as Pompeii served hot food to customers who often lacked kitchens. Modern take-out developed alongside urbanization in the 19th and 20th centuries. In the United States, Chinese-American restaurants popularized take-out from the early 1900s, introducing the iconic folded paper oyster pail as a container. Pizza delivery and drive-through fast food expanded the model after the Second World War.

Packaging

Take-out food requires packaging that retains heat and prevents spillage. Common materials include paper cartons, foam or plastic clamshell containers, aluminium foil trays, and paper bags. Environmental concerns over single-use plastics have driven renewed interest in compostable fibre packaging and, in some countries, bans on expanded polystyrene.

Delivery and apps

From the 2010s, online food-delivery platforms such as DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Deliveroo transformed take-out by aggregating restaurants into apps and outsourcing delivery to gig-economy couriers. The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically accelerated adoption, as dining rooms closed and take-out and delivery became the primary channels for restaurant food.
