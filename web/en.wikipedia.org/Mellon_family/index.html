Mellon family

The Mellon family is an American family of Irish descent based in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, whose fortune in banking, aluminium, oil and investments made it one of the wealthiest families in the United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Its principal members were the banker and industrialist Thomas Mellon (1813–1908), founder of Mellon Bank, and his son Andrew W. Mellon, who served as U.S. Secretary of the Treasury from 1921 to 1932.

Origins

Thomas Mellon emigrated from County Tyrone, Ireland, to Pennsylvania as a child in 1818. Trained as a lawyer and serving as a judge, he founded T. Mellon & Sons' Bank in 1869, which grew into Mellon National Bank, lending to and investing in the industries then rising in western Pennsylvania — coal, steel, aluminium and oil. The family held founding stakes in companies including Alcoa, Gulf Oil and Koppers.

Second and third generations

Andrew Mellon expanded the family's holdings and, as Treasury Secretary under Presidents Harding, Coolidge and Hoover, pursued tax reductions and repayment of First World War debts. He founded the National Gallery of Art in Washington, D.C., donating his art collection and funds for its building. His brother Richard B. Mellon ran the bank and co-founded the Mellon Institute of Industrial Research, which later merged into Carnegie Mellon University.

Philanthropy and later generations

Andrew's children, Paul Mellon and Ailsa Mellon Bruce, became major philanthropists: Paul supported Yale University, the National Gallery and horse racing and breeding; Ailsa founded the Avalon Foundation. Later generations include financier and political donor Timothy Mellon, a major contributor to Republican candidates and super PACs. Estimates in the 2010s put the family's combined wealth in the range of $11–12 billion.
