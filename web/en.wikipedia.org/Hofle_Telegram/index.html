Höfle Telegram

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The intercepted Höfle Telegram, sent on 11 January 1943.

The Höfle Telegram (also Hoefle Telegram) is a secret German radio message sent on 11 January 1943 by SS-Sturmbannführer Hermann Höfle, a deputy of Odilo Globocnik in Operation Reinhard, to SS-Obersturmbannführer Franz Heim in Kraków (with a copy to Adolf Eichmann's office). Intercepted and decrypted by British signals intelligence at Bletchley Park, it reported the number of Jews murdered in the Operation Reinhard extermination camps up to 31 December 1942.

Contents

The message gives cumulative "arrivals" (German: Zugänge) for 1942 in coded form: L (Lublin/Majdanek) 24,733; B (Bełżec) 434,508; S (Sobibór) 101,370; T (Treblinka) 713,555 — a total of 1,274,166. Historians regard these figures as a tally of the victims deported to and murdered in the camps during 1942, the first year of Operation Reinhard.

Discovery and significance

The decrypt remained among classified British files until it was identified by historians Peter Witte and Stephen Tyas, who published it in 2001 after its release to the Public Record Office. Remarkably, the total of 1,274,166 matches exactly the figure in the Korherr Report, prepared for Himmler, confirming that both documents draw on the same underlying statistics. The telegram is regarded as one of the most important documentary proofs of the scale of the murders in the Operation Reinhard camps, none of whose own records survived the war.
