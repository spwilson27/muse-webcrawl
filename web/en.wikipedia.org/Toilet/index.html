A toilet is a piece of sanitary hardware used for the collection or disposal of human urine and faeces. Toilets can be flush toilets, which use water to carry waste through a drainpipe to another location, or dry toilets, which do not require a water seal. They come in a variety of forms in different cultures: sit-down toilets with a seat and bowl predominate in the West, while squat toilets remain common in much of Asia and elsewhere. Access to safe toilets is a central goal of public sanitation policy; billions of people worldwide still lack safely managed sanitation.

History

Some of the earliest toilet-like installations appear in the Indus Valley Civilisation (c. 2500 BCE), where houses at Mohenjo-daro had latrines connected to drains, and in ancient Rome, whose public latrines and the Cloaca Maxima sewer are famous. After a long period in which chamber pots and privies predominated in Europe, the flush toilet was pioneered by John Harington, who installed a flushing device for Elizabeth I in 1596, and was popularised in the 19th century by engineers and manufacturers such as Thomas Crapper. The spread of sewers and piped water in Victorian cities, driven by cholera epidemics and reforms such as London's system built under Joseph Bazalgette, made the water closet a standard household fixture.

Flush toilets

A typical flush toilet consists of a ceramic or porcelain bowl fed by a cistern (tank) holding a measured volume of water. Flushing releases the water to sweep waste through an S- or U-shaped trap — which retains a water seal against sewer gases — into a drainpipe connected to a sewer or septic system. Modern designs include dual-flush mechanisms to save water, pressure-assisted flushing, and high-technology washlet seats with bidet functions, common in Japan.

Non-flush and alternative designs

Dry toilets, pit latrines, composting toilets and urine-diverting designs avoid or reduce water use and are important where water or sewerage is unavailable. Chemical toilets serve aircraft, trains and portable cabins. Spacecraft use specialised airflow-based systems.

Health and access

The United Nations recognises sanitation as a human right, and Sustainable Development Goal 6 targets universal access to adequate sanitation. Inadequate toilets contribute to diarrhoeal disease, cholera and the spread of intestinal parasites, particularly affecting children. World Toilet Day, observed on 19 November, draws attention to the global sanitation crisis.
