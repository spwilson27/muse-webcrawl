The Sierra Nevada is a mountain range in the Western United States, between the Central Valley of California and the Great Basin. The vast majority of the range lies in the state of California, although the Carson Range spur lies primarily in Nevada. The Sierra Nevada is part of the American Cordillera, an almost continuous chain of mountain ranges that forms the western "backbone" of the Americas.

The Sierra runs 400 miles (640 km) north–south and is approximately 70 miles (110 km) across east–west. Notable features include General Sherman, the largest tree in the world by volume; Lake Tahoe, the largest alpine lake in North America; Mount Whitney, at 14,505 feet (4,421 m), the highest point in the contiguous United States; and the Yosemite Valley, sculpted by glaciers out of 100-million-year-old granite.

Geography

The Sierra Nevada lies primarily in central and eastern California, with the Carson Range, a spur of the Sierra, extending into Nevada. West-to-east, the Sierra Nevada's elevation gradually increases from 500 feet (150 m) in the Central Valley to more than 14,000 feet (4,300 m) atop the highest peaks along its eastern crest.

Geology

The bedrock of the Sierra is mostly granite that formed deep underground during the Mesozoic Era, when a subduction zone along the west coast of North America generated a volcanic arc. Beginning about 10 million years ago, the range was uplifted along a major fault on its eastern side, tilting the granitic block westward. Glaciers during the Ice Ages carved the dramatic valleys and lake basins seen today.

Ecology

The Sierra Nevada is divided into several biotic zones, from foothill oak woodland and chaparral on the dry western slopes, through ponderosa pine and giant sequoia forests at mid-elevations, to lodgepole pine, red fir, and alpine tundra near the crest. The range provides habitat for species such as the Sierra Nevada bighorn sheep, the American black bear, and the mountain lion.

History

Native peoples, including the Paiute, Miwok, and Washoe, inhabited the Sierra for thousands of years. The California Gold Rush of 1849 brought hundreds of thousands of miners and settlers. In 1864 the Yosemite Grant was signed by President Abraham Lincoln, an early step toward the national park system; Yosemite, Sequoia, and Kings Canyon national parks now protect large portions of the range.
