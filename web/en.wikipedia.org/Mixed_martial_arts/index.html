Mixed martial arts

Mixed martial arts (MMA) is a full-contact combat sport based on striking, grappling, and ground fighting, incorporating techniques from various combat sports from around the world. Fighters compete in a ring or fenced area — most famously the UFC's Octagon — and can win by knockout, technical knockout, submission, or judges' decision.

History

The ancient Greek sport of pankration combined wrestling and boxing with few rules. Modern MMA grew out of Brazilian vale tudo ("anything goes") contests and the Gracie family's challenge matches promoting Brazilian jiu-jitsu. The Ultimate Fighting Championship was founded in 1993 as a tournament to determine the most effective martial art; the early dominance of Royce Gracie demonstrated the importance of grappling. Japan's PRIDE FC was a major rival promotion in the sport's formative years.

Rules and regulation

Initially marketed as having almost no rules, MMA was widely criticised — U.S. Senator John McCain famously called it "human cockfighting" — and was banned in many jurisdictions. The adoption of the Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts in 2000, with weight classes, timed rounds, and prohibitions on techniques such as eye-gouging, groin strikes, and strikes to the back of the head, enabled sanctioning by athletic commissions and mainstream growth.

Techniques and styles

Effective MMA fighters train across disciplines: striking from boxing, Muay Thai, and karate; clinch work and takedowns from wrestling and judo; and submissions and ground control from Brazilian jiu-jitsu. Modern training is typically divided into striking, wrestling, and submission grappling, together with the transitions between them.
