The all-time medal table for all Olympic Games from 1896 to the present, including Summer Olympic Games, Winter Olympic Games, and a combined total, is tabulated below. These Olympic medal counts do not include the 1906 Intercalated Games, which are no longer recognized by the International Olympic Committee (IOC) as official Games. The IOC itself does not publish all-time tables, and publishes unofficial tables only per single Games.

The United States is by far the most successful nation in Olympic history, leading both the Summer and combined tables. The Soviet Union, which competed from 1952 to 1988 (and as the Unified Team in 1992), ranks second in the Summer table despite its shorter participation. Germany's total is split between several historical teams, including East and West Germany during the Cold War.

Summer and Winter tables

The Summer Olympics account for the majority of medals awarded, owing to the much larger number of events. The United States has won over 2,600 Summer medals. In the Winter Games, Norway is the most successful nation, with more than 400 medals, followed by the United States, Germany, Canada, Austria, and Sweden. Norway also leads the all-time Winter gold medal count.

Medals per capita

Alternative rankings adjust totals by population, GDP, or team size. By medals per capita, small countries such as Liechtenstein (in Winter sports), Bermuda, Grenada, and the Bahamas rank highly, while Finland and Hungary historically achieved exceptional medal rates relative to their populations.
