Katsudon

Katsudon (Japanese: カツ丼) is a popular Japanese dish consisting of a bowl of rice topped with a deep-fried breaded pork cutlet (tonkatsu), simmered with egg, onions, and a sweet-savoury broth of dashi, soy sauce, and mirin. It is a type of donburi, or rice-bowl dish.

Preparation

A freshly fried tonkatsu cutlet is sliced and briefly simmered in a small pan with dashi stock, soy sauce, sugar, and sliced onion; beaten egg is poured over and cooked until just set, then the mixture is slid onto steamed rice and often garnished with mitsuba or scallions. Because simmering softens the crisp coating, some restaurants serve sauce katsudon — a variant from Fukui Prefecture in which the cutlet is dipped in Worcestershire-style sauce and placed on rice without egg.

Cultural associations

The name combines tonkatsu with donburi; it also puns on the word katsu (勝つ), "to win", making katsudon a traditional good-luck meal eaten by students the night before examinations. A long-running popular trope in Japanese crime dramas features suspects being served katsudon during police interrogation, moved to confess by the investigator's kindness.

Variants

Related dishes substitute other breaded proteins: chikinkatsudon uses chicken cutlet, while oyakodon uses simmered chicken and egg without breading. Regional versions vary the sauce and toppings, and convenience stores across Japan sell ready-made katsudon.
