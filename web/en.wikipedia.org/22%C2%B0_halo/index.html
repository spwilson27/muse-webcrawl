22° halo

22° halo
TypeAtmospheric optical phenomenon
CauseRefraction of sunlight or moonlight through hexagonal ice crystals
Angular radiusApproximately 22°

A 22° halo is an atmospheric optical phenomenon that consists of a halo with an apparent radius of approximately 22° around the Sun or Moon. When visible around the Moon, it is known as a moon ring or winter halo. It forms as sunlight or moonlight is refracted in millions of hexagonal ice crystals suspended in the atmosphere.

Formation

The halo is produced when light passes through the 60° prism faces of randomly oriented hexagonal ice crystals in cirrus or cirrostratus clouds. The minimum deviation angle for such prisms is about 22°, so light concentrates at that angle from the light source, producing a bright ring. No light is refracted at angles smaller than 22°, which makes the sky noticeably darker inside the ring. The inner edge of the halo often shows a reddish tint, since red light is refracted slightly less than blue light.

Folklore and weather

In folk weather lore, a ring around the Moon is said to foretell approaching rain or snow, because the high cirrostratus clouds that produce halos often precede a warm front. The 22° halo is among the most common of all halo phenomena, far more frequent than rainbows in many climates.
