In physics, spacetime (also spelled space-time or space–time continuum) is any mathematical model that combines the three dimensions of space and the one dimension of time into a single four-dimensional continuum. Spacetime diagrams can be used to visualize relativistic effects, such as why different observers perceive differently where and when events occur.

Until the turn of the 20th century, the assumption had been that the three-dimensional geometry of the universe (its spatial expression in terms of coordinates, distances, and directions) was independent of one-dimensional time. Physics has found it convenient to regard the universe as a four-dimensional spacetime, an idea given precise geometric form by Hermann Minkowski in 1908, building on Albert Einstein's 1905 theory of special relativity.

Introduction

Non-relativistic classical mechanics treats time as a universal quantity of measurement which is uniform throughout space, separate from space. Classical mechanics assumes that time has a constant rate of passage, independent of the observer's state of motion, or anything external. It assumes an indefinitely long "past" and "future".

In contrast, in relativity, the invariance of the speed of light in a vacuum against which measurements of space and time are made leads to the conclusion that time and space can be combined into a single entity — the spacetime metric — where the spatial and temporal properties of events are unified. An "event" is a point in spacetime, specified by its time and place.

Spacetime in special relativity

In special relativity, the spacetime interval between two events is invariant under Lorentz transformations. Unlike distances in Euclidean space, the spacetime interval combines spatial separation and temporal separation with opposite signs in the metric, leading to the concepts of timelike, lightlike, and spacelike separations, and to causal structure represented by light cones.

Spacetime in general relativity

In general relativity, spacetime is not a fixed background but a dynamical entity: mass and energy curve spacetime, and the curvature in turn governs the motion of matter and light. This curvature is described by the Einstein field equations. Phenomena such as gravitational time dilation, the bending of light by the Sun, and gravitational waves are expressions of spacetime geometry.
