AuthorJohann Gottlieb Fichte
First presentation1794, Jena lectures
SchoolGerman idealism
Literal meaning"Doctrine of science / knowledge"

The Wissenschaftslehre ("Doctrine of Science", or "Doctrine of Knowledge") is the central philosophical system of Johann Gottlieb Fichte, first presented in his 1794–95 Jena lectures and in the Grundlage der gesamten Wissenschaftslehre (Foundations of the Entire Science of Knowledge). Fichte repeatedly revised the system in later lecture cycles, producing numerous distinct versions.

Doctrine

Fichte sought to provide philosophy with a single self-evident first principle from which all knowledge could be derived, completing what he saw as the unfinished project of Kant's critical philosophy. His starting point is the self-positing activity of the I: the I posits itself absolutely, posits a not-I in opposition, and posits a divisible I and divisible not-I in mutual limitation. From this activity Fichte derives the categories, the structure of consciousness, and the foundations of both theoretical and practical reason.

Reception

The system was a founding document of German idealism, influencing Schelling and Hegel, but was also attacked as a form of subjective idealism or "nihilism". Fichte's dismissal from Jena in the atheism controversy of 1799 ended the first phase of the doctrine's development; he continued to present revised versions in Erlangen and Berlin until his death in 1814.
