Business school

A business school is a university-level institution that confers degrees in business administration and management. Typical subjects include accounting, finance, marketing, organisational behaviour, operations, strategy and economics.

Degrees

The flagship graduate degree is the Master of Business Administration (MBA), alongside specialised master's degrees, undergraduate business degrees and doctoral programmes such as the PhD and DBA. Executive education offers short non-degree courses for working managers.

History and accreditation

The first business schools appeared in the nineteenth century, including ESCP in Paris (1819) and the Wharton School (1881) in the United States; Harvard Business School, founded in 1908, popularised the case method of teaching. Leading schools seek "triple crown" accreditation from AACSB, EQUIS and AMBA, and are compared in rankings published by the Financial Times and others.
