Royalties

Royalties

TypePayment for use of an asset
Common basesPercentage of revenue; per-unit fee
Typical assetsCopyrights, patents, trademarks, mineral rights, franchises

Royalties are payments made by one party (the licensee) to another party (the licensor or owner) for the right to ongoing use of an asset, most often intellectual property. The term derives from the rights historically reserved to the Crown over mines and landed property. Royalties are usually calculated as a percentage of the gross or net revenues obtained from the use of the asset, or as a fixed price per unit sold.

Intellectual property royalties

In publishing, authors typically receive royalties of around 10–15 percent of the price of each book sold. In the music industry, songwriters and publishers earn mechanical royalties for recordings and performance royalties collected by societies such as ASCAP and PRS when works are played publicly. Patent holders license inventions to manufacturers for royalties commonly expressed as a percentage of the selling price of the licensed product.

Natural resources

Landowners and governments charge royalties on the extraction of oil, gas and minerals, calculated as a share of production or its value. In the United States, federal offshore oil and gas leases have historically carried royalty rates around 12.5 to 18.75 percent.

Franchising and licensing

Franchise agreements typically include a continuing royalty, often 4–8 percent of turnover, paid by the franchisee for use of the brand, systems and trademarks. Trademark licensing in merchandising works on a similar basis.

Accounting and taxation

Royalty income is generally taxable as ordinary income or business income depending on the recipient's circumstances, and cross-border royalty payments are frequently subject to withholding tax, mitigated by double-taxation treaties. Disputes over royalty rates in patent licensing have given rise to the concept of FRAND (fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory) terms for standard-essential patents.
