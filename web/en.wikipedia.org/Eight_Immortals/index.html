Eight Immortals

The Eight Immortals are a group of legendary xian (immortals) in Chinese mythology, widely depicted in Chinese art and literature.

The Eight Immortals (Chinese: 八仙; pinyin: Bāxiān) are a group of legendary xian ("immortals") in Chinese mythology. Each immortal's power can be transferred to a tool of power (法器) that can bestow life or destroy evil. Together, these eight tools are called the "Covert Eight Immortals" (暗八仙). The group became fixed during the Ming dynasty and remains among the most popular motifs in Chinese art, festivals and folk religion.

The members

The Eight Immortals are Zhongli Quan, fan-bearer and eldest of the group; Zhang Guolao, an eccentric magician who rides a mule backwards; Lü Dongbin, a scholar-swordsman and patron of scholars; Li Tieguai ("Iron-Crutch Li"), a healer with an iron crutch and gourd; Han Xiangzi, a flute-playing nephew of the scholar Han Yu; He Xiangu, the sole woman, carrying a lotus; Lan Caihe, a wandering singer of ambiguous gender carrying a flower basket; and Cao Guojiu, a courtier in official dress holding castanets or a jade tablet.

The Eight Immortals cross the sea

The most famous tale, "The Eight Immortals Cross the Sea" (八仙過海), tells how the immortals, returning from the Queen Mother's peach banquet, chose to cross the sea each by a unique magical means rather than ride clouds together, provoking a battle with the Dragon Kings of the Eastern Sea. The story gave rise to the proverb "the Eight Immortals cross the sea, each showing their divine powers," meaning that everyone has their own way of accomplishing a task.

Cultural significance

Representing a cross-section of society — old and young, male and female, rich and poor, noble and commoner — the Eight Immortals are auspicious symbols of longevity and good fortune, appearing in porcelain, jade carving, embroidery and temple decoration across China and the Chinese diaspora.
