Fibromyalgia

Fibromyalgia is a medical condition defined by the presence of chronic widespread pain, fatigue, cognitive disturbance, and a high percentage of specific tender points occurring in precise locations. It is characterised by heightened pain sensitivity (allodynia and hyperalgesia) and is believed to involve abnormal processing of pain signals in the central nervous system, a process called central sensitisation.

Signs and symptoms

The defining symptoms are chronic widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, sleep disturbance, and cognitive problems often called "fibro fog". Other common symptoms include headaches, irritable bowel syndrome, temporomandibular joint disorders, anxiety, and depression. Symptoms are often triggered or aggravated by physical or psychological stress.

Cause and diagnosis

The exact cause is unknown; genetics and environmental factors (such as stress, trauma, and infections) are thought to contribute. Diagnosis is clinical, based on criteria published by the American College of Rheumatology — the 1990 criteria relied on tender points, while the 2010/2016 criteria use widespread pain and symptom severity indices. There is no laboratory test that confirms the diagnosis, and other conditions must be excluded.

Treatment

There is no cure, and treatment aims at symptom management. Guidelines emphasise aerobic exercise as a first-line intervention, together with cognitive behavioural therapy and, where needed, medications such as duloxetine, milnacipran, and pregabalin, which are approved for fibromyalgia in some countries. Strong opioids are not recommended.
