Battle of Cajamarca

The Battle of Cajamarca, fought on 16 November 1532, was the ambush and capture of the Inca emperor Atahualpa by a small Spanish force under Francisco Pizarro at Cajamarca in the Peruvian Andes. It marked the opening of the Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire and is one of the most consequential encounters of the colonial era.

Background

Pizarro's expedition of fewer than 200 men reached Cajamarca as Atahualpa, victorious in the Inca civil war against his brother Huáscar, rested nearby with an army of tens of thousands. The emperor agreed to meet the strangers in the town's great plaza, entering with a procession of several thousand attendants, largely unarmed for the ceremonial occasion.

The ambush

The Spanish were hidden in the buildings around the plaza with cavalry and a few small cannon. After the friar Vicente de Valverde's address and the ritual requerimiento were rebuffed, Pizarro gave the signal to attack. Cannon fire, cavalry charges and steel weapons threw the packed plaza into panic; thousands of Inca were killed, and Pizarro personally seized Atahualpa. Not a single Spaniard was killed.

Aftermath

Atahualpa filled a room with gold and silver as ransom — the famous treasure of Cajamarca — but was nevertheless tried and executed in July 1533. His capture decapitated the Inca state and enabled the Spanish advance on Cusco, though Inca resistance continued for decades.
