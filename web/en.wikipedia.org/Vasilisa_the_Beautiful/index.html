Vasilisa the Beautiful

Vasilisa the Beautiful (Russian: Василиса Прекрасная) is a Russian fairy tale collected by Alexander Afanasyev in his Narodnye russkie skazki (1855–1863). It is one of the best-known tales featuring the witch Baba Yaga.

Synopsis

Vasilisa is a beautiful girl whose dying mother gives her a small wooden doll and instructs her to feed it and ask its help in times of trouble. After her father's remarriage, her stepmother and stepsisters torment her with endless work, which the doll performs for her. One day, when the household fire goes out, the stepfamily sends Vasilisa to the hut of Baba Yaga to fetch fire, hoping the witch will eat her.

In the forest, Vasilisa passes three horsemen — white, red, and black, representing day, the sun, and night — before reaching Baba Yaga's hut, which stands on chicken legs and is fenced with human bones topped by skulls. The witch sets her impossible tasks — sorting poppy seeds from dust and grains from one another — which the doll accomplishes. In return for her courtesy and the blessing of her mother, Baba Yaga gives Vasilisa a skull lantern whose burning eyes later incinerate the stepmother and stepsisters. Vasilisa subsequently works as a weaver of extraordinary skill, and the Tsar, admiring her linen, marries her.

Interpretation and adaptations

The tale is classified as Aarne–Thompson–Uther type 480, "The Kind and the Unkind Girls," combined with the helpful doll motif. It is frequently read as a story of initiation and maturation. The artist Ivan Bilibin produced famous illustrations of the tale in 1899, and Vasilisa has appeared in numerous modern retellings and films.
