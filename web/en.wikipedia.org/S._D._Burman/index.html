S. D. Burman

Sachin Dev Burman (1 October 1906 – 31 October 1975) was an Indian music director and singer. A member of the Tripura royal family, he started his career with Bengali films in 1937 and later began composing for Hindi movies, becoming one of the most successful and influential Bollywood film music composers of all time.

Early life

Burman was born in Comilla, Bengal Presidency (now in Bangladesh), into the royal family of Tripura. His father, Nabadwipchandra Dev Burman, was a sitarist and Dhrupad singer who gave him his first musical training. Burman studied at Kumar Boarding School and Yusuf School in Comilla before graduating from Victoria College.

Career

Burman began as a radio singer on Calcutta Radio in the late 1920s, recording folk and light classical songs in Bengali and Hindi. He composed for Bengali films from 1937 and moved to Bombay in 1944. His breakthrough in Hindi cinema came with Mashaal (1950), and he went on to score classics including Pyaasa (1957), Kala Pani (1958), Chalti Ka Naam Gaadi (1958), Kaagaz Ke Phool (1959), Bandini (1963), Guide (1965), Aradhana (1969), and Abhimaan (1973).

Over a career spanning more than four decades, Burman composed music for over 100 films. He won the National Film Award for Best Music Direction and multiple Filmfare Awards, and was awarded the Padma Shri in 1969.

Legacy

Burman is regarded as one of the greatest composers of Indian cinema, known for his gift for melody, his use of folk tunes, and his mentoring of singers such as Kishore Kumar, Lata Mangeshkar, Mohammed Rafi, and his own son, Rahul Dev Burman, who became a leading composer in his own right.
