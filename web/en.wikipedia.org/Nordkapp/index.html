Nordkapp (Norwegian for "North Cape"; Northern Sami: Davvenjárga) is a cape on the northern coast of the island of Magerøya in Northern Norway, in Nordkapp Municipality. Its 307-metre-high cliff plateau is commonly, though not strictly accurately, promoted as the northernmost point of Europe reachable by road. The English name North Cape was coined by the explorer Richard Chancellor in 1553, when he sailed past it in search of the Northeast Passage.

The northernmost point

The neighbouring promontory of Knivskjellodden, about 1.5 kilometres farther north, is the actual northernmost point of Magerøya and of Europe's mainland-connected islands, but it is accessible only on foot. Cape Nordkinn, on the mainland, is Europe's northernmost mainland point, while the northernmost point of Europe overall lies further north in the Svalbard archipelago. Nordkapp's fame rests on its accessibility, its dramatic cliff and its position along the Hurtigruten coastal route.

Tourism

Nordkapp is a major tourist destination, drawing some 200,000 visitors a year to the North Cape Hall visitor centre, with its exhibitions, cinema and the iconic steel globe monument. Visitors come especially for the midnight sun, visible from mid-May to late July, and in winter for the polar night and northern lights. The cape is connected by the European route E69, which includes the North Cape Tunnel from the mainland, opened in 1999.
