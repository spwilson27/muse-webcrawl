Zengid dynasty

The Zengid or Zangid dynasty (Arabic: زنكيون) was a Muslim dynasty of Oghuz Turkic origin which ruled parts of the Levant and Upper Mesopotamia on behalf of the Seljuk Empire. The dynasty was founded by Imad ad-Din Zengi, atabeg (governor) of Mosul from 1127, and is renowned in the Islamic world for spearheading the counter-crusade against the Crusader states of the Levant.

Zengi extended his authority over Aleppo in 1128 and, in December 1144, captured Edessa from Joscelin II — the first Crusader state to fall — an event that provoked the Second Crusade. His son Nur ad-Din (r. 1146–1174) united Aleppo and Damascus, built a string of madrasas, hospitals and fortifications, and sponsored the campaigns of his Kurdish general Shirkuh and Shirkuh's nephew Saladin that brought Fatimid Egypt under Sunni control. After Nur ad-Din's death in 1174, Saladin progressively absorbed the Zengid territories of Syria and the Jazira, founding the Ayyubid dynasty, though a Zengid branch continued to rule Mosul under nominal Ayyubid, then Mongol, suzerainty until 1250.

Legacy

The Zengids are remembered for their patronage of Sunni learning, their promotion of jihad against the Crusader states, and major monuments such as the Nur al-Din Bimaristan (hospital) in Damascus and the minaret of the Great al-Nuri Mosque in Mosul.
