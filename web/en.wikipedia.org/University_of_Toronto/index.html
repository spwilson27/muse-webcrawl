The University of Toronto (U of T) is a public research university in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Founded by royal charter in 1827 as King's College, it is Canada's largest university and one of the world's leading research institutions, consistently ranked first in Canada and among the top universities globally. It operates as a collegiate university across three campuses: St. George (downtown Toronto), Scarborough, and Mississauga.

History

Established under Bishop John Strachan, the university became secular in 1850 and adopted its present name. Its collegiate system, modeled on Oxford and Cambridge, federates colleges including University College, Trinity, Victoria, and St. Michael's. In 1921, Frederick Banting and Charles Best, working in J.J.R. Macleod's laboratory, discovered insulin — one of the most celebrated medical breakthroughs of the 20th century — and the university's Connaught Laboratories helped produce it at scale.

Academics

With near 100,000 students and over 700 undergraduate and 200 graduate programs, U of T leads Canadian universities in research funding and output. It is a member of the U15 group and the Association of American Universities. Alumni and faculty include eleven Nobel laureates and leaders in artificial intelligence (Geoffrey Hinton), literary criticism (Northrop Frye), and communications theory (Marshall McLuhan, Harold Innis). The university library system is the third-largest academic library in North America.

Athletics

The Varsity Blues are among Canada's oldest university sports programs; the football team won the first Grey Cup in 1909.
