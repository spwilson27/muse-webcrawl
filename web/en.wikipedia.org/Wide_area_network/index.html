Wide area network

A wide area network (WAN) is a telecommunications network that extends over a large geographic area, typically spanning cities, countries or continents. The Internet is the largest WAN in existence. WANs contrast with local area networks (LANs), which cover a single building or campus, and metropolitan area networks (MANs), which cover a city.

Technologies

WANs connect LANs through routers and links often leased from carriers. Historical technologies include X.25, Frame Relay and ATM; modern enterprise WANs use MPLS, leased lines, and increasingly broadband Internet connections secured by VPNs. Software-defined WAN (SD-WAN) overlays manage multiple link types centrally, steering traffic for performance and cost. Common routing protocols include BGP across provider boundaries and OSPF or IS-IS within networks.

Design considerations

WAN links are slower, costlier and have higher latency than LAN links, so designs emphasize reliability, redundancy and traffic engineering. Transmission may use packet switching over optical fibre, microwave or satellite links.
