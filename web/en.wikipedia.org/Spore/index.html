Spore

Produced byFungi, algae, plants, some bacteria and protozoa
FunctionsReproduction, dispersal, survival of adverse conditions
ContrastGamete; seed

In biology, a spore is a unit of sexual or asexual reproduction that may be adapted for dispersal and for survival, often for extended periods of time, in unfavourable conditions. Spores form part of the life cycles of many plants, algae, fungi and protozoa. They are typically single-celled, haploid and unicellular, and are produced by sporogenesis in structures such as sporangia.

Roles and types

In fungi, spores are the primary means of reproduction and dispersal, released in vast numbers from fruiting bodies and carried by wind, water or animals. In the plant life cycle, the sporophyte generation produces haploid spores by meiosis, which grow into gametophytes. Ferns and mosses reproduce mainly by spores rather than seeds. Some bacteria, notably Bacillus and Clostridium, form endospores — dormant, extremely resistant structures that can survive heat, radiation and desiccation for very long periods.

Properties

Spores are often encased in tough protective coats and have low metabolic activity, enabling them to persist until conditions improve. This durability makes pathogenic spores, such as those of anthrax (Bacillus anthracis), serious biological hazards, and it complicates sterilisation in medicine and the food industry. Conversely, fungal and bacterial spores are exploited in agriculture, fermentation and biotechnology.
