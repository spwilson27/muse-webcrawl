National Convention of Gospel Choirs and Choruses

The National Convention of Gospel Choirs and Choruses (NCGCC) is an American organisation founded in 1932 by Thomas A. Dorsey, the composer widely regarded as the father of gospel music, together with Sallie Martin and others in Chicago. It is one of the oldest gospel music institutions in the United States.

History and mission

Dorsey founded the convention to train singers, choir directors and musicians in the new gospel blues style he was pioneering, and to promote the performance and preservation of gospel music in Black churches. The annual convention, held in rotating cities, brings together thousands of delegates for workshops, rehearsals and nightly musical services, culminating in mass choir performances. Its departments include youth and young adult divisions, directors and musicians auxiliaries, and the alumni chorale.

Influence

Generations of gospel artists passed through the NCGCC, which helped standardise gospel choir technique and spread the repertoire of Dorsey ("Take My Hand, Precious Lord"), Sallie Martin, Roberta Martin and their successors across the country. The organisation continues to hold its annual session each August.
