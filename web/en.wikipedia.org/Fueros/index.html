Fueros

Fueros (Spanish: [ˈfweɾos]) were compendia of laws and privileges granted by monarchs in medieval Spain, particularly in Castile, Navarre, and the Basque territories. The word derives from the Latin forum, meaning an out-of-doors market or public place. Each fuero set out the rights, taxes, and self-governing institutions of a town or region.

History

From the 9th century onward, kings granted fueros to encourage resettlement of frontier lands during the Reconquista. Towns received charters such as the Fuero de León (1017) and the Fuero Juzgo, which standardised legal codes across regions.

Basque fueros

The Basque provinces and Navarre retained extensive fueros — including fiscal autonomy and exemption from customs duties and military conscription — until the 19th century. Their abolition after the Carlist Wars (formally in 1876) led to the modern Concierto Económico, under which the Basque Country still collects its own taxes.
