Public housing in the United Kingdom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Public housing in the United Kingdom, generally known as council housing, is housing provided by local authorities (councils) and housing associations at below-market rents to households in need. At its peak in the late 1970s, council housing accommodated roughly a third of British households; today social housing accounts for around 17 per cent of households in England.

History

Large-scale council housing began after the First World War with the Housing, Town Planning, &c. Act 1919 — the "Homes fit for heroes" legislation associated with Christopher Addison — which subsidised local authorities to build working-class housing. After the Second World War, both Labour and Conservative governments pursued mass housebuilding to replace bomb-damaged and slum housing; under Aneurin Bevan and later Harold Macmillan, hundreds of thousands of council homes were built annually, including high-rise tower blocks and new towns.

Right to Buy and decline

The Housing Act 1980 introduced the Right to Buy, allowing council tenants to purchase their homes at substantial discounts. Over two million homes were sold, greatly reducing the council stock, while receipts were largely not reinvested in replacement housing. From the 1980s onward, housing associations became the main providers of new social housing, and many councils transferred their remaining stock to them.

Allocation and tenancy

Social housing is allocated according to need, using waiting lists and statutory homelessness duties owed by local authorities. Tenants traditionally held secure tenancies; later reforms introduced probationary and flexible tenancies. Rents are regulated and are generally set below market levels.

Contemporary issues

Long waiting lists, the loss of stock through Right to Buy, and debates about building safety — sharply raised by the Grenfell Tower fire of 2017 — have made social housing a persistent theme in British politics. Successive governments have pledged increased affordable housebuilding with mixed results.
