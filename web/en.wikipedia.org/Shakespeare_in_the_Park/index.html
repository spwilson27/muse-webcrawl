Shakespeare in the Park

The Delacorte Theater

Shakespeare in the Park is an annual festival of free performances of William Shakespeare's plays, presented each summer by The Public Theater at the open-air Delacorte Theater in Central Park, New York City. Founded by Joseph Papp, it is one of New York's most beloved cultural institutions, and the concept has been copied by free Shakespeare festivals in cities across North America and beyond.

History

Joseph Papp founded the Shakespeare Workshop in 1954, staging free productions in churches and on a touring truck. Despite opposition from Parks Commissioner Robert Moses — who objected to free admission and demanded the company charge for seats — Papp prevailed in court and in public, and the city built the Delacorte Theater, which opened in 1962 with The Merchant of Venice starring George C. Scott. The theatre, named for philanthropists George T. and Valerie Delacorte, seats about 1,800 and overlooks Turtle Pond and Belvedere Castle.

Productions and performers

Two productions are mounted each summer. Alumni include Meryl Streep, Al Pacino, James Earl Jones, Raul Julia, Kevin Kline, Denzel Washington, Anne Hathaway, and Lupita Nyong'o. Notable productions include the 1976 Henry V, the 1990 Richard III with Denzel Washington, and a widely discussed 2017 Julius Caesar whose title character resembled the then-US president. Tickets are distributed free on the day of performance, famously drawing long queues through the park; lotteries and borough tours broaden access.

Legacy

Shakespeare in the Park embodies Papp's conviction that culture should be as free and public as libraries. Its success inspired free outdoor Shakespeare companies in Louisville, San Francisco, and hundreds of other communities, and its parent organisation, The Public Theater, went on to originate Broadway landmarks such as Hair, A Chorus Line, and Hamilton.
