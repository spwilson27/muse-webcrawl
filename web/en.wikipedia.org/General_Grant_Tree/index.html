General Grant Tree

The General Grant Tree is a giant sequoia (Sequoiadendron giganteum) located in the Grant Grove section of Kings Canyon National Park in the Sierra Nevada of California. It is the second-largest known tree on Earth by trunk volume, after the General Sherman Tree in Sequoia National Park, with a volume of roughly 1,320 cubic metres (about 46,600 cubic feet), a height of about 81.5 metres (267 feet), and a ground circumference of about 33 metres (107.6 feet). Its age is estimated at around 1,650 years.

History

The tree was named in 1867 by Joseph Harding in honour of Ulysses S. Grant, the commanding general of the Union Army during the American Civil War and later President of the United States. Grant Grove was protected first as part of General Grant National Park, established in 1890 — one of the earliest national parks — and was incorporated into the much larger Kings Canyon National Park when it was created in 1940.

The Nation's Christmas Tree

In 1925 the tree was designated the "Nation's Christmas Tree" following a campaign by the nearby town of Sanger, California, whose residents held a Christmas ceremony at its base that year; the designation was officially recognised by the federal government in 1956, when President Dwight D. Eisenhower also proclaimed the tree a National Shrine — the only living object so designated — as a memorial to Americans who have died in war. An annual "Trek to the Tree" ceremony continues to be held each December.
