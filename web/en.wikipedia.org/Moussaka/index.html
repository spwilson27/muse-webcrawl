Moussaka

Moussaka is an eggplant- or potato-based dish, often including ground meat, which is common in the Balkans and the Eastern Mediterranean, with many local and regional variations. The modern Greek variant was created in the 1920s by Nikolaos Tselementes. Many versions have a top layer made of milk-based sauce thickened with egg (custard) or flour (béchamel sauce). In Greece, the dish is layered and typically served hot.

Origins and name

The name derives from the Arabic musaqqaʿa ("chilled" or "moistened"), transmitted through Ottoman Turkish musakka. In the Levant and Egypt, moussaka (Egyptian Arabic: mesa'ah) is a dish of cooked or fried vegetables — eggplant, tomato, and sometimes chickpeas — often served cold as a mezze and usually without meat. The Ottoman Empire spread versions of the dish across the Balkans and the Middle East.

Greek version

Tselementes, a French-trained Greek chef, gave the dish its now-canonical form: slices of fried (or baked) eggplant, a spiced tomato and minced lamb or beef sauce, sometimes with layers of potato or zucchini, topped with a thick béchamel sauce enriched with cheese and egg, then baked until golden. His aim was to refine Greek cooking along French lines, and his version became a national dish of Greece.

Regional variations

In Bulgaria, Serbia, North Macedonia, and Romania (musaca), the dish is often made with potatoes instead of eggplant and minced pork, sometimes topped with a yogurt-egg mixture rather than béchamel. Turkish musakka is not layered but prepared as a casserole of sautéed eggplant, peppers, tomatoes, onions, and minced meat, served warm or at room temperature. Vegetarian and vegan versions substitute lentils, mushrooms, or textured vegetable protein for the meat and plant milk for the béchamel.
