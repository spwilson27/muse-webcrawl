Wounded Knee Occupation
Date27 February – 8 May 1973 (71 days)
LocationWounded Knee, Pine Ridge Indian Reservation, South Dakota
ParticipantsAmerican Indian Movement activists and Oglala Lakota; US federal law enforcement
OutcomeOccupation ended by agreement; two occupiers killed

The Wounded Knee Occupation, also known as the Wounded Knee Incident, began on 27 February 1973, when about 200 Oglala Lakota and followers of the American Indian Movement (AIM) seized and occupied the town of Wounded Knee, South Dakota, on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation.

Background

The site was chosen symbolically: Wounded Knee was where US troops had massacred Lakota men, women and children in 1890. The occupiers protested against the administration of tribal chairman Richard Wilson, whom they accused of corruption and abuse of opponents, and against the US government's failure to honour treaties with Native American nations. Their demands included Senate hearings on broken treaties and an investigation of the Bureau of Indian Affairs.

The occupation

Federal marshals, FBI agents and military advisers surrounded the village, and the 71-day standoff drew national media attention and support from celebrities and civil-rights figures. Sporadic gunfire killed two Native occupiers, Frank Clearwater and Buddy Lamont, and wounded a US marshal. The occupation ended on 8 May 1973 with an agreement for talks, which produced little substantive change.

Aftermath

AIM leaders Russell Means and Dennis Banks were tried on charges arising from the occupation; their case was dismissed in 1974 because of government misconduct. The occupation became a landmark of Native American activism, raising awareness of treaty rights and reservation conditions, though Pine Ridge itself remained marked by poverty and violence in the years that followed.
