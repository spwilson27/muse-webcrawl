The Place de la Concorde (French: [plas də la kɔ̃ɡkɔʁd]; lit. 'Concord Square') is a public square in Paris, France. Measuring 7.6 hectares (19 acres) in area, it is the largest square in the French capital. It is located in the city's eighth arrondissement, at the eastern end of the Champs-Élysées.

History

The square was designed by Ange-Jacques Gabriel and built between 1755 and 1775 as the Place Louis XV, in honour of the king, whose equestrian statue stood at its centre. The square was ringed by a dry moat and balustrade, and two monumental buildings—including the Hôtel de la Marine—were constructed on its northern side.

French Revolution

During the French Revolution the statue of Louis XV was torn down and the square was renamed Place de la Révolution. A guillotine was erected on the square, and it became the principal place of public execution: more than 1,100 people were beheaded there, including King Louis XVI (21 January 1793), Queen Marie Antoinette (16 October 1793), Madame du Barry, Charlotte Corday, Georges Danton, Camille Desmoulins, and Maximilien Robespierre.

Later history

After the Revolution the square received its current name, Place de la Concorde, in 1795, as a symbol of national reconciliation. In the 1830s, under King Louis-Philippe, the square was redesigned by Jacques Hittorff, who added its two monumental fountains—the Fountain of the Seas and the Fountain of the Rivers—and the eight statues representing French cities.

Luxor Obelisk

At the centre of the square stands the Luxor Obelisk, a 3,300-year-old Egyptian obelisk originally marking the entrance to the Luxor Temple. It was gifted to France by Muhammad Ali Pasha, ruler of Egypt, transported to Paris at great expense, and erected in 1836. The obelisk is 23 metres (75 ft) high and covered in hieroglyphs; in 1998 a gold-leafed pyramidion was added to its tip.

Surroundings

The square lies on the historical axis (Axe historique) of Paris, between the Tuileries Garden to the east and the Champs-Élysées to the west; to the south the Pont de la Concorde crosses the Seine to the National Assembly, and to the north the Rue Royale leads to the Madeleine. The Hôtel de Crillon, one of Paris's grand palace hotels, occupies part of its northern side.

Events

The square hosts major public events and celebrations, and served as a venue during the 2024 Summer Olympics, hosting urban sports including skateboarding, BMX freestyle, and breaking in a temporary arena.
