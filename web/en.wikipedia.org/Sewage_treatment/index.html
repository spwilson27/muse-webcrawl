Sewage treatment

SynonymsWastewater treatment (broader term)
InputsMunicipal sewage
OutputsTreated effluent, sewage sludge (biosolids)
StagesPreliminary, primary, secondary, tertiary
Key processActivated sludge

Sewage treatment (or domestic wastewater treatment, municipal wastewater treatment) is a type of wastewater treatment which aims to remove contaminants from sewage to produce an effluent suitable for discharge to the environment or for reuse, thereby preventing water pollution. Sewage contains wastewater from households and businesses and sometimes pre-treated industrial waste.

Stages of treatment

Preliminary treatment removes coarse debris using screens and grit channels. Primary treatment settles out suspended solids in sedimentation tanks, removing about half of the solids and a third of the organic load (BOD). Secondary treatment biologically degrades dissolved and colloidal organic matter using microorganisms — most commonly the activated sludge process, invented in Manchester in 1914, in which air is blown through aeration basins full of microbial flocs, followed by clarifiers. Alternatives include trickling filters, rotating biological contactors, and membrane bioreactors. Tertiary treatment may add nutrient removal (nitrogen and phosphorus, to prevent eutrophication), filtration and disinfection by chlorination, ultraviolet light or ozone.

Sludge treatment and reuse

Solids removed from the sewage form sewage sludge, which is typically thickened, digested anaerobically (producing biogas), dewatered and then reused as biosolids in agriculture, incinerated, or landfilled. Treated effluent is increasingly reused for irrigation and groundwater recharge in water-scarce regions. Historically, large-scale sewerage and treatment followed cholera epidemics in 19th-century cities such as London, where Joseph Bazalgette built a monumental interceptor-sewer system after the Great Stink of 1858.

Global situation

The United Nations estimates that a large share of the world's wastewater is still discharged untreated, contributing to disease and ecosystem damage; Sustainable Development Goal 6 includes a target to halve untreated wastewater. Decentralised systems — septic tanks, constructed wetlands and package plants — serve areas without sewers.
