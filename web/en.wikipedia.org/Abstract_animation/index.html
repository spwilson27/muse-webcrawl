Abstract animation

Also known as: Absolute film, visual music

Pioneers: Walther Ruttmann, Hans Richter, Viking Eggeling, Oskar Fischinger

Abstract animation is a form of experimental film in which animated images do not represent recognisable objects or narratives but instead compose movement, colour, rhythm and form for their own sake — often described as "visual music." It emerged in the 1920s alongside abstract painting and modernist music.

History

German artists Walther Ruttmann (Lichtspiel: Opus I, 1921), Hans Richter (Rhythmus 21, 1921) and Viking Eggeling (Symphonie Diagonale, 1924) created the first abstract films, painting or drawing directly on film or animating geometric shapes. Oskar Fischinger refined the art in Germany and then Hollywood, synchronising flowing forms to music; his work influenced Disney's Fantasia. In Britain and Canada, Len Lye and Norman McLaren pioneered drawing and scratching directly onto film stock, while Mary Ellen Bute and John and James Whitney explored electronic and early computer animation.

Techniques and legacy

Techniques include cameraless animation, pixilation of abstract objects, cymatics, oscilloscope imagery and, later, computer-generated imagery. The Whitney brothers' work helped found motion graphics, and the genre's fusion of sound and image anticipates music videos, VJ culture and contemporary digital art.
