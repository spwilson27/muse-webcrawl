Phylloquinone

Phylloquinone, also known as vitamin K1, is one of the two natural forms of vitamin K, the other being menaquinone (vitamin K2). It is a polycyclic aromatic ketone based on 2-methyl-1,4-naphthoquinone with a phytyl side chain, giving it the chemical formula C31H46O2.

Function

Phylloquinone is essential for the synthesis of certain proteins required for blood coagulation (clotting factors II, VII, IX and X, and proteins C, S and Z) and for the metabolism of bone and other tissues. In plants and cyanobacteria it serves as an electron acceptor in photosystem I during photosynthesis.

Sources and medical use

Green leafy vegetables such as spinach, kale and broccoli are the richest dietary sources. Clinically, phylloquinone is given to newborns to prevent vitamin K deficiency bleeding, and is used as an antidote to warfarin overdose or poisoning.
