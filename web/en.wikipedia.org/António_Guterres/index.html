António Guterres

António Guterres, ninth Secretary-General of the United Nations

António Manuel de Oliveira Guterres (born 30 April 1949) is a Portuguese politician and diplomat who has served as the ninth Secretary-General of the United Nations since 1 January 2017. He previously served as Prime Minister of Portugal from 1995 to 2002 and as United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees from 2005 to 2015.

Early life and career

Born in Lisbon, Guterres studied electrical engineering at the Instituto Superior Técnico, graduating in 1971, and briefly taught systems theory before entering politics after the Carnation Revolution of 1974. A founding member of the Socialist Party, he became its leader in 1992 and led it to victory in the 1995 general election.

Prime Minister and High Commissioner

As prime minister (1995–2002), Guterres oversaw economic growth, Portugal's participation in the euro, and the Expo 98 world's fair in Lisbon; he resigned in 2002 after poor local election results. From 2005 to 2015, as head of the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR), he managed responses to crises in Syria, Iraq, and South Sudan, which swelled the number of displaced people worldwide.

Secretary-General

Guterres succeeded Ban Ki-moon in 2017 and was re-elected in 2021 for a second term ending in 2026. His tenure has emphasised climate action, reform of UN peacekeeping and development systems, and responses to the COVID-19 pandemic and conflicts including the wars in Ukraine and Gaza.
