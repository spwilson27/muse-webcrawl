Jal Jeevan Mission

Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) is a flagship programme of the Government of India, launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 15 August 2019, with the goal of providing safe and adequate drinking water through individual household tap connections — Har Ghar Jal ("water to every home") — to all rural households in India. It is administered by the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation under the Ministry of Jal Shakti.

Objectives

At launch, only about 32 million of roughly 190 million rural households had tap water connections. The mission aimed to supply water of prescribed quality (as per BIS 10500 standards) in adequate quantity — 55 litres per capita per day — on a regular and long-term basis. When launched, the target date was 2024; progress reporting has since tracked coverage rising to well over three-quarters of rural households.

Features

The mission emphasises community participation through Village Water and Sanitation Committees and Paani Samitis, source sustainability (rainwater harvesting, groundwater recharge, greywater management), and convergence with other schemes such as MGNREGA and the Swachh Bharat Mission. Funding is shared between the central and state governments, generally on a 50:50 basis (90:10 for Himalayan and north-eastern states).

JJM subsumed the earlier National Rural Drinking Water Programme. In the Union Budget of 2025, the mission was extended to 2028 with enhanced funding, with a focus on completing remaining connections and ensuring the quality and sustainability of completed schemes.
