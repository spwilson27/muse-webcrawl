SoftBank Vision Fund

The SoftBank Vision Fund is a venture capital fund founded in 2017 by Japan's SoftBank Group and its founder Masayoshi Son. With roughly $100 billion in committed capital at launch, it was by far the largest technology-focused investment fund ever raised, with major backing from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund and Abu Dhabi's Mubadala.

Strategy and investments

The fund made unusually large bets — often hundreds of millions or billions of dollars — on late-stage technology companies in artificial intelligence, ride-hailing, e-commerce, fintech, and robotics. Portfolio companies have included Uber, DoorDash, Coupang, ByteDance, Arm, and the office-sharing company WeWork. Its scale reshaped venture markets, inflating valuations and pressuring rivals to raise larger funds.

WeWork and losses

The fund suffered a major blow in 2019 when WeWork's initial public offering collapsed and its valuation fell from $47 billion to a fraction of that, forcing SoftBank into a rescue package and billions in writedowns. Record losses were also reported in the market downturn of 2021–22, followed by a partial recovery driven by Arm's 2023 IPO and later AI-related investments. A second, smaller fund, Vision Fund 2, was launched in 2019 and funded largely with SoftBank's own capital.

Criticism

Critics have questioned the fund's governance, its reliance on Saudi capital following the murder of Jamal Khashoggi, and whether its mega-rounds distorted competition. Supporters credit it with accelerating innovation in AI and platform businesses.
