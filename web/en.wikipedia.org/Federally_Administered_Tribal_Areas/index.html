Federally Administered Tribal Areas

The Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA; Pashto: وفاقي قبايلي سيمې) was a semi-autonomous tribal region in northwestern Pakistan that existed from 1947 until being merged with the neighbouring province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in 2018. It consisted of seven tribal agencies and six frontier regions along the Durand Line border with Afghanistan, covering about 27,220 square kilometres.

Governance

FATA was governed under the colonial-era Frontier Crimes Regulation of 1901 through political agents and tribal jirgas, and its residents were long excluded from the normal Pakistani legal and political system. Mainstream political parties were only permitted to operate there after 2011, and FATA's representatives sat in the National Assembly without power to legislate for the region.

Merger

Following decades of debate and the displacement caused by militancy and military operations, the Twenty-fifth Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan merged FATA into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in May 2018, extending the regular courts, police and provincial assembly to the former tribal areas.
