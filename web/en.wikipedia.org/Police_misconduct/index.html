Police misconduct

Police misconduct is inappropriate conduct and illegal actions taken by police officers in connection with their official duties. Types of misconduct include wrongful arrest, wrongful search, false imprisonment, police brutality, sexual misconduct, police corruption, racial profiling, and off-duty misconduct.

Types

Police brutality is the use of excessive force, including unjustified shootings and beatings. Police corruption involves officers abusing their authority for personal gain, such as taking bribes or participating in criminal activity. Other forms include perjury ("testilying"), evidence tampering, coerced confessions, and civil rights violations.

Accountability

Mechanisms for accountability include internal affairs investigations, civilian review boards, criminal prosecution, civil lawsuits, body-worn cameras, and consent decrees imposed on departments with patterns of misconduct. In the United States, the doctrine of qualified immunity often shields officers from civil liability, and its reform is widely debated.

Causes and prevention

Contributing factors studied by researchers include poor training, inadequate supervision, and the "blue wall of silence". Proposed reforms include improved data collection, early intervention systems, duty-to-intervene policies, and changes to recruitment and training.
