Goatfell

Goatfell (Scottish Gaelic: Goatfell; from Old Norse geita fjall, "goat mountain") is the highest point on the Isle of Arran, Scotland, rising to 874 metres (2,867 ft) above sea level. Classified as a Corbett, it is one of the most popular climbs in southern Scotland, and its granite summit commands views over the Firth of Clyde to Ayrshire, and on clear days to Ireland.

Ascent

The usual route begins near Brodick Castle and follows a well-made path through woodland and onto the open ridge, reaching the summit in around three hours. The mountain and the surrounding estate are cared for by the National Trust for Scotland. A more demanding circuit continues over the neighbouring peaks of North Goatfell and the jagged ridge of Stacach toward Cir Mhor and the A' Chir ridge, among the finest ridge traverses in the Southern Uplands of Scotland.

History

Goatfell is the remnant of the Arran granite pluton, intruded around 58 million years ago during the opening of the North Atlantic. In 1889 the hill was the scene of a notorious murder case, in which John Watson Laurie was convicted of killing Edwin Rose on the mountain — a case long remembered in Scottish legal history.
