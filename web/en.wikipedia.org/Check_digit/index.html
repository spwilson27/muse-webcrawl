Check digit

A check digit is a form of redundancy check used for error detection on identification numbers, such as bank account numbers, which are used in applications where they will at least sometimes be input manually. It is analogous to a binary parity bit used to check for errors in computer-generated data. A check digit is typically a single digit computed from the other digits in the number, appended to it so that the correctness of the whole can be verified.

Purpose

Check-digit systems are designed to catch the errors people most commonly make when copying numbers: a single mistyped digit, transposition of adjacent digits, twin errors (such as 22 becoming 55), and omitted or extra digits. Validation software recomputes the check digit and compares it with the one supplied; a mismatch indicates the number was entered incorrectly.

Algorithms

Well-known schemes include the Luhn algorithm (mod 10), used on credit card numbers, IMEI numbers and Canadian Social Insurance Numbers; the ISBN-10 system, which uses a modulo-11 check that may take the value X; the Mod 97-10 check used in International Bank Account Numbers (IBAN); and the Verhoeff and Damm algorithms, which detect all single-digit and adjacent transposition errors. Simpler weighted-sum methods underlie EAN and UPC barcodes.
