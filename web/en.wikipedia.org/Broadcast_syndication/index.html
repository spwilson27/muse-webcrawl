Broadcast syndication

A station master control room receiving syndicated programmes

Broadcast syndication is the practice of leasing the right to broadcast television or radio programmes to multiple individual stations without going through a broadcast network. It is especially characteristic of the United States, where locally owned affiliates and independent stations fill much of their schedules with syndicated content.

Types

First-run syndication consists of programmes made specifically for syndication rather than a network, including talk shows such as The Oprah Winfrey Show, game shows such as Jeopardy! and Wheel of Fortune, and court shows. Off-network syndication sells reruns of series that originally aired on a network; historically a series needed around 100 episodes — roughly four to five seasons — to be viable for weekday "stripping" (airing at the same time five days a week), a threshold that made long runs enormously profitable, as with Star Trek, Seinfeld, and Friends. Deals are struck for cash, for barter (the syndicator keeps some advertising time), or a combination.

History

Radio syndication via transcription discs preceded television. In TV, independent producers such as Desilu pioneered rerun sales in the 1950s. FCC rules — the Financial Interest and Syndication Rules (1970–1993) and the Prime Time Access Rule — reshaped the market, encouraging first-run syndication in early-evening "prime access" slots. Consolidation of station groups and the rise of streaming reduced, but did not eliminate, the syndication market; long-running first-run strips and off-network sitcoms still anchor local schedules.

Outside the United States

Most countries have stronger national networks, so syndication is rarer, though American series are widely syndicated internationally through distribution arms of the major studios.
