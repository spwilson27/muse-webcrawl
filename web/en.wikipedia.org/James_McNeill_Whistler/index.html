James Abbott McNeill Whistler (11 July 1834 – 17 July 1903) was an American artist active in Britain, a leading figure of the Aesthetic movement who championed "art for art's sake". His most famous painting is Arrangement in Grey and Black No. 1 (1871), popularly known as Whistler's Mother.

Career

Whistler studied in Paris and settled in London in 1859. His Symphony in White paintings and his atmospheric Nocturnes of the Thames explored tonal harmony, reflected in the musical titles he gave his works.

Ruskin trial

In 1878 Whistler sued critic John Ruskin for libel after Ruskin accused him of "flinging a pot of paint in the public's face" with Nocturne in Black and Gold. Whistler won the case but was awarded only a farthing in damages; the costs bankrupted him, though the trial became a landmark debate about modern art.

Legacy

Whistler also designed interiors, notably the Peacock Room, and wrote The Gentle Art of Making Enemies (1890), a celebrated collection of his witty correspondence and polemics.
