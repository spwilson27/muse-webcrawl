Tokaido Shinkansen

The Tokaido Shinkansen is a Japanese high-speed rail line that is part of the nationwide Shinkansen network. Opened on 1 October 1964, in time for the Tokyo Olympic Games, it was the world's first high-speed railway and remains one of the most heavily travelled.

Route and services

The line runs 515.4 km (320.3 mi) from Tokyo Station to Shin-Osaka Station, serving major cities including Yokohama, Nagoya and Kyoto, and is operated by the Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central). Three service tiers operate: the all-stations Kodama, the intermediate Hikari, and the fastest Nozomi, which completes the journey in about 2 hours 21 minutes at speeds up to 285 km/h (177 mph).

Significance

The Tokaido Shinkansen has carried well over five billion passengers and is noted for its extraordinary safety record, with no passenger fatalities due to derailment or collision in its history, despite operating through an earthquake-prone region protected by early-warning systems. It inspired high-speed rail development worldwide, including France's TGV.
