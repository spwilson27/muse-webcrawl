Cerro de la Mitla

Cerro de la Mitla is a hill and archaeological locality in the Mexican state of Oaxaca, associated with the nearby Zapotec and Mixtec ceremonial centre of Mitla in the eastern arm of the Valley of Oaxaca.

Setting

The hill rises over the town of San Pablo Villa de Mitla, a settlement inhabited for thousands of years. The area is known for the pre-Hispanic ruins of Mitla — whose name derives from the Nahuatl Mictlán, "place of the dead" — famous for intricate stone fretwork (grecas) mosaics, and for caves in the surrounding hills, such as those of Guilá Naquitz, which preserve evidence of early plant domestication in Mesoamerica.

Significance

The caves and rock shelters of the Mitla area, including sites on the slopes around Cerro de la Mitla, form part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Prehistoric Caves of Yagul and Mitla (2010), recognised for containing some of the earliest known remains of domesticated maize and squash.
