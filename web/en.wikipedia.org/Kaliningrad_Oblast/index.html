Kaliningrad Oblast is a federal subject (oblast) of Russia situated on the Baltic coast between Poland and Lithuania. It is Russia's westernmost region and an exclave, having no land connection with the rest of the country. The administrative centre is the city of Kaliningrad, formerly Königsberg. The oblast covers 15,125 square kilometres and has a population of about one million.

History

The territory formed the northern part of German East Prussia, centred on Königsberg, founded by the Teutonic Knights in 1255 and later the coronation city of the Prussian kings and home of the philosopher Immanuel Kant. Under the Potsdam Agreement of 1945, the region was transferred to the Soviet Union; its German population was expelled and replaced by settlers from Russia and other Soviet republics. The oblast was established in April 1946 and renamed for Soviet statesman Mikhail Kalinin. After the dissolution of the USSR in 1991 it became an exclave of Russia, separated from the mainland by Lithuania and Belarus.

Geography

The oblast borders Poland to the south and Lithuania to the north and east, with a Baltic coastline featuring the Curonian Spit (shared with Lithuania) and the Vistula Spit (shared with Poland), the Curonian and Vistula lagoons, and the Sambian Peninsula. The main rivers are the Pregolya and the Neman. The port of Baltiysk, Russia's westernmost town, hosts the Baltic Fleet and is ice-free year-round.

Economy

A special economic zone (Yantar, established 1996) offers tax and customs incentives. The region is known for amber — around 90 percent of the world's extractable amber is mined near Yantarny — shipbuilding, food processing, and manufacturing including the Avtotor car plant. Its exclave status makes transit trade with the EU and railway access through Lithuania strategically sensitive.
