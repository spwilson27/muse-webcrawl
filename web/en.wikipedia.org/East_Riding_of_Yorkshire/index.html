East Riding of Yorkshire

The East Riding of Yorkshire is a ceremonial county and unitary authority area in Yorkshire and the Humber, England. It takes its name from the historic East Riding, one of the three traditional divisions (ridings) of Yorkshire, and borders the North Sea to the east and the Humber Estuary to the south.

Geography

The county includes the Yorkshire Wolds, a range of low chalk hills, the low-lying Holderness plain, and a coastline running from Flamborough Head to Spurn Point. The largest town is Beverley, the county town; other major settlements include Bridlington, Goole, and Driffield. The city of Kingston upon Hull forms a separate unitary authority within the ceremonial county.

History

The riding existed as an administrative division for centuries before being replaced by the county of Humberside in 1974. When Humberside was abolished in 1996, the East Riding of Yorkshire unitary authority was created, reviving the historic name in a modern form.
