Iran hostage crisis

The Iran hostage crisis was a diplomatic standoff between the United States and Iran. Fifty-two American diplomats and citizens were held hostage after a group of militarized Iranian college students belonging to the Muslim Student Followers of the Imam's Line, who supported the Iranian Revolution, took over the U.S. Embassy in Tehran on November 4, 1979. The hostages were held for 444 days, until January 20, 1981.

Causes

The crisis followed the admission of the deposed shah, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, to the United States for cancer treatment, which many Iranians saw as a prelude to another American-backed coup like that of 1953. Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini supported the students' action.

Rescue attempt and resolution

In April 1980 President Jimmy Carter authorized a rescue mission, Operation Eagle Claw, which failed disastrously in the Iranian desert, killing eight American servicemen. The remaining hostages were released minutes after Ronald Reagan's inauguration on January 20, 1981, under the terms of the Algiers Accords. The crisis severely damaged Carter's presidency and poisoned U.S.–Iran relations for decades.
