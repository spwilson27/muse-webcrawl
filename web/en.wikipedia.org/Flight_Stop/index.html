Flight Stop

Flight Stop, subtitled Geese in the Shopping Centre, is a public art installation by the Canadian artist Michael Snow, created in 1979 for the Toronto Eaton Centre in Toronto, Ontario. It consists of sixty life-sized Canada geese, sculpted in fibreglass and hand-painted, suspended in flight at various heights through the mall's glass-roofed atrium. The work is one of Canada's best-known pieces of public art.

Description

Snow modelled the geese from photographs of a single stuffed goose taken from many angles, so that the flock appears frozen in successive phases of flight and landing — wings in differing positions, bodies pitching as if braking to land on an imagined pond. The birds hang at the south end of the Eaton Centre's galleria, where generations of shoppers have treated them as a landmark and meeting point. The centre decorates some of the geese with large red ribbons during the Christmas season.

Copyright case

Flight Stop became the subject of an important Canadian moral-rights case. In 1982 Snow sued the Eaton Centre after it tied ribbons around the geese's necks, arguing that the alteration prejudiced his honour and reputation. The Ontario High Court agreed and ordered the ribbons removed — Snow v. The Eaton Centre Ltd. remains a leading precedent on artists' moral rights in Canada.
