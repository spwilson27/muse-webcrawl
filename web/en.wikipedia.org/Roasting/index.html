Roasting

Roasting is a cooking method that uses dry heat, whether an open flame, an oven or another heat source, to cook food evenly on all sides. Roasting can enhance flavour through caramelisation and Maillard browning of the surface of the food.

Methods

Traditionally, roasting involved cooking meat on a spit over a fire or in front of a hearth. Modern roasting is usually done in an oven, sometimes with the food basted in its own juices or fat. Vegetables, nuts, coffee beans and cocoa beans are also roasted; coffee roasting develops the aroma and flavour locked in green coffee beans through a series of chemical changes as temperatures approach 200 °C and beyond.

Meats

Large joints of beef, lamb, pork and poultry are classic roasting subjects; in Britain the "roast dinner" of roasted meat with potatoes, vegetables, gravy and Yorkshire pudding is a traditional Sunday meal. Resting the meat after roasting allows juices to redistribute before carving.
