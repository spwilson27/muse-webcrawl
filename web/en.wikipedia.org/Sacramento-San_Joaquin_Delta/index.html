The Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta, or California Delta, is a vast network of waterways, channels and reclaimed islands at the confluence of the Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers in Northern California, where fresh water from the Sierra Nevada and Coast Ranges meets the tidal waters of San Francisco Bay.

Geography and use

Since the 19th century, peat marshes have been drained and ringed with levees to create fertile farmland "islands", many of which have subsided below sea level. The Delta is the hub of California's water system: state and federal pumping plants export its water south to farms and cities, making its ecology, salinity and levee stability subjects of continuing conflict. It also supports recreation, shipping to the ports of Sacramento and Stockton, and habitat for species such as the Delta smelt.
