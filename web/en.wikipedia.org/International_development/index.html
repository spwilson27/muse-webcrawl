International development

International development or global development is a broad concept denoting work by governments, international organisations, charities and private actors to improve the living conditions, health, education and economic prospects of people in poorer countries, and the academic study of those efforts.

Scope

Development practice spans poverty reduction, public health, education, water and sanitation, agriculture, infrastructure, governance and climate adaptation. Major actors include the World Bank, United Nations agencies such as UNDP and UNICEF, bilateral aid agencies, and thousands of non-governmental organisations.

Global goals

In 2000 the United Nations adopted the Millennium Development Goals, eight targets for 2015 covering poverty, education, health and the environment. They were succeeded in 2015 by the 17 Sustainable Development Goals, a universal agenda addressing poverty, inequality, climate change and peace by 2030.

Approaches

Theories of development have shifted over time: post-war modernisation theory emphasised industrialisation and capital investment; dependency theory blamed unequal global structures; and later approaches stressed institutions, human capabilities and participatory methods. Amartya Sen's capability approach, which defines development as the expansion of people's real freedoms, has been particularly influential, informing measures such as the Human Development Index.

Measurement

Development is measured through indicators including gross domestic product per capita, life expectancy, literacy, infant mortality and composite indices such as the Human Development Index and the Multidimensional Poverty Index.
