The Battle of Zborov (1–2 July 1917) was a small but symbolically important engagement of the Kerensky Offensive on the Eastern Front of the First World War. It is chiefly remembered for the performance of the Czechoslovak Riflemen's Brigade — part of the Czechoslovak Legions — which broke through Austro-Hungarian positions near the Galician town of Zborov (now Zboriv, Ukraine).

Background

The Russian Provisional Government's summer offensive, directed by war minister Alexander Kerensky, aimed to restore the army's fighting spirit and Russia's standing with its allies. On the southwestern sector, Czechoslovak units — recruited largely from Czech and Slovak prisoners of war and emigrants — were given a chance to prove themselves against Austria-Hungary.

The battle

On 2 July 1917 the Czechoslovak brigade, some 3,500 men, attacked after artillery preparation and captured several lines of trenches, taking thousands of prisoners and dozens of guns. It was one of the few clear local successes of the offensive, which otherwise collapsed amid desertion and mutiny.

Legacy

The victory gave the Czechoslovak national movement, led by Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, powerful evidence that Czechoslovaks deserved an independent state, and it remains a celebrated episode in Czech and Slovak military tradition.
