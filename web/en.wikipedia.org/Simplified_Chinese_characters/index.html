Simplified Chinese characters (简体字; jiǎntǐzì) are standardized Chinese characters prescribed in the Table of General Standard Chinese Characters for use in mainland China. Along with traditional Chinese characters, they are one of the two standard character sets of the contemporary Chinese written language.

The Chinese government in mainland China promulgated the first round of simplification in 1956, aiming to promote mass literacy. A second round, promulgated in 1977, proved unpopular and was officially rescinded in 1986. Simplified characters are used officially in mainland China, Singapore and Malaysia, while traditional characters remain standard in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau.

Methods of simplification

Simplification was achieved by several systematic methods:

  • Reducing the number of strokes in character components, often by adopting cursive (caoshu) forms
  • Substituting homophonous or simpler characters for complex ones
  • Merging variants and eliminating rare forms
  • Replacing complex radicals with simpler shared components across whole character families

Many simplified forms were not newly invented; simplified variants had circulated in handwriting and popular usage for centuries before being standardized in the twentieth century.

History

Calls for character reform began in the early twentieth century. After the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949, the government established committees to design a simplification scheme. The Chinese Character Simplification Scheme was published in 1956, followed by successive lists culminating in the General List of Simplified Characters in 1964. In 2013 the Table of General Standard Chinese Characters, listing 8,105 characters, became the current standard.

Criticism and debate

Supporters argue that simplification raised literacy and is easier to learn and write. Critics argue that merging homophones can create ambiguity, that simplified forms lose etymological and aesthetic information, and that readers of simplified text may struggle with traditional texts still used in other regions and in classical literature.
