Pharmacogenomics

FieldPharmacology, genomics
FocusGenetic influence on drug response
Key genesCYP2D6, CYP2C19, TPMT, VKORC1

Pharmacogenomics, often abbreviated PGx, is the study of how a person's genome affects their response to drugs. It combines pharmacology and genomics to develop effective, safe medications tailored to individual genetic profiles, and is a central component of precision medicine. The related term pharmacogenetics usually refers to the effect of single genes, while pharmacogenomics takes a genome-wide view.

Genetic testing can guide drug choice and dose for individual patients

Classic examples involve the cytochrome P450 enzymes that metabolise many medicines. Variants of CYP2C19 can prevent activation of the antiplatelet drug clopidogrel; CYP2D6 copy number determines whether codeine is converted to morphine dangerously fast or not at all; TPMT deficiency raises the toxicity of thiopurine immunosuppressants; and variants of CYP2C9 and VKORC1 guide dosing of the anticoagulant warfarin. In oncology, tumour genomics directs targeted therapies, as with HER2 testing before trastuzumab treatment. Certain HLA alleles predict severe hypersensitivity reactions — HLA-B*57:01 to the HIV drug abacavir and HLA-B*15:02 to carbamazepine — and screening is now standard of care in many guidelines.

Implementation

Consortia such as CPIC publish peer-reviewed guidelines linking genotypes to prescribing actions, and regulators include pharmacogenomic information in many drug labels. Barriers include cost, clinician familiarity, integration with electronic health records and ensuring that test panels reflect diverse populations.
