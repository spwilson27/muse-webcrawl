Solar energy is radiant light and heat from the Sun that is harnessed using technologies such as photovoltaic panels, which convert sunlight directly into electricity, solar thermal collectors for heating, and mirrors that concentrate sunlight to drive heat engines. It is a renewable energy source central to most strategies for decarbonizing energy systems.

Resource

The Sun delivers about 173,000 terawatts continuously to Earth — far more than total human energy use. At ground level the available flux depends on latitude, season, weather, and time of day; the intermittency this creates is a central engineering challenge addressed with storage, transmission, and demand management.

Technologies

Photovoltaics (PV) use semiconductor cells, usually silicon, that produce direct current when illuminated. Falling module prices made PV the cheapest source of new electricity in many regions during the 2010s. Concentrated solar power focuses sunlight to heat a fluid and run a turbine, sometimes with molten-salt storage. Passive solar design, solar water heating, and solar cooking exploit heat directly without conversion.

Growth and deployment

From a niche technology, solar power grew through feed-in tariffs and auctions in Germany, China, the United States, India, and elsewhere to exceed a terawatt of installed capacity in the early 2020s, with China the largest market and manufacturer. Rooftop and community solar complement utility-scale plants.

Limitations

Challenges include the daily and seasonal variability of sunlight, land requirements, recycling of panels, and the need for grid flexibility and energy storage as solar's share of electricity grows.
