Pascua Yaqui Tribe

Pascua Yaqui Tribe
Location: Arizona, United States
People: Yaqui (Yoeme)

The Pascua Yaqui Tribe is a federally recognized tribe of Yaqui people in southern Arizona, United States. The Yaqui, who call themselves Yoeme, originate from the Río Yaqui valley in Sonora, Mexico, and many fled to Arizona during periods of persecution and conflict in Mexico in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

History

Yaqui refugees established communities such as Pascua and Barrio Libre near Tucson. The United States Congress granted the tribe federal recognition in 1978, and the Pascua Yaqui Reservation was established near Tucson. A second community, the Tortuga community, also lies within tribal lands.

Culture

The tribe is widely known for its elaborate Lenten and Easter (Pascua) ceremonies, blending Catholic and indigenous traditions, including the masked chapayeka figures and deer dances performed by pahko'ola dancers. The Yaqui language remains an important part of tribal identity.

Government and economy

The tribal government provides services to its members and operates enterprises including casinos in the Tucson area.
