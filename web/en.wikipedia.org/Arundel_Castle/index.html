Arundel Castle

Location: Arundel, West Sussex, England

Built: From 1067; largely rebuilt 18th–19th centuries

Owner: Duke of Norfolk

Listing: Grade I listed

Arundel Castle is a restored and remodelled medieval castle in Arundel, West Sussex, England. Established by Roger de Montgomery in 1067, it has been the seat of the Dukes of Norfolk and their ancestors for over 850 years and remains the family home of the Duke of Norfolk, the premier duke and Earl Marshal of England.

History

The original motte-and-bailey castle guarded the gap in the South Downs through which the River Arun flows. It passed through the d'Aubigny and FitzAlan families and survived a siege during the English Civil War, though it was left badly damaged. The castle was extensively rebuilt in the eighteenth and especially the nineteenth century, when Charles Howard, 11th Duke of Norfolk, and later Henry Fitzalan-Howard, 15th Duke, remodelled it in the Gothic Revival style; the Victorian rebuilding was completed around 1900 and the castle was among the first country houses in England to have electric lighting, integral fire-fighting equipment and central heating.

The castle today

The castle contains important collections of furniture, tapestries, paintings by artists such as Van Dyck and Gainsborough, and personal possessions of Mary, Queen of Scots, including her rosary beads. The Fitzalan Chapel, the family mausoleum, stands in the grounds. Much of the castle and its gardens, including the Collector Earl's Garden opened in 2008, are open to the public seasonally, and the grounds host events including jousting tournaments and the Arundel Festival nearby.
