Epidermal growth factor receptor

The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR; ErbB-1; HER1 in humans) is a transmembrane protein that is a receptor for members of the epidermal growth factor family of extracellular protein ligands, including EGF and transforming growth factor alpha (TGFα). It belongs to the ErbB family of receptor tyrosine kinases, along with HER2/neu (ErbB-2), HER3 (ErbB-3) and HER4 (ErbB-4).

Function

On ligand binding, EGFR dimerizes and its intracellular kinase domain autophosphorylates tyrosine residues, activating downstream signalling pathways — principally the RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK (MAPK) pathway, PI3K–Akt and STAT signalling — that drive cell proliferation, survival, migration and differentiation. In humans the protein is encoded by the EGFR gene on chromosome 7.

Clinical significance

Mutations, amplification or overexpression of EGFR are found in many cancers, including lung, colorectal, head and neck cancers and glioblastoma. These discoveries made EGFR a major drug target: small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as gefitinib, erlotinib and osimertinib, and monoclonal antibodies such as cetuximab and panitumumab, are standard therapies in molecularly selected patients. Activating EGFR mutations predict response to these inhibitors in non-small-cell lung cancer, while acquired resistance frequently arises through the T790M "gatekeeper" mutation.
