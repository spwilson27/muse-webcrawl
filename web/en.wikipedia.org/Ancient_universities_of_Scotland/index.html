Ancient universities of Scotland

The ancient universities of Scotland are the four universities founded in Scotland before the 19th century: the University of St Andrews (1413), the University of Glasgow (1451), the University of Aberdeen (1495), and the University of Edinburgh (1583). They are among the oldest universities in the English-speaking world and are governed in part by the Universities (Scotland) Acts.

Characteristics

The Scottish ancients share distinctive institutional features: an elected Rector who presides over the University Court, a General Council of graduates, and the undergraduate Master of Arts (MA) degree awarded as a first degree in the humanities and social sciences. St Andrews, Glasgow, and Aberdeen were founded by papal bull before the Reformation; Edinburgh was founded by the town council and is sometimes called a "burgh university".

History

Modeled in part on continental universities such as Bologna and Paris, the Scottish universities developed a broad, philosophical curriculum that shaped the Scottish Enlightenment. The Universities (Scotland) Act 1858 modernized their governance and curricula, and later legislation extended similar structures to Scotland's newer universities.
