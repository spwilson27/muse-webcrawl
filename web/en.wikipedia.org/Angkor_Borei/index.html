Angkor Borei

Angkor Borei (Khmer: អង្គរបូរី) is a town and archaeological site in southern Cambodia, in Takéo Province near the border with Vietnam. It was an important early city of the kingdom of Funan and later of Chenla, occupied from roughly the 4th century BC, and is considered one of the earliest urban centres in mainland Southeast Asia. The site, arranged around the temple hill of Phnom Da, is famed for some of the oldest Khmer inscriptions and early Hindu sculpture in the region, including celebrated statues of Vishnu and Krishna.

Site and layout

The ancient settlement covers several hundred hectares enclosed by a wall and moat, with a canal system — including the so-called Canal 4 — linking it to Óc Eo in the Mekong Delta, its partner port in the maritime trade between China and India. A brick-lined canal and extensive water-management features testify to the engineering of the Funan state. Just south of the town rises Phnom Da, a solitary hill crowned by an 11th-century sandstone temple from the Angkorian period and caves containing earlier carvings.

History

Occupation at Angkor Borei began in the Iron Age, around the 4th century BC, and by the early centuries AD the city was a major centre of Funan, the first Indianised kingdom of Southeast Asia recorded in Chinese sources. Inscriptions in Sanskrit and Old Khmer found at the site are among the earliest epigraphic evidence in Cambodia. The city continued into the Chenla period (6th–8th centuries) with the construction of brick sanctuaries such as Prasat Komnap, before political gravity shifted north to the Tonlé Sap region and eventually Angkor.

Archaeology

Systematic excavation began in the 1990s under the Lower Mekong Archaeological Project (LOMAP), led by Miriam Stark with Cambodian colleagues, which documented early burials, craft production and the city's canal network. Artefacts from Angkor Borei and Phnom Da — including some of the oldest free-standing stone sculpture in Southeast Asia — are displayed in the small Angkor Borei Museum and in the National Museum of Cambodia in Phnom Penh. Cambodia has placed the site on its UNESCO World Heritage tentative list.
