Volendam is a town in the province of North Holland in the Netherlands, in the municipality of Edam-Volendam, situated on the shore of the Markermeer. Originally the harbour of nearby Edam, it developed into an independent fishing village after a new canal was dug in the 16th century.

Culture and tourism

Volendam is famous for its fishing heritage, its traditional Dutch costume — including the tall lace cap (hul) worn by women — and its wooden houses along the old harbour dike. It is one of the most visited tourist destinations in the Netherlands. The town has a strong Roman Catholic identity and a notable music scene: it produced several popular Dutch pop acts, earning the nickname "Palingsound" (eel sound). Football club FC Volendam represents the town. On New Year's Day 2001, a fire in Café De Hemel killed 14 young people and injured hundreds, an event that profoundly affected the community.
