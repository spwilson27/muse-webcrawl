Kano (Hausa: Kano) is the capital and largest city of Kano State in northern Nigeria, the second most populous city in the country after Lagos. It is the traditional centre of the Hausa people and a historic commercial hub of the trans-Saharan trade.

History

Kano began as a settlement on Dalla Hill around the 7th century, associated by legend with the blacksmith-kingdom founded by Bagauda around 999 CE. It became a powerful Hausa city-state and, in the 15th century under Muhammad Rumfa, a centre of Islamic scholarship and trade in leather, cloth, gold and slaves across the Sahara. The kingdom was conquered in 1807 during the Sokoto jihad and became an emirate of the Sokoto Caliphate. British forces took Kano in 1903. The old city's millennium-old dye pits (Kofar Mata) and its earthen city walls and the Emir's Palace (Gidan Rumfa) still stand.

Economy

Long the terminus of caravan routes, Kano later became the groundnut capital of Nigeria and remains a major commercial and industrial centre for textiles, tanning, food processing and trade for the entire Sahel region. Mallam Aminu Kano International Airport, one of Nigeria's oldest, serves the city.

Culture

The city is predominantly Muslim, governed traditionally by the Emir of Kano, and famous for the Durbar festival held at Eid, featuring processions of horsemen in colourful regalia. Kano's ancient indigo dye pits and leatherworks continue centuries-old crafts.
