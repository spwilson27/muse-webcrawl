Ted Chiang

Ted Chiang (born 1967) is an American science fiction writer. His work has won four Nebula Awards, four Hugo Awards, the John W. Campbell Award for Best New Writer, and six Locus Awards, among others. He is known for meticulously crafted short fiction that explores philosophical and scientific ideas. His novella "Story of Your Life" was the basis of the film Arrival (2016).

Life and career

Chiang was born in Port Jefferson, New York, in 1967, to parents who had emigrated from China via Taiwan. He graduated from Brown University with a degree in computer science and has worked as a technical writer in the software industry while writing fiction. Despite a relatively small body of work—fewer than twenty short pieces over three decades—he is regarded as one of the most acclaimed science fiction writers of his generation.

Works

Chiang's first published story, "Tower of Babylon" (1990), won the Nebula Award. His collection Stories of Your Life and Others (2002) gathered his early fiction, including "Story of Your Life," "Hell Is the Absence of God," and "Liking What You See: A Documentary." His second collection, Exhalation: Stories (2019), included "The Lifecycle of Software Objects," "The Merchant and the Alchemist's Gate," and the title story "Exhalation." It was named one of the top ten books of the year by The New York Times.

Themes

Chiang's fiction frequently engages with determinism and free will, language and perception, memory, artificial intelligence, and the relationship between science and religion. His stories are noted for rigorous internal logic and for developing a single speculative premise to its full philosophical consequences.
