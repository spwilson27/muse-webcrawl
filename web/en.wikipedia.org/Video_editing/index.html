Video editing

Video editing is the process of manipulating and rearranging video shots to create a new work. It is a key stage of post-production for film, television, advertising and online media, encompassing the selection, trimming and sequencing of footage as well as the addition of transitions, titles, effects, colour correction and sound.

History

Early video was edited physically: videotape, introduced in the 1950s with systems such as Ampex's Quadruplex format, initially had to be cut and spliced like film, a laborious process aided by the development of electronic editing for tape-to-tape copying. Linear editing suites dominated television post-production for decades, with editors assembling programmes sequentially from source tapes. The arrival of digital formats in the late 1980s and 1990s made non-linear editing practical: footage is stored on hard disks and accessed randomly from a timeline, with edits recorded as decisions rather than by altering the media.

Non-linear editing systems

Modern editing is performed with software such as Adobe Premiere Pro, Avid Media Composer, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro and open-source tools like Kdenlive and Shotcut. Editors organise clips in a timeline, cut between shots (with devices such as the J-cut and L-cut), and refine pacing before colour grading, mixing audio and mastering deliverables. Cloud collaboration and proxy workflows support remote teams, and machine-learning features now assist with captioning, reframing and search.

Techniques

Core techniques include continuity editing, montage, match cuts, cutaways and inserts, cross-cutting for parallel action, and transitions such as dissolves, wipes and fades. The editor's craft rests on principles theorised since the silent era, notably the Kuleshov effect, which showed that meaning arises from the juxtaposition of shots.
