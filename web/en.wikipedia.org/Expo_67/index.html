Expo 67

Expo 67, officially the Universal and International Exhibition of 1967, was a world's fair held in Montreal, Quebec, Canada, from April 27 to October 29, 1967. Mounted as the centerpiece of Canada's centennial celebrations, it is considered one of the most successful world's fairs of the twentieth century, drawing more than 50 million visitors despite a metropolitan population of only about 2.5 million.

Site and theme

The fair's theme, "Man and His World" (Terre des Hommes), was drawn from Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's 1939 memoir. The site was built largely on new land: Île Notre-Dame was created in the Saint Lawrence River using fill excavated from Montreal's new Metro system, and Île Sainte-Hélène was enlarged. Under Mayor Jean Drapeau, the project was completed at remarkable speed after Montreal won the bid that Moscow had originally been awarded.

Pavilions and legacy

Sixty-two nations participated. Landmarks included Buckminster Fuller's geodesic dome for the United States pavilion (now the Montreal Biosphere), Moshe Safdie's Habitat 67 housing complex, the Czechoslovak pavilion's Laterna Magika multimedia theatre, and the Expo Express transit system. Expo 67 transformed Montreal's self-image, accelerated Quebec's Quiet Revolution-era modernization, and left lasting infrastructure including the islands' parkland and the La Ronde amusement park. The Montreal Expos baseball team, founded in 1969, took its name from the fair.
