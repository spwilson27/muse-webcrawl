Venezuela

Venezuela, officially the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, is a country on the northern coast of South America, consisting of a continental landmass and many islands and islets in the Caribbean Sea. It comprises an area of 916,445 km² (353,841 sq mi), and its population was estimated at 29 million in 2022. The capital and largest urban agglomeration is the city of Caracas.

Geography

The continental territory is bordered on the north by the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean, on the west by Colombia, Brazil on the south, Trinidad and Tobago to the north-east and on the east by Guyana. Venezuela is home to a wide variety of landscapes, including the Andes mountains in the west, the Amazon Basin rainforest in the south, extensive llanos (plains) in the centre, and the Orinoco Delta in the east. The country is extremely biodiverse, ranking seventh in the world's list of nations with the most species. It is home to Angel Falls (Salto Ángel), the world's tallest uninterrupted waterfall, and to the Orinoco River, one of the longest rivers in South America.

History

Venezuela was colonised by Spain in 1522, amid resistance from indigenous peoples. It became one of the first Spanish American colonies to declare independence, in 1811, though independence was not securely established until 1821, when it formed part of the Republic of Gran Colombia. Venezuela gained full independence as a separate country in 1830. The nineteenth century was marked by political turmoil and authoritarian rule, while the discovery of oil in the early twentieth century transformed the economy and made Venezuela, for a time, one of the wealthiest countries in Latin America.

Beginning in the 1980s, falling oil prices and economic mismanagement led to a prolonged crisis. The election of Hugo Chávez in 1998 launched the Bolivarian Revolution, characterised by socialist economic policies and confrontation with the United States. Chávez died in office in 2013 and was succeeded by Nicolás Maduro. In the 2010s the country suffered a severe socioeconomic crisis marked by hyperinflation, shortages, and the emigration of millions of Venezuelans.

Economy

The economy is strongly dependent on petroleum. Venezuela possesses the world's largest proven oil reserves, and oil has historically accounted for the great majority of export earnings and a large share of government revenue. Other important sectors include agriculture and the production of steel, aluminium, cement and other basic industries. Since the mid-2010s the economy has contracted sharply amid hyperinflation and declining oil production.

Demographics

The population is predominantly urban and concentrated in the northern and western highlands. Most Venezuelans are of mixed European, Indigenous and African descent. Spanish is the official language, and Roman Catholicism is the most widely practised religion, though freedom of religion is guaranteed by the constitution.
