Limited overs cricket

Limited overs cricket, also known as white-ball cricket, is a form of cricket in which each team bats once and its innings is limited to a fixed number of overs. Most matches are completed in a single day. The format guarantees a result (subject to weather) and was developed to make the game faster and more attractive to spectators and television.

History

The format originated in England in 1963 with the Gillette Cup, a 65-overs-a-side knockout competition for first-class counties. The first One Day International (ODI) was played between Australia and England in Melbourne in 1971, arranged after rain washed out the first days of a Test match. The inaugural Cricket World Cup followed in England in 1975.

Formats

International limited overs cricket is played as One Day Internationals (50 overs per side) and Twenty20 Internationals (20 overs per side). Domestic competitions include England's One-Day Cup, Australia's Marsh Cup, and franchise T20 leagues such as the Indian Premier League and Big Bash League. List A is the classification for the top-level limited overs matches below ODI status.

Features

The format uses a white ball, coloured team clothing, fielding restrictions (powerplays), limits on overs per bowler, and innovations such as day/night matches under floodlights. Rules target scores and run chases have made limited overs cricket the game's most commercially successful form.
