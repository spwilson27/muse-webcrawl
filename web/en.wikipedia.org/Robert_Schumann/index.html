Robert Schumann

Robert Schumann in 1850

Robert Schumann (German: [ˈʁoːbɛʁt ˈʃuːman]; 8 June 1810 – 29 July 1856) was a German composer, pianist and influential music critic. Widely regarded as one of the greatest composers of the Romantic era, he left the law to pursue a career as a pianist but a hand injury ended that dream, and he turned his energies to composing for piano, voice, chamber ensemble and orchestra.

Life

Born in Zwickau, Schumann studied law at Leipzig and Heidelberg before devoting himself to music. In 1834 he founded the influential journal Neue Zeitschrift für Musik, championing young composers including Chopin and Brahms. After a long legal battle with her father, he married the pianist Clara Wieck in 1840; that "year of song" produced some 168 Lieder, including Dichterliebe.

Music

Schumann's early works were almost exclusively for piano: Papillons, Carnaval, Kinderszenen and the Fantasie in C. He later wrote four symphonies, the beloved Piano Concerto in A minor, chamber works including the Piano Quintet, the opera Genoveva and the oratorio Das Paradies und die Peri.

Final years

Suffering from severe mental illness, Schumann attempted suicide in the Rhine in 1854 and was admitted to an asylum at Endenich, where he died in 1856. His wife Clara and their friend Johannes Brahms preserved and promoted his music.
