Sewage sludge

Dewatered sludge cake at a treatment works.

Sewage sludge is the residual, semi-solid material produced as a by-product of wastewater treatment. It consists of the solids separated from sewage during primary and secondary treatment, and contains organic matter, nutrients such as nitrogen and phosphorus, microorganisms, and variable amounts of contaminants including heavy metals, pharmaceuticals and microplastics.

Production and treatment

Primary sludge settles out in sedimentation tanks, while secondary (activated) sludge consists largely of microbial biomass grown during biological treatment. Sludge is processed to reduce its volume and pathogen content: common steps include thickening, anaerobic digestion — which produces biogas — dewatering, lime stabilisation, composting and thermal drying. The resulting treated material is often termed biosolids.

Uses and disposal

Treated sludge is applied to agricultural land as a fertiliser and soil conditioner in many countries, recycled to forestry or land reclamation, incinerated with energy recovery, or sent to landfill. Land application returns nutrients and organic matter to soil but is controversial because contaminants in sludge — including persistent per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) — can accumulate in soil and enter food chains. Regulations such as the EU Sewage Sludge Directive and the US EPA Part 503 rule set limits for pathogens and metals, and some jurisdictions have restricted or banned land spreading, shifting practice toward incineration and phosphorus recovery from ash.
