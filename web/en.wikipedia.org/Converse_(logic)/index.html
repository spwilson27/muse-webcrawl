Converse (logic)

In logic and mathematics, the converse of a categorical or implicational statement is the result of reversing its two constituent statements. For the implication P → Q, the converse is Q → P.

Truth value

In general, the truth of a statement is independent of the truth of its converse: either may be true while the other is false. For example, "If a figure is a square, then it is a rectangle" is true, but its converse, "If a figure is a rectangle, then it is a square," is false. When a statement and its converse are both true, they may be combined into a biconditional using "if and only if."

Related forms

The contrapositive (¬Q → ¬P) is always logically equivalent to the original implication, while the inverse (¬P → ¬Q) is equivalent to the converse. Mistakenly assuming that a statement implies its converse is a common logical error, sometimes called affirming the consequent.
