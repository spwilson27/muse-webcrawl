The Hedgehog and the Fox

The Hedgehog and the Fox is a 1953 essay by philosopher Isaiah Berlin, later expanded into a book. Its title refers to a fragment by the Greek poet Archilochus: "a fox knows many things, but a hedgehog knows one big thing."

Thesis

Berlin divides writers and thinkers into hedgehogs, who view the world through a single defining idea (Plato, Dante, Dostoevsky, Nietzsche), and foxes, who draw on a wide variety of experiences (Shakespeare, Aristotle, Pushkin, Joyce). The essay's substantive focus is Leo Tolstoy, whom Berlin reads as a fox by talent but a hedgehog by belief, producing the tension at the heart of War and Peace.

Legacy

The distinction became famous in intellectual culture and was later popularized in forecasting research, notably Philip Tetlock's work on expert political judgment. See also The Hero with a Thousand Faces.
