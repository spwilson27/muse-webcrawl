The UCI Cyclo-cross World Cup is a season-long competition in cyclo-cross racing organised by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI). First held in the 1993–94 season, it is the most prestigious series in the sport, ranking above the Superprestige and X²O Badkamers Trophy series. Riders accumulate points across a calendar of rounds held mainly in Belgium and the Netherlands, with additional rounds in other European countries and occasionally North America.

Format

The World Cup consists of roughly a dozen rounds per season. Separate races are held for elite men and elite women, with additional categories for under-23 and junior riders at selected rounds. Points are awarded to the leading finishers in each round, and the rider with the highest total at the end of the season wins the overall World Cup title and wears the leader's jersey during the following rounds.

Starting positions in World Cup races are determined largely by UCI rankings, which makes consistent participation important for contenders. From the 2020–21 season the series was restructured with an expanded calendar under the management of Flanders Classics, the organiser of major Flemish road races.

History

The first World Cup was held in 1993–94 and was won by Belgian Paul Herygers. Belgian and Dutch riders have dominated the series: Sven Nys won the overall title a record number of times in the men's competition, while riders such as Wout van Aert, Mathieu van der Poel, Eli Iserbyt and Michael Vanthourenhout have been prominent in recent seasons. In the women's competition, Marianne Vos, Sanne Cant, Ceylin del Carmen Alvarado, Lucinda Brand and Fem van Empel are among the multiple overall winners.

Races at venues such as Namur (on the citadel circuit), Koksijde (in deep sand), Zonhoven (with its steep sandy bowl), and the Christmas-period races at Gavere, Diegem and Hulst are traditional highlights of the calendar.

Overall winners

Multiple overall winners in the elite categories include Sven Nys and Richard Groenendaal (men), and in later years Mathieu van der Poel and Wout van Aert; on the women's side, Katherine Compton, Marianne Vos and Sanne Cant each won the series several times.
