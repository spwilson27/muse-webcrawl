Kaomoji

Kaomoji (顔文字, literally "face characters") are Japanese emoticons formed from text characters, typically read upright — unlike Western emoticons such as :-) which are tilted sideways. Kaomoji use a wide range of characters drawn from Japanese scripts and ASCII to depict faces, emotions, gestures and even whole figures, for example (＾▽＾), (╯°□°)╯︵ ┻━┻ ("table flip") and ¯\_(ツ)_/¯ (the shrug).

History

Kaomoji emerged on Japanese bulletin-board systems in the 1980s, such as ASCII NET, and flourished on the textboard 2channel (now 5channel), where elaborate ASCII art (AA) culture also grew. Because Japanese character encodings offered many symbols, users could build expressive faces with the eyes as the main carrier of emotion — often explained by the importance of eyes in reading facial expression in Japanese communication, in contrast to Western emoticons that vary the mouth.

Styles

Styles range from simple faces like (^_^) and (T_T) (crying) to action scenes and multi-line ASCII art. Variants spread to Korean, Chinese and global internet culture, and many are accessible on Japanese mobile phone keyboards. Kaomoji are considered a precursor and stylistic cousin of the emoji, which were created in Japan in the late 1990s.
