Sports car

A sports car is a type of automobile designed with an emphasis on dynamic performance, such as handling, acceleration, top speed, the thrill of driving and racing capability. Sports cars originated in Europe in the early 1900s and are currently produced by many manufacturers around the world.

Design

Sports cars are traditionally two-seat or 2+2 coupés and roadsters with low, sleek bodies, rear-wheel or all-wheel drive, powerful engines and a low centre of gravity. Layouts include front-engine, mid-engine and rear-engine designs. Emphasis is placed on driver engagement as much as outright speed, so many sports cars are relatively modest in power but light and nimble.

History

Early sports cars included the Mercer Raceabout and the Bugatti Type 35 racing car of the 1920s. After the Second World War, British roadsters such as the MG TC, Triumph TR series and Austin-Healey popularised the category in the United States, while Italian makers such as Ferrari, Maserati and Alfa Romeo defined the high-performance grand tourer. Iconic later models include the Jaguar E-Type (1961), the Porsche 911 (1964), the Mazda MX-5 (1989) — the best-selling sports car of all time — and modern supercars that blur the line into hypercar territory.

Classification

Related categories include the grand tourer (GT), designed for high-speed long-distance comfort; the muscle car; the hot hatch; and the supercar and hypercar, which push performance to extremes at correspondingly high prices.
