Acadiana

Acadiana
Location: Southern Louisiana, United States
Principal city: Lafayette
Parishes: 22 officially recognised
Culture: Cajun and Louisiana Creole

Acadiana (French and Louisiana French: L'Acadiane) is the official name given to the French Louisiana region that has historically contained the state's Francophone population. It is centred on the city of Lafayette and spans the southern third of Louisiana, from the Sabine River in the west to the Mississippi River in the east. The Louisiana legislature officially recognised 22 parishes as the Acadiana region in 1971.

History

The region takes its name from the Acadians, French settlers expelled from Nova Scotia (Acadia) by the British beginning in 1755, in the event known as Le Grand Dérangement. Thousands eventually settled in the bayous and prairies of Spanish Louisiana, where their descendants became known as Cajuns. They were joined by Louisiana Creoles, Spanish, German, African and Native American communities, producing a distinctive blended culture.

Culture

Acadiana is renowned for Cajun and zydeco music, for its French language heritage — long suppressed in the 20th century and now promoted by immersion schools and the Council for the Development of French in Louisiana (CODOFIL) — and for its cuisine, including gumbo, jambalaya, boudin, étouffée and crawfish boils. Much of the terrain consists of wetlands, bayous and marsh, and the region's economy has been shaped by fishing, agriculture, and the oil and gas industry.
