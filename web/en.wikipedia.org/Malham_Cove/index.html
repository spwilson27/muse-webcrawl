Malham Cove

Malham Cove is a large curved limestone formation near the village of Malham in the Yorkshire Dales, North Yorkshire, England. The cliff is about 80 metres (260 feet) high and 300 metres wide, formed along a fault line by water and glacial action.

Formation

The cove was shaped by a waterfall fed by meltwater at the end of the last Ice Age. Today Malham Beck flows underground and emerges at the foot of the cliff. Above the cove is a remarkable limestone pavement, weathered into blocks (clints) separated by deep fissures (grikes).

Visitors and climbing

Malham Cove is a popular destination for walkers on the Pennine Way, rock climbers — whose routes here include some of Britain's hardest sport climbs — and birdwatchers; peregrine falcons have nested on the cliff. The limestone pavement above the cove appeared as a filming location in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 1.
