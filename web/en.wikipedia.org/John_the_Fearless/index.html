John I of Burgundy, known as John the Fearless (28 May 1371 – 10 September 1419), was Duke of Burgundy from 1404 until his assassination in 1419. His rivalry with the Armagnacs plunged France into civil war during the Hundred Years' War.

Early life

John was the eldest son of Philip the Bold, Duke of Burgundy. He earned his epithet "the Fearless" fighting against the Ottomans at the Battle of Nicopolis (1396), where he was captured and ransomed. He succeeded his father in 1404 and also inherited his mother's Flemish territories in 1405.

Conflict in France

John struggled for control of the regency during the madness of King Charles VI against his cousin Louis, Duke of Orléans, whom he had assassinated in Paris in 1407. The killing sparked the Armagnac–Burgundian Civil War. John seized Paris in 1418 amid popular support for his reform programme, but was driven out by the Armagnacs.

Assassination and legacy

On 10 September 1419, during a parley with the Dauphin (the future Charles VII) on the bridge at Montereau, John was struck down by the Dauphin's men. His murder drove his son and successor, Philip the Good, into alliance with England — a turning point in the Hundred Years' War.
