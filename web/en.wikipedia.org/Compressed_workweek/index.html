Compressed workweek

A compressed schedule fits full-time hours into fewer days

A compressed workweek is an alternative work schedule in which employees work the standard full-time number of hours in fewer days than the traditional five-day week. Common forms include the 4/10 schedule (four 10-hour days), the 9/80 schedule (eight 9-hour days and one 8-hour day in a two-week period, with one day off), and three 12-hour shifts.

Advantages

Compressed schedules give employees longer weekends and fewer commutes, which can reduce travel costs and emissions and improve work–life balance and recruitment. Employers may benefit from extended daily operating hours without overtime, and from reduced absenteeism. Trials of compressed and four-day arrangements — including large pilots in the United Kingdom and elsewhere in 2022–2023 — frequently report maintained or improved productivity and employee well-being, though many such trials shorten total hours rather than compressing them.

Disadvantages

Longer workdays can cause fatigue, reduce late-day productivity, and complicate childcare and scheduling with clients or partners who work standard hours. In some jurisdictions, hours beyond eight per day attract overtime pay unless an approved alternative schedule is in place, which can limit adoption.

Adoption

Compressed schedules are common in healthcare, policing, fire services, and manufacturing, where shift work is long-established, and are increasingly offered in public administration and office settings. The concept is related to, but distinct from, the four-day week movement, which advocates 32 hours with no loss of pay.
