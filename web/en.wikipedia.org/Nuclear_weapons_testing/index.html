Nuclear weapons tests are experiments carried out to determine the performance, yield, and effects of nuclear weapons. Throughout the twentieth century, most nations that developed nuclear weapons tested them. Testing has been conducted underground, underwater, in the atmosphere, and in outer space.

History

The first nuclear test, code-named Trinity, was conducted by the United States at Alamogordo, New Mexico, on 16 July 1945. The Soviet Union followed in 1949, the United Kingdom in 1952, France in 1960, and China in 1964. More than 2,000 tests were carried out in total, over 1,000 of them by the United States and more than 700 by the Soviet Union. The largest test ever was the Soviet Tsar Bomba of 1961, with a yield of about 50 megatons.

Effects and opposition

Atmospheric tests spread radioactive fallout over wide areas, contaminating Pacific islanders, downwind communities, and test-site workers, and provoked growing public opposition. The Partial Test Ban Treaty of 1963 banned tests in the atmosphere, underwater, and in space for its signatories; later testing moved underground. France and China continued atmospheric testing until 1974 and 1980 respectively.

Test-ban regime

The Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty (CTBT), opened for signature in 1996, bans all nuclear explosions, but it has not entered into force. A global monitoring system of seismic, hydroacoustic, infrasound, and radionuclide stations detects clandestine tests. Since the 1990s, the only state to test has been North Korea (2006–2017).
