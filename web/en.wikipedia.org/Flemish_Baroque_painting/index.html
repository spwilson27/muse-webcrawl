Flemish Baroque painting

Flemish Baroque painting refers to the art produced in the Southern Netherlands — roughly modern Belgium — during the Baroque period, from about the late 16th century to the early 18th century, while the region was under Spanish Habsburg rule.

Characteristics

Centred on Antwerp, the style combined the grandeur and drama of the Italian Baroque with a characteristically Flemish attention to surface detail, texture, and exuberant naturalism. Peter Paul Rubens, whose large workshop produced altarpieces, mythologies, and portraits for courts across Europe, dominated the era; his pupils and collaborators included Anthony van Dyck and Jacob Jordaens.

Specialisation

Antwerp's art market encouraged specialisation: Frans Snyders painted animals and market scenes, Jan Brueghel the Elder flower pieces and landscapes, and still-life and genre painting flourished for bourgeois buyers. Collaborations, in which several specialists contributed to one canvas, were common.
