Kenyan cuisine

Kenyan cuisine is the cooking style and dishes of the many ethnic communities of Kenya, shaped by the country's varied geography, from the Indian Ocean coast to the highlands and the shores of Lake Victoria. Staples across much of the country include maize, beans, potatoes, bananas and leafy greens, while grilled meat — nyama choma — holds a central place in social eating.

Staple dishes

Ugali, a stiff porridge of maize flour cooked in water, is the most widespread staple, usually served with sukuma wiki (collard greens), cabbage, spinach or beef stew. Githeri, a boiled mixture of maize and beans, originates with the Kikuyu of central Kenya. Other common dishes include irio (mashed potatoes, peas, maize and greens), matoke (cooked green bananas), pilau (spiced rice, especially on the coast), chapati flatbreads of Indian influence, and samosa pastries.

Regional cooking

Coastal Swahili cooking uses coconut milk, tamarind and spices in dishes such as wali wa nazi (coconut rice) and fish curries. Around Lake Victoria, tilapia and Nile perch are staples, often fried and served with ugali and greens. Among pastoralist groups such as the Maasai, milk, meat and traditionally blood form the core of the diet.

Nyama choma

Roasted meat, usually goat or beef, grilled over charcoal and served with kachumbari (a salad of tomato, onion and chilli) and ugali, is a centrepiece of gatherings across Kenya, sold at dedicated grill restaurants and roadside stands.
