Arquebus

An arquebus is a form of long gun that appeared in Europe and the Ottoman Empire in the early 15th century. An infantryman armed with one was called an arquebusier. The name derives from the German Hakenbüchse, "hook gun", referring to early versions fitted with a hook to brace against fortifications.

History

Early arquebuses were smoothbore matchlock weapons, fired by lowering a burning slow match into a priming pan. They were cheaper and easier to train with than longbows or crossbows, and their shot could penetrate plate armour at short range. Spanish arquebusiers were a key component of the tercio formations that dominated European battlefields in the sixteenth century.

Use and decline

The arquebus spread worldwide through European trade and conquest, notably to Japan in 1543, where it was adopted on a large scale as the tanegashima. From the late sixteenth century it was gradually superseded on European battlefields by the heavier, more powerful musket.
