Foynes

Foynes lies on the southern shore of the Shannon Estuary in County Limerick, Ireland.

Foynes (Irish: Faing) is a town and seaport in County Limerick, Ireland, situated on the southern shore of the Shannon Estuary, about 27 km west of Limerick city. It is one of Ireland's major deep-water ports, handling bulk cargoes through the Shannon Foynes Port Company.

Flying boat era

Foynes is best known internationally as the European terminus of the first transatlantic flying-boat services. Its sheltered estuary made it an ideal alighting area, and from 1939 Pan American's Boeing 314 "Yankee Clipper" flying boats operated passenger services between Foynes and New York. During the Second World War Foynes was a vital link between neutral Ireland and the outside world, carrying diplomats, military personnel and refugees. The era ended in 1945 as landplanes took over; the story is preserved at the Foynes Flying Boat & Maritime Museum, which houses a full-scale replica of a Boeing 314.

Irish coffee

Foynes also claims the invention of Irish coffee: in 1943, on a cold winter night, chef Joe Sheridan is said to have added whiskey to the coffee served to freezing passengers whose flying boat had turned back to Foynes, telling them it was "Irish coffee." The drink was later popularised in San Francisco.

Modern port

Today the port of Foynes handles dry and liquid bulk cargoes, including alumina for the nearby Aughinish plant, petroleum products, fertiliser, animal feed and project cargoes such as wind-turbine components. The town also has a yacht club and marina on the estuary.
