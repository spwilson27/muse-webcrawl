United Arab Republic

The United Arab Republic (UAR) was a sovereign state in the Middle East from 1958 until 1961, formed by the union of Egypt and Syria, and a short-lived experiment in pan-Arab unity under President Gamal Abdel Nasser. For a few months in 1958 it was loosely joined with North Yemen in a confederation called the United Arab States. Syria withdrew from the union after a coup in September 1961; Egypt kept the name United Arab Republic until 1971.

Formation

Syrian officers and Ba'ath Party leaders, fearing a communist takeover and internal instability, pressed Cairo for full union, which many expected Nasser to refuse; instead he accepted on condition of a complete merger, dissolving Syrian parties and merging the armies and administrations. Plebiscites in both countries approved the union on 22 February 1958, and Nasser became president of the new republic with its capital in Cairo.

Breakdown

Egyptian dominance, the imposition of Cairo's socialist decrees and security apparatus, and the marginalization of Syrian officers and businessmen bred resentment. On 28 September 1961 Syrian army units seized Damascus and declared secession. Nasser, unwilling to reconquer Syria by force, accepted the break-up. The episode damaged the practical programme of pan-Arabism, though the ideal remained influential, and later attempts at Arab unions — including the Federation of Arab Republics in the 1970s — were loosely modelled on it.

Legacy

The UAR's flag, with two green stars representing Egypt and Syria, was retained by Syria in modified form for decades, and the union remains a touchstone in debates about Arab nationalism and regional integration.
