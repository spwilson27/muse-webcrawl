Albert Memorial

The Albert Memorial is a Gothic Revival monument in Kensington Gardens, London, commissioned by Queen Victoria in memory of her husband, Prince Albert, who died of typhoid fever in 1861. Designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott and opened in July 1872, with the seated statue of Albert installed in 1875, it stands opposite the Royal Albert Hall and is one of London's most elaborate monuments.

Design

The memorial takes the form of an ornate canopy, or ciborium, 176 feet (about 54 metres) high, sheltering a gilded seated statue of Prince Albert holding the catalogue of the Great Exhibition of 1851, which he had championed. Around the podium runs the Frieze of Parnassus, depicting 169 individual sculptural figures of great artists, poets, composers, painters, sculptors and architects.

Symbolism

At the corners of the podium stand allegorical groups representing the continents of Europe, Asia, Africa and America, while higher groups symbolise agriculture, commerce, engineering and manufactures — the industrial and cultural achievements Albert promoted. Virtues and angels crown the structure above.

Restoration

By the late 20th century the memorial had seriously decayed. English Heritage undertook a major restoration completed in 1998, which included cleaning, repairing the sculpture and re-gilding the statue of Albert and the spire's ornament.
