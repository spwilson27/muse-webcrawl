Put-in-Bay, Ohio

Put-in-Bay is a village on South Bass Island in Lake Erie, within Ottawa County, Ohio, United States. The permanent population is only about 150, but the village is a popular summer resort that draws hundreds of thousands of visitors by ferry and private boat each season. The name derives from sailors "putting in" to the sheltered bay.

History

The bay was the base of Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry's fleet in the War of 1812. From here Perry sailed to victory in the Battle of Lake Erie on September 10, 1813, reporting "We have met the enemy and they are ours." Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial—a 352-foot Doric column completed in 1915, one of the tallest monuments in the United States—commemorates the battle and the lasting peace among the United States, Canada, and Britain; Perry and officers from both sides are interred beneath its rotunda. The village itself was incorporated in 1877 and prospered on fishing, ice harvesting, vineyards, and tourism, anchored by the grand Hotel Victory (1892–1919).

Tourism and attractions

Downtown Delaware Avenue offers restaurants, bars, and live music that give the island a lively summer nightlife. Attractions include the Crystal Cave (a large celestite geode discovered beneath the Heineman Winery), Perry's Cave, the Lake Erie Islands Historical Society museum, and the Aquatic Visitors Center. Tourists tour the island largely by rented golf cart and bicycle.

Access

Ferries run from Port Clinton and Sandusky (Miller Ferry and Jet Express), and the island has a small airport. In winter, when the lake freezes, an ice road and air service connect residents to the mainland.
