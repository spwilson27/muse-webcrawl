Whetstone

A whetstone is a fine-grained stone used for sharpening the edges of steel tools such as knives, chisels, and razors, by grinding and honing. The word "whet" is an old term for sharpening, so "whetstone" simply means "sharpening stone"; many whetstones are also called water stones or oil stones depending on the lubricant used.

Materials and grit

Natural whetstones are quarried from materials such as Arkansas novaculite and Japanese sedimentary stones. Artificial stones are manufactured from abrasives including aluminium oxide, silicon carbide, and ceramic, sometimes diamond-coated. Stones are graded by grit: coarse stones (below 1,000 grit) repair damage and set bevels, medium stones (1,000–3,000) sharpen, and fine stones (4,000–10,000 and above) polish and refine the edge. Combination stones bond two grits in one block.

Use

Water stones are soaked before use and develop a slurry that aids cutting; oil stones use honing oil to float away metal particles. The blade is drawn across the stone at a consistent angle — commonly 10–20 degrees per side for kitchen knives — alternating sides until a burr forms and is removed. Honing steels, by contrast, realign rather than sharpen an edge.
