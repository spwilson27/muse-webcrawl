No wave

No wave was a short-lived underground music and art movement that emerged in New York City in the late 1970s. Reacting against the perceived commercialism of new wave music — the name is a pun on "new wave" — no wave bands embraced abrasive atonality, dissonance, repetitive rhythms and confrontational performance, often rejecting conventional song structures and musicianship altogether.

History

The movement coalesced around downtown Manhattan venues and artist-run spaces. In 1978 Brian Eno produced the compilation No New York, documenting four central bands: the Contortions (led by James Chance), Teenage Jesus and the Jerks (fronted by Lydia Lunch), Mars, and D.N.A. (featuring Arto Lindsay). Other notable acts included Glenn Branca's guitar ensembles, the Swans in the movement's later phase, and artists crossing into film and visual art.

No wave was closely intertwined with the downtown art scene and with no wave cinema, a parallel movement of low-budget underground filmmaking. By the early 1980s the original scene had largely dissolved, though its abrasive aesthetic influenced later noise rock, industrial music and experimental rock.

Characteristics

No wave music typically features atonal guitar textures, skronking saxophone, funk-derived but fractured rhythms, shouted or deadpan vocals, and an emphasis on texture and confrontation over melody. Lyrics often addressed urban alienation, violence and transgression.
