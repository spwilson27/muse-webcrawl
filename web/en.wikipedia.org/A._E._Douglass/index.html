Andrew Ellicott Douglass (July 5, 1867 – March 20, 1962) was an American astronomer who founded the science of dendrochronology, the dating of events and environmental change by studying tree growth rings.

Career

Douglass worked at the Harvard College Observatory and then under Percival Lowell at Flagstaff, Arizona, where he helped establish Lowell Observatory. He later joined the University of Arizona, became its acting president, and founded the Laboratory of Tree-Ring Research in 1937.

Dendrochronology

Seeking a relationship between sunspots and climate, Douglass developed the technique of crossdating tree-ring sequences. His methods allowed archaeologists to date prehistoric pueblos of the American Southwest and became a foundational tool in climatology and archaeology.
