Paul Erdős

Paul Erdős (Hungarian: Erdős Pál; 26 March 1913 – 20 September 1996) was a Hungarian mathematician, one of the most prolific in history. He worked mainly in number theory, combinatorics, graph theory, and probability, publishing around 1,500 papers with more than 500 collaborators.

Life

Born in Budapest, Erdős was a child prodigy who earned his doctorate at 21. He lived an itinerant life, travelling between universities with a suitcase, sleeping at colleagues' homes, and subsisting on coffee — "a mathematician," he said, "is a machine for turning coffee into theorems". He offered cash prizes for solutions to problems he posed, many of which remain famous open questions.

Legacy

The Erdős number measures collaborative distance from him in the co-authorship graph: Erdős himself is 0, his direct co-authors 1, and so on. With Alfréd Rényi he founded the study of random graphs. He died of a heart attack in Warsaw while attending a conference in 1996.
