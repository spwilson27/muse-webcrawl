Electrophilic addition

Electrophilic addition
TypeOrganic reaction
SubstratesAlkenes, alkynes
Key ruleMarkovnikov's rule

An electrophilic addition reaction is an addition reaction in organic chemistry in which a pi bond is broken and two new sigma bonds are formed, initiated by attack of an electrophile on the electron-rich pi system of an alkene or alkyne.

Mechanism

In a typical two-step mechanism, the pi electrons of the double bond attack an electrophile (E+), forming a carbocation intermediate. A nucleophile then attacks the carbocation to complete the addition. Because the more stable carbocation is formed preferentially, addition of reagents such as HBr generally follows Markovnikov's rule, placing the hydrogen on the carbon already bearing more hydrogens.

Examples

Important electrophilic additions include halogenation with Br2 or Cl2 (which proceeds via a cyclic halonium ion and gives anti addition), hydrohalogenation, acid-catalysed hydration to alcohols, and oxymercuration–demercuration.
