Köchel catalogue

The Köchel catalogue (German: Köchel-Verzeichnis) is a chronological catalogue of compositions by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, originally created by Ludwig Ritter von Köchel and published in 1862 as the Chronologisch-thematisches Verzeichniss sämmtlicher Tonwerke Wolfgang Amade Mozarts. Works are identified by their Köchel numbers, abbreviated K. or KV, running from K. 1 (a minuet composed in childhood) to K. 626 (the Requiem).

History

Köchel, an Austrian musicologist, botanist and mineralogist, attempted the first comprehensive chronological ordering of Mozart's output, using autograph manuscripts, letters and earlier catalogues. Because many works are undated, later scholarship has repeatedly revised the numbering; major revisions include the third edition by Alfred Einstein (1937) and the sixth edition (1964) edited by Franz Giegling, Alexander Weinmann and Gerd Sievers. In current usage, numbers from different editions are sometimes distinguished, e.g. K. 550 versus the original numbering for the same work.

Use

The K. numbers are the standard reference system for Mozart's music, used in concert programmes, recordings and scholarship. Fragments, lost works and doubtful attributions are listed in appendices (Anhang). A new complete edition, long in preparation by the Internationale Stiftung Mozarteum, aims to incorporate the latest research on chronology and attribution.
