Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance

The Alliance links the French and Japanese automakers through cross-shareholdings.

The Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance is a strategic partnership between the automobile manufacturers Renault of France, Nissan and Mitsubishi Motors of Japan. The partnership began on 27 March 1999, when Renault took a stake in the financially troubled Nissan, and it came to be led for many years by Carlos Ghosn, who headed both companies. Mitsubishi Motors joined in 2016 after Nissan acquired a controlling 34 per cent stake in it.

Unlike a merger, the Alliance preserved each company's separate identity and brands while coordinating platforms, purchasing, manufacturing, and technology development. At its peak the group sold more than ten million vehicles a year, making it one of the world's largest automotive groupings, and it was an early leader in mass-market electric vehicles with models such as the Nissan Leaf and Renault Zoe.

Governance crisis and rebalancing

The arrest of Ghosn in Japan in November 2018 on allegations of financial misconduct plunged the Alliance into crisis and strained relations between Renault and Nissan, whose cross-shareholdings were long considered unbalanced. After years of negotiation, the partners announced a rebalancing in 2023 under which Renault reduced its stake in Nissan to 15 per cent, matching Nissan's stake in Renault, and the companies refocused cooperation on specific projects in Europe, Latin America, and India, including joint electric-vehicle ventures such as Ampere.
