Federal District (Brazil)

The Federal District (Portuguese: Distrito Federal) is one of the 27 federative units of Brazil. Located in the Center-West Region, it is the smallest federative unit of the country and the only one that has no municipalities, being divided instead into 35 administrative regions. It contains the federal capital, Brasília.

CapitalBrasília
Area5,802 km² (smallest federative unit)
PopulationAbout 2.8 million
Created1960 (transfer of the capital)

History

A federal district for a new inland capital was envisaged in Brazil's first republican constitution of 1891. After the site was selected in the Central Plateau, the city of Brasília was built in the late 1950s under President Juscelino Kubitschek and inaugurated on 21 April 1960, when the capital moved from Rio de Janeiro.

Government

The Federal District has a hybrid status with powers of both a state and a municipality. It elects a governor, a Legislative Chamber of 24 district deputies, and representatives to the National Congress. It may not be divided into municipalities.
