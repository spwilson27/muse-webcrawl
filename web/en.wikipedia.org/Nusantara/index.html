Nusantara

Nusantara is the Indonesian name for the Indonesian Archipelago and, more broadly, Maritime Southeast Asia. The word derives from Old Javanese nusa ("island") and antara ("in between" / "outer"), meaning roughly "the outer islands" as seen from the Javanese heartland of the Majapahit era. Since January 2022, Nusantara is also the official name of Indonesia's planned new capital city in East Kalimantan on the island of Borneo, formally Ibu Kota Nusantara (IKN).

The historical term

The word appears in the 14th-century context of the Majapahit empire; the prime minister Gajah Mada's Palapa oath (1336) vowed not to rest until the islands of Nusantara were brought under Majapahit's sway. In the early 20th century the term was revived by nationalist writers such as Ernest Douwes Dekker (Setiabudi) as a name for a future independent archipelago state, and today it commonly denotes the whole Indonesian archipelago and its shared culture.

The new capital

President Joko Widodo announced in 2019 that Indonesia's capital would move from Jakarta — beset by congestion, flooding and subsidence, with parts of North Jakarta sinking — to a site between Balikpapan and Samarinda in East Kalimantan. The name Nusantara was announced in January 2022, and the capital-city law was passed the same year. Groundbreaking took place in 2022; construction of government buildings, the presidential palace (shaped after the Garuda), and housing followed. An independence-day ceremony was held in Nusantara on 17 August 2024, though the formal transfer of the capital awaited a presidential decree, and the timeline for moving civil servants was repeatedly adjusted, with development continuing under President Prabowo Subianto toward a target of functioning as political capital by around 2028–2029.

Criticism

The project, estimated to cost over US$30 billion, has drawn criticism over its pace, funding (mostly expected from private investment), environmental impact on Borneo's forests and Indigenous communities, and questions about urgency versus Jakarta's own mitigation.
