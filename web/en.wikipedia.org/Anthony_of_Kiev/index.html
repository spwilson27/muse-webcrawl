Anthony of Kiev

Saint Anthony of the Caves

Born: c. 983, Lyubech, Kievan Rus'
Died: 1073, Kiev
Venerated in: Eastern Orthodox Church, Catholic Church
Feast: 10 July

Anthony of Kiev (c. 983 – 1073), also known as Anthony of the Caves, was a monk and, together with Theodosius of Kiev, the founder of East Slavic monasticism. He established the Kiev Monastery of the Caves (Pechersk Lavra), which became the spiritual centre of Kievan Rus'.

Life

Born at Lyubech near Chernigov, Anthony travelled to Mount Athos in Greece, where he became a monk at Esphigmenou Monastery. Sent home by his abbot to plant monastic life in Rus', he settled around 1051 in a cave near Kiev that had previously been used by the priest Hilarion. As disciples gathered, the community burrowed the network of caves that gives the Lavra its name; Anthony withdrew to a separate cave on a nearby hill, preferring solitude, while Theodosius organised the communal life of the monastery.

Legacy

Anthony's Near Caves at the Lavra still bear his name, and his relics — traditionally said to rest hidden in his cave — draw pilgrims to this day. He is honoured as the father of Russian and Ukrainian monasticism, and monasteries across the Slavic world trace their spiritual lineage to his foundation.
