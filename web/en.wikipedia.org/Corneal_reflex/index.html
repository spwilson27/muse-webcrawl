Corneal reflex

ActionInvoluntary blinking of both eyes
Afferent nerveTrigeminal nerve (ophthalmic branch, V1)
Efferent nerveFacial nerve (VII)

The corneal reflex, also known as the blink reflex, is an involuntary blinking of the eyelids elicited by stimulation of the cornea, for example by touch or by a foreign body. It serves to protect the eye from injury and drying.

Pathway

The stimulus is carried by the nasociliary branch of the ophthalmic division of the trigeminal nerve (cranial nerve V) to the spinal trigeminal nucleus in the brainstem. Interneurons project bilaterally to the facial motor nuclei, and the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII) drives contraction of the orbicularis oculi muscles, closing both eyelids — the stimulated eye directly and the other eye consensually.

Clinical use

The reflex is tested in neurological examination, including in comatose patients, where its presence or absence helps localise brainstem lesions and assess depth of coma. It is also absent or reduced in some peripheral nerve disorders affecting the trigeminal or facial nerves.
