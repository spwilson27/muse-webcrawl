Croydon

Croydon is a large town in South London, England, the principal settlement in the London Borough of Croydon. It lies about 9 miles south of Charing Cross, on the route of the old London-to-Brighton road. Once a market town in Surrey, it became a major commercial and office centre in the twentieth century, notable for its cluster of high-rise buildings and for Croydon Airport, London's main airport in the interwar years.

History

Croydon appears in the Domesday Book of 1086. The Archbishops of Canterbury maintained a palace there for centuries, and the town grew as a market and staging post. Croydon Airport opened in 1920 and from it Amy Johnson made her pioneering flight to Australia in 1930; scheduled services moved away after the Second World War and the airport closed in 1959. Post-war redevelopment filled the centre with office blocks, and the Whitgift Centre opened in 1970.

Transport and culture

East Croydon station is one of the busiest in Britain outside central London, and the town is the hub of the Tramlink light rail network. Cultural landmarks include the Fairfield Halls concert venue and the Clocktower arts complex. Crystal Palace Football Club, based at nearby Selhurst Park, is strongly associated with the town.
