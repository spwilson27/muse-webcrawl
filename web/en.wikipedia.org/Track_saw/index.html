Track saw

A track saw (also called a plunge saw) is a handheld circular saw that runs along a rigid guide rail, or "track", clamped or laid onto the workpiece. The saw rides in a channel in the aluminium track, producing straight, accurate cuts with minimal splintering. Because the blade plunges into the material, cuts can start anywhere on a panel, not only at an edge.

Design and use

The track has a rubber splinter guard along its edge that is trimmed to the blade on first use, giving a zero-clearance reference line: the edge of the strip shows exactly where the cut will fall. Most track saws include riving knives or anti-kickback features, depth stops, and scoring settings that make a shallow first pass to prevent tear-out in veneered or melamine-faced sheet goods. Tracks can be joined end-to-end for long cuts in plywood and other sheet materials.

Track saws are popular for breaking down sheet goods on site and in small workshops as a safer, more portable alternative to a table saw for large panels, and for trimming doors and flooring in place. The concept was pioneered by the German manufacturer Festool in the 1960s and has since been adopted by most major power-tool brands.
