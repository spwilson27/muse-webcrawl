Charity trustee

A charity trustee is a person who, under the law of England and Wales, has the general control and management of the administration of a charity. The term is defined in the Charities Act 2011. Depending on the legal form of the charity, trustees may be known by other titles, such as directors of a charitable company, committee members of an unincorporated association, or governors of a charitable incorporated organisation.

Duties

Charity trustees must act in the charity's best interests, further its charitable purposes for the public benefit, manage its resources responsibly, and comply with its governing document and the law. They owe duties of care and prudence, must avoid conflicts of interest, and generally may not benefit personally from the charity unless authorised. Trustees are collectively responsible for the charity's decisions, even where day-to-day work is delegated to staff.

Eligibility

Most people aged 18 or over (16 for charitable incorporated organisations) can be trustees, but certain people are disqualified, for example those with convictions for offences involving dishonesty, undischarged bankrupts, and people removed as trustees by the Charity Commission or a court. The Commission may waive disqualification in some cases. Trustees are usually unpaid volunteers, though expenses may be reimbursed.

Regulation

The Charity Commission for England and Wales regulates trustees, issues guidance on their duties, and can investigate misconduct or mismanagement, suspend or remove trustees, and appoint interim managers.
