San Diego Zoo

Opened: 1916

Location: Balboa Park, San Diego, California

Area: 100 acres (40 ha)

Animals: about 3,700 animals of more than 650 species and subspecies

Operator: San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance

Founder: Dr Harry M. Wegeforth

The San Diego Zoo is a zoo in Balboa Park, San Diego, California, housing some 3,700 animals of more than 650 species. Founded in 1916, it pioneered the concept of open-air, cageless exhibits simulating natural habitats, and it is consistently ranked among the best and most famous zoos in the world. It is operated by the nonprofit San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance (formerly the Zoological Society of San Diego), which also runs the San Diego Zoo Safari Park.

History

Physician and naturalist Dr Harry Wegeforth founded the Zoological Society of San Diego in October 1916 to take over exotic animals left behind by the Panama–California Exposition, remarking after hearing a lion roar that it would be a shame for the city to lack a zoo. The young zoo struggled financially, but Wegeforth’s animal-collecting expeditions, revenue schemes and the 1923 opening of permanent quarters in Balboa Park established it. The zoo became famous for its moated, naturalistic enclosures, for its Skyfari aerial tram (1969), and as an early leader in captive breeding and conservation biology, serving as a founding home of the California condor recovery programme.

Exhibits and animals

The zoo’s collection is organised by bioclimatic zones, including Monkey Trails, the Asian Passage, Elephant Odyssey, the Australian Outback with its koalas (the largest koala population outside Australia), Africa Rocks, and lush canyon gardens of palms and cycads that make the zoo a notable botanical garden in its own right. Its giant pandas were a signature attraction for decades: Bai Yun and her offspring, several of them born at the zoo, drew worldwide attention before the pandas returned to China in 2019; a new pair, Yun Chuan and Xin Bao, arrived in 2024, reopening the zoo’s Panda Ridge.
