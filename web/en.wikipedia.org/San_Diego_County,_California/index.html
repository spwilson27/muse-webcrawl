San Diego County, officially the County of San Diego, is a county in the southwestern corner of the U.S. state of California, bordering Mexico to the south and the Pacific Ocean to the west. As of the 2020 census its population was 3,298,634, making it the fifth most populous county in the United States and the second most populous in California. The county seat and largest city is San Diego, the eighth most populous city in the country.

Geography

The county's terrain rises from coastal plains through foothills to the Peninsular Ranges, including Palomar Mountain and Mount Laguna, and descends eastward into the Anza-Borrego Desert, the largest state park in California. Its mild Mediterranean climate, with more than 70 miles (113 km) of coastline, supports distinctive chaparral and coastal sage scrub ecosystems. San Diego County has more species of birds and plants counted than any other county in the continental United States.

History

The area was inhabited for millennia by Kumeyaay and other Indigenous peoples. Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo claimed San Diego Bay for Spain in 1542, and Mission San Diego de Alcalá, founded in 1769, was the first of the California missions. The county was created in 1850 as one of California's original 27 counties, then covering a vast territory later divided to form Riverside, San Bernardino and Imperial counties.

Economy and military

The county's economy rests on the U.S. military and defense industries, tourism, biotechnology, telecommunications and international trade with Mexico. Major installations include Naval Base San Diego, Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton and Naval Air Station North Island. The San Diego–Tijuana cross-border metropolis forms one of the largest binational conurbations in the world.
