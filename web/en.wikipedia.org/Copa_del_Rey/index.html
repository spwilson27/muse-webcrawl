Copa del Rey

The Copa del Rey (in full, Campeonato de España–Copa de Su Majestad el Rey) is Spain's principal national football cup competition, organised by the Royal Spanish Football Federation. Founded in 1903, it is one of the oldest football tournaments in the world and predates the Spanish league championship by more than two decades.

History

The cup began in 1903 following the success of a one-off tournament held in Madrid in 1902 for the coronation of Alfonso XIII. For years it served as Spain's de facto national championship until La Liga was founded in 1929. The competition's name has changed with Spain's regimes: Copa de Su Majestad el Rey under the monarchy, Copa del Presidente de la República in the 1930s, and Copa de Su Excelencia el Generalísimo during the Franco era, before reverting to Copa del Rey in 1977.

Format

Clubs from across the Spanish football pyramid take part, with regional and lower-division sides entering the early rounds and top-flight clubs entering later. A format introduced in 2019–20 made ties single matches hosted by the lower-division club until the semi-finals, which increased giant-killings, and the final is played at a fixed venue, in recent years La Cartuja in Seville. The winner qualifies for the UEFA Europa League and the Supercopa de España.

Records

FC Barcelona is the most successful club in the competition's history, with more than thirty titles, followed by Athletic Bilbao — whose early dominance and 1903–04 triumphs make it a founding power of the cup — and Real Madrid. Athletic's Telmo Zarra is among the competition's all-time leading scorers.
