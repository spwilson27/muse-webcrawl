Non-associative algebra

A non-associative algebra (or distributive algebra) is a vector space (or module) equipped with a bilinear multiplication that is not assumed to satisfy the associative law. The term is something of a misnomer: it means "not necessarily associative", so ordinary associative algebras are included as a special case.

Examples and classes

Important classes of non-associative algebras are defined by weaker identities. Lie algebras satisfy anticommutativity and the Jacobi identity; Jordan algebras are commutative and satisfy the Jordan identity; alternative algebras, such as the octonions, satisfy weakened forms of associativity. Other examples include cross-product algebras, Poisson algebras, and Leibniz algebras.

Structure theory

Because associativity is a powerful tool, the structure theory of non-associative algebras is more varied, proceeding class by class. Non-associative algebras arise naturally in physics (symmetries, quantum mechanics), genetics (Mendelian inheritance algebras), and the study of exceptional structures connected with the octonions.
