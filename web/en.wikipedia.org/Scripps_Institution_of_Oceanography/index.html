Scripps Institution of Oceanography (SIO) is an American center for ocean and Earth science research and graduate education located in La Jolla, San Diego, California. Founded in 1903 as an independent biological research station by University of California, Berkeley zoologist William Ritter with funding from philanthropists E. W. Scripps and his half-sister Ellen Browning Scripps, it became part of the University of California in 1912. Since 1960 it has been the graduate school of marine science of UC San Diego, which was founded around it.

Research

Scripps scientists conduct research in biological, physical, chemical and geological oceanography, marine biology, seismology, and climate science. It is famous for the Keeling Curve: the continuous record of atmospheric carbon dioxide begun by Charles David Keeling in 1958, foundational evidence for human-caused climate change. Scripps also hosts the CalCOFI programme (since 1949), the Center for Atmospheric Sciences, and contributes the global Argo float network. Notable figures include Roger Revelle, director 1951–1964 and founder of UC San Diego; Walter Munk, pioneer of ocean acoustics and wave forecasting; and Harmon Craig in isotope geochemistry.

Ships and facilities

Scripps operates a fleet including the global-class R/V Roger Revelle (1996) and R/V Sally Ride (2016), among the most capable research vessels in the US academic fleet, as well as the famous FLIP (Floating Instrument Platform), retired in 2023. Its public aquarium, the Birch Aquarium at Scripps, opened in 1992 and draws hundreds of thousands of visitors annually.
