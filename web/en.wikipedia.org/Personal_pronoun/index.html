Personal pronoun

Personal pronouns are pronouns that are associated primarily with a particular grammatical person: first person (the speaker, as in English I and we), second person (the addressee, you), or third person (others, he, she, it, they). Personal pronouns may also vary for number, case, gender and formality depending on the language.

Features

Personal pronouns typically encode person and number; many languages also mark case (subjective, objective, possessive), and third-person pronouns often mark gender or noun class. Some languages distinguish inclusive and exclusive "we" (whether the addressee is included), dual number, and levels of politeness — the T–V distinction, as in French tu/vous or German du/Sie.

English historically had distinct second-person forms (thou/thee singular, ye/you plural); modern standard English uses you for both. Singular they has been used for centuries for a referent of unknown or unspecified gender, and more recently for specific non-binary individuals.

Cross-linguistic notes

Many languages, such as Spanish and Japanese, allow pronouns to be dropped when context makes them clear (pro-drop). Some languages, including many in East and Southeast Asia, use open-class nominal forms rather than a closed pronoun set, with choices sensitive to status and relationship.
