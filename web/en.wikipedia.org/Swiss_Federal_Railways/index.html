Swiss Federal Railways

Swiss Federal Railways (German: Schweizerische Bundesbahnen, SBB; French: Chemins de fer fédéraux suisses, CFF; Italian: Ferrovie federali svizzere, FFS) is the national railway company of Switzerland. It is the largest rail and transport company of Switzerland, operating on most standard-gauge lines of the Swiss network.

History

The company was founded in 1902 following a referendum in which Swiss voters approved the nationalisation of the major private railways. SBB became a wholly state-owned joint-stock company in 1999. The network is noted for its dense clock-face timetable, punctuality and integration with buses, boats and mountain railways.

Network and services

SBB operates roughly 3,200 km of standard-gauge lines, carrying over a million passengers per day in normal years, along with extensive freight operations through SBB Cargo. Landmark infrastructure includes the Gotthard Base Tunnel, opened in 2016 as the world's longest railway tunnel. The famous Swiss railway clock, with its red second hand, was designed for SBB by Hans Hilfiker in 1944 and later licensed by Apple after a design dispute.
