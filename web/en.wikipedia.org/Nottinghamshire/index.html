Nottinghamshire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nottinghamshire (abbreviated Notts.) is a ceremonial county in the East Midlands of England. It borders South Yorkshire to the north-west, Lincolnshire to the east, Leicestershire to the south, and Derbyshire to the west. The county town and largest settlement is the city of Nottingham.

Geography

The River Trent runs through the county from west to east, and the landscape ranges from the coalfield valleys of the west to the flat clay vales and the low hills of the Nottinghamshire Wolds in the south. In the north lies Sherwood Forest, famous worldwide as the legendary home of Robin Hood, and once a royal hunting forest covering a large part of the county. Remnants of the ancient forest survive at Birklands and Bilhaugh, including the great Major Oak.

History

Nottingham was founded by the Anglo-Saxons and captured by the Danes, becoming one of the Five Boroughs of the Danelaw. Nottingham Castle played a role in the English Civil War, which began when Charles I raised his standard at Nottingham in 1642. In the 18th and 19th centuries the county industrialised: framework knitting and lace-making centred on Nottingham, cotton mills lined the Leen valley — where Richard Arkwright built some of his earliest mills — and coal mining in the west of the county became a major industry, later central to the miners' strike of 1984–85, in which most Nottinghamshire miners broke from the national strike and formed the Union of Democratic Mineworkers.

The county is also known for its great country houses, sometimes called the Dukeries because of the concentration of ducal estates in the north of the county, including Clumber Park, Thoresby Hall, Rufford Abbey, and Welbeck Abbey.

Settlements and culture

Besides Nottingham, major towns include Mansfield, Newark-on-Trent, Worksop, Retford, Sutton-in-Ashfield, and West Bridgford. The county's sporting heritage includes Trent Bridge, home of Nottinghamshire County Cricket Club; two historic football clubs in Nottingham (Nottingham Forest and Notts County, the world's oldest professional football club); and the National Ice Centre. The writer D. H. Lawrence was born in the mining town of Eastwood, and Lord Byron is buried at Hucknall, near his ancestral home of Newstead Abbey.
