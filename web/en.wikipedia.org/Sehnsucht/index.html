Sehnsucht

Sehnsucht (pronounced [ˈzeːnˌzʊxt]) is a German noun meaning "longing," "yearning," or "craving." It describes an intense, wistful desire for an alternative state of being — often something distant, unattainable, or undefined — and is regarded as having no exact equivalent in English.

Meaning

The word combines Sehnen (to long for) and Sucht (addiction, craving). Unlike nostalgia, which is directed at the past, Sehnsucht may be directed toward an ideal future or an unknown place. It became a central motif of German Romanticism, expressed in the poetry of Goethe and Novalis and the music of Schubert.

In psychology and literature

C. S. Lewis used the concept extensively, describing it as an "inconsolable longing" for something beyond ordinary experience, which he associated with joy and with his own spiritual development. Psychologists have studied Sehnsucht as a life-longing that can accompany reflection on unrealized possibilities across the lifespan.
