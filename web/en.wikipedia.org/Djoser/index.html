Djoser (also spelled Zoser) was an ancient Egyptian pharaoh of the 3rd Dynasty during the Old Kingdom period. He is best known for the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, the earliest colossal stone building in Egypt, designed by his vizier Imhotep. Djoser's reign is generally dated to the 27th century BC, and he is credited with consolidating royal power and advancing Egyptian architecture, administration, and religion.

Names and identity

Djoser's Horus name was Netjerikhet, meaning "divine of body." The name Djoser itself comes from later sources, including the Turin King List and New Kingdom inscriptions, and means "sacred" or "holy." A famous statue of Djoser, now in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo, was found in a serdab chamber of his pyramid complex at Saqqara.

Reign

Djoser succeeded Khasekhemwy, the last king of the 2nd Dynasty. His reign lasted either 19 or 29 years according to differing king lists. During his rule, Egypt's capital was at Memphis, and the royal court oversaw mining expeditions to the Sinai for turquoise and copper. Inscriptions also record Djoser's concern with the southern border at Elephantine and the cult of the god Khnum.

The Step Pyramid

The Step Pyramid complex at Saqqara began as a square mastaba and was enlarged in stages into a six-stepped pyramid about 62 metres high, surrounded by courtyards, chapels, and a Heb-sed festival court. Its architect, Imhotep, was later deified — a rare honour for a non-royal Egyptian. The complex is regarded as a milestone in the evolution of the pyramid form that culminated in the pyramids of Giza.

Legacy

Djoser was venerated for centuries after his death. The Famine Stela on Sehel Island, carved in the Ptolemaic period, purports to record a decree of Djoser ending a seven-year famine. His reign marks the beginning of the Old Kingdom's monumental age.
