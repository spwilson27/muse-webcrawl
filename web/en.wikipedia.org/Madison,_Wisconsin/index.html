Madison is the capital city of the U.S. state of Wisconsin and the seat of Dane County. It is the second-largest city in Wisconsin after Milwaukee. The city sits on an isthmus between Lakes Mendota and Monona and is home to the University of Wisconsin–Madison.

History

The area was long inhabited by the Ho-Chunk people. Madison was founded in 1836, when former federal judge James Duane Doty purchased land on the isthmus and persuaded the territorial legislature to name the planned town for President James Madison and to designate it the capital. The Wisconsin State Capitol, completed in 1917, dominates the city skyline.

Geography

Madison is built around four lakes — Mendota, Monona, Waubesa, and Kegonsa — and is known for its extensive network of parks, bike paths, and lakefront trails.

Economy and culture

Government and the university are the city's largest employers, alongside a growing technology and health-care sector. Madison is known for its farmers' market on Capitol Square, its progressive politics, and a lively music and festival scene.
