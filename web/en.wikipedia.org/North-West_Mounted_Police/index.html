The North-West Mounted Police (NWMP) was a Canadian paramilitary police force established in 1873 by the government of Prime Minister John A. Macdonald to maintain law and order in the newly acquired North-West Territories (present-day Alberta and Saskatchewan, and parts of neighbouring provinces and territories). The force was modelled in part on the Royal Irish Constabulary and the mounted rifles; its striking red serge uniform — intended to distinguish it from American blue-coated soldiers and recall British traditions — became iconic.

March West

In July 1874, some 275 officers and men, with horses, oxen and artillery, set out from Dufferin, Manitoba, on the gruelling "March West" to suppress the whisky trade centred on posts such as Fort Whoop-Up and to assert Canadian sovereignty following the Cypress Hills Massacre of 1873. The force established posts including Fort Macleod and Fort Calgary, the seeds of later prairie cities.

Later history

The NWMP policed the construction of the Canadian Pacific Railway, conflicts including the North-West Rebellion of 1885, and the Klondike Gold Rush in the Yukon, where Superintendent Sam Steele enforced orderly entry at the passes. In 1904 Edward VII granted the prefix "Royal". In 1920 the Royal North-West Mounted Police merged with the Dominion Police to form the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP).
