Combining character

In digital typography, combining characters are characters that are intended to modify other characters. The most common combining characters in the Latin script are the combining diacritical marks, such as the combining acute accent (U+0301) and the combining tilde (U+0303). Combining characters are ordinarily not used alone; they follow a base character and are rendered on top of, below, or around it.

Unicode

In the Unicode standard, combining characters are assigned to general categories Mn (nonspacing mark), Mc (spacing mark), and Me (enclosing mark). Unicode provides a mechanism for ordering combining marks and for canonical equivalence, so that, for example, the letter "á" can be represented either as the single precomposed code point U+00E1 or as the sequence of the base letter "a" (U+0061) followed by the combining acute accent (U+0301). Normalization forms such as NFC and NFD convert between these representations.

The combining diacritical marks block (U+0300–U+036F) contains the most common marks. Additional blocks include combining diacritical marks extended, combining marks for symbols, and combining half marks. Some characters, such as the combining grapheme joiner (U+034F), have special functions.

OpenType

In OpenType fonts, glyph positioning and substitution rules (GPOS and GSUB) control how combining marks attach to base glyphs, including anchor points for marks above and below.
