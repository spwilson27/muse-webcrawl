Entr'acte

Entr'acte
MeaningFrench: "between the acts"
ContextTheatre, opera, ballet
RelatedIntermission, interlude

An entr'acte (French for "between the acts") is a musical piece, dance, or other performance that takes place between the acts of a play, opera, or ballet, often while the scenery is being changed or the audience takes an intermission.

In music

In opera and musical theatre, entr'acte music may be a purely orchestral interlude that sets the mood for the coming act. Famous examples include the entr'actes in Bizet's Carmen and the orchestral interludes in Britten's operas. In modern musicals, the entr'acte is the overture-like piece that opens the second act.

The film Entr'acte

Entr'acte is also the title of a 1924 French avant-garde film directed by René Clair, with music by Erik Satie, made to be shown during the intermission of the ballet Relâche at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées in Paris. It is a landmark of Dadaist cinema.
