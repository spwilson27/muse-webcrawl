Jean Margéot (3 February 1916 – 17 July 2009) was a Mauritian cardinal of the Roman Catholic Church. He served as Bishop of Port-Louis from 1969 to 1993, and was created a cardinal by Pope John Paul II in 1988, becoming the first — and to date only — cardinal from Mauritius.

Biography

Margéot was born in Quatre Bornes, then in British Mauritius, and studied for the priesthood before being ordained in 1938. He pursued further studies in Rome and served in parish and diocesan ministry on the island, gaining a reputation as an educator and pastor in a religiously and ethnically diverse society that included Hindu, Muslim, Christian and other communities.

Bishop of Port-Louis

Appointed Bishop of Port-Louis on 6 February 1969, shortly after Mauritian independence, Margéot led the diocese for nearly a quarter of a century. He promoted interreligious dialogue, Catholic education and social justice, and founded initiatives for family life and lay formation. His ministry coincided with the implementation of the reforms of the Second Vatican Council in Mauritius.

Cardinal

On 28 June 1988 Pope John Paul II created him Cardinal-Priest of San Gabriele Arcangelo all'Acqua Traversa. He hosted the Pope's pastoral visit to Mauritius in 1989. Margéot resigned as bishop in 1993 on reaching the retirement age and was succeeded by Maurice Piat, who himself later became a cardinal. Having passed the age of 80 in 1996, Margéot did not participate in the conclave of 2005.

Death and legacy

Margéot died in Port-Louis on 17 July 2009, aged 93. He is remembered in Mauritius as a unifying national figure whose church leadership emphasised harmony among the island's communities.
