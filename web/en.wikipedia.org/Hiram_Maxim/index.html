Sir Hiram Stevens Maxim (5 February 1840 – 24 November 1916) was an American-born British inventor best known as the creator of the Maxim gun, the first portable fully automatic machine gun. A prolific and wide-ranging inventor, Maxim held patents on devices as varied as a curling iron, a mousetrap, steam pumps, and electric lamps; he fought a long patent war with Thomas Edison over the incandescent light bulb, experimented with a huge steam-powered flying machine in the 1890s, and founded the arms company that became part of Vickers. He was knighted in 1901.

Early life

Maxim was born in Sangerville, Maine, the son of a farmer and mechanic. Apprenticed at fourteen to a carriage maker, he showed an early talent for invention—his improved mousetrap is still famous—and worked in machine shops in Massachusetts and New York. He became chief engineer of the United States Electric Lighting Company in 1878, where his work on incandescent lamps and regulators brought him into direct competition, and litigation, with Edison's interests.

The Maxim gun

According to his own account, Maxim conceived the idea of using a firearm's recoil to power automatic loading after being knocked about by the kick of a rifle while shooting in 1882; he also remarked that an acquaintance advised him that the fastest way to make a fortune was to "invent something that will enable these Europeans to cut each other's throats with greater facility". In 1884 he demonstrated his recoil-operated, belt-fed, water-cooled machine gun, capable of firing some 600 rounds per minute. He founded the Maxim Gun Company in London and toured Europe demonstrating the weapon; nearly every army adopted it in some form. The gun's devastating effect in colonial warfare—famously in the Matabele War and at Omdurman—inspired Hilaire Belloc's couplet: "Whatever happens, we have got / The Maxim gun, and they have not." Maxim's company merged with Vickers in 1896, and variants of his design (the British Vickers, German MG 08, and Russian PM1910) were used by all sides in the First World War.

Other inventions

Maxim's other work included a "Pipe of Peace" inhaler for bronchitis sufferers (he himself suffered from the condition), fire sprinklers and fire alarms, steam engines, and an amusement ride ("Captive Flying Machines") built to fund his aviation research. His 1894 flying machine—a giant biplane test rig with steam engines of 180 horsepower—lifted off its restraining rails in trials at Bexley but was never flown freely and he abandoned the project. His son, Hiram Percy Maxim, invented the firearm silencer ("Maxim Silencer") and founded the American Radio Relay League.

Later life

Maxim became a British subject in 1900 and was knighted by Queen Victoria in 1901. Partly deaf from years of test-firing his guns, he took an interest in devices for the deaf late in life. He died at his home in Streatham, London, on 24 November 1916, during the war in which his invention was being used on an unprecedented scale, and was buried in West Norwood Cemetery.
