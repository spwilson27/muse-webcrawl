Book of Han

The Book of Han (Hanshu), the standard history of the Western Han dynasty

The Book of Han (Chinese: 漢書; pinyin: Hànshū), also known as the History of the Former Han, is a Chinese history covering the Western Han dynasty from 206 BCE to 23 CE. It was compiled principally by the historian Ban Gu (32–92 CE), continuing a project begun by his father Ban Biao, and completed after Ban Gu's death by his sister Ban Zhao — making her one of the earliest known female historians in China. It is the second of China's Twenty-Four Histories and set the model for all subsequent dynastic histories.

Composition

Ban Biao began the work as a continuation of Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian. Emperor Ming commissioned Ban Gu to compile a proper history of the dynasty, though Ban Gu was at one point arrested for privately writing imperial history before the project received official sanction. Ban Gu died in prison in 92 CE after the downfall of his patron Dou Xian; Emperor He then ordered his sister Ban Zhao to complete the remaining sections, including the treatise on astronomy and the chronological tables, which she did with the assistance of the scholar Ma Xu.

Contents

The work comprises 100 chapters (originally 100 juan, later divided into 120): 12 imperial annals, 8 chronological tables, 10 treatises, and 70 biographies and collective accounts. Building on Sima Qian's structure, the Book of Han introduced innovations that became standard, notably the treatises on law, geography, and bibliography — the "Yiwenzhi" treatise is the earliest surviving Chinese library catalogue, based on the classification of Liu Xin — and biographies devoted to subjects such as foreign peoples, the Xiongnu, and the regions of the southwest.

Significance

The Book of Han is the indispensable primary source for the Western Han period, covering the reign of Emperor Wu, the expansion into Central Asia, and the usurpation of Wang Mang. Unlike the Records of the Grand Historian, which spanned all of Chinese history known at the time, the Book of Han confined itself to a single dynasty, establishing the convention followed by the later dynastic histories.
