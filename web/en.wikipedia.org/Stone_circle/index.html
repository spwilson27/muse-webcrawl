Stone circle

A stone circle in the British Isles

A stone circle is a ring of standing stones. Most are found in the British Isles and Brittany, where archaeologists know of around 1,300 examples, though similar arrangements exist elsewhere in the world. Stone circles were constructed from about 3200 to 2000 BCE during the Late Neolithic and Early Bronze Age.

Distribution and forms

Circles range from small rings of a few stones to vast monuments such as Avebury, the largest stone circle in the world, and Stonehenge, the most famous. Some circles are enclosed by earthworks (henges) or accompanied by avenues, timber circles, and burials. Concentric and recumbent stone circles are regional variants found in Scotland.

Purpose

The purpose of stone circles remains uncertain. Proposed functions include ceremonial and ritual use, burial, astronomical observation of solstices and lunar standstills, and social gathering. Excavations at sites such as the Ring of Brodgar in Orkney continue to refine understanding of their builders.
