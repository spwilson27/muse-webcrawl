Bjarne Stroustrup (born 30 December 1950) is a Danish computer scientist most known for creating the C++ programming language. He has held positions at Bell Labs, AT&T Labs, Texas A&M University, Morgan Stanley and Columbia University, and is the author of the widely used textbook The C++ Programming Language.

Education and early career

Stroustrup grew up in Aarhus, Denmark, and studied mathematics and computer science at Aarhus University, graduating in 1975. He earned his PhD in computer science from the University of Cambridge in 1979, where his work on distributed systems was supervised in the tradition of the Cambridge Computer Laboratory. He then joined Bell Labs in New Jersey.

Creation of C++

At Bell Labs, Stroustrup sought a language combining the efficiency and low-level control of C with the class-based organisation of Simula. In 1979 he began work on "C with Classes", which by 1983 had evolved into C++. The language added object-oriented and generic programming facilities while remaining largely compatible with C. C++ was standardised by ISO in 1998 and has been updated regularly since; Stroustrup remains active in its standards committee and in advocating safe, resource-conscious programming styles.

Honours

Stroustrup was elected to the US National Academy of Engineering in 2004, received the IEEE Computer Pioneer Award in 2018, and was awarded the Charles Stark Draper Prize for Engineering in 2018 for conceiving and developing C++. He is a fellow of the ACM and the IEEE.
