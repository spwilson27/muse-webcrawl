Constitution of 1857
Country: Mexico
Promulgated: 5 February 1857
Replaced: Constitution of 1824 / Bases Orgánicas
Superseded by: Constitution of 1917

The Federal Constitution of the United Mexican States of 1857 was the liberal constitution promulgated on 5 February 1857 during the era of La Reforma. Drafted by a constituent congress dominated by liberals, it enshrined individual rights and curtailed the privileges of the Catholic Church and the military.

Provisions

The constitution established Mexico as a federal republic with separation of powers, a unicameral congress, and an extensive catalogue of individual guarantees, including freedom of speech, press, assembly, and education, and the abolition of slavery and of special legal privileges (fueros). It incorporated the Reform Laws abolishing church fueros and ordered the disentailment of corporate property, while stopping short of full religious toleration.

Reactions and war

Conservatives, the Church hierarchy, and army leaders rejected the constitution, and the Plan of Tacubaya (December 1857) repudiated it, triggering the Reform War (1858–1861) between liberal and conservative governments. The liberals under Benito Juárez prevailed, and the constitution was later amended to include the Reform Laws and, after the Restored Republic, further liberal measures.

Legacy

The Constitution of 1857 remained Mexico's fundamental law, with interruptions during the French intervention and Second Mexican Empire, until it was replaced by the Constitution of 1917, which inherited much of its liberal framework while adding social rights.
