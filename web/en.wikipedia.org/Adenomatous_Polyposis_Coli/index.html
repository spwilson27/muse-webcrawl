Adenomatous Polyposis Coli

Adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the APC gene, located on chromosome 5q22.2. The APC protein is a tumor suppressor that acts as an antagonist of the Wnt signaling pathway and is also involved in other processes including cell migration and adhesion, transcriptional activation, and apoptosis.

Role in cancer

Defects in the APC gene cause familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), an autosomal dominant condition in which hundreds to thousands of adenomatous polyps develop in the colon and rectum, with colorectal cancer occurring in nearly all untreated patients by middle age. Somatic mutations of APC are also found in the great majority of sporadic colorectal cancers, where loss of APC function is often the earliest ("gatekeeper") event in tumor development.

Mechanism

APC is a core component of the β-catenin destruction complex, together with Axin, GSK-3β, and casein kinase 1. In the absence of Wnt signaling, the complex phosphorylates β-catenin, marking it for degradation. When APC is lost, β-catenin accumulates, enters the nucleus, and activates transcription of growth-promoting genes such as MYC and CCND1.
