The Topkapı Palace (Turkish: Topkapı Sarayı, "Cannon Gate Palace") is a large museum and library in the east of the Fatih district of Istanbul, Turkey. From the 1460s it was the administrative centre of the Ottoman Empire and the primary residence of its sultans for nearly four centuries, housing as many as 4,000 people at its height.

Layout

The palace occupies the tip of the historic peninsula between the Golden Horn and the Sea of Marmara. It is organised as a series of four courtyards of increasing privacy behind the Imperial Gate: the First Courtyard held service buildings and the imperial mint; the Second, entered through the Gate of Salutation, contains the Imperial Council (Divan) chamber and the palace kitchens, now displaying the famous porcelain and silver collections; the Third, beyond the Gate of Felicity, contains the Audience Chamber, the sultan's apartments and the Enderun library; and the Fourth Courtyard is a pleasure ground of pavilions, including the Baghdad and Revan kiosks and the tiled Circumcision Room.

Harem and Treasury

The Harem — more than 400 rooms added over centuries — housed the sultan's mother (the valide sultan, often the most powerful figure in the palace), wives, concubines and children, guarded by black eunuchs. The Imperial Treasury displays the jewelled Topkapı Dagger and the 86-carat Spoonmaker's Diamond, while the Privy Chamber guards the Sacred Relics, including items attributed to the Prophet Muhammad. The sultans moved to the European-style Dolmabahçe Palace in 1856; after the fall of the empire, Topkapı was converted into a museum in 1924.
