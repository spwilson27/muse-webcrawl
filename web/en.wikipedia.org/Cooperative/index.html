A cooperative (also known as co-operative or co-op) is "an autonomous association of persons united voluntarily to meet their common economic, social and cultural needs and aspirations through a jointly owned and democratically-controlled enterprise". Cooperatives are democratically controlled by their members, with each member having one vote in electing the board of directors.

History

Cooperative enterprises emerged from the hardships of the Industrial Revolution. The Rochdale Society of Equitable Pioneers, founded in 1844 by 28 working men in northern England, established the Rochdale Principles — open membership, democratic control, limited interest on capital, and distribution of surplus to members in proportion to their purchases — that became the template for the modern movement. In Germany, Friedrich Wilhelm Raiffeisen and Hermann Schulze-Delitzsch pioneered cooperative credit banks that evolved into credit unions.

Cooperative principles

The International Cooperative Alliance states seven principles: voluntary and open membership; democratic member control; member economic participation; autonomy and independence; education, training, and information; cooperation among cooperatives; and concern for community.

Types

Consumer cooperatives are owned by their customers (retail co-ops, food co-ops). Worker cooperatives are owned and governed by their employees, the best-known being Spain's Mondragon Corporation. Producer cooperatives let farmers and artisans jointly process and market output — many agricultural brands, such as Ocean Spray and Land O'Lakes, are cooperatives. Housing cooperatives own residential property collectively, and credit unions are member-owned financial cooperatives. Worldwide, cooperative membership is estimated at over one billion people.
