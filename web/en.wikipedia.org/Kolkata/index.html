Kolkata

Kolkata (formerly Calcutta) is the capital of the Indian state of West Bengal and the third-most populous metropolitan area of India. Located on the eastern bank of the Hooghly River, it was the capital of British India until 1911 and remains a major centre of culture, literature, and commerce in eastern India, often called the "Cultural Capital of India" and the "City of Joy."

History

The city grew from three villages — Sutanuti, Gobindapur, and Kalikata — acquired by the English East India Company under Job Charnock in 1690. It became the capital of British India, survived the attack of Siraj ud-Daulah in 1756 (the "Black Hole of Calcutta"), and after the Battle of Plassey (1757) anchored British rule in Bengal. In the 19th century Kolkata was the epicentre of the Bengal Renaissance, associated with figures such as Ram Mohan Roy, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, and Rabindranath Tagore. The capital shifted to Delhi in 1911. Partition in 1947 brought millions of refugees from East Bengal.

Culture

Kolkata is famed for its literature, cinema (Satyajit Ray), theatre, football culture, and adda (intellectual conversation). The autumn festival of Durga Puja, with its elaborate pandals, was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2021. The city retains India's oldest operating tram network and its first metro railway (1984).

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Victoria Memorial, the Howrah Bridge (Rabindra Setu), the Indian Museum, St. Paul's Cathedral, Marble Palace, Kalighat Temple, Eden Gardens cricket stadium, and the National Library.

Economy

Kolkata is the commercial hub of eastern India, with jute, tea, information technology (Sector V, New Town), and port industries. The Port of Kolkata is India's oldest major port.
