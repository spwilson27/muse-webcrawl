Colony (biology)

In biology, a colony is a group of individual organisms of the same species living closely together, usually to the mutual benefit of the members. The term is applied in several distinct senses: to aggregations of single-celled organisms such as bacteria growing on a culture medium; to groups of social animals such as ants, bees and naked mole-rats; and to colonial organisms, in which many physically connected, interdependent individuals (zooids) form a single integrated unit, as in corals, bryozoans and Portuguese man o' war.

Microbial colonies

In microbiology, a colony is a visible mass of microorganisms growing on the surface of a solid culture medium, typically all descended from a single mother cell. Because the cells are genetically identical (barring mutation), colonies are used to isolate pure cultures. Colony shape, colour, margin and texture are diagnostic characters used to identify bacteria and fungi. A group of identical cells attached together but not interdependent, such as Volvox, is also called a colony; the distinction between colonial protists and true multicellular organisms is a classic problem in evolutionary biology.

Social animal colonies

Eusocial insects such as ants, termites and many bees and wasps live in colonies consisting of one or more reproductive queens and numerous sterile workers that cooperate in brood care, foraging and defence. Colony sizes range from a few dozen individuals to the millions found in army ant and leafcutter ant societies. Colonial seabirds and seals likewise gather in dense breeding colonies on coasts and islands.

Colonial organisms

In a colonial organism, individual zooids are connected by living tissue and may be specialised for feeding, reproduction or defence. Siphonophores such as the Portuguese man o' war are extreme examples: although they appear to be single jellyfish-like animals, they are colonies of highly modified, interdependent zooids that cannot survive alone. Corals, sponges and bryozoans are sessile colonial animals that build large reef and encrusting structures.
