Lord Chief Justice of England and Wales

The Lord Chief Justice of England and Wales is the head of the judiciary of England and Wales and the president of the courts of England and Wales. Since the Constitutional Reform Act 2005 came into force, the Lord Chief Justice has been the senior judicial office-holder, taking over the judicial leadership role previously held by the Lord Chancellor.

Role

The Lord Chief Justice is president of the Criminal Division of the Court of Appeal and of the King's Bench Division of the High Court. Statutory duties include representing the views of the judiciary to Parliament and government, maintaining arrangements for the welfare, training and deployment of judges, and allocating work within the courts. The holder is chosen by a special panel convened by the Judicial Appointments Commission.

History

The office effectively dates to 1268, when the chief justice of the King's Bench emerged as the senior common-law judge. Its holders have included Sir Edward Coke, Lord Mansfield and, in the twentieth century, Lord Hewart and Lord Widgery. The first to take the post-2005 role was Lord Phillips of Worth Matravers.
