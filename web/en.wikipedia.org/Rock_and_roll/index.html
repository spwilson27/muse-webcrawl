Rock and roll

Rock and roll (often written as rock & roll, rock-n-roll, or rock 'n' roll) is a genre of popular music that emerged in the United States in the late 1940s and early 1950s, blending rhythm and blues, jump blues, boogie woogie, gospel, and country music.

Origins

Early rock and roll was driven by a strong backbeat, electric guitars, and energetic performance. Key early figures include Chuck Berry, Little Richard, Fats Domino, Bo Diddley, Sister Rosetta Tharpe, and Elvis Presley, while disc jockey Alan Freed popularised the term "rock and roll" for the music he played in Cleveland and New York. Songs such as "Rocket 88" (1951) and "Rock Around the Clock" (1954) mark its breakthrough into the mainstream.

Impact

Rock and roll reshaped youth culture, challenged racial boundaries in American music, and laid the foundation for rock music in the 1960s and beyond. Its first era is often considered to have faded by the early 1960s as styles diversified into pop rock, surf, and the British Invasion.
