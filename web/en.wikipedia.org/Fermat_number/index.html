Fermat number

A Fermat number, named after Pierre de Fermat, is a positive integer of the form Fn = 22n + 1, where n is a non-negative integer. The first few Fermat numbers are 3, 5, 17, 257, and 65,537 — all of which are prime. Fermat conjectured that all numbers of this form are prime, but the conjecture is false: no further Fermat prime is known.

History

Fermat proposed in the 17th century that every Fn is prime. In 1732, Leonhard Euler disproved this by showing that F5 = 4,294,967,297 = 641 × 6,700,417. Every Fermat number from F5 to F32 is known to be composite, though complete factorisations remain unknown for several of them, and the complete factorisation of F11 was only achieved in 1899 by Cunningham and later verified.

Properties

Fermat numbers grow extremely fast, doubling in the number of digits at each step. Any two distinct Fermat numbers are coprime, which yields a proof that there are infinitely many primes. A prime divisor of Fn must be of the form k·2n+2 + 1 for n ≥ 2, a result that greatly aids factorisation attempts.

Connection to constructible polygons

Carl Friedrich Gauss proved in 1796 that a regular polygon with n sides is constructible with compass and straightedge if the odd prime factors of n are distinct Fermat primes. Thus the regular pentagon (F1 = 5) and 17-gon (F2 = 17) are constructible, while the regular heptagon is not. Whether any Fermat prime beyond F4 exists remains an open problem.
