Herodotus (Ancient Greek: Ἡρόδοτος, c. 484 – c. 425 BC) was a Greek historian and geographer from Halicarnassus in Asia Minor. He is known for his Histories, a vast inquiry into the origins of the Greco-Persian Wars interwoven with geography, ethnography and legend. Cicero called him "the Father of History", while critics since antiquity have also dubbed him "the Father of Lies" for his tall tales.

Life

Little is certain about Herodotus's life. Born into a prominent family in Halicarnassus, then under Persian rule, he may have been exiled for opposition to the tyrant Lygdamis and spent time on Samos. He travelled extensively — to Egypt, the Levant, Mesopotamia, Scythia, and around the Mediterranean — gathering material by inquiry (historia) from local informants. He later joined the Athenian colony at Thurii in southern Italy, where he probably died around 425 BC.

The Histories

Written in the Ionic dialect and later divided into nine books named for the Muses, the Histories opens by declaring its purpose: "so that human achievements may not become forgotten in time, and great and marvellous deeds ... may not be without their glory". The narrative centres on the expansion of the Persian Empire under Cyrus, Cambyses, Darius and Xerxes and its clashes with Greece, culminating in the battles of Marathon, Thermopylae, Salamis and Plataea. Within this frame Herodotus embeds famous digressions: the customs of Egypt (Book 2), the Scythians (Book 4), the story of Croesus and Solon, and the constitutional debate among Persian nobles.

Method and reception

Herodotus combined autopsy, hearsay and written sources, often flagging his doubts: "I am obliged to record the things I am told, but I am not obliged to believe them." Thucydides implicitly criticised his entertaining style, and Plutarch attacked his fairness in On the Malice of Herodotus. Modern scholarship, tested against archaeology, has rehabilitated much of his reporting, especially his ethnographic accounts, while acknowledging errors and tall stories. His work remains the foundational text of Western historiography and a primary source for the ancient world.
