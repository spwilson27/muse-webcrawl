Monocyte

Monocytes are a type of leukocyte (white blood cell) and the largest type, playing multiple roles in the immune system. They circulate in blood and migrate into tissues where they differentiate into macrophages and dendritic cells.

Monocyte
SystemImmune system
Develops intoMacrophages, dendritic cells
Normal share~2–10% of leukocytes

Function

Monocytes perform phagocytosis, antigen presentation, and cytokine production. Subsets are classically defined by CD14 and CD16 expression: classical, intermediate, and non-classical (patrolling) monocytes.

Clinical significance

Monocytosis (raised counts) occurs in chronic infection, inflammation and some leukaemias; monocytopenia can follow bone-marrow suppression. Monocyte-derived macrophages contribute to atherosclerotic plaques and tissue repair.

Development

They arise from haematopoietic stem cells in bone marrow and spend about one to three days in circulation before entering tissues.
