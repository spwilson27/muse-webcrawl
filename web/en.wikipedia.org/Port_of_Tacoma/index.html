Port of Tacoma

The Port of Tacoma is a major seaport in the United States, located in Tacoma, Washington, on Commencement Bay in south Puget Sound, about 30 miles (48 km) south-west of Seattle. Established by Pierce County voters in 1918 under Washington's port district laws, it occupies more than 2,400 acres (970 ha) of waterfront and tideflats, much of it on land reclaimed from the Puyallup River delta, and handles containerised cargo, automobiles, breakbulk, and bulk commodities.

Operations

Tacoma was a pioneer of intermodal container transport on the U.S. West Coast, developing unit-train service to the Midwest in the 1980s. Its terminals have been operated by major global terminal companies, and the port is a significant gateway for trade with Alaska, Asia, and for agricultural exports from the Pacific Northwest and Midwest. In 2015 the Port of Tacoma formed the Northwest Seaport Alliance with the Port of Seattle, a joint operating authority managing the two ports' marine cargo business as a single gateway — one of the largest container gateways in North America.

Environment and land

Much of the port's industrial land lies within the Commencement Bay Superfund site, where a century of smelting, refining, and wood-treatment left contaminated sediments and soil. Extensive cleanup and habitat restoration projects have been carried out in partnership with federal and state agencies and the Puyallup Tribe of Indians, whose treaty rights and reservation lands overlap the port area; the tribe is a major partner in port development following the 1989 Puyallup Land Claims Settlement.
