USS Constellation (1854)

USS Constellation

Constellation moored in Baltimore's Inner Harbor.

Launched: 26 August 1854
Commissioned: 28 July 1855
Status: Museum ship in Baltimore

USS Constellation is a sloop-of-war, the last sail-only warship designed and built by the United States Navy. She was built at the Gosport Shipyard in Portsmouth, Virginia, launched in 1854, and commissioned in 1855. Although long presented as a rebuild of the 1797 frigate Constellation, scholarship has established that she is a new ship constructed using some material from the earlier vessel.

Service

Constellation served with the Mediterranean Squadron and then, from 1859 to 1861, as flagship of the Africa Squadron, capturing slave ships including the slaver Cora. During the American Civil War she patrolled the Mediterranean to deter Confederate commerce raiders. Later used as a receiving and training ship, she was stationed at Newport and Annapolis and even carried food relief to Ireland in 1880.

Museum ship

Since 1955 the ship has been preserved in Baltimore's Inner Harbor, operated as part of Historic Ships in Baltimore. She is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and designated a National Historic Landmark.
