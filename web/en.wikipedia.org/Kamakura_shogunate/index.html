Kamakura shogunate

Kamakura shogunate
Period: 1192–1333 (conventionally)
Capital: Kamakura
First shogun: Minamoto no Yoritomo
Succeeded by: Kenmu Restoration

The Kamakura shogunate (鎌倉幕府) was the feudal military government of Japan from the late 12th century until 1333. Founded by Minamoto no Yoritomo after his victory in the Genpei War, and formally recognized when he received the title of shogun in 1192, it ruled from the city of Kamakura alongside the imperial court in Kyoto, inaugurating nearly seven centuries of warrior government in Japan.

Structure

Yoritomo bound warrior houses (gokenin) to him by grants of land stewardship and military service, creating offices such as the shugo (provincial constables) and jitō (estate stewards). After Yoritomo's death, power passed to his wife's family, the Hōjō clan, who ruled as regents (shikken) for puppet shoguns. The shogunate issued the Jōei Shikimoku (1232), a legal code for the warrior class.

Mongol invasions and fall

The shogunate organized Japan's defence against the Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281, both of which failed with the help of typhoons — the famous kamikaze. Victory brought no new lands to reward warriors, straining the regime's finances and loyalty. In 1333, forces loyal to Emperor Go-Daigo, led by Ashikaga Takauji and Nitta Yoshisada, destroyed the shogunate in the Kenmu Restoration.
