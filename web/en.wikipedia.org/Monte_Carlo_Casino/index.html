Monte Carlo Casino

Location: Monte Carlo, Monaco

Opening date: 1863 (current building 1879)

Architect: Charles Garnier

The Monte Carlo Casino, officially named Casino de Monte-Carlo, is a gambling and entertainment complex located in Monte Carlo, Monaco. It includes a casino, the Opéra de Monte-Carlo, and the office of Les Ballets de Monte-Carlo. The casino is owned and operated by the Société des Bains de Mer, a public company in which the Monaco government and the ruling princely family hold a majority interest.

History

Gambling was introduced to Monaco in the mid-19th century to rescue the House of Grimaldi from bankruptcy after the principality lost the tax revenues of Menton and Roquebrune. After early failures, businessman François Blanc took over the concession in 1863 and made the casino a success. The present building, in the Beaux-Arts style, was designed by Charles Garnier — architect of the Paris Opéra — and opened in 1879.

The casino offers roulette, trente et quarante, baccarat, blackjack, poker, and slot machines. Citizens of Monaco are forbidden to enter the gaming rooms, a rule dating to Princess Caroline, who wished to spare Monégasques the temptation while foreign visitors funded the state. The casino famously inspired the song "The Man Who Broke the Bank at Monte Carlo" and has appeared in several James Bond films, including GoldenEye.
