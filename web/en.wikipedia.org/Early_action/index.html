Early action (college admissions)

Early action
Type: College admission plan (United States)
Typical deadline: November 1 or November 15
Typical notification: Mid-December
Binding: No (except single-choice variants)

Early action (EA) is a type of early admission process offered by some colleges and universities in the United States. Students apply early — typically by November 1 or November 15 — and receive a decision by mid-December, earlier than the usual spring notification. Unlike early decision, early action is not binding: admitted students may wait until the national May 1 reply date to decide, and may apply early to other institutions.

Early action applicants learn their admission decisions in December.

Types

Most early action plans are non-restrictive, allowing students to submit early applications to multiple schools. A restrictive variant, often called restrictive early action (REA) or single-choice early action (SCEA), used by schools such as Harvard, Yale, Princeton and Stanford, prohibits applicants from applying early to other private colleges (with exceptions for public universities and rolling admissions) but remains non-binding.

Advantages and criticism

Early action lets students receive decisions sooner and may relieve stress, and admission rates in early pools are often higher than in regular decision, partly because early pools are self-selected. Critics note that early programmes can favour wealthier applicants who do not need to compare financial aid offers; some institutions, including the University of Virginia, abolished and later reinstated early plans in response to such concerns.
