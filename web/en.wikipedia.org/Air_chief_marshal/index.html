Air chief marshal (Air Chf Mshl or ACM) is a high-ranking air officer rank which originated in the Royal Air Force. The rank is used by the air forces of many countries that have historical British influence. In many Commonwealth air forces it is the second-highest active rank, equivalent to a full general in the army or an admiral in the navy, and senior to air marshal.

Usage

The rank is held by the chiefs of air staff of several countries, including India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria and Ghana. In the Royal Air Force, air chief marshal is a four-star rank held by the Chief of the Air Staff and other senior appointments. In the Indian Air Force, the Chief of the Air Staff holds the rank, and the honorary five-star rank of Marshal of the Indian Air Force has been held once, by Arjan Singh.

History

The rank was first used by the Royal Air Force, which adopted air force officer ranks in 1919. Hugh Trenchard, the first Chief of the Air Staff, was promoted to what became air chief marshal as the senior RAF rank structure settled during the 1920s.
