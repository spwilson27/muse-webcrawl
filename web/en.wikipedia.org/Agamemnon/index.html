In Greek mythology, Agamemnon was the king of Mycenae and the commander-in-chief of the Greek (Achaean) forces in the Trojan War. The son of Atreus and brother of Menelaus, he led the expedition to recover Helen, and on his triumphant return home he was murdered — in most versions by his wife Clytemnestra and her lover Aegisthus.

Mythology

Agamemnon's family was marked by the curse of the House of Atreus. Delayed at Aulis by adverse winds, he was told by the seer Calchas that he must sacrifice his daughter Iphigenia to Artemis — in some versions she was sacrificed, in others miraculously replaced by a deer. During the war, as told in Homer's Iliad, Agamemnon's seizure of the captive Briseis from Achilles provoked the hero's withdrawal from battle, with disastrous consequences for the Greeks; the two were eventually reconciled after the death of Patroclus.

Death and vengeance

Returning to Mycenae with the captive prophetess Cassandra, Agamemnon was killed in his bath (in Aeschylus' Agamemnon, the first play of the Oresteia) by Clytemnestra, who cited the sacrifice of Iphigenia and his infidelity. His son Orestes later avenged him by killing Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, and was pursued by the Furies until acquitted at Athens.

Archaeological echoes

Heinrich Schliemann, excavating at Mycenae in 1876, found a gold funerary mask he dubbed the "Mask of Agamemnon"; it dates to centuries before any plausible Trojan War but remains an icon of Mycenaean civilization.
