Bodhidharma

Bodhidharma was a semi-legendary Buddhist monk who lived during the 5th or 6th century CE. He is traditionally credited as the transmitter of Chan Buddhism to China, and regarded as its first Chinese patriarch. According to Chinese legend, he also began the physical training of the monks of Shaolin Monastery that led to the creation of Shaolin kung fu.

Life and legends

Early sources describe Bodhidharma as a monk from "the Western Regions" — often identified as a South Indian prince or a Persian-born teacher — who arrived in China by sea, landing in Guangzhou before travelling north. A famous legend tells of his audience with Emperor Wu of Liang, a lavish patron of Buddhism, in which Bodhidharma denied that the emperor's temple-building had earned any merit and answered the emperor's question "Who stands before me?" with "I don't know."

Wall-gazing and teaching

Tradition holds that Bodhidharma spent nine years meditating facing a wall at the Shaolin Monastery. He is credited with the saying that summarizes Chan: "A special transmission outside the scriptures; not founded upon words and letters; by pointing directly to the human mind, seeing one's nature and becoming a Buddha." He is said to have transmitted the robe and bowl, and the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra, to his disciple Huike, who legendarily cut off his own arm to prove his resolve.

Legacy

In Japan, where Chan became Zen, Bodhidharma is known as Daruma and is the model for the round, red Daruma dolls used as charms of perseverance and good luck. His historically obscure life has been overlaid by centuries of hagiography, making it difficult to separate fact from legend.
