Dreadlocks

Dreadlocks, also known as locs or dreads, are rope-like strands of hair formed by matting or braiding hair. The style has been worn for centuries by peoples in many parts of the world and is associated in modern times with the Rastafari movement, counterculture, and natural hair movements.

History

Some of the earliest depictions of dreadlocks date back thousands of years. Ancient Egyptian artifacts show Egyptians wearing locked hairstyles and wigs, and the style appears in Minoan art from Crete. In India, dreadlocks known as jata are worn by Hindu ascetics, particularly devotees of the god Shiva, who is himself depicted with matted locks. The style also has a long history among various African peoples, including the Maasai, Ashanti, and Himba.

In the 20th century, dreadlocks became strongly associated with Rastafari, a religious and social movement that developed in Jamaica in the 1930s. For Rastafari, locks are a symbol of devotion, a rejection of Babylon (the materialist Western world), and an expression of natural living; the name itself is said to derive from "dread" of God.

Formation

Dreadlocks can form naturally through neglect, a method sometimes called "freeform" or "organic," in which hair is washed but not combed, allowing it to mat over time. They can also be deliberately created using techniques such as backcombing, twist-and-rip, crochet hooking, or palm rolling. Hair of all textures can lock, though the time required varies; curly and coily hair tends to lock more readily than straight hair.

Cultural significance and debate

Dreadlocks carry religious meaning for Rastafari and for Hindu sadhus, and they are a point of cultural pride for many people of African descent. In recent decades the style has also been adopted by people outside these traditions, which has prompted debate about cultural appropriation. Legal and workplace disputes over dreadlocks have occurred in several countries, and some jurisdictions have enacted legislation prohibiting discrimination based on hairstyles associated with race.

Maintenance

Contrary to a common misconception, dreadlocks are typically washed regularly; cleanliness actually helps hair lock. Maintenance may include separating roots, palm rolling, and moisturising. Mature locks can be worn for many years and grow quite long.
