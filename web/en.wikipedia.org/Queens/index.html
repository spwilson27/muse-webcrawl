Queens

Queens is a borough of New York City, coextensive with Queens County, in the U.S. state of New York. Located at the western end of Long Island, it is the largest of the city's five boroughs by area and, with a population of about 2.4 million in the 2020 census, the second most populous after Brooklyn. Queens is often described as one of the most ethnically diverse urban areas in the world, with no single racial or ethnic group forming a majority.


The Unisphere, symbol of Queens

History

Queens County was established in 1683 as one of the original counties of the Province of New York and was named for Catherine of Braganza, queen consort of Charles II. The area was first settled by the Dutch and English in the 1640s; Flushing was the site of the 1657 Flushing Remonstrance, an early plea for religious freedom. The western part of the county voted to join New York City in 1898, forming the borough, while the eastern towns broke away in 1899 to form Nassau County. Growth accelerated with the Queensboro Bridge (1909) and the subway lines of the 1910s–1930s.

Geography and neighbourhoods

The borough ranges from dense urban districts — Long Island City, Astoria, Jackson Heights, Flushing and Jamaica — to suburban areas in the east and the oceanfront Rockaway Peninsula. Flushing Meadows–Corona Park, site of the 1939 and 1964 World's Fairs, contains the Unisphere, Citi Field and the USTA National Tennis Center, home of the US Open. Queens hosts New York's two metropolitan airports, JFK and LaGuardia.
