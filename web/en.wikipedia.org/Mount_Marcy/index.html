Mount Marcy is the highest point in the U.S. state of New York, with an elevation of 1,629 metres (5,343 feet). Located in the town of Keene in Essex County, within the High Peaks Wilderness Area of the Adirondack Park, it is the tallest of the 46 Adirondack High Peaks.

History

The mountain is named after William L. Marcy, governor of New York when the peak was first climbed and surveyed in 1837 by a party led by geologist Ebenezer Emmons, who gave it its name. In 1901 Vice President Theodore Roosevelt was hiking Mount Marcy when he received the news that President William McKinley was dying after being shot; Roosevelt's descent and dash to Buffalo became famous as his "midnight ride to the presidency".

Geography and hiking

Lake Tear of the Clouds, on the mountain's southwest flank, is the highest lake source of the Hudson River. The most popular ascent is the Van Hoevenberg Trail from Adirondack Loj, a round trip of about 23 km (14.6 mi). Alpine vegetation grows on the exposed summit, which offers panoramic views across the High Peaks.
