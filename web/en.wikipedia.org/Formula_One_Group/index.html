The Formula One Group is a group of companies responsible for the promotion of the FIA Formula One World Championship and the exercising of the sport's commercial rights. The group was previously owned by Delta Topco, a Jersey-based company controlled principally by CVC Capital Partners, before Liberty Media completed its acquisition in January 2017 for an enterprise value of about US$8 billion.

History

The group's origins lie in the commercial empire built by Bernie Ecclestone, who from the late 1970s, through the Formula One Constructors' Association (FOCA), secured the television and promotional rights to Grands Prix and turned Formula One into a global business. A succession of sales and refinancings followed: shares passed to Kirch Group, then to banks, and in 2006 to CVC Capital Partners. Ecclestone remained chief executive until Liberty Media's takeover, after which Chase Carey became chairman and CEO; Stefano Domenicali succeeded him as CEO in 2021.

Operations

The group earns revenue from race promotion (hosting) fees, broadcasting rights, sponsorship and hospitality (the Paddock Club). It also owns or controls the commercial side of the support series, including Formula 2 and Formula 3, and operates the F1 TV streaming service. The FIA remains the sport's regulator and retains ownership of the championship itself, leasing the commercial rights to the group under a 100-year agreement.
