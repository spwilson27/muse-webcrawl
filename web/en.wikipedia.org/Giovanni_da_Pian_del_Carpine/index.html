Giovanni da Pian del Carpine

Giovanni da Pian del Carpine (c. 1185 – 1 August 1252), also known as John of Plano Carpini, was an Italian Franciscan friar and explorer who was among the first Europeans to enter the lands of the Mongol Empire. His written account of his journey, the Ystoria Mongalorum ("History of the Mongols"), is one of the earliest European descriptions of Central and East Asia.

Life and mission

Born at Pian del Carpine (now Magione) near Perugia, Giovanni was one of the earliest companions of Saint Francis of Assisi and helped spread the Franciscan order in Germany and elsewhere in northern Europe, serving as a missionary, provincial, and diplomat.

In 1245, fearing further Mongol invasion after the devastating campaigns in Hungary and Poland, Pope Innocent IV sent Carpini — then in his sixties — as his envoy to the Great Khan. Travelling through Kyiv and across the Eurasian steppe, he reached the Mongol camp near Karakorum in time to witness the enthronement of Güyük Khan in August 1246. He delivered the Pope's letters calling on the Khan to cease attacks on Christians and accept baptism, and returned with Güyük's reply demanding the submission of the Pope and the rulers of Europe.

His account

Carpini's report describes Mongol customs, warfare, religion, food, and government in remarkable detail, based on observation and interpreters' help, and it remained a principal European source on the Mongols for centuries. He returned to the papal court in late 1247 and was later appointed Archbishop of Bar in Montenegro, where he died in 1252. His journey preceded the more famous travels of William of Rubruck and Marco Polo.
