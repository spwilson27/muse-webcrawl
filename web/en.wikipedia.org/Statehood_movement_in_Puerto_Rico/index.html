Statehood movement in Puerto Rico

The statehood movement in Puerto Rico advocates the admission of Puerto Rico, an unincorporated territory of the United States since 1898, as the 51st state of the union. The movement is principally represented by the New Progressive Party (Partido Nuevo Progresista, PNP), founded in 1967.

Background

Puerto Ricans were granted US citizenship by the Jones–Shafroth Act of 1917, but residents of the island cannot vote in presidential elections and are represented in Congress only by a non-voting Resident Commissioner. Since 1952 the island's official status has been that of a commonwealth (Estado Libre Asociado). Advocates of statehood argue it would bring equal voting rights, full federal program funding, and permanent status; opponents favor the commonwealth status, enhanced commonwealth proposals, or independence.

Status referendums

Puerto Rico has held several non-binding status plebiscites, in 1967, 1993, 1998, 2012, 2017, 2020, and 2024. Statehood won pluralities or majorities in several of them, including 52.5 percent "yes" in the 2020 yes-or-no referendum, though turnout disputes and boycotts have complicated interpretation. The admission of a new state ultimately requires an act of the US Congress, which has debated bills such as the Puerto Rico Status Act without enacting them.

Arguments

Supporters cite democratic equality and economic benefits; critics raise questions about language and culture, federal taxation, and the island's fiscal situation. Opinion among US mainland politicians has been divided along shifting partisan lines.
