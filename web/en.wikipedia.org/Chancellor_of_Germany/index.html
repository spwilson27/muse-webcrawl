The chancellor of Germany, officially the Federal Chancellor of the Federal Republic of Germany (German: Bundeskanzler der Bundesrepublik Deutschland), is the head of government of Germany and the commander-in-chief of the German Armed Forces in wartime. The chancellor is elected by the Bundestag (federal parliament) on the proposal of the federal president and is the most powerful figure in German politics.

Role

Under the Basic Law (Grundgesetz) of 1949, the chancellor appoints and dismisses federal ministers, determines the general guidelines of government policy (Richtlinienkompetenz), and can be removed by parliament only through a constructive vote of no confidence, which simultaneously elects a successor — a mechanism designed to prevent the instability of the Weimar era. The office traces its lineage to the imperial chancellorship created by Otto von Bismarck in 1871.

Notable chancellors

Konrad Adenauer (1949–1963) led West Germany's postwar recovery and Western integration; Willy Brandt (1969–1974) pursued Ostpolitik and won the Nobel Peace Prize; Helmut Kohl (1982–1998) presided over German reunification; and Angela Merkel (2005–2021), the first woman to hold the office, served four terms. She was succeeded by Olaf Scholz in December 2021.
