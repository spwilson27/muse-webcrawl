Robert Carhart Merton (born 31 July 1944) is an American economist, Nobel Memorial Prize laureate in Economic Sciences, and a professor at the MIT Sloan School of Management, known for his pioneering contributions to continuous-time finance. He is the son of sociologist Robert K. Merton.

Work

In 1973 Merton published "Theory of Rational Option Pricing", extending the work of Fischer Black and Myron Scholes on the pricing of stock options; the resulting Black–Scholes–Merton model transformed the trading of derivatives. In 1997 Merton and Scholes received the Nobel Memorial Prize for developing a new method to determine the value of derivatives. Merton also developed the intertemporal capital asset pricing model (ICAPM) and a widely used structural model of corporate credit risk known as the Merton model.

Long-Term Capital Management

Merton was a founding partner of the hedge fund Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM), established in 1994. After spectacular early returns, the fund lost $4.6 billion in 1998 following the Russian financial crisis and was bailed out by a consortium of banks arranged by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. Merton later founded Dimensional Fund Advisors' integrated retirement research efforts and has worked on lifecycle finance and retirement-income design.

Education

Merton received a BS in engineering mathematics from Columbia University in 1966, an MS in applied mathematics from Caltech in 1967, and a PhD in economics from MIT in 1970 under Paul Samuelson.
