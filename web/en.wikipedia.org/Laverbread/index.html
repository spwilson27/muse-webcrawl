Laverbread

Laverbread (Welsh: bara lafwr or bara lawr) is a traditional Welsh delicacy made from laver, an edible seaweed (purple laver, Porphyra) collected on the South Wales coast, especially the Gower Peninsula and Pembrokeshire. Despite the name it is not bread: the seaweed is washed, boiled for hours to a dark green purée, and sold fresh or canned.

Use

Laverbread is classically rolled in oatmeal and fried, served with bacon and cockles as part of a traditional Welsh breakfast — a combination Richard Burton famously called "Welshman's caviar", a phrase later championed by producers. It is rich in minerals and iodine, and has EU protected status as "Welsh Laverbread".

Harvest

Historically gathered by women at low tide around Penclawdd and processed in local factories, laver remains associated with Swansea Market, where it is still sold by the pound.
