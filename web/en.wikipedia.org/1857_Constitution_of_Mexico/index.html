1857 Constitution of Mexico

1857 Constitution
Ratified: 5 February 1857
Location: Mexico City
Authors: Constituent Congress of 1856
Superseded: 1824 Constitution (restored 1846)
Succeeded by: 1917 Constitution

The Federal Constitution of the United Mexican States of 1857 was a liberal constitution drafted by the Constituent Congress of 1856 and promulgated on 5 February 1857 under President Ignacio Comonfort. It was the legal centrepiece of La Reforma, the liberal reform movement led by Benito Juárez and others.

Provisions

The constitution established a federal republic, guaranteed individual rights including freedom of speech, press and assembly, abolished special legal privileges (fueros) for the clergy and military, prohibited the Church from owning most property, and mandated free secular primary education. It incorporated the Reform Laws, including the Ley Juárez and Ley Lerdo.

Consequences

Conservative opposition led Comonfort to join the Plan of Tacubaya repudiating the constitution, triggering the Reform War (1858–1861), which the liberals won. The constitution, amended over time, formally remained in force (with interruption during the French intervention and Second Mexican Empire) until replaced by the Constitution of 1917.
