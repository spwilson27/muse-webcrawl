Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze

Established1563 (as Accademia e Compagnia delle Arti del Disegno); 1784 (as academy of fine arts)
LocationFlorence, Tuscany, Italy
TypePublic art academy

The Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze ("Academy of Fine Arts of Florence") is an instructional art academy in Florence, Italy. It descends from the Accademia e Compagnia delle Arti del Disegno founded in 1563 by Cosimo I de' Medici under the influence of Giorgio Vasari — the first academy of drawing in Europe — and was reconstituted in 1784 by Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo as an academy of fine arts open to students.

History

The original academy of 1563 united Florence's artists, previously organised under the guild of Saint Luke, and counted Michelangelo among its first honorary members. In 1784, Pietro Leopoldo merged the city's art schools and established a gallery of old masters' works for students to study — the origin of the Galleria dell'Accademia, which today is a separate museum famous for Michelangelo's David.

Today

The academy occupies former convent buildings near Piazza San Marco and offers degree courses in painting, sculpture, decoration, graphic arts, scenography and related fields under the Italian AFAM system of higher artistic education. Notable past students and teachers include sculptors and painters central to Italian art of the 19th and 20th centuries.
