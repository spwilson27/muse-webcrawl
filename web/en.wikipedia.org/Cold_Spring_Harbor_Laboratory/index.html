Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory

Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory (CSHL) is a private, non-profit research institution in Laurel Hollow, New York, on Long Island. Founded in 1890, it is one of the world's leading centres for research in molecular biology, genetics, cancer, neuroscience, and plant biology.

History

The laboratory began as a biological field station associated with the Brooklyn Institute of Arts and Sciences and hosted the Station for Experimental Evolution of the Carnegie Institution. Barbara McClintock did her pioneering work on transposons there, and James Watson served as director from 1968, shaping the institution's focus on molecular biology and cancer research.

Research and meetings

CSHL is famous for its scientific meetings and courses, including the annual Symposium on Quantitative Biology, begun in 1933, and the Phage Course that helped found molecular biology. Its researchers have been associated with multiple Nobel Prizes. The laboratory also runs a graduate school, a press (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory Press), and extensive education outreach.
