Invasion of Normandy

The invasion of Normandy, codenamed Operation Neptune, was the Allied amphibious landing and associated airborne operations on Tuesday, 6 June 1944 — D-Day — at the start of Operation Overlord, the invasion of German-occupied northwestern Europe during the Second World War. It remains the largest seaborne invasion in history.

Preparations

The Allies, under Supreme Commander Dwight D. Eisenhower, assembled over 150,000 troops for the initial assault. An elaborate deception campaign, Operation Fortitude, convinced German commanders that the main landing would come at the Pas-de-Calais. Artificial Mulberry harbours and the PLUTO fuel pipeline were prepared to supply the beachhead, while the French Resistance and Allied air forces attacked rail and road networks across France.

D-Day

Shortly after midnight, over 24,000 airborne troops of the American 82nd and 101st and the British 6th Airborne Division landed to secure the flanks. At dawn, after heavy naval and air bombardment, infantry landed on five beaches — Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno, and Sword. Resistance was fiercest at Omaha, where American forces suffered heavy casualties before breaking through. By nightfall the Allies had landed about 156,000 troops and established lodgements on all five beaches, at a cost of at least 10,000 casualties, including 4,414 confirmed dead.

Breakout and aftermath

Over the following weeks the Allies built up over two million men in Normandy. Cherbourg fell in late June; Caen was captured in July after bitter fighting. Operation Cobra in late July broke the German line, leading to the encirclement of German forces in the Falaise pocket in August and the liberation of Paris on 25 August. The campaign opened the long-awaited second front and hastened the defeat of Nazi Germany.
