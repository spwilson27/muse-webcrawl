Corsican Republic

The Corsican Republic (1755–1769) was a state formed by Pasquale Paoli after leading a revolt against the Republic of Genoa. It is noted for its 1755 constitution, written in Italian, one of the first modern democratic constitutions.

Government

The constitution established a Diet elected by male citizens over 25, an executive Council of State, and separation of powers influenced by Enlightenment thought; it drew praise from Jean-Jacques Rousseau, who wrote a constitutional project for Corsica.

End

Genoa ceded its claims to France in the Treaty of Versailles (1768). French forces defeated Paoli at Ponte Novu in 1769, and Corsica became French — the year Napoleon was born in Ajaccio.
