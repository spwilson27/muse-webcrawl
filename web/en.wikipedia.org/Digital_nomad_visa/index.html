Digital nomad visa

A digital nomad visa is a type of visa or residence permit that allows a person to live in a foreign country while working remotely for an employer or clients based outside that country. Such programmes are aimed at digital nomads — people who use telecommunications technologies to earn a living while moving between locations — who would otherwise have to rely on tourist visas that prohibit work, or make repeated "visa runs".

History

Before dedicated programmes existed, remote workers commonly stayed in countries on tourist visas, a practice of uncertain legality since most tourist statuses forbid employment of any kind, even for foreign employers. Estonia is widely credited with launching the first formal digital nomad visa in 2020, building on its earlier e-Residency programme. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated adoption: as tourism collapsed and remote work became widespread, dozens of countries — including Barbados, Bermuda, Costa Rica, Croatia, Portugal, Spain, Thailand, Indonesia (Bali), Brazil and the United Arab Emirates — introduced or expanded schemes to attract long-stay, high-spending remote workers.

Typical requirements

Most programmes require applicants to demonstrate employment or self-employment with income sourced outside the host country, above a minimum monthly threshold that varies from roughly US$1,500 to more than US$5,000 depending on the destination. Other common conditions include valid health insurance, a clean criminal record, proof of accommodation, and payment of an application fee. Permits commonly run for six months to two years, are sometimes renewable, and may or may not lead to permanent residence or count toward citizenship. Tax treatment differs significantly: some countries exempt visa holders from local income tax, while others tax residents on worldwide income.

Notable programmes

  • Estonia — Digital Nomad Visa (2020) for location-independent workers.
  • Portugal — remote work visa under its residence visa framework, succeeding the earlier D7 passive-income route used by many nomads.
  • Spain — international telework visa introduced under the 2023 Startups Law.
  • Barbados — 12-month Welcome Stamp.
  • Costa Rica — Rentista/remote worker visa with local tax exemption on foreign income.
  • Thailand — Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa category including "Work-from-Thailand Professionals".

Criticism

Critics argue that nomad visa schemes can contribute to rising rents and gentrification in popular destinations such as Lisbon, Mexico City and Bali, pricing out local residents. Supporters counter that remote workers bring foreign income into local economies without competing for local jobs.
