The 2000 UEFA European Football Championship, also referred to as UEFA Euro 2000, was the 11th UEFA European Championship, a football tournament held for the national teams of UEFA member associations. It was held in Belgium and the Netherlands, the first time the tournament had been co-hosted, from 10 June to 2 July 2000.

Tournament summary

Sixteen teams competed. Spain dramatically beat Yugoslavia 4–3 in the group stage; the Netherlands thrashed Yugoslavia 6–1 in the quarter-finals but lost on penalties to Italy in the semi-finals. In the final in Rotterdam, Italy led France through Marco Delvecchio's goal until Sylvain Wiltord equalized in stoppage time; David Trezeguet then scored a golden goal in extra time to give France a 2–1 victory, making France the first team to hold the World Cup and European Championship simultaneously since West Germany in the 1970s.

Legacy

The tournament is often remembered as one of the highest-quality European Championships, notable for attacking football and dramatic late goals.
