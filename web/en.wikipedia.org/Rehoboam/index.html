Rehoboam

Rehoboam (Hebrew: רְחַבְעָם‎, Rəḥaḇʿām) was, according to the Hebrew Bible, the first king of the Kingdom of Judah. He was the son and successor of Solomon and the grandson of David. In the biblical account, his reign saw the division of the united Kingdom of Israel into the northern Kingdom of Israel, under Jeroboam, and the southern Kingdom of Judah.

Reign: c. 931 – 913 BCE (traditional)
Predecessor: Solomon
Successor: Abijah
Father: Solomon
Mother: Naamah the Ammonite

Accession and the schism

According to 1 Kings 12 and 2 Chronicles 10, after Solomon's death the northern tribes assembled at Shechem and asked Rehoboam to lighten the heavy labour and taxation imposed by his father. The older counsellors advised concession; the young men of Rehoboam's court advised harshness. Rehoboam answered, "My father made your yoke heavy — I will make it even heavier; my father scourged you with whips — I will scourge you with scorpions." The ten northern tribes revolted under Jeroboam son of Nebat, leaving Rehoboam only Judah (with Benjamin) in the south. The biblical text presents this as fulfilment of the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite and as punishment for Solomon's idolatry.

Reign in Judah

The books of Kings and Chronicles describe Rehoboam fortifying Judaean towns and, in the fifth year of his reign, suffering an invasion by Shishak (Shoshenq I) of Egypt, who carried off treasures from the Temple and palace — an episode also recorded in Shoshenq's inscription at Karnak. Chronicles portrays Rehoboam as initially Torah-observant, then unfaithful, then humbled. He reigned seventeen years and was succeeded by his son Abijah.
