Record label

A record label, or record company, is a brand or trademark of music recordings and music videos, or the company that owns it. Sometimes, a record label is also a publishing company that manages such brands and trademarks, coordinates the production, manufacture, distribution, marketing, promotion, and enforcement of copyright for sound recordings and music videos, while also conducting talent scouting and development of new artists.

Major and independent labels

The industry is dominated by three "major" groups — Universal Music Group, Sony Music Entertainment and Warner Music Group — which together control the majority of the global market. Independent labels operate outside these groups, often focusing on particular genres or scenes, and typically rely on independent distributors.

Functions

Labels sign artists to contracts, fund recording sessions, and handle A&R (artists and repertoire). In the streaming era, label services companies offer distribution and marketing without traditional ownership of masters.

History

The term derives from the circular label in the centre of a vinyl record. Early labels such as Columbia and His Master's Voice grew with the phonograph industry in the early 20th century.
