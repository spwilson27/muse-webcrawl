The Canal & River Trust (Welsh: Glandŵr Cymru) is a charitable trust responsible for the canals and navigable rivers of England and Wales. It was established in 2012, taking over the functions of the publicly owned British Waterways in what was one of the largest transfers of public assets to the charitable sector in British history. The Trust cares for around 2,000 miles (3,200 km) of canals, rivers, reservoirs, and docks, together with thousands of locks, bridges, aqueducts, and historic structures.

Role and network

The network includes famous waterways such as the Grand Union, Kennet and Avon, Llangollen, Leeds and Liverpool, and Regent's canals, and landmarks including the Pontcysyllte Aqueduct (part of a World Heritage Site), the Anderton Boat Lift, and the Caen Hill lock flight. The Trust licenses boats, maintains towpaths used by millions of walkers and cyclists each year, supplies water, and manages heritage and wildlife along the corridors. Its responsibilities in Scotland are handled by the separate Scottish Canals.

Funding and challenges

The Trust's income combines a long-term government grant settlement, boat licence fees, moorings, property income, utilities, and charitable fundraising. It has repeatedly warned that the ageing infrastructure — much of it 200 years old — faces growing maintenance costs, storm damage, and funding shortfalls, and it campaigns for continued public investment in the network. Volunteers contribute hundreds of thousands of hours annually as lock keepers, rangers, and restoration workers.
