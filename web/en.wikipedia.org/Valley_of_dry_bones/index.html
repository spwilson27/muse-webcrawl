Source: Book of Ezekiel, chapter 37
Genre: Biblical vision / prophetic oracle
Themes: resurrection, restoration, hope in exile

The Valley of Dry Bones is a vision in the Book of Ezekiel (chapter 37) of the Hebrew Bible. The prophet Ezekiel is carried in a vision to a valley full of very dry bones and is asked by God, "Can these bones live?" Ezekiel answers, "O Lord God, You know." He is commanded to prophesy to the bones.

Ezekiel prophesying over the valley of dry bones.

The vision

At Ezekiel's word the bones rattle together, bone to bone; sinews and flesh cover them, and breath (ruach) enters them, and they stand "an exceedingly great army". God explains: "these bones are the whole house of Israel. They say, 'Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost; we are indeed cut off.' ... I will open your graves and raise you from your graves, O my people."

Interpretation

  • Historical: addressed to the Jewish exiles in Babylon, promising national restoration and return.
  • Eschatological: later Jewish and Christian readers read it as a prophecy of bodily resurrection.
  • Liturgical: the passage is read in synagogues on the Sabbath during Passover; in Christianity it is associated with Easter and Pentecost themes.

Cultural influence

The image has inspired countless artworks, the spiritual "Dem Dry Bones" (Ezekiel connected dem dry bones), and speeches about revival and hope, notably during the Holocaust and the founding of the State of Israel.
