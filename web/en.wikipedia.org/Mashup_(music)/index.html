Mashup (music)

Song created by blending two or more pre-recorded tracks

A mashup (also called a blend, bastard pop, or bootleg in the United Kingdom) is a musical work created by blending two or more pre-existing songs, typically by superimposing the vocal track of one over the instrumental track of another, usually in a compatible key and tempo. Mashups are a form of musical collage and are often produced by DJs and bedroom producers without the permission of the original artists, which has raised persistent copyright questions.

History

The practice has roots in the medleys of earlier pop, the dub and hip hop traditions of remixing, and experimental works such as John Oswald's "plunderphonics" recordings of the 1980s. The genre exploded in the early 2000s with inexpensive audio software: 2 Many DJs' album As Heard on Radio Soulwax Pt. 2 (2002) and Freelance Hellraiser's "A Stroke of Genius" — Christina Aguilera's "Genie in a Bottle" over the Strokes — became emblematic. Danger Mouse's The Grey Album (2004), which combined the Beatles' "White Album" with Jay-Z's The Black Album, brought mashups wide attention, as did Girl Talk's dense sample-collage albums later in the decade.

Technique and legality

Producers use time-stretching and pitch-shifting to align tempo and key, and isolate vocals or instrumentals from mixed recordings. Because mashups ordinarily use uncleared samples, they circulate largely through informal channels; some acts have since released fully licensed mashup albums.
