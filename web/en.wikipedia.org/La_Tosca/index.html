AuthorVictorien Sardou

GenreMelodrama (play)

La Tosca is a French historical melodrama in five acts by Victorien Sardou, first performed in Paris on 24 November 1887 with Sarah Bernhardt in the title role. It is best known today as the source of Puccini's opera Tosca (1900).

Plot

Set in Rome in June 1800 during the Napoleonic Wars, the play follows the singer Floria Tosca, her lover the painter Mario Cavaradossi, and the sinister police chief Baron Scarpia. When Cavaradossi shelters the escaped political prisoner Cesare Angelotti, Scarpia has him arrested and tortured; Tosca, forced to reveal the hiding place, bargains for her lover's life, kills Scarpia, and is finally driven to suicide when the promised mock execution proves real.

Reception

The play was a popular success on tour across Europe and America despite hostile reviews — George Bernard Shaw dismissed Sardou's melodramas as "Sardoodledom". Its tight construction and famous torture scene made it a vehicle for leading actresses.

Adaptations

Puccini's opera, with a libretto by Illica and Giacosa, condensed Sardou's five acts into three and remains a repertory staple. The play also inspired films, including a 1941 Italian version completed with music drawn from the opera.
