Locus Award

The Locus Awards are annual literary awards presented by the science fiction and fantasy magazine Locus. First given in 1971, they are voted on by readers of the magazine and other fans through the Locus Poll, and are presented at an annual banquet, in recent years held in Seattle, Washington.

Categories

The awards honour work from the preceding year in categories that have evolved over time, including Best Novel (split into science fiction and fantasy categories since 1980), Best Novella, Best Novelette, Best Short Story, Best First Novel, Best Young Adult Book, Best Collection, Best Anthology, Best Non-fiction and Best Magazine, Publisher and Editor categories. Works are first surveyed through the magazine's recommended reading lists and year-in-review coverage, which inform the poll ballot.

Significance

Alongside the Hugo Award and the Nebula Award, the Locus Awards are among the most prominent honours in speculative fiction. Because they are voted by a readership overlapping with but broader than convention attendees or professional writers, they are sometimes taken as a middle measure of acclaim between the fan-voted Hugos and the peer-voted Nebulas. Authors such as Ursula K. Le Guin, Connie Willis, China Miéville and Neil Gaiman have won in multiple categories.
