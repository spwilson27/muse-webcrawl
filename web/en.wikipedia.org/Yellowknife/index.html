Yellowknife is the capital, largest community and only city in the Northwest Territories of Canada. It lies on the northern shore of Great Slave Lake, about 400 kilometres south of the Arctic Circle, on the traditional territory of the Yellowknives Dene First Nation, from whom the city takes its name (referring to copper-bladed knives).

History

A trading post operated in the area from the early 19th century, but the modern city began with a gold rush after rich gold deposits were confirmed in 1934–35. The Con Mine and Giant Mine became major producers; Giant Mine in particular operated until 2004 and left a notorious legacy of arsenic trioxide stored underground. Yellowknife became the territorial capital in 1967, when the seat of government moved from Ottawa — a status that brought rapid growth in public administration.

Economy

Government is now the largest employer, followed by services supporting the territory's diamond mines, which opened northeast of the city in the 1990s — Canada's first diamond mines — with ore and supplies staged through Yellowknife. Tourism, including aurora viewing in winter, is also significant.

Geography and climate

The city sits on the Canadian Shield amid lakes, rock outcrops and boreal forest, including a distinctive community of houseboats on Yellowknife Bay. It has a subarctic climate with very cold, dark winters and mild, bright summers. In the summer of 2023, essentially the entire population was evacuated by road and air as a wildfire approached the city; residents were able to return after several weeks.

Transport

Yellowknife is linked south by the all-weather Mackenzie Highway system via the Deh Cho Bridge over the Mackenzie River, and in winter by ice roads, famously including the Tibbitt to Contwoyto route supplying the diamond mines. Its airport is a hub for northern aviation.
