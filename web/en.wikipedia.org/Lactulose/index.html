Lactulose

Lactulose
FormulaC₁₂H₂₂O₁₁
TypeSynthetic disaccharide; osmotic laxative
RoutesOral, rectal

Lactulose is a non-absorbable synthetic disaccharide made of the simple sugars fructose and galactose. It is used in the treatment of constipation and hepatic encephalopathy. It is not found naturally, was first described in 1929, and has been used medically since the 1950s. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

For constipation, lactulose is taken orally; it is not broken down in the stomach or small intestine but is fermented by bacteria in the colon, drawing water into the bowel by osmosis and softening the stool. For hepatic encephalopathy, acidification of the colon converts ammonia (NH₃) into ammonium (NH₄⁺), which cannot be absorbed, thereby reducing blood ammonia levels.

Side effects

Common side effects include abdominal bloating, flatulence, cramps, and diarrhoea. Excessive doses can cause electrolyte disturbances. Lactulose is generally considered safe in pregnancy.
