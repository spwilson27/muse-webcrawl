The United States Capitol, often called the Capitol Building, is the seat of the United States Congress, the legislative branch of the federal government. It is located on Capitol Hill at the eastern end of the National Mall in Washington, D.C.

History

President George Washington laid the cornerstone on 18 September 1793. The original building, designed by Dr. William Thornton, was completed in 1800. British troops burned the Capitol in 1814 during the War of 1812; it was rebuilt under Benjamin Henry Latrobe and Charles Bulfinch. The present cast-iron dome, designed by Thomas U. Walter, replaced the earlier low dome during the 1850s–1860s, and the Senate and House wings were doubled in size. The Statue of Freedom was placed atop the dome in December 1863, during the Civil War.

Architecture and art

The building is a leading example of American neoclassicism. Beneath the dome lies the Rotunda, decorated with large historical paintings and Constantino Brumidi's fresco The Apotheosis of Washington in the canopy of the dome. The National Statuary Hall, the former House chamber, displays statues contributed by the states. The Capitol Visitor Center, a large underground expansion, opened in 2008.

Security and events

The Capitol is protected by the United States Capitol Police. It hosts presidential inaugurations, lying in state ceremonies, and joint sessions of Congress. On 6 January 2021 the building was attacked by a mob seeking to overturn the 2020 presidential election certification; see War in Donbas for a contemporaneous conflict of the same decade.
