Soyuz 19

Soyuz 19 was a July 1975 crewed Soviet spaceflight that carried cosmonauts Alexei Leonov and Valery Kubasov. It was the Soviet half of the Apollo–Soyuz Test Project, the first joint space mission between the Soviet Union and the United States, and the first international docking in space.

Mission

Soyuz 19 launched from Baikonur on 15 July 1975, hours before the American Apollo spacecraft launched from Florida carrying Thomas Stafford, Vance Brand, and Deke Slayton. On 17 July the two craft docked in orbit over the Atlantic; Stafford and Leonov shook hands through the open hatch in one of the Cold War's most symbolic moments of détente. The crews spent about two days docked, conducting joint experiments and ceremonies, before separating. Soyuz 19 landed near Arkalyk on 21 July after a flight of five days, 22 hours — the United States would not fly a crewed mission again until the Space Shuttle in 1981.

Legacy

The mission demonstrated compatible docking and rescue procedures and pioneered the international cooperation that later produced Shuttle–Mir and the International Space Station partnership. It was the final flight of the original Soyuz 7K-T generation in this configuration and Leonov's second and last spaceflight.
