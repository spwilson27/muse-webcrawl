Inn

TypeLodging establishment
ServicesFood, drink, lodging, stabling (historically)
RelatedCoaching inn, innkeeper, public house, ryokan, caravanserai

An inn is an establishment that provides food, drink, and lodging for travellers. Historically, inns also provided stabling and fodder for guests' horses. Inns were a vital part of the infrastructure of road travel from antiquity through the age of the horse-drawn coach, and the word survives today in the names of pubs, small hotels, and motel chains, though modern "inns" may offer little beyond accommodation.

History

Inns existed across the ancient world — Roman mansiones served official travellers along imperial roads, and caravanserais sheltered merchants along the Silk Road. In medieval Europe, monasteries and alehouses gradually gave way to purpose-built inns serving pilgrims, merchants, and royal messengers. The great age of the coaching inn came in the 17th–19th centuries in Britain and Europe, when scheduled stagecoaches and mail coaches changed horses at inns spaced along the turnpike roads; establishments such as London's great coaching inns combined yards, stables, dining rooms, and bedrooms around a courtyard. In Japan, the ryokan developed as the traditional inn serving travellers on the great highways such as the Tōkaidō.

The railway age and after

The coming of the railways in the 19th century destroyed the coaching trade almost overnight, and many inns declined into public houses or disappeared. In the 20th century the role of roadside lodging passed to motels and hotel chains — several of which, such as Holiday Inn, retained the old word. In English law, common-law innkeepers historically bore strict duties toward their guests and their property, and "innkeeper" remains a legal term in some jurisdictions. In London, the Inns of Court are an unrelated survival of the word, denoting the professional societies of barristers housed in former hostelries.

In literature

Inns are classic literary settings: Chaucer's pilgrims meet at the Tabard Inn in Southwark; the Admiral Benbow opens Treasure Island; and the Prancing Pony at Bree figures in The Lord of the Rings.
