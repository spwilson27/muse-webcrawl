Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio

Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio (Chinese: Liaozhai zhiyi, "Strange Tales from the Liaozhai Studio") is a collection of nearly five hundred supernatural and fantastic stories written in Classical Chinese by Pu Songling (1640–1715) during the early Qing dynasty. It is one of the most celebrated works of Chinese fiction.

Contents

The tales feature fox spirits, ghosts, demons, gods and enchanted beings interacting with scholars, officials and merchants. Famous stories include "The Painted Skin", in which a demon disguises itself as a beautiful woman; "Nie Xiaoqian", about a young man who falls in love with a ghost; and numerous tales of fox maidens who are often kinder and cleverer than the humans they meet. Drawing on the classical zhiguai ("records of the strange") tradition, Pu polished the tales in elegant literary language and frequently appended his own commentaries, satirising corrupt officials and the examination system that had repeatedly frustrated his own ambitions.

Publication and influence

Pu circulated the stories in manuscript during his lifetime; the collection was first printed in 1766, decades after his death. It has been continually adapted into opera, film and television — notably the Hong Kong film A Chinese Ghost Story (1987), based on "Nie Xiaoqian". English translations include Herbert Giles's Strange Stories from a Chinese Studio (1880) and later selections by John Minford and others.
