Immunostaining

Immunostaining is any use of an antibody-based method to detect a specific protein or antigen in a sample. It encompasses immunohistochemistry (IHC) on tissue sections, immunocytochemistry (ICC) on cultured cells, immunofluorescence, ELISA, and immunoblotting (western blot).

Principle

A primary antibody binds the target antigen; detection uses a label attached directly to the antibody or to a secondary antibody. Labels include enzymes such as horseradish peroxidase, which produce a coloured precipitate with substrates like DAB, and fluorophores for fluorescence detection.

Procedure

Typical steps are fixation, antigen retrieval, blocking to reduce non-specific binding, antibody incubation, washing, detection, and counterstaining (often with haematoxylin). Protocol details vary with sample type and target.

Applications

Immunostaining is a mainstay of diagnostic pathology — typing tumours and identifying infectious agents — and of basic research mapping protein expression and localisation.
