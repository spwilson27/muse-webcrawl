Royal Naval Air Service

The Royal Naval Air Service (RNAS) was the air arm of the Royal Navy, under the direction of the Admiralty's Air Department, between 1 July 1914 and 1 April 1918, when it was merged with the British Army's Royal Flying Corps to form the Royal Air Force, the world's first independent air force. Although short-lived, the RNAS pioneered many aspects of naval aviation, including the first aircraft carrier operations, torpedo bombing, and strategic airship patrols.

Origins

Naval aviation began with the Naval Wing of the Royal Flying Corps, formed in 1912. On the outbreak of the First World War the Admiralty took direct control of naval flying, and the RNAS came into formal existence under Commander Charles Rumney Samson, one of Britain's pioneer naval aviators. Its tasks included fleet reconnaissance, coastal patrol and anti-submarine work, attacking Zeppelins, and bombing enemy bases.

Operations

The RNAS undertook some of the earliest strategic bombing raids of the war, attacking Zeppelin sheds at Düsseldorf, Cologne, and Friedrichshafen in 1914. RNAS armoured cars served ashore in France and elsewhere. Seaplane carriers such as HMS Engadine took part in the Battle of Jutland in 1916, and HMS Furious launched the first carrier air strike — the Tondern raid of July 1918 was conducted by the successor RAF. Naval squadrons also operated fighters on the Western Front, where Raymond Collishaw and the Canadian "Black Flight" of No. 10 (Naval) Squadron became famous. RNAS airships and aeroplanes flew extensive anti-submarine patrols in the war's later years.

Merger and legacy

The creation of the RAF in April 1918 absorbed the RNAS's roughly 55,000 personnel and 2,900 aircraft. Naval aviation passed out of naval control until the Fleet Air Arm was formed in 1924 and returned to the Admiralty in 1937. The RNAS is remembered for its technical innovation — among its serving officers and designers were pioneers of the aircraft carrier, and the Sopwith Aviation Company built many of its best-known aircraft.
