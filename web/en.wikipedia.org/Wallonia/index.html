Wallonia (French: Wallonie) is one of the three regions of Belgium, occupying the southern, predominantly French-speaking part of the country. It has its own parliament and government seated in Namur and covers about 55% of Belgium's territory with roughly a third of its population.

Geography and economy

The region stretches from the industrial valleys of the Sambre and Meuse — the sillon industriel through Charleroi, Namur, and Liège — to the forested Ardennes in the south-east. Wallonia was one of continental Europe's first industrial heartlands, built on coal, steel, and glass, and suffered severe decline as those industries contracted in the later 20th century. Today's economy centres on services, logistics, biotechnology, and aerospace around Liège, Charleroi, and Walloon Brabant.

Politics and identity

Wallonia was formally created in the Belgian state reforms of 1970–1993 that federalised the country. Politics has long been dominated by the Socialist Party (PS). The Walloon movement promotes regional identity, symbolised by the bold rooster (coq hardi) on its red-and-yellow flag; the French Community (Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles) separately handles French-language education and culture across Wallonia and Brussels. A German-speaking community of about 78,000 lives in the east around Eupen.

History

Historically divided among the Prince-Bishopric of Liège, the Austrian Netherlands, and other states, the area was unified within Belgium after the 1830 revolution. Its heavy industry made Wallonia the country's economic engine in the 19th century before the balance shifted to Flanders after the Second World War.
