The Ranfurly Shield, colloquially known as the "Log o' Wood," is a trophy in New Zealand rugby union and one of the most coveted prizes in the country's domestic game. Unlike a league or knockout cup, the Shield is contested on a challenge basis: the holder must defend it in designated home matches, and any challenger that wins takes the Shield home.

History

The Shield was donated in 1901 by Lord Ranfurly, the Governor of New Zealand, and first contested in 1904, when Auckland — awarded the Shield for the best provincial record — successfully defended it. The wooden trophy, originally intended as a football trophy and modified with a rugby scene at its centre, is ringed with small shields recording each holder.

Famous tenures

Long defences ("eras") are celebrated in New Zealand rugby folklore. Auckland's 1985–1993 tenure of 61 successful defences is the record; Canterbury, Wellington, Waikato and Southland have also held celebrated eras. The Shield's winner-takes-all format, in which a small union can seize the trophy with one upset victory, gives it a romance unmatched by other domestic competitions, and Shield challenges are held during the National Provincial Championship season.
