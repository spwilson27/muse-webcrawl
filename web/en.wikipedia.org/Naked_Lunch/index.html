Naked Lunch is a 1959 novel by American writer William S. Burroughs. A fragmented, nonlinear work arising from his years in Tangier, it follows the addict William Lee through a hallucinatory underworld of drug culture, control systems, and grotesque satire, and became one of the defining texts of the Beat Generation.

Composition and publication

Burroughs wrote the material in Tangier, Morocco, in the mid-1950s; Jack Kerouac and Allen Ginsberg helped assemble the manuscript — Burroughs claimed to have no memory of writing much of it. Olympia Press published the book in Paris in 1959 (as The Naked Lunch). Grove Press's 1962 American edition triggered the last major literary obscenity trial in the United States: the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court ruled in 1966, with testimony from Ginsberg and Norman Mailer, that the book was not obscene, a landmark for free expression.

Content and style

The "novel" is a loose sequence of vignettes featuring characters such as Dr. Benway, a sadistic surgeon, in the fictional locales of Interzone and Freeland. Burroughs said the title means "a frozen moment when everyone sees what is on the end of every fork" — the naked truth of appetite and control. The routines' collage of slang, science fiction, and political allegory influenced later experimental fiction, punk, and cyberpunk. David Cronenberg's loose film adaptation appeared in 1991.
