Temple of Edfu

The Temple of Edfu is an Egyptian temple located on the west bank of the Nile in Edfu, Upper Egypt. It is one of the best-preserved shrine temples in Egypt, dedicated to the falcon god Horus. The temple was built in the Ptolemaic Kingdom between 237 and 57 BC.

Architecture

The temple is fronted by a massive pylon about 36 metres high, decorated with reliefs of Ptolemy XII defeating his enemies before Horus. Beyond lie a peristyle courtyard, hypostyle halls, and the sanctuary, where a polished granite shrine (naos) once held the cult statue. The walls are covered with inscriptions that preserve important information about Egyptian mythology, including the myth of the conflict between Horus and Seth.

History

After the banning of non-Christian worship in the Roman Empire, the temple fell into disuse and was buried under sand and silt. It was excavated by Auguste Mariette in the 1860s.
