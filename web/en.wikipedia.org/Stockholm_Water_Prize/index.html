Stockholm Water Prize

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Stockholm Water Prize is presented during World Water Week in Stockholm.

The Stockholm Water Prize is an international award honouring individuals and organisations whose work contributes to the conservation and protection of the world's water resources. Founded in 1991 and presented by the Stockholm International Water Institute (SIWI), it is often described as the "Nobel Prize of water".

The prize

The prize is awarded annually during World Water Week in Stockholm and is presented by the King of Sweden, Carl XVI Gustaf, who is its patron. The laureate receives a cash award (USD 150,000 in recent years) and a crystal sculpture. A nominating committee reviews candidates from around the world, and a prize committee makes the final selection.

Laureates

Laureates have included hydrologists, ecologists, engineers and policy figures such as Madhav Atmaram Chitale (the first recipient, 1993), Peter Gleick, Asit K. Biswas, Rita Colwell, Sunita Narain, Rajendra Singh, John Briscoe, and the International Water Management Institute. A companion award, the Stockholm Junior Water Prize, recognises young researchers.
