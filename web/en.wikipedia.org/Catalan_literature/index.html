Catalan literature (or Catalan-language literature) is the body of literature written in the Catalan language, native to Catalonia, the Valencian Community, the Balearic Islands, and neighbouring regions. It has a continuous history from the Middle Ages to the present, despite periods of suppression, most severely under the Franco regime in Spain (1939–1975), when public use of Catalan was restricted.

Medieval period

The earliest surviving literary texts in Catalan date from the late 12th and 13th centuries, including the Homilies d'Organyà, among the oldest prose texts in any Iberian Romance language. The Majorcan philosopher and writer Ramon Llull (c. 1232–1315/16) is considered the father of Catalan prose; works such as Blanquerna and the Book of the Gentile and the Three Wise Men combine literature, philosophy, and theology.

The 15th century is known as the Catalan Golden Age (Segle d'Or). Its crowning achievement is Tirant lo Blanch (1490) by Joanot Martorell, a chivalric novel praised by Cervantes in Don Quixote. The poet Ausiàs March (c. 1397–1459) brought a new psychological depth to Catalan verse, and Isabel de Villena wrote the Vita Christi, an important early work by a woman author.

Early modern decline and Renaixença

From the 16th to the 18th centuries, as Castilian became the language of the Spanish court and administration, Catalan literature entered a period traditionally called the Decadència. The 19th-century Renaixença (Renaissance) revival restored Catalan as a literary language, led by figures such as Jacint Verdaguer, whose epic poems L'Atlàntida (1877) and Canigó (1886) became national classics.

Modern and contemporary literature

The 20th century produced major writers including the poet Salvador Espriu, the playwright Àngel Guimerà (earlier, a Renaixença figure), and the novelist Mercè Rodoreda, whose La plaça del Diamant (1962; translated as The Time of the Doves) is the most widely translated Catalan novel. After the restoration of democracy and Catalan autonomy in the late 1970s, Catalan publishing, theatre, and children's literature expanded substantially, and contemporary authors continue to write in Catalan across all genres.
