Guard dog

A guard dog is a dog used to guard property against, and watch for, unwanted or unexpected people or animals. The dog is discriminating so that it does not annoy or attack familiar people, but is trained to alert its handler and, in the case of attack-trained guard dogs, to confront or detain intruders.

Types

Guard dogs are often divided by role. A watchdog barks to alert its owners to an intruder's presence but is not expected to attack; almost any alert breed can serve. A true guard dog is large and capable enough to deter, challenge, or attack an intruder. Livestock guardian dogs are a distinct type: breeds such as the Great Pyrenees, Anatolian Shepherd, Maremma, and Tibetan Mastiff are raised with sheep or goats from puppyhood and live with the flock full-time, bonding with the livestock and driving off predators such as wolves and coyotes rather than guarding against people.

Breeds and training

Breeds commonly used for property guarding include the German Shepherd, Rottweiler, Doberman Pinscher, Bullmastiff, Belgian Malinois, and Caucasian Shepherd. Effective guard dogs require careful selection for stable temperament, extensive socialisation, and professional training; an untrained territorial dog is a liability, and many jurisdictions impose legal responsibility on owners for injuries caused by their dogs. Warning signage is commonly required where guard dogs patrol commercial premises.

History

Dogs have guarded homes, camps, and livestock since early domestication. Ancient Romans kept powerful guard dogs — mosaics and inscriptions with the warning cave canem ("beware of the dog") survive at Pompeii. Guard dogs remain in use by police and military forces, at warehouses and farms, and in homes worldwide.
