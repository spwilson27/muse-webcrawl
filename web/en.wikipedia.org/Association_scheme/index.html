Association scheme


The relations of an association scheme partition all pairs of points.

An association scheme is a concept in algebraic combinatorics, introduced by R. C. Bose and T. Shimamoto in 1952 in connection with the design of experiments. It consists of a finite set of points together with a partition of the pairs of points into a small number of symmetric relations, such that the number of points standing in a fixed relation to two given points depends only on the relation between those two points.

Definition

Formally, a symmetric association scheme with d classes on a set X is a partition of X × X into relations R0, …, Rd, where R0 is the diagonal, each relation is symmetric, and for any pair (x, y) in Rk, the number of points z with (x, z) in Ri and (z, y) in Rj is a constant pijk independent of the choice of the pair.

Algebraic structure

The adjacency matrices of the relations span a commutative algebra called the Bose–Mesner algebra, which is simultaneously diagonalisable; its eigenvalues and dual eigenvalues govern feasibility conditions for designs and codes. Association schemes underlie the theory of distance-regular graphs, strongly regular graphs, block designs, and the Delsarte bounds in coding theory.

Examples

  • The Hamming and Johnson schemes from coding theory
  • Schemes arising from finite groups acting on cosets
  • Strongly regular graphs as two-class schemes
