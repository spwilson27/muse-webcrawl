Polysomnography

Polysomnography (PSG), commonly called a sleep study, is a multi-parametric test used in sleep medicine as a diagnostic tool. It comprehensively records the biophysiological changes that occur during sleep, typically over a full night, and is the reference standard for diagnosing many sleep disorders.

Recorded parameters

A standard polysomnogram simultaneously records brain activity by electroencephalography (EEG), eye movements by electrooculography (EOG), muscle activity by electromyography (EMG, usually at the chin and legs), heart rhythm by electrocardiography (ECG), breathing effort and airflow, blood oxygen saturation by pulse oximetry, and body position. From these signals, sleep is scored in 30-second epochs into wakefulness, non-REM stages N1, N2, and N3, and REM sleep according to American Academy of Sleep Medicine criteria.

Indications

Polysomnography is used chiefly to diagnose sleep-related breathing disorders such as obstructive sleep apnea, quantified by the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI). It also evaluates narcolepsy (together with the multiple sleep latency test), periodic limb movement disorder, REM sleep behavior disorder, and unexplained insomnia. Home sleep apnea testing, which records fewer channels, is an accepted alternative for uncomplicated suspected apnea in adults.

Procedure

The patient arrives at a sleep laboratory in the evening, is fitted with sensors attached by wires, and is monitored overnight by a technologist while video and audio are recorded. Trained scorers then analyze the record, and a sleep physician interprets the results. Split-night studies combine diagnosis and CPAP titration in a single night.
