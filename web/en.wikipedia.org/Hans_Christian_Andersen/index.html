Hans Christian Andersen

Hans Christian Andersen (2 April 1805 – 4 August 1875) was a Danish author best remembered for his literary fairy tales, which have been translated into more than 125 languages and remain beloved worldwide.

Life

Born in Odense, Denmark, the son of a shoemaker, Andersen moved to Copenhagen as a young man seeking work as an actor and singer before turning to writing. He travelled extensively in Europe and was acquainted with many leading writers of his time.

Fairy tales

His most famous tales include The Little Mermaid, The Ugly Duckling, The Emperor's New Clothes, Thumbelina, The Snow Queen, The Steadfast Tin Soldier, and The Little Match Girl. Unlike many folk tales, Andersen's stories often carry melancholy and moral depth. He also wrote novels, plays, poems, and travelogues. See also The Little Mermaid.
