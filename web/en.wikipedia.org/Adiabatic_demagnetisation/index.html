Adiabatic demagnetisation

Adiabatic demagnetisation is a technique used to reach very low temperatures, based on the magnetocaloric effect in paramagnetic salts. It was proposed independently by William Giauque and Peter Debye in the 1920s and first demonstrated in 1933.

Method

A paramagnetic salt is cooled (for example to about 1 K with liquid helium) while in a strong magnetic field, aligning its magnetic moments. It is then thermally isolated and the field is removed; as the moments randomise, they absorb energy, cooling the salt to temperatures well below 1 K.

Applications

Adiabatic demagnetisation refrigerators are used in laboratories and on spacecraft, where their lack of moving parts and gravity independence are advantages. Modern devices use electronic magnetocaloric materials and can reach millikelvin temperatures.
