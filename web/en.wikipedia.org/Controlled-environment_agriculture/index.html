Controlled-environment agriculture

Controlled-environment agriculture (CEA) is a technology-based approach toward food production that aims to provide protection from the elements and maintain optimal growing conditions throughout the development of the crop. Production takes place within an enclosed structure such as a greenhouse or a fully indoor facility.

Methods

CEA systems regulate temperature, humidity, carbon dioxide, and light, often using hydroponics, aeroponics, or aquaponics to deliver water and nutrients. Vertical farming stacks growing layers indoors under LED lighting, allowing year-round production in urban areas with greatly reduced water use and no need for pesticides.

Advantages and challenges

Benefits include higher yields per unit area, local production near consumers, reduced weather risk, and water savings of up to 95% compared with conventional farming. Drawbacks include high capital and energy costs, which have contributed to the failure of several high-profile vertical farming companies, and a crop range largely limited to leafy greens, herbs, and some fruiting plants.
