Bose–Einstein statistics

Bose–Einstein statistics
Applies toBosons (integer spin particles)
Introduced1924, Satyendra Nath Bose; generalised by Albert Einstein
PredictsPlanck's law; Bose–Einstein condensation

Bose–Einstein statistics is one of the two possible ways in which a collection of non-interacting, indistinguishable particles may occupy a set of available discrete energy states at thermodynamic equilibrium. It applies to bosons — particles with integer spin that, unlike fermions, are not subject to the Pauli exclusion principle: any number may occupy the same state.

n̄(ε) = 1 / ( e(ε−μ)/kT − 1 )

Satyendra Nath Bose derived the distribution for photons in 1924, deriving Planck's law without reference to classical electromagnetism; Einstein extended it to massive particles and predicted that at low enough temperature they would condense into the ground state — the Bose–Einstein condensate.

Applications

  • Black-body radiation and the photon gas
  • Helium-4 superfluidity and atomic condensates
  • Phonons in solids and the Debye model of heat capacity
  • Lasers, whose coherent photons exemplify unlimited occupancy of one mode
