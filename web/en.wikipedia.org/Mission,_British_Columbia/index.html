Mission is a city in the Fraser Valley region of British Columbia, Canada, situated on the north bank of the Fraser River, about 65 kilometres east of Vancouver. Originally incorporated as a district municipality in 1892, it adopted the name City of Mission in 2021. It is known as the home of Westminster Abbey, a Benedictine monastery founded in 1939, and of the Mission Folk Music Festival.

History

The area is the traditional territory of the Stó:lō peoples, in particular the Matsqui and Leq'a:mel First Nations. The modern settlement grew around a mission of the Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate established in 1861 at St. Mary's, from which the city takes its name. The arrival of the Canadian Pacific Railway in the 1880s, including the railway bridge at Mission connecting to the line to the United States, spurred growth, and the area became known for berry farming and logging.

Geography and economy

Mission occupies a hilly landscape between the Fraser River and the Coast Mountains, with municipal tree farms covering much of its northern area; it hosts one of the first community forests in British Columbia. Forestry, agriculture, and hydroelectric generation (notably the Ruskin and Stave Falls powerhouses nearby) have historically been important employers, and many residents commute to larger Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver centres.

Transport

The city is served by Highway 7 (the Lougheed Highway) along the north bank of the Fraser, and by the West Coast Express commuter rail service to downtown Vancouver from Mission City station.
