Neurosurgery

Neurosurgery, or neurological surgery, is the medical specialty concerned with the surgical treatment of disorders which affect any portion of the nervous system including the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nervous system. A physician who practises neurosurgery is a neurosurgeon.

Subspecialties

Neurosurgery encompasses cerebrovascular surgery (aneurysms and stroke), neuro-oncology (brain and spinal tumours), spinal surgery, functional neurosurgery (deep brain stimulation and epilepsy surgery), paediatric neurosurgery, trauma care, and peripheral nerve surgery. Endovascular techniques allow some conditions to be treated from within blood vessels rather than by open surgery.

History

Evidence of trepanation dates to prehistoric times, but modern neurosurgery emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with figures such as Harvey Cushing, often considered its father, and Walter Dandy. Advances including the operating microscope, neuroimaging, and stereotactic navigation have steadily expanded what can be treated safely.
