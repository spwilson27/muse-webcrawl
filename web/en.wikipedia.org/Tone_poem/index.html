Tone poem

A tone poem, or symphonic poem, is a piece of orchestral music in a single continuous movement that illustrates or evokes a story, poem, painting, landscape or other extra-musical idea. The form is a central product of musical Romanticism and of programme music in general.

History

Franz Liszt essentially created the genre, composing thirteen symphonic poems in the 1840s and 1850s, including Les Préludes and Mazeppa. The form was taken up by Bedřich Smetana (Má vlast), Antonín Dvořák, Jean Sibelius (Finlandia, The Swan of Tuonela) and above all Richard Strauss, whose Don Juan, Till Eulenspiegel, Also sprach Zarathustra and Ein Heldenleben carried the genre to virtuosic heights.

Characteristics

Unlike the multi-movement symphony, the tone poem follows the shape of its subject rather than abstract musical forms, though it often borrows elements of sonata or rondo structure. Later composers such as Debussy and Respighi continued the tradition into the twentieth century.
