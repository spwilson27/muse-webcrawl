Hexachordal combinatoriality

Hexachordal combinatoriality is a concept in post-tonal music theory describing a property of certain twelve-tone rows and pitch-class sets. A hexachord (a set of six pitch classes) is combinatorial if it can be combined with a transformation of itself — under transposition, inversion, retrograde, or retrograde inversion — to form an aggregate, that is, the complete set of twelve pitch classes, without overlap.

Types

Milton Babbitt identified several orders of combinatoriality. Prime combinatoriality involves transposition; inversional combinatoriality involves inversion. The all-combinatorial hexachords — sets that are combinatorial under all four transformations — are especially prized in serial composition, and Babbitt classified them into source sets of first through sixth order.

Use in composition

Combinatoriality allows a composer to present two forms of a row simultaneously while completing the total chromatic, providing a harmonic and structural resource analogous to tonal procedures. Composers including Milton Babbitt and those influenced by the Princeton school made extensive use of combinatorial and all-partition arrays.
