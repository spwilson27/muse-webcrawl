The Common Application (Common App) is an undergraduate college admission application that applicants may use to apply to any of more than 1,000 member colleges and universities, primarily in the United States but increasingly internationally. Operated by a nonprofit membership association founded in 1975, it allows a student to complete one core application — personal information, educational history, activities, and a personal essay — and submit it to multiple institutions, supplemented where required by college-specific questions and writing.

Applying to multiple colleges through a single platform

History

The Common Application began in 1975, when a small group of colleges agreed to accept a shared paper form to reduce duplication for applicants. It moved online in the late 1990s and 2000s, and by the 2010s it served more than a million applicants per cycle. A major platform relaunch in 2013 (CA4) drew criticism for technical glitches in its first season, prompting apologies and deadline extensions from many colleges.

The application

The core of the application is a personal statement of up to 650 words, chosen from a set of prompts that has remained largely stable since 2017. Applicants also list extracurricular activities, honors, and standardized testing (where required), and arrange teacher and counselor recommendations. Member colleges set their own deadlines, fees, and supplemental requirements; application-fee waivers are available for qualifying students.

Reach for Access and criticism

The organization has promoted initiatives to simplify fee waivers, support first-generation and low-income applicants, and provide a direct admissions pathway at some institutions. Critics argue that one-click multi-application systems encourage students to apply to excessive numbers of colleges, inflate application volumes, depress acceptance rates, and advantage well-resourced applicants with counseling support.

Alternatives

Competing or complementary systems include the Coalition Application, state systems such as ApplyTexas and the University of California application, and institution-specific forms.
