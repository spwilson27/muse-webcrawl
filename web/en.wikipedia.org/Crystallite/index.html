Crystallite

A crystallite is a small or microscopic crystal that forms during the cooling or solidification of many materials. A polycrystalline solid is an aggregate of many crystallites — also called grains — separated by grain boundaries where their orientations meet and mismatch.

Formation and size

Crystallites nucleate at many points in a melt or solution and grow until they impinge on one another. Their size, from nanometres to millimetres, depends on cooling rate and nucleation density; rapid cooling produces finer grains. Crystallite size is commonly estimated from X-ray diffraction line broadening using the Scherrer equation.

Properties

Grain size strongly affects mechanical behaviour: smaller grains generally strengthen metals (the Hall–Petch relation) because grain boundaries impede dislocation motion. Texture — preferred crystallite orientation — influences magnetic, optical and mechanical properties of materials from steel to ice and ceramics.
