Round tower

A round tower is a free-standing circular stone tower. The best-known examples are the Irish round towers (cloigtheach, "bell house"), tall tapered towers built between the 9th and 12th centuries at early Christian monastic sites across Ireland.

Irish round towers

Irish round towers typically stand 18 to 40 metres high, with a conical cap and a single doorway raised several metres above ground, reached by ladder. They served as bell towers, refuges, and stores for monastic treasures during raids, and as symbols of a monastery's prestige. Around 120 are known to have existed; fine surviving examples stand at Glendalough, Clonmacnoise, and Devenish Island.

Other round towers

Round church towers, usually attached to churches, are characteristic of East Anglia in England, where a shortage of building stone favoured flint towers without quoins. Round towers also appear in Scottish and German church architecture and as keep or turret forms in castles.
