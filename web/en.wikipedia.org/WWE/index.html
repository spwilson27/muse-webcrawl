WWE

WWE (World Wrestling Entertainment)
Founded: 1953 (as Capitol Wrestling Corporation)
Headquarters: Stamford, Connecticut, US
Industry: Professional wrestling, entertainment
Parent: TKO Group Holdings (since 2023)

WWE is an American professional wrestling promotion and media company, the largest wrestling promotion in the world. Founded by Jess McMahon and Toots Mondt as the Capitol Wrestling Corporation in 1953, it became the World Wide Wrestling Federation (WWWF), then the World Wrestling Federation (WWF), before adopting the WWE name in 2002. Under Vince McMahon, who bought the company from his father in 1982, it grew from a regional promotion into a global entertainment business.

History

McMahon broke the territorial system of American wrestling by signing stars such as Hulk Hogan and broadcasting nationally, launching WrestleMania in 1985 — still the company's flagship annual event. After surviving the Monday Night War against WCW in the 1990s (won with the edgy "Attitude Era" led by Stone Cold Steve Austin and The Rock), WWE bought WCW in 2001. A 2002 lawsuit by the World Wildlife Fund forced the name change to WWE. In 2023 WWE merged with the UFC under TKO Group Holdings, controlled by Endeavor; Vince McMahon departed the company in 2024, and creative direction passed to Paul "Triple H" Levesque.

Programming and brands

WWE's main weekly shows are Raw and SmackDown, with the developmental brand NXT. Its roster is split between brands, and its premium live events (formerly pay-per-views) include WrestleMania, Royal Rumble, SummerSlam, Survivor Series, and Money in the Bank. The company broadcasts in over 180 countries, and its video library and streaming arrangements (including a landmark Netflix deal for Raw from 2025) make it a major media property.

Championships and Hall of Fame

Top titles have included the WWE Championship and the World Heavyweight Championship for men and corresponding women's championships; the WWE Hall of Fame, established in 1993, honours legends of the industry.
