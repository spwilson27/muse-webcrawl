Harold Scott MacDonald "Donald" Coxeter (9 February 1907 – 31 March 2003) was a British-Canadian geometer, regarded as one of the greatest geometers of the 20th century. He worked for 60 years at the University of Toronto.

Life and career

Coxeter studied mathematics at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he worked with H. F. Baker. After visiting Princeton, he joined the University of Toronto in 1936, becoming a full professor in 1948. He was editor of the Canadian Journal of Mathematics and a Fellow of the Royal Society and the Royal Society of Canada.

Work

Coxeter is best known for his work on regular polytopes and higher-dimensional geometry, summarised in his classic Regular Polytopes (1948). He developed the theory of reflection groups now called Coxeter groups and the associated Coxeter–Dynkin diagrams, which are fundamental in algebra, geometry, and theoretical physics. His popular books, including Introduction to Geometry (1961) and Non-Euclidean Geometry, revived interest in classical geometry at a time when it was falling out of curricula.

Influence

Coxeter's kaleidoscopic constructions influenced the artist M. C. Escher, whose Circle Limit prints arose from their correspondence, and Buckminster Fuller dedicated work to him. The CMS Coxeter–James Prize and numerous mathematical concepts — Coxeter numbers, Coxeter planes, the Todd–Coxeter algorithm — bear his name.
