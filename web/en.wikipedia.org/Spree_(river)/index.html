The Spree (German: [ʃpʁeː]; Sorbian: Sprjewja) is a river that flows through the Saxony, Brandenburg and Berlin states of Germany, and in the town of Šluknov in the Czech Republic. It is a left tributary of the Havel, which it joins in the western Berlin borough of Spandau, and is approximately 400 kilometres (250 mi) long.

Course

The river rises in the Lusatian Highlands near the German–Czech border, with three main sources around Ebersbach-Neugersdorf and Kottmar. It flows north through Bautzen and the Spreewald — a low-lying wetland region of channels and meadows inhabited historically by the Sorbs — then through Cottbus and into Berlin, where it passes Museum Island and the government district before joining the Havel at Spandau.

Berlin

In Berlin the Spree forms the historic heart of the city: the original settlements of Berlin and Cölln grew up on either side of the river around the Mühlendamm crossing. Landmarks on its banks include the Berlin Cathedral, the Reichstag and the State Museums on Museum Island. The Landwehr Canal and the Spree Canal branch from it within the city. During the Cold War, sections of the river formed part of the border between East and West Berlin, and several people died attempting to cross it.

Navigation

Much of the Spree is navigable and forms part of the waterway network linking Berlin with the Oder and Elbe rivers via canals such as the Oder–Spree Canal.
