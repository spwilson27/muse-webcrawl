Tali Sharot

Tali Sharot is a cognitive neuroscientist and professor of cognitive neuroscience at University College London (UCL), where she directs the Affective Brain Lab. She is best known for her research on the optimism bias — the human tendency to expect positive future outcomes — and on how emotion and social interaction shape beliefs and decision-making.

Education and career

Sharot received her bachelor's degree in psychology and economics and her PhD in psychology and neuroscience from New York University, where she worked with Elizabeth Phelps. She subsequently held a Wellcome Trust fellowship at UCL working with Ray Dolan before joining the faculty. Her research combines behavioural experiments, computational modelling, and brain imaging.

Research

Sharot's early work showed that emotionally arousing memories, such as those of the September 11 attacks, engage the amygdala and can feel vividly accurate even when details are wrong. She subsequently demonstrated that people update their beliefs more readily in response to good news than to bad news about the future, a neural asymmetry underlying the optimism bias, and that this asymmetry is reduced in depression.

Books and outreach

Sharot is the author of The Optimism Bias: A Tour of the Irrationally Positive Brain (2011) and co-author, with Cass Sunstein, of The Influential Mind (2017), which won the British Psychological Society Book Award. Her TED talks on optimism and influence have been viewed millions of times.
