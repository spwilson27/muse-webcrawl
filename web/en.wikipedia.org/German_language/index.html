German language

German (Deutsch)
Native speakers: about 95 million
Family: Indo-European → Germanic → West Germanic
Official in: Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Belgium
Writing system: Latin (German alphabet)

German (Deutsch, pronounced [dɔʏtʃ]) is a West Germanic language spoken mainly in Central Europe. It is the most widely spoken first language in the European Union and is an official language of Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, and parts of Belgium and Italy.

Features

Standard German derives largely from High German dialects, shaped by Martin Luther's Bible translation and a standardisation process across the early modern period. The language is known for its four grammatical cases, three genders, verb-second word order in main clauses (with verbs final in subordinate clauses), and productive compounding that creates very long words. Its alphabet uses the umlauted vowels ä, ö, ü and the letter ß.

Dialects and spread

German dialects range from Low German in the north to Alemannic and Bavarian varieties in the south. German-speaking minorities and learners worldwide keep the total number of speakers, including second-language speakers, well above 130 million.
