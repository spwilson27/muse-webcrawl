Clean Water Act

The Clean Water Act (CWA) is the principal United States federal law governing pollution of the nation's surface waters. Enacted in 1972 as the Federal Water Pollution Control Act Amendments, it grew out of earlier legislation and was substantially overhauled in 1977 and 1987. Its objective is to restore and maintain the chemical, physical and biological integrity of the nation's waters.

Key features

The Act made it unlawful to discharge any pollutant from a point source into navigable waters without a permit under the National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES). It set technology-based effluent limitations for industry and municipal sewage treatment plants, with a goal of eliminating pollutant discharges by 1985, and interim goals of fishable and swimmable waters. The federal government also provided large grants for constructing municipal wastewater treatment facilities.

Jurisdiction

The Act applies to "waters of the United States", a phrase whose reach — especially over wetlands and intermittent streams — has been contested in cases such as Rapanos v. United States (2006) and Sackett v. EPA (2023), the latter substantially narrowing federal jurisdiction over wetlands.

Structure

Alongside point-source permits, the Act regulates the discharge of dredged or fill material (Section 404, administered with the Army Corps of Engineers), oil spills, and polluted runoff from nonpoint sources through state management programmes. It is enforced by the Environmental Protection Agency and authorised states.
