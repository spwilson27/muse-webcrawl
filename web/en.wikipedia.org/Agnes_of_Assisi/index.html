Agnes of Assisi

Saint Agnes of Assisi
Born: 1197, Assisi, Italy
Died: 16 November 1253, Assisi
Venerated in: Roman Catholic Church
Feast: 16 November

Agnes of Assisi (1197 – 16 November 1253) was an Italian Catholic abbess and one of the first followers of her elder sister, Saint Clare of Assisi. She was a member of the Order of Saint Clare, commonly called the Poor Clares.

Life

Born Caterina Offreduccio, she was the younger sister of Clare. Two weeks after Clare fled their father's house to follow Francis of Assisi in 1212, Caterina followed her, despite violent opposition from her family, who attempted to drag her home from the monastery of Sant'Angelo di Panzo. She took the religious name Agnes.

Francis sent Agnes to Florence to found and govern the community at Monticelli, and she later established houses in Mantua, Venice, and Padua. She returned to San Damiano to nurse Clare in her final illness and died in November 1253, a few months after her sister.

Veneration

Agnes was canonised by Pope Benedict XIV in 1753. Her relics rest beside those of Clare in the Basilica of Saint Clare in Assisi. Her feast day is 16 November (19 November in some calendars).
