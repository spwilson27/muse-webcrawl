Augustus Saint-Gaudens

Augustus Saint-Gaudens (March 1, 1848 – August 3, 1907) was an Irish-American sculptor of the Beaux-Arts generation who embodied the ideals of the American Renaissance. He rose to prominence with his monuments commemorating heroes of the American Civil War, and his designs for United States coinage are considered among the most beautiful ever produced.

The Shaw Memorial (1884–1897), among Saint-Gaudens's most celebrated works.

Life and career

Born in Dublin to a French father and Irish mother, Saint-Gaudens was brought to New York as an infant. Apprenticed as a cameo cutter, he studied in Paris at the École des Beaux-Arts and in Rome. His Farragut Monument in New York (1881), with a pedestal by Stanford White, launched his career as America's leading monumental sculptor.

Major works

His masterworks include the Adams Memorial in Washington, D.C. (1891), the Robert Gould Shaw Memorial in Boston honouring the African American 54th Massachusetts Regiment, the standing Abraham Lincoln in Chicago, the Sherman Monument in New York with its gilded figure of Victory, and the Diana that topped Madison Square Garden. At the invitation of President Theodore Roosevelt, he redesigned American coinage, producing the "double eagle" twenty-dollar gold piece of 1907, widely regarded as the most beautiful United States coin.

Legacy

From 1885 Saint-Gaudens spent his summers, and later his whole year, at Cornish, New Hampshire, where the Cornish Art Colony formed around him. His home and studio are preserved as the Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park.
