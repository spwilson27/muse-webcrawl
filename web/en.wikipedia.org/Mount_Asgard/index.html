Mount Asgard

Elevation2,015 m (6,611 ft)
LocationAuyuittuq National Park, Baffin Island, Nunavut, Canada
RangeBaffin Mountains
First ascent1953

Mount Asgard is a twin-peaked mountain with two flat-topped cylindrical rock towers separated by a saddle, located in Auyuittuq National Park on the Cumberland Peninsula of Baffin Island, Nunavut, Canada. The peak is named after Asgard, the realm of the gods in Norse mythology. It rises to about 2,015 m (6,611 ft).

Geography

Mount Asgard lies in the Akshayuk Pass area of the Baffin Mountains, a range running along the northeastern coast of Baffin Island. Its two distinctive flat summits were shaped by glacial erosion, which planed off the tops of the towers. Nearby peaks include Mount Thor, which has one of the greatest vertical drops on Earth.

Climbing history

The first ascent was made in 1953 by a Swiss-led Arctic Institute expedition. In 1976 the mountain was the site of a celebrated BASE jump by stuntman Rick Sylvester for the James Bond film The Spy Who Loved Me, in which he skied off the summit and opened a Union Jack parachute — a stunt that became one of the most famous opening sequences in the Bond series.
