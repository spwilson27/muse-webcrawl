League of Militant Atheists

The League of Militant Atheists (Russian: Soyuz voinstvuyushchikh bezbozhnikov), also translated as the League of the Militant Godless, was an atheistic organisation in the Soviet Union, active from 1925 to 1947 and led for most of its existence by Yemelyan Yaroslavsky.

Operating under Communist Party guidance, the League promoted atheism through lectures, exhibitions, anti-religious museums, and publications such as the magazine and newspaper Bezbozhnik ('The Godless'). It claimed millions of members at its peak around the early 1930s and campaigned against religious holidays and institutions, seeking to replace religious observance with secular rituals.

The League's activities declined in the late 1930s and it effectively ceased operating during the Second World War, as the Soviet state relaxed its anti-religious campaign; it was formally disbanded in 1947, its functions passing to the Znanie society.
