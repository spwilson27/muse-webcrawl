Drive-by download

A drive-by download is a download which happens without a person's knowledge, or with their consent but without their understanding of the consequences — for example, a download that installs a malicious executable, browser plug-in, or piece of malware. The term is used for downloads that occur simply by visiting a web page, opening an email message, or clicking on a deceptive pop-up window.

Mechanism

Malicious websites typically exploit vulnerabilities in the visitor's web browser, browser plug-ins (historically Java, Flash, and PDF readers), or operating system. An exploit kit — a commercial or criminal software package such as the formerly notorious Blackhole and Angler kits — automatically fingerprints the visiting system and serves the matching exploit, after which a payload such as ransomware, banking trojans, or spyware is installed. Legitimate websites compromised by attackers, or malicious advertisements ("malvertising") on legitimate sites, are common delivery routes, so victims need not have visited any obviously disreputable page.

Related meanings

The term is also used more benignly for downloads that begin automatically when software is requested, and for installations bundled with desired software that the user may not have expected, though the security literature reserves it for the non-consensual case.

Prevention

Defences include keeping browsers, plug-ins and operating systems patched; disabling or click-to-play for plug-ins; script and ad blocking; sandboxing of browsers; and modern browser features that isolate sites and block known malicious downloads. Enterprises additionally use web filtering gateways and endpoint protection.
