Damon Laurence Lindelof (born April 24, 1973) is an American screenwriter, comic book writer, and producer. He was the co-creator and showrunner of the television series Lost (2004–2010) alongside J. J. Abrams and Carlton Cuse. He is also known as the creator of the critically acclaimed HBO series The Leftovers (2014–2017), adapted with Tom Perrotta from Perrotta's novel, and the limited series Watchmen (2019), which won the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Limited Series.

Early life

Lindelof was born in Teaneck, New Jersey, and attended Teaneck High School. He studied film at New York University's Tisch School of the Arts. A lifelong comic book fan and aspiring writer, he worked his way up from writing specs and staff jobs on series such as Nash Bridges and Crossing Jordan before his big break with Lost.

Career

Lost

Hired by ABC to develop a castaway drama, Lindelof worked with J. J. Abrams on the concept that became Lost. After Abrams left to direct films, Lindelof ran the show with Carlton Cuse, pioneering the heavily serialized, mythology-driven network drama and early fan engagement via podcasts. The show won the Emmy for Outstanding Drama Series for its first season; its 2010 finale remains one of the most debated in television history, and Lindelof has been candid about both pride in and the toll of the experience.

Films and later television

Lindelof's film credits include co-writing Cowboys & Aliens (2011), Prometheus (2012), Star Trek Into Darkness (2013), World War Z (2013, co-writing the final act), and Tomorrowland (2015), plus The Hunt (2020). The Leftovers (2014–2017) was hailed as one of the decade's best series; Watchmen (2019), a "remix" of the Alan Moore comic centered on the Tulsa race massacre and American racism, won 11 Emmys. He later co-created the Peacock series Mrs. Davis (2023) with Tara Hernandez.

Themes

Lindelof's work recurrently explores faith versus science, grief, father–son relationships, trauma, and the human need for stories and closure in the face of unresolved mystery.
