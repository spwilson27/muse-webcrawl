The Government of Australia is the federal executive government of the Commonwealth of Australia, a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy. It is formed by the party or coalition holding a majority in the House of Representatives and is headed by the Prime Minister. The government's structure derives from the Constitution of Australia, which took effect on 1 January 1901 at Federation.

Constitutional framework

Australia's system combines British Westminster conventions with a written federal constitution influenced by the United States. Executive power is formally vested in the monarch, represented by the Governor-General, but by convention is exercised by ministers responsible to Parliament. The governor-general appoints the Prime Minister and, on advice, other ministers, assents to legislation, and serves as commander-in-chief of the Australian Defence Force.

Branches

The legislature is the bicameral Parliament, consisting of the Senate and the House of Representatives. The executive comprises the Prime Minister, the Cabinet, and the public service departments. The judiciary is headed by the High Court of Australia, established in 1903, which interprets the Constitution and serves as the final court of appeal.

Federation

Below the Commonwealth are six states and two self-governing territories, each with its own government, plus hundreds of local councils. Legislative powers are divided by the Constitution, with the Commonwealth holding exclusive or concurrent powers over matters such as defence, foreign affairs, trade, and immigration, while states retain responsibility for schools, hospitals, and policing.
