Big five game

In Africa, the Big Five game animals are the lion, leopard, black rhinoceros, African bush elephant, and African buffalo. The term was coined by big-game hunters to describe the five species considered the most difficult and dangerous to hunt on foot in Africa. Today the "Big Five" are a major draw for safari tourism and wildlife photography.

The animals

The African lion is the continent's apex predator; the leopard is a solitary and elusive hunter; the black rhinoceros is aggressive and critically endangered; the bush elephant is the largest living land animal; and the Cape buffalo is regarded by many hunters as the most dangerous of the five because of its size, temper, and tendency to charge when wounded.

Hunting history

The concept emerged in the era of colonial safari hunting in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when hunting the five became a prestigious achievement. Trophy hunting of all five continues under permit in some countries, though rhinoceros hunting is tightly restricted and trade in ivory is banned internationally.

Conservation and tourism

All five species face threats from habitat loss and poaching. At the same time, their status as safari icons generates substantial tourism revenue that funds protected areas in countries such as Kenya, Tanzania, South Africa, and Botswana. The phrase has also been adapted for other wildlife lists, such as the "little five" of small animals with corresponding names.
