Santa Barbara is a coastal city in southern California and the county seat of Santa Barbara County. Situated on a south-facing stretch of coastline — the longest such section on the West Coast of the United States — the city lies between the steeply rising Santa Ynez Mountains and the Pacific Ocean. Its Mediterranean climate, distinctive Spanish Colonial Revival architecture, and palm-lined beaches have long made it a popular tourist destination, earning it the nickname "the American Riviera."

History

The area was inhabited for millennia by the Chumash people. The Portuguese explorer João Cabrilho (Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo) sailed along the channel in 1542, and Sebastián Vizcaíno named the area in 1602 in honour of Saint Barbara. Permanent Spanish settlement began with the founding of the Presidio of Santa Barbara in 1782 and Mission Santa Barbara in 1786 — the mission, still an active parish, is known as the "Queen of the Missions." Santa Barbara passed to Mexico in 1822 and to the United States after the Mexican–American War; it was incorporated in 1850, the year California became a state. After a major earthquake in 1925 destroyed much of the downtown, the city rebuilt in a unified Spanish Colonial Revival style, encouraged by civic leaders such as Pearl Chase, establishing the white stucco and red-tile character it retains today.

Geography and climate

Santa Barbara enjoys a mild Mediterranean climate with warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters; temperatures are moderated by the ocean. From time to time hot, dry "sundowner" winds descending from the mountains raise temperatures sharply and increase wildfire risk.

Culture and landmarks

Prominent landmarks include the Santa Barbara County Courthouse (1929), with its clock tower and murals; Stearns Wharf (1872); State Street, the main commercial thoroughfare; and the city's beaches and harbour. The Santa Barbara International Film Festival is held annually. The city is home to the University of California, Santa Barbara (in neighbouring Goleta), Santa Barbara City College, and Westmont College.
