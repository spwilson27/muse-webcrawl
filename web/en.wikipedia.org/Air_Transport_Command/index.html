The Air Transport Command (ATC) was a United States Air Force unit that was created during World War II as the strategic airlift component of the United States Army Air Forces. It had two main missions: the first was the air transport of supplies, equipment and personnel for U.S. and Allied forces throughout the world; the second was the ferrying of aircraft from factories to operational units.

History

ATC originated as the Air Corps Ferrying Command, established in 1941 to deliver aircraft to the British under Lend-Lease. It was renamed Air Transport Command in 1942 and grew into a global airline, operating routes across the Atlantic, the Pacific, Africa, and over the Himalayas on the famous "Hump" route supplying China.

At its peak in 1945, ATC operated thousands of aircraft and moved vast quantities of cargo and passengers. After the war, it participated in operations such as the Berlin Airlift's early organization, and in 1948 it was merged with the Naval Air Transport Service to form the Military Air Transport Service (MATS).
