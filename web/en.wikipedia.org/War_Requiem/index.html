War Requiem

The War Requiem, Op. 66, is a large-scale setting of the traditional Latin Requiem Mass by Benjamin Britten, interspersed with poems by the First World War poet Wilfred Owen. It was composed for the reconsecration of Coventry Cathedral, which had been destroyed in the Coventry Blitz of November 1940, and was first performed there on 30 May 1962.

Forces and structure

The work is scored for soprano, tenor and baritone soloists, chorus, boys' choir, orchestra and chamber orchestra. The Latin Mass is sung by the soprano, chorus and full orchestra (with the boys' choir accompanied by organ at a distance), while Owen's poems are set for the tenor and baritone with chamber orchestra — a private, bitter commentary on the public ritual. The six movements are Requiem aeternam, Dies irae, Offertorium, Sanctus, Agnus Dei and Libera me. Britten intended the soloists at the premiere to represent the wartime enemies — an English tenor (Peter Pears), a German baritone (Dietrich Fischer-Dieskau), and a Russian soprano (Galina Vishnevskaya) — as a gesture of reconciliation.

Reception

The premiere was an overwhelming success, and the 1963 Decca recording conducted by Britten sold some 200,000 copies in its first year — unprecedented for a contemporary classical work. The War Requiem is widely considered Britten's masterpiece and one of the great choral works of the 20th century.
