Ahura Mazda

Ahura Mazda (Avestan: Ahurō Mazdā, "Wise Lord") is the supreme deity of Zoroastrianism, the ancient Iranian religion founded by the prophet Zoroaster (Zarathustra). Ahura Mazda is the uncreated creator, the embodiment of truth, light, and goodness (asha), opposed by Angra Mainyu (Ahriman), the spirit of falsehood and destruction.

Theology

In the Gathas, the oldest Zoroastrian scriptures attributed to Zoroaster himself, Ahura Mazda is the source of all good creation. He is served by the Amesha Spentas, six divine emanations or archangels representing qualities such as Good Mind (Vohu Manah) and Truth (Asha Vahishta). Human beings, endowed with free will, are called to choose truth over the lie and thereby assist in the eventual renovation of the world.

History of worship

Ahura Mazda was the god of the Achaemenid Persian kings: Darius I credited him with granting the empire in the Behistun Inscription. Zoroastrianism remained the dominant religion of Iran under the Parthian and Sasanian empires until the Muslim conquest of Persia in the seventh century CE. The Faravahar, a winged disc figure, became the best-known symbol associated with the faith, while the car company Mazda took its name from the deity.
