Circumference

In geometry, the circumference is the perimeter of a circle or ellipse — the length of the curve that bounds it. The term derives from the Latin circumferens, "carrying around".

Relation to π

The circumference C of a circle is related to its radius r and diameter d by the formulas C = 2πr and C = πd. The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter is the constant π (pi), approximately 3.14159, the same for all circles. Archimedes gave the first rigorous bounds for this ratio by approximating the circle with inscribed and circumscribed polygons.

Ellipse

For an ellipse with semi-major axis a and semi-minor axis b there is no simple exact formula in elementary functions; the circumference is given by an elliptic integral. Close approximations include Ramanujan's formula, C ≈ π[3(a + b) − √((3a + b)(a + 3b))].
