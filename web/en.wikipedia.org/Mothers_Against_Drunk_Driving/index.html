Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD) is a non-profit organization in the United States, Canada and Brazil founded in 1980. It seeks to stop drunk driving, support those affected by drunk driving, prevent underage drinking, and strive for stricter impaired driving policies.

History

MADD was founded in Sacramento, California, by Candace (Candy) Lightner after her 13-year-old daughter, Cari, was killed by a drunk driver — a repeat offender who had been released on bail for an earlier hit-and-run — while walking to a church carnival in May 1980. Outrage at the lenient treatment of the case drew national media attention, and Lightner channelled it into a grassroots movement that transformed public attitudes toward drunk driving in the United States.

Campaigns and achievements

MADD's lobbying helped secure passage of the National Minimum Drinking Age Act of 1984, which pressured states to raise the drinking age to 21; the adoption of 0.08 blood alcohol concentration as the legal limit across the United States; administrative license revocation and zero-tolerance laws for young drivers; and sobriety checkpoints. Its red-ribbon "Tie One On for Safety" campaign is one of the most visible holiday-season safety programmes. Lightner left the organisation in 1985 after disputes over its direction and later criticised what she saw as a drift toward neo-prohibitionism; MADD's stated mission has since expanded to include drugged driving and underage drinking.
