Apollonius of Perga

Apollonius of Perga
Bornc. 240 BC, Perga, Pamphylia
Diedc. 190 BC, Alexandria, Egypt
Known forConics; conic sections; epicycles

Apollonius of Perga (c. 240 BC – c. 190 BC) was an ancient Greek geometer and astronomer known for his treatise on conic sections. Working in Alexandria and Pergamum, he built on the work of Euclid and Archimedes and was called "the Great Geometer" by later commentators.

Conics

His eight-book work Conics systematically developed the theory of the curves obtained by slicing a cone with a plane, and gave them the names still used today: the ellipse, parabola, and hyperbola. The first four books survive in Greek; books V–VII survive only in Arabic translation, and book VIII is lost. The treatise remained the foundation of the subject until the analytic geometry of the seventeenth century.

Astronomy

Apollonius also contributed to mathematical astronomy. He is credited with developing the eccentric model of planetary motion and with work on epicycles — deferent-and-epicycle constructions later central to Ptolemy's Almagest — and with relating the epicyclic and eccentric models.

Other works

Pappus and other late sources report numerous lost works by Apollonius, including Cutting-off of a Ratio (which survives in Arabic), works on tangencies — the "problem of Apollonius" of constructing circles tangent to three given circles — and studies of the dodecahedron and icosahedron.
