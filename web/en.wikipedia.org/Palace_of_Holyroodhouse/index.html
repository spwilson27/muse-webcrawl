The Palace of Holyroodhouse, commonly referred to as Holyrood Palace, is the official residence of the British monarch in Scotland. Located at the bottom of the Royal Mile in Edinburgh, opposite the Scottish Parliament and beneath Arthur's Seat, it has served as the principal royal residence in Scotland since the sixteenth century.

History

The palace grew up beside Holyrood Abbey, founded in 1128 by David I. A royal guesthouse by the abbey became a palace under James IV and James V, whose surviving north-west tower housed Mary, Queen of Scots from 1561 to 1567; it was in her private supper room that her secretary David Rizzio was murdered in 1566. The present quadrangular palace was built between 1671 and 1678 for Charles II, to designs by Sir William Bruce. Bonnie Prince Charlie held court there briefly during the Jacobite rising of 1745.

Today

The monarch spends a week in residence each summer ("Holyrood Week"), hosting investitures and the traditional Garden Party and Ceremony of the Keys. State apartments, including the Great Gallery hung with portraits of Scottish kings, are open to the public most of the year. In September 2022 the coffin of Elizabeth II lay at rest in the palace's Throne Room before its journey to London, drawing large crowds along the Royal Mile.
