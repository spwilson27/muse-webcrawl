Sponsorship scandal

The sponsorship scandal, known as commanditâton or the scandale des commandites in Quebec, was a political scandal in Canada involving the use of government funds by the Liberal government of Prime Minister Jean Chrétien. A federal sponsorship program intended to promote Canadian unity in Quebec after the 1995 referendum was found to have misdirected millions of dollars to advertising agencies with Liberal connections for little or no work.

Background

The sponsorship program was created in 1996–1997 to raise the federal profile in Quebec. In 2004, Auditor General Sheila Fraser reported serious mismanagement, estimating that about $100 million of the program's funds had gone to agencies in commissions and fees. The Gomery Commission was established to investigate.

Gomery Commission

The Gomery Inquiry (2004–2005) heard testimony of kickbacks and irregular contracting. Its final report found political involvement in the program's administration and a culture of entitlement. Several advertising executives were criminally convicted.

Consequences

The scandal severely damaged the Liberal Party under Paul Martin; the government was reduced to a minority in 2004 and defeated in the 2006 federal election, won by Stephen Harper's Conservatives. The incoming government passed the Federal Accountability Act in response.
