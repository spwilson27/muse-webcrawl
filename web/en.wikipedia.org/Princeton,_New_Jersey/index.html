Princeton is a municipality in Mercer County, New Jersey, United States. It is best known as the home of Princeton University, founded in 1746 as the College of New Jersey and moved to Princeton in 1756, and of the Institute for Advanced Study, where Albert Einstein worked from 1933 until his death in 1955. The modern municipality was formed in 2013 by the merger of the Borough of Princeton and Princeton Township.

History

The area was settled by Europeans in the late seventeenth century. Princeton played a notable role in the American Revolutionary War: on 3 January 1777, George Washington's Continental Army defeated British forces at the Battle of Princeton, a victory that revived American morale after the crossing of the Delaware. In the summer of 1783, Princeton briefly served as the seat of the Confederation Congress, meeting in Nassau Hall.

Geography and life

Princeton lies roughly midway between New York City and Philadelphia. Its centre, Palmer Square and Nassau Street, faces the university campus. The town and surrounding area host research institutions, technology and pharmaceutical firms, and the Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory. Drumthwacket, the official residence of the Governor of New Jersey, is located in Princeton.
