Guatemala

Capital: Guatemala City
Official language: Spanish
Population: ~18 million
Currency: Quetzal (GTQ)

Guatemala, officially the Republic of Guatemala, is a country in Central America. It is bordered to the north and west by Mexico, to the northeast by Belize, to the east by Honduras, and to the southeast by El Salvador. It is touched by the Pacific Ocean to the south and the Gulf of Honduras to the east. With an estimated population of around 18 million, it is the most populous country in Central America and the 69th largest by area at 108,889 square kilometres.

History

Guatemala was the heartland of the Maya civilisation, whose cities such as Tikal flourished for centuries before many lowland centres were abandoned around the 10th century. The Spanish conquered the region in the 1520s under Pedro de Alvarado, and Guatemala became part of the Captaincy General of Guatemala within New Spain. It declared independence from Spain in 1821 and briefly formed part of the Mexican Empire and then the Federal Republic of Central America.

The 20th century saw dictatorship under Jorge Ubico, a decade of reform (1944–1954) under Juan José Arévalo and Jacobo Árbenz, and the 1954 CIA-backed coup that overthrew Árbenz. A brutal civil war followed from 1960 to 1996, killing some 200,000 people, most of them Maya civilians killed by state forces. Peace accords signed in 1996 formally ended the conflict.

Geography

The country is mountainous, with a chain of volcanoes running parallel to the Pacific coast, including Tajumulco (4,220 m), the highest peak in Central America. The northern Petén lowlands are covered in tropical rainforest. Guatemala is prone to earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and hurricanes.

Culture

Guatemalan culture blends Spanish colonial and Indigenous Maya influences. More than 20 Mayan languages are still spoken alongside Spanish. The country is known for its colourful textiles, the marimba, Holy Week processions in Antigua Guatemala, and coffee production. The writer Miguel Ángel Asturias won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1967.
