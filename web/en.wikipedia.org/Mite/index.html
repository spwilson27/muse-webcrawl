Mite

Mites are small arthropods belonging to the class Arachnida and the subclass Acari (also known as Acarina). Together with ticks, they form the most diverse group of arachnids, with over 48,000 described species and perhaps a million or more undescribed.

Characteristics

Most mites are tiny — less than 1 mm long — with an unsegmented, fused body plan. They occupy an enormous range of habitats: soil, water, plants, and animals. Many are free-living predators or decomposers; others are parasites of plants and animals.

Importance

Economically important mites include spider mites, major pests of crops; the Varroa mite, a destructive parasite of honey bees; dust mites, whose waste particles trigger allergies and asthma in humans; and the scabies mite (Sarcoptes scabiei), which burrows into skin. Predatory mites are also used in biological pest control.
