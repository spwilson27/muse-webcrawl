Yerba Buena Island is an island in San Francisco Bay, located between San Francisco and Oakland, California. The island is connected to the mainland and to San Francisco by the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge, which passes through the island via the Yerba Buena Tunnel — one of the largest single-bore tunnels in the United States. The island is joined by a causeway to the artificial Treasure Island, built for the 1939 Golden Gate International Exposition.

History

The island was originally called Isla de Alcatraces ("Island of the Pelicans") by Spanish explorers. The name Yerba Buena ("good herb") derives from the yerba buena plant (Clinopodium douglasii), a native mint that grew abundantly there; the nearby settlement of Yerba Buena later became the city of San Francisco. During the 19th century the island was informally known as Goat Island because of the herds of goats pastured on it. The United States Navy established a station on the island in 1898, and it long hosted military facilities, including a Coast Guard station and the historic Nimitz House and senior officers' quarters on its northeastern shore.

Geography and development

Yerba Buena Island is a natural rocky island rising to about 103 metres above the bay, in contrast to the flat, human-made Treasure Island beside it. Much of the island is covered by native oak woodland and grassland. In the 21st century, portions of the island's former naval land have been redeveloped with new housing, parks, and trails as part of the joint Treasure Island / Yerba Buena Island development project, while habitat restoration work has aimed to protect native species.

Transportation

The island is served by the Bay Bridge (Interstate 80) and by San Francisco Muni bus routes connecting it to downtown San Francisco. The Bay Bridge's eastern and western spans meet at the island, connected by the double-deck Yerba Buena Tunnel, completed in 1936.
