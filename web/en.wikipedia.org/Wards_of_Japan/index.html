Wards of Japan

Wards (区, ku)
CategoryMunicipal subdivision
Found inDesignated cities; Tokyo Metropolis (special wards)
Number175 ordinary wards; 23 special wards

A ward (区, ku) is a subdivision of a large Japanese city. Two kinds exist. In cities designated by government ordinance (seirei shitei toshi), wards are administrative subdivisions established for the convenience of municipal administration; their offices handle resident registration, welfare, and tax services, but they are not independent municipalities and have no elected councils of their own. The 23 special wards of Tokyo (特別区, tokubetsu-ku), by contrast, are self-governing municipalities in their own right, each with an elected mayor and assembly, handling most functions that elsewhere belong to cities.

Ordinary wards

Twenty designated cities, including Osaka (24 wards), Nagoya (16), Yokohama (18), Kyoto (11), and Fukuoka (7), are divided into wards. Ward offices (kuyakusho) serve as local branches of city hall. Examples often cited internationally include Osaka's Kita-ku and Chūō-ku and Yokohama's Naka-ku.

Special wards of Tokyo

The special wards — including Shinjuku, Shibuya, Minato, Chiyoda, and Setagaya — occupy the area of the former city of Tokyo, abolished in 1943. Since reforms in 2000 they have been treated in law as "special local public entities" broadly equivalent to cities, though the Tokyo Metropolitan Government retains responsibility for certain metropolitan functions such as waterworks, sewerage, and fire services.
