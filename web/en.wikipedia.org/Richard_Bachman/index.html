Richard Bachman is a pen name of the American horror author Stephen King, who used it for several novels published between 1977 and 1985, and revived it later. At the start of King's career, publishers restricted authors to one book per year; King also wished to test whether his success was due to talent or luck, and to write darker, non-supernatural stories without the expectations attached to his name. He persuaded his publishers, Signet Books, to release the earlier, mostly non-horror manuscripts under the Bachman byline.

The early Bachman books were issued as paperback originals to modest sales — until their authorship became known.

The Bachman books

  • Rage (1977) — a school shooting novel, later allowed to go out of print by King.
  • The Long Walk (1979) — dystopian death-march contest.
  • Roadwork (1981) — a man's breakdown as a highway project destroys his life.
  • The Running Man (1982) — dystopian game show novel, written, King says, in a week.
  • Thinner (1984) — a cursed, rapidly thinning lawyer; the first Bachman hardcover and the book that broke the secret.
  • The Regulators (1996) and Blaze (2007) — later revivals of the name.

Exposure

Washington, D.C. bookstore clerk and researcher Steve Brown noticed stylistic similarities, found copyright records naming King, and confronted the publisher; King confirmed the secret in 1985 and announced that Bachman had died of "cancer of the pseudonym, a severe form of schotenfreude". The previously obscure Bachman titles then shot up the bestseller lists when reissued as King's, and the first four were collected as The Bachman Books (1985).

Character

King constructed a mock biography for Bachman, and a photograph of insurance agent Richard Manuel posed as the author on early editions. In King's Dark Tower mythology and essays such as "The Importance of Being Bachman", the alter ego represents King's darker, more pessimistic voice.
