Sous-chef

Sous-chef
French: sous-chef de cuisine
Rank: second-in-command of a professional kitchen
Reports to: executive chef / chef de cuisine

A sous-chef is a chef who is second in command of a kitchen after the executive chef. In a large establishment the sous-chef reports directly to the executive chef (or head chef) and is responsible for the day-to-day running of the kitchen: scheduling staff, supervising the line cooks, expediting orders, monitoring food quality, and stepping in to cook or to run the kitchen entirely when the head chef is absent.

Role and responsibilities

The sous-chef occupies a position in the kitchen brigade system codified by Georges Auguste Escoffier. Typical duties include:

  • Supervising and coordinating the work of the chefs de partie and line cooks
  • Expediting — calling orders and checking plates before they leave the pass
  • Ordering ingredients and managing inventory and food costs
  • Training new kitchen staff and enforcing hygiene and safety standards
  • Developing specials and assisting with menu planning
  • Cooking on the line during service, especially on the busiest stations

Terminology

The term comes from French, where sous means "under" — the sous-chef is literally the "under-chef". In smaller kitchens there may be only one sous-chef, while large hotels and restaurants may employ several, sometimes including a senior sous-chef and an executive sous-chef who ranks just below the executive chef.

Career path

Sous-chefs are typically experienced cooks who have worked several stations as a chef de partie. The sous-chef position is the usual stepping stone to becoming an executive chef or opening one's own restaurant.
