Barisan Nasional (BN; English: National Front) is a political coalition in Malaysia, founded in 1973 as the successor to the Alliance Party. Its core component parties are the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO), the Malaysian Chinese Association (MCA), and the Malaysian Indian Congress (MIC), representing respectively the Malay, Chinese, and Indian communities of the federation under a consociational power-sharing model.

History

The Alliance of UMNO, MCA, and MIC had governed Malaya and then Malaysia since before independence in 1957. After the racial riots of 13 May 1969 and the suspension of parliament, Prime Minister Abdul Razak Hussein broadened the ruling coalition, bringing in additional parties from both Peninsular Malaysia and the Borneo states to form Barisan Nasional in 1973. The coalition then won every general election for more than four decades, governing Malaysia continuously under prime ministers including Mahathir Mohamad (1981–2003), and presiding over rapid economic development as well as increasingly dominant-party politics.

Loss of power and after

In the general election of 9 May 2018, Barisan Nasional lost power for the first time in Malaysia's history to the Pakatan Harapan coalition led by Mahathir — a defeat attributed in part to the 1MDB corruption scandal surrounding Prime Minister Najib Razak. Several component parties left the coalition, reducing it to a handful of members. BN returned to government as part of coalition arrangements following the political crisis of 2020, and after the 2022 general election it joined a "unity government" led by Anwar Ibrahim, with BN figures holding senior posts including a deputy premiership.
