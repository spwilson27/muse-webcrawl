Das Kapital (Capital: A Critique of Political Economy) is a foundational theoretical text in materialist philosophy and political economy written by Karl Marx. Its first volume was published in 1867; Marx died before completing the work, and Friedrich Engels edited and published volumes II and III from Marx's manuscripts in 1885 and 1894.

Contents

Volume I analyses the commodity, the labour theory of value, and surplus value — the value appropriated by capitalists from workers beyond the wage paid for their labour power. It examines the working day, machinery and large-scale industry, and the process of "primitive accumulation" by which a propertyless workforce was created. Volume II addresses the circulation of capital, and Volume III the distribution of surplus value as profit, interest, and rent, including the tendency of the rate of profit to fall.

Method

Marx combined economic analysis with history, philosophy, and extensive use of official reports, such as British factory inspectors' reports. His approach, often called historical materialism, sought to reveal the laws of motion of capitalist society and its internal contradictions.

Reception and influence

Das Kapital became the central theoretical text of Marxism and shaped socialist and labour movements worldwide. Economists continue to dispute its value theory and predictions, while its analyses of commodification, accumulation, and crisis remain influential in academic and political debate.
