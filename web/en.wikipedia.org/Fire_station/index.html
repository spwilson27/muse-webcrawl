Fire station

A fire station with its appliance bay doors open.

A fire station (also called a fire house, fire hall, firemen's hall, or engine house) is a structure or other area for storing firefighting apparatuses such as fire engines and related vehicles, personal protective equipment, fire hoses and other specialised equipment. It may also have residential and work areas for firefighters, since a crew is typically on duty at the station around the clock.

Facilities

A fire station will at a minimum have a garage for housing at least one fire engine ("appliance bay"). There will also be storage space for equipment, though the most important equipment is carried in the vehicles themselves. Approaches to a fire station are often marked with warning signs, and traffic signals may be installed to stop traffic when apparatus leaves.

Because firefighters may be on duty for long shifts — commonly 24 hours — stations include living quarters: a kitchen and dining area, dormitory or bedrooms, a day room, and facilities for training and exercise. Communications equipment, offices, and a watch room connect the station to dispatch. Historically, stations in multi-storey buildings had a brass fireman's pole allowing rapid descent to the appliance bay; modern designs often place accommodation on the same level or use slides, for safety.

History

Organised fire stations emerged in the nineteenth century as municipal fire brigades replaced insurance-company and volunteer brigades; early stations also stabled the horses that pulled steam fire engines. Stations were often built as civic landmarks with distinctive towers originally used for drying hoses. Today, station location is planned around target response times to the surrounding district, and stations may be combined with ambulance or police facilities.
