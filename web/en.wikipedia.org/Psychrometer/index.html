Psychrometer

A psychrometer measures atmospheric humidity using two thermometers — a dry bulb and a wet bulb — and the cooling effect of evaporation.

A psychrometer is a hygrometer that measures relative humidity in the atmosphere. It consists of two thermometers: a dry-bulb thermometer that measures ambient air temperature, and a wet-bulb thermometer whose bulb is covered with a wet wick. Evaporation from the wick cools the wet bulb, and the temperature difference between the two readings is used, with psychrometric tables or charts, to determine humidity and dew point.

Types

In a sling psychrometer, the thermometers are mounted on a handle and whirled in the air to provide ventilation; it is simple and portable but has largely been superseded by electronic instruments. The Assmann aspiration psychrometer uses a fan to draw air past shielded thermometers, giving more accurate readings. Modern digital psychrometers use capacitive or resistive humidity sensors.

Principle

The drier the air, the faster water evaporates from the wet wick and the larger the wet-bulb depression. When the air is saturated, no evaporation occurs and the two thermometers read the same. Measurements must be taken with adequate ventilation and shielding from radiant heat, and the wick must be kept clean and wet with distilled water.
