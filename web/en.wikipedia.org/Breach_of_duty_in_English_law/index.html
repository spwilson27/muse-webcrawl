Breach of duty in English law

Breach of duty is a key element of liability in the tort of negligence in English law. Once a duty of care is established, the claimant must show that the defendant fell below the standard of care expected of a reasonable person in the defendant's position.

The standard of care

The benchmark is the ordinary skilled person exercising the relevant skill, laid down in Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee [1957] for professionals. The standard is objective, though it is adjusted for children (Mullin v Richards) and takes account of the defendant's knowledge at the time of the act.

Factors considered

Courts weigh the likelihood of harm (Bolton v Stone), the seriousness of potential injury (Paris v Stepney Borough Council), the cost and practicability of precautions (Latimer v AEC), and the social utility of the defendant's activity (Watt v Hertfordshire County Council). Together these form part of the informal "calculus of negligence".

Proof

The burden of proof lies on the claimant on the balance of probabilities. In some cases the maxim res ipsa loquitur allows negligence to be inferred from the mere fact that an accident occurred under the defendant's control.
