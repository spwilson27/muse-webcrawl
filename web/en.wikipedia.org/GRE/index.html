Graduate Record Examinations

The Graduate Record Examinations (GRE) is a standardized test created and administered by the Educational Testing Service (ETS) that is an admissions requirement for many graduate schools in the United States and Canada, and a growing number elsewhere. Established in 1936 by the Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching, the GRE aims to measure verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, analytical writing and critical thinking skills acquired over a long period of learning.

Structure

The computer-delivered GRE General Test consists of Analytical Writing, Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning sections, with section-level adaptive testing: performance on the first section of each type determines the difficulty of the second. Verbal and Quantitative scores are reported on a 130–170 scale, and Analytical Writing on a 0–6 scale. In September 2023 ETS shortened the test to just under two hours, removing the unscored section and one essay task.

Use

Scores are valid for five years. Many programmes have made the GRE optional in recent years, though it remains widely accepted for master's, MBA and doctoral admissions, and ETS also administers subject tests in disciplines such as psychology and physics.
