Vincent Mangano (born Vincenzo Giovanni Mangano; March 28, 1888 – disappeared April 19, 1951, declared dead October 30, 1961) was an Italian-born mobster who headed the Mangano crime family of New York City from 1931 to 1951. The family, operating on the Brooklyn waterfront, later became known as the Gambino crime family.

Boss of the family

Mangano took control of the family after the Castellammarese War, in which his predecessor Salvatore D'Aquila had been murdered in 1928 and the organisation passed through the hands of Al Mineo. Mangano was a conservative, old-school Sicilian boss and a founding member of the Mafia Commission. He controlled the Brooklyn docks and the International Longshoremen's Association rackets through his brother Philip Mangano and associates.

Disappearance

Mangano's underboss, Albert Anastasia, rose in power and grew increasingly independent, particularly through his control of Murder, Inc. On 19 April 1951 Philip Mangano was found shot dead near Sheepshead Bay, Brooklyn, and Vincent Mangano disappeared the same day; his body was never found. Anastasia is generally believed to have ordered the killings, and he assumed leadership of the family, renaming the era that followed.
