Laminated glass

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Laminated glass is a type of safety glass consisting of two or more layers of glass bonded together with an interlayer, usually of polyvinyl butyral (PVB) or ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA), under heat and pressure. If broken, the glass fragments adhere to the interlayer rather than scattering into sharp shards, reducing the risk of injury.

History

Laminated glass was invented in 1903 by the French chemist Édouard Bénédictus, who was inspired by a laboratory flask that had cracked but held together because of a cellulose nitrate coating inside. It was used in gas mask lenses during the First World War and became standard in automobile windscreens in the following decades.

Applications

Laminated glass is mandated for vehicle windshields in most countries and is used in skylights, hurricane-resistant glazing, bullet-resistant glass, glass floors, and aquariums. The interlayer also blocks most ultraviolet radiation and improves sound insulation.
