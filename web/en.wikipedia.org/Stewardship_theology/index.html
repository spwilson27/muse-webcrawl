Stewardship theology

The Parable of the Talents, a central stewardship text, in which servants are judged by their care of a master's goods.

Stewardship theology is a theological view within Christianity (with parallels in other faiths) holding that human beings are responsible for the world, and its resources and gifts, as stewards or caretakers on behalf of God rather than as absolute owners. It grounds practices of generosity, tithing, and care for creation in passages of the Bible.

Biblical basis

The concept draws on Genesis 1:28 and 2:15, where humanity is placed in the garden "to work it and take care of it"; on Psalm 24:1 ("The earth is the Lord's, and everything in it"); and on Jesus' parables of the steward and the talents (Matthew 25; Luke 12 and 16), in which servants must render an account of what was entrusted to them.

Applications

Stewardship teaching traditionally addresses three areas: time, talent and treasure — the sharing of resources with the church and the poor, including the tithe. Since the late 20th century it has been extended strongly to environmental ethics: "creation care" movements and documents such as Pope Francis's 2015 encyclical Laudato si' frame protection of the natural world as a core stewardship duty.

Criticism

The dominion language of Genesis has been criticised (notably by historian Lynn White Jr. in 1967) as licensing exploitation of nature; stewardship theologians respond that the texts mandate accountable care, not ownership. Stewardship campaigns in fundraising are also sometimes criticised as reducing theology to giving programmes.
