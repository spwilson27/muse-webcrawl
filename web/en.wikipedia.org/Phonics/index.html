Type: Reading instruction method
Focus: Letter–sound correspondences

Phonics

Phonics is a method for teaching reading and writing of an alphabetic language by developing learners' phonemic awareness — the ability to hear, identify, and manipulate phonemes — in order to teach the correspondence between these sounds and the spelling patterns (graphemes) that represent them.

Approaches

In synthetic phonics, learners are taught letter–sound correspondences one at a time and blend the sounds to read words. Analytic phonics works from whole known words to deduce patterns. Systematic phonics instruction has been found in major reviews, such as the U.S. National Reading Panel (2000), to benefit beginning readers compared with unsystematic or no phonics instruction.

Debate

Phonics has been at the centre of the "reading wars" with whole-language approaches, which emphasise meaning and context. Most modern curricula combine phonics with other instruction, though England and other jurisdictions now mandate systematic synthetic phonics in early reading.
