Generalized taxicab number

In mathematics, the generalized taxicab number Taxicab(k, j, n) is the smallest number that can be expressed as a sum of j k-th powers in n different ways, where the powers are positive integers. The concept generalises the classical taxicab numbers, which concern sums of two cubes.

Classical case

The ordinary taxicab number Ta(n) is Taxicab(3, 2, n): the smallest number expressible as a sum of two positive cubes in n distinct ways. The most famous is Ta(2) = 1729 = 13 + 123 = 93 + 103, known as the Hardy–Ramanujan number after an anecdote in which G. H. Hardy remarked that the number of his taxi, 1729, seemed dull, and Srinivasa Ramanujan replied that it was "a very interesting number" for exactly this property. Ta(3) = 87,539,319 was found by John Leech in 1957, and higher values have been computed by several authors, with Ta(6) established in the early twenty-first century.

General families

Taxicab(1, j, n) is trivial, since any integer has a unique representation up to order as a sum of first powers. For squares, Taxicab(2, 2, 2) = 50 = 12 + 72 = 52 + 52. Sums of three cubes, four fourth powers, and other combinations have been studied extensively; Euler's sum of powers conjecture (disproved in 1966 by Lander and Parkin with fifth powers) concerned the related question of when a single k-th power can equal a sum of fewer than k k-th powers.
