PAL (Phase Alternating Line) is a colour encoding system for analogue television. It was one of three major analogue colour television standards, the others being NTSC and SECAM. PAL was developed in West Germany by Walter Bruch at Telefunken and first introduced in 1967. In most countries it was broadcast at 625 lines and 25 frames per second, with variants such as PAL-M (Brazil) and PAL-N (parts of South America) adapting it to other line and frame standards.

Technical principles

PAL encodes colour as two components in quadrature, like NTSC, but inverts the phase of one component on alternate lines. This alternation allows the receiver — in early sets, by a delay line and averaging of adjacent lines — to cancel out phase errors introduced in transmission, which in NTSC visibly shift hues (a defect sometimes mocked as "Never Twice the Same Color"). PAL therefore generally delivered more consistent colour at the cost of somewhat more complex receiver circuitry and, in some implementations, reduced vertical colour resolution.

History and adoption

After its launch in West Germany and the United Kingdom in 1967, PAL was adopted across most of Western Europe (except France, which used SECAM) and by many countries in Asia, Africa, Australasia, and South America. The PAL/SECAM/NTSC division broadly followed broadcasting and geopolitical alignments of the Cold War era. With the transition to digital television (DVB, ISDB, DTMB and others) in the 2000s and 2010s, analogue PAL transmissions were progressively switched off, though PAL timing standards (576i) persist in digital standard-definition video and in legacy media such as DVDs and video games, where "PAL" often denotes the 50 Hz region as opposed to the 60 Hz "NTSC" region.

Variants

PAL-B/G, PAL-I, PAL-D/K, and related letters specify the accompanying RF channel and sound-carrier arrangements used in different countries. PAL-M combined PAL colour with the 525-line/29.97-frame system in Brazil; PAL-N used a modified subcarrier in Argentina, Paraguay, and Uruguay. PALplus was a 1990s extension for 16:9 broadcasts compatible with ordinary receivers.
