Shatabdi Express


First service: 1988
Operator: Indian Railways
Routes: Daytime intercity, metro to regional hubs
Classes: AC Chair Car, Executive Chair Car

The Shatabdi Express is a series of fast daytime passenger trains operated by Indian Railways, connecting metropolitan areas with nearby cities for same-day return journeys. "Shatabdi" means centenary in Hindi; the first service was introduced in 1988 to commemorate the birth centenary of Jawaharlal Nehru, running between New Delhi and Jhansi (later extended to Bhopal).

Services

Shatabdi trains are among the fastest in India on their routes, with modern air-conditioned chair car coaches, onboard catering included in the fare, and limited stops. Notable services include the Bhopal Shatabdi — long the fastest train in India — and the Mumbai–Ahmedabad and Chennai–Bengaluru Shatabdis. Several routes now use LHB coaches and some have been upgraded to or replaced by Vande Bharat Express services.

Related trains

The concept inspired slower overnight counterparts, the Jan Shatabdi Express with cheaper fares, as well as premium trains such as the Rajdhani and Duronto Expresses for longer distances.
