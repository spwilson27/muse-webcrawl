Walter Philip Reuther (September 1, 1907 – May 9, 1970) was an American leader of organized labor and civil rights activist who built the United Automobile Workers (UAW) into one of the most powerful labor unions in the United States. He served as UAW president from 1946 until his death in a plane crash in 1970, and as president of the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO) from 1952 to 1955.

Early career

The son of a German-American brewery worker and union activist, Reuther moved to Detroit, worked as a tool-and-die maker at Ford, and with his brothers Victor and Roy helped organize autoworkers during the 1930s. He was badly beaten by Ford security men in the 1937 "Battle of the Overpass" outside the Ford River Rouge plant — an incident whose photographs brought national attention to the UAW.

UAW presidency

As UAW president, Reuther led the 113-day strike against General Motors in 1945–46 and negotiated landmark contracts, culminating in the 1950 "Treaty of Detroit," which traded long-term labor peace for cost-of-living raises, productivity-linked wage increases, pensions, and health benefits. He survived two assassination attempts, in 1948 and 1949, that were never solved. Reuther was also a committed civil rights supporter, addressed the 1963 March on Washington, and was a founding figure of Americans for Democratic Action.

Death and legacy

Reuther and his wife May died on May 9, 1970, when their chartered Learjet crashed near Pellston, Michigan. He was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1995.
