Laryngoscopy

Laryngoscopy is the endoscopic examination of the larynx, performed to view the vocal cords and glottis for diagnosis, biopsy, removal of foreign bodies, or to facilitate endotracheal intubation during anaesthesia and emergency care.

Types

Indirect laryngoscopy uses a mirror or flexible fibreoptic nasendoscope in the clinic. Direct laryngoscopy, under anaesthesia or sedation, uses a rigid laryngoscope with curved (Macintosh) or straight (Miller) blades. Videolaryngoscopes carry a camera at the blade tip and have improved intubation success in difficult airways.

History

Manuel García demonstrated mirror laryngoscopy in 1854; Kirstein and Chevalier Jackson developed direct techniques in the 1890s–1910s.
