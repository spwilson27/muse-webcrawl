Papatūānuku

Papatūānuku (also spelled Papa) is the primordial earth mother goddess in Māori mythology. She is the wife of Ranginui, the sky father, and the mother of the major gods of the Māori pantheon.

Mythology

In the Māori creation tradition, Rangi and Papa lay locked in a tight embrace, their children trapped in darkness between them. The children — including Tāne, god of forests — eventually forced their parents apart, bringing light into the world; Tāne pushed Ranginui upward while Papatūānuku remained below as the earth.

Significance

Papatūānuku is revered as the land itself, the source of sustenance and the place to which people return at death. The phrase "whenua" in te reo Māori means both land and placenta, reflecting the connection between people and the earth mother.
