Open access

Open access
Models: Gold, green, hybrid, diamond, bronze
Key statements: Budapest (2002), Bethesda (2003), Berlin (2003)
Repositories: arXiv, PubMed Central

Open access (OA) is a set of principles and practices through which research outputs are distributed online, free of access charges or other barriers, and typically with licences permitting reuse. It applies chiefly to peer-reviewed scholarly journal articles, but also to books, conference papers, data and other outputs.

Models

Gold OA means publication in an open access journal, often funded by article processing charges (APCs) paid by authors or funders. Green OA is self-archiving of manuscripts in repositories such as arXiv (founded 1991) or PubMed Central. Diamond OA journals charge neither readers nor authors, being subsidised institutionally. "Hybrid" journals make individual articles open for a fee, a practice criticised as double-dipping when subscriptions are also charged.

History and policy

The movement crystallised with the Budapest Open Access Initiative (2002) and the Bethesda and Berlin declarations (2003). Funder mandates followed: the US National Institutes of Health public access policy (2008), the European "Plan S" launched by cOAlition S in 2018 requiring immediate open access for funded research, and the 2022 US OSTP memorandum directing immediate public access to federally funded research by 2026.

Debates

Open access promises wider readership, faster dissemination and equity for researchers without wealthy libraries. Critics point to APC inflation, predatory journals that exploit the author-pays model, and embargo disputes with publishers. OA articles are generally read and cited more than paywalled equivalents.
