Colubridae

Colubridae (commonly known as colubrids) is a family of snakes, and by far the largest snake family, with roughly 1,900 species — more than half of all living snake species — distributed on every continent except Antarctica. The family includes familiar species such as grass snakes, garter snakes, rat snakes, kingsnakes and racers.

Characteristics

Colubrids vary widely in size, habitat and diet. Most are non-venomous or possess mild venoms delivered by enlarged rear teeth (opisthoglyphous dentition) that are not dangerous to humans, though a few — notably the boomslang and twig snakes — have caused human fatalities. Many colubrids are active diurnal hunters that subdue prey by constriction or by simply swallowing it alive.

Classification

The family's taxonomy has been repeatedly revised by molecular studies, and several former subfamilies, such as the Natricinae (keelbacks and water snakes) and Dipsadinae, are sometimes recognised as separate families. Colubrids belong to the superfamily Colubroidea within the advanced snakes (Alethinophidia).
