Maniraptora

Maniraptora is a clade of coelurosaurian dinosaurs that includes birds (Aves) and the non-avian dinosaurs most closely related to them. Maniraptorans are characterised by arms and three-fingered hands that are long relative to the hind legs, a semilunate (half-moon-shaped) carpal bone in the wrist permitting a swivelling folding motion, and, in most lineages, feathers.

Groups

The main maniraptoran groups are the Alvarezsauridae, Therizinosauria, Oviraptorosauria, Dromaeosauridae, Troodontidae, Scansoriopterygidae, and Avialae (birds and their closest extinct relatives). They range from the tiny four-winged Microraptor and the long-tailed Archaeopteryx to the giant herbivorous therizinosaur Therizinosaurus and the modern ostrich.

Evolution and biology

Maniraptorans originated in the Jurassic period, probably in Asia, and are central to the study of the origin of birds and of flight. Many were feathered, some were capable of powered or gliding flight, and parental care such as brooding is documented in oviraptorosaurs. Only the avian lineage survived the end-Cretaceous extinction 66 million years ago.
