Small Wild Goose Pagoda

LocationXi'an, Shaanxi, China
Built707–709 AD (Tang dynasty)
Height43 m (141 ft)

The Small Wild Goose Pagoda is one of two significant pagodas in Xi'an, Shaanxi, China, the site of the old Han and Tang capital Chang'an. It was built between 707 and 709 during the Tang dynasty under Emperor Zhongzong, within the Jianfu Temple, to house Buddhist scriptures brought from India by the pilgrim monk Yijing.

Architecture and history

The pagoda originally had 15 storeys, but earthquakes — notably the great Shaanxi earthquake of 1556 — damaged its top, leaving the present 13 storeys and a height of about 43 metres. Its curved silhouette results from its brick construction over a rammed-earth core. A famous feature is a long vertical crack caused by seismic activity that later partly closed after a subsequent quake.
