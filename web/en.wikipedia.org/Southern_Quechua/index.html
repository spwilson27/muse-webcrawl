Southern Quechua
Native name: Qhichwa simi / Runasimi
Region: southern Peru, Bolivia, northern Argentina
Speakers: several million (the most widely spoken Quechua variety group)
Family: Quechuan, Quechua II (Southern)

Southern Quechua (Quechua: Urin Qhichwa) is the group of Quechua varieties spoken in the Andes south of a line through central Peru — principally the Cusco and Ayacucho varieties of Peru, Bolivian Quechua, and the Santiago del Estero variety of Argentina. It is the most widely spoken branch of the Quechuan languages, with several million speakers, and corresponds to the "Quechua IIC" branch in linguistic classifications.

Characteristics

Southern Quechua varieties are agglutinative and suffixing, like all Quechua. Cusco–Bolivian varieties are noted for aspirated and ejective (glottalised) stop consonants — a three-way distinction such as /p/, /pʰ/, /p'/ — generally attributed to contact with Aymara, and for a seven-vowel-like surface realisation of the three underlying vowels /i a u/ near uvular consonants. A standardised written form, sometimes called Qhichwa simi, has been promoted for education and media in Peru and Bolivia.

Status

Quechua is an official language in Peru and Bolivia, and Southern Quechua is used in intercultural bilingual education, radio and a growing body of literature and film. Nevertheless speaker numbers are declining in many areas under pressure from Spanish.
