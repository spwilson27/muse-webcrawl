İzmir (Turkish: İzmir; Greek: Σμύρνη, Smyrna) is a metropolitan city on the Aegean coast of Anatolia and the capital of İzmir Province, Turkey. With a population of around three million in the urban area, it is Turkey's third most populous city after Istanbul and Ankara, and its port is the country's second largest after Istanbul.

History

One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities of the Mediterranean, Smyrna's earliest settlement at Bayraklı (Old Smyrna) dates to the 3rd millennium BC. The city was an important Greek and Roman port — traditionally numbered among the seven churches of Revelation — and later a major Ottoman trading hub with large Greek, Armenian, Jewish and Levantine communities.

The Great Fire of Smyrna in September 1922, during the final phase of the Greco-Turkish War, destroyed much of the Greek and Armenian quarters, and the subsequent population exchange transformed the city. Modern İzmir was rebuilt as a republican city, known for the İzmir International Fair, its Aegean lifestyle and landmarks such as the Konak Clock Tower (1901) and the ancient agora.
