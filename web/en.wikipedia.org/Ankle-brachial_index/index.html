Ankle-brachial index

The ankle-brachial index (ABI) is the ratio of the systolic blood pressure at the ankle to the systolic blood pressure in the upper arm (brachium). It is a simple, non-invasive test used to diagnose peripheral artery disease (PAD) and to assess cardiovascular risk.

Method

With the patient supine and rested, blood pressure cuffs are placed on both arms and both ankles. Systolic pressures are measured with a Doppler ultrasound probe at the brachial arteries and at the dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial arteries of each foot. The ABI for each leg is the higher of the two ankle pressures divided by the higher of the two arm pressures.

Interpretation

A normal ABI ranges from about 1.0 to 1.4. Values of 0.9 or below indicate peripheral artery disease, with lower values reflecting more severe obstruction; values between 0.91 and 0.99 are borderline. An ABI above 1.4 suggests non-compressible, calcified arteries — common in diabetes and chronic kidney disease — in which case a toe-brachial index is preferred.

Clinical significance

A low ABI is a strong independent predictor of cardiovascular events and mortality, reflecting systemic atherosclerosis. Guidelines recommend ABI measurement in patients with exertional leg symptoms, non-healing wounds, or elevated cardiovascular risk. Exercise ABI testing can unmask disease when resting values are normal.
