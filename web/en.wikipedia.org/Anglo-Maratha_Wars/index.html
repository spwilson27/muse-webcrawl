Anglo-Maratha Wars

The Anglo-Maratha Wars were three conflicts fought between the British East India Company and the Maratha Empire in India between 1775 and 1819. They ended in the defeat and dissolution of the Maratha Confederacy and the establishment of British paramountcy over most of the Indian subcontinent.

First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782)

The first war arose from rivalry over the succession to the Peshwa's throne at Pune and the Bombay government's support for Raghunath Rao under the Treaty of Surat (1775). After early British reverses, including the humiliating Convention of Wadgaon (1779), the war ended with the Treaty of Salbai (1782), restoring the status quo ante.

Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–1805)

Peshwa Baji Rao II's flight from Pune and the Treaty of Bassein (1802), accepting a British subsidiary alliance, triggered the second war. British armies under Arthur Wellesley in the Deccan and Gerard Lake in Hindustan won striking victories at Assaye, Argaon, Delhi, and Laswari. The Scindias and Bhonsles ceded extensive territory, though the Holkars fought on until 1805.

Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1819)

Also known as the Pindari War, the final conflict began with a British campaign against Pindari raiders. The Peshwa attacked the British at Khadki in November 1817 but was defeated and eventually captured; he was pensioned off to Bithur and the Peshwaship abolished. Scindia was neutralised, Holkar defeated at Mahidpur, and the Rajput states accepted British protection.

Consequences

With the Marathas destroyed, the East India Company controlled virtually all of India south of the Sutlej. The wars demonstrated the effectiveness of disciplined sepoy armies and subsidiary alliances, and removed the last major indigenous military rival to British rule until 1857.
