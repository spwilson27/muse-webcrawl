A water taxi or water bus is a boat used to provide public or private transport, usually — but not always — in an urban environment. A water taxi service may be scheduled with multiple stops, operating in a similar manner to a bus, or on demand to many locations, operating like a taxi. A boat service shuttling between two points would normally be described as a ferry rather than a water bus or taxi, though the terms overlap in practice.

Locations

Water taxis are centuries old in canal cities such as Venice, where sleek wooden motor launches serve as taxis-for-hire alongside the larger vaporetto water buses. Other cities with notable services include Auckland, Baltimore, Boston, Brisbane, Buenos Aires, Cardiff, Copenhagen, Dubai, Halifax, Hong Kong (where small licensed ferries called kai-to serve remote coastal settlements), Istanbul, Jakarta, Karachi, London, New York City, Odessa, Oslo, Perth, San Francisco, Singapore, Stockholm, Sydney, Toronto, Vancouver (the small Aquabus and False Creek Ferries), Victoria, and Wellington.

Vessels and operation

Vessels range from traditional wooden launches to modern catamarans carrying dozens of passengers, and increasingly include electric and solar-powered craft. In cities with congested roads, water taxis can offer faster, more predictable journeys; in resort and harbour cities they function partly as sightseeing transport. Operations may be franchised by public transit authorities, run by municipal ferry companies, or provided by private charter.
