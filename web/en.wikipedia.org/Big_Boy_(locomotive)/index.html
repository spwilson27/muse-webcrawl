The Union Pacific Big Boy is a type of simple articulated 4-8-8-4 steam locomotive manufactured by the American Locomotive Company (ALCO) between 1941 and 1944 and operated by the Union Pacific Railroad. Twenty-five were built to haul heavy freight trains over the Wasatch Range between Ogden, Utah, and Green River, Wyoming.

Design

The Big Boys were the longest steam locomotives ever built when combined with their tenders, at about 40.5 metres (133 ft), and among the heaviest, at roughly 540 tonnes. They were designed for sustained power — about 6,290 horsepower at the drawbar — at speeds up to 130 km/h, combining the tractive effort of slower freight engines with the speed of passenger types.

Service and retirement

The class worked through the Second World War and the 1950s, each accumulating over a million miles. The last revenue run took place in 1959, and the fleet was retired by 1962 as diesels took over. Eight Big Boys survive in museums across the United States.

No. 4014

Union Pacific reacquired No. 4014 from a museum in Pomona, California, in 2013 and restored it to operating condition at its Cheyenne steam shop. It returned to service in May 2019 for the 150th anniversary of the first transcontinental railroad, becoming the largest operating steam locomotive in the world and the centrepiece of UP's heritage programme.
