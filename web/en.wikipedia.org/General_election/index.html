General election

A general election is an election in which all or most members of a given political body are chosen at the same time. The term is used in many countries to distinguish from by-elections (held to fill a single vacancy) and from primary elections. In parliamentary systems, a general election decides the composition of the legislature and therefore usually determines the government; in presidential systems such as the United States, "general election" refers to the final election for an office as opposed to the primaries that select each party's nominee.

Timing

General elections may be held on fixed dates — as in the United States, where federal elections occur on the Tuesday after the first Monday in November of even-numbered years — or at times chosen within limits by the government of the day, as historically in the United Kingdom and Canada, subject to a maximum parliamentary term (five years in the UK, where the Fixed-term Parliaments Act 2011 briefly imposed fixed terms before its repeal in 2022). Early or "snap" elections occur when a parliament is dissolved before its term expires.

Usage by country

In the United Kingdom, a general election elects all 650 members of the House of Commons. In India, general elections elect the Lok Sabha, the lower house of parliament, in the largest democratic exercise in the world. In Japan, the term refers specifically to elections of the House of Representatives. In the United States, the general election in a presidential year simultaneously elects the president (through the Electoral College), all 435 members of the House of Representatives, and roughly one-third of the Senate, along with many state and local offices.
