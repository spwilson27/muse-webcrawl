Killers of the Flower Moon is the title of both a 2017 nonfiction book by American journalist David Grann and a 2023 epic Western crime drama film directed by Martin Scorsese. Both works recount the Osage Indian murders: a series of killings of wealthy Osage Nation members in Osage County, Oklahoma, during the 1910s–1930s, after large oil deposits were discovered beneath their land.

The Osage murders

After oil was found on Osage land, tribal members receiving "headright" payments became among the wealthiest people on earth. Beginning in 1921, dozens of Osage — and some who investigated the crimes — were murdered, poisoned, or died in suspicious circumstances as white guardians and opportunists sought control of their wealth. The case was taken up by the young Bureau of Investigation under J. Edgar Hoover; agent Tom White led the undercover investigation that exposed rancher William King Hale and his nephew Ernest Burkhart. Hale had orchestrated murders including those of members of the family of Burkhart's Osage wife, Mollie Kyle. Hale and Burkhart were convicted in trials that became national sensations.

The book

Grann's book, subtitled The Osage Murders and the Birth of the FBI, was a bestseller and a National Book Award finalist. Its third section suggests the official death toll of roughly two dozen significantly understates the true number of killings, which may have run into the hundreds.

The film

Scorsese's film adaptation, starring Leonardo DiCaprio as Ernest Burkhart, Lily Gladstone as Mollie Kyle (Burkhart), and Robert De Niro as William Hale, premiered at the Cannes Film Festival in May 2023 and was released by Apple TV+ and Paramount Pictures. Running 206 minutes and made for about $200 million, it was acclaimed by critics and received ten Academy Award nominations, with Gladstone winning the Golden Globe and Screen Actors Guild awards for her performance.
