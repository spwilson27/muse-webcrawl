Streisand effect

The Streisand effect is an unintended consequence of attempting to suppress information: efforts to hide, remove, or censor something instead draw far greater public attention to it. The phenomenon is closely tied to the speed and decentralization of the internet.

Origin

The term was coined by Techdirt founder Mike Masnick after singer Barbra Streisand sued photographer Kenneth Adelman in 2003 to remove an aerial photograph of her Malibu residence from a public database of California coastline erosion photos. The image, which had been downloaded only a handful of times before the suit, was viewed hundreds of thousands of times as a result of the publicity; the lawsuit was dismissed and Streisand was ordered to pay the defendant's legal fees.

Examples

Well-known cases include legal threats over the AACS encryption key in 2007, which spread the number across the web; repeated attempts by organizations to suppress leaked documents and critical reporting; and celebrities' takedown demands that turn obscure material into viral news. The effect is often cited in discussions of censorship, public relations, and internet freedom.
