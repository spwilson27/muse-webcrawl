Legal deposit

Legal deposit is a legal requirement that a person or group submit copies of their publications to a repository, usually a national library, for preservation and public record.

PurposePreservation of published heritage
Typical recipientNational library

History

One of the earliest systems was the French Ordonnance de Montpellier of 1537, requiring copies for the royal library. Many countries adopted similar laws in the 17th–19th centuries.

By country

In the United Kingdom deposit libraries include the British Library and five others. In the United States, mandatory deposit to the Library of Congress is tied to copyright registration. Digital publications are increasingly included. See library and national library.
