Stasi Records Agency

Bundesbeauftragter für die Stasi-Unterlagen (BStU)
Formed: 1990 (as special commissioner); 1991 under the Stasi Records Act
Dissolved: June 17, 2021 (integrated into the German Federal Archives)
Headquarters: Berlin
Commissioners: Joachim Gauck (1990–2000), Marianne Birthler (2000–2011), Roland Jahn (2011–2021)

The Stasi Records Agency (German: Bundesbeauftragter für die Unterlagen des Staatssicherheitsdienstes der ehemaligen Deutschen Demokratischen Republik, abbreviated BStU) was the German federal agency that preserved and protected the archives of the Stasi, the secret police of the former East Germany, and investigated its past activities.

History

During the Peaceful Revolution of 1989–90, East German citizens occupied Stasi offices to prevent the destruction of files. After reunification, the Stasi Records Act (Stasi-Unterlagen-Gesetz) of December 1991 gave the agency a legal basis. Its first head, Joachim Gauck, gave the institution its popular name, the "Gauck Authority" (Gauck-Behörde).

Holdings and access

The archives contain roughly 111 kilometres of files, millions of index cards, photographs, and audio recordings, as well as some 15,000 sacks of shredded documents, a portion of which have been reconstructed by hand and by computer-assisted methods. Citizens, researchers, journalists, and public bodies could apply to inspect files; millions of applications were processed over the agency's existence.

Integration into the Federal Archives

In 2021, the BStU was dissolved as an independent authority and its holdings were transferred to the German Federal Archives (Bundesarchiv), where the Stasi records remain accessible under the same legal provisions.
