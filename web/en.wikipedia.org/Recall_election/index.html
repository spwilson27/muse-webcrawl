Recall election

A recall election (also called a recall referendum or representative recall) is a procedure by which voters can remove an elected official from office through a referendum before that official's term has ended. Recalls, which initiate when sufficient voters sign a petition, have a history dating back to ancient Athenian democracy and feature in several current constitutions.

History

Recall provisions originated in the United States Progressive Era, when reformers sought tools to curb corruption and machine politics. California adopted the recall in 1911 under Governor Hiram Johnson; the first successful statewide recall in the United States removed North Dakota governor Lynn Frazier in 1921. The 2003 California gubernatorial recall removed Gray Davis and elected Arnold Schwarzenegger.

Procedure

Typically, proponents must collect signatures equal to a specified percentage of votes cast in the previous election within a set period. If enough valid signatures are verified, a recall election is scheduled, often combined with a simultaneous vote on a successor.

Use worldwide

Recall mechanisms exist in parts of Canada, Venezuela — where a 2004 referendum attempted to recall President Hugo Chávez — Taiwan, and various local jurisdictions elsewhere. Critics argue recalls encourage perpetual campaigning; supporters see them as a vital check between elections.
