United States v. Windsor

Full case nameUnited States v. Edith Schlain Windsor, in Her Capacity as Executor of the Estate of Thea Clara Spyer, et al.
Argued27 March 2013
Decided26 June 2013
Citation570 U.S. 744
Decision5–4

United States v. Windsor, 570 U.S. 744 (2013), is a landmark United States Supreme Court case concerning same-sex marriage. The Court held that Section 3 of the Defense of Marriage Act (DOMA), which denied federal recognition of same-sex marriages, was a violation of the Fifth Amendment's guarantee of liberty and equality, as it was motivated by an improper purpose of demeaning the existence of same-sex married couples.

Background

Edith Windsor and Thea Spyer, a same-sex couple residing in New York, married in Canada in 2007. When Spyer died in 2009, she left her entire estate to Windsor. Because DOMA barred the federal government from recognizing their marriage, Windsor was denied the marital exemption from the federal estate tax and was required to pay $363,053 in taxes. Windsor sued, arguing DOMA was unconstitutional.

Decision

In a 5–4 decision authored by Justice Anthony Kennedy, the Court held that DOMA's definition of marriage as only between a man and a woman deprived same-sex couples of the liberty protected by the Due Process Clause of the Fifth Amendment. The opinion emphasized that the law's principal purpose was to impose inequality on marriages that states had chosen to recognize.

Impact

Following Windsor, federal agencies extended recognition to lawful same-sex marriages, and the ruling's reasoning paved the way for Obergefell v. Hodges (2015), which legalized same-sex marriage nationwide. Windsor, who became a prominent LGBTQ rights figure, died in 2017.
