Formula 4 (F4) is an open-wheel racing car category intended for junior drivers. There is no global championship, but rather individual nations or regions can host their own championships adhering to a universal set of rules and specifications. The category was created in 2014 by the FIA as an entry-level step for drivers progressing from karting towards Formula 3 and ultimately Formula One.

Background

FIA president Jean Todt and former Formula One driver Gerhard Berger, as head of the FIA Single-Seater Commission, designed F4 to standardise the chaotic landscape of entry-level series and to control costs. The first championship, the Italian F4 Championship, began in 2014; series followed in Germany (ADAC F4), Britain, Spain, Japan, China, the United States, the UAE and many other countries.

The cars

F4 cars use carbon-fibre monocoque chassis built by approved manufacturers such as Tatuus and Mygale, powered by 1.4- to 2.0-litre engines (commonly Abarth, Honda, Ford or Renault units) producing roughly 160–210 horsepower. Second-generation cars introduced from 2022 added the halo cockpit protection device. Drivers as young as 15 may compete, and results earn FIA Super Licence points.

Role in the ladder

F4 sits at the base of the FIA Global Pathway: karting → Formula 4 → Formula Regional / Formula 3 → Formula 2 → Formula One. Notable F4 champions include Lando Norris, Mick Schumacher, Théo Pourchaire and Andrea Kimi Antonelli.
