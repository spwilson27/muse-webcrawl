Port of Ningbo-Zhoushan

The Port of Ningbo-Zhoushan is the busiest port in the world in terms of cargo tonnage, handling well over one billion tonnes of cargo annually. Located in Ningbo and Zhoushan on the coast of the East China Sea in Zhejiang Province, China, the port was formed in 2006 by the merger of the former Port of Ningbo and the Port of Zhoushan. It lies on the southern flank of the Yangtze River Delta economic region and serves as one of the principal gateways for China's foreign trade.

Operations

The port comprises multiple port areas, including Beilun, Zhenhai, Chuanshan, and Jintang, spread across the mainland coast and the islands of the Zhoushan Archipelago. It handles containers (more than 30 million TEU per year, ranking among the top three container ports worldwide), crude oil, iron ore, coal, and liquid chemical products, and has shipping connections to more than 600 ports in over 190 countries and regions. Large crude oil and ore terminals allow it to receive some of the world's biggest vessels, including 400,000-ton very large ore carriers.

History

Ningbo has been a major trading port since the Tang dynasty, when it was known as Mingzhou and served as a terminus of the maritime Silk Road. The modern deep-water port dates from the development of Beilun from the late 1970s, following China's opening up. The administrative merger with Zhoushan in 2006 and the completion of the Hangzhou Bay Bridge in 2008 strengthened its position relative to the nearby Port of Shanghai, with which it competes and cooperates within the Yangtze Delta port cluster.
