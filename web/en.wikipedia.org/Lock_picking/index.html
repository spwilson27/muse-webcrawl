Lock picking

Lock picks and tension tools

Lock picking is the practice of unlocking a lock by manipulating the components of the lock device without the original key. Although lock-picking can be associated with criminal intent, it is an essential skill for the legitimate profession of locksmithing, and is also pursued by law-abiding citizens as a useful skill to learn, or simply as a hobby known as "locksport".

History

Locks have existed for thousands of years, and for as long as they have existed, attempts have been made to defeat them. In the 18th and 19th centuries, celebrated locksmiths such as Alfred Charles Hobbs demonstrated the vulnerability of supposedly unpickable locks, spurring improvements in lock design. The modern hobby of locksport grew in the late 20th century with groups dedicated to the ethical study of locks and locking mechanisms.

Techniques

The most common lock type encountered is the pin tumbler lock. To pick it, a tension wrench applies rotational pressure to the plug while a pick lifts each pin stack to the shear line, where binding pins set one by one until the plug turns. Other techniques include raking, in which a broad pick is moved rapidly in and out to set pins by chance, and single-pin picking, the more methodical approach. Wafer locks, disc detainer locks and tubular locks each require specialised tools and methods.

Legality

The legal status of lock picking tools varies by jurisdiction. In many places, mere possession of picks is legal, but possession with intent to commit a crime is an offence; some jurisdictions treat picks as burglary tools requiring proof of lawful purpose. Locksmiths are licensed or regulated in a number of regions.
