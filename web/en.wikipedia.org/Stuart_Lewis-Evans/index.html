Stuart Lewis-Evans

Stuart Nigel Lewis-Evans (20 April 1930 – 25 October 1958) was a British racing driver from England. He participated in 14 Formula One World Championship Grands Prix, debuting on 19 May 1957. He achieved two pole positions, two podium finishes and scored a total of 16 championship points. He was a protégé of Bernie Ecclestone, who acted as his manager early in his career.

Lewis-Evans was regarded as one of the most promising British drivers of his generation before his death from burns sustained in a crash at the 1958 Moroccan Grand Prix.

Career

Lewis-Evans began racing in Formula Three in the early 1950s, driving Coopers with conspicuous success, and was spotted by the Connaught team, for whom he made his World Championship debut at Monaco in 1957, finishing fourth. Tony Vandervell signed him for the Vanwall team, where he became a front-line driver alongside Stirling Moss and Tony Brooks. In 1957 he took pole position at the Italian Grand Prix at Monza and finished third in Belgium in 1958, also taking pole in the Dutch Grand Prix that year.

Death

At the season-ending Moroccan Grand Prix at Ain-Diab, Casablanca, on 19 October 1958, Lewis-Evans's Vanwall suffered an engine seizure at high speed; the car crashed into the barriers and caught fire. Badly burned, he was flown back to England to the specialist burns unit at East Grinstead, where he died six days later, on 25 October 1958, aged 28. Vanwall, which had just won the inaugural Constructors' Championship, withdrew from full-time racing shortly afterwards, in part because of the tragedy and Vandervell's ill health.
