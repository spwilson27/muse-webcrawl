Cookware and bakeware

Cookware and bakeware are food preparation equipment, such as cooking pots, pans, and baking sheets, for use in a kitchen. Cookware is used on a stove or range cooktop, while bakeware is used in an oven. Some utensils are considered both cookware and bakeware.

Materials

Common materials include aluminium (lightweight and an excellent heat conductor), cast iron (heavy, heat-retentive, and suitable for searing), stainless steel (durable and non-reactive, often clad over an aluminium or copper core), copper (superb conductivity but requiring maintenance), and non-stick coatings that ease release and cleaning but degrade at high temperatures. Bakeware also uses glass, ceramics, and silicone.

Types

Principal forms of cookware include frying pans and skillets, saucepans, stockpots, Dutch ovens, woks, griddles, and roasting pans. Bakeware includes cake tins, loaf pans, muffin tins, baking trays, and casserole dishes. The choice of vessel affects browning, evaporation, and heat distribution, and thus the outcome of recipes.

History

Pottery vessels were used for cooking for millennia before metalworking introduced bronze, iron, and copper pots. Industrialisation in the 19th and 20th centuries brought pressed aluminium, enamelled steel, and eventually non-stick and multi-ply clad cookware to domestic kitchens. See also pan and oven.
