Emperor of Japan

The Emperor of Japan is the monarch and the head of the Imperial Family of Japan. Under the Constitution of Japan, he is defined as "the Symbol of the State and of the Unity of the People" and his title is derived from "the will of the people with whom resides sovereign power". The Imperial Household Law governs the line of imperial succession. The emperor is the head of the Shinto religion, to which he is bound by mythological tradition as a direct descendant of the sun goddess Amaterasu. The current emperor is Naruhito, who ascended the Chrysanthemum Throne on 1 May 2019, following the abdication of his father, Akihito.

History

According to Japanese tradition, the imperial line was founded by Emperor Jimmu in 660 BC, though historians generally date the verifiable line to the 5th–6th centuries AD. For much of Japanese history, actual political power was held by others — Fujiwara regents in the Heian period, and later the shogunates — while the emperor reigned as a symbolic and religious authority. The Meiji Restoration of 1868 restored imperial rule in name and made the emperor head of state of the Empire of Japan. Following Japan's defeat in the Second World War, the 1947 constitution redefined the emperor as a symbol without political power.

Constitutional role

The Emperor performs acts in matters of state prescribed by the Constitution, such as promulgating laws, convening the Diet, appointing the Prime Minister as designated by the Diet, and receiving foreign ambassadors, all with the advice and approval of the Cabinet. He has no powers related to government. He also performs traditional religious rites of the Imperial House, including the annual rice-planting ceremony and the Daijōsai performed once after an accession.

Succession

Succession is governed by the Imperial Household Law of 1947, which limits the throne to male offspring in the male line. The Emperor resides at the Imperial Palace in Tokyo; the enthronement ceremony is held there, while the Daijōsai traditionally takes place in a temporary hall.
