The Paradox of Choice

The Paradox of Choice: Why More Is Less
AuthorBarry Schwartz
Published2004
SubjectPsychology, decision-making

The Paradox of Choice: Why More Is Less is a book written by American psychologist Barry Schwartz and first published in 2004 by Harper Perennial. In the book, Schwartz argues that eliminating consumer choices can greatly reduce anxiety for shoppers and decision-makers in general. Although autonomy and freedom of choice are generally held to be good for well-being, Schwartz contends that an overabundance of options can produce decision paralysis, dissatisfaction, and regret.

Argument

Schwartz describes two styles of decision-making. Maximizers attempt to make the objectively best choice and tend to be less satisfied with their decisions, while satisficers settle for options that meet a "good enough" standard and are generally happier. As the number of options grows, the opportunity costs of the alternatives become more salient, escalation of expectations increases, and people are more likely to blame themselves when outcomes disappoint.

The book draws on studies such as the famous jam experiment by Sheena Iyengar and Mark Lepper, in which shoppers presented with 24 varieties of jam were far less likely to buy than those shown only six.

Reception

The book was widely discussed in popular media and helped popularize the idea of "choice overload." Later meta-analyses found the choice-overload effect to be smaller and more context-dependent than initially reported, a point Schwartz has acknowledged while maintaining that modern consumer environments routinely overwhelm decision-makers.
