Dolby Stereo


Illustration: Dolby Stereo

Dolby Stereo is a sound technology developed by Dolby Laboratories that brought multichannel surround sound to cinemas in the 1970s. It encodes four channels of audio — left, centre, right and a single surround channel — into two optical tracks on 35 mm film (a system known as 4-2-4).

History

The format debuted with films such as A Star Is Born (1976) and achieved landmark success with Star Wars (1977), whose dramatic sound design demonstrated the commercial power of quality cinema audio. A home version, Dolby Surround, and its successor Dolby Pro Logic brought the matrixed surround concept to home theatres in the 1980s.

Legacy

Dolby Stereo was superseded in the 1990s by discrete digital formats such as Dolby Digital, first used on Batman Returns (1992), but its centre and surround channels established the layout still used by modern sound systems.
