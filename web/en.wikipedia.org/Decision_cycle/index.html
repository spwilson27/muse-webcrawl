Decision cycle

The decision cycle is a sequence of steps used by an entity on a repeated basis to reach and implement decisions and to learn from the results. The term has been used to describe many cycles in business, military, design and management contexts, and is closely related to the OODA loop and the Deming cycle.

Overview

A decision cycle typically involves recognising a need or problem, gathering information, generating alternative courses of action, evaluating those alternatives, choosing one, implementing it, and reviewing the outcome. The review stage feeds back into the start of the next cycle, making the process iterative rather than one-off.

Variants

Well-known variants include the PDCA cycle (plan–do–check–act) used in quality management, the OODA loop (observe–orient–decide–act) developed by military strategist John Boyd, and the design thinking cycle of empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test. In competitive settings, completing the cycle faster than an opponent is often seen as an advantage.

Applications

Decision cycles are used in incident response, product development, wargaming and organisational learning, where shortening the cycle and improving the quality of each stage are common goals.
