Desilu Productions

Desilu Productions, Inc.
Founded: 1950
Founders: Lucille Ball and Desi Arnaz
Headquarters: Los Angeles, California
Defunct: 1968 (sold to Gulf+Western)

Desilu Productions, Inc. was an American television production company founded in 1950 by husband-and-wife team Lucille Ball and Desi Arnaz. The name is a portmanteau of their first names. Desilu became one of the most important independent producers in early American television.

I Love Lucy and technical innovation

Desilu's first and greatest success was I Love Lucy (1951–1957). At Arnaz's insistence, the show was filmed in Hollywood using a three-camera setup before a live studio audience, on 35mm film rather than via kinescope recordings. Because Desilu bore the extra cost, it negotiated ownership of the film negatives — a landmark arrangement. The high-quality films could be rerun indefinitely, effectively inventing the syndication rerun business and establishing the model for the modern sitcom.

Expansion

In 1957 Desilu purchased the RKO studio lots, becoming a major landlord and producer. Its catalogue included The Lucy Show, The Andy Griffith Show, The Dick Van Dyke Show, The Untouchables, Mission: Impossible and — after Ball overruled doubts about its cost — Star Trek. The studio also pioneered the use of Desilu's own soundstages by other producers.

Sale

After Ball and Arnaz divorced in 1960, Ball bought out Arnaz's share and became one of the first women to run a major television studio. In 1967 she sold Desilu to Gulf+Western Industries, which merged it into Paramount Television; the Desilu name disappeared by the end of the decade, though its library remains in circulation.
