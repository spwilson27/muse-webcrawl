Janet Louise Yellen (born August 13, 1946) is an American economist who served as the 78th United States secretary of the treasury from 2021 to 2025. She previously served as the 15th chair of the Federal Reserve from 2014 to 2018, the first woman to hold either post, and as chair of the White House Council of Economic Advisers under President Bill Clinton.

Education and academic career

Yellen was born in Brooklyn, New York, and graduated from Pembroke College of Brown University in 1967. She earned her PhD in economics from Yale University in 1971, studying under James Tobin and Joseph Stiglitz. She taught at Harvard University and the London School of Economics before joining the University of California, Berkeley's Haas School of Business, where she became a professor of business administration. With her husband, Nobel laureate George Akerlof, she wrote influential papers on efficiency wages and on how low-quality goods drive out high-quality goods in asymmetric-information markets.

Federal Reserve

Yellen served as a governor of the Federal Reserve Board (1994–1997), chair of the Council of Economic Advisers (1997–1999), and president of the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco (2004–2010). As vice chair of the Fed from 2010, she succeeded Ben Bernanke as chair in February 2014. Her tenure oversaw the gradual normalization of monetary policy after the Great Recession — raising interest rates from near zero and shrinking the Fed's balance sheet — while unemployment fell to multi-decade lows. President Donald Trump chose not to renominate her, replacing her with Jerome Powell in 2018.

Secretary of the Treasury

President Joe Biden nominated Yellen as secretary of the treasury; she was confirmed in January 2021, becoming the first person to have led the Treasury, the Federal Reserve and the Council of Economic Advisers. She managed the federal response financing for the American Rescue Plan, championed a global minimum corporate tax agreed by more than 130 countries in 2021, oversaw sanctions on Russia after its 2022 invasion of Ukraine — including the G7 oil price cap — and negotiated the 2023 debt-ceiling crisis. She left office in January 2025.
