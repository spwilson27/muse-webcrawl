Mercury (element)

SymbolHg
Atomic number80
Atomic mass200.59
Melting point−38.83 °C
Boiling point356.7 °C

Mercury is a chemical element with the symbol Hg (from Latin hydrargyrum) and atomic number 80. It is the only metallic element that is liquid at standard temperature and pressure. A heavy, silvery d-block metal, mercury occurs in deposits throughout the world mostly as cinnabar (mercuric sulfide).

Properties and uses

Mercury freezes at −38.83 °C and boils at 356.7 °C. It was historically used in thermometers, barometers, manometers, float valves, mercury switches and fluorescent lamps, and in the chlor-alkali and gold-mining industries. Many of these uses have been phased out because of mercury's toxicity; mercury and its compounds are now regulated internationally by the Minamata Convention (2013).

Toxicity

Mercury and most of its compounds are extremely toxic. Organic mercury compounds, especially methylmercury, bioaccumulate in fish and caused the mass poisoning at Minamata, Japan. Chronic exposure damages the nervous system, kidneys and developing foetus.

History

Mercury was known to the ancient Chinese and Indians and was found in Egyptian tombs from 1500 BC. Alchemists regarded it as a fundamental substance, and its old name "quicksilver" survives in English usage.
