Interculturalism

Interculturalism is a political and cultural model that promotes dialogue and interaction between distinct cultures within a society, emphasising shared values and mutual exchange rather than the mere coexistence of separate groups. It is often contrasted with multiculturalism, which critics argue can leave communities living parallel lives.

Principles

Advocates argue that intercultural policies should foster contact across ethnic and religious lines, a common civic identity, and equality of opportunity, while still respecting cultural difference. The Council of Europe's 2008 White Paper on Intercultural Dialogue endorsed the approach for European states.

Criticism

Scholars debate whether interculturalism is genuinely distinct from recent forms of multiculturalism, and some argue the contrast is overstated. Others question whether shared-values frameworks risk pressuring minorities to assimilate.
