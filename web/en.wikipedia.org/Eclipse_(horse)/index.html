Eclipse (horse)

Eclipse (1 April 1764 – 26 February 1789) was an undefeated 18th-century British Thoroughbred racehorse who won 18 races and became one of the most influential sires in the breed's history. The majority of modern Thoroughbreds descend from him through the Darley Arabian sire line; the phrase "Eclipse first, the rest nowhere" recalls his habit of winning by huge margins.

Background

Eclipse was bred by the Duke of Cumberland and named for the solar eclipse of 1 April 1764, the day he was foaled. Sold to sheep dealer William Wildman and later to Dennis O'Kelly, the chestnut colt was noted for a long stride and a temperamental nature.

Racing career

In 1769–70 Eclipse won all 18 of his races, usually by enormous distances and often easing up; opponents frequently withdrew rather than face him. He twice won the King's Plate and was never extended to a full gallop, according to contemporary accounts.

Stud career and legacy

At stud Eclipse sired 344 winners including the Derby winners Young Eclipse, Saltram and Sergeant. Genetic studies show his Y-chromosome line now dominates the Thoroughbred population worldwide. His skeleton is preserved at the Royal Veterinary College, and the Eclipse Stakes at Sandown Park, founded in 1886, is named in his honour.
