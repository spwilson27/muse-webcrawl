The Capitol of the Confederate States of America in Richmond, Virginia, housed the Congress of the Confederate States during the American Civil War from 1861 to 1865. The building, completed in 1788 as the Virginia State Capitol to a design inspired by Thomas Jefferson, was shared with the Virginia state government.

History

After Virginia seceded and Richmond became the Confederate capital in May 1861, the Confederate Congress convened in the capitol's halls. President Jefferson Davis's offices were in the nearby Confederate White House. When Richmond fell in April 1865, the building survived the evacuation fire that destroyed much of the city. It subsequently resumed sole use as Virginia's state capitol, a role it retains today, and now includes a museum treating its Confederate history.
