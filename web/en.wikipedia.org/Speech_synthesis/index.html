Speech synthesis

Speech synthesis is the artificial production of human speech. A computer system used for this purpose is called a speech synthesizer, and systems that convert written text into speech are known as text-to-speech (TTS) systems. Synthesized speech can be created by concatenating pieces of recorded speech stored in a database, or by generating speech with models of the vocal tract and voice characteristics.

History

Mechanical speech devices date to the 18th century, including Wolfgang von Kempelen's speaking machine. Electronic synthesis began with the Voder in 1939. The DECtalk system of the 1980s, based on Dennis Klatt's formant synthesis research, became widely known; a DECtalk voice was used by physicist Stephen Hawking.

Approaches

Concatenative synthesis joins pre-recorded units of speech. Formant synthesis generates speech from acoustic parameters without recordings, producing intelligible but robotic voices. Since the 2010s, neural text-to-speech models such as WaveNet and Tacotron have produced speech approaching human naturalness, learning directly from audio data.

Applications

Applications include screen readers and accessibility tools for blind users and people who cannot speak, voice assistants, navigation announcements, audiobook and video narration, and telecommunications.
