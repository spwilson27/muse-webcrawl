Type: Energy conversion device
Components: Rotor blades, nacelle, tower, generator
Applications: Wind farms, distributed generation

Wind turbine

A wind turbine is a device that converts the kinetic energy of wind into electrical energy. Modern turbines are usually horizontal-axis machines with three blades mounted on a tall tower; they range from small units of a few hundred watts to offshore machines exceeding 15 megawatts. Wind turbines are the central technology of wind power, one of the fastest-growing sources of electricity worldwide.

Design

Wind striking the rotor blades produces lift, turning a low-speed shaft connected through a gearbox (or directly, in direct-drive designs) to a generator inside the nacelle. A yaw mechanism keeps the rotor facing the wind, and pitch control of the blades regulates power and protects the machine in high winds. The theoretical maximum fraction of wind energy a turbine can extract is the Betz limit, about 59.3%; practical turbines achieve roughly 75–80% of that limit.

History

Windmills have ground grain and pumped water for centuries. The first electricity-generating wind turbine is credited to James Blyth in Scotland in 1887, and Charles F. Brush built a large turbine in Cleveland in 1888. Denmark pioneered practical turbines in the early 20th century, and the modern industry grew rapidly from the 1970s energy crises onward.

Deployment

Turbines are grouped into wind farms on land and offshore, where winds are stronger and steadier. Global installed wind capacity passed 1 terawatt in 2023. Issues include intermittency, land use, wildlife impacts (notably birds and bats), noise, and recycling of composite blades, balanced against zero fuel cost and very low lifecycle carbon emissions.
