Sooners

The Land Rush of 1889

Sooners were settlers who entered the Unassigned Lands in what is now the U.S. state of Oklahoma before the official start of the Land Rush of 1889, in order to stake claims ahead of legal participants. The term derives from the "soon" clause of the Indian Appropriations Act of 1889, which stated that anyone who entered and occupied the land sooner than the prescribed time would be denied a claim.

Background

The Land Run of 22 April 1889 opened about two million acres of land in central Indian Territory. At noon, tens of thousands of "Boomers" raced from the starting lines to stake homestead claims. The Sooners, by contrast, had slipped across the border earlier — hidden in creek beds and hollows, sometimes aided by sympathetic deputy marshals or railroad employees — and emerged at noon to plant their stakes first. Disputes between Sooners and legal claimants generated thousands of contested cases that took years to resolve in the land offices and courts.

Legacy

Despite its origins in cheating, "Sooner" became a badge of identity: Oklahoma is known as the "Sooner State", and the athletic teams of the University of Oklahoma are the Oklahoma Sooners. The Boomer Sooner fight song and the Sooner Schooner mascot wagon commemorate the land run era.
