Bangladeshi cuisine

Bangladeshi cuisine is the national cuisine of Bangladesh, shaped by the region's riverine geography, monsoon climate, and Mughal and British influences. Rice and fish form its core — reflected in the saying machh-e bhat-e Bangali ("fish and rice make a Bengali").

Staples

Rice is served at nearly every meal, accompanied by fish curries (notably hilsa, the national fish), lentil dal, vegetable bhajis, and bhartas (mashed preparations). Mustard oil and panch phoron spice blends are characteristic.

Notable dishes

Dishes include biryani (especially Dhaka's kacchi biryani), hilsa curry, shorshe ilish (hilsa in mustard sauce), morog polao, and kebabs. Desserts such as mishti doi (sweet yoghurt), roshogolla and sandesh showcase Bengal's famed sweet-making.

Regional variation

Chittagong is known for fiery curries and dried fish, Sylhet for sour and citrus flavours, and the south-west for prawns and coconut.
