Mayor of the Palace

LatinMaior domus
KingdomsAustrasia, Neustria, Burgundy (Frankish kingdoms)
Period6th – 8th centuries
Notable holdersPepin of Landen, Charles Martel, Pepin the Short

The Mayor of the Palace (Latin: maior domus, "chief of the household") was the chief officer of the royal household and, by the 7th century, the effective ruler of the Frankish kingdoms of Austrasia, Neustria and Burgundy under the Merovingian kings. The office became the stepping stone by which the Carolingian family supplanted the Merovingian dynasty.

Development of the office

Originally the manager of the king's household and estates, the mayor gradually took over finance, justice and command of the army, especially during royal minorities and under kings who reigned without ruling — the later Merovingians dismissed by Carolingian propaganda as rois fainéants ("do-nothing kings"). From the early 7th century each Frankish kingdom had its own mayor, and the office became hereditary in the Pippinid (later Carolingian) family in Austrasia.

The Carolingian mayors

Pepin of Landen and his descendants consolidated power; Pepin of Herstal united the mayoralties by victory at the Battle of Tertry (687). His son Charles Martel ruled Francia without a king for several years and defeated an Umayyad invasion at the Battle of Tours (732). Charles's son Pepin the Short, having obtained papal approval, deposed the last Merovingian, Childeric III, in 751 and had himself crowned king — ending the fiction and merging the office of mayor into the Carolingian monarchy.
