The Black Canyon of the Colorado is a steep canyon carved by the Colorado River on the border of the US states of Nevada and Arizona, downstream of the Grand Canyon and immediately south of Lake Mead. It takes its name from the black volcanic rocks found throughout the area. The canyon lies largely within the Lake Mead National Recreation Area and the El Dorado Wilderness.

Geology and geography

The canyon was cut through ancient Precambrian metamorphic and volcanic rocks as the Colorado River eroded its course. Its rugged, dark cliffs rise as much as several hundred metres from the river. Natural hot springs emerge in side canyons along the river corridor, and the area supports desert bighorn sheep, bald eagles and other wildlife adapted to the Mojave Desert.

Hoover Dam

At the northern end of the canyon stands Hoover Dam, completed in 1936, which impounds Lake Mead. The dam site was chosen within Black Canyon partly for its narrow, solid rock walls. During construction, workers and supplies reached the site partly by river through the canyon, and the settlement of Boulder City housed the workforce. The Mike O'Callaghan–Pat Tillman Memorial Bridge, opened in 2010, now spans the canyon just downstream of the dam.

Recreation

The river below the dam is a popular canoeing and kayaking route, launched from near the dam and passing hot springs, caves and the historic Willow Beach. Hiking trails descend to the river from both states.
