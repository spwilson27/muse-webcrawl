Mehregan

Mehregan (Persian: مهرگان) or Jashn-e Mehr (جشن مهر, "Feast of Mithra") is a Zoroastrian and Persian festival celebrated to honour the yazata Mithra (Mehr), which is responsible for friendship, affection and love. It is also widely referred to as the Persian Festival of Autumn, and is the second most important Iranian festival after Nowruz.

History

Mehregan was celebrated in pre-Islamic Persia with the same grandeur as Nowruz. It falls on the day of Mehr in the month of Mehr — in the modern Iranian calendar around 2 October, near the autumn equinox season — when the day's name matches the month's name. Tradition associates the festival with the mythical king Fereydun's victory over the tyrant Zahhak, said to have occurred on this day.

Observance

Celebrants wear new clothes, set a table with a mirror, candles, marjoram, pomegranates and other seasonal fruits, rosewater, and sweets, and gather with family to give thanks for the harvest and for Mithra's light and covenant. The festival is still observed by Zoroastrians and by Iranians and diaspora communities around the world.
