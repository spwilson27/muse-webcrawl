Victoria Falls (Lozi: Mosi-oa-Tunya, “The Smoke That Thunders”; Tonga: Shungu Namutitima) is a waterfall on the Zambezi River in southern Africa, on the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe. It is considered the largest waterfall in the world when its combined width of 1,708 metres and height of 108 metres are taken together, forming the largest sheet of falling water.

History

The falls have long been known to local peoples, including the Kololo who named them Mosi-oa-Tunya. Scottish explorer David Livingstone was the first European recorded to view them, in 1855, and named them in honour of Queen Victoria. The nearby Zambian town of Livingstone bears his name.

Geography

Upstream the Zambezi flows calmly over basalt; at the falls the entire river plunges into a narrow chasm, the First Gorge, and then zigzags through a series of gorges carved over thousands of years as the falls have retreated upstream. Spray can rise hundreds of metres and be seen from many kilometres away, sustaining a rainforest ecosystem on the cliff opposite the falls.

Tourism

Victoria Falls is a major tourist destination for both Zambia and Zimbabwe, with national parks on both sides, white-water rafting below the falls, bungee jumping from the Victoria Falls Bridge, and the natural Devil's Pool at the lip of the falls on the Zambian side during low water.
