Cadence (music)

In Western musical theory, a cadence is the end of a phrase in which the melody or harmony creates a sense of full or partial resolution, especially a chord progression moving a piece, section or phrase to a conclusion. Cadences function as the punctuation of musical form.

Types

  • Authentic cadence: a progression from the dominant (V) to the tonic (I); a perfect authentic cadence has both chords in root position with the tonic in the melody, while an imperfect one relaxes some condition.
  • Half cadence: ends on V, leaving the phrase sounding unfinished.
  • Plagal cadence: IV to I, the "Amen" cadence of hymns.
  • Deceptive cadence: V moves to an unexpected chord, often vi, evading the expected resolution.
  • Phrygian half cadence: a Baroque type ending on V with a characteristic semitone descent in the bass.

The strength of a cadence largely determines whether listeners perceive a phrase as continuing or complete, and cadential formulas mark formal boundaries throughout tonal music from the Baroque era to jazz and popular song.
