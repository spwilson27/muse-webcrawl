Tokyo Metro

The Tokyo Metro (東京メトロ) is a major rapid transit system in Tokyo, Japan, operated by Tokyo Metro Co., Ltd. Together with the Toei Subway, it forms the subway network of the Japanese capital. Tokyo Metro operates nine lines with 180 stations over approximately 195 kilometres of route, carrying several million passengers daily and ranking among the busiest metro systems in the world.

History

Tokyo's first subway, the Ginza Line between Ueno and Asakusa, opened in 1927 under the Tokyo Underground Railway Company. The network expanded under public ownership after the Second World War, administered by the Teito Rapid Transit Authority (Eidan). In 2004 the authority was privatised as Tokyo Metro Co., Ltd., jointly owned by the national government and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government.

Lines

The system comprises the Ginza, Marunouchi, Hibiya, Tōzai, Chiyoda, Yūrakuchō, Hanzōmon, Namboku and Fukutoshin lines, each identified by a colour and letter code. Most lines offer through services onto suburban private railways, allowing trains to run directly between the subway and outlying districts. Stations are numbered within each line to assist visitors, and extensive connections exist with JR East, Toei and private railway lines.

Operations

Tokyo Metro is noted for punctuality, platform screen doors, and congestion management during peak hours. The company was listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in 2024 following a major initial public offering.
