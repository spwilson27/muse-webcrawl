Chartreuse (liqueur)

Chartreuse is a French herbal liqueur made by Carthusian monks since 1737 according to instructions set out in a manuscript given to them by François Annibal d'Estrées in 1605. It is composed of distilled alcohol aged with 130 herbs, plants and flowers, and is named after the monks' Grande Chartreuse monastery in the Chartreuse Mountains.

Varieties

Green Chartreuse (110 proof, 55% ABV) is the original and flagship product, its colour deriving naturally from its ingredients. Yellow Chartreuse (40% ABV), introduced in 1840, is milder and sweeter with a saffron-tinted colour. The monks also produce special editions, including the longer-aged VEP bottlings, and an herbal elixir.

History

The recipe, said to derive from an alchemical manuscript for an "elixir of long life," was perfected by Brother Jérôme Maubec in 1737. The monks were twice expelled from France — after the Revolution and again in 1903 — producing the liqueur in exile in Tarragona, Spain, before returning to distil at Voiron, France, where production continues today. Only a small number of monks know the full recipe at any time, and production volumes are deliberately limited; in 2023 the monks announced they would not increase output, devoting more time to prayer and sustainability.

Use

Chartreuse is drunk as a digestif, often chilled, and features in classic cocktails such as the Last Word and the Bijou.
