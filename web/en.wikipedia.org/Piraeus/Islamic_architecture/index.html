Islamic architecture

Islamic architecture comprises the architectural styles of buildings associated with Islam, developing from the seventh century across the Middle East, North Africa, Spain and Asia. It encompasses religious structures such as mosques and madrasas as well as palaces, tombs, forts and domestic buildings.

Characteristics

Common elements include the pointed and horseshoe arch, the dome, the minaret, the iwan, muqarnas (stalactite vaulting), courtyards, and extensive geometric, vegetal (arabesque) and calligraphic ornament — aniconic decoration being preferred in religious contexts. Regional traditions, from Ottoman to Mughal and Moorish, adapted these elements to local materials and practice.

Notable examples

Landmarks include the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem, the Great Mosque of Córdoba, the Alhambra in Granada, Istanbul's Hagia Sophia and Süleymaniye Mosque, and the Taj Mahal in Agra.
