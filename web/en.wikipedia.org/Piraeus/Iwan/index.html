Iwan

An iwan (from Persian eyvān) is a rectangular hall or space, usually vaulted, walled on three sides and open on the fourth. Originating in ancient Persia, the iwan became a defining feature of Islamic architecture across the Middle East and Central Asia.

History and use

The form appears in Parthian and Sasanian palaces, most monumentally in the Taq Kasra at Ctesiphon, whose great arch remains the largest single-span brick vault of its kind. In Islamic buildings, iwans typically face onto a courtyard: the four-iwan plan is characteristic of Iranian mosques and madrasas, the grandest example being the Shah Mosque in Isfahan.
