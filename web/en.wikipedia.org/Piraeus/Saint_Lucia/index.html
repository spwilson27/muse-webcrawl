Saint Lucia

Capital: Castries
Languages: English (official), Saint Lucian Creole
Currency: East Caribbean dollar

Saint Lucia is an island country in the eastern Caribbean Sea, part of the Lesser Antilles. It gained independence from the United Kingdom in 1979 and remains a member of the Commonwealth. The island is noted for the Pitons, two volcanic spires that are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Colonised successively by France and Britain — the island changed hands repeatedly and was called the "Helen of the West" — Saint Lucia retains strong French Creole cultural influence. Nobel laureates Derek Walcott (literature) and Arthur Lewis (economics) were both born on the island.

Economy

Tourism is the largest source of employment and foreign exchange, supplemented by banana exports and offshore financial services.
