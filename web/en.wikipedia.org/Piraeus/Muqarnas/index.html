Muqarnas

Muqarnas is a form of ornamented vaulting in Islamic architecture, sometimes called "stalactite vaulting" for its resemblance to hanging cave formations. It consists of tiers of small niche-like cells arranged in geometric patterns, mediating the transition between square walls and domes or filling vaults and cornices.

Development

Muqarnas developed around the tenth and eleventh centuries, with early examples in northeastern Iran and North Africa, and spread throughout the Islamic world. Outstanding examples survive in the Alhambra in Spain, in Iranian mosques such as those of Isfahan, and in Mamluk buildings in Cairo, executed in stone, brick, wood or plaster.
