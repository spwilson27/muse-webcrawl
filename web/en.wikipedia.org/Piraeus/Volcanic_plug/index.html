Volcanic plug

A volcanic plug, also called a volcanic neck or lava neck, is a geological landform created when magma hardens within a vent on an active volcano. If the surrounding, softer cone later erodes away, the resistant solidified magma remains as a steep-sided tower or spine.

Formation and examples

Plugs form as viscous lava solidifies in the conduit; erosion then exposes the neck. Famous examples include Devils Tower in Wyoming, Shiprock in New Mexico, and the Pitons of Saint Lucia. A plug left in place can trap pressure beneath it, and renewed eruption may then be violently explosive, as at Mount St. Helens in 1980.
