Piraeus

Piraeus (Greek: Πειραιάς) is a port city in the Athens metropolitan area, in the Attica region of Greece. It lies on the eastern coast of the Saronic Gulf, about 12 kilometres southwest of Athens city centre. Piraeus is the chief port of Greece, the largest passenger port in Europe, and one of the largest in the world by passenger traffic, serving millions of travellers to the Greek islands each year.

History

Piraeus was inhabited in ancient times and was developed as the port of classical Athens from the 5th century BC, when the statesman Themistocles persuaded the Athenians to fortify its three natural harbours — Kantharos, Zea, and Munichia — and later to connect it to Athens by the Long Walls. As the base of the Athenian fleet of triremes, Piraeus underpinned Athens' naval power and empire. The town was laid out on a grid plan by the architect Hippodamus of Miletus.

Piraeus declined after the Macedonian and Roman conquests; a Roman army under Sulla destroyed the port in 86 BC. It remained a modest fishing village, known as Porto Leone, for centuries, and revived only after Greek independence, when Athens became the capital of the modern Greek state in 1834. Refugees from Asia Minor greatly expanded the city after 1922.

Economy and transport

Today Piraeus is the centre of Greek shipping, home to the offices of many of the world's largest shipowning companies. Since 2009, the Chinese company COSCO has operated container terminals at the port under a long-term concession and later acquired a majority stake in the Piraeus Port Authority. The port is connected to Athens by metro lines, tram, and suburban rail.

Culture and sport

Landmarks include the Archaeological Museum of Piraeus, the Hellenic Maritime Museum, the Municipal Theatre, and the Zea Marina. The city is home to Olympiacos, one of Greece's most successful football and multi-sport clubs, based at the Karaiskakis Stadium.
