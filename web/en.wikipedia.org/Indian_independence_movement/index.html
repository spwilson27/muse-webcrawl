Indian independence movement

Indian independence movement
Period: 1857–1947
Outcome: Independence of India and Pakistan, 15 August 1947
Key figures: Mohandas Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, Subhas Chandra Bose

The Indian independence movement was a series of historic events with the ultimate aim of ending British rule in India, also known as the British Raj. It lasted from 1857 to 1947, encompassing the efforts of a large and diverse range of people and political organisations.

Early resistance

The Indian Rebellion of 1857 was a major, though ultimately unsuccessful, uprising against the East India Company. Thereafter rule passed to the Crown. The Indian National Congress, founded in 1885, initially sought reform within the empire, but demands for self-rule grew, spurred by events such as the partition of Bengal in 1905 and the Amritsar massacre of 1919.

Gandhian mass movements

Under Mohandas K. Gandhi, the movement adopted nonviolent civil disobedience (satyagraha) on a mass scale: the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22), the Salt March and Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34), and the Quit India Movement (1942). Parallel strands included revolutionary groups, Subhas Chandra Bose's Indian National Army, and the Muslim League's campaign, led by Muhammad Ali Jinnah, for a separate Pakistan.

Independence and partition

Exhausted by the Second World War, Britain agreed to transfer power. The Indian Independence Act 1947 partitioned British India into the dominions of India and Pakistan on 15 August 1947, accompanied by mass migration and communal violence that killed hundreds of thousands.
