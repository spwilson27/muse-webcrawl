Life support

Life support comprises the treatments and techniques performed in an emergency in order to support life after the failure of one or more vital organs. In medicine it includes interventions such as mechanical ventilation, cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), dialysis, artificial nutrition and hydration, and medications that sustain circulation. The term also describes systems that provide breathable air, water, and temperature control in spacecraft, submarines, and other closed environments.

Medical life support

Basic life support (BLS) covers CPR and airway management by first responders; advanced life support (ALS) and advanced cardiac life support (ACLS) add defibrillation, drugs, and advanced airways. In intensive care, life-sustaining treatment may include ventilators for respiratory failure, renal replacement therapy, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), and vasopressors for shock.

Ethics and law

Decisions about starting, withholding, or withdrawing life support raise ethical and legal questions involving patient autonomy, advance directives, capacity, and surrogate decision-making. Landmark cases have shaped the right of patients and families to refuse life-sustaining treatment.

Environmental life support

In spaceflight, life-support systems recycle air and water and regulate pressure and temperature; the International Space Station's system recovers most of its water from condensate and urine processing.
