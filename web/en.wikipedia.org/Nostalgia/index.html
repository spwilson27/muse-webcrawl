Nostalgia

Nostalgia is a sentimentality for the past, typically for a period or place with happy personal associations. The word nostalgia is a learned formation of a Greek compound consisting of nóstos, meaning "homecoming", a Homeric word, and álgos, meaning "sorrow" or "despair". It was coined by the Swiss physician Johannes Hofer in 1688, in his medical dissertation, to describe the anxious homesickness shown by Swiss mercenaries fighting away from home.

History

Nostalgia was originally considered a medical condition and a form of melancholy. During the 19th century, it gradually changed in meaning from a pathological condition of homesick soldiers and sailors to a more general, often wistful sentimentality for times past. By the late 20th century, nostalgia was studied less as a disease and more as a psychological resource and a cultural phenomenon.

Psychological aspects

Contemporary research treats nostalgia as a largely positive, self-relevant emotion. Studies associate it with increased self-esteem, social connectedness, meaning in life, and optimism about the future. Nostalgic memories typically feature the self in a central role surrounded by close others, and often describe redemption sequences in which negative life events lead to positive outcomes. Nostalgia can be triggered by music, photographs, smells, tastes, and other reminders of the personal past.

Cultural significance

Nostalgia plays a significant role in popular culture, marketing, politics, and design. Retrospective revivals of fashion, music, and media draw on collective nostalgia, and politicians may invoke an idealized past. Critics have described some forms of nostalgia as restorative, seeking to rebuild an imagined lost home, and others as reflective, savouring memory itself.
