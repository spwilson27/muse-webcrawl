Olympic Games

Olympic Games
First modern edition: Athens, 1896
Organiser: International Olympic Committee
Frequency: Every four years
Motto: Citius, Altius, Fortius

The modern Olympic Games are the world's leading international sporting events, featuring summer and winter sports competitions in which thousands of athletes from around the world participate. More than 200 teams, representing sovereign states and territories, now compete. The Games are overseen by the International Olympic Committee (IOC), founded in 1894 by Pierre de Coubertin, who revived the ancient Greek festival held at Olympia.

History

The first modern Games were held in Athens in 1896, with 241 athletes from 14 nations. The Winter Olympics were introduced in 1924 at Chamonix. From 1994 the Summer and Winter Games were staggered to alternate every two years. The Olympic symbol — five interlaced rings coloured blue, yellow, black, green and red on a white field — was designed by Coubertin in 1913.

The Games

The Summer Olympics include athletics, swimming, gymnastics, football, basketball and dozens of other sports; the Winter Olympics include skiing, ice hockey, figure skating and bobsleigh. The opening ceremony features the parade of nations and the lighting of the Olympic flame, kindled at Olympia and carried by relay. Medals of gold, silver and bronze are awarded in each event.

Movement and issues

The Olympic movement also encompasses the Paralympic Games and the Youth Olympic Games. The Games have been marked by boycotts (notably 1980 and 1984), political protest, doping scandals and debate over the costs borne by host cities; only three editions have been cancelled, all due to world wars (1916, 1940, 1944), and the Tokyo 2020 Games were postponed to 2021 because of the COVID-19 pandemic.
