Hérault (French pronunciation: [eʁo]; Occitan: Erau) is a department in the Occitanie region of southern France, on the Mediterranean coast. It is named after the river Hérault which flows through it. Its prefecture and largest city is Montpellier, a major university and technology centre; other important towns include Béziers, Sète, Agde, and Lunel. The departmental number is 34.

Geography

Hérault stretches from the Mediterranean shore — with lagoons such as the Étang de Thau, famous for oyster farming — across a vine-growing coastal plain to the hills and limestone plateaus (causses) of the interior, rising toward the Cévennes foothills in the northwest. The Canal du Midi, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, crosses the department, passing Béziers with its famous Fonseranes locks.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790, from part of the former province of Languedoc. The area has been settled since prehistoric times and was important in Roman Gaul — Agde was a Greek and Roman port, and the Via Domitia crossed the region. In 1907 the department was a centre of the Languedoc winegrowers' revolt, one of the largest protest movements of the French Third Republic.

Economy

Viticulture dominates the agricultural economy — Languedoc is one of the world's largest wine-producing areas — alongside tourism drawn to Mediterranean resorts such as Cap d'Agde and La Grande-Motte, shellfish farming, and a growing technology and research sector around Montpellier.
