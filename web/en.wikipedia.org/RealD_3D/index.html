RealD 3D is a digital stereoscopic projection technology developed by RealD Inc. and used in cinemas worldwide. Unlike older dual-projector 3D systems, RealD projects left- and right-eye images alternately from a single digital projector at 144 frames per second (72 frames per eye), using a device called a ZScreen to switch the circular polarisation of the light between clockwise and anticlockwise. Viewers wear lightweight passive glasses with circular polarising filters, so each eye sees only its intended image.

Technology

Because polarisation would be destroyed by an ordinary white screen, RealD requires a silver screen that preserves polarisation. The rapid alternation of frames, with each frame triple-flashed, minimises flicker and cross-talk ("ghosting"). The use of circular rather than linear polarisation allows viewers to tilt their heads without losing the 3D effect.

Adoption

The first feature released in the format was Disney's Chicken Little in 2005. RealD expanded rapidly with the 3D boom led by Avatar (2009), becoming for a time the most widely installed 3D cinema system in the world, with tens of thousands of screens. Competing systems include Dolby 3D and XpanD 3D, while IMAX uses its own projection formats.
