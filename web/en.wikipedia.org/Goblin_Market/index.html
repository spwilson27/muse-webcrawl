Goblin Market

"Goblin Market" is a narrative poem by Christina Rossetti, written in April 1859 and published in 1862 in her collection Goblin Market and Other Poems. It tells the story of two sisters, Laura and Lizzie, and their encounters with goblin fruit merchants. Although ostensibly a fairy tale for children, the poem has attracted extensive critical interpretation concerning temptation, sacrifice, sisterhood, sexuality and redemption, and is considered Rossetti's most famous work.

Plot

Laura and Lizzie hear goblin merchants crying their luscious, exotic fruits by a stream. Laura, ignoring Lizzie's warnings and the fate of Jeanie, a girl who ate the fruit and pined away and died, cuts off a lock of her golden hair to pay for fruit and feasts on it. Afterwards she can no longer hear the goblins' cry and wastes away, craving more. Lizzie, to save her, goes to the market with a silver penny, refuses to eat, and endures the goblins' violence as they smash fruit against her face. She returns home covered in juice, and Laura, kissing it from her cheeks — "Eat me, drink me, love me" — tastes bitterness, is purged of her craving and restored. In old age the sisters tell their children of the goblin men and teach that "there is no friend like a sister".

Themes and interpretation

The poem's irregular rhyming verse and lush catalogues of fruit have invited readings ranging from a moral fable about resisting temptation and a Christian allegory of fall and redemption through a sister's self-sacrifice, to feminist and economic readings about women's place in the Victorian marketplace and critiques of consumer desire. Rossetti herself resisted allegorical readings and presented it as a fairy story, though she worked in a milieu of Anglo-Catholic devotion and volunteered at a penitentiary for "fallen women", a context often linked to the poem's themes of fallenness and rescue.

Legacy

The poem has inspired illustrations by Dante Gabriel Rossetti (who designed the 1862 frontispiece), Arthur Hughes and later artists, as well as musical settings, stage adaptations and works of children's and fantasy literature.
