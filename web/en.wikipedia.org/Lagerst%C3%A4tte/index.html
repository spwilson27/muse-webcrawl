Lagerstätte

A Lagerstätte (German: [ˈlaːɡɐˌʃtɛtə], from Lager 'storage, lair' and Stätte 'place'; plural Lagerstätten) is a sedimentary deposit that preserves an exceptionally rich or well-preserved fossil assemblage. The term was coined by Adolf Seilacher in 1970.

Types

Two main types are distinguished. Conservation Lagerstätten (Konservat-Lagerstätten) preserve soft tissues and delicate structures, usually because organisms were buried rapidly in anoxic sediment where decay and scavenging were suppressed. Concentration Lagerstätten (Konzentrat-Lagerstätten) contain unusually dense accumulations of hard parts, such as bone beds and shell beds.

Famous examples

Celebrated conservation Lagerstätten include the Cambrian Burgess Shale of Canada and Chengjiang biota of China, the Devonian Hunsrück Slate and Rhynie chert, the Jurassic Solnhofen Limestone of Germany (source of Archaeopteryx), the Cretaceous Jehol Biota of China, and the Eocene Messel Pit and Green River Formation.

Significance

Because they preserve organisms rarely fossilised otherwise, Lagerstätten provide disproportionately large amounts of information about ancient ecosystems, soft-bodied diversity, and major evolutionary transitions.
