Russo-Turkish wars

Russian expansion at Ottoman expense between the 17th and 19th centuries.

The Russo-Turkish wars were a series of twelve conflicts between the Russian Empire (and its predecessor, the Tsardom of Russia) and the Ottoman Empire between the 16th and 20th centuries. Together they constitute one of the longest series of conflicts in European history, progressively shrinking Ottoman territory in the Black Sea region, the Caucasus, and the Balkans as Russia expanded southward.

Background

Hostilities grew out of Russian expansion toward the Black Sea, rivalry over the Crimean Khanate and the Danubian Principalities, and Russia's claimed role as protector of Orthodox Christians under Ottoman rule — the so-called Eastern Question. The wars were frequently entangled with the policies of Britain, France, and Austria, which alternately supported the Ottomans to contain Russia.

The wars

The dated conflicts include the wars of 1568–70, 1676–81, 1686–1700, 1710–11, 1735–39, 1768–74, 1787–92, 1806–12, 1828–29, 1853–56 (the Crimean War), and 1877–78, followed by the Caucasus campaign of the First World War (1914–18). Major treaties — Küçük Kaynarca (1774), Jassy (1792), Bucharest (1812), Adrianople (1829), Paris (1856), and San Stefano and Berlin (1878) — successively transferred Crimea, Bessarabia, and territory in the Caucasus to Russia and recognized the independence of Balkan states.

Legacy

The wars drove the decline of Ottoman power in Europe, reshaped the map of southeastern Europe, and ended only with the dissolution of both empires after 1918, when Soviet Russia and the new Turkish Republic established peaceful relations.
