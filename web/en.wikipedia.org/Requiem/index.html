Requiem

A requiem, or Requiem Mass, is a Mass of the Catholic Church offered for the repose of the souls of the deceased. The name derives from the Latin introit Requiem aeternam dona eis, Domine ("Grant them eternal rest, O Lord"). Requiems are also celebrated in Lutheran, Anglican, and some other traditions, and the term is widely used for musical settings of the liturgical text, whether or not intended for liturgical use.

Musical settings

Over 2,000 requiem settings survive. Early examples are plainchant; polyphonic settings begin with Johannes Ockeghem in the 15th century. Among the most famous are those by Mozart (left unfinished at his death in 1791), Berlioz, Verdi, Brahms (the non-liturgical Ein deutsches Requiem), Fauré, Dvořák, and Britten (the War Requiem, 1962).

Structure

The traditional movements include the Introit, Kyrie, the sequence Dies irae, the Offertory, Sanctus, Agnus Dei, and the Communion Lux aeterna. The Council of Trent standardised the text, though many composers omit or rearrange movements; Fauré famously excluded the Dies irae almost entirely in favour of a gentler vision of death.
