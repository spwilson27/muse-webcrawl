Value chain

A value chain is a set of activities that a firm operating in a specific industry performs in order to deliver a valuable product (i.e., good and/or service) to the market. The concept comes through business management and was first described by Michael Porter in his 1985 best-seller, Competitive Advantage: Creating and Sustaining Superior Performance.

Primary activities

Porter grouped the activities of a firm into primary activities, which directly create and deliver the product: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service. Each of these can be vital in helping a firm create a competitive advantage, whether through cost leadership or differentiation.

Support activities

Support activities sustain the primary ones: procurement, technology development, human resource management, and firm infrastructure (including finance, planning, and quality management). The distinction between primary and support activities does not mean support activities are unimportant; modern analysis emphasises that technology development and human resources increasingly drive competitive advantage.

Value chain analysis

Value chain analysis examines each activity to find where value is created, where costs are incurred, and where linkages between activities offer opportunities for optimisation or outsourcing. The idea has been extended beyond the single firm to industry-level and global value chains, in which design, production, and marketing are distributed across many countries.
