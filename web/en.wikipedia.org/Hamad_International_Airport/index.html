Hamad International Airport (IATA: DOH, ICAO: OTHH) is an international airport in Doha, the capital city of Qatar, and the country's only international airport. It has been the primary hub and headquarters of Qatar Airways since it opened on 30 April 2014, replacing the nearby Doha International Airport. The airport is named after the former Emir of Qatar, Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani.

History

Planning for a new airport began in 2003 as passenger growth outstripped the capacity of Doha International Airport. The new facility was built on land reclaimed from the Persian Gulf, roughly 5 kilometres east of the old airport. Originally scheduled to open in 2009, the project was delayed several times before the first commercial flight, a Qatar Airways service from Bahrain, landed on 30 April 2014. All airlines transferred operations to the new airport by 27 May 2014.

Facilities

The airport occupies about 22 square kilometres. Its passenger terminal covers some 600,000 square metres and was designed with an initial capacity of around 30 million passengers per year, with expansion plans intended to raise capacity beyond 50 million. The terminal is notable for its wave-like roof, extensive art installations, and a large indoor tropical garden known as the Orchard, added as part of an expansion that opened in November 2022. The airport has two parallel runways, one of which, at 4,850 metres, is among the longest in the world.

The airport's iconic lamp post "Lamp Bear" sculpture by Urs Fischer and a series of other works form one of the largest public art collections in an airport. Hamad International has repeatedly been ranked among the world's best airports by Skytrax.

Ground transport

The airport is connected to central Doha by the Doha Metro Red Line, taxis, buses and limousine services, and is situated adjacent to the Corniche road system.
