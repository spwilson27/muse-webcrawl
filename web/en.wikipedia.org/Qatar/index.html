Qatar

Qatar, officially the State of Qatar, is a country in West Asia occupying the Qatar Peninsula on the northeastern coast of the Arabian Peninsula. It shares its sole land border with Saudi Arabia to the south and is otherwise surrounded by the Persian Gulf. Its capital and largest city is Doha. Qatar is one of the world's largest exporters of liquefied natural gas and has among the highest per-capita incomes in the world.

History

The peninsula came under the rule of the Al Thani family in the 19th century. Qatar was a British protectorate from 1916 until independence on 3 September 1971. Oil was discovered in 1939, but the far larger North Field — the world's largest single natural gas field, shared with Iran — transformed the country from the 1990s onward, funding vast infrastructure, the Al Jazeera media network (founded 1996), and a sovereign wealth fund, the Qatar Investment Authority.

Economy and society

Hydrocarbons dominate the economy; the state company QatarEnergy runs the giant LNG trains at Ras Laffan, historically operated by Qatargas and RasGas. The large majority of residents are expatriate workers, and the treatment of migrant labourers — especially during construction for the 2022 FIFA World Cup, hosted by Qatar — drew international criticism and prompted labour reforms. From 2017 to 2021 Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain, and Egypt imposed a blockade on Qatar, which ended with the Al-Ula declaration.
