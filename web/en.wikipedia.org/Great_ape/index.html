Great ape

The great apes, also known as the Hominidae, are a taxonomic family of primates that include humans and their closest living relatives: orangutans, gorillas, chimpanzees and bonobos. The term "great ape" distinguishes members of this family from the "lesser apes", the gibbons of the family Hylobatidae.

Great apes are native to Africa and Southeast Asia. They are the largest living primates, characterized by relatively large brains, forward-facing eyes, opposable thumbs, and the absence of an external tail. With the exception of humans — whose population numbers in the billions — all great ape species are classified as endangered or critically endangered, largely due to habitat loss, hunting and disease.

Species

  • Orangutans (genus Pongo) — three species (Bornean, Sumatran and Tapanuli), the only fully arboreal great apes, native to the rainforests of Borneo and Sumatra.
  • Gorillas (genus Gorilla) — two species (western and eastern), the largest living primates, primarily terrestrial herbivores of equatorial Africa.
  • Chimpanzees (genus Pan) — the common chimpanzee (Pan troglodytes) and the bonobo (Pan paniscus), humans' closest living relatives, sharing about 98.8% of their DNA with Homo sapiens.
  • Humans (genus Homo) — the only extant species is Homo sapiens.

Characteristics

Great apes share a number of anatomical features: a Y-5 pattern of cusps on the lower molars, a rotating shoulder joint suited to arm-hanging (brachiation), no external tail, and an appendix. They exhibit complex social behavior, long childhoods, and advanced cognition, including tool use, self-recognition in mirrors in most species, and — in enculturated individuals — the ability to learn elements of sign language or lexigram systems.

Evolution

The hominid lineage diverged from gibbons roughly 15–20 million years ago. Orangutans split from the remaining great apes about 12–16 million years ago, gorillas about 8–9 million years ago, and the human lineage separated from that of chimpanzees and bonobos roughly 5–7 million years ago. The fossil record of early hominids includes genera such as Dryopithecus, Sivapithecus and Gigantopithecus, the latter being the largest ape known to have existed.

Conservation

All non-human great apes are listed on Appendix I of CITES, prohibiting international commercial trade. Major threats include deforestation for palm oil and timber, the bushmeat trade, mining, and infectious diseases such as Ebola. Conservation efforts focus on protected areas, anti-poaching patrols, and rehabilitation sanctuaries.
