Big Bounce

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Big Bounce hypothesis is a cosmological model in which the universe undergoes cycles of contraction and expansion: instead of beginning in a singularity (the Big Bang), the observable universe is the result of a previous universe collapsing and "bouncing" into expansion.

History

Oscillating-universe models date to the 1930s, including work associated with Richard Tolman, who showed that entropy growth complicates naive cyclic models. Modern bounce scenarios arise in loop quantum cosmology, where quantum geometry effects produce a repulsive force at Planck densities that replaces the classical singularity with a bounce, and in ekpyrotic and cyclic models derived from string theory.

Observational status

Bounce models aim to explain the universe's homogeneity and flatness as alternatives or complements to cosmic inflation. No direct observational evidence for a previous contracting phase has been confirmed, and distinguishing predictions (such as specific gravitational-wave backgrounds) remain an active research topic.
