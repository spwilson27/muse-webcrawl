Greenback Party

The Greenback Party, also known as the Independent Party, the National Independent Party and the Greenback Labor Party, was an American political party active from 1874 to 1889. It drew its support chiefly from farmers and labourers hurt by the deflation that followed the Civil War, and its central demand was the continued issue of paper money — "greenbacks" — not backed by gold, in order to expand the currency, raise crop prices and ease the burden of debt.

History

The party grew out of the Grange movement and agrarian protest against the "Crime of 1873", the demonetisation of silver. In the 1878 midterm elections Greenback candidates won over a million votes and elected more than a dozen members of Congress. Its presidential candidates included Peter Cooper (1876), James B. Weaver (1880), who won over 3 percent of the popular vote, and Benjamin F. Butler (1884). As prosperity returned and the free silver movement absorbed its monetary programme, the party declined; many of its supporters and ideas passed into the Populist Party of the 1890s.
