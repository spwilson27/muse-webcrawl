The Longmen Grottoes (Chinese: 龍門石窟; pinyin: Lóngmén Shíkū, "Dragon's Gate Grottoes") are one of the finest examples of Chinese Buddhist art, comprising tens of thousands of statues and their disciples carved into the limestone cliffs along the Yi River south of Luoyang in Henan province.

History

Carving began in 493 CE under the Northern Wei dynasty, when the imperial court moved to Luoyang, and continued through the Tang dynasty and beyond. The site contains more than 2,300 caves and niches, about 110,000 Buddhist statues and over 2,800 inscriptions. The centrepiece is the Fengxian Temple on the western bank, dominated by a 17-metre statue of Vairocana Buddha said to have been modelled on Empress Wu Zetian.

Damage and preservation

Many sculptures were damaged during anti-Buddhist persecutions, looting in the early 20th century, and the Cultural Revolution; statues from the grottoes are held in museums abroad. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000.
