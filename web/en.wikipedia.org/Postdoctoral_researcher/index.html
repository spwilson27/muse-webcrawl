Postdoctoral researcher

A postdoctoral researcher, postdoc, or postdoctoral fellow is a person who conducts research after completing a doctoral degree (typically a PhD). Postdocs pursue additional training and research experience, usually under the supervision of a principal investigator at a university, research institute, government laboratory, or company, with the aim of acquiring the professional skills needed for an independent research career.

Postdoctoral researchers make up a large share of the research workforce in many scientific fields (placeholder image).

Role and duties

Postdoctoral appointments are temporary, commonly lasting from one to five years, and may be renewed or followed by further postdoc positions. Postdocs design and carry out experiments or studies, publish papers, present at conferences, mentor graduate students, and assist with grant applications. In many laboratory sciences they perform a substantial share of day-to-day research work.

Funding

Postdocs are typically paid a salary or stipend funded by research grants held by their supervisor, by institutional training grants, or by individual fellowships awarded competitively by agencies such as the National Institutes of Health and the National Science Foundation in the United States, the European Research Council, and national research councils elsewhere. Whether postdocs are classified as employees or trainees varies by institution and country, affecting benefits and labor rights.

Career prospects

Historically, a postdoc was a near-required stepping stone to a tenure-track faculty position in the life and physical sciences. Because the number of doctoral graduates exceeds the number of permanent academic posts, many postdocs ultimately move into industry, government, science communication, or other careers. The lengthening of postdoctoral periods and relatively modest salaries have prompted debate and, in some places, unionization campaigns among postdocs.

By country

In the United States, postdocs number in the tens of thousands, concentrated in the biomedical sciences. In the United Kingdom and Europe, equivalent roles include "research associate" and "postdoctoral research fellow"; in Germany, postdocs may work toward the Habilitation. China has expanded postdoctoral stations rapidly since the 1980s.
