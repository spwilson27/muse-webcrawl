The northern royal albatross or toroa (Diomedea sanfordi) is a large seabird of the albatross family. It breeds almost entirely on the Chatham Islands east of New Zealand, with a small mainland colony at Taiari / Pukekura (Taiaroa Head) near Dunedin — the only mainland albatross colony in the Southern Hemisphere.

Life cycle

Like other great albatrosses, the species has an extremely slow breeding cycle: a single egg is laid, and a successful breeding attempt takes nearly a year, so pairs normally breed only every second year. Young birds spend several years at sea before returning to breed, and adults may live beyond 40 years.

Threats

Storm damage to nesting habitat on the Chatham Islands, fisheries bycatch and marine plastic ingestion threaten the population, which numbers roughly 17,000 mature individuals by commonly cited estimates. The colony at Taiaroa Head is intensively managed by rangers.
