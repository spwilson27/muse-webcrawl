Rodman Edward Serling (25 December 1924 – 28 June 1975) was an American screenwriter and television producer best known for creating and narrating the anthology series The Twilight Zone (1959–1964). He also co-wrote the screenplay for Planet of the Apes (1968) and created the later anthology series Night Gallery.

Career

Serling served as a paratrooper in the Second World War, an experience that shaped his writing. In the 1950s he became one of the most celebrated writers of live television drama, winning Emmy Awards for teleplays such as Patterns (1955) and Requiem for a Heavyweight (1956). Frustrated by network censorship of contemporary social issues, he turned to science fiction and fantasy in The Twilight Zone, using speculative stories to explore prejudice, war, conformity and morality. He wrote or co-wrote 92 of the series' 156 episodes and delivered its distinctive opening and closing narrations.

Legacy

Serling won six Emmy Awards during his career. He died in 1975 following open-heart surgery. His work is widely regarded as foundational to modern television storytelling.
