Simon Kuznets

Simon Smith Kuznets (1901–1985) was a Russian-born American economist and statistician who received the 1971 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences. He led the development of national income accounting — the foundation of modern GDP measurement — and proposed the Kuznets curve hypothesis about inequality over the course of economic development.

National income accounting

Working at the National Bureau of Economic Research, Kuznets produced the first comprehensive estimates of United States national income, presented to Congress in 1934. His framework underpinned GDP statistics worldwide, although Kuznets himself warned that "the welfare of a nation can scarcely be inferred from a measure of national income."

Kuznets curve

In his 1955 presidential address to the American Economic Association, Kuznets hypothesised that inequality first rises and then falls as economies industrialise, tracing an inverted-U. The idea influenced development economics and later the environmental Kuznets curve literature, though its empirical status remains debated.
