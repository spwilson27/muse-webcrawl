Developmental disability

Developmental disability is a diverse group of chronic conditions attributable to mental or physical impairments that arise before adulthood — under U.S. law, before age 22 — and are likely to continue indefinitely. They affect areas such as self-care, receptive and expressive language, learning, mobility, self-direction, capacity for independent living and economic self-sufficiency. The term covers intellectual disability, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, autism spectrum disorder, Down syndrome and many other conditions; it overlaps with, but is broader than, intellectual disability alone. Roughly one in six children in the United States has a developmental disability, according to Centers for Disease Control and Prevention surveillance.

Causes

Causes include genetic and chromosomal abnormalities (Down syndrome, fragile X syndrome), prenatal exposures such as alcohol (fetal alcohol spectrum disorders), infections and untreated metabolic disorders, complications of prematurity, and childhood injury or lead poisoning. In many individual cases no single cause is identified.

Services and rights

Support is provided through early intervention, special education under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), vocational rehabilitation, supported employment and community-based Medicaid waiver services. The Developmental Disabilities Assistance and Bill of Rights Act of 2000 defines the U.S. federal framework, funding councils, protection-and-advocacy systems and university centres in each state. Historically, people with developmental disabilities were routinely placed in large state institutions; a deinstitutionalization movement from the 1960s onward, reinforced by the Supreme Court's decision in Olmstead v. L.C. (1999) affirming the right to community integration, shifted services toward homes, schools and workplaces in the community. Self-advocacy movements, under the slogan "Nothing about us without us", press for inclusion, person-centred planning and recognition of legal capacity.
