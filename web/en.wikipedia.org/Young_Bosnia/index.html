Young Bosnia

Young Bosnia (Serbo-Croatian: Mlada Bosna) was a revolutionary movement active in Bosnia and Herzegovina before World War I. Its members were predominantly young Bosnian Serb students, though it also attracted Bosnian Croats and Muslims, united by opposition to Austro-Hungarian rule and by the desire for the unification of all South Slavs (Yugoslavism) or, for many members, union with Serbia.

Background

Austria-Hungary had occupied Bosnia and Herzegovina since 1878 and formally annexed the provinces in 1908, provoking the Bosnian crisis and angering Serbian nationalists who regarded Bosnia's large Serb population as part of a future greater Serbian or Yugoslav state. In the annexed provinces, repression, censorship, and the suspension of constitutional government radicalized a generation of students in Sarajevo and other towns.

Ideology and activities

Young Bosnia was less a single organization than a loose network of student circles, reading groups, and conspiratorial cells inspired by the Young Italy movement of Giuseppe Mazzini, Russian revolutionary literature, anarchism, and South Slav cultural nationalism. Its members read forbidden books, organized demonstrations, and plotted assassinations of Austro-Hungarian officials. In 1910 Bogdan Žerajić attempted to assassinate the provincial governor, an act that made him a martyr-hero to the movement.

Assassination of Franz Ferdinand

Young Bosnia is best known for the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. Gavrilo Princip, Nedeljko Čabrinović, and Trifko Grabež, members of the movement, received weapons and training in Serbia with the help of the Black Hand organization. Princip fired the fatal shots that killed the Archduke and his wife Sophie, triggering the July Crisis and the outbreak of the First World War.

Aftermath

Most of the conspirators were arrested and tried in Sarajevo in October 1914; those under twenty, including Princip, received long prison sentences rather than execution, while older accomplices were hanged. Princip died of tuberculosis in the Theresienstadt fortress prison in 1918, months before the collapse of Austria-Hungary and the creation of the Yugoslav state the movement had sought.
