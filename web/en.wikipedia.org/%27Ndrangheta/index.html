The 'Ndrangheta (from the Greek word andragathía for heroism and virtue) is a prominent Italian organised crime syndicate based in Calabria, in southern Italy. Despite not being as famous abroad as the Sicilian Cosa Nostra, the 'Ndrangheta is widely regarded as the most powerful crime syndicate in the world.

Its power has been attributed to its control over much of the cocaine trade in Europe, estimated to account for a substantial share of the continent's market. Unlike other Italian mafias, the 'Ndrangheta's structure is based on blood families ('ndrine), which makes infiltration and defections much rarer.

History

The organisation's roots are commonly traced to the late 19th century in Calabria. It expanded dramatically from the 1970s onwards, first through kidnapping for ransom and later through the international narcotics trade. Major police operations, including the Rinascimento-Scott trial of 2019–2023, have exposed the extent of its infiltration of politics, business and public contracts.

Structure

The basic unit is the 'ndrina, a family clan controlling a territory. Above the clans sit coordinating bodies for provinces, and a collegial body known as the Provincia or Crimine meets annually near the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Polsi. Secrecy, ritual and kinship bind the organisation together.
