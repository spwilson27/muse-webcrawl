André Maginot (17 February 1877 – 7 January 1932) was a French civil servant, soldier and politician. He is best remembered as the minister who championed the system of border fortifications that bears his name, the Maginot Line, built along France's frontier with Germany in the 1930s.

Early life and First World War

Maginot was born in Paris and educated in law, entering the colonial civil service in Algeria before being elected to the Chamber of Deputies in 1910 as a deputy for the Meuse. When the First World War broke out in 1914 he enlisted as a private soldier, refusing a staff position. He rose to sergeant and was awarded the Médaille militaire after being seriously wounded in the leg at Verdun in November 1914, an injury that left him lame for life. His wartime experience, and the devastation of his home region of Lorraine, shaped his lifelong commitment to French defence and to veterans' welfare.

Political career

Maginot served as Minister of Pensions, where he championed the rights of disabled veterans and war widows, Minister of the Colonies, and twice as Minister of War (1922–24 and 1929–32). Returning to the War Ministry in 1929, he obtained funding from parliament for a permanent system of fortifications along the north-eastern frontier, intended to deter a future German invasion, protect the industrial regions of Alsace-Lorraine, and buy time for mobilisation. Construction of the main works — vast underground forts (ouvrages) linked by tunnels, with retractable turrets and garrisons of thousands — began in 1930.

Death and legacy

Maginot died in Paris on 7 January 1932 after contracting typhoid fever, reportedly from eating contaminated oysters. The line of fortifications completed after his death was named in his honour. In May–June 1940 Germany bypassed the strongest parts of the Line through the Ardennes and Belgium, and the name "Maginot" became a byword for misplaced confidence in static defence, although the fortifications themselves largely fulfilled their narrower purpose of protecting the frontier provinces they covered.
