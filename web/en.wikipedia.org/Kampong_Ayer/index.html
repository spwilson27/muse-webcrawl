Kampong Ayer (Malay for "Water Village") is a prominent traditional settlement in Bandar Seri Begawan, the capital of Brunei. It comprises neighbourhoods of traditional houses, schools, mosques and clinics built on stilts above the Brunei River, and has been inhabited for over a thousand years. It is widely considered the largest stilt settlement in the world.

History

The settlement was the historic centre of the Bruneian Empire; the Venetian traveller Antonio Pigafetta, who visited with the Magellan expedition in 1521, described a city of some 25,000 families built entirely over salt water, prompting the nickname "Venice of the East". The water village remained the capital's core until colonial administrators developed the landward town in the early 20th century.

Life in the village

Modern Kampong Ayer is connected by some 36 kilometres of wooden and concrete boardwalks, and its houses are served by electricity, running water, schools and a fire station. Residents travel by wooden motorboats (perahu tambang) that function as water taxis. Although government housing schemes on land have drawn many families ashore, the village remains a protected heritage area and the cultural heart of Brunei Malay identity.
