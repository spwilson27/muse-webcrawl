Ad Deir

Ad Deir (Arabic: الدير, "the Monastery"), also spelled Al-Deir or El-Deir, is a monumental building carved out of rock in the ancient Jordanian city of Petra. It is the second most visited monument in Petra after the Treasury (Al-Khazneh). The building measures about 47 metres wide by 48.3 metres high.

History

The structure was built by the Nabataeans in the mid-1st century BCE, probably as a temple or a royal tomb. Its interior is a single great chamber with a broad podium at the back. The name "Monastery" derives from crosses inscribed on its back wall during a later period, when the chamber appears to have been reused as a Christian chapel in the Byzantine era, possibly around the 4th–5th centuries CE.

Architecture

The facade combines Nabataean and classical Greek architectural elements: engaged columns, alternating triangular and semicircular pediments in an upper tholos-like register, and a circular tholos at the centre of the top tier. Reaching Ad Deir requires climbing a path of some 850 rock-cut steps from the basin area of Petra.
