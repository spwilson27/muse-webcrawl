Stockholm

Stockholm is the capital and most populous city of Sweden as well as the largest urban area in the Nordic countries. Approximately 1 million people live in the municipality, with about 1.7 million in the urban area and 2.4 million in the metropolitan area. The city stretches across fourteen islands where Lake Mälaren flows into the Baltic Sea, in an archipelago of some 30,000 islands.

History

Stockholm is traditionally credited as founded by Birger Jarl in 1252 to protect Sweden from sea-borne invasions. The old town, Gamla Stan, built largely during the 13th to 16th centuries, remains one of the best-preserved medieval city centres in Europe. Stockholm became Sweden's capital and its political, cultural, and economic centre; the Stockholm Bloodbath of 1520 and the accession of Gustav Vasa in 1523 were pivotal events in Swedish history.

Geography and climate

The city sits at the junction of Lake Mälaren and the Baltic. It has a humid continental climate with mild summers — featuring very long daylight hours around the summer solstice — and cold, dark winters.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Royal Palace, Stockholm City Hall (site of the Nobel Prize banquet), the Vasa Museum housing the preserved 17th-century warship Vasa, and Skansen open-air museum. Stockholm hosts the annual Nobel Prize ceremony each December. See also Sweden, Gamla Stan, and Nobel Prize.
