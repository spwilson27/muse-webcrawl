Lamborghini Murciélago

The Lamborghini Murciélago is a sports car produced by Italian manufacturer Lamborghini from 2001 to 2010. The successor to the Diablo and the flagship V12 model of the range, it was introduced as a coupé in 2001 and was the first new design under Audi ownership. A roadster followed in 2004. The name Murciélago — Spanish for "bat" — honours a legendary fighting bull.

Lamborghini Murciélago
Manufacturer: Lamborghini
Production: 2001–2010 (4,099 built)
Designer: Luc Donckerwolke
Engine: 6.2–6.5 L V12
Layout: mid-engine, all-wheel drive
Successor: Lamborghini Aventador

Overview

The original Murciélago used a 6.2-litre V12 producing 572 PS (580 hp), with a six-speed manual or later automated manual transmission and all-wheel drive. Top speed was about 332 km/h (206 mph). Signature features included scissor doors and active air intakes that rose from the rear deck at speed or high temperature.

Variants

The LP 640 (2006) raised output to 640 PS from a 6.5-litre engine, with revised styling; the LP 670-4 SuperVeloce (2009) produced 670 PS, weighed less thanks to carbon fibre, and was limited in production. Other specials included the 40th Anniversary edition, the LP 650-4 Roadster and the Murciélago R-GT / LP 670 R-SV racing cars.

End of production

The final car, an LP 670-4 SuperVeloce, was produced on 5 November 2010, ending the line after 4,099 cars. The Aventador succeeded it in 2011.
