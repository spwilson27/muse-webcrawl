William Johnson (superintendent)

Born1715, County Meath, Ireland
DiedJuly 11, 1774, Johnstown, New York
OfficeSuperintendent of Indian Affairs for the Northern Department (1756–1774)

Sir William Johnson, 1st Baronet (c. 1715 – 11 July 1774) was an Irish-born official of the British Empire in North America who served as Superintendent of Indian Affairs for the northern colonies and became one of the largest landowners in the Mohawk Valley of New York.

Life

Johnson emigrated to New York in 1738 to manage his uncle's estates and built a trading career among the Mohawk, learning their language and customs and forming a lasting alliance with them; his partner Molly Brant was the sister of the Mohawk leader Joseph Brant. He commanded provincial and Iroquois forces in the French and Indian War, winning fame at the Battle of Lake George (1755). As superintendent he negotiated the Treaty of Fort Stanwix (1768), which moved the boundary of settlement westward.

Legacy

Johnson founded Johnstown, New York, and his manor, Johnson Hall, survives as a historic site.
