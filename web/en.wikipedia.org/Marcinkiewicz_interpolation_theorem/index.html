Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem

Diagram illustrating interpolation of operators between Lebesgue spaces (image placeholder).

The Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, discovered by Józef Marcinkiewicz (1939), is a result in real and harmonic analysis bounding the norms of certain operators between Lebesgue spaces. It is a close cousin of the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem, but applies to operators that are only of weak type at the endpoints rather than strong type.

Statement

Informally, if a sublinear operator T is bounded from Lp0 to weak Lp0 and from Lp1 to weak Lp1, then for every intermediate exponent p between p0 and p1, the operator is bounded from Lp to Lp in the strong sense. The proof proceeds by splitting a function into its "large" and "small" parts relative to a level λ and applying the weak-type bounds to each piece.

Applications

The theorem is a standard tool for proving boundedness of singular integral operators such as the Hilbert transform, and of maximal operators such as the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function. Marcinkiewicz originally used it in connection with the theory of trigonometric series and almost everywhere convergence of Fourier series.
