Thabo Mbeki

Mbeki during his presidency

Thabo Mvuyelwa Mbeki (born 18 June 1942) is a South African politician who served as the second president of South Africa from 14 June 1999 to 24 September 2008, succeeding Nelson Mandela. A leading figure of the African National Congress (ANC), he was the architect of the idea of an "African Renaissance."

Early life and exile

Mbeki was born in Mbewuleni in the Eastern Cape, the son of ANC activists Govan and Epainette Mbeki. He joined the ANC Youth League as a teenager and, after the party was banned, went into exile in 1962, studying economics at the University of Sussex in Britain and later working for the ANC in London, Lusaka, and elsewhere. He rose to become the movement's head of information and a key negotiator in the transition from apartheid.

Presidency

As deputy president under Mandela (1994–1999) and then president, Mbeki pursued market-friendly economic policy (the GEAR programme), promoted the New Partnership for Africa's Development (NEPAD), and championed African solutions to African problems. His presidency was overshadowed by his questioning of the link between HIV and AIDS and the government's delayed provision of antiretroviral drugs, a stance widely blamed for hundreds of thousands of preventable deaths, and by an arms deal corruption scandal and the political crisis in neighbouring Zimbabwe.

Resignation and later work

After losing the ANC leadership to Jacob Zuma at the 2007 Polokwane conference, Mbeki resigned the presidency in September 2008 following an ANC recall, after a court judgment suggested political interference in Zuma's prosecution. He has since worked as an African Union mediator and through the Thabo Mbeki Foundation.
