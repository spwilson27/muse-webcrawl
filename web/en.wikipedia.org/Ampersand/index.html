Ampersand
Character&
Meaning"and"
OriginLigature of Latin "et"

The ampersand, also known as the and sign, is the logogram &, representing the conjunction "and". It originated as a ligature of the letters of the word et, the Latin word for "and".

History

The ampersand can be traced to the 1st century AD and the old Roman cursive, in which E and T were occasionally written together. By the early medieval period the ligature was a standard part of scribal practice. The name "ampersand" is a corruption of the phrase "and per se and", recited by schoolchildren when the character — once recited as the 27th letter of the English alphabet — appeared at the end of the alphabet.

Usage

The ampersand is common in company names, titles of works, and informal writing, but is generally avoided in formal prose except where part of a proper name. In computing, it appears in markup languages: in HTML, & begins character entities, and in many programming languages it denotes logical or bitwise AND.

Forms

Typefaces render the ampersand in forms ranging from a clear et ligature, especially in italic, to the familiar cursive epsilon-like shape.
