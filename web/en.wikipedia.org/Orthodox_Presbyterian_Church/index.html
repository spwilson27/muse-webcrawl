The Orthodox Presbyterian Church (OPC) is a confessional Presbyterian denomination in North America, founded in 1936 by J. Gresham Machen and other conservatives who left the Presbyterian Church in the USA during the Fundamentalist–Modernist Controversy. It adheres strictly to the Westminster Standards and to a Presbyterian form of church government.

History

Machen, a professor at Princeton Seminary, founded Westminster Theological Seminary in 1929 after Princeton's reorganisation and later the Independent Board for Presbyterian Foreign Missions, for which the PCUSA deposed him. The new church — briefly called the Presbyterian Church of America before a lawsuit forced the name change in 1939 — suffered the early death of Machen in 1937 and a split that produced the Bible Presbyterian Church. In 1986 the OPC declined to join the Presbyterian Church in America, remaining a separate denomination.

Beliefs and practice

The OPC confesses the inerrancy of Scripture, salvation by grace alone through faith alone, and covenant theology, and practises infant baptism and psalmody-influenced worship in varying degrees across congregations. It belongs to the North American Presbyterian and Reformed Council and the International Conference of Reformed Churches.
