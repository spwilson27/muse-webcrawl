Apple Inc. v. Samsung Electronics Co. was a series of patent lawsuits between Apple Inc. and Samsung Electronics, the two largest smartphone makers in the world, beginning in April 2011 and fought in courts across the United States, South Korea, Japan, Australia, Germany, the Netherlands, France and the United Kingdom. At its height, the dispute comprised dozens of cases and became the defining legal battle of the "smartphone patent wars."

Background

Apple sued Samsung in April 2011, alleging that Samsung's Galaxy smartphones and tablets slavishly copied the design and software features of the iPhone and iPad, infringing Apple's design patents (covering the rectangular shape with rounded corners and the grid-of-icons home screen), utility patents (including "bounce-back" scrolling and tap-to-zoom), and trade dress. Samsung countersued, asserting its own wireless-technology patents. The companies were simultaneously fierce competitors and major commercial partners: Samsung supplied Apple with processors, displays and memory chips.

The first U.S. trial (2012)

The first U.S. case went to a jury in San Jose, California, before Judge Lucy Koh. In August 2012 the jury found that Samsung had infringed most of the asserted Apple patents, rejected Samsung's claims, and awarded Apple about $1.05 billion in damages. Portions of the award were later vacated and recalculated in retrials, including a finding that Apple was not entitled to damages for trade dress dilution because the claimed trade dress was functional.

Appeals and the Supreme Court

In Samsung Electronics Co. v. Apple Inc. (2016), the U.S. Supreme Court addressed design-patent damages under 35 U.S.C. § 289, which awards the infringer's "total profit" from an "article of manufacture." The Court held unanimously that the relevant "article of manufacture" could be a component of a product rather than the whole end product, and remanded for a new damages determination. On retrial in 2018, a jury awarded Apple about $539 million. In June 2018 the parties settled the remaining U.S. litigation on undisclosed terms, ending the seven-year dispute.

Second U.S. case and wider war

A second California case, covering later devices and software patents such as slide-to-unlock and autocorrect, produced a 2014 jury verdict of about $120 million for Apple (and a small award for Samsung), most of which was reversed on appeal. Related cases in other countries produced mixed results; a British court ordered Apple to publish notices that Samsung had not copied the iPad, after finding the Galaxy Tab devices "not as cool" as Apple's. The broader conflict dwindled as the companies shifted strategy, and a 2014 truce ended all litigation outside the United States.
