High-dynamic-range

High dynamic range (HDR) refers to techniques and formats that reproduce a greater dynamic range of luminosity than is possible with standard digital imaging or display technology. Standard dynamic range (SDR) imagery typically captures or displays a limited contrast ratio, whereas HDR extends both peak brightness and shadow detail.

Imaging

In photography, HDR imaging combines multiple exposures of the same scene — taken at different exposure values — into a single image that preserves detail in both highlights and shadows. The resulting image is usually tone-mapped for display on conventional screens. Consumer smartphones widely adopted computational HDR modes in the 2010s.

Display and video

HDR video formats include HDR10, Dolby Vision, HDR10+ and Hybrid Log-Gamma (HLG). These use wider colour gamuts (such as Rec. 2020), 10-bit or higher colour depth, and perceptual quantizer transfer functions to encode brightness values up to thousands of nits. HDR displays require backlights or emissive pixels capable of high peak luminance and deep blacks.

Computing

In computer graphics, high-dynamic-range rendering stores lighting calculations in floating-point formats before tone mapping, enabling realistic effects such as bloom and exposure adaptation in video games and film production.
