Crystal radio

Illustration: Crystal radio

A crystal radio (or crystal set) is a simple radio receiver that needs no battery or external power: it is powered entirely by the energy of the radio waves it receives. It uses a crystal detector—historically a galena (lead sulfide) crystal probed by a fine wire "cat's whisker"—to demodulate amplitude-modulated signals.

History

Crystal radios were the first widely used type of radio receiver, popular from the early 1900s through the 1920s, before vacuum-tube sets became affordable. During both world wars, soldiers built improvised "foxhole radios" from razor blades and pencils.

Design

A typical set consists of a long wire antenna, a tuned LC circuit (coil and variable capacitor) to select the station, the crystal detector, and high-impedance earphones, because the received audio is very weak and cannot drive a loudspeaker.

Modern use

Crystal sets remain popular as educational projects demonstrating the basic principles of radio reception, tuning, and detection.
