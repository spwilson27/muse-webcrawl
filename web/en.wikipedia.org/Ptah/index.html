Ptah

Cult centre: Memphis
Symbols: djed pillar, was sceptre, ankh
Consort: Sekhmet (or Bastet)
Offspring: Nefertem; Imhotep (by adoption)

Ptah is an ancient Egyptian deity, a creator god and patron of craftsmen and architects. He was the local god of Memphis, the ancient capital of Egypt, where he headed the Memphite triad with his consort Sekhmet and their son Nefertem. Ptah is usually depicted as a bearded man wrapped in a shroud, wearing a skullcap and holding a combined sceptre bearing the was, ankh, and djed symbols.

Creation theology

According to Memphite theology, recorded on the Shabaka Stone, Ptah created the world through the thought of his heart and the speech of his tongue: by conceiving the gods and all things in his heart and speaking their names, he brought them into being. This doctrine, sometimes described as creation by word and intellect, contrasts with the Heliopolitan creation account centred on Atum.

Cult and influence

The high priests of Ptah at Memphis were among the most powerful religious officials in Egypt, and the god's great temple, Hut-ka-Ptah ("House of the ka of Ptah"), stood at the heart of the city; the Greek rendering of this name, Aigyptos, is the origin of the word "Egypt". Ptah was merged with the funerary gods Sokar and Osiris in the New Kingdom, and he was regarded as the patron of artisans, who in later periods were sometimes buried under his protection at Deir el-Medina.
