Meskwaki

The Meskwaki (Meskwaki: Meshkwahkihaki, "People of the Red Earth"), historically known in English and French as the Fox, are a Native American people of the Algonquian language family, closely allied for centuries with the Sauk (Sac). Their language, Meskwaki, is a dialect of the Sauk–Fox–Kickapoo language.

History

Originating in the Great Lakes region around the St. Lawrence valley and present-day Michigan and Wisconsin, the Meskwaki came into conflict with New France in the Fox Wars of the early eighteenth century, after which many joined the Sauk in Wisconsin, Illinois and Iowa. In the Black Hawk War of 1832 the two peoples resisted removal; treaties subsequently pushed them west to Kansas and Indian Territory (Oklahoma).

The Iowa settlement

Uniquely, in 1857 Meskwaki families used pooled funds to purchase 80 acres of land along the Iowa River near Tama, Iowa — land the tribe bought rather than being granted by treaty or reservation act. This Meskwaki Settlement, grown to several thousand acres, remains home to the federally recognised Sac and Fox Tribe of the Mississippi in Iowa. The community maintains its language and ceremonies, an annual powwow dating to 1913, and enterprises including the Meskwaki Bingo Casino Hotel. Related federally recognised nations include the Sac and Fox Nation of Oklahoma and the Sac and Fox Nation of Missouri in Kansas and Nebraska.

Culture

Traditional Meskwaki society was organised in clans, with summer agricultural villages (corn, beans and squash) and winter hunts. The linguist William Jones, himself of Meskwaki descent, recorded extensive texts in the early 1900s that remain foundational for Algonquian linguistics.
