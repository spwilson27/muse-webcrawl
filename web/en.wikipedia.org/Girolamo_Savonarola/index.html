Girolamo Savonarola

Girolamo Savonarola (21 September 1452 – 23 May 1498) was an Italian Dominican friar and preacher active in Renaissance Florence. For a short time after the overthrow of Medici rule in 1494 he was the effective spiritual and political leader of the city, famous for his prophecies, his campaign of moral reform, and the "Bonfire of the Vanities".

Born in Ferrara and educated in the humanities and medicine, he joined the Dominican Order in 1475. After years of teaching and preaching in northern Italy, he returned to Florence in 1490 at the invitation of Lorenzo de' Medici, and from the pulpit of San Marco and the cathedral he denounced corruption in the Church, the tyranny and luxury of rulers, and foretold calamities for Italy — prophecies apparently fulfilled by the French invasion of 1494.

Rule in Florence

After the Medici were expelled, Savonarola helped shape a new republican constitution with a Great Council. His followers, the Piagnoni ("weepers"), enforced strict moral legislation; in February 1497 his supporters burned books, artworks, cosmetics, mirrors, and other "vanities" in the Piazza della Signoria.

Downfall and death

His defiance of Pope Alexander VI led to excommunication in 1497. As famine, plague, and political opposition mounted, a proposed trial by fire in April 1498 collapsed in fiasco; a mob attacked San Marco, and Savonarola was arrested. Under torture he confessed to inventing his prophecies, and on 23 May 1498 he was hanged and burned in the Piazza della Signoria with two fellow friars. He remains a controversial figure — regarded by some as a forerunner of the Reformation and by others as a fanatic — and his cause for beatification has periodically been promoted.
