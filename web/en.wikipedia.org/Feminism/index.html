Feminism is a range of socio-political movements and ideologies that aim to define and establish the political, economic, personal, and social equality of the sexes. Feminism holds that societies prioritise the male point of view and that women are treated unjustly in these societies, and it encompasses efforts to change that — fighting against gender stereotypes and improving educational, professional, and interpersonal opportunities and outcomes for women.

History

Early landmarks include Mary Wollstonecraft's A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792) and the 1848 Seneca Falls Convention. The "first wave" of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries centred on legal rights, above all women's suffrage, won in many countries between 1893 (New Zealand) and 1920 (the United States) and beyond. The "second wave" (1960s–1980s), ignited by works such as Simone de Beauvoir's The Second Sex (1949) and Betty Friedan's The Feminine Mystique (1963), broadened the agenda to sexuality, family, the workplace, reproductive rights and de facto inequality. The "third wave" of the 1990s emphasised diversity and intersectionality — a term coined by Kimberlé Crenshaw — while a "fourth wave" from about 2012 has been characterised by social media, campaigns against sexual harassment such as #MeToo, and a focus on sexual violence.

Branches

Branches include liberal feminism, radical feminism, Marxist and socialist feminism, ecofeminism, postcolonial and Black feminism, and intersectional feminism. Feminist theory extends into philosophy, literary criticism, economics, law and science and technology studies.

Effects and criticism

Feminist movements have campaigned for and achieved women's suffrage, property and contract rights, access to education and employment, maternity leave, equal pay legislation, and protections against domestic and sexual violence. Feminism has also faced criticism from within and without — over exclusion of women of colour and working-class women, over positions on sex work and transgender rights, and from anti-feminist movements — and it continues to adapt to local contexts worldwide.
