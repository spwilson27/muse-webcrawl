East Francia

East Francia (Latin: Francia orientalis) was the eastern part of the Frankish Empire created by the Treaty of Verdun in 843, when the empire of Louis the Pious was divided among his three sons. The eastern kingdom went to Louis the German and is generally regarded as the precursor of the Kingdom of Germany and, through it, the Holy Roman Empire.

History

Louis the German ruled from 843 until 876. On his death the kingdom was divided among his sons, though it was reunited under Charles the Fat, the last Carolingian to rule a briefly reunified empire. After his deposition in 887, Arnulf of Carinthia became king. The East Frankish Carolingian line ended with Louis the Child in 911, when the nobility elected Conrad I, the first non-Carolingian king.

Transition to Germany

The election of the Saxon duke Henry the Fowler in 919 and the reign of his son Otto I, crowned emperor in 962, mark the transition from East Francia to the medieval German kingdom. Unlike West Francia, which evolved into France, the eastern kingdom retained a strong stem-duchy structure — Saxony, Franconia, Bavaria, Swabia, and Lotharingia.
