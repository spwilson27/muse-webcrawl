The Truman Show is a 1998 American psychological comedy-drama film directed by Peter Weir, produced by Scott Rudin, and written by Andrew Niccol. It stars Jim Carrey as Truman Burbank, a man whose entire life is a reality television show broadcast around the clock, unbeknownst to him, from a giant artificial dome containing the fabricated town of Seahaven.

Plot

Truman, adopted at birth by a television corporation, lives in Seahaven surrounded by actors playing his family, friends, and neighbors, under the direction of the show's creator, Christof (Ed Harris), who controls the weather and even a simulated moon from a control room in the dome's sky. A series of production mishaps — a falling stage light, a rain shower that follows only him, and an encounter with a former cast member — awakens Truman's suspicion. Despite engineered obstacles, including a staged fear of water, Truman sails to the edge of the dome, discovers the exit door, and chooses to leave, delivering his catchphrase farewell: "In case I don't see ya, good afternoon, good evening, and good night!"

Themes and reception

The film explores media manipulation, surveillance, simulated reality, and free will, and is widely cited as prescient of the rise of reality television, which exploded shortly after its release. It was a critical and commercial success; Carrey won a Golden Globe for his dramatic performance, and the film received three Academy Award nominations, including Best Director and Best Original Screenplay.
