Camp (style)

Camp is an aesthetic sensibility that regards something as appealing because of its bad taste and ironic value. Camp aesthetics disrupt modernist notions of what art should be and challenge high art by inverting its aesthetic attributes.

Definition and theory

The concept was brought to wide attention by Susan Sontag's 1964 essay "Notes on 'Camp'", which defined camp as a love of the unnatural, of artifice and exaggeration — "the triumph of the epicene style" and of style over content. Sontag distinguished naïve camp, the serious work that fails at its ambitions (often the purest camp), from deliberate camp, which knowingly performs exaggeration. Camp is closely associated with gay culture, which long used it as a coded, appreciative language for mass culture — from opera divas and Hollywood melodrama to drag and the films of John Waters.

Examples and influence

Canonical camp objects range from Tiffany lamps and Art Nouveau to Busby Berkeley musicals, B-movies, and Eurovision Song Contest entries. The 2019 Metropolitan Museum Costume Institute exhibition Camp: Notes on Fashion, accompanied by the Met Gala, examined camp's influence on fashion from Versailles to contemporary designers.
