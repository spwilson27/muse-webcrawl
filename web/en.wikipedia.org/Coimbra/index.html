Coimbra

Coimbra is a city and municipality in central Portugal, in the Centro region, with a population of roughly 140,000 in the municipality. Built on hills above the Mondego River, it was the capital of Portugal from 1131 to 1255 and is best known as the home of the University of Coimbra, one of the oldest universities in continuous operation in the world.

History

The site was the Roman town of Aeminium and later a frontier city between Christian and Muslim Iberia. Afonso Henriques, the first king of Portugal, made it his capital after the founding of the kingdom. The university, founded in Lisbon in 1290, was definitively transferred to Coimbra in 1537, shaping the city's character ever since.

Sights and culture

Highlights include the Old Cathedral (Sé Velha), the Monastery of Santa Clara, the Joanina Library, and the university tower. Coimbra has its own style of fado, sung traditionally by male students in black capes, and student festivals such as the Queima das Fitas.

Economy

The university and its hospital dominate the local economy, alongside research institutes, technology firms, and tourism.
