Phrenic nerve

OriginCervical spinal cord, C3–C5
InnervatesDiaphragm

The phrenic nerve is a mixed motor and sensory nerve which originates from the C3–C5 spinal nerves in the neck. It is the sole motor supply to the diaphragm, the principal muscle of respiration; a common mnemonic is "C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive".

Course

Each phrenic nerve descends through the neck on the anterior surface of the scalenus anterior muscle, passes through the thorax alongside the pericardium, and reaches the diaphragm, which it pierces or passes to supply from below.

Clinical significance

Damage to one phrenic nerve causes paralysis and elevation of the corresponding half of the diaphragm. Irritation of the nerve or diaphragm is a cause of hiccups, and pain from diaphragmatic irritation may be referred to the shoulder tip, which shares the C3–C5 dermatomes.
