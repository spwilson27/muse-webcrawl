Isometric projection

TypeAxonometric projection
Axis angles120° between axes
Viewing angle35.264° rotation, 45° turn
Used inEngineering drawing, video games, pixel art

Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings. It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angles between them are all 120 degrees.

Geometry

An isometric view is obtained by rotating an object 45° about a vertical axis and then tilting it by arcsin(tan 30°) ≈ 35.264° so that one corner points directly at the viewer and the three visible edges are spaced at 120°. Because all axes are foreshortened by the same factor ( √(2/3) ≈ 0.816), measurements can be taken along any axis with a single scale — hence "isometric", Greek for "equal measure". In the common "isometric drawing" convention used by engineers, this foreshortening is ignored and full-scale dimensions are drawn along the axes.

Applications

Isometric views are standard in engineering, architecture, patent illustrations and exploded assembly diagrams because they show three faces of an object without perspective distortion. The style is also famous in video games: because isometric (or near-isometric, 2:1 dimetric) tiles repeat seamlessly and need no scaling with distance, the projection was widely used for strategy and role-playing games such as SimCity 2000, Diablo and Age of Empires, and in pixel art generally.

Limitations

Without shading or occlusion, isometric drawings can produce optical ambiguities such as the Necker cube and impossible objects — paradoxes exploited artistically by M. C. Escher.
