Tryptophan (symbol Trp or W) is an α-amino acid that is used in the biosynthesis of proteins. It is an essential amino acid in humans, meaning it cannot be synthesised by the body and must be obtained from the diet. Tryptophan is notable as the precursor of the neurotransmitter serotonin, the hormone melatonin, and the vitamin niacin (vitamin B3).

Structure and properties

Tryptophan contains an indole side chain, making it the largest and most structurally complex of the standard amino acids. Its indole ring gives it distinctive ultraviolet absorption and fluorescence properties that are widely exploited in protein spectroscopy. In proteins, tryptophan residues are relatively rare but often functionally important.

Metabolism

Most dietary tryptophan is metabolised along the kynurenine pathway, ultimately contributing to the synthesis of NAD. A smaller fraction is converted to serotonin and then melatonin, involved in mood and sleep regulation. Tryptophan is obtained from protein-rich foods such as meat, eggs, dairy products, soy and seeds. Popular culture associates it with post-meal drowsiness, particularly after turkey dinners, though the effect in normal meals is modest and largely due to other factors.

Industrial production

Tryptophan supplements are produced industrially by bacterial fermentation. In 1989, contaminated L-tryptophan supplements from a single manufacturer were linked to an outbreak of eosinophilia–myalgia syndrome, leading to recalls and tighter regulation.
