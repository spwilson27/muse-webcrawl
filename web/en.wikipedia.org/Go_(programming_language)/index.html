Go is a high-level, general-purpose programming language that is statically typed and compiled. It is known for its simplicity and efficiency in building large-scale networked servers and distributed systems. Go was designed at Google by Robert Griesemer, Rob Pike, and Ken Thompson, and publicly announced in November 2009.

The language is often referred to as Golang because of its former domain name, golang.org, but its proper name is Go. There are two major implementations: Google's self-hosting "gc" compiler toolchain, and "gccgo", a frontend to other compilers originally.

History

Go was designed at Google in 2007 to improve programming productivity in an era of multicore, networked machines and large codebases. The designers wanted to address criticism of languages such as C++ while keeping their useful characteristics. Go 1.0 was released in March 2012, and Go 1.18, released in 2022, added generics — the most requested language feature since its inception.

Design

Go is syntactically similar to C, but with memory safety, garbage collection, structural typing, and CSP-style concurrency. Its designers were motivated by a dislike of the complexity of C++, and drew ideas from languages such as Oberon, Newsqueak, and Limbo. The language omits features such as exception handling in favour of explicit error values, and omits class inheritance in favour of composition through interfaces and embedding.

Concurrency

Go's primary concurrency constructs are goroutines, lightweight threads managed by the Go runtime, and channels, typed conduits through which goroutines communicate. This follows the communicating sequential processes (CSP) style: "Do not communicate by sharing memory; instead, share memory by communicating."

Generics

Go 1.18 introduced support for generic programming using type parameters, allowing functions and data structures to be written in terms of types that are specified later.

Applications

Go is widely used in cloud and server-side infrastructure. Notable projects written in Go include Docker, Kubernetes, Hugo, and the backends of many large services. It is also popular for command-line tools, DevOps tooling, and network services.
