Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, officially the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel (Swedish: Sveriges riksbanks pris i ekonomisk vetenskap till Alfred Nobels minne), is an award for outstanding contributions to the field of economics. It was established in 1968 by Sweden's central bank, Sveriges Riksbank, on the bank's 300th anniversary, and is administered by the Nobel Foundation together with the original Nobel Prizes, although it was not one of the prizes created by Alfred Nobel's will of 1895.

Selection and award

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences selects the laureates through an economics prize committee, following a nomination process parallel to those of the physics and chemistry prizes. The prize is presented by the King of Sweden at the same ceremony as the Nobel Prizes in Stockholm on 10 December each year, and its monetary value equals that of the Nobel Prizes. Between one and three laureates may share the award in a given year. The first prize, awarded in 1969, went to Ragnar Frisch of Norway and Jan Tinbergen of the Netherlands "for having developed and applied dynamic models for the analysis of economic processes."

Laureates and fields

More than ninety laureates have received the prize. It has honoured work in macroeconomics, econometrics, game theory (John Nash, 1994), behavioural economics (Daniel Kahneman, 2002; Richard Thaler, 2017), economic history and institutions (Douglass North, 1993; Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson, and James A. Robinson, 2024), and development economics. Elinor Ostrom (2009) was the first woman laureate, recognised for her analysis of economic governance of common-pool resources; Esther Duflo (2019) is the youngest recipient, and Claudia Goldin received the prize in 2023 for research on women's labour market outcomes.

Controversy

Because the prize was created by the central bank rather than by Nobel, some descendants of the Nobel family and critics argue it should not be called a "Nobel Prize"; the Nobel Foundation refers to it as being awarded "in association with" the Nobel Prizes. Critics have also charged the prize with favouring mainstream and neoliberal economics, while defenders note the breadth of schools of thought represented among laureates.
