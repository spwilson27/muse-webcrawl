Spread of Islam

The spread of Islam spans a period of over 1,400 years, beginning with the preaching of Muhammad in Mecca in the early 7th century CE. The early Muslim conquests, following Muhammad's death in 632, established a caliphate stretching from the Iberian Peninsula in the west to Central Asia in the east, while trade, missionary activity and Sufi networks later carried Islam into sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia and Southeast Asia.

Early conquests

Under the Rashidun Caliphate (632–661) and the Umayyad Caliphate (661–750), Muslim armies conquered the Sasanian Empire, Syria, Egypt, North Africa and Visigothic Spain. These conquests created a vast empire, but conversion of subject populations to Islam was generally gradual, occurring over centuries through social, economic and cultural processes rather than immediate mass conversion.

Trade and missionaries

Beyond the frontiers of the caliphates, Islam spread largely peacefully along trade routes. Muslim merchants carried the faith across the Sahara to West African kingdoms such as Ghana and Mali, along the Swahili Coast of East Africa, and by sea to India, Indonesia and Malaysia. Sufi orders played a major role in popular conversion in South and Southeast Asia.

Later expansion

The Ottoman Empire extended Islam's political reach into Southeast Europe, while in the modern era migration has established significant Muslim communities in Europe and the Americas. Islam is today the world's second-largest religion, with roughly two billion adherents.
