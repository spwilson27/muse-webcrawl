Rockwell B-1 Lancer

B-1 Lancer
Manufacturer: Rockwell (now Boeing)
First flight: 23 December 1974
Introduced: 1 October 1986
Role: Supersonic strategic bomber
Nickname: "Bone"

The Rockwell B-1 Lancer is a supersonic variable-sweep wing, heavy bomber used by the United States Air Force. It is commonly called the "Bone" (from "B-One"). It is one of three strategic bombers serving in the U.S. Air Force fleet along with the B-2 Spirit and the B-52 Stratofortress.

Development

The B-1 was first envisioned in the 1960s as a platform that would combine the Mach 2 speed of the B-58 Hustler with the range and payload of the B-52. The original B-1A program was cancelled in 1977, but the design was revived in 1981 as the B-1B, emphasising low-level penetration at reduced speed and with a smaller radar cross-section. One hundred B-1Bs were built and entered service in 1986.

Operational history

Originally designed for nuclear missions, the B-1 was converted to a conventional-only role in the 1990s under the START treaties. It saw its first combat in Operation Desert Fox (1998) and went on to extensive service in Kosovo, Afghanistan, and Iraq, where its large payload, long loiter time, and speed made it a mainstay of close air support and precision strike missions. The Air Force plans to retire the fleet as the B-21 Raider enters service.
