Battles of Khalkhin Gol

Date11 May – 16 September 1939
LocationKhalkha River, Mongolian–Manchurian border
ResultSoviet–Mongolian victory
BelligerentsSoviet Union and Mongolian People's Republic vs. Empire of Japan and Manchukuo
CommandersGeorgy Zhukov; Grigori Shtern vs. Michitarō Komatsubara; Yasuoka Masaomi

The Battles of Khalkhin Gol (Russian: Бои на Халхин-Голе), known in Japan as the Nomonhan Incident, were the decisive engagements of the undeclared Soviet–Japanese border conflict fought between May and September 1939 along the Khalkha River on the border of Mongolia and Japanese-occupied Manchuria. The battles ended in the crushing defeat of the Japanese Sixth Army by Soviet forces under Georgy Zhukov, with lasting consequences for Japanese strategy in the Second World War.

Soviet armour in the August offensive that encircled the Japanese.

Background

The border between Mongolia (a Soviet client state) and Manchukuo (a Japanese puppet state) was disputed along the Khalkha River. Skirmishing in May 1939 between Mongolian and Manchukuo cavalry escalated as both Japan's Kwantung Army and the Red Army fed in reinforcements. Through June and July the fighting grew into full-scale battle, including large tank engagements and air battles, while Japanese attacks failed to dislodge the Soviet bridgehead east of the river.

The August offensive

Zhukov, appointed to command the Soviet 1st Army Group, secretly assembled a powerful force of infantry, artillery, aircraft and some 500 tanks, supplied by truck over hundreds of kilometres of steppe. On 20 August 1939 he launched a classic double envelopment: while the centre pinned the Japanese, armoured spearheads crossed the river on both flanks and closed behind them. The Japanese 23rd Division was effectively annihilated; by 31 August organised resistance in the pocket had ended. Combined casualties are commonly estimated at around 17,000–20,000 on each side, with Japanese losses the heavier.

Aftermath

A ceasefire was signed on 15 September 1939. The defeat discredited the Japanese Army's "strike north" faction, which had favoured expansion against the Soviet Union, and strengthened the "strike south" strategy against the colonial empires and the United States. It paved the way for the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact of 1941, which freed the USSR to transfer Siberian divisions west against Germany. For Zhukov, Khalkhin Gol was the rehearsal — in logistics, deception and armoured encirclement — for his later victories in the Great Patriotic War.
