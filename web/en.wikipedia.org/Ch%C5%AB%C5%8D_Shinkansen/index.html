The Chūō Shinkansen (中央新幹線) is a maglev railway line under construction in Japan between Tokyo and Nagoya, with plans for extension to Osaka. The line is being built by the Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central) using its superconducting maglev (SCMaglev) technology, tested since 1997 on the Yamanashi test track. Trains will operate at up to 500 km/h, covering the Tokyo–Nagoya distance of about 286 kilometres in roughly 40 minutes.

Route

The line runs from Shinagawa Station in Tokyo westward through Kanagawa, Yamanashi, Nagano and Gifu prefectures to Nagoya. About 86% of the first section will be in tunnels, including deep tunnels beneath the Southern Japanese Alps, reflecting a route that passes largely underground beneath the mountains rather than following the populated coast served by the Tōkaidō Shinkansen. Intermediate stations are planned at Sagamihara (Kanagawa), Kōfu (Yamanashi), Iida (Nagano) and Nakatsugawa (Gifu).

Construction and delays

Construction began in 2014 after government approval, with JR Central financing the Tokyo–Nagoya section itself. The original target opening of 2027 was postponed after a dispute with Shizuoka Prefecture over concerns that tunneling under the Ōi River would reduce water flow, which stalled work on the Shizuoka section for years. JR Central abandoned the 2027 opening, and the line is now expected to open in the mid-2030s at the earliest; the Nagoya–Osaka extension, originally planned for 2045 (brought forward to 2037 with a government loan), is expected to follow later.

Technology

The SCMaglev uses superconducting magnets on the train that interact with coils in the guideway for levitation, guidance and propulsion. Vehicles levitate about 10 centimetres above the guideway once above roughly 150 km/h, running on rubber tires below that speed. Test trains reached 603 km/h in 2015, a world record for rail vehicles.
