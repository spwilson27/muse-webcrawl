Competitive intelligence

Competitive intelligence (CI) is the systematic collection and analysis of information about competitors, customers, and market conditions to support strategic decision-making. It is a legal and ethical business practice, distinct from industrial espionage, relying on publicly available sources such as trade publications, financial filings, patent records, job postings, product documentation, and trade shows, as well as interviews and field sources.

Process

CI is often described as a cycle analogous to the intelligence cycle used by government agencies: direction (defining decision-makers' needs, sometimes called key intelligence topics), collection, processing, analysis, dissemination, and feedback. Analytical techniques include competitor profiling, SWOT analysis, Porter's five forces, win/loss analysis, and war gaming.

Organisation and ethics

Many large companies maintain dedicated CI units or employ external firms. The professional association Strategic and Competitive Intelligence Professionals (SCIP), founded in 1986, promulgates a code of ethics requiring practitioners to identify themselves honestly, comply with laws, and avoid illicit collection methods such as deception or misrepresentation.

Related concepts

Related activities include market research, business intelligence (usually focused on internal data), benchmarking, and counterintelligence—protecting one's own information from competitors' collection efforts.
