Longhouse

A longhouse or long house is a type of long, proportionately narrow, single-room building for communal dwelling built by particular peoples in various parts of the world, including Asia, Europe and North America.

Traditions

  • Neolithic Europe: Linear Pottery culture farmers built longhouses up to 45 metres long from around 5000 BC.
  • Scandinavia: Viking Age longhouses, with slightly bowed side walls, housed families and livestock under one roof.
  • Indigenous North America: The Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) longhouse, framed in bent saplings and covered in elm bark, housed extended matrilineal families in compartments along a central corridor of hearths; "longhouse" also became the metaphor for the Iroquois Confederacy itself.
  • Borneo: Dayak peoples build riverbank longhouses on stilts containing many family apartments (bilek) along a shared gallery.
  • Pacific Northwest: Plank houses of cedar served as multifamily dwellings and ceremonial spaces.
