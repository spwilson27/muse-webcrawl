Küstrin (Polish: Kostrzyn nad Odrą) is a historical town at the confluence of the Oder and Warta rivers, on the present border between Germany and Poland. Until 1945 it was a Prussian fortress town in the province of Brandenburg; after the Second World War the part east of the Oder was transferred to Poland, while the western suburb Küstrin-Kietz remained in Germany.

History

First mentioned in 1232, Küstrin became a residence of the Margraves of Brandenburg-Küstrin in the 16th century. John of Brandenburg-Küstrin built a powerful fortress, which later served as a Prussian state prison — most famously for the young crown prince Frederick (later Frederick the Great), who was imprisoned there in 1730 after his attempted flight and was forced to watch the execution of his friend Hans Hermann von Katte.

Second World War

In 1945 the fortress was declared a Festung and was besieged by the Red Army during the Vistula–Oder Offensive. The old town was almost entirely destroyed in the fighting and was never rebuilt; its ruins, sometimes called a "Prussian Pompeii", remain as a memorial area within the modern Polish town of Kostrzyn nad Odrą, which was rebuilt on a different site.
