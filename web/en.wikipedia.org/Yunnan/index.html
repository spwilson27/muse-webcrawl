Yunnan (Chinese: 云南; pinyin: Yúnnán) is a province in southwestern China, bordering Myanmar, Laos and Vietnam, as well as the Chinese provinces of Sichuan, Guizhou, Guangxi and Tibet. Its capital, Kunming, is known as the "Spring City" for its mild climate. Yunnan is noted for extreme geographic and biological diversity and for being China's most ethnically diverse province.

Geography

The province descends from the high mountains and gorges of the northwest — where the Yangtze, Mekong and Salween run in parallel through the Hengduan Mountains — to subtropical lowlands in the south. Major sights include Tiger Leaping Gorge, Jade Dragon Snow Mountain, the Stone Forest near Kunming, Erhai Lake at Dali, and the Honghe Hani rice terraces, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

People and culture

About a third of Yunnan's population belongs to officially recognised ethnic minorities, including the Yi, Bai, Hani, Zhuang, Dai, Miao and Hui. Twenty-five of China's 56 recognised ethnic groups are found in the province. Shangri-La (Zhongdian), Dali and Lijiang are major centres of Bai, Naxi and Tibetan culture.

Economy

Yunnan is a centre for tobacco, flowers, tea — including Pu'er tea — coffee, rubber and non-ferrous metals, and it is famous for wild mushrooms and rich biodiversity, including wild Asian elephants in Xishuangbanna. Tourism is a pillar industry, and the province serves as China's gateway to Southeast Asia via the Kunming–Bangkok corridor and the China–Laos railway, opened in 2021.
