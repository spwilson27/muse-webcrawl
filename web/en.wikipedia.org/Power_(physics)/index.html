Power (physics)

Power

Symbol: P

SI unit: Watt (W)

Definition: P = dW/dt

Other units: Horsepower, dBm

In physics, power is the amount of energy transferred or converted per unit time. In the International System of Units, the unit of power is the watt, equal to one joule per second. In older works, power is sometimes called activity. Power is a scalar quantity.

The average power over an interval is the total work done (or energy transferred) divided by the duration; the instantaneous power is the limiting value as the interval shrinks to zero, and equals the rate of change of work or energy with time: P = dW/dt.

Forms of the definition

For a force moving an object, mechanical power is the scalar product of force and velocity, P = F · v; for rotation it is torque times angular velocity, P = τ · ω. In electric circuits, instantaneous power is voltage times current, P = VI, and for AC circuits the average over a cycle gives real power, distinguished from reactive and apparent power.

Units and scale

Besides the watt, common units include the kilowatt and megawatt for electrical generation, horsepower (about 746 W) for engines, and dBm for radio and optical signals. A resting human dissipates roughly 100 watts; a large power station outputs on the order of a gigawatt.
