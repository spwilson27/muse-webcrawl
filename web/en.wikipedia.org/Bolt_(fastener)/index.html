Bolt (fastener)

A bolt is a threaded fastener, usually made of metal, designed to be assembled with a nut. Bolts are used to fasten two or more parts together in a bolted joint that can be disassembled without destroying the components. A bolt typically consists of a head, which is gripped and turned by a tool, and a cylindrical shank partially or fully threaded along its length.

Bolts versus screws

The distinction between a bolt and a screw is not always rigorously maintained, but generally a bolt passes through unthreaded holes in the parts it joins and is secured with a nut, while a screw forms or mates with a thread in the part itself and is driven by its head. Machine screws are frequently used exactly like bolts in tapped holes, blurring the distinction.

Types and standards

Common head forms include hex, square, socket (Allen), flange, carriage, eye, J, and U shapes. Thread forms are standardized in the ISO metric system and the Unified Thread Standard, with designations such as M10 × 1.5 or 3/8"-16 UNC. Mechanical property classes — for example metric classes 8.8 and 10.9, or ASTM grades — specify proof and tensile strengths, and are marked on the bolt head. Correct preload, achieved by tightening torque or direct tension measurement, is essential to joint integrity.

Materials and applications

Most bolts are made of carbon or alloy steel, often galvanized or otherwise coated against corrosion; stainless steel, brass, titanium, and aluminum bolts are used where corrosion, weight, or electrical properties matter. Bolts are ubiquitous in construction, vehicles, machinery, bridges, and pressure equipment, where their reliability is critical and governed by engineering codes.
