Trabzon is a city on the Black Sea coast of northeastern Turkey and the capital of Trabzon Province. Historically known as Trapezus and later Trebizond, it was founded by Greek colonists from Sinope in antiquity and became famous as a terminus of the Silk Road, where goods from Persia and Central Asia reached the sea. From 1204 to 1461 it was the capital of the Empire of Trebizond, the last Byzantine successor state to fall to the Ottoman Empire.

History

According to tradition, Trapezus was founded by Milesian settlers from Sinope around 756 BC. Xenophon and the Ten Thousand reached the city in 400 BC, famously hailing the sea. The city prospered under Rome and Byzantium as a trading port and naval base. After the Fourth Crusade sacked Constantinople in 1204, the Komnenos brothers Alexios and David founded the Empire of Trebizond, which survived for 257 years — longer than the restored Byzantine Empire itself. Sultan Mehmed II conquered Trabzon in 1461, ending the last remnant of Byzantine rule.

Under the Ottomans, Trabzon remained a major port for trade with Iran and the Caucasus. The 19th century brought consulates, steamship lines and a flourishing cosmopolitan merchant class. The city suffered severely during World War I — Russian occupation, the Armenian genocide in the region, and famine — and its Greek population was exchanged with Greece in 1923 under the Treaty of Lausanne.

Sights and culture

Landmarks include the 13th-century Hagia Sophia of Trabzon, renowned for its frescoes; the hilltop Trabzon Castle walls; Atatürk's villa; and the Sümela Monastery in the cliffs south of the city. Trabzon is the heartland of Turkish hazelnut production and of Black Sea cuisine — hamsi (anchovies), kuymak and Akçaabat köfte. Its football club, Trabzonspor, is one of Turkey's most successful, the first Anatolian club to win the national league.
