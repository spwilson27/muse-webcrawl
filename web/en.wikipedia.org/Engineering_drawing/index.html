Engineering drawing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An engineering drawing is a type of technical drawing that is used to convey information about an object. A common use is to specify the geometry necessary for the construction of a component and is called a detail drawing. Usually, a number of drawings are necessary to completely specify even a simple component. The drawings are linked together by a master drawing or assembly drawing which gives the drawing numbers of the subsequent detailed components, quantities required, construction materials and three-dimensional images that can be used to locate individual items.

Conventions

Engineering drawings follow standardised conventions so that a part can be manufactured unambiguously anywhere in the world. Objects are shown in orthographic projection — typically front, top and side views — using either first-angle (European) or third-angle (American) projection, supplemented by sections, auxiliary views and isometric pictorials. Dimensions and tolerances specify size and allowable variation; geometrical dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) precisely defines form, orientation and location. Drawings carry a title block identifying the part, scale, material, projection system, revision and author, and use standardised line types, symbols for surface finish and welding, and lettering.

Standards and media

International practice is governed by ISO standards, and in the United States by the ASME Y14 series. Drawings were once produced by hand on drafting boards in ink on linen, vellum or film — the classic blueprint and whiteprint processes — but are now almost universally created in CAD software, from which 2D drawings are generated from 3D models; model-based definition increasingly carries the specification in the model itself.
