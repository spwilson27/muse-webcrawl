M-Pesa

An M-Pesa agent outlet in Kenya

Launched: 2007, Kenya
Operators: Safaricom and Vodacom
Service: mobile money transfer and banking

M-Pesa (M for mobile, pesa being Swahili for money) is a mobile phone-based money transfer service, launched in 2007 by Vodafone and Safaricom in Kenya and by Vodacom in Tanzania. It allows users to deposit, withdraw, transfer money, pay for goods and services, access credit and savings, and send international remittances using a mobile device, through a network of human agents who handle cash.

Development and launch

M-Pesa grew out of a project funded by the UK Department for International Development, which observed that people in East Africa were transferring prepaid mobile airtime as an informal currency. The commercial service launched in Kenya in March 2007 and spread rapidly: within a few years a majority of Kenyan adults were users. It works on basic feature phones through SIM toolkit menus and SMS confirmations, with no need for a bank account or smartphone.

Impact

M-Pesa is widely credited with advancing financial inclusion in Kenya, where formal banking had reached only a minority of the population. A widely cited 2016 study in Science estimated that access to mobile money lifted about 194,000 Kenyan households — roughly 2% of the population — out of extreme poverty, with especially strong effects for women-headed households. It has expanded to numerous countries in Africa and beyond, and spawned credit (M-Shwari), merchant (Lipa na M-Pesa), and international remittance services.
