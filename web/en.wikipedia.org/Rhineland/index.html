Rhineland

The Rhineland (German: Rheinland) is a loosely defined region of western Germany along the middle and lower Rhine, roughly corresponding to the territory west of the river between Bingen and the Dutch border, together with adjacent areas on the east bank. Major cities include Cologne, Düsseldorf, Bonn, Aachen, Koblenz, Duisburg and Essen's southern Ruhr margins.

History

The region was Roman frontier territory, with cities such as Cologne (Colonia Agrippina) founded in antiquity. In the Middle Ages it was divided among electorates and duchies including Cologne, Trier, Jülich and Berg. Prussia acquired most of the area in 1815, forming the Rhine Province. After the First World War the Rhineland was demilitarized under the Treaty of Versailles, occupied by Allied troops, and remilitarized by Nazi Germany in 1936 — a key step toward the Second World War.

Culture

The Rhineland is known for its carnival season, Kölsch and Altbier beer traditions, Riesling wines from steep riverside vineyards, and the romantic castles of the Middle Rhine valley, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Bonn was the capital of West Germany from 1949 to 1990.
