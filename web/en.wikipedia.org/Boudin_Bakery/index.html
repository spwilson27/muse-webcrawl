Boudin Bakery is a bakery based in San Francisco, California, best known for its sourdough bread, marketed as "The Original San Francisco Sourdough". Founded in 1849 by Isidore Boudin, a French immigrant baker from Burgundy, it is the longest continually operating business in San Francisco.

The mother dough

Boudin's signature tang comes from its wild-yeast starter — the "mother dough" — begun in the Gold Rush era from local wild yeasts and lactobacilli (the organism once named Lactobacillus sanfranciscensis). A portion of the starter has been saved and replenished every day since 1849; legend holds that Louise Boudin rescued the starter during the 1906 earthquake and fire.

Business

The company operates a flagship bakery and demonstration bakery at Fisherman's Wharf, where visitors watch bakers shape loaves — including alligator- and crab-shaped breads — and eat clam chowder served in hollowed sourdough bowls, plus Boudin SF café locations in California and a location inside Disney California Adventure's Pacific Wharf area.
