Suburb

A suburb is an area within a metropolitan area which is predominantly residential and separate from the city centre. Suburbs may be separate municipalities or communities within a larger city, or districts of a city that lie on its outskirts. The term derives from the Latin suburbium, formed from sub ("under" or "below") and urbs ("city").

History

Suburban areas have existed since antiquity; the earliest suburbs grew outside the walls of ancient cities, housing trades considered too noxious or space-consuming for the centre. In the 19th century, the arrival of railways allowed wealthier residents of industrial cities such as London to live in semi-rural surroundings and commute into the city. The expansion of the tram and later the automobile in the 20th century drove mass suburbanisation, particularly in the United States after the Second World War, when developments such as Levittown in New York offered affordable single-family homes to returning veterans.

Characteristics

Suburbs typically have lower population density than inner-city neighbourhoods, with housing dominated by detached or semi-detached single-family homes. Land use is often strictly separated, with residential districts distinct from commercial and industrial zones. Residents usually depend on private cars for transport, and suburbs are commonly associated with commuting to jobs in the central city.

Suburbanisation around the world

The form of suburbs varies greatly by country. In the United States, Canada and Australia, suburbs are typically low-density and car-oriented. In much of Europe, suburbs may be denser, and in France the word banlieue can describe deprived housing estates on the urban periphery rather than affluent commuter districts. In many developing countries, informal settlements on city edges form a distinct kind of suburb.

Criticism

Critics associate suburbs with urban sprawl, traffic congestion, social segregation and the decline of city centres. Movements such as New Urbanism advocate denser, mixed-use, walkable suburban development. Defenders point to the space, privacy, safety and home ownership that suburbs offer residents.
