Coloratura

Coloratura is the elaborate ornamentation of a vocal melody, featuring runs, trills, leaps and rapid scales, especially in operatic singing of the 18th and 19th centuries. The word derives from the Italian colorare, "to color". By extension, a coloratura soprano is a voice type specialising in such music, combining agility with an extended high range.

In opera

Coloratura flourished in Baroque opera seria, where arias were vehicles for virtuoso display by castrati and prime donne, and in the bel canto operas of Rossini, Bellini and Donizetti. Famous coloratura showpieces include the Queen of the Night's arias in Mozart's The Magic Flute, with their repeated high Fs, Lucia's "mad scene" in Donizetti's Lucia di Lammermoor, and Rosina's music in Rossini's The Barber of Seville (originally written for a coloratura mezzo-soprano).

Voice types

Coloratura sopranos are subdivided into lyric and dramatic types. The lyric coloratura has a light, flexible voice suited to roles such as Gilda in Rigoletto; the dramatic coloratura combines agility with greater power, as required by the Queen of the Night or Norma. Celebrated exponents include Maria Callas, Joan Sutherland, Beverly Sills and Edita Gruberová. Coloratura writing also appears for other voice types, notably the coloratura contralto and Rossini tenor.

Technique

Singing coloratura demands precise breath control, evenness of scale across registers, accurate pitch at speed, and the ability to execute ornaments — trills, mordents, staccato arpeggios — while maintaining legato. Historically, singers were expected to improvise ornamentation, especially in da capo repeats; modern practice usually follows written-out embellishments.
