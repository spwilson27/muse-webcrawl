Fayetteville, Arkansas

Fayetteville is the county seat of Washington County, Arkansas, and home to the University of Arkansas.

Fayetteville is the second-most populous city in Arkansas, United States, and the county seat of Washington County. The city had a population of about 94,000 at the 2020 census. Located on the outskirts of the Boston Mountains, deep within the Ozarks, Fayetteville is part of the Northwest Arkansas metropolitan area, together with Springdale, Rogers, and Bentonville.

Fayetteville is best known as the home of the University of Arkansas, the state's flagship university, founded in 1871. The city's economy and culture are heavily influenced by the university, as well as by the nearby corporate headquarters of Walmart, Tyson Foods, and J.B. Hunt Transport Services in the surrounding metro area.

History

The area was originally settled as Washington in 1828 and renamed Fayetteville in 1829, after Fayetteville, Tennessee. During the American Civil War, the city changed hands several times and was the site of the Battle of Fayetteville in 1863. The arrival of the railroad in the 1880s spurred growth in the regional apple and timber industries.

Culture

Dickson Street is the city's best-known entertainment district, adjacent to the university campus. Fayetteville hosts the Walton Arts Center and is a stop on the Razorback Regional Greenway, a 36-mile shared-use trail system connecting the cities of Northwest Arkansas.
