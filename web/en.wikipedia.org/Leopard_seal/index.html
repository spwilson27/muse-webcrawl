Leopard seal

The leopard seal (Hydrurga leptonyx), also referred to as the sea leopard, is the second largest species of seal in the Antarctic (after the southern elephant seal). Its only natural predator is the orca.

Scientific name: Hydrurga leptonyx
Length: 2.4–3.5 m
Weight: 200–600 kg
Range: Antarctic and subantarctic waters
Conservation status: Least Concern

Description

Leopard seals have long, muscular, serpentine bodies with large reptile-like heads, wide jaws and a spotted coat that gives the species its name. Females are generally larger than males. Their tricuspid cheek teeth are adapted both for sieving krill from the water and for gripping larger prey.

Diet and behaviour

They are versatile predators, eating krill, fish, squid, penguins and other seals, including crabeater seal pups. Leopard seals are mostly solitary and are known to "play" with penguins they have caught. They haul out on Antarctic pack ice to rest and pup.

Interactions with humans

Attacks on humans are rare but documented; in 2003 a leopard seal killed a British biologist snorkelling in Antarctic waters, the only known fatal attack. Populations are estimated in the hundreds of thousands and the species is not considered threatened.
