Bulk carrier

CargoDry bulk: grain, coal, ore, cement

A bulk carrier is a merchant ship specially designed to transport unpackaged bulk cargo — such as grain, coal, iron ore, bauxite, cement, fertiliser and steel products — in its cargo holds. Bulk carriers make up one of the largest segments of the world merchant fleet and carry the raw materials that underpin world trade; iron ore and coal for steelmaking and power generation, and grain, are the dominant trades.

Design

Modern bulk carriers are characterised by a series of large box-shaped holds covered by hydraulically operated hatch covers, a raised forecastle, and an engine room and superstructure at the stern. Many smaller bulkers are "geared", carrying their own cranes or conveyors, while large ore carriers rely on shore equipment. Sizes are described by class: Handysize and Handymax ships serve smaller ports; Panamax ships fit the original Panama Canal locks; Capesize ships are too large for the canals and route via the Cape of Good Hope or Cape Horn; the largest Valemax ore carriers approach 400,000 deadweight tons.

Safety

Bulk carriers suffered a series of losses in the 1980s and 1990s, prompting stricter international rules on hull strength, hatch cover integrity, freeboard and the carriage of heavy, shifting cargoes, which can cause dangerous flooding or structural failure if holds are loaded unevenly.
