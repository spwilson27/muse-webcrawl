Cognac, Charente

Cognac (French pronunciation: [kɔɲak]) is a commune in the Charente department in southwestern France, with a population of roughly 18,000. It lies on the river Charente between Angoulême and Saintes and is world-famous as the home of the brandy that bears its name.

The brandy

Cognac the spirit is a variety of brandy produced by double-distilling white wine made from grapes grown in the delimited region around the town, and ageing the resulting eau-de-vie in oak barrels for at least two years. The great trading houses — Hennessy, Martell, Rémy Martin and Courvoisier — were founded in or near the town, several of them by merchants from the British Isles, and the region's exports shaped the town's wealth and architecture.

History and sights

The old town retains medieval streets, the church of Saint-Léger, and the Château de Cognac, birthplace of King Francis I in 1494, now the home of the Otard cognac house. The town is also known for its summer blues festival, Blues Passions, and for being the hometown of Jean Monnet, a founding father of the European Union.
