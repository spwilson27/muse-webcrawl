AZERTY is a specific layout for the characters of the Latin alphabet on typewriter keys and computer keyboards. The layout takes its name from the first six letters to appear on the first row of alphabetical keys: A, Z, E, R, T, Y. Like the German QWERTZ layout, it is modelled on the English QWERTY layout and is used in France and Belgium, and in some African countries.

Differences from QWERTY

Compared with QWERTY, AZERTY swaps the A and Q keys and the Z and W keys, and moves the M key to the right of L. Digits occupy the same positions but are accessed with the Shift key, while the unshifted number row produces accented characters and punctuation such as é, è and à. Period and other punctuation positions also differ between the French and Belgian variants.

Standardisation in France

For decades France had no official AZERTY standard, and keyboard makers' implementations varied and made some French characters — capital accented letters such as É, the ligature œ, or proper French quotation marks « » — difficult or impossible to type. In 2019 the French standards body AFNOR published standard NF Z71-300, defining both an improved AZERTY layout intended to support correct French typography and an optimised alternative layout (a version of BÉPO). The standard is voluntary but has been encouraged for public-sector procurement.

Variants and use

Belgian AZERTY differs slightly from French AZERTY in some symbol positions. AZERTY layouts adapted for languages such as Arabic (in parts of North and West Africa), Lithuanian and others also exist. In francophone Quebec, the QWERTY-based Canadian French layout is used instead.
