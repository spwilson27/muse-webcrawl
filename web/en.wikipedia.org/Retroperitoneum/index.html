Retroperitoneum

The retroperitoneum (or retroperitoneal space) is the anatomical space behind the peritoneum in the abdomen, between the posterior parietal peritoneum and the muscles and bones of the posterior body wall. Structures within it are described as retroperitoneal.

Contents

Primarily retroperitoneal organs include the kidneys, adrenal glands, ureters, most of the pancreas, the second through fourth parts of the duodenum, the ascending and descending colon, the aorta, and the inferior vena cava. A common mnemonic for the structures is "SAD PUCKER". Some organs, such as the liver and spleen, are intraperitoneal, while others are secondarily retroperitoneal, having lost their mesentery during development.

Clinical significance

The retroperitoneum can harbour haemorrhage (for example after trauma or anticoagulation), infection, and tumours such as retroperitoneal sarcomas and fibrosis. Bleeding or fluid in this space may be difficult to detect clinically and is usually imaged with CT.
