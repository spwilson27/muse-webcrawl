Priscilla Fairfield Bok

Priscilla Fairfield Bok (1896–1975) was an American astronomer known for her work on star clusters and the structure of the Milky Way, and for her role — together with her husband, the Dutch-American astronomer Bart Bok — in establishing and developing major observatories in Australia.

Life and career

Born in 1896, Priscilla Fairfield studied at Boston University and earned her doctorate in astronomy from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1921. She worked at the Lick Observatory and taught at Smith College before moving to Harvard College Observatory, where she met Bart Jan Bok, whom she married in 1929. The two became one of astronomy's most productive partnerships.

Her research focused on star clusters, stellar magnitudes, and the structure of the Milky Way galaxy. With her husband she co-authored The Milky Way (first published in 1941), a popular and influential introduction to galactic astronomy that went through several editions over four decades.

Australia

In 1957 the Boks moved to Australia, where Bart Bok became director of the Mount Stromlo Observatory and Priscilla continued her research and scientific outreach. She remained in Australia after his tenure ended in 1966, living near Canberra until her death in 1975. The Boks' years there are credited with greatly strengthening Australian astronomy.

Legacy

The asteroid 2137 Priscilla is named in her honour, as is a crater on Venus, and the Boks are jointly commemorated in the Bok Prize of the Astronomical Society of Australia for undergraduate research.
