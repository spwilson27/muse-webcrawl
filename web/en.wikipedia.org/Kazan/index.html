Kazan

Kazan (Russian: Каза́нь; Tatar: Казан) is the largest city and capital of Tatarstan, Russia. The city lies at the confluence of the Volga and Kazanka rivers, covering an area of about 425 square kilometres, with a population of roughly 1.3 million, making it the fifth-largest city in Russia. Kazan is famous for its unique blend of Russian and Tatar cultures.

History

Kazan was founded by the Volga Bulgars around the turn of the 11th century. It later became a major trading centre of the Golden Horde and, in 1438, the capital of the Khanate of Kazan. In 1552, after a siege, the city was captured by the troops of Ivan the Terrible and incorporated into Russia; much of the Tatar population was displaced and the city was rebuilt with a white stone kremlin.

In the following centuries Kazan grew into an important commercial and industrial centre on the Volga. Kazan Federal University, founded in 1804, is one of Russia's oldest universities; notable figures associated with the city include the writer Leo Tolstoy, who studied there, and the mathematician Nikolai Lobachevsky.

Landmarks

The Kazan Kremlin, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, contains the Qolsharif Mosque — rebuilt in 2005 and one of the largest mosques in Russia — and the leaning Söyembikä Tower, wrapped in legends about a Tatar princess. Kazan hosted matches of the 2018 FIFA World Cup and is sometimes called the "sports capital of Russia".

Demographics

Ethnic Russians and Tatars each make up close to half of the city's population, and both Russian Orthodoxy and Islam are widely practised, a coexistence often cited as a symbol of interreligious harmony in Russia.
