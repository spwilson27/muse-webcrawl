Dongting Lake

Dongting Lake (Chinese: 洞庭湖; pinyin: Dòngtíng Hú) is a large, shallow lake in northeastern Hunan province, China. It is a flood basin of the Yangtze River, and its size therefore depends on the season, expanding greatly during the summer flood season. The lake is famous in Chinese culture and takes its name from the "grotto court" said to lie beneath it in mythology.

Geography

Dongting is fed by the Xiang, Zi, Yuan, and Li rivers from the south and receives floodwater from the Yangtze through inlets in the north; its waters return to the Yangtze near Yueyang. Historically the second-largest freshwater lake in China after Poyang Lake, Dongting was once far larger — in the 19th century its area may have approached 6,000 km² — but land reclamation and heavy siltation from the Yangtze have steadily shrunk it, splitting it into East, South, and West Dongting. The lake acts as a natural regulator of Yangtze floods, and the surrounding region is a major rice-producing area, often called a land of fish and rice.

Ecology

The lake's wetlands are a protected area of international importance under the Ramsar Convention. They provide wintering habitat for migratory birds including Siberian cranes, and the waters historically supported the Yangtze finless porpoise and the now-functionally-extinct baiji river dolphin. The migratory fish and vast reed beds support an important fishing industry, though overfishing and pollution have caused declines.

Culture

Dongting gave the province names Hunan ("south of the lake") and Hubei ("north of the lake"). The Yueyang Tower, overlooking the lake at Yueyang, is one of the Three Great Towers of Jiangnan, made famous by Fan Zhongyan's celebrated Memorial to Yueyang Tower (1046). Junshan Island in the lake is famed for its silver needle yellow tea. Dragon boat racing is traditionally said to originate here, commemorating the poet Qu Yuan, who drowned himself in the nearby Miluo River in 278 BC.
