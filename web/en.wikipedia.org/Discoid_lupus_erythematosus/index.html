Discoid lupus erythematosus

Discoid lupus erythematosus (DLE) is a chronic skin condition characterised by coin-shaped (discoid), scaly, red plaques that tend to heal with scarring and changes in pigmentation. It is the most common form of chronic cutaneous lupus erythematosus.

Signs and symptoms

Lesions most often appear on sun-exposed areas — the face, scalp and ears — and may cause permanent hair loss when the scalp is involved. A characteristic feature is the "carpet tack" sign: spines of keratin visible on the underside of an adherent scale when it is lifted.

Cause and treatment

DLE is an autoimmune condition aggravated by ultraviolet light; smoking is also a risk factor. A minority of patients progress to systemic lupus erythematosus. Management centres on sun protection, topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors, and antimalarial drugs such as hydroxychloroquine.
