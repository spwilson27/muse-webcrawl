Barcelona Pavilion

Barcelona Pavilion
Architect: Ludwig Mies van der Rohe (with Lilly Reich)
Built: 1929 (rebuilt 1986)
Location: Barcelona, Spain

The Barcelona Pavilion (Catalan: Pavelló alemany; Spanish: Pabellón alemán), designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich, was the German Pavilion for the 1929 International Exposition in Barcelona, Spain. It is one of the most influential works of modernist architecture.

Design

The pavilion is celebrated for its radical simplicity: a flat roof supported by slender cruciform steel columns, with free-standing walls of luxurious materials — green and golden onyx, travertine, and several kinds of marble — arranged in a flowing, open plan over a raised podium. Two reflecting pools and Georg Kolbe's statue Dawn complete the composition. The famous Barcelona chair was designed for the pavilion.

History

Built as a temporary reception space where the King of Spain signed the visitors' book, the pavilion hosted no exhibition — the building itself was the exhibit. It was dismantled in 1930 after the exposition closed.

Reconstruction

Because of its canonical status, the pavilion was meticulously reconstructed on its original site in 1983–1986 using the same materials, and today operates as a museum and venue for architecture events.
