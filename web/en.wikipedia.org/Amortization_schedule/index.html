Amortization schedule

An amortization schedule is a table detailing each periodic payment on an amortizing loan, as generated by an amortization calculator. Each payment is split into a portion that covers interest and a portion that repays principal, and the schedule shows the remaining balance after each payment until the loan is fully repaid at the end of its term.

How it works

For a fixed-rate loan, the payment amount stays constant. Early payments consist mostly of interest, because interest is charged on the large outstanding balance; as the balance falls, interest shrinks and the principal portion grows. By the final payments, nearly the whole payment goes to principal.

Example

For a loan of 100,000 at 6% annual interest over 30 years with monthly payments, the payment is about 599.55. The first payment includes 500.00 of interest and 99.55 of principal; the balance declines slowly at first, then ever faster, reaching zero after 360 payments.

Calculation

The periodic payment A on principal P with per-period rate r over n periods is:

A = P · r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1)

Negative amortization occurs when payments fail to cover interest, so the balance grows instead of shrinking.
