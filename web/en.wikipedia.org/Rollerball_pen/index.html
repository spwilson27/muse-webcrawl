Rollerball pen

A rollerball pen is a pen that uses a small rotating ball at its tip to dispense water-based liquid or gel ink, as opposed to the oil-based paste ink of a ballpoint pen. The rollerball's water-based ink flows more freely, producing a smoother, more saturated line similar to a fountain pen, though it tends to bleed more on paper and dry more slowly.

Characteristics

Rollerball pens were introduced in Japan in the 1960s, notably with Ohto's Ceramic Roller in 1964. Gel pens, developed in the 1980s, use a pigmented gel ink suspended in a water-based gel, combining vibrant colours with smooth writing. Because their inks are thinner, rollerball caps must seal well to prevent drying and leaking, particularly at altitude on aircraft.
