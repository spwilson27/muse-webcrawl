The lower Volga delta, heartland of the Astrakhan Khanate.

The Khanate of Astrakhan was a Tatar feudal state that arose in the lower Volga region during the disintegration of the Golden Horde. Its capital, Xacitarxan (also rendered Hajji Tarkhan), stood near the site of modern Astrakhan, close to where the Volga flows into the Caspian Sea.

History

The khanate is usually dated from about 1466, when Mahmud bin Küchük became its ruler after breaking from the Great Horde. The small state controlled an important trading crossroads between Persia, Central Asia, and Russia, but its khans were frequently deposed or installed by the stronger powers around it — the Nogai Horde, the Crimean Khanate, and Muscovy. Its population included Astrakhan Tatars and Nogais, and its economy rested on trade, salt, fishing, and slave raiding.

Russian conquest

After conquering Kazan in 1552, Ivan IV of Russia turned to Astrakhan. Russian forces installed a compliant khan in 1554, and in 1556, after he allied with Crimea and the Ottomans, Russia deposed him and annexed the khanate outright. The annexation gave Russia control of the entire Volga and a direct outlet to the Caspian Sea.
