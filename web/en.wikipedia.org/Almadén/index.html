Almadén

Almadén is a town in the province of Ciudad Real in the autonomous community of Castile-La Mancha, Spain. Its name comes from the Arabic al-maʿdin, meaning "the mine". Almadén is famous as the site of the richest deposits of cinnabar (mercury ore) in the world, which have been exploited for more than two thousand years.

History

Mercury mining at Almadén began in Roman times, when cinnabar was used as a pigment (vermilion) and the metal extracted for gilding and medicine. Under Spanish rule from the 16th century, the mines became a royal monopoly of great strategic importance, because mercury was essential for the amalgamation process used to refine silver from the mines of Spanish America, such as Potosí and Zacatecas. For a period the Spanish Crown leased the mines to the Fugger banking family of Augsburg.

Working conditions were notoriously lethal: convicts and slaves were sent to the mines, and mercury poisoning was common. Over the 20th century demand declined, and mining finally ceased in the early 2000s.

Heritage

In 2012 the mines of Almadén, together with those of Idrija in Slovenia, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Heritage of Mercury". The mining park preserves furnaces, the 18th-century San Rafael miners' hospital, and the historic mine workings, which visitors can tour.
