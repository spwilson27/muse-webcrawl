Affluence

Affluence refers to an abundance of wealth, goods, and resources available to an individual or society. In economics and sociology, being affluent means possessing a level of financial resources well beyond subsistence, enabling high consumption and savings. Commentators such as John Kenneth Galbraith, in The Affluent Society (1958), examined how postwar prosperity reshaped public priorities.

Measurement

Affluence is commonly measured by income, net worth, and consumption. Households in top income deciles are typically classed as affluent; "mass affluent" describes those with substantial liquid assets below the high-net-worth threshold.

Effects

Research links affluence to better health and educational outcomes but also to overconsumption and environmental impact. Critics coined the term "affluenza" to describe social ills attributed to materialism and the pursuit of wealth.
