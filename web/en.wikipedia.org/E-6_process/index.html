E-6 process

E-6 process
Type: Photographic process
Purpose: Development of color reversal (slide) film
Introduced: 1977 (Kodak)

The E-6 process (often simply called E-6) is a photographic development process used for developing Ektachrome, Fujichrome and other color reversal (slide or transparency) photographic films. It replaced the earlier E-3 and E-4 processes, and became the standard process for color slide film worldwide.

Color reversal film is developed using the E-6 process to produce positive transparencies.

Process

Unlike the simpler black-and-white negative process, E-6 involves a series of chemical baths. The film is first developed in a black-and-white first developer, which produces a negative silver image in each emulsion layer. A reversal bath (or exposure to light) then fogs the remaining unexposed silver halide, after which a color developer forms dye images in the three color layers proportional to the remaining silver. Finally, the silver is removed in bleach and fixer steps, and the film is stabilized and washed.

Steps

The standard E-6 sequence consists of: first developer, wash, reversal bath, color developer, pre-bleach (conditioner), bleach, fixer, final wash, final rinse (stabilizer), and drying. Precise temperature control, typically 38 °C (100.4 °F), is essential for consistent color balance.

Use

E-6 chemistry is used by professional photo labs and by hobbyists with home developing kits. Although slide film use declined sharply with the rise of digital photography, E-6 processing remains available and is still used for films such as Kodak Ektachrome E100 and Fujifilm's Fujichrome range, including Velvia and Provia.
