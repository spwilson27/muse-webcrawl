Vivaha

Vivāha (Sanskrit: विवाह) is the Sanskrit term and traditional Hindu ceremony for marriage, regarded as one of the sixteen samskaras (sacraments) that mark important stages of life. In Hindu tradition, marriage is a sacred, dharmic union rather than a mere contract: it joins not only two individuals but two families, and is believed to bind the couple across lifetimes. The ceremony is conducted by a priest before the sacred fire (Agni), who acts as divine witness.

Key rituals

Ceremonies vary widely by region, caste, and family custom, but common elements include kanyādāna, the giving away of the bride by her father; pāṇigrahaṇa, the clasping of hands; the exchange of garlands (jaimala); tying of the marriage necklace (mangalsutra) or application of sindoor in North India; and the saptapadi, in which the couple takes seven steps together around the fire, reciting vows of nourishment, strength, prosperity, fidelity, progeny, longevity, and companionship. The seventh step is traditionally said to make the marriage irrevocable.

Forms of marriage

Classical dharmashastra texts enumerate eight forms of marriage, from the approved brāhma form, in which a father gifts his daughter to a learned groom, to disapproved forms such as the paiśāca. Modern Hindu marriages in India are legally governed by the Hindu Marriage Act of 1955, which requires the saptapadi or other customary rites for a valid marriage where they are part of the parties' custom.

Festivities

Weddings typically unfold over several days with events such as the haldi (turmeric) ceremony, mehndi (henna), and sangeet (music and dance), followed by the main vivaha and a reception.
