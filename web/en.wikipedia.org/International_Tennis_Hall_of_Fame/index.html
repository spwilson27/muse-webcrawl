International Tennis Hall of Fame

The International Tennis Hall of Fame is a sports hall of fame and museum located in Newport, Rhode Island, United States. It honours players and other contributors to the sport of tennis, and is housed in the historic Newport Casino, a National Historic Landmark whose grass courts hosted the first U.S. National Championships — now the US Open — in 1881.

History

The Newport Casino was designed by architects McKim, Mead & White and opened in 1880. The U.S. National Championships were played there from 1881 until 1914, when they moved to Forest Hills, New York. In 1954, Jimmy Van Alen founded the Tennis Hall of Fame at Newport, and it was recognised as the International Tennis Hall of Fame in 1975 when it began inducting international members and was endorsed by the International Tennis Federation.

Induction

Enshrinement is the highest honour in tennis. Players are generally eligible five years after their last major activity, and candidates are voted on by a panel of tennis media, historians and Hall of Famers. Inductees — numbering more than 260 from over 20 countries — include legends such as Bill Tilden, Rod Laver, Billie Jean King, Björn Borg, Martina Navratilova, Pete Sampras, Steffi Graf, Roger Federer and Serena Williams. Induction weekend is held each July, alongside a professional grass-court tournament at the venue.

Museum

The museum reopened in 2015 after a major renovation, displaying artefacts, trophies, interactive exhibits and a holographic Roger Federer experience. The site retains thirteen grass courts, indoor courts and a court tennis (real tennis) facility, one of the few in the United States where that older game is still played.
