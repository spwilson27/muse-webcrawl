Hagwon

A hagwon (Korean: 학원) is a for-profit private institute, academy or cram school prevalent in South Korea. Students of all ages attend hagwons, most commonly to improve scores in school subjects, English, mathematics and entrance examinations.

Role in education

Private tutoring is a major industry in South Korea, reflecting intense competition for places at prestigious universities and the importance of the College Scholastic Ability Test (Suneung). Many children attend several hagwons after regular school hours, sometimes late into the evening.

Regulation

Governments have repeatedly attempted to curb the sector — including a total ban in 1980, later struck down — and measures such as late-night curfews on hagwon hours, amid debate over educational inequality and student stress.
