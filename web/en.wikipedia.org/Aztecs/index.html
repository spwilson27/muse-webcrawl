Aztecs

The Aztecs were a Mesoamerican civilization that flourished in central Mexico in the post-classic period, from 1300 until the Spanish conquest of 1521. Their empire was centred on the city of Tenochtitlan, built on an island in Lake Texcoco in the Valley of Mexico, on the site of modern Mexico City.

Origins and empire

The Mexica people, the core group of the Aztecs, migrated into the Valley of Mexico and founded Tenochtitlan in 1325. In 1428 they formed the Triple Alliance with Texcoco and Tlacopan, creating an empire that came to dominate much of central Mexico. Subject peoples paid tribute in goods, labour, and captives, though they generally retained their own rulers.

Society and culture

Aztec society was hierarchical, headed by an emperor (the huey tlatoani), with nobles, priests, warriors, merchants, artisans, farmers, and enslaved people below. The Aztecs spoke Nahuatl and kept records in pictographic codices. Their agriculture featured chinampas — floating gardens — that produced abundant food. Their religion involved a complex calendar and the ritual offering of human sacrifices, believed necessary to sustain the sun and the cosmos.

Spanish conquest

In 1519 Hernán Cortés landed in Mexico. Exploiting alliances with peoples resentful of Aztec rule, and aided by European diseases such as smallpox, the Spanish besieged and destroyed Tenochtitlan, which fell on 13 August 1521. The last emperor, Cuauhtémoc, was captured and later executed.
