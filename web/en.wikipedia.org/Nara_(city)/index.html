Nara (Japanese: 奈良市, Nara-shi) is the capital city of Nara Prefecture in the Kansai region of Japan. As one of the country's oldest cities, it served as Japan's capital from 710 to 784 (as Heijō-kyō) during the Nara period, and preserves some of Japan's most celebrated temples, shrines and treasures.

History

Modelled on the Chinese Tang capital Chang'an, Heijō-kyō was the centre of Japanese government, Buddhism and court culture for most of the eighth century. Even after the capital moved to Kyoto, Nara's great temples retained major influence. The "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara" — including Tōdai-ji, Kasuga Taisha, Kōfuku-ji and the Heijō Palace site — were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1998.

Sights

Tōdai-ji houses the Daibutsu, a monumental bronze statue of Vairocana Buddha, in one of the world's largest wooden buildings. Nara Park is famous for its hundreds of free-roaming sika deer, regarded as messengers of the gods of Kasuga Shrine and protected as a national treasure. Other highlights include the Shōsō-in repository, Yakushi-ji and Hōryū-ji (in nearby Ikaruga).

Modern city

Today Nara is a major tourist destination and a residential and commercial centre within the Keihanshin metropolitan sphere, with frequent rail links to Kyoto and Osaka.
