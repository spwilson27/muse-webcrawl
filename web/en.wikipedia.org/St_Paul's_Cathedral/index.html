St Paul's Cathedral

St Paul's Cathedral, seat of the Bishop of London.

St Paul's Cathedral is an Anglican cathedral in London, England, the seat of the Bishop of London and the mother church of the Diocese of London. It is situated on Ludgate Hill at the highest point of the City of London. The present cathedral, designed in the English Baroque style by Sir Christopher Wren, was completed in 1710 after the previous building was destroyed in the Great Fire of London in 1666.

Architecture

The cathedral is famous for its dome, one of the largest in the world, rising 111 metres (365 feet) and remaining a dominant feature of the London skyline. The dome is in fact three structures: an inner brick dome, a hidden brick cone supporting the lantern, and the outer lead-clad timber dome visible from outside. The interior includes the Whispering Gallery, notable for its acoustic properties, and the crypt contains the tombs of Admiral Lord Nelson and the Duke of Wellington.

History

A cathedral has stood on the site since AD 604. The medieval Old St Paul's was one of the largest churches in Europe before its destruction by fire. Wren's building hosted the funerals of Nelson, Wellington and Sir Winston Churchill, the wedding of Charles, Prince of Wales and Lady Diana Spencer in 1981, and services marking the jubilees of Queen Elizabeth II. During the Blitz, the cathedral famously survived bombing, becoming a symbol of London's resilience.

Services and role

St Paul's holds daily services and remains a working church as well as a major visitor attraction. Its steps and churchyard, like St. Peter's Square in Rome, serve as a civic gathering place.
