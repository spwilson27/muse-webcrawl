Giving Tuesday, often stylised as #GivingTuesday, is a global day of charitable giving held annually on the Tuesday after Thanksgiving in the United States — following the shopping events of Black Friday and Cyber Monday. It encourages donations of money, time, goods and advocacy to nonprofit organisations and community causes.

History

Giving Tuesday was created in 2012 by New York's 92nd Street Y in partnership with the United Nations Foundation, as a counterpoint to the consumerism of the post-Thanksgiving shopping season. It spread through social media and partner networks; in 2019 the movement became an independent nonprofit organisation, GivingTuesday, Inc. Country and community movements now operate in dozens of nations under localised names and traditions.

Scale and impact

Online giving on Giving Tuesday in the United States has grown to several billion dollars annually — an estimated $3.1 billion was donated in the US in 2022 and 2023 — alongside widespread in-kind giving and volunteering that the organisers characterise as a broader culture of generosity beyond the single day. An out-of-season "GivingTuesdayNow" was held on 5 May 2020 in response to the COVID-19 pandemic. Critics have raised questions about donor fatigue, the concentration of appeals and the effectiveness of single-day campaigns, while supporters point to its success in engaging younger and first-time donors.
