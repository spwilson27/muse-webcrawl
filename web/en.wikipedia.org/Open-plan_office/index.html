Open-plan office

An open-plan office is a type of office layout where all employees work on the same floor and in the same open space, with few or no private offices, sometimes separated by low partitions or cubicles. The concept originated in early 20th-century Germany as Bürolandschaft and spread widely from the 1960s and especially with technology companies in the 1990s–2000s.

Advocates cite lower real-estate costs, easier communication and collaboration, and flexibility. Critics cite noise, lack of privacy, distraction, increased transmission of illness, and research showing that face-to-face interaction can fall while electronic communication rises after a move to open plan.

Alternatives

Variants include cubicle farms, hot-desking, activity-based working, team pods, and hybrid layouts mixing open areas with focus rooms and phone booths, a trend accelerated by remote and hybrid work after 2020.
