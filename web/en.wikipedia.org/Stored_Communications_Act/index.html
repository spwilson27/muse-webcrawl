Stored Communications Act

Enacted1986, as Title II of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act
Codified18 U.S.C. sections 2701-2712

The Stored Communications Act (SCA) is a United States federal law, enacted in 1986 as Title II of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act, that governs the privacy of electronic communications held by third-party service providers and the conditions under which the government may compel providers to disclose them.

Provisions

The Act prohibits unauthorised access to facilities through which electronic communication services are provided, and restricts providers from voluntarily disclosing the contents of stored communications. Government investigators may compel disclosure using warrants, subpoenas or court orders, with the required legal process varying according to how long a communication has been in storage — a distinction widely criticised as outdated in the era of cloud computing.
