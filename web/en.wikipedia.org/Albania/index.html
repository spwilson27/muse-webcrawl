Albania

Albania (Albanian: Shqipëria), officially the Republic of Albania, is a country in Southeast Europe on the Balkan Peninsula, bordered by Montenegro, Kosovo, North Macedonia and Greece, with a coastline on the Adriatic and Ionian Seas. The capital and largest city is Tirana. Albania has a population of roughly 2.4 million and covers 28,748 km².

History

The region was inhabited in antiquity by Illyrian tribes and later formed part of the Roman, Byzantine and Ottoman empires. The national hero Skanderbeg led resistance to the Ottomans in the 15th century. Albania declared independence from the Ottoman Empire in 1912. After periods as a principality, republic and kingdom under Zog I, and Italian and German occupation during the Second World War, Albania became a communist state under Enver Hoxha, whose regime was notable for its isolationism. The communist system collapsed in 1990–91, and Albania has since developed as a parliamentary republic; it joined NATO in 2009 and is a candidate for European Union membership.

Geography and economy

Albania is largely mountainous, with the Albanian Alps in the north and fertile coastal lowlands in the west. Its economy has shifted from central planning to services, tourism, agriculture, energy and remittances from a large diaspora.

Culture

Albanian culture reflects both Gheg and Tosk traditions; the Kanun, a customary legal code, historically governed much of northern society. Mother Teresa, born to an Albanian family, and novelist Ismail Kadare are internationally known figures associated with Albania.
