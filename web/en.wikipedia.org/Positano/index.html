Positano

Positano is a village and comune on the Amalfi Coast (Province of Salerno) in Campania, Italy, mainly in an enclave in the hills leading down to the coast. It is a celebrated resort town noted for its steep, pastel-coloured houses cascading down to the Tyrrhenian Sea.

History

Positano prospered in the Middle Ages as a port of the Republic of Amalfi, trading with the Middle East. It declined after raids and the 1343 tsunami, and many inhabitants emigrated in the 19th century, largely to the United States. The town revived in the 20th century as an artists' and writers' retreat and then as a fashionable resort.

Main sights

The Church of Santa Maria Assunta features a dome tiled with majolica and a 13th-century Byzantine icon of a black Madonna. The main beach, Spiaggia Grande, sits below the town, and the Path of the Gods (Sentiero degli Dei) hiking trail links Positano with Bomerano in the hills above.

Culture

John Steinbeck's 1953 essay on Positano for Harper's Bazaar famously declared that "Positano bites deep. It is a dream place that isn't quite real when you are there and becomes beckoningly real after you have gone." The town has since appeared in numerous films and remains an icon of Mediterranean leisure.
