Arthur Rock

Born: 19 August 1926, Rochester, New York

Occupation: Venture capitalist

Known for: Early investments in Fairchild Semiconductor, Intel and Apple

Arthur Rock (born 19 August 1926) is an American investor and venture capitalist based in Silicon Valley. He is famous for his early backing of major technology companies including Fairchild Semiconductor, Intel and Apple Computer, and is regarded as one of the founding figures of venture capital.

Career

After graduating from Syracuse University and Harvard Business School, Rock joined the New York investment firm Hayden, Stone & Co. In 1957 he arranged the financing that allowed the "traitorous eight" engineers to leave Shockley Semiconductor and found Fairchild Semiconductor. Moving to California, he formed a partnership with Tommy Davis in 1961 — the venture partnership Davis & Rock — which backed companies such as Scientific Data Systems and Teledyne.

Intel and Apple

In 1968 Rock helped Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore raise the capital to found Intel, serving as its first chairman. He was also an early investor in Apple Computer and served on its board, mentoring Steve Jobs. His investment philosophy emphasised backing outstanding people, a theme of his widely quoted criteria for entrepreneurs.

Philanthropy

Rock and his wife Toni Rembe have been major philanthropists, supporting education and the arts; the Arthur Rock Center for Entrepreneurship at Harvard Business School is named for him. He is also the subject of the term "Rock's law" for the rising cost of semiconductor fabrication plants, sometimes called Moore's second law.
