Corpus callosum

The corpus callosum is the largest white matter structure in the human brain: a thick, flat bundle of about 200 million commissural nerve fibres connecting the left and right cerebral hemispheres.

Anatomy

From front to back it comprises the rostrum, genu, trunk (body) and splenium. It transfers motor, sensory and cognitive information between hemispheres.

Clinical significance

Agenesis of the corpus callosum is a congenital condition with variable effects. Surgical section (corpus callosotomy) is used for severe epilepsy and produced the famous split-brain studies of Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga.
