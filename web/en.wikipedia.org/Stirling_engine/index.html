Stirling engine

Stirling engine
TypeClosed-cycle external combustion heat engine
InventorRobert Stirling (patented 1816)
Cycle phasesIsothermal expansion, isochoric cooling, isothermal compression, isochoric heating

A Stirling engine is a heat engine that operates by cyclic compression and expansion of a fixed quantity of working gas at different temperatures. Patented in 1816 by the Scottish clergyman Robert Stirling, it is an external combustion engine: heat is supplied from outside the cylinders, so it can run on any heat source, including solar and waste heat.

Design

Classic designs come in alpha (two power pistons), beta (piston and displacer in one cylinder) and gamma configurations. A regenerator stores heat between cycles, greatly improving efficiency; in theory the Stirling cycle can approach Carnot efficiency. Working gases include air, hydrogen and helium.

Applications

Stirling engines are valued for quiet operation and multi-fuel capability. They have powered submarines (air-independent propulsion), solar dish generators, cryocoolers and combined-heat-and-power units, though high cost and slow start-up have limited mass adoption.
