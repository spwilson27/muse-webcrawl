Dry needling

Dry needling, also known as trigger point dry needling or intramuscular stimulation, is a technique in which a thin filiform needle is inserted through the skin into areas of muscle, typically myofascial trigger points, with the aim of relieving pain and improving range of motion. It is called "dry" because nothing is injected through the needle.

Practice

The technique is practised mainly by physical therapists, and in some jurisdictions by chiropractors, acupuncturists and physicians. Needling a trigger point may elicit a local twitch response, followed, proponents say, by relaxation of the taut band of muscle. Proposed mechanisms include mechanical disruption of the trigger point, changes in local blood flow and biochemical environment, and effects on pain signalling.

Relationship to acupuncture

Dry needling uses the same type of needle as acupuncture and many of the same points may be targeted, but it is framed within Western anatomy and neurophysiology rather than traditional Chinese medicine concepts such as meridians and qi. The overlap has led to professional and regulatory disputes over scope of practice.

Evidence

Systematic reviews have found some evidence of short-term pain relief for certain musculoskeletal conditions, but studies are heterogeneous and often at risk of bias, and the overall quality of evidence is considered low to moderate. Adverse events are usually minor (soreness, bruising, bleeding), with rare serious complications such as pneumothorax.
