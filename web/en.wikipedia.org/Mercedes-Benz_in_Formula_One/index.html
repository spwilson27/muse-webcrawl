Mercedes-Benz, a German luxury automotive brand of the Mercedes-Benz Group, has been involved in Formula One for multiple periods, as both a constructor and an engine supplier. The works team's cars are often referred to by the nickname "Silver Arrows".

1954–1955: the first works team

Mercedes returned to Grand Prix racing in 1954 with the advanced W196, featuring fuel injection and (in some races) streamlined bodywork. Juan Manuel Fangio won the World Championship with the team in 1954 and 1955, alongside rising British driver Stirling Moss. After the 1955 Le Mans disaster, Mercedes withdrew from motor racing entirely at the end of that season, ending the works team's first era.

Engine supplier era

Mercedes returned to Formula One in 1994 as an engine supplier (through Ilmor, later Mercedes-Ilmor), partnering with Sauber and then McLaren, with whom it won drivers' titles for Mika Häkkinen (1998–1999) and Lewis Hamilton (2008), and a constructors' title in 1998.

2010–present: the modern works team

Daimler purchased the championship-winning Brawn GP team at the end of 2009 and re-entered as a constructor in 2010 with Michael Schumacher and Nico Rosberg. With the switch to 1.6-litre V6 turbo-hybrid power units in 2014, Mercedes began an era of dominance: Lewis Hamilton and the team won eight consecutive Constructors' Championships (2014–2021), with Hamilton taking six drivers' titles in that span and Rosberg one (2016). George Russell joined as race driver from 2022, and Andrea Kimi Antonelli succeeded Hamilton for 2025 after Hamilton's move to Ferrari.
