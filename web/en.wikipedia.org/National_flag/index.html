National flag

A national flag is a flag that represents and symbolizes a given nation. It is flown by the government of that nation, but it can also be flown by its citizens. National flags are often designed with specific meanings for their colours and symbols, which may come from the nation's history, culture, or founding principles.

History

The practice of flying flags representing political entities grew out of maritime and military standards. In the early modern period, ensigns identified ships at sea, and by the 18th and 19th centuries — particularly with the rise of nationalism during and after the French Revolution — distinctive flags became central emblems of nation-states. The tricolour designs popularised by France and the Netherlands influenced many later flags.

Design and symbolism

The study of flags is called vexillology. Common elements include crosses (as in the Scandinavian cross flags), stripes, crescents, stars, and national colours. Some flags carry elaborate coats of arms, such as those of Spain and Mexico. The only national flags that are not rectangular are those of Nepal (a double-pennon shape) and, notably, Switzerland and Vatican City, which are square.

Civil and state flags

Many countries distinguish between a civil flag, flown by private citizens, and a state flag, used by government institutions; some also have separate war flags and naval ensigns. Flag protocols govern display, raising and lowering, flying at half-mast, and respectful disposal.
