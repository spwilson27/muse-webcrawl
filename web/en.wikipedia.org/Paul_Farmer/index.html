Paul Edward Farmer (October 26, 1959 – February 21, 2022) was an American medical anthropologist and physician. A co-founder of the international non-profit Partners In Health (PIH), he was dedicated to providing high-quality health care to impoverished communities, arguing that modern medicine could and should be delivered in the poorest settings on earth. Farmer served as Kolokotrones University Professor at Harvard University and chief of the Division of Global Health Equity at Brigham and Women's Hospital in Boston.

Early life and education

Farmer grew up in Florida, where his family lived at times in a converted bus and on a houseboat. He attended Duke University on scholarship, graduating in 1982, and first visited Haiti as an undergraduate—an experience that shaped his life's work. He earned both an MD and a PhD in anthropology from Harvard University, completing his residency in internal medicine at Brigham and Women's Hospital while spending much of each year in rural Haiti.

Partners In Health

In 1987, Farmer co-founded Partners In Health with Jim Yong Kim, Ophelia Dahl, Thomas J. White, and Todd McCormack, building on the community-based clinic Zanmi Lasante in Cange, Haiti, founded with Haitian partners. PIH demonstrated that multidrug-resistant tuberculosis and HIV/AIDS could be treated effectively in poor communities through community health workers, free care, and social support such as food and housing—a model that influenced global policy at the World Health Organization and the Global Fund.

The organisation expanded to Rwanda, Lesotho, Malawi, Peru, Mexico, and elsewhere. In Rwanda, Farmer helped the government build a national rural health system and the Butaro Hospital and University of Global Health Equity. During the West African Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016, PIH worked in Sierra Leone and Liberia; Farmer criticised the international response as a failure of imagination rooted in disparities in care.

Writings and legacy

Farmer's books include AIDS and Accusation (1992), The Uses of Haiti (1994), Infections and Inequalities (1999), Pathologies of Power (2003), and Reimagining Global Health (2013). His life and work in Haiti were chronicled in Tracy Kidder's bestselling biography Mountains Beyond Mountains (2003). He developed influential concepts in medical anthropology, including "structural violence" applied to health, and a "preferential option for the poor" in medicine.

Farmer died in his sleep of an acute cardiac event in Butaro, Rwanda, on February 21, 2022, while teaching at the University of Global Health Equity.
