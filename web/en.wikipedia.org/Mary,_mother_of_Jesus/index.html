Mary, mother of Jesus

Mary was a first-century Jewish woman of Nazareth, the wife of Joseph and the mother of Jesus. She is venerated in Christianity as the Mother of God (Greek: Theotokos) and is a central figure of devotion, especially in the Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches. Mary also holds an exalted place in Islam: she is the only woman named in the Qur'an, is the subject of its 19th chapter (Surah Maryam), and is regarded as one of the most righteous women in history.

Life in the New Testament

The main sources are the Gospels of Matthew and Luke. According to Luke, the angel Gabriel announced to Mary in Nazareth that she would conceive and bear a son, Jesus, by the power of the Holy Spirit — the Annunciation. Her response, "let it be to me according to your word", and her song of praise, the Magnificat, are foundational texts of Christian spirituality. Mary was betrothed to Joseph, who, Matthew relates, married her after an angel's reassurance. She gave birth to Jesus in Bethlehem, was present at the wedding at Cana where Jesus performed his first sign, stood at the foot of the cross during the crucifixion (John's Gospel), and was among the disciples in Jerusalem after the Resurrection. Tradition concerning her later life divides: Catholic teaching holds that she was assumed body and soul into heaven (the Assumption, defined as dogma in 1950), while Orthodox tradition commemorates her Dormition ("falling asleep"). The doctrine of the Immaculate Conception (1854) teaches that Mary herself was conceived free from original sin; perpetual virginity is affirmed by Catholic, Orthodox and some other traditions.

Veneration

The Council of Ephesus (431) affirmed Mary as Theotokos, "God-bearer". Marian devotion developed through feasts, hymns such as the Akathist, the rosary, and reported apparitions at sites including Lourdes, Fátima and Guadalupe, whose basilicas are among the world's most visited pilgrimage destinations. Mary is the most frequently depicted woman in Western art, from Byzantine icons of the Virgin Eleusa to Renaissance Madonnas. Protestant traditions vary: the early Reformers honoured Mary as mother of God, but most Protestant churches reject invocation of Mary and the later dogmas, while still regarding her as a model of faith and obedience.

In Islam

Maryam bint Imran is honoured as the mother of the prophet Isa (Jesus). The Qur'an narrates her miraculous birth, dedication to the Temple, the annunciation by Gabriel, and the virgin birth beneath a palm tree. Islamic tradition counts her among the four perfect women, and she is a figure of devotion across the Muslim world, though without the doctrines of intercession associated with Catholic practice.
