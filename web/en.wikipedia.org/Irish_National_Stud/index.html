Irish National Stud

The Irish National Stud (Irish: Comhlucht Graí Náisiúnta na hÉireann Teo.) is a Thoroughbred horse breeding facility in Tully, County Kildare, Ireland. It was formally established by incorporation on 31 December 1945 by the National Stud Act 1945, the Irish government having purchased the property from Lord Wavertree (Colonel William Hall-Walker), who had founded the stud in 1900 and gifted it to the British crown in 1915.

Attractions

The stud stands a roster of stallions and is one of Ireland's most popular tourist attractions, welcoming around 130,000 visitors a year. Its grounds include the Japanese Gardens, created between 1906 and 1910 by the Japanese horticulturist Tassa Eida and his son Minoru, and the more recent St Fiachra's Garden. The site also houses the Irish Horse Museum.

Role

Owned by the Irish state through the Department of Agriculture, the stud's statutory purpose is the promotion of the Irish Thoroughbred breeding industry, Ireland being one of the world's leading producers of racehorses. Notable stallions that have stood at Tully include Invincible Spirit's sire-line predecessors and the champion Sea the Stars' contemporary, while its mares and yearlings are sold at public auction.
