Roman Martyrology

The Roman Martyrology (Latin: Martyrologium Romanum) is the official martyrology of the Catholic Church, a catalogue of martyrs and other saints arranged by the day of the year on which they are commemorated, with brief accounts of their lives and deaths. It is traditionally read aloud in religious communities and at the Office of Prime.

History

The Martyrology developed from earlier local lists and the medieval work of Usuard. The first official Roman edition appeared in 1583 under Pope Gregory XIII, with Cardinal Cesare Baronius responsible for a revised, annotated edition. Numerous revisions followed; a major new edition was published in 2001 and corrected in 2004, following the liturgical reforms of the Second Vatican Council. The Martyrology contains over 6,500 entries — the Church's most comprehensive official list of recognised saints and blesseds.
