Arch bridge

An arch bridge is a bridge with abutments at each end shaped as a curved arch. Arch bridges work by transferring the weight of the bridge and its loads partially into a horizontal thrust restrained by the abutments at either side. A viaduct (a long bridge) may be made from a series of arches, although other more economical structures are typically used today.

History

The oldest surviving arch bridges are Mycenaean and Roman; Roman segmental arch bridges such as the Pont Saint-Martin still stand. Chinese builders produced notable stone arch bridges, including the open-spandrel Zhaozhou Bridge of the early 7th century CE. In the industrial era, arches were built first in cast iron (Ironbridge, 1779) and later in steel and reinforced concrete.

Types

Arch bridges are described by the position of the deck relative to the arch: in a deck arch bridge the deck sits on top of the arch; in a through arch bridge the deck passes through the arch and is hung from it by suspenders; a tied arch (bowstring) carries the horizontal thrust in a tie girder rather than the foundations. The Chaotianmen Bridge in Chongqing, with a main span of 552 m, is among the longest arch bridges in the world; other famous examples include the Sydney Harbour Bridge and the New River Gorge Bridge.
