Vadakkunnathan Temple

Vadakkunnathan Temple stands at the centre of Thrissur, surrounded by the Thekkinkadu ground.

Vadakkumnathan Temple is an ancient Hindu temple dedicated to Shiva at Thrissur, Kerala, India. The temple is a classic example of the architectural style of Kerala and has one monumental tower on each of the four sides in addition to a kuttambalam (temple theatre). Mural paintings depicting various scenes from the Mahabharata can be seen inside the temple. The temple, along with its mural paintings, was declared a National Monument by India under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act.

According to popular legend, the temple was first built by Parashurama, the sixth avatar of Vishnu. The Vadakkumnathan temple is the first Shiva temple created by Parashurama. The temple stands on a nine-acre walled ground, the Thekkinkadu Maidan, at the heart of the city, and is surrounded by the Swaraj Round. Thrissur Pooram, one of the largest and most famous temple festivals of Kerala, is hosted on its grounds each year, though the temple itself does not participate in the procession as a competitor — its deities play a presiding role.

History

The temple's origins are placed in legend more than a thousand years ago. The main shrines were renovated over the centuries by various rulers, notably the Perumpadappu Swaroopam (the Cochin royal family) and Sakthan Thampuran, the 18th–19th century ruler of Cochin who reorganised Thrissur Pooram and redesigned the city around the temple ground. The ghee-covered Shiva lingam in the main sanctum is famous: for centuries devotees have offered ghee, which has accumulated in layers over the lingam without melting or smell.

Architecture

Built in the Kerala style, the temple features sloping tiled roofs, wooden carvings, and four gopurams (gateway towers) aligned to the cardinal directions. Within the inner courtyard are shrines to Vadakkunnathan (Shiva), Shankaranarayana, Parvati, Ganesha, Rama, and Krishna, among others. The Kuttambalam, the traditional theatre hall, is used for performances of Koodiyattam, the Sanskrit theatre tradition inscribed by UNESCO.

Festivals

Thrissur Pooram, founded by Sakthan Thampuran in 1798, is celebrated in the Malayalam month of Medam (April–May) and features processions of caparisoned elephants, percussion ensembles such as the panchavadyam and elanjithara melam, and spectacular fireworks. The temple also observes Maha Shivaratri with special rituals, and Aanayoottu, the ceremonial feeding of elephants, in the month of Karkidakam.
