Hindu deities

Overview
TraditionHinduism
Key triadBrahma, Vishnu, Shiva
TermDeva (m.), Devi (f.)

Hindu deities are the gods and goddesses worshipped in Hinduism. Hindu thought ranges from monism — one ultimate reality, Brahman, behind all forms — to the devotional worship of many distinct deities, often understood as manifestations of that one reality. Texts speak poetically of "33 million" or "330 million" gods, expressing innumerability rather than a census.

The Trimurti

The Trimurti groups three great gods by cosmic function: Brahma the creator, rarely worshipped today; Vishnu the preserver, who descends to earth as avatars including Rama and Krishna; and Shiva the destroyer and transformer, associated with asceticism, the Ganges and Mount Kailash.

Goddesses

The goddess, Devi, is worshipped in many forms: Lakshmi, bestower of wealth and fortune and consort of Vishnu; Saraswati, goddess of learning and music; Parvati, gentle consort of Shiva; and fierce forms such as Durga and Kali, celebrated at Navaratri and Durga Puja. In Shaktism the Goddess is herself the supreme reality.

Other major deities

Ganesha, the elephant-headed remover of obstacles, is invoked at the start of undertakings; his brother Kartikeya (Murugan) is a major god in Tamil tradition. Hanuman, the devoted monkey god of the Ramayana, embodies selfless service. Older Vedic gods — Indra, Agni, Surya, Vayu — remain present in ritual even as devotional focus shifted to Vishnu, Shiva and Devi.

Worship

Deities are honoured through puja — offerings of flowers, food, lamps and song — in temples and home shrines, and through festivals, pilgrimage and the chanting of mantras. Images (murti) are treated as vessels of divine presence and are the focus of darshan, the mutual seeing of deity and devotee.
