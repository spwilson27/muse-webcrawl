Executive Order 6102

Executive Order 6102 is an executive order signed on April 5, 1933, by US President Franklin D. Roosevelt, "forbidding the Hoarding of Gold Coin, Gold Bullion, and Gold Certificates within the continental United States." Issued under the Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917, as amended by the Emergency Banking Act of March 1933, the order required all persons to deliver their gold coin, bullion, and certificates to a Federal Reserve Bank or member bank by May 1, 1933, in exchange for payment at the statutory rate of $20.67 per troy ounce.

Provisions and penalties

The order exempted gold used in industry, profession, or art, and jewelry, and allowed individuals to retain up to $100 in gold coins of recognized special value to collectors of rare and unusual coins. Criminal penalties for violation were a fine of up to $10,000, imprisonment of up to ten years, or both. A handful of prosecutions followed, most famously that of New York attorney Frederick Barber Campbell, whose safety-deposit box of gold was seized.

Aftermath

The order was part of Roosevelt's program to combat deflation and the banking crisis of the Great Depression. In January 1934 the Gold Reserve Act transferred the Federal Reserve's gold to the Treasury and authorized the president to devalue the dollar; Roosevelt set the price of gold at $35 per ounce, raising the value of the government's gold stock. Private ownership of gold remained heavily restricted until President Gerald Ford signed legislation that took effect on December 31, 1974, again legalizing it.
