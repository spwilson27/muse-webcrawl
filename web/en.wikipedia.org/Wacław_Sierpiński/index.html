Wacław Franciszek Sierpiński (14 March 1882 – 21 October 1969) was a Polish mathematician known for his contributions to set theory, number theory, topology, and the theory of functions. He published over 700 papers and 50 books, and was one of the founders — with Zygmunt Janiszewski and Stefan Mazurkiewicz — of the Polish School of Mathematics and its journal Fundamenta Mathematicae (1920).

Work

Sierpiński's name is attached to several famous fractal constructions described early in the twentieth century: the Sierpiński triangle (1915), the Sierpiński carpet (1916), and the Sierpiński curve. In number theory he studied what are now called Sierpiński numbers — odd numbers k for which k·2n + 1 is composite for every n — and proved in 1960 that infinitely many such numbers exist. In set theory he worked extensively on the axiom of choice and the continuum hypothesis.

Life

Educated at the University of Warsaw under Georgy Voronoy, Sierpiński was interned in Russia during the First World War, where he worked with Nikolai Luzin in Moscow. After Poland regained independence he took a chair at the University of Warsaw. During the Polish–Soviet War of 1920 he worked in the Polish Cipher Bureau, helping to break Russian ciphers. He survived the Second World War in Warsaw and helped run the clandestine Polish university. The lunar crater Sierpiński is named in his honour.
