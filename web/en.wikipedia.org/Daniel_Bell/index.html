Daniel Bell (10 May 1919 – 25 January 2011) was an American sociologist, writer and professor emeritus at Harvard University. He is best known for his books The End of Ideology (1960), The Coming of Post-Industrial Society (1973) and The Cultural Contradictions of Capitalism (1976).

Career

Born Daniel Bolotsky to Eastern European Jewish immigrant parents, Bell worked as a journalist, including as labour editor of Fortune magazine, before completing his doctorate at Columbia University, where he taught from 1959. In 1969 he moved to Harvard. With Irving Kristol he co-founded the journal The Public Interest in 1965 and was associated with the New York intellectuals and early neoconservatism, though he described himself as "a socialist in economics, a liberal in politics, and a conservative in culture".

Ideas

In The Coming of Post-Industrial Society Bell argued that advanced economies were shifting from manufacturing to services, with theoretical knowledge becoming the central source of innovation and policy. The Cultural Contradictions of Capitalism contended that modernist, anti-institutional culture was at odds with the discipline required by the capitalist economy.
