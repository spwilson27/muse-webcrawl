Hatha yoga

Hatha yoga (Sanskrit: हठ योग) is a branch of yoga that uses physical techniques to preserve and channel vital force or energy. The Sanskrit word हठ haṭha literally means "force", alluding to a system of physical techniques. In India, hatha yoga is associated in popular tradition with the Nath yogis of the Matha (मठ) tradition. Almost all modern styles of physical yoga descend from, or are influenced by, the hatha yoga tradition.

Origins

Hatha yoga emerged within Tantric and ascetic movements of India around the turn of the second millennium CE, drawing on earlier practices of posture, breath control and bodily seals. Its earliest textual sources include the Buddhist and Hindu tantras, and works such as the Amṛtasiddhi (11th century), the first text to teach the physical techniques later characteristic of hatha yoga. The tradition is traditionally traced to the semi-legendary sage Matsyendranath and his disciple Gorakshanath, founders of the Nath order.

Practices and texts

Classical hatha yoga combines asana (postures), pranayama (breath regulation), mudra (seals and gestures intended to retain energy), bandha (locks), and purification practices (shatkriyas), aimed at awakening kundalini energy and attaining liberation (moksha) as well as supernatural powers (siddhis). The major surviving manuals include the Hatha Yoga Pradipika of Svatmarama (15th century), the Gheranda Samhita, and the Shiva Samhita. The Hatha Yoga Pradipika presents hatha as a "stairway to the heights of Raja Yoga".

Modern hatha yoga

In the 20th century, teachers such as Tirumalai Krishnamacharya and his students B. K. S. Iyengar and K. Pattabhi Jois reworked hatha yoga into the exercise-oriented styles now practiced worldwide, often de-emphasizing its esoteric physiology. In the West today, "hatha yoga" commonly denotes a gentle, slower-paced style of asana class, in contrast to vigorous vinyasa styles — a usage quite different from the medieval tradition but descended from it. Modern academic scholarship, notably by James Mallinson and Mark Singleton, has documented how modern postural yoga blends Indian hatha traditions with European gymnastics and physical culture.
