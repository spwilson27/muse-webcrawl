Barossa Valley

The Barossa Valley is a valley in South Australia, about 60 km north-east of Adelaide, and one of Australia's oldest and most prestigious wine regions. Its towns include Tanunda, Nuriootpa and Angaston.

History

The valley was settled from 1842 by German Lutheran immigrants from Silesia and Prussia, whose culture still shapes the region's food, Lutheran churches and architecture. Vines were planted from the 1840s; the Barossa retains some of the world's oldest continuously producing Shiraz vines, spared the phylloxera that devastated other regions.

Wine

The region (a Geographical Indication) is famed for full-bodied Shiraz, along with Grenache, Cabernet Sauvignon and Eden Valley-influenced Riesling from higher ground. Iconic producers include Penfolds, Jacob's Creek, Yalumba, Henschke and Torbreck.

Tourism

More than 150 wineries and 80 cellar doors, farmers' markets and festivals make the Barossa a major food-and-wine tourism destination.
