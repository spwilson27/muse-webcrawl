CP violation

In particle physics, CP violation is a violation of CP symmetry — the combination of charge conjugation (C) and parity (P) — which states that the laws of physics should be the same if a particle is interchanged with its antiparticle and its spatial coordinates are inverted.

Discovery

Parity violation in the weak interaction was established in 1956–57 by Lee, Yang and Wu. CP violation itself was discovered in 1964 by James Cronin and Val Fitch in decays of neutral kaons, earning them the 1980 Nobel Prize in Physics. In the Standard Model CP violation arises from a complex phase in the Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa (CKM) matrix describing quark mixing; Kobayashi and Maskawa shared the 2008 Nobel Prize.

Significance

CP violation is one of the Sakharov conditions required to explain the matter–antimatter asymmetry of the universe, but the amount present in the Standard Model is far too small, motivating searches for new sources in B-meson, charm and neutrino physics. Combined CPT symmetry remains unviolated in all observed phenomena.
