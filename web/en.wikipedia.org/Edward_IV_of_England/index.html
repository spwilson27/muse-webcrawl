Edward IV of England

Edward IV (28 April 1442 – 9 April 1483) was King of England from 4 March 1461 to 3 October 1470, and again from 11 April 1471 until his death. A central figure of the Wars of the Roses, he was the first Yorkist king and twice seized the throne from the Lancastrian Henry VI.

Edward IV

Rise to the throne

Born in Rouen, Edward was the eldest surviving son of Richard, Duke of York. After his father's death at the Battle of Wakefield in 1460, the 18-year-old Edward took up the Yorkist claim, won the bloody Battle of Towton in March 1461 — the largest battle of the wars — and was crowned in London while Henry VI fled to Scotland.

First reign

Edward's secret marriage in 1464 to Elizabeth Woodville, a Lancastrian widow, alienated his chief supporter, Richard Neville, Earl of Warwick, the "Kingmaker," who rebelled in 1469–70 with Edward's brother George, Duke of Clarence. Edward was driven into exile in Burgundy, and Henry VI was restored in the "Readeption" of 1470.

Second reign

Returning in 1471, Edward defeated and killed Warwick at the Battle of Barnet and destroyed the remaining Lancastrian army at Tewkesbury, where Henry VI's son Edward of Westminster died; Henry himself died in the Tower soon after. Edward's remaining years were comparatively peaceful: he rebuilt royal finances, invaded France in 1475 only to accept the lucrative Treaty of Picquigny, and patronised Caxton's printing press. He executed Clarence for treason in 1478.

Death and succession

Edward died unexpectedly at 40 in April 1483. His 12-year-old son succeeded as Edward V but was displaced within months by Edward IV's brother, Richard III; the young king and his brother — the "Princes in the Tower" — disappeared.
