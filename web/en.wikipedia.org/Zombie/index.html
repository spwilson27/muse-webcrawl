Zombie

A zombie (Haitian French: zombi; Haitian Creole: zonbi) is a mythological undead corporeal revenant created through the reanimation of a corpse. In Haitian folklore, a zombie is a dead body reanimated through various methods, most commonly magic by a bokor, a sorcerer of Haitian Vodou. Zombies are featured widely in modern popular culture, where they are usually portrayed as mindless, flesh-eating undead created by an outbreak or other apocalyptic event.

Origins

The concept emerged from West African Vodun traditions carried to the Caribbean by enslaved Africans, blended with Taíno and European beliefs. Ethnobotanist Wade Davis controversially proposed that Haitian zombies could be produced with a combination of tetrodotoxin and dissociative drugs, a claim publicised in his book The Serpent and the Rainbow. The English word first appeared in print in the early 19th century.

In popular culture

The modern zombie archetype owes most to George A. Romero's films, beginning with Night of the Living Dead (1968) and Dawn of the Dead (1978), which established the shambling, cannibalistic ghoul and the use of zombies as social satire. Later works reshaped the creature: fast-moving infected in 28 Days Later, comedy in Shaun of the Dead, and the television phenomenon The Walking Dead. Zombies are also central to video games such as Resident Evil and to the "zombie apocalypse" genre generally.

Other uses

The term is used metaphorically for a person devoid of consciousness or initiative, and technically in computing for a "zombie" machine controlled remotely as part of a botnet, and in philosophy for the hypothetical "philosophical zombie".
