Gingerbread is a broad category of baked goods, typically flavoured with ginger, cloves, nutmeg, cinnamon and sweetened with honey, sugar or molasses. Gingerbread ranges from a soft, moist cake to a crisp biscuit, and takes many regional forms across Europe and North America.

History

Ginger was introduced to Europe from the Middle East by returning Crusaders, and spiced breads became customary at medieval festivals and fairs. Nuremberg in Germany became a famous centre of gingerbread production (Lebkuchen) from the 15th century, thanks to its position on spice trade routes. In England, gingerbread was sold at fairs as decorated "fairings"; the town of Market Drayton in Shropshire is noted for its gingerbread.

Forms

Well-known varieties include German Lebkuchen and Pfeffernüsse, Polish pierniki from Toruń, French pain d'épices, Scandinavian pepparkakor, and the soft ginger cake of northern England (such as Yorkshire parkin, made with oatmeal and treacle). Gingerbread figures — notably the gingerbread man — and gingerbread houses, popularised after the Brothers Grimm published "Hansel and Gretel" in 1812, are enduring Christmas traditions; elaborate gingerbread-house competitions and exhibitions are held annually in several countries.
