Hepatitis A vaccine

Hepatitis A vaccine is a vaccine that prevents hepatitis A, a viral infection of the liver spread mainly through contaminated food and water. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Use and effectiveness

The vaccine is given by injection into muscle, usually in two doses for long-term protection. It is about 95% effective at preventing hepatitis A, and protection is thought to last at least 15–25 years, possibly for life. Vaccination is recommended for children in countries with intermediate rates of disease, for travellers to such regions, and for people at increased risk, including those with chronic liver disease and men who have sex with men.

Most licensed vaccines are made from inactivated (killed) virus; live attenuated vaccines are used in a few countries. Side effects are generally mild, such as pain at the injection site. The first hepatitis A vaccines were approved in Europe in 1991 and in the United States in 1995.
