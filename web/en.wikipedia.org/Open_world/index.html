Open world

An open world is a level design concept in video games in which the player can roam freely through a virtual world and is given considerable freedom in choosing how and when to approach objectives. Open-world games typically structure play non-linearly, with multiple paths to a goal.

History

Early examples include Ultima (1981) and The Legend of Zelda (1986). The genre was popularised in three dimensions by Grand Theft Auto III (2001), and later titles such as The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim and The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild expanded expectations for scale, emergent systems, and environmental storytelling.

Design

Designers balance freedom against narrative coherence and technical constraints such as streaming large environments. Open worlds contrast with linear or "hub" level structures; related terms include "sandbox game", though sandbox emphasises player creativity and tools rather than geography alone.
