Giovanni Falcone (18 May 1939 – 23 May 1992) was an Italian judge and prosecuting magistrate. From his office in Palermo, he spent most of his professional life trying to overthrow the power of the Sicilian Mafia, most famously as a leading figure of the Antimafia Pool that prepared the Maxi Trial of 1986–87, which convicted hundreds of mafiosi. His assassination by the Mafia in the Capaci bombing made him a national hero in Italy.

Career

Falcone joined the judiciary in 1964 and, after early work in Trapani, moved to Palermo's prosecution office. Following the murder of judge Cesare Terranova, he joined the office investigating Mafia finances, where he pioneered "following the money" through bank records across many countries. Working in the Antimafia Pool created by Rocco Chinnici alongside Paolo Borsellino and others, Falcone made crucial use of pentiti (turncoats), above all Tommaso Buscetta, whose testimony revealed the structure of Cosa Nostra and its ruling Commission, underpinning the Maxi Trial, in which 338 defendants were convicted.

Assassination and legacy

On 23 May 1992, Falcone, his wife Francesca Morvillo and three police escorts were killed when a 400-kilogram bomb placed in a culvert beneath the A29 motorway near Capaci was detonated as their convoy passed. Borsellino was murdered two months later. The killings provoked public outrage and a major state crackdown on Cosa Nostra. Palermo's airport is named Falcone–Borsellino Airport in their honour, and a memorial stands at the bombing site.
