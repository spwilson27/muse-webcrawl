Copernican Revolution

The Copernican Revolution was the paradigm shift from the Ptolemaic geocentric model of the heavens, which described the universe as having Earth stationary at the centre, to the heliocentric model with the Sun at the centre of the Solar System. It began with the publication of Nicolaus Copernicus's De revolutionibus orbium coelestium in 1543 and unfolded over the following century.

Development

Copernicus's model still used circular orbits and epicycles, but it explained retrograde motion naturally and ordered the planets by period. Johannes Kepler's elliptical orbits, Galileo Galilei's telescopic observations (including the phases of Venus and the moons of Jupiter), and Isaac Newton's law of universal gravitation completed the transformation, laying the foundations of modern astronomy and physics.

Impact

The revolution is widely regarded as the launching point of the Scientific Revolution. The phrase "Copernican principle" later came to denote the idea that Earth and its observers occupy no privileged position in the universe.
