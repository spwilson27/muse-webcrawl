Mie goreng

TypeNoodle dish
Place of originIndonesia
Main ingredientsNoodles, kecap manis, vegetables, egg, meat or seafood
VariationsMie goreng Jawa, mie goreng Aceh, bakmi goreng

Mie goreng (also spelled mi goreng or mee goreng) is an Indonesian stir-fried noodle dish. It is made of yellow noodles stir-fried in cooking oil with garlic, onion or shallots, and seasoned principally with kecap manis (sweet soy sauce), which gives the dish its characteristic dark color and sweet-savory flavor. Common additions include chicken, prawns, beef, cabbage, tomatoes, egg, and pickles.

History

The dish derives from Chinese chow mein brought to the Indonesian archipelago by Chinese immigrants, and was adapted to local tastes and ingredients. It became widespread through street food vendors (kaki lima) and small restaurants (warung) across Indonesia, and variants are also popular in Malaysia and Singapore, where it is often prepared in a spicier style.

Preparation

Noodles are boiled until nearly cooked, then tossed in a hot wok with aromatics, protein, and vegetables. Kecap manis coats the noodles, caramelizing slightly. The dish is typically garnished with fried shallots, a fried egg, prawn crackers (kerupuk), and acar pickles, sometimes with a wedge of lime and sambal on the side.

Variants

Mie goreng Jawa from Central Java is sweeter and uses thicker noodles. Mie goreng Aceh uses thick yellow noodles in a rich, spicy curry-like sauce. Instant noodle versions, most famously Indomie's Mi Goreng, have made the flavor profile known worldwide.
