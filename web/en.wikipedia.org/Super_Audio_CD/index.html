Super Audio CD

Super Audio CD (SACD) is a read-only optical disc format for audio storage introduced in 1999 by Sony and Philips, the companies behind the Compact Disc. It was intended as a high-resolution successor to the CD, using the Direct Stream Digital (DSD) encoding system instead of the CD's pulse-code modulation.

Technology

DSD records audio as a 1-bit pulse-density stream sampled at 2.8224 MHz (64 times the CD sample rate), giving SACD a theoretical frequency response up to 100 kHz and a dynamic range of about 120 dB across the audible band. A disc can hold both a stereo and a multichannel (up to 5.1) mix. Many releases are hybrid discs with a standard CD layer playable on ordinary CD players. SACD discs use physical copy protection features and cannot be ripped by ordinary computer drives.

History and market

SACD competed with DVD-Audio in a format war that both formats effectively lost: mainstream listeners moved to downloads and streaming, and SACD survived as an audiophile niche, with thousands of mostly classical and jazz titles. Players remain available, and DSD files later circulated as downloads (DSF/DFF formats).
