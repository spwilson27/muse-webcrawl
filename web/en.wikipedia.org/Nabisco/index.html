Nabisco

Nabisco (originally the National Biscuit Company) is an American manufacturer of cookies and snacks headquartered in East Hanover, New Jersey. Founded in 1898 through the merger of regional bakeries, it is a subsidiary of Mondelēz International.

Founded: 1898

Headquarters: East Hanover, New Jersey, U.S.

Parent: Mondelēz International

Products: Cookies, crackers, snacks

History

The National Biscuit Company was formed in 1898 by merging the New York Biscuit Company, the American Biscuit and Manufacturing Company, and others. It pioneered branded, packaged baked goods, beginning with the Uneeda Biscuit sold in moisture-proof In-er-seal packaging. In 1971 the company formally adopted the contraction "Nabisco", which had been used as a brand since the early twentieth century. After mergers with Standard Brands (forming Nabisco Brands) and acquisition by R.J. Reynolds, the firm passed to Kraft Foods in 2000 and then to Mondelēz in 2012.

Brands

Nabisco's brands include Oreo (introduced 1912 and often described as the world's best-selling cookie), Chips Ahoy!, Ritz Crackers, Triscuit, Wheat Thins, Premium saltines, Teddy Grahams, Nilla wafers, and Belvita.
