North Channel (Ontario)

The North Channel is the body of water along the north shore of Lake Huron in the Canadian province of Ontario. It stretches approximately 160 miles (260 km) from the St. Marys River at its western end to Georgian Bay at its eastern end. The channel is bordered on the south by Manitoulin Island, Cockburn Island, and Drummond Island, and on the north by the Ontario mainland and Algoma District.

Geography

The North Channel contains many islands and sheltered anchorages, including the Benjamin Islands and the Whalesback Channel. Its shores are lined with the pink granite and quartzite ridges of the La Cloche Mountains near Killarney. The North Channel connects the main body of Lake Huron with the St. Marys River and Lake Superior via the De Tour Passage and channels around St. Joseph Island.

Recreation

The North Channel is widely regarded as one of the finest freshwater cruising grounds in the world. Its relatively uncrowded anchorages, clear water, and scenic rocky islands attract thousands of sailors each summer. Marinas are located in communities including Little Current, Killarney, Gore Bay, Blind River, and Thessalon. The North Channel forms part of the Great Loop cruising route.

History

The channel was an important route for Indigenous peoples and later for French fur traders and voyageurs travelling between Montreal and the upper Great Lakes. The mainland shore developed with logging and mining communities in the 19th and 20th centuries, including the uranium mining town of Elliot Lake, while Manitoulin Island remained an important Anishinaabe homeland.
