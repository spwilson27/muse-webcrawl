Pike's Peak Gold Rush

The Pike's Peak Gold Rush (later known as the Colorado Gold Rush) was the boom in gold prospecting and mining in the Pike's Peak Country of western Kansas Territory and southwestern Nebraska Territory from July 1858 to February 1861, when Colorado Territory was created. An estimated 100,000 gold seekers took part, their slogan "Pike's Peak or Bust."

Discovery and rush

In July 1858 a party led by William Greeneberry Russell found placer gold along the South Platte River near present-day Englewood, Colorado. News of richer finds near Cherry Creek drew tens of thousands of "Fifty-Niners" westward across the plains in 1859. The settlements of Auraria and Denver City, rivals at the confluence of Cherry Creek and the South Platte, merged as Denver. In May 1859 John H. Gregory discovered the first lode gold vein in Gregory Gulch, spawning Central City and the "Richest Square Mile on Earth."

Legacy

Though many prospectors returned home disappointed ("busted"), the rush populated the region, displaced the Cheyenne and Arapaho from their lands, and led directly to the creation of Colorado Territory in 1861 and statehood in 1876. Mining camps later boomed again at Leadville and Cripple Creek.
