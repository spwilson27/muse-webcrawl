Tsenacommacah (pronounced roughly "tsena-co-MAH-kah", meaning "densely inhabited land") was the name given by the Powhatan people to their homeland in the Tidewater region of present-day Virginia. In the early 17th century it was the territory of the Powhatan Confederacy of about 30 Algonquian-speaking districts under the paramount chief Wahunsenacawh, known to the English as Chief Powhatan.

Geography and people

Tsenacommacah covered roughly 8,000 square miles bounded by the Potomac River to the north, the Atlantic coast, and the fall line of the James River to the west. Its population at English contact in 1607 is estimated between 13,000 and 22,000, living in towns of houses framed with saplings and covered with bark or mats, farming maize, beans and squash.

Contact with Jamestown

The English colony of Jamestown was founded inside Tsenacommacah. Relations shifted between trade and war until the confederacy's defeat after the 1644 attack and the 1646 treaty, which sharply reduced its territory.
