Le Morte d'Arthur

Le Morte d'Arthur (originally spelled Le Morte Darthur) is a prose reworking of Arthurian legend by Sir Thomas Malory, first published in 1485 by William Caxton. Compiled from French and other sources while Malory was imprisoned, it is the first novelistic work of English prose and the most complete single-volume account of the legend of King Arthur in English.

The work recounts the birth of Arthur, his drawing of the sword, the founding of the Round Table, the quests of Lancelot, Gawain, Perceval and Galahad for the Holy Grail, the love of Lancelot and Guinevere, and the final battle in which Arthur and Mordred slay one another. Malory's version became the principal source for later Arthurian literature, drama and film, including T. H. White's The Once and Future King.

A manuscript discovered at Winchester College in 1934, differing in some respects from Caxton's edition, is known as the Winchester Manuscript and underlies modern scholarly editions.
