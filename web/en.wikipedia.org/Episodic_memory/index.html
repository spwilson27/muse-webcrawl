Episodic memory

Episodic memory depends heavily on the hippocampus and surrounding medial temporal lobe structures.

Episodic memory is the memory of everyday events (such as times, locations, associated emotions, and other contextual information) that can be explicitly stated or conjured. It is the collection of past personal experiences that occurred at particular times and places — for example, the memory of one's last birthday party. Together with semantic memory (memory of facts and concepts), it makes up declarative, or explicit, memory.

History of the concept

The term was coined in 1972 by Endel Tulving, who distinguished episodic from semantic memory. Tulving later characterised retrieval from episodic memory as accompanied by autonoetic consciousness — a sense of mentally travelling back in time and re-experiencing an event from one's personal past — which he contrasted with the "knowing" of semantic memory. The amnesic patient K.C., who lost episodic memory while retaining much semantic knowledge, provided influential evidence for the distinction.

Neural basis

Episodic memory formation depends on the medial temporal lobe, especially the hippocampus, which binds together distributed cortical representations of an event. Damage to these structures produces anterograde amnesia, a profound inability to form new episodic memories, as in the famous patient H.M. The prefrontal cortex contributes to encoding strategies and to source monitoring and retrieval.

Development and related phenomena

Episodic memory emerges during early childhood, roughly between ages three and four, around the offset of childhood amnesia. Tulving proposed that episodic memory also underpins the capacity to imagine future events ("mental time travel"), a link supported by studies showing that amnesic patients struggle to construct detailed future scenarios. Debate continues over whether non-human animals possess genuinely episodic memory; "episodic-like" memory has been demonstrated in scrub-jays and other species that remember what happened, where, and when.
