Balboa Park is a 1,200-acre (490 ha) urban cultural park in San Diego, California. Named after Spanish explorer Vasco Núñez de Balboa, it is one of the oldest public recreational spaces in the United States, with land reserved in 1835 and the park formally established in 1868.

Institutions

The park hosts numerous museums and performing arts venues, including the San Diego Museum of Art, the Museum of Us, the San Diego Natural History Museum, the Fleet Science Center, the Old Globe Theatre and the San Diego Zoo. The ornate Spanish Colonial Revival buildings along El Prado were built for the 1915–16 Panama–California Exposition.

History

The Panama–California Exposition of 1915–16 and the California Pacific International Exposition of 1935–36 transformed the park with gardens, plazas and the California Tower. Landscape contributions by Kate Sessions, the "Mother of Balboa Park," shaped its botanical character.

Features

Attractions include the Botanical Building and lily pond, the Japanese Friendship Garden, the Spreckels Organ Pavilion and miles of trails through Florida Canyon.
