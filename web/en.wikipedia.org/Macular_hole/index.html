Macular hole

A macular hole is a full-thickness defect in the macula, the central part of the retina responsible for sharp, detailed vision. Most cases are idiopathic and arise when the ageing vitreous gel exerts traction on the fovea. The condition mainly affects people in their 60s and 70s and is more common in women; it causes blurred and distorted central vision with a central blind spot.

Optical coherence tomography is the standard imaging test for macular holes.

Causes and staging

With age the vitreous shrinks and may pull away from the retina incompletely at the fovea (vitreomacular traction), progressively tearing a defect. The Gass classification describes four stages: stage 1 (impending hole, foveal detachment), stage 2 (small full-thickness hole), stage 3 (larger hole with vitreous still attached), and stage 4 (hole with complete posterior vitreous detachment). Modern OCT-based systems (the International Vitreomacular Traction Study classification) size holes as small (≤250 µm), medium (250–400 µm), and large (>400 µm), which predicts surgical success. Secondary holes can follow trauma, high myopia, macular oedema, or retinal detachment.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Patients notice difficulty reading, straight lines appearing wavy (metamorphopsia), and a grey or blank patch in central vision; peripheral vision is preserved. Diagnosis is confirmed by optical coherence tomography, which shows the defect and any cuff of subretinal fluid. The Watzke–Allen test and Amsler grid can demonstrate the scotoma clinically. About 5–15% of patients develop a hole in the fellow eye over time, which is why both eyes are monitored.

Treatment

Vitrectomy surgery — removing the vitreous gel, peeling the internal limiting membrane around the hole, and filling the eye with a gas bubble — closes over 90% of holes, especially smaller ones, and improves vision in most patients; many must posture face-down for some days while the bubble presses the hole edges together. Large or chronic holes may need inverted flap techniques or retinal grafts. The enzyme ocriplasmin was briefly used to release traction pharmacologically but was withdrawn in many markets. Untreated full-thickness holes usually persist, with vision stabilising around 20/200 in the affected eye.
