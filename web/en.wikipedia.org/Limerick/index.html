Limerick (Irish: Luimneach) is a city in west-central Ireland, in County Limerick, in the province of Munster. It lies on the River Shannon, with the historic core on King's Island between the Shannon and the Abbey River. It is the third-largest urban centre on the island of Ireland.

History

A Viking settlement was founded on the site in 812; the Normans redesigned the city and built King John's Castle around 1200. Limerick is famous for the sieges of 1690–91 during the Williamite War and the Treaty of Limerick (1691), whose broken terms gave rise to the name "the Treaty City". Georgian Limerick expanded with Newtown Pery in the 18th century.

Economy and culture

The city is the commercial centre of Ireland's Mid-West, with Shannon Airport and the Shannon Free Zone nearby. The University of Limerick (founded 1972) and Thomond Park, home of Munster Rugby, are major institutions. The poetical form the limerick is popularly associated with the city, though the connection is uncertain. Frank McCourt's memoir Angela's Ashes depicts 1930s–40s Limerick.
