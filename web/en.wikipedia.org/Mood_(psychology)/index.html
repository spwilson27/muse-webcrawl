Mood (psychology)

In psychology, a mood is an affective state that is longer-lasting and less intense than an emotion, and typically not tied to a specific stimulus or event. Moods colour a person's perceptions and judgements for hours or days: someone in a positive mood tends to interpret ambiguous situations favourably, while a negative mood biases attention toward threats and problems.

Moods are often modelled along dimensions of valence and arousal.

Mood versus emotion

Unlike emotions, which are usually brief, intense, and directed at something, moods are diffuse background states without a clear object or onset. Moods can be influenced by sleep, health, weather, circadian rhythms, recent events and underlying temperament, and in turn they influence cognition, memory and social behaviour — phenomena studied in research on mood-congruent memory and affect-as-information.

Positive and negative mood

Psychologists often describe mood in terms of two largely independent dimensions, positive affect (enthusiasm, alertness, activity) and negative affect (distress, nervousness, irritability), measured for example by the PANAS scales. Instruments such as the Profile of Mood States track mood in clinical and sports settings.

Mood disorders

Persistent disturbances of mood define the mood disorders, principally major depressive disorder, in which a low mood endures for weeks or longer, and bipolar disorder, characterised by episodes of depression and mania or hypomania. Seasonal affective disorder describes mood changes following the seasons, particularly winter depression linked to reduced light.
