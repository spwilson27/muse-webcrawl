Exostosis

SpecialtyOrthopaedics, rheumatology
TypesOsteochondroma, subungual exostosis, surfer's ear, torus
NatureBenign bony outgrowth

An exostosis, also known as a bony spur, is a benign outgrowth of new bone projecting from the surface of an existing bone. Exostoses are among the most common benign bone lesions. They may be sessile (broad-based) or pedunculated (on a stalk), and are frequently capped with cartilage while growing. Many are incidental findings that never cause symptoms; others become troublesome when they press on nerves, blood vessels, muscles, or tendons, or interfere with joint movement.

Types

The most common exostosis is the osteochondroma, a cartilage-capped projection arising near the growth plates of long bones, usually in children and adolescents; it may be solitary or multiple, as in hereditary multiple exostoses, an autosomal dominant condition caused by mutations in the EXT1 or EXT2 genes. Subungual exostosis grows beneath a toenail or fingernail and can be painful. Exostosis of the external auditory canal ("surfer's ear") results from repeated exposure to cold water and wind, producing bony narrowing of the ear canal. Oral exostoses include the palatal and mandibular tori, common benign growths of the jaw. Reactive spurs such as heel spurs form at tendon and ligament attachments.

Diagnosis and treatment

Plain radiographs usually suffice for diagnosis, showing continuity of the lesion's cortex and medulla with the parent bone; MRI or CT is used when anatomy is complex or malignant change is suspected. Malignant transformation is rare — roughly 1% for solitary osteochondroma and higher for hereditary multiple forms, where the cartilage cap may give rise to a chondrosarcoma. Asymptomatic lesions are generally observed; surgery is indicated for pain, functional limitation, deformity, neurovascular compromise, or suspicion of malignancy, and consists of excising the outgrowth flush with the bone.
