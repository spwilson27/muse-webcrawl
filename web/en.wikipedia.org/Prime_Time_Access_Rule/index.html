Prime Time Access Rule

The Prime Time Access Rule (PTAR) was a broadcasting regulation instituted by the U.S. Federal Communications Commission (FCC) in 1970. It restricted television stations in the largest markets from broadcasting more than three hours of network programming during the four-hour prime time block, reserving the "access" hour — usually 7:00–8:00 p.m. — for locally produced or syndicated content.

Effects

The rule, together with the financial interest and syndication rules, aimed to diversify programming and reduce network dominance. It fostered a boom in first-run syndication, producing successful game shows, entertainment news programs, and off-network reruns during the access hour.

Repeal

The FCC relaxed the rule through the 1970s and 1980s and repealed it entirely in 1996, by which time cable television had transformed the media landscape. The access hour nevertheless remains a stronghold of syndicated programming in practice.
