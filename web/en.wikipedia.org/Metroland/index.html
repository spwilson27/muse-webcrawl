Metroland

Metroland (or Metro-land) is a name given to the suburban areas north-west of London, England, that were built up by the Metropolitan Railway in the early 20th century. The term was coined in the title of the Metropolitan Railway's guide to the area, Metro-land, published annually from 1915 to 1932, which promoted the country districts served by the line as ideal commuter country near the metropolis.

History

The Metropolitan Railway obtained powers in 1903 to develop its surplus land for housing, and in 1919 set up the Metropolitan Railway Country Estates company to build garden suburbs at places such as Wembley Park, Pinner, Rickmansworth, Chorleywood and Amersham. The railway promoted the area as a rural idyll within easy reach of central London by fast steam and later electric trains from Baker Street.

Character

Metroland housing was typically semi-detached houses in mock-Tudor and other revival styles on curving tree-lined streets, marketed to the expanding middle classes. The poet John Betjeman later celebrated the area in poems and in his 1973 BBC documentary Metro-Land, and the district's character has become a byword for interwar suburban England.
