Karl Ludwig von Bertalanffy (1901–1972) was an Austrian biologist and one of the founders of general systems theory.

Work

Bertalanffy proposed that organisms should be understood as open systems exchanging matter and energy with their environment, maintaining a steady state rather than equilibrium. Beginning in the 1930s he developed this into a general systems theory, set out in his book General System Theory (1968), seeking principles common to systems in biology, psychology, engineering and the social sciences. He also derived the von Bertalanffy growth function, still used in fisheries science to model organism growth.
