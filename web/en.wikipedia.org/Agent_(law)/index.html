Agent (law)

Agency (law)
AreaCommon law, commercial law
Key partiesPrincipal, agent, third party

In law, an agent is a person who is legally empowered to act on behalf of another person or entity, called the principal. The body of rules governing this relationship is the law of agency. An agent who acts within the scope of authority granted by the principal can bind the principal in dealings with third parties.

Authority

An agent's authority may be actual — either express, granted directly, or implied from the agent's position — or apparent (ostensible), arising where the principal's conduct leads a third party reasonably to believe the agent is authorized. The doctrine of ratification allows a principal to adopt acts performed without prior authority.

Duties

Agents owe their principals fiduciary duties, including loyalty, obedience to lawful instructions, reasonable care, and an obligation to account for money and property received. Principals, in turn, generally owe agents compensation, reimbursement, and indemnification for authorized acts.

Liability

Under doctrines such as respondeat superior, a principal or employer may be liable for torts committed by an agent within the scope of the agency or employment.
