Tie-in

A tie-in work is a work of fiction or other product based on a media property such as a film, video game, television series, comic book, or board game, or vice versa. Tie-ins are authorized, licensed derivative works, usually produced to promote or capitalize on the popularity of the original property and to provide additional revenue.

Forms

Common forms of tie-in include:

  • Novelizations – prose adaptations of films or episodes, often based on the shooting script and sometimes containing scenes not in the finished film.
  • Tie-in novels – original novels set in the universe of a series, such as the extensive lines of Star Trek and Star Wars books.
  • Video games based on films and television series, traditionally released to coincide with the source work.
  • Comic books and graphic novels extending films and TV shows, and adaptations in the opposite direction.
  • Merchandise tie-ins – toys, apparel, and fast-food promotions connected to a media release.

Publishing

Tie-in fiction is a significant segment of the publishing industry. Writers are typically hired under work-for-hire or licensing contracts and must work within a "series bible" and approval process of the licensor, which constrains which characters can change or die and how the shared universe develops. The International Association of Media Tie-In Writers (IAMTW) presents the Scribe Awards for tie-in fiction.

Relation to canon

Tie-ins usually have an ambiguous relationship to the canon of the original work. Some franchises maintain tiers of canonicity, and tie-in material may be contradicted or decanonized by later installments of the primary work; other productions, such as tie-in comics created with the showrunners' involvement, may be treated as fully canonical.
