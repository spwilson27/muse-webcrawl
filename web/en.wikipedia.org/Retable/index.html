Retable

A retable is a frame or shelf enclosing decorated panels — painted, carved, or enamelled — placed on or above and behind an altar in a church. Retables developed in the medieval period as altarpieces grew from simple frontals into elaborate multi-panelled structures, sometimes with hinged wings that could be opened on feast days.

Development

Early examples date from the 12th and 13th centuries; among the most famous is the Pala d'Oro in St Mark's Basilica, Venice, an opulent work of gold and enamel. In northern Europe, carved wooden retables from Antwerp and Brussels workshops were exported widely in the 15th and 16th centuries. The Westminster Retable (c. 1270s) is a rare surviving English example.

Terminology

The term overlaps with reredos, the larger architectural screen behind the altar; a retable is properly the framed panel structure on the altar itself, though art-historical usage varies.
