The Battle of Ankara, also known as the Battle of Angora, was fought on 28 July 1402 on the plain of Çubuk, north-east of Ankara in Anatolia, between the forces of the Ottoman Sultan Bayezid I and the Central Asian conqueror Timur (Tamerlane). It ended in a decisive Timurid victory and the capture of the sultan himself.

Background

By 1400 Bayezid had built a large empire in Anatolia and the Balkans, but his annexation of Turkish beyliks in Anatolia drove their rulers to seek Timur's protection. Timur, fresh from campaigns in Persia, Syria, and India, demanded the surrender of fugitives and, after a long exchange of insulting letters, invaded Anatolia and besieged Ankara. Bayezid raised the siege of Constantinople and marched east to meet him.

The battle

Timur manoeuvred his army to occupy the Ottomans' former camp, cutting Bayezid's troops off from water. Exhausted by a forced march, the Ottoman army gave battle. Timur's wings, including war elephants brought from India, pressed the Ottoman flanks; crucially, the Tatar auxiliaries in Bayezid's army defected to Timur, and many Anatolian troops deserted when they saw their dispossessed beys fighting on Timur's side. By evening the Ottoman army collapsed, and Bayezid was captured while trying to escape.

Aftermath

Bayezid died in captivity in 1403. With no sultan, the Ottoman state fragmented among his sons in the Ottoman Interregnum (1402–1413), delaying Ottoman expansion for a generation and briefly reprieving the Byzantine Empire. Timur went on to sack cities across Anatolia before turning east toward China, where he died in 1405.
