K-pop (Korean: 케이팝; short for Korean popular music) is a popular music genre originating in South Korea, characterised by a wide variety of audiovisual elements. It encompasses idol groups and solo artists performing highly produced songs that blend pop, hip hop, R&B, electronic dance music, and other styles, delivered with synchronised choreography, sophisticated music videos, and strong visual concepts. Since the 2010s K-pop has become a global cultural phenomenon and a flagship of the Korean Wave (Hallyu).

History

South Korean popular music developed through the 20th century under Japanese and American influence, including U.S. military entertainment during and after the Korean War. The modern era is usually dated to 1992, when Seo Taiji and Boys performed a rap-and-dance song on national television, transforming the industry. Agencies founded in the mid-1990s — SM Entertainment (1995), JYP Entertainment, and YG Entertainment — built the "idol" model with groups such as H.O.T., S.E.S., and Fin.K.L. The Asian financial crisis of 1997 prompted the South Korean government to promote cultural exports as an economic strategy.

The idol system

Major agencies scout children and teenagers, who train for years in singing, dance, languages, and media skills under contract before debuting. Groups are marketed with assigned member "positions" (leader, main vocalist, rapper, visual, maknae), fandom names, official colours, and light sticks. The system produces polished performers but has been criticised over long contracts, mental-health pressures, and control of artists' private lives — prompting legal reforms such as the 2009 standard contract rules following a lawsuit by members of TVXQ.

Global breakthrough

Psy's "Gangnam Style" (2012) became the first YouTube video to reach one billion views. BTS topped the Billboard 200 from 2018 and the Hot 100 with "Dynamite" (2020), while Blackpink headlined major Western festivals. K-pop albums became some of the world's best-selling records by the early 2020s, and streaming, social media, and organised fandoms drove sustained global growth.

Criticism and cultural role

Commentators praise K-pop's production quality and its role in South Korean soft power and tourism, while debating its formulaic tendencies, the treatment of trainees, and fans' sometimes intrusive behaviour. The South Korean government treats the genre as a strategic cultural industry.
