Cane (glassworking)

In glassworking, a cane is a rod of glass with color embedded in its cross-section, or running along its length, used to add intricate pattern and color to blown or lampworked glass. Canes are a fundamental building block of Venetian and studio glass techniques.

Making canes

A cane begins as a gather of molten glass layered with one or more colors, often encased in clear glass. The gather is attached to a second punty and drawn out by two glassworkers walking apart, stretching it into a long thin rod sometimes tens of metres long, which is then cut into sections. Twisting during the draw produces spiral patterns; layering and reheating complex gathers produce patterned cross-sections that remain consistent along the cane's length.

Types

Simple canes carry a single color. Latticino and reticello work uses fine white or colored canes arranged in lattices or networks. Zanfirico canes are twisted in alternating directions to form lace-like spirals. Slices of patterned cane, called murrine, are used in millefiori work; when a gather is pulled with a shaped cross-section, the resulting cane can reproduce letters, stars, or figures.

Use

Canes are picked up on a gather of hot glass, fused onto vessels in stripes or grids, or incorporated into paperweights and beads. In lampworking, thin canes are used to draw details directly in the flame. Murano glassmakers of Venice perfected cane techniques during the Renaissance, and the methods remain central to contemporary studio glass.
