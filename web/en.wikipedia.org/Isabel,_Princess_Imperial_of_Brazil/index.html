Isabel, Princess Imperial of Brazil

Isabel, Princess Imperial of Brazil (29 July 1846 – 14 November 1921), nicknamed "the Redemptress", was the elder daughter of Emperor Pedro II of Brazil and Empress Teresa Cristina, and thus a member of the Brazilian branch of the House of Braganza. After the deaths of her two brothers, she became her father's heir presumptive and served as regent of the Empire on three occasions.

Life

Isabel was born in Rio de Janeiro and was declared heiress to the throne as a child. She was educated rigorously under tutors appointed by her father. In 1864 she married Prince Gaston, Count of Eu, a French prince; the couple had three sons. As regent during Pedro II's travels and illnesses, Isabel presided over the final abolition of slavery in Brazil.

Abolition of slavery

On 13 May 1888, Isabel signed the Golden Law (Lei Áurea), which abolished slavery in Brazil, the last Western country to do so. Curiously, she did not attend the mass of thanksgiving for abolition because she was recovering after miscarrying. Pope Leo XIII awarded her a Golden Rose for her role in abolition.

Fall of the monarchy and exile

The abolition, which alienated slave-owning landowners, contributed to the military coup that proclaimed a republic on 15 November 1889. Isabel went into exile in France with her family, and lived quietly near Paris. She died in 1921 at the Château d'Eu in Normandy. In 1971 her remains were repatriated to Brazil and reinterred in the Imperial Mausoleum at Petrópolis.
