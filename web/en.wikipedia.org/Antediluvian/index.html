Antediluvian

Antediluvian (from Latin ante, "before", and diluvium, "flood") refers in biblical terms to the period between the creation of Adam and the global flood described in the Book of Genesis — that is, everything before the time of Noah. In general usage the word has come to mean anything very old, old-fashioned or outmoded, often humorously.

In the Bible

Genesis chapters 1–6 cover the antediluvian age, in which the patriarchs from Adam to Noah are said to have lived extraordinarily long lives: Adam 930 years, Seth 912, Methuselah 969 — the longest lifespan recorded in the Bible. The period ends with the corruption of mankind and God's decision to send the Flood, from which only Noah, his family and the animals of the ark survive.

Other uses

In nineteenth-century geology, "antediluvian" was applied to deposits and creatures thought to predate a supposed universal deluge; the term fell out of scientific use as geology matured. In the fiction of H. P. Lovecraft and other writers, the word evokes vast, primordial antiquity. In everyday speech, calling a practice or machine "antediluvian" implies it is hopelessly outdated.
