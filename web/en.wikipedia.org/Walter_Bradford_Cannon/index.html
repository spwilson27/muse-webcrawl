Walter Bradford Cannon

Walter Bradford Cannon (October 19, 1871 – October 1, 1945) was an American physiologist, professor, and chairman of the Department of Physiology at Harvard Medical School. He coined the term "fight or flight response" and popularised the concept of homeostasis — the maintenance of steady internal conditions — in his book The Wisdom of the Body (1932). He also pioneered the use of X-ray contrast studies of digestion.

Early work on digestion

As a Harvard undergraduate and medical student in the 1890s, Cannon used the newly discovered X-rays with bismuth meals to watch peristalsis in animals, founding the radiological study of the gastrointestinal tract. His monograph The Mechanical Factors of Digestion (1911) summarised this work; he later showed that emotional states strongly affect digestive movements, a bridge to his work on the autonomic nervous system.

Fight or flight and homeostasis

In Bodily Changes in Pain, Hunger, Fear and Rage (1915), Cannon described the coordinated sympathetic–adrenal response that prepares an animal for emergency action. Building on Claude Bernard's notion of the milieu intérieur, he developed the concept of homeostasis and the organisation of physiological self-regulation, including the Cannon–Bard theory of emotion, which located emotional processing in the diencephalon rather than (as the James–Lange theory held) in bodily feedback alone.

Later life

Cannon studied traumatic shock during the First World War, was a leading figure in American medicine and international scientific cooperation, and campaigned on behalf of refugee scholars in the 1930s. His ideas remain foundational to physiology, stress research, and systems biology.
