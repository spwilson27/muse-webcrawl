BAFTA

The British Academy of Film and Television Arts (BAFTA) is an independent British arts charity that supports, develops and promotes the art forms of the moving image — film, television and video games — in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1947 as the British Film Academy, it merged in 1958 with the Guild of Television Producers and Directors to form the Society of Film and Television Arts, before becoming BAFTA in 1976.

Awards ceremonies

BAFTA is best known for its annual awards ceremonies, held to recognise excellence in British and international screen work. The British Academy Film Awards, commonly called the BAFTAs, honour the best films of the year and are regarded as the British equivalent of the Academy Awards. Separate ceremonies cover television (the British Academy Television Awards), television craft, children's media, and video games (the BAFTA Games Awards, first held in 2004).

The mask

The BAFTA award trophy is a theatrical mask designed by American sculptor Mitzi Cunliffe and commissioned in 1955. The mask, cast in phosphor bronze, has become one of the most recognisable symbols of achievement in British film and television.

Membership and work

Beyond its awards, BAFTA runs an extensive programme of learning events, masterclasses, scholarships and initiatives intended to bring new talent into the screen industries and to identify and support excellence at every stage of a career. Its headquarters are at 195 Piccadilly in London.
