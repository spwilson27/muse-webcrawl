Igor Sikorsky

Born: 25 May 1889, Kyiv, Russian Empire
Died: 26 October 1972, Easton, Connecticut, United States
Known for: First successful single-rotor helicopter; large multi-engine aircraft

Igor Ivanovich Sikorsky (25 May 1889 – 26 October 1972) was a Russian-American aviation pioneer in both helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft. He designed and flew the world's first multi-engined fixed-wing aircraft, the Russky Vityaz, in 1913, and the first airliner, the Ilya Muromets, in 1914. After emigrating to the United States in 1919, he founded the Sikorsky Aero Engineering Corporation and developed the VS-300, the first viable American helicopter, which pioneered the single main rotor with tail rotor configuration used by most helicopters today.

Early life and career in Russia

Sikorsky was born in Kyiv, then part of the Russian Empire. He studied at the Kyiv Polytechnic Institute and, inspired by the work of Leonardo da Vinci and the Wright brothers, began experimenting with aircraft designs as a young man. By 1912 he was chief designer of the aviation division of the Russian Baltic Railroad Car Works in Saint Petersburg, where he built the four-engined Russky Vityaz and the Ilya Muromets bomber series used in the First World War.

Career in the United States

Sikorsky emigrated to the United States in 1919, fleeing the Russian Revolution. After working as a schoolteacher and lecturer, he founded his aircraft company in 1923 with support from backers including the composer Sergei Rachmaninoff. His S-42 flying boat became famous as the Pan Am Clipper. In 1939 his VS-300 made its first tethered flight, and the design led directly to the R-4, the first mass-produced helicopter, in 1942.

Legacy

Sikorsky Aircraft, the company he founded, remains a major helicopter manufacturer. Sikorsky was a deeply religious man and wrote several works on philosophy and religion, including The Message of the Lord's Prayer.
