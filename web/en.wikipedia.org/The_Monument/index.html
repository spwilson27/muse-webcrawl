The Monument is a fluted Doric column in London, England, situated near the northern end of London Bridge. Commemorating the Great Fire of London, it celebrates the rebuilding of the City. Standing 202 feet (62 metres) tall, it is the tallest isolated stone column in the world. It was designed by Dr Robert Hooke and Sir Christopher Wren and built between 1671 and 1677.

History

The Monument stands on the site of St Margaret, New Fish Street, the first church destroyed by the Great Fire. Its height equals the distance from its base to the baker's shop in Pudding Lane where the fire began. The column is topped by a gilded urn of fire. A spiral staircase of 311 steps inside leads to a viewing platform; an underground chamber was designed as a zenith telescope and gravity laboratory for the Royal Society, reflecting Hooke's scientific interests.

Inscriptions

Latin inscriptions on the base describe the fire, the destruction it caused, and the rebuilding under Charles II. Anti-Catholic wording blaming "the treachery and malice of the Popish faction" was added originally and finally removed in 1830–31. The allegorical sculpture on the pedestal, by Caius Gabriel Cibber, shows Charles II directing the reconstruction.

Visitor information

The Monument is a Grade I listed building and a popular visitor attraction; a certificate is traditionally given to those who climb its 311 steps. The surrounding square was pedestrianised as part of the Monument Yard development.
