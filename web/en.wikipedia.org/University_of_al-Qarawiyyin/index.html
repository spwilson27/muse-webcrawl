The University of al-Qarawiyyin (Arabic: جامعة القرويين; also written al-Karaouine or al-Qarawiyin) is a university located in Fez, Morocco. It was founded as a mosque and madrasa in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri, the daughter of a wealthy merchant from Kairouan (in modern Tunisia). UNESCO and Guinness World Records recognize it as the oldest existing, continually operating educational institution in the world.

History

Fatima al-Fihri used her inheritance to build the al-Qarawiyyin mosque — named for the community of Kairouan immigrants in Fez — with an associated teaching institution. Under the Almoravid, Almohad and Marinid dynasties the complex was repeatedly enlarged; the Marinids added student housing and made it the centre of a network of madrasas. Its library, founded in 1349, is among the world's oldest and holds thousands of manuscripts, including a 9th-century Qur'an and works of Maliki law and astronomy.

Scholars associated with al-Qarawiyyin include the geographer Muhammad al-Idrisi, the philosopher Ibn Rushd (Averroes, by tradition), the historian Ibn Khaldun, the mathematician Ibn al-Banna, and the traveller Leo Africanus; in the medieval period, non-Muslim students such as the future Pope Sylvester II were traditionally said to have studied in Fez. Teaching centred on Qur'anic exegesis, Maliki jurisprudence, Arabic grammar, theology, logic, mathematics and astronomy, with degrees (ijaza) granted by individual professors.

Modern era

In 1963 the madrasa was formally reorganized as a state university with faculties of Islamic law (sharia), Arabic language and literature, theology and comparative religion, taught in Fez, Tetouan, Marrakesh and Agadir. It remains distinct from Morocco's modern research universities, concentrating on classical Islamic and Arabic studies. The mosque, which can hold some 20,000 worshippers, continues to function as a place of prayer; the restored library reopened to researchers in the 2010s.
