Bundle branch block

Bundle branch block
SpecialtyCardiology
TypesRight bundle branch block (RBBB), left bundle branch block (LBBB)
DiagnosisElectrocardiogram (ECG): QRS duration ≥ 120 ms

A bundle branch block is a defect of the bundle branches or fascicles in the electrical conduction system of the heart. The heart's ventricles are normally activated rapidly and nearly simultaneously through the right and left bundle branches of the His–Purkinje system. When one branch is blocked, the corresponding ventricle is depolarized late, by slower cell-to-cell spread from the other ventricle, producing a widened QRS complex (≥120 ms) on the electrocardiogram with characteristic shapes.

Types

Right bundle branch block (RBBB) shows an rSR′ ("M-shaped") pattern in lead V1 and a wide slurred S wave in lateral leads; it can occur in healthy people but also with atrial septal defect, pulmonary embolism, and right heart strain. Left bundle branch block (LBBB) shows broad monophasic R waves in lateral leads and deep S or QS complexes in V1; it more often signals underlying structural heart disease such as hypertension, aortic stenosis, cardiomyopathy, or coronary artery disease. Blocks of the left fascicles (hemiblocks) cause axis deviation without much QRS widening.

Causes and significance

Causes include ischaemic heart disease, hypertension, cardiomyopathies, valvular disease, congenital heart disease, myocarditis, and age-related fibrosis of the conduction system (Lenègre's and Lev's diseases). New LBBB with chest pain was historically treated as a heart-attack equivalent, though modern criteria (such as the Sgarbossa criteria) refine ECG diagnosis of infarction in LBBB. Management focuses on the underlying cause; cardiac resynchronization therapy (biventricular pacing) benefits selected patients with LBBB and heart failure.
