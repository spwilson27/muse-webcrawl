Drone metal

Drone metal or drone doom is a style of heavy metal that melds the slow tempos and heaviness of doom metal with the sustained, resonant tones of drone music. It is characterised by extremely long songs built on heavily distorted, down-tuned guitars, feedback, minimal or absent drums and vocals, and an emphasis on volume and texture over melody or rhythm.

History

The style emerged in the early 1990s with Earth, whose album Earth 2: Special Low Frequency Version (1993) is considered foundational, alongside Boris and the Melvins' slower work. In the 2000s, Sunn O))) — named in homage to Earth's amplifier brand — brought drone metal wider attention with dense, physically overwhelming live performances played in robes through walls of amplifiers; other notable acts include Khanate, Nadja, and Jesu. Labels such as Southern Lord were central to the scene.

Characteristics

Guitars are typically tuned far below standard pitch, played slowly with heavy sustain, reverb and distortion so that chords blur into standing waves of sound; pieces may run 10 to 30 minutes or longer. Performances emphasise the physical sensation of sound pressure. The genre overlaps with post-metal, dark ambient and doom metal, and draws on influences from La Monte Young's minimalist drones to Black Sabbath.
