Skibbereen (Irish: An Sciobairín, "little boat harbour") is a town in County Cork, Ireland. Located on the River Ilen, it is the commercial centre of the West Cork region and one of the principal towns on the Wild Atlantic Way. Much of the town was built in the nineteenth century, and it is known both for its maritime heritage and for its central place in the history of the Great Famine.

History

A settlement existed at Skibbereen by the early 1600s. Before 1600, most of the land belonged to the MacCarthy Reagh dynasty. The town received a charter in 1675 and developed as a port and market town.

Skibbereen became one of the places most severely affected by the Great Famine of 1845–1852. The Skibbereen Poor Law Union recorded some of the highest mortality in Ireland; contemporary accounts, including the famous 1847 Illustrated London News sketches by James Mahoney, made "Skibbereen" a byword for famine suffering. The mass burial site at Abbeystrewry commemorates thousands of victims. The town is also the subject of the well-known folk song "Dear Old Skibbereen", written from the viewpoint of an emigrant.

In the later nineteenth century the town was a centre of nationalist politics; Jeremiah O'Donovan Rossa, a founder of the Irish Republican Brotherhood, came from nearby Rosscarbery, and the Skibbereen Eagle newspaper became famous for its 1898 editorial claiming to keep "an eye on the Czar of Russia".

Today

Skibbereen is a market and service centre for agriculture and tourism in West Cork, with a famous weekly farmers' market and the Skibbereen Heritage Centre, whose exhibitions cover the Famine and nearby Lough Hyne, Ireland's first marine nature reserve. The town hosts an arts festival and serves as a base for Baltimore, Sherkin Island and Cape Clear.
