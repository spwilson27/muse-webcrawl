Karuṇā

Sanskritकरुणा (karuṇā)
MeaningCompassion
ContextsBuddhism, Hinduism, Jainism
Associated figureAvalokiteshvara, bodhisattva of compassion

Karuṇā (Sanskrit and Pali: करुणा) is a key spiritual concept in the Indian religions, usually translated as compassion: the heartfelt wish, arising from empathy with the suffering of others, that they be freed from that suffering — coupled with the resolve to act to relieve it.

In Buddhism

In Buddhism, karuṇā is one of the four "divine abidings" (brahmaviharas), along with loving-kindness (mettā), sympathetic joy (muditā), and equanimity (upekkhā), and is cultivated through meditation directed toward all beings. In Mahayana Buddhism compassion, united with wisdom (prajñā), is the defining virtue of the bodhisattva, epitomised by Avalokiteshvara (Guanyin, Kannon), who hears the cries of the world. The Dalai Lama has described compassion as the core of his teaching. Karuṇā is distinguished from pity and from mere grief at others' pain: its "near enemy" is sorrow, its "far enemy" cruelty.

In other traditions

In Jainism, compassion toward all living beings is inseparable from non-violence (ahimsa), and karuṇā is one of the four reflections (bhavanas) that restrain harmful karma. In Hindu texts, compassion (dayā, karuṇā) is praised as a divine quality and a duty of the righteous, and the devotional traditions celebrate God's compassion toward devotees.
