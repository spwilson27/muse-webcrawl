Siege of Metz (1870)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The siege of Metz (19 August – 27 October 1870) was a battle of the Franco-Prussian War in which the French Army of the Rhine under Marshal François Achille Bazaine was encircled in the fortress of Metz by Prussian and allied German forces under Prince Friedrich Karl of Prussia. It ended with the surrender of some 170,000 French troops — the largest capitulation of the war — and was a decisive blow to France.

Background

After defeats at the frontier and the battles of Mars-la-Tour and Gravelotte (16–18 August 1870), Bazaine's army — the largest French field force — fell back into the Metz fortifications instead of retreating toward Châlons and Paris. The Germans completed their investment on 19 August. Meanwhile Napoleon III and Marshal MacMahon's relief army were themselves trapped and destroyed at Sedan on 1 September.

The siege

Bazaine made limited breakout attempts, notably at Noisseville (31 August – 1 September) and Bellevue (7 October), but remained largely passive, conserving his army partly for political purposes: the marshal hoped to use it as a bargaining counter in negotiations, possibly to restore the Empire after the fall of Napoleon III and the proclamation of the Republic. Conditions inside Metz deteriorated — food ran out, horses were slaughtered, disease spread, and rain turned the camps to mud.

Surrender and aftermath

Bazaine capitulated on 27 October 1870, surrendering three marshals, some 6,000 officers and about 170,000 men, along with hundreds of guns and the fortress itself. The released German Second Army could then move against the French armies of the Loire. Bazaine was widely condemned as a traitor in France; after the war he was court-martialled in 1873, sentenced to death (commuted to imprisonment), and escaped into exile. Metz was annexed to the German Empire under the Treaty of Frankfurt and remained German until 1918.
