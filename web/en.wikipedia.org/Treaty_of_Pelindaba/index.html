Treaty of Pelindaba

Signed11 April 1996
LocationCairo, Egypt
Effective15 July 2009

The Treaty of Pelindaba, formally the African Nuclear Weapon Free Zone Treaty, establishes a nuclear-weapon-free zone across the African continent. It is named after Pelindaba, South Africa's main nuclear research centre, where South Africa's former nuclear weapons were dismantled. The treaty text was adopted in 1995 and the treaty was opened for signature in Cairo on 11 April 1996.

Provisions

Parties undertake not to research, develop, manufacture, stockpile, acquire, test, or possess nuclear explosive devices, and not to permit their stationing on their territory. The treaty also prohibits attacks on nuclear installations in the zone, requires parties to apply International Atomic Energy Agency safeguards to their peaceful nuclear activities, and addresses the dumping of radioactive waste. It created the African Commission on Nuclear Energy (AFCONE) to oversee compliance.

Status

The treaty entered into force on 15 July 2009 upon deposit of the 28th instrument of ratification. Protocols open to the nuclear-weapon states commit them not to use or threaten to use nuclear weapons against parties to the treaty.
