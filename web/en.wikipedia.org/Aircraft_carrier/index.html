Aircraft carrier

An aircraft carrier is a warship that serves as a seagoing airbase, equipped with a full-length flight deck and facilities for carrying, arming, deploying, and recovering aircraft. Typically the capital ship of a fleet, a carrier allows a naval force to project air power worldwide without depending on local bases for staging aircraft operations.

Types

Carriers vary greatly in size and configuration. Large "supercarriers" of the United States Navy displace around 100,000 tonnes and operate catapult-launched fixed-wing aircraft (CATOBAR); others use ski-jump ramps for short take-off operations (STOBAR or STOVL). Smaller carriers may operate mainly helicopters and are classed as helicopter carriers or amphibious assault ships.

History

Aircraft carriers emerged in the First World War and proved decisive in the Second World War, especially in the Pacific at battles such as Midway. In the postwar era the carrier became the centrepiece of American naval strategy, and today roughly a dozen nations operate carriers of some form, including the United States, China, the United Kingdom, France, India, Italy, and Japan.

Carrier battle groups

Carriers rarely sail alone: they operate at the centre of a carrier strike group of cruisers, destroyers, submarines, and support ships providing defence and logistics.
