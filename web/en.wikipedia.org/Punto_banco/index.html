Punto banco

TypeCard game (comparing)
FamilyBaccarat
Cards6–8 decks
ChanceVery high; no player decisions after betting

Punto banco is the most common form of baccarat played in casinos in North America, the United Kingdom, Australia, and Macau. Unlike the older European variants chemin de fer and baccarat banque, in punto banco the casino banks the game and the drawing rules are fixed: the outcome depends purely on the cards dealt, and the player makes no decisions after placing a bet.

Play

Bettors wager on one of three outcomes: that the punto (player) hand wins, that the banco (banker) hand wins, or that the hands tie. Two cards are dealt to each hand. Card values are counted with tens and face cards worth zero and aces worth one; only the units digit of the total counts, so the highest possible total is nine. Fixed tableau rules determine whether a third card is drawn to either hand.

House edge

Because the banker hand wins slightly more often than the player hand, winning banker bets pay a commission, traditionally five percent. The house edge is roughly 1.06 percent on banker bets and 1.24 percent on player bets, while the tie bet carries a much larger edge, making punto banco one of the lowest-edge table games in the casino when played on the main bets.

Popularity

Punto banco dominates high-stakes casino play in Macau, where baccarat generates the majority of gaming revenue, and it is a staple of VIP rooms worldwide. The game gained cultural prominence through its association with James Bond, although Bond's game in the novels was chemin de fer.
