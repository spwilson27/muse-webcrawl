Karuṇā

Karuṇā (Sanskrit: करुणा; Pali: karuṇā) is generally translated as "compassion" or "mercy" and is a central virtue in Buddhism, Hinduism and Jainism. It signifies the heartfelt wish that others be free from suffering, together with a readiness to act to relieve that suffering.

In Buddhism

Karuṇā is one of the four brahmavihāras (divine abidings), along with mettā (loving-kindness), muditā (sympathetic joy) and upekkhā (equanimity). In Mahāyāna Buddhism, karuṇā is the defining quality of the bodhisattva, who postpones final liberation in order to help all beings. The bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara is the embodiment of compassion.

Together with prajñā (wisdom), karuṇā forms one of the two wings of the Mahāyāna path; the two are said to be inseparable, like the two wings of a bird. The cultivation of compassion is practised through meditation, including meditation on the suffering of others and the aspiration to take it upon oneself, as in the Tibetan practice of tonglen.

In Hinduism and Jainism

In Hinduism, karuṇā appears in texts such as the Bhagavad Gita as one of the divine qualities, and compassion toward all beings is emphasised as part of dharma. In Jainism, compassion for all living beings (jīva-dayā) is closely connected with the vow of ahiṃsā (non-violence).
