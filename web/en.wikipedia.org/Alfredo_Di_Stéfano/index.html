Alfredo Di Stéfano

Alfredo Stéfano Di Stéfano Laulhé (4 July 1926 – 7 July 2014) was an Argentine professional footballer and coach, widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time. Nicknamed "Saeta Rubia" ("the Blond Arrow"), he was the central figure of the Real Madrid team that dominated European football in the 1950s, winning the first five European Cups consecutively (1956–1960) and scoring in every final.

Alfredo Di Stéfano with Real Madrid in the 1950s.

Career

Born in Buenos Aires of Italian descent, Di Stéfano began at River Plate, then starred for Millonarios of Bogotá during Colombia's "El Dorado" period. A disputed transfer in 1953 took him to Real Madrid rather than Barcelona, transforming the Spanish club's fortunes. With Madrid he won eight La Liga titles and five European Cups, and twice won the Ballon d'Or (1957, 1959). Unusually, he played international football for three countries: Argentina, Colombia (unofficially), and Spain.

Style and legacy

Di Stéfano was a complete forward who defended, created, and scored, commanding the whole pitch. Pelé and many historians rank him among the top few players in history. He later coached, leading Valencia to a European Cup Winners' Cup in 1980, and served as honorary president of Real Madrid until his death in 2014.
