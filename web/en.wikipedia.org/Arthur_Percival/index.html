Arthur Percival

Born: 26 December 1887, Aspenden, Hertfordshire

Died: 31 January 1966, London

Rank: Lieutenant-General

Commands: Malaya Command (1941–1942)

Lieutenant-General Arthur Ernest Percival, CB, DSO & Bar, OBE, MC, DL (26 December 1887 – 31 January 1966) was a British Army officer best known as the commander who surrendered Singapore to the Japanese in February 1942 — the largest capitulation in British military history.

Early career

Percival volunteered for service in 1914 and rose rapidly during the First World War, winning the Military Cross and the Distinguished Service Order and ending the war as a brevet lieutenant-colonel after being severely wounded at the Somme. Between the wars he served in Ireland during the War of Independence, in Russia, and on the staff, and he studied Japanese intentions in Malaya, where he served as chief of staff in the late 1930s.

Malaya and Singapore

Appointed General Officer Commanding Malaya in 1941, Percival faced the Japanese invasion of December 1941 with under-equipped and dispersed forces. General Tomoyuki Yamashita's troops advanced rapidly down the Malay Peninsula, and after the fall of the island's reservoirs and heavy fighting, Percival surrendered Singapore on 15 February 1942. Some 80,000 British, Indian and Australian troops became prisoners of war.

Captivity and later life

Percival spent the rest of the war as a prisoner, latterly in Manchuria, and was present aboard USS Missouri for the Japanese surrender in 1945. He retired from the army in 1946, wrote his account The War in Malaya (1949), and worked for prisoner-of-war charities. Historians continue to debate how far the disaster was his fault and how far it resulted from failures by his superiors in London and by pre-war policy.
