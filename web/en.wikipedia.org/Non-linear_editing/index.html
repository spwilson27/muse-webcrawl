Non-linear editing

Non-linear editing (NLE) is a method of editing video (and audio) in which footage is accessed randomly and modifications do not destroy the source material, in contrast to the linear, tape-to-tape editing of earlier decades. Editors assemble clips on a timeline, rearrange them freely, and apply effects non-destructively before rendering the final output.

History

Early computer-based offline systems of the 1970s and 1980s, such as the CMX 600 and EditDroid, pioneered the concept. Avid's Media Composer (1989) and later Adobe Premiere, Apple's Final Cut Pro, and DaVinci Resolve made non-linear editing the industry standard as storage and processing became affordable, eventually reaching consumer and mobile devices.

Workflow

Media is ingested (often with lower-resolution proxies), organized, and edited in sequences; colour correction, sound mixing, and titles are integrated in the same environment. Non-destructive workflows preserve original files and allow multiple versions of a cut.
