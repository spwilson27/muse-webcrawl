The Royal Gorge is a canyon of the Arkansas River near Cañon City in Fremont County, Colorado. Carved into Precambrian granite, the gorge is roughly 10 miles (16 km) long and reaches a depth of about 1,250 feet (380 m), with near-vertical walls in its narrowest reaches. It is sometimes called the "Grand Canyon of the Arkansas".

Geology

The canyon began forming about three million years ago as regional uplift raised the surrounding plateau and the Arkansas River cut downward into hard granite and gneiss roughly 1.4 billion years old. The river, which rises near Leadville in the Sawatch Range, exits the gorge at Cañon City onto the plains of eastern Colorado.

The railroad war

In 1878–79 the gorge was the scene of the Royal Gorge War, a sometimes violent contest between the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad and the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway for the only feasible rail route through the canyon toward the mining boomtown of Leadville. Crews occupied the gorge with armed camps and rival gradings until a federal court settlement and the 1880 "Treaty of Boston" awarded the route to the Denver and Rio Grande. Its line, clinging to the canyon floor and passing beneath the famous Hanging Bridge, still carries the Royal Gorge Route Railroad excursion trains.

Bridge and park

The Royal Gorge Bridge, completed in 1929, crosses the canyon at 956 feet (291 m) above the river and was the world's highest suspension bridge until 2001. Together with an incline railway, aerial gondolas and a surrounding park operated by the city of Cañon City, it anchors a major tourist attraction. A wildfire in June 2013 burned much of the park and damaged the bridge's wooden deck, but the bridge survived and the park was rebuilt and reopened in 2014–15.

The river through the gorge is also a popular whitewater rafting reach, with rapids rated up to class V at high water.
