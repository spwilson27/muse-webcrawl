Baxter State Park is a large wilderness area permanently preserved as a state park in the North Maine Woods in Penobscot and Piscataquis counties in north-central Maine, in the United States. It covers approximately 209,644 acres (848 km²) and is centered on Mount Katahdin, the highest mountain in Maine at 5,267 feet (1,605 m) and the northern terminus of the Appalachian Trail.

The park was created by Percival Proctor Baxter, a former governor of Maine, who purchased the land in stages beginning in 1930 and deeded it to the state over a period of three decades. Baxter insisted that the land be kept forever wild, and the park is administered by the Baxter State Park Authority under the terms of his deeds of trust rather than by the ordinary state park system.

History

Percival Baxter served as governor of Maine from 1921 to 1925. After failing to persuade the state legislature to appropriate funds to buy Mount Katahdin, Baxter decided to purchase the mountain himself. In 1930 he bought a 6,000-acre parcel including Katahdin's summit from the Great Northern Paper Company for $25,000, and deeded it to the state in 1931. Between 1931 and 1962 he completed 28 parcels, acquiring more than 200,000 acres at a personal cost of roughly $3 million.

Baxter also left trust funds — ultimately about $7 million — to maintain the park, and his deeds imposed strict conditions: the land must remain "forever wild," animals must be protected from hunting and trapping (fishing is permitted on some waters), and no roads suitable for automobiles were to reach the mountain itself. The park does not charge Maine residents an entrance fee, though non-residents pay a per-vehicle fee, and use is capped by a limited number of campsites and parking reservations.

Geography and ecology

The park is mountainous, with a series of peaks flanking Katahdin, including Hamlin Peak (4,756 feet), the second-highest summit in Maine. Its terrain was carved by Pleistocene glaciation, leaving glacial cirques such as the Great Basin, tarns, moraines, and the famous Knife Edge, a narrow arête connecting Katahdin's summit to Pamola Peak.

Wildlife includes moose, black bear, Canada lynx, white-tailed deer, and more than 150 species of birds. The forests are predominantly spruce–fir, with northern hardwoods at lower elevations and alpine tundra above the treeline around 3,500–4,000 feet. Scientific Forestry Management Area lands in the northwest of the park — added after Baxter's death — are the one part of the park where timber harvesting is permitted.

Recreation

The park contains 215 miles (346 km) of trails and 337 campsites, including lean-tos, tent sites, and bunkhouses at campgrounds such as Roaring Brook, Abol, Katahdin Stream, Nesowadnehunk, and Chimney Pond. There are no paved roads, stores, or running water within the park, and visitors must carry out all refuse. Climbing Katahdin in winter requires advance registration. The park is open for camping from May 15 to October 15, with day use continuing until weather closes the roads.
