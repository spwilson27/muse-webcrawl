Chinese ceramics

Chinese ceramics range from early Neolithic pottery to the refined porcelain that China exported worldwide and that gave porcelain its common English name, "china".

Development

Pottery was made in China by 18,000 BCE. Painted Yangshao pottery, Han dynasty tomb figures, Tang sancai three-colour ware and Song dynasty celadons and other monochrome glazes mark successive peaks. Jingdezhen became the porcelain capital, producing blue-and-white ware under the Mongol Yuan dynasty using cobalt imported from Persia.

Technology

True porcelain, made from kaolin and petuntse fired above 1300°C, is white, hard and translucent. Imperial kilns produced ware for the court, marked with reign marks, while vast quantities were exported along the Silk Road and by sea, shaping taste in Islamic and European ceramics.

Influence

Chinese ceramics inspired Islamic pottery, European Delftware and Meissen porcelain, and remain central to the decorative arts.
