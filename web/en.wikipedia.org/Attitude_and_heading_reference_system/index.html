Attitude and heading reference system

A compact AHRS unit providing three-axis orientation data.

An attitude and heading reference system (AHRS) consists of sensors on three axes that provide attitude information for aircraft, including roll, pitch and yaw. These are sometimes referred to as MARG (Magnetic, Angular Rate, and Gravity) sensors and consist of either solid-state or microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) gyroscopes, accelerometers and magnetometers. They are designed to replace traditional mechanical gyroscopic flight instruments.

Description

AHRS differ from inertial navigation systems mainly in that they use an onboard processor to provide attitude and heading information, typically at high update rates, rather than full navigation solutions. Sensor fusion algorithms, commonly based on the Kalman filter, combine gyroscope, accelerometer and magnetometer data so that the high-frequency response of the gyroscopes is corrected by the long-term references of gravity (for pitch and roll) and the Earth's magnetic field (for heading).

The principal advantage of AHRS over older attitude-heading reference units is the elimination of moving parts through MEMS technology, which reduces size, weight, power consumption and cost while improving reliability. Modern AHRS units may incorporate GPS aiding to improve heading accuracy and to compensate for magnetic disturbances.

Applications

AHRS are widely used in general aviation glass cockpits, unmanned aerial vehicles, helicopters, marine stabilisation systems, robotics, virtual-reality motion tracking and antenna pointing. In aircraft certified for instrument flight, AHRS-derived attitude information drives primary flight displays and standby instruments.
