Holocaust denial

Holocaust denial is the assertion that the genocide of European Jews during World War II by Nazi Germany and its collaborators did not occur as historically documented, or did not occur at all. Holocaust denial is rejected by historians and is widely regarded as a form of antisemitism and historical negationism.

Claims and refutation

Deniers claim, among other things, that gas chambers did not exist, that death tolls were inflated, or that deaths resulted only from disease and wartime hardship. These claims are contradicted by an extensive body of evidence including Nazi documents, camp records, photographs, perpetrator testimony, survivor accounts, and physical remains of camps.

Legal status

Holocaust denial is a criminal offence in a number of countries, particularly in Europe, while in the United States it is protected speech under the First Amendment. Historians distinguish denial from legitimate scholarly revision of details, which works within the evidence.
