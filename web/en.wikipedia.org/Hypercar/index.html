Hypercar

Hypercar
The highest tier of high-performance sports cars, above supercars, characterised by extreme power, advanced technology and limited production.

A hypercar is a term used to designate the highest-performing and most exclusive class of sports cars, ranked above supercars. Hypercars are typically produced in very limited numbers, employ state-of-the-art — often racing-derived — technology, and command prices in the millions of dollars.

Characteristics

Although there is no formal definition, hypercars generally exceed 1,000 horsepower (or approach it closely), reach top speeds above 350 km/h, and make extensive use of carbon-fibre monocoque construction and active aerodynamics. The "holy trinity" of 2013 — the hybrid McLaren P1, Ferrari LaFerrari and Porsche 918 Spyder — established the template, pairing V8 or V12 engines with electric motors in hybrid powertrains.

Notable examples

  • Bugatti Veyron and Chiron — quad-turbocharged W16 cars that pushed top speeds beyond 400 km/h.
  • Koenigsegg Jesko and Regera — Swedish hypercars noted for extreme power-to-weight ratios.
  • Rimac Nevera — an all-electric hypercar with four motors producing about 1,900 hp, holder of numerous acceleration records.
  • Aston Martin Valkyrie and Mercedes-AMG One — cars bringing Formula One-derived engines and aerodynamics to the road.

Criticism

Commentators have debated whether "hypercar" is a meaningful category or marketing inflation of the supercar label; the term is also used in endurance racing, where the Le Mans Hypercar class became the top category of the World Endurance Championship in 2021.
