Complete game

A starting pitcher who finishes a game is credited with a complete game

In baseball, a complete game (denoted by CG) is the act of a pitcher pitching an entire game without the benefit of a relief pitcher. A pitcher who meets this criterion will be credited with a complete game regardless of the number of innings actually played — a game may be shortened by rain or, in some leagues, a mercy rule, and extra-inning games extend beyond nine innings.

History

In the early decades of professional baseball, complete games were the norm: pitchers were expected to finish what they started. Cy Young holds the career record with 749 complete games. During the 1900s, leading pitchers routinely completed more than 30 games per season.

Decline

The frequency of complete games has fallen steadily with the growth of specialized bullpens, pitch counts, and analytic strategies that favor relief pitchers after a lineup has seen a starter multiple times. By the 1980s, season leaders typically recorded about 10 complete games; in the 21st century, entire teams may record only a handful per season, and a league leader often has just two to five. A complete game shutout — in which the pitcher allows no runs — is correspondingly rarer and more celebrated.

Related statistics

The complete game is tabulated alongside games started (GS) for starting pitchers. Related achievements include the no-hitter and the perfect game, which are almost always also complete games.
