Miguel Ángel Virasoro (physicist)

Born9 May 1940, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Died23 July 2021 (aged 81)
NationalityArgentine / Italian
Known forVirasoro algebra, Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude

Miguel Ángel Virasoro (9 May 1940 – 23 July 2021) was an Argentine-Italian theoretical physicist best known for his foundational work in string theory. The Virasoro algebra — an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra central to two-dimensional conformal field theory and string theory — is named after him.

Career

Virasoro studied at the University of Buenos Aires. Political turmoil in Argentina, including the 1966 "Night of the Long Batons," drove him abroad; he worked in Israel, the United States, and eventually settled in Italy, becoming a professor at Sapienza University of Rome. From 1995 to 2002 he directed the International Centre for Theoretical Physics (ICTP) in Trieste, succeeding Abdus Salam.

Scientific contributions

In 1970, studying dual resonance models, Virasoro introduced the subsidiary conditions generated by an infinite set of operators, leading to the Virasoro algebra and the Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude describing closed-string scattering. His later work ranged across gauge theories, statistical mechanics, and complex systems, including spin glasses and neural networks.

Legacy

The Virasoro algebra and its central extension underpin modern conformal field theory, the AdS/CFT correspondence, and much of string theory. Virasoro is remembered both for these contributions and for championing physics in the developing world during his ICTP directorship.
