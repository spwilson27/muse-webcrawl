The Scandinavian Peninsula is a peninsula of northwestern Europe, occupied mainly by Norway and Sweden, together with the northwestern part of Finland. At roughly 770,000 square kilometres it is one of the largest peninsulas in Europe, extending from the Barents Sea in the north to the Skagerrak and Kattegat in the south. The name derives from Scandinavia, the cultural and linguistic region of the Scandinavian peoples.

Geography

The western and northern coasts are mountainous and deeply indented by fjords carved during the ice ages, while the eastern coast along the Baltic Sea is lower and smoother. The Scandinavian Mountains (Scandes) run along the peninsula's spine, containing its highest peak, Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m) in Norway, and Sweden's Kebnekaise. Southern Sweden is a lowland of lakes and forests, including Vänern and Vättern, the largest lakes of the peninsula.

Geology

Much of the peninsula sits on the Baltic Shield, an ancient craton of Precambrian rock among the oldest in Europe. Glaciers of the last ice age scoured the shield and depressed the land; the region is still undergoing post-glacial rebound, rising by up to about a centimetre per year around the Gulf of Bothnia.

Climate and population

Thanks to the North Atlantic Current, the Norwegian coast has a milder climate than other regions at the same latitude, while inland areas have a continental subarctic climate. The peninsula's population of roughly 21 million is concentrated in the south; the far north lies within the Arctic Circle and includes the traditional homeland of the Sámi people.
