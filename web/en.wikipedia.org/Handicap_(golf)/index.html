Handicap (golf)

A golf handicap is a numerical measure of a golfer's potential ability, used to enable players of different abilities to compete against one another on an equitable basis. Lower handicaps indicate better players; a "scratch" golfer has a handicap of zero.

World Handicap System

Since 2020, most of the world has used the World Handicap System (WHS), developed by the USGA and The R&A. A Handicap Index is calculated from the best 8 of the player's most recent 20 score differentials, where a differential adjusts the gross score for the course rating and slope rating of the tees played. The index converts to a course handicap for each specific course and set of tees.

Use in play

In stroke play, net scores are found by subtracting the handicap from the gross score; in match play, strokes are given on the hardest holes as ranked by the stroke index. Systems include safeguards against manipulation, such as exceptional score reductions and caps.

History

Handicapping developed in Scotland and England in the 19th century and was formalised by national unions in the 20th century, with several regional systems (USGA, CONGU, EGA and others) preceding the unified WHS.
