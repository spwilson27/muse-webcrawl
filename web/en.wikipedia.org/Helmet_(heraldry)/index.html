Helmet (heraldry)

A heraldic helmet surmounting a shield, with mantling and a crest.

In heraldry, a helmet is a component of a coat of arms that is placed above the shield and beneath the crest. The style of helmet shown varies with the rank of the armiger and with the heraldic tradition of the country concerned, and in many traditions the type and orientation of the helmet are strictly prescribed indicators of rank.

History

Heraldic helmets derive from the actual helmets worn in medieval tournaments and battle. The earliest heraldic achievements simply showed the shield; by the 14th century, the helmet, with its crest and the flowing cloth mantling attached to it, had become a standard part of the full achievement of arms. Great helms, tilting helms (frog-mouthed helms used for jousting), and later barred helmets and close helmets all appear in armorial depictions.

Rank distinctions

In British heraldry, for example, the sovereign and princes of the blood use a gold barred helmet facing forwards; peers use a silver barred helmet with gold bars in profile; knights and baronets use a steel helmet with the visor raised, facing forwards; and esquires and gentlemen use a steel closed helmet in profile. In German and Scandinavian traditions, the number of helmets above the shield could multiply as arms were combined by inheritance, with each helmet carrying its own crest.

Components

  • Mantling — the cloth drapery flowing from the helmet, conventionally shown in the principal colours of the arms.
  • Torse — a twisted wreath of cloth, usually in the main metal and colour of the arms, placed on the helmet to hold the crest.
  • Crest — the device borne on top of the helmet.
  • Coronet — in some traditions, a coronet of rank may be placed between the helmet and the crest.

Ecclesiastical heraldry

In Roman Catholic ecclesiastical heraldry, the helmet is replaced by a wide-brimmed hat called a galero, whose colour and number of tassels denote the cleric's rank, because clergy were considered not to take part in battle or tournaments.
