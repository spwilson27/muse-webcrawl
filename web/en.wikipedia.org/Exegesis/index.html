Exegesis

Exegesis (from the Greek exēgēsis, from exēgeisthai, "to lead out") is a critical explanation or interpretation of a text, particularly a religious text. Traditionally the term was used primarily for work with the Bible, but in modern usage it can refer to critical interpretation of any text, including religious scriptures in other traditions, and increasingly of non-religious texts as well.

Methods and distinctions

Exegesis involves a range of critical disciplines: textual criticism, to establish the original wording; historical and literary analysis, to understand context and genre; study of the original languages (Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek for biblical texts); and comparison with related ancient writings. Exegesis is conventionally contrasted with eisegesis — reading one's own ideas into a text — and with hermeneutics, the broader theory of interpretation of which exegesis is the practice. In Roman Catholic tradition, scholars distinguish four senses of Scripture: literal, allegorical, moral (tropological), and anagogical, a scheme rooted in medieval scholarship.

Other traditions

In Islam, tafsir is the discipline of Qur'anic exegesis, ranging from linguistically oriented commentaries to Sufi allegorical interpretation. Jewish exegesis includes the midrashic tradition and the medieval commentators Rashi, Ibn Ezra, and Nachmanides, whose works are printed alongside the biblical text in standard editions. Hindu, Buddhist, and Confucian traditions likewise possess long commentarial lineages interpreting foundational scriptures and classics.
