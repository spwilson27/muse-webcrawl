Popocatépetl (Spanish pronunciation: [popokaˈtepetɬ]) is an active stratovolcano in the states of México, Puebla and Morelos, in Central Mexico, and lies in the eastern half of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt. At 5,400 m (17,717 ft) it is the second highest peak in Mexico, after Citlaltépetl (Pico de Orizaba) at 5,636 m (18,491 ft).

It is linked to the twin volcano of Iztaccihuatl to its northwest by the high saddle known as the Paso de Cortés. Popocatépetl lies 70 km (43 mi) southeast of Mexico City, from which it is regularly visible on clear days. It is one of the most active volcanoes in Mexico, having had more than 15 major eruptions since the arrival of the Spanish in 1519.

Name and legend

The name Popocatépetl comes from the Nahuatl words Popōca ("it smokes") and tepētl ("hill, mountain"), thus meaning "smoking hill". In Aztec mythology, Popocatépetl was a warrior who loved the princess Iztaccihuatl; according to legend, he returned from battle to find her dead of grief after she was falsely told he had died, and he carried her body to the mountains, where the gods turned them both into volcanoes.

Geology and eruptive history

Popocatépetl is an andesitic-dacitic stratovolcano built by repeated eruptions over at least 730,000 years. Its modern cone was built within the caldera of an earlier volcano destroyed by a giant lateral blast roughly 23,000 years ago. The volcano entered a new eruptive phase in December 1994 after 67 years of quiet; a lava dome grew within the summit crater, and ash emissions have continued intermittently ever since, with periodic stronger explosions in 2000, 2013, 2016, 2019 and 2023. The 1994 resumption led to the evacuation of tens of thousands of residents from nearby villages.

Monitoring

The volcano is monitored continuously by Mexico's Centro Nacional de Prevención de Desastres (CENAPRED) together with the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM). A volcanic alert level system, the Semáforo de Alerta Volcánica, communicates risk to the roughly 25 million people who live within view of the volcano and in the surrounding region.
