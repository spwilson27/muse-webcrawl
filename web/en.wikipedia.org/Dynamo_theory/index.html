Dynamo theory

Dynamo theory proposes that a rotating, convecting, electrically conducting fluid can maintain a magnetic field over astronomical timescales. It is the leading explanation for the magnetic fields of the Earth, the Sun and other planets and stars.

Mechanism

In the Earth's case, convection in the liquid iron-nickel outer core, organized by the planet's rotation (the Coriolis effect), stretches and twists magnetic field lines, amplifying the field and offsetting its natural decay — a self-exciting dynamo analogous to an electric generator.

History

The idea was first proposed by Joseph Larmor in 1919 to explain the Sun's magnetic field, and developed by Walter Elsasser and Edward Bullard for the Earth. Numerical simulations since the 1990s have reproduced key features of the geomagnetic field, including polarity reversals.

Anti-dynamo theorems, such as Cowling's theorem, show that certain symmetric motions cannot sustain a field, constraining possible dynamo models.
