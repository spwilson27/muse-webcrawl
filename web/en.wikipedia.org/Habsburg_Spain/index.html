Habsburg Spain refers to the history of Spain during the 16th and 17th centuries (1506–1700) when it was ruled by kings of the House of Habsburg. Under the Spanish Habsburgs, Spain reached the peak of its influence and power, controlling a vast overseas empire and for a time the political, military and cultural life of Europe — the Spanish Golden Age.

Rise

The Habsburg inheritance came through the marriage of Philip the Handsome to Joanna of Castile, daughter of the Catholic Monarchs. Their son Charles I (the Holy Roman Emperor Charles V) inherited Spain, the Spanish Americas, Naples and Sicily, the Low Countries and the imperial title. Under Charles and his son Philip II, Spain also governed Portugal and its empire during the Iberian Union (1580–1640).

Zenith and strain

American silver financed Spanish armies across Europe, but repeated state bankruptcies, revolts in the Netherlands, war with England (including the defeat of the Spanish Armada in 1588), and conflict with France strained the monarchy. The seventeenth century saw a gradual decline of Spanish power, punctuated by the Thirty Years' War and the loss of Portugal.

End of the line

The physically and mentally infirm Charles II died childless in 1700, ending the Spanish Habsburg line and triggering the War of the Spanish Succession, which brought the Bourbon Philip V to the throne.
