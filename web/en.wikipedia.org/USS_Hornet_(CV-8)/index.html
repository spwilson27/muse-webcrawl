USS Hornet (CV-8)

USS Hornet (CV-8), the seventh ship to carry the name, was a Yorktown-class aircraft carrier of the United States Navy. Launched on 14 December 1940 by Newport News Shipbuilding and commissioned on 20 October 1941, she became one of the most famous American carriers of the Pacific War despite a career of barely one year.

Service history

On 18 April 1942, Hornet launched sixteen U.S. Army Air Forces B-25 Mitchell bombers under Lieutenant Colonel James H. Doolittle in the Doolittle Raid, the first American air operation to strike the Japanese home islands, which lifted Allied morale after Pearl Harbor. She then fought at the Battle of Midway in June 1942, where her torpedo squadron, VT-8, was annihilated attacking the Japanese fleet — Ensign George Gay was its sole survivor — while her dive bombers helped sink the cruiser Mikuma. After the carrier Wasp was sunk, Hornet became for a time the only operational American carrier in the South Pacific.

At the Battle of the Santa Cruz Islands on 26 October 1942, Japanese dive bombers and torpedo planes heavily damaged Hornet; with fires raging and the ship dead in the water, she was abandoned and scuttled by American destroyers, though Japanese destroyers finally sank her early on 27 October. She received four battle stars. Her wreck was located in January 2019 by the research vessel Petrel, resting more than 5,300 meters (17,400 ft) deep near the Solomon Islands.
