Harmonic number

In mathematics, the n-th harmonic number is the sum of the reciprocals of the first n natural numbers:

Hn = 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + ⋯ + 1/n

Starting from n = 1, the sequence of harmonic numbers begins 1, 3/2, 11/6, 25/12, 137/60, ... The harmonic numbers are named for their relationship to the harmonic series, which diverges, so Hn grows without bound — but only logarithmically: Hn ≈ ln n + γ, where γ ≈ 0.57721 is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

Identities

The harmonic numbers satisfy many identities, including integral representations such as Hn = ∫01 (1 − xn)/(1 − x) dx, and generating functions linking them to the digamma function: Hn = ψ(n + 1) + γ. Sums involving harmonic numbers appear throughout combinatorics and number theory; for example, the expected number of records in a random sample and the expected number of trials in the coupon collector's problem are given by harmonic numbers.

Generalizations

The generalized harmonic numbers Hn,m are the sums of the reciprocals raised to the m-th power, ∑ 1/km; as n → ∞ they converge to the Riemann zeta function ζ(m) for m > 1. Harmonic numbers can also be extended to real and complex arguments through the digamma function, giving the "harmonic numbers" Hx for non-integer x.

Applications

Harmonic numbers appear in the analysis of algorithms (notably quicksort, whose expected number of comparisons is 2(n + 1)Hn − 4n), in the harmonic series of music, in the physics of the "book stacking problem" (the maximum overhang of stacked blocks grows as half a harmonic number), and in various results in analytic number theory.
