Baby Bells

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Baby Bells were the seven Regional Bell Operating Companies (RBOCs) created by the breakup of AT&T's Bell System, effective January 1, 1984, under the antitrust settlement known as the Modified Final Judgment. The seven were Ameritech, Bell Atlantic, BellSouth, NYNEX, Pacific Telesis, Southwestern Bell, and US West. They provided local telephone service in their regions while AT&T retained long-distance and equipment businesses.

Consolidation

After the Telecommunications Act of 1996 loosened restrictions, the Baby Bells merged with one another and with independent companies in a wave of consolidation. Southwestern Bell, renamed SBC Communications, acquired Pacific Telesis and Ameritech, and in 2005 bought the original AT&T itself, taking its name. Bell Atlantic merged with NYNEX and then with GTE to form Verizon. BellSouth was acquired by the new AT&T in 2006, and US West passed through Qwest to CenturyLink (now Lumen).

Legacy

By the 2000s the seven Baby Bells had effectively recombined into two dominant descendants, AT&T and Verizon, alongside Lumen — a reconfiguration often cited in debates about whether the 1984 breakup endured. The term "Baby Bell" survives in business history for the regional companies and their role in the era of local-exchange competition, the growth of mobile telephony (each RBOC also held cellular assets) and the early commercial Internet.
