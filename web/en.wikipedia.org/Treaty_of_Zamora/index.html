The Treaty of Zamora was a peace agreement concluded on 5 October 1143 at Zamora, in the Kingdom of León, between Alfonso VII, king of León and Castile, and his cousin Afonso Henriques, count of Portugal. It is traditionally regarded as the founding act of Portuguese independence: Alfonso VII recognised Afonso Henriques as king of Portugal, and the County of Portugal thereby became the Kingdom of Portugal.

Background

Afonso Henriques had ruled the County of Portugal since 1128, when he defeated his mother Theresa of León at the Battle of São Mamede near Guimarães. He campaigned southward against the Moors, winning the celebrated victory at the Battle of Ourique in 1139, after which — according to later tradition — his troops acclaimed him king. Attempts to settle the question militarily with Alfonso VII, including the inconclusive encounters around Arcos de Valdevez (1140–1141), led to negotiations. The meeting at Zamora was mediated by the papal legate, Cardinal Guido de Vico, and the archbishop of Braga, João Peculiar, was a key figure in securing recognition.

Terms and recognition

Under the treaty, Alfonso VII recognised the royal title of Afonso Henriques (rex Portugalensium) and the independence of the new kingdom, while Afonso accepted a loosely defined vassalage or "asturian" fealty to León that in practice did not restrict Portuguese self-rule — later reinterpreted when Afonso offered vassalage directly to the Holy See as a papal fief in 1143, paying an annual census. Formal papal recognition of Afonso as king came in 1179, when Pope Alexander III issued the bull Manifestis Probatum, acknowledged as the definitive international recognition of Portuguese independence.

Legacy

The treaty marks the origin of Portugal as a sovereign state, one of Europe's oldest continuing nations, and its consequences unfolded over the following century as Portuguese kings conquered southward to the Algarve (1249) and fixed borders with Castile at the Treaty of Alcañices in 1297 — often described as Europe's oldest unaltered frontier. The date of Zamora is commemorated in Portugal, and Guimarães, Afonso's birthplace and first capital, celebrates its role as the "cradle of the nation".
