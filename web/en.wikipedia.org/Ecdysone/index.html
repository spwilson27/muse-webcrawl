Ecdysone

Ecdysone
Type: Steroid hormone (ecdysteroid)
Function: Controls moulting in arthropods
Produced by: Prothoracic glands (insects)
Active form: 20-hydroxyecdysone

Ecdysone is a steroid hormone that controls moulting (ecdysis) and metamorphosis in arthropods. It was first isolated from silkworm pupae in 1954 by Adolf Butenandt and Peter Karlson, who obtained 25 milligrams of pure hormone from half a tonne of pupae.

Function

In insects, ecdysone is secreted by the prothoracic glands in response to prothoracicotropic hormone (PTTH) from the brain. It is converted in peripheral tissues to the more active 20-hydroxyecdysone, which binds the ecdysone receptor (a nuclear receptor acting with ultraspiracle) and switches on cascades of gene expression that drive the moult. The interplay between ecdysone pulses and juvenile hormone determines whether a larva moults to another larval stage or metamorphoses.

Related compounds

Related steroids, the phytoecdysteroids, occur in many plants, where they may deter insect predators. Ecdysone agonists such as tebufenozide are used commercially as insecticides.
