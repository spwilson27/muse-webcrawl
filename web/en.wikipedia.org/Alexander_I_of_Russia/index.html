Alexander I (23 December 1777 – 1 December 1825) was Emperor of Russia from 1801 until his death in 1825. The eldest son of Paul I and grandson of Catherine the Great, he came to the throne after his father's assassination, in which his own complicity has long been debated. His reign spanned the Napoleonic Wars, during which Russia survived French invasion and emerged as the dominant land power in Europe.

Reign

Raised by Catherine under the Swiss tutor Frédéric-César de La Harpe, the young Alexander began his reign with liberal reforms — reorganising the government into ministries, founding universities, and relaxing censorship — while privately contemplating a constitution and the abolition of serfdom through his Unofficial Committee. Abroad, he oscillated between war and alliance with Napoleon: defeated at Austerlitz in 1805 and Friedland in 1807, he made peace at Tilsit, but relations collapsed, leading to the French invasion of 1812. The destruction of the Grande Armée in Russia made Alexander the liberator of Europe; he entered Paris in 1814 and dominated the Congress of Vienna, while championing the conservative Holy Alliance.

Later years and death

In his final decade Alexander turned toward religious mysticism and reaction, entrusting government to the harsh minister Arakcheyev and his system of military colonies. He died suddenly at Taganrog in December 1825 — his unexpected death and the disputed succession precipitated the Decembrist revolt — and a persistent legend held that he had faked his death to live as the hermit Fyodor Kuzmich in Siberia.
