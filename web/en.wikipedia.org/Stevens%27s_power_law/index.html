Stevens's power law

Stevens's power law is a proposed relationship in psychophysics between the magnitude of a physical stimulus and the intensity or strength that people perceive. Proposed by Stanley Smith Stevens in 1957, it states that perceived intensity is proportional to the stimulus raised to a power: ψ(I) = k·Ia, where the exponent a depends on the sensory modality.

Exponents

For loudness the exponent is about 0.67 (a doubling of perceived loudness for each 10 dB increase); for brightness about 0.5 for a point source; for electric shock about 3.5, so perceived pain grows much faster than the stimulus. Exponents below 1 indicate compressive response, above 1 an expansive one.

Comparison with Fechner

Stevens's law supersedes Fechner's logarithmic law for many continua and is supported by magnitude estimation and cross-modality matching experiments, though its generality remains debated.
