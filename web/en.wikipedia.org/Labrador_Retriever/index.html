The Labrador Retriever or simply Labrador is a British breed of retriever gun dog. It was developed in the United Kingdom from St. John's water dogs imported from Newfoundland (now part of Canada) and was named after the Labrador region of that colony. It is among the most commonly kept dogs worldwide and was the most popular breed by registration in the United States for 31 consecutive years, from 1991 to 2022.

History

The breed descends from working water dogs used by fishermen on Newfoundland, where the smaller St. John's dog retrieved lines, nets and fish from the cold North Atlantic. British sportsmen imported the dogs in the nineteenth century and refined them as game retrievers on shooting estates; the Earl of Malmesbury, the Duke of Buccleuch and others established kennels whose lines form the basis of the modern breed. The Kennel Club recognised the Labrador in 1903.

Characteristics

Labradors are medium-large, strongly built dogs with a broad head, kind expression, webbed toes and a distinctive thick, tapering "otter tail" used as a rudder when swimming. The dense double coat is water-repellent. They are famed for an even, friendly temperament, intelligence and trainability, making them popular family companions and working dogs.

Roles

Beyond gundog work, Labradors serve as guide dogs for the blind — the majority of guide dogs worldwide are Labradors or Labrador crosses — as well as in search and rescue, detection work (explosives, narcotics, contraband), therapy and assistance roles. They are prone to hip and elbow dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy and obesity, so screening and weight management are important.
