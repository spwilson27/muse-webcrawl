The Philadelphia Orchestra is an American symphony orchestra based in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1900, it is one of the "Big Five" American orchestras and is internationally renowned for the rich, warm string sound cultivated under its long-serving music directors.

History

The orchestra was founded in 1900 by Fritz Scheel, who served as its first conductor. Leopold Stokowski, music director from 1912 to 1941, made it world famous through innovative programming, free bowing techniques and early recordings and films, including Disney's Fantasia (1940). Eugene Ormandy, his successor until 1980, consolidated the celebrated "Philadelphia sound" and made hundreds of recordings.

Later music directors included Riccardo Muti, Wolfgang Sawallisch, Christoph Eschenbach and Charles Dutoit. Yannick Nézet-Séguin became music director in 2012. The orchestra performed for many decades at the Academy of Music before moving to the Kimmel Center's Verizon Hall (renamed Marian Anderson Hall in 2024) in 2001. It gives its summer season at the Mann Center and in Saratoga Springs.

Recordings and firsts

The orchestra made the first commercial electrical recording in 1925, gave the first American performance of many major works — including Mahler's Eighth Symphony and Rachmaninoff premieres — and in 1973 became the first American orchestra to tour the People's Republic of China.
