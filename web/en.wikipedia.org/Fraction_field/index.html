Fraction field

Fraction field
FieldAbstract algebra (ring theory)
NotationFrac(R), Quot(R), K(R)
ExampleFrac(ℤ) = ℚ

In abstract algebra, the field of fractions (or fraction field) of an integral domain is the smallest field in which it can be embedded. It consists of all fractions a/b with a, b in the domain and b ≠ 0, generalising the construction of the rational numbers ℚ from the integers ℤ.

Construction

Given an integral domain R, consider pairs (a, b) with b ≠ 0, modulo the equivalence relation (a, b) ~ (c, d) when ad = bc. Addition and multiplication are defined as for ordinary fractions: (a/b) + (c/d) = (ad + bc)/bd and (a/b)(c/d) = ac/bd. The integral domain property guarantees these operations are well defined and that the result is a field.

Universal property

The fraction field is characterised by a universal property: any injective ring homomorphism from R into a field factors uniquely through the natural embedding of R into its fraction field. This makes the construction a functor from integral domains to fields, left adjoint to the forgetful functor.

Examples

The fraction field of ℤ is ℚ. The fraction field of a polynomial ring F[x] over a field F is the field of rational functions F(x). For a field itself, the fraction field is isomorphic to the field.
