Anthony Perkins (April 4, 1932 – September 12, 1992) was an American actor and singer. He is best known for playing Norman Bates in Alfred Hitchcock's Psycho (1960) and its three sequels — a role that defined his career and became one of the most iconic in film history.

Early life

Perkins was born in New York City, the son of stage and film actor Osgood Perkins and Janet Esselstyn Rane. His father died when Anthony was five. He attended Brooks School in Massachusetts, Buckingham Browne & Nichols School in Cambridge, and studied at Rollins College and Columbia University. He made his film debut in The Actress (1953) alongside Spencer Tracy.

Career

Perkins earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actor for Friendly Persuasion (1956), and starred in Fear Strikes Out (1957), The Tin Star (1957) and Green Mansions (1959). In 1960 Hitchcock cast him as the shy, murderous motel keeper Norman Bates in Psycho; the performance won international acclaim and a lasting place in popular culture, though it also typecast him in America. In the 1960s he worked extensively in Europe, winning the Best Actor award at Cannes for Goodbye Again (1961) and playing Joseph K. in Orson Welles's The Trial (1962).

Later films included Pretty Poison (1968), Catch-22 (1970), The Life and Times of Judge Roy Bean (1972), Murder on the Orient Express (1974) and Crimes of Passion (1984). He reprised Norman Bates in Psycho II (1983), Psycho III (1986, which he also directed) and Psycho IV: The Beginning (1990). With Charles Winecoff's biography Split Image and extensive stage work, including the original Broadway production of Look Homeward, Angel, his career also spanned theatre and a parallel career as a pop singer in the 1950s.

Personal life and death

Perkins married photographer Berry Berenson in 1973; they had two sons, actor Osgood Perkins and musician Elvis Perkins. Perkins was bisexual and had relationships with men and women throughout his life. He died of AIDS-related pneumonia on 12 September 1992 at his home in Los Angeles, aged 60. His widow, Berry Berenson, died aboard American Airlines Flight 11 in the September 11 attacks of 2001.
