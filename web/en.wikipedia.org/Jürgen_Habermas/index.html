Jürgen Habermas

Jürgen Habermas
Born: June 18, 1929, Düsseldorf, Germany
Fields: Philosophy, sociology, political theory
School: Frankfurt School; critical theory
Known for: Public sphere; communicative rationality

Jürgen Habermas (born June 18, 1929) is a German philosopher and sociologist in the tradition of critical theory and pragmatism. A leading figure of the second generation of the Frankfurt School, he is widely regarded as one of the most influential living philosophers, known above all for his concepts of the public sphere and communicative action.

Career

Habermas studied at Göttingen, Zurich, and Bonn, completing his doctorate in 1954. He worked as an assistant to Theodor W. Adorno at the Institute for Social Research in Frankfurt, then held chairs at Heidelberg and Frankfurt, and directed the Max Planck Institute for the Study of the Conditions of Life in the Scientific-Technical World at Starnberg from 1971 to 1983 before returning to Frankfurt.

Major ideas

His first major work, The Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere (1962), traced the rise and decline of a bourgeois public sphere in which private persons debated matters of common concern. In The Theory of Communicative Action (1981), he developed an account of rationality grounded in speech oriented toward mutual understanding, distinguishing communicative action from strategic action and diagnosing modern society's pathologies as a "colonization of the lifeworld" by markets and administrative systems. Between Facts and Norms (1992) extended these ideas into a discourse theory of law and deliberative democracy. Habermas has also been a prominent public intellectual in debates over German memory (the Historikerstreit), European integration, and religion in post-secular society.
