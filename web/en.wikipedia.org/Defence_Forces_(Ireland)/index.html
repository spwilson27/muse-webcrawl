Defence Forces (Ireland)

The Defence Forces (Irish: Fórsaí Cosanta, officially styled Óglaigh na hÉireann) are the armed forces of Ireland. They consist of the Irish Army, the Air Corps, the Naval Service, and the Reserve Defence Forces, which include the Army Reserve and the Naval Service Reserve. The Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces is the President of Ireland, while day-to-day military authority rests with the Minister for Defence and the Chief of Staff.

History

The Defence Forces trace their origins to the Irish Volunteers and the Irish Citizen Army of the revolutionary period, and were formally established in 1924 following the Irish Civil War. During the Second World War — known in Ireland as "the Emergency" — the forces expanded greatly even though Ireland remained neutral. Irish troops have a long record of service with the United Nations; since 1958 Ireland has maintained a continuous presence on UN peacekeeping operations, including long-serving deployments in Lebanon with UNIFIL and earlier missions in the Congo, where Irish soldiers fought at the Siege of Jadotville in 1961.

Roles

The primary roles of the Defence Forces include the defence of the State against armed aggression, aiding the civil power (supporting the Garda Síochána, Ireland's police service), participating in multinational peace support and humanitarian operations, and carrying out fishery protection in Irish waters. The Naval Service operates patrol vessels from its base at Haulbowline in County Cork, while the Air Corps, headquartered at Casement Aerodrome, Baldonnel, provides air support, maritime patrol and air ambulance services.

Organisation

The Army is organised into two brigades, with headquarters in Dublin and Cork, together with the Defence Forces Training Centre at the Curragh Camp in County Kildare. Personnel numbers are comparatively small — on the order of several thousand permanent personnel supported by reservists — reflecting Ireland's policy of military neutrality and its lack of participation in mutual defence alliances such as NATO, though Ireland cooperates with partner countries through programmes such as NATO's Partnership for Peace.
