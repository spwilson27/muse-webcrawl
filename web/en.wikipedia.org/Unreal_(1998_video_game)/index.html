Unreal is a first-person shooter video game developed by Epic MegaGames and published by GT Interactive in May 1998. It was powered by an in-house game engine, also called the Unreal Engine, which later became one of the most widely licensed game engines in the industry. The game was supported by the downloadable Unreal Mission Pack I: Return to Na Pali, and the multiplayer-focused Unreal Tournament followed in 1999.

Plot

The player takes the role of Prisoner 849, a convict aboard the prison spacecraft Vortex Rikers, which crash-lands on the planet Na Pali. The planet's peaceful inhabitants, the Nali, are enslaved by the technologically advanced and brutal Skaarj. The prisoner fights across the planet's surface, mines, temples and castles in search of a way to escape, aided by a universal translator that reveals the Nali's writings.

Gameplay

Unreal is a first-person shooter in which the player explores large, predominantly outdoor environments and fights enemies including the Skaarj, Krall and Mercenaries. The arsenal includes the Dispersion Pistol, Stinger, ASMD shock rifle, Eightball grenade launcher, Flak Cannon and Razorjack. Artificial intelligence was a major selling point: enemies dodge, retreat and take cover. Multiplayer modes include deathmatch, team deathmatch and cooperative play, with computer-controlled bots.

Development

Work began in 1994, with James Schmalz of Digital Extremes creating early concept work and Epic's Tim Sweeney writing the engine, while Cliff Bleszinski served as lead designer. The game drew attention at its release for its large open levels, coloured lighting, volumetric fog and reflective surfaces, at a time when it competed with id Software's Quake II.

Reception

Unreal was a commercial and critical success, praised for its graphics, atmosphere, music (by Alexander Brandon and Michiel van den Bos) and enemy AI, while its high system requirements and initial multiplayer netcode were criticised. It spawned a long-running series and established the Unreal Engine licensing business.
