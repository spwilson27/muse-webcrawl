The Queen Elizabeth class is a class of two aircraft carriers built for the United Kingdom's Royal Navy: HMS Queen Elizabeth and HMS Prince of Wales. They are the largest warships ever built for the Royal Navy, displacing about 65,000 tonnes, and form the centrepiece of the UK Carrier Strike Group. The ships were constructed in sections at yards around Britain and assembled at Rosyth Dockyard in Scotland.

Design

The carriers use a short take-off and vertical landing (STOVL) configuration with a ski-jump bow rather than catapults, operating the F-35B Lightning II stealth fighter. A distinctive twin-island layout separates ship navigation (forward island) from air operations control (aft island). Integrated electric propulsion, powered by two Rolls-Royce MT30 gas turbines and diesel generators, drives twin shafts; a highly automated design allows a ship's company of under 700, rising with the air group embarked.

Service

HMS Queen Elizabeth was commissioned in December 2017 and led the UK Carrier Strike Group's inaugural operational deployment (CSG21) to the Indo-Pacific in 2021, embarking British and US Marine Corps F-35Bs. HMS Prince of Wales was commissioned in 2019 and has served as a NATO flagship and as a trials and contingency carrier.
