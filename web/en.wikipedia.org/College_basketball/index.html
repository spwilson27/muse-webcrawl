College basketball

College basketball is basketball played by student-athletes at colleges and universities. In the United States it is governed principally by the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA), and is one of the most popular spectator sports in the country, culminating each spring in the NCAA Division I men's and women's basketball tournaments, popularly known as "March Madness".

History

Basketball was invented in 1891 by James Naismith at Springfield, Massachusetts, and spread rapidly through colleges and YMCAs. The first intercollegiate games were played in the 1890s. The NCAA tournament began in 1939, growing over the decades into a 68-team single-elimination event whose television rights are worth billions of dollars. Historic programmes include UCLA, which won ten championships in twelve years under John Wooden, Kentucky, Duke, North Carolina, Kansas, Indiana and UConn.

Rules and structure

College rules differ from the NBA: men's games are played in two 20-minute halves (the women's game uses four quarters), the shot clock is 30 seconds, and the three-point line and foul rules differ slightly. Teams compete within conferences during the regular season, followed by conference tournaments and national post-season play. Since 2021, student-athletes in the US may profit from their name, image and likeness (NIL), a change that has significantly altered recruiting and the economics of the sport.
