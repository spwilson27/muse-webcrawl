Dihydrofolate reductase

Dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) is an enzyme (EC 1.5.1.3), encoded in humans by the DHFR gene, that reduces dihydrofolic acid to tetrahydrofolic acid using NADPH. Tetrahydrofolate derivatives carry one-carbon units needed for synthesis of purines, thymidylate and certain amino acids, making DHFR essential for cell growth and proliferation.

EC number1.5.1.3
GeneDHFR (chromosome 5, human)
ReactionDihydrofolate + NADPH → Tetrahydrofolate + NADP+

Clinical significance

DHFR is a classic drug target. Methotrexate, an antimetabolite, binds DHFR far more tightly than folate and is used against cancer and autoimmune disease. Trimethoprim selectively inhibits bacterial DHFR (antibiotic), and pyrimethamine inhibits the parasite enzyme (antimalarial / anti-toxoplasma). DHFR deficiency is a rare cause of megaloblastic anaemia and neurological disease.

Research

E. coli DHFR is a model system for enzyme catalysis and protein folding; the mouse Dhfr gene is widely used as a selectable marker, and model organisms studies inform its biology.
