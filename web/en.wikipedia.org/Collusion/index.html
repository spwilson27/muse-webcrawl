Collusion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Collusion is a secret cooperation or deceitful agreement in order to cheat or defraud others, or for another illegal or deceitful purpose. In economics, collusion is a non-competitive, secret, and sometimes illegal agreement between rivals that attempts to disrupt the market's equilibrium. By collaborating with each other, rival firms look to alter the price of a good to their advantage. The parties may collectively restrict the supply of a good and/or agree to increase its price in order to maximize profits. Groups may also collude by sharing market segments or customers.

Forms

Common forms of collusion include price fixing, bid rigging (in which competitors agree in advance who will submit the winning bid at a tender), market allocation, and output restrictions. An explicit cartel, such as OPEC in the oil market, is the most formal type of collusion. Collusion can also be tacit: in an oligopoly, firms may coordinate on high prices without any direct communication, simply by anticipating one another's reactions — a phenomenon economists model with game theory, where repeated interaction sustains cooperation in the manner of the prisoner's dilemma.

Legality and enforcement

Explicit collusion between competitors is illegal under antitrust and competition law in most jurisdictions — including the Sherman Act in the United States and Article 101 of the EU treaty — and can attract heavy corporate fines and imprisonment of executives. Enforcement authorities encourage defection through leniency programs that offer immunity or reduced penalties to the first cartel member to confess, exploiting the instability of collusive agreements. Tacit collusion, being harder to prove, is generally not punishable in itself.

Other contexts

The term is used more broadly for secret conspiracies in other fields: collusion between players in games or sports betting, collusion at auction or in poker, and collusion in politics or between firms and regulators. In game-playing software and network security, detection of colluding adversaries is an active research problem.
