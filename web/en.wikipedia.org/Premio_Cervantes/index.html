Premio Cervantes

Premio Miguel de Cervantes
Awarded for: Lifetime achievement in Spanish-language literature
Country: Spain
First awarded: 1976

The Premio Miguel de Cervantes (English: Miguel de Cervantes Prize) is one of the most prestigious literary awards in the Spanish-speaking world. It is awarded annually by the Ministry of Culture of Spain to honour the lifetime achievement of an outstanding writer in the Spanish language.

History

The prize was established in 1976 and is named after Miguel de Cervantes, author of Don Quixote, widely considered the greatest figure in Spanish literature. The first recipient was the poet Jorge Guillén. The award ceremony traditionally takes place on 23 April, the anniversary of Cervantes's death in 1616, at the University of Alcalá de Henares, and is presided over by the King of Spain.

Recipients

Laureates have included many of the major figures of modern Spanish-language letters, among them Alejo Carpentier, Jorge Luis Borges (who shared the 1979 prize with Gerardo Diego), Octavio Paz, Camilo José Cela, Mario Vargas Llosa, Carlos Fuentes, José Emilio Pacheco, Elena Poniatowska, Eduardo Mendoza, Ida Vitale, Cristina Peri Rossi, Rafael Cadenas and Luis Mateo Díez. By long-standing custom the prize has tended to alternate between writers from Spain and from Latin America.

The prize

The award carries a substantial cash prize (125,000 euros in recent years). Candidates are proposed by the academies of the Spanish language, previous laureates and other cultural institutions, and the winner is selected by a jury convened by the Spanish Ministry of Culture.
