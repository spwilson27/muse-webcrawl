Fort Knox

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Knox is a United States Army installation in Kentucky, south of Louisville and north of Elizabethtown. The 109,000-acre (170 sq mi) base covers parts of Bullitt, Hardin, and Meade counties. It is best known to the public as the site of the United States Bullion Depository, which holds a large portion of the nation's gold reserves, making "Fort Knox" a byword for impregnable security.

History

The site was first used as Camp Knox during the First World War, named for Major General Henry Knox, the first Secretary of War. It became a permanent fort in 1932 and the home of the Army's mechanized cavalry and later the Armor Center, where armoured units trained for decades. Since a 2005 realignment, the post's role shifted toward human resources and cadet training; it now hosts U.S. Army Human Resources Command, Army Cadet Command, and the annual Cadet Summer Training at Fort Knox.

Bullion Depository

The United States Bullion Depository, completed in 1936, is a fortified vault building adjacent to the fort. It stores about 147 million troy ounces of gold — more than half of the U.S. Treasury's gold — in the form of standard bars. During the Second World War it also safeguarded the United States Declaration of Independence, the Constitution, the Magna Carta, and other irreplaceable documents. The phrase "as secure as Fort Knox" entered common usage; the vault's security arrangements are classified, and visits are essentially prohibited.

General George Patton Museum

The post hosts the General George Patton Museum of Leadership, founded around Patton's wartime effects, interpreting the history of Army leadership and the armoured force.
