Turboprop

TypeGas turbine aircraft engine
First run1938–1940s (Jendrassik Cs-1)
ApplicationsRegional airliners, military transports

A turboprop is a turbine engine that drives an aircraft propeller. Unlike a turbojet, in which thrust comes almost entirely from exhaust gases, a turboprop extracts most of the exhaust energy through additional turbine stages to turn a propeller via a reduction gearbox, producing efficient thrust at subsonic speeds.

Design

Air is compressed, mixed with fuel and burned; the hot gases drive turbines that power both the compressor and the propeller shaft. Because propellers are most efficient below about 450 mph (725 km/h) and at lower altitudes, turboprops are favoured for short-haul and regional aircraft such as the ATR 72 and Dash 8, and for military transports like the C-130 Hercules.

History

The first turboprop design was the Hungarian Jendrassik Cs-1 of 1938. Britain's Rolls-Royce Trent powered a modified Gloster Meteor in 1945, and the Vickers Viscount became the first turboprop airliner in regular service in 1953. The Soviet Kuznetsov NK-12, driving contra-rotating propellers on the Tu-95 bomber, remains the most powerful turboprop in service.

Comparison

Turboprops consume significantly less fuel than turbofans on short sectors but are limited in speed and altitude and tend to be noisier in the cabin. Open-rotor "propfan" concepts seek to combine turboprop efficiency with jet-like speeds.
