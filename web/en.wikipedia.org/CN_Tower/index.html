CN Tower

The CN Tower (French: Tour CN) is a 553.3 m-high (1,815.3 ft) communications and observation tower in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Completed in 1976, it was the world's tallest free-standing structure until 2007, and remains the tallest free-standing structure in the Western Hemisphere and an icon of Toronto's skyline.

History

The tower was built by Canadian National (CN), the railway company, to serve as a telecommunications hub and demonstrate the strength of Canadian industry. Construction began in 1973 and involved pouring concrete around the clock; the antenna was raised by helicopter in 1975.

Attractions

Visitors can ride glass elevators to the LookOut level at 342 m, walk on a glass floor, dine in the revolving 360 Restaurant, and, since 2011, take the EdgeWalk — a hands-free walk around the outside of the main pod at 356 m. The tower is struck by lightning dozens of times each year and attracts about two million visitors annually.
