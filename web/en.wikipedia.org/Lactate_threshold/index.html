Lactate threshold

Lactate threshold
FieldExercise physiology
Typical valueabout 4 mmol/L blood lactate (OBLA)

The lactate threshold is the exercise intensity at which lactate begins to accumulate in the blood at a faster rate than it can be cleared. It is a key concept in exercise physiology and endurance training, marking the upper boundary of sustainable steady-state effort.

Physiology

During exercise, pyruvate is produced by glycolysis faster than it can be oxidised, and the excess is converted to lactate. At low intensities lactate is cleared as fast as it is produced. Beyond the threshold, blood lactate rises exponentially; associated hydrogen ions contribute to the burning sensation and fatigue of hard effort. Lactate itself is a useful fuel, shuttled between fibres and organs.

Measurement

The threshold is measured in incremental exercise tests with staged blood samples. Definitions vary: the first rise above baseline (LT1), the onset of blood lactate accumulation at a fixed 4 mmol/L (OBLA), and maximal lactate steady state. Untrained people reach the threshold at roughly 50–60 percent of VO2max; trained endurance athletes may sustain 80–90 percent.

Training

Threshold ("tempo") training — sustained efforts at or near threshold pace — is used to raise the intensity an athlete can hold for about an hour. The threshold pace is a strong predictor of endurance performance.
