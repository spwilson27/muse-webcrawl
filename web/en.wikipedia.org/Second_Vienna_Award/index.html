Northern Transylvania, assigned to Hungary by the award.

The Second Vienna Award, also known as the Vienna Diktat, was the second of two territorial disputes arbitrated by Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy. On 30 August 1940, it returned Northern Transylvania from the Kingdom of Romania to the Kingdom of Hungary, reversing part of the post–First World War Treaty of Trianon settlement.

Background

Hungary under Regent Miklós Horthy sought the revision of Trianon, which had assigned all of Transylvania to Romania. After the First Vienna Award of 1938 returned southern Slovakia, and following the Soviet occupation of Bessarabia in June 1940, Hungary pressed its claim to Transylvania. Germany, wishing to keep both states in its orbit and to protect access to Romanian oil, imposed arbitration at the Belvedere Palace in Vienna, conducted by foreign ministers Ribbentrop and Ciano.

Terms

Romania ceded about 43,000 square kilometres of northern Transylvania, with a population of roughly 2.5 million — mixed Romanian and Hungarian — including the cities of Cluj (Kolozsvár), Oradea (Nagyvárad) and Târgu Mureș (Marosvásárhely). Romanian troops and administration withdrew within two weeks; the Romanian foreign minister reportedly fainted when shown the map.

Consequences

The award humiliated Romania, contributed to the abdication of King Carol II days later, and pushed the country firmly into the Axis under Ion Antonescu. Ethnic tensions and atrocities accompanied the transfer. After the Second World War, the Paris Peace Treaties of 1947 annulled the award and restored Northern Transylvania to Romania, a border that stands today.
