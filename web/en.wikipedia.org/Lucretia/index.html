Lucretia

Lucretia (died c. 510 BC) was a noblewoman in ancient Rome whose fate, in the semi-legendary account of Livy, spurred the overthrow of the Roman monarchy and the founding of the Roman Republic.

Legend

According to Livy, Lucretia, wife of the consul Collatinus, was assaulted by Sextus Tarquinius, son of King Tarquin the Proud. She summoned her husband and father, revealed the crime, exacted an oath of vengeance, and took her own life. Lucius Junius Brutus displayed her body in the Forum, inciting the revolt that expelled the Tarquins and established the Republic. The story has been retold in art and literature by Shakespeare, Benjamin Britten and many painters.
