Haute-Loire

Haute-Loire (French pronunciation: [ot lwaʁ]; Occitan: Naut Léger) is a department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of south-central France. Named after the Loire River, it is the department where the Loire has its source region nearby and through which its upper course flows. Its prefecture is Le Puy-en-Velay, a historic pilgrimage town and starting point of the Via Podiensis, one of the main pilgrimage routes to Santiago de Compostela.

History

Haute-Loire was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790, from parts of the former provinces of Auvergne, Languedoc (the Velay), and Lyonnais (part of Forez). The department corresponds largely to the historic region of the Velay, together with parts of the Brivadois and other adjoining territories.

Geography

The department is dominated by the plateaus and volcanic landscapes of the eastern Massif Central. The Loire and its tributary the Allier cut deep gorges through the basalt plateaus; volcanic formations such as the Devès range define the west, while the Mézenc massif in the east reaches 1,753 metres at Mont Mézenc, the department's highest point shared with Ardèche. Sub-prefectures are Brioude and Yssingeaux.

Culture and economy

The department is known for Le Puy lentils (green lentils with protected designation of origin), lacemaking at Le Puy, and its Romanesque churches, including the chapel of Saint-Michel d'Aiguilhe perched on a volcanic needle. Tourism, agriculture, and food processing are important to the economy.
