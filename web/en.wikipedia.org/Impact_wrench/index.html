Impact wrench

A pneumatic impact wrench used for removing wheel lug nuts.

An impact wrench (also known as an impactor, impact gun, air wrench, air gun, rattle gun, torque gun, or wind gun) is a socket wrench power tool designed to deliver high torque output with minimal exertion by the user, by storing energy in a rotating mass and then delivering it suddenly to the output shaft. It is widely used in automotive repair, heavy equipment maintenance, product assembly, and major construction projects to loosen stubborn fasteners such as lug nuts and large bolts.

Operation

The motor accelerates a heavy hammer mass; a mechanism then engages the hammer with the anvil on the output shaft so that its rotational momentum is transferred in a sharp blow, repeating many times per minute. Because the torque reaction is absorbed by the tool's mechanism rather than the operator's wrist, very high torque can be applied with little fatigue or kickback. Power sources include compressed air (pneumatic), electricity, batteries, and hydraulics. Impact wrenches are driven with standard square drives of 1/4″, 3/8″, 1/2″, 3/4″, and 1″, and require special hardened impact sockets rather than standard chrome sockets, which may shatter.

Uses

The familiar rapid rattling of pneumatic impact wrenches is a hallmark of tire shops and motor racing pit stops, where wheel guns change all four wheels of a racing car in a few seconds. Despite their power, impact wrenches are imprecise for final tightening and are normally supplemented by a torque wrench when a specified clamping force is required.
