Finnic languages

The Finnic (or Baltic Finnic) languages are a branch of the Uralic language family spoken around the Baltic Sea by the Finnic peoples. The two most widely spoken are Finnish and Estonian, the official languages of Finland and Estonia. Together the Finnic languages have roughly seven million speakers.

Languages

The branch descends from Proto-Finnic, spoken around the Gulf of Finland in the first millennium AD. Its members include Finnish (with the closely related Meänkieli and Kven), Karelian, Ludic, Veps, Ingrian, Estonian (including Võro and Seto in the south), Livonian, and Votic. Several of the smaller languages are endangered: Livonian's last native speaker died in 2013, though a revival community exists, and Votic and Ingrian have only small numbers of elderly speakers.

Features

Finnic languages are agglutinative, with rich case systems (Finnish has about fifteen cases), vowel harmony in many varieties, consonant gradation, and no grammatical gender. They have absorbed substantial loanwords from Germanic, Baltic, and Slavic languages over the centuries.
