Tricity (Polish: Trójmiasto) is a metropolitan area in northern Poland consisting of three coastal cities on the Gdańsk Bay of the Baltic Sea: Gdańsk, Sopot and Gdynia. Together they form a continuous urban strip stretching along the western shore of the bay, and together with surrounding towns they form a conurbation of over one million inhabitants.

Geography

Gdańsk, the largest of the three, lies at the mouth of the Motława and Vistula rivers and is one of Poland's principal seaports. Sopot, situated between Gdańsk and Gdynia, is a spa and resort town famous for its long wooden pier. Gdynia, built from a fishing village in the interwar period, is a major modern port city. The three cities are linked by the SKM rapid transit railway.

History

The area has a long and contested history involving Polish, German and Kashubian populations. After the Second World War the cities became part of Poland. Gdańsk's shipyards were the birthplace of the Solidarity movement in 1980, led by Lech Wałęsa, which played a central role in the fall of communism in Eastern Europe.

Cooperation

Although each city retains its own municipal government, the three cooperate through the Gdańsk-Gdynia-Sopot Metropolitan Area association on transport, planning and promotion. The Tricity Landscape Park, a protected forested moraine area, borders the conurbation to the west.
