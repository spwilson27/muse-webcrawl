The Battle of Köse Dağ was fought between the Sultanate of Rum ruled by the Seljuq dynasty and the Mongol Empire on 26 June 1243 at a defile near Köse Dağ, a mountain between Erzincan and Gümüşhane. The Mongols achieved a decisive victory, resulting in the Sultanate of Rum becoming a vassal state of the Mongol Empire.

Background

After the Mongol conquest of Persia, Mongol forces under Baiju Noyan advanced into Anatolia, taking Erzurum in 1242. Sultan Kaykhusraw II assembled a large army including Georgian, Armenian, Trapezuntine and Frankish mercenary contingents to confront them.

The battle

Against the advice of his more experienced commanders, the sultan sent an advance force against the Mongols. The Mongols employed their characteristic feigned retreat, then enveloped the Seljuq troops. When the main Seljuq army learned of the defeat, Kaykhusraw fled during the night, and the Mongols occupied the camp and its treasury almost unopposed.

Aftermath

The sultan was forced to accept Mongol suzerainty and a heavy annual tribute. Köse Dağ marked the beginning of the decline of Seljuq power in Anatolia and its gradual fragmentation into the Anatolian beyliks, from which the Ottoman state would later emerge.
