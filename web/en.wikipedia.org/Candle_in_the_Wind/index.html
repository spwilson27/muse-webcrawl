Candle in the Wind

"Candle in the Wind" is a song written by English musician Elton John and lyricist Bernie Taupin. It was originally released in 1973 as a tribute to American actress Marilyn Monroe, who had died in 1962. The song appears on the album Goodbye Yellow Brick Road.

Original version

Taupin was inspired to write the lyric after hearing the phrase "candle in the wind" used to describe singer Janis Joplin. The song portrays Monroe as a fragile figure exploited by Hollywood and the press, and reflects on fame and early death. Released as a single in 1974, it reached the top ten in the United Kingdom. A live version recorded in Australia in 1986 was also a hit in 1987.

1997 version: "Goodbye England's Rose"

Following the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, in August 1997, Taupin rewrote the opening lines and John performed the revised song at Diana's funeral in Westminster Abbey on September 6, 1997. Released as a double A-side single with "Something About the Way You Look Tonight," it became the best-selling physical single of all time, with sales of over 33 million copies worldwide. Proceeds went to the Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Fund.

Legacy

The 1973 original remains one of John's signature songs and is frequently included in lists of the greatest songs of all time. John has stated he will never perform the 1997 version again except at the request of Diana's sons.
