Wall Street is a street in the Financial District of Lower Manhattan in New York City, running from Broadway west-to-east to South Street and the East River. Over time, the term has become a metonym for the financial markets of the United States as a whole and for the American financial services industry.

History

The name derives from a wall built by the Dutch in 1653 across northern New Amsterdam to defend against the English. In 1792 brokers signed the Buttonwood Agreement beneath a plane tree on Wall Street, founding what became the New York Stock Exchange. The street became the centre of American finance in the 19th and 20th centuries, symbolised by the NYSE building (1903), Federal Hall — where George Washington was inaugurated in 1789 — and the charging bull statue nearby.

Events

Wall Street was the scene of the 1920 Wall Street bombing and the 1929 crash that began the Great Depression. In 2011 the Occupy Wall Street movement protested economic inequality from nearby Zuccotti Park. The White House and Congress in the Capitol regulate the markets centred here.
