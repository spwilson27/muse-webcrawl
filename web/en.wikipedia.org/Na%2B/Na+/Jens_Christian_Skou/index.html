Jens Christian Skou

Jens Christian Skou (8 October 1918 – 28 May 2018) was a Danish biochemist. In 1997 he shared one half of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry — with Paul D. Boyer and John E. Walker receiving the other half jointly — "for the first discovery of an ion-transporting enzyme, Na⁺,K⁺-ATPase."

Skou trained in medicine at the University of Copenhagen and took his doctorate at Aarhus University, where he spent most of his career and became professor of physiology and later biophysics. Studying the membranes of crab nerves in the 1950s, he identified an ATPase enzyme whose activity depended on the presence of sodium and potassium ions in the proper proportions — the molecular basis of the sodium–potassium pump that maintains ion gradients across animal cell membranes.

Legacy

The discovery, published in 1957, was the first demonstration of an enzyme that drives active transport of ions, founding the modern understanding of membrane pumps; related pumps for calcium, protons and other ions were subsequently found. Skou remained active in research at Aarhus for decades after his formal retirement and died in 2018 at the age of 99.
