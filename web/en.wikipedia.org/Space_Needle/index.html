Space Needle

The Space Needle is an observation tower in Seattle, Washington, United States. Built for the 1962 World's Fair, the Century 21 Exposition, it stands 184 metres (605 ft) tall and was the tallest structure west of the Mississippi River when completed. It is the most recognisable symbol of Seattle and the Pacific Northwest.

Design and construction

The design, developed by architects John Graham Jr. and Victor Steinbrueck from Edward E. Carlson's sketch of a tower with a flying-saucer-like top, combined a slender tripod-like waist with a disc-shaped top housing an observation deck and a rotating restaurant. The foundations extend about 9 metres (30 ft) underground and are so massive that the tower's center of gravity lies just a metre above ground level, allowing it to withstand high winds and earthquakes. Construction took about 400 days; the tower opened on 21 April 1962, the first day of the fair, and drew some 2.3 million visitors during the exposition.

Features

The observation deck, at 160 metres (520 ft), offers panoramic views of downtown Seattle, Elliott Bay, Mount Rainier and the Cascade and Olympic mountain ranges. The rotating restaurant, originally the Eye of the Needle, completed a full revolution roughly every hour. A renovation completed in 2018 ("the Century Project") replaced the deck's floors with glass — creating "The Loupe", the world's first rotating glass floor — and installed floor-to-ceiling glass panels.

Cultural significance

The Space Needle has appeared in numerous films and television series set in Seattle and was designated an official Seattle landmark in 1999. It welcomes over a million visitors a year and hosts the city's New Year's Eve fireworks display.
