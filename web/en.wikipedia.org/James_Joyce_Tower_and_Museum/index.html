James Joyce Tower and Museum

LocationSandycove, Dublin, Ireland
TypeMartello tower museum
Opened as museum16 June 1962

The James Joyce Tower and Museum is a Martello tower in Sandycove, County Dublin, Ireland, where writer James Joyce spent six nights in September 1904. It is the setting of the opening chapter of his novel Ulysses and now houses a museum devoted to his life and work.

History

The tower was one of a chain built by the British in 1804–1806 to guard against a Napoleonic invasion. Joyce lodged there with Oliver St John Gogarty — the model for Buck Mulligan in Ulysses — and left abruptly after a nighttime incident in which shots were fired in jest by a housemate.

Museum

The tower opened as a museum on Bloomsday, 16 June 1962. Its collection includes Joyce's death mask, letters, photographs, personal possessions, and editions of his works. The living room is restored to resemble its appearance in the novel's "Telemachus" episode. Admission is free, and the tower is a pilgrimage site for Joyce readers, especially on Bloomsday.
