Ken Arok (also spelled Ken Angrok) was the founder and first king of the Singhasari kingdom of East Java, reigning from 1222 until his assassination in 1227. His rise from commoner and outlaw to king, told in the semi-legendary Pararaton ("Book of Kings"), is one of the most famous stories in Javanese history, and his line became the ancestor of the kings of Majapahit.

Legendary origins

According to the Pararaton, Ken Arok was a charismatic rogue — gambler, thief, and servant — who was told by a priest that a divine light marked him for kingship. Entering the service of Tunggul Ametung, the chief of Tumapel, he fell in love with the chief's wife, Ken Dedes, whom prophecy said would bear kings. He commissioned a magical kris from the smith Mpu Gandring but, impatient, stabbed the smith with the unfinished blade; the dying smith cursed Ken Arok and his descendants to die by the same kris for seven generations.

Founding of Singhasari

Ken Arok murdered Tunggul Ametung with the kris, arranged for another man to be executed for the crime, married Ken Dedes, and took over Tumapel. In 1222 he defeated King Kertajaya of Kediri at the Battle of Ganter and founded a new kingdom, Singhasari, taking the royal name Sri Rajasa Bhatara Sang Amurwabhumi. He thus ended Kediri's dominance and began the dynasty from which Majapahit's rulers would descend.

Death and the curse

In 1227 Anusapati, Ken Dedes's son by Tunggul Ametung, learned the truth about his father's murder and had Ken Arok killed with Mpu Gandring's kris — fulfilling the next step of the curse, which tradition says continued to claim later kings of the dynasty. Ken Arok was enshrined at Kagenengan as a Shivaite deity.
