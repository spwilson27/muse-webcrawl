Scurvy is a disease caused by a deficiency of vitamin C (ascorbic acid). Because humans cannot synthesise vitamin C, they must obtain it from food — chiefly fresh fruit and vegetables. Vitamin C is required for the synthesis of collagen, the main structural protein of connective tissue; without it, blood vessels, gums, skin and bones weaken.

Signs and symptoms

Early symptoms are fatigue, malaise and aching limbs. As the disease progresses, sufferers develop swollen, bleeding gums (with teeth loosening in advanced cases), perifollicular haemorrhages and corkscrew hairs, large bruises, joint swelling and pain, dry skin, and failure of wounds to heal; old scars may even reopen. Anaemia is common. Untreated scurvy causes severe internal bleeding and death, often suddenly from haemorrhage.

History

Scurvy was the great killer of long sea voyages: Vasco da Gama lost about two-thirds of his crew to it in 1497–99, and it likely killed more sailors than storms, combat and all other diseases combined during the age of sail. In 1753 the Scottish naval surgeon James Lind published his Treatise of the Scurvy, reporting a 1747 shipboard experiment in which sailors given oranges and lemons recovered rapidly — often cited as an early controlled clinical trial. The Royal Navy eventually mandated lemon (later lime) juice, earning British sailors the nickname "limeys". Vitamin C itself was isolated and identified as the antiscorbutic factor in 1932 by Albert Szent-Györgyi and others.

Causes and risk today

Scurvy is now rare where diets are varied, but it still occurs in people with highly restricted diets (including some children with developmental disorders who eat very few foods), alcohol dependence, malabsorption, or severe poverty, and in refugees dependent on rations lacking fresh produce. Only about 10 mg of vitamin C a day is needed to prevent it, though recommended intakes are higher (75–90 mg/day for adults).

Treatment

Treatment is vitamin C replacement, by diet or supplement; symptoms such as fatigue and pain often improve within days and most signs resolve within weeks.
