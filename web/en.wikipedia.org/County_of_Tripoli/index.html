County of Tripoli

County of Tripoli
Status: Crusader state
Capital: Tortosa (1102–1109); Tripoli (1109–1289)
Established: 1102
Disestablished: 1289
Last count: Lucia of Tripoli (de facto)

The County of Tripoli was a Crusader state established in the Levant in the aftermath of the First Crusade. Founded in 1102, it was the last of the four major Crusader states to be created, and it endured until 1289 — the second-longest-lived of them, outlasted only by the Kingdom of Jerusalem. Its capital was the coastal city of Tripoli, in modern Lebanon, and at its height the county stretched from Byblos in the south to the mountains beyond Tortosa in the north.

History

Raymond IV of Toulouse, one of the leaders of the First Crusade, began the conquest of the region in 1102, taking Tortosa and building the castle of Mons Peregrinus overlooking Tripoli. Raymond died in 1105 before the city fell; his successors, William Jordan and Raymond's son Bertrand, contested the inheritance until King Baldwin I of Jerusalem divided the territory between them in 1109, the year Tripoli itself surrendered. Reunited under Bertrand, the county became a vassal of Jerusalem but guarded its independence jealously.

Ruled by the house of Toulouse and later by princes of Antioch through inheritance, Tripoli survived the falls of Edessa (1144) and Jerusalem (1187) by avoiding open battle and paying tribute when necessary. The county's end came amid a succession dispute: after Count Bohemond VII died in 1287, the commune of Tripoli repudiated his sister Lucia and sought Genoese protection. Fearing Genoa's dominance, the Mamluk sultan Qalawun attacked, and Tripoli fell on 26 April 1289; its population was massacred or enslaved, and the site of the city was abandoned for a new settlement inland.

Government and society

The county was organized on feudal lines, with lordships such as Botron, Gibelet (Byblos), and Nephin held of the count. Its economy rested on Mediterranean trade — silk, sugar, and glass — conducted largely by Italian merchants, and on agriculture in the fertile coastal plain.
