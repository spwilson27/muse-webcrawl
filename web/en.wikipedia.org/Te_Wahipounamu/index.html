Te Wahipounamu

Te Wāhipounamu – South West New Zealand is a World Heritage Site in the south-west corner of the South Island of New Zealand. Inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1990, it covers some 26,000 square kilometres — about ten percent of New Zealand's land area — and comprises four national parks: Westland Tai Poutini, Aoraki / Mount Cook, Mount Aspiring, and Fiordland, together with surrounding conservation lands.

Natural features

The region displays classic evidence of continental drift and glaciation, with fjords, rocky coasts, towering cliffs, mountain peaks including Aoraki / Mount Cook (3,724 m), lakes, and waterfalls. It preserves the best modern representation of the ancient Gondwanan flora and fauna: extensive temperate rainforests of southern beech and podocarps, and endemic species such as the takahe, kea, kaka, and the Fiordland crested penguin, surviving in a landscape largely unmodified by humans.

Cultural significance

The name Te Wāhipounamu means "the place of greenstone" in Māori, referring to the treasured pounamu (nephrite jade) found in the region's rivers. The area is of great significance to Ngāi Tahu, whose ancestors travelled its trails to gather pounamu, and its mountains and waters figure prominently in tribal tradition.
