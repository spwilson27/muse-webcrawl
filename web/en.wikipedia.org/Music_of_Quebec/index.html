Music of Quebec

The music of Quebec has produced a distinctive francophone popular culture within Canada, alongside classical, jazz and Indigenous traditions. Its folk roots combine French settler songs with Celtic fiddle music, producing the lively reels and call-and-response songs still heard at kitchen parties and festivals.

Chanson and popular music

In the 20th century the figure of the chansonnier — the singer-songwriter — became central to Quebec identity, especially during the Quiet Revolution. Félix Leclerc, Gilles Vigneault (whose "Mon pays" became an unofficial anthem), Robert Charlebois and the band Harmonium defined the genre. Later stars include Céline Dion, one of the best-selling recording artists of all time, Roch Voisine, and the bands Beau Dommage and Les Cowboys Fringants. Montreal has also become a noted centre for indie rock, home to Arcade Fire and the Constellation Records scene, and hosts the Montreal International Jazz Festival, one of the world's largest jazz festivals.

Classical and traditional

The Orchestre symphonique de Montréal gained international fame under Charles Dutoit, and composers such as Claude Vivier achieved posthumous renown. Traditional groups like La Bottine Souriante popularized Quebec folk music worldwide, and the province's fiddle and step-dancing traditions remain central to its cultural festivals.
