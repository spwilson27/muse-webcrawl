Bioprocess engineering

Bioprocess engineering, also known as biochemical engineering, is a specialization of chemical engineering or biological engineering. It deals with the design and development of equipment and processes for the manufacturing of products such as agriculture, food, feed, pharmaceuticals, nutraceuticals, chemicals and polymers, and paper from biological materials, as well as treatment of waste water.

Scope

Bioprocess engineering combines the disciplines of biology — including microbiology, genetics and biochemistry — with engineering principles of mass and heat transfer, fluid mechanics and process control. Key unit operations include fermentation and cell culture in bioreactors, sterilization, and downstream processing steps such as filtration, centrifugation and chromatography used to recover and purify products like insulin, vaccines, enzymes and monoclonal antibodies.

Upstream and downstream

Upstream processing covers strain development, media preparation and cultivation; downstream processing covers separation and purification of the product to specification. Scaling laboratory processes up to industrial volumes while maintaining sterile conditions and product quality is a central challenge of the field.
