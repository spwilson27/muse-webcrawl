Thomas Mudge (horologist)

Born1715, Exeter, Devon
Died14 November 1794, London
OccupationWatchmaker
Known forInventing the lever escapement

Thomas Mudge (1715 – 14 November 1794) was an English horologist who invented the lever escapement, the greatest single improvement in watchmaking accuracy and the escapement still used in almost all mechanical watches today.

Career

Born in Exeter, Mudge was apprenticed to the celebrated watchmaker George Graham in London and later worked with John Ellicott. Around 1755 he devised the detached lever escapement, first applied in a watch made for King Ferdinand VI of Spain. In 1759 he completed the marine timekeeper known as "Green" for the Board of Longitude tests, incorporating a remontoire and a free-sprung balance; he later made further marine chronometers, including two for his son's 1790s trials, for which the Mudge family eventually received £3,000 from Parliament after a long dispute.

In 1770 Mudge went into partnership with William Dutton. His watches were prized for their finishing; he was made watchmaker to King George III. Although Mudge himself made few lever watches, the escapement's potential was developed by others and became the foundation of the modern mechanical watch industry.
