Ergosphere

The ergosphere is a region located outside a rotating black hole's outer event horizon. Its name was proposed by Remo Ruffini and John Archibald Wheeler from the Greek word ergon, meaning "work", because it is possible to extract energy and mass from a black hole using this region. It was first derived by Robert Geroch.

Shape and frame dragging

The ergosphere is bounded on the outside by the static limit (ergosurface), an oblate spheroid touching the event horizon at the poles, and on the inside by the outer event horizon. Within the ergosphere, frame-dragging is so extreme that no object can remain stationary with respect to a distant observer: all objects are forced to co-rotate with the black hole.

Penrose process

In the Penrose process, a particle entering the ergosphere splits in two; one fragment falls through the event horizon with negative energy relative to infinity, while the other escapes with more energy than the original, extracting rotational energy from the black hole. A related electromagnetic mechanism, the Blandford–Znajek process, is thought to power relativistic jets from active galactic nuclei.
