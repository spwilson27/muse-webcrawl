Mogadishu

Mogadishu, capital of Somalia, on the Indian Ocean

Mogadishu (Somali: Muqdisho; Arabic: مقديشو), locally known as Xamar, is the capital and most populous city of Somalia. Situated on the Horn of Africa's Indian Ocean coast in the Banaadir region, it has served for centuries as an important port and trading city linking East Africa with Arabia, Persia, India, and beyond. Its population is estimated at around two to three million, though no reliable census exists.

History

Founded by Arab and Persian traders by around the 10th century, Mogadishu grew rich on the Indian Ocean trade in gold, ivory, and textiles, and was visited by Zheng He and Ibn Battuta, who described a large, prosperous city. It became the centre of the Ajuran and later Muzaffar dynasties. Italy colonised the area in the late 19th century, making Mogadishu capital of Italian Somaliland and building its cathedral and port. After independence in 1960 it was the capital of the Somali Republic, and under Mohamed Siad Barre it gained a reputation as the "White Pearl of the Indian Ocean".

Civil war and reconstruction

The collapse of the central government in 1991 plunged Mogadishu into decades of factional fighting. In 1993 the Battle of Mogadishu — the "Black Hawk Down" incident — saw 18 American soldiers killed during a raid against warlord Mohamed Farrah Aidid. Islamist groups, including the Islamic Courts Union and later Al-Shabaab, controlled the city for periods; African Union forces (AMISOM) drove Al-Shabaab out in 2011, though the group has continued deadly attacks, including the October 2017 truck bombing that killed more than 500 people. Since then the city has seen substantial reconstruction, a returning diaspora, and renewed construction amid continuing insecurity.

Geography and economy

Mogadishu has a hot semi-arid climate tempered by ocean breezes. Its seaport is Somalia's largest, and Aden Adde International Airport connects the capital to the region. Telecommunications, money transfer, construction, and trade anchor the economy.
