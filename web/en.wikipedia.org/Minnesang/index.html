Minnesang (Middle High German for "love song") was a tradition of lyric and song writing in Germany and Austria that flourished in the Middle High German period, roughly from 1150 to 1400. Its practitioners, the Minnesänger, were often nobles and knights.

Themes and forms

The central theme was Minne — courtly love, typically the knight's idealized, unfulfilled devotion to a noble lady. Common forms include the dawn song (Tagelied), in which lovers part at daybreak, the crusading song, and the Leich. The poetry drew on Provençal troubadour and Old French trouvère traditions, adapted to German court culture.

Famous Minnesänger

Walther von der Vogelweide is considered the greatest of the Minnesänger; others include Wolfram von Eschenbach (also author of Parzival), Hartmann von Aue, Neidhart von Reuental and, in the later period, Oswald von Wolkenstein. Their songs survive in illuminated manuscripts such as the Codex Manesse, which depicts many poets in idealized portraits.
