Proportional hazards model

Proportional hazards models are a class of survival models in statistics. Survival models relate the time that passes, before some event occurs, to one or more covariates that may be associated with that quantity of time. In a proportional hazards model, the unique effect of a unit increase in a covariate is multiplicative with respect to the hazard rate.

The Cox model

The most prominent member of the class is the Cox proportional hazards model, proposed by David Cox in 1972. It writes the hazard for an individual as a baseline hazard function multiplied by exp(β·X), leaving the baseline hazard unspecified. Coefficients are estimated by partial likelihood, and hazard ratios summarize covariate effects under the assumption that hazard ratios between individuals are constant over time.

Assumptions and extensions

The proportional hazards assumption can be checked with diagnostics such as Schoenfeld residuals. Extensions include stratified models, time-varying covariates, frailty models, and parametric proportional hazards models with specified baseline hazards.
