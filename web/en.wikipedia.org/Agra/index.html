Agra is a city on the banks of the Yamuna River in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh, about 230 km southeast of New Delhi. It was the capital of the Mughal Empire for much of the 16th and 17th centuries and is world-famous for the Taj Mahal, one of the most celebrated buildings on Earth.

History

Sikandar Lodi founded the modern city in 1504 and made it his capital. After Babur's victory at Panipat in 1526 Agra became the Mughal capital; Akbar built the Agra Fort and the nearby palace-city of Fatehpur Sikri. Shah Jahan, who shifted the capital to Delhi in 1648, built the Taj Mahal (1632–1653) as a tomb for his wife Mumtaz Mahal. After the Mughal decline, Agra passed through Maratha hands before British control in 1803.

Monuments

Three UNESCO World Heritage Sites lie in or near Agra: the Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, and Fatehpur Sikri. Other notable monuments include the tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah (the "Baby Taj"), Akbar's tomb at Sikandra, and the Jama Masjid. Agra forms one corner of India's Golden Triangle tourist circuit with Delhi and Jaipur.

Economy

Tourism dominates the economy, alongside leather goods and footwear manufacturing, for which Agra is a major center, plus handicrafts such as marble inlay (pietra dura) and carpets.
