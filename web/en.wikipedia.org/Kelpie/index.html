Kelpie

A kelpie is a shape-changing aquatic spirit in Scottish folklore, usually described as a horse-like creature inhabiting lochs and rivers. Said to lure travellers — especially children — onto its back, its skin is adhesive, so riders cannot dismount before it dives to drown and devour them.

Kelpies could be captured and put to work by using a bridle marked with a cross, and might take human form, betrayed by water weeds or hooves. The legend likely served to warn children away from dangerous waters. The related each-uisge is a still more malevolent water horse. The monumental Falkirk sculptures The Kelpies (2013) depict two 30-metre horse heads.
