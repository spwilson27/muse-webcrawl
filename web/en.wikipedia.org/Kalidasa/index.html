Kālidāsa

OccupationPoet, playwright
LanguageSanskrit, Prakrit
Periodc. 4th–5th century CE (Gupta period)
GenreSanskrit drama, epic poetry, lyric poetry
Notable worksAbhijñānaśākuntalam, Meghadūta, Raghuvaṃśa, Kumārasaṃbhava

Kālidāsa (Sanskrit: कालिदास, "Servant of Kali") was a Classical Sanskrit author widely regarded as the greatest poet and dramatist in the Sanskrit language of India. His plays and poetry are primarily based on the Vedas, the Rāmāyaṇa, the Mahābhārata and the Purāṇas. His surviving works consist of three plays, two epic poems and two shorter poems.

Life

Almost nothing is known with certainty about Kālidāsa's life. Tradition associates him with the court of a king Vikramāditya, often identified with the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II, which would place his floruit in the late 4th and early 5th centuries CE, though scholars have proposed dates from the 1st century BCE to the 5th century CE. Legends portray him as originally an uneducated man who, blessed by the goddess Kālī, became a great scholar and poet.

Works

His most famous play, Abhijñānaśākuntalam (The Recognition of Śakuntalā), tells of King Duṣyanta's love for Śakuntalā and the curse that makes him forget her. His other plays are Mālavikāgnimitram and Vikramōrvaśīyam. The epic poems Raghuvaṃśa (Dynasty of Raghu) and Kumārasaṃbhava (Birth of Kumāra), and the lyric Meghadūta (Cloud Messenger) and Ṛtusaṃhāra (The Gathering of the Seasons) are masterpieces of Sanskrit kāvya, celebrated for their similes, descriptions of nature and emotional depth.

Legacy

Kālidāsa's works profoundly influenced Sanskrit literature and, after their translation into European languages in the late 18th and 19th centuries, writers such as Goethe, who praised Śakuntalā extravagantly. He is often called the Shakespeare of India.
