Black River (Arkansas)

The Black River is a river in the U.S. states of Missouri and Arkansas, a major tributary of the White River. It is about 300 miles (480 km) long, rising in the Ozark foothills of southeastern Missouri and flowing generally south to join the White River near Newport, Arkansas, at Jacksonport. Its principal tributaries include the Current River, the Spring River, the Eleven Point River and the Strawberry River.

Course

The Black River forms in Wayne County, Missouri, from the confluence of its East, Middle and West forks. It flows southwest past Poplar Bluff, where Clearwater Lake, created by a U.S. Army Corps of Engineers dam, regulates its flow, then enters Arkansas and meanders southeast across the lowlands past Pocahontas, Black Rock and Powhatan. It empties into the White River at Jacksonport, now a state park.

History

The river was an important transportation corridor in the 19th century, navigable by steamboats up to Poplar Bluff, and its valley supported timber, cotton and river commerce. Davidsonville, one of the earliest towns in Arkansas Territory, was founded on its banks in 1815. The river valley contains significant Mississippian and earlier Native American archaeological sites.

Recreation and ecology

The Black River and its tributaries support fisheries for bass, crappie and catfish, and its bottomland hardwood wetlands in Arkansas are managed as waterfowl habitat within state wildlife management areas and the Dave Donaldson Black River Wildlife Management Area.
