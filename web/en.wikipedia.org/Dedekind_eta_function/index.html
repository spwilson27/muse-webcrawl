Dedekind eta function

Named afterRichard Dedekind
DomainUpper half-plane
TypeModular form (weight 1/2)

In mathematics, the Dedekind eta function, named after Richard Dedekind, is a modular form of weight 1/2 and a function defined on the upper half-plane of the complex numbers, where the imaginary part is positive. It is defined as an infinite product: η(τ) = eπiτ/12 ∏n≥1 (1 − e2πinτ).

Properties

The eta function is holomorphic on the upper half-plane but cannot be extended holomorphically to the boundary. It satisfies elegant transformation equations under the modular group, involving 24th roots of unity; the related function Δ(τ) = (2π)12 η(τ)24 is the modular discriminant, a cusp form of weight 12.

Applications

The eta function appears in number theory (partition generating functions and Euler's pentagonal theorem follow from its product expansion), in combinatorics, and in physics — notably in string theory and conformal field theory, where modular forms of this type govern partition functions.
