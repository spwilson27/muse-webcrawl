FormulaC2H3NO (CH3NCO)

Methyl isocyanate (MIC) is an organic compound with the molecular formula CH3NCO, a colourless, poisonous, lachrymatory and flammable liquid. It is the simplest isocyanate and is used as an intermediate in the manufacture of carbamate pesticides such as carbaryl, carbofuran, methomyl and aldicarb. It reacts exothermically with water, giving methylamine and carbon dioxide, and can trimerise violently if contaminated or overheated.

Bhopal disaster

MIC is infamous as the gas leaked on the night of 2-3 December 1984 from a Union Carbide pesticide plant in Bhopal, India, in the world's worst industrial disaster. Water entering a storage tank triggered a runaway reaction; some 40 tonnes of MIC and reaction products escaped over surrounding densely populated neighbourhoods, killing thousands immediately and many more in following years, and injuring hundreds of thousands.

Toxicity

Methyl isocyanate is extremely toxic by inhalation and contact, causing severe irritation and chemical burns of the eyes and respiratory tract, pulmonary oedema and death. There is no specific antidote; treatment is supportive. Because of its hazards, industry has sought processes that generate and consume MIC in situ in small quantities rather than storing it in bulk.

Production

MIC is made by reacting methylamine with phosgene, or by oxidative dehydrogenation chemistry, and is generally handled in closed systems with strict temperature control and safety instrumentation.
