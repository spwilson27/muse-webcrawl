Imatinib

Imatinib, sold under the brand name Gleevec (United States) or Glivec (elsewhere) among others, is an oral chemotherapy medication used to treat cancer. It is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor that specifically blocks the BCR-ABL fusion protein responsible for chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), as well as KIT and PDGFRA kinases involved in gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST).

Medical uses

Imatinib is used to treat chronic myeloid leukemia, Philadelphia chromosome-positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia, gastrointestinal stromal tumors, and several rarer conditions including hypereosinophilic syndrome, systemic mastocytosis, and dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans. In CML it transformed a fatal disease into a manageable chronic condition, raising five-year survival from roughly 30% to about 89%.

Mechanism of action

Imatinib binds to the ATP-binding site of the BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase, locking the enzyme in an inactive conformation and thereby halting the uncontrolled proliferation of leukemia cells. It also inhibits the receptor tyrosine kinases KIT and platelet-derived growth factor receptors.

History

The drug was developed in the 1990s by scientists at Ciba-Geigy (later Novartis), including Nicholas Lydon, together with oncologist Brian Druker of Oregon Health & Science University. Early clinical results were dramatic; the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved imatinib in May 2001 after a record-fast review. It is often cited as the first successful targeted cancer therapy and a landmark of rational drug design.

Adverse effects and cost

Common side effects include nausea, muscle pain, fluid retention and periorbital edema, rash, and fatigue; serious effects can include heart failure and liver toxicity. Resistance may develop through mutations in BCR-ABL, prompting second-generation inhibitors such as dasatinib and nilotinib. Imatinib's high launch price made it a prominent case in debates over drug pricing, although generic versions are now widely available.
