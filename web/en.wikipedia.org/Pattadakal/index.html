Pattadakal, also called Raktapura, is a complex of 7th and 8th century CE Hindu and Jain temples in northern Karnataka, India. Located on the west bank of the Malaprabha River in Bagalkot district, about 23 kilometres (14 mi) from Badami, the site was a royal commemoration and coronation centre (pattadakisuvolalu, "coronation stone") of the Chalukya dynasty. In 1987 UNESCO designated the Group of Monuments at Pattadakal a World Heritage Site.

The complex is celebrated for its harmonious synthesis of northern (Nagara) and southern (Dravidian) architectural styles, which reached a high point under the Chalukya rulers and influenced temple building across India.

Temples

The site contains ten major temples—nine Hindu, dedicated mostly to Shiva, and one Jain—along with numerous smaller shrines and plinths. The largest and most ornate is the Virupaksha Temple, built around 740 CE by Queen Lokamahadevi to commemorate her husband Vikramaditya II's victories over the Pallavas of Kanchipuram. Modelled partly on the Kailasanatha temple at Kanchipuram, it is covered with sculpture depicting scenes from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and the Puranas.

The neighbouring Mallikarjuna Temple, built by the king's second queen Trailokyamahadevi around the same time, is slightly smaller but similar in plan. The Sangameshwara Temple, commissioned by Vijayaditya around 720 CE, is among the oldest at the site. Other notable shrines include the Kashivishvanatha, Galaganatha, Kadasiddheshvara, and Jambulingeshwara temples in the Nagara style, and the Papanatha Temple, which blends both idioms. The Jain Narayana temple, built in the 9th century under the Rashtrakutas, stands at the western edge of the complex.

History

Pattadakal flourished under the Chalukyas of Badami in the 7th and 8th centuries, when it served as a ceremonial capital where kings were crowned and victories celebrated. Inscriptions in Sanskrit and Kannada on temple pillars record royal donations, battles, and the names of the architects, some of whom received honorific titles. After the decline of the Chalukyas, the site came under the Rashtrakutas and later dynasties, but its temples remained in worship or as landmarks of regional heritage.
