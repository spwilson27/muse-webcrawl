Republic of Ireland

Ireland occupies most of the island of Ireland, off the west coast of Europe.

Ireland (Irish: Éire), also known as the Republic of Ireland (Irish: Poblacht na hÉireann), is a country in north-western Europe occupying 26 of the 32 counties of the island of Ireland. The capital and largest city is Dublin. The state shares its only land border with Northern Ireland, part of the United Kingdom, and is otherwise surrounded by the Atlantic Ocean.

History

The state was created as the Irish Free State in 1922 following the Anglo-Irish Treaty, which ended the Irish War of Independence and partitioned the island. A new constitution in 1937 declared the state a sovereign, independent, democratic state named Éire, and the Republic of Ireland Act 1948 formally declared it a republic, severing the last constitutional links with the British Crown. Ireland joined the European Economic Community in 1973 and adopted the euro in 1999. The 1998 Good Friday Agreement transformed relations with the United Kingdom and Northern Ireland, ending most of the violence of the Troubles.

Economy and society

Once among Western Europe's poorer countries, Ireland became a hub for foreign direct investment — especially in technology and pharmaceuticals — drawn by low corporate taxes and an English-speaking workforce, a transformation dubbed the "Celtic Tiger". Its population, about 5.3 million, is among the fastest-growing in Europe, reversing a long history of emigration. The official languages are Irish and English, with English predominant in everyday use.
