Bertrand du Guesclin (c. 1320 – 13 July 1380), nicknamed "the Eagle of Brittany" and "the Black Dog of Brocéliande", was a Breton knight and a prominent military commander during the Hundred Years' War. He served as Constable of France from 1370 until his death.

Career

Of minor Breton nobility, du Guesclin rose through military talent, fighting first in Brittany and then for Charles V of France. Avoiding pitched battles against the English, he waged a war of attrition and sieges that steadily recovered the territories lost under the Treaty of Brétigny. He also campaigned in Spain, intervening in the Castilian Civil War on behalf of Henry of Trastámara.

Death and legacy

Du Guesclin died while besieging Châteauneuf-de-Randon in 1380. He was granted the exceptional honour of burial among the kings of France at the Basilica of Saint-Denis, and he remains a French national hero.
