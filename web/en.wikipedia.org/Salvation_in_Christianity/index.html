Salvation in Christianity

In Christianity, salvation (also called deliverance or redemption) is the saving of human beings from sin and its consequences, above all death and separation from God, accomplished through the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ. The academic study of salvation is called soteriology.

Views across traditions

Roman Catholic teaching holds that salvation is a process involving faith, baptism, and cooperation with God's grace. Eastern Orthodoxy emphasises theosis, the transformation of the believer through union with God. Protestant traditions, rooted in the Reformation, generally stress justification by faith alone (sola fide), though they differ on the roles of grace, free will, and perseverance.

Related concepts

Salvation is closely linked to ideas of atonement, justification, sanctification, and eschatological hope in eternal life and the resurrection of the dead.
