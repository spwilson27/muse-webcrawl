The Needles

The Needles are a row of three chalk sea stacks rising from the sea off the western extremity of the Isle of Wight in the English Channel, close to Alum Bay. They form one of the most famous coastal landmarks in England. A lighthouse, built in 1859, stands at the seaward end of the formation.

Formation and history

The stacks are the eroded remnants of the chalk ridge that once connected the Isle of Wight to the mainland at Old Harry Rocks in Dorset. Their name derives from a fourth, needle-shaped pinnacle called Lot's Wife, which collapsed into the sea during a storm in 1764. The surviving stacks remain a hazard to shipping in the Needles Channel, guarded by the red-and-white banded lighthouse, automated in 1994.

Tourism

The Needles are the centrepiece of a popular visitor attraction above Alum Bay, reached by a chairlift descending the multicoloured sand cliffs. Boat trips run around the stacks in summer, and the headland also preserves the remains of the Old and New Needles Batteries and a Cold War rocket-testing site at High Down.
