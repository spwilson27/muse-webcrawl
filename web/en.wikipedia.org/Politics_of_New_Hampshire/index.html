Politics of New Hampshire

The New Hampshire State House in Concord.

The politics of New Hampshire are shaped by the state's small population, its large citizen legislature, and its traditional role holding the first presidential primary in the United States. New Hampshire's motto, "Live Free or Die", reflects a political culture that prizes limited government and low taxes.

State government

The New Hampshire General Court is among the largest legislative bodies in the English-speaking world, with a 400-member House of Representatives and a 24-member Senate, whose members serve two-year terms and are paid only $100 per year. The governor also serves a two-year term — one of only two states (with Vermont) without four-year gubernatorial terms — and shares executive authority with an independently elected five-member Executive Council.

The state has neither a general sales tax nor a personal income tax on wages, relying heavily on property taxes, a distinctive fiscal structure central to its politics.

Federal politics and the primary

Historically Republican, New Hampshire became a genuine swing state from the 1990s onward, voting for Democratic presidential candidates in most recent elections while frequently electing Republican governors and splitting its legislature. State law requires its presidential primary to be the first in the nation, held at least a week before any similar contest; candidates campaign intensely in small retail-politics settings, and an upset or strong showing in New Hampshire can transform a candidacy.
