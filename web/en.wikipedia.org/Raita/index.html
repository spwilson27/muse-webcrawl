Raita

Raita
Condiment in South Asian cuisine
Yogurt (curd) with raw or cooked vegetables, herbs and spices

Raita is a side dish in Indian cuisine made of dahi (yogurt, often called curd in Indian English) together with raw or cooked vegetables, fruit, herbs and spices. It is served chilled as a cooling accompaniment to spicy main dishes such as biryani, kebabs and curries — it is similar in role, though different in texture and seasonings, to the Greek tzatziki and Turkish cacık.

Preparation and variations

The base is whisked yogurt seasoned with salt, roasted cumin and sometimes chaat masala, mustard seeds or curry leaves tempered in oil. Common additions include cucumber, onion, tomato, mint, carrot, pineapple, pomegranate seeds and boondi (fried chickpea-flour pearls). Sweet raitas with banana or mango also exist. In South India, the closely related dish is called pachadi.

Role in the meal

Raita's coolness and acidity balance hot and rich dishes; perception of capsaicin heat is reduced by the casein and fat in yogurt. It also serves as a dip for breads and snacks such as papadums.
