Stoke-on-Trent (often known simply as Stoke) is a city and unitary authority area in Staffordshire, England, with a population of about 260,000. It is a polycentric city formed in 1910 by the federation of six towns — Hanley, Burslem, Tunstall, Longton, Fenton and Stoke-upon-Trent — along the River Trent. Renowned as the Potteries, it has been the centre of the English pottery industry since the 17th century.

The pottery industry

Abundant local coal and clay made North Staffordshire the heart of ceramics production. Firms founded here include Wedgwood (Josiah Wedgwood, the "Father of English Potters"), Spode, Minton, Royal Doulton and Portmeirion; bone china was developed by Josiah Spode II around 1800. The skyline was once dominated by thousands of coal-fired bottle ovens, of which a few dozen survive as listed monuments, including at the Gladstone Pottery Museum in Longton.

Culture and sport

The novelist Arnold Bennett, born in Hanley, set his "Five Towns" novels in a lightly fictionalised version of the area. The city is home to two professional football clubs, Stoke City (founded 1863, among the world's oldest) and Port Vale, whose name derives from the valley of ports on the Trent and Mersey Canal. Staffordshire University and the main campus of Keele University nearby serve the area.

Transport

Stoke-on-Trent railway station lies on the West Coast Main Line with services to London, Manchester and Birmingham; the Trent and Mersey Canal passes through the city.
