Saadi Shirazi

Abū-Muhammad Muslih al-Dīn bin Abdallāh Shīrāzī (1210 – 1291 or 1292), better known by his pen name Saadi, also known as Saadi of Shiraz, was a Persian poet and prose writer of the medieval period. He is recognized for the quality of his writings and for the depth of his social and moral thoughts.

Saadi is widely recognized as one of the greatest poets of the classical literary tradition, earning him the nickname "the Master of Speech" or simply "Master" among Persian scholars. He has been quoted in Western traditions as well.

Life

Saadi was born in Shiraz, Iran, around 1210. He studied in Baghdad, where he attended the Nizamiyya university, and after his studies he travelled widely through the Middle East, Anatolia, Egypt and Arabia for some thirty years before returning to Shiraz in 1256.

Works

His best known works are Bustan ("The Orchard"), completed in 1257, a book of poetry in rhyming couplets, and Gulistan ("The Rose Garden"), completed in 1258, a mixture of prose and verse containing stories and personal anecdotes. His poem Bani Adam ("Children of Adam"), famous for its celebration of human solidarity, adorns the entrance of the United Nations headquarters in New York and is quoted in Iranian and international settings.

Legacy

Saadi's tomb is located in the north-east of Shiraz. His works have been translated into many languages, and his counsels on ethics, society and governance influenced Persian literature for centuries.
