Crimean-Nogai raids into East Slavic lands

The Crimean–Nogai raids were a series of slave raids conducted by the Khanate of Crimea and the Nogai Horde into the East Slavic lands — present-day Russia, Ukraine, and parts of Poland and Moldavia — from the late 15th century until the late 18th century. The raids were a central economic activity of the Crimean Khanate: captives, known as the "harvest of the steppe", were sold at the great slave market of Caffa (Kefe) and exported throughout the Ottoman Empire and beyond. Historians estimate that millions of East Slavs were captured over the three centuries of raiding.

Background

The Crimean Khanate, founded in 1441, became an Ottoman vassal in 1475. Its cavalry forces, joined by the nomadic Nogais of the steppe north of the Black and Caspian seas, raided the settled lands of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the Grand Duchy of Moscow (later the Tsardom of Russia) along established routes such as the Muravsky Trail and the Black Trail. Major expeditions reached deep into Slavic territory: in 1571 Crimean forces under Devlet I Giray burned Moscow, and raids devastated Kyiv, Galicia, and Podolia repeatedly.

The slave trade

Captives were driven south in coffles to Caffa, where Genoese and later Ottoman merchants bought them for sale in Constantinople and other markets. Slavery supplied agricultural labour, domestic servants, soldiers, and concubines across the Ottoman world. The trade was so central to Crimean finances that regular large-scale raiding continued even in peacetime, despite treaties in which Moscow and Warsaw paid subsidies — effectively tribute — intended to buy peace at the frontier.

Defensive measures

The Slavic states responded with fortified defensive lines (the Russian zasechnaya cherta, or abatis lines), frontier garrisons, and the settlement of military colonists. In Ukraine, the Zaporozhian Cossacks emerged as a frontier military society that both resisted the raids and launched retaliatory sea and land expeditions against Crimea and the Ottoman coast. Gradually, the extension of fortified lines southward and the growth of Russian military power reduced the raiders' reach.

End of the raids

The last major Crimean raid took place in 1769, during the Russo-Turkish War of 1768–1774. By the Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca (1774) the khanate became independent of the Ottomans, and in 1783 Russia annexed Crimea outright, ending the raiding system that had shaped the steppe frontier for three centuries.
