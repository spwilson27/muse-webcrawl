Equifax Inc. is an American multinational consumer credit reporting agency headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia. It is one of the three largest consumer credit reporting agencies, along with Experian and TransUnion. Equifax collects and aggregates information on over 800 million individual consumers and more than 88 million businesses worldwide.

History

The company was founded in 1899 as the Retail Credit Company by brothers Cator and Guy Woolford in Atlanta. By the 1920s it had offices across the United States and Canada and sold insurance reports as well as credit reports. Following criticism of its privacy practices and the passage of the Fair Credit Reporting Act in 1970, the company renamed itself Equifax in 1975 to reflect its focus on information services.

Products and services

Equifax sells credit reports, credit scores, and credit monitoring products to businesses and consumers. Through its Workforce Solutions division (built around The Work Number database) it sells employment and income verification services to employers and lenders. It also provides fraud prevention, identity verification, and marketing data services, and operates in more than two dozen countries.

2017 data breach

In September 2017, Equifax announced a data breach that exposed the personal information of approximately 147 million people in the United States — including names, Social Security numbers, birth dates, and addresses — as well as data of consumers in the United Kingdom and Canada. Attackers exploited a known vulnerability in the Apache Struts web application framework that the company had failed to patch. The breach led to the resignation of senior executives and a settlement with US regulators and state attorneys general of at least $575 million, potentially rising to $700 million.
