Popeye the Sailor Man is a fictional cartoon character created by Elzie Crisler Segar. The character first appeared on January 17, 1929, in the daily King Features comic strip Thimble Theatre. The strip was in its tenth year when Popeye made his debut, but the one-eyed sailor quickly became the main focus of the strip, and Thimble Theatre soon became one of King Features' most popular properties during the early 1930s. The character has also appeared in theatrical and television animated cartoons.

Segar's Thimble Theatre strip was already well established, but it was the introduction of Popeye, cast as a minor walk-on character, that made the strip a huge success. When Popeye appeared, he became so popular that he was given a larger role, and the strip was expanded in many newspapers. In response, initial strips featuring the character led him to be featured on the cover of its own spin-off comic books.

Popeye is known for his catchphrases "I yam what I yam an' tha's all I yam" and "Well, blow me down!", his squinting right eye, bulging forearms, and his love of spinach, which grants him superhuman strength. His distinctive speech is famously mumbled, and he is often depicted smoking a corncob pipe.

History

Popeye was created by E. C. Segar, a cartoonist based in Chester, Illinois. According to local tradition, the character was inspired by Frank "Rocky" Fiegel, a Polish immigrant bartender in Chester known for his strength and his pipe. Segar had worked on Thimble Theatre since 1919, developing the adventures of Olive Oyl and her family; Popeye was hired as a sailor for one story arc but quickly took over the strip.

After Segar's death in 1938, the strip was continued by several writers and artists, most notably Bud Sagendorf, Segar's longtime assistant. Popeye also became the star of a long-running series of animated shorts produced by Fleischer Studios and later Famous Studios for Paramount Pictures, beginning with the 1933 Betty Boop cartoon Popeye the Sailor.

Characteristics

Popeye is a middle-aged sailor with a distinctive squint, a bulbous nose, enormous forearms tattooed with anchors, and a gravelly mumbling voice. His defining trait is his consumption of spinach, which, since the early 1930s cartoons, gives him superhuman strength sufficient to defeat his much larger rival Bluto (also known as Brutus). His girlfriend is Olive Oyl, and his adopted son is Swee'Pea. His arch-enemy in early strips included the Sea Hag. Popeye's friend J. Wellington Wimpy is famous for the line "I would gladly pay you Tuesday for a hamburger today."

In other media

Popeye has appeared in hundreds of animated cartoons, television series, comic books, video games, and a 1980 live-action feature film, Popeye, directed by Robert Altman and starring Robin Williams in his film debut as Popeye and Shelley Duvall as Olive Oyl. The character remains a widely recognized icon of American popular culture.
