The Luberon (Provençal: Leberon) is a mountain massif and celebrated rural region in Provence, southern France, stretching about 60 kilometres east–west south of Apt between the Durance river and the Calavon valley. Its highest summit is the Mourre Nègre (1,125 m). The range divides into the Grand Luberon, Petit Luberon and Luberon Oriental.

Landscape and nature

The massif's limestone ridges, cedar forests (notably the cedar forest planted from 1861 near Bonnieux), oak woods and garrigue support a rich Mediterranean flora and fauna, including Bonelli's eagles and Egyptian vultures. The Luberon Regional Natural Park, created in 1977, protects the area's landscapes, dry-stone architecture (bories) and traditional farming of lavender, cherries, grapes and olives.

Villages and culture

The Luberon is famous for its perched medieval villages — Gordes, Roussillon (with its ochre cliffs), Lacoste, Lourmarin, Ménerbes and Bonnieux among them — several ranked among the "most beautiful villages of France." The region's ochre quarries at Roussillon and Rustrel supplied natural pigments worldwide. Peter Mayle's bestselling memoir A Year in Provence (1989), set near Ménerbes, made the Luberon internationally famous and accelerated tourism and second-home development.

History

The area was settled in prehistory and romanized under the Empire. In the 16th century, Waldensian (Vaudois) villagers of the Luberon suffered persecution, culminating in the 1545 massacres ordered after the Edict of Mérindol. During the Second World War the maquis of the Luberon formed part of the French Resistance.
