Communalism (Bookchin)

Communalism is a political philosophy developed by the American theorist Murray Bookchin in his later work, drawing on his broader system of social ecology and libertarian municipalism. It proposes a confederation of directly democratic citizens' assemblies in towns and cities, which would gradually displace the nation-state and administer the economy as a commons.

Principles

Bookchin argued that municipalities — not the workplace or the state — are the natural arena for a free politics. Citizens in popular assemblies would make policy, while mandated, recallable delegates coordinate at confederal councils with strictly administrative functions. The economy would be municipalised: land and enterprises held by the community and allocated by the assemblies according to need, replacing both capitalism and state socialism.

Development

Bookchin coined "communalism" in the 1990s and 2000s to give libertarian municipalism a distinct name and to distance it from what he criticised as lifestyle anarchism. His writings were summarised in works such as The Next Revolution (2015, posthumous).

Influence

Through Abdullah Öcalan's prison reading of Bookchin, communalist ideas shaped "democratic confederalism", the ideology later implemented in the autonomous administration of North and East Syria (Rojava), where communal assemblies form the base of governance.
