Gilgit-Baltistan

Gilgit-Baltistan is a region administered by Pakistan as an administrative territory in the far north of the country. Formerly known as the Northern Areas, it borders Afghanistan's Wakhan Corridor to the north, China's Xinjiang region to the northeast, and the Indian-administered territory of Ladakh to the southeast. It is part of the larger Kashmir region, which has been disputed between India and Pakistan since 1947.

Geography

Gilgit-Baltistan is one of the most mountainous regions on Earth. Three great ranges meet here — the Karakoram, the western Himalayas, and the Hindu Kush — including K2, the world's second-highest mountain at 8,611 metres, and several of the longest glaciers outside the polar regions, such as the Siachen and Baltoro glaciers. The region covers about 72,971 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 1.5 million. The capital is Gilgit, and the largest city is Skardu.

History and status

Before 1947 the area was part of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir. Following the Gilgit Scouts' rebellion in November 1947, the region came under Pakistani administration. Unlike Pakistan's provinces, Gilgit-Baltistan is not formally part of the Pakistani federation under its constitution; it is governed through federal orders, with an elected legislative assembly and a chief minister, most recently under the Gilgit-Baltistan Order of 2018. Its people speak a variety of languages, including Shina, Balti, Burushaski, Wakhi, and Khowar.

Economy and tourism

The Karakoram Highway, among the highest paved international roads in the world, links the region to China through the Khunjerab Pass and is a key corridor of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor. The region is renowned for mountaineering, trekking, and dramatic scenery, drawing climbers attempting K2, Nanga Parbat, and other eight-thousanders.
