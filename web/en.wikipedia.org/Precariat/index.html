Precariat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Term coined from"Precarious" + "proletariat"
Popularised byGuy Standing, The Precariat: The New Dangerous Class (2011)
Defining featuresInsecure labour, unstable income, lack of occupational identity

The precariat is a social class formed by people suffering from precarity: a condition of existence without predictability or security, typically in relation to insecure employment, income and social rights. The term blends "precarious" with "proletariat" and was popularised by the economist Guy Standing.

Definition

According to Standing, members of the precariat lack the forms of labour-related security once common in industrial economies—employment security, job security, income security and representation security. They typically move between temporary, part-time, agency or gig work, lack an occupational narrative, and often have qualifications above the level of the jobs available to them.

Origins

The word was used by French sociologists in the 1980s and by Italian labour activists in the EuroMayDay protests of the early 2000s. Standing's 2011 book argued that globalisation and labour-market flexibility had produced the precariat as a growing, potentially politically volatile class-in-the-making.

Political responses

Standing advocates policies including basic income to provide security. Critics question whether the precariat is a coherent class, argue that precarious work long predates recent globalisation, or note the term's overlap with established categories such as the working poor.
