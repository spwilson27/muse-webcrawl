Ötzi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: c. 3345 BCE, near present-day Italy/Austria border region
Died: c. 3300 BCE, Ötztal Alps
Discovered: 19 September 1991

Ötzi, also called the Iceman, is the natural mummy of a man who lived between about 3350 and 3105 BCE. The mummy was found in September 1991 by two German tourists in the Ötztal Alps on the border between Austria and Italy, at an elevation of 3,210 metres. He is Europe's oldest known natural human mummy and has offered an unprecedented view of Chalcolithic (Copper Age) Europeans.

Discovery and examination

Ötzi's body and belongings have been scientifically analysed by many institutions. He was about 1.60 m tall and weighed roughly 50 kg, and was about 45 years old when he died. Analysis showed he had eaten meals of chamois, red deer meat and einkorn wheat shortly before his death, suffered from whipworm and Lyme disease bacteria, and carried dozens of tattoos, among the oldest known. His equipment included a copper axe of unusually high purity, a flint knife, a bow, a quiver of arrows, and clothing of hide and grass.

Cause of death

A flint arrowhead found lodged in his left shoulder indicates that Ötzi bled to death after being shot; he also had head injuries and defensive wounds. His body is preserved in a specially designed cold cell at the South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology in Bolzano, Italy.
