Bellwether

A bellwether is a leader or indicator of future trends. The term derives from the Middle English bellewether, the practice of placing a bell around the neck of a castrated ram (a wether) who leads a flock of sheep; the movement of the flock could be detected by the sound of the bell.

Politics

In politics, a bellwether is a constituency, state or county whose electoral results reliably mirror those of the nation as a whole, making it a useful predictor of overall outcomes. In the United States, Missouri was long regarded as a bellwether state, having voted for the winning presidential candidate in all but one election from 1904 to 2004; Ohio is another frequently cited example. In the United Kingdom, constituencies such as Gravesham and Dartford have served as bellwethers.

Economics and other uses

In finance, a "bellwether stock" is a large, well-established company, such as a market leader, whose performance is taken as an indicator of the health of its industry or the wider market. The term is also used in sociology and cultural commentary for whatever heralds broader social change.
