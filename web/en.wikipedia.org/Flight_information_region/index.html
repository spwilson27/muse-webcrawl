Flight information region

A flight information region (FIR) is an aviation term used to describe airspace with specific dimensions, within which flight information service and alerting service are provided. The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) divides the world's airspace into FIRs; a country is normally assigned the FIR above its territory, and may also be responsible for oceanic airspace.

Purpose and structure

Within an FIR, air navigation service providers supply flight information (weather, aerodrome and hazard notices) and alerting services for aircraft in distress. Controlled airspace within an FIR is further divided into sectors and may include an upper information region (UIR) for high-altitude traffic. FIR boundaries need not follow national borders — for example, some small states' airspace is managed by neighbours, and large oceanic FIRs are administered by designated states.

Examples

The United Kingdom is covered by the London and Scottish FIRs; the United States has numerous FIRs administered by the FAA, such as Oakland Oceanic. Delegation and coordination between FIRs allow seamless hand-off of flights along international routes, supported by ICAO standards and regional air navigation agreements.
