The Grand National Assembly of Turkey (Turkish: Türkiye Büyük Millet Meclisi, TBMM), usually referred to simply as the TBMM or Meclis, is the unicameral Turkish legislature. It is the sole body given the legislative prerogatives by the Turkish Constitution. It was founded in Ankara on 23 April 1920, during the Turkish War of Independence, in defiance of the Allied occupation of Istanbul and the Ottoman government, and it proclaimed the Republic of Turkey in 1923.

History

Convened under Mustafa Kemal (Atatürk), the Assembly governed the nationalist resistance, conducted the War of Independence, abolished the sultanate (1922) and the caliphate (1924), and enacted the sweeping Kemalist reforms. Turkey held a multi-party system after 1950, but the parliament was repeatedly interrupted by military interventions (1960, 1971, 1980) and the failed coup attempt of 15 July 2016, during which the Assembly building was bombed while deputies were inside. The 2017 constitutional referendum transformed Turkey into a presidential system, curtailing some of the Assembly's oversight powers while retaining its legislative monopoly.

Composition and elections

The Assembly has 600 members, elected for five-year terms by proportional representation (D'Hondt method) in 87 electoral districts, generally matching the provinces. A national electoral threshold — long 10%, the highest in Europe — was lowered to 7% in 2022. Deputies enjoy parliamentary immunity for votes and speech, and parties need at least 20 deputies to form a parliamentary group.

Functions

Under the constitution the Assembly enacts, amends and repeals laws; adopts the budget; authorizes declarations of war and the dispatch of troops abroad; ratifies international treaties; and elects members of the Constitutional Court and other high bodies. Since 2018, executive power is vested in the directly elected president, who may issue decrees in matters not regulated by statute; the Assembly may override presidential vetoes by absolute majority.

Building

The current parliament building, designed by Austrian architect Clemens Holzmeister and completed in 1961, is the third home of the Assembly in Ankara's Bakanlıklar district, and is considered a landmark of modern Turkish architecture.
