Apple Inc. is an American multinational technology company headquartered in Cupertino, California. It designs, develops and sells consumer electronics, computer software and online services, and is widely regarded as one of the "Big Tech" companies. Apple is the world's largest technology company by revenue and among the most valuable publicly traded companies in history, and its iPhone is the best-selling smartphone line in the world by unit sales profitability.

History

Apple was founded on 1 April 1976 by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak and Ronald Wayne as Apple Computer Company to sell Wozniak's Apple I personal computer. The Apple II, launched in 1977, became one of the first highly successful mass-produced microcomputers. The company went public in 1980. In 1984 Apple introduced the Macintosh, the first successful personal computer with a graphical user interface, famously launched with the "1984" Super Bowl advertisement. Following internal conflicts, Jobs left in 1985 and founded NeXT.

Apple struggled through the early 1990s against Microsoft Windows-based PCs. In 1997, near bankruptcy, it acquired NeXT, bringing Jobs back; he became CEO and, with designer Jony Ive, launched the iMac (1998), iPod (2001), iTunes Store (2003) and iPhone (2007), which redefined the smartphone industry. The iPad followed in 2010. Jobs resigned for health reasons in August 2011 and died that October; Tim Cook succeeded him as CEO.

Products and services

Apple's hardware includes the iPhone, Mac computers, iPad tablets, Apple Watch, AirPods and Apple TV, increasingly built around its own Apple silicon chips. Its software platforms include iOS, macOS, watchOS and tvOS. Services — the App Store, Apple Music, iCloud, Apple Pay, Apple TV+ and others — have become a major and growing share of revenue. Apple designs its products in California and relies on contract manufacturers, principally in Asia, for assembly.

Corporate affairs

Apple became the first publicly traded U.S. company valued at $1 trillion in 2018, and later surpassed $2 trillion and $3 trillion valuations. The company is known for its secrecy, its integration of hardware, software and services, and its premium branding. It has also faced criticism and regulatory scrutiny over App Store policies, tax arrangements, labour conditions in its supply chain, environmental impact and repair restrictions.
