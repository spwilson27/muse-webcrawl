Otho (Marcus Salvius Otho; 28 April 32 – 16 April 69 CE) was Roman emperor from 15 January to 16 April 69 CE, the second ruler of the Year of the Four Emperors. A companion of Nero's youth and former husband of Poppaea Sabina, he governed Lusitania for ten years before backing Galba's revolt against Nero; when Galba adopted another successor, Otho had him murdered and seized power himself.

Rise and reign

Otho had been exiled to Lusitania after Nero took Poppaea as his mistress and wife. As governor he administered the province capably. In 68 he supported Galba's bid for the throne, expecting to be named heir. When Galba instead adopted Lucius Calpurnius Piso in January 69, Otho conspired with the Praetorian Guard, who proclaimed him emperor after murdering Galba and Piso in the Forum on 15 January.

Fall

Meanwhile the legions of Germania had proclaimed their commander Vitellius. Otho marched north with an improvised army, won minor engagements near Cremona, but was defeated at the First Battle of Bedriacum on 14 April 69. Rather than prolong civil war, Otho took his own life two days later — an act of self-sacrifice that surprised contemporaries who had known him as a pleasure-loving courtier, and which earned him a measure of posthumous respect from historians such as Tacitus and Suetonius.
