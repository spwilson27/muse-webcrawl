Autoinducer

Autoinducer
ClassSignalling molecules
RoleQuorum sensing in bacteria
ExamplesN-acyl homoserine lactones (AI-1); AI-2; autoinducing peptides

Autoinducers are signalling molecules produced and released by bacteria as part of quorum sensing, a system of cell-to-cell communication that allows a bacterial population to coordinate gene expression according to its density.

Mechanism

Bacteria secrete autoinducers into their environment. As the population grows, the concentration of the signal increases; when it passes a threshold, receptors trigger changes in gene expression across the population, switching on collective behaviours such as bioluminescence, virulence factor production, biofilm formation and competence.

Types

Gram-negative bacteria commonly use N-acyl homoserine lactones (AHLs), detected by LuxR-type regulators, a system first characterised in the luminous bacterium Aliivibrio fischeri. Gram-positive bacteria typically use processed oligopeptides. Autoinducer-2 (AI-2), derived from the LuxS enzyme product, is used by many species and is sometimes described as a near-universal bacterial signal.
