Tupac Inca Yupanqui

Tupac Inca Yupanqui, the great conqueror of the Inca Empire

Tupac Inca Yupanqui or Túpac Inca Yupanqui (Quechua: Tupaq Inka Yupanki, c. 1441 – 1493) was the tenth Sapa Inca (1471–1493) of the Inca Empire and fifth of the Hanan dynasty. The son of Pachacuti, he is credited with expanding the empire to much of its greatest extent.

Conquests

Before succeeding his father, Tupac Inca led military campaigns that conquered the Chimú Empire in the north — capturing its capital Chan Chan — and subdued peoples of the northern Andes in modern Ecuador, reportedly reaching as far as Quito. As emperor, he extended Inca rule southward into modern Chile, as far as the Maule River, and eastward toward the edges of Amazonia, and administered campaigns in the Collao region around Lake Titicaca. Under his reign the empire stretched from present-day Colombia and Ecuador to central Chile and Argentina.

Administration and legend

Tupac Inca is said to have reorganised the empire into four quarters (suyus), hence its Quechua name Tawantinsuyu, "the four parts together", and to have developed administrative systems including quipu record-keeping and the mit'a labour tax. Spanish chroniclers also recorded a legend that he led a great sea voyage on balsa rafts to distant Pacific islands — a story sometimes connected with the Galápagos or other Polynesian-contact theories, though it remains unproven.

Death and succession

He died around 1493, possibly poisoned, at Chinchero. He was succeeded by his son Huayna Capac, under whom the empire reached its greatest territorial extent before the civil war between Huáscar and Atahualpa and the Spanish conquest.
