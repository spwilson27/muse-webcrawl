Battle of Afabet

Battle of Afabet
Date: 17–20 March 1988
Location: Afabet, Eritrea
Combatants: Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) vs Ethiopia (Derg)
Result: Decisive EPLF victory

The Battle of Afabet was a decisive battle of the Eritrean War of Independence, fought from 17 to 20 March 1988 around the garrison town of Afabet in northern Eritrea. The Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) encircled and destroyed the Ethiopian army's Nadew Command — a force of some 20,000 troops with armour, artillery, and air support — in one of the largest battles fought in Africa since the Second World War. The historian Basil Davidson called it the greatest victory achieved by a liberation movement since the Vietnamese victory at Dien Bien Phu.

Destroyed and captured vehicles littered the Hedai Valley after the battle.

Battle

The EPLF, some 15,000 fighters, attacked the Nadew Command's positions in the mountains around Afabet, breaching the Ethiopian defences and cutting the garrison's road of retreat through the Hedai Valley. Over three days the Ethiopian force collapsed; thousands were killed or captured, including Soviet military advisers taken prisoner, and enormous quantities of equipment — tanks, artillery, and armoured vehicles — were captured or destroyed. The road to the port of Massawa and the provincial capital Keren now lay open, though Massawa itself fell only in February 1990.

Afabet broke the back of the Ethiopian military effort in Eritrea and is widely regarded as the turning point that made Eritrean independence, achieved de facto in 1991 and confirmed by referendum in 1993, inevitable.
