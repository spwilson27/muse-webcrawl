Tempera
Medium: Pigment bound with egg yolk or another emulsion
Properties: Fast-drying, matte, durable
Peak use: Byzantine and early Renaissance panel painting

Tempera, also known as egg tempera, is a permanent, fast-drying painting medium consisting of coloured pigments mixed with a water-soluble binder, classically emulsified egg yolk. It was the principal method of painting on panel until the rise of oil paint after about 1500.

Technique

Tempera dries quickly to a matte finish and cannot be blended wet-in-wet like oil, so painters build up form with fine hatched brushstrokes. It is traditionally applied to rigid panels prepared with gesso. Works such as Sandro Botticelli's panels and Byzantine icons demonstrate its luminous, precise quality, and tempera paintings over 500 years old remain remarkably stable.

Later use

Though largely displaced by oils, tempera saw a revival among 20th-century artists such as Andrew Wyeth, and egg tempera is still taught and practised today. Modern "tempera" poster paints sold for schools are usually gouache-like and contain no egg.
