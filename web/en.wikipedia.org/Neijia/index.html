Neijia

Neijia (Chinese: 內家, "internal family") is a term in Chinese martial arts that groups the styles said to practise neijing, "internal strength," in contrast to waijia, "external" styles emphasizing muscular power. The three classic internal arts are tai chi (taijiquan), xingyiquan, and baguazhang.

Principles

Internal styles emphasize relaxed, coordinated whole-body movement, breath control, awareness, and the cultivation of qi, with roots in Taoist philosophy and the health practices of daoyin. Training typically stresses posture, slow form practice, and sensitivity drills such as pushing hands before fast or combative application.

Origins of the term

The distinction is traced to a 1669 epitaph by Huang Zongxi for the martial artist Wang Zhengnan, which contrasted an "internal school," said to descend from the Taoist immortal Zhang Sanfeng, with the "external" Shaolin school. Modern historians regard the lineage claims as legendary, and many practitioners note that advanced training in both categories converges on similar principles.
