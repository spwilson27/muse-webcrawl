Karl Menger

Born13 January 1902, Vienna, Austria-Hungary
Died5 October 1985, Highland Park, Illinois, USA
FieldsMathematics
Known forMenger's theorem, Menger sponge, dimension theory

Karl Menger (13 January 1902 – 5 October 1985) was an Austrian-American mathematician, the son of economist Carl Menger. He worked on the algebra of geometries, curve and dimension theory, and fuzzy sets, and founded the Mathematical Colloquium in Vienna, whose participants included Kurt Gödel.

Career

Menger studied at the University of Vienna under Hans Hahn, receiving his doctorate in 1924. Invited by L. E. J. Brouwer, he spent two years in Amsterdam before returning to Vienna, where he led the Mathematical Colloquium from 1928; its published proceedings included Gödel's incompleteness results. In 1937 he emigrated to the United States, teaching at the University of Notre Dame and later at the Illinois Institute of Technology in Chicago.

Mathematical work

In graph theory, Menger's theorem (1927) characterises the connectivity of a graph in terms of the number of disjoint paths between vertices, and underlies the max-flow min-cut theorem. In fractal geometry he introduced the Menger sponge, a three-dimensional generalisation of the Cantor set and Sierpiński carpet. He also developed an inductive definition of dimension, contributed to distance geometry and the theory of probabilistic metric spaces, and formulated the St. Petersburg paradox's resolution through unbounded utility considerations.
