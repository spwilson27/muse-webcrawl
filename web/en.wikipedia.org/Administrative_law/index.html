Administrative law

Administrative law is the body of law governing the activities and decision-making of administrative agencies and public authorities. It concerns the powers of agencies to make rules and adjudicate, and the means by which courts review those actions for legality, rationality and procedural fairness.

Scope

The field covers rulemaking, enforcement and adjudication by bodies regulating areas such as taxation, immigration, the environment, benefits and licensing. In the United States, the Administrative Procedure Act of 1946 supplies the basic framework; in the United Kingdom, judicial review applies common-law principles such as ultra vires, natural justice and Wednesbury reasonableness.

Principles

Core doctrines include legality, procedural fairness (notice and a hearing), reason-giving, and proportionality in human-rights contexts.
