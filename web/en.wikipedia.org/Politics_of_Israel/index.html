Politics of Israel

The Knesset in Jerusalem.

The politics of Israel take place within a parliamentary democratic republic. Israel is a unitary state whose legislature is the unicameral Knesset, a 120-member body elected by nationwide proportional representation with a low electoral threshold (3.25 percent). The head of government is the prime minister; the head of state is the president, a largely ceremonial office elected by the Knesset for a single seven-year term.

Constitutional framework

Israel has no single written constitution. Instead, the Knesset has enacted a series of Basic Laws since 1958 that together function as an evolving constitution, dealing with the institutions of state and, after 1992, with human dignity, liberty, and freedom of occupation. The Supreme Court, sitting as the High Court of Justice, exercises judicial review.

Party system

The pure proportional system produces many parties and makes coalition governments the norm; no party has ever won an outright Knesset majority. Historically dominant parties include Mapai/Labour, which governed from 1948 to 1977, and Likud, founded under Menachem Begin. The party landscape also includes religious parties (such as Shas and United Torah Judaism), Arab parties, and centrist and far-right parties, and coalition-building across these blocs shapes Israeli politics.

Issues

Central questions include the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and the status of the territories captured in 1967, the relationship between religion and state, the military draft for ultra-Orthodox Jews, and judicial reform — a proposed overhaul in 2023 provoking mass protests.
