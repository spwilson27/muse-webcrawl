Level sensor

Level sensors detect the level of liquids and other fluids and fluidised solids, including slurries, granular materials, and powders that exhibit an upper free surface. Substances that flow become essentially horizontal in their containers because of gravity, whereas most bulk solids pile at an angle of repose. The substance to be measured can be inside a container or in its natural form, such as a river or lake.

Measurement types

Level measurement is divided into point level measurement, which detects whether the substance reaches a particular point in the vessel (used for high- and low-level alarms and overfill protection), and continuous level measurement, which measures the level within a specified range and provides an output proportional to it.

Technologies

Sensing technologies include float switches and displacers; hydrostatic (pressure) devices; capacitance and conductivity probes; ultrasonic and radar (microwave) time-of-flight instruments; vibrating fork and rotating paddle point switches; optical sensors; and load cells that weigh the vessel contents. Selection depends on the medium, temperature, pressure, required accuracy, and whether contact with the process is acceptable.
