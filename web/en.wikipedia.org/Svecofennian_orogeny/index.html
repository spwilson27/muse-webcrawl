Svecofennian orogeny

The Svecofennian orogeny is a series of related orogenies (mountain-building events) that resulted in the formation of much of the continental crust of what is now Sweden and Finland, together with minor parts of Russia, during the Paleoproterozoic era. The orogeny developed roughly between 2.0 and 1.8 billion years ago as island arcs and microcontinents accreted against the older Archaean craton of the Baltic Shield.

Development

Geologists generally divide the orogeny into several overlapping phases, commonly named the Lapland–Savo, Fennian, Svecobaltic, and Nordic orogenies. Volcanic island arcs formed in an ancient ocean, and their sediments and volcanic rocks were compressed, folded, and intruded by enormous volumes of granitic magma as the arcs collided with the Archaean Karelian craton to the northeast. The process built a mountain range comparable in scale to a modern collision belt, though erosion over nearly two billion years has reduced it to a low-lying shield landscape.

Geology

The Svecofennian domain consists largely of metasedimentary rocks (including the greywackes and mica schists of the Bothnian Basin), metavolcanic belts such as the Skellefte district in Sweden — famous for its volcanogenic massive sulfide ore deposits of copper, zinc, gold, and silver — and vast granitoid batholiths such as the Transscandinavian Igneous Belt's early phases and the Central Finland Granitoid Complex. Deformation and metamorphism peaked during the Fennian phase around 1.89–1.87 billion years ago.

Significance

The Svecofennian orogeny forms part of the global assembly of the supercontinent Columbia (Nuna), when similar-age mountain belts formed on several continents. Its rocks host many of northern Europe's most important metal deposits and constitute the bedrock foundation of Finland and central Sweden.
