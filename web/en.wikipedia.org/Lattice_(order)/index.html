Lattice (order)

A lattice is an abstract structure studied in the mathematical subdisciplines of order theory and abstract algebra. It consists of a partially ordered set in which every pair of elements has a unique supremum (also called a least upper bound or join) and a unique infimum (greatest lower bound or meet). An example is given by the power set of a set, partially ordered by inclusion, for which the supremum is the union and the infimum is the intersection — the setting of Boolean algebras. Lattices can also be characterized as algebraic structures satisfying certain axiomatic identities, paralleling the theory of groups.

Definition

A partially ordered set (L, ≤) is a lattice if for every two elements a, b ∈ L, the set {a, b} has a join a ∨ b and a meet a ∧ b. Equivalently, a lattice is an algebraic structure (L, ∨, ∧) in which ∨ and ∧ are commutative, associative and idempotent binary operations satisfying the absorption laws: a ∨ (a ∧ b) = a and a ∧ (a ∨ b) = a. The two definitions are equivalent: given the algebra, one defines a ≤ b to mean a ∨ b = b.

Examples

  • The natural numbers with the usual ordering, with join = max and meet = min.
  • The positive integers under divisibility, with join = least common multiple and meet = greatest common divisor.
  • The power set of any set under inclusion, with join = union and meet = intersection.
  • The set of subspaces of a vector space under inclusion (a modular lattice).
  • The set of partitions of a set, ordered by refinement.

Special classes

A lattice is bounded if it has a greatest element (top, 1) and a least element (bottom, 0). It is distributive if join and meet distribute over each other; Boolean algebras and Heyting algebras are distributive complemented lattices of central importance in logic. A complete lattice is one in which all subsets have joins and meets, important in domain theory and the semantics of programming languages. Modular lattices, lying between distributive and general lattices, arise from the subgroups of abelian groups.

Morphisms and sublattices

A sublattice is a nonempty subset closed under joins and meets. A homomorphism between lattices is a map preserving joins and meets; order-preserving maps that preserve only arbitrary joins (or meets) play a role in the theory of complete lattices. The collection of all congruences on an algebra forms a lattice, a fact fundamental to universal algebra.

Applications

Lattice theory, systematized by Garrett Birkhoff in the 1930s and 1940s, is applied in formal concept analysis, data-flow analysis in compilers, access-control security models (lattice-based security labels), and the study of concept hierarchies in information science.
