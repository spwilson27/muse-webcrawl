Law enforcement in the Netherlands

A vehicle of the Dutch National Police Corps

Law enforcement in the Netherlands is provided by the National Police Corps (Nationale Politie), a single national police force responsible for general policing throughout the country, alongside specialized agencies such as the Royal Marechaussee.

Structure

Since a major reform on 1 January 2013, the Netherlands has had one unified police force organized into ten regional units, a central unit, and a police services centre. The force is headed by a Chief of Police and operates under the authority of the Minister of Justice and Security, while local public-order authority rests with mayors.

Other agencies

The Royal Marechaussee (Koninklijke Marechaussee) is a military police organization performing border control, airport security at Schiphol, and guarding duties. The Fiscal Information and Investigation Service (FIOD) investigates financial and fiscal crime, and municipal enforcement officers (boa's) handle minor offences and local bylaws.

Characteristics

Dutch policing emphasizes community policing through neighbourhood officers (wijkagenten). Most Dutch officers carry firearms but use them rarely; levels of police use of force are low by international comparison.
