Universal Studios Hollywood is a film studio and theme park in the San Fernando Valley area of Los Angeles County, California. About 70 percent of the studio lies within the unincorporated county island known as Universal City, while the rest lies within the city limits of Los Angeles. It is one of the oldest and most famous Hollywood film studios still in use.

History

Universal founder Carl Laemmle opened Universal City in 1915 and initially charged visitors 25 cents to watch films being made. Tours were discontinued with the advent of sound films but were revived on 15 July 1964, when the modern theme park opened with the Studio Tour tram ride through the backlot. The attraction grew steadily with stunt shows, special-effects demonstrations, and themed lands.

Attractions

The park's signature attraction is the Studio Tour, a roughly hour-long tram ride through working sound stages and famous backlot sets, incorporating experiences such as King Kong: 360 3-D and encounters with the shark from Jaws. Major themed areas include The Wizarding World of Harry Potter (opened 2016), Jurassic World – The Ride, Super Nintendo World (opened 2023), Springfield (based on The Simpsons), and rides based on Transformers, Despicable Me, and The Mummy.

The backlot and incidents

The Universal backlot has been damaged by a series of fires, most notably in 2008, when a three-alarm fire destroyed the King Kong Encounter, the New York Street and Courthouse Square sets, and a video vault containing tens of thousands of recordings. The studio remains an active production facility for film and television alongside its theme park operations.
