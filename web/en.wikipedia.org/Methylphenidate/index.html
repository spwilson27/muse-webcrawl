Methylphenidate

Trade namesRitalin, Concerta, Medikinet, others
ClassCentral nervous system stimulant
UsesADHD, narcolepsy
FormulaC14H19NO2

Methylphenidate, sold under the brand names Ritalin and Concerta among others, is a central nervous system stimulant used to treat attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and narcolepsy. It was first synthesised in 1944 by Leandro Panizzon at Ciba and marketed from the 1950s.

Mechanism of action

Methylphenidate blocks the dopamine and norepinephrine transporters, inhibiting the reuptake of these neurotransmitters and increasing their availability in the synaptic cleft, particularly in the prefrontal cortex and striatum. This improves attention, executive function and impulse control in people with ADHD.

Formulations and use

The drug is available in immediate-release and many extended-release oral formulations, as well as a transdermal patch. Effects of immediate-release preparations begin within about 30 minutes and last 3–5 hours. It is generally considered a first-line pharmacological treatment for ADHD in children over six, adolescents and adults.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include decreased appetite, insomnia, headache, dry mouth and increased heart rate and blood pressure. Methylphenidate has a potential for misuse and dependence and is a controlled substance in most countries.
