Newport (Welsh: Casnewydd) is a city and county borough in Wales, situated on the River Usk near its confluence with the Severn Estuary, 12 miles (19 km) northeast of Cardiff. With a population of about 160,000, it is the third-most populous city in Wales. Newport has been a port since medieval times, when the first Newport Castle was built by the Normans.

History

The city grew greatly in the 19th century, when its port became the focus of coal exports from the eastern South Wales Valleys. It was the scene of the last large-scale armed insurrection in Britain, the Newport Rising of 1839, in which Chartists led by John Frost marched on the town demanding political reform. In the 20th century the docks declined, but Newport gained Llanwern steelworks and later reinvented itself partly as a centre for electronics and public administration.

Landmarks

Newport is known for the Newport Transporter Bridge (1906), one of only eight such bridges still in use worldwide; the remains of Newport Castle; the Roman town of Caerleon, with its legionary fortress, amphitheatre and baths, at the city's northern edge; and the Celtic Manor Resort, host of the 2010 Ryder Cup. The city hosted a NATO summit in 2014.
