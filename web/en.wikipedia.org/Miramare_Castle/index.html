Miramare Castle

Miramare Castle (Italian: Castello di Miramare) is a 19th-century castle direct on the sea, with a park full of rare and exotic tree species, located on the Gulf of Trieste, north-east Italy. It was built from 1856 to 1860 for Austrian Archduke Ferdinand Maximilian and his wife, Charlotte of Belgium.

History

Maximilian, commander-in-chief of the Austrian navy and later the ill-fated Emperor Maximilian I of Mexico, chose the rocky promontory of Grignano for his residence and supervised the design by the Austrian architect Carl Junker. The castle's name derives from the Spanish mirar el mar, "to look at the sea". Maximilian and Charlotte lived there from 1860 until his departure for Mexico in 1864; after his execution in 1867, Charlotte, having lost her reason, returned briefly to Miramare. The castle later served as a residence of the Italian royal house and, after the Second World War, as headquarters of Allied forces in Trieste.

The castle and park

The ground floor preserves Maximilian's apartments, modelled on a ship's cabin in wood panelling, while the first floor holds the state rooms of the later Savoy dukes. The surrounding 22-hectare park, designed by Carl Junker with plantings directed by Maximilian himself, combines Mediterranean species with exotic trees from around the world and includes the smaller Castelletto, stables, and the jetty from which Maximilian embarked for Mexico.

Today

The castle is now a state museum administered by Italy's Ministry of Culture, and the marine area in front of it is a protected reserve, the first established in Italy.
