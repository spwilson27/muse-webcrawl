Hans Krebs (4 March 1901 – 1 May 1945) was a German Army general of infantry who served during World War II. He became the last Chief of the Army General Staff (Oberkommando des Heeres) of Nazi Germany in the final weeks of the war, succeeding Heinz Guderian in March 1945. He is not to be confused with the biochemist Hans Adolf Krebs, discoverer of the Krebs cycle.

Career

Krebs joined the Imperial German Army as a volunteer in 1914, was commissioned in the Reichswehr, and served in staff posts throughout the interwar period. Fluent in Russian, he served as a military attaché in Moscow before the war. During World War II he held senior staff positions, including chief of staff of the 9th Army and of Army Group Centre on the Eastern Front, and was promoted to General der Infanterie in 1944.

Final days in Berlin

Present in the Führerbunker during the Battle of Berlin, Krebs was sent by Joseph Goebbels after Hitler's suicide to negotiate with Soviet General Vasily Chuikov on 1 May 1945, informing him of Hitler's death and seeking a ceasefire. The Soviets demanded unconditional surrender, and Krebs, returning to the bunker, took his own life later that day.
