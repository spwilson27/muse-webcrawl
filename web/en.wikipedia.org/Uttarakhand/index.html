Uttarakhand

Uttarakhand
Capital: Dehradun (winter), Gairsain (summer)
Formed: 2000 (from Uttar Pradesh)
Regions: Garhwal and Kumaon

Uttarakhand is a Himalayan state in northern India, created in 2000 as Uttaranchal (renamed Uttarakhand in 2007) after a long movement for a separate hill state. It is often called Devbhumi, "Land of the Gods," for its many Hindu temples and pilgrimage sites.

Geography

The state spans the foothills and high ranges of the Himalayas, including Nanda Devi, and contains the headwaters of the Ganges and Yamuna. The Char Dham pilgrimage—Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, and Badrinath—draws millions of pilgrims.

Environment

Jim Corbett National Park, India's oldest, and the Valley of Flowers are located here. The state is prone to landslides and flash floods, most devastatingly in June 2013.
