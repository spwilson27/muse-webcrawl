Walpurgis Night (German: Walpurgisnacht), also known as Saint Walpurgis Eve, is the evening of 30 April, the eve of the feast day of Saint Walpurga, an 8th-century abbess in Francia. The night is celebrated in many northern and central European countries with bonfires, dancing, and other spring festivities, and it is associated in folklore with the gathering of witches.

History

Saint Walpurga (c. 710–777/9) was an Anglo-Saxon missionary to the Frankish Empire, canonized on 1 May 870, and her feast fell on that date. Over time the eve of her feast merged with older seasonal customs marking the arrival of spring. The night roughly halfway between the spring equinox and the summer solstice, it parallels the Celtic festival of Beltane on the opposite side of the year from Halloween.

Witches and the Brocken

In German folklore, Walpurgis Night was the night when witches met on the Brocken, the highest peak of the Harz mountains, and held revels with the Devil. The motif was immortalized by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe in Faust (1808), where Mephistopheles takes Faust to the Brocken for the Walpurgisnacht celebration. The association led to the term being used for any wild or chaotic night gathering, and it appears in works by composers such as Felix Mendelssohn (Die erste Walpurgisnacht) and in Modest Mussorgsky's Night on Bald Mountain.

Regional celebrations

Germany: bonfires (Hexenfeuer, "witches' fires") are lit to ward off evil spirits, and in some areas young people play pranks overnight, moving garden furniture or toilet-papering houses.

Sweden (Valborg): one of the country's major festivities, marked by bonfires, choral singing of traditional spring songs, and speeches to spring; students in university towns such as Uppsala and Lund celebrate with traditions including the river rafting race in Uppsala and the donning of student caps.

Finland (Vappu): combined with International Workers' Day, it is a major carnival-style celebration with picnics, balloons, sima (a fermented lemonade) and munkki (doughnuts); students wear their white graduation caps.

Czech Republic (pálení čarodějnic): "burning of the witches" — bonfires are lit and effigies of witches are burned.

Estonia (Volbriöö) and the Netherlands (Walpurgisnacht) also observe the night with fires and festivities.

In modern culture

Beyond Goethe, Walpurgis Night appears in works by Thomas Mann (The Magic Mountain), H. P. Lovecraft ("The Dreams in the Witch House"), Mikhail Bulgakov (The Master and Margarita), and Bram Stoker's short story "Dracula's Guest". Adolf Hitler's death on 30 April 1945 is sometimes noted in connection with the date.
