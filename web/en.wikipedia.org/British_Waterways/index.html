British Waterways

British Waterways
Formation: 1962 (as British Waterways Board)
Dissolved: 2012 (England and Wales functions transferred to the Canal & River Trust)
Type: Statutory corporation

British Waterways, often shortened to BW, was a statutory corporation wholly owned by the government of the United Kingdom. It served as the navigation authority for most of the canals and a number of rivers and docks in England, Scotland and Wales.

British Waterways was created by the Transport Act 1962 as the British Waterways Board, taking over the inland waterways assets of the British Transport Commission. At its height it was responsible for roughly 2,200 miles (3,541 km) of canals and rivers, together with reservoirs and docks.

History

Most of Britain's canals were nationalised in 1948 and placed under the British Transport Commission. The 1962 Act created the British Waterways Board to manage the waterways on a more commercial footing. The Transport Act 1968 classified the waterways into commercial, cruising and remainder categories. From the 1980s onward, leisure boating and regeneration of canal corridors grew in importance.

Transfer to charity

On 2 July 2012, the functions of British Waterways in England and Wales were transferred to a new charity, the Canal & River Trust. In Scotland, the organisation continued as British Waterways Scotland, trading as Scottish Canals.
