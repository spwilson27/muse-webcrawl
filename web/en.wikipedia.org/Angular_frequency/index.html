Angular frequency

In physics, angular frequency (symbol ω), also called angular speed and angular rate, is a scalar measure of the angle through which a rotating or oscillating system turns per unit of time. It is the magnitude of the pseudovector quantity angular velocity. For a system oscillating with ordinary frequency ν (nu), measured in hertz (cycles per second), the angular frequency is ω = 2πν radians per second — one complete cycle corresponds to an angle change of 2π radians. The SI unit of angular frequency is the radian per second (rad/s).

Definition and units

Angular frequency measures how fast the phase of a periodic phenomenon advances. Because one period T corresponds to a phase change of 2π, it follows that ω = 2π/T = 2πν. Although its SI dimension is the same as frequency (inverse time), the radian keeps explicit track of phase, which usually simplifies the mathematics of waves and oscillations. The term "angular speed" is often reserved for rotational motion, while "angular frequency" is more common for oscillations and waves, but the quantities are identical in kind.

Examples

For circular motion, a point moving with angular frequency ω has tangential speed v = ωr and centripetal acceleration a = ω2r. In simple harmonic motion, the position x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ) has ω = √(k/m) for a mass on a spring and ω = √(g/L) for a small-amplitude pendulum. In electrical engineering, AC mains at 50 Hz correspond to ω ≈ 314 rad/s, and the resonance of an LC circuit occurs at ω0 = 1/√(LC). In quantum mechanics, the Planck relation E = ħω connects a photon's energy to its angular frequency.

Angular velocity

More generally, angular velocity is a pseudovector ω whose direction follows the right-hand rule along the rotation axis and whose magnitude is the angular frequency. Angular frequency is accordingly the norm of that vector, and for non-uniform rotation it is defined instantaneously as dθ/dt.
