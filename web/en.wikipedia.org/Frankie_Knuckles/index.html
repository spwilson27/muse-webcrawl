Frankie Knuckles (January 18, 1955 – March 31, 2014) was an American DJ, record producer, and remixer widely known as the "Godfather of House Music". As the resident DJ of Chicago's Warehouse club from 1977, he helped pioneer house music — a genre named, in part, after the club where he played.

Early career

Born in the Bronx, Knuckles began DJing in New York alongside Larry Levan at venues such as the Continental Baths, playing disco, soul, and R&B records. In 1977 he moved to Chicago to become the resident DJ at the Warehouse, a members-only club catering largely to Black and Latino gay men.

The Warehouse and the birth of house

At the Warehouse, Knuckles blended disco classics, European electronic music, and obscure soul records, extending tracks with reel-to-reel edits and drum machines. Records sold as "Warehouse music" in local import shops were soon shortened to "house music" — giving the genre its name. After leaving the Warehouse in 1982, he opened his own club, the Power Plant, in 1983.

Production and remixing

Knuckles produced classics such as "Your Love" (1987, with Jamie Principle) and "Tears" (1989, with Satoshi Tomiie and Robert Owens). His remix work included celebrated versions for Michael Jackson, Diana Ross, Whitney Houston, and Depeche Mode. In 1998 he won the Grammy Award for Remixer of the Year, Non-Classical.

Legacy

Chicago honoured him in 2004 by renaming a stretch of Jefferson Street "Frankie Knuckles Way" and declaring August 25 "Frankie Knuckles Day". He died in 2014 of complications from type 2 diabetes. His influence on electronic dance music is considered foundational.
