Geek

The word geek is a slang term originally used to describe eccentric or non-mainstream people. In current use it typically denotes an expert or enthusiast obsessed with a hobby or intellectual pursuit. With the rise of technology and internet culture, the label has been reclaimed and is often worn as a badge of identity.

Etymology

The word derives from the Low German geck ("fool") and was related to carnival performers — the American circus "geek" was a sideshow performer whose act involved grotesque feats such as biting the heads off live animals. By the mid-20th century the term had shifted to mean a socially awkward, overly studious person.

Modern usage

During the 1990s and 2000s, the explosive popularity of computing, gaming, and fandom culture transformed "geek" into a largely positive self-identifier: a "computer geek," "gaming geek," or "comics geek" is someone with deep, passionate knowledge of a subject. This shift is sometimes called the mainstreaming of geek culture, reflected in the enormous commercial success of superhero films, conventions such as Comic-Con, and the tech industry.

Geek versus nerd

The terms are often used interchangeably but are sometimes distinguished: a geek is seen as defined by enthusiasm and fandom, a nerd by intellectual or technical study and social reserve. Many people embrace both labels.
