Selexipag

Selexipag, sold as Uptravi, is an oral prostacyclin receptor agonist for pulmonary arterial hypertension.

Selexipag, sold under the brand name Uptravi, is a medication used to treat pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). It delays disease progression and reduces the risk of hospitalisation for PAH. It is taken by mouth, usually twice daily, with the dose gradually increased to the highest tolerated level. It was developed by Nippon Shinyaku and Actelion (now part of Janssen/Johnson & Johnson) and approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in December 2015.

Mechanism of action

Selexipag is a selective agonist of the prostacyclin (IP) receptor. It is a prodrug whose active metabolite, ACT-333679, has a much longer half-life and greater potency at the receptor. Activating the IP receptor in pulmonary vascular smooth muscle raises cyclic AMP, producing vasodilation and inhibiting smooth-muscle proliferation — the same pathway targeted by prostacyclin analogues such as epoprostenol and treprostinil, but in an orally available, receptor-selective form designed to reduce the side effects of non-selective prostanoids.

Clinical evidence

The phase III GRIPHON trial, enrolling 1,156 patients, found that selexipag reduced the composite risk of death or a complication of PAH by about 40% compared with placebo, an effect driven mainly by reductions in disease progression and hospitalisation rather than mortality alone. Common side effects reflect prostacyclin activity: headache, diarrhoea, nausea, jaw pain, muscle pain and flushing. It should not be used with strong inhibitors of the liver enzyme CYP2C8.
