Schizophrenia

Schizophrenia is a mental disorder characterized by continuous or relapsing episodes of psychosis. Major symptoms include hallucinations (typically hearing voices), delusions, and disorganized thinking. Other symptoms include social withdrawal, decreased emotional expression, and lack of motivation. Symptoms typically develop gradually, begin during young adulthood, and in many cases never become fully resolved.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms are commonly grouped into positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech and behavior), negative symptoms (flat affect, poverty of speech, anhedonia, lack of motivation, and social withdrawal), and cognitive symptoms (problems with attention, working memory, and executive function). Diagnosis is based on observed behavior, reported experiences, and reports of others familiar with the person; there is no objective diagnostic test.

Causes

The causes of schizophrenia are thought to include genetic and environmental factors. Genetic factors include a variety of common and rare genetic variants; the disorder is highly heritable, with estimates around 80%. Environmental factors include being raised in a city, cannabis use during adolescence, infections, the ages of a person's parents, and malnutrition during pregnancy. About half of those diagnosed with schizophrenia have a lifetime history of substance use disorder.

Mechanism

Research has focused on the role of the neurotransmitter dopamine: the dopamine hypothesis proposes that psychosis results in part from excessive signaling at dopamine D2 receptors, which is consistent with the fact that dopamine-blocking antipsychotic medications are effective treatments. Abnormalities in glutamate signaling and in brain structure and connectivity have also been implicated.

Treatment

The mainstay of treatment is antipsychotic medication, along with counselling, job training, and social rehabilitation. About a third of people do not respond to initial antipsychotics, in which case the antipsychotic clozapine may be used, though it requires blood monitoring. In situations where there is a risk of harm to self or others, a brief involuntary hospitalization may be necessary.

Epidemiology and history

Schizophrenia affects about 1 in 300 people worldwide. It occurs about 1.4 times more frequently in males than females and typically appears earlier in men. The term was coined by the Swiss psychiatrist Eugen Bleuler in 1908, deriving from the Greek roots skhizein ("to split") and phrēn ("mind"); it does not mean a "split personality". Emil Kraepelin earlier described the condition as dementia praecox.
