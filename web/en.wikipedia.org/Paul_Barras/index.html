Paul Barras

Paul François Jean Nicolas, vicomte de Barras (30 June 1755 – 29 January 1829), commonly known as Paul Barras, was a French politician of the French Revolution, and the main executive leader of the Directory regime of 1795–1799.

Early life and revolution

Born into a noble family of Provence, Barras served as an officer in India before returning to France. Elected to the National Convention, he voted for the death of Louis XVI and gained prominence as a representative on mission in the south, where he met the young Napoleon Bonaparte at the Siege of Toulon. He played a leading role in the Thermidorian Reaction that overthrew Maximilien Robespierre in July 1794, and commanded the forces that suppressed the royalist rising of 13 Vendémiaire in 1795—famously delegating artillery work to Bonaparte.

The Directory

Barras was the only member to serve on the five-man Directory throughout its existence, becoming its dominant figure. His regime was notorious for corruption and for reliance on the army to annul election results, as in the coups of Fructidor (1797) and Floréal (1798). He was overthrown in Napoleon's coup of 18 Brumaire (November 1799), in which he acquiesced, and retired into private life. His memoirs, published posthumously, are a colourful if unreliable source on the period.
