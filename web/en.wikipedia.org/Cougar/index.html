The cougar (Puma concolor) is a large felid native to the Americas, also known as the puma, mountain lion, catamount, and panther, among many other names. Its range spans from the Canadian Yukon in the north to the southern Andes in the south, the widest of any large wild terrestrial mammal in the Western Hemisphere. An adaptable generalist species, it occurs in most American habitat types.

The cougar is the second-largest cat in the Americas after the jaguar. Despite its size, it is more closely related to smaller felines, including the domestic cat, than to the "big cats" of the genus Panthera, and it cannot roar. It is a solitary, largely nocturnal ambush predator, preying mainly on ungulates such as deer, but also on smaller animals. A capable stalk-and-ambush hunter, it has powerful hind legs that allow it to leap as far as 5.5 metres vertically and 12 metres horizontally.

Taxonomy and names

Linnaeus described the species in 1771 as Felis concolor. Genetic studies have collapsed the many historically proposed subspecies into a small number; recent classifications recognise two subspecies: the North American P. c. couguar and the South American P. c. concolor. The cougar holds the Guinness record for the animal with the greatest number of common names in English, with over forty, including painter, ghost cat, and deer tiger.

Behaviour and ecology

Cougars are territorial and maintain low population densities; home ranges vary with prey abundance and can exceed 300 km² for males. They avoid humans, and attacks on people are rare, though they have increased in parts of North America as cougar populations recover and human development expands into cougar habitat. Kittens are spotted and born with blue eyes, staying with their mother for up to two years.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, though the Florida panther — a population of the North American subspecies — remains endangered and has been the subject of intensive recovery efforts, including genetic restoration using Texas cougars. The eastern cougar was declared extinct by the US Fish and Wildlife Service in 2018, although individuals dispersing from western populations are increasingly recorded in the Midwest and East.
