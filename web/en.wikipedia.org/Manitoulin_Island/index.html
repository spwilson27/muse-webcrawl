Manitoulin Island is a Canadian island in Lake Huron, within the province of Ontario. With an area of 2,766 square kilometres (1,068 sq mi), it is the largest freshwater island in the world. The name Manitoulin derives from the Anishinaabe Manidoowaaling, "cave of the spirit," referring to an underwater cave said to be home to a powerful spirit.

Geography

The island itself contains more than 100 lakes, several of which contain their own islands. Lake Manitou, at 104 km², is the largest lake on a freshwater island in the world, and Treasure Island within Lake Mindemoya is the largest island in a lake on an island in a lake. The island is separated from the mainland by the North Channel; it is connected to the Bruce Peninsula by a swing bridge at Little Current and by the seasonal ferry Chi-Cheemaun between South Baymouth and Tobermory.

Indigenous peoples

Manitoulin is culturally significant to the Anishinaabe peoples — the Ojibwe, Odawa, and Potawatomi of the Council of Three Fires. A substantial part of the island is First Nations territory, including the Wiikwemkoong Unceded Territory, the only officially recognized unceded Indigenous reserve in Canada. Under the Robinson-Huron Treaty of 1850 and subsequent agreements, the island's Indigenous communities have maintained a strong presence, and roughly a third of the population is Anishinaabe.

Economy and tourism

Tourism, farming, and fishing are the main industries. Popular attractions include Bridal Veil Falls near Kagawong, the Cup and Saucer Trail along the Niagara Escarpment, and the Great Spirit Circle Trail's Indigenous cultural experiences. Archaeological evidence at sites such as Sheguiandah indicates human habitation going back some 10,000 years.
