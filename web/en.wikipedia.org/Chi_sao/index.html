Chi sao

Chi sao
Also known asSticking hands; chi shou (Mandarin)
ArtWing Chun kung fu
FocusSensitivity, trapping, close-range striking

Chi sao (Chinese: 黐手; Cantonese for "sticking hands") is a sensitivity and reflex-training exercise from the Chinese martial art of Wing Chun. Two practitioners maintain forearm contact and roll through structured positions, seeking openings while developing the ability to sense an opponent's pressure, direction, and intention through touch rather than sight.

Practice

Training begins with single-arm dan chi sao and progresses to the two-armed rolling cycle (poon sao) built from three arm positions: tan sao (palm-up hand), bong sao (wing arm) and fook sao (controlling hand). From the roll, partners practise trapping, striking, and countering techniques (luk sao jic koon), and advanced practice is sometimes done blindfolded to heighten tactile sensitivity.

Purpose

Chi sao develops automatic reflexes for the trapping range, teaching the student to yield to stronger force, redirect attacks along the centreline, and strike simultaneously with defence. It is sometimes described as a bridge between Wing Chun's forms and free sparring (gor sao), which builds directly on chi sao platforms by adding disengagement, kicks, and full-speed exchange.

Criticism

Some martial artists criticise overly cooperative chi sao as developing habits that may not transfer directly to unresisting opponents or full-contact fighting; proponents respond that it is a drill for sensitivity and structure rather than a simulation of combat itself.
