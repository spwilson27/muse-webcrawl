List of severe weather phenomena

Severe weather refers to dangerous meteorological phenomena with the potential to cause damage, serious social disruption, or loss of life. National weather services issue warnings when severe conditions — such as large hail, damaging winds, tornadoes, or heavy rainfall — are occurring or imminent. This article lists the principal types of severe weather.

Convective weather

PhenomenonDescription
TornadoA violently rotating column of air extending from a thunderstorm to the ground; rated on the Enhanced Fujita scale.
WaterspoutA tornado-like vortex over water.
SupercellA long-lived thunderstorm with a rotating updraft (mesocyclone), producing the strongest tornadoes and largest hail.
DerechoA widespread, long-lived wind storm associated with a line of severe thunderstorms.
Downburst / microburstA powerful downdraft spreading damaging winds outward at the surface.
HailstormPrecipitation of ice pellets; hailstones above about 2.5 cm (1 in) in diameter are considered severe.
Squall lineAn organised line of thunderstorms, often ahead of a cold front.
LightningElectrical discharge within storms; a leading cause of weather-related deaths in some countries.

Tropical weather

PhenomenonDescription
Tropical cyclone (hurricane / typhoon)An intense rotating storm system over warm oceans, bringing extreme winds, storm surge, and flooding rain.
Storm surgeAbnormal rise of seawater pushed ashore by a cyclone's winds; historically the deadliest cyclone hazard.
Monsoon floodingProlonged seasonal rainfall causing regional floods, especially in South and Southeast Asia.

Winter and other phenomena

PhenomenonDescription
BlizzardSevere snowstorm with winds above 56 km/h and visibility below 400 m for at least three hours.
Ice stormFreezing rain coating surfaces with damaging accumulations of ice.
Heat waveProlonged period of excessively hot weather; among the deadliest weather events.
Dust storm / haboobWall of dust driven by storm outflow, reducing visibility to near zero.
Flash floodRapid flooding of low-lying areas within minutes to hours of intense rain.
Nor'easterA powerful extratropical cyclone along the US East Coast bringing heavy snow, rain, and coastal flooding.
Firestorm conditions / fire weatherExtreme heat, low humidity, and strong winds promoting catastrophic wildfires, sometimes with pyrocumulonimbus clouds.
