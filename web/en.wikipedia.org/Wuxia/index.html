Wuxia

Wuxia (武俠, literally "martial heroes") is a genre of Chinese fiction about the adventures of martial artists in ancient China. Heroes — usually wandering swordsmen and swordswomen bound by a code of chivalry (xia) — right wrongs, fight tyranny and pursue mastery of extraordinary martial arts in the semi-outlaw world of the jianghu.

Characteristics

Wuxia protagonists typically master supernatural-seeming skills: qinggong (weightlessness techniques allowing rooftop leaps and walking on water), internal-energy (neigong) cultivation, pressure-point strikes and legendary weapons and manuals. Plots revolve around honour, loyalty, revenge, sect rivalries and the tension between personal freedom and duty to family, master or state. The genre is distinct from, but overlaps with, kung fu cinema and the later xianxia ("immortal heroes") genre, which adds gods, demons and cultivation toward immortality.

History

Roots lie in Tang-dynasty tales of wandering knights-errant and in historical biographies of assassins in Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian. The genre flowered in the Republican era and reached its classic form in mid-20th-century Hong Kong and Taiwan with authors such as Jin Yong (Louis Cha), Gu Long and Liang Yusheng, whose novels were serialised in newspapers and later adapted into countless films and television series. Ang Lee's Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (2000) and Zhang Yimou's Hero (2002) brought wuxia cinema to global audiences.
