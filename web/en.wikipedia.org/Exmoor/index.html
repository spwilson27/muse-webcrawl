Exmoor is a national park in west Somerset and north Devon in South West England. Named after the River Exe, whose source is in the park, it consists of open moorland, wooded valleys (locally called combes), farmland and a dramatic coastline on the Bristol Channel. The area was designated a national park in 1954, and its highest point is Dunkery Beacon at 519 metres (1,703 ft).

Landscape and wildlife

The moor is underlain mainly by Devonian grits, slates and sandstones. Exmoor is noted for its herds of red deer, and for the semi-feral Exmoor pony, one of Britain's oldest native pony breeds. The coastal cliffs between Minehead and Combe Martin include some of the highest sea cliffs in England, and the park was designated Europe's first International Dark Sky Reserve in 2011.

History

Mesolithic and Bronze Age remains dot the moor, and in the medieval period much of Exmoor was a royal forest reserved for hunting. Novelist R. D. Blackmore set his 1869 novel Lorna Doone in the Badgworthy valley of eastern Exmoor.
