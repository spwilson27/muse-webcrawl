Rotation (mathematics)

In mathematics, a rotation is a concept originating in geometry: any motion of a Euclidean space that keeps at least one point fixed and preserves distances and orientation. Rotations form groups under composition and are represented by orthogonal matrices with determinant +1.

In two and three dimensions

In the plane, a rotation about the origin through angle θ is (x cosθ − y sinθ, x sinθ + y cosθ), equivalent to multiplication by a complex number of unit modulus — a connection to the rotation group SO(2). In three dimensions, every rotation has a fixed axis (Euler's theorem), is specified by axis and angle, and can be parameterised by Euler angles, rotation matrices, axis–angle form or unit quaternions; the group is SO(3), doubly covered by SU(2).

Higher dimensions and applications

In four dimensions, rotations can have two independent planes of rotation. Rotations appear throughout physics, computer graphics, robotics and crystallography, where improper rotations add reflections. See also rotation, rotation matrix and quaternions.
