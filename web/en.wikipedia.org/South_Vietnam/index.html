South Vietnam, officially the Republic of Vietnam, was a state in Southeast Asia from 1955 to 1975, comprising the southern part of modern Vietnam below the 17th parallel. It emerged from the 1954 Geneva partition of French Indochina and fought North Vietnam in the Vietnam War.

History

Prime Minister Ngo Dinh Diem deposed Emperor Bảo Đại in a 1955 referendum and proclaimed the republic, refusing nationwide elections planned for 1956. Backed by the United States, his authoritarian rule and discrimination against Buddhists provoked crisis; he was overthrown and assassinated in a 1963 coup.

After unstable military juntas, Nguyen Van Thieu led the Second Republic from 1967. Despite massive US intervention, the 1973 Paris Peace Accords withdrew American forces. A North Vietnamese offensive in 1975 culminated in the Fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975; formal reunification as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam followed in 1976.

Politics and Society

South Vietnam was anti-communist, aligned with the US and SEATO protocols, with its capital Saigon a major commercial and military hub. Supply lines such as the Sihanouk Trail through Cambodia sustained its communist adversaries during the war.
