Coptic Cairo

Coptic Cairo is a part of Old Cairo which encompasses the Babylon Fortress, the Coptic Museum, the Hanging Church, the Greek Church of St. George and many other Coptic churches and historical sites. It is believed that the Holy Family visited this area and stayed at the site of the Church of Saints Sergius and Bacchus (Abu Serga). Coptic Cairo was a stronghold for Christianity in Egypt until the Islamic era, though most of the current buildings of the churches in Coptic Cairo were built after the Muslim conquest of Egypt in the 7th century.

Sites

The Hanging Church (Saint Virgin Mary's Coptic Orthodox Church), built atop the southern gatehouse of the Babylon Fortress, dates in part to the 3rd or 4th century and is the seat historically associated with the Coptic Pope. The Church of Saints Sergius and Bacchus (Abu Serga) is traditionally held to stand over a cave where the Holy Family rested. The Church of St. Barbara, the Church of the Holy Virgin at Babylon al-Darag, and the Greek Orthodox Church and Monastery of St. George are also in the district.

The Ben Ezra Synagogue, among the oldest synagogues in Egypt, is famous as the site of the Cairo Geniza, a vast cache of medieval Jewish manuscripts discovered in the 19th century. The Church of the Holy Virgin (Haret Zuweila) is the oldest church in central Cairo.

Coptic Museum

The Coptic Museum, founded in 1908 by Marcus Simaika Pasha, houses the world's largest collection of Coptic Christian artifacts, including textiles, icons, manuscripts, and stone carvings, among them the Nag Hammadi library facsimiles and early Gospel manuscripts.
