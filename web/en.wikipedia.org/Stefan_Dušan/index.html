Stefan Uroš IV Dušan (Serbian Cyrillic: Стефан Урош IV Душан; c. 26 July 1308 – 20 December 1355), known as Stefan Dušan and as Dušan the Mighty, was the King of Serbia from 8 September 1331 and Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks from 16 April 1346 until his death in 1355. Under his rule, Serbia reached its territorial peak, becoming one of the most powerful states in Southeast Europe.

Reign

Dušan came to the throne after joining the nobility in deposing his father, Stefan Dečanski. Exploiting the Byzantine civil wars of the 1340s, he conquered much of Macedonia, Albania, Epirus, and Thessaly, roughly doubling the size of the Serbian state. In 1346 he was crowned "Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks" at Skopje, and he raised the Serbian Church to a patriarchate.

Dušan's Code

Dušan is remembered in Serbian historiography as a lawgiver: his Zakonik (Dušan's Code), promulgated in 1349 and expanded in 1354, was one of the most advanced legal compilations of medieval Europe, regulating feudal relations, the church, and criminal justice. His endowments included the Monastery of the Holy Archangels near Prizren and contributions to monasteries on Mount Athos.

Death and aftermath

Dušan died in December 1355, apparently while preparing a campaign against Constantinople. He was succeeded by his son Stefan Uroš V, under whom the empire rapidly fragmented among regional lords — a decline that left the Balkans exposed to Ottoman expansion.
