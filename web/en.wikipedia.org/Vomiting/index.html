Vomiting

Vomiting
Synonyms: Emesis, throwing up
Field: Gastroenterology
Common causes: Gastroenteritis, food poisoning, motion sickness, pregnancy, medications

Vomiting, also known as emesis and colloquially as throwing up, is the involuntary, forceful expulsion of the contents of one's stomach through the mouth and sometimes the nose. It is a protective reflex that helps rid the body of ingested toxins, but it can also arise from many disorders.

Mechanism

Vomiting is coordinated by the vomiting centre in the medulla oblongata, which receives input from the chemoreceptor trigger zone, the vestibular system, the vagus nerve and higher brain centres. The act involves contraction of the abdominal muscles and diaphragm against a closed glottis, forcing gastric contents out.

Causes

Causes range from gastroenteritis, food poisoning and motion sickness to migraine, pregnancy (morning sickness), raised intracranial pressure, metabolic disorders and the side effects of drugs such as chemotherapy agents. Persistent vomiting can lead to dehydration, electrolyte imbalance and aspiration.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause and replaces lost fluids. Antiemetic drugs such as ondansetron, metoclopramide and antihistamines are used when needed.
