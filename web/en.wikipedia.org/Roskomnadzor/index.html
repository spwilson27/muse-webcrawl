Roskomnadzor

Roskomnadzor headquarters, Moscow
Formed:2008
Jurisdiction:Russian Federation
Parent:Ministry of Digital Development, Communications and Mass Media

Roskomnadzor (Russian: Роскомнадзор), officially the Federal Service for Supervision of Communications, Information Technology and Mass Media, is the Russian federal executive agency responsible for regulating media, telecommunications and the internet, and for enforcing Russian laws on information and data protection. It is subordinate to the Ministry of Digital Development, Communications and Mass Media.

Functions

Roskomnadzor licenses broadcasters and telecom operators, allocates oversight of the radio spectrum together with related agencies, registers mass media outlets, and supervises compliance with personal-data legislation, including data-localisation requirements obliging companies to store Russians' data on servers in Russia. It maintains the federal register of prohibited information—websites and pages that internet service providers must block—covering categories such as child sexual abuse material, promotion of drugs and suicide, and content designated "extremist" or otherwise banned by courts and other agencies.

Notable actions

The agency came to international prominence as Russia's internet-blocking authority. Its attempt to block the Telegram messaging service beginning in April 2018, after Telegram refused to hand over encryption keys, led it to blacklist millions of IP addresses belonging to major cloud providers, disrupting numerous unrelated services; the ban was formally lifted in 2020 after proving technically ineffective. Roskomnadzor also blocked LinkedIn in 2016 under the data-localisation law.

Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, Roskomnadzor ordered media to use only official sources in reporting the war, and oversaw the blocking of Facebook, Instagram and Twitter (and the designation of Meta as an extremist organisation), as well as numerous independent Russian and foreign news outlets. It has also throttled services—including YouTube speeds in 2024—demanded removal of VPN applications and content from app stores, and enforced labelling requirements for "foreign agents."

Criticism

Press-freedom organisations such as Reporters Without Borders and the Committee to Protect Journalists characterise Roskomnadzor as an instrument of state censorship. The Russian government presents its work as protecting citizens, children and national sovereignty in the information sphere.
