Internal control

Internal control, as defined in accounting and auditing, is a process for assuring an organization's objectives in operational effectiveness and efficiency, reliable financial reporting, and compliance with laws, regulations, and policies. It involves everything that controls risks to an organization — the policies, procedures, and activities established by management and the board of directors.

COSO framework

The most widely used framework is that of the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO), first published in 1992 and updated in 2013. It defines five components: the control environment, risk assessment, control activities (such as segregation of duties, authorizations, and reconciliations), information and communication, and monitoring activities.

Regulation

Following corporate accounting scandals, the U.S. Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002 (Section 404) required management of public companies to assess and report on internal control over financial reporting, with external auditors attesting to that assessment for larger companies. Internal audit functions test controls and report weaknesses to audit committees.
