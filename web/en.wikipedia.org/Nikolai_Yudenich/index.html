Nikolai Yudenich

Nikolai Nikolayevich Yudenich (30 July 1862 – 5 October 1933) was a Russian Imperial Army general and one of the most successful Russian commanders of the First World War; after the revolution he led White forces in the northwest during the Russian Civil War.

First World War

Commanding the Caucasus Army against the Ottoman Empire, Yudenich won the Battle of Sarikamish (1914–15) and captured Erzurum (1916) and Trebizond, advancing deep into eastern Anatolia — the only sustained Russian successes of the war against the Ottomans. After the February Revolution he was removed by the Provisional Government.

Civil war

From Estonia in 1919, Yudenich led the Northwestern Army in two offensives against Petrograd; the second, in October–November 1919, reached the city's suburbs at the Pulkovo Heights before being thrown back by Trotsky's defense. His army retreated into Estonia, was disarmed and interned, and Yudenich emigrated to France, dying in exile at Saint-Laurent-du-Var in 1933.
