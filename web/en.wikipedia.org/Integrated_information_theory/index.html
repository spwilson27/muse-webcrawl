Integrated information theory

Integrated information theory (IIT) is a theoretical framework originally proposed by neuroscientist Giulio Tononi in 2004 that attempts to explain what consciousness is and why it might be associated with certain physical systems, such as brains. Given any physical system, IIT predicts whether that system is conscious, to what degree it is conscious, and what particular experience it is having. According to IIT, a system's consciousness is an intrinsic property of that system, represented by a quantity denoted Φ ("phi").

Axioms and postulates

IIT starts from five axioms about experience itself: consciousness exists intrinsically; it is structured (composition); it is specific — each experience is the particular way it is (information); it is unified and irreducible (integration); and it is definite in content and spatio-temporal grain (exclusion). From these it derives postulates about the physical substrate: a conscious system must have cause–effect power upon itself, must specify a cause–effect structure that is irreducible to independent parts, and so on. The quantity Φ measures the irreducibility of a system's cause–effect structure: if a system can be partitioned into parts that are causally independent, Φ is zero for the whole.

Versions

IIT has gone through major versions — IIT 2.0, 3.0 (2014) — with later formulations (IIT 4.0, 2023) reformulating the framework in terms of cause–effect structures of "distinctions" and "relations". Computing exact Φ for realistic networks is computationally intractable, so approximations are used in empirical work.

Reception

IIT has influenced research on measuring consciousness in patients with brain injury and under anaesthesia; Tononi's related perturbational complexity index (PCI), developed with Marcello Massimini, is used clinically. The theory has also drawn criticism: some philosophers and scientists argue it implies implausibly widespread consciousness (a form of panpsychism) in simple systems, and in 2023 an open letter signed by over 100 scholars called IIT pseudoscience — a characterization other researchers disputed. In 2023–2025, an adversarial collaboration testing IIT against global neuronal workspace theory reported mixed results for both theories.
