Cathedral of the Dormition, Moscow

Location: Moscow Kremlin, Russia
Built: 1475–1479
Architect: Aristotele Fioravanti
Denomination: Russian Orthodox

The Cathedral of the Dormition (Russian: Успенский собор, Uspensky sobor) is a Russian Orthodox church dedicated to the Dormition of the Theotokos. It is located on the north side of Cathedral Square of the Moscow Kremlin in Russia, where a narrow lane runs along the south wall of the church.

History

The present cathedral was commissioned by Grand Prince Ivan III and built between 1475 and 1479 by the Italian architect Aristotele Fioravanti, who modelled it on the Cathedral of the Dormition in Vladimir. For centuries it was the coronation church of the Russian tsars and emperors, and the burial place of most of the metropolitans and patriarchs of Moscow.

Architecture

The cathedral is built of white stone with five gilded domes and a restrained Renaissance-influenced design combined with traditional Russian forms. Its interior is decorated with frescoes dating largely from the 17th century and houses a celebrated iconostasis and the carved Monomakh Throne of Ivan the Terrible (1551).

Soviet era and today

After the Russian Revolution the cathedral was closed for worship in 1918 and converted into a museum. In 1990 it was returned to the Russian Orthodox Church, and occasional services are held there with the permission of the Kremlin Museums.
