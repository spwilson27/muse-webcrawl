Host (biology)

An ectoparasite attached to its host

In biology and medicine, a host is a larger organism that harbours a smaller organism, whether a parasitic, mutualistic or commensalist guest (symbiont). The guest typically benefits from shelter and nutrients provided by the host, and the relationship may harm the host (parasitism), benefit it (mutualism), or leave it unaffected (commensalism).

Types of host

A definitive or primary host is one in which a parasite reaches maturity and, if applicable, reproduces sexually; an intermediate or secondary host harbours the parasite only during a developmental or asexual stage and is often required to complete its life cycle. A paratenic host serves as a temporary refuge without development, and a reservoir host maintains a pathogen in nature in a way that can spill over to humans or domestic animals. Humans, for instance, are the definitive host of several tapeworms, while mosquitoes serve as hosts in the life cycle of the malaria parasite Plasmodium.

Host range and specificity

Symbionts range from strict specialists confined to a single host species to broad generalists. Host specificity results from long coevolution: parasites adapt to their hosts' defences while hosts evolve resistance, an interplay sometimes described as a coevolutionary arms race. The term host is also used for cells that harbour viruses and for the organism receiving a graft or transplant.
