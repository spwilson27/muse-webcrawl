Mass of Saint Gregory

SubjectVision of Pope Gregory I
TypeReligious art motif
PeriodLate medieval

The Mass of Saint Gregory is a subject in Roman Catholic art depicting a vision said to have occurred to Pope Gregory I during Mass: as he celebrated the Eucharist, Christ appeared on the altar as the Man of Sorrows, surrounded by the instruments of the Passion (the Arma Christi), confirming the doctrine of the Real Presence.

Legend

According to the legend, a woman in the congregation doubted that the consecrated host was truly the body of Christ; as Gregory prayed, Christ appeared visibly above the altar, bleeding, and the host turned to flesh.

In art

The image became extremely popular in the late Middle Ages, appearing in altarpieces, manuscripts, and early prints, often connected with indulgences granted to those who prayed before it.
