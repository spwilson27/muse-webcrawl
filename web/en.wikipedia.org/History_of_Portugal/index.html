The history of Portugal is that of one of Europe's oldest nation-states, with borders essentially fixed since 1297. From the Roman province of Lusitania and the medieval Reconquista, Portugal rose in the 15th century to pioneer the European Age of Discovery and build the first global maritime empire.

Formation

The County of Portugal, a fief of León, became a kingdom under Afonso Henriques, who defeated the Moors at Ourique (1139) and won papal recognition in 1179. His successors completed the conquest of the Algarve by 1249. The 1383–85 crisis and the Battle of Aljubarrota preserved independence from Castile and enthroned the House of Avis.

The Discoveries and empire

Under royal patronage, Portuguese mariners took Ceuta (1415), settled Madeira and the Azores, rounded the Cape of Good Hope (Dias, 1488) and reached India (Vasco da Gama, 1498). Brazil was claimed in 1500; the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) had already divided the non-European world between Portugal and Spain. Spices, gold and later Brazilian sugar, gold and coffee funded a small kingdom's outsized empire, built also on the Atlantic slave trade.

Decline, republic, dictatorship

The Iberian Union (1580–1640) under Spain exposed Portugal's colonies to Dutch attack; independence was restored in 1640. The 1755 Lisbon earthquake reshaped the capital under the Marquis of Pombal. The monarchy fell in 1910; after an unstable republic, António de Oliveira Salazar's Estado Novo dictatorship (1932–68) held power until the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, a nearly bloodless military coup that restored democracy and triggered rapid decolonisation in Africa. Portugal joined the European Community in 1986 and the euro at its launch.
