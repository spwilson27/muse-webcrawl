Tāinui (canoe)

Tāinui was one of the great ocean-going voyaging canoes (waka) in Māori tradition in which Polynesian migrants journeyed to New Zealand from Hawaiki. It is associated with the tribes of the Waikato, Hauraki and Tāmaki regions, who form the Tainui confederation.

Voyage

Traditions recount that the Tāinui, captained by Hoturoa, landed at Whangaparāoa and later at Kāwhia Harbour on the west coast of the North Island, after stops including Rarotonga. The canoe was hauled across the Tāmaki isthmus at Ōtāhuhu, the portage still remembered in place names.

Legacy

The canoe was said to have been buried at Kāwhia, marked by two stone pillars. The Tainui waka confederation remains a major iwi grouping; the Kīngitanga (Māori King movement) is closely tied to Tainui, and the tribe's 1995 Treaty settlement with the Crown was a landmark in modern New Zealand history.
