Wargame

A wargame is a game that realistically simulates warfare, as opposed to abstract games such as chess. Wargames may be played for recreation, to train military officers, or to study the nature of potential conflicts. They range from grand strategic studies conducted by armed forces to hobby board games, miniatures games, and video games.

History

Modern wargaming began with Kriegsspiel, developed in Prussia in the early 19th century by Georg von Reisswitz and refined by Helmuth von Moltke the Elder. H. G. Wells popularised recreational miniature wargaming with Little Wars (1913). The hobby board wargame industry grew after Charles S. Roberts founded Avalon Hill in 1954.

Types

Wargames are commonly divided into miniatures games played with model soldiers on terrain, board wargames using maps and counters, block games, card-driven games, and computer wargames. Military wargaming by defence staffs is used for education, planning, and analysis rather than competition.

Design elements

Typical features include maps divided into hexagons or areas, units rated for combat strength and movement, dice or cards introducing chance, and rules for supply, morale and command.
