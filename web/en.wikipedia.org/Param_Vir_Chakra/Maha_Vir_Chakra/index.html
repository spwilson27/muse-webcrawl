The Maha Vir Chakra (MVC) is the second highest military decoration of India, after the Param Vir Chakra, and is awarded for acts of conspicuous gallantry in the presence of the enemy, whether on land, at sea, or in the air. It was established by the President of India on 26 January 1950, with effect from 15 August 1947.

Description

The medal is made of standard silver and is circular, bearing on the obverse a five-pointed star with the state emblem at its center, and on the reverse the words "Maha Vir Chakra" in Hindi and English with two lotus flowers. It is worn on the left chest suspended from a half-white, half-orange ribbon. A bar is awarded for subsequent acts of gallantry.

Recipients

More than 200 awards have been made, many of them posthumous, covering the Indo-Pakistani wars of 1947, 1965, and 1971, the Sino-Indian War of 1962, and the Kargil conflict of 1999, as well as counter-insurgency operations. Among the best-known recipients is Brigadier Mohammad Usman, the senior Indian officer killed in the 1947–48 war.
