Awards and decorations of the Indian Armed Forces

The Indian Armed Forces are awarded many military decorations for gallantry, distinguished service, and campaign participation. The awards, established after independence in 1947, are announced by the President of India, usually on Republic Day and Independence Day.

Gallantry awards

Wartime gallantry awards, in order of precedence, are the Param Vir Chakra, the Maha Vir Chakra, and the Vir Chakra. Peacetime gallantry awards are the Ashoka Chakra, Kirti Chakra, and Shaurya Chakra. The Param Vir Chakra, instituted on 26 January 1950 with retrospective effect from 15 August 1947, is India's highest military decoration, equivalent in prestige to the British Victoria Cross and the American Medal of Honor; it has been awarded only 21 times, 14 of them posthumously.

Distinguished service and other awards

Distinguished service decorations include the Param Vishisht Seva Medal, Ati Vishisht Seva Medal, and Vishisht Seva Medal, along with service-specific and meritorious awards such as the Sena, Nau Sena, and Vayu Sena Medals. India also issues campaign and service medals for particular operations and for long service and good conduct, as well as mentions in dispatches.
