Param Vir Chakra

The Param Vir Chakra (PVC) is India's highest military decoration, awarded for displaying distinguished acts of valour during wartime. It is broadly equivalent to the British Victoria Cross and the American Medal of Honor. The name translates as "Wheel of the Ultimate Brave".

History

The decoration was instituted on 26 January 1950 by the President of India, with effect from 15 August 1947. It was designed by Savitri Khanolkar. The medal is a circular bronze disc bearing the state emblem in the centre, surrounded by four replicas of Indra's vajra (thunderbolt); it is suspended from a plain purple ribbon.

Recipients

As of recent counts, the medal has been awarded 21 times, 14 of them posthumously, to members of the Indian Army, with one award to an Indian Air Force officer, Flying Officer Nirmal Jit Singh Sekhon. The first recipient was Major Somnath Sharma for his actions in the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947. Recipients include Major Shaitan Singh, Captain Vikram Batra, and Lieutenant Manoj Kumar Pandey, the latter two from the Kargil War of 1999.
