Foraging behaviour is a central topic in behavioural ecology.

Behavioural ecology is the study of the evolutionary basis for animal behavior, examining how behaviour contributes to survival and reproduction (fitness) in an animal's ecological environment. It applies natural selection and game theory to questions such as foraging, mating, parental care and social organisation.

History

The field grew from ethology and sociobiology in the 1970s, drawing on W. D. Hamilton's theory of kin selection, John Maynard Smith's evolutionary game theory, and Tinbergen's four questions about behaviour. The textbook An Introduction to Behavioural Ecology by Davies, Krebs and West helped define the discipline.

Major topics

Optimal foraging theory predicts how animals balance energy gain against risk and handling time. Sexual selection explains elaborate ornaments and mating systems; kin selection and reciprocity explain cooperation and altruism; parent–offspring conflict and signalling theory address communication and conflict within families and groups.
