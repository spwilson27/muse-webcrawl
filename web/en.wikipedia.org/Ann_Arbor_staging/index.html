Ann Arbor staging

The Ann Arbor system classifies lymphoma by the extent of lymph node and organ involvement.

Ann Arbor staging is the staging system developed for Hodgkin lymphoma in Ann Arbor, Michigan, in 1971. It remains, with modifications, the principal staging system for most lymphomas, including non-Hodgkin lymphomas. The system describes the extent of disease by the number and location of involved lymph node regions relative to the diaphragm, the presence of extranodal disease, and systemic ("B") symptoms.

Stages

StageDefinition
IInvolvement of a single lymph node region (I) or a single extralymphatic site (IE)
IIInvolvement of two or more lymph node regions on the same side of the diaphragm (II), or one extralymphatic site and its regional nodes with or without other nodes on the same side (IIE)
IIIInvolvement of lymph node regions on both sides of the diaphragm (III), which may include the spleen (IIIS) or extralymphatic sites (IIIE, IIIES)
IVDisseminated involvement of one or more extranodal organs (e.g. liver, bone marrow, lung), with or without lymph node involvement

Modifiers

A letter is appended to the stage to denote additional features. The absence of systemic symptoms is denoted A; the presence of fever (above 38 °C), drenching night sweats, or unexplained weight loss of more than 10% in six months is denoted B. Other designations include E for extralymphatic involvement, S for splenic involvement, X for bulky disease, and CS/PS for clinical versus pathological staging.

Modifications

The Cotswolds modification (1988) refined the system by incorporating computed tomography findings and defining bulky disease, particularly a mediastinal mass greater than one-third of the intrathoracic diameter. The Lugano classification (2014) updated the system for the era of PET-CT imaging, recommending fluorodeoxyglucose PET-CT for staging FDG-avid lymphomas and de-emphasising routine bone marrow biopsy in Hodgkin lymphoma.

History

The classification was adopted at a 1971 meeting of the Committee on Hodgkin's Disease Staging Classification in Ann Arbor, Michigan, building on the earlier Rye classification of 1965. It was designed when radiotherapy was the principal treatment for localised Hodgkin lymphoma, making the anatomical extent of disease relative to radiation fields central to management.
