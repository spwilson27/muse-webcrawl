Pilgrim Monument

LocationProvincetown, Massachusetts, United States
Height252 ft 7.5 in (77 m)
Built1907–1910
ArchitectWillard T. Sears
Dedicated5 August 1910

The Pilgrim Monument in Provincetown, Massachusetts, was built between 1907 and 1910 to commemorate the first landfall of the Mayflower Pilgrims in North America and the signing of the Mayflower Compact, which took place in Provincetown Harbor in November 1620. At 252 feet 7.5 inches (about 77 metres) tall, it is the tallest all-granite structure in the United States.

Design and construction

The design, by Boston architect Willard T. Sears, was based on the Torre del Mangia in Siena, Italy. The tower is built of granite quarried in Stonington, Maine, and its interior climb combines a series of ramps with 116 steps. The cornerstone was laid in 1907 at a ceremony attended by President Theodore Roosevelt, and the finished monument was dedicated by President William Howard Taft on 5 August 1910.

Provincetown Museum

At the base of the monument stands the Provincetown Museum, founded by the Cape Cod Pilgrim Memorial Association, which organised the monument's construction. The museum's exhibits cover the Pilgrims' arrival, the maritime history of Provincetown and the town's later history as a fishing port and artists' colony. Visitors who climb the tower are rewarded with views across Cape Cod Bay and the length of the Cape.
