Rubiaceae is a family of flowering plants, commonly known as the coffee, madder or bedstraw family. With roughly 13,500 species in about 600 genera, it is the fourth-largest family of flowering plants. Members are cosmopolitan but are especially diverse in the tropics, where many are trees and shrubs of forest understoreys; in temperate regions the family is represented mainly by herbaceous plants such as bedstraws (Galium).

Characteristics

Rubiaceae are readily recognised by simple, opposite leaves with interpetiolar stipules, tubular flowers with a typically four- or five-lobed corolla, and an inferior ovary. Economically important secondary compounds, including alkaloids such as caffeine and quinine, are widespread in the family.

Economic importance

The family's best-known member is coffee (Coffea), one of the world's most valuable agricultural commodities. Others include Cinchona, the source of the antimalarial drug quinine; Rubia tinctorum (madder), historically a major red dye; ipecacuanha, formerly used in medicine; and many ornamentals such as gardenia, ixora and pentas.

Classification

The family is placed in the order Gentianales and is usually divided into three subfamilies: Rubioideae, Ixoroideae and Cinchonoideae. Molecular phylogenetic studies have substantially revised the tribal classification within these subfamilies.
