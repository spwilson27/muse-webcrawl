Begging the question

Begging the question (Latin: petitio principii) is an informal fallacy in which an argument's premises assume the truth of the conclusion. Circular reasoning is a closely related form.

History

Aristotle discussed the fallacy in the Prior Analytics and Sophistical Refutations. The English phrase is a translation of the Latin, in which "begging" means assuming what is at issue.

Modern usage

In common modern usage the phrase is often used to mean "raises the question", a sense that has become widespread though disputed by traditionalists in logic and rhetoric. See Informal fallacy.
