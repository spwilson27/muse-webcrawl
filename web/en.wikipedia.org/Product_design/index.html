Product design

Product design is the process of creating and developing new products — imagining, specifying and refining artefacts that solve problems for users and satisfy market needs. It combines elements of industrial design, engineering, ergonomics, materials science and marketing. In recent decades the term has also been widely applied to digital products, where product designers work on user experience (UX) and user interface (UI) design for software and websites.

Process

The product design process typically moves through research into users and competitors; concept generation by sketching and ideation; development of chosen concepts with computer-aided design (CAD); prototyping and user testing; and design for manufacture, in which details are resolved for production. Approaches such as design thinking emphasise iteration: empathising with users, defining the problem, ideating, prototyping and testing in repeated loops. Successful product design balances form, function, usability, cost, sustainability and brand identity.

History

The discipline emerged from the industrial revolution, when manufacture separated the acts of designing and making. Early figures include Christopher Dresser in Victorian Britain; the German Bauhaus school and designers such as Dieter Rams at Braun codified functionalist principles — Rams' "ten principles of good design" remain widely cited. Post-war consultancies and in-house teams shaped the consumer landscape, and firms such as IDEO later popularised human-centred design methods for products, services and systems.

Sustainability

Modern product design increasingly incorporates lifecycle assessment, design for disassembly and repair, and circular-economy principles, seeking to reduce resource use and waste across a product's life from raw materials to disposal.
