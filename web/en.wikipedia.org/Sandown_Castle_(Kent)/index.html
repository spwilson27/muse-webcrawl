Sandown Castle was an artillery fort constructed by Henry VIII at Sandown, near Deal in Kent, between 1539 and 1540. It formed part of the King's Device programme to protect against invasion from France and the Holy Roman Empire, and defended the strategically important Downs anchorage off the English coast. Comprising a circular keep and four circular bastions, the moated stone castle covered 0.59 hectares and had defensive arrangements for both artillery and handguns.

History

Sandown was one of three forts built along this stretch of coast, alongside Walmer and Deal castles. During the English Civil War the castle was seized by Parliamentarian forces and was briefly held during the Second Civil War in 1648. It continued in military use, in part as a prison — the regicide John Hutchinson died there in confinement in 1664 — and a coastguard station was later established on the site. Coastal erosion progressively undermined the sea-facing walls, and in the 19th century much of the remaining structure was demolished or absorbed into sea defences.

Remains

Today only fragments of the castle survive, incorporated into the sea wall at Sandown; the site is protected as a scheduled monument. The ruins are far less complete than those of its sister castles at Walmer and Deal, which remain major visitor attractions in the care of English Heritage.
