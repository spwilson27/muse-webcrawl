Technetium

SymbolTc
Atomic number43
Group7 (transition metal)
AppearanceShiny gray metal

Technetium is a chemical element; it has symbol Tc and atomic number 43. It is the lightest element whose isotopes are all radioactive. Technetium is a silvery-gray radioactive metal with an appearance similar to platinum, commonly obtained as a gray powder. Nearly all technetium produced is synthetic, and only minute amounts are found in nature.

Technetium was the first element to be produced artificially, a fact reflected in its name, from the Greek tekhnētos (τεχνητός), meaning "artificial." It was discovered in 1937 by Carlo Perrier and Emilio Segrè in a sample of molybdenum sent by Ernest Lawrence, filling a long-standing gap in the periodic table.

Isotopes

No isotope of technetium is stable. The most important isotope in applications is technetium-99m, a metastable nuclear isomer with a half-life of about six hours, used in tens of millions of medical diagnostic procedures annually as a radioactive tracer. Technetium-99, with a half-life of 211,000 years, is produced in quantity as a fission product in nuclear reactors.

Occurrence

Technetium occurs naturally only in trace quantities, formed by spontaneous fission of uranium or neutron capture in molybdenum ores. It has also been detected spectroscopically in certain red giant stars, where it is produced by the s-process, providing evidence for stellar nucleosynthesis.

Applications

Beyond nuclear medicine, technetium has limited commercial uses because of its radioactivity. Pertechnetate salts can act as corrosion inhibitors for steel. Technetium compounds are also studied as catalysts and as materials in low-temperature physics.

Precautions

Technetium is radioactive and must be handled with care. Although its radiation is weak and chemical toxicity is low, contamination control and shielding are standard practice in laboratories and hospitals that use it.
