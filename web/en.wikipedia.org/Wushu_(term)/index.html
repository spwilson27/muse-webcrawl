Wushu (term)

Wushu (simplified Chinese: 武术; traditional Chinese: 武術; pinyin: wǔshù), literally "martial arts", is the Chinese term for the martial arts of China. In contemporary usage it refers both to Chinese martial arts in general — often called kung fu in the West — and, more specifically, to the modern competitive sport of wushu, a standardised performance and combat discipline developed in the People's Republic of China.

Terminology

The word combines wǔ (武, "martial" or "military") and shù (术/術, "art" or "technique"). Historically, terms such as quánfǎ ("fist methods") and gōngfu were also used; "kung fu" became the popular Western term through 20th-century cinema and television, while "wushu" is the official term used by the Chinese state and international federations.

Modern sport wushu

After 1949, the Chinese government standardised the traditional arts into a national sport administered today by the Chinese Wushu Association and, internationally, the International Wushu Federation (founded 1990). Competition comprises taolu, choreographed routines judged on technique and difficulty — including barehand, short weapons, long weapons, and open styles — and sanda, a full-contact fighting format combining strikes and throws on a raised platform (leitai).

International recognition

Wushu was an exhibition sport at the 2008 Beijing Olympics and has featured in the Asian Games since 1990. It was added to the programme of the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games. Repeated campaigns for full Olympic inclusion have so far been unsuccessful.
