Mammoth

Kingdom: Animalia
Family: Elephantidae
Genus: †Mammuthus Brookes, 1828

A mammoth is any species of the extinct elephantid genus Mammuthus. Mammoths lived from the Pliocene epoch into the Holocene, in Africa, Europe, Asia and North America. They are more closely related to living Asian elephants than to African elephants. The best-known species, the woolly mammoth (M. primigenius), was adapted to cold climates with a thick coat of hair, small ears, a short tail and a thick layer of fat.

Evolution and species

The genus originated in Africa about 5 million years ago and spread widely. Species include the southern mammoth (M. meridionalis), the steppe mammoth (M. trogontherii), the Columbian mammoth (M. columbi) of North America, and dwarf forms that evolved on islands such as the Channel Islands of California and Wrangel Island. Mammoths had long, curved tusks, high-crowned ridged molars suited to grazing, and a large domed skull.

Extinction

Most mainland populations died out at the end of the last Ice Age, around 10,000 years ago, under pressure from climate-driven habitat change and human hunting; the relative weight of these causes is debated. Isolated island populations survived much longer: mammoths persisted on St. Paul Island until about 5,600 years ago and on Wrangel Island until about 4,000 years ago. Exceptionally preserved frozen carcasses from Siberia have yielded soft tissue and DNA, fuelling scientific study and proposals for de-extinction.
