Victor (monument)

The Victor (Pobednik)
LocationKalemegdan Fortress, Belgrade, Serbia
Completion1928
SculptorIvan Meštrović

The Victor (Serbian: Pobednik, "The Victorious One") is a monument in the Kalemegdan Fortress in Belgrade, Serbia, and one of the city's most recognisable symbols. It depicts a nude male figure holding a falcon in his left hand — symbolising vigilance and peace — and a lowered sword in his right, standing on a tall stone column overlooking the confluence of the Sava and Danube rivers.

History

The monument was conceived to commemorate Serbia's victories in the Balkan Wars (1912–13). Sculptor Ivan Meštrović completed the statue during the First World War era, but controversy over the figure's nudity delayed its installation. It was finally erected in 1928, marking the tenth anniversary of the Salonika Front breakthrough, at the Upper Town of Belgrade Fortress rather than in the city centre — in part because of the same modesty concerns. It faces away from the city, toward the former Austro-Hungarian lands across the rivers.

Symbolism

The Victor has become the emblem of Belgrade, appearing on postcards and in tourism branding. Conservation work in 2019–2020 cleaned the bronze and repaired the column, after studies showed the statue had leaned slightly over the decades.
