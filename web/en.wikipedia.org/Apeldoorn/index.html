Apeldoorn

Country: Netherlands
Province: Gelderland
Population: approx. 165,000 (municipality)

Apeldoorn is a city and municipality in the province of Gelderland in the centre of the Netherlands. Situated on the edge of the Veluwe, a large forested region, it is one of the largest municipalities in the country by population and land area.

Royal palace

The city is best known for Het Loo Palace, a Baroque palace built in the 17th century for Stadtholder William III of Orange, later King William III of England. It served as a summer residence of the Dutch royal family for three centuries and is now a national museum known for its furnished state rooms and formal gardens.

Attractions

Apeldoorn is home to the Apenheul primate park, one of the first zoos in the world to let monkeys roam freely among visitors, opened in 1971. The city also hosts the national police academy and several major insurance and government offices, and it is a gateway for tourism to the Hoge Veluwe National Park.
