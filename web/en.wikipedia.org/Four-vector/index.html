Four-vector

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In special relativity, a four-vector (or 4-vector) is an object with four components that transform in a specific way under Lorentz transformations. Specifically, a four-vector is an element of a four-dimensional vector space considered as a representation space of the standard representation of the Lorentz group, the (1⁄2, 1⁄2) representation. It differs from a Euclidean vector in how its magnitude is determined: the inner product is taken with the Minkowski metric.

Definition

A four-vector A is written A = (A0, A1, A2, A3), where A0 is the timelike component and the others are the spacelike components. Under a Lorentz transformation, its components mix in the same way as the spacetime coordinates (ct, x, y, z). With the metric signature (−,+,+,+), the squared magnitude A · A = −(A0)² + (A1)² + (A2)² + (A3)² is invariant — the same in all inertial frames.

Examples

  • Position four-vector (four-position): (ct, x, y, z).
  • Four-velocity: the derivative of four-position with respect to proper time, γ(c, v).
  • Four-acceleration: the derivative of four-velocity with respect to proper time; see four-acceleration.
  • Four-momentum: (E/c, p), whose squared magnitude gives the invariant mass, E² − p²c² = m²c⁴.
  • Four-current: (cρ, J), combining charge density and current density in electromagnetism.
  • Electromagnetic four-potential: (φ/c, A).
  • Wave four-vector: (ω/c, k).

Covariant and contravariant forms

Four-vectors can be written in contravariant form (Aμ) or covariant form (Aμ), related by the Minkowski metric tensor; raising and lowering the index changes the sign of the timelike component (in the −+++ convention). The inner product of two four-vectors, AμBμ, is a Lorentz scalar.

History

The four-vector formalism grew out of Hermann Minkowski's 1908 geometrisation of Einstein's special relativity, in which space and time were unified into spacetime; four-vectors provide the natural language for relativistic mechanics and electromagnetism.
