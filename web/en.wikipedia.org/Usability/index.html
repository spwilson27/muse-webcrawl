Usability

Usability is the extent to which a product — such as software, a website, a device or a built environment — can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction in a specified context of use (ISO 9241-11). It is a core concern of human–computer interaction and user experience design.

Components

Jakob Nielsen's widely used model identifies five quality components: learnability (how easy it is to accomplish basic tasks on first encounter), efficiency (how quickly experienced users perform tasks), memorability (how easily proficiency returns after a period away), errors (how few, and how recoverable) and satisfaction (how pleasant the design is to use). Utility — whether the system provides the needed functionality — combined with usability constitutes a system's usefulness.

Evaluation methods

Methods include usability testing, in which representative users attempt tasks while observers record problems; think-aloud protocols; heuristic evaluation, in which experts inspect the interface against principles such as Nielsen's ten heuristics; cognitive walkthroughs; A/B testing; and analytics. Nielsen's research argued that testing with about five users uncovers most problems in iterative design — a claim since refined and debated. Usability engineering emphasizes iterative cycles of design, testing and measurement against quantitative benchmarks.

Related concepts

Usability is distinct from but related to accessibility (design for users with disabilities, codified in guidelines such as WCAG), utility, and the broader user experience, which includes aesthetics, emotion and brand perception. In safety-critical fields — aviation, medicine, nuclear power — usability (human factors/ergonomics) is a matter of preventing lethal errors, and standards and regulators require human-factors validation.

History

The field grew from wartime human factors research, through the "user-friendly" ethos of early personal computing, to the web era, when Nielsen's Designing Web Usability (2000) and discount usability engineering methods spread the practice. ISO standards, including ISO 9241 and ISO/IEC 25010 (which lists usability as a product quality characteristic), formalize definitions and metrics.
