Howard University

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Howard University is a private, federally chartered historically Black research university in Washington, D.C. Chartered by Congress in 1867, it is named for General Oliver Otis Howard, a Civil War hero and commissioner of the Freedmen’s Bureau. Howard is one of the largest historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and is classified among “R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity”.

Founded: March 2, 1867
Type: Private research university, HBCU
Location: Washington, D.C., United States
Motto: Veritas et Utilitas (“Truth and Service”)
Colours: Navy blue and white

History

Howard was founded shortly after the Civil War to educate newly emancipated African Americans, with early departments in medicine, law and theology. Its law school, under dean Charles Hamilton Houston, trained the lawyers — including alumnus Thurgood Marshall, later the first Black Supreme Court justice — who dismantled legal segregation, culminating in Brown v. Board of Education (1954), much of whose legal strategy was developed at Howard.

Academics

The university comprises more than a dozen schools and colleges, including medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, law, business, engineering and divinity, and operates Howard University Hospital. It awards more doctorates to African American students than any other university in the United States, and counts many prominent alumni in politics, the arts and scholarship.
