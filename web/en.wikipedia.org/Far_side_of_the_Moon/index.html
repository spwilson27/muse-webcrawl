Far side of the Moon

The far side of the Moon is the lunar hemisphere that always faces away from Earth, opposite to the near side, because of tidal locking. It was completely unseen by humans until the Soviet Luna 3 probe photographed it in 1959; Apollo 8 astronauts were the first to see it directly in 1968.

Unlike the near side, the far side has few large maria and is heavily cratered. It hosts the South Pole–Aitken basin, one of the largest impact craters in the Solar System. In January 2019 China's Chang'e 4 made the first soft landing on the far side, in Von Kármán crater, using the Queqiao relay satellite.
