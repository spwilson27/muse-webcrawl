Histogram

A histogram is a graphical representation of the distribution of a dataset. It is an estimate of the probability distribution of a continuous variable, usually displayed as a series of adjacent rectangles (bins) whose areas are proportional to the frequency of observations within each interval.

Construction

To construct a histogram, the range of the data is divided into a series of intervals — the bins — and the number of observations falling into each bin is counted. Unlike a bar chart, which represents categorical data with separated bars, a histogram's bins are contiguous and its horizontal axis is quantitative. The choice of bin width and number of bins strongly affects the appearance of the chart; rules of thumb include Sturges' rule, the square-root choice, and the Freedman–Diaconis rule.

History and uses

The term was introduced by Karl Pearson in 1891. Histograms are among the most common tools of exploratory data analysis, used to assess symmetry, skewness, modality, and the presence of outliers. Variants include cumulative histograms and kernel density estimates, which smooth the bin edges into a continuous curve.
