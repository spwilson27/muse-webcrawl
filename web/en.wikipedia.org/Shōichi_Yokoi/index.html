Shōichi Yokoi

Shōichi Yokoi (横井 庄一, Yokoi Shōichi, 31 March 1915 – 22 September 1997) was a sergeant in the Imperial Japanese Army during the Second World War. One of the last Japanese holdouts, he was found in the jungle of Guam in January 1972, nearly 28 years after the Allied forces had regained control of the island in 1944.

Military service and hiding

Yokoi was conscripted in 1941 and sent to Guam in 1943. When American forces captured the island in the summer of 1944, Yokoi went into hiding in the jungle near Talofofo Falls with several other soldiers. Over the years his companions died or were found, and by the mid-1960s he was alone. He survived by hunting, fishing, and gathering food at night, weaving clothing from hibiscus fibres and living in a small underground shelter.

Discovery

On 24 January 1972, Yokoi was discovered by two local hunters, Jesus Duenas and Manuel De Gracia, who subdued him when he feared being killed. He returned to Japan a national sensation, famously remarking, "It is with much embarrassment that I return." He was surprised to learn that Japan had lost the war and had been transformed by economic growth.

Later life

Yokoi married, settled in his native Aichi Prefecture, and became a popular television commentator and advocate of a simple, self-sufficient lifestyle. He received back pay and a small pension. He unsuccessfully ran for the Japanese Diet in 1974. Yokoi died of a heart attack in 1997, aged 82. His survival shelter on Guam became a tourist attraction.
