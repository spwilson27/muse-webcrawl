A forum (Latin: forum, plural fora) was a public square in a Roman municipium or city, principally the marketplace and the centre of public, religious, and political life. Every Roman town of any size had a forum, modelled on the Forum Romanum in Rome, typically at or near the intersection of the main streets, the cardo and the decumanus.

Functions

The forum served as a gathering place of great significance. Around the open plaza stood temples, law courts in basilicas, government offices, archives, shops, and monuments. Political speeches were delivered from platforms such as the rostra, elections and assemblies were held, criminals were tried, and triumphal processions passed through. Inscriptions, statues, and arches commemorated emperors and benefactors.

The Forum Romanum

The most famous example is the Roman Forum (Forum Romanum) in Rome, which developed over centuries from a marshy valley into the political heart of the Republic and Empire, accumulating structures such as the Curia (Senate house), the Temple of Saturn, the Arch of Titus, and the Basilica Julia. After Rome's decline the area was quarried and buried, and its excavation from the 19th century onwards shaped classical archaeology.
