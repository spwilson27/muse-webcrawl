Peel tower


A peel tower: a small fortified tower house of the Anglo-Scottish border.

Peel towers (also spelt pele towers) are small fortified keeps or tower houses built along the Anglo-Scottish border and in northern England and southern Scotland between the mid-14th and 17th centuries. They were the homes of lairds and wealthy farmers, designed as defensible refuges against raiders, particularly the Border Reivers.

Design

A typical peel tower is a squat stone tower of three storeys: a vaulted ground floor for livestock, living quarters above, and a parapet and beacon platform on the roof from which a fire could signal an attack. Walls could be over a metre thick, windows were small, and the entrance was often at first-floor level, reached by a removable stair. Related structures include bastle houses, which combined byre and dwelling in a longhouse form.

Survival

Hundreds survive, many incorporated into later farmhouses and castles; examples include Smailholm Tower in the Scottish Borders and Preston Tower in Northumberland. In the 19th century, some peel towers were romantically restored as follies.
