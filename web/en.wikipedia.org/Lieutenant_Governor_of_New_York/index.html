Lieutenant Governor of New York

The lieutenant governor of New York is a constitutional office in the executive branch of the Government of New York State. It is the second highest-ranking official in state government and first in line to succeed the governor of New York, should the governor die, resign, or be removed from office.

The lieutenant governor also serves as president of the New York State Senate and may cast tie-breaking votes there. The office is elected on the same ticket as the governor in general elections, although in primaries the candidates run separately.

History

The office was created under the New York Constitution of 1777. The first lieutenant governor was Pierre Van Cortlandt, who served from 1777 to 1795 under Governor George Clinton. Over the centuries several lieutenant governors have succeeded to the governorship, including Theodore Roosevelt's successor after his elevation — most notably when a governor resigned or died in office.

Duties and succession

Under the state constitution, the lieutenant governor becomes acting governor whenever the governor is absent from the state or unable to discharge the duties of office, and succeeds permanently if the office becomes vacant. Notable successions include those following the resignations and deaths of governors in the 20th and 21st centuries; in 2008 David Paterson, then lieutenant governor, succeeded Eliot Spitzer as governor.

Notable officeholders

Recent officeholders include Mary Donohue, Betsy McCaughey Ross, Stan Lundine, Robert Duffy, Kathy Hochul — who in 2021 became governor upon Andrew Cuomo's resignation — and Antonio Delgado, appointed in 2022 and elected to a full term that November.
