Michoacán

Michoacán, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Michoacán de Ocampo, is one of the 32 states of Mexico. Located in western Mexico on the Pacific coast, its capital and largest city is Morelia. The state's name comes from the Nahuatl Michhuahcān, "place of the fishermen," referring to the fishing communities around Lake Pátzcuaro.

Geography

Michoacán is crossed by the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt and the Sierra Madre del Sur. It contains Lake Pátzcuaro and Lake Chapala's eastern shore, and the volcano Parícutin, which erupted from a cornfield in 1943 and grew into a cinder cone — one of the few volcanoes whose birth was witnessed by scientists.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, Michoacán was the heartland of the Purépecha (Tarascan) Empire, the second-largest state in Mesoamerica and a rival of the Aztec Empire, famed for its metalwork. Spanish colonization began in the 1520s under Nuño de Guzmán, whose brutality became notorious. The state later produced José María Morelos, a hero of Mexican independence born in Morelia (then Valladolid).

Economy and culture

Michoacán is Mexico's leading avocado-producing state and a major producer of limes, mangoes and berries. It is known for the Day of the Dead celebrations on the island of Janitzio, Purépecha crafts such as the lacquerware of Pátzcuaro and the pottery of Tzintzuntzan, and the Monarch Butterfly Biosphere Reserve in its eastern mountains, where millions of monarch butterflies overwinter.
