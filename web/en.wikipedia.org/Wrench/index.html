Wrench

Other namesSpanner (British English)
ClassificationHand tool
UsesTurning nuts and bolts; gripping fasteners
RelatedSocket wrench, torque wrench, pipe wrench

A wrench or spanner is a tool used to provide grip and mechanical advantage in applying torque to turn objects — usually rotary fasteners, such as nuts and bolts — or keep them from turning. In the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand the tool is usually called a spanner; in North America, "wrench" is standard, and "spanner" refers to a specialized type.

Types

Common designs include the open-end wrench, with a U-shaped jaw; the box-end (ring) wrench, whose closed jaw encircles the fastener and grips it on all flats; the combination wrench, with one of each; and the adjustable wrench, whose movable jaw adapts to different sizes — an invention associated with 19th-century engineers including Johan Petter Johansson. Socket wrenches use interchangeable sockets driven by a ratcheting handle, while torque wrenches measure and limit the tightening force. Pipe wrenches have serrated jaws for gripping round pipe, and Allen (hex) keys drive socket-headed screws.

Sizes

Wrenches are sized to match the distance across the flats of the fastener, in millimetres for metric tools and in fractions of an inch for imperial (SAE) sizes. Using a correctly fitting wrench prevents rounding of the fastener's corners.

History

Wrenches appeared as specialized tools in the 15th century, but became commonplace with the standardized nuts and bolts of the Industrial Revolution in the 19th century, when mass-produced fasteners demanded matching hand tools in every workshop.
