Rail transport in Tibet began with the opening of the Qinghai–Tibet railway, which connected Lhasa, the capital of the Tibet Autonomous Region, to Xining and the rest of China's rail network in 2006. The line is the highest railway in the world, crossing the Tanggula Pass at 5,072 metres above sea level.

Qinghai–Tibet railway

The 1,956 km route from Xining to Lhasa was built in two stages: Xining–Golmud opened in 1984, and the Golmud–Lhasa section — over 960 km of it above 4,000 m, much of it on permafrost — was completed in 2005 and opened to traffic on 1 July 2006. Engineering solutions for permafrost included crushed-rock embankments and cooling thermosiphons. Passenger trains are pressurised and supplied with oxygen to reduce altitude sickness.

Extensions

The Lhasa–Shigatse railway opened in 2014, and the electrified Lhasa–Nyingchi line, the first electrified railway on the plateau, opened in 2021 as part of the Sichuan–Tibet railway under construction toward Chengdu. The railways have sharply increased tourism and freight to Tibet while attracting debate about their cultural and environmental effects.
