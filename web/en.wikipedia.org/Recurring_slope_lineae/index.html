Recurring slope lineae

Recurring slope lineae (RSL) are seasonal flows on warm slopes of Mars, especially in the southern mid-latitudes and equatorial regions such as Valles Marineris. They appear as dark, narrow streaks (typically 0.5 to 5 metres wide) that lengthen downslope during warm seasons and fade in colder seasons, recurring each Martian year.

Hypotheses

When announced in 2011 from HiRISE images aboard the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, RSL were interpreted as possible flows of briny liquid water, since salts such as perchlorates lower the freezing point of water. Later research has favoured dry granular flows, in which slopes rest at the angle of repose and seasonal warming triggers avalanches of dust and sand. Spectral detections of hydrated salts at some RSL sites keep the brine hypothesis alive, and the features remain an active topic of debate with implications for planetary protection and the search for life.
