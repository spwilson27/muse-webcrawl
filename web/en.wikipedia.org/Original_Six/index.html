The Original Six is the group of six teams that composed the National Hockey League (NHL) for the 25 seasons between 1942 and 1967. The teams are the Boston Bruins, Chicago Black Hawks (now Blackhawks), Detroit Red Wings, Montreal Canadiens, New York Rangers and Toronto Maple Leafs. All six remain active franchises, and their rich shared history and rivalries make them the league's most storied clubs.

History

The NHL was founded in 1917 with four teams, none of which is among the Original Six (the Canadiens, founded 1909, predated the league but the "original" label refers to the 1942–67 membership). Financial distress during the Great Depression and the Second World War contracted the league: the Brooklyn Americans suspended operations in 1942, leaving the six teams that would remain unchanged for a quarter-century. Despite the name, the six were not the league's founding franchises, and the Canadiens and Maple Leafs (as the Toronto Arenas/St. Patricks) descend from earlier clubs.

The Original Six era

The era featured a 70-game regular season in which each team played the others 14 times, fostering intense rivalries and familiarity. The Canadiens dominated, winning 10 Stanley Cups in the period, including a record five consecutive championships (1956–1960) with stars such as Maurice "Rocket" Richard, Jean Béliveau and Jacques Plante; the Maple Leafs won nine, the Red Wings seven (led by Gordie Howe), and the Bruins, Rangers and Black Hawks shared the remainder — Chicago's 1961 title, with Bobby Hull and Stan Mikita, was its only one of the era. The period is fondly remembered as a golden age of intimate arenas and territorial player rights, though critics note its restrictive labour practices and the reserve clause binding players to their clubs.

Legacy

The 1967 expansion doubled the NHL to twelve teams and ended the closed era. The Original Six brands remain among the most valuable in hockey; the Canadiens–Maple Leafs rivalry is the oldest in the league, and the six clubs' arenas, sweaters and retired numbers are central to NHL heritage. The last active player from the era, Wayne Cashman, retired in 1983.
