Ogaden

The Ogaden (Somali: Ogaadeen; Amharic: ኦጋዴን) is one of the historical regions forming the Horn of Africa, corresponding broadly to the modern Somali Region of eastern Ethiopia. It is inhabited predominantly by ethnic Somalis, notably the Ogaden clan of the Darod, from whom the region takes its name. Its capital and largest city is Jijiga.

History

The Ogaden was incorporated into the Ethiopian Empire during the conquests of Emperor Menelik II in the late 19th century, a process contested by Somali inhabitants. During the Second Italo-Ethiopian War (1935–36), Italian forces advanced through the Ogaden; the region later became part of Italian East Africa before being restored to Ethiopia.

Ogaden War

In 1977, Somalia under Siad Barre invaded the Ogaden, seeking to unite it with Somalia as part of a "Greater Somalia". Somali forces initially captured most of the region, but a massive Soviet- and Cuban-backed Ethiopian counteroffensive in early 1978 drove them back. The Ogaden War ended in defeat for Somalia and contributed to the long instability that culminated in the Somali Civil War.

Insurgency

The Ogaden National Liberation Front (ONLF), founded in 1984, waged a long insurgency for self-determination, marked by conflict with Ethiopian forces and allegations of abuses on both sides, before signing a peace agreement with the Ethiopian government in 2018.

Geography and resources

The Ogaden is a semi-arid plateau traversed by the Shebelle and Genale river basins. It contains significant natural gas reserves, notably the Calub and Hilala fields, whose development has been a recurring point of contention.
