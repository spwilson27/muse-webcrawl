Count Rumford

Sir Benjamin Thompson, Count Rumford (1753–1814), was an American-born British physicist, inventor and soldier, born in Woburn, Massachusetts. A loyalist in the American Revolutionary War, he later worked in Britain and Bavaria.

Science

Rumford's cannon-boring experiments showed heat could be generated indefinitely by friction, undermining caloric theory and supporting a mechanical theory of heat. He studied thermal radiation, photometry, nutrition and fireplace and kitchen design, inventing the Rumford fireplace and improved stoves and lamps.

Service

In Bavaria he served as minister of war and police, reorganised the army, founded the English Garden in Munich, and was made a Count of the Holy Roman Empire, taking Rumford from the New Hampshire town. He founded the Royal Institution in London in 1799.
