Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex

The Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC), also called the Oxus civilization, was a Bronze Age civilisation of Central Asia that flourished roughly between 2250 and 1700 BC. It occupied the oases of Margiana (in present-day Turkmenistan) and Bactria (in present-day Afghanistan and Uzbekistan), along the upper Oxus River (Amu Darya) and the Murghab delta.

Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex
Period: c. 2250–1700 BC
Region: Turkmenistan, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan
Key sites: Gonur Tepe, Togolok, Sapalli Tepe
Also known as: Oxus civilization

Discovery and sites

The complex was identified in the 1970s through excavations led by the Soviet archaeologist Viktor Sarianidi, notably at Gonur Tepe in the Murghab delta, a large fortified settlement with monumental architecture, temples, and rich burials. Characteristic finds include compartmented stone seals, chlorite and lapis lazuli vessels, bronze tools and weapons, and sophisticated irrigation-based agriculture.

Significance

The BMAC was contemporary with, and traded extensively with, the Indus Valley Civilisation, Mesopotamia, and the Iranian plateau, exchanging lapis lazuli, tin, and other goods along early Silk Road forerunners. Its relationship — if any — to the later Indo-Iranian peoples and to early Zoroastrian practice remains debated among archaeologists.
