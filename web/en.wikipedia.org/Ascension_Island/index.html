Ascension Island

Ascension Island is an isolated volcanic island in the South Atlantic Ocean, about 1,600 kilometres (1,000 mi) from the coast of Africa and 2,300 kilometres (1,400 mi) from the coast of South America. It is a British Overseas Territory, administered as part of Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha. The island is named after the day of its recorded discovery, Ascension Day in 1503.

History

Ascension was discovered by the Portuguese navigator João da Nova in 1501 and "found again" by Afonso de Albuquerque in 1503. Dry and barren, it was used as a victualling station for ships, its population of feral goats and its turtles providing fresh meat. In 1815, after Napoleon was exiled to Saint Helena, the British garrisoned Ascension to prevent its use in a rescue attempt; the Royal Navy administered the island as if it were a ship, HMS Ascension. Charles Darwin visited in 1836 and, with Joseph Hooker, planned the introduction of plants to Green Mountain to improve rainfall capture, creating an early artificial ecosystem.

During the Second World War the United States built Wideawake Field (now RAF Ascension Island), an airbase that remains strategically important. The island hosted a major relay station for the BBC World Service and signals intelligence facilities. In the 1982 Falklands War, Ascension served as a key staging post for the British task force.

Geography and wildlife

The island is a young volcanic peak rising from the Mid-Atlantic Ridge; its last eruptions occurred within the past thousand years. The highest point is Green Mountain (859 m). The climate is tropical but moderated by trade winds. Ascension is an important nesting site for green turtles and sooty terns, and its surrounding waters were designated a large marine protected area in 2019.

Government and population

Ascension has no indigenous population; its roughly 800 residents are mainly Saint Helenian contract workers plus British and American military and civilian personnel. The island is governed by an Administrator subordinate to the Governor of Saint Helena, with an elected Island Council. Residents have no permanent right of abode. The main settlement is Georgetown on the west coast; the US base is at Cat Hill.
