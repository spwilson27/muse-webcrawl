Replisome

Replisome
TypeProtein complex (molecular machine)
FunctionDNA replication at the replication fork
Key partsHelicase, primase, DNA polymerases, sliding clamp, SSB
LocationReplication fork

The replisome is the multiprotein molecular machine that carries out DNA replication. Assembled at each replication fork, it coordinates the unwinding of the parental double helix, the synthesis of new leading and lagging strands, and the handling of the single-stranded DNA in between. Its components work together so efficiently that bacterial replisomes can copy DNA at rates approaching 1,000 nucleotides per second.

Components

A helicase unwinds the double helix at the fork, using ATP hydrolysis to separate the two strands. Single-strand binding proteins (SSB in bacteria, RPA in eukaryotes) coat the exposed strands to protect them and prevent secondary structures. Topoisomerases relieve the torsional stress ahead of the fork. Primase lays down short RNA primers, because DNA polymerases can only extend an existing strand. The main replicative DNA polymerases — DNA polymerase III in bacteria, polymerases ε and δ in eukaryotes — synthesise the new strands, held onto the DNA by a ring-shaped sliding clamp (the β clamp in bacteria, PCNA in eukaryotes) loaded by a clamp-loader complex.

Leading and lagging strands

Because DNA polymerases synthesise only in the 5′→3′ direction, the two new strands are made differently. The leading strand is synthesised continuously toward the fork. The lagging strand is made discontinuously in short Okazaki fragments, each begun by a new primer; the fragments are later joined after primer removal and ligation by DNA ligase. In the widely supported "trombone model", the lagging-strand template loops around so that both polymerases move together with the fork as a single coordinated complex.

Differences between organisms

The overall architecture is conserved, but protein names and details differ: bacteria use the DnaB helicase and Pol III holoenzyme, archaea use MCM helicase with Pol B, and eukaryotes use the CMG helicase (CDC45–MCM–GINS) with polymerases α, ε and δ. Eukaryotic replisomes are also regulated through the cell cycle so that each origin fires only once per cycle.
