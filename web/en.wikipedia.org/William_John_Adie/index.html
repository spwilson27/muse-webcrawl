William John Adie

William John Adie (31 October 1886 – 17 March 1935) was a New Zealand-born British neurologist, remembered for describing the tonic pupil syndrome that bears his name — Adie's pupil (Adie syndrome) — and for work on narcolepsy and other disorders of the nervous system.

Life and career

Adie was born in Geelong, Victoria, and emigrated with his family to New Zealand as a child; he worked as a telegraph messenger before saving enough to study medicine at the University of Edinburgh. After qualifying he specialised in neurology, served as a medical officer in the First World War, and became a physician at the National Hospital for Neurology and Neurosurgery at Queen Square and at Charing Cross Hospital in London. A gifted and humane clinician and teacher, he also had a distinguished knowledge of ornithology. He died prematurely at 48.

Adie's pupil and other work

In 1931 Adie described (independently of others, and building on earlier observations) a benign disorder, chiefly affecting young women, in which one pupil is dilated and reacts very slowly — "tonically" — to light and near effort, often with absent tendon reflexes (the Holmes–Adie syndrome). The cause is damage to the postganglionic parasympathetic fibres of the ciliary ganglion. Adie also wrote on narcolepsy, helping to delineate it as a distinct condition, and on migraine and disorders of the pituitary. The pupil he described remains a standard eponym in neurology and ophthalmology.
