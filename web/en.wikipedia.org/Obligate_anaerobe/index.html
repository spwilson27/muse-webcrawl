Obligate anaerobe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An obligate anaerobe is an organism that cannot survive in the presence of normal atmospheric concentrations of oxygen. Unlike obligate aerobes, which require oxygen, and facultative anaerobes, which can grow with or without it, obligate anaerobes are poisoned by oxygen — often because they lack enzymes such as superoxide dismutase and catalase that detoxify reactive oxygen species.

Metabolism

Obligate anaerobes obtain energy by anaerobic respiration or fermentation, using electron acceptors other than oxygen, such as sulfate, nitrate, iron or carbon dioxide. Methanogens, for example, reduce carbon dioxide to methane, while sulfate-reducing bacteria produce hydrogen sulfide.

Examples and significance

Many obligate anaerobes live in oxygen-free environments: sediments, deep soil, and the gastrointestinal tracts of animals. Medically important examples include Clostridium species (agents of tetanus, botulism and gas gangrene), Bacteroides of the gut flora, and the methanogenic archaea of the rumen. In the laboratory they are cultured in anaerobic jars or chambers using oxygen-scavenging systems.
