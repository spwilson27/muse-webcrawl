Mosque

A mosque (from Arabic masjid, "place of prostration") is a place of worship for Muslims. Any act of worship that follows Islamic rules of prayer can be said to create a mosque in a formal sense, but the term usually denotes a building specifically dedicated to communal prayer, especially the five daily prayers and the Friday midday congregation (jumu'ah).

History

The first mosque was the house of the Prophet Muhammad in Medina, established after the Hijra in 622 CE; the Quba Mosque nearby is traditionally counted the first mosque building, and the Prophet's Mosque in Medina and the Great Mosque of Mecca (Masjid al-Haram, surrounding the Kaaba) remain Islam's holiest sites. As Islam spread, mosque forms absorbed local styles: hypostyle halls in early Iraq and Egypt, courtyard mosques in Persia, domed centralised mosques under the Ottomans, and tiered-roof mosques in Indonesia and China.

Architecture and functions

Common elements include the mihrab, a niche in the wall indicating the qibla (direction of Mecca); the minbar, a short pulpit for the Friday sermon; the minaret, from which the muezzin calls the faithful to prayer; a courtyard (sahn) often with an ablution fountain; and domes that amplify sound and symbolise the vault of heaven. Prayer halls are kept free of images of animate beings in most traditions, decorated instead with calligraphy and geometric patterns.

Beyond prayer, mosques serve as centres of education, charity, social gathering and, historically, public announcement. Large congregational mosques (jami) anchor the Friday service, while neighbourhood mosques host daily prayers.
