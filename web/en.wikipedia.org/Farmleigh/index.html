Farmleigh

Farmleigh is an official Irish state guest house located in the Phoenix Park in Dublin, Ireland. Situated on a 32-hectare (78-acre) estate on the north-west edge of the park, it was formerly the Dublin residence of the Guinness brewing family. The Irish government acquired the estate in 1999 for €29.2 million, and it is now managed by the Office of Public Works. Farmleigh is used to accommodate visiting dignitaries and high-level delegations, and it also hosts the Irish Government's Asia Strategy and various public events.

History

The original house at Farmleigh was a modest two-storey Georgian building dating from the late 18th century. It was purchased in 1873 by Edward Cecil Guinness, later the first Earl of Iveagh, a great-grandson of Arthur Guinness, the founder of the Guinness brewery. Between 1881 and 1884 Guinness commissioned the architect James Rawson Carroll to extend the house substantially, adding a third storey and new wings in a style sympathetic to the original Georgian fabric. Further additions in 1896 included a ballroom, and in 1901 a conservatory.

The estate remained the Dublin home of the Guinness family for most of the 20th century. In 1999 the seventh Earl of Iveagh sold Farmleigh to the Irish State, and after extensive refurbishment the house reopened as an official state guest house. The restoration preserved much of the Guinness family's collection of furniture, paintings and rare books; the library at Farmleigh, assembled by the family, contains significant holdings of Irish interest.

The estate

The grounds include formal gardens, a walled garden, ornamental lakes, a sunken garden and extensive parkland planted with specimen trees. The working farm on the estate keeps rare breeds of livestock, reflecting the Guinness family's agricultural interests. Much of the estate is open to the public free of charge, with guided tours of the house offered seasonally. Farmleigh also hosts farmers' markets, craft fairs and cultural events throughout the year.

Official use

As a state guest house, Farmleigh has hosted numerous heads of state and government on official visits to Ireland, as well as meetings of the European Union during Irish presidencies of the Council. Guests have included Queen Elizabeth II during her state visit to Ireland in 2011, when she planted a tree in the grounds.
