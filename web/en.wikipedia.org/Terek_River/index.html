Terek River

The Terek in the Darial Gorge

The Terek River is a major river in the Northern Caucasus, flowing through Georgia and Russia, principally North Ossetia–Alania, Chechnya, and Dagestan, before emptying into the Caspian Sea. It is about 623 kilometres (387 mi) long, with a drainage basin of roughly 43,200 square kilometres.

Course

The Terek rises in the glaciers of the Greater Caucasus range in northern Georgia, near Mount Kazbek, and flows north through the dramatic Darial Gorge into Russia. It passes Vladikavkaz, turns east across the steppe, and receives tributaries including the Sunzha (which flows through Grozny), the Argun, and the Malka. Near the Caspian lowland it forms a broad swampy delta of about 4,000 square kilometres before reaching the sea near Kizlyar.

History and economy

The Terek marked a historic frontier of Russian expansion into the Caucasus; Cossack settlements along its banks formed the Terek Cossack Host. The valley figures prominently in Russian literature, including Pushkin's and Lermontov's Caucasus poems and Tolstoy's novella The Cossacks. Today the river is used for irrigation and hydroelectric power, and its delta supports important fisheries, including sturgeon.
