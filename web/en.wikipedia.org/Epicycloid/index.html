Epicycloid

In geometry, an epicycloid is a plane curve produced by tracing the path of a point on the circumference of a circle — called the epicycle — that rolls without slipping around the outside of a fixed circle. It is a particular type of roulette. If the rolling circle has radius r and the fixed circle radius R, the shape depends on the ratio k = R/r.

An epicycloid generated by a circle rolling around the outside of a fixed circle

Special cases

When k = 1, the curve is a cardioid with one cusp. When k = 2, it is a nephroid with two cusps. If k is an integer, the curve has exactly k cusps and closes after one revolution; if k is rational the curve still closes, while for irrational k it never closes and eventually fills the annulus between the two limiting circles.

Parametric equations

An epicycloid can be described parametrically by x(θ) = (R + r) cos θ − r cos(((R + r)/r) θ) and y(θ) = (R + r) sin θ − r sin(((R + r)/r) θ).

Applications

Epicycloidal curves appear in the design of gear teeth profiles, in cam mechanisms, and in drawing instruments such as the Spirograph, which produces the related hypotrochoid and epitrochoid curves.
