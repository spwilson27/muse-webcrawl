The androgen receptor (AR), also known as NR3C4, is a type of nuclear receptor that is activated by binding any of the androgenic hormones, including testosterone and dihydrotestosterone, in the cytoplasm and then translocating into the nucleus. It functions as a ligand-dependent transcription factor that regulates gene expression.

Function

The androgen receptor mediates the effects of androgens on the development and maintenance of male sexual characteristics, including the development of male reproductive tissues such as the testes and prostate, increased muscle and bone mass, and the growth of body hair. In the absence of ligand, the receptor resides in the cytoplasm bound to heat shock proteins; hormone binding induces a conformational change, dissociation from chaperones, dimerisation, and nuclear translocation, where it binds androgen response elements (AREs) in DNA.

Structure

The AR protein has three major functional domains: an N-terminal transactivation domain (containing a polymorphic polyglutamine tract encoded by a CAG repeat), a central DNA-binding domain with two zinc fingers, and a C-terminal ligand-binding domain.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the AR gene cause androgen insensitivity syndrome, in which XY individuals are resistant to androgens. Expansion of the CAG repeat beyond about 38 repeats causes spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy (Kennedy's disease). Overexpression and amplification of the receptor are central to the progression of prostate cancer, including castration-resistant disease, and are the target of antiandrogen drugs such as enzalutamide and bicalutamide.
