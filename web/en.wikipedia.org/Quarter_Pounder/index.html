Quarter Pounder

The Quarter Pounder is a hamburger sold by the international fast food chain McDonald's. It is named for its beef patty, which has a precooked weight of 4 oz (113.4 g), just under a quarter of a pound. The sandwich was introduced in 1971 by Al Bernardin, a McDonald's franchise owner in Fremont, California, who devised it to fill what he saw as a gap in the menu for customers wanting a bigger burger.


Quarter Pounder with Cheese

Product description

The standard Quarter Pounder with Cheese consists of a quarter-pound beef patty, two slices of American cheese, onions, pickles, ketchup and mustard on a sesame seed bun. In 2015, McDonald's in the United States switched from frozen to fresh, never-frozen beef patties for the sandwich, cooked when ordered. Variants have included the Quarter Pounder Deluxe, with lettuce, tomato and mayonnaise, and the Double Quarter Pounder, with two patties.

International names

In most French-speaking markets, where the pound is not a customary unit, the sandwich is sold as the Royal Cheese or a variant thereof; in France it is the "Royal Cheese", and in much of Europe similar names are used. The metric naming was famously remarked upon in the 1994 film Pulp Fiction, in which a character notes that in Paris the sandwich is called a "Royale with Cheese". In some markets the name refers to a pre-cooked weight of about 113 grams, while in the United Kingdom the patty is specified by weight in grams on menus.
