Indian diaspora

The Indian diaspora comprises people of Indian origin living outside India, including Non-Resident Indians (NRIs, Indian citizens abroad) and Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs). With well over 35 million people, it is the world's largest diaspora population. The largest communities are in the United Arab Emirates, the United States, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Canada, the United Kingdom, South Africa, and Australia.

History

Indians have lived abroad for millennia as traders and scholars, but the modern diaspora formed in distinct waves. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, the British Empire transported over a million Indian indentured laborers to plantations in the Caribbean, Fiji, Mauritius, Natal, and Malaya, creating old-diaspora communities (Indo-Caribbean, Indo-Fijian, Indian South African). After Indian independence, and especially following the U.S. Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 and later technology booms, professionals, students, and entrepreneurs migrated to North America, Europe, and Australia, while millions of workers — largely from Kerala and other states — took employment in the Gulf states.

Culture and relations with India

Diaspora communities have transplanted Indian languages, religions, cuisine, cinema, and festivals such as Diwali and Holi worldwide. India engages the diaspora through institutions such as Pravasi Bharatiya Divas (observed January 9, marking Gandhi's return from South Africa in 1915) and the Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) card. Remittances from the diaspora are the world's largest, exceeding $100 billion annually in recent years, and diaspora Indians have attained prominent roles abroad, including heads of government and major technology executives.
