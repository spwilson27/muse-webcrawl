The China Academy of Art (CAA; Chinese: 中国美术学院) is a fine arts college in Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China. Founded in 1928 in Hangzhou as the National Academy of Art, it was the first art university and the first graduate school in Chinese history. The academy is regarded as China's most prestigious fine arts institution, and ranks among the country's leading art schools. It operates under the Zhejiang provincial government and the Ministry of Education, and is a participant in China's "Double First-Class" initiative for certain disciplines.

History

Educator Cai Yuanpei proposed founding the academy in 1927, inspired by European art academies. Painter Lin Fengmian became its first president in 1928, gathering leading artists to teach both Western oil painting and traditional Chinese painting (guohua). During the Second Sino-Japanese War the school moved west through several cities before returning to Hangzhou after 1945. In 1950 it was renamed the East China Branch of the Central Academy of Fine Arts, and in 1958 became the Zhejiang Academy of Fine Arts; it took its current name in 1993.

Campuses and architecture

The academy's main sites are the historic Nanshan Campus beside West Lake and the large Xiangshan Campus in Zhuantang, designed by architect Wang Shu — who taught at the academy and won the 2012 Pritzker Architecture Prize — using millions of recycled tiles and bricks from demolished traditional houses. The academy also runs the Shanghai Institute of Design in Zhangjiang. Its Folk Art Museum on the Xiangshan campus was designed by Kengo Kuma.

Academics and alumni

CAA offers programs across fine arts, design, architecture, film and animation, art theory, and crafts, with notable strengths in Chinese painting, calligraphy, printmaking, and sculpture. Distinguished figures associated with the school include Pan Tianshou, Wu Guanzhong, Zao Wou-Ki, Chu Teh-Chun, and Lin Fengmian, who shaped modern Chinese art education by blending Chinese and Western traditions.
