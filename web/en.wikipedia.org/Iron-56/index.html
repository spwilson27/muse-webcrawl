Iron-56

Iron-56 (56Fe) is the most common isotope of iron. About 91.754% of natural iron is iron-56. Of all nuclides, iron-56 has the lowest mass per nucleon, and is often described—slightly imprecisely—as the most stable nucleus; nickel-62 in fact has the highest binding energy per nucleon, but iron-56 is the most common end product of stellar nucleosynthesis.

Formation

Iron-56 is produced in massive stars through the silicon-burning process, in which an equilibrium of capture and photodisintegration reactions (the "e-process") builds up nuclei in the iron peak. It is also the decay product of nickel-56, which is synthesized in large quantities in Type Ia supernovae; the radioactive decay chain 56Ni → 56Co → 56Fe powers the light curves of these supernovae.

Significance

Because fusion reactions producing nuclei heavier than the iron group are endothermic, stars cannot gain energy by fusing iron, and the accumulation of an iron core leads to core-collapse supernovae in massive stars.
