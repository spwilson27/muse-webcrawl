Ken-Betwa River Link

The Ken–Betwa link transfers surplus water from the Ken river basin to the water-deficit Betwa basin.

The Ken-Betwa River Link Project is a river interlinking project in India that transfers water from the Ken River to the Betwa River, both tributaries of the Yamuna. It is the first project to be taken up under India's National Perspective Plan for interlinking of rivers, and is intended to provide irrigation and drinking water to the drought-prone Bundelkhand region, which spans parts of Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh.

Background

Bundelkhand has historically suffered from recurrent drought, low agricultural productivity and distress migration. The Ken basin was assessed to have surplus water, while the Betwa basin was deficit; the link was conceived to balance the two. A tripartite memorandum of understanding was signed in 2005, and the detailed project report was prepared by the National Water Development Agency.

Project components

The project centres on the Daudhan dam on the Ken river in Madhya Pradesh, near the Panna Tiger Reserve. A link canal roughly 221 kilometres long carries water to the Betwa basin, where it feeds existing and new reservoirs. The project is planned in two phases and includes hydroelectric power generation of over 100 MW together with solar power capacity. It is expected to irrigate around 10.6 lakh hectares and supply drinking water to several million people.

Controversy

The project has been criticised by environmentalists and wildlife experts because the Daudhan reservoir submerges a significant part of the core habitat of the Panna Tiger Reserve, home to a reintroduced tiger population as well as vultures and other wildlife. Critics have also questioned the hydrological assumptions about surplus water in the Ken basin and the project's cost, which has risen to tens of thousands of crores of rupees. Supporters argue that the water security benefits for Bundelkhand outweigh these concerns.
