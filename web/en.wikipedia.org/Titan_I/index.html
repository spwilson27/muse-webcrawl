Titan I

The HGM-25A Titan I was the United States' first true intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) built on a two-stage design, and the first in the Titan family of rockets. Developed by the Glenn L. Martin Company for the US Air Force in the late 1950s, it was operational only briefly, from 1962 to 1965, before being replaced by the storable-fuel Titan II.

Design and deployment

Titan I burned RP-1 kerosene with liquid oxygen oxidiser, which had to be loaded shortly before launch, making it slow to respond: the missile, stored horizontally in a hardened underground complex, had to be fuelled and raised to the surface by lift before firing — a process taking about fifteen minutes. Fifty-four missiles were deployed in six squadrons of nine at bases in Colorado, California, Idaho, South Dakota and Washington. Its radio-command guidance was less accurate than later inertial systems.

Retirement

The rapid obsolescence of its cryogenic fuel system led to withdrawal by 1965. Some retired Titan I stages were later used in sounding-rocket and target roles, while many silo complexes were stripped and abandoned.
