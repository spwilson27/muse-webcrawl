Said Halim Pasha

Said Halim Pasha (Ottoman Turkish: سعيد حليم پاشا; 1864 – 6 December 1921) was an Ottoman statesman of Albanian and Egyptian descent who served as Grand Vizier of the Ottoman Empire from 1913 to 1917, during the critical years leading into and through the beginning of the First World War.

Life and career

Said Halim was a grandson of Muhammad Ali of Egypt and was educated in Switzerland. After a career in the Ottoman administration and Council of State, he became Grand Vizier in June 1913 after the assassination of Mahmud Shevket Pasha. A member of the Committee of Union and Progress, he was known for his writings on Islamic political thought.

During his tenure the Ottoman Empire concluded the secret Ottoman–German alliance of August 1914 and entered World War I. He resigned in February 1917 amid disagreements with the CUP leadership. After the war he was exiled to Malta by the British; following his release he settled in Rome, where he was assassinated in 1921 by an Armenian gunman as part of Operation Nemesis.
