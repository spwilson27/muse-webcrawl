Galactic plane

DefinitionPlane containing the majority of a galaxy's mass
Milky WayPasses through the Galactic Center in Sagittarius
CoordinatesGalactic latitude b = 0°

The galactic plane is the plane on which the majority of a disc-shaped galaxy's mass lies — the midplane of its rotating stellar disc. For the Milky Way, it is the reference plane of the galactic coordinate system: objects at galactic latitude 0° lie in the plane, which passes through the Galactic Center in the direction of the constellation Sagittarius.

Relation to the sky

The Milky Way's galactic plane is inclined by about 63° to the celestial equator. Viewed from Earth, the band of the Milky Way traces the plane across the sky, because most of the Galaxy's stars, gas, and dust are concentrated in the thin disc. The Sun lies slightly above the midplane — estimates place it around 50–80 light-years north of the plane — and oscillates through it over tens of millions of years as it orbits the Galactic Center.

Zone of avoidance

Dust concentrated in the galactic plane absorbs visible light, hiding roughly a fifth of the extragalactic sky from optical telescopes — the so-called zone of avoidance — which must be studied instead at infrared and radio wavelengths. The plane also defines subdivisions of galactic structure: the thin disc, thick disc, and the stellar halo that extends far above and below it.

Other galaxies

Edge-on spiral galaxies display their galactic planes as dark dust lanes bisecting a bright stellar disc, as in the Sombrero Galaxy. Galactic planes provide the natural reference for describing spiral structure, satellite galaxy alignments, and galactic rotation.
