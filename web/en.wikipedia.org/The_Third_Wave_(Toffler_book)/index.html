The Third Wave

Author: Alvin Toffler

Published: 1980

Preceded by: Future Shock (1970)

Subject: Social and technological change

The Third Wave is a 1980 book by the American writer and futurist Alvin Toffler. A sequel to Future Shock (1970), it divides human civilisation into three great waves of change: the agricultural revolution (the First Wave), the industrial revolution (the Second Wave), and the post-industrial information age (the Third Wave) then emerging.

Ideas

Toffler argued that Third Wave society would be characterised by decentralised production, the "prosumer" who both produces and consumes, the "electronic cottage" enabling work from home, demassified media, and new family and corporate forms. He coined several terms and predicted much of the texture of the internet age.

Influence

The book was an international bestseller and was influential among politicians and technologists, including in China, where it became a bestseller in the 1980s, and in the United States, where it influenced the politics of Newt Gingrich.
