Planet X

Planet X is a hypothetical planet in the outer Solar System whose gravitational effects were once thought to explain apparent discrepancies in the orbits of Uranus and Neptune. The search for it, led most prominently by Percival Lowell, contributed to the discovery of Pluto in 1930. However, Pluto's mass was found to be far too small to account for the discrepancies, which were eventually resolved when Neptune's mass was revised downward following the Voyager 2 flyby in 1989. The term has since been applied to other hypothesized planets beyond Neptune.

History

After the discovery of Neptune in 1846, discrepancies between the observed and predicted positions of Uranus led several astronomers to propose an additional planet. Percival Lowell founded the Lowell Observatory and calculated a predicted position for "Planet X" in the early 20th century. Following his death, Clyde Tombaugh's systematic sky survey at the observatory discovered Pluto near Lowell's predicted position in 1930—a coincidence, since Pluto could not perturb Uranus measurably.

Later hypotheses

Speculation about distant planets has continued, including the Nemesis hypothesis of a stellar companion, the proposed gas giant Tyche in the Oort cloud, and the current scientific hypothesis of Planet Nine, inferred from the clustered orbits of extreme trans-Neptunian objects, proposed by Konstantin Batygin and Michael Brown in 2016. Planet X has also featured in pseudoscientific and doomsday claims, notably the "Nibiru cataclysm", which have no scientific support.
