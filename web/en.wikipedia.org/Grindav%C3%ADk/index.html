Grindavík

Grindavík is a fishing town on the southern shore of the Reykjanes Peninsula in Iceland, with a population of roughly 3,800. It is one of the few towns in Iceland with a harbour on the south coast, and much of its economy has historically rested on fishing and fish processing. The nearby Blue Lagoon geothermal spa and the Svartsengi power station are in its vicinity.

History

Grindavík has been an important fishing station since the settlement era and was a trading post in the Middle Ages. In 1627 it was attacked in the Turkish Abductions, a series of Barbary pirate raids on Iceland. The town grew with the mechanisation of the fishing fleet in the twentieth century and became a municipality in 1974.

2023–2024 volcanic unrest

In November 2023, intense earthquake swarms and ground deformation associated with the Sundhnúkur volcanic system led to the precautionary evacuation of the whole town. A fissure eruption on 18 December 2023 occurred a few kilometres northeast of Grindavík, and on 14 January 2024 lava from a new fissure just north of the town destroyed several houses. Further eruptions followed through 2024, with defensive barriers built to divert lava away from the town, the Svartsengi plant, and the Blue Lagoon. Most residents were relocated while authorities monitored the ongoing activity.

Sport

The town's sports club, UMF Grindavík, fields teams in football and basketball; its men's football team has played in Iceland's top flight, the Úrvalsdeild karla.
