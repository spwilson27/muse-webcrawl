Stephen Sondheim

Born: 22 March 1930, New York City, US
Died: 26 November 2021 (aged 91), Roxbury, Connecticut
Occupations: Composer, lyricist
Notable works: Company, Sweeney Todd, Into the Woods, Sunday in the Park with George

Stephen Joshua Sondheim (22 March 1930 – 26 November 2021) was an American composer and lyricist, widely regarded as one of the most important figures in 20th-century musical theatre. Mentored as a teenager by Oscar Hammerstein II, he first achieved prominence as lyricist of West Side Story (1957) and Gypsy (1959).

Career

From the 1970s, in collaboration with director Harold Prince, Sondheim created a series of acclaimed "concept musicals", including Company (1970), Follies (1971), A Little Night Music (1973), Pacific Overtures (1976) and Sweeney Todd (1979). Later works, many with book writer and director James Lapine, include Sunday in the Park with George (1984), which won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama, Into the Woods (1987), Passion (1994) and Assassins (1990).

Legacy

Sondheim won an Academy Award, eight Tony Awards (more than any other composer), eight Grammy Awards and a Pulitzer Prize, and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2015. His complex, ambiguous explorations of love, obsession and modern life transformed the scope of the American musical.
