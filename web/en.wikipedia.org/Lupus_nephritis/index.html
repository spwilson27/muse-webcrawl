Lupus nephritis

Lupus nephritis is inflammation of the kidneys caused by systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), an autoimmune disease. It is a type of glomerulonephritis in which immune complexes deposit in the glomeruli, and it is a major cause of morbidity in lupus patients.

Signs and symptoms

Presentation ranges from asymptomatic haematuria and proteinuria to nephrotic syndrome and rapidly progressive renal failure. General lupus symptoms such as fatigue, fever and rash may accompany it.

Classification

The International Society of Nephrology/Renal Pathology Society classification divides lupus nephritis into six classes, from class I (minimal mesangial) through class IV (diffuse proliferative) to class VI (advanced sclerosing). Kidney biopsy guides classification and treatment.

Treatment

Proliferative disease is treated with immunosuppression, typically corticosteroids combined with mycophenolate mofetil or cyclophosphamide; voclosporin and belimumab have been added as options. Blood pressure control and renin–angiotensin blockade support kidney function.
