Roald Hoffmann

Roald Hoffmann, theoretical chemist and Nobel laureate

Roald Hoffmann (born Roald Safran; July 18, 1937) is an American theoretical chemist who won the 1981 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. He has also published plays and poetry. He is the Frank H. T. Rhodes Professor of Humane Letters Emeritus at Cornell University.

Early life

Hoffmann was born in Złoczów, Poland (now Zolochiv, Ukraine), to a Jewish family. He and his mother survived the Holocaust in hiding; his father was killed by the Nazis. The family emigrated to the United States in 1949. He graduated from Stuyvesant High School in New York, Columbia University in 1958, and received his PhD from Harvard University in 1962, working under William Lipscomb.

Scientific work

With Robert Burns Woodward he formulated the Woodward–Hoffmann rules, which explain the stereochemistry of pericyclic reactions through orbital symmetry conservation; the 1981 Nobel Prize, shared with Kenichi Fukui, recognised this work. Hoffmann also developed the extended Hückel method for molecular orbital calculations and the isolobal analogy in organometallic chemistry.

Writing and the arts

Hoffmann has written extensively on the connections between science and the humanities, including the books The Same and Not the Same and Oxygen, a play co-authored with Carl Djerassi. He hosted the television series "The World of Chemistry" and has published several collections of poetry.
