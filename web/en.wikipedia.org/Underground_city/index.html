Underground city

An underground city is a series of linked subterranean spaces that may provide a defensive refuge, a place for living and working, or facilities such as shops, transit stations, and walkways below a surface city.

Historic examples

Ancient multi-level underground cities such as Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı in Cappadocia, Turkey, sheltered thousands of people with dwellings, stables, churches, and ventilation shafts. Beijing's Underground City (Dixia Cheng) was built during the Cold War as a vast air-raid shelter.

Modern pedestrian networks

Many modern cities maintain climate-controlled underground pedestrian systems, such as Montreal's RÉSO, Toronto's PATH, and the tunnels of Houston and Chicago, connecting offices, shops, and transit stations.
