The Michigan–Ohio State football rivalry, commonly known simply as The Game, is an American college football rivalry game played annually between the Michigan Wolverines and the Ohio State Buckeyes. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest rivalries in North American sports, traditionally played on the last Saturday of the Big Ten regular season, frequently with the conference championship and national title implications at stake.

History

The teams first met in 1897, and the rivalry intensified after Ohio State joined the Western Conference (Big Ten) in 1913; Michigan dominated the early decades. Animosity between the states is sometimes traced to the Toledo War of 1835–36, a bloodless border dispute over the Toledo Strip. The rivalry reached its cultural peak during the "Ten-Year War" (1969–1978) between Michigan coach Bo Schembechler — a former Ohio State assistant — and his mentor, Ohio State's Woody Hayes, a span in which each game effectively decided the Big Ten title and Rose Bowl berth.

Memorable games

Famous editions include the 1950 "Snow Bowl", the 1973 10–10 tie that ended with a controversial conference vote sending Ohio State to the Rose Bowl, the 2006 "Game of the Century" between No. 1 Ohio State and No. 2 Michigan played a day after Schembechler's death, and Michigan's 2021 victory that ended an eight-game Ohio State winning streak en route to a run of Big Ten championships and the 2023 national title. Ohio State enjoyed a long period of dominance from 2001 under Jim Tressel and Urban Meyer.

Traditions

Rivalry customs include Michigan fans referring to Ohio State only as "Ohio", Ohio State players receiving gold pants charms for victories over Michigan, and the crossing out of the letter "M" on the Ohio State campus during rivalry week. The all-time series has remained close, with Michigan holding the historical edge in wins.
