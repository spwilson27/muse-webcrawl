Pounamu

Pounamu

Also known as: New Zealand greenstone

Minerals: Nephrite jade, bowenite, serpentinite

Source: South Island, New Zealand

Guardianship: Ngāi Tahu (since 1997)

Pounamu is the Māori name for several types of hard, durable green stone found in the South Island of New Zealand, principally nephrite jade and bowenite. Pounamu is one of the most treasured materials in Māori culture — a taonga (treasure) used for tools, weapons and personal ornaments of great prestige.

The stone occurs in river gravels and mountain sources on the West Coast and in Southland. Named varieties are classified by colour and translucency, including the prized translucent inanga, the deep green kawakawa and the rare kahurangi.

Uses

Because of its toughness, pounamu was worked into adzes (toki), chisels and the short flat club known as the mere, a weapon of high-status warriors and chiefs. Ornaments include the hei tiki pendants, ear pendants (kuru) and neck pendants. Finished pieces were heirlooms, accumulating mana as they passed between generations.

Ownership and protection

Under the Ngāi Tahu Settlement of 1997, ownership of pounamu in its natural state within the tribe's territory was vested in Te Rūnanga o Ngāi Tahu, who manage its extraction and authenticate carved work. Pounamu carving remains a living art and a significant expression of Māori identity.
