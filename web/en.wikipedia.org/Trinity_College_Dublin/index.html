Trinity College Dublin (Irish: Coláiste na Tríonóide, Baile Átha Cliath), officially The College of the Holy and Undivided Trinity of Queen Elizabeth near Dublin, is the sole constituent college of the University of Dublin, Ireland. Founded in 1592 by Queen Elizabeth I, it is Ireland's oldest surviving university and is widely regarded as its most prestigious.

History

Trinity was established by royal charter in 1592 on the site of the former Priory of All Hallows, just outside the old city walls of Dublin. Modelled on the collegiate universities of Oxford and Cambridge, it was founded in part to consolidate Tudor rule in Ireland and to provide a Protestant university education. For much of its history, admission was restricted by religion; Catholics were formally permitted to attend from 1793 but were discouraged by their church from doing so until 1970.

Campus and library

The college occupies a 47-acre site in the centre of Dublin, bounded by College Green and Nassau Street. Landmarks include Front Square, the Campanile (1853), the Chapel, the Old Library, and the Museum Building. The Library of Trinity College is the largest research library in Ireland and a legal deposit library for both Ireland and the United Kingdom. Its Old Library's Long Room holds some 200,000 of the library's oldest books, and the college is home to the Book of Kells, a celebrated illuminated manuscript created around 800 AD, which is displayed in the Old Library and attracts over a million visitors a year.

Academics

Trinity is organised into three faculties — Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences; Engineering, Mathematics and Science; and Health Sciences — comprising 24 schools. It is consistently ranked as Ireland's leading university and among the top universities in Europe. Notable alumni include writers Jonathan Swift, Bram Stoker, Oscar Wilde, and Samuel Beckett; mathematician William Rowan Hamilton; physicist Ernest Walton, a Nobel laureate; and philosopher George Berkeley.

Student life

The college has a long tradition of student societies, including the University Philosophical Society (founded 1683) and the College Historical Society (1770), among the oldest student societies in the world. The annual Trinity Ball is one of the largest private parties in Europe.
