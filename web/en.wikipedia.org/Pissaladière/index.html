Pissaladière (UK: /ˌpɪsəˈlædjɛər/, US: /pɪˌsɑːləˈdjɛər/; French: [pisaladjɛʁ]; Niçard: pissaladiera) is a dish of flatbread with toppings in the cuisines of the city of Nice, France, and the region of Provence. It is often compared to pizza. The dough is usually a bread dough thicker than that of the classic Italian pizza (but a flaky pastry is sometimes used), and the traditional topping consists of caramelised (almost pureed) onions, black olives, and anchovies; no cheese is traditionally used in Nice.

Etymology

The name derives from the Occitan/Niçard pissalat, a condiment of salted, fermented fish paste (from peis salat, "salted fish") that was historically spread on the flatbread. Anchovy fillets are now commonly used in place of the pissalat paste.

History

The dish is thought to have been introduced to the Nice area through Ligurian influence from neighbouring Italy, where similar onion-topped flatbreads (such as the Genoese piscialandrea, named after Admiral Andrea Doria) are traditional. Pissaladière was traditionally a street food and baker's fare, sold by the slice.

Preparation

A yeasted bread dough is rolled out and covered with a thick layer of onions that have been slowly cooked in olive oil with herbs such as thyme and bay until soft and sweet. The onions are spread over the dough, anchovy fillets are arranged in a lattice pattern, and Niçoise olives are placed in the squares of the lattice. The tart is baked until the crust is golden and crisp. It is eaten hot or at room temperature, often as an appetizer or light meal with a green salad and rosé wine.

Variations

Some versions add tomatoes, which purists in Nice reject; others substitute sardines for anchovies. In the western Mediterranean, related onion flatbreads include the Catalan coca de ceba.
