Triple Entente

European alliances on the eve of the First World War

The Triple Entente (from French entente, "understanding") describes the informal alliance between the Russian Empire, the French Third Republic, and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. It was built upon the Franco-Russian Alliance of 1892, the Entente Cordiale of 1904 between Paris and London, and the Anglo-Russian Entente of 1907.

Formation

Following the creation of the German Empire in 1871 and Chancellor Otto von Bismarck's system of alliances, France sought a counterweight in an alliance with Russia, formalised in 1892. Britain, abandoning its policy of "splendid isolation", settled its colonial disputes with France in the Entente Cordiale of 1904 and with Russia in the Anglo-Russian Convention of 1907, which divided Persia into spheres of influence and settled questions concerning Afghanistan and Tibet.

Unlike the rival Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy, the Triple Entente was not originally a treaty of mutual military assistance — the agreements were "ententes", understandings settling colonial questions rather than formal alliances, although the Franco-Russian pact did contain military commitments, and Anglo-French military staff talks from 1906 gradually gave the Entente a strategic character.

First World War

When the July Crisis of 1914 escalated after the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand, the Entente powers entered the First World War against the Central Powers. Russia mobilised in support of Serbia; Germany declared war on Russia and France; and Britain declared war on Germany after the German invasion of Belgium. The Entente, joined by Italy in 1915, Japan, and eventually the United States in 1917, formed the core of the Allied Powers. The alliance effectively dissolved with Russia's withdrawal after the October Revolution and the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk in 1918.
