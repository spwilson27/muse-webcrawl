Battle of Adwa

Date1 March 1896
LocationAdwa, Tigray, Ethiopia
ResultDecisive Ethiopian victory
BelligerentsEthiopian Empire vs Kingdom of Italy

The Battle of Adwa (Amharic: አድዋ) was the climactic battle of the First Italo-Ethiopian War, fought on 1 March 1896 near the town of Adwa in northern Ethiopia. The Ethiopian forces of Emperor Menelik II won a decisive victory over the invading Italian army of General Oreste Baratieri, securing Ethiopian independence and ending Italy's attempt to build an empire in Africa at that time — the sole successful African resistance to European colonisation in the 19th century era of the Scramble for Africa.

After Italy disputed the Treaty of Wuchale, which it claimed authorised an Italian protectorate over Ethiopia, Menelik denounced the treaty and mobilised a vast army — perhaps 100,000 men — from across his empire. Baratieri, under pressure from Rome and short of supplies, advanced with about 15,000–18,000 men in four brigades that became separated in difficult terrain during a night march. On the morning of 1 March, Ethiopian forces engaged and destroyed the Italian brigades piecemeal. Italian losses included some 6,000–7,000 killed — many of them Eritrean askari — while Ethiopian casualties were heavy as well, on the order of several thousand.

News of the disaster brought down the Italian government of Francesco Crispi and led to the Treaty of Addis Ababa (October 1896), by which Italy recognised Ethiopia's absolute independence. The victory at Adwa became a potent symbol for anti-colonial movements worldwide and for pan-Africanism; 1 March is commemorated in Ethiopia as Victory of Adwa Day.
