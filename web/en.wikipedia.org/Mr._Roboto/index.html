Mr. Roboto

"Mr. Roboto" is a song by the American rock band Styx, released in 1983 as the lead single from their eleventh studio album, Kilroy Was Here. Written by keyboardist and vocalist Dennis DeYoung, the song is a rock opera piece built around the album's concept narrative, in which a rock musician named Robert Orin Charles Kilroy (whose initials spell "rock") is imprisoned by a future anti-rock regime and escapes disguised as one of the "Roboto" robots that serve as enforcers and labourers.

Content and themes

The song opens with synthesized Japanese-language phrases — most famously "Domo arigato, Mr. Roboto" ("thank you very much, Mr. Roboto") — and features heavy use of vocoder-processed vocals to give Kilroy's voice a robotic quality. The lyrics explore themes of dehumanization, technology, censorship, and the individual's struggle against a conformist society. The repeated refrain "I've got a secret I've been hiding under my skin" refers to Kilroy's concealed human identity beneath the robot disguise.

Reception and legacy

"Mr. Roboto" reached number three on the Billboard Hot 100 in the United States and was a major hit in Canada and Japan, where the song's Japanese phrases made it especially popular. Despite its commercial success, the song and its parent album were divisive: guitarist Tommy Shaw was reportedly unhappy with the theatrical direction, and tensions around the Kilroy Was Here tour contributed to the band's hiatus. In later decades "Domo arigato, Mr. Roboto" became a widely quoted pop-culture catchphrase.
