Miguel de Cervantes

Miguel de Cervantes Saavedra (29 September 1547 (assumed) – 22 April 1616) was a Spanish writer widely regarded as the greatest writer in the Spanish language and one of the world's pre-eminent novelists. He is best known for his novel Don Quixote, a work often cited as both the first modern novel and one of the pinnacles of world literature. His influence on the Spanish language has been so great that the language is sometimes called la lengua de Cervantes ("the language of Cervantes").

Life

Cervantes was born in Alcalá de Henares, the fourth of seven children of a barber-surgeon. Little is securely known of his education. In 1569 he fled Spain for Italy, apparently after wounding a man in a duel, and in Rome entered the service of Cardinal Giulio Acquaviva. He enlisted as a soldier and fought at the Battle of Lepanto in 1571, where despite a fever he refused to stay below decks; he was shot twice in the chest and once in the left hand, which was maimed for life — an injury he later called a glorious badge of honour.

In 1575, returning to Spain, Cervantes was captured by Barbary pirates and spent five years as a slave in Algiers, making several escape attempts before being ransomed by Trinitarian friars in 1580. Back in Spain he worked as a purchasing agent for the Spanish Armada and later as a tax collector in Andalusia, jobs that brought him debt and brief imprisonments. Tradition holds that he began writing Don Quixote during one such confinement.

Works

The first part of Don Quixote appeared in 1605 and was an immediate success across Europe. A spurious sequel by another author (writing as Alonso Fernández de Avellaneda) appeared in 1614, prompting Cervantes to complete his own second part in 1615. The novel follows the deluded hidalgo Alonso Quixano, who sets out as the knight-errant Don Quixote with his squire Sancho Panza, and is celebrated for its interplay of idealism and realism, its self-conscious narration, and its profound influence on the development of the novel.

His other works include the pastoral romance La Galatea (1585), the Novelas ejemplares (Exemplary Novels, 1613), a collection of twelve short stories, the poem Viaje del Parnaso (1614), and the Byzantine romance Los trabajos de Persiles y Sigismunda, published posthumously in 1617, which he considered his finest work.

Death and legacy

Cervantes died in Madrid on 22 April 1616 of complications associated with diabetes, and was buried the following day — the same date traditionally given for the death of William Shakespeare, though the two dates belong to different calendars (Gregorian and Julian respectively). UNESCO marks 23 April, the burial date, as World Book Day in their honour. His remains, long lost, were reported rediscovered in the Convent of the Barefoot Trinitarians in Madrid in 2015.
