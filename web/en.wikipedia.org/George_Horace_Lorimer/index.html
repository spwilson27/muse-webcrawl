George Horace Lorimer (October 6, 1867 – October 22, 1937) was an American journalist and author, best known as the editor of The Saturday Evening Post from 1899 to 1936, during which he transformed it into one of the most popular magazines in the United States.

Early life

Lorimer was born in Louisville, Kentucky. After working in the meat-packing business in Chicago and studying at Colby College and Yale, he turned to journalism, working for newspapers in Portland, Maine, and Boston.

The Saturday Evening Post

In 1899 Cyrus Curtis hired Lorimer to edit the struggling Saturday Evening Post. Under Lorimer, circulation grew from a few thousand to more than three million. He published leading writers including F. Scott Fitzgerald, Sinclair Lewis, and Jack London, and made illustrator Norman Rockwell's covers an American institution.

Writing

Lorimer wrote the best-selling Letters from a Self-Made Merchant to His Son (1902), a series of folksy business letters that was translated into many languages. He retired as editor at the end of 1936 and died the following year.
