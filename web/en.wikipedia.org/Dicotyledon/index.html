Dicotyledon

The dicotyledons, also known as dicots, are one of the two traditional groups into which flowering plants (angiosperms) were divided, the other being the monocotyledons. The name refers to the typical characteristic that the seed has two embryonic leaves or cotyledons. There are roughly 200,000 species within this group.

Characteristics

Compared with monocots, dicots typically have two seed leaves; net-like (reticulate) leaf venation; flower parts in multiples of four or five; vascular bundles arranged in a ring in the stem, often permitting secondary growth and true wood; and a persistent primary root (taproot) system.

Classification

Modern molecular phylogenetics has shown that the traditional dicots are not a single clade: most dicots belong to the eudicots, which share the distinctive triaperturate (three-grooved) pollen, while a grade of earlier-diverging groups — the magnoliids and basal angiosperms such as water lilies — also have two cotyledons but fall outside both eudicots and monocots. The APG classification systems therefore no longer recognize "dicotyledons" as a formal taxon.
