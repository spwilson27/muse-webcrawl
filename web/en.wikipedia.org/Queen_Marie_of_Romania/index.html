Queen Marie of Romania

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Marie of Romania (born Princess Marie of Edinburgh; 29 October 1875 – 18 July 1938) was the last queen consort of Romania as the wife of King Ferdinand I. Born into the British royal family — a granddaughter of Queen Victoria and of Tsar Alexander II of Russia — she married Crown Prince Ferdinand of Romania in 1893.

Reign

Marie became queen in 1914 on Ferdinand's accession, shortly after the outbreak of the First World War. Romania joined the Allies in 1916; after the fall of Bucharest, Marie worked as a nurse and became a symbol of national resistance at the provisional capital of Iași. At the Paris Peace Conference of 1919 she personally lobbied for Romania's claims, helping secure the union of Transylvania, Bessarabia, and Bukovina with the Old Kingdom — "Greater Romania". She was crowned with Ferdinand at Alba Iulia in 1922.

Later life and legacy

Widowed in 1927, Marie was sidelined during the reign of her son Carol II but remained a popular writer and cultural figure, known for her memoirs and fairy tales, and for her residences at Bran Castle and Balchik. She died in 1938 at Peleș Castle; her heart, kept in a silver casket, was eventually returned to rest at Bran. Marie remains one of Romania's most admired historical figures.
