Nemanjić dynasty

The Nemanjić dynasty (Serbian: Немањићи) was the ruling dynasty of medieval Serbia from about 1166 to 1371. Founded by Stefan Nemanja, it transformed Serbia from a Byzantine-influenced principality into a kingdom (from 1217) and, under Stefan Dušan, a short-lived empire (1346–1355).

History

Stefan Nemanja unified the Serbian lands of Raška and Zeta and abdicated in 1196 to become a monk. His son Stefan the First-Crowned was crowned king in 1217, while another son, Saint Sava, secured autocephaly for the Serbian Orthodox Church in 1219. The dynasty's power peaked under Stefan Dušan, crowned emperor in 1346, who conquered much of the Balkans and promulgated Dušan's Code. After his death in 1355 the empire fragmented; the last Nemanjić ruler, Stefan Uroš V, died in 1371.

Legacy

The Nemanjići endowed many monasteries, including Studenica, Hilandar, Žiča and Gračanica, and most of the dynasty's rulers were canonised as saints in the Serbian Orthodox Church.
