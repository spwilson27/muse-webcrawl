Giant's Causeway

The Giant's Causeway (Irish: Clochán an Aifir) is an area of about 40,000 interlocking basalt columns on the north coast of County Antrim, Northern Ireland, resulting from an ancient volcanic fissure eruption.

LocationBushmills, County Antrim, Northern Ireland
Formedc. 50–60 million years ago
DesignationUNESCO World Heritage Site (1986)

Geology

During the Paleogene, intense volcanic activity in the North Atlantic Igneous Province poured out basaltic lava that cooled and contracted, fracturing into regular, mostly hexagonal columns—similar in origin to Fingal's Cave in Scotland.

Legend

In Irish mythology, the causeway was built by the giant Fionn mac Cumhaill (Finn McCool) as a path across the North Channel to confront the Scottish giant Benandonner. It is the most popular tourist attraction in Northern Ireland and was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.
