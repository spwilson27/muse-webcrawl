Dan Ingalls

Dan Ingalls
Born: Daniel Henry Holmes Ingalls Jr., 1944, Washington, D.C., U.S.
Field: Computer science
Known for: Smalltalk, BitBlt, Squeak

Daniel Henry Holmes Ingalls Jr. (born 1944) is an American computer scientist and a principal architect of the Smalltalk programming language. Working with Alan Kay's Learning Research Group at Xerox PARC in the 1970s, Ingalls implemented the first versions of Smalltalk and collaborated on five generations of the system, helping to define object-oriented programming as it is known today.

Contributions

Ingalls invented BitBlt (bit block transfer), the graphics operation underlying modern window systems, allowing rapid copying and manipulation of rectangular areas of bitmap memory. He also articulated an influential set of design principles behind Smalltalk, published in a 1981 Byte magazine article. Later he led the Squeak project, an open, portable implementation of Smalltalk written largely in itself, and worked on the Lively Kernel, an environment for building web applications directly in the browser. He has also been associated with Interval Research, Sun Microsystems' laboratories, and SAP.

Recognition

Ingalls received the ACM Grace Murray Hopper Award in 1984 for his work on Smalltalk, and shared the ACM Software System Award in 1987 with Alan Kay and Adele Goldberg.
