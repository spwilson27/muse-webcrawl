Cerebral palsy

OnsetEarly childhood
CausesAbnormal brain development or damage before, during or shortly after birth
TypesSpastic, dyskinetic, ataxic, mixed

Cerebral palsy (CP) is a group of permanent disorders of movement and posture that appear in early childhood, caused by abnormal development of, or damage to, the parts of the brain that control movement, balance and posture.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms vary widely, from mild clumsiness to severe disability requiring lifelong care. Common features include stiff or floppy muscles, involuntary movements, poor coordination and delays in reaching motor milestones. Associated conditions can include epilepsy, speech and learning difficulties, and vision or hearing problems.

Causes and management

Risk factors include premature birth, low birth weight, infections during pregnancy and lack of oxygen around the time of birth, though in many cases no single cause is identified. There is no cure, but physical, occupational and speech therapy, medications, orthotics and sometimes surgery can improve function and quality of life.
