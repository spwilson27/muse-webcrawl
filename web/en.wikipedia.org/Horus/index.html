Horus, also known as Heru, is one of the most significant ancient Egyptian deities, worshipped from at least the late prehistoric period until Roman times. He was most often depicted as a falcon, or as a man with a falcon's head, and was associated with the sky, kingship and protection.

Mythology

In the Osirian myth cycle, Horus is the son of Osiris and Isis, conceived after Osiris's murder by his brother Set. Horus grew up to avenge his father, battling Set in a long series of contests for the throne of Egypt; in one famous episode Set tore out Horus's eye, which the god Thoth restored — the origin of the protective Eye of Horus (wedjat) amulet. Horus's eventual triumph legitimized the living pharaoh, who was regarded as the incarnation of Horus, while the deceased king became Osiris.

Kingship

From the earliest dynasties the king's first royal name, the Horus name, was written inside a serekh surmounted by the Horus falcon. Major temples of Horus include those at Nekhen (Hierakonpolis), one of Egypt's oldest cult centres, and the exceptionally well-preserved Ptolemaic temple at Edfu.

Forms

Egyptian theology recognized many local and syncretic forms of the god, including Horus the Elder (Haroeris), the child Horus (Harpocrates), Ra-Horakhty ("Horus of the Two Horizons," fused with the sun god Ra), and the "Four Sons of Horus" who guarded the canopic jars of the dead.
