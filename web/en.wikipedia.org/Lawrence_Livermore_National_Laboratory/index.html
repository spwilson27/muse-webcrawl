Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory

Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) is a federally funded research and development centre in Livermore, California, United States. Founded in 1952 by Edward Teller and Ernest Lawrence, the laboratory is primarily funded by the U.S. Department of Energy and managed by Lawrence Livermore National Security, LLC. Its main mission is to strengthen national security through the application of science and technology, particularly in nuclear weapons stewardship, energy, and national defence.

History

The laboratory was established as a second site for nuclear weapons design, complementing Los Alamos National Laboratory, at the urging of Edward Teller, who was dissatisfied with progress on the hydrogen bomb at Los Alamos. Known for decades as Lawrence Radiation Laboratory at Livermore, it played a central role in developing many United States nuclear warheads and in advancing magnetic and laser fusion research. Since the end of underground nuclear testing in 1992, Livermore's weapons work has centred on stockpile stewardship—maintaining the safety and reliability of the existing arsenal through simulation and experiment rather than explosive testing.

Facilities and programmes

The laboratory is home to the National Ignition Facility (NIF), the world's largest and most energetic laser system, which in December 2022 achieved fusion ignition—producing more fusion energy than the laser energy delivered to the target—for the first time in a laboratory setting. Livermore also operates some of the world's most powerful supercomputers, including systems developed with IBM and, in the exascale era, El Capitan. Research programmes span high-energy-density physics, biosecurity, energy and climate science, advanced manufacturing, and nonproliferation.

Organisation

The 1.3-square-mile main site is located about 40 miles east of San Francisco, adjacent to Sandia National Laboratories' California site. The laboratory employs roughly 8,000 people and has an annual budget of around $2.5–3 billion. A second site, Site 300, is a high-explosives test facility located about 15 miles southwest of the main site.
