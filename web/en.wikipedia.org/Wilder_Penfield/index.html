Wilder Penfield

Wilder Graves Penfield (January 26, 1891 – April 5, 1976) was an American-Canadian neurosurgeon. He expanded brain surgery methods and techniques, including mapping the functions of various regions of the brain such as the cortical homunculus.

Early life and education

Penfield was born in Spokane, Washington, and studied at Princeton University before winning a Rhodes Scholarship to Oxford, where he studied neuropathology under Sir Charles Sherrington. He completed his medical training at Johns Hopkins and trained in neurosurgery in New York.

Montreal Neurological Institute

In 1934 he founded the Montreal Neurological Institute at McGill University, which became a world centre for the treatment of epilepsy and for brain research.

The Montreal procedure and the homunculus

Treating epilepsy surgically, Penfield electrically stimulated the exposed brains of awake patients under local anaesthetic to locate seizure tissue while avoiding essential areas. From these stimulations he mapped the sensory and motor cortices, producing the famous distorted body maps known as the Penfield homunculi, and reported that stimulation could evoke vivid memories and experiential responses.

Later life and legacy

Penfield wrote scientific works and the bestseller The Mystery of the Mind (1975). He is regarded as a founder of modern neurosurgery in Canada.
