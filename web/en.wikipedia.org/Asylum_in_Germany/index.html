Asylum in Germany


Asylum claims in Germany are decided by the Federal Office for Migration and Refugees (BAMF).

Asylum in Germany is governed by the Basic Law, the Asylum Act and European Union law. Article 16a of the Basic Law grants persons persecuted on political grounds a constitutional right of asylum, a provision shaped by Germany's experience of National Socialism and the refugees it produced.

Procedure

Applications are lodged with the Federal Office for Migration and Refugees (BAMF). Applicants are distributed among the federal states by the Königsteiner Schlüssel quota, housed in initial reception facilities, and interviewed about their grounds for flight. Recognised outcomes include constitutional asylum, refugee protection under the Geneva Convention, subsidiary protection, and national deportation bans. During the procedure applicants receive benefits under the Asylum Seekers' Benefits Act and are subject to residence restrictions.

History

After large application numbers in the early 1990s, the 1993 "asylum compromise" restricted the constitutional right by introducing safe-third-country and safe-country-of-origin rules. During the 2015–16 European migrant crisis Germany received some 890,000 arrivals in 2015 alone and about 745,000 asylum applications in 2016, prompting major administrative expansion and integration measures, and further tightening followed in later years.
