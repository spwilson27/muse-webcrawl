GPT (language model)

Generative pre-trained transformers (GPTs) are a family of large language models developed by the American artificial-intelligence company OpenAI. GPT models are neural networks based on the transformer architecture, pre-trained on large corpora of text using the objective of predicting the next token, and then fine-tuned for tasks such as dialogue, summarisation, and question answering.

History

The original GPT (2018) demonstrated that generative pre-training followed by discriminative fine-tuning improved results across natural-language tasks. GPT-2 (2019), with 1.5 billion parameters, was initially withheld in full over concerns about misuse before being released later that year. GPT-3 (2020) scaled to 175 billion parameters and showed strong few-shot learning, performing tasks from examples in its prompt without further training. GPT-3.5, aligned with human feedback through reinforcement learning (RLHF), powered the launch of ChatGPT in November 2022, which brought the technology to mass public attention. GPT-4 (2023) added multimodal (image) input and substantially improved reasoning and exam performance; subsequent models such as GPT-4o and the reasoning-oriented "o" series extended speed, multimodality, and step-by-step inference.

Method

GPT models are decoder-only transformers trained autoregressively: each token is predicted from the tokens before it. Pre-training on hundreds of billions of words endows the model with general linguistic and factual knowledge; alignment techniques (supervised fine-tuning on demonstrations, then reinforcement learning from human preferences) make outputs more helpful and safe. Performance has broadly followed scaling laws relating loss to model size, data, and compute, though the models can produce fluent but false statements ("hallucinations") and reproduce biases present in training data.

Impact

GPT models underpin ChatGPT, API services used across industry, and features such as code assistants. They have reshaped research in natural language processing, stimulated global investment in AI infrastructure, and prompted debates about employment, education, misinformation, copyright, and AI governance.
