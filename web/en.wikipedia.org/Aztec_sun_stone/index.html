Aztec sun stone

The Aztec sun stone, also called the Calendar Stone, is a large monolithic sculpture that was excavated in the Zócalo, the main square of Mexico City, on 17 December 1790. It is 3.6 metres in diameter, about 1.2 metres thick, and weighs more than 24 tonnes.

Description

The stone depicts the Aztec creation myths of the Five Suns, with the face of the sun god Tonatiuh at its centre, surrounded by calendrical glyphs including the 260-day tonalpohualli cycle and representations of the four previous eras or "suns".

History

The stone was carved in the late 15th or early 16th century, probably during the reign of Axayacatl or Moctezuma II. After the Spanish conquest it was buried beneath the Zócalo. Today it is displayed at the National Museum of Anthropology in Mexico City, and reproductions of it appear widely in Mexican art and coinage.
