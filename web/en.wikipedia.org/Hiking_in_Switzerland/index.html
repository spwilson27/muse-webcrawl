Hiking in Switzerland

Hiking in Switzerland is one of the country's most popular outdoor activities, supported by a uniformly signposted network of about 65,000 kilometres of trails — a density unmatched anywhere in the world — maintained by the cantons and coordinated by the Swiss Hiking Trail Federation (Schweizer Wanderwege / Suisse Rando), founded in 1934.

Trail system and signage

Swiss hiking trails are classified by difficulty and marked with a distinctive system of signs. Ordinary hiking trails (Wanderwege) are marked in yellow; mountain trails (Bergwanderwege) require sure-footedness and are marked white-red-white; demanding alpine routes (Alpinwanderwege) are marked white-blue-white. Yellow directional signposts at junctions give destinations with walking times, a system so standard that it is protected under federal law, and Switzerland's network is integrated into public transport timetables.

National and regional routes

The SwitzerlandMobility foundation promotes national long-distance routes, including the Via Alpina across the Swiss Alps, the Alpine Passes Trail, the Jura Crest Trail, and the Trans Swiss Trail from Porrentruy to Chiasso. Mountain huts run by the Swiss Alpine Club (SAC), founded in 1863, provide accommodation along high routes; famous areas include the Bernese Oberland, Zermatt and the Matterhorn, the Engadine, and the Aletsch Glacier region, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Public access

Swiss law guarantees broad freedom to roam in forests and pastures, and the right of way along marked trails is entrenched in the Federal Constitution (Article 88), obliging the cantons to maintain the network. Hiking is a mass-participation pursuit: surveys regularly find that more than half of the Swiss resident population hikes each year.
