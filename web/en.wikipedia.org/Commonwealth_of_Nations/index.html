The Commonwealth of Nations, often simply the Commonwealth, is an international association of 56 member states, most of them former territories of the British Empire from which it developed. The chief institutions of the organisation are the Commonwealth Secretariat, which focuses on intergovernmental aspects, and the Commonwealth Foundation, which focuses on relations between citizens of member countries.


Member states span every inhabited continent.

History

The Commonwealth emerged from the British Empire's self-governing Dominions. The Balfour Declaration of 1926 declared the United Kingdom and the Dominions equal in status, and the Statute of Westminster 1931 gave this legal form. The London Declaration of 1949 allowed republics to remain members while recognising the British monarch as Head of the Commonwealth, enabling India to stay after becoming a republic. From the 1960s, decolonisation transformed the membership into a predominantly African and Asian association; the Secretariat was established in 1965.

Values and activities

The Commonwealth Charter (adopted 2012, signed 2013) commits members to democracy, human rights, good governance, the rule of law, and sustainable development. Heads of government meet biennially at CHOGM. The association runs programmes on elections, education, health, youth, and small-state vulnerability, and stages the Commonwealth Games every four years. English is the working language, and many members share common-law traditions.

Membership

Membership is voluntary and open, by consensus of existing members, to countries with a constitutional association with an existing member — though Mozambique (1995), Rwanda (2009), and Gabon and Togo (2022) joined without a British colonial past. Fifteen members are Commonwealth realms sharing Charles III as head of state. Members have occasionally left or been suspended: South Africa withdrew in 1961 (rejoining in 1994), Pakistan left in 1972 (rejoining in 1989), Zimbabwe withdrew in 2003, and Fiji, Nigeria, and Pakistan have all been suspended at times over democratic breakdowns.
