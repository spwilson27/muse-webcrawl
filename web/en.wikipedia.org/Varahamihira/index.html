Varahamihira

Varāhamihira (c. 505 – c. 587 CE), also called Varāha or Mihira, was an Indian astronomer, astrologer, and polymath who lived in Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh. He was one of the most celebrated scholars of the Gupta period and is counted among the "nine jewels" (Navaratnas) of the legendary court of Vikramaditya.

Works

His best-known work is the Pañca-Siddhāntikā ("Treatise on the Five Astronomical Canons", c. 575 CE), which summarises five earlier astronomical systems, including the Greek-influenced Romaka and Paulisa Siddhantas, and preserves valuable information on earlier Indian astronomy. His encyclopaedic Bṛhat-Saṃhitā covers astrology, architecture, gemology, agriculture, meteorology, and many other subjects, while the Bṛhat-Jātaka became a foundational text of Hindu natal astrology.

Contributions

Varahamihira improved the accuracy of sine tables, worked on trigonometric identities, and described properties of the binomial coefficients (the "Meru-Prastara", or Pascal's triangle). He also wrote on eclipses, planetary motions, and rainfall prediction.
