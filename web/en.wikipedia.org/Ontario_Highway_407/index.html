Highway 407 is a tolled 400-series highway in Ontario, Canada, spanning the Greater Toronto Area from Burlington in the west to Clarington in the east. It was the first highway in the world to use fully electronic open-road tolling, billing users by licence-plate camera or transponder without toll booths.

History and ownership

Planning for a northern bypass of Toronto began in the 1950s; construction started in the late 1980s and the first sections opened in 1997. In 1999 the provincial government leased the original section (Highway 407 ETR — Express Toll Route) to a private consortium for 99 years, a controversial transaction criticized by the provincial auditor and opposition parties. Eastern extensions built later (opened in phases between 2016 and 2019) are owned by the province and tolled at different rates.

Characteristics

Because of its tolls, the 407 generally flows freely while the parallel Highway 401 is congested; its peak-period tolls are among the highest in North America. The highway serves the suburbs of Halton, Peel, York, and Durham regions and connects with most north–south 400-series highways across the Toronto area.
