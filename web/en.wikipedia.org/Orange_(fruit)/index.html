Orange (fruit)

The orange is the fruit of the citrus species Citrus × sinensis in the family Rutaceae, a hybrid between the pomelo and the mandarin. It is a hesperidium — a berry with a leathery rind — cultivated worldwide for its sweet, juicy flesh and as the source of orange juice.

Scientific nameCitrus × sinensis
OriginSouthern China and Southeast Asia
Major producersBrazil, China, India, United States
Notable varietiesNavel, Valencia, blood orange

Description

The orange tree is an evergreen flowering tree generally growing to 9–10 metres. The fruit's rind (peel) is bright orange when ripe, enclosing segmented flesh divided by membranes; most varieties are seedless or nearly so when grown for fresh consumption. Oranges are rich in vitamin C and contain sugars, citric acid and flavonoids such as hesperidin.

History

Sweet oranges arose in southern China and were mentioned in Chinese literature by 314 BC. Portuguese and Spanish traders spread them to the Mediterranean and the Americas in the 15th and 16th centuries; the English word derives from Sanskrit nāraṅga via Persian and Old French. The fruit gave its name to the colour orange in English.

Production and uses

Brazil is the largest producer, and most of its crop is processed into juice. Oranges are eaten fresh, juiced, and used for zest, marmalade and flavourings; the peel also yields essential oils. Citrus greening disease (huanglongbing) is a major threat to production in Florida and elsewhere.
