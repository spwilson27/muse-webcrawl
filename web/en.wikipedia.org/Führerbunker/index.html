Führerbunker

Führerbunker
Location: Berlin, Germany
Coordinates: 52°30′45″N 13°22′52″E
Built: 1936 (Vorbunker); 1944 (extension)
Demolished: 1947; remains removed 1988–89

The Führerbunker ("Leader's bunker") was an air-raid shelter located near the Reich Chancellery in Berlin, Germany. It was part of a subterranean bunker complex constructed in two phases in 1936 and 1944. It was the last of the Führer Headquarters (Führerhauptquartiere) used by Adolf Hitler during World War II.

Hitler took up residence in the Führerbunker on 16 January 1945, and it became the centre of the Nazi regime until the last week of World War II in Europe. Hitler married Eva Braun there on 29 April 1945, less than 40 hours before they committed suicide on 30 April.

Construction

The bunker complex lay roughly 5 metres (16 ft) beneath the garden of the old Reich Chancellery. The older, upper Vorbunker was completed in 1936; the deeper Führerbunker proper, finished in October 1944, had walls of reinforced concrete up to several metres thick, its own water and power supplies, and some thirty small rooms including Hitler's study and living quarters, a conference room, and quarters for Joseph Goebbels and his family.

Events of 1945

During the Battle of Berlin in April 1945, Hitler directed the defence of the city from the bunker, refusing to leave the capital. On 22 April he suffered a collapse after learning that a planned counterattack had not taken place. On 29 April he married Eva Braun and dictated his political testament. The following day, 30 April 1945, Hitler and Braun committed suicide; their bodies were carried up to the Chancellery garden and burned. Goebbels, briefly Chancellor, killed his six children and committed suicide with his wife Magda on 1 May. The remaining occupants surrendered to Soviet forces on 2 May.

Post-war

The Soviets attempted to blow up the bunker in 1947; the ruins were largely buried and the site later lay near the Berlin Wall in East Berlin. In 1988–89, during construction of housing, workers uncovered sections of the complex, and most of the remains were removed. Since 2006 an information board marks the site, which today is a residential car park.
