Boston Mountains

Location: Arkansas and Oklahoma, USA
Highest point: Turner Ward Knob (2,463 ft)
Range: Ozark Mountains (highest part)
Rock type: Pennsylvanian sandstone and shale

The Boston Mountains is a Level III ecoregion designated by the United States Environmental Protection Agency in Arkansas and Oklahoma. It is the highest and most rugged part of the Ozark Mountains, forming the southern portion of the Ozarks in northwestern Arkansas and, to a lesser extent, northeastern Oklahoma.

Geography

The range runs roughly 200 miles east–west. Peaks rise to over 2,400 feet, with Turner Ward Knob in Newton County, Arkansas, among the highest at 2,463 feet. Deep, steep-sided valleys carved by streams such as the Buffalo, Mulberry and White rivers separate broad, flat-topped summits.

Geology

The Boston Mountains are a dissected plateau of Pennsylvanian-age sandstone and shale. Uplift followed by erosion cut valleys more than 1,500 feet deep in places, exposing cliffs and rock shelters used by Indigenous peoples for millennia.

Recreation

Much of the range lies within the Ozark National Forest. The region contains the Buffalo National River, Devil's Den State Park and the Boston Mountains Scenic Loop, and is known for hiking, canoeing and autumn foliage.
