Durham Castle

Durham Castle is a Norman castle in the city of Durham, England, which has been occupied since 1837 by University College, Durham, after its previous role as the residence of the Bishops of Durham. It is designated since 1986 as a UNESCO World Heritage Site along with Durham Cathedral. The castle stands on top of a hill above the River Wear on Durham's peninsula, opposite Durham Cathedral.

History

The castle was originally built in the 11th century as a project of William the Conqueror to show Norman power and authority in the north of England, as the population of England in the north remained rowdy after the conquest in 1066. It was an example of the early motte and bailey castles favoured by the Normans. The holder of the office of Bishop of Durham was appointed by the king to exercise royal authority on his behalf, and the castle was the centre of his command.

It was refortified by Henry de Beaumont, and was held for the King during The Anarchy. The castle remained the bishop's palace for the Bishop of Durham until the abolition of the prince-bishopric in 1836, when Durham Castle was donated to the newly formed University of Durham by Edward Maltby, Bishop of Durham.

It was occupied by University College, Durham in 1837, and still contains over 100 students, most of whom live in the Keep. The castle was substantially restored as part of the restoration of the University buildings between 1968 and 1994.

Architecture

The castle features two chapels: the Norman Chapel, built in 1080, the oldest surviving part of the castle, and Tunstall's Chapel, built in 1540. The keep, added in 1366 by Bishop Hatfield, was ruined by the Civil War and was rebuilt in the 19th century. The castle's Great Hall, created by Bishop Antony Bek in the early 14th century, is 29 metres long and over 14 metres high and is one of the largest in the country.

The Black Staircase, which dates from 1662–65 and leads from the courtyard to the Great Hall, was originally built with 22 steps before being rebuilt. The castle gatehouse leads toward Palace Green at the front of the castle.

World Heritage Site

Durham Castle was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986, along with Durham Cathedral, "because of the importance of the site as evidence of the growth of a monastic city on the site of an early Christian settlement" and the castle's role as a residence of the prince-bishops.
