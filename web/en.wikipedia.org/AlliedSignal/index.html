AlliedSignal

AlliedSignal was an American aerospace, automotive and engineering company created in 1985 through the merger of the Allied Corporation and the Signal Companies. Headquartered in Morristown, New Jersey, it was a component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average.

History

The Allied Corporation traced its origins to the Allied Chemical and Dye Corporation, formed in 1920 by a merger of five chemical companies to reduce American dependence on German chemical imports. The Signal Companies grew out of the Signal Oil and Gas Company founded by Samuel B. Mosher in 1922 and had become a diversified conglomerate with interests including Garrett AiResearch aerospace products. Their 1985 merger created Allied-Signal, a name rendered as AlliedSignal from 1993.

The company manufactured aerospace systems (including auxiliary power units and avionics), automotive products such as Bendix brakes and Fram filters, and specialty chemicals and fibres (including Spectra high-performance polyethylene fibre).

Merger with Honeywell

In 1999 AlliedSignal acquired Honeywell Inc. in a transaction valued at about $15 billion. Because Honeywell had the better-known name, the combined company adopted the name Honeywell International Inc., with headquarters remaining in Morristown. The merger joined AlliedSignal's large aerospace business with Honeywell's controls and automation operations.
