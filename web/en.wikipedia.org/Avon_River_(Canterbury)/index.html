Avon River (Canterbury)

The Avon River (Māori: Ōtākaro) is a river about 14 kilometres long that flows through the city of Christchurch in the Canterbury region of New Zealand's South Island. It rises from springs in the western suburb of Avonhead, winds through the city centre and Hagley Park, and meets the Heathcote River at the Avon Heathcote Estuary.

Name and history

The river was named by the Deans brothers after the River Avon in Scotland, near their homeland; its Māori name Ōtākaro means "the place of a game", referring to children playing on its banks. In 2017 the name was officially altered to Avon River / Ōtākaro. The river became a defining feature of planned Christchurch: its banks carry the city's botanical gardens, the Antigua boat sheds, and punting — flat-bottomed boat rides — which are a signature tourist activity.

Earthquakes

The 2010–2011 Canterbury earthquakes damaged areas along the river, and land in the eastern suburbs along its lower course subsided and was zoned a residential red zone. The cleared corridor is being redeveloped as the Ōtākaro Avon River Corridor, a large green space and flood-management project.
