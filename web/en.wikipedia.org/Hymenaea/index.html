Hymenaea

Hymenaea is a genus of flowering plants in the legume family, Fabaceae. Of the fourteen living species in the genus, all but one are native to the tropical Americas, with one additional species (Hymenaea verrucosa) native to the east coast of Africa. Some species grow as large emergent trees in rainforest canopies, reaching 40 metres in height.

Species and uses

The best known species is Hymenaea courbaril, called jatobá in Brazil and algarrobo in Spanish; its hard, woody fruit pods contain an edible, mealy pulp, and its dense timber is valued for furniture and flooring. Hymenaea trees produce a resin that, when fossilized, forms amber — Dominican and Mexican amber commonly derives from the extinct species Hymenaea protera. Copal resins from living Hymenaea were historically used as incense and varnish.

Ecology

Most Hymenaea species grow in lowland tropical forests. Their large seeds are dispersed by mammals such as agoutis and, in the past, may have been dispersed by extinct megafauna. Several species are threatened by logging and habitat loss.
