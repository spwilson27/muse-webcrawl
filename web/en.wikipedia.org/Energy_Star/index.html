Energy Star

Energy Star
Type: Energy efficiency labeling program
Established: 1992
Administered by: U.S. Environmental Protection Agency; U.S. Department of Energy

Energy Star (trademarked ENERGY STAR) is a program run by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the U.S. Department of Energy to promote energy efficiency. Products that meet strict efficiency criteria earn the Energy Star label, helping consumers identify appliances, electronics, buildings, and homes that use less energy.

History

The program began in 1992 as a voluntary labeling scheme for computers and monitors. It expanded to cover heating and cooling equipment, major appliances, lighting, office equipment, and entire buildings through benchmarking tools such as Portfolio Manager.

Impact

The EPA reports that Energy Star has saved American families and businesses hundreds of billions of dollars in energy costs and avoided billions of metric tons of greenhouse gas emissions since its inception. The program has been adopted or adapted in countries including Canada, Australia, and members of the European Union.
