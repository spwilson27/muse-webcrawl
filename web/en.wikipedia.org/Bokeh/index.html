Bokeh

OriginJapanese boke (ぼけ), "blur" or "haze"
FieldPhotography
RelatedDepth of field; aperture; circle of confusion

In photography, bokeh is the aesthetic quality of the blur produced in out-of-focus parts of an image by a lens. Bokeh has been defined as "the way the lens renders out-of-focus points of light". Differences in lens aberrations and aperture shape cause very different bokeh characteristics between lenses.

Origin of the term

The term comes from the Japanese word boke (暈け or ボケ), meaning "blur" or "haze". It entered English photographic usage in the 1990s, and the spelling "bokeh" was popularised in 1997 by Photo Techniques magazine to suggest the correct pronunciation.

Characteristics

Good bokeh is often described as smooth and creamy, especially in the background, with out-of-focus highlights rendered as soft discs. Lens design factors include spherical aberration correction, the number and shape of aperture blades, and aperture size; wide-aperture lenses with rounded blades typically produce smoother bokeh, while mirror lenses produce distinctive "doughnut" rings.

Uses

Photographers use shallow depth of field and pleasing bokeh to isolate subjects — especially in portraiture — and computational photography in smartphones now simulates the effect in software ("portrait mode").
