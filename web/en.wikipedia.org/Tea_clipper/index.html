Tea clipper

A tea clipper was a fast sailing ship of the 19th century used in the trade carrying tea from China to Britain and other markets. Built as extreme clippers with narrow hulls and large sail areas, they raced to deliver the first teas of the season, which commanded a premium.

History

The trade boomed after the end of the East India Company's monopoly in 1834 and the repeal of the Navigation Acts in 1849 allowed American clippers such as Oriental to compete. The famous Great Tea Race of 1866 saw Taeping, Ariel and Serica arrive in London within hours of each other after some 99 days from Fuzhou. The opening of the Suez Canal in 1869 and improving steamships ended the tea clipper era. The best-known survivor is the Cutty Sark (1869), preserved at Greenwich.
