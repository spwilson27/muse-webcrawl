Shōmyō

Shōmyō (Japanese: 声明) is a style of Japanese Buddhist chant, the ritual recitation of sutras and other sacred texts in melody. The word derives from the Sanskrit śabdavidyā, the "science of sound", one of the five traditional sciences of Indian learning. Shōmyō was transmitted to Japan from China, with roots in Indian Buddhist chant, during the Nara and early Heian periods.

Characteristics

Shōmyō is monophonic and sung a cappella by monks, using melodic formulas governed by a system of neumatic-like notation marks called hakase. Texts may be chanted in Sanskrit (bonsan), Chinese (kansan), or Japanese (wasan) pronunciation. The two main surviving schools are those of the Tendai and Shingon sects, centred at temples such as Enryaku-ji and on Mount Kōya.

Influence

Shōmyō is regarded as one of the roots of later Japanese vocal music, and its melismatic style influenced secular genres. Ensembles of monks still perform shōmyō in concert settings, and it influenced 20th-century composers interested in Japanese traditional music.
