Pennsylvania Dutch

The Pennsylvania Dutch (Deitsch), also known as Pennsylvania Germans, are a cultural group formed by early German-speaking immigrants to Pennsylvania and their descendants. They settled the region from the late 17th to the mid-19th centuries, fleeing religious persecution and seeking economic opportunity. "Dutch" here derives from Deutsch, not the Netherlands.

Groups

The Pennsylvania Dutch include "Plain" groups — the Amish, Old Order Mennonites, and Brethren — known for simple dress and limited use of technology, and "Fancy" (gay Dutch) church groups, mainly Lutheran and Reformed, who assimilated more fully into American life.

Language

Pennsylvania Dutch (Pennsylvania German, Deitsch) is a dialect descending mainly from Palatine German. It is still spoken by many Old Order Amish and Mennonites, while most descendants today speak English.

Culture

The culture is known for hex signs on barns, fraktur folk art, quilting, and a distinctive cuisine including shoofly pie, scrapple, chow-chow, and chicken pot pie. Lancaster County remains its heartland.
