Twin

Twin
Types: monozygotic (identical), dizygotic (fraternal)
Rate: varies widely by population; dizygotic twinning rises with maternal age

Twins are two offspring produced by the same pregnancy. They may be monozygotic ("identical"), developing from a single fertilised egg that splits, or dizygotic ("fraternal"), developing from two separate eggs fertilised independently.

Types

Monozygotic twins share essentially the same genome and are always of the same sex, except in rare chromosomal cases. Dizygotic twins share on average half their segregating genes, like ordinary siblings, and may be of different sexes. Twins may share a placenta and amniotic sacs in various combinations, which categorises the risks of the pregnancy.

Incidence

Dizygotic twinning rates vary greatly across populations and have risen in many countries with assisted reproductive technologies and increasing maternal age, whereas monozygotic rates are roughly constant worldwide at about 3–4 per 1,000 births.
