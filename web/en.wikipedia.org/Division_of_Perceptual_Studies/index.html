Division of Perceptual Studies

The Division of Perceptual Studies (DOPS) is a unit of the Department of Psychiatry and Neurobehavioral Sciences at the University of Virginia School of Medicine in Charlottesville, Virginia. It is one of the few academic units in the world devoted to the scientific study of phenomena suggestive of survival of consciousness after death, including children who claim to remember previous lives, near-death experiences, and deathbed visions.

History

The division was founded in 1967 by the psychiatrist Ian Stevenson as the Division of Personality Studies, following a bequest from Chester Carlson, the inventor of xerography, who wished to support research into reincarnation. Stevenson directed the unit for decades and built a large case collection of children — mainly in Asia and other regions where reincarnation is culturally accepted — who spontaneously described memories of a previous life. On Stevenson's retirement, Bruce Greyson, a psychiatrist known for research on near-death experiences and for developing the Greyson NDE Scale, became director; Jim B. Tucker later assumed leadership of the division's work on children's cases.

Research areas

  • Children who remember previous lives — field investigations of thousands of cases, examining statements attributed to deceased persons and birthmarks corresponding to reported wounds.
  • Near-death experiences — prospective studies of cardiac-arrest survivors and analyses of experiential features.
  • Apparitions, deathbed visions and related experiences, and the study of mediumship under controlled conditions.

Reception

The division's research is published in peer-reviewed journals, and its archives contain case reports gathered over more than half a century. Its conclusions remain controversial: critics argue the cases can be explained by cultural transmission, selective reporting and the suggestibility of children, while the division's researchers hold that some cases resist simple normal explanation and merit continued investigation. Funding has come principally from private bequests rather than government grants.
