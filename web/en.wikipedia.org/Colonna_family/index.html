Colonna family

The Colonna family is an Italian noble family, forming part of the papal nobility. It was powerful in medieval and Renaissance Rome, supplying one pope (Martin V) and many other church and political leaders. The family is notable for its bitter feud with the Orsini family over influence in Rome, which finally ended with the Peace of Castel Gandolfo in 1511.

History

According to tradition, the Colonna are a branch of the Counts of Tusculum, descended from Theophylact I in the tenth century. The first known member is Pietro de Columna, mentioned in 1101. The family took its name from the town of Colonna, near Rome, where it held lands from an early date.

The family's rivalry with the Orsini shaped Roman politics for centuries. In 1297 Cardinal Jacopo Colonna disputed Pope Boniface VIII, who responded by excommunicating the family and destroying their stronghold at Palestrina. The Colonna later supported Emperor Louis IV against the papacy.

The election of Oddone Colonna as Pope Martin V in 1417 ended the Western Schism and marked the family's ascent to the height of its power. Prospero Colonna (1452–1523) was a celebrated condottiero, and Marcantonio Colonna commanded the papal fleet at the Battle of Lepanto in 1571.

Legacy

The Palazzo Colonna in Rome, seat of the family for some eight centuries, houses the Galleria Colonna, one of the city's great private art collections. The family's coat of arms, a single column crowned, appears throughout Rome.
