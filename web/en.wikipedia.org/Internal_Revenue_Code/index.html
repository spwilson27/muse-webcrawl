The Internal Revenue Code (IRC), formally titled Title 26 of the United States Code, is the main body of domestic statutory tax law of the federal government of the United States. It is administered by the Internal Revenue Service and organised into subtitles covering income taxes, payroll taxes, estate and gift taxes, excise taxes, and tax procedure.

The IRC is the statutory foundation of the US federal tax system.

History

Federal income taxation rests on the Sixteenth Amendment (1913), which authorised Congress to tax incomes without apportionment among the states. Statutory tax law was first codified as the Internal Revenue Code of 1939. A comprehensive revision produced the Internal Revenue Code of 1954, which remained the basic structure until the Tax Reform Act of 1986 renamed it the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 and made extensive changes to rates and deductions. Congress amends the Code frequently; major modern changes include the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017.

Structure

  • Subtitle A — Income taxes (including §61 defining gross income, and §1 imposing the tax)
  • Subtitle B — Estate and gift taxes
  • Subtitle C — Employment (payroll) taxes, including FICA
  • Subtitle D — Miscellaneous excise taxes
  • Subtitle F — Procedure and administration, including penalties and interest
  • Subtitle J — Coal industry health benefits
  • Subtitle K — Group health plan requirements

Interpretation

The Code is interpreted through Treasury regulations, IRS revenue rulings and letter rulings, and decisions of the United States Tax Court, federal district courts, and the Court of Federal Claims. Its complexity is frequently criticised: the National Taxpayer Advocate has repeatedly listed Code complexity among the most serious problems facing taxpayers.
