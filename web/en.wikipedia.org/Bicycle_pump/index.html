Bicycle pump

A bicycle pump is a type of positive-displacement air pump specifically designed for inflating bicycle tires. It has a connection or adapter for use with one or both of the two most common types of valves used on bicycles, Schrader or Presta, and sometimes Dunlop. There are several basic types: floor pumps (also known as track pumps), frame pumps, mini pumps, and CO2 inflators.

A floor (track) pump with a pressure gauge.

Types

Floor pumps

A floor pump stands on the ground and is operated with both hands on a T-shaped handle while the user's feet hold the base steady. Floor pumps usually incorporate a pressure gauge and a long hose, and can reach the high pressures required by narrow road-bike tires.

Frame and mini pumps

Frame pumps are designed to fit along a frame tube of the bicycle, held in place by spring tension or pegs. Mini pumps are shorter versions intended to be carried in a jersey pocket or mounted beside a bottle cage, trading volume per stroke for portability.

CO2 inflators

These use a small cartridge of compressed carbon dioxide and a controlled valve head to inflate a tire rapidly, popular for roadside repairs and racing.

Valves

Pump heads must match the tire valve. Schrader valves are the same type used on car tires and are common on lower-pressure and mountain-bike tires; Presta valves are narrower, have a lock nut that must be unscrewed before inflation, and are standard on most road bikes. Many modern pump heads are reversible or dual-headed to fit both.

How it works

Most bicycle pumps use a piston in a cylinder. On the upstroke, air is drawn into the cylinder past a seal; on the downstroke the seal compresses the air and forces it through the hose and a one-way valve into the tire. A leather or synthetic cup washer on the piston provides the moving seal.
