Antithesis

Antithesis (Greek for "setting opposite") is a rhetorical device in which two opposite ideas are put together in a sentence to achieve a contrasting effect. Antithesis emphasizes the idea of contrast by parallel structures of the contrasted phrases or clauses — the structures of phrases and clauses are similar in order to draw the listener's attention.

Examples

Famous examples include "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times" from Charles Dickens's A Tale of Two Cities; John F. Kennedy's "Ask not what your country can do for you — ask what you can do for your country"; and Neil Armstrong's "one small step for [a] man, one giant leap for mankind".

In philosophy

In dialectical philosophy, "antithesis" names the negation or counter-position opposed to a thesis; the conflict is resolved in a synthesis that preserves elements of both. The thesis–antithesis–synthesis scheme is popularly associated with Hegel, though he did not use those exact terms.
