Karl Marx

Born: 5 May 1818, Trier, Prussia
Died: 14 March 1883 (aged 64), London, England
Key works: The Communist Manifesto (1848), Das Kapital (1867–1894)

Karl Marx (5 May 1818 – 14 March 1883) was a German philosopher, political theorist, economist, historian, sociologist, journalist, and revolutionary socialist. His best-known works are the 1848 pamphlet The Communist Manifesto and his three-volume critique of political economy, Das Kapital (1867–1894). Marx's ideas have exercised an enormous influence on modern intellectual, economic, and political history.

Life

Born in Trier to a middle-class family of Jewish origin converted to Lutheranism, Marx studied law and philosophy at the universities of Bonn and Berlin, where he engaged with the Young Hegelians and the philosophy of Hegel. After editing the radical Rheinische Zeitung, he moved to Paris in 1843, where he began his lifelong collaboration with Friedrich Engels. Expelled from several countries for his revolutionary journalism, he settled in London in 1849, where he lived in relative poverty, writing in the British Museum Reading Room while helping to organise the International Workingmen's Association (First International).

Thought

Marx developed historical materialism, the view that societies develop through modes of production and class conflict. He analysed capitalism as a system driven by the extraction of surplus value from wage labour, prone to crises, and destined — in his theory — to be superseded by socialism and ultimately a classless communist society. Other key concepts include alienation, commodity fetishism, the labour theory of value, and the base-and-superstructure model of society.

Legacy

Marx's ideas underpin numerous schools of thought collectively called Marxism and profoundly shaped the 20th century, inspiring socialist and communist movements and states, as well as academic disciplines from sociology to literary criticism. His work remains the subject of intense debate.
