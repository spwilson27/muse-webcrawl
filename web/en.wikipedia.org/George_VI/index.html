George VI, King of the United Kingdom from 1936 until his death in 1952.

George VI (Albert Frederick Arthur George; 14 December 1895 – 6 February 1952) was King of the United Kingdom and the Dominions of the British Commonwealth from 11 December 1936 until his death. He was concurrently the last Emperor of India until August 1947, when the British Raj was dissolved.

Early life

Known as "Bertie" among his family and close friends, George VI was born in the reign of his great-grandmother Queen Victoria and was the second son of the future King George V. He served in the Royal Navy and Royal Air Force during the First World War. In 1923, he married Lady Elizabeth Bowes-Lyon, with whom he had two daughters, Elizabeth and Margaret.

Accession

George VI came to the throne unexpectedly after his elder brother Edward VIII abdicated in December 1936 in order to marry the American divorcée Wallis Simpson. Shy and afflicted by a stammer — which he worked to overcome with the help of speech therapist Lionel Logue — the new king nevertheless won respect for his sense of duty.

Second World War and later reign

During the Second World War, George VI and Queen Elizabeth became symbols of national resistance, remaining in London through the Blitz and visiting bombed areas and troops. His reign saw the acceleration of the dissolution of the British Empire and its transition into the Commonwealth of Nations, the independence of India and Pakistan in 1947, and the start of post-war austerity and the creation of the welfare state. Plagued by ill health in his final years, he died in 1952 and was succeeded by his elder daughter, Elizabeth II.
