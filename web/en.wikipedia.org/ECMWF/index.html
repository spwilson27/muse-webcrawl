European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts

ECMWF
Full name: European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts
Founded: 1975
Headquarters: Reading, United Kingdom
Membership: 23 European member states

The European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) is an independent intergovernmental organisation supported by most of the nations of Europe. It is one of the world's leading centres for numerical weather prediction, best known for its Integrated Forecasting System (IFS), whose global model is frequently ranked among the most accurate medium-range weather models.

ECMWF produces global weather forecasts used by national meteorological services.

Activities

ECMWF operates one of the largest supercomputer facilities in Europe and runs global atmospheric and ocean forecasts out to several weeks ahead, twice-daily deterministic forecasts, and a 51-member ensemble prediction system. Its data are supplied to the national weather services of its member and cooperating states.

Research and services

The centre also runs the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) and the Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) on behalf of the European Union, produces the ERA5 global climate reanalysis, and contributes to flood awareness systems such as EFAS and the fire forecasting service EFFIS.

History

ECMWF was established by a convention signed in 1973 and began operations in 1975, issuing its first operational forecasts in 1979. In 2021 it opened a data centre in Bologna, Italy, housing its supercomputing operations.
