Nanchang

Nanchang (Chinese: 南昌; pinyin: Nánchāng) is the capital of Jiangxi Province in southeastern China. Located on the banks of the Gan River near Lake Poyang, China's largest freshwater lake, it is a prefecture-level city of over five million people and a major transport and industrial hub of central China.

History

The city was founded in the Han dynasty around 201 BCE as the seat of Yuzhang Commandery. Its name, meaning "southern flourishing", dates from 589 CE. The Tengwang Pavilion, a landmark overlooking the Gan, was first built in 653 CE and celebrated in a famous preface by the Tang poet Wang Bo.

Nanchang is revered in China as the "Hero City": on 1 August 1927 the Nanchang Uprising, led by Zhou Enlai, Zhu De and others, marked the founding of the Chinese People's Liberation Army, and 1 August is still celebrated as Army Day. During the Second Sino-Japanese War the city fell to Japanese forces in 1939.

Economy

Nanchang is a centre for aircraft manufacturing (Hongdu), automobiles, electronics and textiles, and is served by Changbei International Airport and an expanding metro system.
