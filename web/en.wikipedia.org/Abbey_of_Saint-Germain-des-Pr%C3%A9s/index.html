Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés

Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés
Order: Benedictine
Founded: 6th century (c. 543–558)
Location: Paris, France

The Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés is a former Benedictine abbey in Paris, France, founded in the 6th century by the Merovingian king Childebert I. Standing just outside the early medieval city walls — hence "des-Prés", "of the fields" — it became the burial place of the Merovingian kings and one of the wealthiest and most learned monasteries in France.

History

The abbey was dedicated to Saint Germanus of Paris, whose tomb it housed. Ravaged by the Normans in the 9th century, it was rebuilt in Romanesque and Gothic styles; its church's bell tower, dating to about the year 1000, is among the oldest in Paris. In the 17th century the abbey became a center of scholarship under the Congregation of Saint Maur.

Dissolution

The monastery was suppressed during the French Revolution, and most of its buildings were demolished; the abbey church survives as a parish church and gives its name to the surrounding Saint-Germain-des-Prés quarter of the 6th arrondissement.
