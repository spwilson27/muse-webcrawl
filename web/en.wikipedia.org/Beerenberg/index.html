Beerenberg

Beerenberg is a stratovolcano dominating the northeastern end of the Norwegian island of Jan Mayen in the Arctic Ocean. At 2,277 metres (7,470 ft) above sea level, it is the world's northernmost subaerial active volcano and the highest mountain in Norway outside mainland Norway's Jotunheimen peaks—indeed, it is the highest volcano in the Norwegian realm.

Geography and geology

Beerenberg's ice-capped cone is about 20 km wide at the base and carries the large Weyprecht Glacier flowing from its crater toward the sea; the crater rim's highest point is named Haakon VII Toppen. The volcano consists mainly of basaltic lava flows with minor tephra and forms part of the Jan Mayen volcanic system on the mid-Atlantic rift zone between the Greenland and Norwegian seas.

Eruptions

Historical eruptions were recorded in 1732, 1818, and 1851. After more than a century of quiet, Beerenberg erupted unexpectedly in September 1970, opening fissures on its northeastern flank and forcing the evacuation of the island's meteorological station staff; further eruptions occurred in 1973 and in January 1985. The name "Beerenberg" ("Bear Mountain") derives from Dutch whalers who frequented the island in the 17th century and reported polar bears in the area.
