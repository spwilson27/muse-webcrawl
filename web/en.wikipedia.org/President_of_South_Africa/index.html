President of South Africa

President of the Republic of South Africa
Incumbent: Cyril Ramaphosa (since 15 February 2018)
Residences: Mahlamba Ndlopfu, Pretoria; Genadendal, Cape Town
Term: Five years, renewable once
First post-apartheid holder: Nelson Mandela (1994)

The Union Buildings in Pretoria house the office of the president.

The president of South Africa is both head of state and head of government of the Republic of South Africa. The president is elected by the National Assembly from among its members, usually the leader of the majority party, and serves a term of up to five years, renewable once. The president appoints and leads the cabinet, commands the defence force, assents to bills, and conducts foreign relations.

History

The office descends from the State President of the republic proclaimed in 1961, a ceremonial post until constitutional reforms in 1984 made it an executive presidency under the tricameral system of late apartheid. F. W. de Klerk, the last apartheid-era president, negotiated the transition to democracy. Nelson Mandela became the first president of the democratic republic in 1994, succeeded by Thabo Mbeki (1999–2008), Kgalema Motlanthe (2008–2009) and Jacob Zuma (2009–2018), who resigned amid corruption scandals. Cyril Ramaphosa succeeded Zuma in February 2018 and was re-elected in 2019 and again in 2024, when the ANC lost its majority and formed a government of national unity.
