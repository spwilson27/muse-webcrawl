Dependent origination

Dependent origination (Sanskrit: pratītyasamutpāda; Pali: paṭiccasamuppāda) is a key doctrine of Buddhism stating that all phenomena arise in dependence upon other phenomena: "if this exists, that exists; if this ceases, that ceases."

The twelve nidānas

The doctrine is commonly presented as a chain of twelve links — from ignorance through volitional formations, consciousness, name-and-form, the senses, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and aging-and-death — explaining the arising of suffering and rebirth.

Interpretation

It underpins the Buddhist ideas of impermanence and no-self: since everything is dependently arisen, nothing possesses independent, permanent essence (svabhāva).
