Officer

An officer is a person who holds a position of authority in an organization. The term is used in many contexts, including the military, police forces, merchant navies, and corporations, where officers exercise delegated authority and bear responsibility for those under their direction.

Officers on parade.

Types of officers

  • Military: a commissioned officer (armed forces) holds a commission from the head of state and commands units; non-commissioned officers hold senior enlisted ranks.
  • Police: a police officer is a warranted employee of a police force with powers of arrest and law enforcement.
  • Corporate: officers of a company, such as the chief executive officer and chief financial officer, are appointed by the board to run its daily operations.
  • Maritime: licensed deck and engineering officers operate merchant vessels under the authority of the master.

Etymology

The word derives from the Old French officier, itself from the medieval Latin officiarius, meaning one who performs a duty or office.
