The York Mystery Plays, more properly the York Corpus Christi Plays, are a cycle of dozens of short plays depicting biblical history from the Creation to the Last Judgment. They were performed in the city of York from the fourteenth century until their suppression in 1569, during the feast of Corpus Christi.

History

The plays were produced by the city's trade guilds, each responsible for a pageant whose subject often reflected the guild's craft — the shipwrights, for example, performed the building of Noah's Ark. The pageants were staged on wagons that processed through the streets, stopping at designated stations. The surviving text, known as the York Register, was compiled in the mid-fifteenth century and contains around 47 plays.

Modern revivals

The cycle was revived in 1951 for the Festival of Britain, staged in the ruins of St Mary's Abbey. Since then the plays have been performed regularly in York, both in traditional pageant-wagon form by the guilds and in modern theatrical productions, including large-scale performances in York Minster.
