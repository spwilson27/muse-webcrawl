Shōnen manga

Shōnen manga (少年漫画) is Japanese comics marketed primarily toward an audience of adolescent boys.

Shōnen manga (少年漫画, also romanised as shonen or shounen) is an editorial category of Japanese comics targeting an audience of adolescent boys. It is, along with shōjo manga (targeted at adolescent girls), the most popular form of manga, and includes best-selling series such as Dragon Ball, One Piece, Naruto, and Demon Slayer. The term describes the target demographic of the magazines in which the works are serialised, rather than a genre or style as such.

Characteristics

Shōnen manga characteristically features high-action plots, often with male protagonists, and emphasises themes of friendship, effort, perseverance and victory — values famously associated with the editorial identity of Weekly Shōnen Jump. Common genres include action, adventure, martial arts, mecha, sports, science fiction and comedy, though romance and other genres also appear. Chapters are typically serialised weekly or monthly in anthology magazines and later collected into tankōbon volumes.

Magazines

The best-known shōnen magazine is Shueisha's Weekly Shōnen Jump, first published in 1968, which at its peak in the mid-1990s had a circulation of over six million copies. Other major magazines include Kodansha's Weekly Shōnen Magazine and Shogakukan's Weekly Shōnen Sunday. In recent decades digital platforms such as Shōnen Jump+ and MANGA Plus have expanded the audience worldwide.
