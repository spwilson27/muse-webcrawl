The Four Hills Tournament (German: Vierschanzentournee) is a ski jumping event held annually in Germany and Austria over the New Year period. Comprising four World Cup competitions staged since 1953, it is, along with the Olympics and the World Championships, one of the most prestigious titles in the sport.

The four events

The tournament opens at Oberstdorf, Germany (Schattenbergschanze) on December 29 or 30, followed by the traditional New Year's Day competition at Garmisch-Partenkirchen (Große Olympiaschanze). The series then crosses into Austria for the event at the Bergisel hill in Innsbruck, and concludes on January 6 (Epiphany) with a night event at Bischofshofen (Paul-Ausserleitner-Schanze), the largest of the four hills.

Format

Uniquely, the first round of each event is contested in a knockout format: 25 pairs of jumpers compete head-to-head, with the winners and five "lucky losers" advancing to the final round of 30. The overall champion is the jumper with the highest aggregate points across all four competitions—not necessarily the winner of any single event.

History and records

The tournament grew out of a post-war initiative by ski clubs in the four towns to promote friendship through sport. Finland's Janne Ahonen holds the record with five overall titles. A "Grand Slam"—winning all four events in one year—was first achieved by Sven Hannawald in 2001–02, and repeated by Kamil Stoch (2017–18) and Ryōyū Kobayashi (2018–19). Jens Weißflog and others feature among the tournament's most successful competitors; the event draws enormous television audiences in Central Europe each winter.
