Henry IV (c. April 1367 – 20 March 1413) was King of England from 1399 to 1413. He was the first king of the House of Lancaster and the first English ruler since the Norman Conquest whose mother tongue was English rather than French.

Early life

Henry was born at Bolingbroke Castle in Lincolnshire, the son of John of Gaunt, Duke of Lancaster, third surviving son of Edward III, and Blanche of Lancaster. He is therefore often known as Henry Bolingbroke. He took part in campaigns in Lithuania with the Teutonic Knights and made a pilgrimage to Jerusalem. In 1397 he was created Duke of Hereford by his cousin Richard II, but the following year the king exiled him. When John of Gaunt died in 1399, Richard seized the Lancastrian estates instead of allowing Henry to inherit.

Usurpation

While Richard was campaigning in Ireland, Henry landed at Ravenspur in Yorkshire in July 1399, ostensibly only to claim his inheritance. Support swelled, Richard surrendered, and in September 1399 Parliament accepted Richard's abdication. Henry was crowned on 13 October 1399. Richard died in captivity at Pontefract Castle early the next year.

Reign

Henry's reign was troubled by rebellions arising from the dubious legitimacy of his title. The Epiphany Rising of Richard's former courtiers failed in 1400. In Wales, Owain Glyndŵr proclaimed himself Prince of Wales and waged a long guerrilla war. The Percy family, once Henry's allies, rebelled; Henry Percy ("Hotspur") was killed at the Battle of Shrewsbury in 1403, and his father, the Earl of Northumberland, rose again later. Henry also faced plots involving Archbishop Richard Scrope, executed in 1405. Financial difficulties forced repeated reliance on Parliament, which pressed for greater control over royal expenditure. In his later years Henry suffered from a disfiguring skin disease and other ailments, and his son, the future Henry V, took an increasing role in government.

Death and legacy

Henry died on 20 March 1413 in the Jerusalem Chamber of Westminster Abbey, fulfilling a prophecy that he would die in Jerusalem. He was buried in Canterbury Cathedral. He is a central figure in Shakespeare's history plays Richard II, Henry IV, Part 1 and Henry IV, Part 2.
