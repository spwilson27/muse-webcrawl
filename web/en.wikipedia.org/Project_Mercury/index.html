Project Mercury

Project Mercury was the first human spaceflight program of the United States, run by NASA from 1958 to 1963. Its goals were to orbit a crewed spacecraft, investigate human performance in space, and recover both safely.

Flights

After uncrewed tests and chimpanzee flights (Ham and Enos), Alan Shepard made a suborbital flight aboard Freedom 7 on 5 May 1961, followed by Virgil Grissom. John Glenn became the first American to orbit Earth on 20 February 1962 in Friendship 7. Three more orbital flights followed, ending with Gordon Cooper's 34-hour Faith 7 mission in May 1963. The seven original astronauts — the Mercury Seven — flew single-person capsules launched on Redstone and Atlas rockets. Mercury laid the groundwork for Gemini and Apollo.
