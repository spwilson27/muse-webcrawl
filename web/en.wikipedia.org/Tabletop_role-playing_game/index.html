Tabletop role-playing game

A tabletop role-playing game (abbreviated TRPG or TTRPG), also known as a pen-and-paper role-playing game, is a form of role-playing game in which participants describe the actions of their characters through speech, guided by a formal system of rules and a referee, typically called the gamemaster (GM). Unlike other forms of role-playing, tabletop games are usually conducted face to face (or, increasingly, online) with small groups of players, using dice, character sheets, and rulebooks.

Gameplay

Players create fictional characters defined by numerical statistics and descriptive traits recorded on a character sheet. The gamemaster describes the fictional world and its inhabitants, presents challenges and scenarios, and adjudicates the outcomes of the players' declared actions using the game's rules, often involving rolls of polyhedral dice to introduce chance. Play proceeds as a structured conversation in which characters explore environments, converse with non-player characters, and engage in combat or other contests.

History

The hobby began with the publication of Dungeons & Dragons in 1974, created by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson out of the miniatures wargaming tradition. Through the late 1970s and 1980s many new games appeared, including Traveller, Call of Cthulhu, and GURPS. The 1990s saw a wave of narratively focused games such as Vampire: The Masquerade. In 2000, Wizards of the Coast released the third edition of Dungeons & Dragons under the Open Game License, which enabled a flourishing of third-party and independent ("indie") games that continues today.

Influence

Tabletop role-playing games have had a major influence on video games, giving rise to the computer role-playing game genre and many of its conventions, such as experience points, character classes, and hit points. Actual-play broadcasts and podcasts in the 2010s and 2020s brought the hobby to a much larger audience.
