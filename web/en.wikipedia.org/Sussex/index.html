Sussex is a historic county in South East England, bounded by Hampshire to the west, Surrey to the north, Kent to the north-east and the English Channel to the south. Since 1974 it has been administered as the two counties of East Sussex and West Sussex.

Geography

The South Downs, a range of chalk hills, run across the county, ending at the white cliffs of Beachy Head and the Seven Sisters. The Weald lies to the north. Coastal settlements include Brighton and Hove, Eastbourne, Hastings and Worthing.

History

The name derives from the South Saxons, whose kingdom was founded traditionally by Ælle in the late 5th century. The Battle of Hastings in 1066 was fought in Sussex, and the county retains strong traditions including bonfire societies and the game of stoolball.

Culture

Sussex is celebrated in Rudyard Kipling's writings and in the folk song tradition; its unofficial anthem is "Sussex by the Sea". The county cricket club, founded in 1839, is among the oldest in England.
