Bernard Williams

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sir Bernard Arthur Owen Williams (21 September 1929 – 10 June 2003) was an English moral philosopher. His publications include Problems of the Self (1973), Ethics and the Limits of Philosophy (1985), Shame and Necessity (1993) and Truth and Truthfulness (2002). He was knighted in 1999.

Life and career

Williams was educated at Chigwell School and Balliol College, Oxford, where he took a first in Greats. After national service with the Royal Air Force in Canada, he held fellowships and chairs at Oxford, University College London, Cambridge — where he was Knightbridge Professor of Philosophy — and the University of California, Berkeley. He served as Provost of King's College, Cambridge, and chaired several public inquiries, including the Committee on Obscenity and Film Censorship.

Philosophy

Williams was noted for his critique of systematic moral theories, particularly utilitarianism and Kantianism, which he argued distorted moral life by demanding an impartiality alien to human motivation. His concepts of "ground projects", moral luck, and integrity are central to contemporary debates in ethics. In Problems of the Self and Moral Luck (1981) he examined personal identity and the role of chance in moral judgement; in Descartes: The Project of Pure Enquiry (1978) he reconstructed Descartes's project from a sympathetic but critical perspective. His final book, Truth and Truthfulness, defended the virtues of accuracy and sincerity through a naturalistic genealogy.
