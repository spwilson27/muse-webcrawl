Locational marginal pricing

Transmission congestion causes electricity prices to differ by location.

Locational marginal pricing (LMP) is a market pricing mechanism used in wholesale electric energy markets. It is a method for valuing electric energy at different locations — nodes — on the transmission grid, reflecting the marginal cost of supplying an additional increment of energy at that node, given generation marginal costs, transmission congestion and losses.

Components

An LMP is commonly decomposed into three components:

  • Energy component — the marginal cost of energy at a reference location.
  • Congestion component — the cost imposed by binding transmission constraints between the node and the reference.
  • Loss component — the cost of marginal transmission losses in delivering energy to the node.

Use

LMP (or nodal pricing) is used by several US independent system operators and regional transmission organizations, including PJM, ISO New England, New York ISO, Midwest ISO, California ISO and ERCOT, and in New Zealand's wholesale market. It is contrasted with zonal pricing, in which prices are uniform within larger zones. Financial transmission rights (FTRs) are used to hedge congestion price differences between nodes.
