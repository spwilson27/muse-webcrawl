Polish wine

Polish wine refers to wine produced in Poland. Viticulture in Poland dates to the Middle Ages, with vineyards around monasteries, but the cold continental climate historically limited production and wine was largely imported from Hungary and elsewhere.

Interest revived in the late 20th and early 21st centuries, especially in western and southern regions — Lubusz (around Zielona Góra), Lesser Poland, Subcarpathia and Lower Silesia. Cold-hardy hybrid varieties such as Solaris, Regent, Rondo and Seyval Blanc are common, alongside Riesling and Pinot Noir experiments aided by a warming climate.

Polish wine law aligned with European Union regulations after accession in 2004, and a growing number of small wineries sell primarily on the domestic market.
