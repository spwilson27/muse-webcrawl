Identity politics

Political mobilization organized around shared identity.

Identity politics is politics based on a particular identity, such as ethnicity, race, nationality, religion, denomination, gender, sexual orientation, or social background. The term arose within political science and sociology to describe political activity in which members of a group that perceives itself as systemically disadvantaged organize to promote their group's interests and to counteract perceived exclusion from mainstream political life.

History

The phrase gained currency in the United States in the late 1970s and 1980s, notably in the 1977 manifesto of the Combahee River Collective, a Black feminist organization that framed its politics around interlocking systems of oppression. Movements associated with identity politics include the civil rights movement, second-wave feminism, LGBTQ rights movements, disability rights, and indigenous movements. The related concept of intersectionality, coined by Kimberlé Crenshaw, analyzes how multiple social identities overlap in experiences of discrimination.

Debates

Supporters argue that identity-based organizing gives voice to marginalized groups and that ostensibly universal politics often reflects the perspective of dominant groups. Critics argue that it can fragment broader coalitions, essentialize group identities, or displace attention from economic inequality. The term is used both descriptively by scholars and pejoratively in political debate, across both the left and right of the political spectrum.
