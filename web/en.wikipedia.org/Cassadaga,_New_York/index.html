Cassadaga is a village in Chautauqua County, New York, United States. The village is located within the town of Stockton, east of Lake Erie and south of the city of Dunkirk. Its name derives from a Seneca word meaning "water under the rocks," referring to the natural springs in the area.

The village sits beside Cassadaga Lake, a small lake that has long been a local recreational resource. Cassadaga is perhaps best known regionally for its proximity to the Lily Dale Assembly, a Spiritualist community founded in 1879 on the east side of the lake in the neighboring town of Pomfret, which draws visitors interested in mediumship and Spiritualist practice.

History

The area was settled in the early 19th century. The village of Cassadaga was incorporated in 1921. The Cassadaga Lakes area became a popular summer destination, and the Chautauqua Institution's broader cultural influence in the county helped shape the region's character as a place of lectures, camps, and retreats.

Geography

According to the United States Census Bureau, the village has a total area of about 1.1 square miles, of which a portion is water from Cassadaga Lake. The village lies along New York State Route 60, which connects it to Dunkirk and Fredonia to the north and Jamestown to the south.

Demographics

As of recent censuses, the village population has been in the range of 550–650 residents. The population is predominantly of European ancestry, and the median age is above the state average, reflecting the rural character of the community.
