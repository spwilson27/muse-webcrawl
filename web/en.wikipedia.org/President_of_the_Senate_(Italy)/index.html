President of the Senate (Italy)

The President of the Senate of the Republic (Italian: Presidente del Senato della Repubblica) is the presiding officer of the Italian Senate, the upper house of the Italian Parliament. The office is the second-highest in the constitutional order of precedence of the Italian Republic, after the President of the Republic.

Role and functions

The President of the Senate chairs debates, maintains order in the chamber, and represents the Senate institutionally. Under Article 86 of the Italian Constitution, the President of the Senate acts as substitute for the President of the Republic whenever the latter is unable to perform the functions of office, and assumes the role of acting head of state if the presidency becomes vacant until a new president is elected.

The President is elected by the Senate at the beginning of each legislature, normally by secret ballot requiring an absolute majority. Notable holders of the office have included Amintore Fanfani, Giovanni Spadolini, Carlo Scognamiglio, Marcello Pera, Renato Schifani, Pietro Grasso, and Maria Elisabetta Alberti Casellati, the first woman to hold the post.
