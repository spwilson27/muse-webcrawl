Karol Świerczewski

Karol Świerczewski
Born: 22 February 1897, Warsaw
Died: 28 March 1947, Jabłonki, Poland
Service: Red Army, Polish People's Army
Known as: "General Walter" in the Spanish Civil War

Karol Świerczewski (1897–1947) was a Polish and Soviet Red Army general, a veteran of the Spanish Civil War and the Second World War, and a leading military figure in post-war communist Poland.

Career

Świerczewski joined the Red Army after the October Revolution and fought in the Russian Civil War. Under the nom de guerre "General Walter" he commanded the XIV International Brigade and later a division in Spain. In the Second World War he helped organise and command the Polish armed forces in the USSR, leading the Polish Second Army in 1945.

Death and legacy

He was killed in March 1947 in an ambush by the Ukrainian Insurgent Army near Jabłonki in the Bieszczady mountains; his death served as a pretext for Operation Vistula, the forced resettlement of Ukrainians in Poland. In the Polish People's Republic he was celebrated as a national hero and his portrait appeared on the 50 złoty banknote. See also International Brigades.
