Cram school

A cram school is a specialised school that trains its students to achieve particular goals, most commonly to pass the entrance examinations of high schools or universities. Cram schools are common in East and Southeast Asia, where they are known as juku in Japan, hagwon in South Korea, buxiban in Taiwan and China, and form a major private supplementary tutoring industry.

Students attend cram schools after regular school hours, on weekends, and during holidays, drilling examination techniques and memorising material in intensive sessions. Because admission to prestigious schools strongly influences career prospects, families may spend heavily on cram schooling, and the phenomenon has drawn criticism for student stress, sleep deprivation, and educational inequality.

Regulation

Several governments have attempted to curb the sector: South Korea once banned private tutoring outright (1980, later overturned), and China imposed sweeping restrictions on for-profit tutoring in core school subjects in 2021 under its "double reduction" policy.
