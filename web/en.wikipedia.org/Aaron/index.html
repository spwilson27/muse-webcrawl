Aaron

Aaron was a prophet, the elder brother of Moses, and the first High Priest of the Israelites in the Hebrew Bible. He is traditionally credited with serving as Moses' spokesman because Moses described himself as slow of speech, and he played a central role in the Exodus from Egypt and the wilderness wanderings.

Biblical narrative

Aaron, the son of Amram and Jochebed of the tribe of Levi, helped Moses confront Pharaoh, and his rod performed miracles, including turning into a serpent and blooming with almonds to confirm his priestly authority. He also fashioned the golden calf while Moses was on Mount Sinai, an act for which he was rebuked. Aaron died on Mount Hor at the age of 123, and the priesthood passed to his son Eleazar.

Legacy

In Judaism, Aaron is remembered as a peacemaker and the ancestor of the hereditary priesthood (kohanim). He is also venerated in Christianity and Islam, where he appears as the prophet Harun.
