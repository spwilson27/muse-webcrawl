Becontree

Becontree is a predominantly residential area of east London, England, within the London Borough of Barking and Dagenham, about 11 miles east of Charing Cross. It is named after the ancient Becontree Hundred of Essex. The area is dominated by the Becontree Estate, a vast cottage estate of around 27,000 council houses built by the London County Council between 1921 and 1935, which was the largest public housing development in the world when completed.

History

After the First World War, the London County Council sought land for its "homes fit for heroes" programme. From 1921 it compulsorily purchased farmland across the parishes of Barking, Dagenham and Ilford and built low-density cottage-style houses with gardens, along with schools, churches, shops and the open space of Parsloes Park. Early tenants were largely families rehoused from East End slums; rents were relatively high for the time, favouring regularly employed skilled workers. The estate initially lacked pubs — the first were resisted on temperance grounds — and was served by what is now Becontree station on the District line, opened in 1932.

Today

Much of the housing has passed into private ownership through Right to Buy. The estate's scale and social history make it a landmark in British town planning and public housing, and it remains home to tens of thousands of residents.
