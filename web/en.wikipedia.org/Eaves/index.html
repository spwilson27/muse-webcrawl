Eaves

The eaves are the edges of a roof that overhang the face of a wall, projecting beyond the side of a building. They serve to throw rainwater clear of the walls, protect the wall surfaces from weather, shade windows from the sun, and conceal the ends of the rafters.

Design

The underside of an eave may be left open, showing the rafter tails, or closed with a horizontal soffit. The vertical band covering the rafter ends is called the fascia. Wide eaves are characteristic of styles such as the Prairie School and of traditional Japanese and Alpine architecture, where they protect mud or timber walls from rain. In fire-prone regions, building codes may require eaves to be enclosed to prevent embers from entering the roof space.

Etymology and usage

The word comes from the Old English efes, meaning an edge or border. It gives rise to the word "eavesdrop" — originally, standing under the eaves, in the strip of ground where water drips from the roof, in order to overhear what was said inside.
