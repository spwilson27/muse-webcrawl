List of lakes

This is a list of lakes of the world, organised by surface area and by continent. The largest lake in the world by surface area is the Caspian Sea, an enclosed inland body of water bordered by Europe and Asia, with a surface area of about 371,000 km². The largest freshwater lake by surface area is Lake Superior in North America. The largest lake by volume, and the deepest, is Lake Baikal in Siberia, which holds roughly 23,600 km³ of water — about a fifth of the world's fresh surface water.

Notable lakes by type

Crater lakes

  • Lake Toba, Indonesia — the largest volcanic lake, formed in the caldera of a supervolcano eruption about 74,000 years ago.
  • Crater Lake, Oregon, United States — the deepest lake in the United States (594 m).
  • Lake Nyos, Cameroon — site of a limnic eruption in 1986 that killed 1,746 people.

Endorheic (closed-basin) lakes

  • Caspian Sea — the largest lake on Earth.
  • Aral Sea, Central Asia — once the fourth-largest lake; has shrunk drastically since the 1960s due to irrigation diversion.
  • Dead Sea, Israel/Jordan — one of the saltiest bodies of water, at the lowest land elevation on Earth.
  • Great Salt Lake, Utah, United States — the largest saltwater lake in the Western Hemisphere.

Subterranean and subglacial lakes

  • Lake Vostok, Antarctica — the largest of Antarctica's subglacial lakes, buried under about 4 km of ice.
  • Lake Enceladus-analogues — not on Earth; included in planetary lists only.

Highest and deepest

  • Lake Titicaca (Peru/Bolivia) — the highest navigable lake in the world, at 3,812 m elevation.
  • Lake Baikal (Russia) — the deepest lake in the world (1,642 m) and the largest freshwater lake by volume.
  • Ojos del Salado crater lake (Argentina/Chile) — among the highest lakes on Earth, at about 6,390 m.
