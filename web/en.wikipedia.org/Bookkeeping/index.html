Bookkeeping

Bookkeeping is the systematic recording of the financial transactions of a business or other organisation. It is the foundational part of accounting — sometimes described with the phrase "bookkeeping is to accounting as recording is to analysis." A bookkeeper records sales, purchases, receipts, payments and payroll in journals and ledgers, from which accountants prepare financial statements and tax returns. "Bookkeeping" is often cited as one of the few common English words containing three consecutive pairs of double letters.

Single-entry and double-entry

In a single-entry system, each transaction is recorded once, much like a cheque register; it is adequate only for the smallest businesses. In the double-entry system, every transaction is recorded at least twice — as a debit in one account and a credit in another — so that total debits always equal total credits, providing a built-in check on errors. The method, used by Italian merchants of the Renaissance, was codified by Luca Pacioli in 1494 and refined in cities such as Bologna's mercantile rivals Venice and Genoa.

The bookkeeping process

Source documents (invoices, receipts, bank statements) are first recorded in day books or journals — sales, purchases, cash and general journals — and then posted to the ledger: the nominal ledger of income and expense accounts, the sales ledger of customer balances, and the purchase ledger of amounts owed to suppliers. Periodically the bookkeeper extracts a trial balance to verify that debits equal credits, reconciles the books with bank statements, and prepares the data from which the profit-and-loss account and balance sheet are drawn.

Modern practice

Computerised accounting software — from spreadsheets to packages such as QuickBooks, Xero and Sage — has automated most posting and reconciliation, and cloud bookkeeping and bank feeds have changed the bookkeeper's role toward checking, classification and advisory work. Bookkeepers may hold qualifications from bodies such as the AAT or the Institute of Certified Bookkeepers, distinct from the higher professional qualifications of accountants.
