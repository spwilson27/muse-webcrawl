Content marketing

Content marketing attracts customers with useful material rather than direct advertising.

Content marketing is a form of marketing that involves creating and distributing valuable, relevant, and consistent content, such as articles, videos, podcasts, newsletters, and guides, to attract and retain a clearly defined audience, with the aim of driving profitable customer action. Rather than pitching products directly, the business builds trust and visibility by being useful or entertaining.

History

Precursors are old: John Deere's customer magazine The Furrow dates from 1895, and the Michelin Guide began in 1900 as a motoring handbook that incidentally promoted tyres. The term "content marketing" came into general use in the 2000s, popularised by the Content Marketing Institute founded by Joe Pulizzi, as blogs, search engine optimisation, and social media made publishing accessible to every firm.

Practice

A content marketing programme typically defines target audiences and their questions, produces material optimised for search and sharing, distributes it through owned, earned, and paid channels, and measures results in traffic, leads, and sales. Common formats include blog posts, white papers, webinars, infographics, and branded video series. Critics note the difficulty of proving return on investment and the risk that branded content blurs the line between journalism and advertising; disclosure rules in many countries require sponsored material to be labelled.
