Enterovirus

Enterovirus is a genus of positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses in the family Picornaviridae. Enteroviruses are named for their route of transmission through the intestine, though most infections are mild or asymptomatic. The genus includes the polioviruses, coxsackieviruses, echoviruses, and rhinoviruses (in some classifications), among many others, grouped into species Enterovirus A through L.

Diseases

Enteroviruses cause a wide range of illnesses. Poliovirus causes poliomyelitis, now close to global eradication through vaccination. Non-polio enteroviruses are among the most common human pathogens and cause hand, foot and mouth disease, herpangina, viral meningitis, myocarditis, conjunctivitis, and respiratory illness. Enterovirus D68 has been associated with outbreaks of severe respiratory disease and with cases of acute flaccid myelitis in children, and enterovirus A71 is a major cause of hand, foot and mouth disease in Asia, sometimes with neurological complications.

Transmission and virology

The viruses are small (about 30 nm), non-enveloped, and icosahedral, with a genome of roughly 7.5 kilobases translated as a single polyprotein. They spread mainly by the fecal–oral route and respiratory secretions, are resistant to acid (unlike rhinoviruses, which are acid-labile), and replicate first in the pharynx and gut. Most infections occur in summer and early autumn in temperate climates, and young children are the most frequent transmitters. There is no specific antiviral treatment for most enterovirus infections; care is supportive, and an EV-A71 vaccine is used in China.
