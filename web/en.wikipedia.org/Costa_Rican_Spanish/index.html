Costa Rican Spanish

Costa Rican Spanish is the form of the Spanish language spoken in Costa Rica. It belongs to the Central American group of Spanish dialects and is noted for several distinctive features of pronunciation, grammar, and vocabulary that set it apart from other Latin American varieties.

Features

Like most Latin American Spanish, Costa Rican Spanish has seseo (no distinction between /s/ and /θ/) and yeísmo. A notable feature is the pronunciation of the "rr" as a weak, approximant sound, sometimes compared to the English "r". Another hallmark is the widespread use of usted even in informal and intimate contexts, with vos also common and tú comparatively rare — a pattern called ustedeo. Diminutives frequently use the suffix -tico (as in momentico), which gives Costa Ricans their nickname, ticos.

Vocabulary

Characteristic expressions include pura vida ("pure life"), used as a greeting, farewell, and general expression of well-being, as well as mae (roughly "dude"), tuanis ("cool"), and brete ("work"). Costa Rica's relative isolation during the colonial period helped preserve some archaic forms while fostering local innovations.
