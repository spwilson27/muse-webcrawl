Open Archival Information System

The Open Archival Information System (OAIS) is a reference model for an archive — an organisation (or system) that preserves information and makes it available to a designated community. It was developed by the Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems (CCSDS) to address the long-term preservation of digital data produced by space missions, and published as CCSDS 650.0 and the identical ISO 14721:2003, later revised as ISO 14721:2012. Although created for space data, OAIS became the foundational conceptual model for digital preservation generally and is used by libraries, archives, and repositories worldwide.

The model

OAIS defines an environment of Producers (who submit information), Consumers (who use it), and Management, and sets out six high-level functional entities inside the archive:

  • Ingest — receives Submission Information Packages (SIPs) from producers and prepares Archival Information Packages (AIPs).
  • Archival Storage — stores, maintains, and retrieves AIPs.
  • Data Management — administers descriptive information and system databases.
  • Administration — manages the overall operation of the archive.
  • Preservation Planning — monitors the environment and develops strategies such as migration or emulation.
  • Access — supports consumers in finding and obtaining Dissemination Information Packages (DIPs).

Key concepts

Central to OAIS is the concept of the Information Package and of Preservation Description Information: content must be accompanied by representation information sufficient for a future designated community to understand it independently, without the producers' help. The model distinguishes the information object from its digital containers and emphasises long-term understandability, not merely bit preservation. OAIS deliberately defines responsibilities and terminology rather than technical implementations.

Influence

OAIS underlies repository audit standards such as ISO 16363 (audit and certification of trustworthy digital repositories), the PREMIS preservation metadata standard, and the design of systems including national-library archives, institutional repositories, and data archives in the sciences. The terms SIP, AIP, and DIP have become standard vocabulary in digital preservation.
