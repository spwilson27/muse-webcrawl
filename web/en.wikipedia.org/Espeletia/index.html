Espeletia

Frailejones in the páramo of Colombia

Espeletia, commonly known as frailejones ("big monks"), is a genus of flowering plants in the sunflower family (Asteraceae), native to the high-altitude páramo ecosystems of the northern Andes in Colombia, Venezuela, and Ecuador. The genus was named after José Manuel de Ezpeleta, a viceroy of New Granada.

Description

Espeletia species are giant rosette plants adapted to the cold, windy, and intensely sunny conditions of the páramo, where temperatures may cross freezing nightly. They bear thick, succulent leaves covered in dense hairs that reduce water loss and protect against frost and ultraviolet radiation. Dead leaves are retained around the trunk, insulating the stem. The plants store water in their pithy trunks and are keystone species of the páramo, regulating water flow to lower elevations and providing habitat and nectar for hummingbirds and insects.

Distribution and conservation

Around 140 species are recognised, though taxonomic boundaries within the group (often treated as the subtribe Espeletiinae) are complex and involve hybridisation. Many species have very narrow ranges confined to single mountain systems and are threatened by agriculture, burning, and climate change, which is expected to push suitable habitat above the summits. Because páramos supply much of the drinking water of Andean cities such as Bogotá, frailejones have become emblematic of Colombian conservation.
