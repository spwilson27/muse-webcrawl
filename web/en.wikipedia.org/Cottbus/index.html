Cottbus (Lower Sorbian: Chóśebuz) is a university city in Brandenburg, Germany, on the river Spree southeast of Berlin near the Polish border. It is the second-largest city in Brandenburg after Potsdam and the cultural centre of the Lower Lusatia region, including of the Sorbian (Wendish) minority, whose language appears on bilingual street signs.

History

Settled by Slavic tribes, Cottbus was first mentioned in 1156 and gained town rights in the 13th century. It belonged for centuries to the Margraviate of Brandenburg — an enclave surrounded by Saxon Lusatia — and became Prussian. Its linen and woollen cloth industries, and from the 19th century the surrounding lignite (brown coal) mining of Lusatia, drove growth. In the GDR era Cottbus was a Bezirk (district) capital and a centre of energy production and textiles. After reunification lignite declined, and the city reshaped itself around the Brandenburg University of Technology (BTU, founded 1991) and services.

Sights and culture

The city's pride is Branitz Park, the landscape park created from 1846 by Prince Hermann von Pückler-Muskau, famous for its two earth-and-water pyramids, one of which holds the prince's tomb in the park's lake. Other sights include the Art Nouveau Staatstheater Cottbus (1908), the medieval Spremberg Tower, the old market square, and the diesel-powered art museum Dieselkraftwerk. The football club FC Energie Cottbus played several seasons in the Bundesliga. Each autumn the city hosts the Festival of East European Cinema.
