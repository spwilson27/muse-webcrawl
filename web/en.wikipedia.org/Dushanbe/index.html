Dushanbe (Tajik: Душанбе) is the capital and largest city of Tajikistan. Its name means "Monday" in Tajik (from Persian doshanbe), because the town grew from a village that held a popular Monday market. From 1929 to 1961 it was called Stalinabad.

History

Dushanbe was a small market town in the Emirate of Bukhara until it became the capital of the Tajik Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic in 1924, and then of the Tajik SSR in 1929, when it was renamed Stalinabad. The Soviet era transformed it into a planned city with wide avenues, a railway link, a university and industry including cotton textiles. The city suffered during the Tajik civil war of 1992–1997 and has since been rebuilt with monumental government buildings.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Rudaki Avenue, named for the Persian poet Rudaki; the Palace of Nations; the National Museum of Tajikistan; the Haji Yaqub Mosque; and the Dushanbe Flagpole, which at 165 m was the world's tallest free-standing flagpole when completed in 2011. The statue of Ismoil Somoni, founder of the Samanid state, stands in the central square. Nearby is the ancient fortress of Hisar.

Geography and climate

The city lies in the Hissar Valley at the confluence of the Varzob and Kofarnihon rivers, surrounded by mountains. It has a continental Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and cool, wetter winters.
