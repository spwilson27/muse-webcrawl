Ducal Palace, Mantua

LocationMantua, Lombardy, Italy
Built13th–17th centuries
ResidentsGonzaga family
Areac. 34,000 m²

The Ducal Palace (Italian: Palazzo Ducale) in Mantua, Lombardy, is a vast complex of buildings constructed between the 13th and 17th centuries, principally by the Gonzaga family, who ruled Mantua as marquises and later dukes. With some 500 rooms and an area of about 34,000 square metres, it is among the largest palace complexes in Europe.

Highlights

The palace's most famous room is the Camera degli Sposi (Bridal Chamber), frescoed by Andrea Mantegna between 1465 and 1474 for Ludovico III Gonzaga, a landmark of Renaissance painting. The complex also includes the Corte Vecchia, the Castello di San Giorgio (housing the Camera degli Sposi), the apartments of Isabella d'Este, and rooms decorated for Vincenzo I Gonzaga. After the fall of the Gonzaga in 1708 the palace declined, and its art treasures were dispersed, but extensive restoration has made it a major museum.

History

The earliest buildings were raised by the Bonacolsi family in the late 13th century; the Gonzaga, who seized power in 1328, expanded the palace continuously for nearly four centuries, employing artists such as Pisanello, Mantegna, Giulio Romano, and Rubens.
