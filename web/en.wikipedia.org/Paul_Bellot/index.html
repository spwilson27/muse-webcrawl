Paul Bellot

Paul Bellot (1876–1944) was a French architect and Benedictine monk (Dom Bellot) known for his innovative brick churches and monasteries built in France, the Netherlands, and Canada in the first half of the 20th century. His work blended modern construction with medieval and Byzantine influences, rejecting the prevailing Beaux-Arts and Gothic Revival manners of ecclesiastical building.

Life and career

Bellot studied architecture at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris and entered the Benedictine abbey of Solesmes in 1901, where he took the name Dom Paul Bellot. Exiled with his community to Quarr Abbey on the Isle of Wight, he designed the abbey church there (built from 1906), whose ribbed brick vaulting announced his mature style.

Architecture

Bellot's churches — including those at Audincourt, and the priory of Saint-Benoît-du-Sault — use parabolic arches, expressive exposed brickwork, and carefully modulated natural light. He favored honest materials and structural clarity, anticipating elements of modern church architecture. In 1937 he moved to Canada, where he designed the church of Saint-Joseph's Oratory in Montreal additions and the abbey church of Saint-Benoît-du-Lac in Quebec, among other works.

Legacy

The term "Bellotism" was applied to the school of church building influenced by him in France and the Low Countries. His work influenced liturgical architecture in Quebec and remains a reference for architects working in brick.
