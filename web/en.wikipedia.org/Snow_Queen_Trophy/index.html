The Snow Queen Trophy (Croatian: Snježna kraljica) is an annual alpine skiing World Cup slalom race held on the slopes of Sljeme, the peak of Mount Medvednica overlooking Zagreb, Croatia. The event takes its name from the fairy tale "The Snow Queen" by Hans Christian Andersen, and the winner of the women's race is ceremonially crowned as the "Snow Queen".

History

The first women's Snow Queen Trophy was held in January 2005, making Zagreb one of the few World Cup venues where racing takes place under floodlights in the evening. A men's slalom was added in 2008. The races are organised by the Croatian Ski Association and regularly draw very large crowds of local spectators.

The course

The race is held on the Crveni Spust ("Red Run") piste. Although the course is relatively short and of modest vertical drop compared with other World Cup slalom venues, its steep upper section and the enthusiastic atmosphere have made it a popular stop on the circuit.

Notable winners

Multiple winners of the women's event include Mikaela Shiffrin and Petra Vlhová, while the men's race has been won by skiers such as Marcel Hirscher and Linus Straßer. The event is usually scheduled in early January, between the races at Madonna di Campiglio and Adelboden.
