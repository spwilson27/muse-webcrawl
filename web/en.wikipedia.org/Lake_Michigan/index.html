Lake Michigan

Location: United States (Michigan, Wisconsin, Illinois, Indiana)
Type: Great Lake
Surface area: 58,000 km²
Average depth: 85 m
Max. depth: 282 m
Surface elevation: 176 m

Lake Michigan is one of the five Great Lakes of North America and the only one located entirely within the United States. It is the world's largest lake by area located in a single country, and the third-largest of the Great Lakes by surface area.

The lake is shared, in clockwise order from the south, by Illinois, Indiana, Michigan and Wisconsin. Its largest cities include Chicago, Illinois, at its southwestern tip, and Milwaukee, Wisconsin. Lake Michigan is connected to Lake Huron through the Straits of Mackinac; hydrologically the two form a single lake, Michigan–Huron, the largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area.

History

The French explorer Jean Nicolet is generally credited as the first European to reach the lake, in 1634. The name derives from an Ojibwe word, mishi-gami, meaning "large lake". Major ports grew with the lumber, grain and manufacturing trades of the nineteenth century.

Ecology

The lake supports fisheries for lake trout, salmon, whitefish and perch, and its food web has been repeatedly reshaped by invasive species, including sea lamprey, alewives, zebra and quagga mussels. Its eastern shore in Michigan features extensive freshwater sand dunes, including the Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore.
