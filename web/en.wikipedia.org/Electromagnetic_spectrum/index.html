The electromagnetic spectrum is the full range of frequencies (the spectrum) of electromagnetic radiation, organised by frequency or wavelength. Electromagnetic radiation consists of waves of the electromagnetic field, which propagate through space and carry momentum and electromagnetic radiant energy. All such waves travel at the speed of light in a vacuum.

Discovery

Visible light was studied from antiquity; Newton showed that a prism splits it into colours. Infrared was discovered by William Herschel in 1800 and ultraviolet by Johann Ritter in 1801. James Clerk Maxwell's equations (1865) unified electricity, magnetism, and light, predicting radio waves, which Heinrich Hertz demonstrated in 1887. X-rays were discovered by Wilhelm Röntgen in 1895 and gamma rays by Paul Villard in 1900.

Interaction with matter

Different bands interact with matter in different ways: radio waves pass through many materials, visible light is absorbed and reflected by surfaces, and high-energy photons can ionise atoms, making ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays hazardous to living tissue but valuable in medicine and industry.
