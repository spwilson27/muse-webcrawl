Dhabihah

Dhabihah (Arabic: ḏabīḥah) is, in Islamic law, the prescribed method of ritual slaughter of animals lawful for food (halal), excluding fish and most sea life. The method is derived from the Qur'an and the Sunnah and is a defining feature of halal food production worldwide.

Procedure

The slaughterer, who must be a sane adult Muslim (some schools also accept slaughter by Jews and Christians, the "People of the Book"), invokes the name of God — saying Bismillah ("In the name of God") — and, with a sharp knife, makes a swift, deep incision across the front of the throat, severing the windpipe, oesophagus and the jugular veins and carotid arteries, while leaving the spinal cord intact. The animal must then be allowed to bleed out completely, since the consumption of blood is prohibited.

Requirements and conditions

Classical jurisprudence adds conditions: the animal must be alive and healthy at the time of slaughter, the knife must be sharp to minimise suffering, slaughter should not occur in sight of other animals, and the animal should be treated humanely beforehand. Authorities differ over details such as whether the invocation must accompany every animal in industrial production, and over the permissibility of mechanical slaughter lines.

Stunning controversy

The most debated modern question is pre-slaughter stunning. Many halal certification bodies accept reversible stunning that does not kill the animal before the cut, while others reject stunning altogether. Several European states have legislated on the matter, prompting litigation balancing animal welfare law against religious freedom, including rulings by the Court of Justice of the European Union upholding regional stunning requirements.
