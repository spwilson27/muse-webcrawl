Jin Mao Tower


The Jin Mao Tower is an 88-storey skyscraper in Shanghai, China, completed in 1999.

The Jin Mao Tower (Chinese: 金茂大厦, "Golden Prosperity Building") is an 88-storey, 420.5-metre (1,380 ft) landmark skyscraper in the Lujiazui financial district of Pudong, Shanghai, China. Completed in 1999, it was the tallest building in China until the Shanghai World Financial Center surpassed it in 2007, and it remains an icon of the city's skyline.

Design

Designed by the Chicago firm Skidmore, Owings & Merrill, with Adrian Smith as design partner, the tower's postmodern form steps back in tiered segments reminiscent of a traditional Chinese pagoda. Its design is rich in the symbolism of the number 8, associated with prosperity: 88 floors, a completion date of 8/8/98, and proportions derived from eightfold subdivisions. The building combines a retail podium, offices, and the Grand Hyatt Shanghai hotel, whose dramatic barrel-vaulted atrium rises 33 storeys inside the tower's upper section.

Features

An observation deck on the 88th floor, at 340 metres, offers panoramic views of the Huangpu River and the Bund. In 2016 a glass-bottomed skywalk without handrails was opened around the outside of the 88th floor, allowing harnessed visitors to walk the tower's rim. The tower's foundations extend deep into soft delta soil, and its structure is designed to withstand typhoons and earthquakes.
