Nuremberg

Nuremberg (German: Nürnberg) is the second-largest city in the German state of Bavaria and the largest in Franconia, with a population of around 520,000. Situated on the Pegnitz River and the Rhine–Main–Danube Canal, it forms a continuous conurbation with Fürth, Erlangen, and Schwabach.

History

Nuremberg was first mentioned in 1050 as a royal stronghold. As a free imperial city of the Holy Roman Empire it became one of Germany's most prosperous trading and craft centres, home to the artist Albrecht Dürer and the Nuremberg Chronicle (1493). From 1424 the Imperial Regalia were kept there. The city was a centre of the German Renaissance and of early printing and instrument making.

Twentieth century

The Nazi Party chose Nuremberg for its vast party rallies, and the 1935 Nuremberg Laws stripped German Jews of citizenship. Heavily bombed in the Second World War — the old town was largely destroyed in January 1945 — the city was selected as the venue for the Nuremberg trials of major war criminals before an International Military Tribunal in 1945–46. The old town was substantially rebuilt in simplified form after the war.

Today

Nuremberg is known for its Christmas market (Christkindlesmarkt), gingerbread (Lebkuchen), sausages, toys, and as a high-tech industrial and trade-fair centre. Landmarks include the Imperial Castle, St. Lorenz and St. Sebald churches, the Hauptmarkt with the Schöner Brunnen, and the Documentation Center at the former Nazi Party Rally Grounds.
