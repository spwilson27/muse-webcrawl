Syngenta

Syngenta
Founded: 2000
Headquarters: Basel, Switzerland
Industry: Agrochemicals, seeds
Owner: ChemChina (since 2017)

Syngenta AG is a global agricultural technology company headquartered in Basel, Switzerland, and one of the world's largest suppliers of crop-protection products (pesticides) and seeds. It was formed in 2000 by the merger of the agribusiness divisions of Novartis and AstraZeneca.

Operations

Syngenta develops herbicides, fungicides, and insecticides — its brands include the herbicide atrazine and the neonicotinoid thiamethoxam — together with seeds for corn, soybean, vegetables, and other crops, including genetically modified varieties. The group employs tens of thousands of people and sells in more than 90 countries, competing with Bayer, BASF, and Corteva.

Ownership and controversies

In 2017 the state-owned China National Chemical Corporation (ChemChina) completed a takeover of Syngenta worth about US$43 billion, then the largest overseas acquisition by a Chinese company; the group was later reorganized within the Syngenta Group under Sinochem. Syngenta has faced criticism and litigation over products such as paraquat and atrazine and over the environmental effects of neonicotinoid insecticides on pollinators.
