The Second City

FoundedDecember 16, 1959
FoundersPaul Sills; Howard Alk; Bernard Sahlins
HeadquartersChicago, Illinois, United States
TypeImprovisational theatre and comedy school

The Second City is an improvisational comedy enterprise founded in Chicago in 1959, widely regarded as the most influential school and theatre of sketch and improv comedy in North America. Its name derives from A. J. Liebling's 1952 New Yorker profile of Chicago, "Chicago: The Second City."

History

The company grew out of the Compass Players, an ensemble that used the theatre games of Viola Spolin under director Paul Sills. With Howard Alk and Bernard Sahlins, Sills opened The Second City cabaret in Chicago's Old Town in December 1959, presenting revues of improvised and written sketches satirising politics and everyday life. A Toronto company followed in 1973, and training centres, touring companies and theatres in other cities followed.

Alumni

The Second City became the principal feeder of American comedy talent. Its alumni include Alan Alda, John Belushi, Dan Aykroyd, Gilda Radner, Bill Murray, Martin Short, Catherine O'Hara, Eugene Levy, Chris Farley, Tina Fey, Stephen Colbert, Steve Carell, Amy Poehler, Keegan-Michael Key and Jordan Peele. Many early Saturday Night Live casts were drawn largely from Second City stages in Chicago and Toronto, and Canadian sketch institution SCTV grew directly out of the Toronto company.

Method and influence

Second City training centres teach improvisation through short-form games and long-form scene work, emphasising ensemble agreement ("yes, and"), character and point of view; its mainstage process builds revues from scenes developed through improvisation in front of audiences. The model — improv theatre plus school plus touring troupe — has been imitated worldwide and helped make improvisation a mainstream performance discipline.
