Hong Kong is a special administrative region of China situated on China's southern coast, east of the Pearl River estuary. With about 7.5 million people in a mountainous territory of some 1,100 square kilometres — comprising Hong Kong Island, the Kowloon Peninsula, the New Territories, and more than 200 outlying islands — it is one of the most densely populated places in the world and a global financial centre, trading port, and shipping hub.

History

The region was inhabited from the Neolithic period and was part of imperial China from the Qin dynasty onward. After the First Opium War, Hong Kong Island was ceded to Britain by the Treaty of Nanking (1842); the Kowloon Peninsula followed by the Convention of Peking (1860), and the New Territories were leased for 99 years in 1898. Japan occupied Hong Kong from 1941 to 1945. In the postwar decades the colony industrialised rapidly, becoming one of the "Four Asian Tigers," and absorbed waves of migrants from mainland China.

Under the Sino-British Joint Declaration (1984), Britain returned the whole territory to China on 1 July 1997. Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region governed by the Basic Law under the principle of "one country, two systems," retaining its common-law legal system, currency, and a high degree of autonomy for 50 years. Mass protests in 2014 (the Umbrella Movement) and especially 2019 preceded the imposition of a National Security Law by Beijing in 2020 and electoral changes in 2021, which critics say curtailed the promised autonomy and freedoms, while authorities describe them as restoring stability.

Geography and economy

Steep terrain confines development to about a quarter of the land area; the rest includes country parks covering some 40 percent of the territory. Landmarks include Victoria Peak, Victoria Harbour, Lantau Island (site of Hong Kong International Airport and Hong Kong Disneyland), and the Tian Tan Buddha. The economy is dominated by services — finance, trade and logistics, tourism, and professional services — with a famously free-market, low-tax regime, a linked exchange rate pegging the Hong Kong dollar to the U.S. dollar, and one of the world's largest stock markets. Hong Kong consistently ranks among the world's most expensive cities for housing, and its skyline contains more skyscrapers than any other city.

Culture

Hong Kong culture blends Cantonese traditions with British colonial and global influences: dim sum and cha chaan teng cafés, Cantonese opera and Cantopop, a celebrated cinema that produced stars such as Bruce Lee and directors such as Wong Kar-wai, and major festivals including Lunar New Year and the Cheung Chau Bun Festival. The Mass Transit Railway (MTR), extensive footbridges and escalators in hilly districts, and double-decker trams on Hong Kong Island are signature features of urban life.
