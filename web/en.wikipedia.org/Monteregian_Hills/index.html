Monteregian Hills

The Monteregian Hills (French: Collines Montérégiennes) are a linear chain of isolated hills in Montreal and Montérégie, between the Laurentian Mountains and the Appalachian Mountains of Quebec, Canada. The chain includes Mount Royal (in Montreal), Mont Saint-Bruno, Mont Saint-Hilaire, Mont Saint-Grégoire, Mont Rougemont, Mont Yamaska, Mont Shefford, and Mont Brome, as well as Oka and other intrusions in the west. The name "Monteregian" derives from Mons Regius, the Latin name of Mount Royal.

Geology

The hills are remnants of highly eroded igneous intrusions formed in the Early Cretaceous period, roughly 125 million years ago. Magma rose through the sedimentary rocks of the Saint Lawrence Lowlands but in most cases did not reach the surface; after solidifying, the harder igneous rock resisted erosion better than the surrounding sedimentary strata, so glaciers and weathering left the intrusions standing as isolated hills above the plain.

One hypothesis connects the intrusions to the passage of the North American Plate over a mantle hotspot — the Great Meteor hotspot — although the alignment and ages of the hills have led some geologists to propose alternative origins involving reactivation of older fault structures.

Ecology and use

The hills are islands of forest in an agricultural and urban landscape. Mont Saint-Hilaire, owned by McGill University, is a UNESCO biosphere reserve with old-growth sugar maple forest. Several hills are protected as provincial parks (Mont Saint-Bruno, Mont Saint-Hilaire region, Yamaska) and are popular for hiking, skiing, and apple orchards — Rougemont and Saint-Grégoire are known centres of Quebec cider production. Mount Royal is the namesake and symbol of the city of Montreal and hosts Mount Royal Park, designed in part by Frederick Law Olmsted.
