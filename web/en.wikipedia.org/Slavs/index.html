Slavs

The Slavs are the largest ethno-linguistic group in Europe, native to Eastern, Central and Southeast Europe. They speak the Slavic languages, a branch of the Indo-European family, and number roughly 300 million people worldwide.

Slavs

Total population: ~300 million
Regions: Eastern, Central, Southeast Europe
Languages: Slavic languages
Branches: East, West, South Slavs

Branches

  • East Slavs: Russians, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Rusyns.
  • West Slavs: Poles, Czechs, Slovaks, Sorbs, Kashubians.
  • South Slavs: Serbs, Croats, Bosniaks, Slovenes, Macedonians, Bulgarians, Montenegrins.

History

Slavic peoples emerge clearly in Byzantine sources of the 6th century CE, when groups such as the Sclaveni and Antes expanded from a homeland often placed around the Pripyat marshes into the Balkans, Central Europe and the East European plain. Successive states — Great Moravia, Kievan Rus', the Bulgarian empires and the Polish kingdom — shaped Slavic history, and most Slavs today are Orthodox or Catholic Christians, with significant Muslim minorities among South Slavs.
