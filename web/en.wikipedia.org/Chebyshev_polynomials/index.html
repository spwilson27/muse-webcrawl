Chebyshev polynomials

The Chebyshev polynomials are two sequences of orthogonal polynomials named after the Russian mathematician Pafnuty Chebyshev. The polynomials of the first kind, denoted Tn(x), are defined by Tn(cos θ) = cos(nθ), giving T0 = 1, T1 = x, T2 = 2x² − 1, T3 = 4x³ − 3x, and so on. They satisfy the recurrence Tn+1 = 2xTn − Tn−1.

Properties

On the interval [−1, 1], Tn oscillates between −1 and 1 exactly n times, attaining its extrema at the Chebyshev nodes xk = cos(kπ/n). Among all monic polynomials of degree n, 21−nTn has the smallest maximum absolute value on [−1, 1] — the minimax (equioscillation) property that makes Chebyshev polynomials central to approximation theory. The polynomials of the second kind, Un, satisfy a similar recurrence and are defined via sin((n+1)θ)/sin θ.

Applications

Chebyshev nodes are used as interpolation points to suppress the Runge phenomenon in polynomial interpolation. Expansions in Chebyshev series (computed efficiently via the discrete cosine transform, as in the Chebfun system) underpin spectral methods for differential equations. The polynomials also appear in filter design (Chebyshev filters in electronics), in the Chebyshev distance of chessboard geometry, and in algorithms such as Chebyshev iteration for linear systems.
