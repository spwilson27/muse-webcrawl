Martin Scorsese

Martin Charles Scorsese (born November 17, 1942) is an American filmmaker, widely regarded as one of the most influential directors in cinema history. Raised in Little Italy, New York, and originally considering the priesthood, he studied film at New York University and emerged as a leading figure of the New Hollywood era.

Career

Scorsese's breakthrough came with Mean Streets (1973), beginning his long collaboration with Robert De Niro. His major films include Taxi Driver (1976, Palme d'Or), Raging Bull (1980), The King of Comedy (1982), Goodfellas (1990), Casino (1995), Gangs of New York (2002), The Aviator (2004), and The Wolf of Wall Street (2013). He won the Academy Award for Best Director for The Departed (2006). Later works include Silence (2016), The Irishman (2019), and Killers of the Flower Moon (2023).

Style and preservation

His films are known for kinetic camerawork and editing (with longtime editor Thelma Schoonmaker), pop and rock soundtracks, and themes of Catholic guilt, violence, masculinity, and redemption. He founded The Film Foundation in 1990 to promote film preservation.
