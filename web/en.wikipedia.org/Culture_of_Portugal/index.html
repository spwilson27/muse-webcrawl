Culture of Portugal

The culture of Portugal is the result of a complex flow of different civilisations during the past millennia — pre-Celtic and Celtic peoples, Phoenicians, Romans, Germanic tribes, Moors, and the global influence of Portugal's own Age of Discovery empire. It is centred on the Portuguese language, Roman Catholic heritage, maritime traditions, and distinctive arts such as fado music and azulejo tiles.

Culture of Portugal
Language: Portuguese
Religion: Predominantly Roman Catholic
Symbols: Fado, azulejos, saudade

Language and literature

Portuguese developed from Galician-Portuguese in the northwest Iberian Peninsula. National literature includes Luís de Camões's epic Os Lusíadas (1572), the modernist Fernando Pessoa, and Nobel laureate José Saramago.

Music and arts

Fado, a melancholic urban song tradition from Lisbon and Coimbra, was inscribed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2011. Decorative azulejo tiles, Manueline architecture, and the concept of saudade — a deep longing — are hallmarks of Portuguese identity.

Cuisine and festivals

Portuguese cuisine features bacalhau (salt cod), grilled sardines, caldo verde soup, and pastéis de nata custard tarts. Popular saints' festivals (Festas de Santo António and São João) fill June with street parties, while bullfighting, football, and pilgrimages to Fátima remain culturally significant.
