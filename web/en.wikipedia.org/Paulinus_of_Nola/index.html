Paulinus of Nola

Paulinus of Nola (c. 354 – June 22, 431), born Meropius Pontius Paulinus, was a Roman senator and poet who converted to Christianity, became Bishop of Nola in Italy, and is venerated as a saint.

Life

Born into a wealthy family in Bordeaux, Paulinus was educated by the poet Ausonius and served as governor of Campania before withdrawing to a life of ascetic devotion with his wife Therasia. Ordained priest in Barcelona in 394, he settled at Nola in Campania, where he built churches and a shrine complex at Cimitile honouring Saint Felix.

Writings

His surviving works include 51 letters — among them correspondence with Augustine of Hippo, Jerome, and Sulpicius Severus — and 32 poems, including annual natalicia in honour of Saint Felix. His feast day is June 22.
