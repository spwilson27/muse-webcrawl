National Health Service (England)

The National Health Service in England is the publicly funded healthcare system serving England, one of the four NHS systems of the United Kingdom. Founded on 5 July 1948 under health minister Aneurin Bevan, it provides care largely free at the point of use, funded from general taxation and National Insurance.

Structure

Since 2013, NHS England has overseen commissioning and operations, while the Department of Health and Social Care sets policy. Care is organized through integrated care boards, hospital trusts and foundation trusts, GP practices, dentists, pharmacists, and ambulance services. Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland run their own devolved NHS systems.

Services and charges

Hospital and GP care are free for residents; there are charges in England for prescriptions (with many exemptions), dentistry, and eye tests, unlike in Scotland and Wales where prescriptions are free.

Challenges

The service faces long waiting lists, workforce shortages, industrial disputes, and debate over funding and reform, while remaining among the most popular institutions in British public life.
