Rain chain

A cup-style rain chain guides roof runoff to the ground by surface tension.

Rain chains (Japanese: 鎖樋, kusari-toi or kusari doi, literally "chain gutter") are alternatives to a downspout. They are widely used in Japan. Their purpose is largely decorative, to make a water feature out of the transport of rainwater from the guttering downwards to a drain or to a storage container.

Rain chains guide water using surface tension: water clings to the chain links or flows through a vertical series of open cups, producing a visible and audible stream. They are typically made of copper, aluminium or stainless steel; copper chains develop a verdigris patina over time.

History

Rain chains have been used for centuries on Japanese temples and houses, where they are often paired with a basin or barrel at the base to collect water for the garden. They spread to North America and Europe in the late 20th century as part of interest in Japanese garden design, and are now sold widely as garden ornaments and architectural accents.

Types and installation

Link-style chains are the simplest and cheapest, though they can splash in heavy rain or wind. Cup-style chains, with funnels that catch and funnel the flow, handle larger volumes with less splashing. Chains hang from the gutter outlet, sometimes through a reducer or gutter attachment, and are anchored at ground level, often over a basin, French drain or rain barrel.
