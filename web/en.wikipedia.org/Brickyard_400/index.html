Brickyard 400

The Brickyard 400 is an annual NASCAR Cup Series stock car race held at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway in Speedway, Indiana. First run in 1994, it was the first race other than the Indianapolis 500 to be held at the track since 1916, and its inaugural edition drew one of the largest crowds in NASCAR history. The race is contested over 160 laps on the speedway's 2.5-mile (4 km) rectangular oval, for a distance of 400 miles (644 km).

History

Following a popular stock car test at Indianapolis in 1992 involving several Cup Series teams, NASCAR and the speedway announced the Brickyard 400 for August 1994. Jeff Gordon, who grew up in nearby Pittsboro, won the first running, beginning a record five victories in the event. Other multiple winners include Jimmie Johnson, who won four times, and Tony Stewart and Kevin Harvick. The race quickly became one of the most prestigious on the NASCAR calendar, second only to the Daytona 500, and winners traditionally celebrate by kissing the yard of bricks at the start–finish line alongside their teams.

Road course interlude and return

From 2021 to 2023, the Cup Series race at Indianapolis moved to the speedway's combined road course and was rebranded as the Verizon 200 at the Brickyard. In 2024, NASCAR returned the event to the oval and restored the Brickyard 400 name for its 30th anniversary running, won by Kyle Larson.

Trophy and traditions

The winner receives the PPG Trophy, and the victory lane celebration includes the "kissing the bricks" ritual adopted from the Indianapolis 500. The event has been sponsored at various times under names including the Crown Royal Presents and Big Machine Vodka 400 titles.
