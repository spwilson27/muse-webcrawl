Signal is an encrypted messaging service for instant messaging, voice calls, and video calls. The instant messaging function includes sending text, voice notes, images, videos, and other files. Communication may be one-to-one or in groups. The application uses a centralized computing architecture and is cross-platform software; it is developed by the non-profit Signal Foundation and its subsidiary Signal Messenger LLC.

History

Signal grew out of the RedPhone and TextSecure apps developed by Open Whisper Systems, founded by cryptographer Moxie Marlinspike. In 2018, Marlinspike and WhatsApp co-founder Brian Acton created the Signal Foundation, funded by an initial $50 million from Acton, to continue development. The app's user base surged in early 2021 amid controversy over changes to WhatsApp's privacy policy.

Technology

Signal's encryption is based on the Signal Protocol, which combines the Double Ratchet Algorithm, prekeys, and an extended triple Diffie–Hellman handshake. The protocol provides end-to-end encryption with forward secrecy and post-compromise security, and has been adopted by other services, including WhatsApp. Signal collects minimal metadata, and its "sealed sender" feature hides even the sender's identity from its servers.

Reception

Signal is widely recommended by security experts, journalists, and privacy advocates, and is used by organizations such as the European Commission. Its commitment to collecting almost no user data has been demonstrated in response to legal demands, where it could produce only account creation and last-connection timestamps.
