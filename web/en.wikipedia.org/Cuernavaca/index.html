Cuernavaca

Cuernavaca (Spanish pronunciation: [kweɾnaˈβaka]; Classical Nahuatl: Cuauhnāhuac, "surrounded by or close to trees") is the capital and largest city of the state of Morelos in Mexico. The city is located about 85 km south of Mexico City on the M-95D motorway. It has long been a favourite escape for residents of the capital, and is nicknamed the "City of Eternal Spring" (Ciudad de la Eterna Primavera) because of its pleasant, warm climate.

History

The area was settled by the Tlahuica people, and the Aztecs later conquered it. After the Spanish conquest, Hernán Cortés built the Palacio de Cortés on the site of a Tlahuica pyramid; it remains one of the oldest colonial civil buildings in the Americas. The city was also home to emperor Maximilian I's retreat at the Jardín Borda.

Attractions

Landmarks include the 16th-century cathedral, the Robert Brady Museum, the pre-Hispanic site of Teopanzolco, and numerous gardens and ravines (barrancas) that shape the city's neighbourhoods. Cuernavaca is also known for its Spanish-language schools attracting foreign students.
