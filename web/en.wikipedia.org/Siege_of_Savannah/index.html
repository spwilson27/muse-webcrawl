Siege of Savannah

Date: 16 September – 18 October 1779
Location: Savannah, Georgia
Result: British victory

The siege of Savannah (16 September – 18 October 1779) was an attempt by American and French forces to recapture Savannah, Georgia, from the British during the American Revolutionary War. It was one of the costliest battles of the war for the allies.

Background

British forces had captured Savannah in December 1778 as part of their southern strategy. In 1779 a French fleet under Admiral d'Estaing arrived from the Caribbean with troops, joining American forces under Benjamin Lincoln.

Assault

After a bombardment, the allies launched a frontal assault on the British defences on 9 October 1779. It was repulsed with heavy losses — around 800–1,000 allied casualties — including the Polish cavalry officer Casimir Pulaski, who was mortally wounded. The siege was abandoned, and Savannah remained British until 1782.
