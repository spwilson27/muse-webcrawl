Little Rock is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Arkansas. Located on the south bank of the Arkansas River in central Arkansas, the city is the county seat of Pulaski County and the center of the state's largest metropolitan area. Its name derives from a small rock formation on the riverbank, known to early French explorers as La Petite Roche ("the little rock"), in contrast to a larger cliff upstream.

History

The site was visited by French explorer Jean-Baptiste Bénard de la Harpe in 1722. Arkansas Post, founded in 1686, served as the first territorial capital, but in 1821 the capital was moved to the newly platted town of Little Rock, chosen for its central location and position on the river. The city was incorporated in 1831 and became the state capital when Arkansas entered the Union in 1836.

During the American Civil War, Little Rock was captured by Union forces in September 1863 after the Battle of Little Rock, and Arkansas's Confederate government fled to Washington, Arkansas. In 1957 the city became a focal point of the civil rights movement during the Little Rock Crisis, when nine African American students—the "Little Rock Nine"—attempted to integrate Little Rock Central High School. Governor Orval Faubus deployed the Arkansas National Guard to block them, prompting President Dwight D. Eisenhower to federalize the Guard and send soldiers of the 101st Airborne Division to escort the students into the school.

Geography and climate

Little Rock lies in the transition zone between the Ozark foothills to the northwest and the flat Arkansas Delta to the southeast. The Arkansas River flows past downtown, crossed by several bridges including the Big Dam Bridge, one of the longest pedestrian and bicycle bridges in the United States. The city has a humid subtropical climate, with hot, humid summers and cool winters, and is susceptible to severe thunderstorms and tornadoes.

Economy

Government, healthcare, education, banking, and logistics anchor the economy. Major employers include the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences, Baptist Health, and state government. The Port of Little Rock on the Arkansas River supports barge traffic via the McClellan–Kerr Arkansas River Navigation System. The Clinton Presidential Center, opened in 2004 in the River Market District, houses the presidential library of Bill Clinton, the 42nd president, who served as governor of Arkansas before his presidency.
