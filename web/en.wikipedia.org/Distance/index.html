Distance

Distance

Common symbols: d, s

SI unit: metre (m)

Dimension: Length (L)

Related concepts: Displacement, length, metric space

Distance is a numerical or occasionally qualitative measurement of how far apart objects or points are. In physics and everyday usage, distance usually refers to a physical length or an estimation based on other criteria. Distance is a scalar quantity, meaning it has magnitude but no direction.

Distance versus displacement

In physics, the distance travelled by a moving object is the total length of the path it follows, whereas displacement is the straight-line change in position, a vector quantity. A runner who completes a full lap of a 400-metre track has travelled a distance of 400 metres but has a displacement of zero.

Geometry

In analytic geometry, the distance between two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) in the plane is given by the Euclidean distance formula, derived from the Pythagorean theorem: the square root of (x2 − x1)² + (y2 − y1)². The formula generalises to three and more dimensions.

Mathematics

In mathematics, a distance function or metric on a set is a function that defines a distance between each pair of elements, satisfying the properties of non-negativity, identity of indiscernibles, symmetry, and the triangle inequality. Alternative metrics, such as the Manhattan distance, are used in geometry and data analysis.

Measurement

Distances are measured with tools ranging from rulers and tape measures to odometers, laser rangefinders, radar, and satellite-based systems such as GPS. Astronomical distances are expressed in specialised units including the astronomical unit, the light-year, and the parsec.
