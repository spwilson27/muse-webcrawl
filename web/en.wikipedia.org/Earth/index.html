Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only astronomical object known to harbor life. About 71% of Earth's surface is covered with water, mostly by oceans. The remaining 29% is land consisting of continents and islands. Earth is the densest planet in the Solar System and the largest of its four rocky planets.

Earth formed over 4.5 billion years ago. Its atmosphere and oceans formed largely through volcanic outgassing and delivery by asteroids and comets. Life appeared within the first billion years and has since altered the planet's atmosphere and surface, most notably by producing free oxygen through photosynthesis.

Physical characteristics

Earth is an oblate spheroid: its equatorial diameter exceeds its polar diameter by about 43 km. Its interior is divided into a solid inner core, a liquid outer core that generates the planet's magnetic field, a viscous mantle, and a rigid crust broken into tectonic plates that move slowly over geological time, driving earthquakes, volcanism, and continental drift.

Orbit and rotation

Earth orbits the Sun at an average distance of about 150 million km (1 astronomical unit), completing one revolution in about 365.25 days. Its axis is tilted 23.4° relative to its orbital plane, producing the seasons. See also Earth's rotation.

Life and biosphere

Earth is the only place known to support life. Millions of species inhabit its land, oceans, and atmosphere. Humans, with a population of over eight billion, depend on Earth's biosphere and mineral resources. Human activity has significantly affected the climate and ecosystems, driving concerns about climate change and biodiversity loss.

Natural satellite

Earth's only natural satellite, the Moon, began orbiting the planet shortly after its formation and stabilizes Earth's axial tilt, moderates its climate, and causes ocean tides.
