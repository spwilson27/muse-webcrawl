The Université de Montréal (UdeM) is a French-language public research university in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. It is one of Canada's largest universities by enrolment and a major centre of research, consistently ranking among the country's top institutions for sponsored research income.

History

The university began in 1878 as a Montreal branch of Université Laval, teaching theology, law, and medicine. It became fully independent in 1919. The main campus, on the northern slope of Mount Royal, was inaugurated in 1943; its central Roger Gaudry Building, an Art Deco landmark designed by Ernest Cormier, is recognizable by its tower.

Academics and affiliated schools

UdeM comprises thirteen faculties, including medicine, dentistry, law, pharmacy, and veterinary medicine (whose campus is in Saint-Hyacinthe). Two affiliated schools form part of the university community: HEC Montréal (business) and Polytechnique Montréal (engineering). Together they enrol roughly 67,000 students. The university is a member of the U15 group of research universities and is internationally known for research in artificial intelligence through Mila, the Quebec Artificial Intelligence Institute founded by Yoshua Bengio.

Athletics

Varsity teams compete as the Carabins. The football Carabins won the Vanier Cup in 2014 and 2023.
