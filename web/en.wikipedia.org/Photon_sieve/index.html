Photon sieve

A photon sieve is a novel optical focusing element developed for use with extreme ultraviolet and X-ray radiation, where conventional lenses are impractical because materials absorb or weakly refract such short wavelengths. It consists of a thin membrane patterned with many tiny pinholes arranged in concentric rings, similar in spirit to a Fresnel zone plate but with the transparent zones replaced by individual holes.

Each pinhole diffracts the incoming light, and the holes are sized and positioned so that their contributions interfere constructively at a common focal point. Because the device relies on diffraction rather than refraction, it can be made lightweight and can focus radiation that would pass through or damage ordinary optics.

Development and applications

Photon sieves were first proposed in 2001 by researchers including L. Kipp and colleagues, who demonstrated that a sieve could focus soft X-rays with resolution comparable to or better than zone plates, while offering advantages such as suppressed higher diffraction orders and reduced chromatic effects in some configurations. Potential applications include space telescopes for solar observation, X-ray microscopy, and lithography.
