Clinical pathway

A clinical pathway, also known as a care pathway, integrated care pathway, critical pathway, or care map, is a multidisciplinary management tool used in healthcare to organise and sequence the care of patients with a specific clinical problem. It details the essential steps in the care of patients with a specific condition, translating evidence-based guidelines into local structures of care.

Purpose

Clinical pathways aim to standardise care for defined groups of patients, reduce unwarranted variation in practice, improve outcomes, shorten length of stay, and control costs. They typically specify, for each stage of care — often day by day for surgical conditions — the assessments, investigations, treatments, medications, consultations, and discharge criteria expected.

Development and use

Pathways are usually developed locally by multidisciplinary teams including physicians, nurses, pharmacists, and allied health professionals, drawing on clinical practice guidelines and the best available evidence. Variance tracking — recording deviations from the pathway and their reasons — is used to monitor performance and refine the pathway over time.

Clinical pathways became widely used in hospitals in the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, and elsewhere from the 1980s and 1990s onwards, particularly for common surgical procedures such as hip replacement and for conditions such as stroke and acute myocardial infarction. Critics note that pathways must allow flexibility for individual patient needs and should not substitute for clinical judgement.
