McDonnell Douglas F-15E Strike Eagle

The McDonnell Douglas (now Boeing) F-15E Strike Eagle is an American all-weather multirole strike fighter derived from the McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle. Designed in the 1980s for long-range, high-speed interdiction without requiring escort, it entered service with the United States Air Force in 1989.

An F-15E Strike Eagle of the United States Air Force

Design

The Strike Eagle retains the air-to-air capability of the F-15 while adding a second crew member — a weapon systems officer — conformal fuel tanks for extended range, and advanced avionics including the APG-70 radar and LANTIRN targeting pods for precision strikes by day or night in all weather. It can carry a wide range of precision-guided munitions, including laser- and GPS-guided bombs.

Service

F-15Es have been deployed in operations over Iraq, the Balkans, Afghanistan, Libya, and Syria, and proved effective in both air-to-ground and air-to-air roles. Export variants include the F-15I of Israel, the F-15K of South Korea, the F-15SG of Singapore, and the F-15SA and advanced F-15QA of Saudi Arabia and Qatar. The U.S. Air Force has acquired the further-developed F-15EX Eagle II.
