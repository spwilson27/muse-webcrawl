Water quality

Water quality refers to the chemical, physical and biological characteristics of water, usually assessed with respect to its suitability for a particular purpose such as drinking, irrigation, industry or supporting aquatic ecosystems. Standards differ by use: water fit for swimming may not be fit to drink.

Parameters

Assessment typically measures pH, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), nutrients such as nitrates and phosphates, heavy metals, pesticides, and microbial indicators such as E. coli and total coliforms, which signal faecal contamination and disease risk.

Threats

Major threats include untreated sewage, agricultural run-off causing eutrophication of lakes and coastal "dead zones", industrial discharges, mining waste, oil spills, and emerging contaminants such as pharmaceuticals and microplastics. Climate change alters water temperature and flow, compounding quality problems.

Regulation and treatment

The World Health Organization publishes drinking-water guidelines adopted by many countries. Laws such as the United States Clean Water Act and Safe Drinking Water Act, and the EU Water Framework Directive, set enforceable standards. Water treatment — filtration, sedimentation and disinfection — and wastewater treatment protect both public health and receiving ecosystems.
