Musica ficta

Musica ficta ("false" or "feigned music") was a term used in European music theory from the late 12th century to about 1600 to describe pitches that lie outside the recognised system of hexachords — the musica recta — and therefore had to be supplied or altered by performers.

Practice

In medieval and Renaissance polyphony, singers routinely inflected notes that were not explicitly signed in the notation: raising a note by semitone to create a leading tone at cadences (causa necessitatis), flattening notes to avoid the tritone (causa pulchritudinis), and similar adjustments governed by rules and conventions that were only partly written down. Because the original sources rarely specify these alterations completely, modern editors must decide where to apply musica ficta, a task that remains a significant and sometimes controversial part of editing early music.
