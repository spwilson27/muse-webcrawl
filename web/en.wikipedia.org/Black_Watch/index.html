Black Watch

Soldiers of the Black Watch wearing the dark tartan and red hackle.

The Black Watch, 3rd Battalion, Royal Regiment of Scotland (3 SCOTS) is an infantry battalion of the Royal Regiment of Scotland, descended from the famous Black Watch regiment of the British Army, first raised in the Scottish Highlands in the 18th century. The name derives from the dark government-issue tartan the soldiers wore and their original role "watching" the Highlands.

History

In 1739, six Independent Highland Companies were amalgamated into a regiment of the line, numbered the 43rd (later 42nd) Regiment of Foot, to police the Highlands after the Jacobite rising of 1715 and prevent cattle raiding and unrest. The regiment went on to serve in the Seven Years' War in North America, the American Revolutionary War, the Napoleonic Wars, the Crimean War, the Boer War, and both World Wars, earning an extensive list of battle honours.

In the Second World War, Black Watch battalions fought in France, North Africa, Italy, Normandy, and Burma. In the postwar era the regiment served in Korea, and later in Iraq and Afghanistan. In 2006, under restructuring of the Scottish infantry, the Black Watch became the 3rd Battalion of the Royal Regiment of Scotland.

Traditions

The battalion is identified by its dark "Government" tartan (the Black Watch tartan) and the red hackle worn on the Tam O'Shanter bonnet. Its castle and regimental museum are at Balhousie Castle in Perth, Scotland.
