Professional certification

Professional certification, trade certification, or professional designation — often called simply certification or qualification — is a designation earned by a person to assure qualification to perform a job or task. Certifications are earned from a professional society or educational provider, or by passing examinations and demonstrating experience, and many must be renewed periodically through continuing education.

Types

Certification differs from licensure: a licence is legally required to practise a profession and is granted by a government body, whereas certification is usually voluntary and granted by a non-governmental organisation. Examples include the Certified Public Accountant (CPA), Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA), Project Management Professional (PMP), and vendor-specific information technology credentials such as Cisco's CCNA and Microsoft's role-based certifications.

Process and standards

Quality certification programmes are typically accredited to standards such as ISO/IEC 17024, which requires that assessment be based on a job analysis, that examinations be valid and secure, and that the certifying body be independent of training providers. Candidates generally must meet education and experience prerequisites, pass an examination, and abide by a code of ethics.

Criticism

Critics note the proliferation of low-value credentials ("certification mills"), the cost of maintenance fees and continuing education, and the risk that employers treat certificates as substitutes for demonstrated skill.
