Karabakh

Karabakh (Azerbaijani: Qarabağ; Armenian: Ղարաբաղ, Gharabagh) is a geographic and historical region in the South Caucasus, in present-day southwestern Azerbaijan. Its name, usually interpreted as "black garden" (from Turkic kara, black, and Persian bāgh, garden), denotes a fertile land divided between the mountainous west — often called Nagorno-Karabakh ("Mountainous Karabakh") — and the lowland plains to the east along the Kura and Aras rivers.

History

In antiquity the region lay between Caucasian Albania and Armenia and was contested by Roman, Persian, Arab, Mongol and Ottoman empires. Armenian melikdoms governed the mountainous part under Persian suzerainty before the Karabakh Khanate emerged in the 18th century; the khanate was ceded to the Russian Empire by the Treaty of Gulistan (1813) and the Treaty of Turkmenchay (1828). In the Soviet period, the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast was created in 1923 within the Azerbaijan SSR, with a mostly Armenian population.

Conflict

From 1988 the region's status became the focus of the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict between Armenia and Azerbaijan. The First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1988–1994) ended with Armenian forces controlling most of the former oblast and surrounding Azerbaijani districts, and the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh governed the mountainous region. Azerbaijan regained the lowlands and southern parts in the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War of 2020, and in September 2023, after a lightning offensive, took full control of Nagorno-Karabakh; nearly its entire Armenian population — over 100,000 people — fled to Armenia, and the Republic of Artsakh formally dissolved on 1 January 2024.

Geography and culture

Mountainous Karabakh is known for its forests, the historic monasteries of Gandzasar, Dadivank and Amaras, the city of Shusha/Shushi (a cultural centre of both Armenians and Azerbaijanis), and the Karabakh horse breed. The lowlands are a cotton- and grain-growing plain irrigated by the Kura and Aras.
