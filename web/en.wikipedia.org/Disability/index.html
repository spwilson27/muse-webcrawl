Disability

Disability is the experience of any condition that makes it more difficult for a person to do certain activities or have equitable access within a given environment. Disabilities may be cognitive, developmental, intellectual, mental, physical, sensory, or a combination of multiple factors. They can be present from birth or acquired during a person's lifetime.

Definitions and models

Understanding of disability has been shaped by competing models. The medical model locates disability in an impairment of the individual to be treated or cured. The social model of disability, developed by disabled people's organisations, distinguishes impairment from disability, arguing that people are disabled by barriers in society — environmental, organisational, and attitudinal — rather than by their impairments alone. The World Health Organization's International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF, 2001) adopts an integrative biopsychosocial framework.

Prevalence

The WHO estimates that about 1.3 billion people — roughly 16% of the world's population — experience significant disability, a proportion rising with population ageing and chronic disease.

Rights and law

The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006) requires states to promote, protect, and ensure the full enjoyment of human rights by persons with disabilities. National measures include the Americans with Disabilities Act (1990) in the United States and the Equality Act 2010 in the United Kingdom, which mandate accessibility and prohibit discrimination.
