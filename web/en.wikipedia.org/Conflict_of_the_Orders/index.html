Conflict of the Orders

The Conflict of the Orders, also known as the Struggle of the Orders, was a political struggle between the plebeians (commoners) and patricians (aristocrats) of the ancient Roman Republic that lasted from 500 BC to 287 BC. The plebeians sought political equality, including access to high offices, relief from debt bondage, and the publication of the laws.

Course of the struggle

The plebeians' chief weapon was the secessio plebis — a collective withdrawal from the city, first in 494 BC, which won them their own magistrates, the plebeian tribunes, and the Plebeian Council. Further milestones included the Law of the Twelve Tables (c. 450 BC), which published the law; the Lex Canuleia (445 BC) permitting intermarriage; the Licinian–Sextian laws (367 BC) opening the consulship to plebeians; and the Ogulnian law (300 BC) opening the great priesthoods.

Resolution

The struggle is conventionally held to have ended with the Lex Hortensia of 287 BC, which made resolutions of the Plebeian Council (plebiscita) binding on all citizens. Thereafter political conflict in Rome was increasingly between a mixed patricio-plebeian nobility of office-holders and outsiders, rather than between the hereditary orders.
