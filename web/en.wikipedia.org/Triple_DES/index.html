Triple DES

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In cryptography, Triple DES (3DES or TDES), officially the Triple Data Encryption Algorithm (TDEA or Triple DEA), is a symmetric-key block cipher that applies the DES cipher three times to each data block. The 56-bit key of DES is no longer considered secure against modern brute-force attacks, and Triple DES increases the effective key size to resist them.

Definition

Triple DES encrypts data in 64-bit blocks using three independent 56-bit keys in an encrypt-decrypt-encrypt (EDE) sequence: the plaintext is encrypted with the first key, decrypted with the second, and encrypted again with the third. Decryption reverses the process. With three distinct keys, the effective key length is 168 bits, though meet-in-the-middle attacks reduce the effective security to about 112 bits. A two-key variant offers roughly 80 bits of security.

History and use

The algorithm was standardized in ANSI X9.52 in 1998 and in NIST Special Publication 800-67. It was widely used in the financial industry, in payment card systems, and in protocols such as IPsec and TLS. Because it operates on 64-bit blocks, Triple DES is vulnerable to the Sweet32 birthday attack on long-lived connections. NIST deprecated Triple DES in 2017 and disallowed its use for encryption after 2023, in favour of the Advanced Encryption Standard.
