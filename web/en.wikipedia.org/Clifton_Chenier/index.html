Clifton Chenier (25 June 1925 – 12 December 1987) was an American musician known as the "King of Zydeco". A French-speaking Louisiana Creole, he fused traditional Creole and Cajun music with blues and rhythm and blues, playing the piano accordion and singing in both English and Louisiana French. He is generally credited with defining the modern zydeco genre and bringing it to international audiences.

Early life

Chenier was born near Opelousas, Louisiana, into a sharecropping family of musicians; his father played accordion and his uncle played fiddle. He worked in the sugar cane fields and later at an oil refinery in Port Arthur, Texas, playing dances on weekends. He began recording in 1954, and his 1955 single "Eh, 'tite fille" for Specialty Records gave him regional fame.

Career

Chenier toured widely with his Red Hot Louisiana Band, featuring his brother Cleveland Chenier on rubboard (frottoir) — an instrument Clifton is said to have helped design — and later the saxophonist John Hart. Signing with Arhoolie Records in the 1960s, he recorded landmark albums such as Louisiana Blues and Zydeco (1965) and Bon Ton Roulet. He performed at festivals worldwide, appeared in films, and won a Grammy Award in 1983 for I'm Here. In 1984 he was named a National Heritage Fellow.

Death and legacy

Diabetes cost Chenier part of a foot and eventually contributed to the kidney failure that killed him in 1987 in Lafayette. His son C. J. Chenier took over the band. Clifton Chenier was inducted posthumously into the Blues Hall of Fame in 1989 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2014. His crown-wearing stage persona and virtuoso accordion work remain the template for zydeco performance.
