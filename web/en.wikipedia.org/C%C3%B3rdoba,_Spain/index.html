Córdoba is a city in Andalusia, Spain, and the capital of the province of Córdoba. Situated on the Guadalquivir River at the foot of the Sierra Morena, it was a Roman city and later the capital of the Islamic Emirate and then Caliphate of Córdoba, when it was among the largest and most advanced cities in the world.

History

Founded as Roman Corduba, the city became the capital of al-Andalus under the Umayyads. Under Abd al-Rahman III and his successors in the 10th century, Córdoba grew into a great centre of learning, famous for its libraries, medicine and philosophy. Ferdinand III of Castile conquered the city in 1236 during the Reconquista.

Landmarks

The city's historic centre, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, includes the Mosque–Cathedral (Mezquita), one of the greatest monuments of Islamic architecture, with a Renaissance cathedral built inside it; the Roman bridge; the Alcázar de los Reyes Cristianos; and the old Jewish quarter with its medieval synagogue.

Culture

Córdoba is known for the Festival of the Courtyards (Fiesta de los Patios) in May, when residents open flower-filled courtyards to the public, and for its traditions of flamenco, leatherwork and silver jewellery.
