Ambush marketing

Ambush marketing or ambush advertising is a marketing strategy in which an advertiser associates its products or services with a particular event — typically a major sporting event such as the Olympic Games or the FIFA World Cup — without paying for official sponsorship rights or with the intention of undermining a rival's sponsorship. The term was popularised by marketing consultant Jerry Welsh in the 1980s.

Techniques and examples

Techniques include sponsoring broadcasts of the event rather than the event itself, sponsoring participating athletes or teams, advertising heavily in the host city around the event, and using imagery or themes that evoke the event without using protected trademarks. Celebrated cases include Nike's campaigns around the 1996 Atlanta Olympics (where Reebok was official sponsor) and Bavaria Beer's orange-dress stunt at the 2010 FIFA World Cup in South Africa, where FIFA was a Budweiser sponsor.

Countermeasures

Event organisers and host governments increasingly enact special legislation granting exclusive association rights — such as the London Olympic Games and Paralympic Games Act 2006 — creating "clean zones" around venues and restricting even generic words. Critics argue such laws are disproportionate and restrict free expression; supporters say they protect the sponsorship revenue on which events depend.
