The Selimiye Mosque (Turkish: Selimiye Camii) is an Ottoman imperial mosque in Edirne, Turkey, commissioned by Sultan Selim II and built by the imperial architect Mimar Sinan between 1568/69 and 1574/75. Sinan considered it his masterpiece, and it is regarded as the crowning achievement of Ottoman architecture and one of the highest accomplishments of Islamic architecture as a whole. The mosque and its social complex (külliye) were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011.

Architecture

The mosque crowns a hill above Edirne, the former Ottoman capital. Its vast central dome, about 31.28 metres in internal diameter, is supported on an elegant system of eight massive piers arranged in an octagon, with four half-domes at the corners of the square prayer hall — a structural refinement of the Hagia Sophia scheme that leaves the interior unusually open and flooded with light from rows of windows. The mihrab is pushed back into an apse-like projection so that it can be seen from everywhere in the hall, and the sultan's loge is decorated with celebrated İznik tiles.

At the four corners stand what were the tallest minarets in the Ottoman world: four exceptionally slender fluted minarets, each with three balconies (şerefe) reached by separate staircases that do not cross. The surrounding külliye includes two madrasas (one now the Selimiye Ethnography Museum, the other a museum of Islamic arts), a covered market (arasta), a primary school, libraries, and a courtyard with a şadırvan ablutions fountain.

History

Selim II chose Edirne — the empire's second capital and his favourite residence — over Istanbul, where all prominent hilltops were already occupied by imperial mosques. During the Balkan Wars the mosque suffered damage: in 1913 Bulgarian forces looted and damaged parts of the complex, and an inscribed tile panel was taken to Bulgaria, an act protested ever since. The mosque remains in active use for worship and dominates the skyline of Edirne.
