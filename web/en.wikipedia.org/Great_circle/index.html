In mathematics, a great circle or orthodrome is the circular intersection of a sphere and a plane passing through the sphere's centre point. Any arc of a great circle is a geodesic of the sphere, so great circles in spherical geometry play the role that straight lines do in Euclidean geometry: the minor arc of a great circle between two points is the shortest path between them on the surface of the sphere. Great-circle distance is therefore the distance measure used for navigation on the Earth's surface, after whom the concept is also known as a "great-circle route" in air and sea travel.

Properties

Every great circle has the same circumference as the sphere itself and divides it into two equal hemispheres. Any two points on a sphere that are not antipodal define a unique great circle; antipodal points lie on infinitely many great circles. Two distinct great circles always intersect at a pair of antipodal points. Circles on a sphere whose planes do not pass through the centre are called small circles — for example, parallels of latitude other than the equator.

Distance

The great-circle distance between two points with latitudes φ₁, φ₂ and longitude difference Δλ, on a sphere of radius r, can be computed with the haversine formula:

d = 2r · arcsin( √( sin²((φ₂−φ₁)/2) + cos φ₁ · cos φ₂ · sin²(Δλ/2) ) )

Navigation

Because the shortest path between two points on Earth follows a great circle, long-distance air routes and shipping lanes are planned as sequences of great-circle segments, approximated in practice by rhumb-line legs between waypoints. On a Mercator projection, a great-circle route appears curved, bowing toward the nearer pole — which is why flights between, for example, North America and East Asia travel over the Arctic. Aircraft flying such routes take advantage of shorter distances and, in that corridor, of favourable winds. The concept has been used in navigation since the 16th century, when it was recognised that ships could shorten voyages by sailing along great circles rather than constant-compass-bearing courses.
