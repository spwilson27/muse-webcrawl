The Passover Seder (Hebrew: סֵדֶר, "order") is a ritual feast that marks the beginning of the Jewish holiday of Passover. It is conducted throughout the world on the eve of the 15th day of Nisan in the Hebrew calendar, and on the 16th day as well outside Israel. The Seder retells the story of the Exodus of the Israelites from slavery in ancient Egypt.

Order of the Seder

The ceremony follows the text of the Haggadah and proceeds through fifteen steps, beginning with Kadesh (the blessing over wine) and Urchatz (hand washing), and including Karpas (a vegetable dipped in salt water), Yachatz (breaking the middle matzah), and Maggid (the telling of the Exodus story, including the Four Questions traditionally asked by the youngest child).

The Seder plate

The Seder plate (ka'arah) holds symbolic foods: maror (bitter herb, symbolizing the bitterness of slavery), chazeret, charoset (a sweet paste recalling mortar), karpas, zeroa (a roasted shank bone, symbolizing the Paschal sacrifice), and beitzah (a roasted egg, symbolizing renewal).

Customs

Participants drink four cups of wine, eat matzah and maror, recline as a sign of freedom, open the door for Elijah the Prophet, and conclude with songs such as "Chad Gadya" and the declaration "Next year in Jerusalem."
