IgG4-related disease

IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic immune-mediated fibroinflammatory condition that can affect multiple organs, characterised by tumefactive (mass-like) lesions, a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate rich in IgG4-positive plasma cells, storiform fibrosis, and often — but not always — elevated serum IgG4 concentrations. The disease concept was established in 2003, when Japanese researchers recognised that autoimmune pancreatitis shared distinctive pathology with extrapancreatic conditions long known by separate names.

Characteristic histopathology: storiform fibrosis with abundant IgG4-positive plasma cells.

Manifestations

Nearly any organ can be involved, typically several at once: the pancreas (type 1 autoimmune pancreatitis), bile ducts (IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis), salivary and lacrimal glands (formerly Mikulicz disease), orbits, kidneys (tubulointerstitial nephritis), retroperitoneum (retroperitoneal fibrosis), aorta, lungs, meninges, thyroid (a subset of Riedel's and Hashimoto's thyroiditis), and prostate. Individual organ manifestations were described as early as the 19th century (Küttner's tumour, 1896), but their unity was recognised only after the link to IgG4 was found — much as IGF-1 united growth effects once attributed to separate "somatomedins".

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on characteristic histology: dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, storiform ("cartwheel") fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis, and increased IgG4-positive plasma cells with an IgG4/IgG ratio over 40%. Serum IgG4 is elevated in about two-thirds of patients but is neither sensitive nor specific enough to stand alone. Imaging and biopsy help distinguish IgG4-RD from malignancy, which it often mimics. International consensus criteria (ACR/EULAR 2019 classification criteria) structure diagnosis and research.

Treatment and prognosis

Most patients respond dramatically to glucocorticoids, the first-line therapy, though relapses are common during or after tapering. Rituximab, which depletes B cells, is effective for relapsing or refractory disease and is increasingly used as steroid-sparing therapy. Untreated disease can cause irreversible organ damage, including pancreatic insufficiency, kidney failure, and aortic aneurysm complications. The underlying immunology — including the roles of plasmablasts, T follicular helper cells, and whether IgG4 antibodies themselves are pathogenic or merely markers — remains under active investigation.
