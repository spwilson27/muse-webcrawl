Isthmus of Corinth

The Isthmus of Corinth is the narrow strip of land that joins the Peloponnese peninsula to the rest of mainland Greece. It lies between the Gulf of Corinth to the west and the Saronic Gulf of the Aegean Sea to the east, and is about 6.3 kilometres (3.9 miles) wide at its narrowest point. Since 1893 it has been cut through by the Corinth Canal, which technically renders the Peloponnese an island.

History

The isthmus was a strategic crossroads from earliest antiquity: the only land route into the Peloponnese, and a portage point between the Ionian and Aegean seas. Near its eastern shore stood the sanctuary of Poseidon at Isthmia, where the Isthmian Games were held, and the city of Corinth grew rich controlling the traffic across it. Around 600 BC the Corinthian tyrant Periander constructed the Diolkos, a paved trackway along which ships could be hauled across the isthmus on wheeled cradles, avoiding the dangerous circumnavigation of the Peloponnese.

Attempts to build a canal

Periander is also said to have considered a canal but was dissuaded by his engineers. Later attempts were begun or planned by Demetrius Poliorcetes, Julius Caesar, Caligula and Hadrian. Nero actually broke ground in AD 67, ceremonially striking the earth with a golden spade and employing thousands of prisoners, but the project was abandoned after his death. The engineering and expense required defeated every ancient effort.

The Corinth Canal

The modern canal was constructed between 1881 and 1893, initially by a French company and completed by a Greek company under banker Andreas Syngros. It is 6.4 kilometres long, 21.4 metres wide at the bottom and 8 metres deep, with nearly vertical limestone walls rising up to 90 metres. Although too narrow for most modern ocean-going ships, it remains a striking feat of engineering and a popular route for smaller vessels and tourist boats. Unusual submersible bridges at each end sink below the water to allow ships to pass. Rail and road crossings link the Peloponnese to Attica across and beside the canal.
