Xian (Taoism)

A xian (Chinese: 仙; pinyin: xiān; Wade–Giles: hsien) is an immortal or transcendent being in Chinese mythology and Daoism: a person who, through spiritual cultivation, alchemy or divine favour, has attained supernatural powers, longevity or immortality, and often ascended to dwell in paradises such as the Kunlun Mountains or the isles of Penglai.

Concept and etymology

The character 仙 combines "person" (人) and "mountain" (山), reflecting the belief that immortals retreat to sacred mountains. Early texts such as the Zhuangzi describe spirit-like beings who "eat nothing but air, drink nothing but dew" and ride on clouds. Daoist traditions classify xian in hierarchies — celestial immortals (tianxian), earthly immortals (dixian) and those who achieve "liberation by corpse" (shijie), ascending while leaving a transformed body behind.

Paths to immortality

Aspirants pursued immortality through external alchemy (waidan) — compounding elixirs of cinnabar and gold, a pursuit that famously poisoned several emperors — and later through internal alchemy (neidan), meditative and physiological practices refining the "three treasures" of essence, breath and spirit. Dietetics, breath control (daoyin), sexual techniques and moral merit were all credited means. Ge Hong's Baopuzi (c. 320 AD) is the classic early treatise on these arts, and the Liexian Zhuan collects biographies of immortals.

In culture

The Eight Immortals — Lü Dongbin, Li Tieguai, He Xiangu and their companions — are the most beloved xian of folklore, celebrated in drama, art and festival. The quest for immortality also influenced Chinese medicine, poetry and the modern xianxia ("immortal heroes") genre of fantasy fiction and games. Note that this term, 仙 (xiān), is unrelated to the xia 俠 (knight-errant) concept despite the similar romanisation.
