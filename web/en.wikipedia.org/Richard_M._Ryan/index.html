Richard M. Ryan

Richard M. Ryan is an American psychologist and professor, best known as the co-founder, with Edward L. Deci, of self-determination theory (SDT), a widely influential framework for understanding human motivation, personality, and well-being.

Career

Ryan earned his PhD in clinical psychology at the University of Rochester, where he taught for many years and co-directed the motivation research group. He later became a professor at the Institute for Positive Psychology and Education at the Australian Catholic University.

Self-determination theory

SDT holds that human well-being depends on the satisfaction of three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. The theory distinguishes autonomous from controlled motivation and has been applied in education, health care, work organisations, sport, and psychotherapy. Ryan and Deci's 1985 book Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior and their 2017 synthesis Self-Determination Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development, and Wellness are standard references.

Recognition

Ryan is among the most cited psychologists of his generation and has received numerous awards for his contributions to motivational science.
