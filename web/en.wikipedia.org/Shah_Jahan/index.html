Shah Jahan

Shah Jahan (1592–1666), whose reign is considered the golden age of Mughal architecture.

Shah Jahan (5 January 1592 – 31 January 1666), born Mirza Shahab-ud-din Muhammad Khurram, was the fifth Mughal emperor, reigning from 1628 to 1658. His reign marked the zenith of Mughal architecture and territorial expansion, and he is best known as the builder of the Taj Mahal in Agra, commissioned in memory of his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal.

Early life and accession

Khurram was the third son of Emperor Jahangir and his Rajput wife Jagat Gosain. A capable military commander in the Deccan and against the Rajputs of Mewar, he rebelled unsuccessfully against his father in 1622 before succeeding to the throne in 1628 after a succession struggle, eliminating rival claimants.

Reign

Shah Jahan's reign is remembered as the golden age of the Mughal Empire: the court was immensely wealthy, trade flourished, and the empire reached near its greatest extent, though costly campaigns in the Deccan, Balkh, and Kandahar strained the treasury. A catastrophic famine struck Gujarat and the Deccan in 1630–1632. He founded the new imperial capital Shahjahanabad (Old Delhi) in 1638, crowned by the Red Fort and the Jama Masjid.

Architecture

His patronage produced the most celebrated Mughal buildings: the Taj Mahal (1632–1653), the Red Fort and Jama Masjid in Delhi, the Moti Masjid and additions to Agra Fort, the Shalimar Gardens in Lahore, and the Peacock Throne, the most opulent seat of power in the empire, later looted by Nader Shah in 1739.

Deposition and death

Falling ill in 1657, Shah Jahan was deposed by his son Aurangzeb after a war of succession among his four sons and confined in Agra Fort, where — legend holds — he gazed across the Yamuna at the Taj Mahal until his death in 1666. He was buried beside Mumtaz Mahal in the Taj.
