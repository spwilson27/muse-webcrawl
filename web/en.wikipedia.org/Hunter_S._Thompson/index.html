Hunter S. Thompson

Biographical summary

Born: 18 July 1937, Louisville, Kentucky

Died: 20 February 2005, Woody Creek, Colorado

Occupation: Journalist, author

Known for: Gonzo journalism; Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas; Hell's Angels

Hunter Stockton Thompson (1937–2005) was an American journalist and author, widely considered the founder of gonzo journalism — a style of reporting in which the reporter becomes a central, openly subjective participant in the events being covered. He is best known for Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas (1971), a hallucinatory account of a drug-fuelled trip to Las Vegas that became a landmark of American countercultural literature.

Career

Thompson served in the United States Air Force, where he began writing for a base newspaper. After freelance work and time in Puerto Rico and South America, he gained fame with Hell's Angels (1967), an immersive account of a year spent riding with the motorcycle gang. His 1970 article "The Kentucky Derby Is Decadent and Depraved," illustrated by Ralph Steadman, is regarded as the birth of gonzo journalism.

Later work

As a political correspondent for Rolling Stone, Thompson covered the 1972 United States presidential campaign in Fear and Loathing: On the Campaign Trail '72. His persona of "Raoul Duke," heavy use of drugs and alcohol, and contempt for authority figures — particularly President Richard Nixon — made him a cult figure. Later works include The Rum Diary and several volumes of letters. Thompson died by suicide at his home in Woody Creek, Colorado, in 2005; his ashes were fired from a cannon in a ceremony he had planned, funded by his friend Johnny Depp, who portrayed him in the 1998 film adaptation of Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas.
