Sachin Ramesh Tendulkar (born 24 April 1973) is an Indian former international cricketer who captained the Indian national team and is widely regarded as one of the greatest batsmen in the history of cricket. He is the highest run-scorer of all time in international cricket, the only player to have scored one hundred international centuries, and the first batsman to score a double century in a One Day International.

Early life

Tendulkar was born in Bombay (now Mumbai) to a Marathi family; his father Ramesh was a novelist. He was introduced to cricket at the age of eleven and was coached by Ramakant Achrekar at Shivaji Park, Dadar. At fourteen, while attending Sharadashram Vidyamandir school, he shared an unbroken partnership of 664 runs with Vinod Kambli in a school match — then a record for any wicket in organised cricket.

International career

Tendulkar made his Test debut against Pakistan at Karachi in November 1989, aged just sixteen. Over a career spanning twenty-four years he played 200 Test matches, scoring 15,921 runs at an average of 53.78 with 51 centuries, and 463 One Day Internationals, scoring 18,426 runs with 49 centuries. In March 2012 he became the first and only cricketer to complete one hundred international centuries.

He was a member of the Indian team that won the 2011 Cricket World Cup — his sixth World Cup appearance — defeating Sri Lanka in the final at Mumbai. He also played for the Mumbai Indians in the Indian Premier League from its inaugural season in 2008 until 2013.

Retirement and later life

Tendulkar retired from all forms of cricket in November 2013 after playing his 200th Test match against the West Indies at the Wankhede Stadium in Mumbai. In 2014 he became the youngest person and the first sportsperson to receive the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award. He served as a nominated member of the Rajya Sabha, the upper house of the Indian Parliament, from 2012 to 2018, and was inducted into the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame in 2019.

Playing style and legacy

A technically complete right-handed batsman, Tendulkar was noted for the balance and economy of his strokeplay, excelling in both the straight drive and shots off the back foot. Don Bradman remarked that Tendulkar's batting reminded him of his own. In India his popularity has long transcended sport: stadiums filled to watch him bat and emptied when he was dismissed, and he is routinely referred to by fans as the "God of Cricket".
