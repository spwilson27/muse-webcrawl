Conductor (music)

In music, a conductor is the person who directs the performance of an orchestra, choir, opera or other musical ensemble, primarily by gestures of the hands and arms, often using a baton. The conductor unifies the performance, sets the tempo, shapes phrasing and dynamics, cues entrances and interprets the score, having prepared the work and the ensemble in rehearsal.

History

Conducting as a distinct role emerged in the early nineteenth century, as orchestras grew too large for leadership from the keyboard or the concertmaster's violin. Early conductors such as Louis Spohr and Felix Mendelssohn popularised the baton. The late nineteenth and twentieth centuries produced the modern maestro figure — figures such as Arturo Toscanini, Herbert von Karajan and Leonard Bernstein became international celebrities.

Technique

The conductor beats regular patterns — typically with the right hand — that trace the metre of the music, while the left hand cues sections and shapes expression. Choral conductors use similar gestures adapted to singers. Many ensembles today, particularly historically informed and chamber groups, also perform without a conductor.
