Icon (computing)

In computing, an icon is a pictogram or ideogram displayed on a computer screen in order to help the user navigate a computer system. The icon is a small picture that represents a file, folder, program, device, or function; activating it, usually with a mouse click or tap, opens or runs the associated item.

History

Icons were pioneered at Xerox PARC in the 1970s as part of the graphical user interface of the Xerox Alto and Star, and were popularised by the Apple Macintosh (1984) and Microsoft Windows. Early icon sets were small bitmaps of 16×16 or 32×32 pixels.

Design

Effective icons are recognisable at small sizes, consistent within a set, and culturally intelligible. Modern systems use scalable vector formats and multiple resolutions. Icons are often combined with text labels, tooltips, and badges indicating status.

Types

Categories include application icons, file-type icons, toolbar icons, system tray icons, and favicons for websites. Religious and cultural uses of the word icon predate computing; the computing sense borrows the notion of a symbolic image.
