Vauxhall Motors
Founded: 1857 (as Alex Wilson and Company); cars from 1903
Headquarters: Luton, England
Parent: Stellantis
Products: Automobiles, vans

Vauxhall Motors Limited is a British car manufacturer based in Luton, England. Founded in 1857 as a pump and marine engine manufacturer in the Vauxhall district of London, it began producing cars in 1903 and moved to Luton in 1905. It was a subsidiary of General Motors from 1925 until 2017, when it was acquired by Groupe PSA; since 2021 it has been part of Stellantis.

History

The company's early cars were sporting machines; the 1913 Prince Henry model is often described as one of the first true sports cars. Under GM ownership Vauxhall moved into mass production, introducing popular models such as the Viva (1963), Astra (1979) and Cavalier. For decades Vauxhall's range has largely mirrored that of its German sister brand Opel, differing mainly in badging and detailing, with cars sold as Vauxhalls in the United Kingdom.

Operations

Vauxhall manufactures the Astra at Ellesmere Port (production of the model ended there in 2022 as the plant converted to electric vans) and vans, including the Vivaro, at Luton. Its Griffin badge derives from the heraldic griffin in the arms of Fulco le Breant, after whom Vauxhall (Fulk's Hall) in London was named.
