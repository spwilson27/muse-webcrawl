Thin-plate spline

Thin-plate spline (TPS) is a spline-based technique for data interpolation and smoothing. It was introduced to geometric design by Duchon. They are an important special case of a polyharmonic spline. The name "thin plate" refers to a physical analogy involving the bending of a thin sheet of metal: points on the sheet are constrained to move to specified heights, and the sheet settles into the shape that minimises its bending energy.

Formally, given a set of data points, the TPS is the function that exactly interpolates (or, in a smoothed version, approximately fits) the data while minimising an energy functional equal to the integral over the plane of the sum of the squares of the second derivatives — the bending energy. The solution is expressed as a sum of radial basis functions of the form U(r) = r² log r centred on each data point, plus an affine term. The linear system defining the weights can be solved directly, and orthogonality conditions ensure the affine part is separated from the bending part.

Applications

Thin-plate splines are widely used in computer vision and medical imaging for non-rigid image registration and shape matching, notably in the work of Fred Bookstein on morphometrics, where TPS visualises the deformation of biological shapes as a warped grid. They are also used for scattered-data interpolation, surface reconstruction, and aligning shapes in statistical shape analysis via Procrustes methods.
