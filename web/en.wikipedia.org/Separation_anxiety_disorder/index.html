Separation anxiety disorder

Separation anxiety disorder (SAD) is an anxiety disorder in which an individual experiences excessive anxiety regarding separation from home or from people to whom the individual has a strong emotional attachment, such as a parent or caregiver. It is one of the most common anxiety disorders in children, though the DSM-5 also permits diagnosis in adults.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms include recurrent excessive distress when anticipating or experiencing separation, persistent worry about losing attachment figures or about harm befalling them, reluctance to go out or to sleep away from home, nightmares about separation, and physical complaints such as headaches and stomach aches when separation occurs or is anticipated.

Diagnosis

Under DSM-5 criteria, the fear or anxiety must be developmentally inappropriate and excessive, persist for at least four weeks in children and adolescents (typically six months in adults), and cause clinically significant distress or impairment.

Treatment

First-line treatment is psychotherapy, particularly cognitive behavioural therapy with gradual exposure to separation. Medication, such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, may be considered in moderate to severe cases or when psychotherapy alone is insufficient.
