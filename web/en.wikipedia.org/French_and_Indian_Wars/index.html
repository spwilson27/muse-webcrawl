French and Indian Wars

The French and Indian Wars were a series of conflicts in North America between 1688 and 1763, fought between France and Great Britain and their respective colonists and Native American allies. They were the American counterpart of European wars between the two powers over colonial dominion and trade.

The wars

The series comprised King William's War (1688–1697), Queen Anne's War (1702–1713), King George's War (1744–1748), and the French and Indian War proper (1754–1763), the North American theatre of the Seven Years' War. Native nations, including the Iroquois Confederacy, the Huron, and the Algonquin peoples, fought on both sides in pursuit of their own interests.

Outcome

The wars culminated in the British conquest of New France: Quebec fell in 1759 and Montreal in 1760, and by the Treaty of Paris (1763) France ceded Canada and its territories east of the Mississippi to Britain. The removal of the French threat and the cost of the war reshaped relations between Britain and its American colonies, contributing to the tensions that led to the American Revolution.
