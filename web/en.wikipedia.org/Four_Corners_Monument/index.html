Four Corners Monument

The Four Corners Monument marks the quadripoint in the Southwestern United States where the states of Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico and Utah meet — the only point in the United States where four states intersect. The monument is managed by the Navajo Nation Department of Parks and Recreation, the surrounding land lying within the Navajo Nation and the Ute Mountain Ute Reservation.

History

The boundaries were fixed by nineteenth-century surveys: the 37th parallel north and the 32nd meridian west of Washington. The corner was first surveyed in 1868 and marked with a sandstone shaft in 1875; a granite-and-brass marker set in a plaza now records the point, which the U.S. Supreme Court has upheld as the legal corner despite small discrepancies from the originally intended coordinates.

Tourism

The remote site, reached by U.S. Highway 160, attracts visitors who pose with a limb in each state. Navajo and Ute artisans sell crafts and food around the plaza. The monument was rebuilt in 1992 and its plaza renovated in 2010.
