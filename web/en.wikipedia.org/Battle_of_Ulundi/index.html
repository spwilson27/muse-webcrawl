Battle of Ulundi

The Battle of Ulundi took place on 4 July 1879 and was the last major battle of the Anglo-Zulu War. A British force under Lord Chelmsford defeated the Zulu army of King Cetshwayo at his capital, Ulundi (oNdini), in present-day KwaZulu-Natal, effectively ending Zulu independence.

Background

After the British disaster at Isandlwana in January 1879 and the costly victory at Khambula, Chelmsford advanced cautiously on Ulundi with about 5,000 British and allied troops. Cetshwayo's peace overtures were rejected, and the king assembled some 12,000–15,000 warriors to defend his capital.

The battle

The British formed a large hollow square with artillery and Gatling guns, advancing across open ground toward the royal kraal. The Zulu regiments charged repeatedly but could not break the square against concentrated rifle and artillery fire, suffering perhaps 1,500 killed. A cavalry pursuit followed, and the great kraal of oNdini was burned to the ground. British casualties were about 13 killed and 69 wounded.

Aftermath

Cetshwayo was captured some weeks later and exiled. Zululand was divided among thirteen appointed chiefs, plunging the kingdom into civil war, and was eventually annexed by Britain in 1887.
