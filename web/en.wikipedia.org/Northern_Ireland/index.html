Northern Ireland is a part of the United Kingdom in the north-east of the island of Ireland. It was created as a separate legal entity on 3 May 1921 under the Government of Ireland Act 1920, when Ireland was partitioned. It consists of six of the nine counties of the historic province of Ulster: Antrim, Armagh, Down, Fermanagh, Londonderry (Derry) and Tyrone.

History

From the late 1960s to 1998, Northern Ireland experienced the conflict known as "the Troubles", between unionists (mainly Protestant, favouring remaining in the UK) and nationalists (mainly Catholic, favouring a united Ireland), in which more than 3,500 people died. The Good Friday Agreement of 1998 established a power-sharing devolved government and the principle of consent: Northern Ireland's constitutional status can change only by majority vote.

Government and identity

The Northern Ireland Assembly at Stormont legislates on devolved matters, with an executive formed on a cross-community basis. People in Northern Ireland may identify as British, Irish, or both, and are entitled to British and/or Irish citizenship. Following the United Kingdom's withdrawal from the European Union, the Northern Ireland Protocol and the subsequent Windsor Framework kept Northern Ireland aligned with the EU single market for goods, creating a distinct trading position.

Geography

Lough Neagh, the largest lake in the British Isles by area, lies at its centre; the Giant's Causeway, an area of interlocking basalt columns formed by ancient volcanic activity, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site on the north coast.
