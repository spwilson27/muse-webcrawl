Goldwater–Nichols Act

The Goldwater–Nichols Department of Defense Reorganization Act of 1986 is a United States federal law that made the most sweeping changes to the Department of Defense since its creation in 1947. Named for Senator Barry Goldwater and Representative William Flynt Nichols, it was enacted on 1 October 1986 to strengthen joint operations among the military services.

Background

Operational failures — including the aborted Iran hostage rescue mission of 1980 (Operation Eagle Claw) and inter-service coordination problems during the 1983 invasion of Grenada — convinced Congress that rivalry among the Army, Navy, Air Force and Marine Corps hindered effective warfare and that reform could not be left to the services themselves.

Provisions

The Act made the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff the principal military adviser to the President and Secretary of Defense, created the position of Vice Chairman, and greatly strengthened the authority of the commanders of unified combatant commands, who now report through the Secretary of Defense. The operational chain of command runs from the President to the Secretary of Defense to the combatant commanders, bypassing the service chiefs. The Act also required officers to serve in joint assignments to be promoted to flag rank, building a cadre of "joint" officers.

Effects

Goldwater–Nichols is widely credited with the improved joint performance of US forces in the Panama operation of 1989 and the Gulf War of 1991. It remains a landmark of American defence organisation, though critics argue it increased the power of the defence bureaucracy and the political role of senior officers.
