Lanai (Hawaiian: Lānaʻi) is the sixth-largest of the Hawaiian Islands and the smallest publicly accessible inhabited island in the chain. It is colloquially known as the Pineapple Island because of its past as the world's largest pineapple plantation. Administratively it is part of Maui County, Hawaii, and its only town is Lanai City.

Geography

Lanai is roughly comma-shaped, 30 km long and up to 29 km wide, separated from Maui by the Auau Channel and from Molokai by the Kalohi Channel. Its highest point, Lānaʻihale, rises to 1,026 metres. The island is dry, lacking perennial streams, and much of its water comes from fog drip collected by Cook pine trees planted on its central ridge.

History

Native Hawaiians settled Lanai by about the 15th century; legend held it was haunted by spirits until the exile of Kaululau. In 1922 James Dole, president of the Hawaiian Pineapple Company (later Dole Food Company), bought the entire island for $1.1 million and developed it into a giant pineapple plantation, building Lanai City and a harbour. The island became the world's largest pineapple producer.

In 1985 the island passed to David Murdock's Castle & Cooke. Pineapple production ended in 1992, replaced by two luxury resorts. In 2012, Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison purchased about 98% of the island — land, resorts, utilities, and much of its housing — for a reported $300 million or more, making Lanai the most prominent example of a near-privately-owned island in the United States.

Sights

Attractions include Hulopoʻe Beach, the Garden of the Gods (Keahiakawelo) rock garden, Shipwreck Beach with its offshore wreck of a WWII-era vessel, and Munro Trail through the upland pines.
