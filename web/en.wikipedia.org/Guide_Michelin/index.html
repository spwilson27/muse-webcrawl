Guide Michelin

The Guide Michelin (Michelin Guide) is a series of guide books published by the French tyre company Michelin since 1900, best known for awarding Michelin stars to restaurants for excellence. The annual guides are among the most influential restaurant ratings in the world.

History

The first guide, published in 1900 by André and Édouard Michelin, was given away free to motorists and contained practical information on maps, tyre repair and hotels. Restaurant listings were added as motoring spread. The guide began charging a price in 1922, and the star system was introduced in 1926: a single star at first, with the three-star hierarchy established in 1931.

Stars and ratings

Michelin's anonymous inspectors award one star for "very good cooking in its category", two for "excellent cooking, worth a detour", and three for "exceptional cuisine, worth a special journey". A separate Bib Gourmand designation, introduced in the 1990s, recognises good food at moderate prices, and a Green Star, added in 2020, highlights sustainable practices. Stars are awarded to the restaurant, not the chef, and can be gained or lost each year.

Influence and criticism

Winning or losing a star can transform a restaurant's fortunes, and the guide's secrecy about its inspectors and criteria has drawn both fascination and criticism, including claims of bias towards French cuisine and formal dining. Michelin now publishes guides for dozens of cities and countries in Europe, Asia and the Americas.
