James Arthur Gosling OC (born May 19, 1955) is a Canadian computer scientist, best known as the founder and lead designer behind the Java programming language.

Early life and education

Gosling was born in Calgary, Alberta. He received a Bachelor of Science in computer science from the University of Calgary in 1977 and a PhD from Carnegie Mellon University in 1983, with a thesis on algebraic manipulation of constraints. While at Carnegie Mellon he wrote a version of Emacs (Gosling Emacs, or Gosmacs) and contributed to a multiprocessor version of Unix.

Sun Microsystems and Java

Gosling joined Sun Microsystems in 1984. In 1991 he led the "Green Project" team — with Mike Sheridan and Patrick Naughton — that set out to build software for interactive devices; the result was a language first called "Oak" and then renamed Java, publicly released in 1995. Java's "write once, run anywhere" design, running compiled bytecode on a virtual machine, made it one of the most widely used programming languages in the world. Gosling designed the original language and implemented its first compiler and virtual machine, and he was the lead author of The Java Language Specification. He also designed the NeWS window system.

After Sun

Gosling left Sun in 2010 shortly after its acquisition by Oracle. He later worked at Google (2011), as chief software architect at Liquid Robotics, and in 2017 joined Amazon Web Services as a Distinguished Engineer. He was made an Officer of the Order of Canada in 2007 and received the IEEE John von Neumann Medal in 2015.
