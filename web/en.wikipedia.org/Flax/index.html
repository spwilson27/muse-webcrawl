Flax

Flax (Linum usitatissimum), also known as common flax or linseed, is a flowering plant of the family Linaceae. It is cultivated as a food and fibre crop in regions of the world with temperate climates. Textiles made from flax are known in English as linen, and the plant's oil is known as linseed oil.

Cultivation and uses

Flax is one of the oldest cultivated crops, with evidence of use reaching back tens of thousands of years; dyed flax fibres found in a Georgian cave date to roughly 36,000 years ago. The plant grows to about 1.2 m tall with slender stems, pale blue flowers and small seeds. Varieties are grown either for seed — rich in omega-3 fatty acids in the form of alpha-linolenic acid — or for fibre, used in linen cloth, paper and composites. Flax straw and shives are also used for animal bedding and bio-based materials.

Nutrition

Flax seeds are eaten ground, whole or as oil, and are among the richest plant sources of lignans. They provide dietary fibre, protein and micronutrients, and are commonly added to cereals, baked goods and smoothies.
