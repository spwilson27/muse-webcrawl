Lower Hutt (Māori: Te Awa Kairangi ki Tai) is a city in the Wellington Region of New Zealand. Administered by the Hutt City Council, it is one of the four cities that constitute the Wellington metropolitan area. The city takes its name from the Hutt River, which was named after Sir William Hutt, a founding member, director and chairman of the New Zealand Company.

Geography

The city lies in the valley of the Hutt River (Te Awakairangi), northeast of Wellington Harbour, and is flanked by hills to the east and west. Lower Hutt is a floodplain city and has a long history of flood-protection works along the river. Suburbs include Petone, Woburn, Maungaraki, Naenae, Taita, Avalon, Wainuiomata (across the Wainuiomata Hill) and Eastbourne across the harbour.

History

The first European settlers arrived in the Hutt Valley in 1840 aboard the New Zealand Company ship Cuba and established a settlement at Petone (then called Britannia). Flooding and conflict with Māori led to the settlement moving into the valley and eventually to Wellington. In the 20th century, large state-housing developments and industries made Lower Hutt a major dormitory and manufacturing city for the capital.

Economy and culture

Petone is home to light industry, cafés and the Dowse Art Museum, a notable public gallery of New Zealand craft and design. The city hosts several Crown research institutes' staff and the suburb of Gracefield's research and manufacturing precinct.
