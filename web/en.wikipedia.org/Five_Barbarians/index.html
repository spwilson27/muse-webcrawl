Five Barbarians

The Five Barbarians, or Wu Hu (Chinese: 五胡), is a Chinese historical exonym for five ancient non-Han Chinese peoples who immigrated to northern China in the Eastern Han dynasty, and who rebelled against and overthrew the Western Jin dynasty during the early 4th century. These peoples were the Xiongnu, Jie, Qiang, Di and Xianbei.

Background

During the Han dynasty, large numbers of steppe and frontier peoples were settled inside China's northern borders, often serving as soldiers or agricultural labourers. As the Jin dynasty weakened during the War of the Eight Princes (291–306), these groups rose in revolt. In 304, the Xiongnu noble Liu Yuan founded the state of Han-Zhao, and in 311 his forces sacked the Jin capital Luoyang, capturing Emperor Huai. Chang'an fell in 316, ending the Western Jin.

The Sixteen Kingdoms

The upheavals inaugurated the Sixteen Kingdoms period (304–439), during which rulers of Xiongnu, Jie, Di, Qiang and Xianbei origin founded a succession of short-lived states across northern China, while the Jin court re-established itself in the south as the Eastern Jin. The period ended when the Northern Wei, founded by the Tuoba clan of the Xianbei, reunified northern China in 439.

Terminology

The term "Five Barbarians" (Wu Hu) appears in later Chinese historiography. Modern scholars note that the groups were internally diverse and that the label reflects the perspective of Han Chinese chroniclers rather than any alliance among the five peoples, who frequently fought one another as well.
