Common book formats compared

The size of a book is generally measured by the height against the width of a leaf, or sometimes the height against the width of its cover. Terms for book formats derive from the traditional method of producing books by folding printed sheets: the number of leaves created by folding a full sheet determines the format, so a folio folds the sheet once, a quarto twice, and an octavo three times.

Sheet sizes and named sizes

Because formats depend on the size of the original sheet, the same format name covers a range of dimensions. British book production traditionally used named sheets — foolscap, crown, demy, royal, imperial — giving compound names such as "crown octavo" or "demy octavo", the latter a standard size for general books in the 19th and 20th centuries. Very large folios had informal names such as "elephant folio" (about 58 cm tall) and "atlas folio".

Modern sizes

Modern paperbacks are produced to standard trade sizes rather than by folding terminology: mass-market paperbacks are about 4.25 × 7 inches, trade paperbacks commonly 5.5 × 8.5 or 6 × 9 inches, and the standard British paperback formats are designated A (110 × 178 mm), B (129 × 198 mm), and C (135 × 216 mm). Libraries record book sizes in cataloguing metadata, traditionally in centimetres of height.
