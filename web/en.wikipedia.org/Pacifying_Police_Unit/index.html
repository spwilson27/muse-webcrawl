Pacifying Police Unit

Portuguese nameUnidade de Polícia Pacificadora (UPP)
Established2008
LocationRio de Janeiro, Brazil
Parent agencyMilitary Police of Rio de Janeiro State

The Pacifying Police Unit (Portuguese: Unidade de Polícia Pacificadora, UPP) is a law enforcement and social services programme of the Military Police of Rio de Janeiro State, Brazil, begun in 2008. Its aim is to reclaim territories — mainly favelas — controlled by gangs of drug traffickers, replacing armed confrontations with permanent community policing.

Model

The programme typically proceeds in phases: a tactical intervention by special police battalions (BOPE) to clear armed groups, followed by the installation of a permanent UPP station staffed by newly trained officers, and the arrival of municipal services under the "UPP Social" initiative. The first UPP was installed in the Santa Marta favela in December 2008.

Development and criticism

By the mid-2010s nearly forty UPPs covered more than a million residents, and the programme was credited with sharp reductions in homicides in pacified areas ahead of the 2014 World Cup and 2016 Olympics. However, the programme faced accusations of police abuses — notably the 2013 disappearance of bricklayer Amarildo de Souza in Rocinha — and suffered from funding shortages, renewed gang violence, and erosion of public trust after 2016.
