Hamaguchi Osachi

Hamaguchi Osachi (Japanese: 濱口 雄幸, also read Hamaguchi Yūkō; 1 April 1870 – 26 August 1931) was a Japanese politician who served as Prime Minister of Japan from 1929 to 1931. A leading figure of the Rikken Minseitō party, he was nicknamed the "Lion Prime Minister" (Raion Saishō) for his leonine appearance and dignified bearing.

Career

Born in Kōchi Prefecture, Hamaguchi graduated from the law faculty of Tokyo Imperial University and entered the Ministry of Finance, rising to vice-minister. He entered the Diet in 1915 and held cabinet posts including Minister of Finance and Minister of Home Affairs. He became president of the Minseitō in 1927 and prime minister on 2 July 1929, succeeding Tanaka Giichi.

Premiership

Hamaguchi's government pursued fiscal retrenchment under Finance Minister Inoue Junnosuke, returned Japan to the gold standard at the old parity in January 1930 — a move that deepened the impact of the worldwide Great Depression on Japan — and ratified the London Naval Treaty of 1930 limiting naval armaments, over the objections of the naval general staff, who accused the government of infringing the right of supreme command. His administration also supported universal male suffrage and sought women's suffrage legislation, which passed the lower house but failed in the House of Peers.

Assassination attempt and death

On 14 November 1930 Hamaguchi was shot at Tokyo Station by Tomeo Sagoya, a member of an ultranationalist society angered by the naval treaty and the government's policies. Hamaguchi survived initially and briefly resumed his duties, but his wounds were severe; he resigned in April 1931 and died on 26 August 1931. His death, and the political violence of the period, marked the weakening of party government in pre-war Japan.
