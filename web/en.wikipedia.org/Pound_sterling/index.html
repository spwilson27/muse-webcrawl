The pound sterling (symbol: £; ISO code: GBP) is the official currency of the United Kingdom, Jersey, Guernsey, the Isle of Man and several British Overseas Territories. It is the oldest currency in continuous use, with origins in the Anglo-Saxon pound of silver from which it takes its name — a pound weight of sterling silver.

Sterling is the world's fourth-most-traded currency in the foreign exchange market, after the US dollar, the euro and the Japanese yen, and it forms part of the basket of the IMF's Special Drawing Rights.

History

The pound began as a unit of account in Anglo-Saxon England and was formalised after the Norman Conquest. For most of its history it was divided into 20 shillings and 240 pence; decimalisation in 1971 replaced this with 100 (new) pence. Britain joined the gold standard in the nineteenth century, left it in 1931, and pegged the pound within Bretton Woods after the Second World War. Sterling floated in the 1970s, and the United Kingdom famously withdrew from the European Exchange Rate Mechanism on "Black Wednesday" in September 1992.

Banknotes and coins

Bank of England notes are issued for England and Wales, while commercial banks in Scotland and Northern Ireland issue their own sterling notes. Coins from 1p to £2 are struck by the Royal Mint, and recent decades have seen a changeover to polymer banknotes bearing portraits including Winston Churchill, Jane Austen, J. M. W. Turner and Alan Turing.
