Diocesan synod

A diocesan synod is a meeting of a bishop with the priests and other faithful of his diocese, convened to discuss matters of pastoral governance. In the Roman Catholic Church, diocesan synods are governed by canons 460–468 of the 1983 Code of Canon Law. In the Church of England, diocesan synods are elected governing bodies for each diocese.

A diocesan synod brings together clergy and laity of a diocese.

Catholic practice

In Catholic usage, the diocesan bishop alone convokes, presides over, and concludes the synod, and he is its sole legislator; the other members have only a consultative voice. Members include auxiliary bishops, vicars, cathedral chapter members, priests, and lay faithful chosen according to diocesan statutes. Synods were historically held frequently—some councils required them annually—but in modern times they are often occasional major events, such as archdiocesan synods held to plan pastoral renewal.

Anglican practice

In the Church of England, each diocese has a diocesan synod composed of a house of bishops, a house of clergy, and a house of laity, elected from deanery synods. It considers matters concerning the Church of England in the diocese, advises the bishop, and approves the diocesan budget. Diocesan synods were established in their modern form following the Synodical Government Measure 1969.
