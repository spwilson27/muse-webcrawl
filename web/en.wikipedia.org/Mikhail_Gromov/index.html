Mikhail Leonidovich Gromov (born 23 December 1943) is a Russian-French mathematician known for his influential work in geometry, analysis and group theory. He introduced or reshaped several fundamental concepts of modern geometry, including metric geometry, symplectic geometry and geometric group theory.

Biography

Gromov was born in Boksitogorsk, near Leningrad (now Saint Petersburg). He studied mathematics at Leningrad State University, receiving his doctorate in 1973 under Vladimir Rokhlin. After teaching in Leningrad and at Stony Brook University in New York, he moved to France in 1982, joining the Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques (IHÉS) in Bures-sur-Yvette, where he has remained a permanent professor. He also taught at the Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences in New York.

Work

Gromov's 1969 thesis introduced the h-principle, a general method for solving partial differential relations that underlies much of modern differential topology. His notion of Gromov–Hausdorff convergence gave a way to compare metric spaces and compactness criteria fundamental to Riemannian geometry. In symplectic geometry, his 1985 paper on pseudoholomorphic curves founded the study of Gromov–Witten invariants. He also created geometric group theory through his work on hyperbolic groups, and proved the polynomial growth theorem classifying groups of polynomial growth.

Awards

Gromov received the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 1993 and the Abel Prize in 2009 "for his revolutionary contributions to geometry." He is a member of the French Academy of Sciences and a foreign member of several other academies.
