Kukulkan

Kukulkan (also spelled K'uk'ulkan, Kukulkán or Gukumatz in K'iche') is the feathered serpent deity of Maya mythology, closely related to the Aztec god Quetzalcoatl.

Mythology and worship

The feathered serpent appears in Mesoamerican religion from early times — at Teotihuacan and among the Maya at sites such as Yaxha and Chichen Itza. Under Toltec influence, Kukulkan's cult grew prominent in Yucatán; Chichen Itza's great pyramid El Castillo is aligned so that at the equinoxes shadows form a serpent descending its staircase, ending at carved serpent heads.

Later Maya tradition also used Kukulkan as the name of a semi-legendary ruler or priest associated with the founding or refounding of cities. In the Books of Chilam Balam, Kukulkan is a culture hero who brought law and calendrical knowledge.
