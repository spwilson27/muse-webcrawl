Smectite

Smectite is a group of phyllosilicate clay minerals characterised by a three-layer (2:1) structure and the ability to swell as water molecules and exchangeable cations enter the interlayer space. Important members include montmorillonite, bentonite (a rock composed largely of montmorillonite), saponite, hectorite and nontronite.

Properties

Smectites have very high cation-exchange capacity and specific surface area, and expand markedly on wetting, which makes them both industrially valuable and geologically significant: smectite-rich shales can cause slope instability and drilling problems, and the transformation of smectite to illite during burial is an important reaction in sedimentary basins.

Uses

Uses include drilling muds, foundry sands, cat litter, sealants and liners, pharmaceuticals and cosmetics, and as adsorbents. Montmorillonite is also studied as a catalyst and as a mineral that may have played a role in prebiotic chemistry.
