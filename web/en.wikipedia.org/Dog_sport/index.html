Dog sport

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dog sports are competitive sports involving dogs and their handlers. Many dog sports began as tests of a dog's working abilities — herding, hunting, guarding, or hauling — and have developed into organized competitions with governing bodies, judging standards, and championships. Participation ranges from local club events to national and world championships.

Popular dog sports

  • Dog agility — dogs race through an obstacle course of jumps, tunnels, weave poles, and seesaws against the clock.
  • Obedience — teams are judged on precise performance of exercises such as heeling, recalls, and retrieves.
  • Flyball — relay teams of four dogs race over hurdles, trigger a ball launcher, catch the ball, and return.
  • Herding trials — herding breeds move livestock through a course under handler direction.
  • Dock jumping — dogs leap from a dock into water for distance or height.
  • Disc dog — dogs catch flying discs in distance and choreographed freestyle events.
  • Sled dog racing and bikejoring — dogs pull sleds, carts, or bicycles.
  • Nose work / scent work — dogs locate hidden target odors.
  • Rally obedience — teams navigate a course of obedience exercise signs.

History

Formal dog competitions trace to nineteenth-century sheepdog trials and conformation shows. Agility, invented as a demonstration at Crufts in 1978, grew rapidly into one of the most popular dog sports worldwide. Modern sports emphasize dog welfare and open most events to mixed-breed as well as purebred dogs.
