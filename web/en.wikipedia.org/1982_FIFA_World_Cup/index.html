The 1982 FIFA World Cup was the twelfth FIFA World Cup, held in Spain from 13 June to 11 July 1982. The tournament was won by Italy, who defeated West Germany 3–1 in the final at the Santiago Bernabéu Stadium in Madrid. It was Italy's third World Cup title and their first since 1938.

The 1982 tournament was the first World Cup to feature 24 teams, expanded from the 16 of previous editions, allowing greater representation for Africa and Asia. It also introduced the penalty shoot-out as a tiebreaker in knockout matches, first used in the semi-final between West Germany and France — widely considered one of the greatest matches in World Cup history, which West Germany won on penalties after a 3–3 draw.

Italy's run

Italy began unimpressively, drawing all three first-round group matches and barely advancing ahead of Cameroon. In the second group stage they produced two famous victories: a 2–1 win over defending champions Argentina and a 3–2 win over favourites Brazil, in which Paolo Rossi scored a hat-trick. Rossi, who had only recently returned from a two-year suspension arising from the Totonero betting scandal, scored twice more in the semi-final against Poland and opened the scoring in the final, finishing as the tournament's top scorer and best player.

Controversies

The tournament is also remembered for the "Disgrace of Gijón", in which West Germany's 1–0 win over Austria in the final group match eliminated Algeria and was widely believed to have been passively arranged to suit both European teams. The scandal led FIFA to schedule all final group matches simultaneously in future tournaments. In the semi-final, German goalkeeper Harald Schumacher's violent collision with French defender Patrick Battiston, which went unpunished, also caused lasting outrage.

Legacy

The Brazil team of Zico, Sócrates, Falcão and Éder — eliminated by Italy — is often regarded as the greatest side never to win the World Cup, and the 1982 tournament is frequently ranked among the best World Cups ever held.
