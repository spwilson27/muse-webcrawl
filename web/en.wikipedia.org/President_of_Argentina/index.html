President of Argentina

President of the Argentine Republic
Residence: Casa Rosada (official office); Quinta de Olivos (residence)
Term length: Four years, renewable once consecutively
First holder: Bernardino Rivadavia (1826)

The President of Argentina (Spanish: Presidente de Argentina), officially known as the President of the Argentine Republic, is both head of state and head of government of Argentina. Under the national constitution, the president is also the commander-in-chief of the armed forces.

Election and term

The president is directly elected by popular vote for a four-year term and may be re-elected for one consecutive additional term; after an interval of one term, a former president may run again. Elections use a modified two-round system: a candidate can win in the first round with at least 45 percent of the vote, or 40 percent with a lead of at least ten percentage points over the runner-up. Argentina's first president is generally considered to be Bernardino Rivadavia, who held the office in 1826–1827, though the modern republic dates from the Constitution of 1853. The presidential seat is the Casa Rosada in Buenos Aires, and the official residence is the Quinta de Olivos.

Powers

The president appoints ministers and federal judges (the latter with Senate agreement), conducts foreign policy, issues decrees, and may declare a state of siege under conditions set by the constitution. The 1994 constitutional reform shortened the presidential term from six years to four, created the office of Chief of the Cabinet of Ministers, and introduced the ballotage system.

History

Argentina's presidency has been interrupted several times by military coups in the twentieth century (1930, 1943, 1955, 1962, 1966 and 1976). Notable presidents include Juan Domingo Perón, founder of Peronism; Raúl Alfonsín, who led the return to democracy in 1983; Carlos Menem; Néstor Kirchner and Cristina Fernández de Kirchner; Mauricio Macri; Alberto Fernández; and Javier Milei, who took office in December 2023.
