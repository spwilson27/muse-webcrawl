Apostolic Fathers

The Apostolic Fathers are a group of early Christian authors and texts from the late first and early second centuries CE, traditionally regarded as standing close to the apostles in time and teaching. Their writings bridge the New Testament era and the later Church Fathers, and are a key source for the organisation and beliefs of the post-apostolic church.

The corpus

The collection usually includes the First and Second Epistles of Clement, the letters of Ignatius of Antioch, the letter of Polycarp to the Philippians and the account of his martyrdom, the Didache, the Epistle of Barnabas, the Shepherd of Hermas, the Epistle to Diognetus, and fragments of Papias and Quadratus.

Importance

These texts show the early development of church offices (bishops, presbyters and deacons), the celebration of the Eucharist, and the emergence of doctrines such as the resurrection of the flesh. Some, like the Shepherd of Hermas and the Didache, were treated as scripture by parts of the early church before the canon was settled.
