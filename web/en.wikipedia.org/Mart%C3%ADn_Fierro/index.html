Martín Fierro, also known as El Gaucho Martín Fierro, is a 2,316-line epic poem by the Argentine writer José Hernández, first published in 1872. It is widely considered the national poem of Argentina and the masterpiece of gaucho literature, written in the voice of a gaucho singer narrating his life in verse.

Plot

Martín Fierro, a gaucho living peacefully with his family on the pampas, is conscripted into the frontier militia and sent to fight indigenous peoples. Abused and unpaid, he deserts, returns to find his home destroyed and his family gone, and becomes an outlaw. In the second part, La vuelta de Martín Fierro (1879), he is reunited with his sons and delivers his accumulated wisdom to them before they part.

Significance

The poem protested the treatment of gauchos by the Argentine state during the era of national consolidation and became a cornerstone of Argentine national identity, famously argued by Leopoldo Lugones in 1913 to be the country's foundational epic.
