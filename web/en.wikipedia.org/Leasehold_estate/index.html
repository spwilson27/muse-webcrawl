Leasehold estate

A leasehold estate is an ownership interest in land or property in which a lessee (tenant) holds rights to possess the property for a period of time under a lease from a lessor (landlord), who retains the freehold or reversion. Leasehold is a form of land tenure common in England and Wales, the Commonwealth, and parts of the United States, used for flats, houses on long leases, and commercial premises.

Common types include the estate for years (fixed term), the periodic tenancy (renewing period to period), the tenancy at will, and the tenancy at sufferance. Long residential leases often require payment of ground rent and service charges, and give leaseholders statutory rights to extend the lease or buy the freehold (enfranchisement) in some jurisdictions.

Unlike freehold, leasehold ownership diminishes as the term runs down, which affects value, mortgageability and sale, and has prompted repeated leasehold reform legislation.
