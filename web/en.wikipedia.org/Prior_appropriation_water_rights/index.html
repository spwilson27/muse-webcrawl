Prior appropriation water rights

Prior appropriation water rights, sometimes known as the "Colorado Doctrine", is a legal doctrine of water law that allocates water rights according to priority of appropriation: "first in time, first in right". It governs water use in most of the arid western United States, in contrast to the riparian rights system of the water-rich east.

Principles

Under prior appropriation, a water right is established by diverting water and putting it to beneficial use; priority dates from the appropriation. In times of shortage, senior right holders are entitled to their full allocation before junior holders receive any. Rights can be lost through abandonment or non-use, and — unlike riparian rights — they need not be tied to ownership of land adjacent to the water.

Origins

The doctrine grew out of customs in California gold-mining camps during the Gold Rush and was adopted in decisions such as Irwin v. Phillips (1855) and Coffin v. Left Hand Ditch Co. (1882) in Colorado.
