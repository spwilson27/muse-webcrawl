The Golden Lion (Italian: Leone d'Oro) is the highest prize given to a film at the Venice Film Festival. The prize was introduced in 1949 by the organizing committee and is now regarded as one of the film industry's most distinguished awards. Until 1942, the highest honour was the Coppa Mussolini; from 1946 to 1948 the top award was the International Grand Prize of Venice, before the Golden Lion — named for the winged lion of Saint Mark, the symbol of Venice — was instituted.

The prize is awarded by the festival's international jury to the best film in the main competition. A Silver Lion is given for best direction, and an additional Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement honours career contributions.

History

The Venice Film Festival, founded in 1932, is the oldest film festival in the world and part of the Venice Biennale. The Golden Lion was not awarded between 1969 and 1979, when the festival was reorganised as a non-competitive event amid political turmoil, before competition was restored in 1980.

Notable winners

Golden Lion-winning films include Rashomon (1951), Ordet (1955), Last Year at Marienbad (1961), Belle de Jour (1967), Atlantic City (1980), The Green Ray (1986), Au revoir les enfants (1987), Short Cuts (1993), Before the Rain (1994), Vera Drake (2004), Brokeback Mountain (2005), Still Life (2006), The Wrestler (2008), Faust (2011), The Shape of Water (2017), Roma (2018), Joker (2019), Nomadland (2020), and Poor Things (2023).
