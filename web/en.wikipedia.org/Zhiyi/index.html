Zhiyi

Zhiyi (538–597 AD), also romanized as Chih-i, was a Chinese Buddhist monk and philosopher who is traditionally credited as the founder of the Tiantai school of Buddhism. He systematized the teachings of the Lotus Sutra into a comprehensive doctrinal framework that profoundly influenced East Asian Buddhism.

Born538, Chen dynasty China
Died597, Mount Tiantai
SchoolTiantai
Major worksMohe Zhiguan; Fahua Xuanyi; Fahua Wenju

Life

Ordained after losing his parents amid dynastic warfare, Zhiyi studied under the meditation master Huisi on Mount Daxian. In 575 he withdrew to Mount Tiantai in present-day Zhejiang, where he attracted imperial patronage from both the Chen and the succeeding Sui dynasties and gathered a large community of disciples.

Teachings

Zhiyi's synthesis includes the classification of the Buddha's teachings into five periods and eight teachings, the doctrine of "three thousand realms in a single moment of life" (ichinen sanzen), and the threefold truth of emptiness, provisional existence, and the middle. His Mohe Zhiguan ("Great Calming and Contemplation") remains a foundational manual of meditation practice.
