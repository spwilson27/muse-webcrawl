First Canadian Army

The First Canadian Army (French: 1re Armée canadienne) was a field army and a formation of the Canadian Army formed in April 1942 in the United Kingdom under British command. It was the largest Canadian formation of the Second World War, and at various times included British, Polish, American, Dutch and Belgian troops under its command in addition to its two Canadian corps.

Operations

The army became operational in Normandy in July 1944 under General Harry Crerar. It fought in the Battle of Normandy, closing the Falaise pocket, and then undertook the grinding task of clearing the Channel ports and the Scheldt estuary — essential to opening the port of Antwerp for Allied supply. In February and March 1945 it conducted Operation Veritable, advancing through the Reichswald into Germany as part of the 21st Army Group, and in April 1945 its corps liberated much of the northern Netherlands.

Composition and legacy

Its principal formations were I Canadian Corps (transferred from Italy in 1945) and II Canadian Corps. By the spring of 1945 the First Canadian Army was one of the largest multinational armies ever commanded by a Canadian. It was disbanded in 1945 following the end of the war in Europe.
