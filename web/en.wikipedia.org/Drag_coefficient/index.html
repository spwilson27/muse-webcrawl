Drag coefficient

Drag coefficients of common shapes.

In fluid dynamics, the drag coefficient (commonly denoted cd, cx or cw) is a dimensionless quantity used to quantify the drag or resistance of an object moving through a fluid such as air or water. It appears in the drag equation: F = ½ρv²cdA, where ρ is fluid density, v is velocity relative to the fluid, and A is a reference area.

Typical values

A smooth sphere has a drag coefficient of about 0.47, a flat plate perpendicular to the flow about 1.28, and a streamlined teardrop body as low as 0.04. Modern passenger cars range from roughly 0.25 to 0.35. The value depends on shape, surface roughness, orientation and Reynolds number; for spheres it drops sharply at the "drag crisis" when the boundary layer becomes turbulent.

Components

Total drag coefficient combines friction (skin) drag, pressure (form) drag and, for lifting bodies, induced drag, making it a central parameter in aerodynamics, vehicle design and sports engineering.
