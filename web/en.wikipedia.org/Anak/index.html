Anak

Anak is a figure featured in a number of passages of the Hebrew Bible. According to the Book of Numbers, he was a forefather of the Anakim, a group of giants or mighty warriors who inhabited the land of Canaan, particularly around Hebron.

Biblical account

In Numbers 13, the spies sent by Moses report that the Anakim, descended from Anak, dwell in the hill country and that "we seemed to ourselves like grasshoppers" by comparison. Joshua 11 recounts that Joshua cut off the Anakim from the hill country, leaving remnants only in Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod. Caleb is said to have driven the three sons of Anak — Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai — from Hebron.

Interpretations

The name Anak has been connected with the Hebrew word for "neck" or "necklace". Scholars have debated whether the Anakim preserve a memory of an actual people of the southern Levant or are purely legendary. In later tradition the Anakim are grouped with the Rephaim and Nephilim among the giants of the ancient world.
