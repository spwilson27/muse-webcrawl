Newar people

RegionsKathmandu Valley, Nepal; diaspora worldwide
LanguageNepal Bhasa (Newar), Nepali
ReligionsHinduism, Buddhism (a distinctive blend)
PopulationAbout 1.3 million (Nepal census)

The Newar people (also spelled Newa or Newah) are the historical inhabitants of the Kathmandu Valley and its surrounding areas in Nepal. They are known for their rich cultural heritage — the art, architecture, festivals, and cuisine of the valley are largely Newar creations — and their language, Nepal Bhasa, a Tibeto-Burman language traditionally written in several scripts.

Culture and religion

Newar society uniquely blends Hinduism and Mahayana–Vajrayana Buddhism, sometimes within the same family, and is organised into castes and community associations (guthi) that manage festivals, temples, and communal lands. The valley's seven UNESCO World Heritage monument zones — including the durbar squares of Kathmandu, Patan, and Bhaktapur — were built under Newar Malla kings, and the living goddess tradition of the Kumari is central to Newar religion.

History

The Newars created a sophisticated urban civilisation under the Malla dynasty (12th–18th centuries), trading with both India and Tibet. The valley was conquered by Prithvi Narayan Shah of Gorkha in 1768–69, after which the Newars' language and political position were marginalised, though their artistic and commercial prominence endured.

Festivals and cuisine

Newars celebrate more festivals than any other community in Nepal, including Indra Jatra, Bisket Jatra, and the month-long Jana Baha Dyah Jatra. Newar cuisine, famous for dishes such as chhoila, bara, and the beaten-rice set meal samay baji, has become synonymous with Kathmandu food culture.
