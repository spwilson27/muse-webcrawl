Medvednica

Medvednica (pronounced [mědʋednit͡sa]) is a mountain in central Croatia, just north of Zagreb, forming a natural border between the city and the Zagorje region. Its highest peak, Sljeme, rises to 1,033 metres (3,389 ft). The name derives from medvjed, the Croatian word for bear, reflecting the wildlife once common in its forests.

Geography and nature

The mountain is heavily forested, chiefly with beech, oak, chestnut and fir, and most of it is protected as the Medvednica Nature Park, established in 1981 and covering about 179 square kilometres. Notable sites include the Veternica cave, the medieval fortress of Medvedgrad, and the Zrinski silver mine. The mountain is seismically active: the strong Zagreb earthquake of 1880 had its epicentre in the Medvednica area, as did the damaging earthquake of March 2020.

Recreation

Medvednica is Zagreb's principal recreation area, with marked hiking trails, mountain lodges and, on Sljeme, the Sljeme transmitter tower and a ski area that has hosted FIS Alpine Ski World Cup slalom races (the Snow Queen Trophy). A cable car from the city to the summit, destroyed by an earlier accident and long out of service, was rebuilt and reopened in 2022.
