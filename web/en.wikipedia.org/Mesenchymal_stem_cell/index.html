Mesenchymal stem cell

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), also known as mesenchymal stromal cells, are multipotent stromal cells that can differentiate into a variety of cell types, including osteoblasts (bone cells), chondrocytes (cartilage cells), myocytes (muscle cells), and adipocytes (fat cells). They were first described in bone marrow by Alexander Friedenstein in the 1960s–70s.

Sources

MSCs can be isolated from many tissues, including bone marrow, adipose tissue, umbilical cord blood and tissue, placenta, dental pulp, and synovial fluid. Bone marrow and adipose tissue are the most commonly used clinical sources.

Defining criteria

The International Society for Cellular Therapy (2006) proposed minimal criteria: MSCs must be plastic-adherent in culture; must express CD105, CD73, and CD90 while lacking CD45, CD34, CD14/CD11b, CD79a/CD19, and HLA-DR; and must differentiate into osteoblasts, adipocytes, and chondroblasts in vitro.

Clinical use

Because of their immunomodulatory and trophic properties, MSCs have been investigated in hundreds of clinical trials for graft-versus-host disease, Crohn's disease, myocardial infarction, osteoarthritis, and autoimmune conditions. Remestemcel-L (Ryoncil) became the first MSC therapy approved by the US FDA, in December 2024, for pediatric steroid-refractory acute graft-versus-host disease. The exosomes and secretome of MSCs are also studied as cell-free therapies.
