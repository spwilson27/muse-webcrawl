Bran Castle

Bran Castle (Romanian: Castelul Bran; German: Schloss Bran; Hungarian: Törcsvári kastély) is a castle in Bran, 25 kilometres (16 mi) southwest of Brașov, Romania. It is one of the country's most popular tourist attractions, commonly marketed as "Dracula's Castle" for its association — largely a creation of tourism rather than history — with Bram Stoker's novel Dracula (1897) and with Vlad the Impaler. The fortress stands on the Transylvanian side of the historical border between Transylvania and Wallachia, guarding the Bran Pass through the Carpathians.

History

A wooden fortress was built on the site by the Teutonic Knights around 1212. The stone castle was erected by the Saxons of Kronstadt (Brașov), who received permission from Louis I of Hungary in 1377 to build a citadel defending the pass against the Ottoman Empire and controlling the trade route between Transylvania and Wallachia; construction was completed by 1388. The castle served as a customs post and fortress for centuries. Contrary to popular lore, Vlad III Dracula (Vlad the Impaler) probably never owned or lived in it, though he may have passed through the area, and the castle was briefly involved in his campaigns.

Royal residence

After Transylvania united with Romania in 1918, the citizens of Brașov offered the castle to Queen Marie of Romania in 1920. She made it a favourite summer residence and commissioned extensive renovations by Czech architect Karel Zdeněk Líman, adding romantic gardens, a tea house and other comforts while preserving the medieval fabric. Her daughter Princess Ileana inherited it in 1938 and ran a hospital there during the Second World War. The communist state seized the castle in 1948 and opened it as a museum in 1957.

Restitution and museum today

In 2006 the castle was legally returned to Princess Ileana's son, Dominic von Habsburg, and his sisters, and in 2009 they took full possession, continuing to operate it as a private museum — among the first properties restituted in Romania to be run this way. Exhibits include furniture and art collected by Queen Marie, Romanian folk costumes and ethnography, and, inevitably, displays on the Dracula legend, including a "torture chamber" exhibition. The castle draws roughly a million visitors a year, making it one of Romania's most visited landmarks.

The Dracula connection

Bram Stoker never visited Romania, and his description of Dracula's castle — perched above a gorge in the Borgo Pass region — does not closely match Bran. Historians link the castle's Dracula identity mainly to 1970s tourism marketing, after which it stuck: Bran's appearance, a romantic fortress on a rock, matches the popular image of the Count's lair better than any documented Vlad site such as Poenari Castle. The association is nonetheless commercially powerful, with Halloween events held annually at the castle.
