John Flamsteed

Born19 August 1646, Denby, Derbyshire, England
Died31 December 1719, Burstow, Surrey
Known forFirst Astronomer Royal; Historia Coelestis Britannica
FieldsAstronomy

John Flamsteed (19 August 1646 – 31 December 1719) was an English astronomer and the first Astronomer Royal. He catalogued over 3,000 stars and was responsible for the first recorded observation of Uranus, which he mistook for a fixed star. His star catalogue, Historia Coelestis Britannica, and his great star atlas, Atlas Coelestis, were published posthumously.

Early life

Flamsteed was born at Denby in Derbyshire and largely taught himself astronomy after ill health disrupted his schooling. He entered Jesus College, Cambridge, as a sizar in 1670 while continuing his observations. His prediction of eclipses and calculations of the position of planets drew the attention of Jonas Moore, who became his patron and brought him to the notice of King Charles II.

Astronomer Royal

In 1675 Charles II appointed Flamsteed his astronomical observator and founded the Royal Observatory at Greenwich, which Flamsteed directed from 1676 until his death. He supplied the observatory's instruments largely at his own expense and spent four decades making meticulous observations for a new star catalogue.

Disputes and legacy

Flamsteed's perfectionism clashed with Isaac Newton and Edmond Halley, who pressed for earlier publication of his data; a pirated edition of his observations appeared in 1712, copies of which Flamsteed later bought up and burned. His widow Margaret oversaw publication of the authorised catalogue in 1725. The numbers he assigned to stars, such as 61 Cygni, are still known as Flamsteed designations.
