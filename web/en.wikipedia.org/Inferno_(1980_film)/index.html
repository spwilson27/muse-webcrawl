Inferno (1980 film)

Inferno is a 1980 Italian supernatural horror film written and directed by Dario Argento. It is the second instalment in Argento's Three Mothers trilogy, following Suspiria (1977) and preceding Mother of Tears (2007). The film stars Irene Miracle, Leigh McCloskey, Eleonora Giorgi, Daria Nicolodi and Alida Valli.

Plot

The film concerns the Three Mothers, powerful witches — Mater Suspiriorum, Mater Tenebrarum and Mater Lachrymarum — who secretly rule the world from houses in Freiburg, New York City and Rome, as recorded in the alchemical book The Three Mothers by the architect Varelli. In New York, poetry student Rose Elliot reads the book and realizes her apartment building is one of the witches' houses. She writes to her brother Mark, a music student in Rome; after Rose is murdered, Mark travels to New York and uncovers the lair of Mater Tenebrarum, the youngest and most beautiful of the three, escaping as the building burns.

Production and style

Argento shot much of the film in Rome, standing in for New York. Cinematographer Romano Albani continued the bold, primary-colour lighting scheme of Suspiria, and Mario Bava — uncredited — contributed visual effects, including the design of some sequences. The score was composed by progressive rock musician Keith Emerson, a contrast with Goblin's music for the preceding film.

Reception

Released in Italy in February 1980, Inferno received a delayed and limited release in the United States through 20th Century Fox, which shelved it for several years. It was initially less acclaimed than Suspiria, but critical reputation grew substantially, and it is now regarded as one of Argento's most visually accomplished works and a key film of Italian horror.
