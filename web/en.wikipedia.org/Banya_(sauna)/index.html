Banya (sauna)

A banya (Russian: баня) is a traditional Russian steam bath, closely related to the Finnish sauna but characterised by higher humidity. The banya typically consists of a steam room heated by a wood-fired stove, a washing room and a rest room; bathers traditionally plunge into cold water or snow between sessions.

Practice

In the steam room, water is thrown onto hot stones to produce bursts of steam called par. Bathers wear felt hats to protect the head from the heat and massage each other with veniks — bundles of birch, oak or eucalyptus twigs — which are said to improve circulation and release aromatic oils. Sessions alternate with cooling, rest, and drinking tea or kvass.

Cultural significance

The banya has been central to Russian life for centuries, used for hygiene, healing, childbirth and socialising, and it features throughout Russian literature and proverb. Village banyas are usually small log buildings heated "in black" (without a chimney) or "in white"; grand urban bathhouses such as Moscow's Sanduny remain social institutions.
