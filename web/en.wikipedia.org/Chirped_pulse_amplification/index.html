Chirped pulse amplification

Chirped pulse amplification (CPA)
Invented: 1985, University of Rochester
Inventors: Donna Strickland and Gérard Mourou
Recognition: Nobel Prize in Physics 2018 (shared)

Chirped pulse amplification (CPA) is a technique for amplifying an ultrashort laser pulse to very high peak powers without destroying the amplifying medium. It enabled tabletop lasers reaching terawatt and petawatt peak powers.

Method

A short pulse is first stretched in time—chirped—by dispersing its frequency components, reducing its peak intensity. The stretched pulse is safely amplified, then recompressed with diffraction gratings to near its original duration, producing an enormous peak power.

Impact

Developed by Donna Strickland and Gérard Mourou in 1985, CPA revolutionised high-intensity laser physics, underpinning applications from LASIK eye surgery and micromachining to plasma physics and particle acceleration. Its inventors shared half of the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics.
