Eindhoven
Province: North Brabant
Country: Netherlands
Population: roughly 230,000 (city proper)
Known for: Philips, technology and design

Eindhoven is a city and municipality in the southern Netherlands, in the province of North Brabant. It is the fifth-largest city in the country and the centre of a major technology region sometimes called "Brainport". Eindhoven grew from a small market town into an industrial city largely through the electronics company Philips, founded there in 1891.

History

Eindhoven received city rights in 1232 from Duke Henry I of Brabant. For centuries it remained a modest town, repeatedly damaged in wars between Brabant and Guelders. Industrialization arrived in the 19th century with cigar and textile factories, but the decisive moment was the founding of Philips by Gerard Philips and his father Frederik, initially making incandescent lamps. The company grew into one of the world's largest electronics firms, earning Eindhoven the nickname "City of Light" (Lichtstad).

During the Second World War the city was bombed by both sides; it was liberated in September 1944 during Operation Market Garden.

Economy and education

Today Eindhoven is a hub for high-tech industry, home to companies such as ASML (headquartered in nearby Veldhoven), NXP Semiconductors, and DAF Trucks, and to the High Tech Campus Eindhoven. The Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e) and the Design Academy Eindhoven give the city a strong research and design profile; Dutch Design Week is held there annually.

Sport

The city is home to PSV Eindhoven, one of the Netherlands' most successful football clubs, playing at the Philips Stadion.
