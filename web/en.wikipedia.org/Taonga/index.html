Taonga

Taonga is a Māori-language word meaning "treasure" or "valued possession". In Māori culture, taonga include tangible objects such as heirloom carvings, greenstone (pounamu) ornaments, cloaks, and canoes (waka), as well as intangible treasures such as the Māori language (te reo Māori), traditional knowledge, and cultural practices.

Treaty of Waitangi

The word has significant legal weight in New Zealand: Article Two of the Treaty of Waitangi (1840) guarantees Māori "te tino rangatiratanga" over their lands, villages, and "o ratou taonga katoa" — all their treasures. This phrase has been central to Waitangi Tribunal claims concerning the protection of te reo Māori, spectrum rights, and indigenous flora and fauna (notably in the Wai 262 claim).

Heritage

Institutions such as the Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa hold taonga in trust, and repatriation of Māori remains and objects from overseas museums is guided by their status as taonga.
