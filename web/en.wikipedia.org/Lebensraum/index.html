Lebensraum

Lebensraum (German for "living space") is a geopolitical concept of expansionism, most notoriously associated with Nazi Germany. It held that Germany needed additional territory, principally in Eastern Europe, for settlement and resources, to secure the future of the German people.

Origins

The term was popularised in the late 19th and early 20th centuries by geographers such as Friedrich Ratzel, who used it more generally for the geographic habitat of an organism or people. Karl Haushofer and others developed related geopolitical ideas after the First World War.

Nazi ideology

Adolf Hitler set out the idea extensively in Mein Kampf (1925–26). In Nazi policy it became a justification for the invasion of Poland and the Soviet Union, the Generalplan Ost, mass expulsion, enslavement and genocide of Slavic and Jewish populations, and German colonisation of conquered lands.

Aftermath

After 1945 the concept was discredited and is studied chiefly as a central element of Nazi expansionist and racial ideology.
