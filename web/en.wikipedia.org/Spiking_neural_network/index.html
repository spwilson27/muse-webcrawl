Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are artificial neural networks that mimic natural neural networks more closely than conventional (second-generation) models. Instead of continuous activation values, their neurons communicate by discrete events called spikes, and information is encoded in spike timing as well as rate.

Neuron models

The simplest widely used model is the leaky integrate-and-fire neuron, which integrates incoming current until its membrane potential crosses a threshold, fires a spike, and resets. More biophysically detailed models include the Hodgkin–Huxley equations, which describe ion-channel dynamics, and the Izhikevich model, which balances realism and efficiency.

Encoding and learning

Information can be encoded by firing rates, by precise spike times, latency or population patterns. The best-known learning rule, spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP), strengthens or weakens synapses according to the relative timing of pre- and post-synaptic spikes. Because spikes are non-differentiable, supervised training often uses surrogate-gradient methods or converts trained conventional networks into spiking ones.

Applications

SNNs are event-driven and therefore highly energy-efficient on neuromorphic hardware, making them attractive for always-on sensing, robotics, and edge computing, as well as for modelling brain function in neuroscience.
