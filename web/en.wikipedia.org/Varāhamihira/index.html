Varāhamihira (c. 505 – 587 CE), also called Varāha or Mihira, was an Indian astrologer-astronomer who lived in Ujjain in present-day Madhya Pradesh. He is considered one of the "nine jewels" (navaratnas) associated with the court of the legendary king Vikramaditya.

Works

His most celebrated work is the Pañcasiddhāntikā ("Treatise on the Five Astronomical Canons", c. 575 CE), which summarizes five earlier astronomical systems — the Surya, Romaka, Paulisa, Vasistha, and Paitamaha siddhantas — preserving material that would otherwise be lost. The Bṛhat Saṃhitā is an encyclopedic work covering astrology, planetary motions, rainfall, architecture, agriculture, gems, and omens. He also wrote the Bṛhajjātaka on horoscopy and works on muhūrta (electional astrology).

Contributions

Varāhamihira improved the accuracy of sine tables, gave trigonometric formulas, and his presentation of Pascal's triangle-like combinatorial arrangements predates later treatments. His works influenced Indian astronomy for centuries and were transmitted to the Islamic world.
