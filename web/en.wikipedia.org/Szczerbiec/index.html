Szczerbiec

Szczerbiec (Polish for “the jagged/notched one”) is the ceremonial coronation sword of the kings of Poland. It is the only surviving piece of the Polish crown jewels and the most precious relic of the medieval Polish monarchy, today kept in the Crown Treasury of Wawel Castle in Kraków.

Legend and actual origins

According to the chronicler Gallus Anonymous and later tradition, King Bolesław I the Brave chipped the blade in 1018 when he struck the Golden Gate of Kyiv with it upon capturing the city, giving the sword its notch and its name; the story is the source of the sword's national symbolism. The actual weapon, however, is a type XIIa arming sword most likely made in the late 13th century, probably for one of the dukes struggling to reunify Poland. Its hilt is decorated with niello plaques bearing evangelist symbols and inscriptions in Hebrew letters and Latin.

History

Szczerbiec was used in coronations from 1320 (Władysław I Łokietek) through the last coronation of Stanisław August Poniatowski in 1764. After the Third Partition of Poland in 1795 it was looted by Prussian troops along with the rest of the treasury, changed hands among collectors in Germany and Russia, and was recovered for Poland under the Treaty of Riga in 1921. Evacuated in 1939, it spent the war in Canada and returned to Wawel in 1959. In modern Poland its image and silhouette remain potent national symbols.
