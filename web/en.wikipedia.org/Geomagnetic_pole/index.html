Geomagnetic pole

A geomagnetic pole is one of the two points on Earth's surface where a fitted magnetic dipole, aligned with Earth's magnetic field, intersects the surface. The geomagnetic north pole lies near northwest Greenland (in recent epochs near Ellesmere Island, Canada), while the geomagnetic south pole lies off the coast of Antarctica.

Distinction from magnetic poles

Geomagnetic poles are model-derived, based on the dipole term of spherical harmonic models such as the International Geomagnetic Reference Field (IGRF). They differ from the dip poles, where the observed field is actually vertical and a compass needle would dip straight down. Because the field is not a perfect dipole, the two geomagnetic poles are not exactly antipodal.

Movement

The geomagnetic poles wander over time due to changes in Earth's core flow. The model north geomagnetic pole has been moving from Canada toward Siberia along with the magnetic north dip pole.
