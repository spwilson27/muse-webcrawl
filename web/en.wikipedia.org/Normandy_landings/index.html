The Normandy landings were the landing operations and associated airborne operations on Tuesday, 6 June 1944 of the Allied invasion of Normandy in Operation Overlord during the Second World War. Codenamed Operation Neptune and often referred to as D-Day), it was the largest seaborne invasion in history. The operation began the liberation of France (and later western Europe) and laid the foundations for the Allied victory on the Western Front.

Planning and deception

Planning began in 1943; the Supreme Commander was General Dwight D. Eisenhower, with General Bernard Montgomery commanding ground forces. The Allies chose Normandy over the Pas-de-Calais and supported the choice with an elaborate deception, Operation Bodyguard (including the fictitious First US Army Group under Patton), which convinced German commanders that the main blow would fall at Calais. Weather forecasts forced a 24-hour postponement from 5 June; Eisenhower gave the go-ahead for 6 June in a narrow window of marginal weather.

The landings

Shortly after midnight, three airborne divisions (the US 82nd and 101st and the British 6th) dropped inland to secure the flanks. From 06:30, five seaborne divisions landed on beaches codenamed Utah and Omaha (American), Gold and Sword (British), and Juno (Canadian). Resistance varied: at Utah losses were light, while at Omaha, defended by the experienced 352nd Infantry Division on bluffs above the shore, US forces suffered the day's heaviest casualties before breaking inland. By nightfall all five beachheads were held, though none of the first-day objectives inland (including Caen and Carentan) had been fully reached.

Aftermath

Within days the beachheads were linked and artificial Mulberry harbours were operating; by the end of June over 850,000 men were ashore. The Battle of Normandy that followed — through the bocage, the fall of Cherbourg, Operation Goodwood and the Falaise pocket — culminated in the liberation of Paris on 25 August 1944. D-Day remains the archetypal amphibious operation and is commemorated annually in Normandy and the Allied nations.
