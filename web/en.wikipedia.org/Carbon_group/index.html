Carbon group

The carbon group is group 14 of the periodic table, consisting of carbon (C), silicon (Si), germanium (Ge), tin (Sn), lead (Pb), and flerovium (Fl). The elements share four valence electrons (an ns²np² configuration) and the group is sometimes called the crystallogens or, in older literature, the tetrels.

Characteristics

The group shows a clear transition in character down the column: carbon is a nonmetal, silicon and germanium are metalloids important as semiconductors, and tin and lead are metals. Carbon is unique in forming strong multiple bonds and long chains (catenation), the basis of organic chemistry and all known life. With increasing atomic size, the inert-pair effect makes the +2 oxidation state increasingly favoured over +4 for the heavier elements.

Occurrence and uses

Carbon occurs as diamond and graphite and in all living matter. Silicon, the second most abundant element in Earth's crust, is the basis of most rocks, sand, and the semiconductor industry. Germanium is used in fibre optics and electronics. Tin is used in solder and coatings, and lead in batteries and radiation shielding, though its toxicity limits many former uses. Flerovium is a synthetic, highly radioactive element known only from a few atoms.
