Colon (anatomy)

The colon is the longest part of the large intestine in mammals. It absorbs water and electrolytes from digested food, compacts the remaining material into feces, and hosts a dense community of gut bacteria that ferment fibre and produce vitamins such as vitamin K and some B vitamins.

Part ofLarge intestine
SectionsAscending, transverse, descending, sigmoid
LengthAbout 1.5 m in adults
FunctionWater absorption, feces formation

Anatomy

The colon runs from the cecum upward on the right side of the abdomen (ascending colon), across the abdomen (transverse colon), down the left side (descending colon), and into the pelvis as the S-shaped sigmoid colon, which connects to the rectum. Its wall has longitudinal muscle gathered into three bands, the taeniae coli, which pucker the colon into pouches called haustra.

Health

Common conditions affecting the colon include colorectal cancer — screened for by colonoscopy — inflammatory bowel diseases such as ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease, diverticulitis, and irritable bowel syndrome. Surgical removal of part or all of the colon is called a colectomy.
