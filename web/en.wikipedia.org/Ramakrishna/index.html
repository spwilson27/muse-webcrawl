Ramakrishna, the 19th-century Bengali mystic of Dakshineswar.

Ramakrishna Paramahamsa (born Gadadhar Chattopadhyay; 18 February 1836 – 16 August 1886) was an Indian Hindu mystic and saint of 19th-century Bengal. As priest of the Dakshineswar Kali Temple near Calcutta, he pursued intense devotion to the goddess Kali and, through successive spiritual disciplines, reported realising God through Hindu paths as well as through Islam and Christianity, concluding that all religions lead to the same goal.

Life and teaching

Ramakrishna taught mainly through parables, songs and conversation, recorded by his disciple Mahendranath Gupta in The Gospel of Sri Ramakrishna. His central messages were the divinity of the soul, the harmony of religions, and the primacy of God-realisation over ritual and learning. His wife, Sarada Devi, is venerated as the Holy Mother. His monastic disciples, led by Swami Vivekananda, founded the Ramakrishna Math and Mission after his death, spreading his teaching worldwide and combining spiritual practice with social service.

Legacy

Ramakrishna profoundly influenced modern Hindu thought and Western interest in Vedanta. The Ramakrishna movement's centres, schools, hospitals and relief work operate across India and abroad, and his life is a central subject of study in the comparative history of mysticism.
