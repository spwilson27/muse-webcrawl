Basic fighter maneuvers

Basic fighter maneuvers (BFM)
TypeAerial combat tactics
ContextDogfighting / within-visual-range combat
Key conceptsEnergy management, turn rate and radius, angles

Basic fighter maneuvers (BFM) are tactical movements performed by fighter aircraft during air combat maneuvering — the art of dogfighting — in order to gain a positional advantage over an opponent, usually to bring weapons to bear or to escape an attacker's weapons envelope.

Principles

BFM rests on the management of an aircraft's energy (airspeed and altitude), its turn rate and turn radius, and the geometry between the two aircraft: nose position, angles off the tail, closure rate, and range. Pilots distinguish between one-circle and two-circle fights depending on whether the opponents turn toward or away from each other after the merge.

Offensive maneuvers

Classic offensive maneuvers include the lag pursuit roll, the high yo-yo (trading speed for altitude to avoid overshooting), the low yo-yo (using gravity to gain speed and close), lead and pure pursuit curves, and the barrel roll attack. The goal is to arrive in the control zone behind the defender within weapons range without excessive closure.

Defensive maneuvers

A defender may use a break turn into the attacker, the scissors (a series of turn reversals to force an overshoot), the guns defense maneuver, jinking, and a last-ditch maneuver combining descent and maximum performance turning. Defensive BFM aims to defeat the attacker's firing solution and neutralize the positional disadvantage.
