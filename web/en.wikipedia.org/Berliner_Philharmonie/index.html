The Berliner Philharmonie is a concert hall in Berlin, Germany, and the home of the Berlin Philharmonic orchestra. Designed by the architect Hans Scharoun and opened in 1963, the building — with its tent-like, golden, asymmetrical form — is a landmark of organic architecture and one of the most influential concert halls of the twentieth century.

Architecture and acoustics

The main hall pioneered the "vineyard" layout: the orchestra sits in the centre, surrounded by terraced blocks of seats rising on all sides, so that no listener is far from the stage and the audience becomes part of the performance space. The hall's acoustics, refined by Lothar Cremer, are widely praised. A smaller chamber music hall (Kammermusiksaal), designed by Edgar Wisniewski, was added in 1987.

History

The Philharmonie's predecessor, the old Philharmonie on Bernburger Straße, was destroyed by bombing in 1944. The new hall formed part of the Kulturforum cultural complex near Potsdamer Platz. A fire in 2008 damaged the roof but the hall reopened the following year.
