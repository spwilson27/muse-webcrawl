Karbala (Arabic: كربلاء) is a city in central Iraq, located about 100 km (62 mi) southwest of Baghdad. It is the capital of Karbala Governorate and one of the holiest cities for Shia Muslims, after Mecca, Medina, and Najaf.

Religious significance

Karbala is the site of the Battle of Karbala in 680 CE (61 AH), in which Husayn ibn Ali, grandson of the Prophet Muhammad, was killed along with his family and companions by the forces of the Umayyad caliph Yazid I. The Imam Husayn Shrine and the Al-Abbas Shrine mark the burial places of Husayn and his half-brother Abbas.

Each year, millions of pilgrims visit the city for Ashura, commemorating the martyrdom of Husayn, and for Arba'in, forty days later, when vast processions walk to Karbala from Najaf and other cities in what is among the largest annual gatherings in the world.

History

The city grew around the shrines in the centuries after the battle. It was repeatedly attacked, most destructively by the Wahhabis in 1802, and was a centre of Shia scholarship. Karbala saw fighting during the 1991 uprisings after the Gulf War, when the shrines were damaged, and again during the Iraq War of the 2000s.
