The Warta (Polish pronunciation: [ˈvarta]; German: Warthe) is a river in western and central Poland and the principal right-bank tributary of the Oder. At 808 kilometres it is the third-longest river within Poland's borders and one of the country's most important waterways. Its drainage basin of about 54,529 km² covers much of the region of Greater Poland (Wielkopolska).

Course

The Warta rises at Kromołów, part of the town of Zawiercie in the Silesian Voivodeship, on the Kraków-Częstochowa Upland. It flows generally northwest through Częstochowa, then west and north across the Wielkopolska lowland, passing Sieradz, Koło, Konin and the city of Poznań — the largest city on its banks — before turning west through Gorzów Wielkopolski. It joins the Oder at Kostrzyn nad Odrą near the German border.

Important tributaries include the Noteć, which joins the Warta at Santok and links its basin via the Bydgoszcz Canal to the Vistula system; the Prosna; the Obra; and the Widawka. Near its confluence with the Oder lies Warta Mouth National Park (established 2001), one of Poland's most valuable wetland bird habitats, protecting the floodplains at the river's mouth.

History and economy

The Warta valley has been a settlement and trade corridor since the early Middle Ages; Poznań, on its banks, was one of the first capitals of the Polish state. The river is navigable in its lower and middle course and forms part of the inland waterway network connecting the Oder and Vistula basins. It is also used for water supply, irrigation and recreation, and its name appears in the second stanza context of Polish regional identity in Greater Poland.
