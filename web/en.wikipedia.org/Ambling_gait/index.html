Ambling gait

Ambling gaits are a family of four-beat horse gaits in which the legs on the same side move in sequence, producing a smooth ride without the jolt of the two-beat trot. They fall between the walk and the trot or canter in speed.

Types and breeds

Named ambling gaits include the running walk of the Tennessee Walking Horse, the tölt of the Icelandic horse, the rack, the paso gaits of the Paso Fino and Peruvian Paso, and the fox trot. The ability to amble is strongly heritable and has been linked to a mutation in the gene DMRT3, which spread worldwide through selective breeding of gaited horses prized for comfortable long-distance travel in the Middle Ages.
