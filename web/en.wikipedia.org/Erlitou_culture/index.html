Erlitou culture

The Erlitou culture (c. 1900–1500 BC) was an early Bronze Age society in the middle reaches of the Yellow River in present-day Henan, China. Named after the Erlitou site near Luoyang, discovered in 1959, it is widely regarded by Chinese archaeologists as evidence of the earliest state-level society in China, and is often associated with the Xia dynasty of traditional histories.

The Erlitou site

Excavations revealed a large urban centre of roughly 300 hectares with palace foundations laid out on rammed-earth platforms, a grid of roads, bronze-casting workshops, and elite burials. Erlitou produced the earliest known Chinese bronze ritual vessels, cast using piece-mould technology, as well as turquoise-inlaid bronze plaques.

Significance

Erlitou marks the transition from the Neolithic Longshan cultures to the Bronze Age world of the Erligang (early Shang) culture. Whether it represents the Xia capital remains debated among scholars, but its palatial architecture, craft specialisation, and control of bronze production indicate a centralised polity.
