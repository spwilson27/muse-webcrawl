Final Four

The Final Four is the semifinal round of a sports tournament, most famously the NCAA Division I men's and women's basketball tournaments in the United States, in which the last four remaining teams compete to reach the championship game. The term is a registered trademark of the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA).

NCAA basketball

In the NCAA tournaments, 68 teams enter a single-elimination bracket. The champions of the four regions advance to the Final Four, held at a single predetermined site, usually a large stadium configured for basketball. The two semifinal games are played on Saturday, and the winners meet in the national championship game on Monday night. The event is one of the most watched in American sports, and the weekend includes concerts and fan festivals in the host city.

Other uses

The term is used informally for the semifinal stage of other knockout competitions, including the EuroLeague basketball Final Four in Europe, and for the last four contestants in various sporting and reality competition contexts. Because the NCAA holds the trademark in the United States, American media generally use "final four" in the basketball context under licence or convention.

History

The NCAA tournament began in 1939, and the Final Four format at a single site became standard as the event grew. Since the 1970s the championship weekend has rotated among major American cities, frequently using domed football stadiums with capacities exceeding 60,000.
