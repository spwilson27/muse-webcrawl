Hungarian People's Republic

Hungarian People's Republic
Hungarian: Magyar Népköztársaság
Existed: 20 August 1949 – 23 October 1989
Capital: Budapest
Ruling party: Hungarian Socialist Workers' Party (MSZMP)
Leaders: Mátyás Rákosi; János Kádár (1956–1988)

The Hungarian People's Republic was the official name of Hungary from 1949 to 1989 during its period as a one-party socialist state aligned with the Soviet Union and a member of the Warsaw Pact. It was established after the communists consolidated power and adopted a Soviet-style constitution in 1949.

Rákosi era

Under Mátyás Rákosi the regime pursued forced industrialisation, collectivisation of agriculture and political terror administered by the ÁVH secret police. Show trials, including that of László Rajk in 1949, and mass internments marked the early 1950s.

Revolution of 1956

In October 1956 a popular uprising under Prime Minister Imre Nagy declared neutrality and withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact. Soviet forces crushed the revolution in November, killing thousands; Nagy was executed in 1958 after a secret trial.

Kádár era

János Kádár, installed after the revolution, gradually liberalised: his “goulash communism” tolerated small private enterprise and consumer goods, and the 1968 New Economic Mechanism introduced market elements. Hungary became the most prosperous and least repressive Warsaw Pact state — the “happiest barrack in the camp” — though political pluralism remained forbidden and debt grew in the 1980s.

End

In 1989 reform communists dismantled the border fence with Austria, helping trigger the collapse of communist regimes in Eastern Europe. On 23 October 1989 the Hungarian People's Republic was formally replaced by the Republic of Hungary with a democratic constitution.
