Yahoo (stylized as Yahoo! in its classic branding) is an American web services provider. It began in 1994 as a human-edited directory of websites created by Stanford graduate students Jerry Yang and David Filo, and grew into one of the most popular web portals of the early internet era, offering search, email, news, finance and sports services.

History

Incorporated in 1995 and taken public in 1996, Yahoo became the archetypal dot-com success, reaching a market capitalization of over US$100 billion at the peak of the dot-com bubble in 2000. The company famously declined opportunities to buy Google in the late 1990s and turned down a US$44.6 billion takeover bid from Microsoft in 2008. Over the following decade it lost search and advertising share to Google and Facebook.

Decline and sale

Under CEO Marissa Mayer (2012–2017), Yahoo acquired Tumblr and attempted a media-focused revival, but continued to decline, and suffered record-breaking data breaches disclosed in 2016 that affected billions of accounts. In 2017 Verizon Communications bought Yahoo's core internet business for about US$4.48 billion and combined it with AOL. The combined unit was sold to Apollo Global Management in 2021 and renamed Yahoo Inc., under which the Yahoo brand, Yahoo Mail, Yahoo Finance and related services continue to operate.

Services

Yahoo Finance and Yahoo Sports remain among the most-visited sites in their categories, and Yahoo Mail continues to serve hundreds of millions of users. Yahoo Japan, operated under license by a separate company, remained highly successful in Japan long after the US business declined.
