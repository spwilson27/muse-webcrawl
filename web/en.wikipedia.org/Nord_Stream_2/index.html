Nord Stream 2

Nord Stream 2 (German name: Nord Stream 2; Russian: Северный поток — 2) is a 1,234-kilometre-long (767 mi) natural gas pipeline from Russia to Germany running through the Baltic Sea, financed by Gazprom and several European energy companies. Construction was completed in September 2021, but the pipeline never entered service.

Route of the Nord Stream pipelines in the Baltic Sea

History

The project, a pair of lines parallel to the original Nord Stream (in operation since 2011-2012), was agreed in 2015. It faced strong opposition from the United States, Ukraine, Poland, and the Baltic states, who argued it would increase European dependence on Russian gas and bypass Ukraine as a transit country. The United States imposed sanctions that delayed construction.

Suspension and sabotage

On 22 February 2022, following Russia's recognition of separatist regions in eastern Ukraine, German Chancellor Olaf Scholz halted the certification process; after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, the project was effectively abandoned. On 26 September 2022, explosions damaged both Nord Stream 1 and Nord Stream 2 near Bornholm, causing large gas leaks; investigations by Denmark, Sweden, and Germany pointed to sabotage, though responsibility remained publicly unresolved.
