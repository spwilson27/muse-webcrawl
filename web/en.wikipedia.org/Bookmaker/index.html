Bookmaker

A bookmaker, or bookie, is a person or organization that accepts and pays out bets on sporting and other events at agreed odds. Bookmaking is the business of facilitating gambling on events ranging from horse racing and football to elections and entertainment awards.

How bookmaking works

A bookmaker sets odds for each possible outcome of an event. The odds are designed so that the sum of the implied probabilities exceeds 100 percent; this built-in margin, known as the overround (or vigorish/juice), is the bookmaker's expected profit. Ideally the bookmaker balances the book — attracting bets on all outcomes in proportion to the odds — so that a profit is guaranteed regardless of the result, though in practice risk is managed by adjusting odds as money comes in.

History and regulation

Bookmaking grew out of British horse racing in the 18th and 19th centuries, with figures such as Harry Ogden operating from the 1790s. Legal status varies widely: betting shops are licensed in the United Kingdom (regulated by the Gambling Commission since 2005), while sports betting in the United States expanded rapidly after the Supreme Court struck down the federal ban in 2018 (Murphy v. NCAA). Major bookmaking firms include bet365, William Hill, Flutter Entertainment (Paddy Power Betfair), and Entain.

Modern practice

Online and mobile betting now dominate the industry. Bookmakers offer cash-out features, in-play betting, and exchange models (such as Betfair) in which customers bet against each other. Illegal bookies continue to operate in jurisdictions where betting is prohibited.
