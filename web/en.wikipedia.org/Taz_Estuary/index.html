Taz Estuary

The Taz Estuary (Russian: Тазовская губа, Tazovskaya Guba) is a long gulf or estuary formed by the Taz River in northern Russia. It is located in Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug, between the Gyda Peninsula and the Taz Peninsula, and opens into the Gulf of Ob, an arm of the Kara Sea in the Arctic Ocean.

Geography

The estuary extends for roughly 250 kilometres, making it one of the largest estuaries in the world. It begins at the mouth of the Taz River near the settlement of Tazovsky and runs roughly northward to its junction with the Gulf of Ob. The Pur River also flows into the estuary from the west near its head. The shores are low-lying tundra, and the waters are shallow and brackish, freezing over for much of the year.

Significance

The region around the Taz Estuary lies within one of Russia's most important natural gas provinces. The Tazovskoye and nearby fields were among the early giants of West Siberian gas development, and the estuary's waters have been studied for navigation and for the environmental impacts of hydrocarbon extraction in the Arctic.
