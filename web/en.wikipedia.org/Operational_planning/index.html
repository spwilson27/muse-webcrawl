Operational planning

Operational planning is the process by which military staffs turn strategy into scheduled, resourced action at the operational level of war — the level between national strategy and battlefield tactics. An operational plan (OPLAN) arranges battles, engagements, and movements in time and space to achieve strategic objectives within a theatre or campaign.

Process

Modern joint planning processes, such as the U.S. military's Joint Planning Process, proceed through stages including mission analysis, course-of-action development, comparison and decision, and the production of orders. Planners consider the adversary, terrain, logistics, timing, and the sequencing of phases, and they build branches and sequels to cope with uncertainty. Deliberate plans are prepared in advance for anticipated contingencies, while crisis-action planning adapts the same logic under time pressure.

Operational art

The discipline is closely connected with operational art — the cognitive approach by which commanders sequence tactical actions to reach strategic ends — a concept developed in Soviet military theory in the 1920s and 1930s by theorists such as Alexander Svechin and Vladimir Triandafillov, and later adopted by Western doctrine.
