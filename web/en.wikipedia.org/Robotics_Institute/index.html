Established: 1979
Location: Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, United States
Parent: Carnegie Mellon University
Director: Matthew T. Mason (former)

The Robotics Institute is a division of the School of Computer Science at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, United States. It is considered to be one of the world's foremost robotics research organizations and was ranked number one in a 2020 survey of robotics graduate programs.

The institute conducts basic and applied research in robotics technologies relevant to industrial and societal tasks. Specific research areas include computer vision, mechanism design, planning and navigation, artificial intelligence, and human–robot interaction.

History

The Robotics Institute was established in 1979 as a university-wide unit for robotics research. Its founding director was computer scientist Raj Reddy, and it grew out of an arrangement in which the Westinghouse Electric Corporation and the university sought to develop research in robotics and artificial intelligence. The institute was the first robotics department in the United States and remains one of the largest, with several hundred researchers, staff, and students.

Research

Major projects have included autonomous vehicles—the institute's Navlab and later Boss, which won the 2007 DARPA Urban Challenge—planetary rovers for NASA, field robotics for agriculture and mining, medical robotics, and the snake robots developed in the biorobotics laboratory. The institute houses the National Robotics Engineering Center (NREC), founded in 1996, which commercializes robotics technologies.

Education

The Robotics Institute offers doctoral degrees in robotics, a master's degree in robotics, and additional majors and minors for undergraduates. Its PhD program, the first of its kind, has trained many of the field's leading researchers.
