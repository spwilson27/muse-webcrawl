Fondant

TypeConfection, icing
Main ingredientsSugar, water, glucose or corn syrup
VariationsRolled fondant, poured fondant, marshmallow fondant

Fondant (from French fondant, "melting") is an icing and confection made principally of sugar and water, used to decorate or sculpt cakes and pastries. In French, the term also refers to a type of chocolate dessert with a molten centre. Fondant centres form the basis of many candies, including the filling of chocolate creams and peppermint patties.

Types

Poured fondant is a sweet, creamy paste made by cooking sugar, water, and glucose or corn syrup, then cooling and beating it so that it crystallises into a smooth, opaque coating. It is poured over petits fours, éclairs, and doughnuts. Rolled fondant includes gelatine or glycerine, giving it a dough-like consistency; it is rolled into sheets and draped over cakes to produce the smooth, porcelain finish characteristic of wedding cakes. Marshmallow fondant, a home-baker's variant, is made from melted marshmallows and icing sugar.

Preparation

To make basic fondant, sugar syrup is cooked to the soft-ball stage (about 112–116 °C), cooled undisturbed to around 50 °C so that fine crystals form, and then agitated. The tiny crystal size gives fondant its characteristic smooth texture and melt-in-the-mouth quality. Fondant can be flavoured, coloured, remelted, and thinned for pouring.

Uses

Beyond cake covering, fondant is moulded into decorative shapes and figures, used as the centre of candies such as fondant creams and the Cadbury Creme Egg filling, and serves as the base for buttercream-style fillings in some confectionery.
