Medley swimming

Medley swimming is a combination of four different swimming styles—backstroke, breaststroke, butterfly and freestyle—into one race. This race is either swum by one swimmer as individual medley (IM) or by four swimmers as a medley relay.

Individual medley

In the individual medley the swimmer swims the four strokes in the order butterfly, backstroke, breaststroke, freestyle, each covering one quarter of the total distance. Olympic and world championship distances are 200 m (short-course 100 m medley also exists) and 400 m. Each stroke must be swum according to the rules of that stroke, and the freestyle leg cannot be backstroke, breaststroke or butterfly. Michael Phelps dominated the Olympic medley events in the 2000s and 2010s.

Medley relay

In the medley relay, four swimmers each swim one stroke in the order backstroke, breaststroke, butterfly, freestyle; the backstroker starts in the water. The standard Olympic distance is 4×100 m, with 4×50 m and 4×100-yard versions in short-course and collegiate competition. A mixed medley relay, added at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, allows teams to choose the gender of each leg's swimmer.
