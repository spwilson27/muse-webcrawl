Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer is a 1964 American Christmas television special produced by Rankin/Bass Productions (as Videocraft International) and animated in Japan by MOM Productions using stop-motion "Animagic" puppetry. First broadcast on NBC on 6 December 1964, it has aired annually ever since, making it one of the longest-running Christmas specials in television history.

Plot

Narrated by Sam the Snowman (Burl Ives), the story follows Rudolph, a young reindeer whose glowing red nose makes him an outcast among Santa's herd. He befriends Hermey, an elf who wants to be a dentist, and the prospector Yukon Cornelius. Fleeing the Abominable Snow Monster (the "Bumble"), they reach the Island of Misfit Toys. When a great storm threatens Christmas Eve, Santa asks Rudolph to guide his sleigh with his shining nose, and Rudolph is finally accepted and celebrated.

Production and songs

The special adapts the story written by Robert L. May in 1939 and the song popularised by Gene Autry in 1949, both written by May's brother-in-law Johnny Marks. Marks composed the score, including "A Holly Jolly Christmas", "Silver and Gold" and "The Most Wonderful Day of the Year", several performed by Ives. The puppets were built in Japan under Tadahito Mochinaga's supervision.

Legacy

The special moved from NBC to CBS in 1972 (and back to NBC in 2024). Its characters — including the Bumble, the misfit toys and Clarice — remain enduring figures of American Christmas culture, and the production is frequently cited among the greatest television specials of all time.
