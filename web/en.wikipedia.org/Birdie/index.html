Birdie

In golf, a birdie is a score of one stroke under par on a particular hole. To achieve a birdie, the golfer completes the hole in one fewer stroke than the par rating for that hole — for example, three strokes on a par-four.

Origin

The term is credited to a 1899 match at Atlantic City Country Club in New Jersey, where golfer Ab Smith described a fine shot as "a bird of a shot" — "bird" being contemporary slang for anything excellent — and proposed that a score of one under par be called a birdie. The term spread widely in the following decades.

Related scores

Scores further under par have their own names: two under par is an eagle, three under is an albatross (also called a double eagle in North America), and four under, almost never achieved, is a condor. One stroke over par is a bogey.
