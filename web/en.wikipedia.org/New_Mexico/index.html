New Mexico (Spanish: Nuevo México) is a state in the Southwestern United States. It is one of the Mountain States of the southern Rocky Mountains, sharing the Four Corners region with Utah, Colorado and Arizona and bordering Texas and Oklahoma, as well as the Mexican states of Chihuahua and Sonora. Its capital is Santa Fe, founded in 1610 and the oldest state capital in the US; its largest city is Albuquerque.

History

Puebloan peoples built large towns such as those at Chaco Canyon centuries before European contact. Spanish explorers, beginning with Francisco Vázquez de Coronado in 1540, colonised the region from 1598; Santa Fe became the capital of Santa Fe de Nuevo México. The Pueblo Revolt of 1680 expelled the Spanish for twelve years. Mexico gained the territory in 1821, and the United States acquired it in 1848 under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo after the Mexican–American War. Despite its long territorial period, statehood came only in 1912, delayed in part by debates over its Hispanic, Catholic majority.

Geography and culture

The state is mostly high desert and mountains drained by the Rio Grande, with a continental, arid climate. New Mexico has the highest percentage of Hispanic and Latino Americans of any state and the second-highest percentage of Native Americans, including Navajo, Pueblo and Apache peoples — a "tricultural" heritage reflected in its adobe architecture, cuisine built around red and green chile ("Christmas" style combines both), and the official state question: "Red or Green?"

Science and economy

During the Second World War the secret Los Alamos laboratory developed the first atomic bombs under the Manhattan Project, tested at Trinity Site in July 1945. Los Alamos and Sandia National Laboratories remain major employers, along with federal installations, oil and gas production in the Permian Basin, tourism and film production. White Sands National Park and Carlsbad Caverns are signature landscapes.
