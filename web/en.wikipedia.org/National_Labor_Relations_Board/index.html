National Labor Relations Board

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) is an independent agency of the United States federal government with responsibilities for enforcing U.S. labor law in relation to collective bargaining and unfair labor practices.

Formed5 July 1935
BasisNational Labor Relations (Wagner) Act
StructureFive-member board plus General Counsel

Functions

Under the National Labor Relations Act of 1935, the NLRB conducts elections for union representation, investigates and remedies unfair labor practices by employers and unions in the private sector, and decides cases through administrative law judges and the five-member board appointed by the President. Its General Counsel acts as a prosecutor, independent of the board.

History

The board's early decisions survived Supreme Court challenge in NLRB v. Jones & Laughlin Steel (1937). The Taft–Hartley Act of 1947 amended its mandate, adding union unfair labor practices and restricting secondary boycotts. Board doctrine on joint employment, election rules, and graduate student and gig-worker status has shifted with successive administrations, making the agency a focal point of American labor politics.
