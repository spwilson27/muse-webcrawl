1924 Summer Olympics

Games of the VIII Olympiad
Host city: Paris, France
Dates: 5–27 July 1924 (some events from May)
Nations: 44
Athletes: 3,089
Top nation: United States

The 1924 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the VIII Olympiad, were held in Paris, France, in 1924 — the second time Paris hosted the Games after 1900. They were the last Olympics organised under the presidency of Pierre de Coubertin, founder of the modern Games.

Highlights

Finnish runner Paavo Nurmi won five gold medals, including the 1500m and 5000m within an hour of each other. British athletes Harold Abrahams and Eric Liddell won the 100m and 400m respectively, a story later dramatised in the film Chariots of Fire. Johnny Weissmuller won three swimming golds. The Games introduced the Olympic motto display, the first Olympic Village, and a standard 50-metre pool; the first Winter Olympics were held the same year in Chamonix.

Medal table

The United States topped the medal table with 45 golds and 99 medals overall, followed by Finland with 14 golds. Germany remained excluded in the aftermath of World War I.
