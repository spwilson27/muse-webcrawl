Peanuts

Peanuts is a syndicated daily and Sunday American comic strip written and illustrated by Charles M. Schulz, which ran from October 2, 1950, to February 13, 2000, continuing in reruns afterward. It is among the most popular and influential comic strips in history, with 17,897 strips published in all.

Characters

The strip centres on a circle of young children: the insecure Charlie Brown; his beagle Snoopy, who imagines himself a World War I flying ace; the blanket-carrying philosopher Linus; his crabby sister Lucy, who dispenses psychiatric advice for five cents; and pianist Schroeder, among others. Adults are almost never seen and, in animated adaptations, speak in muffled trombone tones.

Themes and legacy

Schulz wove gentle humour together with melancholy, theology and psychology, giving the strip unusual depth for its era. Peanuts spawned beloved television specials such as A Charlie Brown Christmas (1965), stage musicals, and a vast licensing empire; Schulz was widely honoured, and the strip's final instalment appeared the day after his death.
