Asthenosphere


The asthenosphere lies below the rigid lithosphere.

The asthenosphere is the mechanically weak, ductile region of the upper mantle of the Earth. It lies beneath the rigid lithosphere, at depths of roughly 80 to 200 kilometres below the surface, and extends down several hundred kilometres. Although solid, its rock deforms and flows slowly over geological time.

Properties

The asthenosphere is characterised by a marked drop in seismic wave velocities (the low-velocity zone), reflecting temperatures close to the melting point of mantle peridotite and possibly a small fraction of partial melt. Its weakness allows the overlying tectonic plates to move and underlies isostatic adjustment of the crust after loading and unloading, as in post-glacial rebound.

Role in plate tectonics

In plate tectonics, convection in the asthenosphere and deeper mantle is associated with the motion of the plates. The boundary between lithosphere and asthenosphere — the LAB — is a thermal and mechanical boundary whose depth varies from a few kilometres beneath mid-ocean ridges to more than 200 km beneath old continental cratons.
