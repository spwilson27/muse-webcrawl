Oneida Nation of Wisconsin

The Oneida Nation of Wisconsin is a federally recognised tribe of Oneida people — part of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy) — located in northeastern Wisconsin near Green Bay. Its citizens descend from Oneida families who, under leaders including Daniel Bread and with the encouragement of missionary Eleazer Williams, moved from New York to the Wisconsin Territory in the 1820s and 1830s, seeking to escape removal pressures and preserve their community.

History

Treaties of 1831 and 1838 secured the tribe a reservation of about 65,400 acres. Under the Dawes Act allotment policy after 1887, most reservation land passed out of tribal ownership, leaving the Oneida with only a fraction of their territory by the 1930s. The tribe reorganised under the Indian Reorganization Act in 1934 and has since worked steadily to repurchase land within the reservation boundaries, recovering well over a third of the original acreage into tribal ownership.

Government and economy

The Nation is governed by an elected Business Committee accountable to its General Tribal Council. Gaming under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act — anchored by the Oneida Casino operations near Green Bay's Austin Straubel airport — along with retail, agriculture (including the Tsyunhehkwa farm and cannery), construction and hospitality, funds tribal services, education, health care and land buy-back. The Oneida also maintain a language revitalisation programme for the Oneida language (Onʌyotaʼa:ka), part of the wider Haudenosaunee cultural renewal.

Relations with neighbours

The tribe is one of the largest employers in the Green Bay area and a major partner in regional events; its relationship with surrounding municipalities and the nearby Oneida Nation of New York and the Oneida at the Thames in Ontario reflects the dispersed history of the Oneida people after the American Revolution, in which most Oneida had sided with the Americans.
