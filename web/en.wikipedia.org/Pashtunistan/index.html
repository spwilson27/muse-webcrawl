Pashtunistan

Pashtunistan (Pashto: پښتونستان, romanized: Pax̌tūnistān) is a historical region located on the Iranian Plateau, inhabited by the indigenous Pashtun people of southern and eastern Afghanistan and northwestern Pakistan. The term has also been used more specifically for a proposed independent Pashtun state encompassing the Pashtun-majority areas divided by the Durand Line.

The Pashtun heartland along the Durand Line between Afghanistan and Pakistan.

The Durand Line

In 1893 the British Indian government and the Emir of Afghanistan, Abdur Rahman Khan, agreed the Durand Line as their mutual frontier, splitting Pashtun tribes between Afghanistan and what became Pakistan. The 1947 partition of India and the creation of Pakistan made the frontier permanent in practice, but Afghanistan voted against Pakistan's admission to the United Nations that year and for decades refused to recognize the line as an international border.

The independence movement

Pashtun nationalists, most famously Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan and his Khudai Khidmatgar movement, campaigned first against British rule and later for a separate Pashtun homeland. Afghan governments of the 1950s–1970s promoted the Pashtunistan cause, broadcasting propaganda and supporting irredentist claims, which repeatedly strained relations with Pakistan. The issue faded after the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979, though Pashtun nationalist and separatist sentiment persists, including in newer movements such as the Pashtun Tahafuz Movement.
