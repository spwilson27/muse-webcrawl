Myllokunmingia

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
GenusMyllokunmingia
SpeciesM. fengjiaoa
PeriodCambrian Stage 3, ~518 Ma

Myllokunmingia is a genus of small chordate from the Lower Cambrian Chengjiang deposits of Yunnan, China, around 518 million years old. The single species, M. fengjiaoa, is considered among the earliest known craniates — animals with a distinct head and a cartilaginous skull — and therefore one of the earliest steps in vertebrate evolution.

Description

The animal was about 28 mm long and only a few millimetres high, with a body divided into a head region and a segmented trunk. Interpretations of the fossils suggest a skull and skeletal rods of cartilage, a dorsal fin with fin radials, a ventral fin fold, five or six gill pouches with filaments, a notochord, and W-shaped muscle segments (myomeres). A related Chengjiang form, Haikouichthys, is often discussed alongside it.

Significance

Together with other Chengjiang chordates such as Haikouichthys and the more primitive Yunnanozoon, Myllokunmingia shows that animals with the basic vertebrate body plan — head, backbone precursor, gills, and paired sense organs — had already evolved by the height of the Cambrian explosion, far earlier than the classic fossil record of fish once suggested.
