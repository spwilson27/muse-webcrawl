John Lennon

Born: John Winston Lennon, 9 October 1940, Liverpool, England
Died: 8 December 1980 (aged 40), New York City, U.S.
Occupations: Singer, songwriter, musician, peace activist
Years active: 1956–1975, 1980
Spouses: Cynthia Powell; Yoko Ono

John Winston Ono Lennon (9 October 1940 – 8 December 1980) was an English singer, songwriter and musician who gained worldwide fame as the founder, co-lead vocalist and rhythm guitarist of the Beatles. His songwriting partnership with Paul McCartney remains the most successful in history.

Beatles years

Lennon formed the Quarrymen in 1956, which became the Beatles in 1960. With McCartney he wrote most of the band's catalogue, including "Strawberry Fields Forever", "A Day in the Life" and "Come Together", moving the group from pop hits toward experimental and introspective work. After the Beatles broke up in 1970, Lennon released solo albums including John Lennon/Plastic Ono Band (1970) and Imagine (1971), whose title track became an anthem of peace.

Activism and later life

With his wife Yoko Ono, Lennon staged "bed-ins" for peace and opposed the Vietnam War, prompting the Nixon administration to attempt his deportation. After a five-year break to raise his son Sean, he returned with Double Fantasy in 1980.

Murder

On 8 December 1980, Lennon was shot and killed outside the Dakota building in New York by Mark David Chapman. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice, as a Beatle and as a solo artist.
