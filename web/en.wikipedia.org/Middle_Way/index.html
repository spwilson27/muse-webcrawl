The Buddha taught the Middle Way between indulgence and self-mortification.

The Middle Way (Pali: Majjhimāpaṭipadā; Sanskrit: Madhyamāpratipada) is a term used by the Buddha to describe the character of the Noble Eightfold Path that he discovered and that leads to liberation. In his first sermon, the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, the Buddha rejected the two extremes of sensual indulgence and self-mortification, which he had practised and found wanting before his awakening.

In Mahayana thought

In Mahayana Buddhism, the Middle Way (Madhyamaka) also refers to the philosophy of Nāgārjuna, which steers between the extremes of eternalism and nihilism: phenomena are empty of inherent existence (śūnyatā) yet arise dependently. Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamakakārikā identifies dependent origination, emptiness, and the middle way with one another.
