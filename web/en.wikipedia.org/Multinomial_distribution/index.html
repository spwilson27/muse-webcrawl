Multinomial distribution


Type: Discrete probability distribution
Generalizes: Binomial distribution
Parameters: n trials; event probabilities p1…pk

In probability theory, the multinomial distribution is a generalization of the binomial distribution to experiments with more than two possible outcomes. It models the probability of counts for each side of a k-sided die rolled n times: for each trial, one of k mutually exclusive outcomes occurs with fixed probability.

Definition

If X1, …, Xk are the counts of each outcome in n independent trials, with probabilities p1 + … + pk = 1, then the probability that the counts equal x1, …, xk (which sum to n) is n! / (x1! … xk!) × p1x1 … pkxk. The expected value of Xi is npi, its variance is npi(1 − pi), and counts for different categories are negatively correlated.

Properties and related distributions

Each individual count marginally follows a binomial distribution. The multinomial is the conjugate prior pairing for the Dirichlet distribution in Bayesian statistics, and its sampling properties underpin chi-squared tests of goodness of fit and independence. As n grows, rescaled counts approach a multivariate normal distribution.

Applications

The distribution is used in natural language processing (document classification with multinomial naive Bayes), genetics, survey analysis, and any setting where observations fall into discrete categories.
