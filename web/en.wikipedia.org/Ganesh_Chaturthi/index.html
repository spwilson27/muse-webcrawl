Ganesh Chaturthi

TypeHindu festival
DeityGanesha
DateShukla Chaturthi of Bhadrapada (August–September)
Duration1 to 11 days (commonly 10, ending on Anant Chaturdashi)
Observed byHindus, principally in Maharashtra and across India and the diaspora

Ganesh Chaturthi is a Hindu festival celebrating the birthday of the deity Ganesha, the remover of obstacles and god of beginnings, wisdom, and prosperity. The festival begins on the fourth day (Chaturthi) of the bright fortnight of the Hindu month Bhadrapada (usually August–September) and culminates on Anant Chaturdashi, when clay idols of Ganesha are immersed in water.

Celebration

Devotees install decorated clay murtis of Ganesha in homes and in elaborate public pandals, offering prayers, hymns, and sweets — especially modak, considered Ganesha's favourite. Daily aarti ceremonies, cultural performances, and community feasts mark the festival days. At its close, processions carry the idols to rivers, lakes, or the sea for visarjan (immersion), symbolizing Ganesha carrying away devotees' troubles as he returns to his divine parents.

Public festival

Though celebrated privately for centuries, the festival was transformed into a large public event in Pune in 1893 by freedom fighter Bal Gangadhar Tilak, who used it to build nationalist solidarity under British rule. Mumbai's celebrations are the most famous, with giant idols such as Lalbaugcha Raja drawing millions of visitors; major festivities also occur across Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, and in diaspora communities worldwide.
