Baku Commune

The Baku Commune was a short-lived Bolshevik-led government that controlled the city of Baku, in present-day Azerbaijan, from April to July 1918, during the Russian Civil War. It is chiefly remembered through the fate of its leaders, the Twenty-six Baku Commissars.

History

After the violence of the March Days of 1918, in which thousands of Azerbaijani Muslims were killed in fighting with Bolshevik and Armenian Dashnak forces, the Baku Soviet under Stepan Shaumian established the Council of People's Commissars of Baku in April 1918. The commune nationalised the oil industry and banks and attempted land and food reforms, but it was militarily weak and dependent on a multi-ethnic coalition.

Fall

Pressed by advancing Ottoman and Azerbaijani forces and by internal opposition, the Soviet voted to invite British help against the Ottomans; the Bolshevik commissars resigned in late July 1918 and the commune collapsed. Its leaders were later arrested by the successor Centro-Caspian government and, after Baku fell in September, were executed by anti-Bolshevik authorities in the Transcaspian region — the origin of the famous Twenty-six Baku Commissars story, which became a major Soviet legend.
