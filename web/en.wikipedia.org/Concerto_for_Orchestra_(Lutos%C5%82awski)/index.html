The Concerto for Orchestra is an orchestral work by the Polish composer Witold Lutosławski, composed between 1950 and 1954 at the request of the conductor Witold Rowicki, to whom it is dedicated. It was premiered in Warsaw on 26 November 1954 and brought Lutosławski international recognition, remaining one of his most frequently performed works.

Music

The concerto draws extensively on Polish folk melodies from the Mazovia region, collected in the anthologies of Oskar Kolberg, which Lutosławski transforms through elaborate counterpoint, dense orchestration, and his developing harmonic language. Like Bartók's concerto of the same name, it treats sections and soloists of the orchestra in a virtuoso, concertante fashion.

Movements

The first movement, Intrada, builds a massive crescendo over an ostinato. The second, Capriccio notturno e arioso, is a fleeting, nocturnal scherzo surrounding a singing central section. The finale, Passacaglia, toccata e corale, is the longest movement: a passacaglia rises to a brilliant toccata and culminates in a grand chorale, bringing the work to a triumphant close.

Reception

Written during the period of socialist realism in Poland, the concerto satisfied official demands for folk-based, accessible music while displaying a craftsmanship that transcended ideology. It received the Polish State Prize and commissions that launched Lutosławski's mature career, in which he moved toward the controlled aleatoricism of works such as Jeux vénitiens.
