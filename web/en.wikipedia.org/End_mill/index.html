End mill

An end mill is a type of milling cutter used in industrial milling applications. It differs from a drill bit in its application, geometry, and manufacture. While a drill bit can only cut in the axial direction, most end mills can cut in the radial direction as well, with cutting edges both on the end face and along the periphery of the tool.

Types

End mills are classified by the shape of the cutting end and the number of flutes. Common types include:

  • Flat (square) end mills — the general-purpose standard, producing flat bottoms and sharp corners.
  • Ball nose end mills — with a hemispherical tip, used for machining contoured three-dimensional surfaces such as moulds and dies.
  • Corner radius (bull nose) end mills — flat end mills with a radius at the corners, giving stronger edges.
  • Roughing end mills — with serrated flutes that break chips into small pieces, allowing heavy material removal.
  • Tapered end mills — whose diameter changes along the flute length.

Two-flute end mills have room for chip evacuation and are traditional for slotting and for softer materials such as aluminium; higher flute counts provide smoother finishes in harder materials but less chip space.

Materials and coatings

End mills are made of high-speed steel or, increasingly, solid tungsten carbide, sometimes with coatings such as titanium nitride (TiN), titanium aluminium nitride (TiAlN), or diamond-like carbon to extend tool life and permit higher cutting speeds.

Use

End mills are held in a milling machine spindle, typically in a collet chuck, and are used for profiling, slotting, pocketing, face milling across narrow surfaces, drilling-like plunging, and contouring in CNC machining. Cutting parameters — spindle speed, feed rate, depth of cut — depend on the workpiece material, tool diameter, and number of flutes.
