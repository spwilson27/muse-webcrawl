Siege of Drogheda

Date: 3–11 September 1649
Location: Drogheda, Ireland
Result: Parliamentarian victory

The siege of Drogheda took place in September 1649 during the Cromwellian conquest of Ireland. Parliamentarian forces under Oliver Cromwell stormed the town of Drogheda, County Louth, and massacred much of its garrison and many inhabitants.

Background

Cromwell landed in Ireland in August 1649 to reconquer the island for the English Commonwealth after the Irish Confederate Wars and the execution of Charles I. Drogheda, held by a Royalist and Confederate garrison under Sir Arthur Aston, was his first target.

Assault

After Aston refused a summons to surrender, Cromwell's artillery breached the walls on 11 September. His troops stormed the town; Cromwell ordered no quarter for those in arms. Estimates of the dead range into the thousands, including soldiers, clergy and civilians. The event remains one of the most notorious and debated episodes of the wars in Ireland.
