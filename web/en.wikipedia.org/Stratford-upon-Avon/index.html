Stratford-upon-Avon

Stratford-upon-Avon, commonly known as just Stratford, is a market town and civil parish in the Stratford-on-Avon district of Warwickshire, England, on the River Avon, 91 miles (146 km) north-west of London, 22 miles (35 km) south-east of Birmingham and 8 miles (13 km) south-west of Warwick. The estimated population in 2020 was 30,495.

Stratford was originally inhabited by Anglo-Saxons and remained a village before the lord of the manor, John of Coutances, set out plans for it to grow as a town in 1196. In that year, Stratford received a charter from King Richard I to hold a weekly market. Stratford is most famous as the birthplace and gravesite of playwright and poet William Shakespeare, considered by many to be the greatest writer in the English language. It receives about 2.7 million visitors a year, mainly for attractions linked to Shakespeare.

Shakespeare connections

Five houses associated with Shakespeare and his family are preserved by the Shakespeare Birthplace Trust: Shakespeare's Birthplace on Henley Street, Anne Hathaway's Cottage at Shottery, Mary Arden's Farm, Hall's Croft, and Shakespeare's New Place. Shakespeare is buried in the chancel of Holy Trinity Church. The Royal Shakespeare Company performs at the Royal Shakespeare Theatre and the Swan Theatre on the banks of the Avon.

History

The name Stratford combines the Old English strǣt (a street, a Roman road) with ford, indicating the site where the Roman road crossed the Avon. The Stratford-upon-Avon Canal and the railway, which arrived in the mid-19th century, aided the town's growth. The town was the centre of Britain's aluminium and brewing trades at various points and is now an important tourist destination and service centre for the surrounding area.
