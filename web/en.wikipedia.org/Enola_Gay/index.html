Enola Gay

The Enola Gay is a Boeing B-29 Superfortress bomber of the United States Army Air Forces. On 6 August 1945 it dropped the atomic bomb Little Boy on Hiroshima, Japan, the first nuclear weapon used in warfare.

Aircraft and mission

The aircraft was flown by Colonel Paul W. Tibbets Jr., who named it for his mother, Enola Gay Tibbets. It was assigned to the 509th Composite Group and modified under the Silverplate program to carry atomic weapons. The Hiroshima mission was followed three days later by Bockscar's mission to Nagasaki.

After the war

After service and storage, the bomber was transferred to the Smithsonian Institution. Restoration and display—especially at the National Air and Space Museum—have made it an enduring focus of debate about memory, the end of the Pacific War, and the ethics of atomic bombing.

Preservation

The fully restored aircraft is exhibited at the Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center in Virginia. See also atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and Boeing B-29 Superfortress.
