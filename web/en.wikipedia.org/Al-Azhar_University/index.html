Al-Azhar University
Established: c. 970–972 AD
Location: Cairo, Egypt
Type: Public university
Affiliation: Sunni Islam

Al-Azhar University (Arabic: جامعة الأزهر) is a public university in Cairo, Egypt. Founded around 970–972 as a centre of learning attached to the Al-Azhar Mosque by the Fatimid Caliphate, it is one of the oldest existing universities in the world and the most prestigious centre of Sunni Islamic scholarship.

History

The mosque-university was founded under the Fatimids, initially as a centre of Ismaili Shi'a learning, and became a bulwark of Sunni scholarship under Saladin and his successors. For centuries its scholars issued legal opinions (fatwas) consulted across the Muslim world, and its Grand Imam (Sheikh al-Azhar) remains a major religious authority.

Modern university

A 1961 law reorganised Al-Azhar as a modern university, adding faculties of medicine, engineering, science, agriculture, and commerce alongside theology, Islamic law, and Arabic. It now educates hundreds of thousands of students, including many from abroad, and runs a nationwide network of schools in Egypt.
