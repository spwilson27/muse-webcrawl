Seabird

Seabirds are birds that have adapted to life within the marine environment. Examples include albatrosses, petrels, gulls, terns, auks and penguins. While seabirds vary greatly in lifestyle and anatomy, they share traits shaped by life at sea: salt-excreting glands, waterproof plumage, and in many species webbed feet and extraordinary flight endurance.

Ecology

Seabirds feed on fish, squid and krill, often foraging far out at sea and travelling thousands of kilometres between feeding and breeding grounds. Most breed in large, dense colonies on islands and cliffs, returning to the same site year after year, and many are long-lived and slow to reproduce, laying only one or a few eggs per season.

Conservation

Seabirds are among the most threatened groups of birds, endangered by invasive predators at colonies, bycatch in fisheries, plastic pollution, overfishing and climate change. Several albatross species are at risk from longline fishing.
