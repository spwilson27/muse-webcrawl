Casual work

Casual work is an employment classification used in a number of countries, including Australia, New Zealand and Canada, for workers engaged on an irregular, as-needed basis without a firm commitment to ongoing work. Casual employees are typically hired by the hour, day or shift and can be rostered flexibly, for example in hospitality, retail, agriculture and health care.

Characteristics

Unlike permanent (continuing) employees, casual workers generally have no guaranteed hours, no paid annual or sick leave, and employment that can end at short notice. In Australia, this insecurity is partly compensated by casual loading — an additional percentage (commonly 25%) paid on top of the base hourly rate. Casual employees may nevertheless accrue long service leave in some jurisdictions, and Australian reforms have created pathways for long-term casuals to convert to permanent employment and rights to request conversion.

Criticism

Critics associate heavy reliance on casual work with precarious employment and income insecurity, while employers argue it provides flexibility for seasonal and fluctuating demand and an entry point into the workforce.
