Chemical process

A chemical process is a method or means of changing one or more chemicals or chemical compounds. Such a process can occur by itself or be caused by an outside force, and involves a chemical reaction of some sort. In the engineering sense, a chemical process is carried out in a chemical plant by chemical engineers using unit operations such as distillation, mixing, separation, and heating.

Classification

Chemical processes may be batch processes, in which reactants are charged, reacted, and discharged as a single lot, or continuous processes, in which materials flow steadily through reactors and separators. Many large-scale commodity chemicals are made continuously, while pharmaceuticals and specialty chemicals often use flexible batch equipment.

Unit operations and reactions

A typical chemical process combines physical unit operations — pumping, heat exchange, filtration, crystallization, and distillation — with chemical reactors where synthesis, oxidation, polymerization, or cracking reactions occur. Process flow diagrams and piping and instrumentation diagrams document how these steps are connected and controlled.

Design and scale-up

Developing a process involves laboratory chemistry, pilot-plant trials, process simulation, and hazard analysis. Engineers balance yield, selectivity, energy use, raw-material cost, safety, and environmental impact when selecting reaction conditions and separation sequences.

Examples

Well-known chemical processes include the Haber process for ammonia synthesis, the contact process for sulfuric acid, the Ostwald process for nitric acid, steam cracking for olefins, and catalytic reforming in petroleum refining.
