Hunter, New York

Hunter is a town in Greene County, New York, United States. The population was 2,732 at the 2020 census. The town contains a village also named Hunter, as well as the hamlets of Tannersville, Haines Falls, and Kaaterskill. Hunter lies in the Catskill Mountains and is home to Hunter Mountain, one of the most popular ski resorts in the New York metropolitan region.

History

The town was formed in 1813 from part of the town of Windham and was first called Edwardsville before being renamed Hunter after John Hunter, an early landowner. In the 19th century the area became a celebrated summer resort, part of the Catskills "Borscht Belt" era, with grand hotels such as the Hotel Kaaterskill and the Catskill Mountain House perched on the escarpment overlooking the Hudson Valley. The landscape inspired the painters of the Hudson River School, including Thomas Cole, who lived nearby in Catskill.

Geography and recreation

Hunter Mountain rises to about 4,050 feet (1,234 m) and offers skiing and snowboarding in winter and festivals and zip-lining in summer. The town includes trailheads for Kaaterskill Falls, one of New York's tallest waterfalls, and access to the Catskill Park's Forest Preserve lands, including Hunter-West Kill Wilderness.
