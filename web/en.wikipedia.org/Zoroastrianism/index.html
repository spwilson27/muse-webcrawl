Zoroastrianism is an Iranian religion founded by the prophet Zoroaster, centred on the worship of the supreme god Ahura Mazda. One of the world's oldest organised faiths, it was the state religion of the Achaemenid, Parthian, and Sasanian empires and profoundly influenced Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

Beliefs

Zoroastrianism teaches that Ahura Mazda sustains the world through asha (truth and order) against the destructive spirit Angra Mainyu and the forces of druj (falsehood). Human beings, endowed with free will, participate in this struggle through "good thoughts, good words, good deeds" (Humata, Hukhta, Huvarshta). After death the soul is judged at the Chinvat Bridge; at the end of time the world will be renewed in Frashokereti, evil destroyed, and the dead resurrected under the saviour Saoshyant.

Practices and scripture

The sacred texts form the Avesta, of which the Yasna — containing Zoroaster's own Gathas — is the liturgical core. Worship centres on fire temples, where a consecrated flame is tended by priests as a symbol of divine presence and purity. Traditionally, the dead were exposed in Towers of Silence (dakhma) so as not to pollute earth, fire, or water, though burial and cremation are now common in many communities.

History and communities

After the Muslim conquest of Persia in the 7th century CE, Zoroastrians declined in Iran; a group migrated to Gujarat in India, where their descendants, the Parsis, became a prosperous and influential community. Today the faith survives in Iran (notably Yazd and Kerman), among Parsis in India, and in a worldwide diaspora.
