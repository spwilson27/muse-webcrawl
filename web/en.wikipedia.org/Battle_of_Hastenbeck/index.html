The Battle of Hastenbeck (26 July 1757) was fought as part of the Invasion of Hanover during the Seven Years' War. The French forces, under the command of Louis Charles César Le Tellier, duc d'Estrées, defeated the Army of Observation, a Hanoverian army commanded by Prince William, Duke of Cumberland, near Hamelin in present-day Lower Saxony.

Background

France invaded Hanover in 1757 in part to tie down British and German resources and to gain a bargaining counter for the peace negotiations. The Army of Observation, composed of Hanoverian, Hessian and Brunswick troops subsidised by Britain, attempted to block the French advance at the River Weser.

The battle

The French turned the Allied left flank by marching troops over wooded hills thought impassable, while their artillery outmatched the Allied guns. Believing his flank was turned and ammunition wagons lost, the Duke of Cumberland ordered a retreat, leaving Hamelin and the road into Hanover open.

Aftermath

The defeat led directly to the Convention of Klosterzeven in September 1757, by which Hanover was occupied and the Army of Observation disbanded — an agreement later repudiated by the British government, which recalled Cumberland and sent new forces under Ferdinand of Brunswick.
