Geography of Greece

Greece occupies the southern Balkan Peninsula and thousands of Aegean islands.

Greece is a country in Southern Europe, situated on the southern tip of the Balkan Peninsula. It is bordered by Albania, North Macedonia and Bulgaria to the north and Turkey to the east. The Aegean Sea lies to the east, the Ionian Sea to the west, and the Mediterranean Sea and Sea of Crete to the south.

Terrain

About 80 per cent of Greece is mountainous. The Pindus mountain range runs down the spine of the mainland, reaching 2,637 m at Mount Smolikas. Mount Olympus, the mythical home of the Greek gods, is the highest peak at 2,918 m. Deep basins and river valleys — including those of the Haliacmon, Achelous and Peneus — separate the ranges.

Islands

Greece has thousands of islands; estimates range from 1,200 to 6,000 depending on the definition, of which about 227 are inhabited. The largest is Crete, followed by Euboea. Major island groups include the Cyclades, the Dodecanese, the Sporades and the Ionian Islands, home to Corfu.

Climate

Greece has a predominantly Mediterranean climate, with mild, wet winters and hot, dry summers. Alpine conditions prevail in the mountainous interior, while the islands enjoy long dry seasons. Rainfall is heaviest in the northwest, around the Pindus range.
