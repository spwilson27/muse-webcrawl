Sloop

A sloop is a sailboat with a single mast and a fore-and-aft rig, typically consisting of a mainsail and one headsail (a jib or genoa) set on the forestay. It is the most common rig for modern recreational sailboats.

Characteristics

In the common Bermuda (Marconi) rig, the mainsail is a tall triangle behind the mast; in the older gaff rig it is a four-sided sail topped by a spar. A single headsail distinguishes the sloop from a cutter, which flies two or more headsails from a longer bowsprit arrangement. Sloops are efficient sailing to windward, simple to handle with a small crew, and scalable from dinghies to large yachts; most racing handicap and one-design classes are sloops.

Historical usage

The word derives from the Dutch sloep. In the age of sail, “sloop” also described small warships: a sloop-of-war was a vessel with guns on a single deck, smaller than a frigate, and the term was revived for escort vessels in both World Wars, such as the Black Swan class. Naval usage thus differs from the sailing-rig definition still used today.
