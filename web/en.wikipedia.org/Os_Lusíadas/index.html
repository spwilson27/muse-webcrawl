Os Lusíadas

Os Lusíadas (in English, The Lusiads) is a Portuguese epic poem written by Luís Vaz de Camões (c. 1524/1525 – 1580) and first published in 1572. It is widely regarded as the most important work of Portuguese-language literature and is frequently compared to the Aeneid, Virgil's great Latin epic.

Structure and subject

The poem is written in ottava rima and consists of ten cantos comprising 1,102 stanzas. Its central subject is the first voyage of Vasco da Gama, who sailed from Lisbon to India around the Cape of Good Hope in 1497–1499. Interwoven with the voyage are retellings of Portuguese history, from the founding of the kingdom to the Battle of Aljubarrota, and mythological episodes involving the classical gods, who alternately aid and oppose the Portuguese sailors.

"As armas e os barões assinalados" — "Arms and the Heroes, who from Lisbon's shore..."

Key episodes

Famous episodes include the tragic romance of Inês de Castro, the giant Adamastor who guards the Cape of Storms, the Isle of Love (Ilha dos Amores), and the prophetic vision of the future Portuguese empire shown to da Gama by the goddess Tethys.

Legacy

Os Lusíadas became the national epic of Portugal. Camões himself, who had served in India and reportedly saved the manuscript from a shipwreck, died in poverty in Lisbon in 1580. The poem has been translated into many languages; an English prose translation by William Julius Mickle appeared in 1776.
