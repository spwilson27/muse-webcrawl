Rashtrakuta dynasty

The Rashtrakuta dynasty was a royal Indian dynasty that ruled much of the Deccan and parts of northern and southern India between the 6th and 10th centuries CE. Its imperial line, founded by Dantidurga around 753 CE, made Manyakheta (modern Malkhed, Karnataka) its capital and for two centuries contested control of Kannauj with the Gurjara-Pratiharas and Palas in the "tripartite struggle".

History

Krishna I (r. c. 756–774) consolidated the empire and commissioned the great Kailasanatha temple at Ellora, carved top-down from a single rock. Govinda III and Amoghavarsha I (r. 814–878) brought the dynasty to its height; Amoghavarsha, a Jain-influenced patron of letters, is associated with the Kavirajamarga, the earliest surviving work on Kannada poetics. The empire fell to the Western Chalukyas under Tailapa II in 973.

Culture

Rashtrakuta rule was a golden age for Kannada and Sanskrit literature, rock-cut architecture at Ellora and Elephanta, and religious tolerance spanning Jainism, Vaishnavism, and Shaivism. The dynasty's mathematicians, such as Mahavira, advanced algebra in texts like the Ganita Sara Sangraha.
