Suspiria is a 1977 Italian supernatural horror film directed by Dario Argento, who co-wrote the screenplay with Daria Nicolodi. It is the first instalment of Argento's Three Mothers trilogy and is widely regarded as one of the most visually distinctive horror films ever made, noted for its saturated primary colours, elaborate set pieces, and the progressive rock score by Goblin.

Plot

American ballet student Suzy Bannion arrives in Freiburg, Germany, during a violent storm to attend the prestigious Tanz Dance Academy. That night another student flees the academy in terror and is brutally murdered. As Suzy settles in, she experiences strange occurrences: maggots raining from the ceiling, a mysterious directress who is never seen, and staff who disappear at night. Investigating, Suzy discovers that the academy is a front for a coven of witches led by the ancient Helena Markos, the "Mother of Sighs" (Mater Suspiriorum). Suzy confronts and kills Markos, escaping as the academy burns.

Production

Argento was partly inspired by the writings of Thomas De Quincey, whose essay collection Suspiria de Profundis (1845) named the three mothers: Mater Suspiriorum (Mother of Sighs), Mater Lachrymarum (Mother of Tears), and Mater Tenebrarum (Mother of Darkness). The film was shot in Technicolor using an imbibition printing process — among the last films made this way — which produced its unnaturally vivid colours. The sequels Inferno (1980) and The Mother of Tears (2007) completed the trilogy.

Reception and legacy

Suspiria was a commercial success and has grown into a canonical cult film, frequently appearing on lists of the greatest horror films. A remake directed by Luca Guadagnino was released in 2018, starring Dakota Johnson and Tilda Swinton.
