Jude the Obscure is a novel by Thomas Hardy, first published in book form in 1895 after serialization in magazine form. It was Hardy's last completed novel. It follows Jude Fawley, a working-class stonemason who dreams of becoming a scholar at "Christminster" (modelled on Oxford), and his doomed relationships with the earthy Arabella Donn and his intellectually kindred cousin Sue Bridehead.

Plot

Jude, orphaned and raised by his great-aunt in the village of Marygreen, teaches himself Latin and Greek while working as a stonemason, hoping to enter the university at Christminster. Tricked into marriage by Arabella, who falsely claims to be pregnant, he is separated from her and moves to Christminster, where he works on the colleges' stonework but is rejected as a scholar. He falls in love with his cousin Sue Bridehead, a schoolteacher trapped in a marriage to the older Phillotson. Sue leaves Phillotson and lives with Jude outside marriage; they have children, and Jude's son by Arabella, the melancholy "Father Time", joins the household. Social ostracism, poverty and tragedy culminate in the deaths of the children and the collapse of Jude's health and ambitions.

Themes and reception

The novel attacks Victorian institutions — the class barrier of the universities, the conventions of marriage, and organized religion — and was received with hostility by many contemporary critics; one called it "Jude the Obscene", and the Bishop of Wakefield reportedly burned his copy. The controversy helped persuade Hardy to abandon novel-writing for poetry. Modern criticism regards it as one of his greatest works, a bleak study of aspiration defeated by class and convention.
