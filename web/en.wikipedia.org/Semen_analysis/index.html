Semen analysis

A semen analysis (plural: semen analyses), also called seminogram or spermiogram, evaluates certain characteristics of a male's semen and the sperm contained therein. It is done to help evaluate male fertility, whether for those seeking pregnancy or verifying the success of vasectomy. Depending on the measurement method, just a few characteristics may be evaluated (such as with home kits) or many characteristics may be evaluated (generally by a diagnostic laboratory).

Parameters measured

Examples of parameters measured in a semen analysis are: sperm count (concentration), motility, morphology, volume, fructose level and pH. The World Health Organization (WHO) laboratory manual provides reference values; the 2021 (sixth) edition gives a lower reference limit for sperm concentration of 16 million per millilitre and for total motility of 42 percent.

Sperm count

Sperm count, or sperm concentration, measures the number of sperm per millilitre of semen. Low counts are termed oligospermia, and a complete absence of sperm in the ejaculate is azoospermia.

Motility and morphology

Motility is graded by the proportion of sperm that move and how many move progressively forward. Morphology assesses the percentage of sperm with normal shape, traditionally using strict (Tygerberg) criteria.

Collection and testing methods

Samples are typically collected by masturbation into a specimen container after a recommended abstinence period of 2–7 days. Laboratory evaluation may be performed manually under a microscope or with computer-assisted semen analysis (CASA) systems.

Factors affecting results

Results vary with abstinence duration, illness, fever, certain medications, and collection completeness, so abnormal results are usually confirmed with a repeat test. Semen analysis is also used after vasectomy to confirm that no sperm remain.
