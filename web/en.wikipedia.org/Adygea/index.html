Adygea

The Republic of Adygea is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus of Eastern Europe. It is an enclave entirely surrounded by Krasnodar Krai, near the northeastern shore of the Black Sea. Its capital is Maykop, and its population is roughly half a million. The republic takes its name from the Adyghe (Circassian) people, who form about a quarter of the population alongside an ethnic Russian majority.

Geography

Adygea covers about 7,800 km², rising from lowland plains in the north to the foothills and peaks of the Greater Caucasus in the south, including Mount Fisht. The Kuban River flows along its northern border. Part of the Caucasian State Nature Biosphere Reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage site, lies within the republic.

History

The Adyghe are indigenous to the northwest Caucasus; the Russo-Circassian War of the 19th century ended in 1864 with mass expulsion and exile of Circassians to the Ottoman Empire, creating a large global diaspora. An Adyghe autonomous oblast was created in 1922; Adygea became a republic within the Russian Federation in 1991. Adyghe, a Northwest Caucasian language, is co-official with Russian.
