Angels Landing

Angels Landing is a 1,488-foot (454 m) tall rock formation in Zion National Park in southwestern Utah, United States, famous for one of the most celebrated — and dangerous — hiking trails in the American national park system.

The trail

The 4.4-mile (7.1 km) round-trip trail follows the West Rim Trail to Scout Lookout via Walter's Wiggles, a series of 21 steep switchbacks. The final half-mile runs along a narrow fin of rock with sheer drops of up to about 1,000 feet on both sides, where chains bolted into the rock assist hikers. The summit offers panoramic views of Zion Canyon. According to legend, a member of the 1916 exploration party remarked that only an angel could land on it, giving the peak its name.

Safety and permits

More than a dozen hikers have died in falls from the trail since 2000. Since April 2022 the National Park Service has required permits, distributed by lottery, for the chained section of the hike.
