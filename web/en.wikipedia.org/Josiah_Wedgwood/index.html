Josiah Wedgwood FRS (12 July 1730 – 3 January 1795) was an English potter, entrepreneur and abolitionist. He founded the Wedgwood company and is credited with the industrialisation of the manufacture of European pottery; his work and his innovative marketing influenced the consumer culture of the later eighteenth century.

Early life and career

Wedgwood was born in Burslem, Staffordshire, into a family of potters; smallpox left him with a weakened leg (later amputated). Apprenticed to his elder brother Thomas, he then partnered with the leading potter Thomas Whieldon, experimenting systematically with clays and glazes. In 1759 he set up independently, developing a durable cream-coloured earthenware — "creamware" — which, after royal patronage from Queen Charlotte, he marketed as "Queen's ware". In 1769 he opened his model factory at Etruria, near Stoke-on-Trent, partnered with the Liverpool merchant Thomas Bentley.

Innovations

Wedgwood's most famous products were his matte stonewares: black basalt and, above all, jasperware (from 1775), typically blue with applied white classical reliefs, including copies of the Roman Portland Vase. He introduced the pyrometer to measure kiln temperatures, pioneered transfer printing and division of labour in pottery, and was a marketing innovator: London showrooms, illustrated catalogues, celebrity endorsement, money-back guarantees and free delivery. He campaigned for turnpikes and canals, backing the Trent and Mersey Canal. A Fellow of the Royal Society, he was a member of the Lunar Society.

Abolitionism and family

Wedgwood was a prominent supporter of the abolition of the slave trade. In 1787 he produced for the Society for Effecting the Abolition of the Slave Trade the famous medallion depicting a kneeling enslaved man with the motto "Am I Not a Man and a Brother?", which became one of the most widely reproduced images of the abolition campaign. He died at Etruria in 1795, leaving a fortune of some £500,000. His daughter Susannah married Robert Darwin; their son was Charles Darwin, whose work Wedgwood family wealth helped to sustain.
