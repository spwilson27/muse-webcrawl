Latite

Latite is an igneous, volcanic rock with an aphanitic-to-porphyritic texture, intermediate in composition between trachyte and andesite/basalt. It is the volcanic equivalent of monzonite.

Composition

Latite contains roughly equal proportions of plagioclase feldspar and alkali (potassium) feldspar, with minor quartz or feldspathoids and mafic minerals such as biotite, amphibole or pyroxene. On the QAPF classification it plots in field 8*. It is named after Latium, Italy, where such lavas occur.

Occurrence

Latites are associated with subduction-related and continental volcanic provinces, including Italy, the western United States and parts of the Andes.
