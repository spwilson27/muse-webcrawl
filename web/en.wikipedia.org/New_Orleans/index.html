New Orleans

State: Louisiana
Founded: 1718
Population: ~384,000 (city)
Nickname: The Big Easy, Crescent City

New Orleans is a consolidated city-parish in southeastern Louisiana, United States, situated on the Mississippi River near the Gulf of Mexico. Founded in 1718 by French colonists and named for Philippe II, Duke of Orléans, it is renowned for its distinctive music, Creole cuisine, Mardi Gras celebrations, and cross-cultural heritage blending French, Spanish, African, and American influences.

History

New Orleans passed from France to Spain in 1763, back to France in 1800, and to the United States in the Louisiana Purchase of 1803. It was the site of the Battle of New Orleans (1815), the final major battle of the War of 1812. Through the 19th century it grew into one of America's largest ports and a center of the domestic slave trade. In 2005, Hurricane Katrina devastated the city when levees failed, flooding about 80% of it; recovery took more than a decade.

Culture

New Orleans is widely considered the birthplace of jazz, associated with musicians such as Louis Armstrong, Jelly Roll Morton, and King Oliver. Its cuisine includes gumbo, jambalaya, po' boy sandwiches, and beignets. Annual events include Mardi Gras and the New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival.
