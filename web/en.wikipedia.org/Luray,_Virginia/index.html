Luray is a town in and the county seat of Page County, Virginia, United States, in the Shenandoah Valley between the Blue Ridge Mountains and Massanutten Mountain. The town was founded in 1812 and named, by tradition, after a settler's home in Lorraine, France (or for a local family name).

Luray Caverns

Luray is best known for Luray Caverns, discovered in 1878, the largest and most popular caverns in eastern America. The cave system contains dramatic stalactite and stalagmite formations, underground pools such as Dream Lake, and the Great Stalacpipe Organ, which produces musical tones by striking stalactites with rubber mallets; it was designated a U.S. National Natural Landmark.

Geography and tourism

The town lies near the Thornton Gap entrance to Shenandoah National Park and the Skyline Drive, making it a base for park tourism. The Hawksbill Greenway follows Hawksbill Creek through the town centre.
