The Tesla Roadster is a battery electric sports car produced by Tesla, Inc. (then named Tesla Motors) from 2008 to 2012. It was the first highway-legal, serial-production, all-electric car to use lithium-ion battery cells, and the first production all-electric car to travel more than 320 kilometres (200 miles) per charge. Based on the Lotus Elise chassis, the Roadster was Tesla's first production vehicle and served to demonstrate the viability of high-performance electric vehicles.

History

Tesla Motors was founded in 2003 by Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning, with Elon Musk joining as an early investor and chairman in 2004. The Roadster was unveiled as a prototype in July 2006 at an event in Santa Monica, California. Production began in March 2008, though early cars were delivered with temporary two-speed transmissions before a redesigned single-speed gearbox was introduced later that year.

The Roadster was significant in shifting public perception of electric vehicles from slow, utilitarian transport to desirable performance cars, helping Tesla secure the funding and attention needed to develop the Model S sedan.

Specifications

The Roadster is powered by a three-phase, four-pole AC induction electric motor producing 185 kW (248 hp) in the original version, and up to 215 kW (288 hp) in the later Sport model. Its 53 kWh lithium-ion battery pack consists of 6,831 cylindrical 18650 cells and provides an EPA-rated range of about 393 km (244 miles). The car accelerates from 0 to 97 km/h (60 mph) in 3.9 seconds, or 3.7 seconds for the Sport, with a top speed limited to 201 km/h (125 mph).

Production and sales

Tesla produced roughly 2,450 Roadsters sold in more than 30 countries before production ended in early 2012, when the supply of Lotus gliders was exhausted. A final upgrade package, Roadster 3.0, was announced in 2014, offering a larger 70 kWh battery, improved aerodynamics, and a range of around 640 km (400 miles).

In space

In February 2018, Elon Musk's personal 2008 Roadster was launched into space aboard the maiden flight of SpaceX's Falcon Heavy rocket as a dummy payload, with a mannequin named "Starman" in the driver's seat. The car entered an elliptical heliocentric orbit crossing the orbit of Mars.
