Stone wall

A dry stone wall in farmland

A stone wall is a kind of masonry construction made of stone that has been used for centuries for walls, fences, and buildings. Stone walls are typically built either with mortar or without it; a wall built of stones fitted together without mortar is called a dry stone wall.

Construction

Dry stone walls are built from carefully selected stones fitted tightly together, with through-stones tying the two faces and a coping of upright stones on top. Mortared walls use lime or cement mortar to bond the stones. Rubble walls use irregular fieldstone; ashlar walls use squared, dressed blocks.

History and distribution

Stone field walls are a defining feature of landscapes from New England to the Yorkshire Dales. In 17th- to 19th-century New England, farmers cleared glacial fieldstone into miles of boundary walls, many of which still run through reforested land. In Britain and Ireland, dry stone walls mark ancient field systems and sheep enclosures; the craft of dry stone walling has been recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.

Ecology

Mature stone walls provide habitat for lichens, mosses, insects, small mammals, and reptiles, acting as wildlife corridors in farmed landscapes.
