Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson

Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson, 1st Duke of Bronte (29 September 1758 – 21 October 1805) was the full formal styling of the Royal Navy vice-admiral universally known as Horatio Nelson. His peerage titles trace the arc of his victories: Baron Nelson of the Nile (1798), Viscount Nelson (1801), and the Sicilian dukedom of Bronte granted by Ferdinand IV of Naples.

Titles and honours

Nelson accumulated an unusually large collection of orders — the Bath, the Neapolitan Order of Saint Ferdinand and of Merit, the Ottoman Order of the Crescent, and the Sardinian Order of Saints Maurice and Lazarus — which he wore conspicuously on his uniform. Critics noted his vanity about decorations; at Trafalgar his medal-bedecked coat may have made him a conspicuous target for the marksman who killed him.

Biography

Born at Burnham Thorpe, Norfolk, the sixth child of a rector, Nelson served in the Caribbean, the Mediterranean, and the Baltic. His victories at the Nile (1798), Copenhagen (1801), and Trafalgar (1805) made him Britain's greatest naval hero. He was given a state funeral and buried in St Paul's Cathedral in January 1806. His elder brother William inherited the viscountcy under a special remainder, becoming 1st Earl Nelson.
