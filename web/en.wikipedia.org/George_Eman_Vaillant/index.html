George Eman Vaillant (born 1934) is an American psychiatrist and professor at Harvard Medical School. He is best known for directing the Grant Study, one of the longest-running longitudinal studies of adult life, and for his research on successful ageing, alcoholism, and the involuntary coping mechanisms known in psychoanalytic theory as ego defenses.

George Eman Vaillant, director of the Harvard Grant Study.

The Grant Study

Vaillant directed the Study of Adult Development for more than three decades, following cohorts that included Harvard sophomores first enrolled in 1938 and inner-city Boston men from the Glueck study. His books — including Adaptation to Life (1977), The Natural History of Alcoholism (1983), Aging Well (2002), and Triumphs of Experience (2012) — drew on these data to argue that warm relationships are the strongest predictor of health and happiness in old age.

Defense mechanisms

Building on the work of Anna Freud, Vaillant proposed a hierarchy of defense mechanisms from psychotic and immature defenses (denial, acting out) through neurotic defenses (intellectualization, repression) to mature defenses such as humour, altruism, sublimation, and suppression. His empirical finding that habitual use of mature defenses predicts flourishing across the lifespan has been influential in personality psychology.
