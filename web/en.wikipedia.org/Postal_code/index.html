Postal code

A postal code (also known locally in various English-speaking countries as a postcode, post code, PIN or ZIP Code) is a series of letters or digits, or both, sometimes including spaces or punctuation, included in a postal address for the purpose of sorting mail.

History

Postal districts were introduced in large cities in the 19th century — London was divided into postal districts in 1857. Modern numeric postal codes were pioneered by Ukraine in 1932 and later adopted widely after the Second World War: Germany introduced a system in 1941, the United States introduced the ZIP Code in 1963, and the United Kingdom completed its nationwide postcode system by 1974.

Formats

Formats vary by country. Most are numeric and range from three to ten digits; some, such as Canada's postal codes and the UK postcode, are alphanumeric. Codes typically identify progressively smaller geographic areas, enabling automated sorting. Postal codes are also used as convenient geographic units for statistics, marketing, insurance rating and service delivery areas.
