Quantization (physics)

In physics, quantization (in British English quantisation) is the systematic transition procedure from a classical understanding of physical phenomena to a newer understanding known as quantum mechanics. It is a procedure for constructing quantum fields and observables from a classical theory, and it underlies the idea that certain physical quantities, such as energy, can take only discrete values.

Canonical quantization

In canonical quantization, classical variables are promoted to operators satisfying commutation relations modelled on Poisson brackets: position and momentum become operators with [x, p] = iħ. Applied to fields, this gives quantum field theory, in which field excitations appear as particles.

History

The concept began with Max Planck's 1900 hypothesis that energy is emitted in discrete packets, or quanta, and matured with the old quantum theory of Bohr and Sommerfeld before the full formalism of Heisenberg, Schrödinger and Dirac in the 1920s.
