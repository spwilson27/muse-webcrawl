Shinya Yamanaka

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: portrait of stem cell researcher Shinya Yamanaka]

Shinya Yamanaka (Japanese: 山中 伸弥; born 4 September 1962) is a Japanese stem cell researcher and the director emeritus of the Center for iPS Cell Research and Application (CiRA) at Kyoto University. He shared the 2012 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with John Gurdon "for the discovery that mature cells can be reprogrammed to become pluripotent".

Trained as an orthopaedic surgeon, Yamanaka switched to basic research after finding surgery difficult, and earned his PhD at Osaka City University. In 2006 his laboratory reported that introducing just four transcription factors — Oct3/4, Sox2, Klf4 and c-Myc, now known as the Yamanaka factors — into mouse fibroblasts could convert them into induced pluripotent stem cells (iPS cells), with a corresponding human result following in 2007.

Impact

iPS cell technology opened new paths in regenerative medicine, disease modelling, and drug discovery, while sidestepping many ethical controversies surrounding embryonic stem cells. Yamanaka has received numerous honours, including the Shaw Prize, the Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences, and membership in the Pontifical Academy of Sciences. He is also a noted marathon runner, having completed the Kyoto Marathon to raise funds for his institute.
