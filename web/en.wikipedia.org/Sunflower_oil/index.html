Sunflower oil

Sunflower oil is the non-volatile oil pressed from the seeds of the sunflower (Helianthus annuus). It is commonly used as a frying oil and in cosmetic formulations. Sunflower oil is primarily composed of linoleic acid, a polyunsaturated fat, and oleic acid, a monounsaturated fat; through selective breeding and manufacturing, oils with differing proportions are produced.

Composition and types

Traditional sunflower oil is high in linoleic acid (about 48–74%). High-oleic varieties, bred in the late 20th century, contain 70% or more oleic acid and are more stable for frying. Mid-oleic types are also marketed. The oil is a rich source of vitamin E and contains lecithin, carotenoids, and waxes. Its smoke point for refined oil is about 232 °C (450 °F).

Production and uses

The sunflower is native to North America and was brought to Europe in the 16th century; large-scale oil extraction began in the Russian Empire in the 18th and 19th centuries, where the oil was prized during Lent because it was not prohibited by Orthodox fasting rules. Today the largest producers include Ukraine, Russia, and Argentina. Sunflower oil is used in cooking, margarine, and processed foods, and the press cake serves as livestock feed. It is also used in biodiesel production and in skin-care products as an emollient.
