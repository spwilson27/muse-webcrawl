Pōwhiri

A pōwhiri is a Māori welcome ceremony involving speeches, dancing, singing and finally the hongi (pressing of noses). It is performed both to welcome guests onto a marae and, in adapted form, at other gatherings such as the start of conferences, school terms and official functions in New Zealand.

The pōwhiri traditionally took place on the marae ātea (the open ground in front of the meeting house), where the hosts (tangata whenua) and visitors (manuhiri) determined each other's intentions. In modern settings, the ceremony may occur in a variety of locations, and it is used by many New Zealand organisations, schools and government agencies to formally welcome new staff, students and dignitaries.

Elements of the ceremony

  • Wero — a ritual challenge, traditionally performed by warriors with taiaha to determine whether visitors come in peace.
  • Karanga — a ceremonial call of welcome, usually performed by women, exchanged between the hosts and visitors as the visitors approach.
  • Whaikōrero — formal speeches, typically given by men, often following a pattern of greeting the dead, the house, and those present, and stating the purpose of the gathering.
  • Waiata tautoko — songs sung in support of each speaker, usually by the group standing behind them.
  • Koha — a gift or donation, customarily laid down by the visitors and picked up by the hosts.
  • Hongi and harirū — the pressing of noses (and shaking of hands), symbolising the sharing of breath and the conclusion of the formal separation between hosts and guests.
  • Hākari — a shared meal, which lifts the tapu of the ceremony and completes the welcome.

Protocol

Local protocol (tikanga) varies between iwi and hapū. In some areas women as well as men may speak during whaikōrero; the order of speakers and the role of the paepae (orators' bench) follow local custom. Visitors are typically briefed beforehand and may be asked to prepare a waiata to support their own speaker. The pōwhiri is characterised by the concepts of mana (prestige) and the establishment of relationships (whakawhanaungatanga) between hosts and guests.
