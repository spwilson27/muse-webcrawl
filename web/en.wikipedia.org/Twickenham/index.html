Twickenham is a suburban district in southwest London, England, on the River Thames, in the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames. Historically in Middlesex, it lies about 10 miles (16 km) southwest of central London.

History

Twickenham was a rural riverside parish favoured in the 18th century for the villas of aristocrats and writers; Alexander Pope's villa and grotto stood there, and Strawberry Hill House, Horace Walpole's Gothic Revival villa, survives as a landmark. Other notable houses include York House and Marble Hill House, set in riverside parkland.

Rugby

Twickenham Stadium, the largest dedicated rugby union stadium in the world with a capacity of about 82,000, is the home of the England national team and the Rugby Football Union, and hosts the annual Six Nations matches and major finals.
