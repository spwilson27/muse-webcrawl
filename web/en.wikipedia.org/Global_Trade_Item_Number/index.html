Global Trade Item Number

The Global Trade Item Number (GTIN) is a family of data structures administered by GS1 that uniquely identifies trade items — products and services — for use in barcodes and electronic commerce. GTINs appear as 8-, 12-, 13-, or 14-digit numbers (GTIN-8, GTIN-12, GTIN-13, GTIN-14), each ending in a modulo-10 check digit.

Formats

The GTIN-13 underlies the EAN-13 barcode used on most retail products outside North America, while the GTIN-12 is encoded in the UPC-A barcode common in the United States and Canada. GTIN-8 serves very small items (EAN-8), and GTIN-14 identifies cases and pallets of identical products, using an indicator digit to distinguish packaging levels. A GTIN combines a GS1 company prefix with an item reference assigned by the brand owner.

Use

Scanning a GTIN at the point of sale retrieves price and product data; the same identifier anchors product listings in online marketplaces, supply-chain messages, and recall systems. Because each variant of a product — size, colour, pack quantity — requires its own GTIN, the system functions as a worldwide "licence plate" for trade items.
