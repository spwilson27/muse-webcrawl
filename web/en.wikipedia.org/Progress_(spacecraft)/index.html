Progress (spacecraft)

Progress is a Russian expendable cargo spacecraft developed in the Soviet Union to resupply space stations. Derived from the crewed Soyuz, it first flew to Salyut 6 in 1978 and has since serviced Mir and the International Space Station.

Design and operation

Progress carries pressurized cargo, propellant, water, and air in three modules, docks automatically using the Kurs system, and can boost the station's orbit. After unloading, it is filled with waste and deorbited to burn up in the atmosphere. Versions include Progress 7K-TG, Progress-M, and Progress-MS, launched on Soyuz rockets from Baikonur. It remains a mainstay of ISS logistics alongside commercial cargo vehicles.
