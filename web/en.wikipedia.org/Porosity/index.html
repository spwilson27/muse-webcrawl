Porosity

Porosity is a measure of the void spaces in a material, expressed as the fraction of the material's total volume that is empty — a value between 0 and 1, or as a percentage between 0 and 100%. It is a fundamental property in geology, materials science, soil science, and engineering, governing how materials store and transmit fluids.

Pores are the void spaces within a material.

Types and measurement

Total porosity counts all voids, while effective porosity counts only interconnected pores through which fluid can flow — dead-end and isolated pores contribute to storage but not transport. Porosity is measured by techniques including helium pycnometry, mercury intrusion porosimetry, water saturation, and, in petroleum geology, well-log analysis and core examination. Values range from under 1 percent in dense crystalline rock to over 50 percent in some soils and chalks.

Applications

In hydrogeology and petroleum engineering, porosity together with permeability determines aquifer and reservoir quality: sandstones with porosity of 15–30 percent make good oil and gas reservoirs. Porosity also affects the strength, density, thermal conductivity, and acoustic properties of ceramics, metals, foams, filters, and building materials, and in soils it controls water retention and root growth. The porosity of a granular packing depends on grain size uniformity, shape, and compaction, described classically by models of sphere packing.
