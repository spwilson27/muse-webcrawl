Treaty of Craiova

The Treaty of Craiova (Bulgarian: Крайовска спогодба; Romanian: Tratatul de la Craiova) was signed on 7 September 1940 in Craiova, Romania, by the Kingdom of Romania and the Tsardom of Bulgaria. Under pressure from Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy following the Second Vienna Award, Romania returned Southern Dobruja — the "Quadrilateral" (Cadrilater) of the counties of Durostor and Caliacra — to Bulgaria, which had lost it after the Second Balkan War in 1913.

Terms and population exchange

The treaty provided for a compulsory population exchange: about 103,000 Romanians and Aromanians settled in Southern Dobruja moved north, while some 62,000 Bulgarians from Northern Dobruja moved south. Property losses were settled by a mixed commission, with compensation largely unfulfilled. Unlike other territorial revisions imposed on Romania in 1940, the Craiova settlement was confirmed after the Second World War by the Paris Peace Treaties of 1947, and the border it drew remains the frontier between Romania and Bulgaria today.
