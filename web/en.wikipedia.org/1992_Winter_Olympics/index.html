The 1992 Winter Olympics, officially known as the XVI Olympic Winter Games, were held in Albertville and the Savoie department of France from 8 to 23 February 1992. They were the last Winter Games to be held in the same year as the Summer Olympics, before the cycle was staggered so that Winter Games moved to even years between Summer Games.

Highlights

Events were spread across 13 venues in the Savoie, making these among the most geographically dispersed Winter Games: ski jumping and Nordic events at Courchevel, bobsleigh and luge at La Plagne, alpine skiing at Val d'Isère, Les Menuires and Méribel, and freestyle skiing at Tignes, where it appeared as a full medal sport for the first time.

Germany, newly reunified, topped the medal table with 26 medals including 10 golds. Post-Soviet athletes competed as the Unified Team, which won the ice hockey gold. Austria's Toni Nieminen became the youngest male winter Olympic champion at 16, winning two ski jumping golds, while Finland's and Norway's cross-country skiers dominated the Nordic events. Speed skating events produced world records from Bonnie Blair of the United States, who repeated her 500 m and 1,000 m double from 1988.
