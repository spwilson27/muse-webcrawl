The 2022 United Kingdom government crisis refers to the collapse of Boris Johnson's government in early July 2022, when an unprecedented wave of resignations by ministers and parliamentary aides forced the Prime Minister to announce his resignation. The term is also applied to the second crisis of that autumn, which ended Liz Truss's premiership after only 45 days.

The July crisis

Johnson's premiership had been weakened by the "Partygate" scandal over lockdown gatherings in Downing Street, for which he became the first sitting prime minister found to have broken the law. The final trigger was the Chris Pincher scandal: Pincher, the deputy chief whip, resigned on 30 June after allegations of sexual misconduct, and it emerged that Johnson had appointed him despite knowing of earlier allegations. On 5 July, Chancellor Rishi Sunak and Health Secretary Sajid Javid resigned within minutes of each other. Over the next two days more than sixty government members resigned — the largest such exodus in modern British history. Having initially vowed to fight on, Johnson announced on 7 July that he would resign as Conservative leader, remaining as caretaker prime minister until Liz Truss succeeded him on 6 September.

The October crisis

Truss's government unravelled after the "mini-budget" of 23 September 2022, delivered by Chancellor Kwasi Kwarteng, triggered a collapse in the pound, a surge in gilt yields and emergency Bank of England intervention. Kwarteng was sacked on 14 October; Home Secretary Suella Braverman resigned days later amid chaotic scenes over a fracking vote. Truss resigned on 20 October after 45 days in office — the shortest premiership in British history — and was succeeded by Rishi Sunak on 25 October. The two collapses together made 2022 a year of extraordinary political instability, with three prime ministers and four chancellors in a single calendar year.
