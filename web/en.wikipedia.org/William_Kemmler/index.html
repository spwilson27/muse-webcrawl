William Francis Kemmler (May 9, 1860 – August 6, 1890) was an American peddler who murdered his common-law wife, Matilda "Tillie" Ziegler, with a hatchet on March 29, 1889. He became the first person in the world executed by electric chair, at Auburn Prison in New York on August 6, 1890.

Background

New York had adopted electrocution as a supposedly more humane alternative to hanging, signing the Electrical Execution Law in 1888 amid the "war of the currents" between supporters of direct and alternating current. Kemmler's lawyers appealed on the grounds that electrocution constituted cruel and unusual punishment; the appeal, quietly backed by the Westinghouse company, whose alternating current was to be used, failed before the courts.

Execution

On the morning of August 6, 1890, Kemmler was strapped into the electric chair and a current of about 1,000 volts was applied for 17 seconds. When the current was switched off Kemmler was found to still be breathing, and the current had to be applied a second time at a higher voltage. Witnesses reported a gruesome scene, and the execution was widely described in the press as "far worse than hanging", although the state's officials maintained the new method would improve with practice.
