Sanctuary of Fátima

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sanctuary of Fátima

Location: Cova da Iria, Fátima, Ourém, Portugal

Commemorates: Marian apparitions of 1917

Visionaries: Lúcia dos Santos, Francisco and Jacinta Marto

Principal buildings: Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary (1953), Chapel of the Apparitions, Basilica of the Holy Trinity (2007)

Pilgrims: Several million per year

The Sanctuary of Fátima (Portuguese: Santuário de Fátima) is a Catholic pilgrimage complex at Cova da Iria in Fátima, central Portugal, built on the site where three shepherd children reported appearances of the Virgin Mary in 1917. One of the most important shrines in the Catholic world, it draws millions of pilgrims each year, especially on the anniversaries of the apparitions on 13 May and 13 October.

The apparitions

Between 13 May and 13 October 1917, Lúcia dos Santos (aged 10) and her cousins Francisco (9) and Jacinta Marto (7) reported six appearances of a lady “brighter than the sun” above a holm oak at Cova da Iria. The children said they were entrusted with three secrets, concerning a vision of hell, the end of the First World War and a coming greater war, and the consecration of Russia. The final apparition, on 13 October 1917, was witnessed by a crowd of tens of thousands and accompanied by the solar phenomenon known as the Miracle of the Sun. The Church declared the apparitions worthy of belief in 1930. Francisco and Jacinta, who died in the influenza pandemic, were canonised by Pope Francis in 2017, during the centenary pilgrimage.

The sanctuary

The little Chapel of the Apparitions (1922) stands on the exact site of the holm oak. The neo-baroque Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary, with its 65-metre tower, was consecrated in 1953 and contains the tombs of the three visionaries. Across the vast plaza, the minimalist Basilica of the Holy Trinity, opened in 2007, seats some 8,600 and is among the largest churches in the world. Popes Paul VI, John Paul II (who credited Our Lady of Fátima with saving his life after the 1981 assassination attempt and placed the bullet in her crown), Benedict XVI and Francis have all made pilgrimages to Fátima.
