The Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata (Spanish: Virreinato del Río de la Plata) was the last to be organized and also the shortest-lived of the four viceroyalties of the Spanish Empire in the Americas. It was created in 1776 by King Charles III, separating territories from the Viceroyalty of Peru, and comprised present-day Argentina, Bolivia, Paraguay, Uruguay and parts of Brazil and Chile, with its capital at Buenos Aires.

History

The viceroyalty was created to defend the southern Atlantic coast of South America against Portuguese and British encroachment and to improve imperial administration and trade. Buenos Aires, previously a backwater, grew rapidly as a legal Atlantic port. British invasions of the Río de la Plata in 1806 and 1807 were repelled largely by local forces, strengthening creole confidence. The May Revolution of 1810 deposed Viceroy Cisneros and began the Argentine War of Independence; royalist authority survived in Upper Peru and Montevideo for some years before the viceroyalty fully dissolved.
