Spoofing attack

In the context of information security, and especially network security, a spoofing attack is a situation in which a person or program successfully identifies as another by falsifying data, to gain an illegitimate advantage.

Types

Common spoofing attacks include IP address spoofing, in which packets are sent with a forged source address to conceal identity or impersonate another system; ARP spoofing on local networks; DNS spoofing, which corrupts name resolution; email spoofing, forging the sender address; and caller ID spoofing in telephony. GPS spoofing broadcasts false satellite signals to mislead receivers, and biometric spoofing attempts to fool fingerprint or facial recognition systems.

Prevention

Defenses include packet filtering, cryptographic authentication (such as TLS and email's SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records), multi-factor authentication, and anomaly detection. Spoofing is often a component of phishing and man-in-the-middle attacks.
