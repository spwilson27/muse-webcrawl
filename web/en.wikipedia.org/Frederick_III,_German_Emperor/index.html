Frederick III, German Emperor

Frederick III (German: Friedrich III.; October 18, 1831 – June 15, 1888) was German Emperor and King of Prussia for 99 days in 1888, the "Year of the Three Emperors." As Crown Prince for nearly three decades, he was a respected military commander in the wars of German unification and a figure of liberal hopes for the Empire.

Life and career

The only son of Wilhelm I and Augusta of Saxe-Weimar, Frederick married Victoria, eldest daughter of Queen Victoria, in 1858; their eldest son became Kaiser Wilhelm II. He distinguished himself commanding armies in the Second Schleswig War (1864), the Austro-Prussian War (1866), and the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71), where his Third Army won key victories at Wörth and Sedan and took part in the siege of Paris. Personally liberal in inclination and often at odds with Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, he spent the years of his father's long reign largely excluded from political influence.

Illness and brief reign

In 1887 Frederick developed throat cancer. Controversy surrounded his treatment: German doctors recommended removal of the larynx, while the British specialist Morell Mackenzie advised against the dangerous operation; a tracheotomy was performed in February 1888. When Wilhelm I died that March, Frederick succeeded already mortally ill and barely able to speak, communicating largely in writing. He died on June 15, 1888, after a reign of 99 days, and was succeeded by Wilhelm II. Whether a longer reign would have liberalised Germany remains one of history's debated counterfactuals.
