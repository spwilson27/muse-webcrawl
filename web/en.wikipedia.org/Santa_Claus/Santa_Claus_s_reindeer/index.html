Santa Claus's reindeer

In traditional Western Christmas folklore, Santa Claus's reindeer are the flying reindeer that pull Santa's sleigh through the night sky on Christmas Eve to deliver gifts to children around the world. The canonical team consists of eight reindeer, later joined by Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer as the ninth.

The eight reindeer

The eight reindeer were first named in Clement Clarke Moore's 1823 poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas" (commonly known as "'Twas the Night Before Christmas"): Dasher, Dancer, Prancer, Vixen, Comet, Cupid, Donder (later often rendered "Donner") and Blitzen. The names Donder and Blitzen derive from Dutch words for thunder and lightning. In the poem Santa calls out to his team, urging them "to the top of the porch! to the top of the wall!"

Rudolph

Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer was created by Robert L. May in a 1939 booklet for the Montgomery Ward department store, and popularized by Johnny Marks's 1949 song, famously recorded by Gene Autry. In the story, Rudolph's glowing red nose lights the way through foggy weather, earning him the lead position in the team — a permanent addition to the folklore.

Origins and popular culture

Reindeer were associated with Santa in print by 1821, before Moore's poem. Commentators have linked the flying reindeer to Norse mythology and to the reindeer-herding peoples of the Arctic. The reindeer appear in countless Christmas films, songs, and advertisements, and their names are a staple of holiday trivia.
