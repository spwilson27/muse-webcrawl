Santa Claus, also known as Father Christmas, Saint Nicholas, Saint Nick or Kris Kringle, is a legendary figure originating in Western Christian culture who is said to bring gifts during the late evening and overnight hours of Christmas Eve to well-behaved children. The modern figure of Santa is based on the Dutch Sinterklaas, itself derived from Saint Nicholas, the 4th-century Greek bishop of Myra famed for secret gift-giving, blended with the English Father Christmas and other folk traditions.

History

Saint Nicholas's feast day of December 6 was long associated with gift-giving in the Low Countries; Dutch settlers carried Sinterklaas to New Amsterdam (New York). In the early 19th century, Washington Irving's satirical history and Clement Clarke Moore's 1823 poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas" ("'Twas the Night Before Christmas") reshaped him as a jolly elf-like figure with eight reindeer. Cartoonist Thomas Nast gave him a North Pole home and workshop during the Civil War era, and Haddon Sundblom's Coca-Cola advertisements of the 1930s popularized his now-standard full-sized, red-suited appearance.

Traditions

Santa is said to live at the North Pole with Mrs. Claus, elves who make toys, and flying reindeer — including Rudolph, added in 1939 — who pull his sleigh. Children write letters to Santa, leave out milk and cookies, and find presents under the Christmas tree or in stockings. NORAD has "tracked" Santa's journey since 1955, a tradition begun by accident through a misprinted telephone number.
