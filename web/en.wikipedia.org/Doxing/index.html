Doxing

Doxing (also spelled doxxing) is the act of revealing identifying information about someone online, such as their real name, home address, workplace, phone number, financial information, or other private data, typically with malicious intent. The term is derived from "documents" or "docs" — originally referring to the practice of compiling and releasing a dossier of personal information about a target.

Doxing can be motivated by harassment, revenge, political disagreement, or extortion. It has been used against journalists, activists, celebrities, and private individuals. Victims may face unwanted contact, threats, identity theft, or physical danger as a result.

Methods

Doxers gather information from a variety of sources, including social media profiles, public records, data breaches, and social engineering. Techniques include searching publicly available databases, piecing together clues from photos and posts, and tricking service providers into disclosing account details. Some doxers use paid data-broker services that aggregate personal information.

Legality

The legality of doxing varies by jurisdiction. In some places, publishing certain categories of personal data, such as addresses or financial details, is a criminal offence. Several countries and U.S. states have enacted specific anti-doxing laws, particularly covering public officials, election workers, and law-enforcement officers. Platforms such as Twitter and Facebook prohibit doxing in their terms of service.

Prevention

Common advice for reducing the risk of being doxed includes limiting the personal information shared on social media, using privacy settings, removing data from people-search sites, using unique passwords and two-factor authentication, and being cautious about metadata in photos.
