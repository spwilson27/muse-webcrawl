Arne Næss
Born: 27 January 1912, Oslo, Norway
Died: 12 January 2009 (aged 96)
Known for: Deep ecology; ecosophy
Occupation: Philosopher, mountaineer

Arne Dekke Eide Næss (27 January 1912 – 12 January 2009) was a Norwegian philosopher and mountaineer, widely regarded as the founder of the deep ecology movement.

Life and work

Næss became Norway's youngest full professor of philosophy at the University of Oslo in 1939, at the age of 27, and held the chair until 1970. His early work dealt with the philosophy of science and semantics; he developed empirical semantics and influenced democratic political theory through studies of ideology.

Deep ecology

In 1973 Næss coined the distinction between "shallow" environmentalism, concerned chiefly with pollution and resource depletion for the benefit of people in developed countries, and "deep" ecology, which questions fundamental assumptions and affirms the intrinsic value of all living beings. With George Sessions he later drafted the eight-point Deep Ecology Platform. His personal philosophy, "Ecosophy T", was named after Tvergastein, his mountain hut.

Mountaineering and activism

A leading Norwegian climber, Næss led the first ascent of Tirich Mir (7,708 m) in Pakistan in 1950. He was active in non-violent environmental protest, including the 1970 Mardøla demonstration against a hydroelectric project.
