Kouros

Kouros
MeaningAncient Greek: "youth, boy"
PeriodArchaic Greece, c. 650–480 BCE
MaterialUsually marble; also limestone or bronze

A kouros (plural kouroi) is the modern term for a type of free-standing ancient Greek sculpture of a nude male youth, produced during the Archaic period (roughly 650 to 480 BCE). Kouroi served as grave markers and as votive offerings in sanctuaries, and are often interpreted as representations of the god Apollo or of idealised young men.

Characteristics

The kouros stands frontally with arms at the sides, fists clenched, and the left foot advanced, a pose influenced by Egyptian statuary. Early examples are rigid and patterned, with stylised hair and the characteristic "Archaic smile"; over time sculptors rendered anatomy more naturalistically, a development illustrated by the Sounion Kouros, the Kroisos Kouros from Anavyssos, and the late Aristodikos.

Function and interpretation

Inscriptions on their bases show kouroi commemorated the dead (as with Kroisos, "fallen in the front ranks") or were dedicated to gods. The female counterpart is the korē, depicted clothed. The kouros type disappeared after the Persian Wars as Classical sculpture introduced contrapposto, exemplified by the Kritios Boy.
