Nadezhda Andreyevna Tolokonnikova (Russian: Надежда Андреевна Толоконникова; born 7 November 1989), nicknamed Nadya Tolokno, is a Russian artist, musician and political activist. She is a co-founder of the feminist protest art collective Pussy Riot and of the independent news outlet Mediazona, and has been recognised by Amnesty International as a prisoner of conscience.

Early life and Voina

Tolokonnikova was born in Norilsk and studied philosophy at Moscow State University. She became involved in the radical street-art collective Voina (War), participating in provocative performance actions, before helping found Pussy Riot in 2011.

Pussy Riot and imprisonment

On 21 February 2012, Tolokonnikova and other Pussy Riot members performed a "punk prayer" at the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour in Moscow protesting the Russian Orthodox Church's support for Vladimir Putin. She was arrested and, on 17 August 2012, convicted of "hooliganism motivated by religious hatred" and sentenced to two years in prison. In autumn 2013 she went on hunger strike protesting conditions in the Mordovian penal colony IK-14, alleging forced labour, and was released in December 2013 under a general amnesty, two months before her sentence expired.

Later work

After her release, Tolokonnikova campaigned for prisoners' rights, co-founding the prisoners' rights organisation Zona Prava and the news site Mediazona with Maria Alyokhina. She published the memoir-manifesto Read & Riot: A Pussy Riot Guide to Activism (2018) and staged immersive art projects including "Inside Pussy Riot" in London. In 2021–2023 she co-founded UnicornDAO and raised funds via NFTs for Ukrainian relief and reproductive rights. In 2023 Russia placed her on a wanted list and sentenced her in absentia; she lives in exile.
