Arlington National Cemetery

Arlington National Cemetery
Location: Arlington County, Virginia, USA
Established: 1864
Area: approx. 639 acres (259 ha)
Administered by the U.S. Department of the Army

Arlington National Cemetery is the largest cemetery in the United States National Cemetery System and the resting place of more than 400,000 active duty service members, veterans, and their families. It lies in Arlington County, Virginia, across the Potomac River from Washington, D.C.

History

The cemetery was established during the American Civil War on the estate of Confederate general Robert E. Lee. The first military burials took place in May 1864. The estate, Arlington House, had been built by George Washington Parke Custis and was the home of Lee and his wife Mary Anna Custis Lee.

Notable sites

Landmarks include the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, guarded continuously by sentinels of the 3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment ("The Old Guard") with a ceremonial changing of the guard; the gravesite of President John F. Kennedy, marked by an eternal flame; the Memorial Amphitheater; and the USS Maine Mast Memorial.

Burial eligibility

Eligibility for in-ground burial at Arlington is restricted largely to active-duty members, retirees, recipients of certain high decorations, and former prisoners of war, along with their eligible dependents.
