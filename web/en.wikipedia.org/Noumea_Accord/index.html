The Nouméa Accord (French: Accord de Nouméa) is an agreement signed on 5 May 1998 in Nouméa, New Caledonia, between the French government and the pro-independence Kanak and Socialist National Liberation Front (FLNKS) and the loyalist RPCR. It set New Caledonia on a path of progressive decolonisation and shared sovereignty.

Provisions

Building on the 1988 Matignon Agreements, the Accord provided for a 20-year transition during which powers would be irreversibly transferred from France to New Caledonia in stages, with France retaining defence, foreign affairs, currency, justice and public order. It recognised the Kanak people's "shadow of colonisation", restricted the local electoral roll for key votes to long-term residents, and created New Caledonian citizenship alongside French citizenship.

Referendums

The Accord scheduled up to three independence referendums. Voters chose to remain with France in 2018 (56.7% against independence) and 2020 (53.3%). A third referendum in December 2021, boycotted by pro-independence parties during the COVID-19 pandemic, returned 96.5% against independence on a low turnout, and its legitimacy remains contested.
