Urethra

Urethra
SystemUrinary system
FunctionConveys urine from the bladder to the exterior; in males also conveys semen
LengthAbout 18–20 cm in males; about 4 cm in females
Opens atExternal urethral orifice (meatus)

The urethra (from Greek ourḗthrā) is the tube that connects the urinary bladder to the external urethral meatus for the removal of urine from the body of both females and males. In human females and other primates, the urethra connects to the urinary meatus above the vagina. In human males, the longer urethra passes through the prostate and the penis and additionally serves as a conduit for semen during ejaculation.

Anatomy

The male urethra is about 18–20 cm long and is conventionally divided into prostatic, membranous and spongy (penile) parts; it begins at the internal urethral orifice in the bladder, traverses the prostate (where the ejaculatory ducts open), pierces the perineal membrane, and runs the length of the penis within the corpus spongiosum. The female urethra is much shorter — about 4 cm — embedded in the anterior vaginal wall and opening in the vestibule; its shortness partly explains women's higher incidence of urinary tract infections.

Function and sphincters

Urine flow is controlled by the involuntary internal urethral sphincter at the bladder neck and the voluntary external sphincter of skeletal muscle. During male ejaculation the internal sphincter closes to prevent urine flow and retrograde ejaculation.

Clinical significance

Conditions affecting the urethra include urethritis (often infectious), urethral strictures from scarring, congenital abnormalities such as hypospadias, and trauma. Catheterisation passes a tube through the urethra to drain the bladder, and urethroscopy allows direct inspection.
