Water supply

Water supply is the provision of water by public utilities, commercial organisations, community endeavours or by individuals, usually via a system of pumps and pipes. Public water supply systems are crucial to properly functioning societies. These systems are what supply drinking water to populations around the globe.

System components

A water supply system draws raw water from sources — rivers, lakes, reservoirs, and aquifers — treats it to drinking-water standards, stores it in service reservoirs and water towers, and distributes it through trunk mains and local pipes to homes, businesses, and fire hydrants. Pressure is maintained by gravity and pumping stations.

Access

Access to safely managed drinking water has expanded steadily but remains incomplete: the WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme reports that billions of people still lack safely managed services, relying instead on wells, standpipes, tanker trucks, or surface water. Improving supply is a target of Sustainable Development Goal 6.

Challenges

Supply systems face ageing infrastructure, leakage (non-revenue water), contamination events, energy costs, and the strain of drought and population growth. Utilities respond with metering, mains renewal, source protection, and diversified sources including reuse and desalination.
