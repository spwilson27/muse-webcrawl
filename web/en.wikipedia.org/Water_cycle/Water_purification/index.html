Water purification

Water purification is the process of removing undesirable chemicals, biological contaminants, suspended solids, and gases from water. The goal is to produce water that is fit for specific purposes. Most water is purified and disinfected for human consumption (drinking water), but water purification may also be carried out for a variety of other purposes, including medical, pharmacological, chemical, and industrial applications.

Methods

Physical processes such as filtration, sedimentation, and distillation are combined with biological processes such as slow sand filters or biologically active carbon, and chemical processes such as flocculation and chlorination. In a conventional treatment train, raw water is coagulated so suspended matter clumps into flocs, settled, filtered through sand or membranes, and finally disinfected — typically with chlorine, chloramine, ozone, or ultraviolet light.

Standards and safety

Purified drinking water must meet standards set by national regulators and the World Health Organization's Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality. Treated water is monitored for turbidity, microbial indicators such as E. coli, residual disinfectant, and chemical contaminants including lead and nitrates.

Point-of-use purification

At household scale, purification methods include boiling, activated-carbon filters, reverse osmosis, distillation, and solar disinfection — important in regions without reliable municipal supplies.
