Alpha (finance)

Alpha (α) is a measure of the active return on an investment, the performance of that investment compared with a suitable market index. The alpha of an investment is its excess return relative to the return of the benchmark index, adjusted for risk, and is widely used to evaluate the performance of fund managers.

Definition

In the capital asset pricing model (CAPM), alpha (often called Jensen's alpha when measured per that model) is the intercept of the regression of the asset's excess returns on the market's excess returns. A positive alpha indicates the investment performed better than predicted by its beta (market risk), while a negative alpha indicates underperformance.

Use in practice

Investors seeking active returns look for securities or funds with positive alpha. Because fees reduce returns, many studies find that average alpha across actively managed funds is near zero or negative after costs, a point frequently raised in debates about active versus passive investing.
