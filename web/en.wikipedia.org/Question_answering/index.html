Question answering

Question answering (QA) is a field within computer science and natural language processing concerned with building systems that automatically answer questions posed by humans in natural language. Unlike search engines, which return documents, QA systems aim to return concise, direct answers.

History

Early systems such as BASEBALL (1961) and LUNAR (1970s) answered questions over narrow structured domains. The TREC question answering track, begun in 1999, drove progress on open-domain QA over large text collections. IBM's Watson system famously defeated human champions on the quiz show Jeopardy! in 2011. Since the late 2010s, neural models based on the transformer architecture, trained on datasets such as SQuAD, have achieved human-level performance on reading-comprehension benchmarks, and large language models have made conversational QA broadly available.

Approaches

Systems may be extractive (selecting an answer span from a passage), abstractive (generating an answer in new words), or knowledge-based (querying structured databases or knowledge graphs). Typical pipelines combine document retrieval with a reader model; retrieval-augmented generation couples search with a generative model.

Evaluation

Common metrics include exact match and token-level F1 on benchmarks such as SQuAD, Natural Questions, and TriviaQA. Open challenges include multi-hop reasoning, factuality and hallucination, and answering questions over tables, images, and other modalities.
