The horse (Equus ferus caballus) is a domesticated, one-toed, hoofed mammal. It belongs to the taxonomic family Equidae and is one of two extant subspecies of Equus ferus. The horse has evolved over the past 45 to 55 million years from a small multi-toed creature, close to Eohippus, into the large, single-toed animal of today. Humans began domesticating horses around 4000 BCE, and their domestication is believed to have been widespread by 3000 BCE.

Biology

Horses are adapted to running, allowing them to quickly escape predators, and possess an excellent sense of balance and a strong fight-or-flight response. They are herbivorous grazers with a hindgut fermentation digestive system. Horse breeds are loosely divided into three categories based on general temperament: spirited "hot bloods", "cold bloods" such as draft horses, and "warmbloods" developed from crosses between the two.

Domestication and uses

Horses have been central to human history in warfare, agriculture, transportation, sport, and companionship. Selective breeding has produced more than 300 breeds worldwide, ranging from miniature horses to massive draft breeds. Horses are used in equestrian sports including racing, dressage, and show jumping, and continue to work in agriculture, policing, and therapy.

Care and management

Domestic horses require shelter, fodder, water, hoof care from a farrier, and regular veterinary attention. Their age can be estimated by examining the teeth. A horse's height is measured in hands, one hand being four inches.
