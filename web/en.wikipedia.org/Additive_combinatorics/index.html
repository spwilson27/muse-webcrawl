Additive combinatorics

Additive combinatorics is an area of combinatorics in mathematics that studies combinatorial estimates of the size of sumsets and related structures in abelian groups and other algebraic settings. One of the central results of additive combinatorics is the study of the interplay between the additive and multiplicative structure of subsets of fields and groups, and the field blends methods from combinatorics, harmonic analysis, number theory and ergodic theory.

Basic objects

For subsets A and B of an abelian group, the sumset is A + B = {a + b : a ∈ A, b ∈ B}, and the difference set is A − B. A trivial bound gives max(|A|, |B|) ≤ |A + B| ≤ |A||B|. The subject studies how close a set is to these extremes: sets with small doubling (|A + A| close to 2|A|) must have arithmetic structure, while sets with large doubling behave more randomly.

Key results

  • Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequalities, relating the sizes of iterated sumsets to the doubling constant.
  • Freiman's theorem: a finite set of integers with small doubling is contained in a low-dimensional generalised arithmetic progression of comparable size.
  • Szemerédi's theorem on long arithmetic progressions in dense sets of integers, and its finitary and polynomial generalisations.
  • The sum–product phenomenon (Erdős–Szemerédi, and Bourgain–Katz–Tao in finite fields): a set of numbers cannot be simultaneously additively and multiplicatively structured.
  • Gowers uniformity norms and higher-order Fourier analysis, developed to prove results such as the Green–Tao theorem on arithmetic progressions in the primes.

Terence Tao's monograph Additive Combinatorics (with Van Vu, 2006) helped establish the field's modern identity.
