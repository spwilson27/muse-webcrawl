Market risk

Market risk is the risk of losses in positions arising from movements in market variables — interest rates, equity prices, foreign exchange rates and commodity prices. It is also referred to as systematic risk, in contrast to the specific risk of individual securities.

Types

The principal categories are interest rate risk, equity risk, currency risk and commodity risk, along with related risks such as volatility risk and correlation risk in portfolios of derivatives.

Measurement

Firms measure market risk with sensitivities (duration, convexity, the "Greeks"), scenario analysis and stress testing, and statistical measures such as value at risk (VaR), which estimates the maximum loss over a horizon at a given confidence level, and expected shortfall, which averages losses beyond VaR.

Regulation

Bank capital regulation under the Basel framework requires capital against market risk in the trading book; the Fundamental Review of the Trading Book (FRTB) tightened these standards after the 2008 financial crisis exposed the limits of VaR-based models.

Management

Market risk is managed through diversification, position limits, hedging with derivatives, and stop-loss policies, though it cannot be eliminated entirely because it stems from economy-wide factors.
