12 Downing Street

12 Downing Street is one of the buildings situated on Downing Street in the City of Westminster in London, England. Historically the official residence of the First Lord of the Treasury and later associated with the Chief Whip, the building is interconnected with 10 Downing Street and 11 Downing Street and forms part of the complex at the heart of the British government.

History

The houses on Downing Street were built in the 1680s by Sir George Downing. Number 12 was originally used for various government purposes and by the 19th century housed the Colonial Office briefly, then the offices of the government's Chief Whip. In practice, the Prime Minister's press and communications operations have long been based in the building, and for periods the Downing Street Press Office and the Prime Minister's official spokesperson worked from No. 12.

Current use

Following reorganisation, No. 12 has housed elements of the Prime Minister's Office, and a media suite in the building has been used for televised press briefings. The three houses — 10, 11 and 12 — communicate internally, and the flat above No. 11 has in recent decades often been occupied by the Prime Minister instead of the smaller flat at No. 10.

Architecture

Like its neighbours, No. 12 is a terraced house of yellow London stock brick (long since blackened by pollution and painted dark), fronting a street closed to the public since 1989 by security gates. Internally the buildings have been repeatedly remodelled, most substantially during the reconstruction of 10–12 Downing Street in the early 1960s.
