Sales tax

A sales tax is a consumption tax imposed by a government on the sale of goods and services. A conventional or retail sales tax is levied on the final sale to the end consumer and is usually calculated as a percentage of the purchase price, collected by the retailer at the point of sale and remitted to the taxing authority. Sales taxes are a major source of revenue for state and local governments in the United States and exist in various forms in many other countries, though most nations rely instead on a value-added tax (VAT) applied at each stage of production and distribution.

Types

The classic retail sales tax applies only once, at the final purchase, with business inputs exempted — typically through resale certificates — to avoid tax pyramiding. Related levies include use taxes, which complement the sales tax on goods bought out of jurisdiction but used locally; excise taxes on specific goods such as fuel, alcohol, and tobacco; gross receipts taxes on the total revenue of businesses; and seller's privilege or transaction taxes found in a few jurisdictions.

Sales taxes in the United States

The United States has no federal sales tax. Forty-five states and the District of Columbia levy statewide sales taxes, and thousands of counties, cities, and special districts add their own, producing more than ten thousand distinct taxing jurisdictions with differing rates, bases, and exemptions. Combined rates exceed 10% in some localities. Many states exempt groceries, prescription drugs, or clothing to reduce regressivity, and most hold sales-tax holidays on specified goods. Following the Supreme Court's decision in South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018), states may require out-of-state and online sellers with sufficient economic presence to collect the tax.

Economic analysis

Economists note that sales taxes are relatively efficient to collect and hard to evade at the retail level, but they are generally regressive, since lower-income households spend a larger share of income on taxable consumption. Exemptions for necessities, refundable credits, or reliance on VAT with broad bases are common policy responses. Because rates vary across jurisdictions, sales taxes can distort shopping location choices near borders and encourage cross-border and remote purchasing.

History

Taxes on sales date to antiquity — Rome levied the centesima rerum venalium — and general sales taxes were adopted by many European states in the early twentieth century. Most were later converted into value-added taxes; the retail sales tax survives chiefly in North America.
