St. George Island (Alaska)

St. George Island (Aleut: Anmorasi) is one of the Pribilof Islands of the U.S. state of Alaska, lying in the Bering Sea. It is the second-largest of the Pribilofs by area and hosts the community of St. George, a city of under 100 residents. The island's cliffs are a major breeding site for seabirds and its shores host one of the largest northern fur seal colonies in the world.

History

The island was sighted by Russian navigator Gavriil Pribylov in 1786 while searching for fur seal rookeries, and named for his ship, the St. George. The Russian-American Company relocated Aleut (Unangax̂) families to the island to harvest fur seals, a trade that continued after the United States purchased Alaska in 1867. During the Second World War the island's residents were evacuated to internment camps in Southeast Alaska.

Wildlife

St. George is renowned among birdwatchers: its high cliffs host vast colonies of red-legged kittiwakes, thick-billed murres and other seabirds, and vagrant birds from Asia are regularly recorded. The island rises to over 300 metres at High Bluffs, the highest point in the Pribilofs.
