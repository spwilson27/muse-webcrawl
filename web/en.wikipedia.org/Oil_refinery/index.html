Oil refinery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An oil refinery or petroleum refinery is an industrial process plant where petroleum (crude oil) is transformed and refined into products such as gasoline (petrol), diesel fuel, kerosene and jet fuel, liquefied petroleum gas, heating oil, lubricants, bitumen and petrochemical feedstocks. Refineries are typically large, sprawling complexes with extensive piping, and are usually located near coastal ports, pipeline terminals and centres of demand.

Processes

The first stage is distillation: crude oil is heated and separated in a fractionating column into fractions by boiling range. Subsequent conversion processes rearrange molecules to increase the yield of valuable products — catalytic cracking and hydrocracking break large hydrocarbons into smaller ones; catalytic reforming raises the octane of naphtha; alkylation combines light gases into petrol blendstock. Treatment processes such as hydrodesulfurisation remove sulphur and other impurities, and blending produces finished fuels to specification. The largest refineries process more than a million barrels per day; the Jamnagar refinery in India is the world's largest complex.

Economics and environment

Refinery profitability is measured by the "crack spread" — the difference between crude costs and product prices. Refineries emit greenhouse gases and air pollutants, consume large amounts of water and energy, and can be hazardous workplaces; accidents such as Texas City (2005) have prompted stricter process-safety regulation. The energy transition is reshaping the industry, with some refineries converting to biofuels. See also petroleum, fractional distillation and cracking.
