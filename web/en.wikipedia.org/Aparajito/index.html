Aparajito (Bengali: অপরাজিত, "The Unvanquished") is a 1956 Indian Bengali-language drama film written and directed by Satyajit Ray. It is the second part of the celebrated Apu Trilogy, following Pather Panchali (1955) and preceding Apur Sansar (1959). Adapted from the second half of Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay's novel Pather Panchali and part of Aparajito, the film follows Apu from childhood in Varanasi through his schooling and student life in Calcutta, and his deepening estrangement from his devoted mother, Sarbajaya.

Plot

The Roy family — Harihar, Sarbajaya, and their young son Apu — have settled in Varanasi, where Harihar preaches at the ghats. When Harihar falls ill and dies, Sarbajaya takes work as a cook, and the family eventually moves to a village in Bengal. There Apu begins school and shows such promise that he wins a scholarship to study in Calcutta. Increasingly absorbed in the wider world, he visits home less and less often. Sarbajaya, lonely and ailing, hopes in vain for his return; she dies before he can reach her. Apu performs her funeral rites and returns to the city, determined to make his own way.

Production

Ray made the film with the same core collaborators as Pather Panchali: cinematographer Subrata Mitra, art director Bansi Chandragupta, and composer Ravi Shankar, whose sitar score underpins some of the film's most famous sequences. Karuna Banerjee reprised her role as Sarbajaya; the adolescent Apu was played by Smaran Ghosal.

Reception and legacy

Aparajito won the Golden Lion at the 1957 Venice Film Festival, together with the FIPRESCI Prize and other honours, confirming Ray's international standing. Time magazine later included it in its "All-Time 100 Movies" list, and critics have called the mother–son relationship at its heart among the most moving in cinema. While sometimes considered the least celebrated entry of the trilogy in India, it is widely regarded elsewhere as its emotional summit.
