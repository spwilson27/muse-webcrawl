Renault Formula One crash controversy

The Renault Formula One crash controversy, often called "Crashgate", concerned allegations that Renault F1 driver Nelson Piquet Jr. deliberately crashed during the 2008 Singapore Grand Prix to help his teammate Fernando Alonso, who went on to win the race. It was one of the most serious cheating scandals in Formula One history.

The incident

At the inaugural Singapore night race on 28 September 2008, Alonso made an unusually early pit stop on lap 12. On lap 14, Piquet crashed into the wall at Turn 17 — a corner with no nearby crane, guaranteeing a safety car period. The safety car bunched the field and shuffled the pit sequence, propelling Alonso to the front; he won the race.

Exposure and investigation

After Renault dropped Piquet in mid-2009, he alleged to the FIA that team principal Flavio Briatore and engineering director Pat Symonds had asked him to crash deliberately at that corner. Leaked statements led the FIA to charge Renault with conspiracy and race-fixing. On 16 September 2009, Renault announced it would not contest the charges, and Briatore and Symonds left the team. On 21 September 2009, the FIA World Motor Sport Council handed Renault a disqualification suspended for two years; Briatore was banned indefinitely from FIA-sanctioned events and Symonds for five years. Those lifetime bans were later overturned by a French court in 2010, though both men agreed not to work in Formula One for a period. Alonso, who denied knowledge of the scheme, was not punished.

Aftermath

The scandal cost Renault title sponsor ING and other backers, hastened Renault's withdrawal as a works team, and prompted lasting changes to the safety-car and pit-lane rules. Felipe Massa, who lost the 2008 championship by one point after his race was ruined by the safety car period and a botched pit stop, later pursued legal action over the handling of the affair.
