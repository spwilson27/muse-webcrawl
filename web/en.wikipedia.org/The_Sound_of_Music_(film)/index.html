The Sound of Music (film)

The Sound of Music is a 1965 American musical drama film produced and directed by Robert Wise, starring Julie Andrews and Christopher Plummer. It adapts the 1959 Rodgers and Hammerstein stage musical, itself based on Maria von Trapp's memoir.

Plot

In 1938 Salzburg, novice Maria becomes governess to the seven children of widowed naval captain Georg von Trapp, teaching them to sing and restoring joy to the household. Maria and Georg fall in love and marry; after the Anschluss, the family escapes Nazi pressure by fleeing over the mountains — in reality they left by train, a liberty the film takes. Songs include "My Favorite Things," "Do-Re-Mi," "Edelweiss" and the title song.

Reception

The film won five Academy Awards including Best Picture and became one of the highest-grossing films of all time adjusted for inflation. See also The Sleeping Beauty.
