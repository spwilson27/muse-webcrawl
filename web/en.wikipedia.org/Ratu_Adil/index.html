Ratu Adil

Ratu Adil, literally the "Just King" (or "Just Queen", as ratu is gender-neutral in Javanese), is a messianic figure in Javanese folklore and prophecy. According to the prophecies attributed to the 12th-century Kediri king Jayabaya, a time of great suffering and disorder will be followed by the coming of the Ratu Adil, a righteous ruler who will restore justice, prosperity, and harmony to Java.

Origins

The prophecy is associated with the Jangka Jayabaya, a body of prophetic literature ascribed to Jayabaya (r. 1135–1157), the king of Kediri famous in Javanese tradition. The prophecies describe cycles of history (zaman edan, the "mad age", among them) and predict that after an age of calamity a just king will emerge, often said to come from humble circumstances and to appear at a time of catastrophe.

Role in Indonesian history

Expectation of the Ratu Adil repeatedly fuelled peasant revolts and anti-colonial movements in Java under Dutch rule. Prince Diponegoro, leader of the Java War (1825–1830), was widely regarded by his followers as the Ratu Adil. Millenarian movements invoking the prophecy recurred through the 19th and early 20th centuries, and leaders of the Indonesian independence movement, including Sukarno, were sometimes cast in the role by their supporters. The idea remains a recurring motif in Indonesian political culture, invoked during periods of crisis such as the fall of Suharto in 1998.
