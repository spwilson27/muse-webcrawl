Constantine XI Dragases Palaiologos (8 February 1405 – 29 May 1453) was the last Byzantine emperor, reigning from 1449 until his death in battle at the fall of Constantinople on 29 May 1453. His death marked the definitive end of the Roman Empire, which had lasted for nearly 1,500 years since Augustus — and, counting the Roman Republic, over two thousand.

Early career

Constantine was the fourth son of Emperor Manuel II and the Serbian princess Helena Dragaš. He spent most of his career in the Morea (the Peloponnese), the empire's most prosperous province, where as despot he campaigned successfully against the Latin states of Greece and briefly rebuilt the Hexamilion wall across the Isthmus of Corinth before Ottoman raids undid his gains.

Reign

On the death of his brother John VIII in 1448, Constantine was crowned at Mistra on 6 January 1449 and entered Constantinople — by then little more than a city-state surrounded by Ottoman territory. He sought Western aid by affirming the union of the Greek and Latin churches, agreed at Ferrara-Florence, which was deeply unpopular with his subjects. The young Sultan Mehmed II, who came to the throne in 1451, prepared methodically to take the city, building the fortress of Rumeli Hisarı on the Bosphorus in 1452.

The siege and death

In April 1453 Mehmed invested Constantinople with an army of perhaps 80,000 men and a fleet, together with giant bombards that battered the Theodosian Walls. Constantine commanded a garrison of only about 7,000 men, including some 700 Genoese under Giovanni Giustiniani. When the final assault came before dawn on 29 May, the emperor died fighting — according to tradition, casting off his imperial insignia and rushing into the melee. His body was never securely identified. Constantine is venerated as a saint and national hero in Greece, and legends held that he would one day return to reclaim his city.
