Wangari Muta Maathai (1 April 1940 – 25 September 2011) was a Kenyan environmental and political activist. She founded the Green Belt Movement, an environmental non-governmental organization focused on tree planting, environmental conservation, and women's rights. In 2004 she became the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize, honoured "for her contribution to sustainable development, democracy and peace".

Early life and education

Maathai was born in the village of Ihithe in Tetu, Nyeri District, in the central highlands of colonial Kenya. Educated by Catholic missionaries, she won a Kennedy Airlift scholarship to study in the United States, earning a bachelor's degree in biology from Mount St. Scholastica College (Benedictine College) in Kansas (1964) and a master's degree from the University of Pittsburgh (1966). She returned to Kenya and earned a PhD in veterinary anatomy from the University of Nairobi in 1971 — the first woman in East and Central Africa to earn a doctorate. She went on to chair the Department of Veterinary Anatomy at Nairobi, another first for a woman in the region.

Green Belt Movement

In 1977 Maathai founded the Green Belt Movement under the auspices of the National Council of Women of Kenya. The movement organized rural women to plant trees — to combat deforestation, soil erosion, and desertification while providing firewood and income — and grew into a pan-African network. By the time of her death, tens of millions of trees had been planted across Kenya. The movement also ran civic and environmental education seminars, linking environmental degradation to governance and human rights.

Political activism

Maathai became a prominent voice for democracy during the authoritarian rule of President Daniel arap Moi. In 1989 she led a campaign that stopped the construction of a 60-storey tower complex in Nairobi's Uhuru Park, and in 1992 she was beaten unconscious by police while protesting at "Freedom Corner" with mothers of political prisoners. She endured arrests, assaults, and vilification in the state press. After Kenya's transition to multiparty politics, she was elected to Parliament in 2002 as the member for Tetu and served as Assistant Minister for Environment and Natural Resources in President Mwai Kibaki's government from 2003 to 2005.

Later life and legacy

Maathai authored several books, including The Green Belt Movement (2003), the memoir Unbowed (2006), The Challenge for Africa (2009), and Replenishing the Earth (2010). She served on international bodies including the UN Secretary-General's advisory efforts on disarmament and climate. She died of complications from ovarian cancer in Nairobi on 25 September 2011 and was given a state funeral. The Wangari Maathai Institute for Peace and Environmental Studies at the University of Nairobi and Wangari Maathai Forest in Nairobi carry her name.
