John McLaughlin (born 4 January 1942) is an English guitarist, bandleader and composer. A pioneer of jazz fusion, he combined jazz with rock, Indian classical music, Western classical music and flamenco. He played on Miles Davis's landmark albums In a Silent Way (1969) and Bitches Brew (1970), and led the Mahavishnu Orchestra, whose virtuosic fusion made him one of the most influential guitarists of the 1970s.

Early career

Born in Doncaster, McLaughlin worked in London in the 1960s with Georgie Fame, Graham Bond and Brian Auger, and taught guitar to a young Jimmy Page. In 1969 he moved to New York, joining Tony Williams's Lifetime and then Miles Davis's band. His first solo album, Extrapolation (1969), and Devotion (1970) established his reputation.

Mahavishnu Orchestra and Shakti

In 1971 McLaughlin, a devotee of the guru Sri Chinmoy who gave him the name "Mahavishnu," formed the Mahavishnu Orchestra. Albums such as The Inner Mounting Flame (1971) and Birds of Fire (1973) fused high-volume rock with complex time signatures and modal improvisation. With the acoustic group Shakti (1975–77), alongside violinist L. Shankar and tabla player Zakir Hussain, he pioneered an East–West fusion rooted in Indian raga and tala. His 1981 album Friday Night in San Francisco, with Al Di Meola and Paco de Lucía, became a guitar landmark.

Later work

McLaughlin continued to tour and record into his eighties, leading the 4th Dimension band and releasing albums including Black Light (2015) and the Grammy-winning live material with Remember Shakti. Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time.
