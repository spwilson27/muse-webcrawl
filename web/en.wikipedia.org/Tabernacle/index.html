Tabernacle

The Tabernacle (Hebrew: מִשְׁכָּן, mishkan, "residence" or "dwelling place") was, according to the Hebrew Bible, the portable earthly dwelling place of Yahweh used by the Israelites from the Exodus until the conquest of Canaan. Moses was instructed at Mount Sinai to construct and transport the Tabernacle with the Israelites on their journey through the wilderness and their subsequent conquest of the Promised Land.

The fullest description of the Tabernacle appears in the Book of Exodus. It describes an inner shrine housing the Ark of the Covenant — the Holy of Holies — veiled by a curtain, and an outer chamber (the "Holy Place") containing a golden menorah, a table for showbread and an altar of incense. A courtyard surrounded the structure, containing an altar for burnt offerings and a bronze laver.

Later history

After the conquest, the Tabernacle stood at Shiloh for centuries, before its functions were absorbed into Solomon's Temple in Jerusalem. In later usage the word "tabernacle" has been applied to other worship spaces, including the container in Catholic churches where consecrated hosts are reserved, and the meeting halls of some Latter-day Saint congregations.
