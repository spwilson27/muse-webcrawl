The RBMK (Russian: реактор большой мощности канальный, reaktor bolshoy moshchnosti kanalnyy, "high-power channel-type reactor") is a class of graphite-moderated nuclear power reactors designed and built by the Soviet Union. It is moderated by graphite blocks and cooled by boiling light water flowing through individual pressure tubes containing the fuel.

Design

The RBMK-1000 produces 1,000 MWe from about 3,200 MW of thermal power. Its design descends from plutonium-production reactors, allowed online refuelling, and lacked a full containment building. Notable characteristics include a large positive void coefficient at low power and control rods whose graphite displacer tips could briefly increase reactivity when inserted from fully withdrawn positions.

Chernobyl disaster

Unit 4 at the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant, an RBMK-1000, exploded on 26 April 1986 during a safety test, in the worst nuclear accident in history. Investigations attributed the disaster to a combination of the reactor's design flaws and operator actions during the test.

Aftermath and remaining units

After Chernobyl, surviving RBMKs were retrofitted with faster scram systems, redesigned control rods, increased fuel enrichment, and other measures to reduce the void coefficient. Units continued operating in Russia (Kursk, Smolensk, Leningrad) and Lithuania's Ignalina plant (RBMK-1500) closed its last unit in 2009 as a condition of EU accession.
