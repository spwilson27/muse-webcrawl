Climate change denial

Climate change denial, or global warming denial, is the rejection, dismissal, or unwarranted doubt of the scientific consensus on climate change, including the extent to which it is caused by humans, its impacts on nature and human society, or the potential for adaptation. Those promoting denial commonly use rhetorical tactics to give the appearance of a scientific controversy where there is none, and some campaigns have been documented as organised efforts funded by fossil fuel interests and conservative foundations to manufacture uncertainty and delay policy action.

Forms of denial

Scholars distinguish several kinds of denial: trend denial (the Earth is not warming), attribution denial (humans are not the cause), impact denial (the consequences will not be serious or may be beneficial), and policy denial (action is unnecessary, impossible, or too costly). Researchers describe a progression in contrarian arguments over time from denying the warming itself toward disputing its causes and opposing solutions.

Organised campaigns

Investigations and leaked documents have shown that some fossil fuel companies and trade associations promoted doubt about climate science while internally acknowledging its validity, a strategy historians compare to the tobacco industry's earlier campaigns against the evidence on smoking. Think tanks and front groups disseminated these messages, and "denial machine" networks have been mapped by sociologists studying the countermovement.

Psychology and public opinion

Research on the psychology of denial links rejection of climate science to political ideology, free-market beliefs, conspiratorial thinking, and identity-protective cognition. Public acceptance of the consensus varies sharply by country and political affiliation, and misinformation on social media continues to be studied as a factor in polarised opinion.
