Cyclotomic polynomial

In mathematics, the nth cyclotomic polynomial, for any positive integer n, is the unique irreducible polynomial with integer coefficients that is a divisor of xⁿ − 1 and is not a divisor of xᵏ − 1 for any smaller k. Its roots are the primitive nth roots of unity.

Definition and examples

The polynomial is defined by Φₙ(x) = ∏ (x − ζ), where the product runs over the primitive nth roots of unity ζ. The first few are Φ₁(x) = x − 1, Φ₂(x) = x + 1, Φ₃(x) = x² + x + 1, Φ₄(x) = x² + 1, and Φ₆(x) = x² − x + 1. The degree of Φₙ is Euler's totient φ(n), and xⁿ − 1 factors as the product of Φ_d(x) over all divisors d of n, allowing Φₙ to be computed recursively.

Properties

Although cyclotomic polynomials have integer coefficients and many small examples suggest the coefficients are only −1, 0, or 1, this fails at n = 105, whose polynomial has a coefficient of −2. Cyclotomic polynomials are central to Galois theory: the cyclotomic field ℚ(ζₙ) has Galois group isomorphic to (ℤ/nℤ)×.
