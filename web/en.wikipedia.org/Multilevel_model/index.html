Multilevel model
FieldStatistics
Also known asHierarchical linear model, mixed-effects model, random-effects model

Multilevel models (also known as hierarchical linear models, linear mixed-effect models, mixed models, nested data models, random coefficient or random-effects models, and variance components models) are statistical models of parameters that vary at more than one level. They are fitted to data organised in a hierarchy — for example, students nested within schools, patients within hospitals, or repeated measurements within individuals.

An example could be a model of student performance that contains measures for individual students as well as measures for classrooms in which the students study. Unlike ordinary regression, multilevel models account for the non-independence of observations within clusters: residuals for individuals in the same group tend to be correlated, and ignoring this clustering underestimates standard errors.

Structure

A random-intercept model allows each group its own intercept, drawn from a distribution whose variance measures between-group heterogeneity — quantified by the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Random-slope models additionally let the effect of a predictor vary across groups, and cross-level interactions test how group-level variables moderate individual-level relationships. Multilevel models handle unbalanced data and missing observations naturally, and their partial pooling (shrinkage) estimates for small groups are pulled toward the overall mean, improving accuracy.

Applications

They are widely used in education research, epidemiology, longitudinal medicine, psychology, and survey methodology, and are estimated by maximum likelihood (ML/REML) or Bayesian methods in software such as R (lme4, brms), Stata, SAS and MLwiN.
