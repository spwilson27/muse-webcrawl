Fair

A fair is a gathering of people for a variety of entertainment or commercial activities. Fairs are typically temporary, with scheduled times lasting from an afternoon to several weeks. They encompass trade fairs, agricultural (county and state) fairs, funfairs, and travelling carnivals.

History

Fairs in medieval Europe were major trading events, often held on religious feast days; the great Champagne fairs of the 12th and 13th centuries linked the cloth towns of Flanders with Italy and the Mediterranean. As permanent markets and shops developed, fairs increasingly emphasised entertainment, livestock shows and agricultural competitions.

Types

  • Trade fair — an exhibition for companies in a specific industry (e.g. book fairs, motor shows)
  • Agricultural show / county fair / state fair — livestock judging, produce competitions, food and rides; in the United States, state fairs such as the Iowa State Fair and the State Fair of Texas draw over a million visitors
  • Funfair / travelling carnival — temporary amusement rides and games
  • World's fair — large international expositions (see World's Columbian Exposition)

Entertainment

Traditional fairground attractions include the Ferris wheel, carousel (merry-go-round), roller coasters, sideshows, and food vendors selling items such as candy floss (cotton candy), corn dogs and funnel cake.
