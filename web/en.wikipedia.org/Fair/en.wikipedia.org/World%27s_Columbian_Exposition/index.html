World's Columbian Exposition

DatesMay 1 – October 30, 1893
LocationChicago, Illinois, United States
Visitorsover 27 million

The World's Columbian Exposition, also known as the Chicago World's Fair, was a world's fair held in Chicago in 1893 to celebrate the 400th anniversary of Christopher Columbus's arrival in the New World in 1492.

The White City

The fairgrounds in Jackson Park were designed under the direction of Daniel Burnham and Frederick Law Olmsted. Most of the more than 200 buildings were designed in the Beaux-Arts style and faced in white stucco, earning the exposition the nickname the "White City". Large central water features, including a grand basin and lagoon, tied the site together.

Attractions and legacy

The fair introduced the original Ferris Wheel, built by George Washington Gale Ferris Jr., and showcased advances in electricity, including extensive use of alternating current lighting. The Midway Plaisance offered entertainments and ethnological exhibits. The fair profoundly influenced American architecture, city planning, and popular culture; the Palace of Fine Arts survived as the Museum of Science and Industry.
