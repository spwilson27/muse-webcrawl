Motown is an American record label owned by the Universal Music Group. Founded by Berry Gordy Jr. as Tamla Records on January 12, 1959, and incorporated as Motown Record Corporation on April 14, 1960, its name, a portmanteau of motor and town, has become a nickname for Detroit, where the label was originally headquartered.

Motown played a vital role in the racial integration of popular music, as it was the first record label owned by an African American to become a major commercial success in crossing over to the pop charts. In the 1960s, Motown and its subsidiary labels (including Tamla, Motown, Gordy, Soul, and V.I.P.) were the most successful proponents of the Motown sound, a style of soul music with a mainstream pop influence. Motown was the most successful soul music label, with a net worth of $61 million.

The Motown sound

Operating from a converted house at 2648 West Grand Boulevard in Detroit — dubbed "Hitsville U.S.A." — Gordy built an assembly-line production system modelled on the city's automobile factories. In-house songwriting and production teams, notably Holland–Dozier–Holland, and the house band known as the Funk Brothers created a polished sound built on tambourines, handclaps, prominent bass lines played by James Jamerson, and orchestral strings blended with rhythm and blues.

Artists

Motown's roster included the Supremes, the Temptations, the Four Tops, Smokey Robinson and the Miracles, Marvin Gaye, Stevie Wonder, Martha and the Vandellas, the Jackson 5, and Diana Ross. Between 1960 and 1969 the label produced dozens of number-one pop hits.

Later years

Gordy moved the company to Los Angeles in 1972. He sold Motown to MCA in 1988 for $61 million; the label later passed to PolyGram and then Universal Music Group. The original Hitsville building is now the Motown Museum.
