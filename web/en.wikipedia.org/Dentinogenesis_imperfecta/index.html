Dentinogenesis imperfecta

Teeth affected by dentinogenesis imperfecta showing opalescent discoloration

Dentinogenesis imperfecta (DI) is a genetic disorder of tooth development in which dentin forms abnormally, producing discolored — often blue-grey or yellow-brown — translucent ("opalescent") teeth that are weaker than normal and prone to rapid wear and breakage.

Cause

Most cases arise from mutations in the DSPP gene and are inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. One type is associated with osteogenesis imperfecta (mutations in COL1A1/COL1A2). Classification schemes distinguish types I, II, and III.

Treatment

Management focuses on protecting the teeth with crowns, preserving vertical dimension, and early intervention in childhood to prevent severe attrition.
