Limited-access road

A limited-access road, also called a limited-access highway, is a highway or arterial road designed for high-speed traffic with access restricted to designated points. Unlike a fully controlled-access highway (freeway or motorway), which permits entry and exit only at grade-separated interchanges, a limited-access road may have some at-grade intersections and direct property access, but limits their number and spacing to preserve traffic flow and safety.

Characteristics

Limited-access roads typically have divided carriageways or a central median, no direct driveway access from adjoining properties (or strictly controlled access), prohibition of slow or non-motorised users such as pedestrians and cyclists, and minimum design speeds higher than ordinary streets. Examples include many British dual carriageways, American expressways in the older sense of the term, and divided arterials in numerous countries.

Access management

The discipline of access management governs the spacing of junctions, median openings and driveways on such roads. Reducing conflict points improves both capacity and crash rates; studies for transport agencies consistently link higher access density with higher accident frequency. Jurisdictions often designate routes as limited-access by statute, allowing road authorities to acquire access rights from fronting landowners.
