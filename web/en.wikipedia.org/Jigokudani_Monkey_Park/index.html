Jigokudani Monkey Park

The Jigokudani Monkey Park (Japanese: 地獄谷野猿公苑, Jigokudani Yaen Kōen) is located in Yamanouchi, Nagano Prefecture, Japan. It is part of the Joshinetsu Kogen National Park and lies in the Yokoyu River valley. The park is famous for its large population of wild Japanese macaques (Macaca fuscata), commonly called snow monkeys, which bathe in a man-made pool fed by the local hot springs (onsen) during the winter months.

History and behaviour

The park was established in 1964. The name Jigokudani, meaning "Hell's Valley", refers to the steam and boiling water that bubble from the frozen ground. Local macaques began visiting the hot springs in the 1960s, apparently learning the behaviour from one another and from observing human bathers at a nearby inn. Bathing is a learned, culturally transmitted behaviour in this troop, and the sight of monkeys soaking in the steaming pool during heavy snow has made the park a major tourist attraction, reached by a 1.6 km walking trail through the forest.
