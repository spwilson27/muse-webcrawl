The Beyond (Italian: L'aldilà, lit. "The Afterlife"), also released in the United States in a re-edited form as Seven Doors of Death, is a 1981 Italian Southern Gothic supernatural horror film directed by Lucio Fulci. It is the middle film of Fulci's unofficial "Gates of Hell" trilogy, preceded by City of the Living Dead (1980) and followed by The House by the Cemetery (1981). The film stars Catriona MacColl and David Warbeck.

Plot

In 1927 Louisiana, an artist named Schweick is accused of witchcraft and brutally murdered in the basement of the Seven Doors Hotel, which stands over one of the seven gateways to Hell described in the ancient book of Eibon. In 1981, Liza Merril inherits the hotel and begins renovations to reopen it. A series of gruesome accidents and supernatural occurrences follows: a plumber is killed in the flooded basement, a blind woman named Emily warns Liza to leave, and the boundaries between the world of the living and the realm of the dead begin to dissolve. Liza and a local doctor, John McCabe, attempt to escape, but find themselves trapped in a desolate, grey afterlife landscape.

Production

The film was shot on location in Louisiana, including New Orleans, and at studios in Rome. Its dreamlike, nonlinear narrative and graphic effects sequences — involving tarantulas, acid, and eye trauma, all Fulci trademarks — were the work of special effects artist Giannetto De Rossi. The score was composed by Fabio Frizzi.

Reception and legacy

Initially released in heavily cut form in some territories, The Beyond was placed on the British "video nasties" list in the 1980s. It has since been widely re-evaluated and is now regarded as one of Fulci's finest works and a classic of Italian horror cinema, praised for its atmosphere, surreal imagery, and downbeat ending.
