Université Laval is a public research university in Quebec City, Quebec, Canada. Founded in 1852 under a charter granted by Queen Victoria, it traces its origins to the Séminaire de Québec founded in 1663 by François de Laval, the first bishop of New France, making it the oldest French-language university in Canada and one of the oldest institutions of higher learning in North America.

History

The university grew out of the Séminaire de Québec and received its royal charter in 1852, with the right to confer degrees. For over a century it maintained affiliated colleges across French Canada; its former Montreal branch became the independent Université de Montréal in 1919. In the 1950s the university moved from Old Quebec to a new modern campus in Sainte-Foy.

Academics

Université Laval offers more than 500 study programs to roughly 45,000 students, including some 10,000 graduate students, taught primarily in French. Its faculties include medicine, law, forestry and geomatics, science and engineering, and business administration (FSA ULaval). The university is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research universities and is noted for research in northern studies, optics and photonics, cardiology, and sustainable development.

Athletics

The university's varsity teams are known as the Rouge et Or ("Red and Gold"). Its football team is among the most successful in Canadian university sport, having won numerous Vanier Cup championships, and the team plays at the PEPS stadium on campus.
