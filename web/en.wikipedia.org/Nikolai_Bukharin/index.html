Nikolai Bukharin

Nikolai Ivanovich Bukharin (9 October 1888 – 15 March 1938) was a Russian revolutionary, Soviet politician, and Marxist theorist. Lenin called him "the favourite of the whole Party" and its most valuable theorist; Bukharin authored Imperialism and World Economy and the popular ABC of Communism (with Yevgeni Preobrazhensky).

Rise and fall

As editor of Pravda and head of the Comintern, Bukharin championed the market-oriented New Economic Policy and the slogan "Enrich yourselves!" addressed to the peasantry. He allied with Stalin against Trotsky and the Left Opposition, then led the Right Opposition against forced collectivization in 1928–29 and was defeated. In 1938 he was the most prominent defendant at the third Moscow show trial (the Trial of the Twenty-One), confessed to absurd charges of treason and espionage, and was executed. His final plea, written to a future generation of party leaders, was memorized by his wife Anna Larina and published decades later.

Rehabilitation

Bukharin was posthumously rehabilitated in 1988 under Mikhail Gorbachev.
