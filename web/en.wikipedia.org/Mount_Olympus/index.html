Mount Olympus (Greek: Όλυμπος, romanized: Ólympos) is the highest mountain in Greece. It is in the Olympus Range on the border between Thessaly and Macedonia, between the regional units of Larissa and Pieria, about 80 km (50 mi) from Thessaloniki. Mount Olympus has 52 peaks and deep gorges. The highest peak, Mytikas (Μύτικας Mýtikas), meaning "nose", rises to 2,917.727 metres (9,572.60 ft). It is one of the highest peaks in Europe in terms of topographic prominence.

In Greek mythology, Olympus is the home of the Greek gods, on Mytikas peak. The mountain has exceptional biodiversity and rich flora, and has been a National Park, the first in Greece, since 1938. It is also a World Biosphere Reserve.

Mythology

In Ancient Greek religion and mythology, "Olympus" was the name of the home of the Twelve Olympian gods of the ancient Greek world. It was conceived of as a lofty mountaintop, and in all regions settled by Greek tribes, the highest local elevation tended to be so named; among the numerous peaks called Olumpos in antiquity are mountains in Mysia, Laconia, and Cyprus, among others. Thessalian Olympus is the highest peak in any territory with Greek settlement and came to be seen as the pan-Hellenic representative of the mythological seat of the gods. Zeus was said to sit enthroned on the summit, hurling thunderbolts, and the gods feasted on ambrosia in palaces built by Hephaestus.

Geography and nature

The Olympus massif is a compact group of peaks rising steeply from the Thermaic Gulf, cut by deep ravines including the Enipeas gorge above the town of Litochoro, the main gateway for climbers. The massif's isolation from other high mountains has encouraged the evolution of endemic plants; the park hosts more than 1,700 plant species and subspecies, around a quarter of the Greek flora, including the endemic Bosnian pine stands and rare Jankaea heldreichii. Fauna includes chamois, roe deer, wild boar, and golden eagles. Mount Olympus National Park was established in 1938, and UNESCO declared it a Biosphere Reserve in 1981.

Climbing

Although local guides had surely climbed the summit earlier, the first recorded ascent of Mytikas was on 2 August 1913 by Swiss photographer Frédéric Boissonnas and writer Daniel Baud-Bovy, led by the hunter Christos Kakkalos of Litochoro. The standard route starts at Prionia (1,100 m), reached by road from Litochoro, and climbs via the Spilios Agapitos refuge (Refuge A) to the Skala ridge, from which Mytikas is reached by a scramble. Tens of thousands of visitors hike in the park each year, though conditions on the summit ridges can change rapidly.
