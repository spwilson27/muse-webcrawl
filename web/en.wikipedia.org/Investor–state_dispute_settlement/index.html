Investor–state dispute settlement

Legal basisBilateral investment treaties, free trade agreements, Energy Charter Treaty
Main forumICSID (World Bank); also UNCITRAL and other arbitral rules
ClaimantsForeign investors
RespondentsHost states

Investor–state dispute settlement (ISDS) is a system through which foreign investors can bring arbitration claims directly against a host state for alleged breaches of an investment treaty, without first exhausting local courts. It is provided for in thousands of bilateral investment treaties (BITs), in investment chapters of free trade agreements, and in the Energy Charter Treaty.

How it works

An investor who believes a state has expropriated its property, denied fair and equitable treatment, or discriminated against it may file a claim, often at the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID) in Washington, D.C., or under UNCITRAL rules. Tribunals of one or three arbitrators can award monetary damages; more than a thousand known cases have been filed since the 1990s, against developed and developing states alike. Notable awards include large claims arising from Venezuela's nationalisations and from regulatory changes in the energy sector in Europe.

Criticism and reform

Critics argue ISDS can chill legitimate regulation in health, environment, and finance, lacks transparency and appeal mechanisms, and grants foreign investors rights unavailable to domestic firms. Defenders say it depoliticises disputes, protects investors from arbitrary government action, and promotes cross-border investment. The European Union has proposed replacing ISDS with a standing investment court, and recent treaties increasingly narrow or omit ISDS provisions.
