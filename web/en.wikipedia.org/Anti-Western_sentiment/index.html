Anti-Western sentiment

Anti-Western sentiment, also known as anti-Atlanticism or Westophobia, is a broad attitude of hostility or opposition towards the people, culture, or policies of the Western world. In part, anti-Western sentiment is caused by past and present Western imperialism and colonialism, as well as by opposition to Western foreign policy interventions and cultural influence.

Causes and history

Such sentiment has roots in European colonial expansion, the transatlantic slave trade, and the imposition of foreign rule across Asia, Africa and the Americas. In the 19th and 20th centuries it was expressed in movements such as Occidentalism critiques, Slavophilism in Russia, and anti-imperialist nationalism. During the Cold War it was promoted by communist and non-aligned states; since 1991 it has been associated with criticism of globalization, the Iraq War and Western support for Israel, among other issues.

Regional expressions

Anti-Western sentiment appears in various forms in Russia, China, the Middle East, Latin America and elsewhere, and is sometimes encouraged by governments to deflect domestic criticism. It is distinct from, but may overlap with, anti-Americanism and opposition to specific Western policies.
