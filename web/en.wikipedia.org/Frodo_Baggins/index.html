Frodo Baggins is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings. A hobbit of the Shire, he inherits the One Ring from his cousin Bilbo Baggins and undertakes the quest to destroy it in the fires of Mount Doom, becoming the central figure of the War of the Ring.

Role in the story

Frodo volunteers at the Council of Elrond to take the Ring to Mordor and joins the Fellowship of the Ring. Guided by Gollum after the Fellowship breaks up, and sustained by his gardener and companion Samwise Gamgee, Frodo crosses Mordor and reaches Mount Doom — but at the Cracks of Doom he claims the Ring for himself, and it is destroyed only when Gollum seizes it and falls into the fire. Wounded in body and spirit, Frodo eventually sails from the Grey Havens to the Undying Lands with Bilbo and the Ring-bearers.

Character and themes

Scholars have read Frodo as an unlikely, non-martial hero whose endurance, pity — especially his mercy toward Gollum, which proves decisive — and lasting trauma reflect Tolkien's experience of the First World War. His failure at the final moment is often cited as central to Tolkien's themes of providence and the limits of free will.

Portrayals

Frodo was played by Elijah Wood in Peter Jackson's film trilogy (2001–2003) and voiced by Christopher Guard in Ralph Bakshi's 1978 animated film.
