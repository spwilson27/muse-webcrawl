The Zócalo, officially the Plaza de la Constitución, is the main square of Mexico City, located in the historic centre of the city. It is one of the largest public squares in the world and has been a gathering place since Aztec times, occupying the site near the ceremonial centre of Tenochtitlan, beside the Templo Mayor.

Surroundings and name

The square is flanked by the Mexico City Metropolitan Cathedral, the National Palace, the Old Portal de Mercaderes, and the Mexico City government buildings. The nickname "Zócalo" (meaning plinth or base) arose in the nineteenth century when a planned independence monument was abandoned after only its base was built; the term has since spread in Mexico to mean the main square of any town.

Uses

The Zócalo hosts concerts, festivals, political rallies, and national celebrations, including the annual Grito de Dolores ceremony each 15 September, when the President rings the bell of the National Palace before a huge crowd. A monumental Mexican flag is raised and lowered daily by soldiers in the centre of the square.
