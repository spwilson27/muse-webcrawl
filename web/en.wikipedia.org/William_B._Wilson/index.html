William B. Wilson

William Bauchop Wilson (April 2, 1862 – May 25, 1934) was an American labor leader and progressive politician, who represented Pennsylvania in the United States House of Representatives and served as the first United States Secretary of Labor.

Early life and union career

Born in Blantyre, Scotland, Wilson emigrated to the United States as a child and worked in the Pennsylvania coal mines from the age of nine. He became an active trade unionist and served as secretary-treasurer of the United Mine Workers of America.

Congress

A Democrat, Wilson represented Pennsylvania's 15th congressional district from 1907 to 1913. As chairman of the House Committee on Labor, he championed the creation of a cabinet-level Department of Labor.

Secretary of Labor

When President Woodrow Wilson established the Department of Labor in 1913, he appointed William B. Wilson as its first secretary; he served until 1921, overseeing wartime labor policy, industrial mediation, and the creation of agencies such as the United States Employment Service.

Later life

After leaving office he served on the International Joint Commission and continued to be involved in coal industry labor affairs until his death in 1934.
