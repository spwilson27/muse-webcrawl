Bibliotheque nationale de France

Established1461 (royal library); 1537 (legal deposit)
LocationParis, France
CollectionOver 40 million items, including ~15 million books and periodicals
DirectorPresident of the BnF

The Bibliotheque nationale de France (BnF; National Library of France) is the national library of France and one of the largest libraries in the world. It traces its origin to the royal library founded at the Louvre by Charles V in 1368 and has accumulated its collections through nearly five centuries of legal deposit, instituted by Francis I in 1537.

Sites

The historic Richelieu site, in the 2nd arrondissement of Paris, houses the departments of manuscripts, prints and photographs, maps, music, and coins and medals, and is famed for its oval reading room (Salle Ovale). The modern François-Mitterrand site, opened in 1996 in the 13th arrondissement, consists of four 79-metre glass towers designed by Dominique Perrault to resemble open books, surrounding a sunken garden; it holds the main printed collections. Other sites include the Bibliotheque de l'Arsenal and the Maison Jean Vilar in Avignon.

Collections and Gallica

The BnF holds some 40 million items: books, journals, manuscripts (including medieval illuminated treasures), prints, photographs, maps, music scores, sound recordings, and video games — it is also the legal-deposit library for French websites. Its digital library, Gallica, launched in 1997, provides free online access to millions of digitized documents. The library famously preserves both Gutenberg Bibles held in France and vast collections of French literature.

History

Successively the King's Library, the Imperial Library, and the National Library, the institution grew through revolutionary confiscations of aristocratic and church libraries, and was renamed Bibliotheque nationale de France in 1994, two years before the Mitterrand site opened.
