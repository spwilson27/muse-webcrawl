Battle of Pharsalus

The Battle of Pharsalus was the decisive battle of Caesar's Civil War, fought on 9 August 48 BC near Pharsalus in central Greece. Julius Caesar and his Populares allies defeated the forces of the Optimates under Pompey the Great, supported by much of the Roman Senate.

Background

Having crossed the Rubicon in 49 BC and driven Pompey from Italy, Caesar pursued him across the Adriatic. After an initial reverse at Dyrrhachium, Caesar withdrew into Thessaly, and Pompey — urged on by impatient senators who accused him of prolonging the war — followed and accepted battle on the plain west of Pharsalus, despite his advantage in numbers and supplies.

The battle

Pompey fielded roughly twice Caesar's infantry and seven times his cavalry, planning to sweep away Caesar's weak right wing with a massed cavalry charge led by Labienus. Caesar anticipated the move: he thinned his line and hid six cohorts behind his own cavalry with orders to thrust their pila upward at the faces of Pompey's horsemen. The cavalry charge was routed, the hidden cohorts fell on Pompey's exposed left flank, and Caesar's fresh third line broke the Pompeian infantry. Pompey's camp was stormed and his army dissolved.

Aftermath

Caesar claimed Pompey lost 15,000 men against only 200 of his own — figures doubtless polished, but the victory was overwhelming. Pompey fled to Egypt, where he was murdered on landing by the court of Ptolemy XIII. Caesar followed, becoming embroiled in the Alexandrian War and his liaison with Cleopatra. Further campaigns in Africa and Spain were needed to crush the remaining Optimates, but Pharsalus had decided the war's outcome and, with it, the fate of the Roman Republic.
