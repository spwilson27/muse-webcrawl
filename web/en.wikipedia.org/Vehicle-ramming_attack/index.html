Vehicle-ramming attack
Type: attack tactic; form of vehicular homicide
Weapon: motor vehicle (car, truck)
Countermeasures: security bollards, hostile vehicle mitigation

A vehicle-ramming attack is an attack in which a perpetrator deliberately drives a motor vehicle into a crowd of people, a building, or other vehicles in order to kill or injure. It is a form of vehicular homicide typically carried out as an act of terrorism or mass murder, requiring no specialized weapons or training.

Security bollards are a common countermeasure in pedestrian areas.

History

Isolated ramming attacks occurred throughout the automobile era, but the tactic spread markedly in the 2010s: the 2014 Jerusalem tractor attacks, the 2016 Nice truck attack (86 killed during Bastille Day celebrations), the 2016 Berlin Christmas market attack, and attacks in Stockholm, Barcelona, London, and New York in 2017. Extremist propaganda promoted the method precisely because vehicles are easy to obtain.

Motives and perpetrators

Attackers have included jihadist terrorists, far-right extremists, and individuals with grievances or mental illness acting alone; some rammings are spontaneous escalations of road incidents rather than premeditated attacks.

Prevention

Cities respond with bollards, planters, and rated barriers around pedestrian zones, vehicle checkpoints at mass events, and rental-truck screening measures, collectively known as hostile vehicle mitigation.
