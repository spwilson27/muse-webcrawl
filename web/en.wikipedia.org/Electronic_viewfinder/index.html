Electronic viewfinder

An electronic viewfinder (EVF) is a camera viewfinder where the image captured by the lens is displayed on a miniature electronic display, so the photographer sees an electronic preview of the final image rather than an optical view.

Operation

The camera's image sensor reads the scene continuously and feeds a small LCD or OLED screen viewed through an eyepiece. The display can overlay exposure information, histograms, focus peaking, and a live preview of depth of field and exposure settings.

Comparison with optical viewfinders

Unlike the optical viewfinders of SLR cameras, an EVF shows exactly what the sensor records and works well in low light, but may suffer from refresh lag, reduced dynamic range in the preview, and battery drain. Resolution and refresh rates have steadily improved, with high-end EVFs exceeding five million dots.

Use

EVFs are standard on mirrorless cameras, camcorders, and many compact cameras; some models offer hybrid viewfinders combining optical and electronic views.
