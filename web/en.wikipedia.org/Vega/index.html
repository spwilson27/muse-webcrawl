Vega

Vega is the brightest star in the northern constellation of Lyra. It has the Bayer designation α Lyrae (Alpha Lyrae). With an apparent magnitude of +0.026, it is the brightest star in Lyra, the second-brightest star in the northern celestial hemisphere (after Arcturus), and the fifth-brightest star in the night sky. It lies at a distance of about 25 light-years (7.7 parsecs) from the Sun, making it one of the Sun's closer stellar neighbours.

Observation

Vega forms one corner of the Summer Triangle asterism together with Deneb and Altair. It is prominent in northern summer skies. Vega was the northern pole star around 12,000 BC, and will become so again around the year 13,727, when its declination will be +86°14′. It was the first star other than the Sun to have its photograph taken and the first to have its spectrum recorded.

Properties

Vega is a main-sequence star of spectral type A0 Va, with about 2.1 times the mass of the Sun and roughly 40 times its luminosity. It rotates rapidly, completing a rotation in about 12.5 hours, which makes it strongly oblate: its equatorial diameter is more than 20% larger than its polar diameter. Infrared observations have revealed a circumstellar disk of dust around the star, suggesting the possible presence of a planetary system.

Name and mythology

The name Vega derives from a loose transliteration of the Arabic al-nasr al-wāqiʿ, "the falling eagle". In Chinese astronomy the star is part of the asterism associated with the Weaver Girl, linked to the legend of the Qixi Festival.
