Kodiak bear


Kodiak bear (Ursus arctos middendorffi), a subspecies of brown bear native to the Kodiak Archipelago, Alaska.

The Kodiak bear (Ursus arctos middendorffi), also known as the Kodiak brown bear or Alaskan grizzly bear, is a subspecies of brown bear that inhabits the islands of the Kodiak Archipelago in southwestern Alaska. It is among the largest bears in the world, rivalling the polar bear in size; large males may weigh up to 680 kg (1,500 lb) and stand over 3 metres (10 ft) tall on their hind legs.

Biology and behaviour

Kodiak bears have been isolated from other brown bears since the last ice age, roughly 10,000 to 12,000 years. They are omnivorous, feeding on grasses, berries, roots and, importantly, spawning salmon during the summer runs. In autumn they enter dens and hibernate for up to eight months. Females typically give birth to litters of two or three cubs in the den during winter.

The population of the archipelago is estimated at about 3,500 bears and is carefully managed; hunting is regulated by permit. Much of their habitat is protected within the Kodiak National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1941.
