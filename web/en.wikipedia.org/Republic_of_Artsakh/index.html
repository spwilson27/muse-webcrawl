The Republic of Artsakh (Armenian: Արցախի Հանրապետություն), also known as the Nagorno-Karabakh Republic, was a breakaway state in the South Caucasus whose territory was internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan. It controlled most of the former Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast and surrounding districts from the end of the First Nagorno-Karabakh War in 1994 until its dissolution in 2023–2024.

History

Ethnic Armenians of Nagorno-Karabakh voted for independence from Azerbaijan in a referendum on 10 December 1991, amid the collapse of the Soviet Union. The ensuing First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1988–1994) ended with an Armenian victory and a Russian-brokered ceasefire, leaving Artsakh de facto independent but unrecognised by any UN member state, including Armenia. A fragile "line of contact" separated Artsakh forces from Azerbaijani troops for over two decades.

In the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War of 2020, Azerbaijan regained the surrounding districts and part of Nagorno-Karabakh itself, including Shusha; a Russian-brokered ceasefire left Artsakh a rump territory connected to Armenia by the Lachin corridor under Russian peacekeepers. Azerbaijan blockaded the corridor from December 2022, then launched an offensive on 19–20 September 2023. Artsakh's authorities capitulated; on 28 September 2023, President Samvel Shahramanyan signed a decree dissolving all state institutions by 1 January 2024. Almost the entire Armenian population — over 100,000 people — fled to Armenia within days.

Government

Artsakh was a presidential republic with a National Assembly, its own defence army, and institutions closely integrated with Armenia's. Its leaders included Arkadi Ghukasyan, Bako Sahakyan and Arayik Harutyunyan.
