The French Second Republic (French: Deuxième République) was the republican government of France between the Revolution of 1848 and the coup d'état of Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte that founded the Second Empire in 1852. Brief but consequential, it introduced universal male suffrage and definitively abolished slavery in the French colonies.

Revolution of 1848

When the February 1848 revolution toppled King Louis-Philippe, a provisional government proclaimed the republic. Elections in April, the first by universal male suffrage, returned a moderate-to-conservative Constituent Assembly. The closure of the National Workshops — a work-relief scheme — provoked the June Days uprising of Parisian workers, which General Cavaignac suppressed at a cost of thousands of lives.

Constitution and presidency

The constitution of November 1848 created a strong president elected by universal suffrage for a single four-year term, facing a single legislative assembly. In December 1848 Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte, nephew of Napoleon I, won the presidency by a landslide, drawing support from peasants who associated his name with glory and order. His relations with the conservative assembly deteriorated as it restricted the suffrage (May 1850) and resisted his ambitions.

Coup and end

Barred from re-election, Louis-Napoléon seized power in the coup of 2 December 1851, dissolved the assembly, and crushed republican resistance. A plebiscite ratified the coup; a year later, on 2 December 1852, he proclaimed himself Emperor Napoleon III, ending the republic. Karl Marx's pamphlet The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte is the classic analysis of these events.
