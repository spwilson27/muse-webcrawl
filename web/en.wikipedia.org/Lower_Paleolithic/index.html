Lower Paleolithic

The Lower Paleolithic (or Lower Palaeolithic) is the earliest subdivision of the Paleolithic, or Old Stone Age. It spans from the first known stone tools, about 3.3 million years ago, to roughly 300,000 years ago, when major changes mark the beginning of the Middle Paleolithic.

Industries

The period encompasses the Oldowan industry of simple choppers and flakes, associated with early Homo, and the later Acheulean industry, characterised by bifacial hand axes and associated with Homo erectus and Homo heidelbergensis. During this long span, hominins spread out of Africa across Eurasia and learned to control fire — evidence at sites such as Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa dates to around one million years ago.
