Trinity Church, Boston


Trinity Church facing Copley Square

Trinity Church is an Episcopal parish church in the Back Bay neighborhood of Boston, Massachusetts, located on Copley Square. Designed by architect Henry Hobson Richardson and consecrated in 1877, it is regarded as the finest example of the Richardsonian Romanesque style and one of the most significant buildings in American architecture.

Architecture

The church is built of rough-faced Dedham granite with Longmeadow sandstone trim, arranged in a Greek-cross plan with a massive central tower rising about 211 feet. Its richly colored interior features murals and painted decoration by John La Farge, stained glass by La Farge, William Morris, and Edward Burne-Jones, and carved stonework by Augustus Saint-Gaudens. Because it stands on filled marshland in the Back Bay, the building rests on thousands of submerged wooden piles.

History

The parish was founded in 1733; its earlier downtown church was destroyed in the Great Boston Fire of 1872, prompting the move to Copley Square. The building was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1970 and in 1885 was voted the most important building in America in a poll of architects—a standing it retained in later surveys.
