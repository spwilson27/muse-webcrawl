Insulae

Insulae (singular insula) were apartment buildings in ancient Rome that housed most of the urban population. The word insula means "island" in Latin, and the buildings were so named because they occupied a whole city block surrounded by streets.

Construction

Insulae were typically three to five storeys high, built of concrete, brick, and timber around a central courtyard. The ground floor often contained shops (tabernae) opening onto the street, while upper floors contained apartments (cenacula) that grew smaller and poorer toward the top. Wealthier tenants lived on the lower floors in larger, better-appointed rooms.

Living conditions

Many insulae were built speculatively and were notorious for poor construction, fire risk, and collapse. Roman law under Augustus limited their height to about 70 Roman feet (roughly 21 metres), and Trajan later reduced the limit. Insulae generally lacked running water and heating; residents used public fountains, baths, and latrines. Well-preserved examples survive at Ostia Antica, such as the Insula of the Charioteers and the House of Diana.
