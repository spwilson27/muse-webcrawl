Norm Smith

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Norman Walter "Norm" Smith (21 November 1915 – 29 July 1973) was an Australian rules footballer and coach in the Victorian Football League (VFL). He is regarded as one of the greatest coaches in the game's history, leading the Melbourne Football Club to six premierships during its golden era.

Playing career

Smith played 227 games for Melbourne from 1935 to 1948, mostly as a full-forward, kicking 546 goals and playing in the 1939, 1940 and 1941 premiership teams under coach "Checker" Hughes. He later played briefly for Fitzroy in 1949.

Coaching career

After coaching Fitzroy from 1949 to 1951, Smith returned to Melbourne in 1952 and built a dynasty: premierships in 1955, 1956, 1957, 1959, 1960 and 1964, and grand final appearances in 1954 and 1958. His sudden sacking mid-season in 1965 — though he was reinstated within days — is one of the most famous episodes in VFL history and is often linked to the "Curse of Norm Smith" said to have haunted Melbourne until its 2021 premiership. Smith later coached South Melbourne from 1969 to 1972.

Legacy

The Norm Smith Medal, awarded to the best player in the AFL Grand Final since 1979, is named in his honour. He was named coach of Melbourne's Team of the Century and inducted into the Australian Football Hall of Fame as a Legend.
