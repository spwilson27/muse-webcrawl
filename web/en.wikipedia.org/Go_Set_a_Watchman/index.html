Go Set a Watchman

Go Set a Watchman is a novel by Harper Lee, published on July 14, 2015, by HarperCollins in the United States and William Heinemann in the United Kingdom. Although marketed as a sequel to Lee's Pulitzer Prize-winning To Kill a Mockingbird (1960), it was actually an early draft of that novel. The manuscript had been presumed lost and was discovered in 2014 among Lee's papers.

Plot summary

The novel is set twenty years after the events of To Kill a Mockingbird. Jean Louise Finch — "Scout" — now in her mid-twenties and living in New York City, returns to her childhood home of Maycomb, Alabama, to visit her aging father, Atticus Finch. Over the course of her visit she is confronted with uncomfortable truths about her family and her hometown, including the discovery that Atticus holds segregationist views she finds abhorrent. Her uncle, Dr. Jack Finch, helps her come to terms with the conflict between her conscience and her father's.

Discovery and publication

The manuscript was written in the mid-1950s. Lee's editor at J.B. Lippincott, Tay Hohoff, encouraged her to rewrite the story from Scout's childhood perspective, producing To Kill a Mockingbird. The earlier draft was rediscovered in a safe-deposit box in Monroeville, Alabama, in 2014, and its announcement in February 2015 generated enormous interest: the book became one of the most pre-ordered titles in Amazon's history. Questions were raised about whether the elderly Lee, then 89 and in poor health, had consented to publication; an investigation by the state of Alabama concluded there was no evidence of elder abuse, and Lee's representatives maintained she was pleased by the response.

Reception

The novel received mixed reviews. Critics praised its historical importance while noting that it lacked the polish of its famous successor. The portrayal of Atticus Finch as a man sympathetic to segregation shocked many readers who had regarded him as a moral hero. The book sold over a million copies in its first week in North America.
