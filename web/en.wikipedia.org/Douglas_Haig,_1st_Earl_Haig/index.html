Douglas Haig, 1st Earl Haig

Field Marshal Sir Douglas Haig

Field Marshal Douglas Haig, 1st Earl Haig (19 June 1861 – 29 January 1928) was a senior officer of the British Army. During the First World War, he commanded the British Expeditionary Force (BEF) on the Western Front from late 1915 until the end of the war, including the Battle of the Somme in 1916, the Battle of Arras, the Third Battle of Ypres (Passchendaele) in 1917, and the Hundred Days Offensive in 1918.

Early career

Born in Edinburgh into the family that owned the Haig whisky distillery, he was educated at Clifton College and Brasenose College, Oxford, before entering Sandhurst. He served in the Sudan campaign of 1898 and in the Second Boer War, and rose quickly in staff and cavalry appointments.

First World War

Haig commanded I Corps in 1914 and the First Army in 1915 before succeeding Sir John French as commander-in-chief in December 1915. His tenure saw the BEF grow into a mass army and achieve victory in 1918, but at enormous cost in casualties.

Legacy

Haig's reputation has been fiercely debated: critics have branded him an incompetent “butcher” indifferent to losses, while defenders credit him with adapting the army to modern industrial warfare and presiding over its eventual success. After the war he devoted himself to veterans' welfare, helping to found the Royal British Legion, and was created Earl Haig in 1919.
