Tabbouleh

Tabbouleh is a Levantine salad made mostly of finely chopped parsley, with tomatoes, mint, onion, soaked uncooked bulgur, and seasoned with olive oil, lemon juice, salt and sweet pepper. Some variations add lettuce, or use semolina instead of bulgur.

History

Tabbouleh originated in the mountains of Lebanon and Syria, where wild herbs were gathered and mixed with grains. The dish became a staple of Levantine cuisine and spread throughout the Middle East and, with the Lebanese and Syrian diaspora, around the world.

Preparation

The salad is dominated by parsley, which is chopped very fine, rather than serving merely as a garnish. Bulgur is soaked in water or lemon juice until tender, then combined with diced tomatoes, spring onions, mint, olive oil and lemon juice. It is traditionally served with lettuce leaves used as scoops, and often accompanies mezze spreads alongside hummus and pita bread.

Regional variations

In Lebanon, the parsley-to-bulgur ratio is heavily weighted toward parsley. Turkish kısır and Armenian eetch are related salads with more bulgur or tomato paste. In 2009, a team in Lebanon prepared a tabbouleh weighing over 4,300 kilograms, setting a world record.
