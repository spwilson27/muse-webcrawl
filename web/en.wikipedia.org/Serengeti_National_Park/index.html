Serengeti National Park

Serengeti National Park is a large national park in northern Tanzania, spanning about 14,750 km² (5,700 sq mi) of grassland plains, savanna, riverine forest, and woodlands. It is famous for the annual migration of over 1.5 million white-bearded wildebeest and hundreds of thousands of zebras and gazelles.

Wildlife

The park supports large populations of lions—possibly the largest in Africa—as well as leopards, elephants, black rhinoceroses, buffalo, cheetahs, and some 500 bird species including ostriches and secretary birds. Its name derives from the Maasai word siringet, meaning "endless plains".

Conservation

Established in 1951, the park was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981. Together with Kenya's Maasai Mara National Reserve to the north, it forms one of the world's largest protected savanna ecosystems.
