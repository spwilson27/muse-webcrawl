Knowledge graph

A knowledge graph, also known as a semantic network, represents a network of real-world entities — such as objects, events, situations or concepts — and illustrates the relationships between them. Information is typically stored in a graph database and visualised as a graph structure, with nodes for entities and edges for relationships.

History and uses

The term was popularised by Google in 2012 with the launch of its Knowledge Graph, used to enhance search results with information gathered from many sources, displayed in knowledge panels. Similar systems power Microsoft's Satori, Facebook's social graph, Wikidata, DBpedia and enterprise data-integration platforms.

Knowledge graphs are used in question answering, recommendation, information retrieval, natural-language understanding and, increasingly, as structured context for large language models and retrieval-augmented generation.

Representation

Knowledge is commonly encoded as triples of the form (subject, predicate, object), for example (Paris, capitalOf, France), using standards such as RDF and OWL and query languages such as SPARQL. Ontologies define the types and permissible relationships.
