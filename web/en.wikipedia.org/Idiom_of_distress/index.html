Idiom of distress

An idiom of distress is a culturally recognised way of expressing suffering, illness, or affliction within a particular social and cultural context. The concept is used in medical anthropology and transcultural psychiatry to describe how distress is communicated through local symbols, behaviours, and bodily complaints.

Concept

The term was introduced by Mark Nichter in 1981. Idioms of distress may include somatic symptoms, possession states, or culturally specific syndromes, and they can convey personal suffering, social protest, or appeals for help in forms that the surrounding community recognises.

Examples

Examples discussed in the literature include nervios in Latin American communities, kufungisisa ("thinking too much") in Zimbabwe, and various forms of spirit possession. Clinicians are encouraged to elicit patients' own idioms of distress to improve diagnosis and care.
