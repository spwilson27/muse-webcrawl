Road stud

A road stud is a raised pavement marker used on road surfaces to delineate lane lines, edges and hazards, particularly at night and in wet weather. Unlike painted markings, studs sit proud of the surface and reflect vehicle headlights back to the driver through small retroreflective lenses.

History

The best-known early design is the "cat's eye", invented in 1934 by Percy Shaw of Halifax, England, after he noticed a cat's eyes reflecting his headlights. The device consisted of two retroreflective glass spheres in a cast-iron housing set into a rubber pad, designed to be depressed by passing tyres, which also cleaned the lenses.

Types and colours

Studs may be temporary or permanent, surface-mounted or inset. Colour conventions vary by country: in the United Kingdom, white studs mark lane lines, red studs the edge by the central reservation or hard shoulder on the offside, green studs slip-road junctions, and amber studs the central reservation of a dual carriageway or motorway. Solar-powered, LED and "smart" studs are increasingly used at crossings and on airport or tunnel routes.
