Projective special linear group

In mathematics, especially in the group theory of Lie groups, the projective special linear group, written PSL(n, F), is the quotient of the special linear group SL(n, F) of n × n matrices of determinant 1 over a field F by its centre — the subgroup of scalar matrices. Equivalently, it is the group of automorphisms induced on projective space by linear transformations of determinant one. The projective special linear groups are among the most important families of finite simple groups.

Structure

The centre of SL(n, F) consists of scalar matrices λI with λn = 1. For finite fields Fq, PSL(n, q) has order equal to that of SL(n, q) divided by gcd(n, q − 1). With a handful of small exceptions — such as PSL(2, 2) and PSL(2, 3), which are isomorphic to the symmetric groups S3 and alternating group A4 — the finite projective special linear groups are simple, forming one of the infinite families in the classification of finite simple groups.

Notable examples

Several small cases have famous alternative descriptions: PSL(2, 4) ≅ PSL(2, 5) ≅ A5, the icosahedral group; PSL(2, 7), of order 168, is the automorphism group of the Klein quartic and the second-smallest non-abelian simple group; PSL(2, 9) ≅ A6; and PSL(3, 2) ≅ PSL(2, 7). PSL(2, 11) acts on 11 points and appears in the theory of the Mathieu groups.

Real and complex cases

Over the real numbers, PSL(2, ℝ) is the group of orientation-preserving isometries of the hyperbolic plane, isomorphic to the Möbius group modulo sign, and plays a central role in hyperbolic geometry and the theory of modular forms. Over ℂ, PSL(2, ℂ) is the group of all Möbius transformations of the Riemann sphere and the Lorentz group of special relativity in disguise — it is the double cover relation to SO(3, 1) that links it to spin.
