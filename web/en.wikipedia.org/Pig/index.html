Pig

The pig (Sus scrofa domesticus), also called the domestic pig or swine, is a domesticated large even-toed ungulate descended from the wild boar. Pigs are among the most numerous large mammals in the world, with around one billion alive at any time, and are raised globally for their meat, called pork, as well as for leather and other products.

Biology and behaviour

Pigs have a large head with a long, mobile snout specialized for digging and an acute sense of smell. They are omnivorous, highly social, and intelligent — among the most cognitively capable of domestic animals, able to learn symbols and solve novel problems. Lacking functional sweat glands, they wallow in mud to cool themselves. Females (sows) give birth to litters of piglets after a gestation of about 114 days.

Domestication and farming

Pigs were independently domesticated in the Near East and in China from wild boar populations in the Neolithic. Modern breeds range from the prolific Yorkshire and Landrace to the fatty Mangalica. Pork is the most widely eaten meat in the world, and pigs also supply insulin and heart valves historically used in medicine, bristles, and gelatin. Consumption is forbidden in Islam and Judaism, and pigs occupy ambivalent roles in folklore and literature worldwide.
