Kongur Tagh

Kongur Tagh reflected in Lake Karakul, on the Karakoram Highway.

Kongur Tagh (Uyghur: قوڭۇر تاغ, "brown mountain"; Chinese: 公格尔峰, Gōnggé'ěr Fēng) is the highest peak of the Pamir Mountains lying entirely within China, reaching 7,649 metres (25,095 ft) in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region, near the borders with Tajikistan, Afghanistan and Pakistan. It forms the dominant summit of the Kongur massif in the eastern Pamirs, part of the Kunlun Shan system, and overlooks Lake Karakul and the Karakoram Highway.

Geography and climbing

Because it lies entirely within China and is overshadowed by the nearby, slightly lower Muztagh Ata (7,546 m) — a far easier climb — Kongur Tagh long attracted little mountaineering attention. The massif's complex glaciers and remote position made approaches difficult: the mountain was only reconnoitred in detail by Eric Shipton's expeditions in the 1940s. The first ascent was achieved on 16 June 1981 by a British expedition — Chris Bonington, Al Rouse, Peter Boardman and Joe Tasker — who climbed the mountain by a new route on its south side. Kongur Tagh remains a serious and infrequently climbed objective, and it is the highest peak in the world entirely visible from the low-lying Silk Road oasis of Kashgar on clear days.
