Military funeral

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A military funeral is a burial rite given by a nation's armed forces to a service member who died in battle, a veteran, or another person entitled to military honours. Such funerals combine religious or civil funeral customs with distinctive martial ceremonies that honour the deceased's service.

Elements

Common elements include a flag draped over the casket, an honour guard and casket team, a caisson or gun carriage, and a rifle party firing a three-volley salute. In the United States, funeral honours for eligible veterans include the folding and presentation of the national flag to the next of kin and the playing of "Taps" by a bugler. Other traditions include the riderless horse with reversed boots in the stirrups for cavalry and senior officers, flyovers in missing-man formation for aviators, and the sounding of the "Last Post" in British and Commonwealth services.

By country

In the United Kingdom, state and ceremonial funerals for senior figures employ companies of the armed forces, gun carriages of the King's Troop, Royal Horse Artillery, and processions through London. In the United States, Arlington National Cemetery is the best-known setting for military funerals, where the 3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment ("The Old Guard") conducts honours. Many nations maintain similar customs, regulated by service manuals and offered to families on request.
