Taharqa (also spelled Taharka or Tirhakah) was a pharaoh of the Twenty-fifth Dynasty of Egypt and a king of the Kingdom of Kush (in modern Sudan), reigning from about 690 to 664 BCE. The most powerful of the Kushite rulers of Egypt, he sponsored extensive building programs at Thebes, Memphis, and throughout Nubia, and he led Egypt's resistance against the expanding Neo-Assyrian Empire.

A statue of Taharqa, the most powerful king of Egypt's 25th Dynasty

Reign

Taharqa came to the throne at about age twenty, succeeding Shebitku. His reign is remembered in Egyptian tradition as a time of prosperity and an abundant Nile flood in his sixth regnal year. He restored temples, built a colonnade in the great temple of Amun at Karnak, and constructed monuments at Kawa, Sanam, and Napata in his Nubian homeland. His mother, Abar, traveled from Nubia to Thebes to see her son crowned, an event commemorated on stelae.

Wars with Assyria

Taharqa supported the Levantine states rebelling against Assyria and is generally identified with "Tirhakah king of Cush" mentioned in the Bible as marching against Sennacherib during the siege of Jerusalem in 701 BCE (as crown prince or commander). Later, the Assyrian kings Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal invaded Egypt itself: Memphis fell to Esarhaddon in 671 BCE, and although Taharqa briefly recovered the city, Ashurbanipal's campaign of 667–666 BCE drove him south to Nubia, where he died in 664 BCE. He was buried in the largest pyramid at Nuri.

Legacy

Monumental statues of Taharqa, including sphinxes from Kawa now in the British Museum, attest to the scale of his building work. His reign is often seen as the high point of Kushite power in Egypt before the Assyrian conquest ended the 25th Dynasty's rule in the north.
