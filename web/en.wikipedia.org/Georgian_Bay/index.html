Georgian Bay

Georgian Bay is a large bay of Lake Huron, in the Laurentian Great Lakes, located entirely within the borders of Ontario, Canada. Sometimes called a sixth Great Lake in its own right, it is about 190 kilometres long by 80 kilometres wide and covers approximately 15,000 square kilometres.

The Thirty Thousand Islands of Georgian Bay, the world's largest freshwater archipelago.

Geography

The bay's eastern shore, carved from the Precambrian rocks of the Canadian Shield, is fragmented into the Thirty Thousand Islands, the largest freshwater archipelago in the world, and is protected in part by Georgian Bay Islands National Park. Manitoulin Island, the largest freshwater island in the world, separates the bay from Lake Huron's main body and from the North Channel. The Bruce Peninsula divides Georgian Bay from the rest of Lake Huron to the west.

History and use

The bay was named in 1822 in honour of King George IV. Samuel de Champlain reached it in 1615, and it became a corridor of the fur trade and later of lumbering and commercial fishing. Today its shores are prime Ontario cottage country, and the Georgian Bay Biosphere Reserve, designated by UNESCO in 2004, covers much of the eastern coast. The windswept white pines and pink granite of the bay were immortalised in paintings by the Group of Seven.
