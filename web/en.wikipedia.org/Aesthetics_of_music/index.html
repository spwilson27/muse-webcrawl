Aesthetics of music

Aesthetics of music is a branch of philosophy that deals with the nature of art, beauty, and taste in music, and with the creation or appreciation of beauty in music. In the pre-modern tradition, musical aesthetics was most often concerned with the mathematical and cosmological dimensions of rhythmic and harmonic organization. In the eighteenth century, focus shifted to the experience of hearing music, and thus to questions about its beauty and human enjoyment (plaisir and jouissance) of music.

History

Ancient Greek thinkers such as Pythagoras studied the mathematical ratios of musical intervals, while Plato and Aristotle debated music's ethical effects on character. In the nineteenth century, Eduard Hanslick argued in On the Musically Beautiful (1854) that music's content is "tonally moving forms," opposing the view that music primarily expresses emotions. The debate between absolute music and program music—represented by Brahms and Wagner respectively—dominated late-Romantic aesthetics.

Questions

Philosophers ask what makes music expressive, how listeners perceive musical structure, whether musical meaning requires a program or text, and how judgments of musical value are justified. Modern scholars also consider recording, improvisation, and cross-cultural listening.
