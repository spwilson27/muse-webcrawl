Recycling codes

Recycling codes are a set of symbols appearing on products that identify the material from which the item is made, in order to facilitate recycling. The best known are the resin identification codes for plastics: a number from 1 to 7 inside a triangle of chasing arrows, introduced by the Society of the Plastics Industry in 1988 and now administered by ASTM International as standard D7611.

Meaning and limitations

The number identifies the polymer type — for example, 1 is PET, used for drinks bottles; 2 is HDPE; 5 is polypropylene — and does not by itself mean that an item is recyclable in a given locality, which depends on collection systems and markets for the material. Because consumers widely mistook the symbol for a guarantee of recyclability, ASTM changed the chasing-arrows triangle to a solid triangle in 2013, and some jurisdictions have legislated against the arrows' use on plastics that are not widely recycled. Analogous coding systems exist for other materials, including batteries, paper, metals, glass, and composite materials, with standardised codes for packaging materials used in the European Union and elsewhere.
