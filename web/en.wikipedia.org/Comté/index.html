Comté

Comté is a French cheese made from unpasteurised cow's milk in the Franche-Comté region of eastern France, bordering Switzerland. It has held Appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) status since 1958 and is among the most popular of the French AOC cheeses, with around 66,500 tonnes produced annually.

Production

Comté is made from the milk of Montbéliarde and French Simmental cows grazed in the Jura Massif. The milk is collected daily and made into large wheels of 35–55 kg in small village dairies called fruitières. The pressed curd is cooked and aged in cellars — famously the former military fort of Fort des Rousses — for between 4 and 24 months or longer, during which the wheels are turned, brushed, and salted regularly.

Character

The texture is firm and slightly grainy, and the flavour is complex and nutty, with notes that vary by season and cellar, ranging from butter and hazelnut to roasted onion and spice. Comté is used in fondue, gratins, soufflés, and as a table cheese.
