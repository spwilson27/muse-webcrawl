Jacques Tourneur (November 12, 1904 – December 19, 1977) was a French-American film director, best known for his atmospheric low-budget horror films made for RKO Radio Pictures in the 1940s under producer Val Lewton, and for the classic film noir Out of the Past (1947).

Early life

Tourneur was born in Paris, the son of silent film director Maurice Tourneur. He moved with his family to the United States in 1914 and became an American citizen. He began his career as an actor and script clerk in his father's films, then returned to France in the late 1920s, where he worked as an editor and director.

Career in Hollywood

Back in Hollywood, Tourneur directed second-unit work and short subjects for MGM before being assigned by producer Val Lewton to direct horror films at RKO. The first of these, Cat People (1942), became a surprise hit and established Tourneur's signature style: suggestion, shadow and ambiguity rather than explicit monsters. He followed with I Walked with a Zombie (1943) and The Leopard Man (1943).

Tourneur went on to direct the adventure films Anne of the Indies (1951) and The Flame and the Arrow (1950), the western Stars in My Crown (1950) and Wichita (1955), and the horror film Night of the Demon (1957), adapted from M. R. James's story "Casting the Runes". His 1947 noir Out of the Past, starring Robert Mitchum and Jane Greer, is regarded as one of the finest films in the genre.

Later years and legacy

Tourneur's later career was largely in television, directing episodes of series such as The Twilight Zone. He retired to France, where he died in 1977. His understated, moody visual style influenced generations of filmmakers and is now celebrated by critics and the Criterion Collection.
