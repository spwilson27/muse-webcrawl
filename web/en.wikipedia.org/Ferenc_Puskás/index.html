Ferenc Puskás

Ferenc Puskás (1 April 1927 – 17 November 2006) was a Hungarian footballer and manager, widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time. An inside forward with a devastating left foot, he captained Hungary's legendary "Mighty Magyars" team and later starred for Real Madrid, scoring 84 goals in 85 international matches for Hungary.

Club career

Puskás was born in Budapest and joined the army club Budapest Honvéd, where his father had played. He made his debut at 16 and became the centrepiece of the Honvéd side that dominated Hungarian football. He scored 514 goals in 529 league matches in Hungary and Spain combined. After the Hungarian Uprising of 1956, Honvéd toured abroad and Puskás refused to return home, receiving a two-year ban from UEFA.

Real Madrid

In 1958, aged 31 and overweight, Puskás signed for Real Madrid, forming a famous partnership with Alfredo Di Stéfano. He won three European Cups (1959, 1960, 1966) and five consecutive La Liga titles. In the 1960 European Cup Final against Eintracht Frankfurt at Hampden Park, he scored four goals in a 7–3 victory, still one of the most celebrated performances in the competition's history.

International career and later life

Puskás led Hungary to the gold medal at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics and to the 1954 World Cup final, which the Magyars surprisingly lost to West Germany. Hungary's 6–3 win over England at Wembley in 1953 remains iconic. After becoming a Spanish citizen, he played four matches for Spain at the 1962 World Cup. As a manager he led Panathinaikos to the 1971 European Cup Final. He died in Budapest in 2006; Hungary's national stadium is named the Puskás Aréna, and FIFA's award for the most beautiful goal of the year is the FIFA Puskás Award.
