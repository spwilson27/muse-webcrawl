Colm Tóibín

Colm Tóibín (born 30 May 1955) is an Irish novelist, short story writer, essayist, journalist, critic, playwright and poet. He is regarded as one of Ireland's leading contemporary writers, known for a spare, precise prose style and themes of exile, identity, and Irish society.

Early life

Tóibín was born in Enniscorthy, County Wexford, the second youngest of five children. His father died when he was twelve. He was educated at St Peter's College, Wexford, and University College Dublin, graduating in 1975. He lived in Barcelona and worked as a journalist before returning to Ireland.

Career

His first novel, The South (1990), was followed by The Heather Blazing (1992), The Story of the Night (1996), and The Blackwater Lightship (1999). The Master (2004), a fictionalised account of Henry James, won international acclaim. Brooklyn (2009) tells of Eilis Lacey emigrating from Enniscorthy to New York in the 1950s; it won the Costa Novel Award and was adapted into an acclaimed 2015 film. Its sequel Long Island appeared in 2024.

Other works

Later novels include Nora Webster (2014), House of Names (2017), and The Magician (2021), about Thomas Mann. He has taught at Princeton, Stanford, and Columbia University, and is Chancellor of the University of Liverpool.
