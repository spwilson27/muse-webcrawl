Digital elevation model

A digital elevation model (DEM) is a representation of a terrain's surface topography as data, most commonly a raster grid in which each cell carries an elevation value. DEMs are a core dataset in geographic information systems (GIS) and are used for mapping, hydrology, land-use planning, and 3D visualisation.

Shaded-relief rendering of a digital elevation model.

Types

  • Digital terrain model (DTM): represents the bare earth, with vegetation and buildings removed.
  • Digital surface model (DSM): represents the surface including trees, buildings, and other features.

Data sources

DEMs are produced from ground surveys, photogrammetry, LiDAR, and satellite radar interferometry. Major global datasets include the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM), which mapped most of Earth's land between 60°N and 56°S at about 30–90 m resolution, the ASTER Global DEM, and the TanDEM-X mission data.

Applications

Uses include watershed and flood modelling, line-of-sight and radio propagation analysis, slope and aspect mapping, orthorectification of imagery, precision agriculture, and computer graphics terrain rendering.
