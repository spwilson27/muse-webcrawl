Howl

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

“Howl”, also known as “Howl for Carl Solomon”, is a poem written by Allen Ginsberg in 1954–1955 and published in his collection Howl and Other Poems (1956). It is considered one of the great works of American literature and a defining text of the Beat Generation.

Author: Allen Ginsberg
First published: 1956 (City Lights Books)
First performed: October 7, 1955, Six Gallery, San Francisco
Form: Free verse, three parts and a “Footnote”

Content and style

The poem opens “I saw the best minds of my generation destroyed by madness” and proceeds in long, breath-driven lines influenced by jazz, Walt Whitman and William Carlos Williams. Part I catalogues the experiences of Ginsberg’s friends; Part II denounces the Moloch of industrial conformity; Part III addresses Carl Solomon, a fellow poet Ginsberg met in a psychiatric hospital; the “Footnote” ends in ecstatic affirmation: “Holy! Holy! Holy!”

Obscenity trial

Publisher Lawrence Ferlinghetti and City Lights manager Shigeyoshi Murao were prosecuted for obscenity after police seized imported copies in 1957. Judge Clayton W. Horn acquitted them, ruling that the poem had “redeeming social importance” — a landmark decision for free expression in American publishing.
