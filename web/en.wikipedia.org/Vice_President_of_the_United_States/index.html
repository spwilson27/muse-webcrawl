The vice president of the United States (VPOTUS) is the second-highest ranking official in the executive branch of the U.S. federal government, after the president of the United States, and ranks first in the presidential line of succession. The vice president is also an officer in the legislative branch, as the president of the Senate under Article One of the U.S. Constitution. In this capacity, the vice president is empowered to preside over Senate deliberations and may cast tie-breaking votes.

Constitutional roles

The vice president's primary constitutional duties are to succeed to the presidency upon the president's death, resignation, or removal, and to preside over the Senate. The Twelfth Amendment governs the election of the vice president together with the president via the Electoral College, and the Twenty-fifth Amendment (1967) provides procedures for filling vice-presidential vacancies and for presidential disability.

History

The office was initially filled by the runner-up in the presidential election, a system that produced friction under John Adams and Thomas Jefferson and was replaced by the Twelfth Amendment in 1804. For much of the 19th and early 20th centuries the vice presidency was a minor office with few duties. Its influence grew markedly from the later 20th century, as presidents assigned vice presidents roles as senior advisers, diplomats, and legislative liaisons; Walter Mondale's tenure (1977–1981) is often cited as the model for the modern, West Wing-based vice presidency.

Suc­cession record

Nine vice presidents have succeeded to the presidency upon the death or resignation of the incumbent, beginning with John Tyler in 1841. Fourteen vice presidents have gone on to become president by succession or election.

Residence and staff

Since 1974 the vice president's official residence has been Number One Observatory Circle on the grounds of the U.S. Naval Observatory in Washington, D.C. The vice president maintains offices in the West Wing, the Eisenhower Executive Office Building, and the Capitol.
