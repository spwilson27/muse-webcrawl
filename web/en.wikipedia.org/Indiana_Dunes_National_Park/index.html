Indiana Dunes National Park

Indiana Dunes National Park is a United States national park in northwestern Indiana, managed by the National Park Service. It comprises about 15,000 acres along 15 miles of the southern shore of Lake Michigan, encompassing sand dunes, beaches, wetlands, prairies, rivers, and oak savanna and forest ecosystems of remarkable biodiversity — more than 1,100 flowering plant species are found here.

Geology and ecology

The dunes were formed by the retreat of glaciers at the end of the last ice age, roughly 14,000 years ago, as lake levels fell and sand was piled by wind into ridges. Mount Baldy, a "living" moving dune, rises about 126 feet above the lake. Botanist Henry Chandler Cowles's studies of plant succession on the dunes in the 1890s made the area a birthplace of the science of ecology.

History

A preservation campaign led by Stephen Mather and the Prairie Club began in the 1910s. The area was authorized as Indiana Dunes National Lakeshore in 1966, saving it from industrial port development — though the Port of Indiana and steel mills were built adjacent. It was redesignated Indiana Dunes National Park on February 15, 2019, becoming the 61st national park. The separate Indiana Dunes State Park (established 1925) lies within its boundaries.

Recreation

Visitors swim and picnic at beaches such as West Beach, hike trails including the Cowles Bog and Pinhook Bog trails, camp at Dunewood Campground, and view historic structures such as the Century of Progress homes from the 1933 Chicago World's Fair.
