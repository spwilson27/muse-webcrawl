Sheck Exley


Born: 1 April 1949
Died: 6 April 1994, Zacatón, Mexico
Occupation: Cave diver, author
Known for: Pioneering cave diving safety and exploration

Sheck Exley (1 April 1949 – 6 April 1994) was an American cave diver widely regarded as one of the pioneers of the sport. He explored and mapped many of Florida's underwater cave systems and wrote foundational safety literature, including Basic Cave Diving: A Blueprint for Survival, which analysed cave diving fatalities and distilled rules — the "five rules of accident analysis" — that remain central to cave diving training.

Career

Exley began diving as a teenager and by age 23 had logged thousands of cave dives. He was the first to dive numerous systems in north Florida, worked with the National Speleological Society Cave Diving Section, and helped found standardised cave diving training. He also set depth records in open water sinkholes, diving to over 260 metres.

Death and legacy

Exley died in April 1994 while attempting a deep dive in El Zacatón, a flooded sinkhole in Tamaulipas, Mexico, then the world's deepest known water-filled pit. His body was recovered by his diving partner Jim Bowden. Exley's safety writings and exploration legacy strongly shaped modern technical diving.
