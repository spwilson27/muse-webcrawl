Ramon Llull (c. 1232 – c. 1315/16), also known in English as Raymond Lully, was a Majorcan philosopher, theologian, poet and missionary, and one of the most significant figures in the history of Catalan literature. A prolific writer in Catalan, Latin and Arabic, he devised a combinatorial system of thought — his Ars — intended to demonstrate the truths of the Christian faith through logical necessity, and he is regarded as a pioneer of computation and information theory.

Life

Llull was born in Palma, the capital of the newly conquered Kingdom of Mallorca, into a wealthy family from Barcelona. He served as a courtier and seneschal at the court of the future James II of Mallorca and led a worldly life, writing troubadour poetry. Around 1263 he experienced a series of visions of the crucified Christ, which transformed his life: he resolved to convert Muslims to Christianity, to write books demonstrating the faith, and to promote the founding of language schools for missionaries.

After years of study — including learning Arabic — Llull founded a missionary college at Miramar in Mallorca. He travelled extensively around the Mediterranean, disputed with Muslim and Jewish scholars, attended councils, and lobbied popes and kings for crusading and missionary projects. Tradition holds that he was stoned by an angry crowd during a mission to Tunis or Bougie and died of his wounds around 1315–1316.

The Ars

Llull's Ars Magna ("Great Art"), refined through versions such as the Ars generalis ultima (1308), used rotating concentric wheels inscribed with letters representing divine attributes and other basic concepts. By combining them systematically, the user could generate and evaluate propositions. The system foreshadows later developments in logic, combinatorics and mechanical computation, and influenced thinkers including Nicholas of Cusa, Giordano Bruno and Leibniz.

Writings

Llull wrote some 250 works. His novel Blanquerna (c. 1283), which contains the utopian Book of the Lover and the Beloved, is a landmark of Catalan prose. Other major works include the encyclopaedic Tree of Science, the Book of Contemplation, and numerous theological, philosophical and scientific treatises. He is celebrated as the first major author to write philosophy and literature in Catalan.

Legacy

Llull was beatified in the 19th century. His thought gave rise to the school of Lullism, and his combinatorial method is studied today as an early step toward artificial intelligence and computing. Ramon Llull University in Barcelona bears his name.
