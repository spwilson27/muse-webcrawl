Salar de Atacama

The Salar de Atacama is the largest salt flat in Chile. It is located 55 km south of San Pedro de Atacama in the Antofagasta Region, at about 2,305 metres above sea level in the endorheic basin between the Andes and the Cordillera Domeyko. The salar covers some 3,000 km² and contains a rough, hard salt crust over an enormous reservoir of lithium-rich brine. It is one of the world's principal sources of lithium and also yields potassium and boron, and its lagoons support distinctive high-altitude wildlife, including three species of flamingo.

Geology and hydrology

The salar occupies a tectonic depression filled with evaporite deposits washed down from the surrounding mountains. Its surface is a rugged crust of halite, unlike the smooth mirror of Bolivia's Salar de Uyuni. Beneath lies a brine body of exceptional richness, containing on average some of the highest lithium concentrations known in natural brines. Fresh and brackish water entering from the eastern cordillera forms marginal lagoons such as Chaxa, Tebenquiche, and Cejar, part of the Los Flamencos National Reserve.

Lithium extraction

Since the 1980s, brine has been pumped from beneath the salt crust into vast evaporation ponds, where the desert sun concentrates it over 12–24 months before processing into lithium carbonate and lithium hydroxide, largely by the companies SQM and Albemarle under concessions from the Chilean state agency Corfo. The salar has supplied a substantial share of world lithium for batteries. Extraction has generated controversy over water use in one of the driest places on Earth: indigenous Lickanantay (Atacameño) communities and scientists have raised concerns about falling water tables and impacts on lagoon ecosystems and flamingo breeding. In 2023 Chile announced a national lithium strategy envisioning greater state participation in the salar.

Fauna

The lagoons host Andean, Chilean, and James's flamingos, which feed on brine shrimp and diatoms, as well as Andean avocets, Puna plovers, and other high-altitude birds. Extremophile microorganisms in the salar's waters and crusts are studied as analogues for life in Martian environments.
