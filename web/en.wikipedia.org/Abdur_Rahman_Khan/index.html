Abdur Rahman Khan

Emir of Afghanistan1880–1901
Bornc. 1840–1844, Kabul
Died1901, Kabul
DynastyBarakzai

Abdur Rahman Khan (c. 1840/44 – 1901), known as the Iron Amir, was Emir of Afghanistan from 1880 to 1901. A grandson of Dost Mohammad Khan, he spent years in exile in Samarkand after losing a succession struggle, returning during the Second Anglo-Afghan War with British support.

Reign

Abdur Rahman centralized the Afghan state with unusual ruthlessness, crushing the power of tribal chiefs and regional governors, reorganizing taxation and the judiciary, and building a standing army with workshops producing modern weapons in Kabul. His campaigns subdued Hazarajat and Kafiristan (renamed Nuristan after the forced conversion of its inhabitants). In foreign affairs he accepted British control of Afghanistan's foreign policy in exchange for subsidies, while resisting direct British presence in the country. Boundary settlements under his rule included the Panjdeh incident resolution and the Durand Line agreement of 1893 with British India, and the fixing of the Wakhan Corridor.

Legacy

Abdur Rahman is credited with creating the modern centralized Afghan state, though by methods remembered as brutal. His autobiography remains an important source for nineteenth-century Afghan history. He was succeeded by his son Habibullah Khan.
