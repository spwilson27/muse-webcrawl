Boney M., one of the defining acts of the disco era

Boney M. was a German disco group created by record producer Frank Farian. Based in West Germany, the four original members — Liz Mitchell and Marcia Barrett from Jamaica, Maizie Williams from Montserrat, and Bobby Farrell from Aruba — formed the band's classic lineup. During the height of the disco era in the late 1970s, Boney M. became one of the most successful pop acts in Europe, selling an estimated 100 million records worldwide.

History

Farian, who had recorded the song "Baby Do You Wanna Bump" under the Boney M. name in 1974 (the name partly inspired by the Australian television detective series Boney), assembled a performing group after the song became a hit in the Netherlands and Belgium. The group's breakthrough came with "Daddy Cool" (1976), followed by chart-topping singles including "Sunny," "Ma Baker," "Belfast," and "Rivers of Babylon"/"Brown Girl in the Ring" (1978), one of the best-selling singles in UK chart history. Their version of "Mary's Boy Child – Oh My Lord" was the UK Christmas number one of 1978.

Other hits included "Rasputin" (1978) — which experienced a major viral revival on social media in the 2020s — "Painter Man," "Hooray! Hooray! It's a Holi-Holiday," "El Lute," and "Kalimba de Luna."

Production and vocals

Farian famously provided the deep male vocals in the studio himself, while Farrell — a dancer and showman — mimed them on stage; Farian publicly acknowledged the arrangement in the 1980s, a revelation that foreshadowed the later Milli Vanilli scandal, another Farian production. Farrell died on 30 December 2010 in Saint Petersburg, Russia — coincidentally the city and anniversary associated with Rasputin, the subject of the group's famous song.

Legacy

Boney M. was among the first Western pop acts officially permitted to perform in the Soviet Union, playing Moscow in 1978. Various versions of the group, led by different original members, have continued to tour.
