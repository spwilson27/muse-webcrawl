OSIRIS-REx

OSIRIS-REx (Origins, Spectral Interpretation, Resource Identification, Security, Regolith Explorer) is a NASA asteroid-study and sample-return mission. Launched on 8 September 2016, it rendezvoused with the carbonaceous near-Earth asteroid 101955 Bennu, collected a sample of its surface material in October 2020, and returned the sample capsule to Earth on 24 September 2023.

Mission

The spacecraft arrived at Bennu in December 2018 and spent nearly two years mapping the small (about 490-metre) spinning-top-shaped rubble pile, unexpectedly finding a rugged, boulder-strewn surface and observing particles being ejected into space. On 20 October 2020 the TAGSAM sampling arm touched the Nightingale crater site and collected material — so much that the sample container leaked particles before stowage; analysis later confirmed about 121.6 grams were returned, double the mission requirement.

Sample return and science

The capsule landed in the Utah desert and the pristine Bennu material was curated at Johnson Space Center. Studies found the regolith rich in carbon, hydrated minerals, and organic molecules including amino acids and nucleobases, supporting the idea that asteroids like Bennu may have delivered ingredients for life to the early Earth. Bennu also matters for planetary defence: its orbit brings it near Earth, with a small chance of impact in the late 22nd century.

Extended mission

After dropping the capsule, the spacecraft — renamed OSIRIS-APEX — continued toward a 2029 rendezvous with the asteroid 99942 Apophis shortly after its close Earth flyby.
