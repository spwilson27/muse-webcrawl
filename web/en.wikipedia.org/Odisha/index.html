Odisha

Country: India
Capital: Bhubaneswar
Largest city: Bhubaneswar
Official language: Odia

Odisha (Odia: ଓଡ଼ିଶା), formerly Orissa, is a state in eastern India, located along the coast of the Bay of Bengal. It is the eighth-largest state by area and the eleventh-largest by population, with over 41 million inhabitants. The capital and largest city is Bhubaneswar. The region corresponds largely to the ancient kingdom of Kalinga, whose invasion by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in 261 BCE is a landmark in Indian history.

History

After the Kalinga War, the region was ruled in succession by the Mahameghavahana, Gupta, Somavamshi and Eastern Ganga dynasties, the last of which built the great temples of Puri and the Sun Temple at Konark. Odisha came under Mughal, Maratha and then British rule; it became a separate province in 1936, the first Indian state created on a linguistic basis, and was renamed Odisha in 2011.

Geography and economy

The Mahanadi, Brahmani and Baitarani rivers form a fertile coastal plain, backed by the hills of the Eastern Ghats. The state is rich in minerals, holding large shares of India's reserves of iron ore, bauxite, chromite and coal, and has major steel and aluminium plants and the port of Paradip. Agriculture, especially rice, employs much of the workforce.

Culture

Odia is a classical language of India. Odisha is known for Odissi classical dance, Pattachitra painting, silver filigree work, and the Jagannath temple at Puri, whose annual Ratha Yatra chariot festival draws millions of pilgrims. Chilika Lake, Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon, is a major wetland for migratory birds.
