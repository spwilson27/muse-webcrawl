The Yerevan Opera Theatre, officially the Armenian National Academic Theatre of Opera and Ballet named after Alexander Spendiaryan, is the principal opera and ballet house of Armenia, located at Freedom Square (formerly Opera Square) in central Yerevan. It was designed by the architect Alexander Tamanian, master planner of modern Yerevan, whose design won a grand prix at the 1937 Paris International Exhibition.

History

The theatre opened on 20 January 1933 with Spendiaryan's opera Almast. The building, constructed in two stages and completed to Tamanian's design after his death, contains two halls back to back: the Opera and Ballet Theatre (1,200 seats) and the Aram Khachaturian Concert Hall (1,400 seats), sharing a single monumental structure ringed by an open colonnade. Statues of Spendiaryan and composer Aram Khachaturian stand in the surrounding square.

Role

Freedom Square in front of the Opera is a focal point of Yerevan's public life and the customary site of concerts, gatherings, and political demonstrations, including those of the 2018 Armenian revolution. The surrounding parkland, cafes, and the nearby Swan Lake make the area a centre of the city's social life.
