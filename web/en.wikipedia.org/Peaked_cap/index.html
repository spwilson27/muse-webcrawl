Peaked cap


A peaked cap, standard headgear for many uniformed services.

The peaked cap, also called a service cap or combination cap, is a form of headgear worn by the armed forces of many nations, as well as police, firefighters, and other uniformed civilian organisations. It consists of a soft crown, a band, and a stiff, usually leather or patent peak (visor) at the front.

History

The design evolved in the early 19th century from military shakos and forage caps; the Prussian and Russian armies were early adopters, and the style spread worldwide. Later variants include the German Tellermütze and the stiff-crowned caps of British officers. Distinctive features — cap badges, coloured bands, chin straps, and scrambled-egg embroidery on the peak for senior officers — denote rank and branch.

Use

Peaked caps remain part of dress uniforms for ceremonial occasions in most services, though practical field dress usually substitutes berets, side caps, or helmets. Civilian wear includes airline pilots, security guards, chauffeurs, and some railway staff.
