Nirvana (Buddhism)

Nirvana (Sanskrit: nirvāṇa; Pali: nibbāna) is a concept in Indian religions (Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, and Sikhism) that represents the ultimate state of soteriological release — freedom from the cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra). In Buddhism it is the goal of the Noble Eightfold Path.

Etymology and meaning

The word combines the prefix nis- ("out") with the root vā ("to blow"), giving the sense of "blowing out" or "quenching" — the extinction of the "three fires" of greed (rāga), aversion (dveṣa), and ignorance (moha). When these fires are extinguished, the practitioner is released from suffering (dukkha) and the cycle of rebirth.

In Buddhism

Buddhist traditions describe nirvana as the unconditioned, deathless state attained by the Buddha at his awakening (bodhi) and by arhats and buddhas thereafter. A distinction is drawn between nirvana with remainder — attained in life, as by the Buddha — and parinirvana, the final nirvana at death. Mahayana traditions further develop the concept, associating it with emptiness (śūnyatā) and the non-duality of nirvana and saṃsāra.
