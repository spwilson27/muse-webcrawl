Ataraxia

Ataraxia (Greek: ἀταραξία, "not disturbed," "not troubled") is a Greek philosophical term for a lucid state of robust equanimity characterized by ongoing freedom from distress and worry. It was a central goal of several Hellenistic schools of philosophy.

Epicureanism

For Epicurus and his followers, ataraxia was the state of freedom from mental disturbance that, together with aponia (absence of bodily pain), constituted the highest pleasure and the goal of life. It was achieved by limiting desires, avoiding politics, and cultivating friendship and philosophical reflection.

Stoicism and Skepticism

The Pyrrhonist skeptics held that ataraxia followed from epoché, the suspension of judgment about all non-evident matters; Sextus Empiricus compared its arrival to a shadow following a body. The Stoics valued a related state of tranquility, though their ideal was apatheia, freedom from destructive passions through virtue.

Modern usage

The word survives in the naming of the drug class "ataractics" (tranquilizers) and as a general literary term for imperturbable calm.
