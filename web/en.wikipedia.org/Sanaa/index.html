Sanaa, also spelled Sana'a or Sana (Arabic: صنعاء, Ṣanʿāʾ), is the capital and largest city of Yemen. Situated at an elevation of about 2,300 metres (7,500 ft) in the western highlands, it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world and one of the highest capital cities. The Old City of Sanaa has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986.

History

According to Yemeni legend, Sanaa was founded by Shem, the son of Noah. The city was an important centre from at least the 2nd century CE, when it came under the Himyarite Kingdom and later Sabaean influence. Sanaa became a centre of Islam in the 7th century, and the Great Mosque of Sanaa, built in that era, is one of the oldest mosques in the world. The city was ruled at various times by the Ziyadids, the Sulayhids, the Ayyubids, the Rasulids, the Tahirids, and the Zaydi imams, and was incorporated into the Ottoman Empire twice.

In the 20th century Sanaa became the capital of the Mutawakkilite Kingdom of Yemen, then of the Yemen Arab Republic (North Yemen) in 1962, and of the unified Republic of Yemen in 1990. Since 2014, during the Yemeni civil war, the city has been controlled by the Houthi (Ansar Allah) movement, while the internationally recognised government operates from Aden.

The Old City

The Old City is renowned for its distinctive architecture: tower houses rising up to eight storeys, built of rammed earth and burnt brick, decorated with elaborate white gypsum friezes. The city contains over 100 mosques, 14 hammams (bathhouses), and more than 6,500 houses dating from before the 11th century. The Great Mosque (Jami' al-Kabir) is said to incorporate stones from the palace of Ghumdan.

Geography and climate

Sanaa sits on a plateau bordered by Mount Nuqum to the east and Mount Ayban to the west. Despite its tropical latitude, its altitude gives it a mild, semi-arid climate, with cool nights and occasional frost in winter, and most rainfall in spring and late summer.

Economy and culture

The city is Yemen's political, economic and cultural centre. Souks in the Old City, such as Souk al-Milh, sell silver jewellery, jambiya daggers, coffee, spices and textiles. Sanaa is also home to Sanaa University, founded in 1970.
