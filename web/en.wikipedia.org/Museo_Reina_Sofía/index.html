Museo Reina Sofía

The Museo Nacional Centro de Arte Reina Sofía is Spain's national museum of 20th- and 21st-century art, located in Madrid near Atocha station. It forms the "Golden Triangle of Art" together with the Prado Museum and the Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum.

Collection

The museum's most famous work is Pablo Picasso's monumental anti-war painting Guernica (1937), transferred from the Prado in 1992. The collection also includes major works by Salvador Dalí, Joan Miró, Juan Gris and many contemporary artists, alongside temporary exhibitions, a library and a media archive.

Building

The museum occupies the former General Hospital of Madrid, an 18th-century building converted by architect Antonio Fernández Alba, with external glass lift towers added in the 1980s. A major extension designed by Jean Nouvel opened in 2005.
