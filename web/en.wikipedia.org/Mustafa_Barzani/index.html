Mustafa Barzani

Mustafa Barzani (14 March 1903 – 1 March 1979) was a Kurdish leader and politician who led armed resistance movements for Kurdish autonomy in Iraq. He founded the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) in 1946 and remained its dominant figure until his death.

Early life

Barzani was born in Barzan, in what was then the Ottoman Empire. From a young age he was involved in revolts against the Iraqi state, was exiled several times, and spent years in the Soviet Union after the collapse of the short-lived Republic of Mahabad in Iran in 1946, in which he had served as a military commander.

Struggle in Iraq

Returning to Iraq after the 1958 revolution, Barzani alternated between negotiation and armed rebellion against successive governments in Baghdad. The March 1970 agreement promised Kurdish autonomy but broke down, and the renewed war of 1974–75 ended in defeat after Iran withdrew support under the Algiers Agreement.

Death and legacy

Barzani died of lung cancer in Washington, D.C., in 1979. He is regarded as the pre-eminent national figure of the Kurds in Iraq; his son Masoud Barzani later became president of the Kurdistan Region.
