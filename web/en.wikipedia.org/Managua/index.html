Managua

Managua (Spanish pronunciation: [maˈnaɣwa]) is the capital and largest city of Nicaragua, and one of the largest cities in Central America. Located on the south shore of Lake Managua (Lake Xolotlán), the city had an estimated population of about 1.06 million in the city proper, with over 1.4 million in the metropolitan area. Founded in 1819 and made the capital in 1852 as a compromise between the rival cities of León and Granada, Managua is the country's political, cultural and economic centre.

History

The site was an indigenous fishing settlement on the lake's shore long before Spanish colonisation, and human footprints preserved in volcanic mud at nearby Acahualinca date back roughly 2,100 years. Elevated to a city in 1846, Managua became the permanent capital in 1852, ending the León–Granada rivalry. The city suffered catastrophic earthquakes in 1931 and, most devastatingly, on 23 December 1972, when a magnitude 6.3 quake destroyed the city centre and killed an estimated 10,000 people. Corruption in the Somoza regime's handling of reconstruction aid helped fuel the Sandinista revolution of 1979.

Geography and climate

Managua sits on the southern shore of Lake Xolotlán amid a chain of crater lakes, including the Tiscapa lagoon within the city. It has a tropical savanna climate with a wet season from May to November and hot, dry months from December to April, with average temperatures around 28 °C.

Landmarks and culture

The old centre around Lake Tiscapa includes the ruined Old Cathedral (damaged in 1972), the National Palace of Culture, the Rubén Darío National Theatre and the Loma de Tiscapa lookout with its silhouette of Augusto César Sandino. The modern commercial centre has shifted southwest along the Carretera a Masaya. Augusto C. Sandino International Airport serves the city, and the Universidad Nacional Autónoma de Nicaragua is based there. Managua's historic centre remains unusually low-rise, a legacy of seismic risk and of the 1972 earthquake, after which many districts were never rebuilt.
