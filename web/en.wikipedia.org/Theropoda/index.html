Theropoda (from Greek thēr, "wild beast", and pous, "foot") is a clade of saurischian dinosaurs characterised by hollow bones and three-toed limbs. Theropods first appeared during the Late Triassic, about 230 million years ago, and include all the large terrestrial carnivores of the Mesozoic, such as Allosaurus, Spinosaurus and Tyrannosaurus rex, as well as birds, which descend from small feathered theropods of the Jurassic.

Biology

Most theropods were bipedal predators with sharp, serrated teeth, grasping hands and keen senses, although some lineages became herbivorous or omnivorous (for example therizinosaurs and ornithomimosaurs). Feathers, first evolved in theropods for insulation or display, are now known from many groups, and numerous specimens from China preserve plumage in detail. Theropods ranged in size from the bee hummingbird — the smallest living dinosaur — to the largest terrestrial predators ever known.

Evolution and classification

Major theropod lineages include the ceratosaurs, the megalosauroids, the allosauroids, the tyrannosauroids, and the maniraptorans, which encompass dromaeosaurids, troodontids and birds (Aves). Birds are the only theropods to survive the Cretaceous–Palaeogene extinction event 66 million years ago; roughly 11,000 bird species are living theropod dinosaurs.
