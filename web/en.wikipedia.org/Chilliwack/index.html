Chilliwack


Chilliwack and the Cheam Range

Chilliwack is a city in the province of British Columbia, Canada. It is located 102 km (63 mi) east of Vancouver in the Fraser Valley, on the south side of the Fraser River. The city is surrounded by mountain peaks such as Mount Cheam and Slesse Mountain, and by rivers and extensive natural areas. At the 2021 census, Chilliwack had a population of about 93,200, making it one of the larger cities in the Fraser Valley Regional District.

History

The area is the traditional territory of the Stó:lō peoples, whose name for the area, Tselxwéyeqw, is the origin of the city's name, roughly translated as "quieter waters" or a term referring to the backwaters and sloughs of the Fraser. European settlement began during the Fraser Canyon Gold Rush of 1858, when steamboats carried prospectors up the Fraser River. The Township of Chilliwhack was incorporated in 1873, the third-oldest municipality in British Columbia. The City of Chilliwack and the Township merged in 1980, and the spelling was standardised to "Chilliwack".

Economy and culture

Agriculture is a major part of the local economy; Chilliwack is known for its corn, dairy farming and a growing number of craft breweries and farm markets. The city is home to a campus of the University of the Fraser Valley, the Chilliwack Cultural Centre, and the headquarters of a Canadian Armed Forces presence at the former CFB Chilliwack site (now Garrison Crossing). Outdoor recreation — fishing for salmon and sturgeon, hiking, mountain biking and paragliding — draws many visitors, and nearby Cultus Lake and Chilliwack Lake are popular destinations. The rock band Chilliwack, known for hits such as "My Girl (Gone, Gone, Gone)", took its name from the city.
