Lake Powell is a reservoir on the Colorado River in the southwestern United States, straddling the border between northern Arizona and southern Utah. It was created by the flooding of Glen Canyon by the Glen Canyon Dam, completed in 1966. The lake is a major vacation destination within the Glen Canyon National Recreation Area and is the second-largest artificial reservoir by maximum water capacity in the United States, behind Lake Mead.

The reservoir is named for explorer John Wesley Powell, who in 1869 led the first recorded expedition down the Colorado through the Grand Canyon. When full, the lake holds about 30 km³ (24 million acre-feet) of water and its intricate shoreline, with nearly 2,000 miles of winding canyons, exceeds the length of the entire U.S. West Coast shoreline.

History and controversy

Glen Canyon Dam, a 216-metre concrete arch dam near Page, Arizona, was built by the Bureau of Reclamation to store water for the Upper Basin states of the Colorado River Compact and to generate hydroelectric power. The flooding of Glen Canyon — a landscape many considered comparable to the Grand Canyon — became a landmark loss for the American conservation movement; the Sierra Club's campaign against the dam helped define modern environmentalism, and groups such as the Glen Canyon Institute have advocated draining the reservoir.

Drought

Since 2000, prolonged drought in the Colorado Basin has drawn the lake down drastically; it reached roughly a quarter of capacity in 2022–2023, its lowest level since initial filling, threatening hydropower generation (the "dead pool" threshold) and exposing long-submerged canyon features. Heavy snowpack in 2023 partially refilled it, but long-term levels remain a central issue in western water politics.
