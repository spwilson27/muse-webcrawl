Fascia (architecture)

TypeArchitectural element
LocationRoof edge; classical architrave bands

The word fascia derives from Latin fascia meaning "band, bandage, ribbon". The term is used in architecture in several related senses. In classical architecture, a fascia is one of the horizontal bands of an architrave: the Ionic architrave is typically divided into two or three fasciae stepped slightly outward from one another.

Roof fascia

In modern construction, a fascia (or fascia board) is the vertical finishing edge connected to the ends of rafters, trusses, or the area where a gutter is attached to the roof. It protects the roof's edge from weather, supports the lower edge of the bottom row of tiles or shingles, and provides a mounting surface for gutters. The horizontal surface beneath it, enclosing the underside of the roof overhang, is the soffit.

Other uses

In commercial buildings, "fascia" also denotes the flat surface above a shopfront or across the front of a building where signage is mounted — the sign band or parapet fascia.
