The Avro Vulcan (later Hawker Siddeley Vulcan from July 1963) is a jet-powered, tailless, delta-wing, high-altitude, strategic bomber, which was operated by the Royal Air Force (RAF) from 1956 until 1984. Aircraft manufacturer A.V. Roe and Company (Avro) designed the Vulcan in response to Specification B.35/46. Of the three V bombers produced, the Vulcan was considered the most technically advanced, hence the riskiest option.

Design and service

The Vulcan's huge delta wing, with four Rolls-Royce Olympus engines buried within it, gave it near-sonic performance at high altitude and remarkable agility for its size. As part of Britain's V-force, Vulcans carried the nation's nuclear deterrent — first Blue Danube and later WE.177 bombs — before the role passed to the Royal Navy's Polaris submarines in 1969, after which the Vulcan continued in low-level conventional and maritime radar reconnaissance roles.

Operation Black Buck

In 1982, during the Falklands War, Vulcans flying from Ascension Island carried out the Black Buck raids against Port Stanley airfield — at the time the longest-range bombing missions in history, requiring complex chains of Victor tanker refuellings. The type was retired from bomber service in 1984; the last display aircraft, XH558 "The Spirit of Great Britain", flew at airshows until 2015.
