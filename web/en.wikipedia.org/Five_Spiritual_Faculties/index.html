Five Spiritual Faculties

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Five Spiritual Faculties (Pali: pañc'indriyāni) are a set of qualities in Buddhism that, when developed and balanced, lead toward awakening. They form one of the groups within the thirty-seven bodhipakkhiyādhammā, the qualities conducive to enlightenment, and appear throughout the Indriya Samyutta of the Pali Canon.

The five faculties

FacultyPaliMeaning
Faith / convictionsaddhāTrust in the Buddha's awakening and the teaching
Energy / persistenceviriyaSustained effort to abandon unwholesome states and cultivate wholesome ones
MindfulnesssatiAttentive presence to body, feelings, mind, and phenomena
ConcentrationsamādhiCollectedness and unification of mind
Wisdom / discernmentpaññāInsight into arising and passing away, and into the Four Noble Truths

Balance

Buddhist commentaries stress that the faculties must be balanced in pairs: faith balanced by wisdom (strong faith with weak wisdom risks credulity; strong wisdom with weak faith risks cunning), and energy balanced by concentration (excess energy leads to restlessness; excess concentration to idleness). Mindfulness, the third faculty, is traditionally said to be useful in all circumstances and protects the others from excess or deficiency.

Faculties and powers

The same five qualities appear in the lists of the Thirty-Seven Aids to Awakening twice: as faculties (indriya) and as the Five Powers (bala). Commentarial tradition distinguishes them by degree — as faculties they govern or control their respective domains, while as powers they are unshakable by their opposites: faithlessness, laziness, forgetfulness, distraction, and delusion respectively.
