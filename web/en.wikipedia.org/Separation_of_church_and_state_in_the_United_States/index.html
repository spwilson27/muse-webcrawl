Separation of church and state in the United States

The separation of church and state in the United States is the legal and political principle that government institutions and religious institutions should be kept separate. It derives chiefly from the First Amendment to the US Constitution (1791): "Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof."

Origins

The metaphor of a "wall of separation between Church and State" comes from Thomas Jefferson's 1802 letter to the Danbury Baptists. The Establishment Clause and Free Exercise Clause were applied to the states through the Fourteenth Amendment, notably in Everson v. Board of Education (1947).

Case law

Landmark cases include Lemon v. Kurtzman (1971), which set a three-part test for establishment; Engel v. Vitale (1962) on school prayer; and Kennedy v. Bremerton (2022), which moved doctrine toward history and tradition. Debate continues over public funding of religious schools, religious displays on public property, and exemptions from general laws.
