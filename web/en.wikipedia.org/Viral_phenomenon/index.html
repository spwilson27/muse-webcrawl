Viral phenomenon
Also: "going viral"
Related concepts: internet memes, virality, viral marketing, network effects

A viral phenomenon occurs when content, information or behaviour spreads rapidly through a population by imitation and sharing, by analogy with the spread of a virus. In the internet age the term usually describes videos, images, posts or memes that achieve sudden, exponential popularity through social media.

Mechanism

Virality depends on high sharing rates: if each viewer on average recruits more than one new viewer, the audience grows explosively until the susceptible population is exhausted. Emotional arousal — awe, humour, anger, surprise — increases the likelihood of sharing, as do social currency (sharing makes the sharer look good) and practical value. Platform recommendation algorithms can amplify or suppress spread.

Examples and impact

Famous cases range from the "Dancing Baby" of the 1990s and "Gangnam Style" (the first YouTube video to reach a billion views) to charity campaigns such as the Ice Bucket Challenge of 2014, which raised over $100 million for ALS research. Marketers pursue "viral marketing", though true virality is notoriously hard to manufacture; the same dynamics also spread misinformation, prompting research into fact-checking and content moderation.
