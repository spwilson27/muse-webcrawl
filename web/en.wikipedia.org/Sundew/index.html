Sundew

Sundews, genus Drosera, are one of the largest genera of carnivorous plants, with at least 194 species. Members of the family Droseraceae, they lure, capture, and digest insects using stalked mucilaginous glands covering their leaf surfaces. The insects supplement the poor mineral nutrition of the soil in which the plants grow.

Description

Sundews are perennial (or occasionally annual) herbaceous plants forming prostrate or upright rosettes between 1 and 100 centimetres in height. Their leaves are covered with tentacles tipped with glistening droplets of sticky mucilage that sparkle like dew in sunlight — the source of the common name. When an insect lands, the tentacles bend toward it, trapping it, and the leaf may curl around the prey while digestive enzymes dissolve it.

Distribution and habitat

The genus is distributed on every continent except Antarctica, with the greatest diversity in Australia. Sundews typically grow in seasonally moist or permanently wet habitats with acidic, nutrient-poor soils, such as bogs, fens, and sandy or peaty heaths.

Cultivation and uses

Many species are cultivated by carnivorous-plant enthusiasts. Sundews were historically used in herbal medicine, and the plant's mucilage and naphthoquinone compounds such as plumbagin remain of research interest.
