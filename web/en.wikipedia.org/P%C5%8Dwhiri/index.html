Pōwhiri

A pōwhiri (also rendered pōhiri) is a Māori welcome ceremony involving speeches, dancing, singing and finally the hongi (pressing of noses). It is used to both welcome guests onto a marae or other venue, and as an opening ceremony for conferences, hui and other formal gatherings in New Zealand.

Elements of the ceremony

A traditional pōwhiri may include a wero (challenge by a warrior), a karanga (ceremonial call performed by women), whaikōrero (formal speeches, often in te reo Māori), waiata tautoko (supporting songs sung after each speech), the exchange of koha (gifts), and the hongi, in which hosts and guests press noses to share the breath of life, signifying the lifting of tapu (sacred restrictions) and the joining of the two groups as one.

Significance

The pōwhiri embodies the values of manaakitanga (hospitality) and whanaungatanga (relationship-building). Historically, the ceremony also served a practical purpose: the challenge and speeches allowed the host group to determine whether visitors came in peace. Today, pōwhiri are performed at marae, schools, workplaces, and official state occasions in Aotearoa New Zealand.
