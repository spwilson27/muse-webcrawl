Jumpstart Our Business Startups Act (JOBS Act)
Enacted5 April 2012
Public Law112-106
Signed byPresident Barack Obama
PurposeEase securities regulation for small businesses and startups

The Jumpstart Our Business Startups Act, or JOBS Act, is a United States federal law enacted on 5 April 2012 that loosened securities regulations to encourage funding of small businesses and startups. Passed with broad bipartisan support in the aftermath of the Great Recession, it amended the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 in several significant ways.

Main provisions

Title I created a category of "emerging growth companies" — firms with under billion in revenue — that could file draft IPO registration statements confidentially, delay certain financial disclosures and reduce compliance burdens for up to five years after going public. Title II lifted the ban on general solicitation for certain private placements sold only to accredited investors. Title III, implemented through the SEC's Regulation Crowdfunding (effective 2016), allowed ordinary investors to buy securities in small offerings through registered online portals, legalizing equity crowdfunding. Other titles raised the shareholder threshold triggering full SEC reporting, expanded Regulation A ("mini-IPOs") and adjusted rules for community banks.

Reception

Supporters argued the Act reduced the cost of raising capital and reversed a decline in IPOs; critics, including some investor advocates and former regulators, warned that reduced disclosure and crowdfunding exposed retail investors to fraud and risk. Follow-on legislation ("JOBS Act 2.0" measures and the 2018 Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act) extended several of its deregulatory approaches.
