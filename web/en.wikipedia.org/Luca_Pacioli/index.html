Luca Pacioli

Fra Luca Bartolomeo de Pacioli (c. 1447 – 19 June 1517) was an Italian mathematician, Franciscan friar, collaborator with Leonardo da Vinci, and an early contributor to accounting. He is often called the "Father of Accounting" because his Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalita (1494) contained the first published description of the double-entry bookkeeping system used by Venetian merchants.

Life

Pacioli was born in Sansepolcro in Tuscany. He studied mathematics, worked as a tutor in Venice, joined the Franciscan order, and taught at universities in Perugia, Naples, Pisa and Milan, where he befriended Leonardo da Vinci and tutored him in mathematics.

Works

His Divina proportione (written 1496–98, published 1509), illustrated by Leonardo, explored the golden ratio in art and architecture. He also published a treatise on chess, De ludo scachorum, long thought lost.
