Cairo–Cape Town Highway

Cairo–Cape Town Highway
Length: c. 10,228 km (6,355 mi)
Route: Trans-African Highway 4 (TAH 4)
From: Cairo, Egypt — To: Cape Town, South Africa

The Cairo–Cape Town Highway is Trans-African Highway 4 in the transcontinental road network being developed by the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa (UNECA), the African Development Bank (ADB), and the African Union. The route has a length of 10,228 km (6,355 mi) and runs the length of eastern Africa from Cairo, Egypt, to Cape Town, South Africa.

Route

The highway passes through Egypt, Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Botswana and South Africa, largely following the corridor envisioned by Cecil Rhodes's Cape to Cairo scheme of the British colonial era. Major cities on the route include Khartoum, Addis Ababa (via a spur), Nairobi, Lusaka and Harare (via an alternative section), and Johannesburg.

Condition and development

Most of the route is paved, but sections in northern Kenya and parts of Sudan and Ethiopia long remained unpaved or difficult, and border crossings and conflicts have hampered through travel. Completion of the paved Kenyan section between Isiolo and Moyale in the 2010s was a major milestone toward a fully paved transcontinental route.
