Treaty of Portsmouth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaty of Portsmouth was a treaty signed on 5 September 1905 that officially ended the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–1905. It was negotiated at the Portsmouth Naval Shipyard near Portsmouth, New Hampshire, in the United States, with mediation by President Theodore Roosevelt, who received the Nobel Peace Prize for his role.

Terms

Russia recognised Japan's paramount political, military and economic interests in Korea, ceded the southern half of Sakhalin Island (below the 50th parallel) to Japan, and transferred to Japan the lease of the Liaodong Peninsula (including Port Arthur) and the South Manchuria Railway, subject to China's consent. Both powers agreed to evacuate Manchuria. No war indemnity was paid, a point that provoked the Hibiya riots in Tokyo because the Japanese public had expected one.

Consequences

The treaty confirmed Japan's emergence as a great power and foreshadowed its annexation of Korea in 1910. Russia redirected its expansionist ambitions toward the Balkans, with consequences for European stability in the decade before the First World War.
