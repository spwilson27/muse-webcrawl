Lithostratigraphy

[Image: Layered sedimentary strata in a cliff face]
Sedimentary strata, the raw material of lithostratigraphy.

Lithostratigraphy is a sub-discipline of stratigraphy, the geological science associated with the study of strata or rock layers. It deals with the description and naming of rock bodies based on their lithology (physical character) and their stratigraphic relations, without direct reference to their age.

Principles

Lithostratigraphy rests on two fundamental laws formulated in the seventeenth century by Nicholas Steno: the law of superposition, that in an undisturbed sequence younger layers lie above older ones, and the principle of original horizontality, that sedimentary layers are deposited approximately horizontally. Later workers added the principle of lateral continuity and the law of cross-cutting relationships.

Lithostratigraphic units

The fundamental unit is the formation: a body of rock identified by its lithic characteristics and stratigraphic position, mappable at the surface or traceable in the subsurface. Formations may be subdivided into members and individual beds, or combined into groups and supergroups. Unit names combine a geographic name with a lithic term or the word "Formation", as in the Morrison Formation.

Relation to other stratigraphies

Lithostratigraphy contrasts with biostratigraphy, which orders rocks by their fossil content, and chronostratigraphy, which relates rocks to geologic time. Because rock types can repeat through time and a single lithology may be deposited at different times in different places, lithostratigraphic boundaries are commonly diachronous — they cross time lines.
