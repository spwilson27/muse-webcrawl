Postpartum period

Postpartum period
Other namesPuerperium, puerperal period, fourth trimester
BeginsImmediately after delivery of the placenta
DurationTraditionally c. 6 weeks (42 days)
PhasesAcute (6–12 h), subacute (2–6 weeks), delayed (up to 6 months)

The postpartum period, also called the puerperium or the "fourth trimester", is the period beginning immediately after childbirth during which the mother's body, including hormone levels and the size of the uterus, returns to a non-pregnant state. It is conventionally taken to last about six weeks. The World Health Organization describes the first 24 hours and the first week after birth as the most critical phase for mother and newborn.

Physiological changes

In the weeks after delivery the uterus undergoes involution, shrinking from about 1 kg to under 100 g, and the discharge known as lochia gradually ceases. Hormonal shifts accompany the onset of lactation: prolactin stimulates milk production and oxytocin drives the let-down reflex. Common experiences include afterpains, perineal soreness, breast engorgement, night sweats, hair shedding and fatigue. Ovulation and menstruation typically resume later in non-breastfeeding than in breastfeeding mothers.

Health issues

Complications of the postpartum period include postpartum haemorrhage, infection (such as endometritis and mastitis), venous thromboembolism, and hypertensive disorders including postpartum preeclampsia. Mental health conditions are also significant: transient "baby blues" affect many mothers, while postpartum depression affects roughly one in ten, and postpartum psychosis is a rare psychiatric emergency. Routine postnatal care includes checks of blood pressure, bleeding, wound healing, feeding support and contraception counselling.

Cultural practices

Many cultures observe a traditional confinement period of rest for new mothers, such as the Chinese zuò yuè zi ("sitting the month"), the Latin American cuarentena, and similar customs in South and West Asia, usually lasting around 40 days and involving special foods and support from female relatives.
