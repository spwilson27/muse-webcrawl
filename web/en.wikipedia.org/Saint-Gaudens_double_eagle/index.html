The Saint-Gaudens double eagle is a twenty-dollar gold coin produced by the United States Mint from 1907 to 1933. It is widely considered the most beautiful coin ever struck by the United States. The coin takes its name from its designer, the sculptor Augustus Saint-Gaudens.

Origins

In 1904 President Theodore Roosevelt, dissatisfied with the artistic quality of American coinage, commissioned Saint-Gaudens — the foremost American sculptor of the day — to redesign the nation's coins in what Roosevelt called his "pet crime". Roosevelt wanted coins modelled on the high-relief coinage of ancient Greece. Saint-Gaudens produced a dramatic design: on the obverse, a full-length figure of Liberty strides out of the coin toward the viewer, holding a torch in her right hand and an olive branch in her left, with the United States Capitol visible in miniature at lower left; the reverse shows an eagle in flight above a rising sun.

Production difficulties

The artist's extremely high relief proved impractical: the Mint's chief engraver Charles E. Barber repeatedly clashed with Saint-Gaudens and reduced the relief over successive patterns. Even so, the first production coins of 1907 required multiple blows of the press and wore dies quickly, and the relief was lowered further for mass production. Saint-Gaudens died in August 1907, shortly before the coin entered circulation.

Design changes

The original 1907 coins bore the date in Roman numerals (MCMVII) and, controversially, omitted the motto "In God We Trust", which Roosevelt disliked; Congress ordered the motto added in 1908, and the date was changed to Arabic numerals. Minting continued annually until 1933, when President Franklin D. Roosevelt ended the production of gold coinage during the Great Depression.

The 1933 double eagle

Some 445,500 double eagles dated 1933 were struck but never officially issued, as the United States abandoned the gold standard; nearly all were melted down. A handful escaped the melting pot, and one example — the only one the United States government has authorised for private ownership — sold at auction in 2002 for $7.59 million, then a world record for a coin, and again in 2021 for $18.9 million, making it among the most valuable coins ever sold.

Legacy

The Saint-Gaudens obverse design was revived in 1986 for the American Gold Eagle bullion coin series, where it remains in use. Numismatists routinely rank the coin at or near the top of lists of the greatest coin designs in history.
