Summation

In mathematics, summation is the addition of a sequence of numbers, called addends or summands; the result is their sum or total. Besides numbers, other types of values can be summed as well: functions, vectors, matrices, and polynomials, among others.

Notation

Capital-sigma notation, introduced by Leibniz in 1675, writes a sum as Σ with an index of summation: the sum of ai for i from m to n is am + am+1 + ⋯ + an. An empty sum (no terms) is by convention equal to zero, the additive identity.

Finite and infinite sums

Sums over finitely many terms always have a definite value in ordinary arithmetic. Sums over infinitely many terms are series, which are evaluated as limits of partial sums and may converge or diverge.

Useful identities

Familiar closed forms include Σi = n(n+1)/2 for the sum of the first n positive integers, and the geometric sum Σrk = (1 − rn+1)/(1 − r). Summation is linear and satisfies index-shift and splitting rules exploited throughout algebra, calculus, and statistics.
