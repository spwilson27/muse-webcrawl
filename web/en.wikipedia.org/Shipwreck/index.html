Shipwreck

A shipwreck is the wreckage of a ship that has been destroyed or lost at sea, whether sunk, beached, or broken up on rocks or reefs. The term also refers to the event itself. Causes include storms, collisions, grounding, fire, war, structural failure, and navigational error.

History and notable wrecks

An estimated three million shipwrecks lie on the world's ocean floors. Famous wrecks include the Mary Rose (1545), the Spanish Armada wrecks (1588), RMS Titanic (1912, located in 1985 at a depth of about 3,800 metres), the Bismarck (1941), and modern disasters such as the Exxon Valdez oil spill (1989) and Costa Concordia (2012).

Legal and cultural aspects

Wrecks raise questions of salvage law, ownership, and — for warships — sovereign immunity and war-grave status. Under the UNESCO Convention on the Protection of the Underwater Cultural Heritage (2001), wrecks over a century old are protected as heritage. Shipwrecks also create artificial reefs and popular diving sites, though they may leak oil and hazardous cargo for decades.
