Mezzo-soprano

A mezzo-soprano (mezzo meaning "half" in Italian) is a type of classical female singing voice whose vocal range lies between the soprano and the contralto. The typical range is from the A below middle C (A3) to the A two octaves above (A5), with a tessitura lower and a generally darker, warmer timbre than the soprano voice.

Roles

Mezzo-sopranos frequently sing secondary female leads, older women, villains and "trouser roles" (young men played by women), such as Cherubino in Le nozze di Figaro and Octavian in Der Rosenkavalier. Signature mezzo roles include Carmen in Bizet's opera, Rosina in The Barber of Seville, Amneris in Aida and Dalila in Saint-Saëns's Samson et Dalila. Celebrated mezzo-sopranos include Cecilia Bartoli and Marilyn Horne.

Subtypes

Subtypes include the coloratura mezzo-soprano, lyric mezzo-soprano and dramatic mezzo-soprano, distinguished by agility, weight of tone and typical repertoire.
