Spline (mathematics)

In mathematics, a spline is a special function defined piecewise by polynomials. In interpolating problems, spline interpolation is often preferred to polynomial interpolation with a single high-degree polynomial because it yields similar results, even when using low-degree polynomials, while avoiding Runge's phenomenon for higher degrees.

Definition

A spline of degree n is a piecewise polynomial function of degree n whose pieces join at points called knots, with specified continuity of derivatives at the knots. The most common are cubic splines, which have continuous first and second derivatives. A B-spline (basis spline) forms a basis for the space of splines on a given knot sequence.

History and etymology

The term "spline" comes from the flexible drafting tool — a thin strip of wood or metal — used by shipbuilders and draughtsmen to draw smooth curves, held in place by weights. Isaac Jacob Schoenberg introduced the mathematical term in 1946.

Applications

Splines are used extensively in computer-aided design, computer graphics, data fitting, and numerical solution of differential equations.
