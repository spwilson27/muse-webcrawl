Point (geometry)

In geometry, a point is an abstract idealization of an exact position, with no size, area, length or any other dimensional attribute. Points, lines and planes are the undefined primitive notions of Euclidean geometry, upon which all other concepts are built. Euclid originally defined a point as "that which has no part".

Points in Euclidean geometry

In a two-dimensional Euclidean space, a point is represented by an ordered pair (x, y) of numbers, and in three dimensions by an ordered triple (x, y, z) — Cartesian coordinates. Two points determine a unique line; three non-collinear points determine a unique plane.

Dimension theory

Informally, a point has zero dimensionality. In modern mathematics, a point is an element of the underlying set of a topological or metric space, and a space consisting of a single point is used to define 0-dimensional objects.

Point masses and point geometry

In physics, the idealization of a point mass — a mass concentrated at a point — underlies much of classical mechanics and gravitation. In vector algebra, points and displacement vectors are frequently identified through choice of an origin.
