The 2001 anthrax attacks, also known as Amerithrax after the FBI case name, occurred in the United States beginning one week after the September 11 attacks. Letters containing anthrax spores were mailed to several news media offices and to two Democratic US senators, killing five people and infecting 17 others.

The letters

Two sets of letters were mailed from Trenton, New Jersey: the first, postmarked 18 September 2001, went to media organisations including NBC News, the New York Post and American Media in Florida; the second, postmarked 9 October, was addressed to Senators Tom Daschle and Patrick Leahy. Photo editor Robert Stevens of the Sun tabloid was the first fatal victim. The attacks shut down postal facilities and contaminated the Hart Senate Office Building, which was closed for months of decontamination.

Investigation

The FBI's Amerithrax investigation was one of the largest in its history. Media focus fell for years on former biodefence scientist Steven Hatfill, who was later exonerated and received a settlement. In 2008 federal prosecutors identified USAMRIID scientist Bruce Ivins as the perpetrator; Ivins died by suicide in July 2008 before charges were filed. The case was formally closed in February 2010, though a 2011 National Research Council review found the scientific evidence consistent with, but not definitively proving, the FBI's conclusion.
