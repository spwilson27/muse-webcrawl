Bellini transformed Venetian painting with luminous colour and oil technique.

Giovanni Bellini (c. 1430 – 29 November 1516) was an Italian Renaissance painter, probably the most famous member of the Venetian Bellini family of painters. Raised in the workshop of his father Jacopo Bellini alongside his elder brother Gentile, he revolutionized Venetian painting, moving it toward a more sensuous, colouristic style and mastering the new medium of oil paint. His pupils and followers included Giorgione and Titian, through whom his influence shaped the Venetian High Renaissance. Albrecht Dürer, visiting Venice in 1506, called him "the best painter of all."

Early life and training

Bellini was born in Venice around 1430. He trained under his father Jacopo, a leading early Renaissance painter, and was strongly influenced by his brother-in-law Andrea Mantegna, whose sculptural, linear style shows in Bellini's early works such as the Agony in the Garden (c. 1465, National Gallery, London). After Gentile became the family's most sought-after portraitist, Giovanni emerged in the 1470s as Venice's leading painter of altarpieces and Madonnas.

Mature work

Bellini's encounter with the Sicilian painter Antonello da Messina, who brought Flemish-style oil painting to Venice in 1475, helped him develop the glowing, layered colour that became the hallmark of Venetian art. His numerous Madonna and Child paintings combine serene devotion with soft, atmospheric landscapes. Major works include the San Giobbe Altarpiece (c. 1487), the Frari Triptych (1488), and the luminous San Zaccaria Altarpiece (1505), a sacra conversazione of unmatched tranquility. He also painted sensitive portraits, including that of Doge Leonardo Loredan (1501), and late in life turned to mythological subjects, completing The Feast of the Gods in 1514 for Alfonso d'Este — a canvas later reworked by Titian.

Workshop and legacy

From 1483 Bellini was official painter to the Venetian Republic, overseeing the narrative canvases of the Doge's Palace (destroyed by fire in 1577). His large workshop trained or influenced the next generation of Venetian masters. He died in Venice in 1516, still active and esteemed, and was buried in the church of Santi Giovanni e Paolo.
