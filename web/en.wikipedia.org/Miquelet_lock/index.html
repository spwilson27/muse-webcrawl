Miquelet lock

The miquelet lock is a style of flintlock firearm ignition mechanism developed in Spain in the late sixteenth century and widely used across the Mediterranean world — in Spain, Italy, the Balkans, North Africa and the Ottoman sphere — for more than two centuries. It is named after the miquelets, Catalan irregular troops.

The mechanism is distinguished by placing the mainspring on the outside of the lock plate, where it acts directly on the toe of the cock, and by combining the steel (frizzen) and pan cover in a single piece. Its horizontal sears engage the cock through the lock plate, giving the familiar half-cock and full-cock positions. Gunsmiths prize the lock for its robustness: its external spring and simple, sturdy geometry made it reliable in rough service and easy to repair in the field.

Variants and use

Regional variants include the classic Spanish patilla ("little foot") form, the Italian alla romana style, and Caucasian and Balkan versions with distinctive decoration. Miquelet locks remained in production in some regions into the nineteenth century, long after the "true" flintlock of French pattern had become standard elsewhere, and fine examples are noted for elaborate steel work and engraving.
