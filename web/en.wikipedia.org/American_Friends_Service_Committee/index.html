American Friends Service Committee

The American Friends Service Committee (AFSC) is a Religious Society of Friends (Quaker) founded in 1917 in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. It works for peace and social justice in the United States and around the world. In 1947, the AFSC and its British counterpart, the Friends Service Council, jointly received the Nobel Peace Prize.

History

The AFSC was formed during the First World War to provide conscientious objectors with an alternative to military service; its volunteers ran relief and reconstruction programs in France and, after the war, fed children in Germany and Austria and aided victims of the Russian famine. During the Second World War it assisted refugees and Japanese Americans released from internment camps. In later decades the AFSC supported the American civil rights movement, opposed the Vietnam War, and campaigned on behalf of farmworkers, Native Americans, and prisoners.

Programs

Current AFSC programs address economic justice, immigrant rights, criminal justice reform, demilitarisation, and peace-building in regions including the Middle East, Africa, Asia, and Latin America. The organisation is guided by Quaker testimonies of peace, integrity, community, and equality, though most of its staff and programme participants are not Quakers.
