Former eastern territories of Germany

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The former eastern territories of Germany (German: ehemalige deutsche Ostgebiete) are the territories east of the postwar Oder–Neisse line that belonged to Germany before 1945 and were placed under Polish or Soviet administration after the Second World War. They comprised most of Silesia, East Prussia, Farther Pomerania, the Neumark (eastern Brandenburg), northern East Prussia's border regions of West Prussia, and the Free City of Danzig — roughly a quarter of Germany's 1937 territory.

Transfer in 1945

Under the Potsdam Agreement of August 1945, the territories east of the Oder and Lusatian Neisse were placed under Polish administration, and northern East Prussia, including the city of Königsberg (renamed Kaliningrad), was annexed by the Soviet Union, pending a final peace settlement. Between 1944 and 1950, some 12–14 million Germans fled or were expelled from these territories and from elsewhere in Eastern Europe, and the regions were repopulated by Poles — many of them themselves displaced from Poland's eastern territories (Kresy) annexed by the USSR — and by Russians in the Kaliningrad Oblast.

Legal status and recognition

The Federal Republic of Germany initially did not recognise the Oder–Neisse line. The Treaty of Zgorzelec (1950) between East Germany and Poland, the Treaty of Warsaw (1970) between West Germany and Poland, and finally the Two Plus Four Treaty and the German–Polish Border Treaty of 1990 definitively settled the border, with a reunified Germany formally renouncing all claims to the territories.

Legacy

The expellees (Vertriebene) became a significant political and cultural force in postwar West Germany through organisations such as the Federation of Expellees. German place names and cultural heritage remain visible across the regions, and the cities of Wrocław (Breslau), Gdańsk (Danzig), Szczecin (Stettin), and Kaliningrad (Königsberg) retain major German architectural and historical legacies.
