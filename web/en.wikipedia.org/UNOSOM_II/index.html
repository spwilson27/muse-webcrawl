UNOSOM II

The United Nations Operation in Somalia II (UNOSOM II) was the second phase of the United Nations intervention in Somalia, established by Security Council Resolution 814 in March 1993. It took over from the US-led Unified Task Force (UNITAF) in May 1993 and continued until its withdrawal in March 1995. UNOSOM II was the first UN operation authorised as a peace enforcement mission under Chapter VII of the UN Charter.

Mandate

UNOSOM II's mandate went far beyond the protection of humanitarian relief that had characterised UNOSOM I and UNITAF. Resolution 814 authorised it to use force to disarm Somali factions, rehabilitate the country's political institutions and economy, and promote national reconciliation after the civil war that followed the fall of Siad Barre in 1991.

Conflict with Aideed

The mission deteriorated sharply on 5 June 1993, when 24 Pakistani peacekeepers were killed in an ambush attributed to forces of warlord Mohamed Farrah Aideed. Resolution 837 authorised the arrest of those responsible, and UNOSOM II, supported by US forces including Task Force Ranger, conducted a manhunt for Aideed through the summer of 1993. The campaign culminated in the Battle of Mogadishu on 3–4 October 1993, in which 18 US soldiers were killed and two Black Hawk helicopters shot down — events dramatised in the film Black Hawk Down.

Withdrawal and legacy

After Mogadishu, the United States withdrew its forces by March 1994, and other contributors followed; UNOSOM II itself closed in March 1995. The operation is widely regarded as a cautionary example of "mission creep" and it profoundly affected subsequent UN and US willingness to intervene in conflicts, including during the Rwandan genocide of 1994. Somalia remained without an effective central government for many years afterwards.
