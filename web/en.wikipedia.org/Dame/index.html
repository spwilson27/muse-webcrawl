Dame

Dame is an official title of honour and the feminine equivalent of the title of Sir used for knights. A woman appointed to the grades of Dame Commander or Dame Grand Cross of the orders of chivalry in the British honours system — such as the Order of the British Empire, the Order of St Michael and St George, the Royal Victorian Order and the Order of the Bath — is addressed as "Dame" followed by her given name, for example Dame Judi Dench.

Usage

The title derives from the Latin domina, "lady" or "mistress". Historically "dame" was also a respectful form of address for a woman more generally, and survives in uses such as the pantomime dame character of British theatre and in American slang of the early 20th century. In some orders, wives of knights are styled "Lady", while a dame in her own right uses "Dame".

Other uses

The word is also the name for a female titleholder in some other countries' honours systems modelled on the British one, and "Notre Dame" (French for "Our Lady") refers to the Virgin Mary in the names of churches and institutions such as the University of Notre Dame.
