Breton music

Breton music is the traditional and contemporary music of Brittany, the Celtic region in the northwest of France. It forms part of the wider family of Celtic music, sharing characteristics with the music of Ireland, Scotland, Wales, and Galicia, while possessing strongly distinctive features of its own — above all the pairing of the biniou kozh (a small, high-pitched bagpipe) with the bombarde (a double-reed shawm), and the bagad, a large ensemble modelled on the Scottish pipe band.

Traditional forms

Breton folk music accompanies dance and song. The fest-noz ("night festival"), a communal dance gathering inscribed by UNESCO on its list of intangible cultural heritage in 2012, is the central social setting for the music. Dance tunes are played for dances such as the an-dro, hanter-dro, gavotte, and plinn. Singing traditions include the kan ha diskan ("singing and counter-singing"), a call-and-response technique in which a second singer picks up the last phrase of the leader, sustaining an unbroken line for dancing.

The gwerz is a long narrative ballad, often tragic or historical, while the sonn is a lighter, more lyrical song. Instrumental traditions centre on the bombarde and biniou kozh pair, often competing and performing in formalised contests at festivals, and on the bagad, which combines bombardes, bagpipes (biniou braz), and drums.

Revival

Interest in Breton culture revived strongly from the 1950s onwards. Alan Stivell brought Breton harp music to an international audience in the 1970s, blending folk sources with rock instrumentation. Groups such as Tri Yann and Dan Ar Braz carried the music to large audiences, and the annual Festival Interceltique de Lorient, founded in 1971, became the largest Celtic festival in the world, attracting hundreds of thousands of visitors. Contemporary Breton musicians continue to mix traditional forms with jazz, rock, and electronic music.

Instruments

  • Bombarde — double-reed instrument of the oboe family
  • Biniou kozh — small traditional bagpipe
  • Biniou braz — large bagpipe similar to the Scottish great Highland bagpipe
  • Telenn — Celtic harp
  • Veuze — bagpipe of the Loire-Atlantique region
  • Accordion, clarinet (treujenn-gaol), and violin
