Mulhacen


Elevation: 3,482 m (11,424 ft)
Prominence: 3,285 m
Location: Sierra Nevada, Andalusia, Spain
Range: Penibaetic System

Mulhacén is the highest mountain in mainland Spain and in the Iberian Peninsula, rising to 3,482 metres in the Sierra Nevada range in the province of Granada, Andalusia. Only Mount Teide on Tenerife, in the Canary Islands, is higher within Spanish territory.

Name

The name derives from Muley Hacén (Abu l-Hasan Ali), the 15th-century Nasrid king of Granada, who according to legend was buried at the summit. The mountain's name is conventionally written without an accent in English.

Geography and climbing

Mulhacen's south flank is long and gentle, allowing a straightforward ascent from the villages of Capileira and Trevélez in the Alpujarras, while the north face is steep and rugged, falling to the Laguna de la Caldera. The summit can be reached in a day from the Poqueira refuge. Its prominence of about 3,285 m makes it one of the most prominent peaks in Europe; on clear days it can be seen from the Mediterranean coast and across to Morocco.

Environment

The mountain lies within the Sierra Nevada National Park, a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve noted for endemic plant species. Small glacial lakes (lagunas) dot the high valleys, and the surrounding range is known for the Sierra Nevada ski station on the slopes of nearby Veleta.
