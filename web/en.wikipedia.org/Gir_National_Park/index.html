Gir National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary, also known as Sasan Gir, is a protected area in Gujarat, western India, established to protect the Asiatic lion. The Gir forest is the only place in the world where Asiatic lions occur in the wild, and it is regarded as one of Asia's most important protected areas. The total protected area covers about 1,412 km² of dry deciduous forest, scrub jungle and grassland, of which roughly 258 km² forms the fully protected national park core, notified in 1975, surrounded by the sanctuary declared in 1965.

History

The lions of Gir were once hunted almost to extinction: by the early twentieth century, after decades of sport hunting under British colonial officers and Indian princes, perhaps only a few dozen survived. The Nawab of Junagadh, in whose princely state the forest lay, banned lion hunting and began their protection — a policy continued after Indian independence. The first official census in 1936 counted 234 lions. Conservation measures, including the resettlement of some Maldhari herding families from the core area and protection of prey species, steadily increased the population; the 2020 count estimated 674 lions across the wider Gir landscape, some having dispersed well beyond the sanctuary into coastal and agricultural districts of Saurashtra.

Flora and fauna

Gir's vegetation is dominated by teak mixed with acacia, ber, jamun and flame of the forest, watered by perennial rivers including the Hiran, Shetrunji and Machhundri and by several reservoirs. Besides lions, the park supports one of India's largest populations of leopards, plus striped hyenas, golden jackals, jungle cats, and ungulates such as chital, sambar, nilgai, chinkara and wild boar. Marsh crocodiles inhabit the reservoirs, and over 300 bird species have been recorded, including the crested serpent eagle and the endangered white-backed and long-billed vultures.

Human communities and tourism

The semi-nomadic Maldhari pastoralists have lived in and around Gir for generations in settlements called nesses, grazing buffalo that sometimes fall to lions — an enduring focus of human–wildlife coexistence and compensation policy. The park's headquarters is at Sasan Gir, and jeep safaris in the tourism zone make Gir one of Gujarat's principal visitor attractions, open roughly from mid-October to mid-June.
