Soyuz


A Soyuz spacecraft in orbit

Soyuz (Russian: Союз, meaning "union") is a series of spacecraft developed by the Soviet Union and operated today by Russia. Designed in the 1960s by the OKB-1 bureau under Sergei Korolev, the Soyuz programme is the longest-running crewed spaceflight programme in history: Soyuz spacecraft have been in continuous service since 1967 and remain a principal means of transporting crews to and from the International Space Station.

Design

The Soyuz spacecraft consists of three modules: a spherical orbital module providing living space, a bell-shaped descent module in which the crew of up to three rides during launch and re-entry, and an unpressurized service module carrying propulsion, power, and life-support systems. It is launched atop the Soyuz rocket, itself derived from the R-7 intercontinental ballistic missile family. The current version, the Soyuz MS, first flew in 2016.

History

The first crewed flight, Soyuz 1 in April 1967, ended in disaster when cosmonaut Vladimir Komarov was killed during re-entry. Soyuz 11 in 1971 also ended fatally when the crew asphyxiated after a valve opened during module separation. After redesigns, Soyuz became the workhorse of the Soviet and Russian space programmes, ferrying crews to the Salyut and Mir stations. A Soyuz variant participated in the Apollo–Soyuz Test Project of 1975, the first joint Soviet–American mission.

From the retirement of the US Space Shuttle in 2011 until the first crewed flights of Commercial Crew vehicles in 2020, Soyuz was the only spacecraft able to carry crews to the ISS. The related uncrewed Progress cargo vehicle is derived from the Soyuz design.
