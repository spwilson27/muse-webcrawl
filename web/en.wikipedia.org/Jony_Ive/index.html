Sir Jonathan Paul Ive (born 27 February 1967) is a British-American industrial and product designer. As chief design officer at Apple, he oversaw the design of the iMac, iPod, iPhone, iPad, MacBook and Apple Watch, products that defined modern consumer electronics and made him one of the most influential designers of his generation. In 2019 he left Apple to found the creative collective LoveFrom.

Early life and education

Ive was born in Chingford, London, the son of a silversmith who taught design technology. He studied industrial design at Newcastle Polytechnic (now Northumbria University), where his student work won RSA awards, and co-founded the London design agency Tangerine before moving to California.

Apple

Ive joined Apple in 1992 and nearly quit during the company's mid-1990s decline, but the return of Steve Jobs in 1997 transformed his role: the two formed a close creative partnership. The translucent, candy-coloured iMac of 1998 revived Apple and rewired consumer expectations of computers. Ive's designs followed a philosophy of radical simplicity and integrity of materials — the white iPod (2001), the aluminium unibody MacBooks, the multitouch iPhone (2007) and iPad (2010). He also directed the design of Apple Park and narrated the company's famously minimalist product films. He holds thousands of design patents.

LoveFrom and later work

After leaving Apple in 2019, Ive founded LoveFrom, whose clients have included Airbnb, Ferrari and Moncler. His studio designed the emblem for the coronation of King Charles III. In 2024 LoveFrom collaborated with OpenAI on a new venture, io, to develop AI-native devices; OpenAI acquired io in 2025 with Ive taking on design responsibilities across the company.

Honours

Ive was appointed a Knight Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 2012 for services to design and enterprise, and has received honorary doctorates from institutions including the Royal College of Art, whose chancellor he became in 2017.
