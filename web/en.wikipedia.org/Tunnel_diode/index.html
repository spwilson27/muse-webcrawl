Tunnel diode

A tunnel diode or Esaki diode is a type of semiconductor diode that exhibits negative resistance due to the quantum mechanical effect of tunnelling. It was invented in 1957 by Japanese physicist Leo Esaki, who received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1973 for the discovery.

Operation

The diode uses a heavily doped p–n junction so narrow that electrons can tunnel through the depletion region. Over part of its forward characteristic, current decreases as voltage increases — a negative differential resistance that allows use in very fast switches and high-frequency oscillators operating into the microwave range.

Applications

Tunnel diodes were used in early high-speed logic, amplifiers, and oscillators, but were largely displaced by other semiconductor devices. They remain of interest for specialized low-noise and terahertz applications.
