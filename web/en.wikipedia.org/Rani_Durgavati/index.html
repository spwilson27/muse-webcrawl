Rani Durgavati

Born5 October 1524, Kalinjar Fort, Banda, Uttar Pradesh
Died24 June 1564 (aged 39), near Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh
ReignRegent/queen of Gondwana, 1550–1564
SpouseDalpat Shah of Gondwana

Rani Durgavati (5 October 1524 – 24 June 1564) was a queen of the Gond kingdom of Gondwana in central India, remembered for her able administration and her resistance to the Mughal Empire of Akbar. Born into the Chandel Rajput royal family of Mahoba, she married Dalpat Shah, heir to the Gond Raja of Garha-Mandla, and after his early death ruled the kingdom as regent for her young son.

Rule

During her fourteen-year rule, Gondwana prospered: tradition credits her with building reservoirs and temples, shifting her capital to the strategically stronger Chauragarh fort, and maintaining a large army with a substantial elephant corps. She repelled invasions by Baz Bahadur of Malwa and governed a kingdom said to comprise some 70,000 villages.

War with the Mughals and death

In 1564 the Mughal general Asaf Khan invaded Gondwana, attracted by its wealth and elephants. Durgavati chose to resist despite being advised to negotiate. After an initial success, her forces were overwhelmed in the battle at Narrai, near Jabalpur. Wounded and facing capture, she killed herself with her own dagger on 24 June 1564 — a day still commemorated as Balidan Diwas ("martyrdom day") in Madhya Pradesh.

Legacy

Rani Durgavati is honoured across central India: Jabalpur's university and a railway station bear her name, and her story is a staple of regional folklore and school textbooks as an emblem of courage and self-sacrifice.
