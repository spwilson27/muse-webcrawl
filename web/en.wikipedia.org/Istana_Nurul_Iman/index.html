Istana Nurul Iman

The Istana Nurul Iman (Malay for "Palace of the Light of Faith") is the official residence of Hassanal Bolkiah, Sultan of Brunei, and the seat of the Brunei government. Located on a leafy hilltop overlooking the Brunei River near Bandar Seri Begawan, it is the world's largest residential palace by many measures.

Construction

The palace was designed by Filipino architect Leandro V. Locsin, with interiors by Khuan Chew of KCA International, and built by the Philippine construction firm Ayala International. Completed in 1984 for Brunei's independence celebrations, it reportedly cost around US$1.4 billion.

Features

The palace contains 1,788 rooms, 257 bathrooms, a banquet hall seating 5,000 guests, a mosque for 1,500 worshippers, a 110-car garage, an air-conditioned stable for the Sultan's polo ponies, and five swimming pools. Its Malay-Islamic design incorporates golden domes and vaulted roofs echoing Bruneian motifs.

Public access

The palace is not normally open to the public, but for three days at the end of Ramadan (Hari Raya Aidilfitri) it opens to well-wishers — often over 100,000 visitors — who greet the Sultan and receive festive food and small gifts.
