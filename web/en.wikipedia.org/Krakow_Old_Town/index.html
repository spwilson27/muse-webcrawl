Kraków Old Town
LocationKraków, Poland
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site since 1978
CentreRynek Główny (Main Market Square)

The Kraków Old Town (Stare Miasto) is the historic central district of Kraków, Poland, and one of the country's most famous areas. In 1978 it was inscribed among the very first sites on UNESCO's World Heritage List, recognised for its exceptionally well-preserved medieval urban layout and architecture.

Main Market Square

At its heart lies the Rynek Główny, laid out in 1257 and the largest medieval town square in Europe, at roughly 200 by 200 metres. It is dominated by the Renaissance Cloth Hall (Sukiennice), the Town Hall Tower, and St Mary's Basilica, famous for its Gothic altarpiece by Veit Stoss and the hourly hejnał bugle call played from its taller tower — a melody that famously breaks off mid-note in memory of a legendary trumpeter shot by an arrow.

Streets and landmarks

The Royal Road leads from St Florian's Gate and the Barbican — remnants of the medieval fortifications — along Floriańska Street to the square and on to Wawel Castle. Grodzka Street passes the Romanesque church of St Andrew and the Baroque church of Saints Peter and Paul. The ring of the old walls is now the Planty, a leafy park encircling the district.

Character

The Old Town contains some 1,200 historic buildings, the Collegium Maius of the Jagiellonian University, numerous churches, museums, theatres and cafés, and hosts festivals including the Jewish Culture Festival in neighbouring Kazimierz and the Christmas market on the square.
