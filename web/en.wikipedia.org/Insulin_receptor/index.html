The insulin receptor spans the cell membrane; insulin binds its extracellular α subunits.

The insulin receptor is a transmembrane receptor, encoded in humans by the INSR gene, that is activated by the hormone insulin and belongs to the receptor tyrosine kinase family. Binding of insulin triggers the receptor's own kinase activity, initiating signalling cascades — chiefly the PI3K/Akt and Ras/MAPK pathways — that stimulate glucose uptake into muscle and fat cells, promote glycogen and lipid synthesis, and regulate growth. Defective insulin-receptor signalling is central to insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes.

Structure

The receptor is synthesised as a single precursor that is cleaved into α and β subunits, assembled as a disulfide-linked α2β2 tetramer. The extracellular α subunits form the insulin-binding site; each β subunit crosses the membrane and carries the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. Alternative splicing produces two isoforms, IR-A and IR-B, differing by twelve amino acids, with somewhat different tissue distributions and ligand affinities.

Activation and signalling

Insulin binding induces a conformational change that activates the kinase, which autophosphorylates tyrosine residues and phosphorylates substrates such as the IRS (insulin receptor substrate) proteins. Downstream, Akt activation moves GLUT4 glucose transporters to the cell surface and turns on anabolic enzymes while suppressing hepatic glucose production. The signal is terminated by endocytosis of the receptor and by phosphatases.

Clinical significance

Rare mutations in INSR cause severe insulin-resistance syndromes, including Donohue syndrome (leprechaunism), Rabson–Mendenhall syndrome and type A insulin resistance. Far more commonly, receptor signalling is blunted by obesity-related mechanisms in type 2 diabetes. The receptor is a pharmacological target indirectly through insulin therapy and insulin-sensitising drugs. It is unrelated in function to the insular areas of US law, despite the shared Latin root insula ("island") — insulin itself was named for the pancreatic islets.
