Karl John Friston
Born: 12 March 1959, York, England
Fields: Neuroscience, neuroimaging
Institutions: University College London
Known for: Free energy principle; SPM; dynamic causal modelling

Karl John Friston (born 1959) is a British neuroscientist at University College London, known for his work on brain imaging and theoretical neurobiology. He invented statistical parametric mapping (SPM), the standard software approach for analysing neuroimaging data, as well as voxel-based morphometry and dynamic causal modelling.

Free energy principle

Friston is the originator of the free energy principle and active inference, a theoretical framework proposing that biological systems maintain themselves by minimising a quantity (variational free energy) that bounds surprise, framing perception and action as forms of Bayesian inference.

Recognition

He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 2006 and has been among the most highly cited neuroscientists in the world. See also Statistical parametric mapping.
