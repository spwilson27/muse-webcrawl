Tricuspid regurgitation

SpecialtyCardiology
SynonymsTricuspid insufficiency
Diagnostic methodEchocardiography

Tricuspid regurgitation (TR), also called tricuspid insufficiency, is a type of valvular heart disease in which the tricuspid valve of the heart, located between the right atrium and right ventricle, does not close completely when the right ventricle contracts (systole). This causes blood to flow backwards from the right ventricle into the right atrium, thereby increasing the volume and pressure of the blood both in the right atrium and the right ventricle, which may increase central venous pressure and the pressure in the veins draining into it.

Causes

Most cases are "functional" (secondary): the valve leaflets are structurally normal but fail to coapt because the right ventricle and tricuspid annulus dilate, commonly from pulmonary hypertension or left-sided heart disease. Primary causes include infective endocarditis (especially in intravenous drug use), rheumatic fever, carcinoid syndrome, Ebstein's anomaly, myxomatous degeneration, trauma, and damage from pacemaker or defibrillator leads crossing the valve.

Signs and symptoms

Mild TR is common and harmless, but severe TR may cause fatigue, ascites, hepatic congestion, peripheral oedema, and jugular venous distension with prominent v waves. A pansystolic murmur may be heard at the lower left sternal border, increasing with inspiration (Carvallo's sign).

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause; diuretics relieve congestion. Surgery (usually annuloplasty repair) is considered for severe symptomatic TR, and transcatheter therapies have emerged for patients at high surgical risk.
