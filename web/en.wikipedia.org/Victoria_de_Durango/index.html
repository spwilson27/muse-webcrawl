Victoria de Durango, commonly known simply as Durango, is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Durango. It stands at an altitude of about 1,895 metres in the Valley of Guadiana, on the eastern side of the Sierra Madre Occidental in north-central Mexico.

History

The city was founded on 8 July 1563 by the Basque explorer Francisco de Ibarra, who named it after Durango in his native Biscay, Spain. It became the capital of the province of Nueva Vizcaya in the Viceroyalty of New Spain and an outpost of Spanish settlement and silver mining on the northern frontier. During the colonial era it grew around its cathedral and the Plaza de Armas; in 1824 it became capital of the new state of Durango.

In the 20th century the surrounding mountains and desert made Durango a favoured location for film production, particularly Westerns — the state bills itself as the "land of cinema", and productions such as John Wayne films were shot at purpose-built sets outside the city.

Geography and climate

Durango has a semi-arid highland climate with mild days, cold winter nights and most rain falling in summer. The historic centre preserves colonial architecture including the Cathedral Basilica of the Immaculate Conception, built in the Mexican baroque style on the main square.

Economy and culture

The economy is based on services, commerce, timber and mining industries, and food processing. The city is famous for its scorpions — the local alacrán is an emblem appearing in crafts and even cuisine — and for being the birthplace of revolutionary general Francisco "Pancho" Villa, who was born in the municipality, and of silent-film actress Dolores del Río. The Ricardo Castro Theatre and annual Durango National Fair are cultural fixtures.
