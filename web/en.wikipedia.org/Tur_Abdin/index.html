Tur Abdin (Syriac: ܛܘܼܪ ܥܲܒ݂ܕܝܼܢ, "Mountain of the Servants of God") is a hilly region in south-eastern Turkey, in the northern part of historic Mesopotamia, centred on the town of Midyat in Mardin Province. It is one of the historic heartlands of Syriac Christianity and of the Syriac Orthodox Church, famed for its ancient monasteries and churches, many built in late antiquity.

History

The region's name reflects its dense population of monks and ascetics in the early Christian centuries. Mor Gabriel Monastery, founded in 397, became the region's spiritual centre, while Mor Hananyo Monastery (Dayro d-Kurkmo, the "Saffron Monastery") served as the seat of the Syriac Orthodox Patriarch from the late 13th century until 1932. Tur Abdin's Christian population suffered severely in the massacres of 1915 (known in Syriac as the Sayfo), and most of its people emigrated during the twentieth century, especially from the 1960s to the 1990s, to Western Europe, where a large diaspora preserves the Turoyo language and Tur Abdin identity. A small community remains, alongside returnees rebuilding villages and monasteries.

Culture

The Syriac Christians of Tur Abdin (sometimes called Suraye or, in modern discourse, Assyrians/Syriacs/Arameans) speak Turoyo, a Central Neo-Aramaic language written in the Syriac script, while Classical Syriac remains the liturgical language. The plateau holds dozens of historic churches and monasteries — including the churches of Midyat, Hah (with the Church of the Mother of God), and the village monasteries of the region — which were inscribed on UNESCO's tentative World Heritage list in 2021 as the "Late Antique and Medieval Churches and Monasteries of Midyat and Surrounding Area (Tur Abdin)".
