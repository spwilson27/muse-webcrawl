Norfolk Broads

The Broads
LocationNorfolk and Suffolk, England
Area303 km² (117 sq mi)
StatusProtected area; part of the Broads National Park

The Norfolk Broads, commonly called The Broads, is a network of mostly navigable rivers and lakes in the English counties of Norfolk and Suffolk. The lakes, known as "broads", were formed by the flooding of peat workings. The Broads, and some surrounding land, were constituted as a special area with a level of protection similar to a national park by the Norfolk and Suffolk Broads Act 1988. In 2015 the area was renamed the Broads National Park, reflecting its status as a member of the national parks family.

Geography

The Broads cover an area of 303 square kilometres, much of it in Norfolk, with over 200 kilometres of navigable waterways. There are seven rivers and 63 broads, mostly less than 4 metres deep. Thirteen broads are generally open to navigation, and a further three have navigable channels. Some broads have navigation restrictions imposed on them in autumn and winter, although the legality of these restrictions has been questioned.

The seven rivers are the River Bure, River Yare, River Waveney, River Ant, River Thurne, River Chet and River Wensum.

History

For many years the broads were thought to be natural features of the landscape. In the 1960s, however, Dr Joyce Lambert and her colleagues proved that they were artificial features, the result of medieval peat digging. The Romans had first exploited the rich peat deposits, and in the Middle Ages the monasteries dug large quantities of peat for fuel. The water levels rose and the workings flooded, creating the lakes that are the Broads of today.

Tourism and ecology

The Broads are a popular area for boating holidays, with hire cruisers, sailing boats and day boats available from towns such as Wroxham, Hoveton and Potter Heigham. The area is also of great ecological importance, supporting rare wildlife including the swallowtail butterfly, Britain's largest butterfly, and the Norfolk hawker dragonfly. Much of the area is designated as a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a Special Area of Conservation.
