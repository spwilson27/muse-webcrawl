Syllable

Syllable
Unit of: Phonology
Parts: Onset, nucleus, coda
Example: "cat" = one syllable

A syllable is a unit of organization for a sequence of speech sounds typically made up of a syllable nucleus (most often a vowel) with optional initial and final margins (typically consonants). Syllables are often considered the phonological "building blocks" of words and determine the rhythm and prosody of speech.

Structure

Phonologists divide the syllable into the onset (consonants before the nucleus), the nucleus (usually a vowel, though syllabic consonants can serve), and the coda (consonants after the nucleus); nucleus and coda together form the rhyme. Languages differ widely in permitted structures, from the simple consonant–vowel syllables of Japanese or Hawaiian to the complex clusters allowed in English or Georgian.

Syllables in writing and verse

Some writing systems, called syllabaries, assign a sign to each syllable. In poetry, syllable counts define metres such as the haiku's 5–7–5 pattern and the lines of French classical verse. Word stress and syllable weight also govern poetic metre in quantitative verse traditions like those of Greek and Latin.
