Steinway & Sons

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Steinway & Sons grand piano.

Steinway & Sons is an American piano company founded in 1853 in Manhattan, New York City, by the German immigrant Heinrich Engelhard Steinweg (later Henry E. Steinway) and his sons. The company is widely regarded as the maker of the benchmark concert grand piano, and its instruments are the overwhelming choice of performing pianists worldwide.

History

Henry Steinway immigrated to the United States in 1850 and, with his sons, founded the firm three years later. The company accumulated dozens of patents, including the overstrung scale and the modern cast-iron frame arrangements, and won international prizes that established its reputation. A factory in Queens, New York, still supplies the Americas, while a second factory, opened in Hamburg in 1880, supplies the rest of the world.

Instruments

The flagship Model D concert grand, at 8 feet 11¾ inches, is the standard instrument of concert halls and is played by the great majority of soloists in season surveys. Steinway also builds smaller grands (Models B, A, O, M and S), upright pianos, and the Steinway-designed Boston and Essex lines. Since 2015 the company has offered Spirio, a high-resolution player-piano system built into its grands.

Influence

The Steinway Artist programme, begun in the nineteenth century, counts many of history's leading pianists among its roster, who agree to perform exclusively on Steinway instruments.
