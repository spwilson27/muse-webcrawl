Seven Second Summits

The Seven Second Summits are the second-highest mountains of each of the seven continents.

The Seven Second Summits are the second-highest mountains of each of the seven continents. They are a popular mountaineering challenge, regarded by many climbers as considerably harder than the more famous Seven Summits, because the second-highest peaks on several continents are technically more difficult or more remote than the highest ones.

The list depends on the definitions used for continental boundaries, particularly for Australia/Oceania and for the border between Europe and Asia. Common versions of the list include K2 in Asia, Mount Logan in North America, Ojos del Salado in South America, Mount Kenya in Africa, Mount Tyree in Antarctica, Dykh-Tau in Europe, and either Mount Townsend (in Australia) or Puncak Mandala (in New Guinea, on the Bass list variation).

Climbing history

The Austrian mountaineer Christian Stangl was the first person widely recognised to have climbed all of the Seven Second Summits, completing the challenge in 2013, although his claims were scrutinised and a disputed earlier claim to K2 was withdrawn. The challenge is considered more demanding than the Seven Summits because peaks such as K2 and Mount Tyree are far more technically challenging, dangerous, or rarely climbed than Everest and Vinson Massif respectively.
