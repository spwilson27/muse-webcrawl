Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa

The Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa, known in Arab history as the Battle of Al-Uqab, was fought on 16 July 1212 near Las Navas de Tolosa in the Sierra Morena (modern Jaén Province, Spain). A coalition of Christian kingdoms led by Alfonso VIII of Castile, together with Sancho VII of Navarre, Peter II of Aragon and Portuguese and military-order contingents, decisively defeated the Almohad caliph Muhammad al-Nasir. The victory is considered a turning point of the Reconquista, breaking Almohad power in Iberia and opening the Guadalquivir valley to Christian conquest in the following decades.

Course of the battle

Pope Innocent III had proclaimed a crusade after the Almohads retook territory following their victory at Alarcos (1195). Guided through an unguarded pass of the Sierra Morena, the Christian army surprised al-Nasir's host. Hard fighting on the plain of Las Navas was decided when the kings personally led the reserve into the Almohad centre; Sancho VII's Navarrese broke through to the caliph's chained bodyguard, and al-Nasir fled to Jaén and then Morocco, dying the next year. Almohad losses were remembered as catastrophic. Although exploitation was slowed by plague and truces, the balance of power in Iberia had shifted permanently: Córdoba fell in 1236, Jaén in 1246 and Seville in 1248.
