United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland

The United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland was the sovereign state formed by the union of the Kingdom of Great Britain and the Kingdom of Ireland on 1 January 1801, under the Acts of Union 1800. It existed until 1922, when the Irish Free State was established, and the state was formally renamed the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland in 1927. At its height the state stood at the centre of the British Empire, then the largest empire in history, and was a leading industrial, naval and commercial power.

Formation

The union followed the Irish Rebellion of 1798 and fears in London that Ireland might be used as a base by revolutionary France. The Acts of Union were passed by both the Irish and British parishes — by the Parliaments of Ireland and Great Britain — abolishing the Irish Parliament in Dublin and giving Ireland 100 MPs and 32 peers at Westminster. The Union Flag was redesigned to add the red saltire of St Patrick to the crosses of St George and St Andrew. A promise of Catholic emancipation, which had helped secure Irish support, was blocked by George III, and emancipation was not achieved until 1829.

The nineteenth century

The United Kingdom fought in the Napoleonic Wars, culminating in victory at Waterloo in 1815. The 19th century saw rapid industrialisation, the expansion of the franchise through the Reform Acts of 1832, 1867 and 1884, the abolition of slavery in most of the empire (1833), and the catastrophe of the Great Famine in Ireland (1845–1852), in which about a million people died and a million more emigrated, halving Ireland's population over following decades. Irish nationalism grew through the century, from Daniel O'Connell's repeal movement to the Fenian movement and Charles Stewart Parnell's campaign for Home Rule, which dominated politics in the 1880s–1910s but was repeatedly defeated or deferred.

War and partition

The United Kingdom entered the First World War in 1914. The Easter Rising in Dublin in 1916, the conscription crisis of 1918, and Sinn Féin's victory in the 1918 election led to the Irish War of Independence (1919–1921). The Government of Ireland Act 1920 partitioned Ireland; the Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921 created the Irish Free State as a dominion, with six north-eastern counties remaining in the United Kingdom as Northern Ireland. The Free State came into being on 6 December 1922, and in 1927 the remaining state's name was changed by the Royal and Parliamentary Titles Act to reflect the new reality.
