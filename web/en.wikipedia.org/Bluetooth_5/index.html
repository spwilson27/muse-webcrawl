Bluetooth 5

Introduced: June 2016 (specification); December 2016 (release)
Developer: Bluetooth Special Interest Group
Predecessor: Bluetooth 4.2
Successor: Bluetooth 5.1

Bluetooth 5 is the fifth major version of the Bluetooth wireless technology standard, announced by the Bluetooth Special Interest Group (SIG) in June 2016 and released in December 2016. Its improvements focus mainly on Bluetooth Low Energy (LE), the variant used by Internet of Things devices, beacons, and wearables.

Features

Compared with Bluetooth 4.2, Bluetooth 5 offers up to twice the data rate (2 Mbit/s in the LE 2M PHY mode), up to four times the range through a new coded PHY with forward error correction (at reduced data rates of 125 or 500 kbit/s), and an eightfold increase in advertising broadcast capacity, with advertising payloads expanded from 31 bytes to 255 bytes. These changes allow richer beacon messages and more reliable long-range connections at low power. Slot availability masks help devices avoid interference from LTE bands.

Versions

Point releases followed: Bluetooth 5.1 (2019) added direction finding via angle of arrival and angle of departure, and Bluetooth 5.2 (2020) introduced LE Audio and the LC3 codec. Bluetooth 5.3 and 5.4 added further efficiency and security refinements.
