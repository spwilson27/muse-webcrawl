Runway incursion

A runway incursion is an incident at an airport in which an aircraft, vehicle or person is incorrectly present on the protected area of a surface designated for the landing and take-off of aircraft. The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) definition covers any occurrence involving "the incorrect presence of an aircraft, vehicle or person on the protected area" of such a surface. Incursions are classified by severity from Category A (a serious incident in which a collision was narrowly avoided) to Category D (little or no risk).

Causes

Most incursions result from human error: pilots crossing a runway without clearance or mishearing instructions, air traffic control errors, unclear taxiway markings, complex airport layouts, low-visibility operations and language problems in radiotelephony. The US Federal Aviation Administration attributes the majority of incursions to pilot deviations, followed by controller and vehicle/pedestrian deviations.

Major accidents

The deadliest accident in aviation history, the 1977 Tenerife airport disaster, was a runway collision in fog between two Boeing 747s that killed 583 people. Other fatal incursion accidents include the 1990 Detroit Metropolitan Airport collision, the 2001 Linate Airport disaster in Milan (118 killed), and the 2024 Haneda Airport collision in Tokyo, in which five of six crew aboard a coast guard aircraft died after it entered the runway in front of a landing airliner.

Prevention

Countermeasures include surface-movement radar and multilateration (ASDE-X and equivalents), runway status lights that automatically warn crews, direct voice alerting in control towers, improved signage and "hot spot" marking on airport diagrams, standardised phraseology, and readback requirements for runway-crossing clearances. Despite decades of effort, incursions remain one of the highest-priority safety categories for ICAO, the FAA and EASA.
