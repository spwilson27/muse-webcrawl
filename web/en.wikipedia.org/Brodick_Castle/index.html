Brodick Castle

Brodick Castle
Location: Brodick, Isle of Arran, Scotland
Owner: National Trust for Scotland (since 1958)
Designation: Category A listed building

Brodick Castle is a castle near the village of Brodick on the Isle of Arran, off the west coast of Scotland. Set above Brodick Bay with views across the Firth of Clyde, the site has been fortified since Viking times and was a stronghold of the Hamilton family, Dukes of Hamilton, for centuries. It is now in the care of the National Trust for Scotland and is open to the public, together with its extensive gardens and country park.

History

A fortress stood at Brodick during the Wars of Scottish Independence — the castle was held for Edward I of England and later taken by Robert the Bruce's supporters when Bruce returned via Arran in 1307. The present castle was largely built by the Hamiltons from the 16th century, with the eastern tower house dating to around 1588, and was damaged during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms. Major Victorian additions were made in 1843–44 to designs by the architect James Gillespie Graham for the 10th Duke of Hamilton, giving the castle its Scots Baronial appearance. It passed to the National Trust for Scotland in 1958 on the death of Mary, Duchess of Montrose, daughter of the 12th Duke of Hamilton.

Gardens and collections

The castle contains collections of paintings (including works associated with the Hamiltons' role as art collectors), silver, porcelain and furniture. The woodland gardens, developed from the 1920s by the Duchess of Montrose, are renowned for their rhododendron collection — original specimens came from plant-hunting expeditions to the Himalayas — and the grounds include a walled garden of 1710 and the Brodick Country Park, with trails up towards Goatfell, Arran's highest mountain.
