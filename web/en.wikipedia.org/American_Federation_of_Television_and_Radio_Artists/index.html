American Federation of Television and Radio Artists

The American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (AFTRA) was a United States labor union representing performers and media professionals in television, radio, sound recordings, commercials, and non-broadcast media. Founded in 1937 as the American Federation of Radio Artists (AFRA), it added television to its jurisdiction and name in 1952.

History and merger

AFTRA negotiated contracts covering actors, announcers, broadcast journalists, disc jockeys, singers, dancers and stunt performers. For decades it coexisted — sometimes rivalrously — with the Screen Actors Guild (SAG), which covered film work. After earlier merger attempts failed in 1998 and 2003, AFTRA and SAG members voted in 2012 to merge, forming SAG-AFTRA, a union of more than 160,000 members, on 30 March 2012.

Role

The union bargained for minimum fees, residuals, working conditions and pension and health benefits, and administered major contracts including the network television code and the sound recordings code. Notable presidents included Eddie Cantor (its first), Frank Nelson, and Roberta Reardon.
