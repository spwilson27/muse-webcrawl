Book runner

FieldInvestment banking
FunctionLead manager of a new securities issue
Related termsUnderwriter; book-building; syndicate; lead arranger

A book runner (or bookrunner) is the lead underwriter or manager in charge of an issuance of new equity, debt, or hybrid securities. The book runner maintains the "book" — the record of investor orders gathered during book-building — and coordinates the syndicate of banks that market and allocate the issue.

Role

In an initial public offering or bond issue, the issuer appoints one or more book runners (joint book runners are common in large deals). The book runner organises roadshows, collects indications of interest, advises on pricing, and makes final allocation decisions. Its name appears at the top-left of the offering prospectus, a position of prestige reflected in league tables.

Fees and league tables

Book runners earn the largest share of underwriting fees in a syndicate, and banks compete fiercely for book runner mandates because league-table rankings by deal volume influence future business. Senior roles below the book runner include co-lead managers and co-managers.

History

The term became standard with the spread of book-building as the dominant method of pricing IPOs in the 1990s, replacing fixed-price offers in most major markets.
