Enthesitis

Enthesitis is inflammation of the entheses, the sites where tendons, ligaments or joint capsules insert into bone. It is a characteristic feature of the spondyloarthropathies, including ankylosing spondylitis and psoriatic arthritis, and is considered by many rheumatologists to be the primary pathological process distinguishing these conditions from rheumatoid arthritis.

Sites and symptoms

Common sites include the insertion of the Achilles tendon and the plantar fascia on the calcaneus, the patellar tendon insertions, and the origins of the forearm extensors at the lateral epicondyle. Symptoms are pain, tenderness and sometimes swelling localized precisely at the insertion point, often worst in the morning and improving with activity in inflammatory disease.

Causes

Enthesitis may arise from inflammatory disease (the spondyloarthropathies, including those associated with psoriasis and inflammatory bowel disease), from mechanical overuse and microtrauma ("enthesopathy"), from infection, and from metabolic or degenerative processes. Enthesitis-related arthritis is also a category of juvenile idiopathic arthritis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is primarily clinical, supported by ultrasound showing hypoechogenicity, erosions and Doppler signal at the insertion, or by MRI. Treatment depends on the cause: mechanical enthesopathy is managed with load modification, physiotherapy and NSAIDs, while inflammatory enthesitis may require disease-modifying or biologic therapy directed at the underlying spondyloarthropathy.
