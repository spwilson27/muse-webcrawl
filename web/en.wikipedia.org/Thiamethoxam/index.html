Thiamethoxam

IUPAC name(EZ)-3-(2-chloro-1,3-thiazol-5-ylmethyl)-5-methyl-1,3,5-oxadiazinan-4-ylidenenitroamine
CAS Number153719-23-4
Molecular formulaC8H10ClN5O3S
Molar mass291.71 g/mol
ClassNeonicotinoid insecticide

Thiamethoxam is a systemic insecticide in the neonicotinoid class, developed by Syngenta and sold under trade names including Cruiser and Actara. It acts as an agonist of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor in insects, interfering with the transmission of nerve impulses and leading to paralysis and death. It is used against a wide range of sucking and chewing pests, including aphids, whiteflies, thrips, beetles and soil insects, and is commonly applied as a seed treatment as well as to foliage and soil.

Thiamethoxam is highly water-soluble and is taken up by plant roots and leaves, spreading systemically through the plant's tissues, which makes it effective but also means residues can appear in pollen and nectar. In insects it is partly metabolised to clothianidin, another neonicotinoid insecticide.

Environmental concerns and regulation

Like other neonicotinoids, thiamethoxam has been at the centre of controversy over its effects on pollinators, particularly honey bees and wild bees, as well as on aquatic invertebrates and birds. In 2013 the European Union restricted its use on bee-attractive crops, and in 2018 the EU banned all outdoor uses of thiamethoxam (along with imidacloprid and clothianidin), permitting use only in permanent greenhouses. Other jurisdictions, including the United States and Canada, have imposed label restrictions and phased reviews rather than full bans. Syngenta and other manufacturers maintain that the products are safe when used according to label directions.
