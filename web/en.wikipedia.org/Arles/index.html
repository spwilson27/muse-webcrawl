Arles (Provençal: Arle) is a commune in the Bouches-du-Rhône department in the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region of southern France. Situated on the Rhône where the river divides near the Mediterranean, it is one of the largest communes in France by area, encompassing much of the Camargue delta. The city is renowned for its extensive Roman monuments and its association with Vincent van Gogh.

History

Settled by Phoenicians and Greeks, Arles became an important Roman city (Arelate) after Julius Caesar rewarded it for supporting him against Pompey; it grew wealthy on river trade and became a favourite residence of Constantine the Great. The Council of Arles (314) was an early church council. After periods of Visigothic, Frankish and Saracen rule, Arles was capital of the medieval Kingdom of Arles and later part of Provence, joining France in 1481.

Sights

The UNESCO-listed monuments include the Roman amphitheatre (c. 90 AD, still used for bull games), the Roman theatre, the cryptoporticus, the Alyscamps necropolis, the Baths of Constantine, and the Romanesque Church of St. Trophime with its celebrated carved portal and cloister.

Van Gogh

Vincent van Gogh lived in Arles in 1888–89, producing over 300 paintings and drawings there, including The Night Café, Sunflowers versions and Starry Night Over the Rhône. The city also hosts the Rencontres d'Arles, an internationally important photography festival founded in 1970, and the Fondation Vincent van Gogh Arles.
