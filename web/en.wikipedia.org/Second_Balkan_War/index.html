Second Balkan War

Date29 June – 10 August 1913
LocationBalkan Peninsula
ResultBulgarian defeat; Treaties of Bucharest and Constantinople
BelligerentsBulgaria vs. Serbia, Greece, Montenegro, Romania, Ottoman Empire

The Second Balkan War was a conflict that broke out when Bulgaria, dissatisfied with its share of the spoils of the First Balkan War, attacked its former allies Serbia and Greece on the night of 29–30 June 1913. The war ended little more than a month later with Bulgaria defeated on all fronts.

Territorial settlement after the Treaty of Bucharest (1913).

Background

During the First Balkan War (1912–1913), the Balkan League of Bulgaria, Serbia, Greece, and Montenegro had driven the Ottoman Empire out of almost all of its European territory. The division of Macedonia became the central dispute: Serbia, denied an Adriatic outlet by the creation of Albania, demanded compensation in Macedonia at the expense of the share promised to Bulgaria in their pre-war treaty. Greece likewise held Salonika (Thessaloniki), claimed by Bulgaria.

Course of the war

On 29 June 1913 Bulgarian forces attacked Serbian and Greek positions in Macedonia. The offensive initially made progress against the Greeks at Kilkis but was halted and then thrown back. Serbia defeated Bulgaria at the Battle of Bregalnica, and Greece won at Kilkis–Lachanas and advanced into Bulgarian territory. On 10 July Romania, which had remained neutral in the first war, invaded from the north, advancing almost unopposed toward Sofia; the Ottoman Empire also joined, retaking Edirne (Adrianople) on 21 July.

Peace settlement

The Treaty of Bucharest (10 August 1913) awarded most of Macedonia to Serbia and Greece, gave Southern Dobruja to Romania, and left Bulgaria with only a small part of Macedonia (the Pirin region) and Western Thrace. The subsequent Treaty of Constantinople (29 September 1913) fixed Bulgaria's border with the Ottomans, leaving Edirne in Ottoman hands. Bulgaria's humiliation reshaped its foreign policy and pushed it toward the Central Powers in the First World War.
