Tyranny of the majority

Tyranny of the majority
Associated thinkers: Alexis de Tocqueville, John Stuart Mill
Related concept: ochlocracy (mob rule)
Antidotes proposed: constitutional rights, federalism, judicial review

The tyranny of the majority is a concept in political philosophy describing a weakness of direct democracy and unrestrained majority rule: a majority may impose its will on minorities in ways that are oppressive, even though decisions are formally democratic.

Origins

Alexis de Tocqueville warned in Democracy in America (1835–1840) of the moral and political dominance of the majority in the United States, both through legislation and through social pressure toward conformity. John Stuart Mill developed the idea in On Liberty (1859), arguing that society itself can be a tyrant and that individual liberty needs protection against prevailing opinion as much as against rulers.

Responses

Constitutional democracies typically counter the risk with entrenched bills of rights, separation of powers, bicameralism, supermajority requirements for constitutional change and independent courts, though critics debate how far such checks should limit majority decision-making.
