Hungarian wine

Summary

Wine regions: 22, including Tokaj, Eger, Villány, Balaton

Famous wines: Tokaji Aszú, Egri Bikavér (Bull's Blood)

Indigenous grapes: Furmint, Hárslevelű, Kékfrankos, Kadarka

Hungarian wine has a history dating back to Roman times, when vineyards were planted in Pannonia. Hungary sits at a crossroads of continental climate zones, with volcanic, limestone, and loess soils that support a wide variety of wine styles across its 22 wine regions.

Tokaj

The Tokaj region in the northeast is Hungary's most famous, producing Tokaji Aszú, a sweet wine made from grapes affected by noble rot (Botrytis cinerea). Classified by puttonyos according to sweetness, Tokaji was prized at European courts for centuries and was among the world's first appellation-controlled wines, with a vineyard classification dating to the 18th century. The dry wines of Tokaj, made principally from the Furmint grape, have gained international attention in recent decades.

Other regions

Eger is known for Egri Bikavér ("Bull's Blood"), a red blend whose name is linked to the 1552 siege of Eger. Villány in the south produces full-bodied reds from Bordeaux varieties and Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch). The Balaton region produces fresh whites, Sopron is known for Kékfrankos, and the Great Plain (Alföld) accounts for the largest volume of production.

Modern era

Phylloxera devastated Hungarian vineyards in the late 19th century, and quality suffered under state collectivisation in the communist era, which favoured quantity. Since 1990, private wineries have revived quality production, restoring Hungary's reputation in international markets.
