Propagule

In biology, a propagule is any material that functions in propagating an organism to the next stage in its life cycle, such as by dispersal. Propagules are produced by organisms including plants, fungi, and bacteria, and can consist of a single unit or a structure capable of giving rise to a new individual.

Types of propagules

In plants, propagules include seeds, spores, bulbs, bulbils, and detached vegetative fragments capable of rooting. Fungi disperse by spores and hyphal fragments, while bacteria may spread via spores or cysts. In invasion biology, the number of propagules introduced to a new location, termed propagule pressure, is a key predictor of establishment success for non-native species.

Propagule pressure

Propagule pressure combines the number of individuals released and the number of release events. Higher propagule pressure increases the chance that a founding population survives stochastic losses and overcomes Allee effects, making it one of the most consistent determinants of biological invasion success.
