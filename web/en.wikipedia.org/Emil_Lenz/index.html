Emil Lenz

Heinrich Friedrich Emil Lenz (February 12, 1804 – February 10, 1865) was a Russian physicist of Baltic German descent who is most noted for formulating Lenz's law in electrodynamics in 1834.

Lenz was born in Dorpat (now Tartu, Estonia) and studied at the University of Dorpat. He accompanied Otto von Kotzebue on his third expedition around the world (1823–1826), making observations on ocean currents and the physical properties of seawater. He later worked at the University of St. Petersburg, where he became dean of mathematics and physics and eventually rector.

Lenz's law states that the direction of an induced electric current is such that the magnetic field it creates opposes the change in flux that produced it — a consequence of the conservation of energy. Lenz also independently discovered Joule's law of electrical heating and conducted pioneering studies of electrical resistance and inductance.
