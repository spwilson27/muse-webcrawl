United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement

The United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA; known as CUSMA in Canada and T-MEC in Mexico) is a free trade agreement between the United States, Mexico and Canada. It replaced the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) and entered into force on 1 July 2020.

Background

Renegotiation of NAFTA began in 2017 after the agreement became a major issue in United States politics. Negotiators reached a deal in the autumn of 2018, and a protocol of amendment addressing labour, environmental and pharmaceutical provisions was signed in December 2019.

Key provisions

The USMCA largely preserves NAFTA's tariff-free trade while adding updated rules on digital trade, intellectual property, and automotive rules of origin requiring a higher share of North American content and a portion of production by workers earning at least US$16 per hour. It also expanded United States access to Canada's dairy market and introduced a 16-year sunset clause with periodic joint reviews.

Review

The agreement provides for a joint review six years after entry into force, with the first such review scheduled for 2026.
