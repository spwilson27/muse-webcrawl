Yōkai

Japanese妖怪 (yōkai)
OriginJapanese folklore
TypesOni, kappa, tengu, kitsune, tsukumogami, yūrei
Famous catalogueToriyama Sekien's Gazu Hyakki Yagyō (1776)

Yōkai (妖怪) are a class of supernatural entities and spirits in Japanese folklore. The word combines the characters for "bewitching" and "mystery", and the beings it covers range from malevolent demons to mischievous tricksters and neutral or even helpful spirits: shape-shifting foxes (kitsune), long-nosed tengu, river-dwelling kappa, ogre-like oni, and tsukumogami, household objects that come alive after a hundred years.

History

Belief in yōkai grew out of Shinto animism and imported Buddhist and Chinese lore. In the Edo period, encyclopedias and picture scrolls catalogued hundreds of them; Toriyama Sekien's illustrated bestiaries of the 1770s–1780s fixed the appearance of many famous yōkai and invented others. Scholars such as Inoue Enryō later studied the beliefs as "yōkaiology" while seeking to dispel superstition.

Modern culture

Yōkai were revived in the twentieth century by folklorist Kunio Yanagita's collections and by Shigeru Mizuki's manga GeGeGe no Kitarō, and they now saturate popular culture: the Pokémon franchise, Yo-kai Watch, Studio Ghibli films, and countless anime and games draw on their menagerie. Ghosts (yūrei) are sometimes classed among yōkai and sometimes treated separately.
