Chronicle of a Summer (French: Chronique d'un été) is a 1961 French documentary film directed by anthropologist and filmmaker Jean Rouch and sociologist Edgar Morin. Shot in Paris and Saint-Tropez during the summer of 1960, the film asks ordinary people a deceptively simple question — "Are you happy?" — and follows a cross-section of Parisians, including students, workers, artists and a concentration-camp survivor, as they discuss their lives against the background of the Algerian War.

Cinéma vérité

The film is a foundational work of cinéma vérité — a term Morin coined by translating Dziga Vertov's Kino-Pravda ("film truth"). Made possible by new lightweight cameras and synchronous sound equipment, it abandoned the detached "fly-on-the-wall" stance: the filmmakers appear on screen, provoke situations, and finally screen the finished footage to their participants, filming their reactions and debating whether the result is truthful.

Reception and legacy

The film won the International Critics' Prize at the 1961 Cannes Film Festival. Its self-reflexive structure and participatory method profoundly influenced documentary practice and the French New Wave, and it regularly appears in critics' polls of the greatest documentaries ever made.
