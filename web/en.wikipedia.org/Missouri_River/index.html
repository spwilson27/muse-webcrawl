The Missouri River is a river in the Central and Mountain West regions of the United States. At 2,341 miles (3,767 km), it is the longest river in the United States and the principal tributary of the Mississippi River. Rising in the Rocky Mountains of Montana at the confluence of the Jefferson, Madison and Gallatin rivers near Three Forks, it flows east and south through the Great Plains before joining the Mississippi just north of St. Louis.

Course and basin

The Missouri drains a basin of over half a million square miles, parts of ten U.S. states and two Canadian provinces. Major tributaries include the Yellowstone, Platte, Kansas and Osage rivers. Because of its heavy sediment load it is nicknamed the "Big Muddy". Cities on its banks include Great Falls, Bismarck, Pierre, Sioux City, Omaha, Kansas City and Jefferson City.

History

Indigenous peoples lived along the Missouri for thousands of years; nations such as the Mandan, Hidatsa, Arikara, Sioux and Missouri built substantial earth-lodge towns on its bluffs. The Lewis and Clark Expedition (1804–1806) ascended the river to its headwaters, mapping the newly acquired Louisiana Purchase. In the nineteenth century the river was a major steamboat artery for the fur trade and westward settlement.

Development

The Flood Control Act of 1944 authorised the Pick–Sloan Plan, which built six large mainstem dams — Fort Peck, Garrison, Oahe, Big Bend, Fort Randall and Gavins Point — for flood control, irrigation, navigation and hydroelectric power. Channelisation of the lower river created a navigable channel to Sioux City but greatly altered the river's ecology, contributing to declines of species such as the pallid sturgeon.
