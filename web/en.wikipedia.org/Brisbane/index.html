Brisbane

Brisbane
State: Queensland, Australia
Established: 1825 (penal settlement)
Population: approx. 2.5–2.7 million (Greater Brisbane)
Named for: Sir Thomas Brisbane

Brisbane is the capital and largest city of the Australian state of Queensland, and the third-most populous city in Australia. It is situated on the Brisbane River on the coast of the Coral Sea in south-east Queensland. The metropolitan area has a population of roughly two and a half million people.

History

The area was inhabited for tens of thousands of years by Aboriginal peoples, principally the Turrbal and Jagera. In 1824 a penal settlement was established at Redcliffe and moved in 1825 to the present city site on the Brisbane River, named after Sir Thomas Brisbane, Governor of New South Wales. Free settlement began in 1842, and when Queensland separated from New South Wales in 1859 Brisbane became its capital.

The city was an Allied headquarters in the Second World War, hosting General Douglas MacArthur's South West Pacific command. Brisbane hosted the 1982 Commonwealth Games and World Expo 88, which spurred the development of the South Bank Parklands on the river's south bank. The city suffered severe flooding in 1974, 2011 and 2022. Brisbane is scheduled to host the 2032 Summer Olympic and Paralympic Games.

Geography and climate

Brisbane has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild, dry winters. The city spreads across the Brisbane River's floodplain between Moreton Bay and the Taylor and D'Aguilar ranges. Landmarks include Story Bridge, South Bank, Mount Coot-tha, and the central business district around Queen Street Mall.
