Dipole

In physics, a dipole is a pair of equal and opposite charges or magnetic poles separated by a small distance. Dipoles are characterised by their dipole moment, a vector quantity whose magnitude is the product of the charge (or pole strength) and the separation.

Electric dipole

An electric dipole arises whenever positive and negative charges are separated, as in a polar molecule such as water, whose uneven distribution of electrons gives it a permanent dipole moment. In an external electric field a dipole experiences a torque tending to align it with the field, and a net force in a non-uniform field.

Magnetic dipole

A magnetic dipole is produced by a loop of electric current or by the intrinsic spin of particles such as electrons. The Earth itself behaves approximately as a magnetic dipole, its field resembling that of a bar magnet tilted relative to the rotation axis.

Other meanings

In antenna engineering a dipole antenna is a simple radio antenna made of two conducting elements. In chemistry, dipole–dipole interactions are intermolecular forces between polar molecules.
