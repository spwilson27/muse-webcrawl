Acura

Acura is the luxury and performance division of Japanese automaker Honda, marketed primarily in North America. It was launched on March 27, 1986, making it the first Japanese luxury car brand, ahead of Toyota's Lexus and Nissan's Infiniti (both 1989). Acura's early slogan-era models, the Legend sedan and the Integra sports coupe, established a reputation for engineering quality at prices below European rivals.

History

Acura's first flagship, the Legend, was developed with Britain's Rover Group and was followed in 1990 by the NSX, an all-aluminum mid-engine sports car developed with input from Ayrton Senna that forced established supercar makers to improve reliability. The brand shifted toward crossovers in the 2000s with the MDX and RDX becoming its volume sellers, and revived its performance image with a second-generation NSX hybrid supercar (2016–2022) and the return of the Integra nameplate in 2022.

Motorsport

Acura has a long record in American sports car racing, winning manufacturers' championships in IMSA competition with prototypes such as the ARX-05 and ARX-06 in the Daytona Prototype international and GTP classes, and fielding the NSX GT3 in GT racing worldwide.

Markets

Acura vehicles are sold mainly in the United States and Canada, with past or limited presence in Mexico, China and other markets. Unlike its Japanese rivals, Acura has never been marketed as a brand in Honda's home market of Japan.
