Turkmen language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Turkmen (Turkmen: Türkmen dili, Түркмен дили) is a Turkic language of the Oghuz branch spoken in Central Asia, mainly in Turkmenistan, where it is the official language, as well as by Turkmen communities in Iran, Afghanistan, Russia and Turkey. It has roughly 4.3 million native speakers in Turkmenistan and perhaps 7–8 million speakers in total.

Classification and features

Turkmen belongs to the eastern Oghuz group, alongside Khorasani Turkic, and is closely related to Azerbaijani and Turkish, though it preserves features lost in its western relatives, such as long vowels and the interdental fricatives /θ/ and /ð/ (written s and z). Like other Turkic languages it is agglutinative, has vowel harmony, and follows subject–object–verb word order.

Writing systems

Turkmen was historically written in a Perso-Arabic script. In the Soviet era it was shifted first to Latin (1928) and then to Cyrillic (1940). After independence, Turkmenistan adopted a new Latin-based alphabet in 1993 (modified in subsequent years), which remains official today; Cyrillic and Arabic scripts persist among diaspora communities.

Dialects

The standard language is based on the Teke dialect of the Ashgabat region. Other major tribal dialects include Yomut, Ersary, Goklen, Salyr and Saryk, each associated with the traditional Turkmen tribal groups.
