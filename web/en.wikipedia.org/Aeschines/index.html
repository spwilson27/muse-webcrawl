Aeschines

Aeschines (389–314 BC) was a Greek statesman and orator of Athens, counted among the ten Attic orators. He is best known as the great rival of Demosthenes.

Life

Beginning as an actor and clerk, Aeschines rose in politics and served on embassies to Philip II of Macedon. Demosthenes accused him of taking Macedonian bribes (in On the False Embassy), and Aeschines replied in kind. Their feud culminated in 330 BC in the trial over Ctesiphon's proposal to crown Demosthenes: Aeschines' speech Against Ctesiphon was answered by Demosthenes' On the Crown. Aeschines lost, left Athens, and spent his remaining years in Rhodes and Samos.

Works

Three speeches survive: Against Timarchus, On the Embassy, and Against Ctesiphon.
