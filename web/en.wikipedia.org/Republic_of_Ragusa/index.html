The Republic of Ragusa (Latin: Respublica Ragusina) was an aristocratic maritime republic centred on the city of Ragusa — modern Dubrovnik, Croatia — which existed from 1358 until 1808. A small but prosperous trading state on the eastern Adriatic, it survived for four and a half centuries between the great powers of Venice, Hungary and the Ottoman Empire through skilled diplomacy, tribute payments and a powerful merchant fleet.

History

Ragusa grew from a settlement founded by Roman refugees from destroyed Epidaurum, developing under Byzantine and then Venetian rule. After the Treaty of Zadar in 1358, Venice ceded Dalmatia to Hungary, and Ragusa became a de facto independent republic under the Hungarian crown, governed by its own aristocracy through a Great Council, a Senate, and a Rector elected for a term of only one month to prevent tyranny. From the 15th century the republic paid an annual tribute to the Ottoman Sultan in exchange for trading privileges throughout the Ottoman world, while remaining formally independent — a balancing act that preserved its liberty while Venice and the Ottomans clashed around it.

Trade and society

Ragusina merchants traded grain, salt, wool, hides and silver across the Balkans and the Mediterranean; at its 16th-century peak, the republic's merchant fleet of some 180 large ships was among the largest in the Mediterranean. Ragusa was notably progressive: it abolished the slave trade in 1416, among the first European states to do so, built early quarantine stations (lazarettos) in 1377 to control plague, and maintained public water supply and orphanages. Its citizens included the scientist Roger Joseph Boscovich and the playwright Marin Držić.

Decline and fall

A catastrophic earthquake in 1667 killed thousands, including the Rector, and destroyed much of the city; the republic never fully recovered its commercial position as Atlantic trade routes eclipsed the Mediterranean. Napoleon’s general Marmont formally abolished the republic on 31 January 1808, and its territory was absorbed into the French Illyrian Provinces; after 1815 it passed to Austria, ending Ragusa's centuries of independence.
