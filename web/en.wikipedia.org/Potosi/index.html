Potosí

Country: Bolivia

Department: Potosí Department

Founded: 1545

Elevation: ~4,090 m (13,420 ft)

Famous for: Cerro Rico silver mines

Potosí is a city in southern Bolivia, capital of the department of the same name, lying at the foot of the Cerro Rico ("Rich Mountain") at an altitude of about 4,090 metres — one of the highest cities in the world. Founded by the Spanish in 1545, it grew around the richest silver deposits ever discovered, and for two centuries its mines underwrote the economy of the Spanish Empire.

The silver of Potosí was minted into the famous "pieces of eight" and carried around the world; the expression vale un Potosí — "worth a Potosí" — entered Spanish as a synonym for immense wealth. At its peak around 1610, Potosí's population rivalled that of London or Seville.

The mines

Extraction depended on the forced labour of indigenous Andeans under the mita draft system, in conditions that killed enormous numbers of workers; the Cerro Rico is sometimes called "the mountain that eats men". Amalgamation with mercury from Huancavelica made the ores workable on an industrial scale. As rich ores were exhausted, the city declined sharply in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Today

Potosí's colonial centre, with its churches, mint (Casa de la Moneda) and civic architecture, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Mining continues on a smaller scale through cooperatives working the Cerro Rico's remaining veins of silver, tin and zinc, often in dangerous conditions.
