Lingam

A lingam (Sanskrit: लिङ्ग, liṅga, "sign" or "mark"), sometimes spelled linga or Shiva linga, is an abstract or aniconic representation of the Hindu god Shiva in Shaivism. The lingam is typically the revered symbol in temples dedicated to Shiva, found in his shrines across the Indian subcontinent and in diaspora temples worldwide.

The lingam is often represented within a lipped, disc-shaped pedestal called the yoni, symbolizing the goddess Shakti. Together, the lingam and yoni represent the union of masculine and feminine creative energies. In Shaiva theology the lingam is interpreted as an emblem of generative power, of the formless cosmic pillar (stambha) described in the Shiva Purana's account of the endless pillar of fire seen by Brahma and Vishnu.

History and types

Worship of lingam-like stones in the Indus Valley civilization has been suggested by some archaeologists, though this remains debated. Explicit lingam worship is attested from the early centuries CE; the Gudimallam lingam in Andhra Pradesh, carved with a standing figure of Shiva, is among the oldest known examples. Lingams may be self-manifested (svayambhu), naturally formed (such as the ice lingam at Amarnath), or carved from stone, metal, clay, or crystal, and are classified in texts such as the Agamas by material and proportion.
