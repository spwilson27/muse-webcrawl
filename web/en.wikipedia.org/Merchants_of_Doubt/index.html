Merchants of Doubt

Merchants of Doubt: How a Handful of Scientists Obscured the Truth on Issues from Tobacco Smoke to Global Warming is a 2010 book by historians of science Naomi Oreskes and Erik M. Conway. It identifies parallels between the public debates over tobacco smoking, acid rain, the ozone hole, secondhand smoke, DDT and climate change, arguing that in each case a small group of scientists, often with funding from affected industries, deliberately kept controversies alive by magnifying scientific uncertainty.

Central figures and argument

The book focuses on a handful of Cold War physicists — including Fred Seitz, Fred Singer, William Nierenberg and Robert Jastrow — who moved from weapons and missile-defence work into challenging environmental and public-health science. Oreskes and Conway argue that the strategy borrowed directly from the tobacco industry's documented approach of the 1950s: since the science was settled, opponents sought to cast doubt on the evidence ("doubt is our product", as an industry memo put it), thereby delaying regulation.

Reception

The book was widely reviewed and praised for its archival research tracing funding and networks through think tanks such as the George C. Marshall Institute. A documentary film of the same name, directed by Robert Kenner, was released in 2014. Critics of the book argued it oversimplified scientific debates, but it became a touchstone in scholarship on science denial and manufactured controversy.
