Ferdinand Mannlicher

Ferdinand Mannlicher
Born30 January 1848, Mainz
Died20 January 1904, Vienna
OccupationEngineer, firearms designer

Ferdinand Ritter von Mannlicher (1848–1904) was an Austrian engineer and firearms designer, famous for his pioneering bolt-action rifles and the en-bloc clip loading system. He worked for the Österreichische Waffenfabriksgesellschaft at Steyr and designed more than 150 models of repeating and automatic firearms.

Designs

Mannlicher's straight-pull bolt actions — including the Model 1886, 1888, and the Austro-Hungarian service rifle Model 1895 — were renowned for their speed of operation. His en-bloc clip, in which a complete clip of cartridges is inserted into the magazine, was adopted in French, German, Italian, Romanian, Dutch, and later American (M1 Garand) service rifles. He also produced early semi-automatic pistol designs, including the Model 1894 and the Steyr Mannlicher M1901.

Legacy

Mannlicher's designs equipped armies across Europe and influenced rifle development for decades. The Mannlicher–Schönauer sporting rifle, with its rotary magazine, became a classic hunting arm. He was ennobled by Emperor Franz Joseph I in 1892 and appointed to the Austrian upper house (Herrenhaus) in 1899.
