Province of Maine

Established: 1622 (patent)
Status: English proprietary colony
Proprietors: Sir Ferdinando Gorges and heirs
Absorbed into: Massachusetts Bay Colony, 1650s–1691

The Province of Maine refers to any of several English colonial territories in the region that became the U.S. state of Maine. The first was created by a patent granted in 1622 to Sir Ferdinando Gorges and John Mason by the Plymouth Council for New England. The name derives from the French province of Maine, said to have been chosen in honour of Queen Henrietta Maria, who held lands there.

History

In 1622 the Plymouth Council for New England issued a patent for the territory between the Merrimack and Kennebec rivers to Gorges and Mason. The grant was divided in 1629 along the Piscataqua River: Mason took the southern portion, which became the Province of New Hampshire, while Gorges retained the northern portion. In 1639 Gorges obtained a royal charter for his Province of Maine, with its capital at Gorgeana (present-day York).

Absorption by Massachusetts

After Gorges's death in 1647, the province lacked effective leadership, and the Massachusetts Bay Colony gradually extended its jurisdiction over the Maine settlements during the 1650s. In 1677 Massachusetts purchased the Gorges heirs' claims, and the 1691 charter of the Province of Massachusetts Bay formally incorporated Maine. Maine remained part of Massachusetts until it became a separate state in 1820 under the Missouri Compromise.
