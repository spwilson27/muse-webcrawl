Pratapgad (literally "Fort of Valour") is a mountain fortress in Satara district in the Indian state of Maharashtra, near the hill station of Mahabaleshwar. It was built in 1656 under the Maratha ruler Shivaji and commands a strategic pass in the Western Ghats between the coastal Konkan and the Deccan plateau.

History

The fort is best known as the site of the Battle of Pratapgad on 10 November 1659, in which Shivaji's forces defeated the much larger army of the Bijapur general Afzal Khan. At a meeting at the foot of the fort, Shivaji killed Afzal Khan—according to Maratha accounts, in self-defence against a treacherous attack—using concealed weapons, and the leaderless Bijapuri army was routed. The victory was a turning point in the founding of the Maratha Empire.

Layout

The fort is divided into an upper and lower fort, protected by bastions and walls following the contours of the hill. It contains temples, including one dedicated to the goddess Bhavani, and the tomb of Afzal Khan stands at the base of the fort.

Today

Pratapgad is a popular tourist destination, reached by road from Mahabaleshwar. A large equestrian statue of Shivaji stands within the fort, unveiled in 1957.
