Dredging

Dredging is the excavation of material from beneath water, usually from the bed of a sea, lake or river. It is carried out to deepen navigation channels and harbours, reclaim land, obtain sand and gravel, lay pipelines, and remove contaminated sediments. The specialised vessels and machines used are called dredgers.

Types

Mechanical dredgers — bucket ladder, grab, and backhoe dredgers — lift material in buckets or grabs. Hydraulic dredgers move loosened sediment as a slurry through pipelines: cutter suction dredgers swing a rotating cutter head across the face, while trailing suction hopper dredgers drag suction heads along the bed and store spoil in onboard hoppers for later discharge, including by "rainbowing" material ashore for land reclamation.

Applications

Maintenance dredging keeps ports and shipping routes at design depth as silt accumulates; capital dredging creates new channels, berths and artificial islands. Dredging also supplies construction aggregates and beach nourishment sand, and supports offshore mining.

Environmental effects

Dredging disturbs benthic habitats and creates turbidity plumes; spoil disposal can smother seabed life, and contaminated sediments may release pollutants. Modern projects are therefore subject to environmental assessment, and beneficial-use schemes place clean material in habitat restoration and shoreline protection projects.
