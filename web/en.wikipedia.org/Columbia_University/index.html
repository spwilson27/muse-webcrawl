Columbia University

Columbia University, officially Columbia University in the City of New York, is a private Ivy League research university in New York City. Established in 1754 as King's College by royal charter from King George II, it is the oldest institution of higher education in New York and the fifth-oldest in the United States.

History

After the American Revolution the college reopened as Columbia College; it became Columbia University in 1912. It moved to its current Morningside Heights campus, designed by McKim, Mead & White, in 1897. Columbia administers the Pulitzer Prizes and has educated numerous heads of state, Nobel laureates, and founders.

Academics

The university comprises Columbia College, the Fu Foundation School of Engineering and Applied Science, the School of General Studies, and graduate schools including Law, Business, Medicine (Vagelos), Journalism, and Teachers College (affiliated). Its Core Curriculum, begun in 1919, is a landmark of general education.

Campus

Landmarks include Low Memorial Library, Butler Library, St Paul's Chapel, and the Alma Mater statue. A second campus, Manhattanville, is expanding north of 125th Street.
