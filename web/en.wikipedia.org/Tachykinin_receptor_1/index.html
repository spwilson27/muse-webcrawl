Tachykinin receptor 1 (TACR1), also known as the neurokinin 1 (NK1) receptor or the substance P receptor, is a G protein-coupled receptor found in the central and peripheral nervous systems. Its principal endogenous ligand is the neuropeptide substance P.

Function

Activation of the NK1 receptor mediates pain transmission, inflammation, smooth muscle contraction, salivation and emesis (vomiting). It couples mainly to Gq proteins, increasing intracellular calcium via phospholipase C pathways.

Clinical significance

NK1 receptor antagonists such as aprepitant block the receptor and are used to prevent chemotherapy-induced and postoperative nausea and vomiting. Researchers also investigated the receptor's roles in depression, anxiety and respiratory conditions, though those indications were not established clinically.
