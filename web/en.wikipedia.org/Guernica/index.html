Guernica

Guernica is a large 1937 oil painting by the Spanish artist Pablo Picasso. It is one of Picasso's best-known works, regarded by many as the most powerful anti-war painting in history.

History

The painting was created in response to the bombing of Guernica, a Basque town in northern Spain, by Nazi Germany's Condor Legion and Fascist Italy's Aviazione Legionaria at the request of the Spanish Nationalists during the Spanish Civil War. It was exhibited at the Spanish pavilion of the 1937 Paris International Exposition.

Composition

Painted in black, white and grey, the mural-sized canvas (3.49 m × 7.77 m) depicts suffering figures — a bull, a screaming horse, a mother with a dead child, and dismembered bodies — under the glare of an electric light.

Legacy

After touring internationally, the painting was held at the Museum of Modern Art in New York until 1981, when it returned to Spain; it is now displayed at the Museo Reina Sofía in Madrid.
