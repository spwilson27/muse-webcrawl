Mont Sainte-Victoire

Elevation1,011 m (3,317 ft)
LocationBouches-du-Rhône, Provence, France
GeologyLimestone

Mont Sainte-Victoire is a limestone mountain ridge in the south of France which extends over 18 kilometres (11 miles) between the départements of Bouches-du-Rhône and Var. Its highest point is the Pic des Mouches at 1,011 metres (3,317 ft). The mountain is celebrated for its association with the painter Paul Cézanne, who made it the subject of dozens of paintings.

History and geology

The mountain is a long east–west ridge of Jurassic limestone. In Roman times it was known as mons Victoriae, commemorating the victory of Gaius Marius over the Teutones and Ambrones at the Battle of Aquae Sextiae in 102 BC nearby.

Cézanne

Paul Cézanne, who was born in nearby Aix-en-Provence, could see the mountain from his house and painted it over 80 times between the 1880s and his death in 1906. The series is a landmark of Post-Impressionism and a precursor of Cubism. The mountain has also been painted by Pablo Picasso, who bought the Château de Vauvenargues at its foot, and by Wassily Kandinsky.

Nature protection

A major fire in 1989 devastated over 50 km² of the mountain's south side; extensive replanting followed. The area is now protected as a Grand Site de France and part of the Natura 2000 network.
