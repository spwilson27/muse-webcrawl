Human enhancement

Human enhancement is the natural, artificial, or technological alteration of the human body in order to enhance physical or mental capabilities. It covers interventions that go beyond restoring or maintaining health — from performance-enhancing drugs and cosmetic surgery to genetic modification, neural implants, and prosthetics that exceed normal function.

Technologies

Current and emerging enhancement technologies include pharmacological cognitive enhancers (such as stimulants used off-label), anabolic steroids and other doping agents, reproductive technologies and embryo selection, gene therapy and genome editing, brain–computer interfaces and deep brain stimulation, and advanced prosthetic limbs and exoskeletons. A distinction is often drawn between therapies, which treat disease or disability, and enhancements, which aim to improve traits beyond typical human ranges — a boundary many commentators find blurred.

Ethics and debate

Human enhancement is a central topic in bioethics. Transhumanists and other proponents argue that individuals should be free to improve their bodies and minds and that enhancement can reduce suffering and extend healthspan. Critics — sometimes called bioconservatives — raise concerns about safety, fairness and unequal access, coercion (for example in sport or the workplace), the value of human nature and dignity, and irreversible changes to the human germline. Regulatory responses vary widely by technology and jurisdiction; sporting bodies ban many performance-enhancing methods, and germline genome editing is broadly prohibited following the 2018 CRISPR-baby controversy.
