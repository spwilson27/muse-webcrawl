Screw conveyor


Image placeholder: Screw conveyor

A screw conveyor or auger conveyor is a mechanism that uses a rotating helical screw blade, called a "flighting," usually within a tube or trough, to move liquid or granular material. They are used in many bulk handling industries, to transport material horizontally, at a slight incline, or (with design changes) vertically. As the screw rotates, material trapped between the flights advances along the trough without rotating with the blade.

History and types

The device descends from the Archimedes' screw of antiquity, traditionally attributed to Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287–212 BC), which was used to raise water for irrigation. Modern variants include shafted and shaftless screw conveyors, flexible screw conveyors, screw feeders and meters, and live-bottom screws under hoppers and silos. Grain augers are ubiquitous in agriculture for loading silos and filling seed drills, and screw conveyors are central to food processing, wastewater treatment (moving grit and sludge), plastics compounding and mining.

Design considerations

Capacity depends on the screw diameter, pitch, rotational speed and the fill level of the trough; Inclined operation reduces efficiency because material tends to fall back. Wear, power draw and the tendency of sticky or abrasive materials to build up on the flighting are key engineering concerns, addressed through material choice, hard-facing and shaftless designs.
