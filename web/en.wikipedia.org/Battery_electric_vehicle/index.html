Battery electric vehicle

A battery electric vehicle (BEV), pure electric vehicle, only-electric vehicle, fully electric vehicle or all-electric vehicle is a type of electric vehicle (EV) that uses chemical energy stored in rechargeable battery packs to power one or more electric motors. BEVs produce no exhaust emissions at the point of use, and their overall emissions depend on how the electricity used to charge them is generated.

History

Electric vehicles were among the most popular automobiles in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, but cheap petrol cars and the mass production of the Ford Model T pushed them aside. Interest revived during the oil crises of the 1970s and again in the 1990s with vehicles such as the General Motors EV1. The modern era began with the Tesla Roadster (2008) and the Nissan Leaf (2010), the first mass-market highway-capable BEV, and by the 2020s most major manufacturers offered BEV models, with sales growing rapidly worldwide, led by China.

Technology

A BEV drivetrain consists of a large traction battery — almost always lithium-ion — an inverter, and one or more electric motors driving the wheels. Regenerative braking recovers energy during deceleration. Charging takes place from the grid through AC charging at home or public points, or rapid DC fast charging that can replenish most of the battery in tens of minutes. Range varies with battery size, efficiency, temperature and driving conditions, with many modern models exceeding 300 km (190 mi) per charge.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include lower running and maintenance costs, instant torque and quiet operation, and zero tailpipe emissions. Disadvantages include higher purchase prices (though falling), longer refuelling times than petrol cars, range limitations, and concerns about battery raw materials such as lithium, cobalt and nickel, and about battery recycling.

Market and policy

Governments have promoted BEVs through purchase incentives, emissions standards and announced phase-out dates for new internal-combustion cars. Battery-electric cars became the majority of new car sales in Norway in the early 2020s, and global sales passed ten million per year.
