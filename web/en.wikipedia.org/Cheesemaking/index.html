Cheesemaking

Cheesemaking (or caseiculture) is the craft of producing cheese from milk, typically of cows, goats or sheep, by coagulating the milk protein casein and separating curds from whey, then ripening the curd under controlled conditions.

Process

Milk is usually pasteurised and warmed, a starter culture of lactic acid bacteria is added, and rennet (or another coagulant) sets the milk into a gel. The curd is cut to release whey, then drained, salted, shaped and pressed according to the variety.

Ripening

During ageing, enzymes and microbes transform texture and flavour — from mild, elastic young cheeses to hard, crystalline aged cheeses, bloomy-rind cheeses ripened by moulds, and blue cheeses veined with Penicillium roqueforti.

History

Cheesemaking is ancient: residues in pottery from around 5500 BCE in Poland show milk processing, and cheese was known in ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome. Industrial production developed in the 19th century, beginning with factories in Switzerland and the United States.
