West Side Story is a 2021 American musical romantic drama film directed and co-produced by Steven Spielberg from a screenplay by Tony Kushner. It is the second feature-length adaptation of the 1957 stage musical of the same name, which was itself inspired by William Shakespeare's play Romeo and Juliet. The film stars Ansel Elgort and Rachel Zegler in her film debut as Tony and Maria, alongside Ariana DeBose, David Alvarez, Mike Faist, and Rita Moreno — who had starred in the 1961 film adaptation — in supporting roles.

Plot

In 1957 New York City, the Puerto Rican gang the Sharks and the white American gang the Jets fight for control of the Upper West Side neighborhood of San Juan Hill, which is being demolished to make way for Lincoln Center. Tony, a former Jet recently released from prison, falls in love with Maria, the younger sister of Sharks leader Bernardo, after meeting her at a dance at the local gym. Their romance deepens the hatred between the two gangs, leading to a rumble in which Tony, intervening to stop the fight, kills Bernardo. Chino, Maria's intended fiancé, later shoots Tony, who dies in Maria's arms; her grief shames both gangs into an uneasy truce.

Production

Spielberg had long wanted to direct a musical, and first publicly discussed remaking West Side Story in 2014. Principal photography took place in New York City and New Jersey in 2019. The film uses much of the original Bernstein score, conducted by Gustavo Dudamel, and choreography by Justin Peck, which reworks Jerome Robbins's original routines. Spanish dialogue in the film was deliberately left unsubtitled.

Reception

The film premiered at Jazz at Lincoln Center in New York on November 29, 2021, and was released in the United States on December 10, 2021, by 20th Century Studios. Critics praised Spielberg's direction, the performances, and the cinematography, though the film underperformed at the box office, grossing $76 million against a $100 million budget amid the COVID-19 pandemic. It received seven nominations at the 94th Academy Awards, including Best Picture, with Ariana DeBose winning Best Supporting Actress for her role as Anita — the same role for which Moreno won in 1962.
