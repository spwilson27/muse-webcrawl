Tito–Stalin split

The Tito–Stalin split, also known as the Soviet–Yugoslav split or the Informbiro period in Yugoslav historiography, was the political conflict between the leaders of Yugoslavia and the Soviet Union that culminated in Yugoslavia's expulsion from the Communist Information Bureau (Cominform) in 1948. It was the first major schism within the communist world during the Cold War.

Causes

Yugoslavia's communist partisans had liberated much of their country with limited Soviet help, giving Tito an independent power base. Friction grew over Yugoslav ambitions in Trieste and the Balkans, Tito's support for Greek communists, plans for a Balkan federation with Bulgaria, and Moscow's economic demands and attempts to control the Yugoslav party and security services.

The split

After an exchange of increasingly hostile letters in spring 1948, the Cominform meeting in Bucharest adopted a resolution on 28 June 1948 condemning the Yugoslav leadership and expelling the Communist Party of Yugoslavia. Stalin expected the Yugoslav party to overthrow Tito; instead it rallied around him.

Consequences

Yugoslavia survived economic blockade by turning to Western aid while developing its own model of socialist self-management. Suspected pro-Soviet communists were purged and imprisoned, notably on Goli Otok. Abroad, the split shattered the image of a monolithic communist bloc, and Yugoslavia went on to co-found the Non-Aligned Movement in 1961. Relations with Moscow were only partly normalised after Stalin's death, with Khrushchev's visit to Belgrade in 1955.
