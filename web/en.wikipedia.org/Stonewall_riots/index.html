Stonewall riots

The Stonewall riots (also known as the Stonewall uprising or Stonewall rebellion) were a series of spontaneous demonstrations by members of the gay community in response to a police raid that began in the early hours of 28 June 1969 at the Stonewall Inn in the Greenwich Village neighbourhood of New York City. They are widely considered the single most important event leading to the modern gay liberation movement and the fight for LGBT rights in the United States.

Background

In the 1960s, gay bars in New York operated under constant threat of raids, since serving homosexuals was treated as disorderly conduct and the State Liquor Authority revoked licences of establishments that did so. The Stonewall Inn, a Mafia-run bar on Christopher Street, catered to a diverse clientele including drag queens, transgender people, and homeless gay youth — groups marginalised even within the gay community.

The uprising

When police raided the bar shortly after midnight on 28 June, patrons resisted instead of dispersing quietly. A crowd gathered outside, confrontations escalated, objects were thrown at police, and officers barricaded themselves inside the bar until reinforcements arrived. Protests and clashes continued for several nights. Figures frequently associated with the first night include Marsha P. Johnson and Sylvia Rivera, although accounts of individual roles vary.

Aftermath and legacy

Within months, activist groups such as the Gay Liberation Front and the Gay Activists Alliance formed in New York, and similar organizations appeared across the United States and abroad. On the first anniversary of the riots, marches in New York, Los Angeles and Chicago began the tradition of Pride parades, now held worldwide each June. The Stonewall Inn and surrounding streets were designated a New York City landmark in 2015 and the Stonewall National Monument in 2016.
