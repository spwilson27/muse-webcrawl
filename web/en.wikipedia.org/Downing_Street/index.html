Downing Street

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Downing Street is a street in Westminster in London, United Kingdom, known for the official residences and offices of two of the most senior British cabinet ministers: the Prime Minister at 10 Downing Street, and the Chancellor of the Exchequer at 11 Downing Street. The street was built in the 1680s by Sir George Downing.

History

The street was built on the site of a mansion called Hampden House. Sir George Downing, a soldier and diplomat, acquired the lease and built a row of terraced houses in the 1680s. Downing invested his fortune in the property, intending houses "for persons of good quality to inhabit in". The houses had poor foundations on marshy ground — a recurring source of structural problems ever since.

Number 10

10 Downing Street has been the official residence of the Prime Minister since 1735, when King George II offered it to Sir Robert Walpole as First Lord of the Treasury. Its famous black door and modest appearance conceal a large complex of offices and state rooms behind the terrace. The street is closed to the general public by security gates installed in 1989.

Other buildings

Number 11 is the Chancellor of the Exchequer's residence, Number 12 has served as a press and communications office, and Number 9 houses government departments. The Foreign and Commonwealth Office occupies the western side of the street. "Downing Street" is widely used as a metonym for the British government.

Chief Mouser

10 Downing Street is also home to the Chief Mouser to the Cabinet Office, a resident cat employed to catch mice; since 2011 the post has been held by a cat named Larry.
