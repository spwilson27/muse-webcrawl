Suez

Suez (Arabic: السويس, as-Suways) is a seaport city in north-eastern Egypt, located on the Red Sea, at the southern end of the Suez Canal. The city has a population of roughly 750,000 and serves as an important industrial, commercial and port centre, linked closely with the history of the canal that bears its name.

History

The site of Suez has been of strategic importance since antiquity. In ancient times, the nearby settlement of Clysma stood near the head of the Gulf of Suez, and the pharaohs and later Ptolemies dug early canals connecting the Nile to the Red Sea. Under Islamic rule the town grew as a trading and pilgrimage port, serving travellers to Mecca and commerce with the Arabian Peninsula and beyond.

The modern city expanded rapidly after the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869, which connected the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea and transformed global shipping routes. Suez suffered heavily during the wars of 1967 and 1973, when much of the city was evacuated and damaged, but it was rebuilt after the reopening of the canal in 1975.

Geography

Suez lies at the northern end of the Gulf of Suez, a northwestern arm of the Red Sea, at the meeting point of the African and Asian parts of Egypt. The terrain around the city is largely desert, with the Eastern Desert to the west and the Sinai Peninsula across the canal to the east.

Economy

The economy of Suez is dominated by the canal, shipping services, petroleum refining, petrochemicals, fertilisers and port activity. Port Tawfiq, at the southern entrance of the canal, handles cargo and passenger traffic. The city is also a free-zone and industrial area attracting investment tied to canal traffic.

Transport

Suez is connected by road and rail to Cairo, about 130 kilometres to the northwest, and to the canal cities of Ismailia and Port Said to the north. The Ahmed Hamdi Tunnel passes under the canal south of the city, linking mainland Egypt with the Sinai.
