Schreier graph

A Schreier graph of a group action.

In mathematics, a Schreier graph is a graph associated with a group, a generating set, and a subgroup or group action, named after Otto Schreier. The vertices of the graph are the cosets of a subgroup (or the elements of a set on which the group acts), and two vertices are joined by a directed edge labelled by a generator whenever the generator maps one vertex to the other.

Definition

Given a group G, a subgroup H, and a generating set S of G, the Schreier graph has as vertices the right cosets Hg, and for each coset Hg and each generator s in S a directed edge from Hg to Hgs. When the subgroup is trivial, the Schreier graph is the Cayley graph of the group.

Applications

Schreier graphs are used in combinatorial group theory to prove results such as the Nielsen–Schreier theorem, which states that every subgroup of a free group is itself free; a proof proceeds by studying spanning trees of Schreier graphs. They also appear in the theory of expander graphs, in automata theory, and in the study of self-similar groups, where iterated Schreier graphs approximate fractals such as the Sierpiński gasket.
