Igorot people

The Igorot people are an ethnolinguistic group comprising several Austronesian ethnic groups inhabiting the Cordillera Administrative Region of Luzon in the Philippines. The name derives from an old Tagalog term meaning "people from the mountains".

Groups

Peoples commonly included are the Bontoc, Ibaloi, Ifugao, Isneg (Apayao), Kalinga, and Kankanaey. They speak related languages of the Northern Luzon group and maintain distinct but related traditions.

Culture

The Igorot are renowned for wet-rice terrace agriculture, including the Banaue Rice Terraces often attributed to Ifugao builders over many centuries. Traditional religion centres on anito (spirits) and the sky god Kabunyan, alongside widespread Christianity today.

History

The mountain peoples largely resisted Spanish colonisation, which never fully subdued the Cordillera interior, while gold from Igorot mines attracted colonial expeditions. Under American rule the region was organised as the Mountain Province.
