Book of Sui

Chinese: 隋書 (Suí Shū)

Author: Wei Zheng and others

Completed: 636 CE (treatises 656 CE)

Volumes: 85

Series: Twenty-Four Histories

The Book of Sui (隋書, Suí Shū) is the official history of China's Sui dynasty (581–618), compiled under the succeeding Tang dynasty and counted among the Twenty-Four Histories. Edited chiefly by the chancellor Wei Zheng, the work was presented to the throne in 636 CE, though its monographic treatises were completed twenty years later. It comprises 85 volumes: imperial annals, treatises, and biographies.

Contents

The annals cover the reigns of the Sui emperors Wen, Yang, and Gong. The biographical chapters recount officials, generals, scholars, and neighbouring peoples, including accounts of Korea, Japan, and the Turks that are important early sources for those countries. The treatises deal with rites, music, the calendar, astronomy, geography, administration, law, and economics.

The bibliography treatise

The Book of Sui is prized especially for its bibliographic treatise (經籍志), the earliest surviving Chinese library catalogue organised by the fourfold classification of classics, histories, masters, and belles-lettres. Recording thousands of works, many now lost, it remains fundamental evidence for the state of Chinese literature up to the seventh century and preserves the first Chinese mention of some foreign texts.

Historical significance

Written soon after the dynasty's fall, the history presents the Sui as a cautionary tale: praised for reunifying China in 589 and for projects such as the Grand Canal, but condemned for the extravagance and failed Korean campaigns of Emperor Yang that provoked rebellion. Modern historians use it alongside archaeology to give a more balanced picture of a short but consequential dynasty.
