Eba

Eba, also known as ẹ̀bà, is a staple food of West Africa, especially Nigeria, made from cassava flour (garri) stirred into hot water to form a firm, smooth dough. It belongs to the family of stiff dough dishes often called "swallows," eaten by hand with soups.

Preparation

Garri — roasted, fermented cassava granules — is sprinkled into boiling water and stirred vigorously until it forms a smooth, elastic mass. The eba is then moulded into balls, wrapped, and served alongside soups and stews such as egusi soup, okra soup, or vegetable soup. Diners pinch off a piece, roll it into a ball with an indentation, and use it to scoop up the soup.

Regional variations

The colour of eba ranges from white to yellow depending on whether the garri was made with palm oil added during processing. Similar cassava doughs are eaten across West and Central Africa under different names and with different preparations, alongside related staples such as fufu and ugali.
