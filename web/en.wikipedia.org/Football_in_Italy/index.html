Football in Italy

Football (calcio in Italian) is the most popular sport in Italy. The Italian Football Federation (FIGC), founded in 1898, governs the sport, and the Italy national football team is one of the most successful in the world, having won the FIFA World Cup four times (1934, 1938, 1982, and 2006) and the UEFA European Championship twice (1968 and 2020). Italy is also credited with strong tactical traditions, notably the defensive catenaccio system.

Serie A is the top tier of Italian club football.

History

Modern football was introduced to Italy in the late 19th century, with Genoa Cricket and Football Club (1893) among the earliest clubs. An Italian championship was first contested in 1898. Italy hosted and won the 1934 World Cup and retained the title in 1938 under coach Vittorio Pozzo. The post-war Grande Torino team dominated domestically before the Superga air disaster of 1949. Italian clubs became European powers in the 1960s, and Serie A was widely regarded as the world's strongest league in the 1980s and 1990s.

League system

The professional pyramid is headed by Serie A (20 clubs), followed by Serie B and Serie C. Major clubs include Juventus, AC Milan, and Inter Milan, which have collectively won numerous European Cups/Champions Leagues. The Coppa Italia is the principal national cup competition.

Culture and issues

Football is deeply embedded in Italian culture, with fierce local derbies such as the Derby della Madonnina (Milan) and Derby della Capitale (Rome). The sport has also faced scandals, most prominently the 2006 Calciopoli match-fixing affair, which led to Juventus's relegation and stripped titles.
