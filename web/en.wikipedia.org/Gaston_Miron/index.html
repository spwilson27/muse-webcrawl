Gaston Miron (1928–1996) was a French-Canadian poet, publisher and nationalist intellectual from Quebec. As a co-founder of the publishing house Éditions de l'Hexagone in 1953, he helped create an institutional base for modern Quebec poetry.

Poetry

Miron's best-known collection, L'homme rapaillé (1970; often translated as The March to Love in selected form), gathers poems of love, language, land and political emancipation. Its speaker seeks to “re-member” a fragmented self and people, making the book a central text of Quebec's Quiet Revolution era.

Activism and honours

Miron supported Quebec sovereignty and francophone cultural affirmation. He was arrested during the October Crisis of 1970. He received major honours including the Prix Athanase-David and the Order of Francophones of America, and was made a Grand Officer of the National Order of Quebec.

Legacy

After his death Miron was given a state funeral in Montreal. The Bibliothèque Gaston-Miron in Paris and public commemorations in Quebec preserve his place as a national poet.
