Equity crowdfunding

Equity crowdfunding is the online offering of private company securities (shares) to a group of people for investment, and is a type of crowdfunding. In return for relatively small amounts of money, members of the public receive equity in start-up and early-stage businesses, using internet platforms as intermediaries.

How it works

A company sets a funding target and offers a percentage of its equity on a regulated platform such as Crowdcube, Seedrs (in the United Kingdom), Wefunder or Republic (in the United States). If the target is reached within the campaign period, the investment completes and backers become shareholders; if not, money is returned. Investments are illiquid and high-risk, since most start-ups fail.

Regulation

Because selling securities to the general public is heavily regulated, equity crowdfunding developed only after legal reforms: the US JOBS Act of 2012 (Title III, implemented as Regulation Crowdfunding in 2016), UK authorisation of platforms by the Financial Conduct Authority, and the EU's European Crowdfunding Service Providers Regulation (2020). Rules typically cap how much non-accredited investors may invest and impose disclosure requirements on issuers.

History

Early platforms appeared around 2007–2011 (Australia's ASSOB predates the term; Crowdcube launched in the UK in 2011). The model has since funded films, consumer products, breweries and technology start-ups, with the largest campaigns raising tens of millions of pounds or dollars.
