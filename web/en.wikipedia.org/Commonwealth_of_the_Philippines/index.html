Commonwealth of the Philippines

StatusCommonwealth / unincorporated territory of the United States
Existed1935–1946
CapitalManila (Baguio seasonal; government-in-exile in Washington, D.C., 1942–1944)

The Commonwealth of the Philippines was the administrative body that governed the Philippines from 1935 to 1946 as a transitional government in preparation for full independence from the United States. Established under the Tydings–McDuffie Act of 1934 and the Philippine Constitution of 1935, it replaced the Insular Government of the Philippine Islands.

Government

Manuel L. Quezon was elected the Commonwealth's first president, with Sergio Osmeña as vice president. The Commonwealth had a unicameral National Assembly (later a bicameral Congress after constitutional amendments in 1940), its own courts, and responsibility for domestic affairs, while the United States retained control of foreign policy and defence. Women's suffrage was approved by plebiscite in 1937.

War and exile

Following the Japanese invasion in December 1941, the Commonwealth government evacuated Manila, moved to Corregidor, and then went into exile in Washington, D.C. Quezon died in 1944 and was succeeded by Osmeña, who returned with General Douglas MacArthur's forces at Leyte in October 1944. Meanwhile Japan sponsored a puppet Second Philippine Republic under José P. Laurel, which the Commonwealth never recognised.

Independence

Manuel Roxas won the presidential election of April 1946. On 4 July 1946 the United States recognised Philippine independence under the Treaty of Manila, the Commonwealth gave way to the Third Philippine Republic, and Roxas became its first president.
