Crown land

Crown land is land owned by the state, or in constitutional monarchies formally by the monarch or the Crown as a corporation sole. Such land is distinct from the monarch's private property and is typically administered by a government agency for public purposes such as forestry, conservation, grazing and resource development.

By country

In Canada, most land outside private holdings is provincial or federal Crown land, subject to Aboriginal title and treaty rights. In Australia, Crown land is administered by the states and territories under Crown land management legislation. In the United Kingdom, the Crown Estate is held by the monarch "in right of the Crown" and managed independently, with revenues paid to the Treasury.

Uses

Crown land may be leased for agriculture, mining or forestry, reserved as parks, or set aside for Indigenous peoples. Disputes over its use and title have been central to land-rights movements in several Commonwealth countries.
