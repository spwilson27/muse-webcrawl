John Venn

John Venn (4 August 1834 – 4 April 1923) was an English mathematician, logician, and philosopher noted for introducing the Venn diagram, used in logic, set theory, probability, statistics, and computer science. He worked primarily in the areas of logic and probability theory. Venn also wrote about the history of his alma mater, Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, and compiled a biographical history of the University of Cambridge.

Life

Venn was born in Hull, Yorkshire, into a prominent evangelical family. He studied at Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, graduating in 1857, and was ordained a priest in the Church of England in 1859, though he later resigned his orders in 1883 while remaining a fellow of Caius, where he spent most of his career teaching logic and probability.

Work

In an 1880 paper, Venn introduced the diagrammatic representation of sets that now bears his name, generalizing Euler diagrams to show all possible logical relations between sets. His book The Logic of Chance (1866) presented the frequency theory of probability, and Symbolic Logic (1881) developed the logic of George Boole. A stained glass window at Gonville and Caius commemorates him with a Venn diagram.
