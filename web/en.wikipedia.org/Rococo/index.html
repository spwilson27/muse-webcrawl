Rococo

Rococo
Periodc. 1720s – 1760s (later in Central Europe)
OriginParis, France
CharacteristicsAsymmetry, C- and S-scrolls, shells, pastel colours, lightness, playfulness
Key artistsAntoine Watteau, François Boucher, Jean-Honoré Fragonard

Rococo is an exceptionally ornamental style of architecture, art and decoration that originated in Paris in the early 18th century as a reaction against the grandeur and symmetry of the Baroque of Louis XIV's court. Characterised by lightness, elegance and playful asymmetry, it spread across Europe and flourished until the rise of Neoclassicism in the 1760s.

Origins and the Régence

The style emerged after the death of Louis XIV (1715), when the aristocracy moved from Versailles back to Parisian townhouses (hôtels) whose smaller, intimate rooms invited light, curving decoration. The word "rococo" is usually derived from the French rocaille (shell-and-pebble ornament) combined with baroque. Designs by Juste-Aurèle Meissonnier and Nicolas Pineau established the vocabulary of C-scrolls, shell motifs and naturalistic flowers, deliberately avoiding straight lines.

Painting

In painting, Antoine Watteau's fêtes galantes — elegantly dressed lovers in dreamy parks — set the tone before his early death in 1721. François Boucher and his pupil Jean-Honoré Fragonard (whose The Swing, 1767, is the style's most famous image) painted mythological and amorous scenes in soft pastels for patrons such as Madame de Pompadour. Rococo portraiture, pastel work by Rosalba Carriera, and the porcelain sculpture of Meissen and Sèvres carried the style across the arts.

Spread and decline

Rococo reached its most exuberant forms in the Catholic courts and pilgrimage churches of Bavaria and Austria, and in Frederick the Great's Prussia ("Frederician Rococo" at Sanssouci). From the 1760s, Enlightenment critics such as Voltaire and Diderot attacked it as frivolous, and archaeologically inspired Neoclassicism displaced it — though its influence lingered in the decorative arts and, later, the interiors displayed in collections such as the Rodin Museum's Paris mansion, the Hôtel Biron.
