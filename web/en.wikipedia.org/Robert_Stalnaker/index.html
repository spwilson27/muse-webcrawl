Robert Stalnaker
Born: 1940
Fields: philosophy of language, philosophy of mind, decision theory

Robert C. Stalnaker (born 1940) is an American philosopher, long a professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, known for his work in philosophy of language, metaphysics, philosophy of mind and decision theory.

Work

Stalnaker developed an influential theory of conditionals using possible-worlds semantics related to that of David Lewis, with whom he debated the analysis of counterfactuals. His account of assertion and common ground — the idea that conversation proceeds against a shared set of presupposed propositions, modelled as a context set — has become a standard framework in linguistic pragmatics. He has also written extensively on two-dimensional semantics, propositional attitudes and the problem of mental content, defending a form of externalism.

Books

His works include Inquiry (1984), Context and Content (1999) and Our Knowledge of the Internal World (2008).
