The Boeing 787 Dreamliner is an American wide-body airliner developed and manufactured by Boeing Commercial Airplanes. It was the first airliner with an airframe made primarily of composite materials — about 50 percent by weight — and was designed to be roughly 20 percent more fuel-efficient than the Boeing 767 it was intended to replace. The 787 is produced in three variants: the 787-8, the stretched 787-9, and the further-stretched 787-10.

Development

After cancelling its Sonic Cruiser concept, Boeing announced the 7E7 in 2003, marketing it around efficiency rather than speed. Launch customer All Nippon Airways ordered 50 aircraft in April 2004. The program relied on an unprecedented global supply chain, with major structures built by partners in Japan, Italy, and elsewhere and flown to final assembly in Everett, Washington — later also in Charleston, South Carolina. Extensive delays pushed the first flight to December 2009, and entry into service to October 2011.

Design

The Dreamliner replaced most pneumatic and hydraulic systems with electrically driven ones, uses lithium-ion batteries, and offers operators a choice of General Electric GEnx or Rolls-Royce Trent 1000 engines. Passenger amenities include larger windows with electronic dimming, higher cabin humidity, and a cabin pressurized to a lower equivalent altitude of about 6,000 feet.

Operational issues

In January 2013, following overheating incidents involving its lithium-ion batteries, aviation authorities worldwide grounded the 787 fleet — the first grounding of an entire airliner type since 1979. Boeing redesigned the battery system with a containment and venting enclosure, and the aircraft returned to service that spring. The type went on to open many new ultra-long-haul routes, including nonstop services between Australia and Europe.
