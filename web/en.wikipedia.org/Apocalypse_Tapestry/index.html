Apocalypse Tapestry

Datec. 1377–1382
Commissioned byLouis I, Duke of Anjou
MediumWool tapestry
LocationChâteau d'Angers, France

The Apocalypse Tapestry is a large medieval French tapestry cycle depicting scenes from the Book of Revelation. Commissioned by Louis I, Duke of Anjou, and woven in Paris between about 1377 and 1382, it is the oldest surviving French medieval tapestry of its scale and a masterpiece of Gothic art.

Description

The set originally comprised six sections, each about 24 metres long and 5.5 metres high, with some 90 scenes. About 71 scenes survive, now displayed in a purpose-built gallery at the Château d'Angers. The designs were drawn by the court painter Hennequin de Bruges (Jean Bondol), based on illuminated manuscripts of the Apocalypse lent by King Charles V.

History

The tapestry was bequeathed to Angers Cathedral in the 15th century, where it was later neglected, cut up, and partly lost during the Revolutionary period. It was recovered and restored in the 19th century under Canon Joubert.
