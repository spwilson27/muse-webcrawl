Cannery Row is a street in the New Monterey section of Monterey, California, on the shore of Monterey Bay. It is famous both as the former center of the sardine canning industry and as the setting of John Steinbeck's novels Cannery Row (1945) and Sweet Thursday (1954). The street was officially renamed Cannery Row in January 1958, having previously been known as Ocean View Avenue.

The Cannery Row waterfront, Monterey, California.

History

The sardine canning industry on Monterey's waterfront grew rapidly in the early twentieth century, driven by abundant Pacific sardine schools and demand created by the First World War. By the 1920s and 1930s, dozens of canneries and reduction plants lined Ocean View Avenue, processing sardines for canning and for fish meal and oil. The industry reached its peak during the Second World War.

The sardine fishery collapsed in the late 1940s because of overfishing combined with unfavorable ocean conditions. The canneries closed one by one, and the last, the Hovden Food Products Corporation, shut its doors in 1973. The derelict industrial buildings were later redeveloped, and from the 1960s onward the street became a major tourist destination with hotels, restaurants, wine tasting rooms, and shops occupying the old cannery shells.

Steinbeck's Cannery Row

Steinbeck's novel immortalized the street's denizens, including the marine biologist Ed Ricketts, fictionalized as "Doc," whose Pacific Biological Laboratories stood at 800 Cannery Row. The real laboratory building still stands and is a National Historic Landmark. Steinbeck described the Row as "a poem, a stink, a grating noise, a quality of light, a tone, a habit, a nostalgia, a dream." Other real figures of the Row included the grocers and madams fictionalized in the novels.

Today

Today Cannery Row is one of the Monterey Peninsula's principal visitor districts. The Monterey Bay Aquarium, which opened in 1984 in the former Hovden Cannery at the northwestern end of the street, anchors the area. The street also hosts the annual Cannery Row festivities and is a gateway to the Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary.
