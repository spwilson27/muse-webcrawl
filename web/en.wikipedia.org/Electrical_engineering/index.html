Electrical engineering


Electrical engineers work on power systems spanning generation, transmission and distribution

Electrical engineering is an engineering discipline concerned with the study, design and application of equipment, devices and systems which use electricity, electronics and electromagnetism. It emerged as an identifiable occupation in the latter half of the nineteenth century after the commercialisation of the electric telegraph, the telephone and electrical power generation, distribution and use.

History

Electricity had been studied since antiquity, but its practical engineering began with the experiments of Michael Faraday and the mathematical formulation of electromagnetism by James Clerk Maxwell in the 1860s. The invention of the practical incandescent lamp, the transformer and alternating current systems in the 1880s — the era of the "war of the currents" between Thomas Edison's direct current and the alternating current promoted by Nikola Tesla and George Westinghouse — established electrical engineering as a profession. The first university courses appeared in the 1880s, and the field expanded with radio, electronics, and later computing.

Subdisciplines

  • Power engineering – generation, transmission and distribution of electric power, and the design of motors, generators and transformers.
  • Electronics – circuits using transistors, diodes and integrated circuits.
  • Control systems – modelling and control of dynamic systems using feedback.
  • Signal processing – analysis and manipulation of analogue and digital signals.
  • Telecommunications – transmission of information by radio, fibre optics and other channels.
  • Microelectronics and computer engineering – design of integrated circuits and computer hardware.

Practice

Electrical engineers typically hold a degree in electrical engineering or a related field and, in many jurisdictions, a professional licence for work affecting public safety. Projects range from millimetre-scale integrated circuits to national power grids. Professional bodies such as the IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) publish standards and journals for the field.
