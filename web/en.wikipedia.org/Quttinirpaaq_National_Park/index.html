Quttinirpaaq National Park

Tanquary Fiord, Quttinirpaaq National Park

Quttinirpaaq National Park is a Canadian national park located on the northeastern corner of Ellesmere Island in Nunavut. It is the second most northerly park on Earth — only Northeast Greenland National Park is farther north — and the country's second largest, covering about 37,775 km2. Its name means "top of the world" in Inuktitut.

Geography

The park encompasses the Grant Land Mountains, extensive ice caps and glaciers, and deep fjords including Tanquary Fiord and Lake Hazen, one of the largest lakes by volume in North America. Mount Barbeau, at 2,616 m the highest peak in eastern North America, rises within the park. The region is a polar desert receiving very little precipitation.

Wildlife and history

Wildlife includes muskoxen, Peary caribou, Arctic wolves, Arctic hares, and lemmings. Archaeological sites record human presence by Paleo-Eskimo peoples from about 2000 BC. The area saw expeditions by 19th- and 20th-century Arctic explorers and the Cold War-era research station at Fort Conger.

Establishment

Established in 1988 as Ellesmere Island National Park Reserve, it received its present name and full national park status under the Nunavut Land Claims Agreement in 2000. Access is by charter aircraft to Tanquary Fiord or Lake Hazen, and the park receives only a handful of visitors each year.
