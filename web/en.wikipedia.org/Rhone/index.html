Rhone

"Rhone" redirects here in unaccented spelling. For the river whose name is conventionally written with a circumflex, see Rhône.


The Rhone in its upper valley

Rhone is the unaccented English rendering of the Rhône, one of the major rivers of Europe. Because English-language keyboards and older typesetting often omitted the circumflex, the spelling "Rhone" is widespread in English texts, maps and place-names derived from the river. The river rises at the Rhône Glacier in the Swiss Alps, flows through Lake Geneva, and continues through south-eastern France to the Mediterranean Sea, a course of about 813 kilometres (505 miles).

Uses of the unaccented form

The spelling without the accent appears in the names of several French departments — Rhône, Bouches-du-Rhône and the former region Rhône-Alpes — when written in English or in ASCII contexts, and in the names of the Rhone wine region's appellations, such as Côtes du Rhône, frequently rendered "Cotes du Rhone" on English-language wine labels. In viticulture, "Rhone" also gives its name to the informal grouping of "Rhone varieties" — grape varieties associated with the valley, including Syrah, Grenache, Mourvèdre and Viognier — and to the California winemakers dubbed the "Rhone Rangers", who championed those varieties in the United States.

The river

From its source in the canton of Valais, the Rhône flows west through Lake Geneva, then south past Lyon, where it is joined by the Saône, and on through Valence, Avignon and Arles. Near its mouth it divides into the Grande Rhône and Petit Rhône, enclosing the Camargue delta before emptying into the Mediterranean near Port-Saint-Louis-du-Rhône. With an average discharge of about 1,700 cubic metres per second, it is one of the most voluminous rivers flowing into the Mediterranean, and its course is heavily engineered with hydroelectric dams and navigation locks.
