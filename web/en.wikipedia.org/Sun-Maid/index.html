Sun-Maid Growers of California is an American privately owned cooperative of raisin growers headquartered in Fresno, California, and the world's largest producer and processor of raisins and dried fruits. Its red boxes of raisins, bearing the "Sun-Maid Girl" trademark, are among the most recognisable dried-fruit packages in the United States.

History

The cooperative was founded in 1912 as the California Associated Raisin Company; the Sun-Maid name was adopted in 1915. Growers in California's San Joaquin Valley dry Thompson Seedless grapes in the sun between rows of vines, a method that gave both the brand and its iconography.

The Sun-Maid Girl

The trademark image of a young woman in a red sunbonnet holding a tray of fresh grapes is based on Lorraine Collett, who posed for the illustration in 1915 after being seen drying her hair in a Fresno garden. The image has been updated several times but remains recognisably the same.
