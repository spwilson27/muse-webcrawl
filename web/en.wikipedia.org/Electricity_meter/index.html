Electricity meter

Electricity meter
MeasuresElectrical energy
UnitKilowatt hour (kWh)
TypesElectromechanical, electronic, smart

An electricity meter is a device that measures the amount of electric energy consumed by a residence, business, or electrically powered device. Utilities use meters at customers' premises for billing, and the standard unit of measurement is the kilowatt hour (kWh).

Electromechanical meters

The classic induction meter, patented in the late nineteenth century and refined by Oliver Shallenberger and others, uses an aluminium disc rotated by eddy currents in proportion to power consumption; a gear train drives a cyclometer-style register of dials. These meters dominated for most of the twentieth century.

Electronic and smart meters

Electronic meters measure voltage and current digitally, display consumption on an LCD, and can record time-of-use tariffs. Smart meters communicate readings automatically to the utility over power-line, radio or cellular networks (advanced metering infrastructure), enabling remote reading, outage detection and demand-response programmes.

Accuracy

Meters are classified by accuracy class under standards such as IEC 62053 and ANSI C12, and are subject to type approval and periodic verification in many jurisdictions.
