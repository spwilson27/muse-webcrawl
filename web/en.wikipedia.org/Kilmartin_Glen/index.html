Kilmartin Glen

Kilmartin Glen is a valley in Argyll, on the west coast of Scotland, noted for one of the richest concentrations of prehistoric monuments in Britain. Within a few kilometres of the village of Kilmartin lie more than 300 ancient sites — cairns, standing stones, stone circles, rock carvings and hillforts — spanning some 5,000 years from the Neolithic to the early medieval period.

Major monuments

The glen's most famous feature is its "linear cemetery": a line of five Bronze Age burial cairns running for several kilometres along the valley floor, including Glebe Cairn, Nether Largie North, Mid and South cairns, and Ri Cruin. Nearby stand the Temple Wood stone circles, the Nether Largie standing stones arranged in an X-shaped setting, and extensive panels of cup-and-ring rock art at sites such as Achnabreck, among the largest in Scotland.

Later history

Overlooking the glen is Dunadd, a rocky hillfort generally identified as the royal seat of the early medieval Gaelic kingdom of Dál Riata, where kings were inaugurated beside a carved footprint in the rock. The village churchyard contains a notable collection of medieval carved grave slabs.

Interpretation

Kilmartin House Museum, opened in 1997 and extensively redeveloped in the 2020s, interprets the glen's archaeology and displays artefacts excavated from its monuments.
