In Greek mythology, Achilles was a hero of the Trojan War, the central character of Homer's Iliad, and the greatest of all the Greek warriors. He was the son of the Nereid Thetis and Peleus, king of Phthia, and was raised by the centaur Chiron. Achilles led the Myrmidons against Troy.

In the Iliad

The Iliad opens with Achilles withdrawing from battle after Agamemnon seizes his prize, the captive Briseis. His absence leads to Greek defeats, and his companion Patroclus, wearing Achilles' armour, is killed by the Trojan prince Hector. Enraged, Achilles returns to battle, kills Hector, and drags his body around Troy before finally returning it to King Priam. Later traditions (not in the Iliad) tell that Achilles was killed by Paris, who shot him in the heel with an arrow guided by Apollo.

The heel

A later legend holds that Thetis dipped the infant Achilles in the river Styx to make him invulnerable, holding him by one heel, which remained his single weak point — the origin of the expression "Achilles' heel" for a fatal vulnerability.

Legacy

Achilles became the archetype of the heroic warrior who chooses a short life of glory over a long obscure one. He was worshipped as a hero in several Greek cults, and his story has been retold in works from Statius's unfinished Achilleid to modern novels and films.
