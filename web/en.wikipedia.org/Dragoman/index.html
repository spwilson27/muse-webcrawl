Dragoman

A dragoman in the Ottoman Empire.

A dragoman was an interpreter, translator and guide employed in the Middle East, particularly in the Ottoman Empire, by embassies, consulates, merchants and travellers. The word derives from the Arabic tarjumān (translator), the same root as "truchman" and, ultimately, the term targum.

Role

Dragomans were indispensable intermediaries between Europeans and Ottoman authorities, translating diplomatic correspondence, negotiating with officials and arranging travel. At the Sublime Porte the Grand Dragoman—usually a Phanariot Greek—was a powerful official who effectively managed much of the empire's foreign relations in the 17th and 18th centuries; the Dragoman of the Fleet held a similar role for the Aegean. European embassies maintained hereditary dragoman families, often Levantine Catholics, whose linguistic skills gave them considerable influence.

Decline

The profession declined in the 19th and 20th centuries as European diplomats learned Turkish and other regional languages themselves and as modern diplomatic services professionalised translation.
