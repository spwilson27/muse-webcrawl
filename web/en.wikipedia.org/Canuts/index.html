Canuts
Silk weavers of Lyon, France
Known for the revolts of 1831 and 1834

The canuts were the silk weavers of Lyon, France, who worked on Jacquard looms, chiefly in the Croix-Rousse district. Their uprisings were among the first clearly defined worker revolts of the Industrial Revolution.

Revolts

In November 1831 the canuts seized Lyon after manufacturers refused negotiated piece rates, marching under the banner "Live working or die fighting." A second, republican-linked revolt in April 1834 was bloodily suppressed.

Legacy

The revolts inspired later labour movements across Europe; Karl Marx cited them as an early example of workers acting as an independent political force. The Croix-Rousse is still called "the hill that works."
