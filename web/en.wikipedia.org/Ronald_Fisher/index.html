Sir Ronald Aylmer Fisher (17 February 1890 – 29 July 1962) was a British polymath — statistician, geneticist and eugenicist — regarded as one of the founders of modern statistics and, with Sewall Wright and J. B. S. Haldane, of population genetics.

Career

Fisher studied mathematics and physics at Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge. In 1918 he published the paper that reconciled Mendelian genetics with continuous variation, founding quantitative genetics. From 1919 he worked at Rothamsted Experimental Station, where he developed the analysis of variance, principles of experimental design (randomisation, blocking, factorial designs) and much of modern statistical inference, set out in Statistical Methods for Research Workers (1925) and The Design of Experiments (1935). His The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection (1930) was a landmark of the modern synthesis, stating the fundamental theorem of natural selection and theories of sex ratios and runaway sexual selection.

Later life and legacy

Fisher became Galton Professor at University College London (1933) and then Balfour Professor of Genetics at Cambridge (1943). He was knighted in 1952 and spent his final years in Adelaide, Australia. Richard Dawkins called him "the greatest of Darwin's successors". His advocacy of eugenics, and his disputes with contemporaries, remain controversial aspects of his legacy.
