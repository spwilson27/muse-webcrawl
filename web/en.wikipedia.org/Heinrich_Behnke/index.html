Heinrich Behnke

Heinrich Adolph Louis Behnke (9 October 1898 in Horn, near Hamburg – 10 October 1979 in Münster) was a German mathematician best known for his work in complex analysis in several variables. He studied at the University of Hamburg, where he received his doctorate in 1923 under Erich Hecke, and later held the chair of mathematics at the University of Münster from 1927 until his retirement.

Career and work

Together with his student Karl Stein and others, Behnke built Münster into a leading centre for the theory of functions of several complex variables. The Behnke–Stein theorem (1936) states that an increasing union of domains of holomorphy is itself a domain of holomorphy, a foundational result connected with the concept of Stein manifolds. With Peter Thullen he wrote the classic monograph Theorie der Funktionen mehrerer komplexer Veränderlichen (1934), which organised the young field.

Legacy

Behnke was also influential in mathematics education and university organisation in post-war Germany, serving as dean and as an editor of the journal Mathematische Annalen. Among his doctoral students were Karl Stein and Friedrich Hirzebruch. His memoirs, Semesterberichte, give a vivid account of German mathematical life through the middle of the twentieth century.
