Appliqué

Appliqué
TypeNeedlework technique
MaterialsFabric, thread
UsesQuilts, garments, banners, flags

Appliqué is a needlework technique in which pieces of fabric are sewn onto a larger background fabric to form pictures or patterns. The word derives from the French verb appliquer, meaning "to apply". Appliqué is used decoratively on quilts, clothing, banners, and flags, and appears in textile traditions around the world.

Techniques

In hand appliqué, fabric shapes are attached with small, nearly invisible stitches such as the blind stitch; in machine appliqué, edges are commonly secured with satin stitch or other decorative stitching. Raw-edge appliqué leaves edges unfinished for a textured effect, while fusible methods use heat-activated adhesive webbing to bond pieces before stitching. Reverse appliqué, as in the Panamanian mola, cuts through layered fabrics to reveal colours beneath.

Traditions

Notable appliqué traditions include the molas of the Guna people of Panama, the appliqué banners and Fon cloths of the former kingdom of Dahomey in Benin, Hawaiian quilts with their bold symmetric botanical designs, and the story cloths of Hmong communities. In Europe, appliqué has long been used in heraldic banners and ecclesiastical textiles.
