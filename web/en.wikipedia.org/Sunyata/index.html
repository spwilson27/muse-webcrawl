Sunyata

Śūnyatā (Sanskrit: शून्यता; Pali: suññatā), often translated as "emptiness" or "voidness," is a central concept in Buddhism. It denotes that all phenomena are empty (śūnya) of inherent, independent existence or essence (svabhāva). Because everything arises dependently — through causes and conditions — nothing possesses a fixed, self-contained nature.

Development

The term appears in early Buddhist texts in connection with the teaching of non-self (anattā): the five aggregates are empty of a self. In the Prajñāpāramitā sutras and the Madhyamaka philosophy of Nāgārjuna (2nd century CE), emptiness is extended to all dharmas, including emptiness itself — the "emptiness of emptiness." Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamakakārikā famously identifies dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), emptiness, and the middle way.

Interpretation

Śūnyatā is not nihilism: it does not claim that things do not exist at all, but that they exist only dependently and conventionally. Realizing emptiness is held to dissolve attachment and conceptual reification, and is closely linked with compassion (karuṇā) in Mahāyāna thought: the bodhisattva, seeing all beings as empty yet suffering, acts to liberate them. Related concepts include the two truths doctrine, distinguishing conventional from ultimate truth.
