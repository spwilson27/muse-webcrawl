Russian Internet

The Russian Internet — often called the Runet (Russian: Рунет), from ".ru" and "net" — is the Russian-language community on the Internet and the websites and services used predominantly in Russia and the Russian-speaking world. It is notable for its strong domestic platforms, including the search engine Yandex, the social network VK, the email and portal service Mail.ru, and the classifieds site Avito, which historically outperformed global competitors in the Russian market.

History

Networking in the Soviet Union began with academic and cooperative networks such as Relcom (1990), which carried news of the 1991 coup attempt to the world. The .ru country-code domain was registered on 7 April 1994, a date now marked as Runet Day. Commercial providers, Cyrillic-friendly software and portals such as Rambler and Yandex (launched 1997) drove rapid growth in the 2000s, and broadband and mobile access spread widely in the 2010s.

Regulation and censorship

The Russian state has progressively tightened control: a 2012 blacklist law introduced a register of banned sites, followed by data-localisation requirements, the "Yarovaya" surveillance package (2016), restrictions on VPNs and anonymisers, and the 2019 "sovereign internet" law enabling centralised traffic management. After 2022, major Western platforms including Facebook and Instagram were blocked or designated extremist, Twitter/X was throttled and blocked, and many independent media sites were restricted, while domestic services faced increased pressure to remove prohibited content.

Usage

By the 2020s Russia had well over 100 million internet users, among the largest online populations in Europe, with high use of messaging apps such as Telegram — founded by the Russian entrepreneur Pavel Durov — and of state and commercial online services. The Runet also extends to Russian-speaking diaspora communities worldwide.
