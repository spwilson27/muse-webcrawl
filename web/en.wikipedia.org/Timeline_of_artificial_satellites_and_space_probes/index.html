Timeline of artificial satellites and space probes

This timeline of artificial satellites and space probes includes uncrewed spacecraft launched to Earth orbit and beyond. It lists selected landmark missions in chronological order.

1950s – The first satellites

  • 1957 – Sputnik 1 (USSR), the first artificial satellite, launched 4 October. Sputnik 2 carries the dog Laika in November.
  • 1958 – Explorer 1, the first U.S. satellite, discovers the Van Allen radiation belts.
  • 1959 – Luna 1 is the first spacecraft to reach the vicinity of the Moon; Luna 2 impacts the Moon; Luna 3 photographs its far side.

1960s – Early exploration

  • 1960 – TIROS-1, the first weather satellite; Echo 1, an early communications satellite.
  • 1962 – Telstar 1 relays the first transatlantic television signals; Mariner 2 flies by Venus.
  • 1964 – Syncom 3 becomes the first geostationary communications satellite.
  • 1965 – Mariner 4 returns the first close-up photographs of Mars.
  • 1966 – Luna 9 makes the first soft landing on the Moon.
  • 1969 – Mariner 6 and 7 fly by Mars; Apollo missions return lunar data and samples with robotic support craft.

1970s – Outer planets and stations

  • 1970 – Venera 7 lands on Venus, the first soft landing on another planet; Ōsumi is Japan's first satellite; Dong Fang Hong I is China's first.
  • 1971 – Salyut 1, the first space station; Mars 3 lands on Mars.
  • 1973 – Pioneer 10 flies by Jupiter; Skylab is launched.
  • 1975 – Venera 9 sends the first images from the surface of Venus.
  • 1976 – Viking 1 and 2 land on Mars and search for life.
  • 1977 – Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 are launched on their grand tour of the outer planets.
  • 1978 – The International Ultraviolet Explorer and Seasat Earth observation missions.

1980s–1990s

  • 1983 – IRAS, the first space-based infrared observatory.
  • 1986 – The Vega and Giotto probes encounter Halley's Comet.
  • 1989 – Galileo is launched to Jupiter; COBE maps the cosmic microwave background.
  • 1990 – The Hubble Space Telescope is deployed; Ulysses is launched to study the Sun's poles.
  • 1995 – SOHO begins solar observations; Galileo arrives at Jupiter.
  • 1997 – Mars Pathfinder lands with the Sojourner rover; Cassini–Huygens launches to Saturn.
  • 1998 – The first modules of the International Space Station are launched; Deep Space 1 tests ion propulsion.

2000s–present

  • 2001 – NEAR Shoemaker lands on the asteroid Eros.
  • 2004 – Cassini arrives at Saturn; SMART-1 reaches the Moon.
  • 2005 – Huygens lands on Titan, the most distant landing made.
  • 2006 – New Horizons launches toward Pluto.
  • 2011 – Juno launches to Jupiter; Dawn orbits Vesta.
  • 2012 – Voyager 1 enters interstellar space; Curiosity rover lands on Mars.
  • 2014 – Rosetta's Philae lander touches down on comet 67P.
  • 2015 – New Horizons flies by Pluto.
  • 2018 – Parker Solar Probe launches toward the Sun's corona.
  • 2020 – Perseverance rover and the Ingenuity helicopter head to Mars (landing 2021); Chang'e 5 returns lunar samples.
  • 2021 – The James Webb Space Telescope is launched; DART launches to test asteroid deflection (impact 2022).
