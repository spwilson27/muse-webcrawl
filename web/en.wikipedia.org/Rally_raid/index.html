Rally raid

Rally raid vehicles race cross-country over hundreds of kilometres of desert and rough terrain.

A rally raid, also known as a cross-country rally, is a form of long-distance off-road racing that takes place over several days. The length of the event can be as short as 2–3 days for a cross-country baja, or as long as fifteen days for major marathon rallies. Each day's competition consists of timed special stages run mainly off-road — across desert, dunes, savanna, mountains and dry lake beds — connected by untimed liaison sections on public roads.

Navigation is a defining feature: competitors follow a roadbook and, in premier events, limited GPS information, without prior reconnaissance of the route. Vehicle categories include motorcycles, quads, cars (T1 prototypes and production-based T2), lightweight side-by-side vehicles, and heavy trucks.

Major events

The Dakar Rally, founded by Thierry Sabine and first run from Paris to Dakar in 1979, is the archetypal rally raid. After security concerns forced its move from Africa, it was held in South America from 2009 to 2019 and in Saudi Arabia since 2020. Other leading events include the Abu Dhabi Desert Challenge, the Rallye du Maroc, the Sonora Rally and the Silk Way Rally. Since 2022 the FIA and FIM have run a combined World Rally-Raid Championship (W2RC) with the Dakar as its centrepiece.

Format and rules

Crews overnight at bivouacs, where service is permitted; in "marathon" stages, outside assistance is restricted or forbidden. Motorcyclists must navigate while riding alone, while car crews pair a driver with a co-driver/navigator. Time penalties apply for missing waypoints, speeding in control zones and receiving unauthorised assistance.
