The HVDC Inter-Island link is a high-voltage direct current electricity transmission system connecting New Zealand's North and South Island power grids. It runs from the Benmore hydroelectric station in the South Island's Waitaki Valley, overland to Fighting Bay near Picton, across Cook Strait by submarine cable, and from Oteranga Bay to the Haywards substation near Wellington. It is often simply called "the Cook Strait cable".

Operation

The link normally carries South Island hydroelectric power north to the more populous North Island, but it can reverse flow — for example during South Island droughts or North Island surplus. It operates as two poles (Pole 2 and Pole 3), with a combined capacity of about 1,200 MW, and also provides frequency-keeping and reserve services between the two island grids, which are not synchronised with each other.

History

The original link, using mercury arc valves, entered service in 1965 — among the world's earliest HVDC schemes and notable for its long submarine cable crossing. Thyristor converters (Pole 2) replaced the original equipment in the early 1990s, and Pole 3, using modern converter technology, was commissioned in 2013, retiring the last original plant. The ageing Cook Strait cables are planned for eventual replacement.
