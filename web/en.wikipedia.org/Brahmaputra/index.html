Brahmaputra River

The Brahmaputra is a trans-boundary river flowing through Tibet (China), north-eastern India, and Bangladesh, about 3,969 km long. It rises near Mount Kailash in the Himalayas as the Yarlung Tsangpo, flows east across the Tibetan Plateau, then turns sharply south through some of the world's deepest gorges before entering Arunachal Pradesh as the Siang and Assam as the Brahmaputra.

In Assam the river is exceptionally wide and braided, carrying an enormous sediment load during the monsoon, when it frequently floods. It joins the Ganges in Bangladesh — where it is called the Jamuna — and their combined waters form the world's largest river delta before emptying into the Bay of Bengal.

The river is vital for irrigation, transport, and fisheries for tens of millions of people, and is revered in Hindu tradition as the son of Brahma. Dams built or planned on its upper reaches are a source of regional geopolitical concern.
