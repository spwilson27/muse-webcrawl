Roger Charles Louis Guillemin (1924–2024) was a French-American neuroscientist who shared the 1977 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discoveries of the peptide hormones produced by the hypothalamus, laying the foundations of neuroendocrinology.

Life and career

Guillemin was born in Dijon, France, took his medical degree in Lyon in 1949, and earned a PhD in Montreal under Hans Selye. Emigrating to the United States, he worked at Baylor College of Medicine in Houston and from 1970 at the Salk Institute in La Jolla, California, becoming a US citizen in 1965.

Hypothalamic releasing hormones

Geoffrey Harris had proposed that the brain controls the pituitary gland through chemical messengers released into the portal blood vessels. Guillemin and his great rival-turned-co-laureate Andrew Schally raced for two decades, processing millions of sheep and pig hypothalami, to isolate these vanishingly scarce substances. Guillemin's laboratory established the structures of thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH, 1969), gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH, 1971) and somatostatin (1973) — work that transformed endocrinology and led to widely used drugs and diagnostics, including GnRH analogues for prostate cancer and fertility treatment. His group later isolated endorphins and growth-hormone-releasing factor.

Nobel Prize and later life

Guillemin and Schally shared half of the 1977 Nobel Prize, with Rosalyn Yalow receiving the other half for radioimmunoassay. In retirement he became a noted digital artist. He died in San Diego in February 2024, shortly after turning 100.
