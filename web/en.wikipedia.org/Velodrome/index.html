Velodrome

A velodrome is an arena for track cycling. Velodromes feature steeply banked oval tracks consisting of two 180-degree circular bends connected by two straights. Modern velodromes are built to standards set by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI); Olympic-standard tracks are 250 metres long.

Design

Banking on the bends can exceed 45 degrees on short tracks (for example, 250 m tracks are banked at roughly 45°), while longer outdoor tracks of 333.33 m or 400 m have gentler banking around 25–35°. The banking compensates for the lateral forces on riders cornering at speed, allowing them to keep their bicycles perpendicular to the surface. Track surfaces are typically timber (such as Siberian pine) for modern indoor velodromes, or concrete and asphalt for outdoor tracks. Key track markings include the measurement line, the blue "côte d'azur" apron, the sprinters' line and the stayers' line.

History

Velodromes proliferated during the bicycle boom of the 1890s; famous examples include the Vélodrome d'Hiver in Paris, which also hosted mass roundups during the Second World War, and the Herne Hill Velodrome in London (1891). Track cycling has been part of the modern Olympic Games since 1896. Notable contemporary venues include the Lee Valley VeloPark in London, built for the 2012 Olympics, and the velodromes of Manchester and Newport in Britain.

Events

Velodrome racing disciplines include the sprint, team sprint, keirin, individual and team pursuit, time trial, points race, scratch race, madison and omnium. Hour record attempts are also traditionally made on velodromes.
