Arbroath Abbey is a ruined Benedictine abbey in the town of Arbroath in Angus, Scotland. It was founded in 1178 by King William the Lion and dedicated to St Thomas Becket, and was colonised by monks of the Tironensian order from Kelso Abbey. For centuries it was among the wealthiest and most powerful monasteries in Scotland.

History

The abbey is celebrated as the place where, in 1320, the Declaration of Arbroath was prepared—a letter from the nobles of Scotland to Pope John XXII asserting Scotland's independence from England during the Wars of Scottish Independence. The abbey declined after the Scottish Reformation, its buildings were quarried for stone, and the famous round window, the "Round O", in the south transept became a landmark for sailors. In 1951 the Stone of Destiny, stolen from Westminster Abbey, was found at the abbey's high altar site.

The ruins

Surviving remains include the gatehouse range, the abbot's house (among the best preserved in Britain), parts of the church including the twin western towers and south transept, and the sacristy. The site is cared for by Historic Environment Scotland and includes a visitor centre.
