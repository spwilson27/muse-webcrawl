United Fruit Company

Founded1899
Defunct1970 (merged into United Brands)
SuccessorChiquita Brands
IndustryAgriculture (bananas)

The United Fruit Company (later United Brands) was an American corporation that traded in tropical fruit — primarily bananas — grown on plantations in Latin America and sold in North America and Europe. Formed in 1899 from the merger of Minor C. Keith's banana trading interests with the Boston Fruit Company, United Fruit became one of the most powerful and controversial corporations of the twentieth century.

Dominance in Central America

United Fruit controlled vast tracts of land, railroads (through the International Railways of Central America), ports, telegraph lines, and the famous "Great White Fleet" of refrigerated steamships. In Guatemala it was the country's largest landowner and employer, and its political influence was so pervasive that Latin Americans nicknamed it el pulpo, "the octopus". Its enclave towns set wages, prices, and scrip, giving rise to the term "banana republic" for countries dominated by a single export corporation.

Controversies

The company was implicated in the 1928 Banana Massacre in Colombia, in which troops fired on striking United Fruit workers. Its most notorious intervention came in Guatemala: when President Jacobo Árbenz's land reform (Decree 900, 1952) expropriated the company's uncultivated holdings, United Fruit lobbied the United States government, and the CIA overthrew Árbenz in the 1954 coup d'état, with the involvement of Secretary of State John Foster Dulles and CIA Director Allen Dulles, both of whom had ties to the company.

Decline and legacy

Antitrust actions, disease among its banana stock, and political pressure eroded the company's position. In 1970 it merged with AMK Corporation to form United Brands; after the 1975 suicide of chairman Eli Black amid a bribery scandal, the company became Chiquita Brands in 1985. Its business model helped create the modern vertically integrated fruit industry and popularised the banana in American diets.
