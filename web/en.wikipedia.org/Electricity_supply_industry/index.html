Electricity supply industry

Electricity supply industry
SectorsGeneration, transmission, distribution, retail
ProductElectrical energy

The electricity supply industry comprises the generation, transmission, distribution and sale of electric power to the general public and industry. Because electricity is difficult to store economically at scale, supply must be balanced with demand in real time across interconnected power grids.

Structure

Historically the industry was dominated by vertically integrated utilities, often state-owned or regulated monopolies, that owned power stations, networks and customer supply businesses. From the 1980s and 1990s many countries restructured the industry, separating competitive generation and retail from regulated monopoly networks and creating wholesale electricity markets.

Generation

Electricity is generated from fossil fuels, nuclear fission, and renewable sources such as hydro, wind, solar and geothermal power. System operators dispatch generators in merit order and procure reserves and ancillary services to maintain grid frequency and stability.

Networks and retail

Transmission system operators run high-voltage grids, distribution network operators deliver power locally, and retailers buy wholesale energy and sell it to end users under regulated or competitive tariffs.
