Companion animal

A companion animal, or pet, is an animal kept primarily for a person's company, entertainment, or protection rather than as a working animal, livestock, or laboratory animal. The most popular pets worldwide include dogs, cats, freshwater fish, birds, and small mammals such as rabbits, hamsters, and guinea pigs.

History

Pet keeping has ancient roots: the dog was domesticated at least 15,000 years ago, and cats were associated with human settlements by around 7500 BCE. Pet keeping as a widespread middle-class practice spread in the 19th century, along with organised breeding, kennel clubs, and the first animal welfare legislation and societies such as the RSPCA (founded 1824).

Pet ownership

Surveys in many countries find that a majority of households keep at least one pet. Ownership is associated in studies with companionship and reduced loneliness, though findings on health benefits are mixed. Owners have legal duties of care under animal welfare law, and pets are generally treated in law as property, though some jurisdictions have begun to recognise their sentience in statute.

Issues

Issues associated with pet keeping include abandonment and shelter overpopulation, invasive species released into the wild, zoonotic disease, the ethics of breeding for extreme traits, and the environmental footprint of pet food.
