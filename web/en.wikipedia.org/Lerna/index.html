Lerna

Lerna is a region of springs and a former lake near the Gulf of Argos in the Argolis, Greece, and the name of an ancient settlement occupied from the Neolithic through the Bronze Age. In Greek mythology it was the home of the Lernaean Hydra.

Location: Argolis, Greece
Periods: Neolithic – Mycenaean
Notable structure: House of the Tiles

Mythology

Lerna was said to be one of the entrances to the Underworld. Its marshes housed the Hydra, the many-headed serpent slain by Heracles as the second of his Twelve Labours; each time one head was cut off, two grew in its place, until Heracles' nephew Iolaus cauterised the stumps.

Archaeology

Excavations by the American School of Classical Studies under John Caskey in the 1950s revealed one of the most important prehistoric sites in Greece. The Early Helladic II "House of the Tiles" (c. 2500 BC) was a large monumental corridor house — among the earliest examples of palace-like architecture in mainland Greece — roofed with terracotta tiles and decorated with clay sealings. The settlement was destroyed by fire and later reoccupied in the Middle and Late Bronze Age, with shaft graves of the Mycenaean period.
