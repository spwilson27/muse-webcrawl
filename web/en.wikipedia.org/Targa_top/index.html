Targa top

A targa top, or targa for short, is a semi-convertible car body style with a removable roof section and a full-width roll bar behind the seats. The term was first used on the 1966 Porsche 911 Targa, and it remains a registered trademark of Porsche AG.

Targa top
Type: Semi-convertible body style
Introduced: 1965 (Porsche 911 Targa, shown at Frankfurt)

History

Porsche introduced the Targa in response to anticipated safety regulations in the United States that threatened to ban full convertibles. The design combined open-air driving with a fixed rollover bar and a removable panel over the front seats. The name comes from the Targa Florio road race in Sicily, where Porsche had scored many victories.

Other cars

The style was widely imitated in the 1970s and 1980s by cars such as the Ferrari Dino 246 GTS, the Toyota Supra, the Chevrolet Corvette and the Honda NSX. Generic versions are sometimes called "T-tops" when a central spine divides two removable panels.
