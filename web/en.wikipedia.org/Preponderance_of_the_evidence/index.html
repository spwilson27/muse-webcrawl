Preponderance of the evidence

Preponderance is often visualised as a scale tipped ever so slightly in one party's favour.

Preponderance of the evidence is the standard of proof used in most civil cases in the United States and other common-law jurisdictions. A fact is proved by a preponderance when, considering all the evidence, it is more likely than not to be true — sometimes expressed as a probability slightly greater than 50%, or as the scales of evidence tipping, however slightly, in favour of the party bearing the burden of proof.

Comparison with other standards

Preponderance is the lowest of the three main standards in U.S. law. Above it sits clear and convincing evidence, an intermediate standard used for matters such as civil fraud or termination of parental rights, and highest is beyond a reasonable doubt, required for criminal convictions. The preponderance standard governs ordinary civil litigation — contracts, torts, most administrative proceedings, and preponderance-based findings in areas such as Title IX campus proceedings (in some periods), tax disputes, and probate. In many civil-law countries and in English civil practice the equivalent test is called the "balance of probabilities."
