Whip antenna

A whip antenna is an antenna consisting of a straight flexible wire or rod. The bottom end is connected to the radio receiver or transmitter, and the antenna is designed to be flexible so that it does not break easily; the name derives from the whip-like motion it exhibits when disturbed. Whip antennas are the most common type of monopole antenna and are widely used on portable radios, vehicles, boats and aircraft.

Design

A whip is usually operated over a ground plane formed by the radio's chassis or the vehicle body, and is most efficient when its length is a quarter of the wavelength (λ/4) of the operating frequency, making it resonant. At VHF and UHF, quarter-wave whips are conveniently short — about 19 cm at 400 MHz — and give an omnidirectional radiation pattern in the horizontal plane with vertical polarisation. Shorter whips, common on handheld devices, use loading coils at the base or centre to make them electrically resonant despite their reduced size, at the cost of bandwidth and efficiency; very short flexible "rubber ducky" antennas are helically wound whips.

Uses

Applications include handheld transceivers (walkie-talkies), cordless and mobile phones (before internal antennas became standard), Citizens Band and amateur radio, police and military manpack radios, automobile radio and emergency-service antennas, and marine VHF antennas mounted on boats. Long fibreglass whips are also used for HF communications on ships and vehicles.
