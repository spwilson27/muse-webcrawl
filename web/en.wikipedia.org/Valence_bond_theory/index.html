Valence bond theory

Overlap of atomic orbitals in the valence bond picture.

In chemistry, valence bond (VB) theory is one of the two basic theories, along with molecular orbital (MO) theory, that use quantum mechanics to explain chemical bonding. Valence bond theory states that overlaps between atomic orbitals of participating atoms form a chemical bond; an electron pair is shared in the region of overlap, and the bond strengthens as overlap increases.

History and concepts

The theory began with the Heitler–London treatment of the hydrogen molecule in 1927, the first quantum-mechanical calculation of a chemical bond. Linus Pauling generalised the approach in the 1930s, introducing orbital hybridisation (sp, sp², sp³) to explain molecular geometry and the concept of resonance, in which a molecule such as benzene is described as a hybrid of several contributing structures. VB theory naturally gives a localised picture of bonds and lone pairs, closely matching the Lewis structures chemists draw.

Comparison with molecular orbital theory

MO theory, which delocalises electrons over the whole molecule, more readily explains properties such as the paramagnetism of oxygen and electronic spectra, and has been computationally dominant; modern VB methods, however, have been revived with computer algorithms and remain valued for the chemical insight of their localised, structure-based descriptions.
