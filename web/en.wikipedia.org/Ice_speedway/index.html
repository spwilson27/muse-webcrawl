Ice speedway

Ice speedway is a form of motorcycle speedway racing held on frozen lakes or purpose-built ice stadiums. Riders race counter-clockwise around oval tracks of 260 to 425 metres on machines fitted with hundreds of sharp spikes — up to about 28 mm long — screwed into treadless tyres, giving extraordinary grip that allows riders to lean at extreme angles in the corners.

The bikes and racing

Ice speedway bikes are purpose-built 500 cc machines with two-speed gearboxes, no brakes, and rigid frames, run on methanol fuel. Riders wear extensive protective gear, and guards cover the spiked wheels. Races typically consist of four riders over four laps.

Championships

The sport is dominated historically by riders from Russia (and formerly the Soviet Union) and Sweden. The FIM Ice Racing World Championship has been contested as an individual Grand Prix series since the 1960s, along with a team world championship. Meetings are held in Russia, Scandinavia, Germany, the Netherlands and Kazakhstan.
