Thomas Bewick

Thomas Bewick (August 1753 – 8 November 1828) was an English wood-engraver and natural history author from Newcastle upon Tyne, credited with reviving wood engraving and popularising illustrated natural history.

Work

Bewick perfected engraving on the end grain of boxwood, allowing fine detail that could be printed alongside type. His masterpieces are A General History of Quadrupeds (1790) and the two-volume History of British Birds (1797–1804), whose small tail-piece vignettes of rural life are as celebrated as the bird portraits.

Legacy

Bewick's swan, the Bewick's swan, and the Bewick's wren are named for him. His workshop trained a generation of engravers, and Charlotte Brontë and John Ruskin were among his admirers; his Memoir, published posthumously in 1862, is a classic account of a craftsman's life.
