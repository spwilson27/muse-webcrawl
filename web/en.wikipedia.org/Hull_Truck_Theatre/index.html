Hull Truck Theatre is a producing theatre company and venue in Kingston upon Hull, East Riding of Yorkshire, England. Founded as a touring company in 1971, it is known for new writing rooted in northern working-class life, and since 2009 has been based in a purpose-built theatre on Ferensway in Hull city centre.

History

Actor Mike Bradwell founded the Hull Truck Theatre Company in 1971, naming it after the truck used to tour productions to community venues. The company developed a house style of ensemble-devised, music-filled plays. Playwright John Godber became artistic director in 1984 and defined the company for nearly three decades with popular hits such as Bouncers, Up 'n' Under, Teechers and April in Paris, many premiered at the company's Spring Street theatre, its home from 1983.

The Ferensway building

In 2009 Hull Truck moved to a new £14.5 million building on Ferensway, designed with a 437-seat main auditorium and a 135-seat studio. The company played a prominent role in Hull's year as UK City of Culture 2017. Subsequent artistic directors have included Mark Babych, with productions touring nationally and transferring to London.
