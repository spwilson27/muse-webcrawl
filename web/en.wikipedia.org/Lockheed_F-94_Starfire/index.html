Lockheed F-94 Starfire

The Lockheed F-94 Starfire is an American first-generation jet powered all-weather day/night interceptor. Developed from the two-seat T-33 Shooting Star trainer, it was the first production fighter equipped with an afterburner and the first jet-powered all-weather fighter to enter combat.

Development

Built for the United States Air Force in response to the appearance of Soviet bombers capable of reaching North America, the F-94 combined a radar-equipped nose — with a radar operator seated behind the pilot — and an armament of 12.7 mm machine guns and later unguided rockets. The F-94A entered service in 1950, followed by the improved B model and the extensively redesigned F-94C, renamed Starfire, with a thinner wing, swept tail, J48 engine and rocket-only armament in the nose and wing pods.

Service

Starfires served with Air Defense Command intercepting potential bomber threats, and operated in the Korean War from 1951, flying night interdiction and escort missions. The type was also flown by Air National Guard units before replacement by supersonic interceptors in the late 1950s; 855 were built.
