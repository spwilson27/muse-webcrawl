Vermilion Parish is a parish located in the U.S. state of Louisiana, on the Gulf of Mexico coast in the heart of Acadiana. Its seat and largest community is Abbeville. The parish was created in 1844 from part of Lafayette Parish and named for the Vermilion River and Vermilion Bay.

Coastal marsh and bayous in Vermilion Parish.

Geography and culture

The parish's low-lying landscape of bayous, marshes, and chenier ridges makes it both fertile and hurricane-prone; major storms such as Rita (2005) and Ike (2008) drove storm surges deep inland, and coastal erosion claims Louisiana wetlands at a nationally notable rate. Vermilion Parish is strongly Cajun in culture: many residents historically spoke Louisiana French, and traditions such as fais do-do dances, boucheries, and zydeco music persist. Abbeville is nicknamed a gateway to Cajun culture and hosts the Louisiana Cattle Festival.

Economy

Agriculture (rice, sugarcane, cattle), commercial fishing and shrimping, crawfish aquaculture, and oil and gas activity along the coast form the economy. Towns include Abbeville, Erath, Kaplan, Delcambre, and Gueydan — the latter famous as the "Duck Capital of America".
