The Commercial Crew Program (CCP) is a human spaceflight program operated by NASA in partnership with private aerospace companies, created to transport astronauts to and from the International Space Station (ISS). It succeeded the Space Shuttle program, which was retired in 2011, ending a period during which the United States depended on Russian Soyuz spacecraft for crewed access to the station.

A commercial crew vehicle docking with the ISS

Background

Following the retirement of the Space Shuttle, NASA sought to stimulate the development of commercial human spaceflight capabilities. Building on the Commercial Orbital Transportation Services (COTS) cargo program, NASA structured Commercial Crew as a series of funded Space Act Agreements followed by fixed-price service contracts, under which companies retained ownership of their vehicles while NASA purchased seats as a customer.

Development phases

The program progressed through several phases: CCDev (2010–2011), CCDev2 (2011–2012), and CCiCap (2012–2014), which funded competing designs from Boeing, SpaceX, and Sierra Nevada Corporation. In September 2014, NASA awarded Commercial Crew Transportation Capability (CCtCap) contracts to Boeing (for the CST-100 Starliner) and SpaceX (for Crew Dragon), worth up to $4.2 billion and $2.6 billion respectively.

Operational flights

SpaceX flew the uncrewed Demo-1 mission in March 2019 and the crewed Demo-2 mission in May 2020, carrying astronauts Doug Hurley and Bob Behnken — the first crewed orbital launch from US soil since 2011. Regular operational rotations (Crew-1 onward) began in November 2020. Boeing's Starliner completed an uncrewed orbital flight test in May 2022 and its first crewed flight test in June 2024, though propulsion issues led NASA to return that vehicle uncrewed, with its astronauts reassigned to a later Crew Dragon flight.

Significance

The program restored independent US crewed access to low Earth orbit, introduced reusable crewed spacecraft, and established a model of public–private partnership that NASA has extended to lunar landers under the Artemis program.
