Latvian mythology

Traditional Latvian symbols, including the sign of Saule

Latvian mythology is the collection of myths that have emerged throughout the history of Latvia, elaborated by successive generations and preserved in folk songs (dainas), legends, and seasonal festivals. It is closely related to Lithuanian mythology within Baltic mythology.

Deities

The central deity is Dievs, the sky god, alongside Saule, the sun goddess who carries the sun across the sky, and Mēness, the moon god. Laima is the goddess of fate and childbirth, while Pērkons is the god of thunder. Māra is an important mother goddess associated with the earth, fertility, and the care of cattle.

Folklore and festivals

The midsummer festival of Jāņi, celebrating the summer solstice with bonfires, songs, and oak-leaf wreaths, is the most popular Latvian holiday. Hundreds of thousands of short folk quatrains called dainas record mythological themes, particularly the celestial wedding and the journey of the sun.

Survival and revival

Because Latvia was Christianized relatively late, in the 13th century, many pagan customs survived in folk practice. In the 20th century the Dievturi movement revived the pre-Christian Latvian religion as an organized faith.
