Himeji Castle

Himeji Castle
Location: Himeji, Hyogo Prefecture, Japan
Built: 1601–1609 (present form)
Status: National Treasure; UNESCO World Heritage Site (1993)

Himeji Castle is a hilltop Japanese castle complex in the city of Himeji, Hyogo Prefecture. Nicknamed "White Heron Castle" (Shirasagi-jō) for its brilliant white plastered exterior, it is regarded as the finest surviving example of early seventeenth-century Japanese castle architecture and comprises 83 buildings with advanced defensive systems.

History

A fort existed on the site from the fourteenth century. The castle reached its present form under the daimyo Ikeda Terumasa between 1601 and 1609, following the Battle of Sekigahara. Remarkably, Himeji was never attacked: it survived the abolition of the feudal system, an 1871 auction for demolition that was averted, the bombing of Himeji in the Second World War, and the 1995 Kobe earthquake, largely intact.

Architecture

The six-storey main keep (tenshu) is linked to three smaller keeps and surrounded by a labyrinth of moats, walls, gates and twisting paths designed to confuse attackers, with loopholes and stone-dropping chutes for defence. Designated a National Treasure of Japan, the castle was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993, and a major restoration completed in 2015 returned the roofs and walls to their bright white finish.
