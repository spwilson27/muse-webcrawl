Treasure Island

Treasure Island is an adventure and historical novel by the Scottish author Robert Louis Stevenson. It was first serialised in the children's magazine Young Folks in 1881–1882 under the title The Sea Cook, and published in book form in 1883. The story of "buccaneers and buried gold" is a coming-of-age tale that has had a decisive influence on popular perceptions of pirates, including treasure maps marked with an X, schooners, the Black Spot, tropical islands and one-legged seamen bearing parrots on their shoulders.

Plot

The narrator, Jim Hawkins, is a boy whose parents run the Admiral Benbow Inn on the English coast. A mysterious sailor, Billy Bones, dies at the inn, leaving behind a map showing the location of the buried treasure of the pirate Captain Flint. Jim joins Squire Trelawney, Dr Livesey and Captain Smollett on the schooner Hispaniola to seek the treasure. The ship's cook, Long John Silver, is secretly the leader of a gang of Flint's former crew who plan a mutiny. Jim overhears the plot, and after landfall on the island, battles, shifting alliances and the help of the marooned Ben Gunn, the loyal party recovers most of the treasure. Silver escapes with a portion before the ship returns to England.

Background and influence

Stevenson began the book during a rainy holiday in Braemar, Scotland, drawing a map of an imaginary island that became the story's foundation. The novel was his first major success. Its characters and motifs have been adapted in dozens of films, plays and sequels, and Long John Silver remains the archetypal fictional pirate.
