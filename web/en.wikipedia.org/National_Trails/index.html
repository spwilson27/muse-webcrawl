National Trails

National Trails is the collective name for the series of long-distance walking and riding routes in England and Wales managed as a national network. This overview article summarises the system; the companion article National Trail describes the designation and standards in detail.

Overview

There are sixteen established National Trails, each passing through some of the finest landscapes in England and Wales, including national parks and Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The newest addition, the England Coast Path, is being opened in sections and will connect the entire English coastline — around 2,700 miles — into a single route, much of it under the coastal access provisions of the Marine and Coastal Access Act 2009.

Management

Each trail has a dedicated trail officer or partnership, funded largely by Natural England or Natural Resources Wales, responsible for promotion, maintenance standards and liaison with the highway authorities that maintain the underlying public rights of way. The trails are used by an estimated tens of millions of visitors annually and contribute significantly to rural tourism economies.

Related systems

Scotland has its own network of Scotland's Great Trails, and the concept is echoed internationally by systems such as the U.S. National Trails System — including the Appalachian and Pacific Crest trails — established under the National Trails System Act of 1968.
