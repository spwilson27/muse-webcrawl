Laplace's equation

Laplace's equation is a second-order partial differential equation named after Pierre-Simon Laplace, written ∇²f = 0, where ∇² is the Laplace operator. Solutions, called harmonic functions, are smooth, satisfy the mean value property, and attain maxima and minima only on the boundary of their domain.

Applications

The equation arises in electrostatics (electric potential in charge-free regions), gravitation, fluid dynamics (irrotational flow), heat conduction at steady state, and the theory of analytic functions in complex analysis, where real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic function are harmonic.

Boundary value problems

Typical problems specify f on the boundary (Dirichlet problem) or its normal derivative (Neumann problem). Green's functions, separation of variables, and conformal mapping are standard solution methods.
