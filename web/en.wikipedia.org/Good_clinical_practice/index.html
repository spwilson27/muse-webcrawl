Good clinical practice


A clinical trial conducted under GCP.

Good clinical practice (GCP) is an international quality standard for the design, conduct, performance, monitoring, auditing, recording, analysis and reporting of clinical trials involving human participants. It provides assurance that the rights, safety and well-being of trial subjects are protected and that the data generated are credible.

ICH guideline

The most influential formulation is the guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), ICH E6, first adopted in 1996. It sets out 13 core principles, including that trials be conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, that foreseeable risks be weighed against anticipated benefits, that participants give freely informed consent, and that trials be scientifically sound and described in a clear protocol.

Roles and requirements

GCP defines the responsibilities of sponsors, investigators, monitors and ethics committees (institutional review boards). It requires source documentation and case report forms, essential documents for audit trails, standard operating procedures, trial registries of investigational product accountability, and reporting of serious adverse events. Regulators such as the FDA and EMA require GCP compliance for trials supporting marketing applications; inspections can issue findings that jeopardise a product's approval.

Related standards

GCP is one of a family of "GxP" quality standards, alongside Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) and Good Laboratory Practice (GLP).
