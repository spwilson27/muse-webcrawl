Durdle Door (sometimes written Durdle Dor) is a natural limestone arch on the Jurassic Coast near Lulworth in Dorset, England. It is one of the most photographed and iconic landmarks of the British coast. The name "Durdle" is thought to derive from an Old English word meaning "bore" or "drill".

Geology

The arch formed where the sea eroded a band of hard Portland limestone that runs along this coast. Behind the resistant limestone lay softer clays and sands, which the sea cut away to form Lulworth Cove and the bays around the arch. Erosion first opened a crack and cave through the narrow limestone ridge and then the roof collapsed, leaving the free-standing arch; continued erosion will one day topple it into a stump like those offshore nearby.

Tourism and culture

Durdle Door lies on the South West Coast Path and is privately owned by the Lulworth Estate but open to the public. The nearby shingle beach is popular for swimming, although swimming through the arch is hazardous. The arch has appeared in films, music videos and television programmes, and on book covers, and the surrounding coastline forms part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed for its geological importance.
