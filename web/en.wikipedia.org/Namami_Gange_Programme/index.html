Namami Gange Programme

Namami Gange is a flagship integrated conservation programme of the Government of India, launched in June 2014 with an initial budget outlay of ₹20,000 crore, to clean and rejuvenate the River Ganga (Ganges) and its tributaries. It is implemented by the National Mission for Clean Ganga (NMCG) under the Ministry of Jal Shakti.

Objectives

The programme's twin objectives are the effective abatement of pollution in the Ganga and the conservation and rejuvenation of the river ecosystem. Its interventions include building and upgrading sewage treatment plants in towns along the river, riverfront development, cleaning of the river surface, afforestation, biodiversity conservation (including rescue of the Ganges river dolphin), industrial effluent monitoring, and public-awareness campaigns in the five main-stem states: Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand and West Bengal.

Background

Namami Gange succeeded the earlier Ganga Action Plan (1985) and is supported in part by international lenders including the World Bank. A second phase, Namami Gange Mission-II, extended the programme's horizon, and monitoring studies have reported improvements in some water-quality indicators alongside continuing challenges from sewage and industrial discharge.
