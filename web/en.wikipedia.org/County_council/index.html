A county council is the elected governing body of a county in local government systems derived from the British model, including in England, Wales, and Ireland. County councils typically handle the larger-scale services of an area, such as education, social services, transport, and fire and rescue.

England

Elected county councils were created across England and Wales by the Local Government Act 1888 and took office in 1889, replacing the unelected quarter sessions of magistrates. In two-tier areas, county councils form the upper tier above district councils; their responsibilities include schools, children's and adult social care, highways, waste disposal, libraries, and trading standards. Successive reforms have replaced many with unitary authorities.

Ireland

In Ireland, county councils are the principal local authority for each county (outside Dublin's four-authority arrangement) and are responsible for housing, roads, planning, environmental services, and community development. A chief executive manages day-to-day operations while elected councillors set policy and budgets.

Other countries

Similar bodies exist elsewhere: Sweden's regional councils (historically county councils) run healthcare, Norway's county municipalities oversee upper-secondary education and transport, and various US states have county councils or legislatures as part of county government.
