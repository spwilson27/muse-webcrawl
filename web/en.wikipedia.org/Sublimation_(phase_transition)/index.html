Sublimation (phase transition)

Sublimation
TransitionSolid → gas (without liquid phase)
Reverse processDeposition
Common examplesDry ice, iodine, naphthalene, snow in sunlight

Sublimation is the transition of a substance directly from the solid to the gas state, without passing through the liquid state. It is an endothermic process that occurs at temperatures and pressures below a substance's triple point in its phase diagram, which corresponds to the lowest pressure at which the substance can exist as a liquid.

The best-known substance that sublimes at ordinary pressures is solid carbon dioxide ("dry ice"), which passes directly into gas at −78.5 °C at atmospheric pressure. Iodine, naphthalene (mothballs), camphor, and solid air fresheners also sublime appreciably at room temperature. Snow and ice sublime slowly even below freezing, which is one reason old snowfields shrink in dry, sunny, windy conditions without melting.

Applications

  • Freeze-drying (lyophilisation): water is removed from frozen food and pharmaceuticals by sublimation under vacuum, preserving structure and flavour.
  • Dye-sublimation printing: solid dyes are vaporised onto fabrics and coated materials.
  • Purification: volatile solids such as caffeine can be purified by sublimation and re-condensation.
  • Forensics: iodine fuming develops latent fingerprints on paper.

Deposition

The reverse process, in which a gas transforms directly into a solid, is called deposition or desublimation. Frost forming on a cold window and the growth of snowflakes from water vapour are everyday examples.
