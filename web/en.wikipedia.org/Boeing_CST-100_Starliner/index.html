Boeing CST-100 Starliner

The Boeing CST-100 Starliner is a class of partially reusable spacecraft designed to transport crew to the International Space Station (ISS) and other low-Earth-orbit destinations. It is manufactured by Boeing, with the Commercial Crew Program of NASA as the lead customer. The capsule has a diameter of 4.56 m (15 ft) and can carry up to seven astronauts, though NASA missions typically fly four.

Development

Boeing developed Starliner under NASA's Commercial Crew Development and Commercial Crew Transportation Capability contracts, receiving a contract in 2014 alongside SpaceX's Crew Dragon. The spacecraft launches on a United Launch Alliance Atlas V rocket from Cape Canaveral, lands on solid ground in the western United States using parachutes and airbags, and is designed for reuse up to ten times.

Test flights

Starliner's first uncrewed Orbital Flight Test in December 2019 failed to reach the ISS due to a mission elapsed timer error, though the capsule landed safely. A second uncrewed test in May 2022 docked successfully. The first crewed flight, launched in June 2024 with astronauts Barry Wilmore and Sunita Williams, experienced helium leaks and thruster failures; NASA ultimately returned the capsule uncrewed in September 2024, and the astronauts remained aboard the ISS until returning on a SpaceX vehicle in March 2025. The program's resulting delays and cost overruns — Boeing recorded substantial losses on the fixed-price contract — made Starliner a prominent example of the challenges of commercial crew development.
