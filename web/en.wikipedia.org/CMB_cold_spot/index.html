CMB cold spot

The CMB cold spot or WMAP cold spot is a region of the sky, in the constellation Eridanus, where the cosmic microwave background (CMB) is unusually cold relative to the expected Gaussian fluctuations of the standard cosmological model.

Discovery

The spot, roughly 5–10 degrees across, was identified in 2004 in data from the Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP) and later confirmed by the Planck satellite. Its temperature is about 70 µK colder than the average CMB temperature deviation in the region.

Explanations

Proposed explanations include a statistical fluke or foreground effect, the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect of a huge supervoid along the line of sight (the Eridanus Supervoid), and more exotic ideas such as a cosmic texture or a collision with another universe in a multiverse scenario. No explanation is conclusively established.
