The Tour Down Under is a road cycling race held annually in January in and around Adelaide, South Australia. First staged in 1999, it is the opening event of the UCI World Tour and the first top-level race of the professional season, traditionally drawing the world's leading teams out of the northern-hemisphere winter into Australian summer heat.

Race character

The race is a short stage race, usually of six stages, based entirely within reach of Adelaide — an unusual logistical arrangement in which the whole caravan returns to the same hotels each night. Stages visit the Barossa Valley, the McLaren Vale wine region and the Adelaide Hills; the signature climb of Willunga Hill, tackled twice on the penultimate stage, often decides the general classification. The overall leader wears the ochre jersey, its colour evoking the Australian outback. A city-centre criterium, the Down Under Classic, traditionally precedes the race.

History

The Tour Down Under was created partly to fill the sporting void left when the Australian Formula One Grand Prix moved from Adelaide to Melbourne. It grew rapidly: in 2008 it became the first race outside Europe admitted to the UCI ProTour (now World Tour), and Lance Armstrong's comeback appearances in 2009–11 drew enormous crowds. Australian Simon Gerrans holds the record with four overall victories; other winners include André Greipel-era sprinters, Stuart O'Grady, Richie Porte, Rohan Dennis and Daryl Impey. A women's Tour Down Under has been held alongside the men's race and joined the Women's World Tour in 2023.
