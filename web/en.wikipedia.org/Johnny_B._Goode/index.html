Johnny B. Goode

"Johnny B. Goode" is a 1958 rock and roll song written and first recorded by Chuck Berry. Released as a single, it peaked at number two on Billboard magazine's Hot R&B Sides chart and number eight on its Hot 100 chart. The song is one of Berry's most famous recordings and one of the most recognizable songs in popular music, renowned for its guitar opening riff.

Content and composition

The lyrics tell of a poor country boy from near New Orleans who plays guitar "just like ringing a bell" and dreams of fame. The song is partly autobiographical: Berry was born on Goode Avenue in St. Louis, though he changed "colored boy" to "country boy" for wider radio appeal. Its famous opening riff was adapted from Louis Jordan's "Ain't That Just Like a Woman" (1946), played by guitarist Carl Hogan.

Legacy

"Johnny B. Goode" is the only rock and roll song on the Voyager Golden Record, sent into interstellar space aboard the Voyager probes in 1977. It was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and ranked among the greatest guitar songs of all time. A performance in the 1985 film Back to the Future introduced it to a new generation.
