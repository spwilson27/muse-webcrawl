Swimming (sport)

Swimming is an individual or team racing sport that requires the use of the entire body to move through water. The sport takes place in pools or open water. Competitive swimming is one of the most popular Olympic sports, with events in freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke, and butterfly over distances from 50 metres to 1500 metres, as well as medley races.

History

Swimming races were held in Britain in the 1830s, and the sport appeared at the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896 (in open water); women's events were added in 1912. The international governing body, founded in 1908, is now known as World Aquatics.

Competition

Most competitive pools are 25 metres (short course) or 50 metres (long course, "Olympic-size"). Races begin with a dive from starting blocks (except backstroke, which starts in the water), and strict rules govern strokes, turns, and finishes. Major competitions include the Olympic Games, the World Aquatics Championships, and continental and national championships.

Technique and training

Swimmers train for speed and endurance, refining stroke mechanics, starts, turns, and underwater dolphin kicks. Advances in swimsuit technology led to restrictions after 2009 on full-body polyurethane suits, which had contributed to a flood of world records.
