"Song of Myself" is a poem by Walt Whitman (1819–1892), first published in the 1855 edition of his collection Leaves of Grass. It is considered the core of Whitman's poetic achievement and one of the most influential poems in American literature.

Composition and publication

The poem appeared untitled in 1855, was called "Poem of Walt Whitman, an American" in 1856, then "Walt Whitman" in 1860, and only received its final title in the 1881 edition. Whitman revised it throughout his life. It consists of 52 numbered sections comprising 1,336 lines, written in free verse with long, cataloguing lines.

Themes

The poem celebrates the self as both individual and universal: "I am large, I contain multitudes." The speaker identifies with all people — slave and master, woman and man, the living and the dead — and with the natural world, most famously in the symbol of grass, "the flag of my disposition". Other major themes include democracy, equality, the unity of body and soul, death as part of life's cycle, and pantheistic spirituality.

Reception

Initially controversial for its frank treatment of the body and its break with meter and rhyme, the poem came to be seen as a founding text of American poetry, influencing generations of poets from the Beats onward.
