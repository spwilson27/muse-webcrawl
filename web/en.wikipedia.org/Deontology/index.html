Deontology

Deontology is the normative ethical theory that the morality of an action should be based on whether the action itself is right or wrong under a series of rules and principles, rather than on its consequences.

Kantian deontology

Immanuel Kant argued that rational beings must act according to maxims they could will to become universal laws — the categorical imperative — and must treat humanity always as an end, never merely as a means.

Contrast

Deontology contrasts with consequentialism, which judges actions by outcomes, and with virtue ethics, which focuses on character. Duties, rights and obligations are central concepts; W. D. Ross developed a pluralist version based on prima facie duties.
