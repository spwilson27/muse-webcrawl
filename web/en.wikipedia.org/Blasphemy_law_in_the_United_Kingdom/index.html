Blasphemy law in the United Kingdom historically punished blasphemy against Christianity through the common-law offences of blasphemy and blasphemous libel. The offences protected the doctrines of the Church of England and were progressively narrowed before abolition.

History

The common-law offence developed in the 17th century. The last successful prosecution for most of the 20th century's span was in 1922, until the famous 1977 private prosecution Whitehouse v. Lemon, in which Gay News editor Denis Lemon was convicted of blasphemous libel for publishing James Kirkup's poem about a Roman centurion's love for Christ. The Law Commission recommended abolition in 1985.

Abolition

The offences of blasphemy and blasphemous libel were abolished in England and Wales by the Criminal Justice and Immigration Act 2008. Scotland abolished its common-law offence through the Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021. Related conduct may still be prosecuted under public order and religiously aggravated offences legislation.
