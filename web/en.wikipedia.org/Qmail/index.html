qmail

qmail is a mail transfer agent (MTA) that runs on Unix. It was written, starting December 1995, by Daniel J. Bernstein as a more secure alternative to the popular Sendmail program. Originally licensed as all-rights-reserved freeware, qmail was released to the public domain in November 2007.

Design

qmail is structured as a set of small, specialised programs that communicate with each other, each running with the minimum privileges required, in contrast to the monolithic design of Sendmail. Bernstein offered a reward to anyone who could find a verifiable security hole in the code, which went unclaimed for many years, and qmail became noted for its security record. The system uses the Maildir format for storing mail, designed to avoid locking problems and corruption, as well as the Quick Mail Transport Protocol (QMTP) and QMQP protocols for faster transfers between qmail servers.

Features and legacy

qmail supports virtual domains, user-controlled email addresses of the form user-extension, mailing lists through the related ezmlm software, and extensive anti-spam hooks, though its strict adherence to standards and its patched-extension ecosystem generated long-running debates about interoperability. Because the original source long could not be distributed in modified form, many users relied on large patch sets, and a community fork called netqmail consolidated the most common patches. Bernstein placed qmail in the public domain in 2007. Although its popularity has declined relative to Postfix and Exim, qmail influenced the design of later mail servers and remains in use on many systems.
