Campus radio

Campus radio (also known as college radio, university radio or student radio) is a type of radio station that is run by the students of a college, university or other educational institution. Stations may be operated for the purpose of training professional radio personnel, or as a voice of alternative programming that is not available on commercial stations.

Campus stations are usually licensed and regulated by national broadcasting authorities, and many operate on low-power FM frequencies, cable or carrier-current systems, or by internet streaming.

Concept

Campus radio stations are generally non-commercial and volunteer-run. Programming typically reflects the tastes and interests of students: independent and local music, campus news, sports coverage, talk programmes and specialist shows that commercial broadcasters would consider unviable. Because they are free of commercial pressure, college stations have historically been important outlets for new and experimental music, and in North America the term "college rock" emerged in the 1980s to describe the alternative music championed by such stations.

History

Some of the earliest radio stations in the United States were founded at universities in the 1910s and 1920s, when academic institutions experimented with wireless technology. Stations such as WHA at the University of Wisconsin grew out of physics-department experiments. In the United Kingdom, student radio began in the 1960s with stations such as University Radio York (1968), the first independent radio station in the country. In 1949 the Intercollegiate Broadcasting System was founded in the US to support college stations.

Around the world

United States and Canada: hundreds of colleges hold non-commercial educational FM licences, coordinated loosely through bodies such as College Broadcasters, Inc. and Canada's National Campus and Community Radio Association.

United Kingdom: student stations are represented by the Student Radio Association and broadcast on FM under restricted service licences, on AM, or online.

Australia: student stations such as SYN and Radio Fodder broadcast under community radio arrangements.

India: campus community radio stations licensed since the mid-2000s serve universities and their surrounding localities.

Internet era

Many campus stations migrated to or added online streaming in the 2000s, lowering costs and removing geographic limits on their audience, while podcasts and on-demand archives have extended the life of student programming. Some long-running stations have alumni who went on to prominent broadcasting careers; the training function remains a central purpose of the sector.
