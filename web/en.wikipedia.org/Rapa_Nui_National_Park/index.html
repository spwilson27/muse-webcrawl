Rapa Nui National Park

Rapa Nui National Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Rapa Nui) is a national park and UNESCO World Heritage Site located on Easter Island, Chile. It protects the island's celebrated archaeological heritage, including its moai statues, ahu (ceremonial platforms), rock art, and caves.

History

The park was created in 1935 by the Chilean government, and the island was declared a national monument the same year. In 1995 UNESCO designated the park a World Heritage Site under cultural criteria. In 2017 administration of the park was transferred to the Ma'u Henua Polynesian Indigenous Community, the first time an indigenous community had taken over management of a Chilean national park.

Features

The park covers around 7,130 hectares, about 44% of the island. Major sites include Rano Raraku, the quarry where most moai were carved; Ahu Tongariki, the largest ahu, with fifteen standing moai; Anakena beach, a traditional landing site of the island's first settlers; and the ceremonial village of Orongo at the rim of the Rano Kau crater, centre of the tangata manu (birdman) cult.
