Roman navy

The Roman navy (Latin: Classis) comprised the naval forces of ancient Rome, from the improvised fleets of the Punic Wars to the permanent standing fleets of the Empire that policed the Mediterranean, the Channel, the Rhine and Danube frontiers.

History

Rome built its first large war fleet in 260 BC during the First Punic War, copying a wrecked Carthaginian quinquereme and adding the corvus boarding bridge, which won the Battle of Mylae. Sextus Pompey and later Octavian fought major naval campaigns in the civil wars, culminating in the Battle of Actium (31 BC).

Imperial fleets

Augustus established permanent fleets at Misenum and Ravenna in Italy, with provincial squadrons at Alexandria, Syria, Britain (Classis Britannica) and on the Rhine and Danube. Ships ranged from large quinqueremes to swift Liburnian biremes. Naval personnel, mostly non-citizen peregrini, earned citizenship after 26 years of service. The navy suppressed piracy, transported legions and guarded grain shipments to Rome.
