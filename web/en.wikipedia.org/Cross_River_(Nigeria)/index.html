Cross River (Nigeria)

The Cross River (native name: Oyono) is the main river in southeastern Nigeria, giving its name to Cross River State. It rises in the Cameroon mountain range near the border with Cameroon, flows southwest through dense rainforest, and empties into the Atlantic Ocean through a large estuary shared with the Calabar River. Together with its tributaries, including the Manyu River from Cameroon, it drains a basin rich in tropical forest.

Course and estuary

The river's lower course forms a broad estuary — the Cross River estuary — fringed by mangrove swamps and tidal creeks, and navigable inland to Ikom, where rapids interrupt navigation. The colonial-era port of Calabar, at the junction with the Calabar River, was a major centre of the palm-oil trade and, earlier, the transatlantic slave trade.

Ecology

The basin contains some of the last remaining tropical rainforest in Nigeria, protected in part by Cross River National Park. It is a biodiversity hotspot home to the critically endangered Cross River gorilla, forest elephants, and numerous endemic fish and primate species. The estuary supports important artisanal fisheries and mangrove ecosystems.
