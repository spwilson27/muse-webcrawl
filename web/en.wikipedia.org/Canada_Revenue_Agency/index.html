Canada Revenue Agency

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA; French: Agence du revenu du Canada) is the revenue service of the Canadian federal government, and most provincial and territorial governments. The CRA collects taxes, administers tax law and policy, and delivers benefit programs and tax credits for the Government of Canada and for most provinces and territories.

History

The agency was created in 1999 as the Canada Customs and Revenue Agency (CCRA), combining the former Department of National Revenue with customs functions. In December 2003, the customs component was transferred to the newly created Canada Border Services Agency, and the CCRA became the Canada Revenue Agency.

Responsibilities

The CRA administers the Income Tax Act, the Excise Tax Act (including the goods and services tax / harmonized sales tax), and various benefit programs such as the Canada Child Benefit and the GST/HST credit. It also collects corporate and personal income taxes on behalf of most provinces and territories; Quebec administers its own taxes through Revenu Québec, and Alberta administers its own corporate income tax.

Organization

The agency is headed by a Commissioner of Revenue and Chief Executive Officer and is overseen by a Board of Management established in 1999, a governance model intended to give the agency greater administrative flexibility than a conventional government department.
