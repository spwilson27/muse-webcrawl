Anticyclone

An anticyclone is a large-scale circulation of winds around a central region of high atmospheric pressure, clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and counterclockwise in the Southern Hemisphere as viewed from above. Anticyclones are the opposite of cyclones and are usually associated with settled, fair weather.

Formation and characteristics

Anticyclones form where air descends (subsides), warming as it sinks and suppressing cloud formation. Surface winds spiral gently outward from the high-pressure centre. The semi-permanent subtropical highs, such as the Azores High and the Pacific High, are large anticyclones that shape the trade winds and the location of deserts. Blocking highs can persist for weeks, bringing heat waves and drought in summer and severe cold in winter.

Weather effects

Because air sinks rather than rises, anticyclones typically bring light winds, clear skies, and little precipitation. In summer they produce warm, sunny weather; in winter, cold bright days with frost and fog, or in polluted regions, temperature inversions that trap smog near the ground.
