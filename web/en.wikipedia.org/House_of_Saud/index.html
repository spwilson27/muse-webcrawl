House of Saud

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The House of Saud (Arabic: Āl Saʻūd) is the ruling royal family of Saudi Arabia. It descends from Muhammad bin Saud, the 18th-century ruler of Diriyah, and comprises many thousands of members, of whom a few hundred exercise political influence. Saudi Arabia is named after the family — the only modern state named for its ruling house.

Founded: 1727 (Diriyah); 1744 (First Saudi State)
Founder: Muhammad bin Saud
Current head: King Salman
Titles: King of Saudi Arabia; Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques

History

In 1744 Muhammad bin Saud formed an alliance with the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, creating the First Saudi State (Emirate of Diriyah), which the Ottomans destroyed in 1818. A Second Saudi State (Emirate of Nejd) lasted from 1824 to 1891, when the rival Rashidi family drove the Saudis into exile in Kuwait. Abdulaziz ibn Saud recaptured Riyadh in 1902, conquered the Hejaz in 1924–25, and in 1932 proclaimed the unified Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

Succession and government

All Saudi kings since Abdulaziz have been his sons: Saud, Faisal, Khalid, Fahd, Abdullah and Salman. The crown prince is chosen by the king, with the Allegiance Council (established 2006) formalising family consultation. The family’s extensive patronage networks and control of key ministries make it the central institution of the Saudi state.
