Donald Maclean

Donald Duart Maclean
Born: 25 May 1913, London, England
Died: 6 March 1983, Moscow, Soviet Union
Occupation: Diplomat, spy
Known for: Member of the Cambridge Five

Donald Duart Maclean (1913–1983) was a British diplomat and member of the Cambridge Five, a ring of spies who passed information to the Soviet Union during the Second World War and the early Cold War. Recruited by Soviet intelligence while at Cambridge in the 1930s, he transmitted large quantities of secret material from postings in London, Washington, and Cairo.

Espionage career

As first secretary at the British Embassy in Washington (1944–1948), Maclean had access to atomic energy information and passed details of Anglo-American policy to Moscow. In May 1951, under imminent suspicion, he fled to the Soviet Union together with fellow spy Guy Burgess, an event that caused a major scandal in Britain.

Life in the USSR

Maclean lived in Moscow under an assumed identity, worked at the Institute of World Economy and International Relations, and wrote on British foreign policy. He died in Moscow in 1983.
