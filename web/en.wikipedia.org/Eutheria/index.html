KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
ClassMammalia
CladeEutheria (Huxley, 1880)

Eutheria is a clade of mammals consisting of the placental mammals and all extinct mammals more closely related to placentals than to marsupials. The name, meaning "true beasts", was introduced by Thomas Henry Huxley in 1880.

Characteristics

Eutherians are distinguished from other mammals by various features of the feet, ankles, jaws and teeth, and by the absence of epipubic bones. In living placentals, the young develop within the uterus, nourished through a placenta, and are born at a relatively advanced stage of development compared with marsupials.

Evolution

The earliest known eutherian is Juramaia, from the Late Jurassic of China, about 161 million years ago. Molecular clock studies suggest that the crown group Placentalia diversified around the Cretaceous–Palaeogene boundary, although the timing of the placental radiation remains debated between fossil and molecular evidence.

Classification

Living eutherians, the placental mammals, comprise more than 5,000 species grouped into orders including Primates, Rodentia, Chiroptera, Carnivora and Artiodactyla, organised into the superorders Xenarthra, Afrotheria, Euarchontoglires and Laurasiatheria.
