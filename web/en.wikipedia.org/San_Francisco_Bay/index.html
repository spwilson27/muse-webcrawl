San Francisco Bay

San Francisco Bay is a large tidal estuary in the U.S. state of California, opening to the Pacific Ocean through the Golden Gate strait. It is surrounded by the San Francisco Bay Area, home to more than seven million people and the cities of San Francisco, Oakland and San Jose.

Geography

The bay receives water from the Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers, which drain California's Central Valley through the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta and Suisun Bay. It consists of several connected bays and embayments, including San Pablo Bay to the north and the South Bay, and contains islands such as Alcatraz, Angel Island, Treasure Island and Yerba Buena. Despite its size — roughly 1,040 to 4,160 square kilometres depending on which sub-bays are counted — the bay is relatively shallow, averaging only a few metres deep, with a deeper shipping channel maintained by dredging.

Crossings

Eight major bridges span the bay and its arms, including the Golden Gate Bridge, the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge, the San Mateo–Hayward Bridge, the Dumbarton Bridge and the Richmond–San Rafael Bridge.

Ecology

The bay's wetlands, much reduced by filling and diking since the 19th century, are critical habitat for migratory birds on the Pacific Flyway. Salt evaporation ponds in the South Bay are famous for their vivid colours produced by micro-organisms. Conservation efforts since the 1960s, led by groups such as Save The Bay, have halted most bay fill and begun restoring marshes.
