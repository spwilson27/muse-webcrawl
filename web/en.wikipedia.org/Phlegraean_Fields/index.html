Phlegraean Fields

The Phlegraean Fields (Italian: Campi Flegrei, "burning fields") is a large volcanic caldera situated west of Naples, Italy. The area comprises numerous craters and volcanic edifices, most of them under the Gulf of Pozzuoli. It is a restless, actively monitored volcanic system and home to hundreds of thousands of people.

Geology

The caldera formed in stages, principally through the Campanian Ignimbrite eruption about 39,000 years ago — one of the largest eruptions in Europe in the last 200,000 years — and the Neapolitan Yellow Tuff eruption about 15,000 years ago. Within the caldera lie the Solfatara crater, famous for its fumaroles, Lake Avernus, and the town of Pozzuoli, long noted for bradyseism: slow uplift and subsidence of the ground that raised the coastline in 1982–84 by nearly two metres and prompted the evacuation of 40,000 people.

History and mythology

In Greek and Roman antiquity the area was associated with the underworld: Lake Avernus was an entrance to Hades, and the Cumaean Sibyl prophesied nearby. The last eruption, in 1538, created the cone of Monte Nuovo in about a week. Since 2012 the alert level has been "yellow" amid ongoing ground deformation and seismic swarms closely monitored by the Vesuvius Observatory.
