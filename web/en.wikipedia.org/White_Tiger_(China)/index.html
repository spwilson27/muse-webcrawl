White Tiger (China)

The White Tiger (Chinese: 白虎, Bái Hǔ) is one of the Four Symbols of the Chinese constellations, representing the west and the autumn season. It is one of four great mythological creatures in Chinese astronomy and mythology, alongside the Azure Dragon of the east, the Vermilion Bird of the south, and the Black Tortoise of the north.

The White Tiger, symbol of the west in Chinese astronomy

Astronomy

The White Tiger governs the seven lunar mansions (xiu) of the western quarter of the sky, including Kui, Lou, Wei, Mao (the Pleiades), Bi, Zi and Shen. Each of the Four Symbols was assigned seven of the twenty-eight lunar mansions, dividing the celestial sphere for calendrical and astrological purposes.

Symbolism

In Chinese thought the White Tiger is associated with metal among the five elements, with autumn, and with righteousness and courage. In feng shui, the White Tiger represents the right-hand side (west) of a site, which should ideally be lower than the Azure Dragon side. The tiger was a popular motif on tomb murals of the Han dynasty, where the Four Symbols guarded the four walls.

In other cultures

The symbol spread with Chinese influence: in Japan it is known as Byakko, in Korea as Baekho, and in Vietnam as Bạch Hổ. In Korean tradition, the White Tiger is connected with the western mountains and depicted in Goguryeo tomb murals.
