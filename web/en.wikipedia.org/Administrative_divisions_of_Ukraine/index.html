Administrative divisions of Ukraine

The administrative divisions of Ukraine organise the unitary state into several levels of subnational entities. At the first level there are 27 regions: 24 oblasts (provinces), one autonomous republic (the Autonomous Republic of Crimea) and two cities with special status, Kyiv and Sevastopol. Since the decentralisation reform culminating in 2020, the second level consists of raions (districts), and the third level of hromadas (municipalities).

First level: oblasts

Oblasts are named (with a few exceptions) after their administrative centres. Each oblast has a state administration whose head is appointed by the President, alongside an elected oblast council. The Autonomous Republic of Crimea has its own constitution and parliament, although since 2014 Crimea has been administered by Russia following its annexation, which Ukraine and most of the international community do not recognise; parts of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts likewise came under Russian occupation after 2022.

Second level: raions

The reform of 17 July 2020 consolidated Ukraine's 490 raions into 136 larger raions (140 counting those in Crimea), each with an elected council. Raions are subdivided into hromadas.

Hromadas — urban, settlement and rural amalgamated territorial communities — are the basic unit of local self-government, responsible for primary education, primary healthcare, utilities and local roads, financed partly by retained local taxes under the decentralisation programme.
