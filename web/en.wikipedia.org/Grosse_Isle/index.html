Grosse Isle

The Celtic cross on Grosse Isle commemorates the Irish emigrants who died there in 1847.

Grosse Isle (French: Grosse Île) is an island in the St. Lawrence River in Quebec, Canada, about 46 km downstream from Quebec City. From 1832 to 1937 it served as a quarantine station for immigrants arriving at the port of Quebec, and it is remembered above all for the tragedy of 1847, when thousands of Irish emigrants fleeing the Great Famine died of typhus on the island. It is now a National Historic Site of Canada, administered by Parks Canada.

Quarantine station

The station was established in 1832 in response to a cholera epidemic, since ships bound for Quebec were required to stop for inspection. Facilities grew to include hospitals ("fever sheds"), a disinfection building, churches and cemeteries.

The summer of 1847

In 1847, the peak year of Irish emigration during the Great Famine, over 100,000 emigrants sailed for Canada, many on overcrowded "coffin ships". Typhus spread aboard; at Grosse Isle the station was overwhelmed, with dozens of ships queued in the river and the dead buried in mass graves. At least 5,424 people were buried on the island in 1847 — the largest mass grave in Canada — and thousands more died at sea or after landing. Doctors, nurses, priests, nuns and the station's staff also died, among them the mayor of Montreal, John Easton Mills, who volunteered to nurse the sick. A large Celtic cross, erected in 1909 by the Ancient Order of Hibernians, stands as a memorial, and the names of the dead are recorded at the site.

Later history and heritage

The station operated until 1937, later also used for prisoner-of-war and military purposes. Declared a national historic site in 1974 and developed for visitors in the 1990s, Grosse Isle today preserves the quarantine buildings, the disinfection station, the lazaretto and cemeteries, and interprets the story of immigration, disease and the Irish diaspora in Canada.
