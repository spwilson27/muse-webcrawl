Transverse plane

The transverse plane (also called the axial plane or horizontal plane) is an anatomical plane that divides the body into superior (upper) and inferior (lower) parts. It is perpendicular to the coronal plane and the sagittal plane, and in a human in the standard anatomical position it is parallel to the ground.

Use in anatomy and imaging

Anatomists use the transverse plane together with the frontal (coronal) and sagittal planes to describe locations, movements, and sections of the body. In medical imaging, CT and MRI scans are commonly acquired or reconstructed in the axial (transverse) plane, and axial slices are the standard view in much of radiology. Movements that occur in the transverse plane include rotation of the head, trunk, and limbs.

Terminology

Related terms include "transverse section" or cross-section, and directional terms cranial/caudal and superior/inferior defined relative to the plane. In veterinary anatomy and in non-human animals, the transverse plane divides the body into cranial and caudal portions.
