Agesilaus II

Agesilaus II (c. 444 – c. 360 BC) was the 18th Eurypontid king of Sparta, reigning from about 399 BC until his death. One of the most celebrated Spartan kings, he led Spartan armies on campaigns across Greece and Asia Minor for most of his forty-year reign, the longest of the classical period, but presided over the decline of Spartan power after the defeat at Leuctra in 371 BC.

Accession and character

Agesilaus was the son of Archidamus II and half-brother of Agis II. Lame in one leg from birth, he succeeded unexpectedly when Agis's son Leotychidas was declared illegitimate, with support from the general Lysander. Xenophon, who served under him and admired him, wrote an encomium, the Agesilaus, praising his piety, frugality, and endurance; Plutarch also wrote his biography.

Campaigns

In 396–394 BC Agesilaus campaigned in Asia Minor against Persia, posing as a new Agamemnon liberating the Ionian Greeks, and won victories in Lydia and Phrygia before being recalled to fight the Corinthian War in Greece, where he won the Battle of Coronea (394 BC). He repeatedly invaded Boeotia in the struggle with Thebes. After the catastrophic Spartan defeat at Leuctra (371 BC) by Epaminondas, Agesilaus defended Sparta itself when the Thebans invaded Laconia, and worked to restore Spartan fortunes, though Sparta never regained its former dominance.

Later years and death

In his eighties, Agesilaus served as a mercenary commander to raise money for Sparta: first for the Egyptian pharaoh Tachos, then, switching sides, for Nectanebo II. He died in Cyrenaica in 360 BC on the voyage home; his body was preserved in wax or honey and carried back to Sparta for burial.
