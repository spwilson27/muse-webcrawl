Moksha

Moksha (Sanskrit: मोक्ष, mokṣa), also called mukti, is a central concept in Indian religions — Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism — denoting liberation, release and emancipation from saṃsāra, the cycle of death and rebirth. In Hindu philosophy moksha is the fourth and ultimate puruṣārtha, or goal of human life, beyond dharma (duty), artha (prosperity) and kama (pleasure).

In Hinduism

Hindu schools differ on what liberation is and how it is attained. In Advaita Vedanta, moksha is the realisation that the individual self (ātman) is identical with the ultimate reality (Brahman); ignorance of this identity is what binds one to rebirth, and liberation is therefore a matter of knowledge, attainable in life (jīvanmukti). Dualist schools such as Dvaita conceive moksha instead as eternal blissful existence in the presence of God, reached through devotion (bhakti) and divine grace. Classical Yoga and Samkhya describe liberation as the isolation (kaivalya) of pure consciousness from matter.

Traditions commonly describe three paths to liberation: the path of knowledge (jñāna), the path of devotion (bhakti) and the path of selfless action (karma yoga), all discussed in the Bhagavad Gita. Renunciation (sannyāsa) of worldly life is traditionally regarded as the final stage of life, dedicated to the pursuit of moksha.

In other Indian religions

In Jainism, moksha is the release of the soul from all karmic matter, after which it rises to the top of the universe in omniscient bliss; it is the state attained by the liberated siddhas. In Buddhism the corresponding goal, nirvana, is the extinguishing of craving and ignorance and the end of rebirth — Buddhist schools generally avoid the term moksha for their goal, though it appears in some texts. In Sikhism, liberation (mukti) is release from the cycle of rebirth through remembrance of God's name and righteous living, often understood as union with God.

Characteristics

Although described variously, moksha is consistently portrayed as freedom from suffering and limitation, a state beyond ordinary description, and the definitive end of the soul's transmigration. It remains one of the most influential soteriological concepts in world religion.
