Swedish Navy

The Swedish Navy (Swedish: Svenska marinen) is the naval branch of the Swedish Armed Forces. Founded in 1522, it is one of the oldest standing navies in the world. It is composed of surface and submarine naval units — the Fleet (Flottan) — together with the Naval Amphibious Corps, formerly the Coastal Artillery.

History

King Gustav I purchased ships from Lübeck in 1522, an event traditionally taken as the navy's founding. In the 17th century Sweden was a great power and its navy contested control of the Baltic Sea with Denmark and Poland; the warship Vasa, which sank on her maiden voyage in 1628, is the era's most famous monument. After Sweden's great-power period the navy declined, and the naval city of Karlskrona, founded in 1680, became its principal base. During the Cold War the navy focused on defending Sweden's long coastline and archipelagos against invasion, with submarines, fast attack craft and mine warfare forces; the 1981 grounding of the Soviet submarine U 137 near Karlskrona became a defining incident of the era.

Modern fleet

Today the navy operates Visby-class stealth corvettes, Göteborg- and Stockholm-class corvettes, mine countermeasure vessels, and submarines of the Gotland class — notable for their Stirling air-independent propulsion — with the new Blekinge class under construction. Sweden's accession to NATO in 2024 reshaped the navy's role toward collective defence in the Baltic region.

Organisation

Principal units include the 1st Submarine Flotilla and the 3rd and 4th Naval Warfare Flotillas, based at Karlskrona and Berga, and the Älvsborg Amphibious Regiment and Stockholm Amphibious Regiment.
