Family farm

DefinitionFarm owned and/or operated by a family
Share of farmsAbout 98% of US farms; ~90%+ of farms worldwide
Related conceptsSmallholding, subsistence farming, corporate farming

A family farm is a farm owned and/or operated by a family. In the definition used by the United States Department of Agriculture, a family farm is one in which the majority of the business is owned by the operator and individuals related to the operator, and such farms account for the overwhelming majority of farms — and a large majority of agricultural production — in the United States and worldwide. Family farms range from small subsistence smallholdings worked by hand to very large commercial operations employing hired managers and labour.

Characteristics

Family farms typically pass land and knowledge between generations, combine household and business finances, and rely substantially on family labour. Globally, most of the world's roughly 600 million farms are family-run, and family farmers produce an estimated 70–80% of the world's food by value on a minority of its farmland, according to the FAO, which proclaimed 2014 the International Year of Family Farming and leads the UN Decade of Family Farming (2019–2028).

Pressures and debate

Family farms face consolidation pressure as average farm sizes grow, input costs rise, and commodity prices fluctuate; succession — transferring the farm to the next generation — is a persistent challenge as farmers age. Supporters value family farms for food security, rural employment, landscape stewardship, and community life. Policy debates concern subsidies, contract farming for integrators (especially in poultry and hogs), access to credit and land for beginning farmers, and whether small family farms can compete with industrial-scale corporate operations. In practice the categories blur: many large "family farms" are incorporated, while corporate agriculture often contracts production out to family growers.
