Mountain range

A mountain range is a geographic area containing numerous geologically related mountains. Ranges are formed by orogeny — the folding, faulting and uplift of the Earth's crust, usually at the boundaries of tectonic plates — though some ranges arise from volcanism or rifting.

Examples and structure

The longest terrestrial range is the Andes of South America (about 7,000 km); the Himalayas contain the highest peaks, while the mid-ocean ridge system, mostly underwater, is the longest mountain chain on Earth at some 65,000 km. Individual ranges are grouped into larger systems called cordilleras, belts or chains. Mountain ranges influence climate profoundly, forcing moist air upward (orographic lift), creating rain shadows, and serving as barriers to species and to human movement.
