Tōrō nagashi

Tōrō nagashi (灯籠流し) is a Japanese ceremony in which people float paper lanterns down a river or out to sea. Tōrō is the Japanese word for a lantern, and nagashi means "cruise" or "flow". The ritual traditionally takes place on the final evening of the Bon Festival (Obon), the Buddhist season honouring the spirits of one's ancestors.

Meaning

The lanterns are believed to guide the spirits of the dead back to the world of the dead after their brief return visit during Obon. Participants often write names or messages on the lanterns; the sight of hundreds of lights drifting on dark water is both a farewell and a prayer for the repose of the departed.

Notable ceremonies

Famous tōrō nagashi events include those held in Nagasaki and Kyoto's Arashiyama district. In Hiroshima, on 6 August each year, lanterns are floated on the Motoyasu River past the Atomic Bomb Dome in memory of the victims of the atomic bombing, attended by thousands who write messages of peace. Similar memorial lantern-floatings are held in Hawaii and other places with Japanese diaspora communities, including ceremonies honouring victims of war and disaster.
