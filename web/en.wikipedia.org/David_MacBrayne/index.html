David MacBrayne Ltd is a Scottish shipping and ferry company, wholly owned by the Scottish Government, whose principal subsidiary, Caledonian MacBrayne (CalMac), operates lifeline ferry services to the Hebrides and islands of the Firth of Clyde. The company traces its origins to 1851 and is among the oldest shipping firms in the United Kingdom.

History

The business began in 1851 when David and Alexander Hutcheson took over the Clyde and Hebridean steamer services of G. & J. Burns. David MacBrayne, a nephew, became a partner and gave the company its enduring name in 1879. MacBrayne's steamers — the famous "Royal Route" from Glasgow through the Crinan Canal to Oban and the Western Isles — carried mail, cargo, tourists, and generations of islanders, and the company's red-and-black funnels became icons of the Hebrides.

Modern era

In 1973 the car-ferry operations merged with the Caledonian Steam Packet Company to form Caledonian MacBrayne. The group was restructured in 2006: David MacBrayne Ltd became the parent holding company owned by Scottish Ministers, while the ferry services were tendered and operated by CalMac Ferries Ltd, and the vessels and piers placed in the ownership of Caledonian Maritime Assets Ltd (CMAL). The company also operated the Northern Isles services through NorthLink Ferries until 2012.

Role

The MacBrayne/CalMac network is the largest ferry operation in Scotland, serving dozens of routes vital to island economies on the west coast.
