Han Yu

Born768, Mengzhou, Tang China
Died824
OccupationEssayist, poet, official
Known forClassical prose movement; Confucian revival

Han Yu (768–824) was a Chinese writer, poet, and government official of the Tang dynasty. He is regarded as one of the greatest masters of Chinese prose and a leading figure in the revival of Confucianism and the Classical Prose Movement.

Career and exile

Orphaned young, Han Yu passed the jinshi examination in 792 and held a succession of official posts. In 819 he wrote a famous memorial denouncing the imperial veneration of a relic of the Buddha's finger bone; for this he was demoted and exiled to Chaozhou in the far south. His essay written to his nephew on the journey and his address to the crocodiles of the Han River are celebrated works. He was later recalled and rose to high office in the Ministry of Rites.

Writing and thought

Han Yu championed guwen ("ancient writing"), a clear, direct prose modelled on pre-Han texts, against the ornate parallel prose then fashionable. A vigorous defender of Confucian orthodoxy against Buddhism and Daoism, he helped establish the intellectual groundwork later developed by Song-dynasty Neo-Confucianism. He also wrote notable poetry and mentored younger writers such as Li Ao and (briefly) Li He.
