Central charge

In theoretical physics, a central charge is an operator that commutes with all other symmetry operators in a theory. The term is used most prominently in two-dimensional conformal field theory, where the central charge c is the coefficient appearing in the commutator of the Virasoro algebra's generators.

Conformal field theory

In the Virasoro algebra, the central charge measures the conformal anomaly: the failure of the stress–energy tensor's trace to vanish under quantisation. It counts, roughly, the number of degrees of freedom of the theory — a free real boson contributes c = 1 and a free Majorana fermion c = 1/2. Zamolodchikov's c-theorem shows that a suitably defined c-function decreases along renormalisation group flows in two dimensions.

Other contexts

In supersymmetric theories, central charges appear in the supersymmetry algebra and are related to the masses of BPS states. The term also arises in the representation theory of affine Lie algebras, where it corresponds to the level of the representation.
