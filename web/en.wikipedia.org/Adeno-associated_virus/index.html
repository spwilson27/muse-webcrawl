Adeno-associated virus

Adeno-associated viruses (AAV) are small viruses that infect humans and some other primate species. They belong to the genus Dependoparvovirus in the family Parvoviridae. AAVs are not currently known to cause disease, replicate only with the help of a helper virus such as an adenovirus, and are widely used as vectors in gene therapy.

Biology

The AAV genome is a single-stranded DNA of about 4.7 kilobases encoding the rep and cap genes, flanked by inverted terminal repeats. Numerous natural serotypes (AAV1–AAV12 and many more variants) differ in capsid proteins and therefore in tissue tropism.

Gene therapy

Recombinant AAV vectors, in which the viral genes are replaced by a therapeutic cassette, deliver genes to dividing and non-dividing cells with mostly episomal persistence and low immunogenicity. Approved AAV-based therapies include voretigene neparvovec (Luxturna) for inherited retinal dystrophy, onasemnogene abeparvovec (Zolgensma) for spinal muscular atrophy, and etranacogene dezaparvovec (Hemgenix) for hemophilia B.

Limitations

Small packaging capacity (~4.7 kb), pre-existing immunity to capsids, and high-dose toxicities constrain clinical use and are active areas of research.
