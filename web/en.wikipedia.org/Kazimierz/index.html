Kazimierz

Kazimierz (Polish pronunciation: [kaˈʑimjɛʂ]; Yiddish: קוזמיר, Kuzmir) is a historical district of Kraków, Poland. Founded as a separate city by King Casimir III the Great in 1335, it later became the centre of Jewish life in Kraków, and from the late 15th century until the Holocaust its Jewish quarter was one of the most important centres of Jewish culture in Europe. Since the 1990s it has been revived as a cultural and tourist district and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site covering Kraków's historic centre.

History

Kazimierz was chartered in 1335 on an island in the Vistula south of Kraków's Old Town, named for its founder, Casimir III. In 1495 the Jews of Kraków were expelled from the city and resettled in Kazimierz, whose northeastern quarter developed into a largely self-governing Jewish town with synagogues, schools and markets. Scholars such as Moses Isserles (the Rema) made it a centre of rabbinic learning, and its community grew to tens of thousands by the 20th century.

The Holocaust

In March 1941 the German occupation authorities forced Kraków's Jews into a ghetto in Podgórze, across the river. The ghetto was liquidated in March 1943; its inhabitants were murdered on the spot or deported to Płaszów and Auschwitz. Almost the entire Jewish community of Kazimierz perished, and the district fell into dilapidation in the postwar decades.

Revival

Interest in Kazimierz revived after Steven Spielberg filmed Schindler's List in its streets in 1993. The annual Jewish Culture Festival, founded in 1988, became one of the largest events of its kind, and synagogues were restored as museums and cultural venues: the Old Synagogue (the oldest surviving in Poland), the Remuh Synagogue with its Renaissance cemetery, the Tempel and Izaak synagogues. Plac Nowy, with its round market hall, anchors a busy nightlife scene, while institutions such as the Galicia Jewish Museum document the district's past.
