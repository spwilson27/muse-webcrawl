Twentieth Amendment to the United States Constitution

Proposed2 March 1932
Ratified23 January 1933
Nickname"Lame Duck Amendment"

The Twentieth Amendment to the United States Constitution moved the beginning and ending of the terms of the president and vice president from 4 March to 20 January, and of members of Congress from 4 March to 3 January. It also provides procedures for cases in which a president-elect dies or no president has been chosen by inauguration day. Often called the "Lame Duck Amendment", it was ratified on 23 January 1933.

Background

Under the original constitutional calendar, a long gap separated the November elections from the March start of new terms, reflecting eighteenth-century travel times. By the twentieth century this interval left defeated "lame duck" officeholders legislating for months and left the country waiting for a new administration during crises — most conspicuously during the secession winter of 1860–61 and the banking crisis following the 1932 election. Senator George W. Norris of Nebraska championed the reform for more than a decade.

Provisions

Section 1 fixes the term dates; Section 2 requires Congress to assemble at least once a year, beginning on 3 January; Section 3 provides that if the president-elect dies before the term begins, the vice president-elect becomes president, and allows Congress to legislate for cases where no president or vice president qualifies; Section 4 addresses deaths among candidates in contingent elections in the House and Senate.
