Inclusion (value and practice)

Inclusion, as a value and practice, is the idea and the corresponding set of actions by which people of all backgrounds, identities and abilities are fully welcomed, valued and enabled to participate in social, educational, economic and political life. It is closely associated with disability rights, where it denotes educating and employing people with disabilities within mainstream settings rather than segregating them, and more broadly with diversity, equity and inclusion programmes in organisations.

In education

Inclusive education places students with special educational needs in general classrooms with appropriate support, a model endorsed by UNESCO and the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. Advocates argue it benefits both disabled and non-disabled students; debates concern resourcing, teacher training and the appropriateness of full inclusion for every learner.

In the workplace

Organisational inclusion involves practices such as accessible facilities, flexible working, inclusive hiring and cultures in which employees feel belonging. It is frequently paired with diversity (the composition of the workforce) in corporate and public-sector strategy.

Social inclusion

In social policy, inclusion is the opposite of social exclusion: the process of improving the terms on which individuals and groups, such as minorities, migrants and people in poverty, take part in society.
