James Mirrlees

Born5 July 1936, Minnigaff, Scotland
Died29 August 2018, Cambridge, England
FieldsEconomics
AwardsNobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences (1996)

Sir James Alexander Mirrlees (5 July 1936 – 29 August 2018) was a Scottish economist who shared the 1996 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with William Vickrey for their fundamental contributions to the theory of incentives under asymmetric information.

Work

Mirrlees's 1971 paper on optimal income taxation founded a major branch of public economics: he modelled how a government should design taxes when it cannot observe individuals' earnings ability, balancing equity against incentives to work. His work underlies modern analyses of taxation, insurance, and contract design.

Career

Mirrlees taught at the University of Oxford and then at Cambridge, where he was professor of political economy from 1995. He led the Mirrlees Review of the UK tax system for the Institute for Fiscal Studies, published in 2011.
