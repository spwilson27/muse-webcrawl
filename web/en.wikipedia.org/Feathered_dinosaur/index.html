Feathered dinosaur

Feathered dinosaurs are dinosaurs that possessed feathers, ranging from simple hair-like filaments to complex pennaceous feathers identical in structure to those of modern birds. Evidence for feathers in non-avian dinosaurs accumulated rapidly from the 1990s, principally from the exceptionally preserved fossils of the Jehol Biota in Liaoning, China. The discovery of feathered dinosaurs has transformed understanding of dinosaur appearance and biology and provided strong evidence that birds evolved from small theropod dinosaurs.

History of discovery

The first known dinosaur with evidence of feathers was Archaeopteryx, described in 1861 from the Solnhofen limestone of Germany, but for more than a century it was regarded as a unique bird-like exception. In 1996 Chinese palaeontologists described Sinosauropteryx, a small compsognathid theropod covered in simple filamentous structures — the first non-avian dinosaur known to bear a feather-like covering. Dozens of feathered species followed, including the four-winged Microraptor, the giant tyrannosauroid Yutyrannus (2012), and ornithischian dinosaurs such as Kulindadromeus, suggesting that filamentous coverings may have been widespread among dinosaurs generally.

Types of feathers

Fossil dinosaurs preserve a spectrum of integumentary structures: simple unbranched filaments, tufts of filaments, downy feathers with a central shaft and loose barbs, and fully pennaceous feathers with interlocking barbules like those used in flight by birds. Melanosome analysis of preserved feathers has allowed scientists to infer the colours of some species; Anchiornis, for example, appears to have been largely black and grey with a reddish crest.

Function

Feathers almost certainly originated for purposes other than flight — most likely insulation and display — and were later co-opted for aerodynamic use. This pattern of exaptation is now a standard example in evolutionary biology. The presence of insulating feathers in many theropods also supports the view that those dinosaurs were warm-blooded, active animals.
