The Union Company, legally the Union Steam Ship Company of New Zealand Limited, was once the biggest shipping line in the southern hemisphere and New Zealand's largest private-sector employer. It was founded in 1875 in Dunedin by James Mills, who had managed the Harbour Steam Company.

History

With backing from Scottish shipbuilder Peter Denny, the company acquired modern iron steamers built on the Clyde and rapidly expanded beyond coastal New Zealand services to trans-Tasman routes to Australia, Pacific island trades, and services to India, North America, and Britain. By the early twentieth century it operated a fleet of dozens of vessels and ran the main inter-island and trans-Tasman passenger services.

Twentieth century

The company was controlled from 1917 by the British P&O group. Its ships served as troopships in both world wars. Famous vessels included the Maori, Wahine, and the inter-island ferry Rangatira. The 1968 sinking of the Wahine in Wellington Harbour, with the loss of 53 lives, was one of New Zealand's worst maritime disasters. The company later diversified into aviation, trucking, and other transport businesses. After a series of ownership changes it was sold in the 1990s, and the Union name disappeared in 2000.
