In Greek mythology, Orthrus (Greek: Ὄρθρος, Orthros, "dawn") or Orthus (Ὄρθος, Orthos, "straight") was the two-headed dog of the monster Geryon. He and the herdsman Eurytion guarded Geryon's magnificent red cattle on the island of Erytheia at the far western edge of the world, near the garden of the Hesperides.

Family

Orthrus was the offspring of the monsters Typhon and Echidna, and thus brother of many famous creatures of Greek myth: the three-headed hound Cerberus, the many-headed Lernaean Hydra, the fire-breathing Chimera, the Sphinx, and the Nemean lion. According to Apollodorus, Orthrus was also the father, by the Chimera, of the Sphinx and the Nemean lion.

Death

Orthrus was slain by Heracles during his tenth labour, the fetching of the cattle of Geryon. The hero killed Eurytion and Orthrus, then Geryon himself, before driving the cattle back across Europe to Mycenae. Orthrus appears in early Greek art, for example on a Caeretan hydria of about 530 BC, shown dead, pierced by an arrow, with a serpent's tail and — unusually — a single head in that depiction, though literature consistently describes him as two-headed.

The name Orthrus has also been given to Orthrus, a genus of jumping spiders, and to a two-headed dog character in various modern works.
