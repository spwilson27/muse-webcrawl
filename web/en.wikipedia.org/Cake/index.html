Cake

Cake
TypeBaked dessert
Main ingredientsFlour, sugar, eggs, butter or oil, leavening
Common occasionsBirthdays, weddings, anniversaries, celebrations

Cake is a form of sweet baked food made from flour, sugar and other ingredients, usually bound with eggs and fat and leavened with baking powder or whipped egg foam. Cakes are central to celebrations in many cultures, notably birthday cakes with candles and elaborate multi-tiered wedding cakes. The word derives from the Old Norse kaka.

History

Ancient peoples made cake-like breads sweetened with honey; the Egyptians are often credited with early baking skills, and the Greeks and Romans made cheesecakes and fruitcakes. Modern cakes emerged in Europe in the 17th century as oven technology and refined ingredients became widely available. The Industrial Revolution brought chemical leaveners such as baking soda and baking powder, and the boxed cake mix appeared in the 20th century.

Types and methods

Cakes are commonly grouped by method: butter or creamed cakes (such as pound cake and Victoria sponge) beat fat and sugar together; foam cakes (sponge, angel food, chiffon) rely on whipped eggs for volume; and flourless or low-flour cakes include many cheesecakes and chocolate tortes. Familiar varieties include chocolate cake, carrot cake, red velvet cake, fruitcake, Black Forest gâteau and tiramisu-style layered cakes. Cakes are typically finished with icing or frosting — buttercream, fondant, ganache or glaze — and may be filled with jam, cream or fruit.

Cultural role

Cutting the cake is a ritual at weddings, and blowing out candles on a birthday cake while making a wish is a widespread custom. Cake also appears in idioms and politics, from "let them eat cake" (popularly attributed to Marie Antoinette) to "a piece of cake" meaning something easy.
