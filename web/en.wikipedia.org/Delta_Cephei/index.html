Delta Cephei

Delta Cephei is a variable star in the constellation Cepheus, about 887 light-years from the Sun. It is the prototype of the Cepheid variable stars, a class of pulsating stars that became fundamental distance indicators for measuring the scale of the universe.

Variability

John Goodricke discovered in 1784 that Delta Cephei varies regularly in brightness between magnitudes about 3.5 and 4.4 with a period of 5.366 days. The star is a yellow supergiant whose surface expands and contracts: as it pulsates, its spectral type shifts between roughly F5 and G2, and its radius changes by several percent. Henrietta Swan Leavitt's discovery of the period–luminosity relation for Cepheids in 1908–1912 turned such stars into "standard candles"; Edwin Hubble's Cepheid measurements in the Andromeda Nebula proved it lay far beyond the Milky Way.

Properties

Delta Cephei has roughly 4.5 times the Sun's mass, some 40 times its radius and about 2,000 times its luminosity. It is also a double star, with a hotter companion, and it ejects matter in a stellar wind that interacts with the surrounding interstellar medium.
