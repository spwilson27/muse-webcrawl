GT World Challenge Europe

GT World Challenge Europe fields large grids of GT3 machinery

The GT World Challenge Europe is a sports car racing championship organised by the SRO Motorsports Group. It is contested by GT3-specification cars and consists of two series: the Endurance Cup, headlined by the 24 Hours of Spa, and the Sprint Cup, made up of short one-hour races. The championship adopted its current name in 2020, having previously been known as the Blancpain GT Series.

History

The championship traces its origins to the Blancpain Endurance Series, founded in 2011 around long-distance races at circuits including Monza, Silverstone, Paul Ricard, and the Nürburgring, anchored by the 24 Hours of Spa. A companion sprint series, descended from the FIA GT Series, was added in 2014, and the two were combined under the Blancpain GT Series banner with an overall championship for drivers and teams contesting both. When title sponsorship changed, the series was rebranded GT World Challenge Europe for 2020, aligning it with sister championships in America, Asia, and Australia.

Format

Entries are divided into classes — Pro, Gold Cup, Silver Cup, and Bronze Cup — based on driver grading, so professional and amateur line-ups compete for separate honours within the same race. Performance is equalised between different manufacturers' cars through Balance of Performance adjustments. Sprint Cup weekends consist of two one-hour races with a mandatory pit stop and driver change; Endurance Cup races run from three hours up to the 24 hours at Spa, which is also a round of the Intercontinental GT Challenge. Major manufacturers represented have included Audi, BMW, Ferrari, Lamborghini, McLaren, Mercedes-AMG, and Porsche.
