Nelumbo lutea

Nelumbo lutea, commonly called the American lotus or yellow lotus, is an aquatic plant native to North America, one of only two species in the genus Nelumbo (the other being the Asian sacred lotus, N. nucifera).

Description

The plant grows in lakes and swamps, as well as areas subject to flooding. Its roots are anchored in the mud, while the large, circular leaves may float or stand above the surface. The flowers — pale yellow and up to 25 cm across — are the largest of any North American wildflower, according to many accounts. The distinctive woody seed pods resemble shower heads and are used in floral arrangements.

Distribution and uses

Its range extends from Ontario and the eastern United States through the Mississippi basin to Texas, Mexico, and the Caribbean. Indigenous peoples ate the starchy rhizomes and seeds, and the species is thought to have been spread partly by human cultivation. The seeds remain viable for remarkably long periods.

Conservation

While widespread, it is listed as threatened or endangered in some northern states at the edge of its range.
