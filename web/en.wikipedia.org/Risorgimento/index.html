The Risorgimento ("Resurgence") was the political and social movement that consolidated the different states of the Italian Peninsula into the single state of the Kingdom of Italy during the 19th century. Beginning after the Congress of Vienna in 1815, the process culminated in the proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy in 1861 and was completed with the capture of Rome in 1870.

Background

After 1815 Italy was a patchwork of states: the Austrian-dominated Lombardy–Venetia, the Kingdom of Sardinia (Piedmont), the Papal States, the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, and several duchies. Liberal and nationalist ideas, spread by secret societies such as the Carbonari, inspired failed revolts in 1820–21 and 1831. Giuseppe Mazzini founded Young Italy in 1831, advocating a unified democratic republic; his repeated insurrections failed but kept the national cause alive.

The revolutions of 1848

In 1848 revolutions swept Italy: Piedmont's King Charles Albert granted a constitution (the Statuto Albertino) and went to war with Austria, but was defeated at Custoza and Novara, abdicating in favour of Victor Emmanuel II. The Roman Republic proclaimed under Mazzini and defended by Giuseppe Garibaldi was crushed by French troops restoring the Pope.

Cavour and the war of 1859

Under Prime Minister Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour, Piedmont modernised and allied with France. In 1859 Franco-Piedmontese forces defeated Austria at Magenta and Solferino, gaining Lombardy; plebiscites then attached Tuscany, Parma, Modena and Romagna to Piedmont.

Garibaldi and unification

In 1860 Garibaldi led the Expedition of the Thousand — volunteer Redshirts who conquered Sicily and Naples from the Bourbons. To prevent a republican outcome, Cavour sent Piedmontese troops through the Papal States, and Garibaldi handed the south to Victor Emmanuel II at Teano. On 17 March 1861 the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed. Venice was gained in 1866 through alliance with Prussia, and Rome was taken on 20 September 1870 after French protection was withdrawn during the Franco-Prussian War; Rome became the capital in 1871.

Legacy

Unification left unresolved issues — the "Southern Question" of the poorer Mezzogiorno, the Vatican's refusal to recognise the state until 1929, and "Italia irredenta" claims on Trento and Trieste, satisfied only after the First World War.
