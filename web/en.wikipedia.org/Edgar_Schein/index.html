Edgar Schein

Edgar Henry Schein (March 5, 1928 – January 26, 2023) was a Swiss-born American business theorist and psychologist who was professor emeritus at the MIT Sloan School of Management. He is regarded as a founder of the field of organizational development and is best known for his model of organizational culture and his concepts of career anchors, process consultation, and "humble inquiry."

Career

Schein was born in Zurich and emigrated to the United States as a child. He studied at the University of Chicago and Stanford University and earned a PhD in social psychology from Harvard University in 1952. After research at the Walter Reed Army Institute of Research, where he studied repatriated Korean War prisoners and coined an influential account of "coercive persuasion," he joined the MIT Sloan School of Management in 1956, remaining there for the rest of his career.

Contributions

In Organizational Culture and Leadership (first published 1985), Schein defined culture as a pattern of shared basic assumptions learned by a group, and analyzed it at three levels: visible artifacts, espoused beliefs and values, and underlying taken-for-granted assumptions. His research on careers produced the concept of career anchors — the self-perceived talents, motives, and values that stabilize a person's career choices. In Process Consultation he argued that effective helping requires building a joint diagnostic relationship rather than acting as expert or doctor, an approach he later developed into the practice of "humble inquiry" — asking questions from genuine curiosity rather than telling.
