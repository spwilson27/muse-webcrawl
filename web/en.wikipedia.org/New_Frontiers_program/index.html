The New Frontiers program is a series of space exploration missions conducted by NASA with the purpose of furthering the understanding of the Solar System. The program selects medium-class missions that can achieve high-priority scientific goals identified by the planetary science community in its decadal surveys, at a cost intermediate between the smaller Discovery program missions and the large Flagship missions.

Missions are led by a principal investigator and selected competitively through an Announcement of Opportunity. The program is managed for NASA's Science Mission Directorate by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the Marshall Space Flight Center's Planetary Missions Program Office.

Missions

New Horizons (launched 2006) flew by Pluto in July 2015 and the Kuiper Belt object Arrokoth on 1 January 2019, the most distant object ever visited by a spacecraft at the time. Juno (launched 2011) entered orbit around Jupiter in 2016 to study the planet's composition, gravity and magnetic fields, and polar magnetosphere. OSIRIS-REx (launched 2016) visited the asteroid Bennu, collected a sample, and returned it to Earth on 24 September 2023; the spacecraft continues as OSIRIS-APEX toward asteroid Apophis. Dragonfly, a rotorcraft lander for Saturn's moon Titan, is the fourth mission, selected in 2019 and scheduled to launch in the late 2020s.

Selection process

Each New Frontiers opportunity specifies a list of eligible mission themes drawn from the decadal survey — for example a comet surface sample return, a lunar south pole sample return, a Saturn or Venus in-situ explorer, an ocean worlds mission to Enceladus or Titan, or a Jupiter trojan tour. The cost cap for each mission (excluding launch) has risen over successive opportunities, most recently to about US$1.2 billion.
