Data warehouse

A data warehouse is a system used for reporting and data analysis, and is a core component of business intelligence. Data warehouses are central repositories of integrated data from one or more disparate sources. They store current and historical data in one single place, organised to support analytical queries rather than transaction processing.

Characteristics

Bill Inmon characterised a warehouse as subject-oriented, integrated, time-variant and non-volatile. Data is loaded through ETL (extract, transform, load) or ELT processes that clean and standardise records from operational systems. Analytical models commonly use dimensional modelling — fact and dimension tables in star or snowflake schemas, as promoted by Ralph Kimball — and subsets called data marts serve individual departments.

Evolution

The concept developed in the 1980s–90s with Inmon's and Kimball's competing methodologies. Later developments include columnar and massively parallel processing (MPP) databases, cloud data warehouses such as Snowflake, BigQuery and Redshift, and the "lakehouse" pattern combining warehouse querying with data-lake storage.
