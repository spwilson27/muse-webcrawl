Bevel gear

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bevel gears are gears in which the axes of the two shafts intersect and the tooth-bearing faces of the gears themselves are conically shaped. They are most often mounted on shafts set at 90 degrees to one another, but can work at other angles.

Types

Straight bevel gears have conical teeth cut straight along the cone; spiral bevel gears have curved, oblique teeth giving smoother, quieter operation and higher load capacity; hypoid gears have offset pinion axes, as used in automobile final drives. Zerol bevel gears are an intermediate form with curved teeth but zero spiral angle.

Applications

Bevel gears appear in differential drives, hand drills, marine propulsion, printing presses and locomotives. In an automotive differential, bevel gearing allows the driven wheels to rotate at different speeds while receiving power from a perpendicular drive shaft.
