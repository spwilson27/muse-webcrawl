Bellevue Palace, Germany

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bellevue Palace (German: Schloss Bellevue) is a palace in Berlin's Tiergarten district, on the northern edge of the Großer Tiergarten park near the Spree river. It has been the official residence of the President of Germany since 1994.

History

Built in 1786 for Prince Augustus Ferdinand of Prussia, younger brother of Frederick the Great, to designs by Philipp Daniel Boumann, Bellevue was the first neoclassical palace in Germany. It later served as a royal residence, a museum, and offices under Nazi and Allied administrations. Severely damaged in the Second World War, it was rebuilt in the 1950s.

Use

The palace comprises a central block with two wings arranged around a courtyard. The president's official apartment is modest; the palace is used mainly for state receptions, accreditation of ambassadors and official ceremonies, with a large oval office on the first floor. The presidential administration (Bundespräsidialamt) occupies a modern building in the palace park.
