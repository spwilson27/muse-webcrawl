Sperm donation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sperm donation is the provision by a man (a sperm donor) of his sperm with the intention that it be used in the artificial insemination or other "fertility treatment" of one or more women who are not the man's sexual partner, in order that they may become pregnant by this man. Sperm may be donated privately or through a sperm bank or fertility clinic. It is used by single women, lesbian couples, and heterosexual couples experiencing male infertility.

Process

Donors at sperm banks undergo screening for infectious diseases such as HIV and hepatitis, genetic conditions, and semen quality. Donated sperm is typically frozen and quarantined for a period (often six months) before use, pending retesting of the donor. Recipients select donors from catalogues listing physical characteristics, background, and sometimes childhood photographs or essays. Conception is achieved by intracervical or intrauterine insemination, or by IVF.

Legal and ethical issues

Laws governing sperm donation vary widely. Issues include donor anonymity versus the rights of donor-conceived people to know their genetic origins (several countries, beginning with Sweden in 1985 and the United Kingdom in 2005, ended anonymous donation), limits on the number of families or offspring per donor, payment to donors, and the legal parentage of children conceived. A sperm donor is generally not the legal parent of the resulting child when donation occurs through licensed clinics.

Known donation

Alternatively, a recipient may use a known donor — a friend or acquaintance — either privately or through a clinic. Private arrangements can raise legal questions about parental rights and responsibilities that formal clinic donation usually avoids.
