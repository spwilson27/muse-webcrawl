Messier 87 (also known as Virgo A or NGC 4486, generally abbreviated to M87) is a supergiant elliptical galaxy in the constellation Virgo, and one of the most massive galaxies in the local universe. It was discovered by Charles Messier in 1781.

Characteristics

M87 lies near the centre of the Virgo Cluster and dominates its surroundings. Its stellar mass is trillions of solar masses, and it is surrounded by an enormous halo of hot gas and one of the largest known systems of globular clusters, numbering perhaps 12,000 or more — far more than the Milky Way's roughly 150. Unlike spiral galaxies, it has little dust and very low rates of ongoing star formation; its stars are old and yellowish.

The jet

A prominent feature is a relativistic jet of plasma ejected from the core, extending at least 4,900 light-years and shining by synchrotron radiation. The jet was discovered photographically in 1918 by Heber Curtis, who described "a curious straight ray". A second, counter-jet is much fainter. Knots in the jet, such as HST-1, have shown dramatic variability in X-rays and radio.

Central black hole

At the heart of M87 sits one of the largest known supermassive black holes, with a mass of about 6.5 billion solar masses. On 10 April 2019 the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration released the first-ever direct image of a black hole's shadow, showing a glowing ring of hot gas around M87's central black hole. The image was widely seen as a confirmation of general relativity in the strong-field regime.

Observational history

M87 is a strong source of radio waves, gamma rays and X-rays, and was identified as a probable source of high-energy cosmic rays. It is bright enough to be seen in amateur telescopes under dark skies.
