Florida State Capitol

The Florida State Capitol is the government building in Tallahassee, Florida, that houses the state's executive and legislative branches. The Capitol complex includes the 22-story modern Capitol building (completed in 1977), the Historic Capitol (built in 1845 and restored), the House and Senate office buildings, and other state offices.

History

Tallahassee was chosen as the territorial capital in 1824, midway between the older cities of St. Augustine and Pensacola. A log cabin served as the first capitol. The Historic Capitol, completed in 1845 just before statehood, was expanded over the following century with wings and a dome. By the 1970s the growing state government required a larger facility.

The new Capitol

The new Capitol, designed by Edward Durell Stone in association with Reynolds, Smith & Hills, was completed in 1977. The 22-story tower houses the executive offices of the Governor and Cabinet on the plaza level, the Senate chamber on the fourth floor, and the House chamber on the fifth floor. Its observation deck on the top floor offers views of the city and surrounding hills.

Historic Capitol

The old Capitol building was slated for demolition but was saved by citizen action and restored to its 1902 appearance, reopening in 1982 as the Florida Historic Capitol Museum, with restored legislative chambers, the governor's office, and the state supreme court chamber.
