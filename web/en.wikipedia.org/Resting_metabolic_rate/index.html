Resting metabolic rate

Resting metabolic rate (RMR) is the rate at which the body burns energy (calories) while at rest, measured under less strict conditions than basal metabolic rate (BMR): typically after a period of rest, without requiring an overnight fast and sleep in a laboratory. RMR accounts for roughly 60–75% of daily energy expenditure in sedentary people.

Measurement and estimation

RMR can be measured by indirect calorimetry, which analyses oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production in expired air. In practice it is often estimated with prediction equations such as the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, which uses weight, height, age and sex. Fat-free (lean) body mass is the strongest determinant of RMR.

Factors

RMR falls with age and with loss of lean mass, and is influenced by thyroid hormones, fever, pregnancy, and energy restriction (adaptive thermogenesis). Together with the thermic effect of food and activity energy expenditure, RMR determines total daily energy needs.
