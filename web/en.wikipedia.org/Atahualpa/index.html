Atahualpa (c. 1502 – 26 July 1533), also Atawallpa, was the last effective Sapa Inca (emperor) of the Inca Empire. He became emperor after defeating his half-brother Huáscar in a civil war, shortly before the arrival of the Spanish conquistadors.

Civil war

After the death of their father Huayna Capac, Atahualpa ruled the northern part of the empire from Quito while Huáscar ruled from Cusco. War broke out between them in 1529; Atahualpa's experienced generals won decisive victories, and Huáscar was captured in 1532.

Capture and death

In November 1532, Francisco Pizarro and a small Spanish force ambushed Atahualpa at the Battle of Cajamarca, capturing him. Atahualpa famously offered a room filled with gold and twice over with silver as ransom. The treasure was delivered and melted down, but the Spanish tried him on charges including the murder of Huáscar and executed him by garrote in July 1533 after a baptism. His death marked the effective end of the Inca Empire.
