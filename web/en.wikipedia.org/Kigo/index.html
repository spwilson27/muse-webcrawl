Kigo

A kigo (Japanese: 季語, "season word") is a word or phrase associated with a particular season, used in traditional forms of Japanese poetry such as haiku, haikai and renga. Kigo anchor a poem in the turning year and evoke a shared body of seasonal associations.

Use

Classical haiku traditionally contain a kigo — cherry blossoms for spring, cicadas or heat for summer, the moon or red leaves for autumn, snow or cold for winter. Compilations called saijiki list thousands of kigo organised by season, with example poems. Linked verse (renga) had strict rules about where season words and topics such as the moon and blossoms must appear.

Debate

Modern and free-form haiku poets sometimes write without kigo (muki haiku), and haiku written outside Japan adapt season words to local climates — raising questions about whether kigo remain essential to the form.
