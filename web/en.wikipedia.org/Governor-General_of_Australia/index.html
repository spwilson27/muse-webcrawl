Governor-General of Australia

The governor-general of Australia is the representative of the monarch of Australia (currently Charles III) in the Commonwealth of Australia. The governor-general performs the federal viceregal functions, including appointing the prime minister and ministers, assenting to legislation, issuing writs for elections, and serving as commander-in-chief of the Australian Defence Force. The office is appointed by the monarch on the advice of the prime minister of Australia.

Incumbent: Sam Mostyn (since 1 July 2024)

Appointer: Monarch of Australia, on advice of the Prime Minister

Residence: Government House, Canberra (Yarralumla); Admiralty House, Sydney

Term: At His Majesty's pleasure (usually about 5 years)

First holder: The Earl of Hopetoun (1901)

Role and powers

Under the Constitution of Australia, executive power is formally vested in the monarch and exercisable by the governor-general. By convention, the governor-general acts almost always on the advice of the prime minister and cabinet, commissioning as prime minister the person able to command the confidence of the House of Representatives, granting a dissolution of parliament, and giving royal assent to bills. "Reserve powers" exist for exceptional circumstances. The governor-general also has ceremonial duties, is Chancellor of the Order of Australia, and acts as patron of many organisations.

The 1975 dismissal

The office's most controversial moment came on 11 November 1975, when Governor-General Sir John Kerr dismissed Prime Minister Gough Whitlam and his Labor government — which held a majority in the House of Representatives but was blocked in the Senate over supply bills — and commissioned opposition leader Malcolm Fraser as caretaker prime minister. The dismissal remains a central episode in debates about the reserve powers and an Australian republic.

History

The first governor-general, Lord Hopetoun, took office at federation in 1901. Early holders were British aristocrats; from 1931, following the Statute of Westminster, appointees were Australians chosen on Australian advice, beginning with Sir Isaac Isaacs. Sam Mostyn, a business and community leader, became the 28th governor-general and the second woman to hold the office, succeeding David Hurley in 2024.
