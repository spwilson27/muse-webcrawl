Spelling alphabet

A spelling alphabet is a set of words used to stand for the letters of an alphabet in oral communication, particularly over radio or telephone where letters can be easily confused. Each word in the set is chosen so that its pronunciation is distinct and recognisable.

NATO phonetic alphabet

The most widely used spelling alphabet is the NATO phonetic alphabet (Alfa, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu), adopted in the 1950s for military, aviation, and maritime communication.

History

Earlier alphabets included the Able-Baker alphabet used by the U.S. military in the Second World War and various national and commercial radiotelephony alphabets dating to the early twentieth century.
