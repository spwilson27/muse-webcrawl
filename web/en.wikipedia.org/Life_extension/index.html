Life extension

Life extension is the concept of extending the human lifespan, either modestly through improvements in medicine, or dramatically by increasing the maximum lifespan beyond its generally settled biological limit of around 125 years. Research in animals such as nematodes, fruit flies, and mice has achieved lifespan increases through genetic, dietary, and pharmacological interventions.

Strategies

Proposed strategies include calorie restriction and its mimetics, genetic modification of ageing pathways such as insulin/IGF-1 and mTOR signalling, senolytic drugs that clear senescent cells, stem cell therapies, and partial cellular reprogramming. Compounds studied include rapamycin, metformin, and NAD+ precursors.

History

The desire to extend life is ancient, from the Epic of Gilgamesh and alchemical elixirs to modern gerontology. Scientific study accelerated in the 20th century with model-organism research, and organisations and companies dedicated to ageing research have grown since the 2000s.

Ethics and debate

Debate concerns overpopulation, resource distribution, inequality of access, and whether radical life extension is desirable. Proponents argue that ageing-related disease causes immense suffering and that extending healthy life (healthspan) is a medical good; critics raise social and philosophical concerns, and note that no intervention has yet been proven to extend human maximum lifespan.
