Bennett Lake

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bennett Lake is a lake in northwestern British Columbia and the Yukon Territory, Canada, forming part of the headwaters of the Yukon River system. The Chilkoot Trail and the White Pass trail both end at the lake, which made it famous during the Klondike Gold Rush.

Geography

The lake is about 41 km long and lies at an elevation of roughly 656 m. The West Arm extends toward the Coast Mountains; the community of Bennett, British Columbia, at its southern end, was a major staging point where stampeders built or bought boats to float down the Yukon River to Dawson City.

History

The lake was named in 1883 by U.S. Navy Lieutenant Frederick Schwatka for James Gordon Bennett Jr., editor of the New York Herald. In 1898 some 30,000 gold seekers passed through Bennett. Today the White Pass and Yukon Route railway runs along its eastern shore, and the lake drains via the short Bennett–Tagish connection into Tagish Lake.
