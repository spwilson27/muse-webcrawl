Fireman's pole

A fireman's pole (also called a sliding pole or fire pole) is a pole that firefighters slide down to move rapidly from the upper floors of a fire station to the ground floor, where the fire engines are housed. Because the pole occupies little floor space and allows a much faster descent than stairs, it became a standard feature of multi-storey fire stations from the late 19th century onward.

History

The sliding pole is credited to David Kenyon of Chicago's Engine Company 21, who in 1878 adapted a wooden hay-loft pole used for loading feed; the first brass pole was installed in Boston in 1880. The invention cut response times dramatically, since crews could reach the apparatus floor in seconds. What began as an improvised convenience became an icon of the fire service.

Decline

Many modern fire stations are built on a single level, and some services have removed poles because of injuries from falls or friction burns, replacing them with stairs or slides. Nevertheless, poles remain in use — and fondly regarded — in many older stations around the world.
