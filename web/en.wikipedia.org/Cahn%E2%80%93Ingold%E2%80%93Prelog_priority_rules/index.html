Cahn–Ingold–Prelog priority rules

The Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) priority rules are a standard method in organic chemistry for assigning priorities to the substituents of a molecule so that its stereochemistry can be described unambiguously. They are used to assign the descriptors R and S to stereocenters and E and Z to double bonds, as part of the IUPAC nomenclature of organic chemistry.

The rules were developed by Robert Sidney Cahn, Christopher Kelk Ingold, and Vladimir Prelog, first published in 1956 and refined in subsequent papers, notably in 1966.

The rules

Rule 1: Atoms directly attached to the stereocenter are ranked by atomic number; the higher atomic number receives higher priority. Rule 2: If a tie cannot be broken at the first atoms, the substituents of each tied atom are compared as sorted lists, working outward one sphere at a time until a difference is found. Rule 3: Multiple bonds are treated as if each bonded atom is duplicated: a double-bonded atom is duplicated once and a triple-bonded atom twice. Isotopes are ranked by atomic mass, and lone pairs are treated as atoms of atomic number zero.

Assigning R and S

To assign configuration, the molecule is oriented so the lowest-priority group points away from the viewer. If the sequence from priority 1 to 2 to 3 runs clockwise, the center is R (rectus); if counterclockwise, it is S (sinister). For alkenes, the higher-priority groups on each carbon are compared: if they lie on the same side, the descriptor is Z (zusammen); if opposite, E (entgegen).
