Ridda wars

The Ridda wars (Arabic: حُرُوب ٱلرِّدَّة, lit. 'Wars of Apostasy') were a series of military campaigns launched by the first caliph, Abu Bakr, against rebellious Arabian tribes during 632 and 633 CE, following the death of the Islamic prophet Muhammad.

Background

After Muhammad's death in 632, many tribes that had accepted his authority regarded their allegiance as personal to him, withheld the zakat tax, or rallied to rival claimants to prophecy, including Musaylima in the Yamama, Tulayha, Sajah and Aswad Ansi.

Campaigns

Abu Bakr insisted on the submission of all Arabia before sending the army of Usama ibn Zayd to Syria. His generals, most famously Khalid ibn al-Walid, defeated Tulayha at Buzakha and Musaylima at the hard-fought Battle of Yamama (633), where many memorizers of the Qur'an were killed — a loss that prompted the first compilation of the Qur'an. Campaigns in Bahrain, Oman, Yemen and Hadhramaut completed the reunification of Arabia.

Consequences

Victory consolidated the Rashidun Caliphate and made possible the subsequent Muslim conquests of Persia and the Levant.
