The Tiber (Italian: Tevere; Latin: Tiberis) is the third-longest river in Italy, rising in the Apennine Mountains and flowing 406 kilometres through Tuscany, Umbria and Lazio to the Tyrrhenian Sea. It is the river on whose banks Rome was founded and grew.

Course and history

From its source on Mount Fumaiolo, the Tiber flows south past Perugia and Todi, then through Rome, where Tiber Island divides the stream, before reaching the sea at Ostia, ancient Rome's port. The river was central to Roman trade and water supply but also prone to destructive floods, which prompted embankment works in the 19th and 20th centuries. In Roman myth the river god Tiberinus was said to have rescued Romulus and Remus, and the phrase "swimming the Tiber" became slang for converting to Catholicism.

The river today

Modern Rome's high embankments (the muraglioni) keep the river largely out of daily view, though its banks host summer festivals and cycle paths. Pollution and low flows remain environmental concerns for the basin.
