Macau or Macao (Portuguese: Macau; Chinese: 澶門), officially the Macao Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China (MSAR), is a city and special administrative region in the Pearl River Delta on the western shore of the Pearl River estuary, bordering Zhuhai (Guangdong) to the north and facing Hong Kong across the delta, some 60 km (37 mi) to the east. With a population of around 680,000 in an area of just 33 km², it is the most densely populated region in the world.

Macau was a Portuguese colony and trading post from 1557 until its handover to China in 1999, making it the first and last European colony in China. Long a major entrepôt linking China, Japan and Europe, it declined after Hong Kong's rise in the 19th century, then reinvented itself through gambling and tourism: since the liberalisation of the casino industry in 2002, Macau's gaming revenue has surpassed that of Las Vegas, and it is the only place in China where casino gambling is legal.

History

Portuguese settlement

Portuguese traders settled in Macau from 1557 with the acquiescence of Ming authorities, paying ground rent to China while administering the town themselves. Macau became the hub of the lucrative China–Japan silk-for-silver trade and a base for Jesuit missions to China and Japan; Matteo Ricci passed through in the 1580s. The Dutch attacked in 1622 and were repelled, an event still commemorated locally. China's 1887 Treaty of Peking recognised Portugal's "perpetual occupation and government" of Macau, and the colony survived the Second World War as neutral Portuguese territory, sheltering refugees.

Return to China

After the 1974 Carnation Revolution, Portugal offered to return Macau; the 1987 Sino-Portuguese Joint Declaration set the transfer for 20 December 1999. Under the Basic Law of Macau and the "one country, two systems" formula, Macau retains a high degree of autonomy, its own legal system (based on Portuguese civil law), currency and customs territory until at least 2049.

Geography

Macau consists of the Macau Peninsula and the islands of Taipa and Coloane, the latter two now joined by the Cotai reclamation, home to the Cotai Strip casino resorts. Extensive land reclamation has grown the territory from about 11 km² in the 19th century to nearly 33 km². The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, opened in 2018, is the world's longest sea crossing at 55 km.

Economy

Gambling and tourism dominate: before the COVID-19 pandemic, gaming contributed around half of GDP and over 70% of government revenue, and Macau received nearly 40 million visitors in 2019, mostly from mainland China. The economy contracted sharply under China's zero-COVID border controls (2020–22) and recovered after reopening in 2023. Macau also hosts the Macau Grand Prix street race (since 1954) and the Historic Centre of Macao, a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2005) blending Portuguese and Chinese architecture, including the Ruins of Saint Paul's.

Culture and languages

Cantonese is the everyday language of most residents; Portuguese remains an official language used in law and administration, and the creole Patuá is critically endangered. The Macanese community, of mixed Portuguese and Asian ancestry, maintains a distinct cuisine combining Portuguese, Chinese, Malay, Indian and African influences. Macau's public holidays and festivals blend Chinese and Catholic traditions.
