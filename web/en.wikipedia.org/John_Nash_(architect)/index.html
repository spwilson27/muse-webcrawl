John Nash (architect)

Born18 January 1752, Lambeth, London, England
Died13 May 1835 (aged 83), Cowes, Isle of Wight, England
OccupationArchitect
BuildingsBuckingham Palace, Royal Pavilion, Regent Street, Marble Arch

John Nash (18 January 1752 – 13 May 1835) was an English architect of the Georgian and Regency eras, responsible for the design of many London landmarks, including Buckingham Palace, Marble Arch, Regent Street and the layout of Regent's Park.

Early career

Trained under Sir Robert Taylor, Nash had a chequered early career in London and Wales, designing country houses and pioneering picturesque planning, before bankruptcy in 1783 forced him to rebuild his practice in Wales.

Work for the Prince Regent

Under the patronage of the Prince Regent, Nash remodelled the Royal Pavilion at Brighton into its famous Indian-style palace, and planned Regent Street and Regent's Park with its terraces and villas, creating the ceremonial route from Carlton House to the park. From 1825 he rebuilt Buckingham Palace, though escalating costs led to his dismissal in 1830.

Style and legacy

Nash blended classical orders, Italianate and Gothic revivals, and stucco-fronted urban design with a flair for picturesque composition. His terraces around Regent's Park and the procession route of Regent Street remain defining features of London.

He died at East Cowes Castle on the Isle of Wight in 1835.
