Venus

Venus is the second planet from the Sun. It is a terrestrial planet and is the closest in mass and size to its orbital neighbour Earth. Venus has by far the densest atmosphere of the terrestrial planets, composed mostly of carbon dioxide with clouds of sulfuric acid, and a surface pressure 92 times that of Earth and temperatures around 737 K (464 °C) — hot enough to melt lead — making it the hottest planet in the Solar System due to a runaway greenhouse effect.

Rotation

Venus rotates extremely slowly and backwards: its sidereal day of 243 Earth days is longer than its year, and its retrograde rotation means the Sun would appear to rise in the west. Its axis is tilted only about 2.6° (effectively 177.4°), so it has almost no seasons.

Surface and geology

Radar mapping by the Magellan orbiter revealed a young volcanic surface with vast lava plains, highland regions such as Ishtar Terra and Aphrodite Terra, and few impact craters, suggesting global resurfacing roughly 300–600 million years ago. Maxwell Montes is the highest point, about 11 km above the mean surface.

Observation and exploration

As the brightest planet in the sky, Venus has been known since prehistory as the "morning star" and "evening star", and shows phases when viewed through a telescope — an observation Galileo used as evidence for heliocentrism. The Soviet Venera probes achieved the first landings on another planet. Recent and planned missions (Akatsuki, DAVINCI, VERITAS, EnVision) aim to unravel how Venus diverged so drastically from Earth.
