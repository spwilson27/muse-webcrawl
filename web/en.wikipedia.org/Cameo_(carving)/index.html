Cameo (carving)

Cameo
MediumShell, agate, onyx, glass, coral
TechniqueRelief carving

A cameo is a method of carving an object into relief on a material with layers of contrasting colours, such that the design stands out against a background of a different colour. Cameos are most often carved in shell, agate, onyx or other banded stones, and are commonly used in jewellery such as brooches, pendants and rings.

History

Relief carving in layered stone dates to the ancient world; the Hellenistic and Roman periods produced masterpieces such as the Gemma Augustea and the Gonzaga Cameo. The art revived during the Renaissance and reached a popular peak in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, when shell cameos carved in Torre del Greco, Italy, became fashionable across Europe. Portrait cameos of classical deities, mythological scenes and profiles were especially popular in the Victorian era.

Technique

The carver exploits natural banding: the raised figure is cut in the lighter upper layer while the darker layer beneath forms the background. Shell cameos, typically from helmet or conch shells, show a white or pale figure on a pinkish-brown ground. Modern cameos may also be moulded in glass or resin, though hand-carved stone and shell pieces remain the most valued.
