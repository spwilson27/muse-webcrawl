Airbus SE is a European multinational aerospace corporation and the world's largest manufacturer of airliners for much of the period since 2019. It designs, manufactures and sells civil and military aircraft, helicopters, and space systems. The company grew out of the Airbus Industrie consortium founded in 1970 by French, German and later Spanish and British partners to compete with American manufacturers, principally Boeing.

History

The consortium's first product, the A300 (1972), was the world's first twin-engine widebody airliner. The A320 family (launched 1984) pioneered digital fly-by-wire controls in airliners and became one of the best-selling aircraft families in history. Airbus was restructured as an integrated company (EADS, later Airbus Group/SE) in 2000–2001. In 2017 it acquired a majority stake in the Bombardier CSeries programme, renamed the Airbus A220.

Products

Commercial aircraft include the A220, A320neo family, A330neo, A350 and the double-deck A380 (produced 2003–2021). Airbus Helicopters is a leading rotorcraft maker, and Airbus Defence and Space builds the A400M military transport, Eurofighter Typhoon (as a consortium partner), satellites and launch services through ArianeGroup.

Competition

Airbus and Boeing form a duopoly in large commercial aircraft, with a long-running rivalry and a series of trade disputes over government subsidies at the WTO.
