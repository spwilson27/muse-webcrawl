Celje Castle

Celje Castle (Slovene: Celjski grad) is a castle ruin in Celje, Slovenia, formerly the seat of the Counts of Celje, the most influential dynasty in late medieval Slovenia. It is the largest medieval castle in the country and stands on a hill southeast of the city centre, above the Savinja River.

History

The castle's origins go back to the 12th century, when a smaller fortification stood on the site under the Carinthian Heunburg family. After the Counts of Celje inherited the estate in the 14th century, they expanded it into a powerful fortress-palace with a large keep known as Frederick's Tower. The star-and-crown emblem of the Counts of Celje later became part of the coat of arms of Slovenia.

After the murder of Ulrich II, the last male Count of Celje, in 1456, the castle passed to the Habsburgs. It gradually lost importance, was quarried for building stone — some of it used for Celje's churches — and fell into ruin, though its romantic ruins inspired 19th-century visitors and painters.

Today

The castle has been partially restored and is a popular tourist attraction and venue for weddings, concerts, and summer events, including living-history performances. It offers views over Celje and the Savinja valley.
