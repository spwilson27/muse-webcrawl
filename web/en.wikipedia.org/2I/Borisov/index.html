2I/Borisov, originally designated C/2019 Q4 (Borisov), is the first observed interstellar comet and the second observed interstellar object after 1I/ʻOumuamua. It was discovered by Crimean amateur astronomer Gennadiy Vladimirovich Borisov on 30 August 2019 using a 0.65-metre telescope he had designed and built himself.

Trajectory

Borisov arrived from the direction of the constellation Cassiopeia with a hyperbolic excess velocity of roughly 32 km/s, far too fast to be bound to the Sun. Its orbit has an eccentricity of about 3.36, the highest ever recorded for a comet at the time. It reached perihelion at about 2.0 AU on 8 December 2019 and passed about 1.9 AU from Earth later that month before heading back into interstellar space toward the constellation Telescopium.

Physical characteristics

Unlike the enigmatic ʻOumuamua, Borisov looked like an ordinary comet, with a dusty coma and a short tail driven by sublimating ices. Observations with the Hubble Space Telescope constrained its nucleus to less than about one kilometre across, with later estimates suggesting roughly 400–500 metres. Its composition appeared broadly similar to Solar System comets, though unusually rich in carbon monoxide, which hinted that it formed in a cold region around a red dwarf star.

Significance

Because its interstellar origin was confirmed while it was still approaching, astronomers worldwide were able to study an object formed around another star in unprecedented detail, demonstrating that interstellar comets can resemble our own comets closely.
