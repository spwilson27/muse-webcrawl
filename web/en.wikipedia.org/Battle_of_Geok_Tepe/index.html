Battle of Geok Tepe

The Battle of Geok Tepe was the decisive engagement of the Russian conquest of Central Asia, fought in December 1880 – January 1881 at the fortress of Geok Tepe (Gökdepe) in present-day Turkmenistan. A Russian army under General Mikhail Skobelev stormed the stronghold of the Akhal-Teke Turkmens after a formal siege, completing the subjugation of the Turkmen tribes.

Background

A first Russian expedition against Geok Tepe in 1879 had been repulsed with heavy losses. Skobelev returned in 1880 with some 7,000 men, artillery and a carefully organised supply line across the desert. Inside the great mud-walled fortress, the Teke had concentrated perhaps 20,000–25,000 people, fighters and families, under leaders including Makhdum Kuli Khan and the young Berdi Murad.

The siege and storm

Skobelev invested the fortress, dug approach trenches under fire, and bombarded the walls. On 24 January 1881 (12 January Old Style), Russian engineers exploded a mine under the wall, and assault columns stormed through the breach. The defenders and thousands of non-combatants fled into the desert, pursued by Russian cavalry; estimates of Turkmen dead range from several thousand to as many as 20,000, while Russian losses were a few hundred.

Aftermath

The fall of Geok Tepe broke Turkmen resistance; Ashgabat was occupied days later and the Akhal oasis annexed, followed by the submission of Merv in 1884. The battle remains a central event in Turkmen national memory, commemorated by the great Saparmurat Hajji Mosque at the site, while Skobelev's reported maxim — that the harder the blow, the longer the peace — became emblematic of the conquest's brutality.
