Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala

Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) is a landmark decision of the Supreme Court of India that established the basic structure doctrine of the Constitution of India.

Background

Swami Kesavananda Bharati challenged Kerala land reform laws and constitutional amendments. A 13-judge bench — the largest ever — heard the case for 68 days.

Judgment

By 7–6, the Court held that Parliament may amend the Constitution but cannot alter its basic structure, including judicial review, secularism, federalism and fundamental rights' essence. It remains foundational to Indian constitutional law.
