The Arctic Circle is one of the two polar circles and the most northerly of the five major circles of latitude shown on maps of Earth. It marks the latitude above which, for at least one day each year, the sun does not set (the "midnight sun") at the summer solstice and does not rise at the winter solstice (the polar night).

Position

Because Earth's axial tilt fluctuates, the Arctic Circle is slowly drifting northward, currently at roughly 66°33′49″ N. The region north of it is the Arctic, covering about 4% of Earth's surface.

Climate and habitation

Despite the harsh climate, several million people live within the Arctic Circle, concentrated in northern Russia and Scandinavia. The largest cities include Murmansk and Norilsk in Russia and Tromsø in Norway.
