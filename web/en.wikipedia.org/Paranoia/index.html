Paranoia

FieldPsychiatry, psychology
SymptomsDistrust, hypervigilance, persecutory beliefs
Related conditionsDelusional disorder, paranoid personality disorder, schizophrenia

Paranoia is an instinct or thought process that is believed to be heavily influenced by anxiety or fear, often to the point of delusion and irrationality. Paranoid thinking typically includes persecutory beliefs, or beliefs of conspiracy concerning a perceived threat towards oneself (e.g. "Everyone is out to get me"). The word derives from the Greek paránoia, "madness" or "deranged mind".

Symptoms

Common features include intense and irrational mistrust or suspicion, hypervigilance, difficulty with forgiveness, and a defensive attitude in response to imagined criticism. People experiencing paranoia may misinterpret benign remarks or events as hostile, bear grudges persistently, and perceive themselves as the target of plots. Paranoid ideation exists on a continuum in the general population, from everyday suspiciousness to fixed persecutory delusions.

Causes

Contributors identified in research include genetic vulnerability, disrupted dopamine signalling, sleep deprivation, social adversity, trauma, discrimination, hearing loss, and use of drugs such as cannabis, cocaine, and amphetamines. Cognitive models emphasise reasoning biases — jumping to conclusions, attributing negative events to hostile external causes, and difficulties in mentalising — together with low self-esteem and worry.

Diagnosis and related conditions

Paranoia is a feature of several diagnoses. In delusional disorder, persecutory type, a single system of delusions dominates. Paranoid personality disorder is a pervasive pattern of distrust beginning by early adulthood. Persecutory delusions are among the most common symptoms of schizophrenia, in which paranoia was historically used to name a subtype. Paranoia can also occur in dementia, bipolar mania, and severe depression with psychotic features.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying condition and may include antipsychotic medication and psychological therapies. Cognitive behavioural therapy for psychosis aims to reduce conviction and distress by examining evidence for threat beliefs and reducing safety behaviours such as avoidance and hypervigilant scanning.

In culture

"Paranoia" also denotes, informally, exaggerated suspicion in politics and culture; conspiracy theories are a common social expression of paranoid styles of thought.
