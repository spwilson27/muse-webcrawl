Einstein on the Beach

Composer: Philip Glass

Director/designer: Robert Wilson

Premiere: 25 July 1976, Festival d'Avignon, France

Duration: about 4½–5 hours, no intermission

Libretto: Christopher Knowles, Samuel M. Johnson, Lucinda Childs, Philip Glass

Einstein on the Beach is an opera in four acts composed by Philip Glass with stage direction and design by Robert Wilson. It premiered in Avignon on 25 July 1976 and was subsequently performed at the Metropolitan Opera House in New York, where its two sold-out performances became a landmark in the history of avant-garde theatre. The work broke with nearly every convention of opera: it has no linear narrative, no conventional libretto, runs about five hours without intermission (audience members may come and go), and portrays Albert Einstein only obliquely, through recurring images — trains, a trial, a spaceship, a violin-playing Einstein figure — associated with his life and with the nuclear age he helped inaugurate.

Structure

The opera is built from Glass's minimalist music — repeating arpeggiated patterns, additive rhythms, and amplified ensemble of winds, keyboards, voices, and solo violin — combined with Wilson's slow-moving, precisely lit stage pictures. Five short interludes called "Knee Plays" connect the acts. The sung and spoken texts consist largely of numbers, solfège syllables, and writings by Christopher Knowles, Samuel M. Johnson (who played the Judge), and choreographer Lucinda Childs; the final scene includes a text about lovers on a park bench by Mr. Johnson. The acts move through three great themes: the Train, the Trial, and the Field/Spaceship.

Production history and legacy

Despite its triumph, the original production left Glass and Wilson in debt, famously forcing Glass to work as a taxi driver afterward. Major revivals were mounted in 1984 (Brooklyn Academy of Music), 1992, and a world tour in 2012–2016 that brought the work to London, Los Angeles, and elsewhere to wide acclaim. Einstein on the Beach is the first panel of Glass's "Portrait Trilogy", followed by Satyagraha (about Gandhi) and Akhnaten. It is widely regarded as one of the most influential stage works of the twentieth century, establishing minimalism in opera and redefining the possibilities of music theatre.
