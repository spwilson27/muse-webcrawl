Maryland is a state in the Mid-Atlantic region of the United States. It borders Virginia to its south, West Virginia to its west, Pennsylvania to its north, Delaware and the Atlantic Ocean to its east, and the national capital of Washington, D.C., which it surrounds on three sides. With a total area of 12,407 square miles, Maryland is the ninth-smallest state by land area, but the fifth-most densely populated. Its capital is Annapolis, and its most populous city is Baltimore.

History

Maryland began as the Province of Maryland, an English colony founded in 1634 by Cecil Calvert, 2nd Baron Baltimore, as a haven for English Catholics, and named for Queen Henrietta Maria. It became the seventh state to ratify the U.S. Constitution in 1788. During the Civil War, Maryland — a slave state that remained in the Union — was the site of the Battle of Antietam (1862), the bloodiest single day in American history. Francis Scott Key wrote "The Star-Spangled Banner" while witnessing the bombardment of Fort McHenry in Baltimore harbor in 1814.

Geography and economy

The Chesapeake Bay divides the state, with the Eastern Shore lying on the Delmarva Peninsula. Maryland's terrain ranges from Atlantic beaches and bay wetlands through the Piedmont to Appalachian ridges in the far west, earning it the nickname "Little America". The economy is anchored by the federal government and its contractors around Washington, biotechnology along the I-270 corridor, the Port of Baltimore, Johns Hopkins University and its medical institutions, and military installations such as Fort Meade, home of the National Security Agency.
