Dvaraka (Sanskrit: द्वारका, Dvārakā, "the many-gated [city]") is a legendary ancient city in Hindu mythology, described as the capital of the Yadava kingdom and the dwelling place of the god Krishna after he left Mathura. It is prominent in the Mahabharata, the Harivamsa, and the Puranas, and is counted among the Sapta Puri, the seven sacred cities of Hinduism, as well as one of the four Char Dham pilgrimage sites. The modern city of Dwarka in Gujarat is identified with it.

Description in scripture

According to the texts, Dvaraka was built on land reclaimed from the sea at Krishna's request—Varuna granted twelve yojanas of land—and was constructed by the divine architect Vishvakarma (in some accounts with the help of Garuda bringing Mount Raivata's materials). The city had wide boulevards, hundreds of palaces of gold and crystal, gardens, lakes, and fortified walls rising from the sea. The Harivamsa and Bhagavata Purana describe Sudharma, its royal assembly hall, and the palaces of Krishna's queens, including Rukmini and Satyabhama.

The move from Mathura

The texts recount that Krishna led the Yadavas from Mathura to escape repeated attacks by Jarasandha of Magadha and his ally Kalayavana, earning Krishna the epithet Ranchhod ("he who left the battlefield"). Dvaraka's island-like, sea-protected location made it impregnable, and it became a prosperous maritime capital and port trading with distant lands.

Submergence

After Krishna's death at Prabhasa and the self-destruction of the Yadava clan in the Mausala Parva of the Mahabharata, Dvaraka is said to have been swallowed by the sea, witnessed by Arjuna after Krishna's departure from the world. The submergence is traditionally dated to the beginning of the Kali Yuga, conventionally 3102 BCE. Pilgrims continue to visit the Dwarkadhish Temple, dedicated to Krishna as Dwarkadhish, "King of Dwarka."

Archaeology

Marine archaeology off modern Dwarka and Bet Dwarka, conducted since the 1980s by the National Institute of Oceanography and S. R. Rao, has reported submerged stone structures, anchors, and pottery in the Gulf of Khambhat region and off Dwarka. Onshore excavations at Dwarka found evidence of settlement layers; however, claims associating the submerged remains with the legendary city and very early dates remain debated among archaeologists, and the earliest securely dated occupation is generally placed in the early centuries BCE/CE.
