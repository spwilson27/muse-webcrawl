Big data

Big data refers to data sets that are too large or complex to be dealt with by traditional data-processing application software. Data with many entries (rows) offer greater statistical power, while data with higher complexity (more attributes or columns) may lead to a higher false discovery rate.

Characteristics

Big data is commonly characterised by the "three Vs": volume (the quantity of data), velocity (the speed at which it is generated and processed), and variety (the range of data types and sources). Later formulations added veracity and value. Sources include sensors, transactions, social media, scientific instruments, and the Internet of things.

Technologies

Processing big data commonly relies on distributed computing frameworks such as Apache Hadoop and Apache Spark, NoSQL databases, and cloud storage. Analytical approaches include data mining, stream processing, and machine learning, which can reveal patterns not apparent at smaller scales.

Applications and concerns

Applications range from genomics and climate modelling to fraud detection, logistics, and targeted advertising. Big data raises concerns about privacy, surveillance, data quality, and algorithmic bias, and has driven regulation such as the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation.
