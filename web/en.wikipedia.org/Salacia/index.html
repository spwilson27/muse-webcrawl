Salacia

Salacia is the goddess of salt water in Roman mythology, consort of Neptune, god of the sea. She personifies the calm, sunlit aspect of the ocean and is often identified with the Greek goddess Amphitrite, wife of Poseidon.

Mythology

According to a tale retold by later writers, Neptune desired Salacia but she fled his advances and hid in the Atlantic Ocean. Neptune sent a dolphin to find her and persuade her to return; in gratitude the dolphin was placed among the stars as the constellation Delphinus, and Salacia became Neptune's queen. With Neptune she was the mother of Triton, the merman herald of the sea.

The name Salacia derives from Latin sal, "salt." She was associated both with the sea's bounty and its dangers: Varro and other authors connect her name with the surging motion of the waves. As Salacia Neptuni she received cult alongside Neptune, whose festival, the Neptunalia, was held on 23 July.

In astronomy

The trans-Neptunian object 120347 Salacia, a large Kuiper belt object discovered in 2004 and considered a likely dwarf planet, is named after the goddess. Its moon is named Actaea, after a Nereid.
