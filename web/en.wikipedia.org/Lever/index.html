Lever

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lever amplifies an input force to provide a greater output force.

A lever is a simple machine consisting of a beam or rigid rod pivoted at a fixed hinge, or fulcrum. It is one of the six classical simple machines defined by Renaissance scientists. A lever amplifies an input force to provide a greater output force, which is said to provide leverage, whose ratio is the mechanical advantage of the lever. This principle of leverage was first described in the 3rd century BC by Archimedes, who is famously quoted as saying, "Give me a place to stand, and I shall move the Earth with it."

History

The earliest evidence for the lever can be dated to around 5000 BC, when it was used in a simple balance scale. By about 4400 BC in Egypt, a foot pedal was used for a horizontal loom, and the shadoof, a crane-like device that uses a lever, was in use in Mesopotamia by about 3000 BC. The earliest remaining writings about levers are provided by Archimedes in the 3rd century BC. In his work On the Equilibrium of Planes, Archimedes proved the law of the lever mathematically.

Force and levers

The force applied at the ends of the lever is proportional to the ratio of the lengths of the lever arm measured between the fulcrum and the application point of the force. In idealized form, the mechanical advantage is given by the ratio of the effort arm to the load arm. The law of the lever states that, at equilibrium, the torque (force multiplied by distance from the fulcrum) on one side equals the torque on the other side. A small force applied over a long distance can therefore balance or move a much larger load over a short distance.

Classes of lever

Levers are classified by the relative positions of the fulcrum, effort, and load. In a class 1 lever, the fulcrum is between the effort and the load, as in a seesaw or crowbar. In a class 2 lever, the load is between the fulcrum and the effort, as in a wheelbarrow or bottle opener; these always provide mechanical advantage. In a class 3 lever, the effort is between the fulcrum and the load, as in a pair of tweezers or the human forearm; these sacrifice force for speed and range of motion.

Levers in the body

The human body contains many levers formed by bones (the beam), joints (the fulcrum), and muscles (the effort). The forearm during a biceps curl is a class 3 lever; standing on tiptoe uses the foot as a class 2 lever.
