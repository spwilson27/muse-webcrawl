The Godfather is a 1972 American epic crime film directed by Francis Ford Coppola, who co-wrote the screenplay with Mario Puzo, based on Puzo's best-selling 1969 novel. It stars Marlon Brando as Vito Corleone, the aging patriarch of an organized crime dynasty, and Al Pacino as his youngest son Michael, whose reluctant descent into the family business forms the film's dramatic arc. Widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made, it won the Academy Awards for Best Picture, Best Actor (Brando), and Best Adapted Screenplay.

Plot

In 1945 New York, Don Vito Corleone presides over his daughter's wedding while conducting the business of his crime family. When he refuses to invest in the narcotics trade proposed by the dealer Virgil "the Turk" Sollozzo, an assassination attempt leaves him gravely wounded and ignites a war among the Five Families. His son Michael, a decorated war veteran who had intended to stay out of the family business, murders Sollozzo and a corrupt police captain, hides in Sicily, and after the death of his brother Sonny returns to take control. By the film's end Michael has eliminated the heads of the rival families and become the new Don, closing the door — literally — on his wife Kay's questions about his crimes.

Production

Paramount Pictures, skeptical of the gangster genre, hired the young Coppola partly because of his Italian heritage and low salary. Casting was contentious: the studio resisted both Brando, considered box-office poison, and the unknown Pacino. Gordon Willis's low-key cinematography and Nino Rota's score became hallmarks of the film. Released in March 1972, it was the highest-grossing film of its year.

Legacy

The film revitalized the gangster genre, influenced generations of filmmakers, and embedded phrases such as "I'm going to make him an offer he can't refuse" in popular culture. It was followed by The Godfather Part II (1974), regarded by many as its equal, and The Godfather Part III (1990). In 1990 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
