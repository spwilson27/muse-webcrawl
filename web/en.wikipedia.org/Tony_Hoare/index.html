Sir Charles Antony Richard Hoare (born 11 January 1934), generally known as Tony Hoare or C. A. R. Hoare, is a British computer scientist who has made foundational contributions to sorting algorithms, program verification, and concurrent programming. He received the ACM Turing Award in 1980 "for his fundamental contributions to the definition and design of programming languages".

Career and contributions

Hoare studied classics and philosophy at Oxford before doing national service and graduate work in statistics and Russian translation. Hired by the computer manufacturer Elliott Brothers in 1960, he developed Quicksort (1960–61), still among the most widely used sorting algorithms, while working on an early sorting routine and on an ALGOL 60 compiler.

In 1968 he became professor at Queen's University Belfast and in 1977 moved to Oxford, where he led the Programming Research Group until his retirement in 1999, afterwards joining Microsoft Research in Cambridge. His 1969 paper "An Axiomatic Basis for Computer Programming" introduced Hoare logic, the system of preconditions and postconditions ({P} C {Q}) used to reason rigorously about program correctness. His model of Communicating Sequential Processes (CSP, 1978; book 1985), with its process-algebraic treatment of concurrency, influenced languages such as occam and, later, Go's channels.

The null reference

Hoare famously apologised for inventing the null reference in ALGOL W in 1965, calling it (in a 2009 talk) his "billion-dollar mistake", because of the countless program errors, crashes and vulnerabilities it later caused. He was knighted in 2000 for services to education and computer science.
