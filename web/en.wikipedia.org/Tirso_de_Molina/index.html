Tirso de Molina (born Gabriel Téllez, 24 March 1579 – 12 March 1648) was a Spanish Baroque playwright, poet and Mercedarian friar. He is one of the most prolific dramatists of Spain's Golden Age and is best remembered as the creator of Don Juan in El burlador de Sevilla y convidado de piedra (The Trickster of Seville and the Stone Guest), the first dramatisation of the Don Juan legend.

Life

Téllez was born in Madrid and studied at the University of Alcalá. In 1600 he entered the Order of Mercy (Mercedarians), taking the religious name by which he wrote. He travelled widely in Spain on the order's business, spent two years (1616–1618) in Santo Domingo in the New World, and later served as the order's official historian and as a commander in Trujillo. His plays, which satirised the nobility and court, brought him into conflict with the authorities: in 1625 the Council of Castile ordered him to stop writing for the theatre, and he was exiled from Madrid to the monastery of Cuenca for a time. He nevertheless continued to write prose, including the miscellanies Cigarrales de Toledo (1624) and Deleitar aprovechando (1635).

Works

Tirso claimed to have written some 400 plays, of which about 80 survive. His output includes biblical dramas, historical plays, saints' plays and comedies of intrigue. El burlador de Sevilla (c. 1616–1630) introduced Don Juan Tenorio, whose damnation after seducing and dishonouring a series of women became one of the enduring myths of European literature, later taken up by Molière, Mozart and da Ponte, Byron and many others. Other celebrated works include El condenado por desconfiado (The Doubted Damned), a theological drama often ranked among the finest of the Spanish stage, Don Gil de las calzas verdes, and Marta la piadosa.

Style and influence

Tirso's comedies are noted for their strong, resourceful female characters, intricate plots, and psychological depth. A follower of Lope de Vega's comedia nueva, he nonetheless developed an independent voice marked by moral seriousness and irony.
