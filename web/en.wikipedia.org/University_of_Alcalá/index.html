University of Alcalá

Type: Public university
Established: 1499 (refounded 1977)
Location: Alcalá de Henares, Spain
Students: ~28,000

The University of Alcalá (Universidad de Alcalá, UAH) is a public university in Alcalá de Henares, in the Community of Madrid, Spain. It was originally founded by Cardinal Francisco Jiménez de Cisneros in 1499 as one of the great Renaissance universities, famed for the Complutensian Polyglot Bible. Suppressed in the nineteenth century when its faculties moved to Madrid, the university was refounded in 1977.

Heritage

The historic campus in the old city — whose university quarter is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — includes the Colegio Mayor de San Ildefonso, where the Premio Cervantes, the most important literary prize in the Spanish-speaking world, is presented annually by the King of Spain in the Paraninfo hall. Miguel de Cervantes was born in Alcalá, and alumni include Lope de Vega and St John of the Cross.

Modern university

The modern UAH operates campuses in Alcalá and Guadalajara and offers degrees across the sciences, medicine, humanities, engineering and social sciences.
