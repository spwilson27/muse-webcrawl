The Panavia Tornado is a family of twin-engine, variable-sweep wing multirole combat aircraft, jointly developed and manufactured by Italy, the United Kingdom, and West Germany through the Panavia Aircraft GmbH consortium. There are three primary Tornado variants: the Tornado IDS (interdictor/strike) fighter-bomber, the Tornado ECR (electronic combat/reconnaissance) SEAD aircraft, and the Tornado ADV (air defence variant) interceptor, the last operated principally by the Royal Air Force.

The Tornado's variable-sweep wing allows efficient flight at both low and high speeds.

Development

The Tornado emerged from a multinational study begun in 1968 to replace various aircraft in European NATO air forces with a single type capable of low-level strike missions. The first prototype flew on 14 August 1974, and deliveries began in 1979. The aircraft was designed to penetrate hostile airspace at very low altitude and high subsonic speed, using terrain-following radar, and to deliver conventional and nuclear weapons. It is powered by two Turbo-Union RB199 turbofan engines and crewed by a pilot and a navigator/weapons systems officer.

Operational history

Tornados saw extensive combat service, beginning with the 1991 Gulf War, in which British, Italian, and Saudi aircraft flew low-level attack missions, and later over the Balkans, Iraq, Afghanistan, Libya, and against Islamic State targets in Iraq and Syria. The RAF retired its Tornados in 2019; Germany and Italy have planned to replace theirs with types including the F-35 and Eurofighter, while Saudi Arabia continues to operate upgraded IDS aircraft.
