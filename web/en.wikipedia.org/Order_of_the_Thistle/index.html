Order of the Thistle

The Most Ancient and Most Noble Order of the Thistle is an order of chivalry associated with Scotland. The present order was instituted by Queen Anne in 1703, who revived a foundation attributed to James VII in 1687 and, by legend, to much earlier Scottish kings. It is the second-most senior order of chivalry in the United Kingdom, ranking after the Order of the Garter, and is the Scottish counterpart to it.


The star of the Order of the Thistle

Membership

The order consists of the sovereign and sixteen Knights and Ladies (KT/LT), with additional royal and extra knights. Appointments are in the personal gift of the monarch and are typically made for distinguished public service in Scotland. The motto is Nemo me impune lacessit ("No one provokes me with impunity"), shared with Scotland itself. The thistle, the national flower of Scotland, is the order's emblem; its chapel is the Thistle Chapel in St Giles' Cathedral, Edinburgh, completed in 1911.

Insignia

The insignia include a star bearing St Andrew's cross with a thistle at its center, a badge (the "jewel") showing St Andrew holding his cross, and a collar of thistles and rue sprigs. Knights wear a dark green sash and mantle. Vacant stalls in the chapel bear the helmets, crests, and banners of deceased knights, in ceremonies centered on St Andrew's Day.
