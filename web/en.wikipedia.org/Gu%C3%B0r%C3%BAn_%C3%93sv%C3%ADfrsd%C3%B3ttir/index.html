Guðrún Ósvífrsdóttir

Guðrún Ósvífrsdóttir (c. 974 – 1060) is the principal female character of Laxdæla saga, one of the best-known Icelandic family sagas, and also appears in several other sagas. Renowned in the saga tradition for her beauty and pride, she was married four times, and her love for Kjartan Óláfsson and her marriage to his cousin and foster-brother Bolli Þorleiksson form the tragic centre of the saga, culminating in Kjartan's killing by Bolli's band.

Life in the saga

Guðrún grew up at Laugar in the Laxárdalur valley. Her four prophetic dreams, interpreted by Gestr Oddleifsson, were said to foretell her four marriages. After brief marriages to Þorvaldr Halldórsson and Þórðr Ingunnarson (the latter drowned), she loved Kjartan Óláfsson, but while he was abroad in Norway she was persuaded that he would not return and married his foster-brother Bolli instead. On Kjartan's return, rivalry between the households escalated until Bolli and his brothers ambushed and killed Kjartan in 1003; Bolli was in turn slain in vengeance by Kjartan's brothers in 1007.

Later years

Guðrún married her fourth husband, the chieftain Þorkell Eyjólfsson, who drowned in 1026. She spent her last years at Helgafell, becoming a nun and anchoress, and is traditionally regarded as the first Icelandic woman to become a nun. Asked by her son Bolli which of her husbands she had loved most, she gave the saga's famous, ambiguous answer: "I was worst to the one I loved most."
