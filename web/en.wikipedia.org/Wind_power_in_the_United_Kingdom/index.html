Wind power in the United Kingdom is the country's largest source of renewable electricity. The UK has some of the best wind resources in Europe, particularly offshore in the North Sea and Irish Sea, and has become a world leader in offshore wind generation.

History and development

Modern development began with small onshore farms in the 1990s, such as Delabole in Cornwall, and accelerated in the 2000s with large offshore projects. Successive rounds of leasing by the Crown Estate and subsidy schemes (Renewables Obligation, then Contracts for Difference) drove rapid growth and steep cost reductions. The Hornsea projects off Yorkshire are among the largest offshore wind farms in the world, and the Dogger Bank project is set to surpass them with a capacity of about 3.6 GW.

Role in the energy system

Wind regularly supplies a quarter or more of UK electricity and on some days more than half, though its variability requires backup, storage, and interconnection. Government targets under net-zero policy foresee a large further expansion of offshore capacity by 2030.
