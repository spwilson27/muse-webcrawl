ACCOBAMS

ACCOBAMS — the Agreement on the Conservation of Cetaceans of the Black Sea, Mediterranean Sea and contiguous Atlantic area — is an international treaty aimed at reducing threats to whales, dolphins, and porpoises in those waters. It was concluded in 1996 under the auspices of the Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species (the Bonn Convention) and entered into force on 1 June 2001.

Scope

The agreement area covers the Black Sea, the Mediterranean Sea, and the contiguous Atlantic waters west of the Strait of Gibraltar. More than twenty range states are parties. All cetacean species present in the area — around thirty, including the fin whale, sperm whale, and Black Sea harbour porpoise — are covered.

Conservation measures

Parties commit to prohibiting the deliberate taking of cetaceans, reducing bycatch in fisheries, controlling noise pollution and ship strikes, and establishing a network of marine protected areas. Landmark measures include the Pelagos Sanctuary for Mediterranean marine mammals, created jointly by France, Italy, and Monaco.

Organization

The agreement's secretariat is based in Monaco. Meetings of the Parties set conservation plans and research priorities, such as the ACCOBAMS Survey Initiative, a basin-wide census of cetacean abundance conducted in 2018.
