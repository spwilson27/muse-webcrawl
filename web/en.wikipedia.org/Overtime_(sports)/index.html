Overtime (sports)

Overtime or extra time is an additional period of play specified under the rules of a sport to bring a game to a decision and avoid it being declared a tie or draw where the scores are level. Different sports impose different overtime procedures and terminology.

Association football

In knockout football matches, a draw after 90 minutes is followed by extra time of two 15-minute periods; if still tied, the match is decided by a penalty shoot-out. The "golden goal" and "silver goal" variants, under which the first goal in extra time ended or effectively decided the match, were abolished by the IFAB in 2004.

North American sports

In American and Canadian football, overtime typically guarantees each team a possession in the regular season, with modified formats in the playoffs. Ice hockey's NHL uses a short 3-on-3 sudden-death period followed by a shootout in the regular season, and continuous 5-on-5 sudden-death periods in the playoffs. Basketball uses five-minute overtime periods until a winner emerges.

Other formats

Baseball proceeds to extra innings; tennis historically used advantage final sets but now employs tiebreaks; rugby codes use extra time and, in some cases, drop-goal "golden point" rules.
