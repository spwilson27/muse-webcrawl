The Santa Lucia Mountains (also called the Santa Lucia Range) are a mountain range in the California Coast Ranges of central California. They extend for roughly 170 km from Monterey County southeastward into San Luis Obispo County, rising steeply from the Pacific Ocean along the Big Sur coast — one of the steepest coastal slopes in the continental United States.

Geography and nature

The range's highest point is Junipero Serra Peak (1,785 m). Much of the range lies within Los Padres National Forest, including the Ventana Wilderness. The rugged terrain supports chaparral, oak woodland, and groves of Santa Lucia fir, an endemic conifer, and the canyons shelter the southernmost natural stands of coast redwood. California condors, reintroduced in the region, soar over the range.

History and culture

The range was named by the Portolá expedition in 1769 in honour of Saint Lucy. It was home to the Esselen and Salinan peoples. Notable sites include Mission San Antonio de Padua, the Hearst Castle estate at San Simeon on the range's western edge, and the New Camaldoli Hermitage. Highway 1 winds along the range's seaward face and is famous for its dramatic scenery.
