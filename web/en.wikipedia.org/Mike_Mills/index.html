Mike Mills

Michael Edward Mills (born 17 December 1958) is an American multi-instrumentalist, singer, and composer who co-founded the alternative rock band R.E.M. and served as its bass guitarist and backing vocalist throughout its career from 1980 to 2011. Mills contributed piano, keyboards, guitar, and occasional lead vocals, and co-wrote many of the band's songs, including "What's the Frequency, Kenneth?", "Nightswimming" (music), and "Texarkana".

Early life and R.E.M.

Mills was born in California and raised in Macon, Georgia, where he met drummer Bill Berry in school. He attended the University of Georgia in Athens, where he, Berry, Peter Buck, and Michael Stipe formed R.E.M. in 1980. The band became a cornerstone of American alternative rock, progressing from college-radio favourites (Murmur, 1983) to global stardom with Out of Time (1991) and Automatic for the People (1992).

Musicianship

Mills's melodic, contrapuntal bass lines and harmony vocals are signature elements of R.E.M.'s sound; his high backing harmonies underpin songs such as "Fall on Me" and "Near Wild Heaven", the latter sung as a duet with Stipe. He is also an accomplished pianist and arranged much of the band's orchestral material. Mills sang lead on a handful of tracks, including "Texarkana" and a cover of The Clique's "Superman".

After R.E.M.

Following the band's amicable split in 2011, Mills has performed with the Baseball Project and as part of Big Star tribute performances, composed a concerto for violin and rock band (Concerto for Violin, Rock Band and String Orchestra, 2016) with Robert McDuffie, and toured with the ensemble project. He remains an active commentator on music and politics on social media.
