The Rouran Khaganate was a confederation of nomadic tribes on the Eurasian Steppe, dominant in Mongolia and northern China from the late 4th century until the mid-6th century AD. It was the first polity known to use the title khagan (qaghan) for its supreme ruler, a title later adopted by the Turks and Mongols. The Rouran are sometimes identified with the Avars who later appeared in Europe, though this remains debated.

Origins

The Rouran emerged from the Xianbei-descended peoples of the eastern steppe. Around 402, the leader Shelun unified the tribes and took the title of khagan, organising the confederation on military lines with decimal units borrowed in part from Chinese practice. Their power stretched from Manchuria to the Altai Mountains and from the Gobi Desert to Lake Baikal.

Relations with China

The khaganate was a persistent rival of the Northern Wei dynasty, which fought repeated campaigns against it and strengthened the northern frontier walls; the Chinese general and poet commemorated in the Ballad of Mulan is traditionally placed in a soldier's family serving in these wars. Relations alternated between warfare, tribute, trade, and marriage alliances, and the Rouran intervened in the politics of the Chinese successor states.

Fall

In 552 the Rouran's own subject tribe, the Göktürks under Bumin, revolted. Bumin defeated the Rouran khagan Anagui, who killed himself; the last khagans were captured and executed by 555 with Northern Qi complicity. Rouran remnants fled west, and the First Turkic Khaganate succeeded them as the steppe's dominant power.
