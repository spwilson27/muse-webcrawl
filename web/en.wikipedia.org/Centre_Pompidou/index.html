Centre Pompidou

Established31 January 1977
LocationPlace Georges-Pompidou, Paris, France
ArchitectsRenzo Piano, Richard Rogers, Gianfranco Franchini
TypeModern art museum, library, music centre

The Centre Pompidou (French pronunciation: [sɑ̃tʁ pɔ̃pidu]), more fully the Centre national d'art et de culture Georges-Pompidou, also known as the Pompidou Centre in English, is a complex building in the Beaubourg area of the 4th arrondissement of Paris, near Les Halles and the Marais. It was designed in the style of high-tech architecture by the architectural team of Renzo Piano, Richard Rogers, and Gianfranco Franchini. It is named after Georges Pompidou, the President of France from 1969 to 1974 who commissioned the building, and was officially opened on 31 January 1977 by President Valéry Giscard d'Estaing.

The centre houses the Bibliothèque publique d'information, a vast public library; the Musée National d'Art Moderne, the largest museum for modern art in Europe; and IRCAM, a centre for music and acoustic research. Because of its location, the centre is known locally as Beaubourg. The Place Georges Pompidou in front of it is noted for the presence of street performers, such as mimes and jugglers.

Architecture

The building is famous for its "inside-out" design: all of its functional structural elements—escalators, ducts, pipes, and circulation—are placed on the exterior of the building, freeing the interior floors as uninterrupted open spaces. The services are color-coded: blue for air, green for water, yellow for electricity, and red for circulation and movement, including the famous external escalator enclosed in a transparent tube that climbs the west façade and offers panoramic views of Paris.

The design emerged from an international competition in 1971 that attracted 681 entries. The young team of Piano, Rogers, and Franchini won with a flexible, free-plan structure supported by a cast-steel gerberette system. Initially controversial—critics compared it to an oil refinery—the building has become one of the most beloved landmarks of Paris and a canonical work of high-tech architecture.

Museum

The Musée National d'Art Moderne holds one of the world's great collections of modern and contemporary art, with over 100,000 works from 1905 onward, including major holdings of Kandinsky, Matisse, Picasso, Duchamp, Miró, and Pollock. Temporary exhibitions on the sixth floor have drawn millions of visitors. The centre has been among the most visited cultural institutions in France.

Renovation

From late 2023, the Centre Pompidou closed for a major renovation program lasting several years, addressing asbestos removal, accessibility, energy performance, and a redesign of spaces, with reopening planned toward the end of the decade. During the closure, works from the collection travel to partner institutions, and a satellite venue, Centre Pompidou-Metz, opened in 2010, continues to present exhibitions.
