Francis Ysidro Edgeworth FBA (8 February 1845 – 13 February 1926) was an Anglo-Irish philosopher and political economist who made significant contributions to the methods of statistics and to economic theory. He was a leading figure in the development of mathematical economics and the first editor of The Economic Journal, a post he held for some 35 years.

Life

Edgeworth was born at Edgeworthstown, County Longford, into the literary Edgeworth family; the novelist Maria Edgeworth was his father's aunt. He studied at Trinity College Dublin and Balliol College, Oxford, taking a first in classics, and later qualified as a barrister. Largely self-taught in mathematics, he became a lecturer at King's College London, then Tooke Professor of Economic Science and Statistics there in 1890, and in 1891 Drummond Professor of Political Economy at Oxford and a fellow of All Souls College.

Contributions

His first book, Mathematical Psychics (1881), applied calculus to utility and introduced indifference curves and the contract curve. The Edgeworth box, refined by Pareto and Bowley, remains a standard tool for analysing exchange between two agents. He also contributed the Edgeworth expansion in statistics, work on index numbers, the theory of taxation — including the startling result that taxing commodities could improve welfare (the Edgeworth paradox) — and limit pricing in oligopoly theory. Edgeworth was president of the Royal Statistical Society (1912–14) and of Section F of the British Association.
