Therapsida

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
CladeSynapsida
CladeTherapsida Broom, 1905
Fossil rangeEarly Permian – Holocene (as mammals)

Therapsida is a major group of eupelycosaurian synapsids that includes mammals, their ancestors and close extinct relatives. Therapsids first appeared in the Early Permian, about 275 million years ago, evolving from earlier sphenacodontid synapsids such as Dimetrodon-grade forms, and they became the dominant large land animals of the later Permian.

Characteristics

Compared with earlier synapsids, therapsids show limbs held more vertically beneath the body, differentiated teeth (incisors, canines and cheek teeth), a larger temporal fenestra for jaw muscles, and in many groups a secondary palate — features associated with a more active, possibly warm-blooded lifestyle. The therapsid fossil record documents the gradual assembly of mammalian traits, including changes in the jaw joint that eventually produced the mammalian middle ear.

Evolution and diversity

Major therapsid groups include the dinocephalians, the herbivorous anomodonts (including the widespread dicynodonts), the predatory gorgonopsians, the therocephalians, and the cynodonts, from which true mammals arose in the Late Triassic or Jurassic. Therapsids were severely affected by the end-Permian mass extinction, though dicynodonts such as Lystrosaurus and cynodonts survived and diversified in the Triassic, alongside the rising dinosaurs (see Theropoda).

By cladistic convention, Mammalia itself is nested within Therapsida, so therapsids are not extinct: more than 6,000 living mammal species are surviving therapsids.
