Guqin


The guqin, China's ancient scholar's zither

The guqin (古琴, literally "ancient zither") is a plucked seven-string Chinese musical instrument of the zither family. It has been played since ancient times and has traditionally been favoured by scholars and literati as an instrument of great subtlety and refinement; it is sometimes called "the father of Chinese music" or "the instrument of the sages". In 2003, guqin music was proclaimed one of the Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity by UNESCO.

Description

The guqin is a long, narrow wooden soundbox, typically about 120 cm in length, lacquered, with seven strings (historically of twisted silk, now often steel-nylon) stretched over movable bridges and tuned by pegs at the underside. It has no frets; instead, thirteen mother-of-pearl inlays (hui) mark harmonic positions. Its range is about four octaves, and its quiet, refined timbre suits intimate, contemplative performance.

Playing technique and notation

Players use a rich vocabulary of finger techniques—over 1,000 distinct sounds are recognised, including open strings (san yin), stopped notes (an yin), and harmonics (fan yin), along with slides, vibrato, and glissandi. Music is written in jianzipu, a unique tablature that specifies strings, positions, and finger techniques in abbreviated characters; early pieces were preserved in the older wenzipu descriptive notation. The piece Jieshi Diao You Lan (Solitary Orchid) is the oldest surviving notated guqin melody, from a Tang-dynasty manuscript.

History and cultural role

Tradition attributes the instrument's creation to legendary emperors; historical qin are reliably dated back more than two thousand years. Confucius was said to play the qin, and mastery of it was one of the four arts of the scholar (qin, chess, calligraphy, painting). The instrument features in the famous story of Bo Ya and Zhong Ziqi, whose mutual understanding through music gave Chinese the word zhiyin—"one who truly understands one's music"—for a soulmate.

Remarkably, a recording of the guqin piece Flowing Water (Liu Shui), performed by Guan Pinghu, was included on the Voyager Golden Record sent into space in 1977.
