Cebu is an island and province in the Central Visayas region of the Philippines, consisting of the main island and 167 surrounding islands and islets. Its capital and largest city is Cebu City, the oldest city and first capital of the Philippines, and the centre of Metro Cebu, the country's second-largest metropolitan area after Metro Manila.

History

Before colonisation, Cebu was a trading polity — the Rajahnate of Cebu — exchanging goods with China and Southeast Asian neighbours. In 1521 Ferdinand Magellan landed in Cebu, converted Rajah Humabon and his subjects to Christianity, and was killed days later at the Battle of Mactan by the warriors of Lapulapu, now honoured as the first Filipino hero. In 1565 Miguel López de Legazpi established the first Spanish settlement in the Philippines at Cebu, which served as capital until 1571.

Geography and economy

The elongated island runs 196 km north to south with a rugged central spine. Cebu is a major centre of trade, education, tourism, and business process outsourcing; Mactan–Cebu International Airport is the country's second-busiest. The province is famed for dried mangoes, lechon, guitar making, and diving destinations such as Moalboal and Malapascua Island.

Culture

The Sinulog festival, held each January in honour of the Santo Niño (whose image, given by Magellan's expedition, is kept in the Basilica del Santo Niño), is one of the Philippines' largest celebrations. Magellan's Cross and Fort San Pedro are landmarks in Cebu City's old district.
