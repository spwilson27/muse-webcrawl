Kering is a French multinational holding company specializing in luxury goods, based in Paris. It owns a portfolio of fashion and leather-goods houses including Gucci, Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, Balenciaga, and Alexander McQueen, as well as jewellery brands and the eyewear company Kering Eyewear.

History

The group began in 1963 as a timber-trading business founded by François Pinault in Brittany. It grew into retail through the Pinault-Printemps-Redoute (PPR) group, acquiring chains such as Conforama, Printemps, Fnac, and La Redoute. Beginning with a stake in Gucci in 1999, Pinault progressively reoriented the company toward luxury, selling off its retail assets. The group was renamed Kering in 2013; the name combines the Breton word ker (home) with the English gerund ending "-ing."

Business

Kering competes with LVMH and Richemont at the top of the global luxury market. Gucci has historically been its largest revenue source, followed by Saint Laurent and Bottega Veneta. The company has emphasized sustainability programs and acquired a stake in Valentino in 2023. François-Henri Pinault has led the group since 2005.
