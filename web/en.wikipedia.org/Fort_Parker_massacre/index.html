The Fort Parker massacre was a raid on May 19, 1836, in which a large force of Comanche and allied Native American raiders attacked Fort Parker, a fortified settlement established by the Parker family near the Navasota River in what is now Limestone County, Texas. Five settlers were killed and five others were carried into captivity.

The attack

Fort Parker had been built in 1834 by Silas M. Parker and his family, members of Elder John Parker's extended clan, who had emigrated from Illinois. On the morning of the attack, a large party of mounted warriors—numbering in the hundreds—approached the fort under a white flag and asked for beef and directions. When settler Benjamin Parker went out to parley, he was killed, and the raiders stormed the lightly defended stockade while most of the men were working in the fields.

Elder John Parker, his grandson-in-law's family members, and others were killed. Those taken captive included Rachel Plummer, her young son, Elizabeth Duty Kellogg, and nine-year-old Cynthia Ann Parker and her six-year-old brother John Richard Parker.

Captives and legacy

Cynthia Ann Parker was adopted into the Comanche and lived with them for nearly 25 years, marrying the chief Peta Nocona and becoming the mother of Quanah Parker, the last war chief of the Comanche. She was recaptured by Texas Rangers in 1860 at the Battle of Pease River. Rachel Plummer was ransomed after about 21 months and published one of the first captivity narratives in Texas. A reconstruction of Fort Parker now stands at Fort Parker State Park.
