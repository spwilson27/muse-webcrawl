Promontory Summit

Elevationabout 1,470 m (4,900 ft)
LocationBox Elder County, Utah, United States
EventCompletion of the first transcontinental railroad, May 10, 1869

Promontory Summit is a pass in the Promontory Mountains of Box Elder County, Utah, United States, north of the Great Salt Lake. It is best known as the point where the Union Pacific Railroad and the Central Pacific Railroad joined their rails on May 10, 1869, completing the first transcontinental railroad across the United States. The event was marked by the driving of a ceremonial golden spike — the "Last Spike" — and was photographed in the famous image known as The Champagne Photo, showing the locomotives Jupiter and No. 119 facing one another.

The ceremony

Officials and workers of both companies, along with soldiers, dignitaries, and onlookers, gathered at the summit for the ceremony. After speeches and the presentation of ceremonial spikes of gold, silver, and iron, the final spike of gold was tapped into a pre-drilled hole in a laurelwood tie, and the word "DONE" was telegraphed to the nation. The original golden spike is now held by the Cantor Arts Center at Stanford University.

Later history

The summit route was bypassed as the main line in 1904 by the Lucin Cutoff across the Great Salt Lake, and the rails over Promontory were removed in 1942. In 1957 the site was authorized as a national historic site, and in 2019 it was redesignated the Golden Spike National Historical Park. Reconstructed track and operational replica locomotives now occupy the summit, and the completion ceremony is reenacted annually.
