Karakum Canal

The Karakum Canal (Turkmen: Garagum kanaly; Russian: Каракумский канал) in Turkmenistan is one of the largest irrigation and water-supply canals in the world. It diverts water from the Amu Darya river near the Afghan border and carries it westward about 1,375 km through the Karakum Desert, past Ashgabat, to the foothills of the Kopet Dag and toward the Caspian region near Bereket.

Construction

Construction began in 1954 under the Soviet Union and proceeded in stages for decades; the canal reached Ashgabat by 1962 and was extended repeatedly to supply irrigation districts and the capital's drinking water. The canal is mostly unlined and loses a substantial share of its flow to seepage, though this recharge has also created desert lakes and wetlands along its course.

Impact

The canal irrigates roughly half a million hectares of cotton and other crops and supplies much of Turkmenistan's population. However, it diverts around 13 km³ of water a year — a large fraction of the Amu Darya's flow — contributing, together with irrigation withdrawals in Uzbekistan, to the desiccation of the Aral Sea, one of the worst human-made environmental disasters. Water quality and salinisation are continuing concerns.
