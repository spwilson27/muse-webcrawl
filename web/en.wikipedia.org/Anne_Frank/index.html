Annelies Marie Frank (12 June 1929 – c. February 1945) was a German-born Jewish diarist and victim of the Holocaust. Her wartime diary, published after her death as The Diary of a Young Girl (also known as The Diary of Anne Frank), is one of the most widely read books in the world and the best-known first-hand account of Jewish life under Nazi occupation.

Anne Frank in May 1940, photographed two years before the family went into hiding.

Early life

Anne was born in Frankfurt am Main, the younger daughter of Otto and Edith Frank. After the Nazis came to power in 1933, the family emigrated to Amsterdam, where Otto founded a business and the girls attended school — Anne at a Montessori school, later the Jewish Lyceum. Germany invaded the Netherlands in May 1940 and progressively restricted Jewish life; Anne was stripped of her German citizenship in 1941.

In hiding: the Achterhuis

On her thirteenth birthday, 12 June 1942, Anne received a red-and-white checked autograph book, which she used as a diary. When her sister Margot received a call-up notice for a labour camp, the family went into hiding on 6 July 1942 in concealed rooms — the Achterhuis ("Secret Annexe") — behind Otto's office at Prinsengracht 263, supplied by trusted helpers including Miep Gies. They were joined by the van Pels family and the dentist Fritz Pfeffer: eight people in all. Anne's diary records two years of confinement, adolescent growth, conflicts and hopes, addressed largely to an imaginary friend, "Kitty". After hearing a radio appeal for wartime diaries, she began revising her entries for eventual publication.

Arrest and death

On 4 August 1944 the annexe was raided after a betrayal whose source remains uncertain. The eight occupants were deported via Westerbork to Auschwitz, where the group was separated; Anne and Margot were later transferred to Bergen-Belsen, where both died — probably of typhus — in early 1945, weeks before the camp's liberation. Otto Frank was the only one of the eight to survive. Miep Gies had preserved Anne's diary papers, and she returned them to Otto after the war.

The diary and legacy

Otto Frank prepared his daughter's diary for publication; it appeared in Dutch in 1947 as Het Achterhuis, followed by English, German and dozens of other languages, stage and film adaptations, and scholarly critical editions authenticating the text. The hiding place is now the Anne Frank House museum. Anne's famous reflection — "in spite of everything, I still believe that people are really good at heart" — has made her a universal symbol of the victims of the Holocaust and of youthful resilience.
