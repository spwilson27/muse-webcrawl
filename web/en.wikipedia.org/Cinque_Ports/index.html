The Cinque Ports (pronounced "sink ports") is a confederation of coastal towns in Kent and Sussex in south-east England, formed originally to provide ships and men for the Crown's naval needs in return for privileges.

History

The five original "head ports" were Hastings, New Romney, Hythe, Dover, and Sandwich, joined by the two "Antient Towns" of Winchelsea and Rye, and by numerous subsidiary "limbs". In return for providing 57 ships for 15 days' service annually, the ports received privileges including exemption from certain taxes, rights of self-government, and their own courts.

The confederation reached its height in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, but declined as harbours silted up (New Romney and Sandwich are no longer on the sea) and the Royal Navy developed. The office of Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports survives as an honorary position, historically associated with Walmer Castle, and the confederation continues in a ceremonial role.

Privileges and institutions

Barons of the Cinque Ports historically carried the canopy over the monarch at coronations, a right last exercised in the nineteenth century. The ports' court of Brotherhood still meets, chiefly for ceremonial purposes.
