Bernard Pyne Grenfell (16 December 1869 – 18 May 1926) was an English scientist, Egyptologist, and papyrologist. With his friend and colleague Arthur Surridge Hunt, he excavated the ancient Egyptian city of Oxyrhynchus and recovered the Oxyrhynchus Papyri — hundreds of thousands of papyrus fragments that transformed the study of the ancient world — in excavations from 1896 onward.

Career

Grenfell was educated at The Queen's College, Oxford, where he took a first in classical moderations and literae humaniores. Under the Egypt Exploration Fund he began fieldwork in Egypt in the early 1890s, initially on Coptic texts. At Oxyrhynchus (modern el-Bahnasa), Grenfell and Hunt recognised that the town's ancient rubbish mounds preserved papyri on an unmatched scale. Their first season (1896–97) yielded major finds, and successive seasons produced literary texts (lost works of Sappho, Pindar, Menander, Euripides, and a substantial fragment of Sophocles' Ichneutae), early Christian writings (including the 1897 "Sayings of Jesus," the Logia, later identified as part of the Gospel of Thomas), and a vast documentary archive illuminating everyday life in Ptolemaic and Roman Egypt.

Grenfell became professor of papyrology at Oxford in 1908, one of the first such chairs. He and Hunt edited the monumental series The Oxyrhynchus Papyri (from 1898) and the Hibeh and Tebtunis volumes; the project they founded continues at Oxford under the Egypt Exploration Society, with several hundred thousand fragments still unpublished.

Later life

Grenfell's later years were marred by recurrent mental illness, and he withdrew from excavation after 1920, though he continued editorial work. Hunt survived him and carried the edition forward. Grenfell's contribution, together with Hunt's, is credited with founding papyrology as a modern discipline; the pair are commemorated in Oxford by the Grenfell–Hunt papyrology room of the Sackler Library (now the Weston Library collections).
