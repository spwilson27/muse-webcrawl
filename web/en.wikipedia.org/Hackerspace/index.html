Hackerspace

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hackerspace (also referred to as a hacklab, hackspace, makerspace or hackbase) is a community-operated, often non-profit workspace where people with common interests — such as computing, electronics, technology, science and digital art — can meet, socialise and collaborate. Hackerspaces provide shared tools and equipment that members could not individually afford, from 3D printers and laser cutters to electronics benches and woodworking machinery.

History

The model traces to German community spaces of the 1990s associated with the Chaos Computer Club, such as c-base in Berlin (founded 1995). The movement spread internationally in the 2000s, helped by design patterns published by visiting American hackers who toured European spaces in 2007; prominent early United States spaces included NYC Resistor and Noisebridge in San Francisco. By the 2010s there were well over a thousand active hackerspaces worldwide.

Activities

Beyond shared infrastructure, hackerspaces host classes, workshops, presentations and social events, and incubate projects ranging from open hardware to community radio. They are usually run by their members under principles of openness and consensus, and are funded by membership dues.
