Algebraic quantum field theory

Algebraic quantum field theory (AQFT), also known as the Haag–Kastler approach, is an axiomatic framework for quantum field theory that emphasises the role of local observable algebras. It was developed principally by Rudolf Haag and Daniel Kastler in the 1960s.

Axioms

The framework assigns to each open region of spacetime a C*-algebra (or von Neumann algebra) of observables measurable within that region. These assignments must satisfy isotony (larger regions have larger algebras), locality (algebras of spacelike-separated regions commute), covariance under the Poincaré group, and the existence of a vacuum state. States of the system are positive linear functionals on the overall algebra.

Key results

Important achievements of the approach include the Doplicher–Haag–Roberts theory of superselection sectors, which reconstructs internal symmetry groups from observable data, and rigorous results on thermal states and the KMS condition. The Reeh–Schlieder theorem, showing that the vacuum is cyclic for every local algebra, is also a landmark of the subject.

Relation to other approaches

AQFT contrasts with the Lagrangian, path-integral methods used in most particle-physics calculations: it gives up explicit field operators in favour of mathematical rigour and conceptual clarity, and it has found applications in curved spacetime quantum field theory and the foundations of quantum statistical mechanics.
