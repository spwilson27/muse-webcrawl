Alzheimers disease

Alzheimer's disease is a neurodegenerative disease that most often begins in people over 65 years of age. This article is filed under the literal slug "Alzheimers_disease" as written in the crawl frontier; the standard spelling is Alzheimer's disease. It is the most common cause of dementia, accounting for roughly 60–70% of cases.

Alzheimer's disease
Symptoms: Memory loss, confusion, language problems
Onset: Typically over 65
Named for: Alois Alzheimer (1906 case)

Signs and course

Early symptoms — difficulty remembering recent events — are often mistaken for ageing. As the disease advances, symptoms include problems with language, disorientation, mood swings, loss of motivation, self-neglect, and behavioural issues. Gradually, bodily functions are lost, ultimately leading to death, typically 3–9 years after diagnosis.

Causes and pathology

The cause is poorly understood. Risk factors include genetics (notably the APOE ε4 allele; rare early-onset forms involve APP, PSEN1 and PSEN2 mutations), head injury, depression, and cardiovascular factors. The disease is characterised by loss of neurons and synapses, extracellular amyloid-beta plaques, and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles of hyperphosphorylated tau protein.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines cognitive testing, brain imaging, and increasingly amyloid/tau biomarkers in cerebrospinal fluid or blood. No treatment cures the disease. Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) and memantine give modest symptomatic benefit; anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies such as lecanemab and donanemab modestly slow decline in early disease. Care focuses on support, safety and carer wellbeing, and prevention research emphasises exercise, vascular health, hearing, and cognitive engagement.
