Battle of El Alamein

DateFirst battle: 1–27 July 1942; Second battle: 23 October – 11 November 1942
LocationEl Alamein, Egypt
ResultAllied victory; turning point of the North African campaign
BelligerentsBritish Commonwealth and allies vs. Germany and Italy
CommandersClaude Auchinleck, then Bernard Montgomery (Eighth Army); Erwin Rommel (Panzer Army Africa)

The Battles of El Alamein were two major engagements of the Western Desert campaign of the Second World War, fought in 1942 near the Egyptian railway halt of El Alamein, where the Mediterranean coast and the impassable Qattara Depression create a narrow front of about 40 miles. The First Battle (1–27 July 1942) halted the Axis advance into Egypt; the Second Battle (23 October – 11 November 1942) decisively broke the Axis line and sent Erwin Rommel's Panzer Army Africa into a retreat that ended with the Axis expulsion from North Africa.

First battle

After the fall of Tobruk in June 1942, Rommel pursued the British Eighth Army deep into Egypt, threatening Alexandria and the Suez Canal. General Claude Auchinleck took personal command, chose the El Alamein position — the only defensible line between the coast and the Qattara Depression — and in a month of fluid fighting stopped the Axis advance, inflicting losses Rommel could not replace. Both sides then paused to build up strength.

Second battle

Auchinleck was replaced by Harold Alexander in theatre command and Bernard Montgomery in command of the Eighth Army, which by October had some 195,000 men and over 1,000 tanks against roughly 116,000 Axis troops and 550 tanks, many Italian. Montgomery opened Operation Lightfoot on the night of 23 October with a massive artillery barrage. Infantry and engineers breached the deep Axis minefields ("the Devil's gardens"), and after days of attritional "crumbling" operations, the breakthrough attack Operation Supercharge on 2 November ruptured the line. The Axis retreat began on 4 November; Montgomery's methodical pursuit, hampered by rain and caution, failed to cut off the retreating army, but Egypt, the Suez Canal, and Middle Eastern oil were secured for good.

Significance

El Alamein, coinciding with the Allied landings in French North Africa (Operation Torch) and the Soviet victory taking shape at Stalingrad, marked the turning of the tide against the Axis in late 1942. Winston Churchill famously wrote: "Before Alamein we never had a victory. After Alamein we never had a defeat."
