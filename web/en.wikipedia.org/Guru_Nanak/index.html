Guru Nanak

Born15 April 1469, Rai Bhoi Ki Talvandi (now Nankana Sahib, Pakistan)
Died22 September 1539, Kartarpur (now in Pakistan)
Known forFounder of Sikhism; first of the ten Sikh Gurus
SuccessorGuru Angad

Guru Nanak (1469–1539) was the founder of Sikhism and the first of the ten Sikh Gurus. His birth is celebrated worldwide as Guru Nanak Gurpurab. Born in the village of Talvandi, now Nankana Sahib in present-day Pakistan, he worked as a storekeeper before a transformative spiritual experience around 1499, after which he is said to have declared: "There is no Hindu, there is no Muslim" — meaning that the divine path transcends religious labels.

Teachings

Nanak taught devotion to one God (Ik Onkar), the equality of all human beings regardless of caste or creed, honest work (kirat karo), sharing with others (vand chhako), and remembrance of God's name (naam japo). He rejected empty ritual, asceticism and caste distinctions, advocating instead the life of a householder engaged in the world. His teachings are recorded in 974 hymns in the Guru Granth Sahib, including the Japji Sahib.

Journeys

According to the traditional accounts (janamsakhis), Nanak undertook long journeys known as udasis, travelling across India and beyond — to Tibet, Sri Lanka, Bengal, Mecca and Baghdad — accompanied by his Muslim companion and musician Bhai Mardana, teaching through hymn and dialogue.

Final years

Nanak settled at Kartarpur on the banks of the Ravi, founding a community where disciples lived, worked and worshipped together, practising the communal kitchen (langar) that fed all comers without distinction. Before his death he appointed Bhai Lehna, renamed Guru Angad, as his successor.
