Mi Teleférico ("My Cable Car") is an aerial cable car urban transit system serving the La Paz–El Alto metropolitan area in Bolivia. Opened on 30 May 2014, it became the largest urban cable car network in the world, using gondola cabins to carry passengers over the steep terrain separating the high-altitude city of El Alto (about 4,150 m) from the bowl of La Paz below.

Development

The system was proposed under President Evo Morales to relieve chronic traffic congestion between El Alto and La Paz, where minibuses descended winding roads. Built largely by the Austrian company Doppelmayr, the first phase comprised three lines — Red, Yellow and Green, named after the colours of the Bolivian flag — and a second phase added further lines (Blue, Orange, White, Sky Blue, Purple, Brown and Silver), bringing the network to ten lines and more than 30 kilometres of track by 2019.

Operations

Cabins carry ten passengers each and depart stations every few seconds, with frequencies around every 12 seconds and capacities of several thousand passengers per hour per direction. The system carries on the order of 250,000–300,000 passengers daily. Fares are low and integrated tickets connect cable car lines with the municipal PumaKatari bus system.

Impact

Mi Teleférico drastically cut travel times between El Alto and the southern districts of La Paz, reduced congestion and pollution, and became a symbol of Bolivian modernity and a model cited by planners of other mountainous and congested cities.
