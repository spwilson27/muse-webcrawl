Nye Committee

Full nameSpecial Committee on Investigation of the Munitions Industry
Active1934–1936
ChairSenator Gerald Nye (R-ND)
SubjectProfits and influence of the US arms industry in World War I

The Nye Committee, officially the Special Committee on Investigation of the Munitions Industry, was a United States Senate committee chaired by Senator Gerald Nye of North Dakota that investigated the financial and political interests of American arms manufacturers between 1934 and 1936. Its hearings popularised the "merchants of death" thesis — the charge that munitions makers and bankers had drawn the United States into the First World War for profit.

Investigation

Over 93 hearings, the committee examined the records of firms such as DuPont, Colt, and Remington, as well as the Morgan banking interests. It documented enormous wartime profits, industry lobbying against arms embargoes, bribery of foreign officials, and the sale of arms in ways that frustrated US foreign policy. Its reports stopped short of proving that the munitions industry had directly caused American entry into the war, but the publicity powerfully strengthened isolationist sentiment in the mid-1930s.

Impact

The committee's findings helped drive the passage of the Neutrality Acts of 1935, 1936, and 1937, which restricted arms sales and loans to belligerents. Historians debate the extent to which the "merchants of death" interpretation was justified, but the Nye Committee remains a landmark case of congressional investigation shaping foreign-policy debate; it also damaged the reputation of the Wilson administration by suggesting banking interests had biased American neutrality before 1917.
