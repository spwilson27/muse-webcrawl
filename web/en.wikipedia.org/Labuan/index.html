Labuan (Jawi: لابوان), officially the Federal Territory of Labuan, is a federal territory of Malaysia comprising Labuan Island and six smaller islands off the coast of Sabah in East Malaysia, in the South China Sea near the northern coast of Borneo. Its capital is Victoria.

History

Labuan was part of the Bruneian Empire until the Sultan of Brunei ceded it to the British in 1846; it became a Crown Colony in 1848 and served as a coaling station and naval base. After the Second World War it joined North Borneo (Sabah) and in 1963 became part of Malaysia. In 1984 Sabah ceded Labuan to the federal government, making it Malaysia's second federal territory, and in 1990 it was declared an international offshore financial centre and free trade zone.

Economy

The economy is centred on the Labuan International Business and Financial Centre (Labuan IBFC), oil and gas services supporting nearby offshore fields, ship repair, and duty-free commerce. Labuan is a duty-free port, and its financial centre hosts numerous offshore banks, insurance companies, and trust companies.

Geography

Labuan Island is largely flat and low-lying, with a deep natural harbour. The territory lies close to the Brunei Bay and the mouth of the Brunei River, about 8 km off the Borneo mainland.
