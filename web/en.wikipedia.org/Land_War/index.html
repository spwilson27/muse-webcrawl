The Land War (Irish: Cogadh na Talún) was a period of agrarian agitation in rural Ireland from 1879 into the early twentieth century, in which tenant farmers campaigned for the "Three Fs" — fair rent, fixity of tenure, and free sale of their interest in their holdings.

Course

Amid agricultural depression after 1877, Michael Davitt founded the Irish National Land League in 1879 with Charles Stewart Parnell as president. The League organised rent strikes, resistance to evictions, and the social ostracism of land-grabbers — a tactic named "boycotting" after Captain Charles Boycott, an agent in County Mayo shunned in 1880. The campaign involved widespread unrest and harsh coercion legislation in response.

Legacy

Gladstone's Land Law (Ireland) Act 1881 conceded much of the Three Fs, and the Kilmainham Treaty of 1882 eased the conflict. Successive land acts, culminating in the Wyndham Act of 1903, enabled mass tenant purchase and ultimately transferred most Irish farmland from landlords to occupiers, dissolving the old landlord system.
