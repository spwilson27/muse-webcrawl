Tyrol (German: Tirol) is an Austrian federal state (Bundesland) in western Austria, comprising the Austrian part of the historic County of Tyrol. It consists of two non-contiguous parts — North Tyrol and East Tyrol — separated by a strip of Salzburg state. The capital and largest city is Innsbruck.

The region is entirely mountainous, dominated by the Alps, with the Inn river valley as its main corridor. Tourism, especially skiing in resorts such as Kitzbühel, St. Anton, and Ischgl, is the dominant industry, alongside hydropower, agriculture, and manufacturing.

History

The County of Tyrol was a Habsburg possession from 1363 and a crossroads between Germany and Italy via the Brenner Pass. After the First World War, the Treaty of Saint-Germain (1919) divided historic Tyrol: the southern part (South Tyrol and Trentino) went to Italy, while the north and east remained with Austria. East Tyrol has been administered separately from North Tyrol since the loss of South Tyrol severed the land connection.
