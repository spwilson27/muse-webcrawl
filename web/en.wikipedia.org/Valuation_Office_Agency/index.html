Valuation Office Agency

The Valuation Office Agency (VOA) is an executive agency of the United Kingdom government, sponsored by HM Revenue and Customs. It provides valuations and property advice to government departments and public bodies, and is responsible for assessing properties for council tax and non-domestic rates (business rates) in England and Wales.

History

The agency's origins lie in the Valuation Office established under the Finance (1909–10) Act 1910, which introduced a valuation of land for duty purposes. The modern agency was launched as an executive agency in 1991. Its work expanded over the twentieth century as rating systems developed.

Functions

The VOA compiles and maintains the council tax valuation lists for England and Wales and the non-domestic rating lists for England. It also provides valuation services on a repayment basis to other government departments and public sector bodies, including work on compulsory purchase, taxation valuations for capital gains and inheritance tax, and advice on public land and property assets.

Organisation

The agency is headed by a chief executive and employs several thousand staff, including chartered surveyors, across a network of offices. In Scotland, equivalent functions are carried out by local authority assessors, and in Northern Ireland by Land and Property Services.
