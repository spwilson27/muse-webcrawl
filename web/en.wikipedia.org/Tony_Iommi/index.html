Anthony Frank Iommi (born 19 February 1948) is an English guitarist, best known as the co-founder and primary composer of the heavy metal band Black Sabbath. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential guitarists in heavy metal, famous for his down-tuned, riff-driven playing.

Early life and injury

Working in a sheet-metal factory as a teenager in Birmingham, Iommi lost the tips of the middle and ring fingers of his right (fretting) hand in an accident on his last day of work. He fashioned plastic thimbles for his fingers, used lighter strings and eventually tuned down, inadvertently creating the dark, heavy guitar sound that defined Sabbath's music.

Black Sabbath and beyond

With Geezer Butler, Ozzy Osbourne and Bill Ward he formed Black Sabbath in 1968; albums such as Paranoid (1970) and Master of Reality (1971) laid the foundations of heavy metal. Iommi was the band's one constant member through decades of line-up changes, and he has also released solo work and collaborated widely.
