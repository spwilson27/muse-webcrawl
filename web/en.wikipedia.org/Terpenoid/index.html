Terpenoid

The terpenoids, also known as isoprenoids, are a class of naturally occurring organic chemicals derived from the five-carbon compound isoprene, and its oligomers, the terpenes. Multiples of isoprene units are joined in characteristic patterns and often extensively modified by oxidation and rearrangement, yielding more than 80,000 known compounds — the largest class of natural products. Terpenoids give many plants their scents and flavours and include essential medicines such as the antimalarial artemisinin and the anticancer drug paclitaxel (Taxol).

Biosynthesis and classification

Terpenoids are built from the activated isoprene donors isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP) and dimethylallyl pyrophosphate (DMAPP), produced by the mevalonate pathway or the MEP pathway. Chain elongation gives geranyl (C10), farnesyl (C15), and geranylgeranyl (C20) pyrophosphates, from which cyclases fashion the skeletons. They are classified by the number of isoprene units: hemiterpenoids (C5), monoterpenoids (C10, e.g. menthol, limonene), sesquiterpenoids (C15, e.g. artemisinin), diterpenoids (C20, e.g. retinol precursors, paclitaxel), sesterterpenoids (C25), triterpenoids (C30, e.g. steroids and squalene), tetraterpenoids (C40, the carotenoids), and polyterpenoids such as natural rubber.

Roles in nature

In plants, volatile terpenoids attract pollinators, deter herbivores, and mediate allelopathy and defence; conifer resins and essential oils are terpenoid-rich. Animals use terpenoid-derived compounds as hormones (steroids, built from triterpene squalene), visual pigments (retinoids), and electron carriers (quinones with isoprenoid side chains). Chlorophyll and haem also carry terpenoid-derived components.

Uses

Terpenoids supply fragrances and flavourings (citral, camphor, menthol), industrial solvents (turpentine), vitamins A, E, and K, and major pharmaceuticals. Leopold Ružička's elucidation of their structures and the "biogenetic isoprene rule" earned a share of the 1939 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and modern metabolic engineering produces terpenoids such as artemisinic acid in engineered yeast at commercial scale.
