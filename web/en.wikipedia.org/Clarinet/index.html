Clarinet

The clarinet is a musical instrument in the woodwind family. It has a single-reed mouthpiece, a straight, cylindrical tube with an almost cylindrical bore, and a flared bell. A person who plays the clarinet is called a clarinettist. The instrument has an exceptionally large pitch range of nearly four octaves and a distinctive timbre, and it is a standard member of the symphony orchestra, the concert band, and many chamber ensembles, as well as a prominent instrument in jazz, klezmer and folk traditions.

History

The clarinet developed around 1700 in Nuremberg, Germany, when the instrument maker Johann Christoph Denner added a register key to the earlier chalumeau, extending its range and giving it a clear, bright upper register. Its name derives from the Italian clarinetto, "little trumpet," because its high register resembled the trumpet's clarino register. By the mid-18th century the clarinet had entered orchestras; Mozart was an early enthusiast, writing his Clarinet Quintet and Clarinet Concerto for Anton Stadler. During the 19th century keywork was greatly improved, notably by Hyacinthe Klosé, who adapted the Boehm flute-key system in the 1840s, and by German makers who developed the Oehler system still common in Germany and Austria.

Construction

The standard soprano clarinet in B♭ consists of five parts: the mouthpiece (with a single reed of cane, usually Arundo donax), the barrel, the upper joint, the lower joint, and the bell. Bodies are traditionally made of grenadilla (African blackwood); cheaper instruments use ebonite or ABS resin. Because its bore is essentially cylindrical and it is stopped at one end, the clarinet overblows at the twelfth (a perfect twelfth) rather than the octave, unlike the flute or oboe. Its registers are named chalumeau (low), clarion (middle), and altissimo (high).

The clarinet family

Clarinet-type instruments exist in many sizes. The most common are the sopranino in E♭, the soprano in B♭ and A, the alto in E♭, the bass clarinet in B♭, and the contrabass clarinet. The basset horn in F, favoured by Mozart, is also a member of the family. A clarinettist in an orchestra typically owns both B♭ and A instruments to avoid difficult transpositions.

Repertoire and use

Important classical works include Mozart's Clarinet Concerto, concertos by Weber, Spohr, Nielsen, Copland and Finzi, Brahms's two clarinet sonatas and quintet, and Stravinsky's Three Pieces. In jazz, the clarinet was a lead instrument of early New Orleans jazz (Sidney Bechet, Johnny Dodds) and the swing era, when Benny Goodman and Artie Shaw were celebrated bandleaders. The clarinet is central to klezmer music and appears in Balkan, Turkish, Greek and Brazilian choro traditions.
