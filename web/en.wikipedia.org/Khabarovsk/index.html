Khabarovsk (Russian: Хаба́ровск) is the largest city and the administrative centre of Khabarovsk Krai, Russia, located at the confluence of the Amur and Ussuri rivers, about 30 kilometres (19 miles) from the border with China. It is the second-largest city in the Russian Far East after Vladivostok and a major transport, industrial and cultural hub on the Trans-Siberian Railway.

History

The city was founded in 1858 as the military post of Khabarovka, named for the 17th-century Russian explorer Yerofey Khabarov, following the Treaty of Aigun by which Russia annexed the left bank of the Amur from Qing China. It received city status and its present name in 1893. The Khabarovsk Bridge across the Amur, completed in 1916 and featured on the Russian 5000-ruble banknote together with the city's monument to Governor-General Nikolay Muravyov-Amursky, completed the Trans-Siberian link.

Geography and climate

Khabarovsk has a monsoonal humid continental climate with hot, wet summers and very cold, dry Siberian winters. The central districts are built on rolling hills above the Amur, with broad Soviet-era avenues and the central Lenin Square.
