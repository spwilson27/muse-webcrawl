Crossing lemma

In graph theory, the crossing lemma gives a lower bound on the number of edge crossings in any drawing of a graph, in terms of the numbers of edges and vertices. It states that for a graph with n vertices and m edges, if m is sufficiently large relative to n, then the crossing number cr(G) is at least proportional to m3/n2.

More precisely, if m ≥ 7n, then cr(G) ≥ m3 / (29n2). The lemma, proved by Ajtai, Chvátal, Newborn, and Szemerédi (1982) and independently by Leighton (1983), is established by combining Euler's formula with a probabilistic argument: removing one edge per crossing and applying the planar bound gives a weak bound, which is then strengthened by applying it to random subgraphs.

Applications

The crossing lemma underlies proofs of the Szemerédi–Trotter theorem on point-line incidences and has applications in computational geometry, VLSI design, and additive combinatorics. It also explains why dense graphs cannot be drawn without very many crossings, even when their crossing number parameters seem favourable compared with arbitrary drawings.
