Get out the vote

"Get out the vote" (GOTV) is the effort by political campaigns, parties, civic groups and activists to encourage eligible supporters — or citizens generally — to actually cast their ballots in an election. It is the final mobilisation phase of a campaign, distinct from persuasion advertising aimed at undecided voters.

Methods

GOTV techniques include door-to-door canvassing, phone calls, text messages, mailings, e-mail reminders, social media campaigns, and providing information about polling places, early voting and mail-in ballots or rides to the polls. Campaigns use voter files and past turnout history to target likely supporters, calculating "turnout scores" that predict who is most likely to vote if contacted.

Effectiveness research

Field experiments by political scientists, prominently summarised in Donald Green and Alan Gerber's book Get Out the Vote, show that personal, face-to-face canvassing measurably raises turnout, while robocalls have little effect and commercial phone banks only modest effects. Non-partisan GOTV drives by civic organisations aim to raise participation across the electorate, whereas partisan operations concentrate on their own registered supporters, making turnout operations central to close elections.
