Zeus is the chief deity of the Greek pantheon. He is a sky and thunder god in ancient Greek religion and mythology, who rules as king of the gods on Mount Olympus. His name is cognate with the first syllable of his Roman equivalent Jupiter.

Zeus is the child of Cronus and Rhea, the youngest of his siblings to be born, though sometimes reckoned the eldest as the others required disgorging from Cronus's stomach. In most traditions, he is married to Hera, by whom he is usually said to have fathered Ares, Eileithyia, Hebe, and Hephaestus. At the oracle of Dodona, his consort was said to be Dione, by whom the Iliad states that he fathered Aphrodite. According to the Theogony, Zeus's first wife was Metis, by whom he had Athena. Zeus was also infamous for his many affairs, which resulted in many divine and heroic offspring, including Apollo, Artemis, Hermes, Persephone, Dionysus, Perseus, Heracles, Helen of Troy, Minos, and the Muses.

Myths

Cronus, warned that one of his children would overthrow him, swallowed each of the first five as they were born. Rhea hid the infant Zeus in a cave on Mount Ida in Crete and gave Cronus a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes to swallow instead. On reaching manhood, Zeus forced Cronus to disgorge his siblings, then led them in the ten-year Titanomachy against the Titans, whom he imprisoned in Tartarus. Drawing lots with his brothers, Zeus received the sky and air, Poseidon the waters, and Hades the underworld. Later, with the help of the giants Briareos, Cottus, and Gyges, Zeus defeated the monster Typhon, who had stormed Olympus.

Cult

Zeus was worshipped throughout the Greek world. His most famous sanctuary was at Olympia, where the Olympic Games were held in his honour every four years and where the colossal chryselephantine Statue of Zeus by Phidias — one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World — stood in his temple. At Dodona in Epirus, priests interpreted the rustling of a sacred oak as his oracles.
