Raphael Rooms

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Raphael Rooms (Italian: Stanze di Raffaello) are a suite of reception rooms in the Apostolic Palace in the Vatican, now part of the Vatican Museums, famous for their frescoes painted by Raphael and his workshop between 1508 and 1524. Commissioned by Pope Julius II and continued under Leo X, the four rooms formed the private apartments of the popes and contain some of the most celebrated works of the High Renaissance, including The School of Athens.

The rooms

Stanza della Segnatura

The first room painted (1508–1511), used as the pope's library and study, celebrates the great branches of human knowledge. Its four walls carry The School of Athens (Philosophy), the Disputation of the Holy Sacrament (Theology), the Parnassus (Poetry) and a wall devoted to the virtues and Law (Jurisprudence), with a harmonising ceiling of allegorical figures. The School of Athens, centred on Plato and Aristotle amid the philosophers of antiquity, is regarded as a masterpiece of Renaissance perspective and humanist thought.

Stanza di Eliodoro

Painted 1511–1514 as a private audience chamber, this room depicts miraculous interventions protecting the Church: the Expulsion of Heliodorus from the Temple, the Mass at Bolsena, the Repulse of Attila and the Deliverance of Saint Peter, the latter notable for its dramatic night lighting.

Stanza dell'Incendio del Borgo

Painted largely by Raphael's workshop (1514–1517) from his designs, the room shows episodes from the lives of popes named Leo, above all the Fire in the Borgo, in which Leo IV halts a blaze near St Peter's by making the sign of the cross.

Sala di Costantino

The largest hall, painted mainly by Raphael's pupils — including Giulio Romano and Giovanni Francesco Penni — after his death in 1520, celebrates the triumph of Christianity over paganism through scenes from the life of the emperor Constantine, including the Vision of the Cross and the Battle of the Milvian Bridge.
