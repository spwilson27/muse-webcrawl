The Little Mermaid

"The Little Mermaid" (Danish: Den lille Havfrue) is a literary fairy tale written by the Danish author Hans Christian Andersen. First published in 1837 as part of a collection of fairy tales for children, it tells the story of a young mermaid who is willing to give up her life in the sea to gain a human soul.

Plot

The Little Mermaid, the youngest daughter of the Sea King, saves a human prince from drowning and falls in love with him. She visits a sea witch and trades her voice for legs, knowing that if the prince marries another, she will dissolve into sea foam. The prince marries someone else, but the mermaid, refusing to kill him to save herself, becomes a spirit of the air with the chance to earn an immortal soul.

Adaptations

The tale has inspired numerous adaptations, most famously Walt Disney's 1989 animated film The Little Mermaid, which gave the story a happy ending. A bronze statue of the Little Mermaid, unveiled in Copenhagen in 1913, is one of Denmark's most famous landmarks.
