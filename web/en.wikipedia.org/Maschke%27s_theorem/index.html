Maschke's theorem

Named after: Heinrich Maschke
Field: Representation theory, group theory

Maschke's theorem, named after the German mathematician Heinrich Maschke, is a fundamental result in the representation theory of finite groups. It states that if the characteristic of the field does not divide the order of the group, then every representation is completely reducible — a direct sum of irreducible representations.

Statement

Let G be a finite group and V a vector space over a field F whose characteristic does not divide |G|. If W is a G-invariant subspace of a representation V, then W has a G-invariant complement in V.

Proof idea

The standard proof averages an arbitrary linear projection over the group, using the factor 1/|G| — which is why invertibility of the group order in the field is essential. The theorem fails in modular representation theory, where the characteristic divides |G|.
