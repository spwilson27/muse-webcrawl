The World Sportscar Championship was the world series run for sports car racing by the FIA from 1953 to 1992. The championship series evolved from small independent sports car events and races to become, at its height, one of the most popular forms of motorsport, featuring classic events such as the 24 Hours of Le Mans, the 24 Hours of Daytona, the 12 Hours of Sebring, and the Mille Miglia.

Group C cars, such as the Porsche 962, dominated the championship's final decade.

History

In its early years the championship was contested by manufacturers including Ferrari, Maserati, Jaguar, Aston Martin, and Mercedes-Benz, and was a manufacturers' championship only. A drivers' title was added in 1981. The 1982 introduction of Group C fuel-formula regulations ushered in a golden era: Porsche, Jaguar, Mercedes-Benz, Nissan, and Toyota competed with sleek prototypes, and grids and audiences grew.

Decline

Rule changes in the early 1990s aligning Group C with Formula One engine regulations raised costs sharply, entries dwindled, and the championship was cancelled after the 1992 season. Top-level endurance racing returned to a world championship format in 2012 with the FIA World Endurance Championship.

Notable champions

Ferrari is the most successful manufacturer in the championship's history, while drivers such as Jacky Ickx, Derek Bell, and Stefan Bellof became synonymous with its Group C era.
