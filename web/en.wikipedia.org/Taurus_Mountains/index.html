Taurus Mountains

The Taurus Mountains (Turkish: Toros Dağları) are a mountain complex in southern Turkey, running roughly parallel to the Mediterranean coast and forming the southern rim of the Anatolian plateau. They stretch from the region around Lake Eğirdir in the west to the upper reaches of the Euphrates and Tigris in the east, where they join the highlands of eastern Anatolia and the Zagros system.

Geography

The range is composed largely of limestone and is heavily karstified, with caves, sinkholes, and underground rivers. The Central Taurus includes the Aladağlar massif, whose highest summit, Demirkazık, rises to about 3,756 metres. Other high groups include the Bolkar and Tahtalı ranges. Snow feeds important rivers, among them the Göksu, Seyhan, and Ceyhan; the Manavgat and the sources of the Euphrates rise in or near the range. The Cilician Gates, a narrow pass through the mountains north of Tarsus, has been a strategic route between Anatolia and Syria since antiquity, used by Alexander the Great and many armies after him.

History and culture

The mountains were a source of timber and metals — the name Taurus is traditionally linked with bulls and with the storm gods of Anatolian religion. Bronze Age metalworking, including tin and silver mining, was centred in these ranges. For centuries, Yörük nomads have moved flocks between coastal winter pastures and high summer yaylas (plateaus). Forestry, including cedar of Lebanon stands, and modern tourism and mountaineering are also significant.
