Kefir

A fermented milk drink made with kefir "grains", originating in the North Caucasus.

Kefir (also spelled keefir, from Russian кефир) is a fermented milk drink similar to a thin yogurt or ayran, made by inoculating milk with kefir "grains"—gelatinous colonies of lactic acid bacteria, acetic acid bacteria and yeasts embedded in a matrix of proteins, lipids and the polysaccharide kefiran. It originated in the North Caucasus, where it was traditionally made in goatskin bags hung in doorways and knocked by passers-by to keep the contents mixed.

Kefir grains are not true grains and cannot be manufactured artificially; they grow and multiply during fermentation and are passed between producers, a practice that historically made them jealously guarded household possessions. The microbiologist Ilya Mechnikov's interest in the longevity of Caucasus and Balkan peoples helped popularise fermented milks in the early 20th century, and the Russian physician-backed effort to obtain grains from the Caucasus (the story of Irina Sakharova and Prince Bek-Mirza Barchorov) is a famous piece of kefir lore.

Production

Milk (cow, goat or sheep, or plant-based alternatives) is combined with grains and left to ferment at room temperature for roughly 12–24 hours. The bacteria ferment lactose into lactic acid, giving the characteristic tang; yeasts produce small amounts of carbon dioxide and ethanol (typically up to about 0.5–2 per cent in traditional preparations), which give slight effervescence. The grains are then strained out and reused. A separate culture, "water kefir" (tibicos), ferments sugar water or fruit juice and is not historically related despite the shared name.

Nutrition and health

Kefir provides protein, calcium and B vitamins, and its fermentation reduces lactose content, making it tolerable for many people with lactose intolerance. It is marketed as a probiotic; evidence for specific health benefits beyond those of other fermented milks remains limited, and regulators in the EU have rejected most probiotic health claims for want of evidence.
