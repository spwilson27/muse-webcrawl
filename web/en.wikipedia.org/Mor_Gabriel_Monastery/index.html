Mor Gabriel Monastery

Mor Gabriel Monastery (Syriac: Dayro d-Mor Gabriel), also known as Deyrulumur, is the oldest surviving Syriac Orthodox monastery in the world. It stands on the Tur Abdin plateau near Midyat in Mardin Province, south-eastern Turkey, close to the Syrian border.

Founded in 397 AD by Mor Samuel and his student Mor Simeon of Qartmin, the monastery has been a centre of Syriac Christian life for more than sixteen centuries.

History

The monastery grew rapidly; tradition records that it housed up to a thousand monks and was endowed by Roman and Byzantine emperors including Arcadius, Honorius, Theodosius II and Anastasius. The main church contains notable fifth- and sixth-century mosaics.

It has served as a bishopric (the Diocese of Tur Abdin) and the seat of metropolitans, and it houses the tombs of many saints and bishops in its domed burial chamber, the 'House of Saints'. Despite massacres and pressures during the Sayfo of 1915 and later periods, a small community of monks, nuns and students remains.

Land disputes

From 2008 the monastery became involved in widely reported legal disputes with neighbouring villages and the Turkish Treasury over land it had used for centuries, including the forest it had planted within its walls. The cases drew international attention, including from the European Parliament; some judgments went against the monastery, while other parcels were formally registered to the monastery's foundation in 2013–2014 following a government democratisation package.
