Straw

Straw is an agricultural byproduct consisting of the dry stalks of cereal plants after the grain and chaff have been removed. It makes up about half of the yield by weight of cereal crops such as barley, oats, rice, rye and wheat. It has a number of different uses, including fuel, livestock bedding and fodder, thatching and basket making.

Production and collection

Straw is usually gathered and stored in a straw bale, which is a bale, or bundle, of straw tightly bound with twine, wire, or string. Straw bales may be square, rectangular, star shaped or round, and can be very large depending on the type of baler used. Straw is typically left scattered in the field during harvesting and then rolled up into bales for transport and storage.

Uses

Traditionally, straw has been used as bedding for livestock (absorbing moisture and providing insulation), as a low-grade feed, for thatching roofs, and for making baskets, hats and other woven goods. It is ploughed back into soil as a source of organic matter, used as mulch in gardening, and employed in straw-bale construction, where bales form insulating wall infill. Straw is also a feedstock for biofuel production and for paper making, and is used in mushroom cultivation and in erosion control (straw wattles and mulches). Straw must be distinguished from hay, which is cut green and dried as animal fodder and retains much more nutritional value.
