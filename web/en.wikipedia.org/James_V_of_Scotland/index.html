James V (10 April 1512 – 14 December 1542) was King of Scotland from 1513 until his death at thirty. Crowned at seventeen months after his father James IV fell at Flodden, he matured into a vigorous ruler who strengthened the crown's finances and authority, renewed the French alliance, and built in the Renaissance style — but died broken by defeat at English hands, leaving his six-day-old daughter Mary as queen.

Minority and personal rule

James's long minority was contested between his mother Margaret Tudor, Henry VIII's sister, and the pro-French regent John Stewart, Duke of Albany, and later controlled by the Douglas Earl of Angus, who held the young king virtually prisoner. In 1528 James escaped and began his personal rule, exiling the Douglases and subduing the rebellious Border reivers and the Lord of the Isles. He extracted revenue from the Church — taxing the clergy and securing lucrative abbacies for his illegitimate sons — while posing as the "King of the Commons", reputedly travelling incognito among his subjects as the "Gudeman of Ballengeich".

French marriages and building

Rejecting Henry VIII's attempts to draw Scotland into his break with Rome, James married first Madeleine of Valois, daughter of Francis I of France, in Paris in 1537; she died within months of arriving in Scotland. He then married Mary of Guise, who brought him powerful French connections and bore two sons (both dying in infancy) and his successor Mary. James poured resources into palaces at Stirling, Falkland, Linlithgow and Holyrood, creating Scotland's finest Renaissance architecture.

War and death

When James refused to meet Henry VIII at York in 1541 and rejected demands to break with Rome, war followed. After a Scottish victory at Haddon Rig, his army was routed at Solway Moss on 24 November 1542, with many nobles captured. The king, ill and despairing, died at Falkland a few weeks later, days after the birth of Mary, Queen of Scots — tradition attributes to him the prophecy that the Stewart crown, having "came with a lass, will gang with a lass".
