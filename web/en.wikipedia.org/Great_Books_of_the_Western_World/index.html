Great Books of the Western World

Great Books of the Western World is a series of books originally published in the United States in 1952, by Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc., presenting the Western canon in a single package of 54 volumes. The series was edited by Robert Maynard Hutchins and Mortimer J. Adler, and grew out of their work on the Great Books curriculum and the "great conversation" of Western ideas.

Contents

The set begins with The Great Conversation, Hutchins's introductory volume, followed by the two-volume Syntopicon, Adler's index of 102 "Great Ideas" cross-referenced across the whole set. The remaining volumes collect works by 74 authors spanning some 3,000 years — among them Homer, Plato, Aristotle, the Bible, Virgil, Aquinas, Dante, Shakespeare, Cervantes, Newton, Locke, Adam Smith, Kant, Darwin, Marx, Tolstoy and Freud — including complete works of literature, philosophy, mathematics, natural science, economics and social science.

Second edition

A second edition appeared in 1990 in 60 volumes, adding works by twentieth-century authors and additional texts by earlier writers, though the selection continued to be criticized for the near-absence of women authors and for its largely European focus.

Reception and legacy

The set was an ambitious commercial and educational venture, sold by door-to-door salesmen to middle-class families. Critics such as Dwight Macdonald mocked its marketing, while educators debated its canonical choices. The series remains a prominent embodiment of the perennialist, Great Books approach to liberal education still used at colleges such as St. John's College.
