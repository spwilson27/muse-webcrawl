Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh (born Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark, later Philip Mountbatten; 10 June 1921 – 9 April 2021), was the husband of Queen Elizabeth II and consort of the British monarch from her accession on 6 February 1952 until his death in 2021, making him the longest-serving royal consort in British history.

[Image placeholder: Prince_Philip,_Duke_of_Edinburgh.png — Prince Philip in later life]

Early life and naval service

Philip was born in Corfu into the Greek and Danish royal families, but his family was exiled from Greece when he was an infant. He was educated in France, Germany and the United Kingdom, and joined the Royal Navy in 1939. During the Second World War he served with distinction in the Mediterranean and Pacific fleets, rising to the rank of lieutenant.

Marriage and role

He married Princess Elizabeth in November 1947, having renounced his Greek and Danish titles and become a naturalised British subject under the surname Mountbatten. On Elizabeth's accession he became her consort, supporting her duties, undertaking tens of thousands of engagements, and serving as patron of hundreds of organisations. In 1956 he founded the Duke of Edinburgh's Award, a youth development programme now operating in many countries.

Later life and death

Philip retired from public duties in 2017 at the age of 96. He died of old age at Windsor Castle on 9 April 2021, aged 99, two months before his 100th birthday. His funeral was held at St George's Chapel, Windsor.
