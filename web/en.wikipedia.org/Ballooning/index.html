Ballooning
Discipline: Aviation / sport
First manned flight: 21 November 1783, Paris

Ballooning is the activity of flying hot air balloons or gas balloons. It is the oldest form of human flight: the first untethered manned flight took place in Paris on 21 November 1783, when Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d'Arlandes ascended in a Montgolfier brothers' hot air balloon.

Principles

A balloon rises because the air inside its envelope is heated (or filled with a lighter-than-air gas such as helium or hydrogen), making the craft buoyant. Pilots control altitude with a burner and venting, and steer only indirectly by seeking wind currents at different heights.

Sport and competition

Modern ballooning is a popular leisure activity and sport. Competitions involve precision tasks such as dropping markers on targets. Major festivals, including the Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta in New Mexico, draw hundreds of balloons. Balloonists also set distance and duration records, including the first non-stop circumnavigation of the Earth by balloon, achieved by Bertrand Piccard and Brian Jones in 1999.

Types

Hot air balloons dominate recreational flying; gas balloons are used for long-distance flights and races such as the Gordon Bennett Cup. Tethered balloons and special-shape balloons are common at events.
