Wong Kar-wai

Wong Kar-wai (born 17 July 1958) is a Hong Kong film director, screenwriter and producer, internationally celebrated for his visually lush, highly stylised films about love, longing and the passage of time. He is a central figure of the Hong Kong Second Wave and one of the most influential filmmakers in world cinema.

Career

Born in Shanghai, Wong moved to Hong Kong at age five. After working as a television production assistant and screenwriter, he made his directorial debut with the crime drama As Tears Go By (1988). Days of Being Wild (1990) began a loose trilogy continued in In the Mood for Love (2000) — frequently ranked among the greatest films ever made — and 2046 (2004).

Style

Working closely with cinematographer Christopher Doyle, Wong developed a signature style: saturated colours, step-printing and slow motion, neon-lit urban spaces, voice-over narration, and motifs of clocks, expiry dates and missed connections. His films, including Chungking Express (1994), Fallen Angels (1995) and Happy Together (1997) — which won him Best Director at Cannes — are often shot without finished scripts and edited over long periods. Later works include The Grandmaster (2013) and the television series Blossoms Shanghai (2023).
