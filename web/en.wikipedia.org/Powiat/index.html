Powiat

A powiat (pronounced [ˈpɔvjat]; plural: powiaty) is the second-level unit of local government and administration in Poland, equivalent to a county, district, or prefecture (LAU-1, formerly NUTS-4) in other countries. A powiat is part of a larger unit or province called a voivodeship (Polish: województwo). Generally speaking, a powiat is subdivided into smaller units, or municipalities, known as gminas. However, larger cities and towns have powiat status in their own right (miasto na prawach powiatu, a city with powiat rights, often called an independent city).

History

The history of Polish powiats goes back to the second half of the 14th century, when they became the lowest level of territorial unit used for organizing the state's system of justice, replacing the castellanies. Powiats survived in various forms through the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the partition period, and the interwar republic. They were abolished in 1975 in favour of a two-tier system of 49 small voivodeships and municipalities, and restored on 1 January 1999 under the local government reforms that reintroduced the three-tier structure of voivodeships, powiats, and gminas.

Government and functions

A powiat is governed by an elected council (rada powiatu), which elects an executive board (zarząd powiatu) headed by a starosta. Cities with powiat rights are run by a city mayor (prezydent miasta) and city council, which jointly exercise gmina and powiat functions. Powiat responsibilities include secondary education, hospitals and public health, county roads, public transport organization, social welfare institutions, building supervision, geodesy, and consumer protection.

As of the 2020s, Poland has 380 powiats: 314 land powiats (powiaty ziemskie) and 66 city powiats (miasta na prawach powiatu). The term is rendered in English sources variously as "county" or "district", and is analogous to the German Landkreis.
