Lexicographic order

In mathematics, the lexicographic or lexicographical order (also known as lexical order or dictionary order) is a generalization of the alphabetical order of dictionaries to sequences of ordered elements.

Definition

Given two sequences of elements from a totally ordered set, the first sequence is smaller in lexicographic order if, at the first position where the two sequences differ, its element is smaller than the corresponding element of the second sequence. If one sequence is a prefix of the other, the shorter sequence is considered smaller.

Examples

Words are ordered lexicographically in dictionaries: "cat" precedes "catalog" because it is a prefix, and "catalog" precedes "cattle" because at the first differing position 'a' precedes 't'. The same order applies to tuples of numbers: (1, 2, 9) precedes (1, 3, 0) because 2 < 3 at the second position.

Properties and uses

Lexicographic order on tuples of totally ordered sets is itself a total order. It is used in combinatorics for ordering permutations and combinations, in computer science for sorting strings and comparing version numbers, and in optimization, where lexicographic ordering can rank solutions by a sequence of criteria in decreasing priority.
