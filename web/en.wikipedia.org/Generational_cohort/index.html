Generational cohort

A timeline of named Western generational cohorts from the Lost Generation to Generation Alpha.

A generational cohort is a group of individuals born within a defined period who are collectively influenced by a similar set of historical events, cultural trends, and social changes during their formative years. The concept is widely used in demography, sociology, marketing, and popular culture to describe how shared experiences shape the values and behaviours of people of similar ages.

Concept

The idea that people are shaped by the era in which they come of age was developed by sociologists such as Karl Mannheim in his 1928 essay "The Problem of Generations". A cohort's members typically experience major events — wars, economic crises, technological revolutions — at similar points in their lives, which may leave a lasting imprint on their outlook.

Named cohorts

In the Western world, commonly recognised cohorts include the Lost Generation, the Greatest Generation, the Silent Generation, Baby Boomers, Generation X, Millennials (Generation Y), Generation Z, and Generation Alpha. The exact birth-year ranges used for each cohort vary between researchers and countries, and the labels are less consistently applied outside the United States.

Criticism

Critics argue that generational labels can be overly broad stereotypes that exaggerate differences between age groups while ignoring differences of class, region, and culture within them. Empirical studies often find that variation within a cohort exceeds average differences between cohorts.
