Chinese historiography


Ancient records on bamboo slips

Chinese historiography is the study of the techniques and sources used by historians of China, and of the historical tradition itself, which is among the world's oldest continuous traditions of historical writing. From early court annals to the dynastic histories compiled under state sponsorship, historical writing held a central place in Chinese political and literary culture, closely tied to Confucian ideas of moral instruction and legitimate rule.

Early traditions

The foundations were laid by works such as the Spring and Autumn Annals of the state of Lu and its commentary, the Zuo Zhuan. The historian Sima Qian (c. 145–86 BC) created the definitive model with his Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian), which combined imperial annals, chronological tables, treatises on topics such as ritual and astronomy, accounts of hereditary houses, and biographies — the "annals-biography" (jizhuanti) format followed by nearly all later official histories. His successor Ban Gu applied the format to a single dynasty in the Book of Han.

Dynastic histories

From the Tang dynasty onward, each dynasty established a history office to compile the official record of its predecessor, drawing on court diaries, veritable records and archives. The resulting Twenty-Four Histories, spanning from the Shiji to the History of Ming, form an immense continuous record. Alongside them developed annalistic works such as Sima Guang's Zizhi Tongjian (1084), a chronological narrative intended as a "comprehensive mirror for aid in government", as well as local gazetteers, encyclopaedias (leishu) and private histories. Chinese historiography conventionally interpreted history through the dynastic cycle and the Mandate of Heaven, with historians expected to render moral judgements — praising and blaming — on rulers and officials.

Modern developments

In the 20th century, historians such as Liang Qichao called for a "new historiography" influenced by Western methods, while the Gu Jiegang school subjected ancient texts to radical source criticism. After 1949, Marxist frameworks structured official historiography in the People's Republic, even as archival, social and economic history broadened; in recent decades Chinese historiography has diversified considerably, engaging global debates on social, cultural and environmental history.
