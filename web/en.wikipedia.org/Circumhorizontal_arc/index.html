Circumhorizontal arc

A circumhorizontal arc is an optical phenomenon, a halo formed by the refraction of sunlight (or moonlight) through horizontally oriented, plate-shaped ice crystals in cirrus or cirrostratus clouds. It appears as a bright, rainbow-like band running roughly parallel to the horizon, well below the Sun, and is sometimes popularly called a "fire rainbow," though it involves no fire and is not a rainbow.

Formation

The phenomenon occurs only when the Sun is high in the sky, at an elevation of at least 58 degrees, so it is more common at lower latitudes and in summer. Light enters the vertical side faces of the ice crystals and exits through their horizontal bottom faces, producing strong spectral separation and vivid colour, often brighter and purer than that of a rainbow. The arc is the counterpart of the circumzenithal arc, which appears when the Sun is low.
