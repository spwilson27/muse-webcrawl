History
Word originGreek historia, "inquiry"
"Father of History"Herodotus
ScopeThe human past, chiefly from written records

History is the systematic study and documentation of the human past. As an academic discipline it analyses and interprets evidence — written records above all — to construct narratives and explanations of past events, societies and change over time. The period before writing is called prehistory and is studied chiefly by archaeology.

The discipline

The word derives from Greek historia, learning by inquiry, the title of Herodotus's 5th-century BCE account of the Greco-Persian Wars. Historians gather sources, subject them to criticism, and synthesise them into accounts that weigh causes, contexts and consequences. The study of how history is written is historiography; the techniques are the historical method.

Development

Historical writing arose independently in many cultures: Sima Qian in China, al-Tabari and Ibn Khaldun in the Islamic world, medieval chronicles in Europe. In the 19th century history was professionalised in universities, centred on archives and political narrative. The 20th century widened it dramatically — the Annales school and its successors added economic, social, cultural, environmental and global history, and voices once excluded: women, colonised peoples, the poor.

Branches

History subdivides by period (ancient, medieval, modern), place (national and regional histories), and theme — political, military, economic, social, intellectual, religious and environmental history among many. It borders and borrows from archaeology, anthropology, geography, economics and literary studies, and it supplies the raw material of public memory, museums and historical fiction.

Why it matters

History informs identity and citizenship, tests claims about how societies work, and preserves the record against which present decisions can be judged — resumés of dead people, as the saying goes, from which the living still learn.
