Andreessen Horowitz (often stylised a16z, a numeronym from the sixteen letters between the "a" of Andreessen and the "z" of Horowitz) is an American venture capital firm founded in 2009 by Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz. Headquartered in Menlo Park, California, it became one of the largest and most influential venture firms in Silicon Valley, managing tens of billions of dollars across funds devoted to software, consumer internet, enterprise, fintech, biotechnology, crypto and other sectors.

History

Andreessen, co-creator of the Mosaic web browser and co-founder of Netscape, and Horowitz, his partner at the enterprise software company Opsware, launched the firm with an initial fund of US$300 million. Its model differed from traditional venture firms: it built a large staff of operating specialists — in recruiting, marketing, business development and policy — to serve portfolio companies, arguing that venture capital should function as a platform rather than merely finance.

Investments

Early investments included Facebook, Twitter, Airbnb, GitHub, Lyft, Pinterest, Slack and Coinbase, many of which became defining technology companies of the 2010s. The firm launched a dedicated crypto fund in 2018 and grew into one of the largest investors in blockchain startups, as well as major funds for bio/health and "American dynamism" (defence, manufacturing and infrastructure technology).

Influence and criticism

a16z is known for an extensive media operation of blogs and podcasts and for Marc Andreessen's essays, including "Software is eating the world" (2011) and the "Techno-Optimist Manifesto" (2023). Critics have questioned its influence over startup governance, its hype-driven marketing, and heavy losses in some crypto ventures. In 2019 the firm restructured as a registered investment adviser, allowing it to make larger and more flexible investments.
