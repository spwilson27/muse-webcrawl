Turkish constitutional history

The Grand National Assembly, Turkey's legislature

Turkish constitutional history covers the development of fundamental laws governing the Ottoman Empire and the Republic of Turkey, from the first written constitutional documents of the 19th century to the present-day Constitution of 1982 and its amendments.

Ottoman period

The first steps toward constitutional government came with the Sened-i İttifak (1808), the Tanzimat edicts of Gülhane (1839) and Islahat (1856). The Constitution of 1876 (Kanun-i Esasi), drafted under Midhat Pasha, was the first constitution of the Ottoman Empire, establishing a parliament and the First Constitutional Era; it was suspended by Abdülhamid II in 1878 and restored by the Young Turk Revolution of 1908, beginning the Second Constitutional Era.

Republic of Turkey

The Constitution of 1921, adopted during the Turkish War of Independence, asserted national sovereignty. The Constitution of 1924 governed the early Republic, providing for a parliamentary system and (after 1928 and 1937 amendments) a secular republic. Following the 1960 coup, the liberal Constitution of 1961 introduced bicameralism, a constitutional court and proportional representation. After the 1980 coup, the military regime promulgated the Constitution of 1982, which — though heavily amended over the years, including the 2017 referendum that introduced an executive presidential system — remains in force.
