The California Department of Parks and Recreation, known as California State Parks, manages the California state park system, the largest and most diverse state park system in the United States. It administers roughly 280 park units comprising more than 1.4 million acres, including state parks, state recreation areas, state historic parks, beaches and reserves, attracting tens of millions of visitors each year.

History

The system began with the protection of Yosemite Valley and the Mariposa Grove in 1864 and the creation of Big Basin Redwoods State Park in 1902, the first park established specifically to save old-growth redwoods. The modern department dates to 1927, when California created the State Park Commission; a 1928 bond act funded rapid expansion under landscape architect Frederick Law Olmsted Jr.'s survey of potential parklands.

Scope

California State Parks protects about one-third of the state's coastline, the largest remaining old-growth redwood forests in parks such as Humboldt Redwoods, desert landscapes in Anza-Borrego Desert State Park — the largest state park in California — and historic sites including Hearst Castle, Old Town San Diego and Marshall Gold Discovery State Historic Park. The system also includes off-highway vehicle recreation areas and the state historic park at Columbia, a preserved Gold Rush town.

Challenges

The department has faced recurrent funding crises, deferred-maintenance backlogs measured in the hundreds of millions of dollars, and threats of park closures during state budget shortfalls, notably in 2009 and 2011. Wildfire, drought and coastal erosion pose growing management challenges, and a 2012 scandal over hidden funds led to leadership changes and reforms.
