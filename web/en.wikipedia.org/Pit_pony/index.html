Pit pony

A pit pony was a type of pony used underground in coal mines, mainly from the mid-18th to the mid-20th century, to haul tubs of coal from the coal face to the shaft bottom. Small, sturdy ponies — often Shetland or Welsh types — were preferred because of the low roofs in mine roadways.

Working life

Ponies were stabled underground for much of the year, working shifts hauling coal tubs and typically brought to the surface only during holidays. In Britain their numbers peaked at around 70,000 in 1913; welfare reforms and mechanisation steadily reduced their use, and the last British pit ponies were retired in the 1990s.
