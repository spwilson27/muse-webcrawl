Sharm el-Sheikh

Sharm el-Sheikh (Arabic: شرم الشيخ) is an Egyptian city on the southern tip of the Sinai Peninsula, on the coastal strip between the Red Sea and Mount Sinai. It is a major resort town and administrative centre of the South Sinai Governorate.

Geography and tourism

Situated at the mouth of the Gulf of Aqaba near the Straits of Tiran, Sharm el-Sheikh is renowned for clear waters, coral reefs, and diving sites, including Ras Muhammad National Park and the SS Thistlegorm wreck. Naama Bay is the centre of its resort strip, with hotels, restaurants, and nightlife.

History

Long a small fishing and naval anchorage, the area was occupied by Israel after the Six-Day War of 1967 and returned to Egypt under the 1979 peace treaty, with Israeli withdrawal completed in 1982. Egypt then developed it rapidly for tourism. The city has hosted numerous international diplomatic conferences, including Middle East peace summits and, in 2022, the COP27 climate conference.

The city is served by Sharm el-Sheikh International Airport and is connected by road to Dahab, Taba, and Cairo via tunnel under the Suez Canal.
