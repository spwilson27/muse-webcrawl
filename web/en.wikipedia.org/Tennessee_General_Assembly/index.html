Tennessee General Assembly

The Tennessee State Capitol, seat of the General Assembly

The Tennessee General Assembly is the state legislature of the U.S. state of Tennessee. It is a bicameral body consisting of the Tennessee Senate (33 members) and the Tennessee House of Representatives (99 members). It meets in the Tennessee State Capitol in Nashville.

Constitutional basis

The legislature derives its authority from the Tennessee Constitution of 1870. Senators serve four-year staggered terms; representatives serve two-year terms. Members must be qualified voters, and there are age and residency requirements for each chamber. The General Assembly convenes annually, typically beginning on the second Tuesday in January, with sessions limited by the state constitution.

Leadership

The Senate is presided over by the Lieutenant Governor and Speaker of the Senate, while the House elects its own Speaker. Tennessee's legislature is considered a part-time "citizen legislature," though its workload has grown steadily. The governor may call special sessions on extraordinary occasions; the General Assembly may also call itself into special session on the written request of two-thirds of the members of each house.

Notable actions

The General Assembly played a central role in the ratification of the Nineteenth Amendment to the U.S. Constitution in 1920, when Tennessee became the 36th and decisive state to approve women's suffrage. It was also the legislature that passed the Butler Act of 1925, prohibiting the teaching of human evolution in public schools, which led to the Scopes "Monkey Trial."
