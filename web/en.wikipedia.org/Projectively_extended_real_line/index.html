Projectively extended real line

In real analysis, the projectively extended real line (also called the one-point compactification of the real line, or the real projective line) is the extension of the number line by a single additional point, the point at infinity, denoted ∞. Unlike the affinely extended real line, which adds two endpoints +∞ and −∞, the projective extension identifies the two ends: the line is closed into a loop, topologically a circle, so that lines through the origin meet the line "at infinity" in one point regardless of direction.

Properties

The projectively extended real line is denoted ℝ̂ or ℝℙ1. It is the real projective space of dimension one: points correspond to lines through the origin in ℝ2, or equivalently to ratios [x : y] of homogeneous coordinates, with ∞ = [1 : 0]. Arithmetic is partially extended: for nonzero a, a/0 = ∞ and a/∞ = 0, while 0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, and ∞ + ∞ remain undefined. The sign of infinity is not defined, since approaching ∞ from either side converges to the same point.

Möbius transformations

The structure is the natural domain of real Möbius transformations, x ↦ (ax + b)/(cx + d), which become continuous everywhere once ∞ is included, with the point where the denominator vanishes mapping to ∞. The group of these transformations is the projective general linear group PGL(2, ℝ), closely related to the projective special linear group PSL(2, ℝ). Stereographic-type projection wraps the line onto a circle, exhibiting the compactification concretely.

Uses

The construction underlies projective geometry, the study of perspective in art and computer graphics, and the treatment of poles and essential singularities via the Riemann sphere in the complex analogue, where ℂ gains a single point at infinity to form ℂ̂.
