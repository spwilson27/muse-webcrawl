Tepic

Tepic (Spanish pronunciation: [teˈpik]) is the capital and largest city of the western Mexican state of Nayarit, as well as the seat of the Tepic municipality. It stands in a valley in the foothills of the Sierra Madre Occidental at an elevation of about 920 metres (3,020 ft). The metropolitan area, which includes the neighbouring municipality of Xalisco, has a population of roughly half a million, making it one of the mid-sized urban centres of western Mexico.

History

The area was inhabited before the Spanish conquest by peoples including the Cora and Huichol. Tepic was founded in 1531 by Nuño Beltrán de Guzmán, the conquistador who used it as a base for expeditions into north-western Mexico; the first Spanish settlement in the region was established at nearby Compostela before the capital was moved. During the colonial era Tepic grew as an agricultural and commercial centre on the road from Guadalajara to the port of San Blas. It became the capital of the territory of Tepic in 1884 and of the new state of Nayarit when it was created in 1917.

Geography and climate

The extinct Sangangüey volcano overlooks the city. Tepic has a humid subtropical climate strongly influenced by altitude and the Pacific rainy season: summers are warm and wet, winters mild and dry. The surrounding municipality produces sugar cane, tobacco, coffee, mangoes, and avocados.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the neoclassical Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception facing the main plaza, the regional museum housed in an 18th-century mansion, and the Juan Escutia museum. The poet Amado Nervo, one of Mexico's best-known writers, was born in Tepic in 1870; the revolutionary-era general and politician Juan Escutia, counted among the Niños Héroes, is also associated with the city. Tepic is served by Amado Nervo International Airport and lies on the highway between Guadalajara and Mazatlán.
