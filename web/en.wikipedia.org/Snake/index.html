Snake

Snakes are elongated limbless reptiles of the suborder Serpentes. Like all squamates, snakes are ectothermic, amniote vertebrates covered in overlapping scales. Many species of snakes have skulls with several more joints than their lizard ancestors and highly mobile jaws, enabling them to swallow prey much larger than their heads. Living snakes are found on every continent except Antarctica, and on most smaller land masses, with around 4,000 known species ranging in size from the 10 cm Barbados threadsnake to the 7.6 m reticulated python.

Evolution

The fossil record suggests snakes evolved from burrowing or aquatic lizards during the Cretaceous period; the earliest true snake fossils date to about 112–94 million years ago. Some primitive snakes retained vestigial hind limbs. Molecular evidence indicates snakes are most closely related to anguimorph lizards such as monitors.

Biology

Snakes are carnivores that eat small animals including rodents, birds, frogs, fish, and insects, swallowed whole. Their internal organs are elongated to fit their narrow bodies, most species having one functional lung. Most snakes are nonvenomous and kill prey by constriction or by swallowing it alive; about 600 species are venomous, using modified saliva delivered through fangs to subdue prey and for defence. Snakes perceive their environment by flicking their forked tongues to collect scent particles for the Jacobson's organ, and many can sense infrared radiation and ground vibrations.

Locomotion and skin

Despite lacking limbs, snakes move efficiently using lateral undulation, rectilinear movement, concertina movement, and sidewinding. Snakes periodically shed their outer skin (ecdysis), typically before growth spurts and in preparation for reproduction.

Interaction with humans

Snakes appear prominently in mythology, religion, and medicine; the Rod of Asclepius remains a symbol of medicine. Snakebites cause tens of thousands of deaths annually, chiefly in rural tropical regions, while snakes also provide essential rodent control and are kept as pets and farmed for skin and meat.
