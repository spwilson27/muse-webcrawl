Nazi book burnings

The Nazi book burnings were a campaign by the German Student Union and Nazi authorities in 1933 to ceremonially burn books deemed "un-German". The largest burning took place on 10 May 1933 at the Opernplatz (now Bebelplatz) in Berlin, where some 20,000 books were burned, with similar actions in university towns across Germany.

Background

Weeks after Hitler came to power in January 1933, the student union's "Action against the Un-German Spirit" targeted works by Jewish, pacifist, socialist, and other disfavoured authors. Propaganda Minister Joseph Goebbels spoke at the Berlin burning. Libraries were looted, notably the Institute for Sexual Science of Magnus Hirschfeld.

Authors targeted

Burned writers included Heinrich Mann, Erich Maria Remarque, Sigmund Freud, Karl Marx, Bertolt Brecht, Thomas Mann, Albert Einstein, and Helen Keller. Heinrich Heine's 1821 line — "Where they burn books, they will also ultimately burn people" — from his play Almansor, is now inscribed at the Bebelplatz memorial, designed by Micha Ullman (1995): an underground room of empty white shelves visible through a glass plate.

Legacy

The burnings became an enduring symbol of censorship and the road to the Holocaust, commemorated annually in Germany and cited in free-expression campaigns worldwide.
