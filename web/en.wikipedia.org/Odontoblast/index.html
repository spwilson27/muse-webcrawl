Odontoblast

In vertebrates, an odontoblast is a cell of neural crest origin that is part of the outer surface of the dental pulp, whose biological function is dentinogenesis, the production of dentin, the substance beneath the tooth enamel and cementum.

Structure

Odontoblasts form a single layer of tall columnar cells at the periphery of the pulp, immediately inside the dentin. Their cell bodies lie in the pulp while slender cytoplasmic processes, called odontoblast processes (or Tomes fibers), extend into dentinal tubules within the dentin. The cells are aligned with their processes pointing outward toward the enamel-dentin junction.

Function

During tooth development, odontoblasts secrete primary dentin. After the tooth has formed, they continue to produce secondary dentin slowly throughout life, which gradually reduces the size of the pulp chamber. In response to injury or caries, they can produce tertiary (reparative or reactionary) dentin. Odontoblasts also appear to play a role in sensing stimuli transmitted through the dentinal tubules and in innate immune defense of the pulp, expressing pattern-recognition receptors and producing antimicrobial molecules.

Development

Odontoblasts differentiate from cells of the dental papilla under signals from the inner enamel epithelium, mediated by growth factors such as TGF-beta. Once differentiated, they remain post-mitotic for the life of the tooth.
