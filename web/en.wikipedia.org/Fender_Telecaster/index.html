Fender Telecaster
Manufacturer: Fender
Period: 1950–present
Body type: Solid
Scale: 25.5 in (648 mm)
Pickups: Typically two single-coils

The Fender Telecaster, colloquially known as the Tele, is an electric guitar produced by Fender. Together with its sister model, the Esquire, it was the world's first mass-produced, commercially successful solid-body electric guitar. Its simple yet effective design and revolutionary sound broke ground and set trends in electric guitar manufacturing and popular music.

History

Developed by Leo Fender in Fullerton, California, in 1950, the guitar was introduced first as the single-pickup Esquire and then as the two-pickup Broadcaster. After a trademark objection from Gretsch (which sold Broadkaster drums), the guitar was briefly sold without a name ("Nocaster") before becoming the Telecaster in 1951, its name evoking the new medium of television.

Design

The Telecaster has a flat, single-cutaway slab body (typically ash or alder), a bolt-on maple neck, and a bridge assembly with three adjustable saddles through which the strings pass through the body. Its bridge pickup, mounted in the metal bridge plate, gives the Telecaster its characteristic bright, cutting "twang". Controls are a volume knob, a tone knob and a pickup selector switch on a metal plate.

Legacy

The design has remained in continuous production for over seventy years with only minor changes. It is strongly associated with country music but has been used across rock, blues, jazz, punk and indie; notable players include James Burton, Keith Richards, Bruce Springsteen, Muddy Waters, Albert Collins and Jonny Greenwood.
