United Nations General Assembly Resolution 194

United Nations General Assembly Resolution 194 is a resolution adopted on 11 December 1948, near the end of the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. It established the United Nations Conciliation Commission for Palestine and is best known for its Article 11 concerning refugees displaced by the conflict.

Content

Resolution 194 endorsed the principles set out by the assassinated UN Mediator, Folke Bernadotte, called for demilitarization and international control of Jerusalem, and instructed the Conciliation Commission to facilitate the repatriation, resettlement and rehabilitation of refugees.

Article 11

Article 11 resolves that refugees wishing to return to their homes and live at peace with their neighbours should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date, and that compensation should be paid for the property of those choosing not to return. The interpretation of this article has remained central to debates over the Palestinian right of return ever since.

Legacy

The resolution is reaffirmed annually by the General Assembly and is frequently cited in discussions of a settlement of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, alongside Security Council Resolution 242.
