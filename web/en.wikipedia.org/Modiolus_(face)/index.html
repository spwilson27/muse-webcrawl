Modiolus (face)


Location: Corner of the mouth
Structure: Fibromuscular mass

In facial anatomy, the modiolus is a chiasma of facial muscles held together by fibrous tissue, located lateral to each corner of the mouth. It is sometimes described, by analogy with the hub of a wheel — modiolus is Latin for "the hub of a wheel" — as the point at which several muscles of facial expression converge.

Muscles involved

As many as nine muscles converge at the modiolus, including the orbicularis oris, buccinator, levator anguli oris, depressor anguli oris, zygomaticus major, risorius, platysma, levator labii superioris, and depressor labii inferioris. Their coordinated action allows the corner of the mouth to be raised, lowered, and drawn laterally, contributing to expressions such as smiling, grimacing, and speech articulation.

Clinical significance

The modiolus is important in dentistry and maxillofacial surgery: impressions for dentures must account for its movement so that prostheses are not displaced, and the region is a reference point in reconstructive surgery of the lips and cheeks. Its position can often be felt as a slight thickening when the corner of the mouth is moved against resistance.
