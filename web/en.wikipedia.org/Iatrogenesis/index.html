Iatrogenesis

Iatrogenesis is the causation of a disease, a harmful complication, or other ill effect by any medical activity, including diagnosis, intervention, error, or negligence. From the Greek iatros ("physician") and genesis ("origin"), the term refers to harm "brought forth by the healer". Examples include surgical complications, adverse drug reactions, hospital-acquired infections and errors in diagnosis or treatment.

Causes

Iatrogenic harm can arise from medical error, adverse effects of otherwise appropriate treatment, faulty systems or equipment, and the inherent risks of diagnostic and therapeutic procedures. Ivan Illich's 1975 book Medical Nemesis popularized a broader social critique, distinguishing clinical, social and cultural iatrogenesis.

Epidemiology

Studies of patient safety have found adverse events in a significant proportion of hospital admissions, many of them preventable. Landmark reports such as the Institute of Medicine's To Err Is Human (1999) drew attention to medical error as a leading cause of death and spurred the modern patient-safety movement.
