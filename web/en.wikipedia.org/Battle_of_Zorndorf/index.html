Battle of Zorndorf

The Battle of Zorndorf was fought on 25 August 1758 during the Seven Years' War, near the village of Zorndorf in the Neumark (now Sarbinowo, Poland). A Prussian army of about 36,000 men under King Frederick the Great engaged a Russian army of about 43,000 under Count William Fermor. One of the bloodiest battles of the 18th century, Zorndorf ended without a decisive victor: the Russians withdrew from the field in good order but abandoned their invasion of Brandenburg, while Frederick could claim only a costly tactical success.

Background

Having occupied East Prussia in January 1758, the Russian army advanced west toward the Oder river, threatening Berlin itself. Frederick, fresh from checking the French and Imperial armies, marched rapidly east, crossed the Oder north of Küstrin, and manoeuvred to strike the Russians from an unexpected direction.

The battle

Frederick attempted his characteristic oblique attack against the Russian right wing, but the opening infantry assault was launched prematurely and against the wrong part of the Russian line. The battle degenerated into a series of ferocious frontal attacks and counter-attacks in clouds of dust and smoke. Prussian cavalry under Seydlitz repeatedly rescued the infantry at critical moments. The Russian infantry's stubbornness astonished the Prussians; many Russian soldiers reportedly fought on rather than retreat. By nightfall both armies were exhausted, holding parts of the same battlefield.

Aftermath

Fermor withdrew across the Warthe toward Landsberg in the following nights, and the Russian threat to Brandenburg receded for the year. Nevertheless Zorndorf shattered any Prussian notion of easily defeating Russia, and the slaughter — roughly a third of those engaged — shocked contemporaries. The war in the east would culminate in the Russian victory at Kunersdorf in 1759.
