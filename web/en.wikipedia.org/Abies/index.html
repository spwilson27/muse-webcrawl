Abies

FamilyPinaceae
Common nameFirs
SpeciesAbout 48–56
DistributionNorth and Central America, Europe, Asia, North Africa

Abies is a genus of about 48–56 species of evergreen coniferous trees in the family Pinaceae, known as the firs. They are found through much of temperate North and Central America, Europe, Asia and North Africa, typically in mountains. The name derives from the Latin abies, the silver fir.

Identification

Firs are distinguished from other pines by their needle-like leaves, which are attached singly to the branches by a base resembling a small suction cup and leave a smooth, circular scar when they fall. Their cones stand upright on the branches (unlike the hanging cones of spruces) and disintegrate at maturity, scattering their seeds while the cone's central spike remains. Trees are typically tall and conical, with whorled branches.

Uses

Species such as the European silver fir (Abies alba), balsam fir (A. balsamea), Nordmann fir (A. nordmanniana) and noble fir (A. procera) are valued for timber, pulp and as Christmas trees; the wood is generally light and not resistant to decay. Balsam fir yields Canada balsam, once used in optics and microscopy.
