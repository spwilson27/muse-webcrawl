The First Lady of the United States (abbreviated FLOTUS) is the title conventionally held by the hostess of the White House, usually the wife of the president of the United States, concurrent with the president's term in office. Although the first lady's role has never been codified or officially defined, she figures prominently in the political and social life of the United States. When the president is unmarried or widowed, the role has sometimes been filled by a relative acting as White House hostess.

History

Martha Washington set the template for the role, hosting regular receptions in the temporary capitals. Dolley Madison became celebrated for her social skill and political networking in the early republic. The term "first lady" came into general use in the mid-nineteenth century and was applied to figures such as Harriet Lane, the niece who served as hostess for the bachelor president James Buchanan. Edith Roosevelt professionalised the role around 1901, hiring the first social secretary; Eleanor Roosevelt transformed it, holding her own press conferences, writing a newspaper column and championing civil rights.

The modern role

Since the 1960s first ladies have typically adopted public initiatives: Lady Bird Johnson promoted environmental beautification, Nancy Reagan led the "Just Say No" anti-drug campaign, Hillary Clinton took a direct policy role in health-care reform and later became a senator and secretary of state, Laura Bush emphasised literacy, Michelle Obama launched the "Let's Move!" campaign against childhood obesity and supported military families, and Jill Biden continued teaching while serving as first lady. The Office of the First Lady, formalised in the Carter administration, supports these activities with its own chief of staff, press secretary and social office.

Influence and study

Scholars debate the democratic legitimacy and the influence of the unelected role; first ladies are consistently among the most publicly scrutinised figures in American life, and historians rank them alongside presidents. The Smithsonian Institution maintains a popular permanent exhibition of first ladies' inaugural gowns.
