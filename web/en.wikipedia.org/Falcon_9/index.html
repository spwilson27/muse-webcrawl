Falcon 9 is a reusable, two-stage-to-orbit, medium-lift launch vehicle designed and manufactured by American aerospace company SpaceX. The first version, Falcon 9 v1.0, was launched on 4 June 2010; the current operational version, Falcon 9 Block 5, entered service in May 2018. The rocket is powered by Merlin engines burning liquid oxygen and rocket-grade kerosene (RP-1) propellants.

Falcon 9 is the first orbital-class rocket capable of reflight: its first stage can land propulsively on a landing pad or on an autonomous drone ship at sea and be refurbished and flown again. This reusability has substantially reduced the cost of access to orbit.

History

SpaceX announced the Falcon 9 design in 2005 as a follow-on to the smaller Falcon 1, intending it to compete for NASA cargo and crew transport contracts to the International Space Station. Development was partly funded under NASA's Commercial Orbital Transportation Services (COTS) program. The first commercial resupply mission flew in October 2012. In December 2015, a Falcon 9 first stage landed successfully at Landing Zone 1 at Cape Canaveral, the first propulsive landing of an orbital rocket booster; the first successful drone-ship landing followed in April 2016.

Design

The Falcon 9 first stage is powered by nine Merlin 1D engines arranged in an "octaweb" configuration. The second stage uses a single vacuum-optimized Merlin engine. The interstage houses grid fins used to steer the first stage during atmospheric reentry, and cold gas thrusters for attitude control. Landing legs made of carbon fiber deploy shortly before touchdown.

Versions

  • v1.0 (2010–2013) – initial version with nine Merlin 1C engines.
  • v1.1 (2013–2016) – stretched tanks, Merlin 1D engines, 60% more thrust.
  • Full Thrust (2015–2018) – densified propellants, improved landing capability.
  • Block 5 (2018–present) – final major revision, optimized for rapid reuse and human-rating.
