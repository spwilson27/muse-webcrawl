Mordred

Mordred

Also known as: Medraut, Modredus
Occupation: Knight of the Round Table
Family: King Arthur (father/uncle), Morgause (mother)
First appearance: Annales Cambriae (10th century)

Mordred or Modred (Welsh: Medraut) is a figure of Arthurian legend, best known as a knight of the Round Table whose betrayal brings about the fall of King Arthur's kingdom. In later medieval tradition he is Arthur's illegitimate son by his half-sister Morgause, though earlier Welsh sources mention Medraut without clearly defining his relationship to Arthur or his villainy.

Early mentions

The earliest surviving reference is in the Annales Cambriae, which note the Battle of Camlann, "in which Arthur and Medraut fell", without saying whether they fought on the same side or against each other. Geoffrey of Monmouth's Historia Regum Britanniae (c. 1136) made Mordred Arthur's nephew and a usurper who seizes the throne and Queen Guinevere while Arthur campaigns abroad, leading to their mutual destruction at Camlann.

Later tradition

In the French Vulgate Cycle and Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur, Mordred is the incest-born son of Arthur and Morgause, prophesied to destroy the kingdom. Appointed regent during Arthur's absence, he forges news of the king's death, crowns himself, and attempts to marry Guinevere. Arthur returns, and at the final battle near Salisbury the two deal each other mortal wounds: Arthur kills Mordred with a spear but is himself fatally struck by Mordred's sword. Mordred is thus the archetypal traitor of the Arthurian world, though some modern retellings portray him more sympathetically.
