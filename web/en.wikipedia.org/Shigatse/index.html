Shigatse


Tashilhunpo Monastery, seat of the Panchen Lama.
Country: China
Region: Tibet Autonomous Region
Elevation: approx. 3,840 m (12,600 ft)
Population: approx. 800,000 (prefecture-level city)

Shigatse, officially known as Xigazê (Tibetan: གཞིས་ཀ་རྩེ་; Chinese: 日喀则), is a prefecture-level city of the Tibet Autonomous Region of the People's Republic of China. The urban centre, Samzhubzê District, is the second-largest city in Tibet after Lhasa, lying at the confluence of the Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra) and Nyang rivers at an elevation of about 3,840 metres.

History

Shigatse was the capital of the historical Tsang province and a major commercial and religious centre. Its great monastery, Tashilhunpo, founded in 1447 by the First Dalai Lama, Gendun Drup, became the traditional seat of the Panchen Lama, the second-highest figure in the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism. The Shigatse Dzong, a fortress dominating the town and once a model for Lhasa's Potala Palace architecture, was destroyed during the Cultural Revolution era and rebuilt in 2007.

Geography and climate

The prefecture-level city covers a vast area of southwestern Tibet, including much of the northern slopes of the Himalayas and bordering Nepal, Bhutan, and India. Mount Everest (Qomolangma) lies within its territory, and the Everest North Base Camp is reached through the city of Tingri. The climate is a high-altitude continental one, with cold winters, strong sunshine, and most rainfall concentrated in the summer monsoon.

Transport

Shigatse is connected to Lhasa by the Lhasa–Shigatse railway, opened in 2014, and by the China–Nepal Friendship Highway running to Kathmandu. Shigatse Peace Airport serves domestic flights. The city is the usual staging point for travellers heading to Everest Base Camp and Mount Kailash.
