Sultan Agung of Mataram

Sultan Agung Adi Prabu Hanyokrokusumo (1593 – 1645) was the third sultan of the Mataram Sultanate in Java, reigning from 1613 to 1645. Under his rule Mataram reached its greatest territorial extent, dominating most of Java and Madura, and he is remembered in Indonesia as a national hero.

Reign

Succeeding his father Panembahan Hanyokrowati, Sultan Agung spent his first decade subjugating the principalities of eastern Java, culminating in the conquest of Surabaya in 1625 after a long war. He moved the capital to the Yogyakarta region and pursued a self-consciously Islamic style of kingship, obtaining the title of Sultan from Mecca in 1641. In 1633 he introduced the Javanese calendar reform, fusing the Hindu Saka era with the Islamic lunar calendar — a system still used in Java.

War with the Dutch

Determined to expel the Dutch East India Company (VOC), Sultan Agung twice besieged Batavia (Jakarta), in 1628 and 1629. Both sieges failed owing to Dutch sea power, disease, and Mataram's overstretched supply lines, though they came close to success. He was buried at Imogiri, the royal cemetery he founded south of Yogyakarta. His reign is the subject of the famous Javanese chronicle tradition and of the novel Arus Balik by Pramoedya Ananta Toer in part; he was declared a National Hero of Indonesia in 1975.
