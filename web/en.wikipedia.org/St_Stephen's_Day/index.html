Saint Stephen's Day, also called the Feast of Saint Stephen, is a Christian saint's day to commemorate Saint Stephen, the first Christian martyr (the protomartyr), celebrated on 26 December in the Latin Church and 27 December in Eastern Christianity. It is a public holiday in many countries, and in several — the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa among them — 26 December is more widely known as Boxing Day.

Traditions

In Ireland the day is called Lá Fhéile Stiofáin or Wren Day (Lá an Dreoilín): groups of "wren boys" in straw dress traditionally hunted the wren — "the king of all birds" — and now parade with music collecting money for charity. The custom echoes the legend that a wren betrayed the hiding Stephen.

In Catalonia, Sant Esteve is a major family feast day, traditionally because families needed a day to travel home after Christmas gatherings; cannelloni made from Christmas leftovers are typical. In Finland (Tapaninpäivä) the day is associated with sleigh rides and visiting; in parts of Germany and Austria it is the "second day of Christmas" (Zweiter Weihnachtsfeiertag), a public holiday. Horse racing and football fixtures are traditional in Britain and Ireland, and in South Africa the day was renamed the Day of Goodwill in 1994.

Saint Stephen

Stephen, one of the first seven deacons of the early Church in Jerusalem, is described in the Acts of the Apostles as preaching before the Sanhedrin and being stoned to death (c. 34 CE), forgiving his executioners as he died; the young Saul (later Paul the Apostle) witnessed the execution. His relics were said to have been discovered in 415 CE, and his cult spread widely across Europe.
