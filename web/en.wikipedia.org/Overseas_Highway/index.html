Overseas Highway

The Overseas Highway is a 113-mile (182 km) highway carrying U.S. Route 1 through the Florida Keys, from Key Largo to Key West, Florida. Completed in its modern form in 1938, it crosses 42 bridges over the open waters of the Atlantic Ocean, Florida Bay, and the Gulf of Mexico, and is famed as one of the most scenic drives in the United States — "the highway that goes to sea".

History

The highway largely follows the route of Henry Flagler's Overseas Railroad, completed in 1912 as the Key West Extension of the Florida East Coast Railway. The catastrophic Labor Day Hurricane of 1935 destroyed much of the railroad, which was never rebuilt; the state of Florida purchased the right-of-way and converted the rail bridges to carry automobiles, opening the Overseas Highway in 1938. Many bridges were replaced in the 1980s; the longest, the Seven Mile Bridge near Marathon, was among the longest bridges in the world when built. The older Seven Mile Bridge segment to Pigeon Key is now a recreational trail.

Route

The highway passes through Key Largo, Islamorada, Marathon, Big Pine Key, and the Lower Keys before ending at Key West, the southernmost city in the contiguous United States. Mile markers counting down to zero at Key West are a local institution.
