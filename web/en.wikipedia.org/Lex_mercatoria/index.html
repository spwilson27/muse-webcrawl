Lex mercatoria

Lex mercatoria (from Latin for "merchant law") is the body of commercial law used by merchants throughout Europe during the medieval period. It evolved as a system of customs and best practices observed in international trade, enforced in merchant courts at fairs and ports rather than by state courts.

History

From roughly the 11th to the 16th centuries, merchant courts applied shared customs governing bills of exchange, maritime contracts, insurance and partnership, favouring speed, informality and good faith. Much of this customary law was later absorbed into national legal systems, including English common law through judges such as Lord Mansfield.

Modern debate

Scholars debate whether a "new lex mercatoria" exists today in the form of transnational commercial principles, model laws, standard terms and arbitral practice — reflected in instruments such as the UNIDROIT Principles and the CISG — independent of any single national law.
