Russian alphabet

The letters of the modern Russian alphabet.

The Russian alphabet is the script used to write the Russian language. It is a variant of the Cyrillic script and consists of 33 letters. It derives from the alphabet introduced to Kievan Rus' with Christianisation in the 10th century, itself based on the Early Cyrillic alphabet developed in the First Bulgarian Empire.

The letters

The modern alphabet contains 33 letters: А Б В Г Д Е Ё Ж З И Й К Л М Н О П Р С Т У Ф Х Ц Ч Ш Щ Ъ Ы Ь Э Ю Я. Of these, 21 are consonants, 10 are vowels, and two (Ъ and Ь) are signs that do not represent sounds on their own: the hard sign and the soft sign, which modify the preceding consonant.

History

The alphabet took its modern form through a series of reforms. Peter the Great's civil script reform of 1708–1710 simplified letterforms and dropped several letters. The last major change was the spelling reform of 1917–1918, prepared before the Russian Revolution and enacted by the Soviet government, which removed the letters Ѣ (yat), Ѳ (fita), and І, and eliminated the hard sign at the ends of words. The letter Ё, introduced in the 18th century, is often written simply as Е in print.

Transliteration

Russian is romanised by several competing systems, including the ALA-LC system used by libraries, ISO 9, and the systems used on passports and road signs. Transliteration is not fully reversible because of ambiguities such as Е, Ё, and Ю.
