Diabetic retinopathy

Diabetic retinopathy is a complication of diabetes mellitus in which chronically high blood sugar damages the blood vessels of the retina, the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye. It is a leading cause of vision loss among working-age adults in developed countries.

Stages

The disease progresses through stages. In non-proliferative retinopathy, weakened vessel walls develop microaneurysms and may leak fluid and blood into the retina; swelling of the macula (diabetic macular edema) can impair central vision. In proliferative retinopathy, the advanced stage, the retina grows fragile new blood vessels that can bleed into the vitreous, form scar tissue, and cause retinal detachment and severe vision loss.

Risk factors and symptoms

Risk increases with the duration of diabetes and poor control of blood glucose, blood pressure, and cholesterol. Early disease often has no symptoms, so people with diabetes are advised to have regular dilated eye examinations.

Treatment

Management centres on controlling blood sugar and blood pressure. Advanced disease may be treated with intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF drugs, laser photocoagulation, corticosteroids, and, for persistent bleeding or detachment, vitrectomy surgery.
