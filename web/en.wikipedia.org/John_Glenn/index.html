John Glenn

Born: 18 July 1921, Cambridge, Ohio, U.S.
Died: 8 December 2016 (aged 95), Columbus, Ohio
Occupations: Marine Corps aviator, astronaut, U.S. senator
Space missions: Mercury-Atlas 6; STS-95
Senate: Ohio, 1974–1999

John Herschel Glenn Jr. (July 18, 1921 – December 8, 2016) was an American Marine Corps aviator, engineer, astronaut, businessman and politician. In 1962 he became the first American to orbit the Earth, circling it three times aboard Friendship 7.

Military career

Glenn flew 59 combat missions in the Second World War and 90 in the Korean War, shooting down three MiG-15s. As a test pilot in 1957 he set a transcontinental speed record, flying from Los Angeles to New York in 3 hours 23 minutes.

Astronaut

Selected in 1959 as one of the Mercury Seven, Glenn flew the Mercury-Atlas 6 mission on 20 February 1962, becoming the first American in orbit. Concerns about his heat shield during re-entry made the flight especially dramatic. In 1998, at age 77, he flew aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery (STS-95), becoming the oldest person to fly in space.

Senate career

Glenn represented Ohio in the United States Senate from 1974 to 1999. He sought the Democratic presidential nomination in 1984. He chaired the Governmental Affairs Committee and was the chief author of the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Act of 1978.
