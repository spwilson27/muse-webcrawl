Crazy Horse
Lakota: Tȟašúŋke Witkó
Born: c. 1840, near Rapid Creek, present-day South Dakota
Died: September 5, 1877 (aged ~36), Fort Robinson, Nebraska
Nationality: Oglala Lakota
Known for: Battle of the Little Bighorn, resistance to U.S. encroachment

Crazy Horse (Lakota: Tȟašúŋke Witkó, literally “His-Horse-Is-Crazy”; c. 1840 – September 5, 1877) was a Native American war leader of the Oglala Lakota in the 19th century. He took up arms against the United States federal government to fight against encroachment by white American settlers on Native American territory and to preserve the traditional way of life of the Lakota people.

Early life

Crazy Horse was born near Rapid Creek in the Black Hills region. His father, also named Crazy Horse (later called Waglula, “Worm”), passed the name to him after he distinguished himself in battle; as a boy he was known as “Curly” because of his light, curly hair. As a young man he had a famous vision quest that shaped his life and his reputation as a fearless warrior.

War leadership

Crazy Horse fought in Red Cloud's War (1866–1868), including the Fetterman Fight of 1866, in which he acted as a decoy. During the Great Sioux War of 1876–77 he led warriors at the Battle of the Rosebud (June 17, 1876) and played a central role in the Lakota and Northern Cheyenne victory at the Battle of the Little Bighorn on June 25–26, 1876.

Surrender and death

Pressured by hunger and relentless military pursuit, Crazy Horse surrendered at Fort Robinson, Nebraska, on May 5, 1877. Amid fears he was plotting to leave the reservation, he was arrested on September 5, 1877; during a struggle he was bayoneted by a guard and died that night. No authenticated photograph of Crazy Horse is known to exist.

Legacy

The Crazy Horse Memorial, a mountain carving under construction in the Black Hills of South Dakota since 1948 under sculptor Korczak Ziolkowski and his family, is dedicated to his memory.
