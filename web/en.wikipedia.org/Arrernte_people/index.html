Arrernte people

The Arrernte people are an Aboriginal Australian people of Central Australia, whose traditional lands surround Alice Springs (Mparntwe) in the Northern Territory. Their language, Arrernte, is one of the most widely spoken Aboriginal languages.

Culture

Arrernte society is organised around a kinship system of skin names and a rich body of Dreaming (Altyerre) stories tied to features of the landscape, such as the MacDonnell Ranges. Ceremonial life includes song cycles and rock art traditions that encode law and history.

History

European settlement reached Arrernte country in the 1870s with the Overland Telegraph Line. The Hermannsburg Mission, founded in 1877, became an important centre; the watercolour artist Albert Namatjira was an Arrernte man from Hermannsburg. Land rights struggles in the 20th century led to the return of some traditional lands to Arrernte ownership.
