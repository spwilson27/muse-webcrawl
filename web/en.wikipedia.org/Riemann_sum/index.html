Riemann sum

In mathematics, a Riemann sum is a certain kind of approximation of an integral by a finite sum. It is named after nineteenth-century German mathematician Bernhard Riemann. One very common application is in numerical integration, i.e., approximating the area of functions or lines on a graph, where it is also known as the rectangle rule.

Definition

Let f be a function defined on an interval [a, b], and let the interval be divided into n subintervals of width Δx = (b − a)/n. Choosing a sample point xi* in each subinterval, the Riemann sum is:

Σi=1n f(xi*) Δx.

Types

Depending on where the sample point is chosen, one obtains the left Riemann sum (left endpoint), the right Riemann sum (right endpoint), or the midpoint Riemann sum (midpoint of each subinterval). The trapezoidal rule and Simpson's rule can be viewed as refinements based on related averages of Riemann sums.

Riemann integral

As the partition gets finer, the Riemann sum approaches the Riemann integral: a function is Riemann integrable if the sums converge to a single value as the maximum subinterval width tends to zero, regardless of the choice of sample points. Under mild conditions (such as continuity) this limit exists and equals the definite integral.
