CD4

CD4 (cluster of differentiation 4) is a glycoprotein that serves as a co-receptor for the T-cell receptor (TCR) on the surface of immune cells such as T helper cells, monocytes, macrophages and dendritic cells. It was discovered in the late 1970s and is encoded by the CD4 gene in humans.

Function

CD4 binds to MHC class II molecules on antigen-presenting cells, stabilising the interaction between a T helper cell and its target and amplifying the TCR signal through the associated kinase Lck. The number of CD4-expressing T cells ("CD4 count") is a key clinical measure of immune function.

Role in HIV

CD4 is the primary receptor used by the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) to enter host cells, together with co-receptors such as CCR5 and CXCR4. Progressive loss of CD4+ T cells is the hallmark of HIV infection and leads to AIDS when counts fall below critical levels.
