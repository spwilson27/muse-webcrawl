Trifunctional hypothesis
Proposed by: Georges Dumézil
Subject: Proto-Indo-European society and mythology

The trifunctional hypothesis of prehistoric Proto-Indo-European society holds that their ideology reflected a tripartite division of society into three functions: sovereignty, warfare and fertility (or productivity). It was developed by the French comparative mythologist Georges Dumézil from the 1920s onward.

The three functions

The first function, sovereignty, covered both magical-religious and legal authority, often split between two complementary figures. The second function represented military force and defence. The third function encompassed fertility, prosperity, herding, farming and crafts. Dumézil traced reflections of the scheme in Vedic India, where the varnas of Brahmin, Kshatriya and Vaishya correspond to the functions, as well as in Roman and Norse mythology.

Criticism

Scholars have debated how far the correspondences reflect a genuine inherited ideology rather than selective pattern-matching, and the hypothesis remains influential but contested within Indo-European studies.
