Robert Joseph Barro (born 28 September 1944) is an American macroeconomist and the Paul M. Warburg Professor of Economics at Harvard University. Barro is considered one of the founders of new classical macroeconomics, along with Robert Lucas Jr. and Thomas J. Sargent. He is currently a senior fellow at Stanford University's Hoover Institution and co-editor of the influential Quarterly Journal of Economics.

Contributions

Barro is best known for reviving and formalizing the idea of Ricardian equivalence in his 1974 paper "Are Government Bonds Net Wealth?", which argues that, under certain conditions, government deficits financed by borrowing rather than taxes have no effect on aggregate demand, because rational households save in anticipation of future tax liabilities. With Herschel Grossman he developed influential models of disequilibrium macroeconomics, and his later work turned to the empirical determinants of economic growth, culminating in the widely used textbook Economic Growth (with Xavier Sala-i-Martin).

His research on growth regressions across countries examined the effects of education, fertility, government consumption, the rule of law, and terms of trade on long-run growth rates. Barro has also studied the economics of religion with Rachel McCleary, the probability of rare macroeconomic disasters, and the pricing of disaster risk in asset markets.

Education and career

Barro earned a bachelor's degree in physics from the California Institute of Technology in 1965, where he studied under Richard Feynman, before switching to economics and receiving his PhD from Harvard in 1970. He taught at Brown University, the University of Chicago, and the University of Rochester before joining Harvard in 1987.
