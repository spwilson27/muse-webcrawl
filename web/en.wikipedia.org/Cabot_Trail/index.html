Cabot Trail

The Cabot Trail is a scenic highway on Cape Breton Island in Nova Scotia, Canada, renowned as one of the world's most spectacular drives. The 298-kilometre (185 mi) loop traverses the Cape Breton Highlands, passing through Cape Breton Highlands National Park and along dramatic coastal cliffs.

Route

Beginning and ending near Baddeck — summer home of Alexander Graham Bell — the trail climbs North Mountain, MacKenzie Mountain and French Mountain, with steep grades and hairpin turns overlooking the Gulf of St. Lawrence and the Atlantic Ocean. Communities along the route include St. Ann's, Ingonish, Neil's Harbour, Pleasant Bay and Chéticamp, an Acadian fishing village.

History and tourism

The highway was completed in 1932 and named after the explorer John Cabot (Giovanni Caboto), who landed in Atlantic Canada in 1497. It is a major tourist draw, especially in autumn for its fall colours, and supports whale watching, hiking — notably the Skyline Trail — and Celtic and Acadian cultural attractions.
