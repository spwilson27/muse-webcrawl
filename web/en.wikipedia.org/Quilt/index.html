Quilt

A patchwork quilt

A quilt is a multi-layered textile, traditionally composed of three layers: a woven top, a layer of batting or wadding, and a woven back, combined using the technique of quilting, the process of sewing the three layers together. The surface may be a single piece of fabric or a patched construction known as patchwork, often combined with appliqué.

History

Quilted garments were known in ancient Egypt and China, and quilted armour such as the medieval gambeson was worn under or instead of metal armour. In North America, quilting developed into a major folk art: settlers pieced tops from scraps, and communal "quilting bees" became social occasions. Signature quilts, Amish quilts, and African-American quilting traditions, including the celebrated quilts of Gee's Bend, Alabama, are recognised as significant art forms.

Construction

A quilt top may be whole-cloth, patchwork, or appliqué. Batting is traditionally cotton, wool, or polyester. The layers are basted and then stitched through with quilting stitches, by hand or machine, in patterns that may follow the piecing or form independent decorative motifs. The raw edges are finished with binding.

Uses

Quilts serve as bed coverings, wall hangings, and commemorative objects. The NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt, begun in 1987, is among the largest works of community folk art, commemorating people who died of AIDS-related causes.
