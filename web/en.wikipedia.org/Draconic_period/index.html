Draconic period

The draconic period, also called the draconitic period or nodal period, is the time taken for the Moon to return to the same node of its orbit — that is, to the same point where its orbit crosses the ecliptic. It lasts approximately 27.21222 days.

The name derives from the Latin draco (dragon), because in ancient and medieval astronomy the lunar nodes were called the "dragon's head" (ascending node) and "dragon's tail" (descending node). Eclipses can only occur when the Moon is near a node, so the draconic period is fundamental to eclipse prediction.

Relation to other lunar periods

The draconic period is slightly shorter than the sidereal month (27.32166 days) because the nodes themselves move slowly westward along the ecliptic, completing a full circuit in about 18.6 years. This nodal regression means the Moon reaches a node a little earlier each orbit.

The draconic period combines with the synodic month (29.53059 days) to determine the saros cycle of 223 synodic months, after which eclipses repeat with similar geometry.
