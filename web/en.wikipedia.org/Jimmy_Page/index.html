James Patrick Page OBE is an English musician, best known as the guitarist and founder of the rock band Led Zeppelin. He is widely considered one of the most influential guitarists in rock history.

Early career

Page began as a sought-after London session musician in the 1960s, playing on numerous recordings, before joining The Yardbirds in 1966, first on bass and then on lead guitar alongside Jeff Beck.

Led Zeppelin

In 1968 Page formed Led Zeppelin with Robert Plant, John Paul Jones and John Bonham. As the band's producer and principal composer of much of its music, he crafted landmark albums including Led Zeppelin IV (1971), featuring "Stairway to Heaven". His riffs on songs such as "Whole Lotta Love", "Kashmir" and "Black Dog", and his use of alternate tunings, bowing and studio experimentation, defined hard rock guitar.

Later work

After Led Zeppelin disbanded in 1980 following Bonham's death, Page worked with The Firm, Coverdale–Page, and Page and Plant, and performed at Led Zeppelin's 2007 reunion concert at London's O2 Arena.
