Mesrop Mashtots

Born: c. AD 362, Hatsik, Armenia

Died: AD 440, Vagharshapat

Known for: Creating the Armenian alphabet

Venerated as: Saint in the Armenian Church

Mesrop Mashtots (c. 362 – 440) was an Armenian linguist, composer, theologian, statesman, and hymnologist. He is best known for inventing the Armenian alphabet around AD 405, an achievement central to Armenian national and religious identity.

Creation of the alphabet

After Armenia adopted Christianity, the Bible and liturgy were read in Greek and Syriac, which most people could not understand. With the support of King Vramshapuh and Catholicos Sahak Partev, Mashtots devised a 36-letter alphabet (two letters were added later) suited to Armenian phonetics. He and his students then translated the Bible and other works into Armenian, launching a golden age of Armenian literature.

Other work

Mashtots founded schools and monasteries throughout Armenia and is credited in some traditions with helping devise scripts for Georgian and Caucasian Albanian as well. He is venerated as a saint, and his alphabet remains in use today, nearly unchanged.
