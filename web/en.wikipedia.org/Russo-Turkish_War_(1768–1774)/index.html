The Russo-Turkish War of 1768–1774 was one of the many conflicts between the Russian and Ottoman empires and a decisive episode in Russia's southward expansion. Sparked by border incidents connected with the Bar Confederation in Poland and a raid into Russian territory by Crimean Tatar forces, the war ended with the Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca, which established Russia as the dominant power on the northern Black Sea coast.

Course of the war

Russian armies under Pyotr Rumyantsev won brilliant victories at the Larga and Kagul rivers in 1770, overrunning Moldavia and Wallachia. In the same year a Russian Baltic squadron, sailing around Europe under Alexei Orlov, destroyed the Ottoman fleet at the Battle of Chesma in the Aegean — launching the "Orlov Revolt" among Greeks of the Morea. Alexander Suvorov won further victories at Turtukai and Kozludzha, while Russian forces occupied the Crimea in 1771.

Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca

The treaty, signed on 21 July 1774, gave Russia the fortresses of Kerch, Yenikale and Kinburn, free navigation on the Black Sea, and the steppe between the Bug and Dnieper rivers. The Crimean Khanate was declared independent of the Sultan (Russia annexed it in 1783). France's traditional protégé was humbled, and vague clauses granting Russia a right to make representations on behalf of Ottoman Orthodox Christians were later used to justify extensive interference in Ottoman affairs.

Significance

The war marked the beginning of the Ottoman Empire's long decline as a Black Sea power and elevated Catherine the Great's Russia to the front rank of European states. It also laid groundwork for the "Greek Project" and the next conflict, the Russo-Turkish War of 1787–1792.
