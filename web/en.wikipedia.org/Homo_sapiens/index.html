Homo sapiens

Homo sapiens
Temporal range: 0.3 million years ago – present
Family: Hominidae
Genus: Homo
Population: about 8 billion

Homo sapiens is the only extant species of the genus Homo. Anatomically modern humans originated in Africa about 300,000 years ago, as evidenced by fossils at Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, and later spread across the world, interbreeding in small amounts with archaic humans such as Neanderthals and Denisovans.

Biology

Humans are bipedal primates with large brains, opposable thumbs (see Hand), reduced body hair and complex language. Traits include prolonged childhood, omnivorous diet and highly social behaviour.

Evolution

The lineage diverged from chimpanzees roughly 6–7 million years ago. Earlier Homo species include H. erectus and H. neanderthalensis.

Culture and population

Humans create cumulative culture, technology and institutions; population passed 8 billion in 2022.
