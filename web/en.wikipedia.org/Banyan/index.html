Banyan

A banyan is a fig that develops accessory trunks from adventitious prop roots, allowing the tree to spread outwards indefinitely. This distinguishes many Ficus species commonly called banyans from other trees. Older banyan trees develop aerial prop roots that mature into thick, woody trunks indistinguishable from the primary trunk, so a single tree can cover a vast area and resemble a grove.

Ecology

Banyans typically begin life as epiphytes, germinating in the branches of a host tree; their roots reach the ground and thicken, eventually strangling the host, for which reason they are sometimes called strangler figs. The seeds are dispersed by fruit-eating birds. The Great Banyan in Kolkata, India, is one of the largest trees in the world by canopy coverage, spanning several acres.

Cultural significance

The banyan, particularly Ficus benghalensis, is the national tree of India and holds religious significance in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. The Buddha is said to have attained enlightenment while meditating under a related fig, the Bodhi tree.
