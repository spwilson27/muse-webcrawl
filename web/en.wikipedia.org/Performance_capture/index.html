Performance capture

Performance capture is a form of motion capture that records not only an actor's body movements but also facial expressions, fingers and subtle performances, so that the data can drive a digital character. The term was popularised by filmmakers using the technology for fully computer-generated characters in live-action films.

Technology

Actors wear suits with reflective or active markers and head-mounted camera rigs that record facial markers at close range. The captured data is solved onto a digital skeleton and face rig, then refined by animators. Modern systems may use markerless machine-learning tracking.

Notable uses

Andy Serkis's portrayal of Gollum in The Lord of the Rings films helped establish the technique, followed by King Kong (2005), Avatar (2009) — where director James Cameron used a virtual camera to view performances in the digital environment — and the Planet of the Apes reboot films, where performance capture was used on location.

Debate

Performance capture has raised questions about authorship and awards: how much of a digital performance belongs to the actor versus animators, and whether such roles should be eligible for acting awards.
