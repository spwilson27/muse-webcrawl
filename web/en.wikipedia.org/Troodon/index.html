Troodon

Troodon is a former wastebasket taxon and a potentially dubious genus of relatively small, bird-like theropod dinosaurs known from the Late Cretaceous period (about 77–66 million years ago) of North America. The name was first given to a single tooth described by Joseph Leidy in 1856, among the first dinosaur fossils described from North America.

Description and taxonomy

Animals formerly assigned to Troodon were small, lightly built predators with large brains relative to body size, large forward-facing eyes suggesting good vision, and grasping hands. Because the holotype is only a tooth, most material was later reassigned to other troodontid genera such as Stenonychosaurus and Latenivenatrix, the latter described in 2017 from specimens long called Troodon. Modern studies generally regard Troodon as a nomen dubium.

Cultural depictions

Troodon gained fame through Dale Russell's 1982 "dinosauroid" thought experiment, which imagined how troodontids might have evolved human-like intelligence had they survived the end-Cretaceous extinction.
