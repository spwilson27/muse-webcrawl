Hematite (also spelled haematite) is a common iron oxide compound with the formula Fe2O3 and is widely found in rocks and soils. Hematite crystals belong to the rhombohedral lattice system, the same crystal system as ilmenite and corundum. It is one of the most important ores of iron, having been mined since ancient times.

The name hematite is derived from the Greek word for blood, haima, because hematite can appear red, as in red ochre, or produce a red streak when drawn across a surface — a key diagnostic test for identifying the mineral.

Properties

Hematite occurs in colours ranging from metallic steel-grey to black in crystalline varieties, and dull to bright red ("blood red") in earthy forms. Its streak is characteristically red to reddish-brown regardless of the specimen's external colour. Hematite is weakly magnetic and becomes more strongly magnetic when heated.

Occurrence and formation

Hematite forms as a primary mineral and as an alteration product in igneous, metamorphic, and sedimentary rocks. The largest deposits are sedimentary, in banded iron formations laid down in the oceans more than two billion years ago. Major deposits occur in Brazil, Australia (the Hamersley Range), China, India, Russia, Ukraine, and the Lake Superior region of North America. The red colour of many soils, of Mars' surface, and of the Red Sea region is largely attributable to hematite.

Uses

Hematite is the world's most important ore of iron, the basis of steel production. Finely ground hematite (red ochre) has been used as a pigment since prehistoric cave painting and remains in use in paints and cosmetics. Dense, polished hematite is used as jewellery and ornamental stone, and its high density makes it a weighting agent in drilling fluids.

History

Humans used hematite as a pigment tens of thousands of years ago; ochre burials and cave art are found worldwide. The ancient Greeks associated the stone with blood and the god of war, Ares. In the industrial era, hematite mining underpinned the steel industries of Britain, Germany, and the United States.
