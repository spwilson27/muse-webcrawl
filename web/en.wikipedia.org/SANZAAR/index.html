SANZAAR (South Africa, New Zealand, Australia and Argentina Rugby) is the body that administers rugby union competitions for the national unions of South Africa, New Zealand, Australia and Argentina. It organises The Rugby Championship, the annual international tournament contested by the four countries' national teams.

History

The organisation was founded in 1996 as SANZAR (South Africa, New Zealand and Australia Rugby), a joint venture of the three unions formed at the dawn of professional rugby union. Its founding competitions were the Super 12 club tournament and the Tri Nations Series for national teams. A famous spur to its creation was the threatened breakaway during the 1995 Rugby World Cup period, when a lucrative broadcasting deal with News Corporation secured the game's professional future in the southern hemisphere.

In 2016 Argentina's union, the UAR, became a full partner and the body was renamed SANZAAR. Argentina had joined the Tri Nations in 2012, creating The Rugby Championship, and its Jaguares franchise entered Super Rugby in 2016.

Competitions

SANZAAR's flagship event is The Rugby Championship, contested annually by the Springboks (South Africa), All Blacks (New Zealand), Wallabies (Australia) and Los Pumas (Argentina). It also formerly ran Super Rugby, the cross-border provincial competition; governance of that competition changed after the COVID-19 pandemic, with South African franchises moving to European competitions and the Australasian sides reorganising Super Rugby Pacific under their own unions. SANZAAR continues to administer The Rugby Championship and age-grade and representative fixtures between its members.
