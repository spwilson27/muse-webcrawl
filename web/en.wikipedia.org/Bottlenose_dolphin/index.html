Bottlenose dolphin

Genus: Tursiops
Family: Delphinidae
Order: Artiodactyla (Cetacea)
Conservation: Least Concern (common species)
A bottlenose dolphin breaching

Bottlenose dolphins are marine mammals in the genus Tursiops. They are common and cosmopolitan members of the oceanic dolphin family Delphinidae, found in warm and temperate seas worldwide. The genus contains species including the common bottlenose dolphin (Tursiops truncatus) and the Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphin (Tursiops aduncus).

The name derives from the dolphin's short, stubby beak, which resembles an old-fashioned gin bottle. Adults measure roughly 2 to 4 metres in length and weigh 150 to 650 kilograms.

Behaviour and intelligence

Bottlenose dolphins live in social groups called pods and communicate using a repertoire of whistles and echolocation clicks. They are noted for their intelligence, having large brains relative to body size, and have demonstrated self-recognition, tool use (such as carrying marine sponges to protect their rostrums while foraging), and cultural transmission of behaviours. Each dolphin develops a unique "signature whistle" used as an identifier.

Diet

They feed mainly on fish and squid, hunting cooperatively and using echolocation to locate prey. Some populations cooperate with human fishers.

Relation to humans

Bottlenose dolphins are frequently kept in captivity and trained for shows, therapy, and military programmes, and they feature prominently in popular culture, such as the television series Flipper.
