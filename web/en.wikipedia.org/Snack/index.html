A snack is a small portion of food generally eaten between meals. Snacks come in a wide variety of forms, including packaged snack foods and other processed foods, as well as items made from fresh ingredients at home, such as fruit, nuts and leftovers.

Types

Packaged savoury snacks include potato crisps (chips), corn chips, pretzels, popcorn, crackers and roasted or salted nuts. Sweet snacks include biscuits (cookies), chocolate bars, candy, cake slices and ice cream. Health-oriented snacks include fresh and dried fruit, raw vegetables with dips, yoghurt, cheese, trail mix and granola bars. Around the world, street foods such as samosas, takoyaki, empanadas and spring rolls often serve as snacks.

Snacking and health

Snacking is near-universal: surveys in many countries find most adults snack at least once a day, and snacks can supply a significant share of daily energy intake. Nutrition research distinguishes nutrient-dense snacks (fruit, nuts, dairy) from energy-dense, ultra-processed snacks high in salt, sugar and fat, which are associated with weight gain and dental caries when consumed frequently. "Grazing" patterns and snacking in response to stress or boredom, rather than hunger, are active research topics.

Cultural aspects

Many cultures have named snack occasions: the British elevenses and afternoon tea, Spanish tapas and merienda, Japanese oyatsu, Indian chaat and tiffin, and the Swedish fika. In schools and workplaces, vending machines and tuck shops make snacks ubiquitous, and the global snack-food industry is worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually.
