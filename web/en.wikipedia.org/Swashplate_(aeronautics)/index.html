Swashplate (aeronautics)

A helicopter swashplate assembly around the main rotor mast.

In aeronautics, a swashplate is a mechanical device that translates the pilot's fixed (non-rotating) flight control inputs into motion of the rotating helicopter main rotor blades. It is a key component of most helicopter rotor heads and is also used, in related forms, in some other variable-pitch rotating machinery.

Construction and operation

The swashplate consists of two concentric plates separated by a large ball bearing, mounted around the rotor mast. The lower (stationary) plate is connected to the pilot's cyclic and collective controls via pushrods and servos; it can tilt in any direction and slide up and down the mast. The upper (rotating) plate turns with the rotor and is linked by pitch links to the blade grips, controlling the pitch angle of each blade.

Sliding the whole swashplate up or down changes all blade pitches equally — the collective control, which alters total lift. Tilting the swashplate makes each blade's pitch vary sinusoidally as it rotates — the cyclic control, which tilts the rotor disc and hence the direction of thrust, allowing the helicopter to move horizontally and to pitch and roll.

Variants

Coaxial and intermeshing rotor helicopters use two swashplates. Small electric radio-controlled helicopters use miniature swashplates with electronic mixing. Some modern designs replace mechanical linkages with individual blade control actuators, but the swashplate remains the standard solution.
