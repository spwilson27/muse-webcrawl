Baltic states

Baltic states
Members: Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania
Combined population: ~6 million
Capitals: Tallinn, Riga, Vilnius

The Baltic states are the three countries on the eastern coast of the Baltic Sea: Estonia in the north, Latvia in the middle, and Lithuania in the south. All three are members of the European Union, NATO and the eurozone.

History

The three countries gained independence from the Russian Empire in 1918, were occupied and annexed by the Soviet Union in 1940 under the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, then occupied by Nazi Germany before returning to Soviet control in 1944–45. Their restoration of independence in 1990–91 — marked by the "Singing Revolution" and the Baltic Way human chain of 1989 — was a defining moment in the collapse of the USSR.

Languages and people

Lithuanian and Latvian are Baltic Indo-European languages, while Estonian is a Finnic language closely related to Finnish. All three have significant Russian-speaking minorities.

Cooperation

The states cooperate through the Baltic Assembly and Baltic Council of Ministers, and share concerns about regional security and energy independence, including joint infrastructure projects such as Rail Baltica and synchronisation of their electricity grids with continental Europe.
