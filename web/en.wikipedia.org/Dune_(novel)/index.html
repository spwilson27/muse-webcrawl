Dune (novel)

Dune is a 1965 epic science fiction novel by American author Frank Herbert, originally published in two parts in Analog magazine. It is one of the best-selling science fiction novels of all time and the first instalment of the Dune Chronicles.

Plot

Set in the distant future in a feudal interstellar society, the novel follows Paul Atreides, whose family takes control of the desert planet Arrakis, the only source of the spice melange, a drug that extends life and expands consciousness and makes interstellar travel possible. Betrayal drives Paul into the desert among the Fremen, setting in motion a jihad in his name.

Themes and legacy

The novel explores politics, religion, ecology, and power, and won the inaugural Nebula Award and shared the Hugo Award. Herbert wrote five sequels; the saga has been adapted for film and television several times, notably Denis Villeneuve's films of 2021 and 2024.
