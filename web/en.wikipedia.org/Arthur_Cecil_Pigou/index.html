Arthur Cecil Pigou

Born: 18 November 1877, Ryde, Isle of Wight

Died: 7 March 1959, Cambridge

Field: Welfare economics

Arthur Cecil Pigou (1877–1959) was an English economist, a leading figure of the Cambridge school and a founder of welfare economics. He succeeded Alfred Marshall as professor of political economy at Cambridge in 1908.

Contributions

In The Economics of Welfare (1920) Pigou systematised the analysis of externalities, arguing that where private and social costs diverge, taxes or subsidies — now called Pigouvian taxes — can restore efficiency. He also described the Pigou effect, in which falling prices raise real wealth and hence consumption, and worked on unemployment and business cycles.

Later life

Pigou's reputation was partly eclipsed by Keynes, his Cambridge colleague and critic, but his concepts remain central to environmental economics and public policy, including carbon taxation.
