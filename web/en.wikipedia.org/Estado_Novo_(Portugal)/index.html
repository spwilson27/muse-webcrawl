Estado Novo (Portugal)

The Estado Novo (1933–1974) was the authoritarian regime of António de Oliveira Salazar and Marcelo Caetano.

The Estado Novo (Portuguese for "New State") was the corporatist authoritarian regime installed in Portugal in 1933, following the military dictatorship (Ditadura Nacional) that arose from the 28 May 1926 coup d'état. It remained in power without interruption until 1974, making it one of the longest-surviving authoritarian regimes in 20th-century Europe, and was overthrown by the Carnation Revolution.

Salazar era

António de Oliveira Salazar, an economics professor appointed Minister of Finance in 1928, became President of the Council of Ministers (prime minister) in 1932 and was the regime's architect and dominant figure until ill health removed him in 1968. The constitution of 1933 reorganised the state on corporatist lines; the União Nacional was the sole political party, elections were controlled, and opposition was suppressed by censorship and the PIDE (later DGS) political police. The regime promoted the values of "Deus, Pátria e Família" (God, Fatherland and Family), maintained a conservative fiscal policy, and kept Portugal neutral in the Second World War, though it allowed Allied use of the Azores from 1943.

Colonial wars and the Caetano era

From 1961 the regime fought colonial wars in Angola, Mozambique and Portuguese Guinea to retain its African empire, conscripting hundreds of thousands of Portuguese soldiers and consuming a large share of the budget. Marcelo Caetano, who succeeded Salazar in 1968, promised liberalisation ("evolution within continuity") but did not dismantle the regime or end the wars. On 25 April 1974 the Armed Forces Movement overthrew the regime in the nearly bloodless Carnation Revolution, ending the Estado Novo and beginning Portugal's transition to democracy and decolonisation.
