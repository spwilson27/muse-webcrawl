Baguazhang (Chinese: 八卦掌; pinyin: bāguàzhǎng; literally "eight trigram palm") is one of the three major internal Chinese martial arts, alongside tai chi and xingyiquan. It is broadly grouped under the wushu tradition.

History

Baguazhang was created by Dong Haichuan in the 19th century, reportedly drawing on Taoist circle-walking practices. Dong taught in Beijing, where his students—among them Yin Fu and Cheng Tinghua—founded the major lineages still practiced today.

Practice

The art's signature training method is walking the circle while holding static postures, from which the practitioner learns continuous change, evasive stepping and palm changes. The eight basic palms correspond to the eight trigrams of the I Ching. Weapons associated with the style include the deer horn knives and the bagua broadsword.

Styles

Major branches include Yin style, Cheng style, Gao style, Fan style and Liang style, each emphasizing different aspects of the founder's teaching.
