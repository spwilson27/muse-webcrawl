Capital market

Capital market
SegmentsPrimary market, secondary market
InstrumentsStocks, bonds, other long-term securities
ContrastMoney market (short-term instruments)

A capital market is a financial market in which long-term debt (over a year) or equity-backed securities are bought and sold, in contrast to a money market where short-term debt is traded. Capital markets channel the savings of savers to those who can put them to productive use, such as companies and governments making long-term investments.

Structure

The primary market is where new securities are issued and sold to investors, for example through an initial public offering. The secondary market is where existing securities are traded among investors, providing liquidity that makes primary issuance possible. Stock exchanges and bond markets are the best-known venues.

Participants and regulation

Participants include individual investors, pension funds, insurance companies, banks, and sovereign wealth funds. Capital markets are supervised by securities regulators such as the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, which enforce disclosure and anti-fraud rules.
