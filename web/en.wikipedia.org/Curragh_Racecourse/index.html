Curragh Racecourse

The Curragh Racecourse, usually referred to as simply The Curragh, is Ireland's most important Thoroughbred racecourse. It is situated on the Curragh plain in County Kildare, between the towns of Newbridge and Kildare, and hosts all five Irish Classic Races.

Curragh Racecourse
Location: County Kildare, Ireland
Type: Flat racing
Course: Right-handed, horseshoe-shaped, about 2 miles
Major races: Irish Derby, Irish Oaks, Irish Guineas

Racing

The Curragh stages the Irish 1,000 Guineas, Irish 2,000 Guineas, Irish Derby, Irish Oaks, and Irish St Leger, along with many other Group races including the Phoenix Stakes, National Stakes, Moyglare Stud Stakes, and the Tattersalls Gold Cup. The Irish Derby, first run in 1866, is the centrepiece of the summer season.

History and redevelopment

Racing on the Curragh plain dates to the 18th century, in an area long associated with horses — the name derives from the Irish Cuirreach, meaning "place of the running horse." Many of Ireland's leading training yards are based on the surrounding gallops. A major redevelopment, completed in 2019, provided a new grandstand and facilities.
