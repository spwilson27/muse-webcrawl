Edward Joseph Snowden (born June 21, 1983) is an American former National Security Agency (NSA) intelligence contractor and whistleblower who leaked classified information from the NSA in 2013. His disclosures revealed numerous global surveillance programs, many run by the NSA and the Five Eyes intelligence alliance with the cooperation of telecommunication companies and European governments, prompting a cultural discussion about national security and individual privacy.

Early career

Snowden served in the U.S. Army Reserve briefly in 2004, worked as a security guard for an NSA facility at the University of Maryland, and joined the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) in 2006, receiving a posting to Geneva under diplomatic cover. In 2009 he left the CIA to work for private contractors, including Dell and Booz Allen Hamilton, attached to NSA facilities in Japan and Hawaii.

The 2013 disclosures

In May 2013 Snowden flew to Hong Kong, where he met journalists Glenn Greenwald, Laura Poitras, and Ewen MacAskill and handed over a cache of classified documents. Beginning in June 2013, newspapers published revelations about programs including PRISM, bulk telephone metadata collection under Section 215 of the Patriot Act, and the tapping of allied leaders' communications. The U.S. government charged Snowden under the Espionage Act.

Asylum in Russia

After his passport was revoked while he was transiting Moscow's Sheremetyevo Airport, Snowden received temporary asylum in Russia, later extended to permanent residency in 2020 and Russian citizenship in 2022. He has continued to speak publicly on privacy and surveillance, served as president of the Freedom of the Press Foundation, and published the memoir Permanent Record in 2019.

Reactions

Snowden's actions remain controversial: supporters regard him as a whistleblower and privacy advocate, while officials have described the leaks as damaging to national security. The disclosures contributed to legal reforms, including the USA Freedom Act of 2015, and to court rulings that the bulk telephony program exceeded its statutory authority.
