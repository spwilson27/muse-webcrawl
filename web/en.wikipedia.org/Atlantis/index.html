Atlantis

Atlantis (Ancient Greek: Ἀτλαντὶς νῆσος, "island of Atlas") is a fictional island mentioned in Plato's works Timaeus and Critias as part of an allegory on the hubris of nations. In the story, Atlantis is a naval empire located in the Atlantic Ocean beyond the "Pillars of Hercules" that conquers much of Europe and Africa before sinking into the ocean "in a single day and night of misfortune."

Plato's account

Plato (c. 360 BC) describes Atlantis as larger than Libya and Asia together, ruled by kings descended from Poseidon, rich in metals including orichalcum, and laid out in concentric rings of land and water. After its failed attack on Athens, the island was destroyed by earthquakes and floods about 9,000 years before Solon.

Interpretations

Most scholars regard Atlantis as an invention of Plato's philosophy. Nevertheless, from the Renaissance onward, many writers and pseudo-historians proposed real locations, including the Atlantic, the Mediterranean, Santorini (linked to the Minoan eruption), the Americas, and Antarctica. Atlantis became a staple of occultism, Theosophy, and popular fiction.

Legacy

The legend has inspired countless novels, films, and speculative histories, and the name has been applied to places, hotels, and a Space Shuttle.
