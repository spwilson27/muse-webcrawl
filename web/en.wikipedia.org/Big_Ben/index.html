Big Ben

Big Ben
Location: Palace of Westminster, London
Bell weight: 13.5 long tons (13.7 tonnes)
First struck: 11 July 1859
Tower renamed: Elizabeth Tower (2012)

Big Ben is the nickname for the Great Bell of the Great Clock of Westminster, and by extension for the clock tower itself, at the north end of the Palace of Westminster in London, England. The tower was officially renamed the Elizabeth Tower in 2012 to mark the Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II.

The tower and clock

The tower, designed by Charles Barry and Augustus Pugin in the Gothic Revival style, was completed in 1859 and stands 96 metres (316 ft) tall. The Great Clock, designed by Edmund Beckett Denison and installed by Edward John Dent, was renowned for its accuracy, regulated by placing penny coins on the pendulum. Each of the four clock dials is 6.9 metres (22.5 ft) in diameter.

The bell

The present Great Bell is the second cast for the tower; the first, made by John Warner & Sons, cracked during testing in 1857. The replacement, cast by the Whitechapel Bell Foundry in 1858, weighs 13.5 long tons and also developed a crack, which was accommodated by turning the bell and using a lighter hammer. The origin of the name "Big Ben" is disputed; it may honour Sir Benjamin Hall, the first Commissioner of Works, or the boxer Benjamin Caunt.

Cultural significance

Big Ben is one of the most prominent symbols of the United Kingdom and of London. Its chimes, broadcast by the BBC since 1924, mark the hour in the "Westminster quarters" pattern and are used in the countdown to New Year. The tower underwent a major conservation programme from 2017 to 2022.
