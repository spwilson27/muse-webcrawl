NTT Docomo


Labelled image: NTT Docomo Yoyogi Building, Tokyo

NTT Docomo, Inc. (stylized NTT DOCOMO) is a Japanese mobile phone operator and the largest in Japan, with tens of millions of subscribers. It is a wholly owned subsidiary of Nippon Telegraph and Telephone (NTT), having been spun off from NTT in 1991–92 and fully re-acquired in 2020. The name "Docomo" is an abbreviation of the phrase "do communications over the mobile network", and also plays on the Japanese word dokomo, "everywhere".

History and services

Docomo pioneered mobile internet services with i-mode, launched in 1999, which offered email, web access, and content services on feature phones and was widely studied as a model for the mobile internet. It launched FOMA in 2001, one of the world's first commercial 3G (W-CDMA) services, followed by Xi LTE service in 2010 and commercial 5G in 2020. Its Osaifu-Keitai mobile wallet platform, using Sony's FeliCa technology, made phones a standard means of payment and transit access in Japan.

Business

Beyond consumer mobile service, Docomo operates the d POINT loyalty program, content and finance services (including the d card payment business), and enterprise IoT offerings, and it merged NTT's regional communications units NTT Com and NTT Resonant into its group in 2022. In 2020 NTT took Docomo fully private in a tender offer worth about ¥4.25 trillion, then Japan's largest, aiming to integrate the group amid government pressure to lower mobile tariffs.
