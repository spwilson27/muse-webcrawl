The Institution of Civil Engineers (ICE) is an independent professional association and learned society for civil engineers, based in London. Founded in 1818, it is the world's first professional engineering body. The ICE qualifies and regulates civil engineers, publishes technical knowledge, and advises government and industry on infrastructure policy. Its members work in more than 150 countries, and membership grades — including Fellow (FICE), Member (MICE) and Graduate member — are widely recognised marks of professional standing. The institution is a licensed body of the UK Engineering Council for the award of Chartered Engineer (CEng), Incorporated Engineer (IEng) and Engineering Technician (EngTech) status.

History

The institution was founded on 2 January 1818 at the Kendal Coffee House in Fleet Street, London, by eight young engineers, among them Henry Robinson Palmer. It was the initiative of men who, unlike the older Society of Civil Engineers (founded 1771 by John Smeaton's circle), wanted a body open to practitioners seeking to establish civil engineering as a profession distinct from military engineering. Thomas Telford became its first president in 1820 and secured a royal charter in 1828. The charter's famous definition of civil engineering, drafted by Tredgold, describes it as "the art of directing the great sources of power in Nature for the use and convenience of man". The ICE moved to One Great George Street in Westminster in 1913, where it remains.

Activities

The ICE sets standards for civil engineering education and professional development, accredits university courses, and runs examinations and reviews for professional qualification. It publishes the Proceedings of the Institution of Civil Engineers (published since 1836 — the institution's journal series is among the oldest in engineering), technical books through ICE Publishing, and the practitioner magazine New Civil Engineer. It maintains one of the largest engineering libraries in the world, awards medals including the Gold Medal and the Telford Medal, and issues influential policy reports, notably its periodic State of the Nation assessments of UK infrastructure. The president serves a one-year term beginning in November and delivers a presidential address; early presidents included Telford, George Stephenson, Robert Stephenson and Isambard Kingdom Brunel.
