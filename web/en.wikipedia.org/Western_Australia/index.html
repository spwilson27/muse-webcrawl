Western Australia (commonly abbreviated as WA) is a state occupying the western third of the land area of Australia. It is bordered by the Indian Ocean to the north and west, the Southern Ocean to the south, and South Australia and the Northern Territory to the east. The state has a land area of about 2.53 million square kilometres, making it the largest state in Australia and the second-largest subdivision of any country in the world. Its capital and largest city is Perth, home to roughly three-quarters of the state's population.

History

Aboriginal Australians have inhabited Western Australia for at least 40,000 years. Dutch explorer Dirk Hartog made the first recorded European landing on its coast in 1616. British settlement began at King George Sound (Albany) in 1826, followed by the Swan River Colony in 1829. The discovery of gold in the 1890s transformed the colony, and Western Australia joined the Commonwealth of Australia at federation in 1901, after voting in favour in a referendum. In 1933, a referendum on secession passed but was not acted upon by the British Parliament.

Geography and climate

The state spans tropical monsoonal regions in the Kimberley, vast deserts including the Great Victoria, Gibson, and Great Sandy deserts in the interior, and a Mediterranean climate in the south-west, which is one of the world's biodiversity hotspots. Major regional centres include Albany, Bunbury, Geraldton, Kalgoorlie, Broome, and Karratha.

Economy

Western Australia's economy is dominated by mining and energy exports — it is the world's largest producer of iron ore, with major operations in the Pilbara — along with gold, liquefied natural gas, lithium, and agriculture such as wheat. The state's resource royalties make it one of the wealthiest sub-national regions in the world by some measures.
