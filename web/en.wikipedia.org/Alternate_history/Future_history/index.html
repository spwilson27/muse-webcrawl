Future history

Genre: Science fiction device
Definition: A postulated history of the future
Notable example: Robert A. Heinlein's Future History series
Related: Steampunk, alternate history

A future history is a postulated history of the future, presented as though it were actual history, that some authors of science fiction and speculative fiction construct as a background for their fiction. Unlike alternate history, which diverges from the known past, a future history projects events forward from the author's present, often setting many stories and novels in a single shared timeline.

Examples

The term is most closely associated with Robert A. Heinlein's Future History, a chart first published in 1941 that organised his stories from the late twentieth century to the twenty-third. Other notable examples include Isaac Asimov's combined Robot, Empire, and Foundation universe; Larry Niven's Known Space; Poul Anderson's Psychotechnic League; and the meticulously worked-out timelines behind Olaf Stapledon's Last and First Men (1930), itself framed as a history written far in the future.

Characteristics

Future histories let authors explore long-term social and technological change across many works. As real history overtakes their early predictions, such series often accrue anachronisms, prompting authors to revise, retcon, or embrace the divergence — at which point an outdated future history effectively becomes a kind of alternate history, cousin to retro-futurist genres such as steampunk.
