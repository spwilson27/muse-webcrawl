Harry Turtledove (born 1949)

Harry Norman Turtledove (born June 14, 1949) is an American author, best known for his alternate history, historical fiction, and science fiction works. Within the alternate history genre he is so prominent that he is frequently called "the master of alternate history".

Turtledove was trained as a historian of Byzantium: he received a PhD in Byzantine history from the University of California, Los Angeles in 1977, and published a translation of the Chronicle of Theophanes before turning to fiction full-time. His scholarly grounding is often credited for the detailed texture of his fictional timelines.

Alternate history works

Turtledove's most famous alternate history is the Southern Victory series (beginning with How Few Remain, 1997), in which the Confederate States win the American Civil War in 1862 and remain an independent nation into the twentieth century, fighting analogues of the World Wars against the United States. The series runs to eleven volumes, ending with The Grapple and In at the Death.

His stand-alone novel The Guns of the South (1992), in which time-travelling South African extremists supply Robert E. Lee's army with AK-47 rifles, won wide attention. Other works include the Worldwar series, in which alien reptilians invade Earth in the middle of the Second World War; Ruled Britannia, set in a Spanish-conquered England after the Armada succeeds; and Joe Steele, in which Joseph Stalin is born in the United States and becomes its president.

Style and influence

Turtledove typically tells his stories through large casts of viewpoint characters, historical and fictional, and constructs timelines that echo real events with displaced details. He has won the Sidewise Award for Alternate History, named after the genre's early classic, and has twice written award-recognised works in the field.
