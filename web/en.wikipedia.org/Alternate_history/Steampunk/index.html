Steampunk

Genre: Science fiction / retrofuturism
Setting: Alternative Victorian or Edwardian era
Term coined: 1987, by K. W. Jeter
Key works: The Difference Engine (1990)

Steampunk is a subgenre of science fiction that incorporates retrofuturistic technology and aesthetics inspired by nineteenth-century industrial steam-powered machinery. Its works are often set in an alternative history of the Victorian era or the American frontier, where steam power remains dominant and inventions such as airships, mechanical computers, and brass goggles abound. The term was coined jokingly in 1987 by author K. W. Jeter as a play on "cyberpunk".

Literary origins

Steampunk draws on the scientific romances of Jules Verne and H. G. Wells and on Edwardian adventure fiction. Early modern examples include Jeter's Morlock Night (1979) and Infernal Devices (1987), Tim Powers's The Anubis Gates (1983), and James Blaylock's Homunculus series. William Gibson and Bruce Sterling's The Difference Engine (1990), imagining a Victorian Britain transformed by Babbage's computers, is often credited with defining the genre's alternate-history form.

Culture and aesthetics

Beyond literature, steampunk has become a design and fashion movement celebrating DIY craftsmanship, corsets, waistcoats, and modified modern devices housed in brass, wood, and leather. Conventions, makers, and musicians sustain a global subculture. Critics discuss the genre's ambivalent nostalgia for the British Empire, and some authors use its settings to interrogate colonialism rather than celebrate it.
