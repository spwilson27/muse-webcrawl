Speculative fiction

Speculative fiction is an umbrella term encompassing narrative fiction with supernatural, fantastical or futuristic elements, in contrast to strictly realistic or mimetic fiction. It most commonly covers science fiction, fantasy and horror, along with their subgenres and hybrids such as alternate history, utopian and dystopian fiction, apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction and superhero fiction.

Use of the term

The phrase has been used both as a broad category — Margaret Atwood, for example, distinguishes speculative fiction (things that could conceivably happen) from science fiction's more far-fetched inventions — and, in a narrower sense promoted by Robert A. Heinlein in 1947, as a synonym for serious science fiction. Critics and scholars sometimes use it to discuss works in the fantastic tradition that cross the boundaries of the commercial genres, including magic realism and slipstream.

Scope

As an umbrella category, speculative fiction includes ancient myth-inflected epics and romances as well as modern genre publishing. Its stories typically depart from the real world in at least one significant respect — a new technology, a magical system, a changed history — and explore the human consequences of that departure.
