Point of divergence

In alternate history fiction, a point of divergence (POD) — also called a break-point, point of departure, or (after Murray Leinster's "Sidewise in Time") a Jonbar hinge — is the point in time at which the fictional timeline departs from recorded history. Everything before the point of divergence is shared with real history; everything after may differ as consequences unfold.

Usage

Authors choose points of divergence both ancient and recent: a battle ending differently (such as the South winning at Gettysburg), a leader surviving or dying early, or an invention arriving sooner or later. Some scenarios rely on deliberate intervention, as when a time traveller changes the past, while others posit a single small change whose effects cascade. Closely analysed points of divergence also feature in academic counterfactual history, where historians ask "what if" questions to test causal claims.

Related concepts

Robert A. Heinlein and later writers popularised parallel-world stories in which many divergent timelines coexist. The term "Jonbar hinge" derives from John Barr in Jack Williamson's The Legion of Time (1938), where a trivial choice between a pebble and a magnet leads to two radically different futures.
