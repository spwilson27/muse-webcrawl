Sidewise Award for Alternate History


Selected winners of the Sidewise Award, which honours the year's best alternate history fiction.

The Sidewise Award for Alternate History is an annual literary award established in 1995 to recognise the best alternate history stories and novels of the year. It is named after Murray Leinster's 1934 short story "Sidewise in Time", one of the earliest works to popularise the idea of parallel timelines branching from points of divergence in history. The award is presented in two categories: Best Long Form, for works longer than 60,000 words (typically novels), and Best Short Form, for shorter works.

History

The award was founded by a panel of judges drawn from alternate-history writers, editors and enthusiasts, and the first awards were presented in 1996 for works published in 1995. The inaugural Long Form winner was Paul J. McAuley's Pasquale's Angel, and the Short Form award went to Stephen Baxter's "Brigantia's Angels". Over the years the judging panel has typically included figures such as Steven H Silver, one of the award's administrators.

Notable winners

Long Form winners have included Mary Gentle's Ash: A Secret History (published in the United States as four volumes), Harry Turtledove's work, Stephen Baxter's Voyage, and more recent titles such as Lavie Tidhar's Ungentlemanly Warfare-era work and Adrian Tchaikovsky's Cage of Souls-adjacent alternate histories; among widely cited winners are Ian R. MacLeod's The Summer Isles, Michael Chabon's The Yiddish Policemen's Union (which first won a special recognition before the eligibility rules were clarified), and Jo Walton's Farthing sequence. Short Form winners include stories by Ted Chiang ("Seventy-Two Letters", a special mention), Gardner Dozois, and Ken Liu.

The award has also occasionally given special awards for lifetime achievement or for works that appeared before the award's creation but exemplified the genre, such as recognition of L. Sprague de Camp's classic Lest Darkness Fall.

Eligibility and judging

Works are eligible if they were first published in English during the year under consideration and depict a history that diverges from the historical record at an identifiable point of divergence. A panel of judges reads nominated and submitted works throughout the year and announces a shortlist before naming the winners, typically at a science fiction convention ceremony. The award's focus on historical rigour as well as literary quality has made it the principal genre prize devoted specifically to alternate history, a subgenre of speculative fiction.
