The Man in the High Castle

The Man in the High Castle is a 1962 novel by American author Philip K. Dick, and one of the best-known works in the alternate history genre. Set in 1962, fifteen years after the Axis powers won the Second World War, it depicts a North America partitioned between Imperial Japan and Nazi Germany.

AuthorPhilip K. Dick
Published1962
GenreAlternate history, science fiction
AwardHugo Award for Best Novel (1963)

Plot

The novel follows several characters living in the Japanese Pacific States and the neutral buffer zone of the Rocky Mountain States. In the fictional backstory, President Franklin D. Roosevelt was assassinated in 1933, leading to American isolationism; the United States was defeated, and by 1947 the Axis had conquered the country. Within the story circulates a banned novel-within-the-novel, The Grasshopper Lies Heavy, which imagines an Allied victory — though not quite the victory of the reader's own timeline. Dick wrote the book consulting the I Ching, which also guides the decisions of several characters.

Reception and adaptation

The novel won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1963 and is widely credited with bringing alternate history to literary prominence. Amazon Studios adapted it as a television series that ran from 2015 to 2019.
