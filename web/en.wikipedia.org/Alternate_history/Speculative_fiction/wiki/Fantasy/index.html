Fantasy

Fantasy is a genre of speculative fiction involving magical and supernatural elements, typically set in a fictional world and involving mythical creatures, magic and quests. It draws on mythology, folklore and medieval romance, and is distinguished from science fiction and horror by the absence of scientific or macabre themes, though the genres overlap considerably.

Subgenres and history

Modern fantasy was shaped by authors such as William Morris, Lord Dunsany and E. R. Eddison, and reached mass popularity through J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit (1937) and The Lord of the Rings (1954–55), which defined the "high fantasy" tradition of fully realised secondary worlds. Other major forms include sword and sorcery (Robert E. Howard's Conan stories), urban fantasy, dark fantasy and fairy-tale retellings. Later bestsellers include C. S. Lewis's Narnia books, Ursula K. Le Guin's Earthsea, George R. R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire and the Harry Potter series by J. K. Rowling.

Media

Fantasy extends across film, television, role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, video games and comics. Its conventions — invented languages, maps, magic systems and medieval-inspired settings — are among the most recognisable in popular culture.
