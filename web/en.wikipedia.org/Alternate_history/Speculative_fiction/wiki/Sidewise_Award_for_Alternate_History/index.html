Sidewise Award for Alternate History

The Sidewise Award for Alternate History is an annual literary award established in 1995 to recognise the best alternate history fiction of the year. It is named after Murray Leinster's 1934 short story "Sidewise in Time", a pioneering work of the genre, and is usually presented at the World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) or the North American Science Fiction Convention.

Categories

The award is given in two categories: Best Long Form (novels and longer works) and Best Short Form (stories and novellas). The first winners, for works published in 1995, were Paul J. McAuley's novel Pasquale's Angel and Stephen Baxter's story "Brigantia's Angels". A Special Achievement Award has occasionally been presented for works published before the award's founding, including honours for L. Sprague de Camp's Lest Darkness Fall and classics by Winston Churchill and Ward Moore.

Notable winners

Long-form winners have included Mary Gentle (Ash: A Secret History), Harry Turtledove, Robert Conroy, Michael Chabon (The Yiddish Policemen's Union), Jo Walton, and C. J. Sansom (Dominion). The award is judged by a panel of alternate-history specialists.
