Science fiction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Science fiction (often shortened to sci-fi or SF) is a genre of speculative fiction dealing with imaginative and futuristic concepts such as advanced science and technology, space exploration, time travel, parallel universes, and extraterrestrial life. It explores the possible consequences of scientific and social innovations.

History

Precursors include Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818) and the works of Jules Verne and H. G. Wells. The genre took shape in the pulp magazines of the 1920s under editor Hugo Gernsback, who coined the term. The post-war decades brought a golden age of writers such as Isaac Asimov, Arthur C. Clarke, and Robert A. Heinlein, followed by the New Wave of the 1960s and cyberpunk in the 1980s.

Subgenres

Science fiction includes hard SF, space opera, military SF, cyberpunk, steampunk, time travel stories, and alternate history. Major awards include the Hugo and Nebula Awards.
