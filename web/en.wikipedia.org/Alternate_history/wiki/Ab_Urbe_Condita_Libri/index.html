Ab Urbe Condita Libri

Ab Urbe Condita Libri ("Books from the Founding of the City") is a monumental history of Rome written by the Roman historian Titus Livius, known in English as Livy. Composed in Latin beginning around 27 BC, it originally comprised 142 books covering Roman history from the legendary founding of Rome in 753 BC down to the reign of Augustus.

Survival

Only 35 of the 142 books survive in full — books 1–10 and 21–45 — together with summaries (periochae) of most of the lost books and a few fragments. The surviving portions cover the early Republic, the Samnite Wars, and the Second Punic War, including Hannibal's crossing of the Alps.

Style and influence

Livy wrote in an annalistic framework, year by year, blending documented fact with legend, particularly for the earliest centuries. His history was a major source for later writers on Rome and was read continuously through the Middle Ages and Renaissance; Machiavelli's Discourses on Livy is a commentary on its first decade.
