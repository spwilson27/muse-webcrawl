Future history

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A future history is a series of science fiction works constructed around a consistent imagined history of the future. The term is especially associated with Robert A. Heinlein, whose "Future History" chart, published in 1941, organized his stories and novels — including the Lazarus Long books — into a single timeline of future events.

Other examples

Other notable future histories include Isaac Asimov's Foundation and Robot series, eventually merged into one saga; Larry Niven's Known Space; Poul Anderson's Psychotechnic League and Polesotechnic League; and Jerry Pournelle's CoDominium series.

Characteristics

Future histories extrapolate technological and social trends, providing a shared background for otherwise independent stories. As real events overtake predicted dates, such series may become a form of alternate history.
