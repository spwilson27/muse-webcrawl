Harry Turtledove

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Harry Norman Turtledove (born June 14, 1949) is an American author best known for alternate history, historical fiction, and science fiction. Trained as a historian of Byzantium — he earned a PhD in Byzantine history from UCLA — he is often described as a master of the alternate history genre.

Works

His best-known books include The Guns of the South (1992), in which time-travelling South African radicals arm the Confederacy with AK-47 rifles; the Worldwar series, in which reptilian aliens invade during the Second World War; and the Southern Victory series, an eleven-volume saga beginning with How Few Remain, in which the Confederacy wins the American Civil War. He has also written Byzantine-inspired fantasy such as the Videssos cycle.

Awards

Turtledove won the Sidewise Award and other honours; his novella "Down in the Bottomlands" won the Hugo Award for Best Novella in 1994.
