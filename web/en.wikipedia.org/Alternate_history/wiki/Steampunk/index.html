Steampunk

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Steampunk is a subgenre of science fiction that imagines retrofuturistic technology powered by steam, typically set in an alternate history of the Victorian era or the American frontier. Its aesthetic draws on the industrial machinery, clothing, and design of the nineteenth century, combined with anachronistic inventions such as mechanical computers and airships.

Origins

The term was coined in 1987 by author K. W. Jeter, by analogy with cyberpunk, for works by himself, Tim Powers, and James Blaylock. Foundational novels include Morlock Night (1979), The Anubis Gates (1983), and William Gibson and Bruce Sterling's The Difference Engine (1990), set in a Victorian age transformed by Babbage's computers.

Culture

Steampunk has grown into a broader design and lifestyle movement, influencing fashion, music, film, and maker culture, with brass goggles, clockwork, and corsets as signature motifs.
