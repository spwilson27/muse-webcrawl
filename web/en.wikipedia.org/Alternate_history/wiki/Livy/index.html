Livy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Livy (Titus Livius; 59 BC – AD 17) was a Roman historian whose monumental history of Rome, Ab Urbe Condita ("From the Founding of the City"), covered Roman history from the legendary founding of Rome to the reign of Augustus. Of its 142 books, only 35 survive complete.

Life and work

Born in Patavium (modern Padua), Livy spent much of his life in Rome, where he enjoyed the favour of Augustus without holding public office. His history was written in a flowing, dramatic style, drawing on earlier annalists and on Polybius for eastern affairs.

Alternate history connection

Book 9 of Livy's history contains a famous digression asking whether Rome could have defeated Alexander the Great had Alexander turned west — often cited as one of the earliest surviving exercises in counterfactual history and the ancestor of the alternate history genre.

Legacy

Livy influenced later historians and thinkers from Dante to Machiavelli, whose Discourses are a commentary on Livy's first ten books.
