Historiography

Historiography is the study of the methods of historians in developing history as an academic discipline, and by extension the body of historical work on a particular subject. It examines how historical narratives are constructed, which sources and interpretations are used, and how understandings of the past change over time.

Approaches

Historiographical traditions have included annalistic and chronicle writing in antiquity and the Middle Ages, the source criticism pioneered by historians such as Leopold von Ranke in the 19th century, Marxist historiography, the French Annales school with its focus on long-term social structures, and more recent turns toward cultural, gender, and postcolonial history.

Key questions

Historiographers ask who wrote a given history, for what audience, with what evidence and biases. The discipline also studies counterfactual and alternate-history writing as a way of probing contingency in historical explanation.
