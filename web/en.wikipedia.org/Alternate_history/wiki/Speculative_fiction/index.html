Speculative fiction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Speculative fiction is an umbrella term for genres that speculate about worlds unlike the real one, encompassing science fiction, fantasy, horror, dystopian and utopian fiction, supernatural fiction, and alternate history. The term is used both as a marketing category and by writers who wish to distance their work from narrow genre labels.

Usage

Robert A. Heinlein helped popularize the term in a 1947 essay, using it broadly for stories about new situations in human society. Margaret Atwood has famously described her own work as speculative fiction rather than science fiction, emphasizing plausible extrapolation from present conditions.

Scope

Under the speculative umbrella sit subgenres including hard and soft science fiction, high fantasy, slipstream, weird fiction, and superhero stories, unified by their departure from strict realism.
