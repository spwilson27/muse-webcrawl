TransMilenio

TransMilenio is the bus rapid transit (BRT) system that serves Bogotá, the capital of Colombia, and the neighbouring city of Soacha. Opened on 18 December 2000, it is one of the largest BRT systems in the world by ridership and is frequently cited as a model in the development of bus rapid transit internationally.

System

The system consists of dedicated trunk corridors (troncales) reserved for articulated and bi-articulated buses, with enclosed stations at which passengers prepay fares before boarding through wide doors at platform level, enabling rapid loading. Feeder buses (alimentadores) connect neighbourhoods to the trunk terminals. The network comprises more than 100 kilometres of trunk corridors and over 140 stations, plus hundreds of feeder routes.

TransMilenio is operated by private concessionaires under contract to the city-owned company TransMilenio S.A. Fares are integrated through a smart card system (TuLlave). At peak times the trunk corridors can carry more than 40,000 passengers per hour per direction, comparable to some metro systems.

History and development

The system was built under mayor Enrique Peñalosa as part of a broader programme of urban reform. Phase I opened in 2000 on the Avenida Caracas and Calle 80 corridors; Phase II extended the network along Avenida de las Américas, NQS and Avenida Suba; Phase III added corridors on Carrera Décima and Calle 26, among others. The system also includes the TransMiCable aerial tramway in Ciudad Bolívar, opened in 2018.

Criticism

TransMilenio has faced criticism for severe overcrowding at peak hours, high fares relative to income, and fare evasion. Nevertheless it substantially reduced travel times and emissions compared with the previous unregulated bus system, and influenced BRT projects in cities such as Mexico City, Johannesburg and Jakarta.
