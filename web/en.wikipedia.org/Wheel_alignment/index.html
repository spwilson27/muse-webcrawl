Wheel alignment

Wheel alignment
Adjustable anglesCamber, caster, toe
PurposeTyre wear, handling, straight-line stability
EquipmentAlignment rack with laser/camera sensors

Wheel alignment, sometimes referred to as tracking, is the part of standard automobile maintenance that consists of adjusting the angles of the wheels so that they are set to the car manufacturer's specifications. The purpose of these adjustments is to reduce tyre wear, ensure that vehicle travel is straight and true, and give the steering predictable handling. Alignment angles can also be intentionally altered beyond the maker's specs to obtain a specific handling characteristic, as in motorsport.

Primary angles

  • Camber — the inward or outward tilt of the wheel viewed from the front; excessive negative or positive camber wears one edge of the tyre.
  • Caster — the forward or backward tilt of the steering axis viewed from the side; positive caster improves straight-line stability and steering self-centring.
  • Toe — the extent to which the wheels point inward (toe-in) or outward (toe-out) viewed from above; incorrect toe is the most common cause of rapid tyre wear.

On most modern cars only toe is easily adjustable at the front; camber and caster may be fixed or adjustable depending on suspension design. Alignment is typically checked with computerised laser or camera equipment after suspension work, tyre replacement, or if the vehicle pulls to one side or shows uneven tyre wear.
