Jean Todt

Jean Henri Todt (born 25 February 1946) is a French motor racing executive and former rally co-driver. He was director of Scuderia Ferrari's Formula One team from 1993 to 2007, overseeing its dominant championship era with Michael Schumacher, and served as president of the FIA, world motorsport's governing body, from 2009 to 2021.

Rally career

Born in Pierrefort in the Cantal, Todt began his career as a rally co-driver in 1966, partnering drivers including Hannu Mikkola, Timo Mäkinen and Guy Fréquelin, with whom he finished runner-up in the 1981 World Rally Championship for Talbot. He then moved into team management at Peugeot Talbot Sport, masterminding Peugeot's World Rally Championship titles and Paris–Dakar victories in the 1980s and Le Mans wins in the early 1990s.

Ferrari

In 1993 Todt became sporting director of Ferrari, the first non-Italian to run the team. He rebuilt the organisation, recruiting designer Rory Byrne and technical director Ross Brawn, and luring Michael Schumacher from Benetton in 1996. The result was a period of unprecedented dominance: six consecutive constructors' championships (1999–2004) and five consecutive drivers' titles for Schumacher (2000–2004). Todt also served as Ferrari's chief executive before leaving the company in 2009.

FIA presidency

Todt succeeded Max Mosley as FIA president in October 2009 and was re-elected in 2013 and 2017. His tenure emphasised road safety — he also served as the UN Secretary-General's Special Envoy for Road Safety — and the introduction of the halo cockpit protection device and hybrid power units in Formula One. He was succeeded by Mohammed Ben Sulayem in December 2021. He is married to the actress Michelle Yeoh.
