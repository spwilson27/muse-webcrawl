Invisible

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

For the physical property, see Invisibility. For other uses, see Invisible (disambiguation).

Invisible is the state of an object that cannot be seen — that is, an object which is either transparent to the wavelengths of light detected by an observer's eyes, emits or reflects too little light to be perceived, or is concealed from view. The opposite of invisibility is visibility. An invisible object may still be detectable by other senses (sound, smell, touch) or by instruments sensitive to other parts of the electromagnetic spectrum, such as infrared or ultraviolet radiation.

The word derives from the Latin invisibilis, "not able to be seen". Strictly invisible phenomena include air and other colourless gases under ordinary conditions, microscopic organisms and particles too small for the unaided eye, and the contents of opaque containers. In physics, many entities — fields, dark matter, neutrinos — are "invisible" in everyday terms yet are studied through their measurable effects.

In culture

Invisibility is a longstanding theme of myth, folklore and fiction: the cap of Hades and Perseus's helmet in Greek legend, magic rings and cloaks in fairy tales, H. G. Wells's novel The Invisible Man (1897), the One Ring in Tolkien's works, and the invisibility cloak in the Harry Potter series. In military technology, camouflage and stealth technology reduce detectability, and research on metamaterials has demonstrated limited "invisibility cloaks" for narrow wavelength ranges.
