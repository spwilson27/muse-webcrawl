Yule

Yule is a winter festival historically observed by the Germanic peoples, associated with the winter solstice and spanning a season in midwinter. Its name (Old English geol, Old Norse jól) later transferred to Christmas itself in English and Scandinavian languages.

Pagan origins

Sagas and the writings of Bede describe jól as a major Norse feast: several nights of feasting, sacrifice (jólablót), oath-swearing on a boar (sonargöltr), toasting to Odin, Njörðr, and Freyr, and drinking in memory of dead kin. Bede records the Anglo-Saxon months around the solstice, Giuli, and "Modranicht" (Mothers' Night). Scholars connect the season with the Wild Hunt, the god Odin as Jólnir ("the Yule One"), and the disir, female ancestral spirits.

Christianisation and customs

King Haakon the Good of Norway (10th century) is said to have rescheduled Yule to coincide with Christmas. Numerous Christmas customs — the Yule log, Yule goat of Scandinavia, wassailing, feasting, and evergreens — are popularly traced to Yule traditions, though many attributions are debated. "Yuletide" survives as an archaic English synonym for the Christmas season, and the Yule log cake (bûche de Noël) carries the name into cuisine.

Modern celebrations

Yule is one of the eight sabbats of the modern pagan Wheel of the Year in Wicca and is celebrated by Heathen groups at the solstice or at midwinter dates calculated by the old Norse calendar.
