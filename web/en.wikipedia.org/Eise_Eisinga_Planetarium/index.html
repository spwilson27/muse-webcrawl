Eise Eisinga Planetarium

Location: Franeker, Friesland, Netherlands

Built: 1774–1781

Builder: Eise Eisinga

Status: Museum; oldest working planetarium in the world

UNESCO: World Heritage List (inscribed 2023)

The Eise Eisinga Planetarium (Dutch: Koninklijk Eise Eisinga Planetarium) in Franeker, Friesland, Netherlands, is a working orrery — a mechanical model of the Solar System — built between 1774 and 1781 by the wool comber and amateur astronomer Eise Eisinga (1744–1828) into the ceiling of his own living room. It is the oldest planetarium still in operation anywhere in the world, and in 2023 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Construction

In 1774 a conjunction of the Moon and several planets caused widespread panic in Friesland, stoked by a pamphlet predicting that the planets would collide and knock the Earth from its orbit. To demonstrate the true motions of the planets, Eisinga — largely self-educated, having studied mathematics and astronomy while working as a wool comber — spent seven years building a moving model suspended from his ceiling. The mechanism, driven by a pendulum clock and weights in the attic above, contains more than a thousand hand-made gears and still shows the positions of Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn (the planets known at the time), together with the Moon, the Sun, the zodiac, and the date. Saturn, with its 29.5-year orbit, determined the size of the mechanism; a full cycle of the slowest display takes decades.

History

The planetarium drew visitors during Eisinga's lifetime, including King William I, who purchased it for the Dutch state in 1825, granting Eisinga a pension. Eisinga's descendants continued to maintain it, and it became a museum in the twentieth century. Remarkably, the mechanism still runs on its original parts. In 2023 UNESCO recognised the planetarium as a World Heritage Site for its outstanding testimony to the spread of scientific knowledge in the Enlightenment.

The museum today

The museum includes Eisinga's former house and wool workshop, interactive astronomical exhibits, and his manuscripts. The planetarium remains in its original room, the painted starry ceiling dial and hanging model planets preserved exactly as he made them.
