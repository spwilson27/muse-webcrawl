Mount Sharp, officially Aeolis Mons, is a mountain on Mars. It forms the central peak within Gale Crater and is situated around 5.2°S 137.8°E, rising about 5.5 km (18,000 ft) high from the valley floor. It is the geological feature the NASA Curiosity rover was sent to explore, as part of the Mars Science Laboratory mission.

The mountain is a layered mound of sedimentary rock that rises from the centre of the 154 km (96 mi) wide Gale Crater. Scientists believe the mound was built up by sediments deposited in an ancient lake and later eroded by wind, leaving the towering stack of layers that preserves a record of Mars's changing climate billions of years ago. On 6 August 2012, Curiosity landed on the plains north-west of the mountain (a site named Bradbury Landing) and began the long traverse toward its foothills.

Naming

The name "Mount Sharp" honours the American geologist Robert P. Sharp (1911–2004), a planetary scientist and an early mentor to many Mars researchers. In May 2012 the International Astronomical Union officially named the feature Aeolis Mons, while NASA and the U.S. Geological Survey also use Mount Sharp; the NASA rover team uses both names informally.

Exploration

Curiosity reached the base of Mount Sharp in September 2014 and has been ascending through its layers ever since. The rover examined the Murray formation's mudstones, deposited in lakebeds, then climbed into the clay-bearing unit and the sulfate-bearing unit, each recording progressively drier conditions in Gale Crater's past. Orbital spectroscopy had revealed clays and sulfates in the mountain's lower strata, making them prime targets in the search for habitable environments and organic matter.

Geology

The mound's layers record billions of years of Martian history. Wind erosion — including the gap known as Gediz Vallis — carved channels into the mountain as the climate shifted from wet to dry. Yardangs, inverted channels and cross-bedded sandstones on its slopes testify to the interplay of water, wind and burial that shaped the peak.
