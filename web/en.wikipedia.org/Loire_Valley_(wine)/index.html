Loire Valley (wine)

The Loire Valley wine region includes the French wine regions situated along the river Loire from the Muscadet region near the city of Nantes on the Atlantic coast to the region of Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé just southeast of the city of Orléans in north-central France. It is France's most diverse wine region, producing wines in nearly every style.

Regions and grapes

The valley is conventionally divided into three sections. The Lower Loire, around Nantes, is dominated by Muscadet made from Melon de Bourgogne. The Middle Loire includes Anjou, Saumur, and Touraine, known for Chenin blanc whites (Vouvray, Savennières) and Cabernet Franc reds (Chinon, Bourgueil). The Upper Loire is famed for Sauvignon blanc from Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé.

Styles

Alongside still dry whites, the region produces sparkling wines (Crémant de Loire), rosés, and sweet botrytised dessert wines such as Coteaux du Layon. The cool continental-maritime climate and varied soils — tuffeau limestone, silex, and Kimmeridgian marl — shape the wines' characteristic freshness and acidity.
