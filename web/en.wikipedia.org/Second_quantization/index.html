Second quantization

Second quantization, also called the occupation-number representation or canonical quantization of fields, is a formalism of quantum mechanics used to describe many-particle systems of identical particles. Instead of tracking each particle, the state is specified by how many particles occupy each single-particle state, and the dynamics is expressed through creation and annihilation operators acting on Fock space.

Creation and annihilation operators add or remove particles in a given quantum state.

Formalism

For bosons, the creation and annihilation operators a† and a obey the commutation relations [ai, a†j] = δij; for fermions they obey anticommutation relations {ai, a†j} = δij, which automatically enforce the Pauli exclusion principle: occupation numbers are 0 or 1 for fermions and any non-negative integer for bosons. Hamiltonians are written as sums of one-body and two-body operator terms, making particle interactions and scattering easy to express.

History and applications

The formalism was developed in the late 1920s by Dirac, Jordan, Klein, Wigner, Fock, and others as quantum mechanics was extended to systems with variable particle number. Despite the name, it is not a second round of quantization in modern understanding: it is the quantization of a classical field, or equivalently a convenient reformulation of many-body Schrödinger theory. It is the standard language of quantum field theory, condensed matter physics, and quantum chemistry, underlying phenomena from superconductivity and superfluidity to particle creation in relativistic collisions.
