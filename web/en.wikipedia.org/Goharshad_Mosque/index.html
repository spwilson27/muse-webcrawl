Goharshad Mosque


The turquoise dome and minarets of the mosque.

The Goharshad Mosque (Persian: مسجد گوهرشاد) is a grand mosque in Mashhad, Iran, within the precinct of the Imam Reza shrine. It was commissioned in 1418 by Goharshad, the wife of Shah Rukh, ruler of the Timurid Empire, and designed by the architect Qavam al-Din ibn Zayn al-Din Shirazi.

Architecture

The mosque is a classic four-iwan Timurid structure built around a large courtyard (about 50 × 55 m). Its surfaces are richly decorated with mosaic faience in turquoise, blue and other colours, calligraphic bands by the prince Baysunghur, and a monumental pishtaq. Two minarets flank the main iwan, and the sanctuary is crowned by a turquoise dome roughly 41 m high at its apex. The mosque's minbar and inscriptions are considered masterpieces of Timurid art.

History

The mosque has been damaged and restored repeatedly, including after earthquakes and during the 20th century. In July 1935 it was the centre of the Goharshad Mosque rebellion, when protesters against Reza Shah's dress reforms gathered in the shrine and were violently suppressed by the army, with heavy loss of life — an event still commemorated in Iran. The mosque remains in active use for prayer and forms one of the most visited parts of the Imam Reza complex.
