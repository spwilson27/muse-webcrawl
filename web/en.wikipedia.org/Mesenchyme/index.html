Mesenchyme

Mesenchyme
OriginMesoderm; also ectoderm (neural crest)
Gives rise toConnective tissue, bone, cartilage, muscle, blood and lymph vessels
PeriodEmbryonic development

Mesenchyme is a type of loosely organized animal embryonic connective tissue of undifferentiated cells that gives rise to most tissues of the body, including the dermis, bones, cartilage, muscles, tendons, ligaments, the circulatory and lymphatic systems, and parts of the urinary and reproductive systems. It is characterized by a sparse population of stellate cells embedded in an abundant extracellular matrix of ground substance and reticular fibres, with little intercellular adhesion — a structure suited to the extensive cell migration that shapes the embryo.

Development

Most mesenchyme derives from the mesoderm germ layer during gastrulation, particularly from the paraxial and lateral plate mesoderm; the mesenchyme of much of the head and neck instead derives from ectoderm via the neural crest, and is sometimes called ectomesenchyme. Mesenchymal cells are pluripotent: they differentiate into the fibroblast cell family, producing the body's connective tissues, and also into chondroblasts, osteoblasts, myoblasts, adipocytes, and haematopoietic cells. In the adult, populations of mesenchymal stem (or stromal) cells persist in bone marrow, adipose tissue, and other niches, where they contribute to tissue maintenance and repair and are widely studied for regenerative medicine.

Epithelial–mesenchymal transition

Mesenchyme is generated from epithelia by epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), in which cells lose apical–basal polarity and cell–cell junctions, express markers such as vimentin, and become migratory. EMT is essential in gastrulation, neural crest delamination, and heart development; the reverse process (MET) occurs when mesenchymal cells settle and form epithelia, as in kidney tubules. Pathologically, EMT programmes are reactivated in fibrosis and in carcinoma progression, where they contribute to invasion and metastasis.

Clinical relevance

Tumours arising from mesenchymal tissues are the sarcomas, classified by their tissue of differentiation (e.g., osteosarcoma, liposarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma). Developmental disorders of mesenchyme underlie many connective tissue syndromes. Mesenchymal stromal cells are used experimentally in cell therapy for their immunomodulatory and trophic effects, although approved clinical applications remain limited.
