Hero of the Soviet Union

Hero of the Soviet Union (Russian: Герой Советского Союза) was the highest distinction in the Soviet Union, awarded personally or collectively for heroic feats in service to the Soviet state and society. Established on 16 April 1934, the title was conferred together with the Order of Lenin and, from 1939, the Gold Star medal.

Hero of the Soviet Union
Established: 16 April 1934
Total awarded: 12,777
First recipients: Polar aviators, 1934

History

The first Heroes were pilots who rescued the crew of the icebreaker Chelyuskin in 1934. The title was awarded on a large scale during the Great Patriotic War (1941–45), when over 11,000 people received it. Later recipients included cosmonauts — Yuri Gagarin among them — and figures honored posthumously. Some individuals received the title multiple times, notably marshals Georgy Zhukov and Leonid Brezhnev (four times each).

Privileges and legacy

Recipients enjoyed material privileges, prestige, and public commemoration. After the dissolution of the USSR in 1991, successor states created their own hero titles, such as Hero of the Russian Federation. See also Hero of Socialist Labour.
