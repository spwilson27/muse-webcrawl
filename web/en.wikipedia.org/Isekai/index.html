Isekai

Meaning"Another world" (Japanese: 異世界)
MediaLight novels, manga, anime, video games
Common deviceProtagonist transported, summoned or reincarnated into a parallel world

Isekai (Japanese: 異世界, "different world" or "otherworld") is a genre of Japanese fantasy fiction in which a protagonist from Earth is transported to, reborn in, or trapped in a parallel universe, often a fantasy world resembling a role-playing game. It is one of the most popular genres of light novels, manga and anime.

Characteristics

The protagonist usually enters the other world through a portal, summoning ritual, virtual-reality game, or death followed by reincarnation — being run over by a truck has become such a cliché that it is affectionately parodied as "Truck-kun". Once there, the hero often possesses cheat-like powers, game-style status screens, or knowledge from modern life that makes them exceptional. Reverse isekai, in which fantasy beings come to Earth, also exists.

History and notable works

Portal fantasies are much older — folk tales such as Urashima Tarō and Western works like Alice's Adventures in Wonderland share the device — but the modern genre coalesced in the 1990s and 2000s with series such as The Vision of Escaflowne and games like .hack. Web-novel sites, especially Shōsetsuka ni Narō, fuelled an explosion in the 2010s. Landmark titles include Sword Art Online, Re:Zero − Starting Life in Another World, That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime, Overlord, Mushoku Tensei, KonoSuba and The Rising of the Shield Hero.

Reception

Isekai dominates light-novel publishing and anime adaptation slates, but it is frequently criticised for formulaic plots and power-fantasy wish fulfilment; some Japanese writing contests have even barred isekai submissions. Parodies and deconstructions have become a subgenre of their own.
