Fairchild Semiconductor

Fairchild Semiconductor International, Inc. was an American semiconductor company founded in San Jose, California, in 1957. A pioneer of silicon transistors and integrated circuits, Fairchild is regarded as one of the founding companies of Silicon Valley: dozens of later firms, including Intel and AMD, were founded by its former employees, earning them the nickname the "Fairchildren".

History

The company was founded by eight young scientists and engineers — the so-called "traitorous eight", including Gordon Moore, Robert Noyce, Julius Blank, Victor Grinich, Jean Hoerni, Eugene Kleiner, Jay Last and Sheldon Roberts — who left Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory to work under the backing of Sherman Fairchild's Fairchild Camera and Instrument. At Fairchild they developed the planar process (invented by Hoerni), which made reliable silicon transistors possible, and in 1961 produced one of the first commercially practical integrated circuits using Noyce's monolithic approach. Fairchild components powered the Apollo Guidance Computer and Minuteman missile systems.

Decline and later history

Despite its technical leadership, Fairchild lost key staff throughout the late 1960s as employees left to found start-ups. The parent company was acquired by Schlumberger in 1979 and later sold to National Semiconductor in 1987. The Fairchild name was revived in 1997 when the power and discrete businesses were spun out, and the new Fairchild Semiconductor operated independently until it was acquired by ON Semiconductor in 2016 for about $2.4 billion.
