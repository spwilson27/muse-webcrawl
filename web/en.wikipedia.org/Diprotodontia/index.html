Diprotodontia

Diprotodontia is the largest extant order of marsupials, with about 155 species, including the kangaroos, wallabies, possums, koala, wombats, and many others. Extinct diprotodonts include the hippopotamus-sized Diprotodon, the largest marsupial that ever lived, and the "marsupial lion" Thylacoleo.

Characteristics

The name refers to the defining feature of the group: two (di-) prominent forward-facing (pro-) teeth (-dontia) in the lower jaw. Most living diprotodonts are herbivores, as were most extinct members. Syndactyly — the fusion of the second and third toes of the hind foot — is common in the order, except in wombats and the koala. The dental formula of the lower jaw, with its large procumbent incisors, is unique among marsupials.

Classification

Diprotodontia is divided into three suborders: Vombatiformes (koalas and wombats), Phalangeriformes (possums and gliders), and Macropodiformes (kangaroos, wallabies, rat-kangaroos and the musky rat-kangaroo). Most species are found in Australia and New Guinea.

Evolution

The earliest known diprotodont fossils date to the late Oligocene of central Australia. The group diversified through the Miocene, producing giant forms such as Diprotodon optatum, which survived until roughly 44,000 years ago, around the time humans arrived in Australia.
