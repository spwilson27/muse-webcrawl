The Blue Ridge Parkway is a National Parkway and All-American Road in the United States, noted for its scenic beauty. It runs for 469 miles (755 km) through 29 counties in Virginia and North Carolina, mostly along the Blue Ridge, a major mountain chain that is part of the Appalachian Mountains. The parkway links Shenandoah National Park in the north with Great Smoky Mountains National Park in the south, and it is the most visited unit of the National Park System, drawing well over ten million visitors in typical years.

Construction

Construction began in 1935 as a New Deal public-works project of the Franklin D. Roosevelt administration, providing jobs during the Great Depression. Landscape architect Stanley Abbott directed the design, aiming for a road that would follow the mountain contours and unfold a varied sequence of views. Work proceeded in sections over five decades; the final segment, the Linn Cove Viaduct around Grandfather Mountain — an engineering landmark built to minimise environmental damage — was completed in 1987.

Features

The two-lane parkway has a maximum speed limit of 45 mph, no commercial vehicles, and limited access from mileposted overlooks. Highlights include Mabry Mill (milepost 176), the Blue Ridge Music Center (milepost 213), the Moses H. Cone Memorial Park, Linville Falls, Mount Mitchell — the highest peak east of the Mississippi, reachable by a spur road — and the Folk Art Center near Asheville. The parkway passes through a remarkable range of habitats and is famous for spring wildflowers and autumn colour. Twenty-six tunnels pierce the ridges along its length.
