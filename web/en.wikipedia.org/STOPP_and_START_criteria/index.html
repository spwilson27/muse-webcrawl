STOPP and START criteria


Image placeholder: STOPP and START criteria

The STOPP (Screening Tool of Older Persons' Prescriptions) and START (Screening Tool to Alert to Right Treatment) criteria are explicit criteria used to identify potentially inappropriate prescribing in older people. They were developed in Ireland, first published in 2008 by Gallagher, Ryan, Byrne and O'Mahony, and updated in subsequent versions (version 2 in 2015 and version 3 in 2023). The criteria are organized by physiological system and are designed to be applied during medication review.

STOPP criteria

STOPP lists medications or medication combinations that are potentially inappropriate in older adults because the risks outweigh the benefits, the duration of treatment exceeds recommended limits, or safer alternatives exist. Examples include long-term use of benzodiazepines (risk of falls and sedation), duplicate drug classes, and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs in patients with severe hypertension or heart failure.

START criteria

START complements STOPP by identifying prescribing omissions — evidence-based medicines that are potentially beneficial but have been omitted without a valid reason. Examples include anticoagulation in chronic atrial fibrillation, statins in established cardiovascular disease, and vaccination against influenza and pneumococcus in older adults.

Use and evidence

The criteria have been validated in multiple countries and clinical settings, translated into several languages, and shown in randomized trials to improve prescribing quality when applied as part of a structured medication review, although effects on hard clinical outcomes such as hospitalization have been mixed.
