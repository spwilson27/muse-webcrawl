La Monte Thornton Young (born 14 October 1935) is an American composer, musician and performance artist widely recognised as the first minimalist composer. His works, built on sustained tones and just intonation, are among the landmarks of drone and minimal music.

Career

Young studied at UCLA, where he encountered serialism, then moved to New York in 1960 and became associated with the Fluxus circle. His Compositions 1960 — instruction pieces such as "draw a straight line and follow it" — influenced conceptual art. In 1962 he formed the Theatre of Eternal Music (Dream Syndicate) with Tony Conrad, John Cale, Angus MacLise and his partner Marian Zazeela, performing extremely long sustained-tone works. He later studied Hindustani vocal music under Pandit Pran Nath.

Major works

His ongoing projects include The Well-Tuned Piano (begun 1964), a many-hours-long piano improvisation in just intonation, and the Dream House, a permanent sound-and-light installation created with Zazeela, versions of which have run continuously for years. Young's influence extends through minimalism to ambient, drone metal and electronic music.
