Fountain pen

A fountain pen is a writing instrument that uses a metal nib to apply water-based ink to paper. Unlike dip pens, it carries its ink in an internal reservoir, and feeds it to the nib by capillary action and gravity through a feed. Fountain pens range from inexpensive disposable and school pens to luxury instruments costing thousands of dollars, and remain valued for their smooth writing, expressiveness, and refillability.

How it works

The key components are the reservoir, the feed — a finned plastic or ebonite piece beneath the nib whose channels and fins regulate ink and air exchange — and the nib, traditionally made of gold alloy or stainless steel, tipped with a hard iridium-alloy pellet and slit so that ink flows when pressure is applied. As ink leaves the reservoir, air must enter to replace it; the feed's fins act as a buffer, storing excess ink during changes in pressure or temperature. Filling systems have included eyedropper filling, rubber sacs squeezed by levers or buttons, piston and vacuum fillers, converters, and disposable cartridges.

History

Reservoir pens were described as early as the 10th century, but reliable designs arrived only in the 1880s. In 1884 Lewis Waterman patented a feed with fissures that controlled ink flow, overcoming the blotting that plagued earlier pens, and the Waterman company became an industry leader. Competitors such as Parker, Sheaffer, and Montblanc (founded 1906) made the fountain pen the standard writing instrument of the early 20th century. The invention of the ballpoint pen in the 1940s and its mass production in the 1950s dramatically reduced fountain pen use, though they survived in European schools, in professions such as law and medicine, and as luxury goods. Since the 2000s the fountain pen has enjoyed a revival among hobbyists and collectors.

Inks

Fountain pen inks are water-based dye solutions, far less viscous than ballpoint or gel inks; pigment, iron-gall, and "bulletproof" inks offer varying water resistance. Only inks formulated for fountain pens should be used, since India ink and calligraphy dip inks can clog or corrode the feed.
