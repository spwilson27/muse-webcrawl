Neurodiversity

Neurodiversity is a framework for understanding human brain function and mental illness which argues that diversity in human cognition is normal and that certain conditions classified as neurodevelopmental disorders are differences and disabilities rather than diseases or defects to be cured. The term is commonly attributed to Australian sociologist Judy Singer, who used it in her 1998 honours thesis, and it was popularised around the same time by journalist Harvey Blume in The Atlantic.

People described as neurodivergent include those with autism, ADHD, dyslexia, dyspraxia, Tourette syndrome and various other neurological or developmental conditions, in contrast to the neurotypical majority. The neurodiversity paradigm draws on the social model of disability, holding that much of the disadvantage experienced by neurodivergent people arises from societal barriers and expectations rather than from individual deficits alone.

The neurodiversity movement

The neurodiversity movement, which grew out of the autism rights movement of the 1990s and the online autistic community, advocates for acceptance, inclusion and civil rights for neurodivergent people, supports identity-first language (e.g. "autistic person"), and promotes the idea of neurodiversity as a form of human variation comparable to biodiversity. It has influenced corporate hiring programmes, educational practice and public discussion of conditions like ADHD and dyslexia.

Criticism

Critics argue that the paradigm, if applied too broadly, can minimise the profound support needs of people with severe impairments, and that it may not adequately represent non-speaking autistic people or those with co-occurring intellectual disability. Others contend that the concept is vague, or that casting all neurological difference as benign variation conflicts with people's own experience of suffering and their desire for treatment. Supporters reply that neurodiversity is compatible with providing support and treatment, while challenging the assumption that difference itself is pathology.
