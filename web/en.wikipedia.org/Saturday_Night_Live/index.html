Saturday Night Live

Saturday Night Live (SNL) is an American late-night live television sketch comedy and variety show created by Lorne Michaels. It premiered on NBC on 11 October 1975 under the original title NBC's Saturday Night. Each episode is hosted by a celebrity guest who delivers an opening monologue and performs in sketches alongside the ensemble cast, with a musical guest performing during the show.

Format

Each show opens with a "cold open" sketch ending in the exclamation "Live from New York, it's Saturday Night!", followed by the host's monologue, a series of live and pre-taped sketches (including fake commercials), the satirical news segment Weekend Update, and two performances by the musical guest. The episode is written and rehearsed in a famously gruelling single week, beginning with a pitch meeting on Monday and a table read on Wednesday.

History and impact

The original 1975 cast — the "Not Ready for Prime Time Players" — included Dan Aykroyd, John Belushi, Chevy Chase, Jane Curtin, Garrett Morris, Laraine Newman and Gilda Radner. The show has launched the careers of generations of comedians, including Eddie Murphy, Bill Murray, Tina Fey, Amy Poehler, Will Ferrell and Kate McKinnon, and its political satire — notably impressions of U.S. presidents — regularly shapes public discussion. SNL has won more than 80 Primetime Emmy Awards, the most of any television programme.
