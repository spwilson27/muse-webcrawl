Regulatory T cell

Regulatory T cells (Tregs) are a subpopulation of T cells that suppress immune responses, maintaining tolerance to self-antigens and preventing autoimmune disease. They are typically characterized by expression of CD4, high levels of CD25, and the transcription factor FOXP3.

Development and function

Most Tregs develop in the thymus (natural or thymic Tregs), while others are induced in the periphery from conventional T cells. They suppress effector immune cells through mechanisms including secretion of inhibitory cytokines (IL-10, TGF-beta), metabolic disruption, and modulation of dendritic cells.

Clinical significance

Mutations in FOXP3 cause the severe autoimmune syndrome IPEX, illustrating the cells' essential role. Tregs also restrain anti-tumor immunity, making them a target in cancer immunotherapy, while boosting Treg function is explored for treating autoimmunity and transplant rejection.
