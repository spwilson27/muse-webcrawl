Mass in B minor, BWV 232

Choral work by Johann Sebastian Bach

The Mass in B minor, BWV 232, by Johann Sebastian Bach is a musical setting of the complete Latin text of the Roman Catholic Mass — a missa tota — for five voices, choir and orchestra. Compiled and completed in the last years of Bach's life (the full work was assembled by around 1749), it is regarded as one of the supreme achievements of Western music, a summation of the composer's art of counterpoint and of the Baroque era.

Composition

Bach, a Lutheran, built the Mass largely by adapting earlier music (the "parody" technique). He had presented its Kyrie and Gloria to the Catholic court of Dresden in 1733 when seeking the title of court composer; the Sanctus reworked a 1724 setting; and movements such as the Crucifixus and Osanna drew on earlier cantatas. The complete Mass was never performed in Bach's lifetime — it was too long for liturgical use in either church — and its first complete performance took place only in 1859, in Leipzig.

Structure and reception

The work falls into four parts: the Missa (Kyrie and Gloria), the Symbolum Nicenum (Credo), the Sanctus, and a final section with the Osanna, Benedictus, Agnus Dei and Dona nobis pacem. Nineteenth-century editor Hans Georg Nägeli called it "the greatest musical art-work of all times and nations." UNESCO added Bach's autograph manuscript to its Memory of the World register in 2015.
