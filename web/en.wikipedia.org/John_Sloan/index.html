John French Sloan (August 2, 1871 – September 7, 1951) was an American painter and etcher, one of the founders of the Ashcan School of American art. He is best known for scenes of everyday urban life in New York City in the early twentieth century.

Early life

Sloan was born in Lock Haven, Pennsylvania, and grew up in Philadelphia. He worked as an illustrator for the Philadelphia Inquirer and the Philadelphia Press, and studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts, where he fell under the influence of Robert Henri.

Career

Moving to New York in 1904, Sloan painted the streets, rooftops, saloons and tenements of the city in works such as McSorley's Bar (1912) and Backyards, Greenwich Village (1914). He was one of "The Eight" who exhibited together in 1908 in reaction to the conservatism of the National Academy of Design, and he helped organize the Armory Show of 1913. Also a noted etcher, he taught for many years at the Art Students League of New York.

Later life

Sloan was a socialist and contributed drawings to The Masses magazine. In his later years he spent summers in Santa Fe, New Mexico. He served as president of the Society of Independent Artists.
