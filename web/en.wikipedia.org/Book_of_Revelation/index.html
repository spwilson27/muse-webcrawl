The Book of Revelation is the final book of the New Testament and consequently of the Christian Bible. Written in Koine Greek, its title derives from the first word of the text, apokalypsis, meaning "unveiling" or "revelation". The author names himself John and states he was on the island of Patmos when he received the visions. The book is the only fully apocalyptic book in the New Testament canon.

Structure and content

After a prologue and letters to seven churches in Asia Minor, the book unfolds a series of visions: the heavenly throne room; the Lamb opening a scroll sealed with seven seals; seven trumpets and seven bowls of wrath; the woman and the dragon; the Beast and the number 666; the fall of Babylon; the millennium and the binding of Satan; the Last Judgment; and finally a new heaven and new earth, with the New Jerusalem descending. Famous images include the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse and the Battle of Armageddon.

Authorship and dating

Church tradition identified the author with John the Apostle, but the book's Greek style differs markedly from the Gospel of John, and most modern scholars attribute it to a distinct figure, "John of Patmos," writing during the reign of Domitian (81–96 CE) or possibly Nero's era. Revelation's canonicity was disputed in the early church, especially in the East, but it was ultimately accepted.

Interpretation

Major interpretive schools include preterism (events fulfilled in the 1st century), historicism (a panorama of church history), futurism (events yet to come), and idealism (timeless spiritual symbolism). Revelation has profoundly influenced Western art, literature, and eschatology.
