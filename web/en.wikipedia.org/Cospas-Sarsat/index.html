Cospas-Sarsat

Formation1979 (agreement); operational 1985
TypeInternational satellite-based search and rescue programme
HeadquartersMontreal, Quebec, Canada
Founding statesCanada, France, Soviet Union, United States

Cospas-Sarsat is an international satellite-based search and rescue (SAR) distress alert detection and information distribution system. Its name combines COSPAS (from the Russian Cosmicheskaya Sistema Poiska Avariynyh Sudov, "space system for the search of vessels in distress") and SARSAT ("Search and Rescue Satellite-Aided Tracking"). The system detects emergency locator beacons carried by aircraft, ships, and individuals, and relays their positions to rescue authorities worldwide.

History

The programme originated in a 1979 memorandum of understanding between Canada, France, the USSR, and the USA. The first satellite was launched in 1982, and the system was declared fully operational in 1985 under a formal intergovernmental agreement signed in 1988. Since its inception, Cospas-Sarsat has assisted in the rescue of tens of thousands of people.

System architecture

Distress beacons transmit on 406 MHz. Signals are received by instruments aboard satellites in low Earth orbit (LEOSAR), geostationary orbit (GEOSAR), and medium Earth orbit (MEOSAR, using navigation satellites such as GPS, Galileo, and GLONASS). Local user terminals compute beacon locations, often by Doppler processing, and mission control centres distribute alerts to rescue coordination centres.

Beacons

The system supports emergency position-indicating radio beacons (EPIRBs) for maritime use, emergency locator transmitters (ELTs) for aviation, and personal locator beacons (PLBs) for individuals. Older 121.5 MHz analogue beacons ceased to be processed by satellite in 2009.
