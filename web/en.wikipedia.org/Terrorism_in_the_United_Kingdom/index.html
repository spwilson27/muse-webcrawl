Terrorism in the United Kingdom

Terrorism in the United Kingdom has a long history, encompassing Irish republican and unionist loyalist violence related to Northern Ireland, Islamist extremist attacks, far-right extremism, and earlier campaigns by anarchists and suffragettes. The United Kingdom's threat level is assessed by the Joint Terrorism Analysis Centre (JTAC), and counterterrorism policing is coordinated by Counter Terrorism Policing headquarters in London.

Northern Ireland

From the late 1960s until the Good Friday Agreement of 1998, the conflict known as the Troubles saw paramilitary violence by republican groups such as the Provisional Irish Republican Army (IRA) and loyalist groups such as the Ulster Volunteer Force (UVF). The IRA conducted a bombing campaign on the British mainland, including attacks in Birmingham (1974), Guildford (1974), Brighton (1984), Warrington (1993), and Manchester (1996). Dissident republican groups, including the Real IRA — responsible for the Omagh bombing of 1998, the deadliest single attack of the Troubles — continued limited violence after 1998.

Islamist terrorism

The 7 July 2005 London bombings ("7/7"), in which four suicide bombers killed 52 people on the transport network, were the first Islamist suicide attacks in the UK. Subsequent major incidents include the murder of Fusilier Lee Rigby (2013), the Westminster attack, Manchester Arena bombing, and London Bridge attack (all 2017), and the Fishmongers' Hall stabbings (2019) and Reading stabbings (2020). The domestic security service MI5 and the government's CONTEST strategy, including the Prevent programme, form the backbone of the official response.

Far-right terrorism

Far-right violence includes the 1999 London nail bombings by David Copeland and the 2016 murder of the MP Jo Cox by Thomas Mair. The neo-Nazi organisation National Action was proscribed as a terrorist group in 2016, the first far-right group to be banned under the Terrorism Act 2000.

Legislation

Key legislation includes the Prevention of Terrorism Acts during the Troubles, the Terrorism Act 2000, the Terrorism Act 2006, and the Counter-Terrorism and Sentencing Act 2021. The official threat level system, introduced in 2006, ranges from low to critical.
