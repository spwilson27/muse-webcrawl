Apple silicon

Apple silicon is a series of system on a chip (SoC) and system in a package (SiP) processors designed by Apple Inc., primarily using the ARM architecture. Apple silicon powers the iPhone (A series), iPad, Apple Watch (S series), Apple TV, AirPods (H series), and, since 2020, the Macintosh line of computers (M series).

Mac transition

In June 2020 Apple announced a two-year transition of the Mac away from Intel x86 processors to its own ARM-based chips. The first, the Apple M1, launched in November 2020 in the MacBook Air, MacBook Pro and Mac mini, winning praise for its performance per watt. Successors M1 Pro, M1 Max and M1 Ultra were followed by the M2 (2022), M3 (2023) and M4 (2024) generations, with the transition completed across the Mac range. The Rosetta 2 translation layer allowed software written for Intel Macs to run on the new machines.

Architecture

The chips use a unified memory architecture in which CPU, GPU, Neural Engine and other accelerators share one pool of high-bandwidth memory, contributing to their efficiency. Apple's in-house designs derive from its A-series mobile chips, whose lineage traces back to the A4 of 2010.
