Science fantasy

Science fantasy blends the trappings of science fiction with magic and myth.

Science fantasy is a hybrid genre within speculative fiction that blends tropes and elements from both science fiction and fantasy. Unlike the more strictly rational explanations of science fiction, science fantasy presents worlds in which advanced technology and magic coexist, or in which seemingly magical phenomena are given a thin scientific gloss—or none at all.

Characteristics

The genre includes "planetary romance" stories of sword-and-planet adventure, such as Edgar Rice Burroughs's Barsoom series; tales of a far-future "Dying Earth", such as Jack Vance's stories and Gene Wolfe's The Book of the New Sun; and works like Anne McCaffrey's Dragonriders of Pern, in which dragons are genetically engineered. Franchises such as Star Wars are frequently described as science fantasy because of their combination of spaceships with quasi-magical powers like the Force.

History

The label grew out of the American pulp magazines of the early twentieth century, which published science fiction and fantasy side by side. Critics continue to debate the boundary: a common formulation holds that science fiction makes the implausible possible, while fantasy makes the impossible plausible; science fantasy deliberately mixes the two.
