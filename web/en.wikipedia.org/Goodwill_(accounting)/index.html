Goodwill (accounting)

In accounting, goodwill is an intangible asset recognised when a company acquires another business for more than the fair value of its identifiable net assets. It captures the value of things that cannot be separately identified and sold — brand reputation, customer relationships, skilled employees, and expected synergies. Under both IFRS and US GAAP, goodwill is not amortised but must be tested at least annually for impairment.

Calculation

Goodwill = Purchase consideration transferred − Fair value of identifiable net assets acquired

For example, if a buyer pays $10 million for a company whose identifiable assets minus liabilities are fairly valued at $7 million, the $3 million excess is recorded as goodwill on the acquirer's balance sheet.

Internally generated goodwill

Goodwill created internally — the value a business builds up through its own reputation and customer loyalty — is not recognised as an asset, because it cannot be measured reliably and is not separable from the business. Only purchased goodwill arising in an acquisition appears in financial statements.

Impairment

Because goodwill is not amortised, reporting entities must compare its carrying amount with the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which it belongs. If the carrying amount exceeds the recoverable amount, the difference is written off as an impairment loss in profit or loss, and under IFRS such a write-down can never be reversed. Large impairments — for example, the write-downs following failed acquisitions — can materially reduce reported earnings and equity.

Negative goodwill

If the fair value of net assets acquired exceeds the price paid (a "bargain purchase"), the difference, sometimes called negative goodwill, is recognised immediately as a gain in profit or loss under IFRS 3 after the acquirer reassesses the measurements.
