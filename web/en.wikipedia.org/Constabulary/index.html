Constabulary

A constabulary is a law enforcement body or police force organized on military lines. The term derives from constable, originally the keeper of the stable of a lord or king, later a senior officer of the peace. In British usage, a constabulary usually means the police force of a county or region, while elsewhere it often denotes a paramilitary gendarmerie-style force responsible for both policing and internal security, especially in rural areas.

British constabularies

England and Wales are policed by territorial constabularies, most named after their county or metropolitan area — for example, the Hampshire and Isle of Wight Constabulary — whose front-line officers hold the office of constable. The Police Service of Northern Ireland succeeded the Royal Ulster Constabulary, itself descended from the Royal Irish Constabulary, the influential armed colonial police model founded in Ireland in the 19th century.

Colonial and Commonwealth forces

The constabulary model spread widely through the British Empire: the Philippine Constabulary (1901), the Solomon Islands and Papua New Guinea constabularies, and various African forces combined soldiering with policing in territories where civil police were absent or thin. The United States Constabulary performed occupation and border duties in Germany after the Second World War.

Gendarmerie comparison

Constabularies resemble the gendarmeries of France, Italy, and Spain in their military organization and rural deployment, though in Britain the term usually denotes a regular civilian police force rather than a military one.
