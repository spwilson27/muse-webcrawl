Waldi

Waldi was the official mascot of the 1972 Summer Olympic Games in Munich, West Germany. A multicoloured dachshund, Waldi was the first official mascot in Olympic history and began the tradition of an official mascot for every subsequent Olympic Games.

Design

The dachshund was chosen as a characteristically Bavarian dog and an emblem of endurance, tenacity, and agility — qualities associated with athletes. Waldi's design came from the studio of Otl Aicher, the graphic designer responsible for the Munich Games' celebrated visual identity; a design dated to a studio Christmas party in 1970, when staff drew dachshunds, is credited in part to designer Elena Winschermann. The mascot's body was striped in the pastel colours of the Games — light blue, green, yellow, and orange — deliberately avoiding the red and black associated with the Nazi-era 1936 Berlin Games.

Legacy

Waldi appeared on posters, merchandise, and as a costumed figure, and the marathon course in Munich was even laid out in the shape of the mascot. Earlier Games had unofficial mascots, but Waldi was the first to be officially designated, setting the model followed by every Olympics since.
