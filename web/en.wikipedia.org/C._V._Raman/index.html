Sir Chandrasekhara Venkata Raman FRS (7 November 1888 – 21 November 1970) was an Indian physicist known for his work in the field of light scattering. Using a spectrograph that he developed, he discovered that when light traverses a transparent material, some of the deflected light changes wavelength. This phenomenon, subsequently known as Raman scattering, results from the Raman effect. In 1930 Raman received the Nobel Prize in Physics for this discovery, becoming the first Asian and first non-white person to receive a Nobel Prize in any branch of science.

Life and career

Born in Tiruchirappalli in Tamil Nadu to a physics lecturer father, Raman earned his master's in physics from Presidency College, Madras, at 18. Unable initially to find a scientific post, he worked as a civil servant in the Indian Finance Department while publishing research in his spare hours. In 1917 he became Palit Professor of Physics at the University of Calcutta, and from 1933 directed the Indian Institute of Science in Bangalore, where he later founded the Raman Research Institute (1948).

The Raman effect

Returning by ship from England in 1921, Raman was struck by the Mediterranean's blue colour and began investigating light scattering in liquids. On 28 February 1928, with K. S. Krishnan, he demonstrated that scattered light contained new spectral lines characteristic of the scattering molecules. The effect gave chemists a powerful spectroscopic tool (Raman spectroscopy) for identifying molecular vibrations; 28 February is celebrated in India as National Science Day.
