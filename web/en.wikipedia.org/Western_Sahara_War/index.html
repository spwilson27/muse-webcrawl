Western Sahara War

Western Sahara War
Date: 1975–1991
Location: Western Sahara, Morocco, Mauritania
Belligerents: Polisario Front vs Morocco and Mauritania
Result: UN-brokered ceasefire (1991)

The Western Sahara War (1975–1991) was an armed conflict between the Sahrawi nationalist Polisario Front and the states of Morocco and Mauritania over the territory of Western Sahara, a former Spanish colony. It is the principal phase of the wider Western Sahara conflict.

Background

As Spain withdrew in 1975, the Madrid Accords partitioned the territory between Morocco and Mauritania, and the "Green March" of Moroccan civilians asserted Morocco's claim. The Polisario Front, founded in 1973 and backed by Algeria, proclaimed the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic in 1976 and fought a guerrilla war against both occupying armies.

Course

Mauritania, its army strained and its government shaken by a 1978 coup, signed a peace treaty with the Polisario in 1979 and withdrew, whereupon Morocco annexed the southern sector. Morocco gradually secured the populated and economically useful areas behind a system of defensive sand walls (the Berm), completed in the 1980s, while Polisario raided from bases near Tindouf in Algeria.

Ceasefire

In 1991 the United Nations brokered a ceasefire under the Settlement Plan, monitored by the MINURSO mission, which envisaged a referendum on independence or integration. The referendum was never held, and the ceasefire collapsed in November 2020 following clashes at Guerguerat.
