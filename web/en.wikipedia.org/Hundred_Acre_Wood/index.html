Hundred Acre Wood

Summary

Created by: A. A. Milne

First appearance: Winnie-the-Pooh (1926)

Inspired by: Ashdown Forest, East Sussex, England

Inhabitants: Winnie-the-Pooh, Piglet, Eeyore, Owl, Rabbit, Tigger, Kanga and Roo

The Hundred Acre Wood (also spelled the Hundred-Acre Wood) is the fictional forest setting of A. A. Milne's Winnie-the-Pooh stories. It is the home of Winnie-the-Pooh and his animal friends, and most of the adventures in Winnie-the-Pooh (1926) and The House at Pooh Corner (1928) take place there.

Inspiration

The wood was inspired by the Five Hundred Acre Wood in Ashdown Forest in East Sussex, England, near Milne's country home at Cotchford Farm in Hartfield. Many landmarks from the stories correspond to real places in the forest, including Poohsticks Bridge, the Enchanted Place, and the North Pole discovered by Pooh. E. H. Shepard's illustrations helped fix the wood's appearance in the public imagination.

Locations

Notable locations include Pooh's house in a tree, Piglet's house in a beech tree, Owl's house (The Chestnuts), Rabbit's burrow, Eeyore's Gloomy Place, Kanga's house, the Six Pine Trees, the Pooh Trap for Heffalumps, and the sandy pit where Roo plays. Christopher Robin, the human boy based on Milne's son, visits the animals from the edge of the wood.

Legacy

The Hundred Acre Wood has been reproduced in Disney's animated adaptations of Winnie the Pooh and in attractions at Disney parks. Ashdown Forest itself remains a popular destination for visitors seeking the real-world settings of the stories.
