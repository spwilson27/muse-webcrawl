The San Francisco Municipal Railway, known as Muni, is the public transit system for the city and county of San Francisco, California. Founded in 1912, it was one of the first publicly owned transit systems in the United States and today operates buses, trolleybuses, the Muni Metro light rail system, historic streetcars on the F Market & Wharves line, and the city's famous cable cars.

History

Muni began service on December 28, 1912 with the Geary Street streetcar line, after voters approved municipal ownership. It absorbed the private Market Street Railway in 1944, unifying the city's transit. The Muni Metro subway under Market Street opened in 1980.

Services

The system carries hundreds of thousands of passengers daily across dozens of routes. The cable car system, designated a National Historic Landmark, is the world's last manually operated cable car network.
