Subglacial lake

A subglacial lake is a lake that is found under a glacier, typically an ice cap or ice sheet. In this environment, "glacier" refers to any ice mass permanent enough to have a body of water resting in hydrostatic equilibrium with the ice over it. The ice sheet above the lake acts as an insulating blanket that helps retain geothermal heat from the Earth's interior, and water at the interface may be kept liquid by pressure lowering of the melting point.

Characteristics

Subglacial lakes exist where the bed of a glacier reaches the pressure melting point. Water collects in topographic depressions in the bedrock, often several kilometres below the surface of an ice sheet. Because the water's density differs from that of the surrounding ice, the surface of the lake may tilt significantly even where the ice surface appears nearly flat.

The best-studied examples lie beneath the Antarctic ice sheet. Lake Vostok, beneath Russia's Vostok Station in East Antarctica, is the largest known subglacial lake, roughly 250 km long and 50 km wide, lying nearly 4 km below the ice surface. Other major examples include Lake Ellsworth and Lake Enigma in West Antarctica and beneath the Greenland ice sheet. By the 2020s more than 400 subglacial lakes had been identified in Antarctica alone, many of them connected by subglacial water networks that periodically drain and refill.

Scientific significance

Subglacial lakes may have been isolated from the surface for millions of years, making them natural laboratories for the evolution of microbial life in extreme, dark, cold environments. They are also studied as analogues for the subsurface oceans of icy moons such as Europa and Enceladus. Drilling into a subglacial lake requires great care to avoid contaminating the pristine water with surface organisms and chemicals; the first direct sampling of an Antarctic subglacial lake was achieved at Subglacial Lake Whillans in 2013.
