Vanessa (butterfly)

Vanessa is a genus of brush-footed butterflies in the family Nymphalidae, commonly called the red admirals and painted ladies. The genus has a nearly worldwide distribution and includes some of the most familiar and wide-ranging butterflies.

Species

Well-known species include the painted lady (Vanessa cardui), one of the most widespread butterflies in the world, found on every continent except Antarctica and South America; the red admiral (Vanessa atalanta); the West Coast lady (Vanessa annabella); the American lady (Vanessa virginiensis); and the Australian painted lady (Vanessa kershawi). Around twenty species are recognised, depending on classification.

Biology

Vanessa larvae feed chiefly on plants in the nettle family (Urticaceae) and mallow family (Malvaceae), as well as thistles. Adults are strong fliers; the painted lady undertakes a long-distance multi-generational migration spanning thousands of kilometres between Africa and Europe. The genus name Vanessa was coined by Johan Christian Fabricius in 1807.
