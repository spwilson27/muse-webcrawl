Carya ovata

Carya ovata, the shagbark hickory, is a common hickory native to eastern North America, from southeastern Nebraska and southern Minnesota east through southern Ontario and Quebec to southern Maine, and south to Georgia and eastern Texas. It is found throughout the eastern United States as a dominant species in oak–hickory forests.

Description

It is a large deciduous tree, growing up to 46 m tall, recognisable by its mature bark which peels away in long loose vertical strips — the "shaggy" appearance that gives it its name. The leaves are pinnately compound with five (rarely three or seven) leaflets. The nuts are edible, sweet and an important food for wildlife including squirrels and bears.

Uses

Shagbark hickory wood is strong, hard and shock-resistant, used for tool handles, ladder rungs and firewood; it is prized for smoking meats. Andrew Jackson was nicknamed "Old Hickory" after the tree's toughness.
