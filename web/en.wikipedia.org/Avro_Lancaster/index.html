The Avro Lancaster is a British Second World War heavy bomber designed and manufactured by Avro. It was developed from the twin-engined Avro Manchester and first flew in 1941. The Lancaster became the most successful British heavy bomber of the war and the principal aircraft of RAF Bomber Command's night offensive against Germany.

Design

Powered by four Rolls-Royce Merlin engines, the Lancaster featured an exceptionally large, unobstructed 33-foot bomb bay, allowing it to carry the largest bombs used by the RAF, including the 22,000 lb Grand Slam "earthquake bomb", as well as the spinning Upkeep "bouncing bomb" in a modified form.

Operational history

Lancasters flew 156,000 sorties and dropped over 600,000 tons of bombs. The type is famous for Operation Chastise (the "Dambusters" raid) of May 1943 by No. 617 Squadron, and for sinking the German battleship Tirpitz in 1944. Losses were heavy: of 7,377 built, more than 3,200 were lost in action.

Legacy

Derivatives included the Avro Lincoln and the maritime Shackleton. Today only two Lancasters remain airworthy, one with the Battle of Britain Memorial Flight in the UK and one in Canada.
