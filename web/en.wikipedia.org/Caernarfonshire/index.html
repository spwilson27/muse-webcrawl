Caernarfonshire

Caernarfonshire (Welsh: Sir Gaernarfon) is one of the thirteen historic counties of Wales, in the north-west of the country. Its county town was Caernarfon, and it included much of the mountainous region of Snowdonia as well as the Llŷn Peninsula.

Geography

The county was bounded by the Irish Sea and Caernarfon Bay to the west, the Menai Strait to the north, Denbighshire to the east, and Merionethshire and Cardigan Bay to the south. It contains Snowdon (Yr Wyddfa), the highest mountain in Wales, and much of what is now Snowdonia National Park.

History

The county was created under the Statute of Rhuddlan in 1284 following the conquest of Wales by Edward I, from the lands of the former principality of Gwynedd. It functioned as an administrative county from 1889 until 1974, when it was merged with Anglesey and Merionethshire to form the new county of Gwynedd. The name is still used informally and for ceremonial and sporting purposes.
