East Bergholt is a village in the Babergh district of Suffolk, England, close to the border with Essex and the River Stour. It lies in the heart of "Constable Country", the Dedham Vale landscape made famous by the painter John Constable, who was born in the village in 1776.

History

The village grew prosperous on the medieval cloth trade of the Stour valley. Its parish church, St Mary the Virgin, was begun in the early 1500s but its tower was never completed; since the 16th century the church's bells have instead been rung from a wooden bell cage in the churchyard, one of the heaviest five-bell peals in England, rung by hand directly on the bells.

Constable connection

John Constable was born at East Bergholt House, the home of his father Golding Constable, a wealthy corn merchant who owned Flatford Mill nearby. The village and surrounding vale — Flatford, Willy Lott's Cottage and the Stour — provided the subjects for many of Constable's best-known paintings, including scenes around Flatford Lock and the Hay Wain landscape. A monument and the Constable connection continue to draw visitors.

Today

East Bergholt retains a substantial historic core with listed timber-framed buildings, a high school, shops and pubs, and is popular with commuters using Manningtree station nearby.
