Postmodern dance

Postmodern dance is a performance movement that emerged in the United States in the 1960s and 1970s as a reaction against the compositional and presentational conventions of modern dance. Its practitioners rejected narrative, virtuosity and spectacle in favour of ordinary ("pedestrian") movement, task-based improvisation, chance procedures and an analytical approach to choreography.

Judson Dance Theater

The movement coalesced around the Judson Dance Theater, a collective that presented concerts at Judson Memorial Church in New York City from 1962 to 1964. Its members included Yvonne Rainer, Trisha Brown, Lucinda Childs, Steve Paxton, David Gordon and Simone Forti. Rainer's 1965 "No Manifesto" famously refused spectacle, glamour, and the star image of the dancer.

Methods

Choreographers used game structures, scores, repetition and everyday actions such as walking, running and carrying objects. Steve Paxton developed contact improvisation, while Trisha Brown explored equipment pieces and accumulation structures, and Lucinda Childs adopted minimalist repetition aligned with the music of Philip Glass. Later generations, sometimes called post-Judson choreographers, reintroduced theatricality and virtuosity while retaining the movement's experimental frame.
