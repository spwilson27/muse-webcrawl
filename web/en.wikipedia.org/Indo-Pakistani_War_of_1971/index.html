Indo-Pakistani War of 1971

The Indo-Pakistani War of 1971 was a military confrontation between India and Pakistan that occurred during the Bangladesh Liberation War in East Pakistan from 3 December 1971 to the fall of Dhaka on 16 December 1971. The war began with preemptive aerial strikes by the Pakistan Air Force on Indian air stations, following which India formally entered the war. It was one of the shortest wars in history, lasting just 13 days, and resulted in the independence of Bangladesh.

Background

The crisis began after Pakistan's 1970 general election, in which the Awami League of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman won a majority but was denied power by the military regime of Yahya Khan. On 25 March 1971 the Pakistan Army launched Operation Searchlight, a crackdown in East Pakistan that killed large numbers of Bengali civilians and drove an estimated ten million refugees into India. India supported and trained the Mukti Bahini guerrillas and, in August 1971, signed a treaty of friendship with the Soviet Union.

Course of the war

In the eastern theatre, Indian forces under Lt. Gen. Jagjit Singh Aurora, fighting alongside the Mukti Bahini, advanced rapidly on Dhaka, bypassing fortified towns and using heliborne and airborne operations, including the Tangail airdrop. The Indian Navy's Eastern Naval Command blockaded East Pakistan, and the aircraft carrier INS Vikrant struck coastal targets; in the west, Operation Trident, a missile-boat attack on Karachi on the night of 4–5 December, became celebrated as Navy Day in India. On the western land front, fighting included the Battle of Longewala, where a small Indian detachment held off a Pakistani armoured brigade with air support.

Surrender and aftermath

On 16 December 1971, Lt. Gen. A. A. K. Niazi signed the Instrument of Surrender in Dhaka before General Aurora, and some 93,000 Pakistani personnel became prisoners of war — the largest military surrender since the Second World War. Bangladesh was recognised as an independent state, and the 1972 Simla Agreement between India and Pakistan sought to settle outstanding disputes bilaterally; the prisoners were repatriated by 1974.
