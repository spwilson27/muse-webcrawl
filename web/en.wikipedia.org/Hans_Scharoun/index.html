Bernhard Hans Henry Scharoun (20 September 1893 – 25 November 1972) was a German architect best known for designing the Berliner Philharmonie concert hall and the Schminke House in Löbau, Saxony. He was a leading figure of organic architecture and of the expressionist movement in the 1920s, associated with the Gläserne Kette (Glass Chain) group organised by Bruno Taut.

Career

Scharoun studied architecture in Berlin and served in the First World War. In the 1920s he joined Der Ring and taught at the Breslau Academy. His early works included housing at the Siemensstadt estate in Berlin (1929–31) and the Schminke House (1933), a flowing, ship-like private home considered a masterpiece of International Modernism with organic planning. Under the Nazi regime he worked quietly on private houses, staying in Germany in "inner emigration." After 1945 he briefly directed Berlin's planning department and promoted the collective plan for rebuilding the city. His mature work culminated in the Berliner Philharmonie (1960–63), with its tent-like form, vineyard-terraced seating and "music at the centre" concept, and the nearby State Library (Staatsbibliothek, completed posthumously) at the Kulturforum.

Other works

Other notable projects include the German Maritime Museum in Bremerhaven, the Wolfsburg theatre, the Liederhalle in Stuttgart (with Wilhelm Frank), the Berlin Philharmonic's Chamber Music Hall, and late housing such as the "Romeo and Juliet" towers in Stuttgart.

Legacy

Scharoun's buildings are celebrated for their dynamic, landscape-responsive forms and humane interiors, and he is regarded as one of the most important German architects of the twentieth century.
