Paul Bocuse (11 February 1926 – 20 January 2018) was a French chef based in Lyon, widely regarded as one of the finest and most influential chefs of the 20th century. He was a leading figure of nouvelle cuisine, which emphasised lighter, fresher dishes and elegant presentation in contrast to the heavier cuisine classique. His flagship restaurant, L'Auberge du Pont de Collonges near Lyon, held three Michelin stars continuously from 1965 until 2020, an extraordinary record in French gastronomy.

Early life and training

Bocuse was born in Collonges-au-Mont-d'Or, just north of Lyon, into a family of cooks whose lineage as innkeepers and chefs stretched back generations. He began his apprenticeship during the Second World War and served in the French army, where he was wounded. After the war he trained under Eugénie Brazier—the celebrated "mother of modern French cooking"—and later worked with Fernand Point at La Pyramide in Vienne, learning alongside chefs who would define postwar French cuisine.

In 1956 Bocuse returned to his family's inn on the banks of the Saône. He won his first Michelin star in 1958, his second in 1960, and his third in 1965.

Career and legacy

During the 1970s Bocuse became the public face of nouvelle cuisine, promoting the movement internationally alongside chefs such as Michel Guérard and the Troisgros brothers. His signature dishes included truffle soup V.G.E.—a black-truffle and foie-gras soup in a puff-pastry dome created for French president Valéry Giscard d'Estaing in 1975—sea bass in a pastry crust, and Bresse chicken cooked in a bladder.

In 1987 he founded the Bocuse d'Or, a biennial international cooking competition held in Lyon that became known as the "culinary Olympics." He built a restaurant empire including brasseries in Lyon, outlets in Japan, and restaurants at Epcot in Florida. Many of his students—among them Eckart Witzigmann and numerous Meilleurs Ouvriers de France—became leading chefs in their own right.

Bocuse was named "Chef of the Century" by the Gault Millau guide and by the Culinary Institute of America. He died on 20 January 2018, in the same inn at Collonges-au-Mont-d'Or where he was born, and thousands of chefs from around the world attended his funeral at Lyon Cathedral.
