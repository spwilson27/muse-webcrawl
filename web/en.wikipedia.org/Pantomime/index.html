Pantomime

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pantomime (informally panto) is a type of musical comedy stage production designed for family entertainment, developed in England and performed mainly during the Christmas and New Year season in the United Kingdom, Ireland and other English-speaking countries. Modern pantomime generally adapts a well-known fairy tale or folk story — such as Cinderella, Aladdin, Jack and the Beanstalk, Dick Whittington or Puss in Boots — and combines songs, slapstick comedy, topical jokes and audience participation.

Conventions

Characteristic features include: the principal boy played by a young woman in breeches; the pantomime dame, an older female character played by a man in extravagant costume; audience call-and-response ("Oh no it isn't!" — "Oh yes it is!"; "He's behind you!"); a comic animal costume, such as a pantomime horse or cow; topical and local references; slapstick routines such as the slosh scene; and a walkdown finale. Celebrities from television and popular music frequently headline professional productions.

History

The name derives from the ancient Greek and Roman mime (the pantomimus), but the British form developed from the Italian commedia dell'arte and the English harlequinade of the eighteenth century, promoted by performers such as John Rich. In the nineteenth century the harlequinade shrank and the fairy-tale opening grew into the full evening's entertainment, shaped by stars such as the clown Joseph Grimaldi and music-hall performers like Dan Leno. Pantomime remains a major part of British theatre economics, with professional productions in large theatres and countless amateur productions each season.
