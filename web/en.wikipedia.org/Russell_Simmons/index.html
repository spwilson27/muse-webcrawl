Russell Wendell Simmons (born October 4, 1957) is an American entrepreneur, record executive, and author. He co-founded the hip-hop label Def Jam Recordings with Rick Rubin in 1984 and created the clothing fashion lines Phat Farm, Argyleculture, and Tantris. Simmons is widely credited with helping bring hip-hop music into the mainstream of American popular culture.

Early life

Simmons was born in the Hollis neighborhood of Queens, New York City. His father was a public school administrator and his mother a recreation director. He attended August Martin High School and briefly studied at the City College of New York, where he was introduced to hip-hop culture and began promoting parties and managing artists.

Career

In the late 1970s Simmons founded Rush Management, managing early hip-hop acts including his younger brother Joseph's group Run-DMC and Kurtis Blow. In 1984 he and Rick Rubin founded Def Jam Recordings from Rubin's NYU dorm room; the label launched the careers of LL Cool J, the Beastie Boys, Public Enemy, and many others. Simmons later expanded into television with HBO's Def Comedy Jam, film production, fashion, and financial services. His estimated net worth has been reported in the hundreds of millions of dollars.

Personal life and advocacy

A longtime practitioner of yoga and Transcendental Meditation, Simmons has written books on success, meditation, and veganism, and has been active in animal-rights and social-justice causes. In 2017, multiple women accused Simmons of sexual misconduct, which he denied; he subsequently stepped down from his companies.
