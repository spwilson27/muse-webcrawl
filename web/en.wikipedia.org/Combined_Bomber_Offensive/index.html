Combined Bomber Offensive

The Combined Bomber Offensive (CBO) was an Anglo-American strategic bombing campaign against Nazi Germany during the Second World War, agreed at the Casablanca Conference in January 1943.

Conduct

RAF Bomber Command attacked by night with area bombing while the US Eighth Air Force bombed by day, aiming for round-the-clock pressure under the Pointblank directive against the Luftwaffe and German industry. Costly unescorted raids such as Schweinfurt in 1943 forced a pause until long-range P-51 Mustang escorts arrived in 1944.

Effects

By 1944–45 the offensive, refocused on oil plants and transportation, devastated German fuel production and logistics, contributing decisively to Allied victory, at the cost of tens of thousands of aircrew and German civilian lives.
