Noodle

Noodles are a type of food made from unleavened dough which is rolled flat and cut, stretched, or extruded into long strips or strings. Noodles are a staple food in many cultures and made into a variety of shapes. The earliest written record of noodles is found in a book dated to China's Eastern Han period (25–220 CE), and a 4,000-year-old bowl of millet noodles was excavated at the Lajia site in Qinghai.

Varieties

Wheat noodles include Chinese lamian and Japanese ramen and udon; rice noodles are common across Southeast Asia; buckwheat gives soba; mung-bean starch makes glass noodles; and egg noodles appear in both Asian and European cuisines. Italian pasta is the closest Western analogue, though definitions vary by jurisdiction. Instant noodles, invented by Momofuku Ando in 1958, are pre-cooked and dried blocks sold worldwide.

Preparation

Noodles are usually boiled in water or broth and served in soups, stir-fried, or dressed with sauces and accompaniments. Dough composition, alkalinity (kansui in ramen) and cutting or pulling technique determine texture.
