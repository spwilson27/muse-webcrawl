Needleman–Wunsch algorithm

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Needleman–Wunsch algorithm is an algorithm used in bioinformatics to align protein or nucleotide sequences. It was one of the first applications of dynamic programming to biological sequence comparison, published by Saul B. Needleman and Christian D. Wunsch in 1970. It performs a global alignment: it finds the optimal alignment of two sequences along their entire lengths, under a chosen scoring scheme for matches, mismatches, and gaps.

Algorithm

The algorithm builds a matrix whose axes are the two sequences. Each cell holds the best alignment score for the prefixes ending at that position, computed from the scores above, to the left, and diagonally, modified by match/mismatch scores and gap penalties. After filling the matrix, a traceback from the final cell to the origin recovers an optimal alignment. Its running time and memory are proportional to the product of the sequence lengths.

Applications

Needleman–Wunsch alignment remains a standard teaching example and reference method in computational biology; related variants include the Smith–Waterman algorithm for local alignment and modern heuristic tools such as BLAST for database searches. See also Sequence alignment and Dynamic programming.
