Lady Macbeth

Lady Macbeth is a leading character in William Shakespeare's tragedy Macbeth (c. 1603–1607). As the wife of the play's tragic hero, Macbeth, a Scottish nobleman, Lady Macbeth goads her husband into committing regicide, after which she becomes queen of Scotland.

After Macbeth murders King Duncan, Lady Macbeth's conscience begins to torment her. She dies off-stage in the fifth act, presumably by suicide, after a famous bout of sleepwalking in which she imagines bloodstains that will not wash from her hands: "Out, damned spot! Out, I say!"

Role in the play

Lady Macbeth is introduced in Act 1, Scene 5, reading a letter from her husband describing the witches' prophecy that he will become king. Fearing Macbeth is "too full o' the milk of human kindness", she invokes spirits to "unsex me here" and persuades him to murder Duncan that night. When Macbeth hesitates, she questions his manhood and resolve. After the murder she returns the daggers to the chamber and smears the sleeping grooms with blood.

Analysis

The character has long been debated by critics — whether she is a monstrous villainess, a victim of her own ambition, or a woman destroyed by grief and guilt. Her sleepwalking scene is among the most celebrated in English drama. Notable performers of the role include Sarah Siddons, Ellen Terry, Vivien Leigh, Judi Dench and Marion Cotillard (in the 2015 film adaptation).

Historical basis

Shakespeare's character is loosely based on Gruoch, the historical wife of Macbeth, King of Scots, who appears in Holinshed's Chronicles, Shakespeare's main source.
