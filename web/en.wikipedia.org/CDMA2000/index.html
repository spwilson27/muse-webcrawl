CDMA2000

CDMA2000 (also known as C2K or IMT Multi-Carrier, IMT-MC) is a family of 3G mobile telecommunications standards based on code-division multiple access (CDMA). It was developed by Qualcomm and the 3GPP2 partnership as the successor to the 2G cdmaOne (IS-95) standard.

Standards

The family includes CDMA2000 1X (1xRTT), which doubled voice capacity over cdmaOne, and the data-optimised EV-DO (Evolution-Data Optimized) revisions, which raised peak download speeds from 2.4 Mbit/s (Revision 0) to 3.1 Mbit/s (Revision A) and beyond. CDMA2000 was recognised by the ITU as part of the IMT-2000 family of 3G standards.

Deployment

CDMA2000 networks were widely deployed in the United States (notably by Verizon and Sprint), South Korea, Japan, India and parts of Latin America and China. Most operators have since shut down their CDMA networks in favour of LTE and 5G during the early 2020s.
