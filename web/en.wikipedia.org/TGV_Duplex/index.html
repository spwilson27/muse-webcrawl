TGV Duplex

Manufacturer: Alstom
Operator: SNCF (France)
Entered service: 1995
Configuration: 2 power cars + 8 double-deck trailers
Capacity: ~510–634 seats
Maximum speed: 320 km/h (service)

The TGV Duplex is a series of French double-decker, high-speed trains operated by the SNCF as part of the TGV family. Built by Alstom, the Duplex was developed to increase capacity on busy high-speed lines — notably the LGV Sud-Est from Paris to Lyon — without lengthening trains beyond platform limits, by seating passengers on two levels.

Design

Each trainset consists of two power cars and eight double-deck passenger trailers, with a capacity roughly 45% greater than a single-deck TGV of the same length. The Duplex design has been produced in several generations, including the Réseau Duplex, the DASYE series (with asynchronous traction motors) and the Euroduplex (TGV 2N2), the latter equipped to run in several European countries and used for international services and the low-cost OUIGO brand.

Service

Duplex sets entered service in 1995 and now form one of the largest parts of the TGV fleet, running on most French high-speed routes, often in pairs coupled together. A test train derived from Duplex stock set the world rail speed record of 574.8 km/h in 2007, although that record train used specially modified equipment.
