Vada (food)

Vada is a category of savoury fried snacks originating from South India. Vadas are made from a batter or paste of legumes — most commonly pigeon pea, chickpea, black gram (urad dal) or mung bean — sometimes combined with vegetables or sago, seasoned, shaped, and deep-fried. They are eaten across India as breakfast items and snacks, typically served with coconut chutney and sambar.

Varieties

The best-known variety is the medu vada (also called ulundu vadai), a doughnut-shaped fritter made from fermented urad dal batter, crisp outside and soft inside, often flavoured with black pepper, cumin, green chillies, curry leaves and ginger. Masala vada (paruppu vadai) is made from chana dal and is denser and crunchier. Other forms include the thayir vadai (vada soaked in curd, similar to dahi vada in North India), sambar vadai, and vadas incorporating spinach, cabbage or banana flower.

Preparation and serving

The legumes are soaked, ground to a thick batter, seasoned, and shaped — medu vada is formed by making a hole through a ball of wet batter before it is slid into hot oil. Vada forms a classic part of the South Indian tiffin meal and of the combination dishes idli-vada and vada pav (in Maharashtra, where a potato vada is served in a bun). On festivals such as Pongal, vadas are prepared as offerings and shared food.
