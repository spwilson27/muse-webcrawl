Bed

A bed is a piece of furniture used as a place to sleep, rest, and relax. Most modern beds consist of a mattress resting on a bed frame or base, dressed with bedding such as sheets, blankets, duvets, and pillows. Beds vary enormously across cultures and eras, from floor mats and futons to four-poster canopy beds and adjustable hospital beds.

History

Raised beds appeared in ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, while ordinary people long slept on pallets of straw, leaves, or skins on the floor. Among the oldest known bedding is a grass-and-ash sleeping mat from Border Cave, South Africa, dated to about 200,000 years ago. Greeks and Romans dined and slept on couches; medieval Europeans developed curtained bedsteads that were among a household's most valuable possessions. The modern coil-spring mattress dates to the 19th century, followed by latex and memory-foam mattresses in the 20th.

Components and sizes

A typical bed includes a frame or foundation (such as a box spring or slatted base), a mattress (innerspring, foam, latex, air, or water), and bed linen. Standard sizes in North America include twin, full, queen, and king; the United Kingdom and Europe use different conventions. Other forms include sofa beds, bunk beds, daybeds, hammocks, waterbeds, and Murphy (fold-away) beds.

Health and culture

Adults spend roughly a third of their lives in bed, and mattress firmness, allergens such as dust mites, and bed hygiene all affect sleep quality. Beds carry strong cultural meanings—as the setting of birth, illness, intimacy, and death—and the bedchamber was historically a semi-public space for receiving guests in early modern Europe.
