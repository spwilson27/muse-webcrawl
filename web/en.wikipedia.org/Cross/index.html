Cross
Type: Geometric figure / religious symbol
Forms: Latin cross, Greek cross, saltire, ankh
Unicode: † (U+2020 variants)

A cross is a geometrical figure consisting of two lines or bars perpendicular to each other. Crosses predate Christianity and appear in many cultures, but the form is best known as the principal symbol of Christianity, recalling the crucifixion of Jesus.

Forms

The Latin cross has a longer vertical bar; the Greek cross has equal arms. Other forms include the saltire (diagonal X), the tau cross, the patriarchal cross with two horizontal bars, the Celtic cross with a circle, and the Egyptian ankh, a cross with a loop.

Uses

Beyond religion, crosses appear in heraldry (the Cross of St George, the Nordic cross on Scandinavian flags), medicine (the Red Cross emblem), mathematics (the plus sign), and as markers — wayside crosses, memorial crosses, and market crosses across Europe.
