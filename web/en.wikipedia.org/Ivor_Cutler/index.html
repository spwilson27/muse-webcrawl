Ivor Cutler (15 January 1923 – 3 March 2006) was a Scottish poet, singer and humorist, celebrated for his gentle, deadpan surrealism, delivered in a soft Glasgow voice often accompanied by a harmonium. He became a cult figure through his BBC radio appearances, dozens of recordings — many for John Peel's programmes — and his absurdist poetry collections.

Life and career

Born into a middle-class Jewish family in Govan, Glasgow, Cutler served as an RAF navigator in the Second World War and afterwards worked for many years as a schoolteacher in London, famous for unconventional lessons. His first broadcasts came in the late 1950s; he appeared in the Beatles' 1967 film Magical Mystery Tour, playing the courier Buster Bloodvessel, and on Robert Wyatt's Rock Bottom (1974). Albums such as Dandruff (1974), Velvet Donkey (1975) and Jammy Smears (1976), several made with his long-time collaborator Phyllis King, mingled songs, poems and "letters to Joyce".

Style and legacy

Cutler's work, often labelled "surreal" despite his own preference for the absurd in everyday life, ranged from the whimsical advice-songs of his Life in a Scotch Sitting Room stories to deliberately solemn notices about silence and listening. He was an inveterate communicator by postcard and sticker (distributed to strangers), disliked noise — he campaigned with the Noise Abatement Society — and performed in a distinctive uniform of plus fours and decorated caps. He retired from performing in 2004 and died in London in 2006.
