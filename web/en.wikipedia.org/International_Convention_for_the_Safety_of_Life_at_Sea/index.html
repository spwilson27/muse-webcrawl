The International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) is an international maritime treaty which sets out minimum safety standards in the construction, equipment and operation of merchant ships. The convention requires signatory flag states to ensure that ships flagged by them comply with at least these standards.

The current version, adopted on 1 November 1974, entered into force on 25 May 1980. As of recent counts, SOLAS 1974 has well over 160 contracting states, covering nearly the entire world merchant fleet by tonnage. Signatories must adhere to all amendments under the "tacit acceptance" procedure.

History

The first version of the treaty was passed in 1914 in response to the sinking of RMS Titanic two years earlier. It prescribed numbers of lifeboats and other emergency equipment along with safety procedures, including manning of radio equipment. Further versions followed in 1929 and 1948, before the 1960 convention adopted under the newly formed IMCO (now IMO), and the complete revision of 1974.

Provisions

The 1974 Convention is organised in chapters covering construction and subdivision, fire protection, life-saving appliances, radiocommunications, safety of navigation, carriage of cargoes including dangerous goods, nuclear ships, ship management (the ISM Code), high-speed craft, maritime security (including the ISPS Code), bulk carriers and polar shipping.
