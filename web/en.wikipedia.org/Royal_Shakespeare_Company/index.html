The Royal Shakespeare Theatre, Stratford-upon-Avon

The Royal Shakespeare Company (RSC) is a major British theatre company, based in Stratford-upon-Avon, the birthplace of William Shakespeare. Founded by Peter Hall in 1961, it is dedicated to performing the works of Shakespeare and his contemporaries, alongside new writing, and reaches over a million audience members a year through productions in Stratford, London, on tour and in cinemas worldwide.

History

Shakespeare has been performed in Stratford since the Shakespeare Memorial Theatre opened in 1879. In 1961 Peter Hall reorganised the company as the Royal Shakespeare Company, creating Britain's first permanent ensemble with a London base (the Aldwych Theatre) as well as Stratford, and a remit spanning classical and new work. Landmark productions include the 1963 Wars of the Roses cycle, Peter Brook's 1970 white-box A Midsummer Night's Dream, and Trevor Nunn and John Caird's 1980 Nicholas Nickleby. The Swan Theatre, a galleried Jacobean-style playhouse built inside the shell of the old Memorial Theatre, opened in 1986.

Theatres and organisation

The Royal Shakespeare Theatre was transformed between 2007 and 2010, gaining a 1,000-seat thrust-stage auditorium that brings audiences far closer to the actors. The company also performs in the Swan Theatre (430 seats) and the studio theatre The Other Place, revived in 2016. Alongside Stratford seasons, the RSC maintains a long association with Newcastle upon Tyne, tours nationally and internationally, and broadcasts productions to cinemas under the "Live from Stratford-upon-Avon" programme begun with Richard II in 2013.

Notable work and alumni

Generations of leading British actors — among them Judi Dench, Ian McKellen, Helen Mirren, Patrick Stewart and David Tennant — have been associated with the company. Later artistic directors after Hall included Trevor Nunn, Terry Hands, Adrian Noble, Michael Boyd and Gregory Doran. The RSC commissioned the musical Matilda (2010, with Tim Minchin and Dennis Kelly), one of its greatest commercial successes, and its education department works with schools across England through the Associate Schools programme.
