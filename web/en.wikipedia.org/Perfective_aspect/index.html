Perfective aspect

The perfective aspect is a grammatical aspect that describes an action viewed as a complete whole—an event bounded in time, with a beginning and an end, rather than unfolding or repeated. It contrasts with the imperfective aspect, which views a situation as ongoing, habitual or incomplete. The perfective/imperfective distinction is fundamental in the grammar of the Slavic languages, and perfective forms occur in many other language families.

In Slavic languages

In Russian, Polish, Czech and other Slavic languages, most verbs occur in aspectual pairs: an imperfective verb (e.g. Russian pisatʹ, "to write / be writing") and a perfective counterpart (napisatʹ, "to write [to completion]"), the latter often formed with prefixes or suffix changes. Perfective verbs in the present-tense form typically refer to future completed events, since a bounded event cannot be ongoing at the moment of speech.

Semantics

Perfectivity does not merely mean that an action was completed in the past; it encodes the speaker's construal of the event as a single, indivisible whole. The same real-world occurrence can be reported with either aspect depending on whether its internal duration or its boundaries are in focus.

Other languages

Ancient Greek distinguished perfective (aorist) and imperfective stems; Mandarin Chinese uses the particle le (了) as a perfective marker; and Georgian and many other languages mark the distinction morphologically. English lacks a dedicated perfective form, using the simple past for both bounded and some unbounded readings.
