Telenovela

Telenovela
GenreSerialized television melodrama
OriginLatin America (Cuba, Brazil, Mexico)
Typical length120–200 episodes, one season
Major producersTelevisa (Mexico), Globo (Brazil), Telemundo

A telenovela is a type of serialized television drama, originating in Latin America, that — unlike the open-ended soap opera — tells a self-contained story with a definite conclusion, typically over 120 to 200 daily episodes. Telenovelas blend romance and melodrama with themes of class conflict, family secrets, and rags-to-riches transformation.

Origins

The format descends from Cuban radio serials (radionovelas) and nineteenth-century serialized novels, moving to television in the 1950s in Cuba, Brazil, and Mexico. Venezuela, Colombia, and Argentina also became major producers. Mexican telenovelas from Televisa and Brazilian ones from TV Globo were exported around the world; titles such as Los ricos también lloran (1979), María la del Barrio (1995), and Colombia's Yo soy Betty, la fea (1999) — adapted in dozens of countries, including as Ugly Betty — achieved enormous international audiences.

Characteristics

Telenovelas air in prime time, target a broad family audience, and are produced on fast schedules at relatively low cost per episode. Storylines typically follow a virtuous heroine and a romantic arc complicated by villains, hidden identities, and dramatic reversals, ending in a climactic finale watched by mass audiences.

Global influence

The format has been adapted worldwide, from Philippine and Turkish dizi exports to American English-language versions. In the streaming era, producers have experimented with shorter "súper series" and narconovelas, while classic telenovelas remain a staple of Spanish- and Portuguese-language television.
