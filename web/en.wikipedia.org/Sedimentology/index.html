Sedimentology

Sedimentology encompasses the study of modern sediments such as sand, silt, and clay, and the processes that result in their formation (erosion and weathering), transport, deposition, and diagenesis. Sedimentologists apply their understanding of modern processes to interpret geologic history through observations of sedimentary rock and sedimentary structures.

Scope

The discipline studies sedimentary rocks including clastic rocks (conglomerate, sandstone, mudrock), carbonates (limestone and dolomite), and evaporites. Key tools include the description of grain size, sorting, sedimentary structures such as cross-bedding and ripple marks, and the reconstruction of depositional environments or facies.

Relation to stratigraphy

Sedimentology is closely linked to stratigraphy, which studies the relationships between rock layers. Principles such as superposition, original horizontality, and Walther's law — the idea that facies found in a conformable vertical succession also occur laterally adjacent to one another — underpin environmental interpretation.

Applications

Sedimentology is applied in petroleum and groundwater exploration, engineering geology, environmental remediation, and the reconstruction of past climates and landscapes.
