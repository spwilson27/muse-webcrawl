Muhammad Ali of Egypt

Muhammad Ali Pasha al-Mas'ud ibn Agha (1769 – 2 August 1849) was the Ottoman governor (Wāli) of Egypt from 1805 to 1848, considered the founder of modern Egypt and of the Muhammad Ali dynasty, which ruled Egypt and Sudan until 1952. At the height of his rule, he controlled Egypt, Sudan, Hejaz, the Levant and parts of Arabia and Anatolia.

Rise to power

An Albanian tobacco merchant's son from Kavala, Muhammad Ali arrived in Egypt in 1801 with an Albanian contingent of the Ottoman expedition against the French. Amid the power struggle following the French evacuation, he maneuvered himself into the governorship in 1805, and eliminated the Mamluk elite in the Citadel massacre of 1811.

Reforms and expansion

Muhammad Ali built a conscript modern army and navy with European instructors, founded schools, factories and canals, monopolized agriculture and trade, and introduced long-staple cotton cultivation. His son Ibrahim Pasha conquered Sudan (1820–24), Arabia and Syria; war with the Sultan in 1839–41 ended only with great-power intervention, which confirmed Muhammad Ali's hereditary rule over Egypt while limiting his army. He abdicated due to mental illness in 1848 and died in 1849.
