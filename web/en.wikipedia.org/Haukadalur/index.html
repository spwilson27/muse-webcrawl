Haukadalur (Icelandic: [ˈhœykaˌtaːlʏr̥], "hawk valley") is a valley in the Southern Region of Iceland, situated north of the lake Laugarvatn on the Golden Circle tourist route. It is best known for its geothermal area, which contains the famous hot springs Geysir and Strokkur.

Geothermal area

The Haukadalur geothermal field lies at the edge of the western volcanic zone of Iceland. Its springs have been documented since 1294, when an earthquake activated springs in the area. Geysir, the "Great Geysir", gave its name to all such erupting hot springs worldwide; it can hurl boiling water up to about 70 metres into the air, though its eruptions have become infrequent in modern times. Its neighbour Strokkur erupts much more reliably, every 5 to 10 minutes, to heights of 15–30 metres.

The area also contains numerous smaller hot springs, fumaroles, and mud pots, including the vivid blue pool Blesi and the springs Litli-Strokkur and Smiður. Geothermal activity in the valley has been linked to earthquakes, which can revive or alter springs.

Tourism

Haukadalur is one of the three principal stops on Iceland's Golden Circle, together with Þingvellir National Park and the Gullfoss waterfall. Visitor facilities cluster on the east side of the valley, including hotels, a campsite, and an information centre.
