Henry II of England

Henry II (5 March 1133 – 6 July 1189), also known as Henry Curtmantle, was King of England from 1154 until his death in 1189. During his reign he controlled England, Scotland, Wales, eastern Ireland, much of France (including Normandy, Anjou and Aquitaine) and, at one point, Brittany — an empire known as the Angevin Empire. Henry became involved in a long-standing dispute with Louis VII of France and endured conflicts with his family, often driven by Geoffrey, Richard and John's desire for greater authority.

Early life

Henry was the eldest son of Geoffrey Plantagenet, Count of Anjou, and Empress Matilda, the daughter of Henry I of England. He became Duke of Normandy at the age of 17 and inherited Anjou in 1151. In 1152 he married Eleanor of Aquitaine, whose vast duchy greatly expanded his domains. Following the Treaty of Winchester with King Stephen, Henry became heir to the English throne and succeeded Stephen in 1154, ending the period of civil war known as the Anarchy.

Reforms

Henry's reign saw major legal reforms that laid the foundations of the English common law: the Assize of Clarendon (1166) established trial by jury and itinerant judges, while the Assize of Arms (1181) reorganised the militia. His attempt to bring the clergy under royal law through the Constitutions of Clarendon (1164) led to a bitter conflict with Thomas Becket, Archbishop of Canterbury, whose murder in Canterbury Cathedral in 1170 shocked Christendom and forced Henry to do public penance.

Family revolts and death

Henry's sons, supported by their mother Eleanor, rebelled in the Great Revolt of 1173–74, which Henry survived. Further revolts followed in the 1180s, culminating in Richard's alliance with Philip II of France. Defeated and ill, Henry died at Chinon in 1189 and was succeeded by Richard I.
