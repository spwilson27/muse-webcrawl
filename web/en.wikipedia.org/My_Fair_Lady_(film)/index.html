My Fair Lady (film)

My Fair Lady is a 1964 American musical drama film adapted from the 1956 Lerner and Loewe stage musical of the same name, which itself was based on George Bernard Shaw's 1913 play Pygmalion. Directed by George Cukor, the film stars Audrey Hepburn as Eliza Doolittle and Rex Harrison as Professor Henry Higgins.

Directed by: George Cukor

Starring: Audrey Hepburn, Rex Harrison

Music: Frederick Loewe; lyrics by Alan Jay Lerner

Release date: 21 October 1964

Running time: 170 minutes

Plot

Phonetics professor Henry Higgins wagers that he can pass off Cockney flower girl Eliza Doolittle as a duchess by teaching her impeccable speech. After months of gruelling lessons, Eliza triumphs at the Embassy Ball, but her triumph forces both teacher and pupil to confront what has changed between them.

Production

Produced by Jack L. Warner for Warner Bros., the film retained Harrison from the original Broadway cast. Hepburn's singing was largely dubbed by Marni Nixon. Cecil Beaton designed the costumes and production, winning an Academy Award for his work.

Reception and awards

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $72 million. It won eight Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Actor for Harrison. It is preserved in the United States National Film Registry.
