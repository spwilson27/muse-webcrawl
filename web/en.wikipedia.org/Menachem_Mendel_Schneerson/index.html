Menachem Mendel Schneerson (1902–1994), known to many as the Lubavitcher Rebbe or simply the Rebbe, was a Russian-born American Orthodox rabbi and the seventh and last leader (rebbe) of the Chabad-Lubavitch Hasidic movement. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential Jewish leaders of the 20th century.

Life

Born in Nikolaev (now Mykolaiv, Ukraine), Schneerson studied in Berlin and Paris, where he took courses in engineering and philosophy, before escaping Nazi-occupied France in 1941 and settling in Crown Heights, Brooklyn. In 1951 he succeeded his father-in-law, Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, as leader of Chabad.

Leadership

From Chabad's headquarters at 770 Eastern Parkway, Schneerson built a worldwide network of emissaries (shluchim) who established thousands of Chabad centers, schools, and synagogues in more than 100 countries. He promoted outreach to non-observant Jews, public menorah lightings, and campaigns encouraging observance of commandments such as tefillin. His published talks and letters fill many volumes, and his Sunday distribution of dollar bills for charity drew thousands of visitors.

Legacy and controversy

Schneerson posthumously received the Congressional Gold Medal. A strand of his followers came to believe he was the Messiah, a claim that intensified near the end of his life and remained contentious after his death in 1994. No successor has been appointed, and his grave in Queens, the Ohel, is a major pilgrimage site.
