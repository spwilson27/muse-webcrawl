Hindu temple

A Hindu temple (mandir) is a place of worship for Hindus, designed as the house of a deity and a meeting place between humans and gods. Its architecture follows principles set out in the Vastu Shastra and Agamas.

Architecture

The core is the garbhagriha (womb chamber), a small dark sanctum housing the main image, surmounted by a tower — the shikhara in northern (Nagara) style or vimana in southern (Dravida) style, where entrance gateways called gopurams may dominate. Around it are halls (mandapas), circumambulatory paths, temple tanks and walls laid out on a mandala grid (vastu purusha mandala).

Worship

Daily puja offers light, flowers, food and water to the deity, who is treated as an honoured resident. Temples dedicated to Vishnu, Shiva and the Goddess are most common; Brahma temples are rare.

History and spread

Surviving stone temples date mainly from the Gupta period onward. Temple culture spread to Southeast Asia, producing monuments such as Angkor Wat in Cambodia and Prambanan in Central Java, dedicated to the Trimurti.
