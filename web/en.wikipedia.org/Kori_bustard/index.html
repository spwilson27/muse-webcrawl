Kori bustard

The kori bustard is among the heaviest flying birds.

OrderOtidiformes

The kori bustard (Ardeotis kori) is the largest flying bird native to Africa. It is a member of the bustard family, which are all terrestrial birds restricted in distribution to the Old World. Two subspecies are recognised.

Description

Males may weigh 7–18 kg (15–40 lb), stand up to 120 cm tall and span up to 275 cm across the wings, making them arguably the heaviest birds capable of flight, rivalling the great bustard. The plumage is cryptically coloured in greys and browns with fine black-and-white barring, aiding camouflage on the savanna.

Distribution and habitat

The nominate subspecies occurs in southern Africa (Botswana, Namibia, South Africa, Zimbabwe), while A. k. struthiunculus is found in East Africa (Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda). Kori bustards inhabit open grasslands and arid savannas, often in association with acacia trees.

Behaviour

Kori bustards are omnivorous, feeding on insects, small vertebrates, seeds and berries. During the breeding season, males perform conspicuous displays, inflating their neck feathers into a white balloon. They are reluctant fliers and spend most of their time walking on the ground.
