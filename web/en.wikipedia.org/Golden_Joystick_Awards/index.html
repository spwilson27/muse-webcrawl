The Golden Joystick Awards, also known as the People's Gaming Awards, are a British video game award ceremony held annually since 1983. They are among the longest-running video game awards in the world, and are notable because the winners are chosen by public vote.

History

The first ceremony was held in 1983 in London's Berkeley Square and organised by the magazine Computer and Video Games; the first Ultimate Game of the Year award went to Jetpac. Over the decades the awards passed through several publishers and venues, and the voting has attracted millions of ballots cast online by players around the world.

Categories

The flagship prize is Ultimate Game of the Year, alongside categories such as Best Storytelling, Best Multiplayer Game, Best Indie Game, Most Wanted Game, and platform-specific awards. The ceremony also presents a Lifetime Achievement Award, whose recipients have included Hideo Kojima and Ken and Roberta Williams.

Significance

Because they are decided entirely by fans, the Golden Joysticks are often described as the "people's" gaming awards, in contrast to jury-based ceremonies such as The Game Awards and the BAFTA Games Awards.
