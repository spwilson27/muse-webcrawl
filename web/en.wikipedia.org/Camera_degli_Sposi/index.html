Camera degli Sposi


Placeholder image: Frescoes of the Camera degli Sposi in Mantua

The Camera degli Sposi ("bridal chamber"), sometimes known as the Camera picta ("painted chamber"), is a room in the Palazzo Ducale of Mantua, Italy, frescoed by Andrea Mantegna between about 1465 and 1474 for Ludovico III Gonzaga, Marquis of Mantua.

The frescoes

The walls depict scenes from the life of the Gonzaga court. The west wall shows the Meeting Scene, in which Ludovico greets his son Cardinal Francesco Gonzaga on his return from Rome; the north wall shows the Court Scene, with Ludovico in conversation with his wife Barbara of Brandenburg, surrounded by family members and courtiers. Two other walls are painted as illusionistic marble and open loggias.

The ceiling is among the most famous examples of Renaissance quadratura: an illusionistic balustrade opens onto a sky with putti and onlookers peering down into the room, painted with extreme foreshortening (di sotto in su). The room is considered one of the masterpieces of early Renaissance court art.
