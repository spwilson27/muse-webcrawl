Güyük Khan

Güyük Khan
Born: c. 1206, Mongol Empire
Died: 20 April 1248
Reign: 1246–1248
Predecessor: Ögedei Khan
Successor: Möngke Khan

Güyük Khan or Kuyuk (c. 1206 – 20 April 1248) was the third Khagan of the Mongol Empire, the eldest son of Ögedei Khan and grandson of Genghis Khan. He reigned from 1246 until his death in 1248.

Accession

After Ögedei's death in 1241, a long regency under Güyük's mother Töregene Khatun delayed the election. Güyük, who had campaigned in Europe and quarrelled with Batu Khan during the western campaign, was finally enthroned at a kurultai in 1246 attended by foreign envoys including the Franciscan friar Giovanni da Pian del Carpine, sent by Pope Innocent IV. Güyük's reply to the Pope, demanding the submission of the Christian princes, survives as a famous letter written in Persian.

Reign and death

Güyük worked to purge his mother's officials, centralised administration, and prepared for a confrontation with Batu. He died in 1248 in Qum-Senggir in Xinjiang while marching west, possibly from illness aggravated by alcoholism, though poisoning was suspected. His widow Oghul Qaimish served as regent until the election of Möngke Khan in 1251 shifted power to the Toluid line.
