Anak Krakatau ("Child of Krakatoa") is an island volcano in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra in Indonesia. It emerged from the caldera formed by the catastrophic 1883 eruption of Krakatoa, which destroyed the original island.

Growth

Submarine eruptions began in 1927, and the new island broke the surface in 1930. Periodic eruptions grew the cone steadily, at times adding several metres of height per year, and the island served as a natural laboratory for the study of ecological colonization of new volcanic land.

2018 eruption and tsunami

On 22 December 2018, an eruption caused a flank collapse of the volcano: roughly two-thirds of its height slid into the sea, triggering a tsunami that struck the coasts of Banten and Lampung and killed more than 400 people. The volcano's height fell from about 338 metres to about 110 metres, and eruptive activity has continued intermittently since.

Monitoring

The volcano is monitored by Indonesia's Center for Volcanology and Geological Hazard Mitigation (PVMBG).
