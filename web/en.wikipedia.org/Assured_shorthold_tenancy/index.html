Assured shorthold tenancy

JurisdictionEngland (historically England and Wales)
IntroducedHousing Act 1988; extended by Housing Act 1996
Typical fixed term6–12 months

An assured shorthold tenancy (AST) is the most common type of residential tenancy agreement in England's private rented sector. Introduced by the Housing Act 1988 and made the default tenancy for new private lettings by the Housing Act 1996, it gives the tenant security for a fixed term while allowing the landlord to recover possession more readily than under a full assured tenancy.

Features

An AST is usually granted for an initial fixed term of six or twelve months, after which it typically continues as a periodic tenancy. During and after the term, a landlord may seek possession using the accelerated "Section 21" no-fault eviction procedure (subject to statutory notice periods and compliance requirements), or a "Section 8" notice where grounds such as rent arrears exist. Tenants' deposits under an AST must be protected in a government-backed tenancy deposit scheme.

Reform

The insecurity associated with no-fault evictions has been a longstanding subject of policy debate. Reform proposals to abolish Section 21 and move private tenancies toward open-ended, assured periodic arrangements were pursued in the 2020s through the Renters' Reform Bill and the subsequent Renters' Rights Bill, which proposed replacing ASTs with a new tenancy regime in England.
