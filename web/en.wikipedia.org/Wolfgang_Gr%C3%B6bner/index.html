Wolfgang Gröbner (2 February 1899 – 20 August 1980) was an Austrian mathematician whose name is best known for the Gröbner basis, used in computer algebra and computational algebraic geometry. Gröbner bases were introduced in his 1965 doctoral advising of his student Bruno Buchberger, who named the concept after his advisor.

Life

Gröbner was born in Gossensaß (now in South Tyrol, Italy) and studied engineering at the Technical University of Graz before turning to mathematics. He worked at the University of Vienna and, after the Second World War, held posts in Rome and at the University of Innsbruck, where he spent the remainder of his career.

Work

His research concerned Lie algebras, algebraic geometry, and polynomial ideals. The algorithmic theory of Gröbner bases developed by Buchberger in 1965 became a cornerstone of computer algebra systems.
