Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor

Presented byAcademy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS)
First awarded1937 (for 1936 films)
First winnerWalter Brennan, Come and Get It
Most winsWalter Brennan (3)

The Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences to honour an actor who has delivered an outstanding performance in a supporting role in a film. It was first presented at the 9th Academy Awards in 1937, honouring films of 1936; before that, supporting performances competed against leading ones.

History

Walter Brennan was the first recipient, for Come and Get It, and went on to win three times in the category's first five years — still the record. Early winners received plaques rather than statuettes until 1944. The youngest winner is Timothy Hutton, aged 20, for Ordinary People (1980); the oldest is Christopher Plummer, who won at 82 for Beginners (2011) and was nominated again at 88. Heath Ledger won posthumously for playing the Joker in The Dark Knight (2008), only the second posthumous acting Oscar.

Selection

Nominees are chosen by the actors' branch of the Academy, and the winner is selected by the full voting membership. Since the 5th ceremony, five performances are nominated each year. The award is conventionally presented by the previous year's winner of the corresponding actress category.
