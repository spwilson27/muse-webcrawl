Homozygous

HomozygousField: genetics
Related terms: heterozygous, hemizygous
Notation: AA (dominant) or aa (recessive)

Homozygous describes a genotype in which an individual carries two identical alleles of a particular gene, one inherited from each parent. By contrast, a heterozygous individual carries two different alleles at that locus.

Types

An organism may be homozygous dominant (two copies of the dominant allele, e.g. AA) or homozygous recessive (two copies of the recessive allele, aa). Recessive traits and many recessive genetic disorders are expressed only in homozygotes (or compound heterozygotes), whereas dominant traits appear in both homozygotes and heterozygotes.

Significance

Homozygosity increases with inbreeding, exposing deleterious recessive alleles and reducing genetic diversity — a concern in conservation genetics and animal breeding. Conversely, plant and animal breeders exploit homozygous "true-breeding" lines for uniform offspring. At the molecular level, stretches of homozygosity in the genome (runs of homozygosity) are used to study ancestry and consanguinity. In twin studies and knockout experiments, animals are routinely made homozygous for engineered alleles.
