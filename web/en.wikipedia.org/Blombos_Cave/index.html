Blombos Cave
LocationBlombos Private Nature Reserve, near Still Bay, Western Cape, South Africa

PeriodsMiddle Stone Age, c. 100,000–70,000 BP; Later Stone Age

Blombos Cave is an archaeological site located about 300 km east of Cape Town, on the Southern Cape coastline of South Africa. The cave contains Middle Stone Age (MSA) deposits dated to between about 100,000 and 70,000 years ago, and Later Stone Age deposits from 2,000 to 300 years ago. It is famous for its evidence of early symbolic behaviour by Homo sapiens.

Archaeological finds

Excavations led by Christopher Henshilwood from 1991 uncovered engraved ochre pieces about 75,000 years old, including a fragment bearing a cross-hatched pattern deliberately drawn with an ochre crayon, announced in 2018 as the earliest known drawing. The cave also yielded perforated Nassarius kraussianus shell beads, finely made bifacial Still Bay points, bone tools, and a 100,000-year-old ochre-processing workshop consisting of abalone shells holding a liquefied pigment mixture.

Significance

The Blombos finds pushed back the earliest widely accepted evidence of symbolic culture by tens of thousands of years, supporting the view that modern human cognition evolved gradually in Africa rather than appearing suddenly in Europe during the Upper Palaeolithic "human revolution".
