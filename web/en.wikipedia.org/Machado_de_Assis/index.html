Machado de Assis

Joaquim Maria Machado de Assis (21 June 1839 – 29 September 1908) was a Brazilian novelist, poet, playwright, and short story writer, widely regarded as the greatest writer of Brazilian literature and one of the major figures of world literature. He founded the Brazilian Academy of Letters and served as its first president.

Life

Born in Rio de Janeiro to a poor family — his father was a painter of walls and his mother a washerwoman — Machado was largely self-educated. He worked as a typographer and journalist, gradually rising in the literary world despite the racial and social prejudices of imperial Brazil. He married Carolina Augusta Xavier de Novais in 1869, a partnership that lasted until her death in 1904.

Work

Machado's career is usually divided into two phases. His early work, including the novel Helena (1876), belongs to a romantic mode. The turning point came with The Posthumous Memoirs of Brás Cubas (1881), a radically innovative novel narrated by a "defunct author" from beyond the grave, which inaugurated his mature realist phase. Other major works include Quincas Borba (1891), Dom Casmurro (1899) — famous for its unreliable narrator and the unresolved question of Capitu's fidelity — and masterful collections of short stories such as Papéis Avulsos (1882), which contains "The Alienist".

Legacy

Machado's ironic, psychologically penetrating style influenced generations of writers in Latin America and beyond. Critics such as Harold Bloom have placed him among the canonical authors of Western literature.
