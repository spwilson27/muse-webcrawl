Archibald Leman Cochrane (1909–1988) was a Scottish doctor and epidemiologist widely regarded as the father of evidence-based medicine. He served as a medical officer in the Spanish Civil War and, as a prisoner of war in the Second World War, conducted some of the earliest controlled clinical experiments.

Career

After the war Cochrane worked with the Medical Research Council in the Rhondda Fach in South Wales, studying pneumoconiosis in coal miners. His 1972 book Effectiveness and Efficiency: Random Reflections on Health Services argued that healthcare interventions should be proven effective through randomized controlled trials before being adopted.

Legacy

His call for systematic summaries of all randomized trials led to the founding of the Cochrane Collaboration (now Cochrane) in 1993, the world's largest organization producing systematic reviews of medical interventions.
