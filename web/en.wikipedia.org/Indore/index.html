Indore

Indore is the largest and most populous city in the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh, located on the Malwa plateau about 190 km west of the state capital Bhopal. With a city population of over two million, it is a major commercial, industrial, and educational hub of central India. Indore has been ranked India's cleanest city in the national Swachh Survekshan survey for several consecutive years.

History

Indore grew as a trading post on the route between the Deccan and Delhi. In the 18th century it became the seat of the Holkar dynasty of the Maratha Empire; its most famous ruler, Ahilyabai Holkar (r. 1767–1795), is remembered for just administration and for building temples and charitable works across India. The Holkars' Rajwada palace remains a landmark of the old city. After the Third Anglo-Maratha War, Indore became the capital of the princely state of Indore under British paramountcy, merging into Madhya Bharat and then Madhya Pradesh after independence.

Economy and culture

Historically a cotton-textile centre, Indore now has significant pharmaceutical, automobile, and information-technology sectors, with nearby Pithampur one of India's largest industrial areas. The city is unique in hosting both an Indian Institute of Technology (IIT Indore) and an Indian Institute of Management (IIM Indore). Indore is famed for its street food, especially around the Sarafa Bazaar night market and the Chappan Dukan ("56 shops") food street, with specialities such as poha-jalebi, bhutte ka kees, and garadu.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Rajwada, the Lal Bagh Palace of the later Holkars, the Kanch Mandir (a Jain temple inlaid with glass), and the Annapurna Temple. The city serves as a base for visiting the historic town of Mandu and the pilgrimage cities of Ujjain and Omkareshwar.
