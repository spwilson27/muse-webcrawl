2019 redefinition of the SI base units

The 2019 redefinition of the SI base units took effect on 20 May 2019, World Metrology Day, following adoption by the 26th General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) in November 2018. The redefinition redefined the kilogram, ampere, kelvin and mole in terms of fixed numerical values of fundamental physical constants.

The new definitions

Under the revised system, the SI is defined by fixing the numerical values of seven defining constants: the caesium hyperfine frequency, the speed of light, the Planck constant, the elementary charge, the Boltzmann constant, the Avogadro constant, and the luminous efficacy of monochromatic radiation of frequency 540 THz.

Changes to units

The kilogram is now defined via the Planck constant instead of the International Prototype of the Kilogram, a platinum-iridium artefact kept in France. The ampere is defined via the elementary charge, the kelvin via the Boltzmann constant, and the mole via the Avogadro constant.

Rationale

The change removed the last SI unit defined by a physical artefact, making all units realisable anywhere from invariant constants of nature and improving long-term stability and reproducibility of measurements.
