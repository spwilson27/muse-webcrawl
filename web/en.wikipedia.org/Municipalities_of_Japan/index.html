Municipalities of Japan

CountryJapan
TypesCities (shi), towns (machi/chō), villages (mura/son), special wards (ku)
Number1,718 (plus 23 special wards of Tokyo)

The municipalities of Japan are the second level of government below the 47 prefectures. Under the Local Autonomy Law they comprise cities (shi), towns (machi or chō) and villages (mura or son), together with the 23 special wards (tokubetsu-ku) of Tokyo, which function as municipalities.

Structure

Each municipality has a directly elected mayor and assembly. Cities of more than 500,000 may be designated by government ordinance (seirei shitei toshi), gaining ward subdivisions and broader powers; core cities and special cities have intermediate status. Towns and villages are grouped into rural districts (gun), which have no administrative function today.

Mergers

Successive merger waves — the Meiji, Shōwa and Heisei mergers — reduced the number of municipalities from over 70,000 in the 1880s to roughly 1,700, aiming to improve administrative efficiency and fiscal capacity.
