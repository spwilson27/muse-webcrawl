Front de libération du Québec

The Front de libération du Québec (FLQ) was a militant Quebec separatist group active in Canada during the 1960s and early 1970s. Founded in 1963, the FLQ sought the independence of Quebec from Canada through violent means, carrying out a campaign of bombings, robberies and kidnappings that culminated in the October Crisis of 1970.

History

The FLQ emerged amid the Quiet Revolution, a period of rapid social and political change in Quebec. Its members, drawn largely from young francophone nationalists, believed that Quebec's French-speaking majority was economically and culturally subordinated within Canada. Between 1963 and 1970, FLQ cells planted more than 200 bombs, targeting symbols of anglophone economic power, federal institutions and military installations. Several people were killed, including a night watchman at a Montreal shoe factory and stockbroker Walter LeMay.

October Crisis

In October 1970, an FLQ cell kidnapped British trade commissioner James Cross, and a second cell kidnapped Quebec labour minister Pierre Laporte. Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau responded by invoking the War Measures Act, suspending civil liberties and deploying troops in Quebec and Ottawa; hundreds of people were arrested. Laporte was found murdered, while Cross was released after negotiations and his captors were exiled to Cuba. The crisis effectively destroyed the FLQ as an organization.

Legacy

The FLQ's violence was widely condemned, and the mainstream Quebec sovereignty movement pursued independence through democratic politics, notably through the Parti Québécois. The October Crisis remains a defining and controversial episode in modern Canadian history, frequently debated for the federal government's use of emergency powers.
