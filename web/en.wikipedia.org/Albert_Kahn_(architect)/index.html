Albert Kahn (architect)

Albert Kahn (March 21, 1869 – December 8, 1942) was an American architect known as the "architect of Detroit". He designed industrial plant complexes, commercial buildings, and residences, and his firm Albert Kahn Associates became one of the most influential architectural firms of the 20th century.

Albert Kahn
Born: March 21, 1869, Rhaunen, Prussia
Died: December 8, 1942, Detroit, Michigan
Occupation: Architect
Practice: Albert Kahn Associates

Career

Kahn was born in Rhaunen, Kingdom of Prussia, and emigrated with his family to Detroit in 1880. Largely self-taught, he joined the firm of Mason and Rice in 1884. He is credited with developing reinforced concrete industrial buildings, notably for Henry Ford's automobile factories, including the Highland Park Ford Plant (1909) and the Ford River Rouge Complex (1917 onwards). His factories were characterized by large, open, well-lit floor spaces made possible by reinforced concrete construction.

Notable works

Among Kahn's many commissions are the Fisher Building, the General Motors Building, the Packard Automotive Plant, numerous buildings at the University of Michigan (including Hill Auditorium and the Burton Memorial Tower), and more than 500 factories built in the Soviet Union during its first Five-Year Plan. His firm designed over 2,000 buildings by the time of his death.
