Homer and Langley

Homer & Langley is a 2009 historical novel by the American writer E. L. Doctorow. It is a fictionalised account of the lives of Homer and Langley Collyer, the reclusive New York brothers whose Harlem mansion, crammed with tons of accumulated possessions, became notorious when both were found dead in it in 1947. Narrated by Homer, the blind brother, the novel stretches their lives across the whole of the 20th century and beyond — departing substantially from the historical record to make the brothers witnesses to a hundred years of American history.

Background

The real Homer (1881–1947) and Langley (1885–1947) Collyer were sons of a prominent Manhattan physician. After their parents' deaths they withdrew into their brownstone at 2078 Fifth Avenue, where Langley — an engineer and pianist — accumulated pianos, cars, medical equipment, and some 14 tons of newspapers he claimed to be saving for Homer's eventual recovery of sight. In March 1947 police, responding to reports of a smell, found Homer dead in the cluttered house; Langley's body was discovered weeks later only metres away, crushed in one of his own booby-trapped tunnels of junk. The brothers became the archetypal case in the study of hoarding, and "Collyer brothers syndrome" entered informal usage.

Plot and departures from fact

Doctorow's novel retells the story in Homer's voice, with Homer composing his memoir on a Braille typewriter and later dictating and using assistive technology as decades pass. Major liberties include reversing the brothers' traits (the novel's Langley returns shell-shocked from the First World War and propounds a theory of history as cyclical "replacements"), extending their lives far past 1947 — through the Second World War, the hippie era (the brothers briefly host a commune), the Moon landing, organised-crime intimidation, and into the age of personal computers — and giving them a long-serving cook and a succession of vividly drawn visitors. The historical mansion's contents become a museum of the century as the brothers retreat ever deeper into it.

Themes and reception

Reviewing the novel widely and favourably, critics praised Doctorow's ventriloquism and the book's meditation on accumulation and memory: the brothers' hoard functions as both pathology and archive, a private counter-history of a century defined by disposable goods. Homer & Langley was among Doctorow's last novels — he died in 2015 — and is generally ranked with his celebrated historical fictions Ragtime (1975) and Billy Bathgate (1989), which similarly blended real figures with invented narration.
