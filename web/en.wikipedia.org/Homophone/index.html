Homophone

Homophone
DefinitionWords pronounced the same with different meanings
Examplesto / too / two; there / their / they're
Related termsHomograph, homonym, heteronym

A homophone is a word that is pronounced the same as another word but differs in meaning, and often in spelling. The word derives from Greek homos (same) and phōnḗ (voice, sound). Examples in English include flour and flower, night and knight, and right, rite, write and wright.

Related concepts

Homophones contrast with homographs, which share spelling but not necessarily pronunciation (lead the metal and lead the verb, the latter pair being heteronyms), and with homonyms, a broader category sometimes used for words sharing both spelling and sound but not meaning. Whether words count as homophones can depend on dialect: in accents with the cot–caught merger, or with wine–whine distinctions lost, sets of homophones differ.

In language and culture

Homophones are a rich source of puns and wordplay, a frequent cause of spelling errors and confusion for learners, and a central difficulty for speech-recognition software. In tonal languages such as Mandarin Chinese, homophony is extensive and is exploited in poetry, naming taboos and internet slang substitutions.
