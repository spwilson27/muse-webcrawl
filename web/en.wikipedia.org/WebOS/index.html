webOS, also known as LG webOS and previously as Palm webOS, is a Linux-based multitasking operating system originally developed by Palm, Inc. for smartphones. It was launched in 2009 on the Palm Pre and was acclaimed for its intuitive card-based multitasking interface, gesture navigation and "Synergy" integration of contacts and calendars from online services. Applications were written with web technologies — HTML, CSS and JavaScript — hence the name.

History

Palm introduced webOS in January 2009 at CES as the successor to Palm OS. Devices included the Pre, Pixi, Veer and the Pre 3. Hewlett-Packard acquired Palm in 2010 for $1.2 billion and launched the webOS-based TouchPad tablet in 2011, but discontinued all webOS hardware just weeks later amid poor sales. HP subsequently open-sourced the system as Open webOS. In 2013 LG Electronics acquired the webOS source code and patents from HP for use in smart televisions.

LG era

Since 2014, LG has shipped webOS on its smart TVs, where its card-style launcher and Magic Remote pointer interface have been well received; the platform has since spread to projectors, signage and smart appliances, including refrigerators. In 2021 LG began licensing webOS TV software to other television brands.

Influence

webOS pioneered features later standard in mobile operating systems, including card-based app switching, gesture areas replacing buttons, wireless charging (the Touchstone dock), and unified notifications — a legacy that outlived its brief life as a phone platform.
