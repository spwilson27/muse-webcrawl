Persian Gulf

The Persian Gulf is a mediterranean sea in Western Asia. It is an extension of the Indian Ocean located between Iran and the Arabian Peninsula, connected to the Gulf of Oman in the east by the Strait of Hormuz. The Shatt al-Arab river delta forms its northwestern shoreline.

Geography

The Persian Gulf is about 989 kilometres (615 mi) long, with the narrow Strait of Hormuz at its eastern end connecting it to the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea. The countries surrounding it are Iran to the north and east, and Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, and Oman to the south and west. The gulf is relatively shallow, with a maximum depth of about 90 metres.

Oil and gas

The Persian Gulf and its coastal areas are among the world's largest sources of crude oil, and related industries dominate the region. Safaniya Oil Field, the world's largest offshore oilfield, is located in the Persian Gulf. Large gas finds have also been made, with Qatar and Iran sharing the South Pars / North Dome gas-field, the world's largest gas field.
