International waters

Also calledThe high seas
Legal frameworkUN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS, 1982)
BoundaryBeyond the exclusive economic zone, i.e. more than 200 nautical miles from baselines (where applicable)
Governing principleFreedom of the seas (mare liberum)
JurisdictionGenerally the flag state of a vessel

The terms international waters or high seas refer to areas of the ocean that are not included in the territorial sea or internal waters of any state, nor in any state's exclusive economic zone (EEZ). No state may validly subject any part of the high seas to its sovereignty; they are open to all states under the principle of freedom of the seas.

Maritime zones under UNCLOS: the high seas begin beyond the exclusive economic zone.

Maritime zones

Under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, the ocean is divided into zones measured from coastal baselines: internal waters landward of the baseline; the territorial sea extending 12 nautical miles, where the coastal state is sovereign subject to innocent passage; the contiguous zone to 24 nautical miles, where limited enforcement is allowed; and the exclusive economic zone to 200 nautical miles, where the coastal state has sovereign rights over resources but other states retain freedoms of navigation and overflight. Waters beyond the EEZ — or beyond the territorial sea where no EEZ exists — are the high seas.

Freedoms of the high seas

Article 87 of UNCLOS lists the freedoms of the high seas: navigation, overflight, laying of submarine cables and pipelines, construction of artificial islands permitted under international law, fishing, and scientific research. Ships on the high seas are generally subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of their flag state. Exceptions include universal jurisdiction over piracy, rights of visit for suspected slave trading or stateless vessels, and hot pursuit begun in coastal zones.

Governance issues

The high seas cover nearly half the planet's surface and most of its ocean volume. Weak governance has been associated with illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing, and with environmental harm. In March 2023 states agreed the High Seas Treaty (the BBNJ Agreement) under UNCLOS to provide for marine protected areas and environmental impact assessment in areas beyond national jurisdiction; the treaty required 60 ratifications to enter into force. Deep seabed mineral resources beyond national jurisdiction ("the Area") are administered by the International Seabed Authority under the principle of the common heritage of mankind.
