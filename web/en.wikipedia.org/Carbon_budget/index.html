Carbon budget

A carbon budget is a concept used in climate policy to estimate the maximum amount of cumulative carbon dioxide emissions that can be emitted while keeping global warming below a given temperature limit with a given probability. Because peak warming is roughly proportional to cumulative CO2 emissions, the budget translates a temperature target into a finite total quantity of emissions.

Estimates

The IPCC's Sixth Assessment Report estimated that, from the start of 2020, the remaining carbon budget for limiting warming to 1.5 °C with a 50% likelihood was about 500 gigatonnes of CO2, and correspondingly larger budgets for 1.7 °C and 2 °C. At current annual emissions of roughly 40 GtCO2, the 1.5 °C budget would be exhausted within about a decade without rapid reductions.

Policy use

Carbon budgets inform national legislation — the United Kingdom, for example, sets statutory five-year carbon budgets under the Climate Change Act 2008 — and corporate and financial net-zero planning.
