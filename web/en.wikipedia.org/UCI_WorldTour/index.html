UCI WorldTour

The UCI WorldTour is the premier annual male elite road cycling tour, administered by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI). It comprises a series of races held across Europe, North America, Asia, and Australia, including the three Grand Tours, the five Monument one-day classics, and most of the sport's other leading stage races and one-day events.

Structure

WorldTour races are contested by UCI WorldTeams — the top tier of professional teams, licensed by the UCI and obliged to participate in every WorldTour event — together with invited UCI ProTeams. Riders and teams accumulate points in the UCI world rankings based on results. The series was launched in 2011 as the successor to the UCI ProTour (2005–2010) and the UCI World Ranking system of 2009–2010, unifying them under a single calendar of roughly 35 events running from January's Tour Down Under in Australia to autumn races in Italy, Canada, and China.

The calendar's centerpiece events are the Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, and Vuelta a España — and the Monuments: Milan–San Remo, the Tour of Flanders, Paris–Roubaix, Liège–Bastogne–Liège, and Il Lombardia. A parallel women's series, the UCI Women's WorldTour, was established in 2016.

History

The WorldTour grew out of a long-running dispute between the UCI and the organizers of the Grand Tours, notably ASO, over team selection and television revenues. The 2011 merger of the ProTour calendar with the "historical" events of the major organizers settled the conflict, and subsequent reforms introduced promotion and relegation between WorldTeams and ProTeams based on ranking points over three-year cycles.
