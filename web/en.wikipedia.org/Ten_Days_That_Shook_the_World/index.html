AuthorJohn Reed
Published1919
SubjectOctober Revolution

Ten Days That Shook the World (1919) is a book by the American journalist and socialist John Reed about the October Revolution in Russia in 1917, which Reed experienced firsthand. The book is a landmark of first-person revolutionary reportage.

Contents

Reed describes the fall of the Provisional Government, the Bolshevik seizure of power in Petrograd, and the debates of the Second Congress of Soviets, drawing on speeches, proclamations, and his own interviews with participants including Lenin and Trotsky.

Reception and legacy

The book was praised by Lenin, who wrote an introduction to it. It was later suppressed during the Stalin era because of its favorable portrayal of Trotsky. Parts of the book were adapted in the films October (1927) and Reds (1981). Reed died in Moscow in 1920 and is buried in the Kremlin Wall Necropolis.
