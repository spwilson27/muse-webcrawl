Manfred von Richthofen

Born: 2 May 1892, Breslau, German Empire
Died: 21 April 1918 (aged 25), near Vaux-sur-Somme, France
Nickname: The Red Baron
Victories: 80 confirmed aerial victories

Manfred Albrecht Freiherr von Richthofen (2 May 1892 – 21 April 1918), known in English as the Red Baron, was a German fighter pilot with the Imperial German Army Air Service during the First World War. He is considered the ace-of-aces of the war, being officially credited with 80 air combat victories — more than any other pilot.

Career

Originally a cavalry officer, Richthofen transferred to the air service in 1915 and was mentored by Oswald Boelcke in the fighter squadron Jasta 2. He took command of Jasta 11 in January 1917 and later of the first German fighter wing, Jagdgeschwader I, the "Flying Circus". His aircraft — most famously a red Albatros and later a Fokker Dr.I triplane — were painted bright red, making him a feared and recognised opponent; he was awarded the Pour le Mérite, Imperial Germany's highest military honour, and became a national hero.

Death and legacy

Richthofen was shot down and killed near Vaux-sur-Somme on 21 April 1918, aged 25. Debate continues over whether he was killed by Canadian pilot Roy Brown in aerial combat or by anti-aircraft fire from the ground; medical evidence suggests a ground-fired bullet was most likely. He was buried with full military honours by the Australians who recovered his body. His autobiography and carefully cultivated image made him an enduring popular-culture figure, from music-hall songs to the Peanuts comic strip's Snoopy fantasies.
