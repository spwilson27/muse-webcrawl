Aspartic acid

SymbolAsp, D
FormulaC4H7NO4
ClassificationAcidic, non-essential amino acid
CodonsGAU, GAC

Aspartic acid (symbol Asp or D) is an α-amino acid used in the biosynthesis of proteins. It contains an α-amino group, an α-carboxylic acid group, and a side-chain carboxylic acid, making it an acidic, polar amino acid. It is non-essential in humans, meaning the body can synthesise it.

Biological roles

Aspartate, the ionised form of aspartic acid, functions as an excitatory neurotransmitter in the central nervous system, though less prominently than glutamate. It participates in the urea cycle and in gluconeogenesis, and serves as a precursor in the biosynthesis of several other amino acids, including methionine, threonine, isoleucine, and lysine in plants and microorganisms.

Industrial production

Aspartic acid is produced commercially, largely by enzymatic conversion of fumaric acid and ammonia using aspartase, for use in the manufacture of the sweetener aspartame and various biodegradable polymers.
