Paris Peace Treaties, 1947

The Paris Peace Treaties were signed on 10 February 1947 following the end of World War II in 1945. The peace treaties were designed at the Paris Peace Conference held between 29 July and 15 October 1946 and concluded the formal state of war between the Allies and the former Axis satellites: Italy, Romania, Hungary, Bulgaria, and Finland.

Background

During the Second World War, Italy, Romania, Hungary, Bulgaria, and Finland had fought alongside Nazi Germany, though several changed sides as the war turned. By 1944–45, armistices had been concluded with each of them, and the Council of Foreign Ministers of the United States, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, and France was charged with drafting the peace settlements. The resulting conference in Paris in 1946 brought together 21 nations, but the real decisions were taken by the great powers.

Terms

Italy

Italy lost its colonial empire in Africa (Libya, Eritrea, and Italian Somaliland) and its concessions in China. Istria, most of the Julian March, the island of Lastovo, and the city of Zadar passed to Yugoslavia, while the Dodecanese islands went to Greece. Trieste became the Free Territory of Trieste under the United Nations. Italy was required to pay reparations amounting to some $360 million, shared among Yugoslavia, Greece, the Soviet Union, Ethiopia, and Albania, and its armed forces were restricted in size.

Romania

Romania regained Northern Transylvania from Hungary, reversing the Second Vienna Award, but confirmed the loss of Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina to the Soviet Union and Southern Dobruja to Bulgaria. Reparations of $300 million were owed to the Soviet Union.

Hungary

Hungary was reduced to its frontiers of 1937, losing the territories gained under the Vienna Awards, and additionally ceded a small bridgehead opposite Bratislava to Czechoslovakia. It owed $300 million in reparations, principally to the Soviet Union.

Bulgaria

Bulgaria was the only former Axis satellite to emerge with more territory than it held before the war, retaining Southern Dobruja, but it lost its Aegean coastline claims and owed reparations to Greece and Yugoslavia.

Finland

Finland confirmed the cession of Karelia and other territories to the Soviet Union, ceded the Petsamo area, and leased the Porkkala peninsula to the Soviets for fifty years (it was returned in 1956). Finland paid $300 million in reparations and accepted limits on its military.

Consequences

The treaties, which entered into force on 15 September 1947, formally closed the wartime chapter for the five states and allowed them to join the United Nations. No peace treaty was concluded with Germany itself, because of the emerging division between East and West; Germany's final settlement came only with the Treaty on the Final Settlement in 1990. The Paris settlements left several disputes unresolved, and many of their territorial provisions were later overtaken by the politics of the Cold War.
