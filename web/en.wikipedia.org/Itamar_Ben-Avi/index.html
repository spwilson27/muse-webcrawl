Itamar Ben-Avi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Itamar Ben-Avi (Hebrew: איתמר בן־אב״י; 31 July 1882 – 8 April 1943) was the first native speaker of modern Hebrew, the son of Eliezer Ben-Yehuda. Born Ben-Zion Ben-Yehuda in Jerusalem, he was raised in a household where only Hebrew was spoken, as part of his father's project to revive the Hebrew language as a living vernacular.

Life

Ben-Avi grew up largely isolated from other children because his father forbade him to hear any language other than Hebrew. He later studied in Europe and became a journalist and Zionist activist, working on Hebrew-language newspapers in Jerusalem and promoting the revival of Hebrew throughout his life. He adopted the name Ben-Avi ("son of my father") as a pen name and later as his legal name.

Legacy

As the first child raised speaking modern Hebrew natively, Ben-Avi became a symbol of the Hebrew revival. His life story is recounted in his memoir and in numerous works about the Ben-Yehuda family.
