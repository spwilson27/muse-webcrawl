The Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople is one of the fifteen to seventeen autocephalous churches (or "jurisdictions") that together compose the Eastern Orthodox Church. It is headed by the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople, currently Bartholomew I, who is regarded as primus inter pares ("first among equals") within Orthodoxy and as the spiritual leader of roughly 300 million Orthodox Christians worldwide.

The Church of St. George, seat of the Patriarchate

History

The see traces its foundation to Andrew the Apostle, said to have founded the church at Byzantium. Constantinople rose in importance as the capital of the Roman (Byzantine) Empire; the Second Ecumenical Council (381) granted its bishop precedence of honour after Rome, and the Council of Chalcedon (451) confirmed broad jurisdiction. After the East–West Schism of 1054, the Patriarchate remained the leading church of Greek Orthodoxy. Following the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople in 1453, the Patriarch functioned as ethnarch of the Orthodox population under the millet system.

Modern situation

The Patriarchate is based at the Church of St. George in the Fener district of Istanbul, Turkey. Its direct flock in Turkey is small, but it exercises jurisdiction over Mount Athos, much of Greece's semi-autonomous church territories, and most Orthodox diaspora eparchies in the Americas, Western Europe, Australia, and Asia. In 2019 the Patriarchate granted autocephaly (a tomos) to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, a decision disputed by the Moscow Patriarchate and a major factor in the subsequent Moscow–Constantinople schism. The Turkish state officially recognises the Patriarchate only as the religious institution of Istanbul's Greek minority, a status the Patriarchate disputes.
