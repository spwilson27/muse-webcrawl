Sealift

Sealift is the transport of military equipment, supplies and personnel by sea. It is the principal means by which large armed forces deploy and sustain operations overseas, because ships can move far greater tonnage than aircraft, albeit more slowly. Sealift is the maritime counterpart of strategic airlift.

Organisation

In the United States, sealift is managed by the Military Sealift Command of the Navy, which operates government-owned and chartered cargo ships, including large roll-on/roll-off vehicle carriers and prepositioning ships stationed near potential trouble spots. A reserve fleet of ships is maintained in reduced readiness for activation in emergencies.

Role in operations

During the 1990–91 Gulf War, the great majority of coalition equipment and supplies reached the theatre by sea. Sealift remains essential to logistics planning: airlift delivers the first forces quickly, while sealift delivers the heavy equipment and bulk fuel and ammunition needed to sustain them.
