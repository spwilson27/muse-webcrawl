VTOL

A vertical take-off and landing (VTOL) aircraft is one that can take off and land vertically without relying on a runway. The category includes helicopters and other rotorcraft as well as fixed-wing aircraft capable of vertical flight, such as the Harrier jump jet and the F-35B.

Types

VTOL designs achieve vertical lift through powered rotors, tiltrotors such as the V-22 Osprey, tiltwing designs, vectored-thrust jets, or lift fans and separate lift engines. Some aircraft are described as STOVL (short take-off and vertical landing) because they take off with a short roll to carry more fuel or weapons.

Advantages and challenges

VTOL capability allows operations from small ships, clearings, and damaged airfields, but it demands high power, complex controls, and usually reduces payload and range compared with conventional aircraft. The concept is central to modern electric air-taxi (eVTOL) development.
