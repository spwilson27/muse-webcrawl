Lexeme

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lexeme groups together all the inflected forms of a word.

A lexeme (from Greek léxis, "word") is a unit of lexical meaning that underlies a set of words that are related through inflection. It is a basic abstract unit of meaning, a unit of morphological analysis in linguistics that roughly corresponds to a set of forms taken by a single root word. For example, in English, run, runs, ran and running are forms of the same lexeme, which can be represented as RUN.

Concepts

A lexeme is conventionally written in capital letters or small capitals, and dictionaries list lexemes as headwords or lemmas: all the forms of a lexeme are found under a single dictionary entry. Lexemes are distinguished from individual word forms (the actual inflected variants), from morphemes (the smallest meaningful units, which may be smaller than a lexeme), and from lexical items, which may consist of more than one word, as in multiword expressions like kick the bucket.

Lexemes and derivation

Inflection changes the form of a word without creating a new lexeme (e.g., plural cats from cat), whereas derivation creates new lexemes: teach and teacher are separate lexemes. Similarly, homographs such as bank (financial institution) and bank (river's edge) are separate lexemes despite sharing a form, while polysemous senses of a single word are usually treated as one lexeme.
