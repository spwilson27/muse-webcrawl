Medeu (Kazakh: Медеу, Medeu) is a valley and high-altitude sports complex located in the Malaya Almatinka river gorge in the Trans-Ili Alatau mountains, about 15 kilometres southeast of Almaty, Kazakhstan. It is famous for the Medeu Skating Rink, one of the highest Olympic-size speed-skating and bandy rinks in the world, and for the Medeu Dam, an enormous mudflow-protection dam that guards Almaty from catastrophic debris flows.

Skating rink

Built at an elevation of about 1,691 metres, the Medeu rink opened in its modern form in 1972 and became legendary in speed skating: its thin mountain air and pure glacial water ice made it the setting for more than a hundred world records, earning it the nickname "the factory of records". The 10,500-seat complex also hosts bandy, figure skating and ice hockey, and was a principal venue of the 2011 Asian Winter Games. A gondola links Medeu to the Shymbulak ski resort higher in the gorge.

Medeu Dam

The Medeu mudflow protection dam, completed in 1972 across the Malaya Almatinka gorge below the rink, is a rock-fill dam roughly 150 metres high that retains several million cubic metres of debris capacity. It was built after devastating mudflows (notably those of 1921 and 1973) threatened Almaty. A long public staircase, the "Health Staircase" of 842 steps, climbs the dam and is a popular fitness challenge.
