Seldinger technique

The Seldinger technique, also known as Seldinger wire technique, is a medical procedure to obtain safe access to blood vessels and other hollow organs. It is named after Dr. Sven-Ivar Seldinger (1921–1998), a Swedish radiologist who introduced the procedure in 1953.

Procedure

The target vessel is punctured with a hollow needle; a flexible guidewire is passed through the needle into the vessel; the needle is withdrawn; and a catheter (or dilator and sheath) is advanced over the wire into position. The wire is then removed, leaving the catheter in place. The method replaced earlier techniques requiring larger needles and surgical cutdown.

Uses

The technique underlies angiography and much of interventional radiology, as well as central venous catheterization, percutaneous gastrostomy, nephrostomy, and drainage of fluid collections. A modified Seldinger technique is used for chest drains and suprapubic catheters.

Complications

Possible complications include bleeding, infection, arterial puncture, hematoma, and loss of the guidewire; ultrasound guidance reduces many of these risks.
