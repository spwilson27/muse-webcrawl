Erytheia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Erytheia (Ancient Greek: Ἐρύθεια, "the red [island]") is a mythical island in Greek mythology, located at the far western edge of the world in the stream of Ocean, roughly beyond the Pillars of Heracles near the site of Gadeira (modern Cádiz). Its name is usually explained by the red light of the setting sun in the west.

The cattle of Geryon

Erytheia is famous as the home of Geryon, the three-bodied (or three-headed) giant who kept a herd of magnificent red cattle, guarded by the herdsman Eurytion and the two-headed dog Orthrus, brother of Cerberus. The tenth of the Twelve Labours of Heracles required the hero to sail to Erytheia, kill Geryon, Eurytion, and Orthrus, and drive the cattle back across Europe to Mycenae. The archaic poet Stesichorus told the story in his Geryoneis, of which fragments survive, unusually sympathetic to the giant.

The Hesperid nymph

The name Erytheia (or Erytheis) is also given to one of the Hesperides, the nymphs who guarded the golden apples in a garden in the far west — fittingly, since their garden too lay near the sunset at the world's edge. In Apollodorus's list the Hesperides are Aegle, Erytheia, Hesperia, and Arethusa.
