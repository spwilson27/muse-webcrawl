Dragør

Dragør is a town on the island of Amager in Denmark, about 12 kilometres southeast of central Copenhagen, at the entrance to the Øresund. It is the seat of Dragør Municipality. The town is known for its exceptionally well-preserved old quarter of yellow-washed, red-tiled houses and cobbled lanes dating from the 18th and 19th centuries, and for a long maritime history as a fishing port, pilot station, and ferry harbour facing Sweden.

History

Dragør's origins lie in the great herring markets of the Øresund in the Middle Ages, when Hanseatic merchants traded at seasonal fairs on the coast. In the 1520s Christian II invited Dutch farmers to settle on Amager; their descendants' influence is still seen locally, especially in the nearby village of Store Magleby. From the 18th century Dragør flourished on shipping, pilotage through the Drogden channel, salvage, and fishing; many residents became skippers and shipowners, and in the 19th century the town's fleet was among the largest in Denmark outside Copenhagen. During the Second World War, in October 1943, Dragør fishermen played an important role in ferrying Danish Jews across the Øresund to safety in neutral Sweden.

The old town and harbour

The compact old town, largely spared by fires, preserves some 300 protected buildings: low yellow-plastered houses with red tile roofs along narrow cobbled streets and small gardens — a townscape often cited as one of Denmark's best preserved. Geese, traditionally kept by residents, still graze the town's commons. The harbour combines fishing boats, a marina, the old pilot tower, and the Dragør Museum; a ferry to Limhamn in Sweden operated until 1999, when it was made redundant by the Øresund Bridge. Dragør Fort, built 1910–15 on an artificial island south of the harbour, guarded the approach to Copenhagen.
