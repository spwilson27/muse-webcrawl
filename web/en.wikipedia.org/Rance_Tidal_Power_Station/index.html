The Rance Tidal Power Station is a tidal power station located on the estuary of the Rance River in Brittany, France. Opened on 26 November 1966, it was the world's first and largest tidal power station for 45 years, until the Sihwa Lake Tidal Power Station in South Korea surpassed it in 2011.

Description

The facility is operated by Électricité de France (EDF) and has an installed capacity of 240 megawatts, generated by 24 bulb turbines of 10 MW each. The barrage is 750 metres long and impounds a basin of 22 km². The Rance estuary has one of the largest tidal ranges in the world, averaging 8 metres and reaching up to 13.5 metres at spring tides.

Operation

The turbines can generate power in both directions as the tide flows in and out, and the plant can also operate as a pumped-storage facility. Annual generation averages around 500 GWh, supplying roughly 0.12% of France's electricity demand and serving some 130,000 households in Brittany. The road crossing the barrage forms part of the route between Dinard and Saint-Malo, and a lock allows the passage of boats.

Environmental impact

Construction of the barrage caused progressive silting of the estuary ecosystem, and some species, such as the sand eel, disappeared from the estuary. A diverse ecosystem has since re-established itself, though the estuary's fauna differs from its pre-barrage state. The station remains a landmark of French engineering and a popular tourist attraction.
