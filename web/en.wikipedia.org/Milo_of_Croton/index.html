Milo of Croton

BornCroton, Magna Graecia (6th century BC)
SportWrestling
Olympic titlesSix (wrestling)

Milo of Croton (Greek: Μίλων, Mílōn) was a celebrated athlete of ancient Greece from the Greek city of Croton in southern Italy. He won the wrestling title at the Olympic Games six times in the late 6th century BC, along with numerous victories at the Pythian, Isthmian, and Nemean games, making him one of the most successful athletes of antiquity.

Legendary feats of strength attached themselves to Milo. He was said to have trained by carrying a newborn calf on his shoulders every day until it grew into a full-grown bull — a story still cited as an illustration of progressive overload in strength training. Other tales credit him with holding a pomegranate without crushing it, standing on a greased shield that no one could push him off, and eating an entire ox in a day.

Death

According to tradition, Milo died when he attempted to split a tree trunk with his bare hands: his fingers became trapped in the wood and he was devoured by wolves (or lions, in some versions). His dramatic death became a popular subject in European art and sculpture. Milo was also connected with the philosopher Pythagoras, whose daughter he is said to have married.
