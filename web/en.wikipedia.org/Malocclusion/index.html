Malocclusion

A malocclusion is a misalignment or incorrect relation between the teeth of the upper and lower dental arches when they approach each other as the jaws close. It is not a disease but a variation of growth and development, most often hereditary in origin, though habits such as thumb sucking, tongue thrusting, and prolonged pacifier use, as well as trauma and early loss of teeth, can contribute.

Classification

Edward Angle's classification, based on the relationship of the first molars, remains widely used. Class I malocclusion has a normal molar relation but crowding or misalignment of individual teeth. Class II (distocclusion) has the lower molars positioned distally, often with prominent upper front teeth (overjet), and Class III (mesiocclusion) has the lower molars positioned mesially, often with an underbite. Malocclusions also include overbite, crossbite, open bite, and crowding.

Treatment

Most malocclusions require no treatment, but severe cases may cause difficulty chewing, speech problems, increased risk of dental injury or decay, and psychosocial concerns. Correction is by orthodontics — braces, aligners, and appliances — sometimes combined with orthognathic surgery in adults.
