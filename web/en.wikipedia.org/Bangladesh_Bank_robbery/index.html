Bangladesh Bank robbery

The Bangladesh Bank robbery of February 2016 was one of the largest cyber heists in history. Attackers used malware to compromise the systems of Bangladesh Bank, the country's central bank, and issued fraudulent instructions through the SWIFT payment network to transfer nearly US$1 billion from the bank's account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York.

The heist

Of the attempted transfers, about US$101 million succeeded: US$20 million to Sri Lanka, which was recovered after a spelling error ("fandation" for "foundation") aroused suspicion, and US$81 million to accounts in the Philippines, where most of it was laundered through casinos and remains unrecovered. A malfunctioning printer at the bank, apparently sabotaged, delayed discovery of the fraud over a weekend.

Attribution and aftermath

Investigators and the United States government attributed the attack to North Korea-linked hackers, often identified with the Lazarus Group. The case prompted reforms in SWIFT customer security controls and investigations and litigation in Bangladesh, the Philippines and the United States.
