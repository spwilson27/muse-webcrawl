Colocation centre

A colocation centre (also spelled co-location, and often shortened to "colo") is a type of data centre in which an operator rents out space, power, cooling, bandwidth, and physical security to multiple customers, who own and manage their own servers and networking equipment.

Services

Customers lease space by the rack unit, cabinet, cage, or entire room. The provider supplies redundant electrical power with backup generators and UPS systems, climate control, fire suppression, biometric access controls, and connectivity to multiple telecommunications carriers ("carrier-neutral" facilities).

Business model

Colocation lets companies avoid building their own data centres while retaining control over their hardware. Major providers include Equinix and Digital Realty, which operate hundreds of facilities worldwide, often near internet exchange points where customers interconnect cheaply and with low latency.

History

The industry grew in the late 1990s with the commercial internet, as internet service providers and dot-com companies needed secure, well-connected facilities. It has since consolidated and expanded to serve cloud providers, financial firms, and enterprises.
