Abdulaziz bin Abdul Rahman Al Saud (Arabic: عبد العزيز بن عبد الرحمن آل سعود; 15 January 1875 – 9 November 1953), known in the West as Ibn Saud, was the founder and first king of Saudi Arabia, the third Saudi state. He ruled parts of the kingdom from 1902 and the whole of it from its formal proclamation in 1932 until his death in 1953.

Early life and exile

Ibn Saud was born in Riyadh into the House of Saud, which had historically ruled much of the Arabian Peninsula. In 1891, when he was a teenager, his family was driven into exile by their rivals, the Al Rashid of Ha'il, after the fall of the second Saudi state. The family eventually settled in Kuwait under the protection of Sheikh Mubarak Al Sabah, where the young Abdulaziz observed politics and warfare and planned the recovery of his ancestral lands.

Reconquest of Riyadh and unification

In January 1902 Ibn Saud led a small raiding party from Kuwait and recaptured Riyadh in a daring night assault, killing the Rashidi governor. The recapture of Riyadh launched a campaign of unification that lasted three decades: he took Nejd, defeated the Rashidis with Ottoman support, conquered al-Hasa from the Ottomans in 1913, and eventually overran the Hejaz in 1924–1925, ending Hashemite rule there. On 23 September 1932 the kingdoms of Hejaz and Nejd were formally united as the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

Reign as king

As king, Ibn Saud consolidated a state bound together by his personal authority, marriage alliances, and the religious establishment. He settled nomadic Bedouin into agricultural colonies (the Ikhwan settlements) and later suppressed the Ikhwan revolt when its raiding threatened his relations with Britain. In 1933 his government granted an oil concession to Standard Oil of California; oil was discovered in commercial quantities in 1938 at Dammam Well No. 7, transforming the kingdom's finances. During the Second World War Saudi Arabia remained formally neutral but leaned toward the Allies, and in 1945 Ibn Saud met U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt aboard USS Quincy, inaugurating the long Saudi–American partnership.

Death and legacy

Ibn Saud died at Taif in 1953 and was succeeded by his son Saud. All subsequent kings of Saudi Arabia have been his sons. He is remembered as the unifier of most of the Arabian Peninsula into a single state and the founder of a dynasty that continues to rule one of the world's principal oil-producing nations.
