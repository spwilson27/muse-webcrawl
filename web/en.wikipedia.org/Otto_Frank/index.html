Otto Frank

Otto Heinrich Frank (12 May 1889 – 19 August 1980) was a German businessman who later became a resident of the Netherlands. He was the father of Anne Frank and her sister Margot. After World War II he was the only member of his immediate family to survive the Holocaust, and he dedicated much of his later life to publishing his daughter's diary and preserving her legacy.

Early life

Frank was born in Frankfurt am Main into a liberal Jewish family. He studied economics at the University of Heidelberg and served as an officer in the Imperial German Army during World War I, receiving the Iron Cross for his service.

Marriage and family

In 1925 he married Edith Holländer. Their daughters Margot (born 1926) and Anne (born 1929) were raised in Frankfurt. After the Nazis came to power in 1933, the family emigrated to Amsterdam, where Frank founded the Opekta company, which traded in pectin, and later a spice company.

Hiding and deportation

When the Nazis occupied the Netherlands, the Frank family went into hiding in July 1942 in a concealed annex behind Frank's office building at Prinsengracht 263 — the Achterhuis. The annex also sheltered the van Pels family and Fritz Pfeffer. The group was betrayed and arrested on 4 August 1944. Frank was deported to Auschwitz, where he remained until its liberation by Soviet forces in January 1945.

After the war

Returning to Amsterdam, Frank learned that Edith had died at Auschwitz and that Anne and Margot had died at Bergen-Belsen. Miep Gies, one of the family's helpers, gave him Anne's diary, which she had preserved. Frank arranged its publication in 1947 under the title Het Achterhuis ("The Secret Annex"). He remarried in 1953 to Elfriede Geiringer, also an Auschwitz survivor, and settled in Basel, Switzerland. He founded the Anne Frank Foundation in 1963 and worked to preserve the Anne Frank House as a museum. He died in 1980 at the age of 91.
