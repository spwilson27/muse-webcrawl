Pale Blue Dot

Pale Blue Dot is a photograph of Earth taken on 14 February 1990 by NASA's Voyager 1 space probe from a record distance of about 40.5 astronomical units (6 billion kilometres, or 3.7 billion miles). In the photograph, Earth's apparent size is less than a pixel; the planet appears as a tiny dot within the darkness of space and against a band of scattered light caused by the Sun's glare in the camera optics.

Background

The image was acquired as part of the Family Portrait series, a mosaic of the planets of the Solar System taken as Voyager 1 turned its cameras back toward the inner Solar System on its way out. The portrait was championed by astronomer Carl Sagan, who had proposed as early as 1981 that Voyager take such a picture, arguing for its perspective on humanity's place in the universe, despite its lack of scientific value for the mission.

The photograph

Earth appears as a crescent only 0.12 pixels in size, in a pale ray of scattered light. Voyager 1 was about 32 degrees above the ecliptic at a distance of roughly 40.5 AU when the image was taken. The narrow-angle camera used a coloured filter and an exposure of 0.4 seconds.

Legacy

Sagan titled his 1994 book Pale Blue Dot: A Vision of the Human Future in Space after the photograph, and his reflection on the image became one of his most famous writings:

"Look again at that dot. That's here. That's home. That's us. On it everyone you love, everyone you know, everyone you ever heard of, lived out their lives."

In 2020, for the 30th anniversary, NASA released a remastered version of the image using modern processing techniques.
