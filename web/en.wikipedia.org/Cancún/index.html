Cancún

Cancún is a city in southeast Mexico on the northeast coast of the Yucatán Peninsula in the Mexican state of Quintana Roo. It is a significant tourist destination in Mexico and the seat of the municipality of Benito Juárez. The city is on the Caribbean Sea and is one of Mexico's easternmost points.

History

The area was settled by the Maya in pre-Columbian times, and the island of Cancún takes its name from the Maya kaan kun, often interpreted as "nest of snakes". In the 1970s the Mexican government, through the FONATUR agency, selected the sparsely populated sand spit as the site of a purpose-built resort after computer analysis of climate and beach conditions. Construction began in 1970, and from a handful of residents Cancún grew into a city of hundreds of thousands.

Geography and tourism

The city is divided between the mainland downtown (Ciudad Cancún), where most residents live, and the Zona Hotelera, a 22.5 km L-shaped barrier island of beaches and large resort hotels facing the Caribbean and the Nichupté Lagoon. Cancún is famed for its white sand and turquoise water, its nightlife, and as a gateway to Maya archaeological sites such as Chichén Itzá and Tulum. It hosted the 2010 United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP 16) and the WTO Ministerial Conference of 2003. Cancún International Airport is among Mexico's busiest.
