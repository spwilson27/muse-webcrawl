Vieux Lyon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Vieux Lyon ("Old Lyon") is the old town of Lyon, France, lying on the west bank of the Saône river at the foot of the Fourvière hill. Covering about 24 hectares, it is one of the largest surviving Renaissance quarters in Europe and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed for the historic site of Lyon in 1998.

Quarters and history

The district comprises three quarters: Saint-Georges to the south, Saint-Jean in the middle — centred on the Gothic cathedral of Saint-Jean-Baptiste with its astronomical clock — and Saint-Paul to the north. In the 15th and 16th centuries Lyon's trade fairs made the city a banking and printing centre, and Italian merchant families built ornate townhouses here with inner courtyards, spiral towers and galleries.

A distinctive feature is the traboules: covered passageways that cut through buildings and courtyards, originally used by silk workers (canuts) to move fabric sheltered from the weather, and later famous as routes used by the French Resistance during the Second World War.

Preservation

By the mid-20th century the quarter was run down and threatened with demolition for road schemes. Under the Malraux Law of 1962, Vieux Lyon became France's first protected sector (secteur sauvegardé) in 1964, and its restoration transformed it into a lively district of museums, churches, restaurants — including the traditional bouchons serving Lyonnaise cuisine — and the Musée Gadagne, which houses the city's history museum and a world puppet museum honouring Guignol, created in Lyon.
