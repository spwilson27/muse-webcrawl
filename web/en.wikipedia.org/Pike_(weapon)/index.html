Pike (weapon)

A pike is a long thrusting spear used extensively by infantry both for attacking enemy infantry and as a defence against enemy cavalry. Unlike many similar weapons, the pike is not intended to be thrown. Pikes were used regularly in European warfare from the Late Middle Ages and the Renaissance to the early 18th century, and were wielded by foot soldiers deployed in pike square formations.

Design and use

The pike consisted of a wooden shaft, typically ash, 3 to 7.5 metres (10 to 25 feet) long, tipped with a small steel spearhead and sometimes reinforced with metal langets to prevent the head being cut off. It was too unwieldy for single combat and was used in dense formations: ranks of pikemen presented a hedge of points to charging cavalry and pushed against enemy infantry in the "push of pike."

History

Long spears were used by the Macedonian phalanx (the sarissa) in antiquity. The weapon was revived by Swiss infantry in the 14th century, whose victories over armoured cavalry made pike blocks the core of Renaissance armies, combined with arquebusiers in the Spanish tercio and later in "pike and shot" formations. The plug and socket bayonet, which turned every musket into a short pike, made the weapon obsolete by around 1700.
