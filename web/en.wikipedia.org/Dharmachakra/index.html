The dharmachakra (Sanskrit: धर्मचक्र; Pali: dhammacakka) or wheel of dharma is a widespread symbol used in Buddhism, Hinduism, and Jainism. In Buddhism it represents the Buddha's teaching and the universal law of dharma, as well as the cycle of birth and rebirth (saṃsāra) that the teaching transcends.

Symbolism in Buddhism

The Buddha's first sermon at Sarnath is described as "setting in motion the wheel of dharma" (Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta). The wheel most often has eight spokes, representing the Noble Eightfold Path. Its circular shape symbolises the perfection and completeness of the teaching, and its motion the spread of the dharma. Wheels may also be depicted with 12, 24, or 31 spokes, corresponding to other doctrinal lists such as the twelve links of dependent origination.

History and use

The wheel appears among the earliest aniconic symbols of the Buddha, for instance on the pillars of Ashoka (3rd century BCE), where it is associated with the chakravartin, the ideal universal ruler who turns the wheel of law. The Ashoka Chakra, a 24-spoke version, appears at the centre of the modern flag of India. In Jainism the wheel symbolises dharma and is associated with ahimsa; in Hinduism it is linked to Vishnu's Sudarshana Chakra and to the solar deity Surya.
