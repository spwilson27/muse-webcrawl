Supernatural

The supernatural refers to phenomena or entities that are beyond the laws of nature. The term is derived from Medieval Latin supernaturalis, from Latin super- (above) and natura (nature). Supernatural concepts are characteristic of folklore and religious belief, and stand in contrast to the naturalism of modern science, which holds that all phenomena are in principle explicable by natural laws.

Concepts

Entities commonly described as supernatural include deities, angels, demons, spirits of the dead, and forces such as karma or fate; phenomena include miracles, prophecy, telekinesis, hauntings and magical effects. Whether such things exist, and how "supernatural" should be defined, is contested: theologians have argued that God transcends rather than violates nature, while sceptics hold that claims of the supernatural reflect unexplained natural phenomena or cognitive biases.

History of the concept

The sharp distinction between natural and supernatural is largely a product of medieval Christian theology, developed by scholastic thinkers such as Thomas Aquinas, who distinguished what God does through created causes from what he does above or beyond them. The term entered wider European use in the early modern period. During the Enlightenment and the scientific revolution, methodological naturalism in science progressively displaced supernatural explanations of natural phenomena, a process sociologists associate with the "disenchantment" of the world.

In culture

The supernatural is a staple of horror and fantasy fiction, from Gothic novels such as Dracula to the long-running American television series Supernatural (2005–2020). Belief in supernatural beings and forces remains widespread worldwide and is a subject of study in anthropology, psychology and religious studies.
