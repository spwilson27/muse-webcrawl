Eoin MacNeill

Eoin MacNeill (1867–1945) was an Irish scholar, nationalist, and politician. A leading figure in the Gaelic revival and a founder of the Gaelic League, he later became chief of staff of the Irish Volunteers and a minister in the Irish Free State.

Scholarship and language revival

MacNeill taught and wrote on early Irish history, law, and literature. With Douglas Hyde and others he helped found the Gaelic League in 1893 to promote the Irish language. His academic work emphasized native sources and helped shape modern study of medieval Ireland.

Volunteers and the Easter Rising

In 1913 MacNeill's article “The North Began” helped prompt formation of the Irish Volunteers, and he became their chief of staff. When he learned that the 1916 Easter Rising was planned without his authorization, he issued a countermanding order that reduced mobilization, though the rising proceeded in Dublin.

Later career

MacNeill served as Minister for Education in the Irish Free State and represented Ireland at the League of Nations. He also sat on the Boundary Commission whose failure to alter the Northern Ireland border led to controversy. See also Gaelic League and Easter Rising.
