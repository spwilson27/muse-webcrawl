Pilkington is a British glass manufacturer based in St Helens, Merseyside, England. Founded in 1826 as the St Helens Crown Glass Company and long known as Pilkington Brothers, the company grew into one of the world's leading makers of flat glass for buildings and vehicles. Since 2006 it has been part of Japan's Nippon Sheet Glass group, trading within the group as Pilkington Group Limited while the Pilkington brand remains widely used on its products.

Float glass

Pilkington's most celebrated achievement is the float glass process, developed in the 1950s by Sir Alastair Pilkington and Kenneth Bickerstaff and announced in 1959. In the process, molten glass is poured onto a bath of molten tin, on which it floats and spreads to form a ribbon of uniform thickness with naturally polished surfaces—eliminating the costly grinding and polishing previously needed for plate glass. Licensed worldwide, the float process became the universal standard for making flat glass.

Products and history

The company's products include architectural glazing, automotive glass, self-cleaning and fire-resistant glass, and the tough rolled glass developed under the K Glass and other brands. Originating as a partnership of the Pilkington and Greenall families, the business was a major St Helens employer throughout the nineteenth and twentieth centuries; its former head office and works in the town are landmarks of the British glass industry. Pilkington was listed on the London Stock Exchange until its acquisition by NSG in 2006.
