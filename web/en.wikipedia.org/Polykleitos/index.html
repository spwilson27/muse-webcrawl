Polykleitos

Lifefl. c. 460–420 BC
OriginArgos / Sicyon
Known forDoryphoros; Diadumenos; the Canon
MediumBronze sculpture

Polykleitos was an ancient Greek sculptor, active in the fifth century BC, regarded alongside Phidias and Myron as one of the masters of the Classical style. Working mainly in bronze at Argos and elsewhere in the Peloponnese, he is best known for the Doryphoros (Spear-Bearer) and the Diadumenos, both known today through Roman marble copies, and for his theoretical treatise, the Canon.

The Canon and contrapposto

In the Canon, Polykleitos set out a system of ideal proportions—symmetria—in which the beauty of the body arises from the commensurability of its parts, each measured against the others and against the whole. The Doryphoros embodied these principles in visual form, including the characteristic contrapposto stance, in which the weight rests on one leg so that engaged and relaxed limbs alternate across the body in a chiastic balance. According to Galen and Pliny, the statue itself was also called the Canon, and artists treated it as a model of correct proportions.

Other works

Polykleitos won the competition, reported by Pliny, among sculptors including Phidias and Myron, for a statue of an Amazon for the Temple of Artemis at Ephesus. His colossal gold-and-ivory statue of Hera for the Heraion of Argos was ranked by ancient writers with the works of Phidias. None of his bronze originals survive; his style is reconstructed from Roman copies and from descriptions in authors such as Pliny the Elder and Quintilian, who judged his figures graceful but lacking the grandeur of Phidias.
