Sherwood Forest

Sherwood Forest is a royal forest in Nottinghamshire, England, famous worldwide as the legendary home of Robin Hood and his Merry Men. In the Middle Ages it was a large hunting preserve of the English kings, covering roughly a fifth of the county — some 100,000 acres between Nottingham and Worksop. Today the surviving core, centred on the village of Edwinstowe, is a National Nature Reserve and country park of ancient oak woodland managed for conservation and visitors.

History

Sherwood — the "shire wood" of Nottinghamshire — was woodland on poor, sandy soils (the Sherwood Sandstone) that supported oak and birch heath rather than rich farmland. After the Norman Conquest it became a royal hunting forest, governed by strict forest law protecting the deer (venison) and the trees (vert); offences were punished severely, and figures such as the hated Sheriff of Nottingham administered it. The medieval forest contained royal lodges at Clipstone and elsewhere, and the great road north — the ancestor of the Great North Road — ran through it, preyed upon, in ballad tradition, by outlaws. Over the centuries most of the forest was enclosed, cleared or absorbed into great ducal estates (the "Dukeries"), leaving fragments.

The Major Oak

The forest's most famous tree is the Major Oak near Edwinstowe, a huge ancient pedunculate oak with a girth of about 10 metres, estimated to be 800–1,100 years old and weighing some 23 tonnes. Local tradition says Robin Hood's men sheltered within its hollow trunk. Its branches are now supported by scaffolding, and its acorns have been planted in famous locations around the world.

Nature and conservation

Sherwood holds one of Britain's most important populations of ancient oaks and the rich invertebrate fauna that depends on decaying wood, including rare beetles and spiders; it is designated a Special Area of Conservation. Conservation work focuses on protecting veteran trees, restoring heathland, and managing the large visitor numbers drawn by the Robin Hood legend, centred on the Sherwood Forest Visitor Centre.

Robin Hood

From the earliest ballads (such as Robin Hood and the Monk and the Gest of Robyn Hode) Sherwood has been the outlaw's greenwood refuge from the Sheriff and Prince John. Whether a historical Robin Hood existed remains unresolved, but the forest's identity is permanently bound to the legend, commemorated in Nottingham by the castle and the Robin Hood statue.
