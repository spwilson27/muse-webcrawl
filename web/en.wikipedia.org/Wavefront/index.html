Wavefront

In physics, a wavefront is the set of all points at which a propagating wave has the same phase at a given instant — for example, the surface formed by all the crests of a water wave or the points of equal phase in a light wave. The concept applies to sound, electromagnetic radiation, and other wave phenomena.

Description

For a point source in a uniform medium, wavefronts are expanding spheres (or circles in two dimensions); for a plane wave they are parallel planes. Light rays are drawn perpendicular to the wavefronts, in the direction of propagation. In Huygens' principle, every point on a wavefront is treated as a source of secondary wavelets, from which the wavefront at a later time can be constructed — a model used to explain reflection, refraction, and diffraction.

Applications

Wavefront analysis is important in optics, where deviations from an ideal spherical or planar wavefront are called wavefront aberrations; adaptive optics and ophthalmic instruments such as wavefront aberrometers measure and correct these errors. In seismology, wavefronts describe the propagation of seismic energy through the Earth.
