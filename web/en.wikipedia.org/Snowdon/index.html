Snowdon (Welsh: Yr Wyddfa) is the highest mountain in Wales, at an elevation of 1,085 metres (3,560 ft) above sea level, and the highest point in the British Isles other than the Scottish Highlands. It is located in Snowdonia National Park (Parc Eryri) in Gwynedd, north-west Wales.

Geology and environment

The rocks of Snowdon were formed by volcanoes in the Ordovician period, and the mountain's dramatic ridges and cirques were carved by glaciation during the ice ages. The massif includes several peaks, of which Yr Wyddfa is the highest; others include Garnedd Ugain, Crib Goch, and Y Lliwedd. Rare plants such as the Snowdon lily grow on its cliffs, and much of the mountain is protected as a national nature reserve.

Ascent

Snowdon is one of Britain's most climbed mountains, with around 600,000 walkers reaching the summit each year. Six main paths ascend from Llanberis, Pen-y-Pass, and other trailheads, including the Llanberis Path, the Pyg Track, the Miners' Track, and the Watkin Path. The Snowdon Mountain Railway, a rack railway opened in 1896, carries passengers from Llanberis to the summit station, near the Hafod Eryri visitor centre opened in 2009.

In culture

The mountain is prominent in Welsh mythology and legend, associated with the giant Rhitta Gawr and with King Arthur; its Welsh name, Yr Wyddfa, means "the tumulus" or "burial mound". Snowdon is also one of the three peaks used in the National Three Peaks Challenge.
