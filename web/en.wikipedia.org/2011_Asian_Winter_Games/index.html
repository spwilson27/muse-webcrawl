The 2011 Asian Winter Games were a multi-sport event held in Astana and Almaty, Kazakhstan, from 30 January to 6 February 2011. It was the seventh edition of the Asian Winter Games and the first major international winter multi-sport event hosted by Kazakhstan. Around 843 athletes from 26 National Olympic Committees competed in 69 events across 11 sports.

Venues

Events were divided between the capital, Astana — which hosted ice hockey, figure skating, short track and speed skating at venues including the Kazakhstan Sports Palace and the Alau Ice Palace — and Almaty, where alpine events were held at the Shymbulak ski resort, skating at the high-altitude Medeu rink, ski jumping at the Sunkar complex, and cross-country skiing and biathlon at the Soldatskoe gorge venues near the city.

Sporting highlights

Host nation Kazakhstan dominated the medal table with 32 gold medals and 70 medals overall, its strongest performance in the event's history, ahead of Japan and South Korea. Bandy appeared at the Asian Winter Games for the first time, with Kazakhstan winning the gold medal. Ski orienteering also made its debut. Premier and demonstration interest aside, the Games were seen as a showcase of Kazakhstan's ambitions in international sport, preceding Astana–Almaty's (ultimately unsuccessful) bid for the 2022 Winter Olympics, which was awarded to Beijing.

Legacy

The Games left Kazakhstan with modernised winter sports infrastructure that continues to host World Cup events in speed skating, ski jumping and freestyle skiing, and they are remembered as the moment the country established itself as a credible host of major winter competitions.
