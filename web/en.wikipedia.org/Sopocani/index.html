The Sopoćani Monastery (Serbian: Сопоћани) is a Serbian Orthodox monastery near the source of the Raška River in southern Serbia, close to modern Novi Pazar. It was founded around 1260 by King Stefan Uroš I as his endowment and intended mausoleum.

Architecture

The church, dedicated to the Holy Trinity, is built in the Raška school style, combining Romanesque exterior forms — a single-nave basilica with a dome, built of dressed stone with a bell tower added later — with Byzantine spatial concepts. A narthex and chapels were added in the 14th century.

Frescoes

Sopoćani is celebrated above all for its frescoes, executed around 1265 and considered masterpieces of medieval European painting. The monumental Dormition of the Virgin on the west wall of the nave is famed for its classical balance, luminous colour, and sculptural figures, reflecting the Palaeologan Renaissance in Byzantine art through Serbian patronage.

History

The monastery flourished as the burial place of Uroš I and members of the Nemanjić dynasty. After Ottoman raids it was burned and abandoned in the late 17th century, standing roofless for some two centuries before 20th-century restoration. In 1979 it was inscribed, together with Stari Ras, on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
