Virelai

A virelai is a form of medieval French verse used mostly in poetry and music. It is one of the three formes fixes (the others being the ballade and the rondeau) and was one of the most common verse forms set to music in Europe from the late 13th to the 15th centuries.

Musical form

The musical virelai follows an A b b a A pattern: a refrain (A) alternates with stanzas whose first part (b) uses new text with the refrain's music, and whose second part (a) returns to the refrain's music with new text, concluding with a repeat of the refrain itself. Guillaume de Machaut composed many virelais, including the famous "Douce Dame Jolie". The form is closely related to the Italian ballata and the Spanish villancico and virelay.

Poetic form

As pure poetry (called the virelai ancien), the form uses only two rhymes with short lines of varying length, and lines from the opening stanza recur as refrains. Later poets such as Christine de Pizan kept the fixed forms alive in verse after they faded from music.
