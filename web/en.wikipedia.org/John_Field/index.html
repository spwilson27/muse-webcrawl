John Field

Born26 July 1782, Dublin, Ireland
Died23 January 1837 (aged 54), Moscow, Russia
OccupationComposer, pianist
Known forInventing the nocturne

John Field (26 July 1782 – 23 January 1837) was an Irish pianist, composer and teacher widely credited as the inventor of the nocturne, a short, lyrical piano piece in a dreamy night mood that strongly influenced Frédéric Chopin.

Early life

Field was born in Dublin into a musical family and studied piano with Tommaso Giordani. He moved to London in 1793, where he was apprenticed to Muzio Clementi, under whom he developed into a brilliant virtuoso and made acclaimed debut performances.

Career in Russia

In 1802 Field travelled with Clementi to St Petersburg and settled in Russia, where he enjoyed great success as a performer and society teacher; his pupils included Mikhail Glinka. He toured widely in Europe before ill health curtailed his performing career.

Music

Field composed seven piano concertos, numerous chamber works, and about twenty nocturnes. His flowing, bel canto-like melodies over broken-chord accompaniments shaped the Romantic piano style, particularly the nocturnes of Chopin, who admired his playing.

Death

Field died in Moscow in 1837 after a long illness.
