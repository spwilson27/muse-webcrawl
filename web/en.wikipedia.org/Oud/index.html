Oud

The oud is a short-neck lute-type, pear-shaped, fretless stringed instrument, usually with 11 strings grouped in six courses. It is a defining instrument of music in the Middle East, North Africa, and the Mediterranean, and an ancestor of the European lute.

Construction

The oud's body is built from strips of wood forming a rounded bowl, with a flat spruce soundboard pierced by decorative sound holes (rosettes). The neck is short and the pegbox is bent back at nearly a right angle. Players pluck the strings with a plectrum traditionally made from an eagle feather, now often plastic.

History and playing traditions

Instruments of this type appear in ancient Mesopotamian and Egyptian art. The oud is central to Arabic, Turkish (ud), Persian (barbat), Greek, Armenian, and Azerbaijani traditions, each with distinct tunings and ornamentation. Noted modern masters include Munir Bashir and Anouar Brahem.
