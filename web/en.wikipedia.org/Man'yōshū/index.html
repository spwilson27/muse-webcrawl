Man'yōshū

The Man'yōshū (万葉集, "Collection of Ten Thousand Leaves") is the oldest extant collection of Japanese waka poetry, compiled sometime after AD 759 during the Nara period. It contains more than 4,500 poems in 20 books, composed over roughly four centuries, by poets ranging from emperors and court nobles to frontier guards and peasants.

Compilation and contents

The anthology is traditionally associated with the poet Ōtomo no Yakamochi, among others. Its poems include long chōka and short tanka, as well as sedōka and other forms, touching on love, travel, nature, legend and laments for the dead. Major poets represented include Kakinomoto no Hitomaro, Yamanoue no Okura, Ōtomo no Tabito and Yakamochi himself.

Language and script

The poems are written in Old Japanese using Chinese characters adapted phonetically — a system called man'yōgana, an ancestor of the kana syllabaries. The collection is a crucial source for the historical linguistics of Japanese and for the culture and society of early Japan, and it gave its name to the era name Reiwa, drawn from a poem in Book 5.
