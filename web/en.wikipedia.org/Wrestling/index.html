Wrestling

Wrestling is a martial art, combat sport, and form of entertainment that involves grappling with an opponent and striving to obtain a position of advantage through different throws or techniques, within a given ruleset. Wrestling is one of the oldest sports in the world, depicted in cave paintings and in the epics of ancient cultures, and wrestling competitions were part of the ancient Olympic Games.

History

Wrestling appears in the art and literature of ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, India, China, and Greece. Greco-Roman wrestling was codified in 19th-century France, while freestyle (catch-as-catch-can) wrestling developed in Britain and the United States. Both styles have been contested at the modern Olympic Games since the early 20th century; women's freestyle wrestling was added in 2004.

International styles

The international styles governed by United World Wrestling are Greco-Roman wrestling, in which holds below the waist are forbidden, and freestyle wrestling, which permits the use of the legs in offence and defence. Folk wrestling traditions exist worldwide, including Mongolian wrestling, Turkish oil wrestling, Senegalese wrestling, sumo in Japan, and schwingen in Switzerland.

Professional wrestling

Professional wrestling evolved from competitive catch wrestling into a form of sports entertainment in which outcomes are predetermined and matches are performed as theatrical spectacles, achieving worldwide popularity through promotions such as WWE.
