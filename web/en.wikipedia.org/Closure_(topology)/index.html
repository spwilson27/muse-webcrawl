Closure (topology)

In topology, the closure of a subset S of a topological space consists of all points in S together with all limit points of S. The closure of S may equivalently be defined as the union of S and its boundary, and also as the smallest closed set containing S. A set that is equal to its closure is called a closed set. The closure is commonly denoted cl(S), or with an overline, S̅.

Definitions

For a subset S of a topological space X, the following definitions of closure are equivalent:

  • The smallest closed subset of X that contains S (the intersection of all closed supersets of S).
  • The set S together with all of its limit points.
  • The union of S and its boundary: cl(S) = S ∪ ∂S.
  • The set of all points x in X such that every neighbourhood of x intersects S; such points are called points of closure of S.

Properties

The closure operator satisfies the Kuratowski closure axioms: it preserves the empty set, every set is contained in its closure, the closure of a union is the union of the closures, and applying closure twice gives the same result as applying it once (idempotence). The interior of a set is the complement of the closure of its complement.

Examples

In the real line with the usual topology, the closure of the open interval (0, 1) is the closed interval [0, 1], and the closure of the rational numbers is the entire real line, since every real number is a limit of rationals.
