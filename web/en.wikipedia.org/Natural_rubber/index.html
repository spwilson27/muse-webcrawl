Natural rubber

Natural rubber, also called India rubber or latex, is an elastomer composed of polymers of isoprene, obtained mainly from the milky latex of the rubber tree Hevea brasiliensis. It is harvested by tapping the bark and collecting the latex in cups.

Main sourceHevea brasiliensis
Polymercis-1,4-polyisoprene
Top producersThailand, Indonesia, Vietnam, India, China

History

Mesoamerican peoples used rubber balls and waterproofing millennia ago. Vulcanization, patented by Charles Goodyear (1844) and Thomas Hancock, made rubber stable and commercially transformative. Seeds smuggled from Brazil in 1876 (the Wickham collection) founded plantations in British Malaya and the Dutch East Indies, shifting production to Southeast Asia, where most natural rubber is still grown.

Properties and uses

Natural rubber has high tensile strength, resilience, and abrasion resistance, but poor resistance to oils and ozone. About 70 percent of production goes into tyres; the rest into gloves, condoms, footwear, hoses, and medical and industrial goods. Synthetic rubbers, developed during the Second World War, now supply the majority of total rubber use.
