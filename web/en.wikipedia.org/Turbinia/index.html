Turbinia was the first steam turbine-powered steamship. Built as an experimental vessel in 1894, she was designed by the inventor of the steam turbine, Sir Charles Parsons, to demonstrate the revolutionary potential of turbine propulsion for marine use.

Development

Parsons formed the Parsons Marine Steam Turbine Company in 1893 and had the experimental vessel Turbinia built by Brown and Hood at Wallsend on Tyne. Launched on 2 August 1894, initial trials with a single propeller were disappointing because of cavitation — a phenomenon Parsons studied with the first cavitation tunnel. The final configuration used three shafts, each carrying three propellers (nine in total), driven by three turbines.

The Spithead demonstration

Parsons publicised the ship with an audacious, uninvited appearance at the Royal Navy Fleet Review for Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee at Spithead on 26 June 1897. Turbinia, flying the Red Ensign, steamed at 34.5 knots through the lines of anchored warships, easily outrunning the Navy's fastest picket boats sent to intercept her. The stunt convinced the Admiralty: within a few years turbine propulsion powered the destroyers HMS Viper and HMS Cobra, the liner Mauretania, and the battleship HMS Dreadnought.

Later life

Turbinia was accidentally cut in two by the steamer Crosby in 1907 but was repaired. She is preserved today as a museum exhibit at the Discovery Museum in Newcastle upon Tyne.
