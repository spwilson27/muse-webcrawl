Eurofighter Typhoon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Eurofighter Typhoon is a European twin-engine, canard–delta wing, multirole fighter aircraft. Designed and built by a consortium of Airbus, BAE Systems and Leonardo (through the Eurofighter Jagdflugzeug company), it was developed by the United Kingdom, Germany, Italy, and Spain. Talks began in 1983 and the type first flew on 27 March 1994; it entered service in 2003.

Design

The Typhoon is an agile, aerodynamically unstable aircraft controlled by a quadruplex digital fly-by-wire system. It is powered by two Eurojet EJ200 afterburning turbofans, giving it a maximum speed of Mach 2 and the ability to supercruise. Its airframe uses extensive carbon-fibre composites and incorporates measures to reduce its radar cross-section, though it is not a full stealth design. Armament includes a 27 mm Mauser BK-27 cannon and thirteen hardpoints for air-to-air missiles such as the Meteor, AMRAAM, IRIS-T and ASRAAM, plus precision-guided bombs including Paveway IV and the Storm Shadow cruise missile.

Operational history

Typhoons are operated by the partner nations and by export customers including Austria, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Kuwait, and Qatar. The type saw combat in the 2011 military intervention in Libya, operations against the Islamic State over Iraq and Syria, and air-policing duties across Europe and the Middle East.

Development and upgrades

Production is divided into tranches with progressively improved multirole capability. The Captor-E active electronically scanned array radar and successive software standards (such as P2E/P3E and the P4E package) have extended the aircraft's air-to-ground and electronic warfare abilities. The Typhoon is expected to remain in service into the 2060s with partner nations, alongside next-generation programmes such as GCAP and FCAS.
