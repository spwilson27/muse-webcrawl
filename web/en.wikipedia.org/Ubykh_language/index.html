Ubykh language

Ubykh

Hüseyin Kozan, the last native speaker of Ubykh.

Native to: Circassia (later Turkey)
Extinct: 7 October 1992
Family: Northwest Caucasian
Consonants: ~80

Ubykh is an extinct Northwest Caucasian language once spoken by the Ubykh people, originally from the eastern coast of the Black Sea in the region of modern Sochi. After the Russian conquest of the Caucasus, the Ubykh were expelled to the Ottoman Empire in 1864 and settled in Turkey, where the language gradually gave way to Turkish and Circassian.

Phonology

Ubykh is famous among linguists for its enormous consonant inventory — around 80 consonants in some analyses, one of the largest of any language without clicks — paired with an extremely small vowel system of as few as two or three phonemic vowels. Its grammar is agglutinative and highly polysynthetic, with complex verbal morphology.

Documentation and extinction

The language was extensively documented by scholars including Georges Dumézil, working with the last fluent speakers. The last native speaker, Hüseyin Kozan (Tevfik Esenç), died in Turkey on 7 October 1992, and the language became extinct with him, though revival interest exists among descendants using the recorded materials.
