Thomas Andrew Dorsey (July 1, 1899 – January 23, 1993) was an American musician, composer and Christian evangelist influential in the development of early blues and 20th-century gospel music. He penned more than 1,000 gospel songs and 500 songs in all, and is known as the "Father of Gospel Music".

Early career

Born in Villa Rica, Georgia, the son of a Baptist preacher, Dorsey moved to Chicago and first found success as a blues pianist under the name "Georgia Tom", recording popular (and risqué) songs such as "It's Tight Like That" with Tampa Red in 1928. He also accompanied Ma Rainey and arranged for blues and jazz performers.

Gospel pioneer

After a religious conversion and, in 1932, the death of his wife Nettie and infant son in childbirth, Dorsey wrote his most famous song, "Precious Lord, Take My Hand". He fused blues feeling and jazz rhythm with sacred lyrics, founded the first gospel choir at Pilgrim Baptist Church in Chicago, co-founded the National Convention of Gospel Choirs and Choruses (1932), and mentored singers including Mahalia Jackson. "Peace in the Valley" and "There'll Be Peace in the Valley for Me" are among his other widely recorded works.

Legacy

Dorsey was inducted into the Gospel Music Hall of Fame and the Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame. His blend of sacred and secular styles established the template for modern gospel, soul and rhythm and blues.
