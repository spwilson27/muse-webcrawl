The Arabian Sea (Arabic: بَحرُ ٱلْعَرَبْ, baḥr al-ʿarab) is a region of the northern Indian Ocean bounded on the north by Pakistan, Iran and the Gulf of Oman, on the west by the Gulf of Aden, Guardafui Channel and the Arabian Peninsula, on the southeast by the Laccadive Sea and the Maldives, and on the east by India. With a surface area of about 3.86 million square kilometres, it is one of the largest seas in the world, and its maximum depth is around 4,652 metres.

Geography and oceanography

The Arabian Sea's principal arms are the Gulf of Aden, connecting to the Red Sea through the Bab el-Mandeb strait, and the Gulf of Oman, connecting to the Persian Gulf through the Strait of Hormuz. Its largest islands include Socotra (Yemen), Masirah (Oman) and the Indian island groups of Lakshadweep. The Indus River is the largest river flowing directly into the sea, depositing enormous quantities of sediment that have built the Indus Fan, one of the largest deep-sea sediment fans on Earth. The sea is famous among oceanographers for its seasonally reversing monsoon winds and currents — including the Somali Current, which flows northward in summer — and for a pronounced oxygen minimum zone and productive upwelling off the coasts of Oman and Somalia.

History and trade

The Arabian Sea has been a major trade route since antiquity, linking the Middle East, East Africa, the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia. Arab, Persian, Indian and later Portuguese, Dutch and British sailors rode the predictable monsoon winds between ports such as Aden, Muscat, Karachi, Mumbai (Bombay) and Kozhikode, trading spices, textiles, frankincense, horses and dates. The ancient Greek text known as the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea describes these routes in detail. Today the sea carries a large share of the world's oil and container traffic between the Suez Canal, the Persian Gulf and Asia, and its shipping lanes have been affected by piracy off the Horn of Africa.

Climate and ports

Tropical cyclones form in the Arabian Sea mainly in the pre- and post-monsoon seasons and can strike India, Pakistan and the Arabian Peninsula. Major ports include Mumbai, Karachi, Mundra, Gwadar, Muscat, Salalah, Aden and Chabahar.
