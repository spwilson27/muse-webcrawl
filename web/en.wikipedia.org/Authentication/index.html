Authentication

Authentication is the process of verifying that an entity — a person, device, document or piece of data — is what it claims to be. In information security it is usually the first step of access control, preceding authorisation, which determines what the authenticated entity is permitted to do.

Factors

Authentication factors are conventionally grouped into three classes: something you know (passwords, PINs), something you have (security tokens, smart cards, phones), and something you are (biometrics such as fingerprints or facial recognition). Combining factors from different classes gives multi-factor authentication, which is substantially stronger than any single factor. Passwords remain the most common method but are vulnerable to phishing, reuse and database breaches; cryptographic approaches such as public-key authentication, challenge–response protocols, Kerberos, OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect underpin most modern systems, and standards such as FIDO2/WebAuthn enable passwordless, phishing-resistant sign-in.

Beyond computing

The term is also used for verifying the genuineness of artworks, documents, products and collectibles, where provenance research and forensic testing replace credentials.
