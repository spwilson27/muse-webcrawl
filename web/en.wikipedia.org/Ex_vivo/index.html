Ex vivo

Ex vivo (Latin: "out of the living") refers to experimentation or measurements done in or on tissue from an organism in an external environment with minimal alteration of natural conditions. Ex vivo conditions allow experimentation on cells or tissues under more controlled conditions than is possible in in vivo experiments, though a major consideration is that the environment differs from that inside the organism.

Applications

Common ex vivo models include organ culture, tissue slices (such as brain or intestinal slices), isolated perfused organs (for example the Langendorff heart), and primary cell cultures derived directly from biopsies. Such systems are widely used in pharmacology and toxicology for drug screening, and in fertility medicine, where ex vivo techniques relate to procedures such as in vitro fertilisation.

Related terms

In vivo denotes experiments within a whole, living organism; in vitro refers to work in glassware or culture vessels with isolated cells or biomolecules; in silico refers to computer simulation. The boundary between ex vivo and in vitro is sometimes drawn by whether an intact tissue architecture is preserved.
