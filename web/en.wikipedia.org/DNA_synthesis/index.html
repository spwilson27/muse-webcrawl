DNA synthesis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

DNA synthesis is the natural or artificial creation of deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) molecules. In nature, DNA synthesis occurs during DNA replication, when a cell duplicates its genome before division, and in repair processes that patch damaged sequences. In the laboratory, DNA synthesis refers to chemical or enzymatic methods used to build defined DNA sequences for research, biotechnology, and synthetic biology.

Natural DNA synthesis

DNA polymerases synthesise a new strand in the 5′→3′ direction, reading the template 3′→5′, and require a primer with a free 3′-hydroxyl group. At the replication fork, the leading strand is made continuously, while the lagging strand is made discontinuously as short Okazaki fragments that are later joined by DNA ligase. Because polymerases can only extend an existing primer, the enzyme primase first lays down a short RNA primer. Proofreading exonuclease activity keeps error rates as low as roughly one mistake in 107–109 bases added.

Chemical (artificial gene) synthesis

Commercial DNA synthesis typically uses solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry, in which protected nucleotide building blocks are added one at a time to a growing chain anchored to a solid support. The cycle of deblocking, coupling, capping, and oxidation is repeated for each base. Oligonucleotides of up to roughly 200 bases are routine; longer genes are assembled from overlapping oligonucleotides by methods such as polymerase cycling assembly or Gibson assembly, and increasingly by enzymatic (template-free) synthesis using terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase.

Applications

Synthetic DNA supplies primers and probes for PCR and sequencing, enables gene cloning and mutagenesis, and underpins synthetic genomics projects such as the chemically synthesised Mycoplasma mycoides genome (JCVI-syn1.0, 2010) and the Synthetic Yeast Genome Project (Sc2.0). Falling synthesis costs have raised dual-use and biosecurity concerns, addressed through sequence-screening frameworks by synthesis providers.
