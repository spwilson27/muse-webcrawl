Standard Oil

Founded1870, Cleveland, Ohio
FoundersJohn D. Rockefeller, William Rockefeller, Henry Flagler, Stephen V. Harkness, Oliver B. Jennings
Dissolved1911 (broken up by court order)
IndustryPetroleum refining and marketing
SuccessorsExxonMobil, BP, Chevron, Marathon Petroleum and others

Standard Oil Company was an American oil-producing, transporting, refining and marketing company established in 1870 by John D. Rockefeller and partners. It became the archetypal industrial monopoly, dominating the U.S. oil industry until the Supreme Court ordered its dissolution in 1911.

Rise

From its Cleveland base, Standard Oil grew by absorbing or driving out rival refiners — controversially during the "Cleveland Massacre" of 1872 — and by negotiating secret rebates and drawbacks from railroads. In 1882 its interests were consolidated in the Standard Oil Trust, one of the first and most famous business trusts. By the 1880s it controlled around 90 percent of U.S. oil refineries and pipelines, integrating vertically into production, transport and marketing, and pioneering the modern corporate structure.

Criticism and dissolution

Muckraking journalist Ida Tarbell's The History of the Standard Oil Company (1904) exposed its aggressive practices and galvanised public opposition. In Standard Oil Co. of New Jersey v. United States (1911), the Supreme Court held the trust an unreasonable restraint of trade under the Sherman Antitrust Act and ordered it split into 34 independent companies.

Legacy

The successor "Baby Standards" include ExxonMobil, Chevron, BP's American operations, Marathon Petroleum and ConocoPhillips — several of which remain among the world's largest companies. Rockefeller became the archetype of the Gilded Age "robber baron" and, in retirement, a pioneering philanthropist.
