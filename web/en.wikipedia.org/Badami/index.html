Badami

Badami, formerly known as Vatapi, is a town in the Bagalkot district of Karnataka, India. It was the royal capital of the Badami Chalukyas from about 540 to 757 CE and is famous for its rock-cut cave temples and structural temples of red sandstone.

The cave temples

Four cave temples are carved into the cliff south of Agastya lake: three Hindu (two dedicated to Vishnu, one to Shiva) and one Jain. Dating mainly to the sixth and seventh centuries, they contain celebrated sculptures including Shiva as Nataraja with 18 arms, Vishnu as Trivikrama, and Varaha rescuing the earth goddess.

History

Founded by Pulakeshin I, Badami flourished under kings such as Pulakeshin II, who defeated the northern emperor Harsha. The Pallavas of Kanchipuram briefly occupied Vatapi in the 640s, an event commemorated in inscriptions; the Rashtrakutas finally displaced the Chalukyas in the eighth century.

Other monuments

Above the lake stand the Bhutanatha group of temples and the fort on the northern cliff, while the nearby sites of Aihole and Pattadakal complete a celebrated complex of early Chalukyan art. See also Chalukya dynasty.
