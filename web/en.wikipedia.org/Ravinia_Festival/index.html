Ravinia Festival

Ravinia Festival
Location: Highland Park, Illinois, USA
Founded: 1904 (park); festival from 1936
Resident orchestra: Chicago Symphony Orchestra
Season: Summer

The Ravinia Festival is an outdoor music festival held every summer at Ravinia Park in Highland Park, Illinois, a suburb of Chicago. It is the oldest outdoor music festival in the United States and the summer home of the Chicago Symphony Orchestra (CSO), which has performed there since 1936.

History

Ravinia Park opened in 1904, built by the A.C. Frost Company to attract riders to its Chicago and Milwaukee Electric Railroad; it featured a casino, dining hall and a pavilion. When the railroad failed, local residents purchased the park in 1911 and devoted it to music, hosting opera and orchestral concerts. After a Depression-era closure, the park reopened in 1936 with the CSO, beginning the modern festival.

Performances and grounds

The festival presents classical concerts, chamber music, jazz, pop and rock across a roughly three-month season. The open-sided pavilion seats about 3,400, while the surrounding lawn accommodates many thousands of picnickers — lawn listening, with food and wine brought from home, is a signature of the Ravinia experience. The grounds also include the Martin Theatre and the Bennett Gordon Hall. Ravinia's Steans Music Institute, founded in 1988, trains young professional musicians each summer.

Access

The park has its own stop on Metra's Union Pacific North Line, served by special "Ravinia Park" trains on concert days, a tradition dating to the park's origins as a railroad attraction.
