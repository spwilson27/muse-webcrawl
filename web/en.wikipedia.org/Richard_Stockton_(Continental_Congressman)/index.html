Richard Stockton (Continental Congressman)

Richard Stockton (October 1, 1730 – February 28, 1781) was an American Founding Father, lawyer, and signer of the United States Declaration of Independence as a representative of New Jersey. He was also a judge, a member of the Continental Congress, and a trustee of Princeton University.

Life

Stockton was born at his family home, Morven, in Princeton, and educated at the College of New Jersey (now Princeton University), graduating in its first class in 1748. He studied law, was admitted to the bar in 1754, and built one of the colony's leading practices. He served on the Royal Council of New Jersey and as a justice of the provincial supreme court.

Revolutionary career

Elected to the Continental Congress in 1776, Stockton voted for and signed the Declaration of Independence. Returning to New Jersey later that year, he was captured by Loyalists, turned over to the British, and imprisoned in New York, where harsh treatment ruined his health. Released on parole in 1777, he never fully recovered and died in 1781. He remains notable as the only signer of the Declaration to be taken prisoner by the British, and controversy later arose over whether he had recanted under duress.

Legacy

Stockton University in New Jersey is named in his honour, and his statue represents New Jersey in the National Statuary Hall collection in the U.S. Capitol.
