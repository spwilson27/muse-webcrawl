Cincinnati is a city in and the county seat of Hamilton County, Ohio, United States. Settled in 1788, the city is located in the southwest corner of the state, on the north bank of the Ohio River opposite Northern Kentucky. It is the third-most populous city in Ohio and anchors a metropolitan area of over two million people spanning Ohio, Kentucky, and Indiana.

History

The settlement was founded in 1788 as Losantiville and renamed Cincinnati in 1790 by territorial governor Arthur St. Clair, in honor of the Society of the Cincinnati. Its river position made it a major port and pork-packing center — it was nicknamed "Porkopolis" — and by the mid-19th century it was among the largest cities in the United States. Cincinnati was an important stop on the Underground Railroad, sitting across the river from the slave state of Kentucky. It was also a center of German immigration, whose heritage survives in the Over-the-Rhine neighborhood and local beer culture. Procter & Gamble was founded there in 1837.

Geography

Cincinnati lies on a sweeping bend of the Ohio River, built amid a series of hills that give many neighborhoods elevated views of the river valley. The city has a humid continental climate with hot, humid summers and cold winters.

Economy and culture

The metropolitan economy includes the headquarters of Procter & Gamble, Kroger, and Fifth Third Bank. Cultural institutions include the Cincinnati Art Museum, the Cincinnati Zoo and Botanical Garden, the Cincinnati Symphony Orchestra, and the National Underground Railroad Freedom Center. The city is known for Cincinnati chili, served over spaghetti, and for sports teams the Reds (Major League Baseball, founded as the first professional baseball team), the Bengals (NFL), and FC Cincinnati (MLS).
