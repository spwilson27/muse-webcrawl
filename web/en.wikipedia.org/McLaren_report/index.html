McLaren Report
AuthorRichard McLaren, for the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA)
PublishedPart 1: 18 July 2016; Part 2: 9 December 2016
SubjectState-sponsored doping in Russia

The McLaren Report is an independent investigation commissioned by the World Anti-Doping Agency and led by Canadian law professor Richard McLaren. It concluded that Russia operated a state-sponsored doping programme across Olympic sports, involving the manipulation of anti-doping samples on an unprecedented scale.

Background

Allegations surfaced in a 2014 German television documentary and were amplified by whistleblower Grigory Rodchenkov, the former director of Moscow's anti-doping laboratory, who described a system for swapping urine samples at the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympics through a hole in the laboratory wall, with the involvement of Russian security services.

Findings

The first part, released in July 2016, confirmed a state-directed system operating from 2011 to 2015. The second part, in December 2016, reported evidence involving more than 1,000 Russian athletes across summer, winter and Paralympic sports, including tampering with samples at Sochi and London 2012.

Consequences

The findings led the International Olympic Committee to ban Russia as a team from the 2018 Pyeongchang Winter Olympics (with cleared athletes competing as "Olympic Athletes from Russia") and contributed to later sanctions under which Russian athletes competed as the ROC or as neutrals. Russia disputed the report's evidence and methods, though a number of individual sanctions were upheld by the Court of Arbitration for Sport.
