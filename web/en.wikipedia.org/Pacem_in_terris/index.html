Pacem in terris (Latin: "Peace on Earth") is a papal encyclical issued by Pope John XXIII on 11 April 1963, subtitled "On Establishing Universal Peace in Truth, Justice, Charity and Liberty". It was the last encyclical drafted by John XXIII, who died two months later, and it is regarded as one of the most significant documents of modern Catholic social teaching.

Content

Unusually for an encyclical, it was addressed not only to Catholics but to "all men of good will". The document sets out an extensive catalogue of human rights — including rights to life, food, clothing, shelter, medical care, education, worship, free assembly, and emigration — and the corresponding duties. It treats in turn the order between individuals, between citizens and public authorities, between states, and the need for a world political authority adequate to the universal common good. It condemned the arms race, called for negotiated disarmament with effective controls, and stated that in the atomic age war could no longer be regarded as a fit instrument for repairing violated rights.

Context and reception

The encyclical appeared months after the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962, in which John XXIII had appealed publicly for peace, and amid the Cold War division of Europe. It received exceptional attention outside the Church: the New York Times printed the full text, and the Soviet press published summaries. It influenced the Second Vatican Council's pastoral constitution Gaudium et spes and the Church's later teaching on human rights and religious freedom. Themes such as the rights of women and of workers, and its relatively positive treatment of international organisations including the United Nations, marked developments in papal teaching.

Legacy

Successive popes have cited Pacem in terris in addresses on peace and diplomacy, and its fiftieth anniversary in 2013 was marked by conferences and papal messages revisiting its proposals. The document remains a standard reference in discussions of Catholic just-war reasoning, arms control, and global governance.
