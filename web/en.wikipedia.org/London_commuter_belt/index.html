The London commuter belt is the area surrounding London, England, from which a significant number of residents travel daily into the capital for work. It has no single official definition, but it broadly corresponds to the parts of the Home Counties and beyond where commuting to London is a dominant pattern of travel.

Extent

Depending on the definition used, the belt can extend 40–70 km from central London, taking in towns such as Guildford, Reading, St Albans, Chelmsford, Maidstone and Luton. Statistical definitions include the London Travel to Work Area and the wider metropolitan region used for planning purposes, which covers much of South East England.

Development

The belt grew with the suburban railways of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries — including the "Metro-land" developments of the Metropolitan line — and was contained after the Second World War by the Metropolitan Green Belt and the construction of New Towns such as Stevenage, Hemel Hempstead and Milton Keynes designed partly to house London's overspill population.
