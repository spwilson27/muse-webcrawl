Quaternary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Quaternary is the current and most recent of the three periods of the Cenozoic Era in the geologic time scale. It follows the Neogene Period and spans from about 2.58 million years ago to the present. The Quaternary is divided into two epochs: the Pleistocene (2.58 Ma to 11.7 thousand years ago) and the Holocene (11.7 thousand years ago to today).

Overview

The period is defined by the growth and decay of continental ice sheets and the repeated glacial cycles driven by Milankovitch variations; during glacial maxima, ice covered large parts of North America and Eurasia and global sea level fell by more than 100 metres, exposing land bridges such as Beringia. The Quaternary also encompasses nearly the whole evolution of the genus Homo, including anatomically modern humans, the development of agriculture in the Holocene, and the ongoing late-Quaternary extinction of much of the world's megafauna.

Definition

The name was introduced by Jules Desnoyers in 1829 for sediments in France's Seine basin, in a scheme that divided strata into Primary, Secondary, Tertiary, and Quaternary; only the last survives in formal use. In 2009 the base of the Quaternary was lowered from 1.8 to 2.58 million years, transferring the Gelasian Stage from the Pliocene to the Pleistocene, after long debate within stratigraphy.
