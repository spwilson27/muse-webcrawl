Thailand, officially the Kingdom of Thailand and historically known as Siam, is a country in Southeast Asia at the centre of the Indochinese Peninsula. It borders Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia and Malaysia and has coasts on the Gulf of Thailand and the Andaman Sea. Bangkok is its capital and largest city.

History

Thai peoples migrated from southwestern China over the first millennium CE. The Sukhothai (from 1238) and Ayutthaya (1350–1767) kingdoms flourished; after Ayutthaya's fall to Burma, Thonburi and then the Chakri dynasty's Rattanakosin kingdom (from 1782) reunified Siam. Siam was the only Southeast Asian state to avoid formal European colonisation, ceding territory to Britain and France while modernising under Mongkut and Chulalongkorn. The 1932 revolution ended absolute monarchy. Thailand allied briefly with Japan, then with the United States during the Cold War, hosting US bases and receiving refugees including Hmong after 1975. Politics since has alternated between elected governments and military coups, most recently in 2014.

Geography and economy

Northern mountains, the Khorat Plateau, the Chao Phraya plain and the Malay Peninsula give Thailand varied climates dominated by the monsoon. Tourism, manufacturing (automobiles, electronics), agriculture (rice, rubber) and services drive an upper-middle-income economy; the country is a founding member of ASEAN.

Culture

Theravada Buddhism shapes Thai culture, visible in wats (temples), festivals such as Songkran and Loy Krathong, classical dance, Muay Thai and a renowned cuisine balancing sweet, sour, salty and spicy flavours. The king is head of state and revered under strict lèse-majesté laws.
