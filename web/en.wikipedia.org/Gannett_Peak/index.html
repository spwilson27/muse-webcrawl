Gannett Peak is the highest mountain peak in the U.S. state of Wyoming at 13,810 feet (4,209 m). It lies in the Wind River Range within the Bridger Wilderness of the Bridger-Teton National Forest, straddling the border between Fremont and Sublette counties. It is the highest point in the Central Rockies and the second-highest peak in the Rocky Mountains overall after Mount Elbert in Colorado.

Geography and climbing

Straddling the Continental Divide, Gannett Peak is surrounded by the largest glaciers in the American Rockies, including the Gannett Glacier on its north slope. The peak is remote: the shortest approach requires a hike of roughly 20 miles each way, and standard routes cross Dinwoody Glacier and the Gooseneck Glacier to a final rock and snow climb. It was first climbed in 1922 by Arthur Tate and Floyd Stahlnaker. The mountain was named in 1906 for Henry Gannett, chief geographer of the U.S. Geological Survey.

Climate

Weather on Gannett Peak is severe, with frequent afternoon thunderstorms, high winds, and snow possible in any month; it is often regarded as among the most difficult state high points ("highpointing" summits) to climb in the contiguous United States.
