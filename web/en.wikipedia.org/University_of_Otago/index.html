The University of Otago (Māori: Ōtākou Whakaihu Waka) is a public research collegiate university based in Dunedin, Otago, New Zealand. Founded in 1869, it is New Zealand's oldest university and one of the oldest in Oceania. The university was created by a committee led by Thomas Burns, and officially established by an ordinance of the Otago Provincial Council in 1869.

History

Planning for the university began in the 1860s, driven by settlers who wanted a university for the province. The university opened in July 1871 with a staff of three professors, teaching chemistry, classics and natural science. In 1874 it became an affiliate college of the federal University of New Zealand, and in 1961, when the federal university was dissolved, Otago regained its independent degree-awarding status.

Campuses

The main campus is in Dunedin's city centre, organised around the Water of Leith and the university's landmark clocktower building, completed in 1879 and built of bluestone in a Gothic Revival style. The university also has health sciences campuses in Christchurch and Wellington, a campus in Auckland, and the University of Otago College of Education campus in Invercargill.

Academics

Teaching and research are organised into four academic divisions: the Division of Humanities, the Division of Sciences, the Division of Health Sciences, and the Otago Business School. The university is particularly noted for its health sciences programmes, including New Zealand's oldest medical school, founded in 1875, and its dentistry, pharmacy and physiotherapy schools.

Student life

Students at Otago are traditionally known as "Scarfies", after the scarves worn in Dunedin's cold winters. Much of the student population lives in the suburb of North Dunedin, in residential colleges and flatting areas around Castle Street and Hyde Street. The Otago University Students' Association (OUSA), founded in 1890, organises events including Orientation Week.
