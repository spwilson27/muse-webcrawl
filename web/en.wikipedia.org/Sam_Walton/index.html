Sam Walton

Sam Walton, founder of Walmart.

Samuel Moore Walton (March 29, 1918 – April 5, 1992) was an American business magnate best known for founding Walmart and Sam's Club, which grew into the world's largest retailer. His strategy of bringing discount stores with low prices and efficient logistics to small-town America transformed global retail.

Early life

Born in Kingfisher, Oklahoma, and raised in Missouri during the Great Depression, Walton worked his way through the University of Missouri, where he studied economics. After military service in the Second World War, he bought a Ben Franklin variety store in Newport, Arkansas, in 1945 with a loan from his father-in-law, and later opened a store in Bentonville.

Founding Walmart

In 1962 Walton opened the first Walmart Discount City in Rogers, Arkansas, based on his insight that rural customers would drive to well-stocked discount stores. Walmart incorporated in 1969 and went public in 1970. By emphasising everyday low prices, centralised distribution and information technology, the company overtook Kmart and Sears to become America's largest retailer by 1990. Walton also founded the Sam's Club warehouse chain in 1983.

Legacy

Walton received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1992, shortly before his death from multiple myeloma. His autobiography, Sam Walton: Made in America, set out his "ten rules" of business. His heirs, the Walton family, remain among the wealthiest families in the world, while Walmart's labour and sourcing practices have been the subject of continuing debate.
