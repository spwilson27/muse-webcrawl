Synth-pop

Synth-pop put the synthesizer at the centre of popular music.

Synth-pop (short for synthesizer pop; also called techno-pop) is a music genre that first became prominent in the late 1970s and features the synthesizer as the dominant musical instrument. It was prefigured in the 1960s and early 1970s by the use of synthesizers in progressive rock, electronic, art rock, disco, and particularly the Krautrock of bands like Kraftwerk. It arose as a distinct genre in Japan and the United Kingdom in the post-punk era as part of the new wave movement of the late 1970s.

History

Electronic musical synthesizers that could be used practically in a recording studio became available in the mid-1960s, and by the late 1970s cheaper instruments and drum machines made all-electronic line-ups viable. Early synth-pop pioneers included Yellow Magic Orchestra, Ultravox, the Human League, Gary Numan, and Japan. In the early 1980s, acts such as Depeche Mode, Pet Shop Boys, Soft Cell, Eurythmics, and New Order brought the style to global chart success, often paired with striking fashion and music videos on MTV.

Characteristics and influence

Synth-pop typically combines electronic textures with pop song structures, dance rhythms, and often cool or detached vocals. The genre waned in the late 1980s but has seen repeated revivals, influencing electropop, dance music, and much of 21st-century pop production.
