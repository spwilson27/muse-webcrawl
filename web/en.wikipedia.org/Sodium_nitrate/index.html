Sodium nitrate

Sodium nitrate is the chemical compound with the formula NaNO3. This alkali metal nitrate salt is also known as Chile saltpeter (large deposits were mined in Chile) to distinguish it from ordinary saltpeter, potassium nitrate. The mineral form is known as nitratine. It is a white, highly water-soluble crystalline powder.

History

Vast deposits of caliche in the Atacama Desert of Chile and Peru were mined from the 1820s, and Chilean saltpeter became a strategic commodity as a nitrogen fertilizer and explosives ingredient — a cause of the War of the Pacific (1879–1884). The industry collapsed after the Haber–Bosch process made synthetic ammonia cheap in the early 20th century.

Applications

Sodium nitrate is used in fertilizers, as a food preservative and color fixative in cured meats (additive E251, related to its conversion to sodium nitrite), in pyrotechnics and oxidizers, in glass and enamel manufacture, and as a heat-storage and heat-transfer salt in solar thermal plants. It is also used in wastewater treatment as an oxygen source for bacteria.

Health considerations

Nitrates in food and water can be reduced to nitrites, which in excess can cause methemoglobinemia in infants and contribute to the formation of nitrosamines; regulatory limits therefore apply to drinking water and cured meats.
