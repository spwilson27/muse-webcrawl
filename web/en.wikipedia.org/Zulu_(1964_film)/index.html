Zulu is a 1964 British epic war film depicting the Battle of Rorke's Drift between the British Army and the Zulus in January 1879, during the first British invasion of Zululand in southern Africa. The film was directed by American screenwriter and director Cy Endfield, who also co-wrote the screenplay with John Prebble.

The film stars Stanley Baker as Lieutenant John Chard, and introduces Michael Caine in his first major role as Lieutenant Gonville Bromhead. The plot concerns some 150 British soldiers, many of them sick or wounded patients in a field hospital, who defend the mission station at Rorke's Drift against roughly 4,000 Zulu warriors after the British defeat at Isandlwana earlier that day.

Reception

Zulu was a critical and commercial success and remains one of the best-known British war films. It was shot on location in Natal, South Africa, with the cooperation of the Zulu community; thousands of Zulu extras appeared in the film, and Chief Mangosuthu Buthelezi played his own great-grandfather, King Cetshwayo. The film's battle sequences and its depiction of the defenders' singing of "Men of Harlech" in answer to Zulu war chants are especially celebrated.

Legacy

The film launched Michael Caine's film career and was followed in 1979 by a prequel, Zulu Dawn, depicting the Battle of Isandlwana. Eleven Victoria Crosses were awarded to the defenders of Rorke's Drift, the most ever awarded to a single regiment for one action, a fact highlighted in the film's closing narration by Richard Burton.
