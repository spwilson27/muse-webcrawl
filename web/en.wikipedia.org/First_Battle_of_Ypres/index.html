First Battle of Ypres

The First Battle of Ypres (19 October – 22 November 1914) was a battle of the First World War, fought on the Western Front around Ypres in West Flanders, Belgium. The battle was part of the First Battle of Flanders, in which German, French, Belgian and British forces fought to control the strategic port cities on the Belgian coast after the "Race to the Sea".

Course of the battle

German forces, including many young volunteer reserve corps, attacked to break through toward the Channel ports of Calais and Dunkirk. The German assaults at Langemarck were later mythologised in Germany as the Kindermord ("massacre of the innocents") because of the heavy losses among student volunteers. The British Expeditionary Force, reinforced by Indian and Territorial troops and supported by the French and the Belgian army, held the Ypres Salient despite being outnumbered. Desperate fighting around Gheluvelt at the end of October nearly broke the British line before the position was stabilised.

Significance

By late November, cold weather and exhaustion ended the battle. The BEF's pre-war regular battalions were largely destroyed, and the flat, waterlogged terrain around Ypres settled into the trench stalemate that would characterise the Western Front. Ypres remained a focal point for the rest of the war, seeing major battles again in 1915 and 1917.
