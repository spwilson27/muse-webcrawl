Acheron

The Acheron is a river in the Epirus region of northwestern Greece, and in Greek mythology one of the great rivers of the underworld, Hades. Its name was traditionally interpreted as "river of woe" (from achos, "pain").

The real river

The Acheron flows about 52 km from its sources in the mountains of Epirus to the Ionian Sea near the village of Ammoudia, passing through a dramatic limestone gorge. In antiquity the river was associated with the Necromanteion, an oracle of the dead located near its banks at Ephyra, where the Acheron was believed to meet the Styx and Cocytus. The river's springs, emerging from dark rock clefts, encouraged the belief that it descended into the underworld.

Mythology

In Homer, Odysseus travels to the land of the Cimmerians at the edge of the world, where the Acheron flows into Oceanus, to consult the shades of the dead. In later tradition — Plato's Phaedo, Virgil's Aeneid, and Dante's Inferno — the Acheron is the boundary river of Hades across which the ferryman Charon carries the souls of the newly dead, for the price of a coin placed in the mouth of the corpse. In Dante, those souls who died in anger with God are gathered on its banks awaiting passage.

The Acheron is counted among the five rivers of the Greek underworld, alongside the Styx, Lethe, Phlegethon, and Cocytus. In some myths Acheron is personified as a river god, son of Oceanus and Gaia, punished by being cast into Hades for giving water to the Titans.
