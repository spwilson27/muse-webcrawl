Henry II of Castile

Henry II (13 January 1334 – 29 May 1379), called Henry of Trastámara or the Fratricidal (el Fratricida), was the first king of Castile and León from the House of Trastámara. He became king in 1369 by defeating and killing his half-brother, Peter, after the Castilian Civil War.

Early life

Henry was the fourth of ten illegitimate children of King Alfonso XI of Castile and his mistress Eleanor de Guzmán, a noblewoman from León. He was born in Seville and was raised by Rodrigo Álvarez de las Asturias, whose lands he inherited, and was granted the county of Trastámara and lordships in Galicia. After Alfonso's death in 1350, his legitimate son Peter succeeded to the throne, and Peter's regime turned violently against Eleanor de Guzmán — who was executed in 1351 — and her sons.

Rebellion and civil war

Henry led a series of revolts against Peter, whose perceived brutality, favouritism toward his mistress's relatives, and reliance on Jewish and converso officials alienated much of the Castilian nobility. Henry's cause drew support from France and Aragon, while Peter was backed by England.

In 1366, with the aid of the French commander Bertrand du Guesclin and the Free Companies, Henry was proclaimed king in Burgos. Peter reclaimed the throne the following year after the Black Prince's victory at the Battle of Nájera (1367), where Henry was defeated. Henry returned with renewed French support, and on 14 March 1369 the brothers met at the Battle of Montiel. Peter was defeated and captured; days later, on 23 March 1369, Henry killed Peter with his own hand during a confrontation in du Guesclin's tent, securing the crown.

Reign

As king, Henry II founded the Trastámara dynasty, which would rule Castile for more than a century and, through Ferdinand II of Aragon's marriage to Isabella I of Castile (Henry's great-great-niece), eventually unify Spain. To reward the nobles and mercenary captains who had supported him, Henry distributed extensive grants known as the mercedes enriqueñas, which reshaped the Castilian aristocracy. He also had to fight wars against Portugal, England, and Navarre, and to fend off claims by John of Gaunt, who asserted a right to the Castilian crown through his wife Constance, Peter's daughter.

Henry's reign saw growing antisemitic agitation in Castile; in his political struggle against Peter he had exploited popular resentment of Peter's Jewish officials, and pogroms and discriminatory measures increased, foreshadowing the violence of 1391.

Death and succession

Henry II died on 29 May 1379 at Santo Domingo de la Calzada and was succeeded by his son, John I of Castile. He was buried in the Royal Chapel of the Cathedral of Toledo.

Marriage and children

Henry married Juana Manuel, daughter of Juan Manuel, Prince of Villena, in 1350. Their children included John I of Castile and Eleanor of Castile, queen consort of Navarre.
