William McDonough

William Andrews McDonough (born February 20, 1951) is an American architect, designer and author. He is the co-creator of the Cradle to Cradle design framework and a leading voice in sustainable design and the circular economy.

Career

McDonough was born in Tokyo and educated at Dartmouth College and Yale University. He founded William McDonough + Partners in 1981, designing buildings noted for energy efficiency and ecological sensitivity, including work for clients such as Ford Motor Company (the redeveloped River Rouge plant with its vast green roof) and NASA (the Sustainability Base in California).

Cradle to Cradle

With German chemist Michael Braungart he co-authored Cradle to Cradle: Remaking the Way We Make Things (2002), which proposes that products and materials be designed as "biological" or "technical" nutrients that circulate safely rather than becoming waste. The follow-up The Upcycle appeared in 2013. The associated Cradle to Cradle Certified program certifies products against sustainability criteria.

Recognition

McDonough has been recognised as a "Hero for the Planet" by Time magazine and has advised governments, cities and companies on circular-economy strategy.
