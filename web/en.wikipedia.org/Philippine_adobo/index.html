Philippine adobo

Philippine adobo is a popular Filipino dish and cooking process in Philippine cuisine. It involves meat, seafood, or vegetables marinated and braised in vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, bay leaves, and black peppercorns. It is frequently considered the unofficial national dish of the Philippines.

History

The cooking method predates Spanish colonization: indigenous Filipinos preserved food with vinegar and salt, and local vinegar-based stews were already common. The name derives from the Spanish word adobar, meaning "marinade" or "sauce," applied by colonizers to the native dish. Soy sauce was introduced through Chinese trade.

Variations

Common versions include chicken adobo and pork adobo, and the two are often combined (chicken-pork adobo). Regional variants include adobong puti ("white adobo," without soy sauce), adobo with coconut milk in the Bicol region, and adobong dilaw with turmeric. In March 2023 the Philippines' Department of Trade and Industry moved to standardize a reference recipe while recognizing regional diversity.
