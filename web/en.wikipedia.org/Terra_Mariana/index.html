Terra Mariana (Latin for "Land of Mary") was the official name for the medieval Livonian confederation in the territories that comprise modern Estonia and Latvia. It was established in 1207 during the Livonian Crusade, when German crusaders conquered the lands of the Livonian, Latgalian, Estonian and other Baltic peoples and dedicated the conquered territory to the Virgin Mary.

History

The conquest began with the founding of Riga in 1201 by Bishop Albert of Buxhoeveden. The military order of the Livonian Brothers of the Sword, founded in 1202, carried out much of the fighting. In 1207 the conquered lands were formally organized as Terra Mariana, a nominal principality of the Holy Roman Empire and a possession of the Church.

After the Brothers of the Sword were crushed at the Battle of Saule in 1236, their remnants were absorbed into the Teutonic Order as its Livonian branch in 1237. Terra Mariana thereafter existed as a loose confederation of five ecclesiastical states — the Archbishopric of Riga, the Bishoprics of Courland, Dorpat and Ösel-Wiek, and the lands of the Livonian Order — coordinated loosely through the Livonian Diet at Walk.

Dissolution

The confederation collapsed during the Livonian War (1558–1583). In 1561 the Livonian Order was secularized under its last master, Gotthard Kettler, who became Duke of Courland and a vassal of Poland-Lithuania. Northern Estonia submitted to Sweden, while Riga and southern Livonia passed to Polish-Lithuanian control, ending Terra Mariana as a political entity.

Legacy

The name survives in Latvian and Estonian historical tradition, and a stylized cross of Terra Mariana is the basis of the Estonian Order of the Cross of Terra Mariana, a state decoration established in 1995.
