Thai cuisine

A selection of central Thai dishes

Thai cuisine is the national cuisine of Thailand, celebrated for its balance of sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and spicy flavours. A typical Thai meal centres on rice—especially jasmine rice, or sticky rice in the north and northeast—accompanied by dishes shared among diners.

Characteristics and regions

Key ingredients include lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves, bird's eye chillies, fish sauce, shrimp paste, coconut milk, tamarind, and Thai basil. Influences came from China (stir-frying, noodles), India (curry spices), and the Portuguese (chillies arrived via the Americas, and egg-based sweets). Regional styles differ markedly: central Thai food is refined and coconut-rich (green curry, tom yum goong); the northeast (Isan) favours fiery salads such as som tam, grilled meats, and sticky rice; the north (Lanna) offers khao soi and herbal sausages; the south features intensely spicy, turmeric-laden curries and seafood.

Famous dishes

Internationally known dishes include pad thai (stir-fried rice noodles, designated a national dish), tom yum (hot and sour soup), tom kha (coconut soup), massaman curry—once ranked first among the world's best foods in a CNN poll—som tam (green papaya salad), and mango sticky rice. Street food, exemplified by Bangkok's vendors, is integral to the cuisine's reputation.
