The Plunger class was a class of seven submarines built for the United States Navy in the early 20th century. Also known as the "A" class, they were designed by John Philip Holland's Electric Boat Company and were among the first submarines commissioned by the US Navy, following the pioneering USS Holland (SS-1).

Design

The boats were gasoline-electric submarines, using a gasoline engine on the surface and an electric motor powered by batteries when submerged. They were armed with a single 18-inch torpedo tube in the bow with reloads carried internally. Their small size and limited endurance made them suitable mainly for harbor and coastal defense, as well as for training.

Ships in class

The class comprised Plunger (SS-2), Adder (SS-3), Grampus (SS-4), Moccasin (SS-5), Pike (SS-6), Porpoise (SS-7), and Shark (SS-8). They were renamed A-1 through A-7 in 1911. Several served in the Philippines in the years before the First World War.

Service

None of the class saw combat. By the end of the First World War the surviving boats were obsolete; most were decommissioned in 1919–1921 and expended as targets or scrapped.
