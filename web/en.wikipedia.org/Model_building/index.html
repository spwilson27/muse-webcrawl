Model building

Model building is the hobby of creating scale models of real or imaginary subjects, typically by assembling prefabricated kits or scratch-building from raw materials such as plastic, wood, metal, or resin. Common subjects include aircraft, ships, automobiles, military vehicles, railways, buildings, and science-fiction spacecraft.

History

Scale modelling has roots in the shipwright's and architect's models of earlier centuries. The modern plastic kit hobby grew rapidly after the Second World War, when manufacturers such as Airfix, Revell, and Tamiya began mass-producing injection-moulded polystyrene kits. By the 1960s and 1970s model building was a mainstream hobby across North America, Europe, and Japan.

Techniques

Builders assemble parts with adhesives, then finish models with painting, weathering, and decals to increase realism. Advanced techniques include airbrushing, photo-etched detail parts, resin aftermarket sets, and dioramas that place the model in a scenic setting. Related disciplines include model checking in computing and the construction of architectural models.

Scales

Popular scales include 1:72 and 1:48 for aircraft, 1:35 for armour, 1:24 for cars, and 1:700 and 1:350 for ships. Railway modelling uses its own family of scales such as HO (1:87) and N (1:160).
