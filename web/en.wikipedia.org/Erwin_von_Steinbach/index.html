Erwin von Steinbach

Erwin von Steinbach (c. 1244 – 17 January 1318) was a German architect and master mason, celebrated as the designer of the west façade of Strasbourg Cathedral, one of the masterpieces of High Gothic architecture. The surname "von Steinbach" derives from a town in Baden and was applied to him by later tradition rather than in his lifetime.

Work at Strasbourg

Erwin became master of the Strasbourg cathedral works around 1275. The foundation stone of the new west front was laid in 1277, in the presence of Bishop Conrad of Lichtenberg. Drawing on the newest French Rayonnant designs of Paris — notably Notre-Dame and the Sainte-Chapelle — Erwin devised a façade screened by an unprecedented lattice of openwork tracery: a great rose window framed by slender gables and a forest of pinnacles, its stonework described as a "harp of stone". He supervised construction until his death in 1318, by which time the façade had risen to the platform level; his sons and successors continued the towers on modified plans, and only one of the two intended spires was completed (in 1439, by Ulrich d'Ensingen).

Family and legacy

Erwin's family formed a dynasty of masons: his son Johannes succeeded him at Strasbourg, and his daughter Sabina is traditionally credited with sculpture on the south transept, though this is debated. Goethe's 1772 essay "On German Architecture" made Erwin a heroic symbol of the German Gothic genius, restoring his fame after centuries of obscurity; Goethe wrote that the cathedral façade unfolded before him "like a great work of God". A bust of Erwin appears among the notables in the Walhalla temple in Bavaria, and his name is commemorated across Strasbourg.
