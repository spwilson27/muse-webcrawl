The Sámi languages are a group of Uralic languages spoken by the Sámi people in Northern Europe, in the region of Sápmi spanning northern Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia's Kola Peninsula. They are closely related to the Finnic languages such as Finnish and Estonian.

Classification

The group divides into western and eastern branches. Western languages include Northern Sámi — by far the largest, with around 20,000 speakers — Lule Sámi, Pite Sámi, Ume Sámi and Southern Sámi. Eastern languages include Inari Sámi, Skolt Sámi, Akkala Sámi (now extinct) and Kildin Sámi.

Status

Most Sámi languages are endangered, a legacy of assimilation policies in the 19th and 20th centuries. Several have official status and their own written standards: nine Sámi languages have official written forms. Revitalization efforts include language nests, broadcasting (such as NRK Sápmi) and university teaching.
