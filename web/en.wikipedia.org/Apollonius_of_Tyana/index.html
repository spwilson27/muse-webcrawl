Apollonius of Tyana

Apollonius of Tyana (c. 3 BCE – c. 100 CE) was a Greek Neopythagorean philosopher and wandering teacher from the town of Tyana in Cappadocia, Anatolia. He travelled widely through the Roman Empire and beyond, reportedly as far as India, teaching an ascetic philosophy and performing wonders that later tradition magnified into miracles.

Life

The principal source is the Life of Apollonius by Philostratus, written in the early 3rd century at the request of the empress Julia Domna. According to Philostratus, Apollonius embraced the Pythagorean life: vegetarianism, silence for five years, celibacy and simple dress. He visited Babylon and India, debated with philosophers and kings, and on returning to the Greek world founded circles of disciples. Late in life he was summoned before the emperor Domitian on charges of sorcery and, the biography claims, vanished from the courtroom.

Reputation and legacy

In late antiquity Apollonius was venerated by some pagans as a holy man and miracle worker, and was sometimes set up as a pagan counterpart to Jesus, a comparison debated by Christian writers such as Eusebius. Modern scholars treat Philostratus's account as a blend of history, legend and philosophical romance.
