The London Mithraeum, also known as the Temple of Mithras, is a Roman mithraeum discovered in Walbrook, London, during rebuilding work after the Second World War. Built around AD 240 beside the River Walbrook, it was a temple of the mystery cult of Mithras, popular among Roman soldiers and merchants.

Discovery

The ruins were excavated in 1954 under W. F. Grimes; on the final day of the dig the sculpted head of Mithras was unearthed, drawing crowds of tens of thousands of visitors. Finds included fine marble sculptures now held by the Museum of London, among them a tauroctony relief and figures of Minerva, Mercury and Serapis.

Reconstruction

The temple was dismantled and rebuilt nearby in 1962. In 2010 the site was acquired by Bloomberg, and the mithraeum was returned close to its original location, opening to the public in 2017 as the London Mithraeum Bloomberg SPACE, set at Roman street level some seven metres below modern ground level.
