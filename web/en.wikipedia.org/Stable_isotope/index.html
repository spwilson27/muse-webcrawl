Stable isotope
Field: Nuclear chemistry, geochemistry
Property: Nucleus does not decay
Examples: Carbon-12, carbon-13, oxygen-16

A stable isotope is a nuclide that does not undergo radioactive decay. Stable isotopes of an element have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons, and their relative abundances can be measured precisely by mass spectrometry.

Applications

Ratios of stable isotopes (such as δ¹³C and δ¹⁸O) are used in isotope geochemistry to reconstruct past climates from ice cores and sediments, trace food webs in ecology, authenticate food and wine, and study metabolic processes in medicine.

Fractionation

Physical, chemical and biological processes slightly favour lighter or heavier isotopes — an effect called isotope fractionation — producing the measurable signatures exploited by scientists.
