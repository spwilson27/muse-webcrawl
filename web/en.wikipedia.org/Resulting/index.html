Resulting

Resulting is the present participle and adjective form of result, used for something that follows as a consequence of an event, action, process, or condition. In technical language, the word appears in several established concepts where the distinction between a process and the state or interest it produces is important.

In language

A resulting state is the condition that holds after a change of state. Linguistics discusses related meanings under the heading of resultative constructions, in which part of a sentence describes the state produced by the action of the verb. For example, in “She hammered the metal flat,” flat describes the state resulting from hammering. Theories of aspect analyse result states in relation to telicity, causation, and the internal structure of events.

In law

The best known legal usage is the resulting trust, an implied trust recognised in common-law systems. It may arise when one person supplies purchase money for property placed in another person's name, or when an express trust fails to dispose of the whole beneficial interest. The resulting trust is generally understood to return or “result back” the equitable interest to the person who provided the property or created the trust, subject to presumptions and evidence of intention.

In mathematics and science

Related forms such as resultant are more common. A resultant vector is the combined effect of several vectors, while the resultant of polynomials is a quantity that vanishes when they have a common root. In ordinary scientific writing, “resulting” modifies quantities, products, forces, mixtures, or states produced by a specified transformation.

The bare word is highly context-dependent. Technical readers usually identify the intended object from the noun it modifies or from a compound term such as resulting trust, resulting state, or resulting force.
