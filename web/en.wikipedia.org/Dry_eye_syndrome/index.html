Dry eye syndrome

Dry eye syndrome, also known as dry eye disease or keratoconjunctivitis sicca, is a multifactorial condition in which the eyes do not produce enough tears, or the tears evaporate too quickly, leading to inflammation and damage of the ocular surface. Symptoms include dryness, burning, a gritty or sandy sensation, redness, blurred vision, and sensitivity to light; paradoxically, reflex tearing may cause watery eyes.

Causes

The two main mechanisms are aqueous-deficient dry eye, from reduced tear production by the lacrimal glands (associated with ageing and autoimmune conditions such as Sjögren's syndrome), and evaporative dry eye, most often from meibomian gland dysfunction, in which the oily layer of the tear film is deficient. Risk factors include female sex, older age, contact lens wear, certain medications, and prolonged screen use that reduces blink rate.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines symptom questionnaires with tests such as tear break-up time, Schirmer's test, and ocular surface staining. Treatment is stepped: artificial tears and environmental changes first, then anti-inflammatory drops such as ciclosporin or lifitegrast, punctal plugs, warm compresses and lid hygiene for gland dysfunction, and in severe cases autologous serum drops or specialty contact lenses.
