Yonatan "Yoni" Netanyahu (Hebrew: יוֹנָתָן נְתַנְיָהוּ; 13 March 1946 – 4 July 1976) was an Israeli military officer who commanded the elite Sayeret Matkal commando unit during Operation Entebbe, the 1976 hostage-rescue mission at Entebbe Airport in Uganda. He was killed in action during the operation, the only Israeli commando to die, and is regarded as a national hero in Israel.

Career

Netanyahu, the elder brother of future prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu, grew up partly in the United States and enlisted in the IDF in 1964. He served with the Paratroopers, fought and was wounded in the Six-Day War, and later commanded Sayeret Matkal operations during the War of Attrition and the Yom Kippur War, in which he led his unit behind Syrian lines and was awarded the Medal of Distinguished Service.

Operation Entebbe and legacy

On 4 July 1976 Netanyahu led the assault team that stormed the terminal at Entebbe where over 100 hostages from a hijacked Air France flight were held; 102 hostages were rescued. He was mortally wounded by a Ugandan sniper. The operation was renamed Mivtsa Yonatan (Operation Jonathan) in his honour, and his published letters, The Letters of Jonathan Netanyahu, became a widely read work in Israel.
