Socca

Socca sold at a market stall in Nice

Socca is a traditional street food from Nice, in the south of France: a large, thin, unleavened pancake or crêpe made from chickpea flour, water, olive oil and salt, baked in a very hot oven — traditionally wood-fired — in a large round copper pan. It is served hot, cut into pieces and generously seasoned with black pepper.

Origins and variations

Socca belongs to a family of chickpea-flour flatbreads found around the western Mediterranean. In Liguria, Italy, the near-identical dish is called farinata; in Tuscany it is cecina, and in Gibraltar and Cádiz it is known as calentita or calentica. Legend in Nice attributes its arrival to Genoese sailors or to soldiers during sieges, though its precise history is uncertain.

Consumption

In Nice, socca is sold by street vendors and in markets such as the Cours Saleya, traditionally eaten with the fingers as an apéritif snack, often accompanied by rosé wine. The ideal socca is crisp and slightly charred at the edges and soft in the centre. Because it is made from chickpea flour, it is naturally gluten-free and vegan.
