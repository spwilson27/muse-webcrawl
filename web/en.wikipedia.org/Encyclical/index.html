Encyclical

An encyclical is a letter issued by a pope or bishop concerning Catholic doctrine, sent out to be widely circulated. The word derives from the Latin encyclia and the Greek egkyklios, meaning "circular" or "in a circle", and thus a general or circular letter. In modern usage the term is most associated with papal encyclicals, documents in which the Pope addresses the bishops of the world, or a particular subset, and through them the faithful, on matters of doctrine, morality, or social teaching.

Use in the Catholic Church

Papal encyclicals are titled by their opening Latin words. Benedict XIV issued what is often considered the first modern encyclical, Ubi primum, in 1740. Since the pontificate of Leo XIII, whose Rerum novarum (1891) addressed the condition of the working classes, encyclicals have been a principal vehicle of Catholic social teaching. Well-known examples include Pius XI's Quadragesimo anno (1931), John XXIII's Pacem in terris (1963), Paul VI's Humanae vitae (1968), John Paul II's Centesimus annus (1991), Benedict XVI's Deus caritas est (2005), and Francis's Laudato si' (2015) on the environment and Fratelli tutti (2020). Encyclicals rank among the most authoritative forms of ordinary papal teaching, below only solemn dogmatic definitions.

Other churches

The term is also used in Eastern Orthodoxy for letters issued by patriarchs or synods to their churches, and in the Anglican Communion the bishops assembled at the Lambeth Conference traditionally issued an encyclical letter at the close of each conference.
