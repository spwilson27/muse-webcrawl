Paul William Scott Anderson (born 4 March 1965) is an English film director, screenwriter, and producer. He is best known for directing science fiction action and horror films, in particular video game adaptations: he directed Mortal Kombat (1995), six films in the Resident Evil series (2002–2016), Alien vs. Predator (2004), and Monster Hunter (2020). The initials "W. S." distinguish him from the American director Paul Thomas Anderson. His films, though often poorly received by critics, have grossed over US$1.7 billion worldwide.

Early life and breakthrough

Anderson was born in Wallsend, Newcastle upon Tyne, and made amateur films from childhood. He studied at the University of Warwick and worked in British television before writing and directing his debut feature, Shopping (1994), a controversial drama starring Jude Law. Hollywood breakthrough came with Mortal Kombat (1995), adapted from the fighting game series; it topped the US box office and remains one of the better-regarded early video game films.

Career

Anderson followed with the science fiction horror film Event Horizon (1997), which underperformed on release but became a cult favourite, and the Kurt Russell action film Soldier (1998). In 2002 he wrote and directed Resident Evil, starring Milla Jovovich as Alice, and launched a franchise that ran for six films through Resident Evil: The Final Chapter (2016), becoming the highest-grossing film series based on a video game at the time, with over $1.2 billion in ticket sales.

Other works include Alien vs. Predator (2004), the remake Death Race (2008), The Three Musketeers (2011), the disaster romance Pompeii (2014), and Monster Hunter (2020), again starring Jovovich, whom he married in 2009. He has frequently acted as producer and writer on films directed by others, and co-created the television series Origin (2018).

Style and reception

Anderson is noted for elaborate production design, kinetic editing, and heavy use of slow motion and 3D in his later films. Critical reception has generally been negative, though some reviewers and scholars have praised his visual craftsmanship, and several of his films have been reassessed as cult works.
