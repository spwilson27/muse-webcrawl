Zenobia

Zenobia (c. 240 – c. 274 AD) was queen of the Palmyrene Empire in Syria during the third century AD. As regent for her young son Vaballathus, she conquered much of the Roman East before being defeated by the emperor Aurelian.

Rise to power

Zenobia was the wife of Odaenathus, the powerful ruler of Palmyra who had defended Rome's eastern frontier against Persia. After his assassination in 267, she ruled in the name of their son and pursued an expansionist policy, invading Roman Egypt and Anatolia and cutting off Rome's grain supply.

Defeat

The emperor Aurelian marched east in 272, defeated her armies at Antioch and Emesa, and besieged Palmyra. Zenobia was captured while fleeing toward Persia. According to some accounts she was displayed in Aurelian's triumph in Rome; later tradition holds that she lived out her life in Italy.

Legacy

Zenobia remains a national symbol in Syria and a celebrated figure in literature and opera, remembered for her learning, ambition, and defiance of Rome.
