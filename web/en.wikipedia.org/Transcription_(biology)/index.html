Transcription (biology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transcription
ProcessDNA → RNA synthesis
EnzymeRNA polymerase
LocationNucleus (eukaryotes); cytoplasm (prokaryotes)

Transcription is the process of copying a segment of DNA into RNA. The segment copied is typically a gene, and the RNA product may be messenger RNA (mRNA), which is later translated into protein, or a functional RNA such as transfer RNA or ribosomal RNA. Transcription is the first step of gene expression and is carried out by enzymes called RNA polymerases.

Stages

Initiation: transcription factors and RNA polymerase bind the promoter region of the gene, unwinding the DNA. Elongation: the polymerase moves along the template strand, adding complementary RNA nucleotides (with uracil in place of thymine) at roughly 40 nucleotides per second in eukaryotes. Termination: the polymerase is released at a terminator sequence and the RNA transcript is freed.

Eukaryotic processing

In eukaryotes, the initial pre-mRNA is modified by 5′ capping, addition of a poly(A) tail, and splicing that removes introns before export from the nucleus. Eukaryotes use three polymerases: Pol I for most ribosomal RNA, Pol II for mRNA, and Pol III for transfer and small RNAs.

Regulation

Cells regulate transcription through enhancers, silencers, and transcription factors, allowing different cell types to express different gene sets from the same genome. Reverse transcription, used by retroviruses, copies RNA back into DNA.
