First-class cricket

First-class cricket, along with List A cricket and Twenty20 cricket, is one of the highest-standard forms of cricket. A first-class match is one of three or more days' scheduled duration between two sides of eleven players each, officially adjudged to be worthy of the designation by virtue of the standard of the competing teams. Matches must allow for the teams to play two innings each, although in practice a team might play only one innings or none at all.

Competitions

First-class cricket includes Test matches and domestic competitions such as the County Championship in England, the Ranji Trophy in India, the Sheffield Shield in Australia and the Plunket Shield in New Zealand. Test cricket is its highest form, played between national representative teams.

History and status

The term came into general use in the late 19th century; the Imperial Cricket Conference formally defined first-class status in 1947, and retrospectively statisticians have classified matches as far back as the 17th and 18th centuries, with statistical first-class cricket generally held to begin in 1864. In 2021, the England and Wales Cricket Board's review led to women's domestic matches gaining first-class and List A status retrospectively in some cases, and the ICC has since worked to standardise the status of women's matches.
