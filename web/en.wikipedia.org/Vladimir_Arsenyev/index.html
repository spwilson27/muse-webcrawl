Vladimir Klavdiyevich Arsenyev (1872–1930) was a Russian explorer of the Far East, geographer, ethnographer and writer. A military officer in the Imperial Russian Army, he led numerous expeditions to explore and map the Sikhote-Alin mountains and the Ussuri region of Primorsky Krai between 1902 and 1927.

Explorations

Arsenyev's expeditions surveyed the geography, flora, fauna and indigenous peoples of the Russian Far East, including the Udege, Nanai and Oroch peoples. His guide on several journeys was the Nanai hunter Dersu Uzala, whose knowledge of the taiga Arsenyev documented with great respect.

Works

His books Po Ussuriyskomu Krayu (1921) and Dersu Uzala (1923) became classics of Russian travel literature. Dersu Uzala was adapted into films, most famously Akira Kurosawa's 1975 Soviet-Japanese production, which won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.

Legacy

The city of Arsenyev in Primorsky Krai, a museum in Vladivostok, glaciers and species are named after him.
