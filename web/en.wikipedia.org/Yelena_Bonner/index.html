Yelena Georgievna Bonner (Russian: Еле́на Гео́ргиевна Бо́ннэр; 15 February 1923 – 18 June 2011) was a Soviet and Russian human rights activist, dissident, and writer. She was the wife of physicist and dissident Andrei Sakharov, and a leading figure in the Soviet human rights movement.

Life

Born in Merv (now Mary, Turkmenistan), Bonner lost her father, a Comintern official, to the Great Purge in 1937; her mother was imprisoned in the Gulag. Bonner served as a nurse during the Second World War and was twice wounded. Trained as a paediatrician, she became involved in the dissident movement in the 1960s, co-founding the Moscow Helsinki Group in 1976. When Sakharov was exiled to Gorky in 1980, Bonner was his link to the outside world until she too was exiled there in 1984 for "anti-Soviet slander".

Later years

After Sakharov's death in 1989, Bonner preserved his legacy through the Andrei Sakharov Foundation and continued to campaign on human rights, criticising the Chechen wars and the government of Vladimir Putin. She spent her final years in Boston, Massachusetts, near her children, and died there in 2011.
