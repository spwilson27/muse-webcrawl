Tender offer

A tender offer is a public bid by a prospective acquirer to purchase some or all of the shares of a target company directly from its shareholders, at a specified price, usually at a premium over the current market price, and within a specified time window. Tender offers are a common mechanism in mergers and acquisitions and can be friendly (supported by the target's board) or hostile (opposed by it).

Regulation

In the United States, tender offers are regulated under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended by the Williams Act of 1968, administered by the Securities and Exchange Commission. Offerors must file a Schedule TO, offers must generally remain open for at least 20 business days, and shareholders receive withdrawal and proration rights. The target board must state its position on a Schedule 14D-9.

Types and defenses

Offers may be for cash, securities, or a combination, and may be conditioned on a minimum number of shares being tendered. In a two-step transaction, a successful tender is followed by a merger to acquire the remaining shares. Targets may deploy defenses such as the poison pill, staggered boards, or "Pac-Man" counteroffers, subject to fiduciary-duty review by the courts.
