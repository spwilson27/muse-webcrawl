Capital: Critique of Political Economy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Capital: A Critique of Political Economy (German: Das Kapital. Kritik der politischen Ökonomie), also known as Das Kapital, is a foundational theoretical text in materialist philosophy and political economy written by Karl Marx. The first volume was published in 1867; volumes II and III were edited by Friedrich Engels from Marx's manuscripts and published posthumously in 1885 and 1894.

Content

Volume I analyses the commodity, value, and the production of surplus value, arguing that the profits of capitalism derive from the unpaid labour of workers (labour-power) and describing processes of primitive accumulation and the accumulation of capital. Volume II examines the circulation of capital, and Volume III the distribution of surplus value as profit, rent, and interest, including the tendency of the rate of profit to fall.

Influence

Capital became the central theoretical reference of Marxist thought and a major influence on political economy, sociology, and labour movements worldwide. It has been praised as one of the most important books of the 19th century and criticised by mainstream economists on grounds including its labour theory of value.
