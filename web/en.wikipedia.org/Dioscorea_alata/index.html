Dioscorea alata

KingdomPlantae
OrderDioscoreales
FamilyDioscoreaceae
GenusDioscorea
SpeciesD. alata

Dioscorea alata — also known as the purple yam, ube, or greater yam — is a species of yam, a tuberous root vegetable. It is native to Southeast Asia and is one of the most widely cultivated yams in the world.

Description

The plant is a vigorous climbing vine with four-angled, winged stems. Its tubers can grow very large and many cultivars have vivid purple flesh owing to anthocyanin pigments; others are white or cream.

Cultivation and uses

D. alata was carried across the Pacific by Austronesian voyagers and later around the tropics, becoming a staple in Asia, the Pacific islands, West Africa and the Caribbean. In the Philippines, the purple type (ube) flavours ice cream, cakes and the dessert halo-halo. Wild forms can be bitter and toxic and require careful processing.

Invasive potential

In places such as Florida it has escaped cultivation and is listed as an invasive species, forming bulbils that spread readily.
