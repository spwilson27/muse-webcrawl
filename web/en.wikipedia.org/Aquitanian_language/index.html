Aquitanian language

The Aquitanian language was spoken in ancient Aquitaine, in the region between the Pyrenees and the Garonne River in what is now southwestern France, before and during the Roman period. It is known almost entirely from personal names and theonyms preserved in Latin inscriptions of the first to third centuries CE.

Relationship to Basque

Aquitanian is widely regarded as an ancestor of, or a close relative to, the Basque language. Many Aquitanian name elements correspond strikingly to Basque words: for example, Andere (Basque andere, "woman, lady"), Nescato (Basque neskato, "girl"), and elements resembling Basque words for "son", "wolf" and "good". Aquitanian is thus the only well-attested pre-Indo-European language of western Europe with a living descendant.

Decline

Under Roman rule the language gradually gave way to Latin, though it survived long enough in the countryside to shape the development of Gascon and to leave a substrate in place names across the region.
