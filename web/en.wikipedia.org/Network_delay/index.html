Network delay

Network delay is a design and performance characteristic of a telecommunications network. It specifies the time it takes for a bit of data to travel across the network from one node or endpoint to another, and is usually measured in multiples or fractions of a second.

Components

Total delay is the sum of several components. Processing delay is the time a router takes to examine a packet header and decide where to send it. Queuing delay is the time a packet waits in a queue before being transmitted. Transmission delay is the time required to push all of a packet's bits onto the link, determined by packet size and link bandwidth. Propagation delay is the time a signal takes to travel through the medium, bounded by the speed of light.

Significance

Delay, along with packet loss and throughput, determines the quality of service of a network. Interactive applications such as voice calls and online games are highly sensitive to delay and its variation (jitter), while bulk transfers tolerate it well.
