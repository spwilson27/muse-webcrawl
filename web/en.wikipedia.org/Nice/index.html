Nice (French: [nis]; Niçard: Niça) is a city in southern France on the Mediterranean coast, the prefecture of the Alpes-Maritimes department and the second-largest French city on the Mediterranean after Marseille. It is the principal city of the French Riviera (Côte d'Azur) and a major tourist destination, known for its mild climate, the Baie des Anges, and the seafront Promenade des Anglais.

History

Nice was founded around 350 BC by Greek settlers from Massalia (Marseille), who named it Nikaia after Nike, goddess of victory. It later became a Roman town (neighbouring Cemenelum, now Cimiez), and in the Middle Ages passed among Provence, Savoy and Genoa. As part of the Kingdom of Sardinia it was ceded to France in 1860 by the Treaty of Turin, a transfer ratified by a local plebiscite. From the late eighteenth century, first British and then European high society wintered in Nice, giving the resort its promenade and grand hotels such as the Negresco; Queen Victoria stayed at Cimiez. In 2021 UNESCO inscribed "Nice, Winter Resort Town of the Riviera" as a World Heritage Site.

Geography and sights

The old town (Vieux Nice) of narrow lanes and Italianate façades sits below Castle Hill, which separates the city from its port. Landmarks include the Cours Saleya flower market, Nice Cathedral, the Matisse and Chagall museums, and the Russian Orthodox Cathedral. Nice Carnival, first mentioned in 1294, is among the world's oldest and largest. Local cuisine includes salade niçoise, socca (chickpea crêpe), pissaladière and ratatouille (of Provençal origin). The city was the site of a terrorist attack on 14 July 2016, when a truck was driven into crowds on the promenade, killing 86 people.
