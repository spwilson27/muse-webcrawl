Adityas

GroupSolar deities (devas)
ParentsAditi and Kashyapa
Members (later list)Varuna, Mitra, Aryaman, Bhaga, Daksha, Amsha, Surya, and others; twelve in total in later texts
TraditionVedic religion, Hinduism

In Hindu mythology, the Adityas (Sanskrit: आदित्य, "sons of Aditi") are a group of solar deities who are the offspring of the goddess Aditi and the sage Kashyapa. In the Rigveda, where they are usually seven or eight in number, they are led by Varuna and Mitra and are associated with the maintenance of cosmic and moral order (rita), light, and sovereignty. Among the Vedic Adityas are Mitra, Aryaman, Bhaga, Varuna, Daksha and Amsha.

In later Hindu texts the number of Adityas is standardised at twelve, corresponding to the twelve months of the year and the twelve signs of the zodiac, and they become identified increasingly with the Sun itself; Vishnu is counted among them in his dwarf avatar Vamana. The sun god Surya is referred to as Aditya as well, and the Gayatri mantra is addressed to the solar deity Savitr, another of Aditi's sons.

Development of the concept

In the earliest Vedic hymns the Adityas are moral guardians who watch over truth, punish transgression and release supplicants from sin and disease. Varuna binds wrongdoers with his nooses, while Mitra oversees compacts and friendship. As Hinduism developed, the individual prominence of most Adityas declined — Varuna becoming chiefly a god of waters — while their collective identity merged with solar worship. A parallel group, the Adityas of Iranian tradition (such as Mithra), reflects the common Indo-Iranian heritage of these deities.
