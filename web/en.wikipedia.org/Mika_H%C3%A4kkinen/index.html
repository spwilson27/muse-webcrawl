Mika Pauli Häkkinen (born 28 September 1968) is a Finnish former racing driver who won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship in 1998 and 1999 with McLaren. Nicknamed "the Flying Finn," he is regarded as one of the fastest drivers of his era and was Michael Schumacher's principal rival at the turn of the century; Schumacher himself described Häkkinen as the opponent he most respected.

Career

Häkkinen entered Formula One with Lotus in 1991 and moved to McLaren in 1993, initially as a test driver before replacing Michael Andretti mid-season. A near-fatal crash in qualifying at the 1995 Australian Grand Prix left him in a coma, but he recovered to win his first Grand Prix at Jerez in 1997. With the Adrian Newey-designed McLaren MP4/13, he won eight races in 1998 to take his first title, and retained it in 1999 in a close contest with Eddie Irvine.

Later career and retirement

After losing the 2000 championship to Schumacher and enduring a disappointing 2001 season, Häkkinen announced a sabbatical that became permanent retirement from Formula One. He later raced in the DTM touring car series (2005–2007) and in GT competition, and has worked as a driver manager, ambassador, and commentator.

Legacy

Häkkinen's qualifying speed — 26 pole positions — and his overtaking move on Schumacher at the 2000 Belgian Grand Prix, passing around the lapped Ricardo Zonta on the Kemmel straight, are frequently cited among Formula One's finest moments.
