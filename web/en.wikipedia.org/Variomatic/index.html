Variomatic

Type: Continuously variable transmission
Manufacturer: DAF
Introduced: 1958 (DAF 600)
Inventor: Hub van Doorne

Variomatic is the continuously variable transmission (CVT) developed by Hub van Doorne and used by the Dutch car manufacturer DAF from 1958. It was the first CVT to be used in production cars, debuting in the DAF 600.

Design

The Variomatic uses two pairs of conical pulleys connected by rubber V-belts. One pulley of each pair can change its effective diameter under the control of engine vacuum and centrifugal weights, so the transmission ratio varies steplessly. Because each rear wheel was driven by its own belt, the system also acted as a limited-slip differential. A characteristic of the system was that a DAF could in principle drive as fast in reverse as forwards, a quirk exploited in Dutch "reverse racing" events.

Legacy

After Volvo acquired DAF's car division in the 1970s, the Variomatic continued in the Volvo 340 series. Van Doorne's company, Van Doorne's Transmissie (later Bosch Transmission Technology), went on to develop the steel pushbelt CVT used in many modern cars.
