Braemar Castle

Braemar Castle is a five-storey L-plan tower house in Aberdeenshire, Scotland, near the village of Braemar. It was built in 1628 by John Erskine, Earl of Mar, as a hunting lodge and stronghold in the Highlands.

The castle was burned during the Jacobite rising of 1689 and rebuilt; after the 1745 rising it was garrisoned by government troops, acquiring its curtain wall with star-shaped defences. In the 19th century it became associated with the Farquharson family of Invercauld and with royal visits, lying not far from Balmoral.

Since 2007 the castle has been leased to and operated by the local community through the Braemar Community charity, which runs it as a visitor attraction — an early example of a Scottish castle in community management.
