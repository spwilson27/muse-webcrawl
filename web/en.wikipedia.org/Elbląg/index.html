Elbląg

Elbląg (Polish: [ˈɛlblɔŋk]; German: Elbing) is a city in northern Poland, in the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship, with a population of around 115,000. It lies on the Elbląg River, close to the Vistula Lagoon and about 55 km southeast of Gdańsk. Founded in the 13th century by the Teutonic Order, it became an important Baltic trading port and a member of the Hanseatic League.

History

Near the later city lay the Viking-age trading settlement of Truso, visited by the traveller Wulfstan around 890. The Teutonic Knights built a castle here in 1237 and founded the town, which received Lübeck law in 1246. Elbląg joined the Prussian Confederation against the Order in 1454 and came under the Polish Crown by the Second Peace of Thorn (1466). It was a major port for English and Dutch trade in the 16th and 17th centuries, though silting of the Vistula Lagoon later reduced its importance.

After the First Partition of Poland (1772) the city passed to Prussia and later Germany, where it was known for the Schichau shipyards and locomotive works. Heavily destroyed in 1945, Elbląg became part of Poland after the Second World War and its German population was expelled. Workers from the city joined the 1970 Polish protests.

Sights and economy

The Old Town, destroyed in 1945, has been rebuilt since the 1990s in a distinctive "retroversion" style, with modern buildings echoing the original plots and façades. Landmarks include St. Nicholas Cathedral and the Market Gate (Brama Targowa). The Elbląg Canal, built in the 19th century, is famous for its system of inclined planes that carry boats over land. Today the city has industries including turbines, brewing and furniture, and a river port.
