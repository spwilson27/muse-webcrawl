A ballade (French: [balad]) is a form of medieval and Renaissance French poetry, one of the formes fixes along with the rondeau and the virelai. It was frequently set to music in the 14th and 15th centuries.

Form

The classic ballade consists of three stanzas of the same form, each ending with an identical refrain line, followed by a short concluding envoi addressed to a prince or patron. Stanzas are typically of seven, eight or ten lines with a consistent rhyme scheme and syllable count.

History

Guillaume de Machaut composed over 200 ballades. The form flourished with Eustache Deschamps, Christine de Pizan and Charles d'Orléans, and reached a late peak in the work of François Villon, whose "Ballade des pendus" is among its most famous examples. In English, Geoffrey Chaucer wrote ballades, and the form was revived by 19th-century poets including Dante Gabriel Rossetti.

Musical ballade

The term was later applied to instrumental music: Frédéric Chopin's four Ballades for piano are large-scale narrative works without a fixed formal pattern, and Johannes Brahms and others followed his example.
