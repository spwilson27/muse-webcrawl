Group Theatre (New York City)

The Group Theatre was an American theater collective based in New York City and formed in 1931 by Harold Clurman, Cheryl Crawford and Lee Strasberg. It existed until 1941 and is widely regarded as one of the most influential theatre companies in American history, pioneering an ensemble approach to acting based on the Stanislavski system and producing socially engaged plays, most famously the works of Clifford Odets.

History

The Group grew out of the Theatre Guild and gathered some twenty-eight actors for a summer of training in 1931. Its first production was Paul Green's The House of Connelly (1931). The company's breakthrough came with Clifford Odets's one-act Waiting for Lefty (1935), a pro-union agitprop piece whose premiere provoked a sensation, followed by Odets's Awake and Sing! the same year. Other successes included Sidney Kingsley's Men in White (1933), which won the Pulitzer Prize, and Irwin Shaw's The Gentle People.

Method and influence

Lee Strasberg's teaching, drawn from Konstantin Stanislavski, emphasised emotional memory and psychological truth; disagreements over interpretation — notably Stella Adler's after her 1934 study with Stanislavski in Paris — shaped the later development of "the Method". After the Group disbanded in 1941, its members went on to found or teach at the Actors Studio and other institutions, profoundly influencing American acting on stage and screen.

Legacy

The Group Theatre is credited with creating the template for ensemble theatre companies in the United States and with bringing serious social drama to the American stage during the Great Depression.
