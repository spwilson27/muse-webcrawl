Han Feizi

Bornc. 280 BCE, State of Han
Died233 BCE, State of Qin
SchoolLegalism (Fajia)
Major workHan Feizi (book)

Han Fei (c. 280 – 233 BCE), often known as Han Feizi, was a Chinese philosopher and statesman of the State of Han during the late Warring States period. He is regarded as the greatest exponent of Legalism, synthesising the ideas of earlier thinkers into a systematic philosophy of statecraft.

Life

A prince of Han, Han Fei studied under the Confucian Xunzi alongside Li Si. Said to have had a speech impediment, he expressed his ideas in writing, sending memorials to the king of Han that went unheeded. His writings impressed the king of Qin, the future Qin Shi Huang, but at Qin court intrigue led by Li Si resulted in Han Fei's imprisonment and death, traditionally by forced suicide.

Thought

The text named after him, the Han Feizi, combines three strands of Legalism: fa (law and standards), shu (administrative techniques), and shi (the power of position). It argues that a ruler should govern through clear laws, rewards and punishments, and institutional authority rather than personal virtue or moral exhortation. His philosophy strongly influenced the Qin dynasty's governing practice.
