Carl von Clausewitz

Carl von Clausewitz
Born: June 1, 1780, Burg bei Magdeburg, Prussia
Died: November 16, 1831, Breslau, Prussia
Occupation: Soldier, military theorist
Notable work: Vom Kriege (On War)

Carl Philipp Gottfried von Clausewitz (June 1, 1780 – November 16, 1831) was a Prussian general and military theorist who stressed the moral, psychological, and political aspects of waging war. His most important work, Vom Kriege (translated as On War), was unfinished at his death and published posthumously by his wife Marie in 1832. It remains one of the most influential works of military strategy ever written.

Life and career

Clausewitz joined the Prussian Army as a young ensign and fought in the Rhine campaigns of the French Revolutionary Wars. He attended the War Academy in Berlin, where he became a protégé of Gerhard von Scharnhorst. He fought at Jena–Auerstedt (1806), was captured by the French, and later worked on the reform of the Prussian army. Resigning in 1812 over Prussia's alliance with Napoleon, he served in the Russian army during the French invasion of Russia, then rejoined Prussian service and served as chief of staff of a corps at Waterloo. From 1818 until 1830 he was director of the War Academy in Berlin, where he wrote most of his great work. He died of cholera in 1831 while serving as chief of staff to Gneisenau during the Polish uprising.

On War

Clausewitz argued that war is "merely the continuation of policy by other means" — the famous dictum that war must always be subordinated to political purpose. He analysed war through concepts including the "fog of war" (the uncertainty of information), "friction" (the accumulation of obstacles that distinguish real war from war on paper), the "culminating point" of an attack, and the "remarkable trinity" of passion (the people), chance and probability (the army), and reason (the government). His work was later taken up, sometimes selectively, by strategists and political leaders from Moltke to Lenin and Mao, and remains central to military education worldwide.
