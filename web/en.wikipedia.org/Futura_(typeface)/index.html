Futura (typeface)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Futura is a geometric sans-serif typeface designed by Paul Renner and released by the Bauer Type Foundry in 1927. Based on simple geometric forms — near-perfect circles, triangles, and straight lines — it became one of the emblematic typefaces of twentieth-century modernism and of the Bauhaus era, and it remains widely used in branding, signage, and film.

Design

Renner, who was not himself a Bauhaus member, pursued the same goal of a form stripped of historical ornament. Futura's lowercase letters have tall ascenders, a single-storey "a" with a nearly circular bowl, and an "M" with vertical sides and a vertex that does not reach the baseline. Its capitals are modelled on Roman inscriptional proportions. To keep the face legible at text sizes, Renner departed from pure geometry in details such as the narrowed "o" and non-circular curves.

History and usage

Futura was an immediate commercial success in Europe and, after its import to the United States, in American advertising and publishing. It was the typeface of the plaque left on the Moon by Apollo 11 in 1969 and of NASA-related graphics. Directors Stanley Kubrick and Wes Anderson made it a signature. IKEA used Futura for its catalogue and logo until 2009, and Volkswagen used a customised Futura for decades.

Digital versions

Numerous foundries sell digital revivals; because "Futura" is a trademark, similar designs have been sold under other names. Futura PT (ParaType) and the URW version are common. The design's geometric model influenced later faces including Century Gothic and Apple's former corporate font.
