Santa Cruz, California

Santa Cruz is a city in Northern California and the county seat of Santa Cruz County, located on the northern edge of Monterey Bay about 74 miles (119 km) south of San Francisco. Its population was about 63,000 at the 2020 census. It is known for its coastline, redwood forests, surfing culture, and the University of California, Santa Cruz.

History

The area was inhabited for thousands of years by the Awaswas-speaking Ohlone people. Mission Santa Cruz, founded in 1791, was the twelfth of the Spanish missions in California; the adjacent pueblo of Branciforte was founded in 1797. After Mexican independence and U.S. annexation, the town grew on logging, lime production, and fishing. The Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk, opened in 1907 with its Giant Dipper wooden roller coaster, became a landmark seaside amusement park. The city was heavily damaged by the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake, whose epicenter was nearby.

Culture and economy

Santa Cruz is closely associated with surfing — mainland surfing in the United States is often traced to Hawaiian princes riding redwood boards at the San Lorenzo river mouth in 1885 — and Steamer Lane is a famous break. The city has a reputation for progressive politics and alternative culture. Major employers include the university, tourism, and organic agriculture; nearby redwood state parks such as Henry Cowell Redwoods draw many visitors.
