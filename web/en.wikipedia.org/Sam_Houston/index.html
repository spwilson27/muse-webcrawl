Samuel Houston (2 March 1793 – 26 July 1863) was an American statesman and soldier who played a central role in the creation of the state of Texas. He led the Texian Army to victory over Mexico at the Battle of San Jacinto in 1836, securing the independence of the Republic of Texas, and twice served as the republic's president. He is the only person in American history to have served as governor of two different states (Tennessee and Texas), and also served as a US representative and senator.

Early life

Houston was born in Virginia and moved with his widowed mother and siblings to Tennessee in 1807. As a teenager he ran away to live with the Cherokee, whose chief John Jolly adopted him and gave him the name Colonneh, "the Raven". He served under Andrew Jackson in the War of 1812 and was wounded at the Battle of Horseshoe Bend in 1814. With Jackson's patronage he studied law, entered Tennessee politics, and was elected to the US House of Representatives in 1823 and as governor of Tennessee in 1827.

In 1829 his brief marriage to Eliza Allen collapsed amid scandal, and Houston resigned the governorship and returned to the Cherokee, drinking heavily and taking a Cherokee wife, Tiana Rogers. He later rebuilt his public life, including a notorious 1832 incident in which he caned Congressman William Stanbery on a Washington street after Stanbery impugned his honesty.

Texas Revolution

Houston settled in Mexican Texas in 1832. When the Texas Revolution began in 1835 he was made commander-in-chief of the Texian Army. After the fall of the Alamo and the Goliad massacre, he conducted a controversial retreat eastward, pursued by the Mexican general Santa Anna. On 21 April 1836, at the Battle of San Jacinto, Houston's roughly 900 men surprised and routed Santa Anna's larger force in about eighteen minutes, shouting "Remember the Alamo!" Houston, wounded in the ankle, captured Santa Anna the following day and compelled him to order Mexican troops out of Texas.

Republic and statehood

Houston was elected the first president of the Republic of Texas in 1836 and served two non-consecutive terms (1836–38 and 1841–44), balancing relations with Mexico, the United States and Native American nations while struggling with the republic's finances. When Texas was annexed by the United States in 1845 he became one of its first two US senators, serving until 1859 and distinguishing himself as a Unionist who opposed the sectional agitation leading toward civil war.

Governor of Texas and final years

Elected governor of Texas in 1859, Houston refused, when Texas seceded in 1861, to swear allegiance to the Confederate States of America, and was deposed by the state secession convention — the only southern governor removed for refusing to join the Confederacy. He retired to Huntsville, Texas, where he died in 1863.

Legacy

The city of Houston, founded in 1836, was named in his honour, as were counties, schools, a national forest and Sam Houston State University. A 67-foot statue of Houston near Huntsville, erected in 1994, is among the tallest statues of an American figure.
