Diyya

Diyya (Arabic: دية, plural diyāt) is compensation paid to the heirs of a victim in Islamic law. In Islamic tradition, the diyya is a form of financial restitution for homicide or bodily injury when the victim's family chooses compensation over retaliation (qisas).

Basis in Islamic law

The Quran prescribes the payment of diyya for unintentional killing, and classical jurisprudence developed detailed schedules of amounts for death and various injuries, traditionally reckoned in camels or their value in gold or silver. The standard diyya for a free adult male was set at 100 camels, or equivalent amounts of 1,000 dinars of gold or 12,000 dirhams of silver in classical reckonings.

Modern practice

Diyya remains part of the legal systems of several countries, including Saudi Arabia, Iran, Pakistan, and the United Arab Emirates, where courts may set amounts and the payment can avert or mitigate criminal punishment for certain offenses, particularly in cases of manslaughter and traffic deaths.
