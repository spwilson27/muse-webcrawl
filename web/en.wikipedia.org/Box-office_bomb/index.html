Box-office bomb

A box-office bomb, or box-office flop, is a film that is unprofitable or considered highly unsuccessful during its theatrical run, losing money for the studio, distributors, or investors. Because theaters keep roughly half of ticket revenue and marketing costs are substantial, a film generally must gross well over twice its production budget to break even.

Notorious bombs include Heaven's Gate (1980), which helped sink United Artists; Cutthroat Island (1995); Battlefield Earth (2000); and John Carter (2012), which prompted a writedown of about $200 million for Disney. Some bombs later gain cult followings or recoup their costs through home media, while others bankrupted studios, as The Adventures of Baron Munchausen and Carolco's failures illustrate.

A bomb contrasts with a sleeper hit, a film with modest opening that succeeds over time.
