Xi Jinping (born 15 June 1953) is a Chinese politician who has served as general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and chairman of the Central Military Commission since 2012, and as president of China since 2013. He is the paramount leader of China and is widely regarded as its most powerful leader since Mao Zedong and Deng Xiaoping.

Early life and rise

Xi is the son of Xi Zhongxun, a Communist revolutionary veteran. During the Cultural Revolution his father was purged, and Xi was sent to work in the village of Liangjiahe in Shaanxi as a "sent-down youth". He joined the CCP in 1974, studied chemical engineering at Tsinghua University, and rose through provincial posts in Fujian and Zhejiang before becoming party chief of Shanghai in 2007 and vice president in 2008.

Leadership

As leader, Xi launched a sweeping anti-corruption campaign, centralised decision-making, abolished presidential term limits in 2018, and enshrined "Xi Jinping Thought" in the party and state constitutions. His signature foreign-policy initiative, the Belt and Road Initiative, expanded China's global infrastructure investment. His tenure has also seen intensified tensions with the United States, a security crackdown in Hong Kong, and assertive policies toward Taiwan — issues frequently discussed alongside other 21st-century conflicts such as the War in Donbas.
