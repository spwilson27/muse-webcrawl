Lewis Carroll

Born: Charles Lutwidge Dodgson, 27 January 1832, Daresbury, Cheshire
Died: 14 January 1898, Guildford, Surrey
Occupation: Author, mathematician, logician, photographer, Anglican deacon

Charles Lutwidge Dodgson (27 January 1832 – 14 January 1898), better known by his pen name Lewis Carroll, was an English author, poet, mathematician, logician, photographer, and Anglican deacon. His most notable works are Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (1865) and its sequel Through the Looking-Glass (1871), among the most famous works of English-language fiction.

Life

Dodgson was educated at Rugby School and Christ Church, Oxford, where he became a lecturer in mathematics. He was ordained a deacon in 1861 but never took full priestly orders. The Alice books originated in stories told to Alice Liddell and her sisters during a boat trip on the Thames in 1862.

Works

Carroll was noted for his facility with word play, logic, and fantasy. His poems include "Jabberwocky" and The Hunting of the Snark (1876). As C. L. Dodgson he published works on mathematics and logic, including Symbolic Logic, and he was an accomplished portrait photographer, particularly of children.
