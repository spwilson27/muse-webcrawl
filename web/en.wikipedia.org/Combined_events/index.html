Combined events

Combined events are competitions in athletics (track and field) in which athletes contest a series of different events over one or two days, earning points for each performance according to scoring tables; the athlete with the highest total wins. The best-known combined events are the men's decathlon and the women's heptathlon contested at the Olympic Games.

Principal competitions

The decathlon comprises ten events: 100 metres, long jump, shot put, high jump, and 400 metres on the first day, then 110 metres hurdles, discus throw, pole vault, javelin throw, and 1500 metres. The heptathlon has seven: 100 metres hurdles, high jump, shot put, and 200 metres, followed by long jump, javelin throw, and 800 metres. Indoor championships use shortened versions — the men's heptathlon and women's pentathlon — and an outdoor women's decathlon is increasingly contested.

Scoring

Performances are converted to points with formulas devised by World Athletics that reward world-class marks progressively, so that improvements at the highest level earn disproportionately more points. A decathlon total above 9,000 points is considered world record class; the heptathlon benchmark is 7,000 points.

History

Combined events descend from the all-around competitions of the ancient Olympic pentathlon and were formalised at the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, where Jim Thorpe won both the pentathlon and decathlon and was hailed as the world's greatest athlete.
