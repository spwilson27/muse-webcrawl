Lychee

Lychee fruits with their rough red rind and translucent white flesh.

The lychee (Litchi chinensis; also spelled litchi) is a tropical fruit tree native to southern China, in the provinces of Guangdong, Fujian, and Yunnan. It is the sole member of the genus Litchi in the soapberry family, Sapindaceae. The tree bears small fleshy fruits with a fragrant, sweet, translucent pulp.

Description

The lychee is an evergreen tree reaching 15–28 metres in height, with pinnate leaves. Its fruit is a drupe about 3–4 cm long, covered by a rough, brittle, pink-red rind that is easily peeled to reveal the edible white aril surrounding a single dark seed.

Cultivation and history

Lychees have been cultivated in China for over 2,000 years and were a favourite of the Tang dynasty court; legend tells of lychees being relayed at great speed to the capital for the consort Yang Guifei. The fruit spread across Southeast Asia and to India, Madagascar, South Africa, Australia, and Florida. Fresh lychees are highly perishable, so they are also sold canned, dried, or frozen.

Culinary use

Lychees are eaten fresh and used in desserts, jellies, drinks, and sorbets. They are rich in vitamin C and polyphenols. The seeds and unripe fruit contain hypoglycin-related compounds and should not be eaten in quantity.
