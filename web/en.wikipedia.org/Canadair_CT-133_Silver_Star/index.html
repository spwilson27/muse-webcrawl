Canadair CT-133 Silver Star

The Canadair CT-133 Silver Star (known within the company as the CL-30) is the Canadian licence-built version of the Lockheed T-33 jet trainer aircraft, in service from the 1950s to 2005. A total of 656 CT-133 aircraft were built by Canadair.

Design and development

The CT-133 was powered by a Rolls-Royce Nene 10 turbojet instead of the Allison J33 used in the American T-33, giving it somewhat greater thrust. The aircraft entered service with the Royal Canadian Air Force in 1953 as its standard advanced jet trainer and later served in roles including target towing, electronic warfare training, and communications.

Operational history

Known affectionately as the "T-Bird", the Silver Star remained in Canadian service for more than fifty years — far longer than originally planned — because of its reliability and low operating cost. It was finally retired by the Canadian Forces in 2005, although a small number continued to fly with civilian contractors and the National Research Council. Silver Stars also served with the air forces of several other nations, including France, Greece, Portugal, Turkey, and Bolivia.
