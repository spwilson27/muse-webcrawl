Herne Bay is a seaside town in Kent, south-east England, on the south shore of the Thames Estuary, within the City of Canterbury district. It developed as a fashionable Victorian bathing resort and retains a two-mile seafront, a pier, and one of the first freestanding purpose-built clock towers in the United Kingdom.

History

The hamlet of Herne Bay grew around an inn serving travellers to the nearby village of Herne. From the 1830s a speculation by London builders created a planned resort with a promenade, parade of lodging houses, and a wooden pier, boosted by paddle-steamer traffic from London and, from 1861, the railway. Its clock tower was erected in 1837, funded by Ann Thwaytes, a London grocer's widow.

Pier

The town's pier was rebuilt several times; the third pier, opened in 1899, was once the second longest in the country. Storm damage in 1978 destroyed its central section, leaving the pier head isolated in the sea, though a shorter pier survives and remains a local landmark.

Second World War

Offshore from Herne Bay, Barnes Wallis's bouncing bomb was tested in 1943 in the waters between the town and Reculver, where a prototype recovered from the seabed is displayed in the local museum. The Maunsell sea forts still stand in the estuary offshore.
