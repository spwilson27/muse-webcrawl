Link Trainer

The Link Trainer, also known as the Link Basic Trainer and by the wartime nickname "Blue Box", was an early flight simulator invented by Edwin Albert Link. Patented in 1929 (filed after his earlier work with pneumatic organs), it used bellows and vacuum-driven mechanisms taken from the Link family's pipe-organ business to move a small fuselage-like cockpit in response to the controls.

Use in training

The device allowed pilots to practise instrument flying on the ground: the trainee sat under a hood while an instructor at a desk tracked the simulated flight path with a motorised "crab" on a chart. Initially sold to amusement parks and only slowly adopted, the trainer's fortunes changed in 1934 when the US Army Air Corps, suffering fatal accidents flying the mail in bad weather, ordered trainers for instrument instruction.

Wartime production and legacy

During the Second World War, Link trainers were produced in the tens of thousands in the United States and under licence abroad, and over half a million Allied aircrew received some instruction in them. The Link Trainer is regarded as the ancestor of the modern flight simulator industry.
