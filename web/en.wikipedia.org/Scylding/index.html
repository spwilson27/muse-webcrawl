Scylding

The Scyldings (Old English: Scyldingas; Old Norse: Skjöldungar) were a legendary royal dynasty of Denmark, named for the mythical king Scyld (Skjöldr), and prominent in Germanic heroic legend and early Scandinavian tradition.

In Beowulf

The Old English epic Beowulf opens with the funeral of Scyld Scefing, the dynasty's founder, whose body is set adrift in a ship laden with treasure. His descendants include Hrothgar, the king whose great hall, Heorot, is attacked by the monster Grendel and defended by the hero Beowulf. The poem treats the Scyldings as the exemplary royal house of the Danes.

In Norse tradition

In Icelandic sources such as the Skjöldunga saga and Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, the Skjöldungar are the ancient kings of Denmark, said to descend from Skjöldr, a son of Odin. Later Danish kings claimed descent from the line to legitimise their rule.

Historicity

Some figures associated with the dynasty, such as Hrothgar and his nephews, also appear in Scandinavian sources and may preserve memories of real Migration Age rulers, though the dynasty as presented in legend is largely mythical.
