Foreign portfolio investment

Foreign portfolio investors buy securities abroad without taking control of the issuer.

Foreign portfolio investment (FPI) is the purchase of securities and financial assets of a foreign country by investors who do not obtain control over the issuing enterprise. It includes investment in stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and depositary receipts traded abroad. FPI contrasts with foreign direct investment (FDI), in which an investor acquires a lasting interest and a degree of control — conventionally a stake of 10% or more of voting power — in a foreign enterprise.

Characteristics

Portfolio investment is motivated by returns — interest, dividends, and capital gains — and by portfolio diversification, rather than by managing a business. Because portfolio assets are usually traded on organized exchanges, they are highly liquid: investors can enter and exit quickly. This liquidity makes FPI flows volatile and sensitive to interest-rate differentials, exchange-rate expectations, and shifts in global risk appetite; sudden reversals ("sudden stops") have contributed to financial crises, as in the Asian financial crisis of 1997–98.

Measurement

In balance-of-payments statistics compiled under the IMF's framework, portfolio investment is recorded as a category of the financial account, divided into equity and investment fund shares on the one hand and debt securities on the other. The 10% voting-power threshold separates portfolio equity from direct investment equity.

Policy issues

Governments weigh the benefits of FPI — deeper capital markets, lower cost of capital, improved price discovery, and pressure for better corporate governance — against risks of volatility and contagion. Some countries have imposed taxes or controls on short-term inflows, such as Brazil's former IOF tax on foreign fixed-income purchases. In India, foreign portfolio investors (formerly "foreign institutional investors") are a major presence in equity and bond markets and are regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), with investment limits applying in government and corporate debt.
