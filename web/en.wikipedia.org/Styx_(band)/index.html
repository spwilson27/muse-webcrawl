Styx (band)

Styx performing in concert.

Styx is an American rock band formed in Chicago, Illinois, in 1972. They are best known for melding hard rock guitar balanced with acoustic guitar, synthesizers mixed with acoustic piano, upbeat tracks with power ballads, and incorporating elements of international musical theatre. The band established themselves with a progressive rock sound in the 1970s, and began to incorporate pop rock and soft rock elements in the 1980s.

Career

The band's classic line-up featured Dennis DeYoung, James "J.Y." Young, Tommy Shaw, Chuck Panozzo and John Panozzo. After early success with the single "Lady" (1975), Styx achieved a run of four consecutive multi-platinum albums: The Grand Illusion (1977), Pieces of Eight (1978), Cornerstone (1979) and Paradise Theatre (1981), the last reaching number one on the Billboard 200. Well-known songs include "Come Sail Away", "Renegade", "Blue Collar Man (Long Nights)", "Babe" and "Mr. Roboto", from the 1983 concept album Kilroy Was Here.

Internal tensions, particularly over musical direction between DeYoung and Shaw/Young, led to breakups and reformations; DeYoung was dismissed in 1999 and replaced by Lawrence Gowan. The band has continued to tour and record, releasing albums including Cyclorama (2003), The Mission (2017) and Crash of the Crown (2021). Styx has sold more than 54 million records worldwide.
