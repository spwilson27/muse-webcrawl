Sir Anthony van Dyck (22 March 1599 – 9 December 1641) was a Flemish Baroque artist who became the leading court painter in England under Charles I. A child prodigy and the most important pupil of Peter Paul Rubens, he is celebrated for his graceful, elegant portraits of the European aristocracy, as well as religious and mythological paintings.

Early career

Born in Antwerp to a prosperous silk merchant's family, van Dyck was painting professionally in his teens and became a master in the Antwerp Guild of St Luke in 1618. He worked closely with Rubens, whose influence is visible in his early religious works. In the 1620s he spent several years in Italy — chiefly Genoa — where the art of Titian shaped his mature style and his portraits of Genoese nobles set the pattern for his later portraiture.

Court painter in England

After periods at the courts of Brussels and The Hague, van Dyck settled in London in 1632 as principal painter to Charles I, who knighted him and granted him a pension. His portraits of the king, Queen Henrietta Maria and the Caroline court — including the famous triple portrait of Charles I (1635–36) and equestrian portraits — created an enduring image of aristocratic refinement and profoundly influenced English portrait painting for more than a century, through Lely, Reynolds and Gainsborough.

Death and legacy

Van Dyck married Mary Ruthven in 1639 and died in London in December 1641, shortly after the birth of his daughter; he was buried in St Paul's Cathedral. His workshop produced a large output with studio assistance. The "Van Dyke beard" and the rich brown pigment and costume style called "Vandyke" preserve his name in everyday language.
