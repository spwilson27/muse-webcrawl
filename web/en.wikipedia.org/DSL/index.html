DSL

DSL may refer to several things. Most commonly, it stands for Digital Subscriber Line, a family of technologies that provide internet access by transmitting digital data over telephone lines. The term is also used for a domain-specific language in computer science.

Digital Subscriber Line

Digital Subscriber Line technology delivers high-speed data over the ordinary copper wires of the local telephone network. Because DSL uses higher frequencies than voice calls, data and voice can share the same line simultaneously, separated by filters or splitters. Common variants include ADSL (asymmetric DSL), with faster download than upload speeds, SDSL (symmetric DSL), and VDSL (very-high-bit-rate DSL), which offers much higher speeds over shorter distances. DSL service became a widespread form of residential broadband in the late 1990s and 2000s, competing with cable modem service.

Domain-specific language

In software engineering, a domain-specific language is a computer language specialized to a particular application domain, in contrast to a general-purpose language. Examples include HTML for web documents, SQL for database queries, and various hardware description languages.
