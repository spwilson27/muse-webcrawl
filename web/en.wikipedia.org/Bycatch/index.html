Bycatch

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (recreated from memory)

Bycatch, in the fishing industry, is a fish or other marine species that is caught unintentionally while fishing for specific target species and target sizes of fish, crabs, or other marine life. Bycatch is either the wrong species, the wrong sex, or undersized or juvenile individuals of the target species. Much bycatch is discarded at sea, dead or dying, though some is landed and sold, sometimes under names such as "trash fish."

Scale and impacts

Global estimates of discards have historically run to tens of millions of tonnes per year, though improved management has reduced the figure in many fisheries. Shrimp trawling is notorious for high bycatch ratios, sometimes catching several kilograms of other species per kilogram of shrimp. Bycatch in longline and gillnet fisheries kills large numbers of seabirds, sea turtles, dolphins, and sharks, contributing to the decline of endangered species such as the vaquita.

Mitigation

Technical measures to reduce bycatch include turtle excluder devices (TEDs) in trawl nets, circle hooks and bird-scaring lines on longlines, acoustic pingers on gillnets, larger mesh sizes, and bycatch reduction devices such as Nordmøre grids. Management measures include bycatch quotas, real-time area closures, onboard observers, and full-retention rules intended to make fishers accountable for everything they catch.

Utilization

In some regions discarded bycatch is landed for fish meal, aquaculture feed, or direct human consumption, raising questions about whether utilization creates perverse incentives to target juvenile fish.
