Adipic acid

Adipic acid or hexanedioic acid is the organic compound with the formula (CH2)4(COOH)2. From an industrial perspective, it is the most important dicarboxylic acid: about 2.5 billion kilograms of this white crystalline powder are produced annually, mainly as a precursor to nylon.

Production

Adipic acid is mainly produced by the oxidation of "KA oil" — a mixture of cyclohexanone and cyclohexanol — with nitric acid, a process that generates nitrous oxide (N2O), a potent greenhouse gas, as a by-product; manufacturers increasingly abate these emissions. Greener routes, including bio-based production from sugars, are under development.

Uses

The dominant use of adipic acid is the manufacture of nylon 6,6, where it is polymerized with hexamethylenediamine. Smaller quantities go into polyurethanes, plasticizers, and adhesives. In food, adipic acid (E355) is used as a gelling aid and acidity regulator, imparting a smooth, tart taste to some drinks and desserts. In medicine it has been incorporated into controlled-release formulations.
