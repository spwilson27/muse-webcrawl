Staatsoper Berlin

The Berlin State Opera on Unter den Linden.

The Staatsoper Berlin (Berlin State Opera), also known as the Staatsoper Unter den Linden, is a German opera company and opera house based in Berlin. Its permanent home is on the boulevard Unter den Linden in the Mitte district. The building was commissioned by Frederick the Great and opened in 1742, making its company one of the oldest in Europe.

History

Designed by Georg Wenzeslaus von Knobelsdorff, the opera house was the first freestanding theatre in Germany and the largest opera house of its time. It burned down in 1843 and was rebuilt; it was destroyed again during the Second World War and reconstructed in the 1950s in the German Democratic Republic. From 1992 to 2022 its music director was Daniel Barenboim, who founded the associated Staatskapelle Berlin's modern international profile and the annual Festtage festival.

Renovation

The house closed in 2010 for an extensive renovation and modernization, during which the company performed at the Schiller Theater. It reopened in October 2017 with a production of Schumann's Szenen aus Goethes Faust. The renovation raised the ceiling of the auditorium to improve acoustics while preserving the building's historic appearance.

The company

The Staatsoper's orchestra is the Staatskapelle Berlin, one of the world's oldest orchestras. The house ranks among Germany's leading stages alongside venues such as Paris's national landmarks in the wider map of European cultural institutions, and it shares Berlin's operatic life with the Deutsche Oper and the Komische Oper.
