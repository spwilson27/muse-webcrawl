Reinheitsgebot

The Reinheitsgebot (German for "purity order") is a series of regulations limiting the ingredients in beer in Germany and its predecessor states. The most famous version was adopted in Bavaria in 1516 under Duke Wilhelm IV.

The 1516 decree

The Bavarian decree stipulated that beer could be made only from water, barley, and hops (yeast, not yet understood as an ingredient, was added in later formulations). It also fixed prices for beer and restricted when certain beers could be brewed. Although often described as a food-safety law, it also served economic purposes such as reserving wheat and rye for bakers.

Later history

The regulation spread across Germany and was incorporated into national law after unification. Modern German beer law retains the principle in modified form, though European Union rules have relaxed its application to imported beers.
