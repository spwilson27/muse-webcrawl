Power over Ethernet

Power over Ethernet (PoE) describes any of several standards or ad hoc systems that pass electric power along with data on twisted-pair Ethernet cabling. This allows a single cable to provide both a data connection and enough electricity to power networked devices such as wireless access points (WAPs), IP cameras and VoIP telephones.

Standards

The original IEEE 802.3af standard (2003) supplies up to 15.4 W of DC power at the sourcing equipment, of which 12.95 W is assured at the powered device. IEEE 802.3at (2009), known as PoE+, raised this to 30 W (25.5 W at the device). IEEE 802.3bt (2018), marketed as PoE++ or 4PPoE, defines Type 3 (60 W) and Type 4 (90–100 W) operation using all four pairs of the cable, enabling power for devices such as pan-tilt-zoom cameras, video conferencing systems and thin clients.

Operation

Power is supplied by power sourcing equipment (PSE), typically an Ethernet switch with PoE capability or a midspan injector inserted between a non-PoE switch and the device. Before applying full power, the PSE detects whether the connected device is PoE-compatible and negotiates a power class, protecting legacy equipment from damage. Power is delivered over the data pairs, the spare pairs, or (in newer standards) all pairs.

Advantages and uses

PoE simplifies installation by eliminating separate electrical wiring and outlets at the device location; devices can be placed on ceilings or outdoors and centrally backed up by a UPS in the wiring closet. It is widely used for wireless access points, security cameras, VoIP phones, access control systems, LED lighting and building automation.
