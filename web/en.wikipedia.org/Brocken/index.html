Brocken

Elevation1,141 m (3,743 ft)
RangeHarz
LocationSaxony-Anhalt, Germany

The Brocken, also sometimes referred to as the Blocksberg, is the highest peak in the Harz mountain range and also the highest peak in Northern Germany; it is near Wernigerode in the state of Saxony-Anhalt, between the rivers Weser and Elbe. Its summit, at 1,141 metres (3,743 ft) above sea level, lies above the tree line and is known for its harsh weather, frequent fog, and distinctive optical phenomenon, the Brocken spectre.

Geography and climate

The Brocken is composed of granite and forms the centre of the Harz National Park. Its climate is comparable to that of northern Iceland or high alpine regions: the summit experiences fog on more than 300 days a year, strong winds, and snow cover for much of the winter. A weather station has operated on the summit since the 19th century.

History

The Brocken has long been surrounded by legend and superstition. It features in Goethe's Faust as the site of the witches' Walpurgis Night gathering. During the Cold War the summit, which lay in East Germany close to the inner German border, was used as a listening station by Soviet and East German intelligence services. Since reunification it has again become a popular tourist destination, served by the narrow-gauge Brocken Railway, the highest railway summit station in Germany.

Brocken spectre

The mountain gave its name to the Brocken spectre, an optical phenomenon in which the shadow of an observer, cast onto fog or cloud below, appears greatly magnified and surrounded by rainbow-like rings (a glory). It is frequently observed on the foggy summit.
