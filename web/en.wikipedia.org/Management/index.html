Management

Management (or managing) is the administration of an organisation, whether a business, a non-profit or a government body. It includes setting the strategy of the organisation and coordinating the efforts of its staff (or volunteers) to accomplish its objectives through the application of available resources — financial, natural, technological and human.

Functions and levels

Classical accounts, following Henri Fayol, describe management through functions such as planning, organising, leading (commanding) and controlling. Organisations typically distinguish senior management (executives who set strategy), middle management (who translate strategy into departmental plans) and line or front-line management (who supervise individual workers). Managers may also be specialised by function — operations, marketing, finance, human resources, information systems — or by project.

Theory and history

Modern management thought developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with Frederick Winslow Taylor's scientific management, Fayol's administrative theory and Max Weber's analysis of bureaucracy. Later schools emphasised human relations (the Hawthorne studies, Maslow, McGregor), systems and contingency approaches, and strategic management. Peter Drucker popularised management by objectives and argued that management is a distinct practice central to modern society. Management is taught in business schools, commonly through the MBA degree.
