Senryū

Senryū (川柳) is a Japanese form of short poetry similar in structure to haiku: three phrases of 5, 7 and 5 on (sound units). Whereas haiku traditionally treat nature and the seasons, senryū tend to be about human nature, often humorous, ironic or satirical, and frequently deal with everyday foibles.

Origins

The form is named after the Edo-period poet Karai Senryū (1718–1790), a judge of comic linked verse (maekuzuke) whose selections popularised the independent short verse. Senryū developed from the humorous side of haikai poetry.

Characteristics

Senryū generally lack a seasonal reference word (kigo) and a cutting word (kireji); their tone ranges from playful to darkly cynical. In modern Japan, "salaryman senryū" contests collect witty verses about office life.
