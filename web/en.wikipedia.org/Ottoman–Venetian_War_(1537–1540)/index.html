Ottoman–Venetian War (1537–1540)

The Ottoman–Venetian War of 1537–1540 was the third war fought between the Ottoman Empire and the Republic of Venice for control of the eastern Mediterranean. It ended with substantial Venetian territorial losses in Greece and the Aegean.

Background

Relations between Venice and Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent deteriorated as Ottoman power grew in the Mediterranean, accelerated by the corsair admiral Hayreddin Barbarossa, who entered Ottoman service and built a formidable fleet. In 1537 Suleiman attacked the Venetian-held island of Corfu and Ottoman forces besieged it, though the island's fortress held out.

The Holy League and Preveza

In response, Pope Paul III organized a Holy League in 1538 uniting Venice, the Papal States, Spain, and the Habsburg monarchy. The League's fleet, commanded by Andrea Doria, met Barbarossa's fleet at the Battle of Preveza on 28 September 1538. Despite numerical superiority, the Christian fleet was decisively defeated, giving the Ottomans naval supremacy in the eastern Mediterranean for decades.

Peace settlement

Venice, abandoned by its allies, sued for peace. By the treaty concluded in 1540, Venice ceded its last possessions in the Peloponnese — Monemvasia and Nauplia — along with several Aegean islands including Naxos, and paid a large indemnity of 300,000 ducats. The war confirmed Ottoman dominance of the eastern Mediterranean sea lanes.
