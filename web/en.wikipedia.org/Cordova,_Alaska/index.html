Cordova is a small town in the Chugach Census Area near the mouth of the Copper River in the U.S. state of Alaska. It lies at the head of Orca Inlet on the eastern side of Prince William Sound. The population is roughly 2,600.

History

The area is the traditional homeland of the Eyak people. The modern town grew after 1906 as the port terminus of the Copper River and Northwestern Railway, which carried copper from the Kennecott mines until 1938. Cordova was also known for its razor clam cannery and, from 1916, a famous feud with neighbouring Katalla over the railway terminus.

Economy

Commercial fishing dominates the economy; the Copper River salmon fishery, especially its early-season king and sockeye salmon, is celebrated by chefs and commands premium prices. The town was affected by the 1989 Exxon Valdez oil spill in Prince William Sound. Cordova is not connected to the state highway system by road; it is served by the Alaska Marine Highway ferry and by air.
