Go strategy and tactics

The game of Go has simple rules but deep strategy and tactics. In the opening (fuseki), players stake claims to territory, usually beginning in the corners, then the sides, and only later the centre, because corners offer the most efficient enclosure of territory. Standard corner sequences called joseki are studied as locally optimal exchanges, though choosing the right joseki depends on the whole-board position.

Basic tactics

Tactical play revolves around the life and death of groups of stones. A group lives if it can form two separate eyes — internal liberties that the opponent can never fill — or connect to a living group. Fundamental tactical patterns include the ladder (shicho), in which a group is chased across the board in a zigzag of ataris; the net (geta), which captures more loosely; and ko fights, in which a position can repeat and players must play elsewhere (a ko threat) before recapturing. Snapback and shortage of liberties are other common capturing mechanisms.

Strategy

In the middle game, players invade or reduce the opponent's frameworks (moyo), attack weak groups for profit, and defend their own. Good strategy balances territory against thickness — strong, influential positions that may not yet enclose points but exert power over a wide area. Concepts such as aji (latent potential in a position), sente (initiative) and gote (losing the initiative) guide the choice of moves. In the endgame (yose), players fill in the remaining boundaries, counting the value of each move in points; correct endgame play can decide close games.

Proverbs

Go teaching is rich in proverbs, such as "the empty triangle is bad shape", "hane at the head of two stones", and "don't approach thickness". These heuristics summarise principles of good shape and efficient play, though strong players know when the position demands exceptions.
