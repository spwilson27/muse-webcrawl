In vivo

In vivo (Latin for "within the living") refers to studies and experiments conducted within a whole, living organism, as opposed to a partial or dead organism, or to in vitro ("in glass") experiments performed outside an organism. Animal testing and clinical trials are major forms of in vivo research, used to verify the overall effects of a substance or procedure on a living subject, where complex interactions between tissues, organs, and systems can be observed.

In vivo studies observe processes inside a living organism.

Usage in research

In vivo experimentation is valuable because living organisms are more than the sum of their parts: results obtained in isolated cells or tissues may not predict behaviour in a whole animal. Drug development typically proceeds from in vitro assays to in vivo animal studies and then to human clinical trials. Imaging techniques such as bioluminescence imaging allow processes to be tracked in living animals over time.

Related terms

Related Latin terms include in vitro (in the glass), in situ (in place), in silico (in computer simulation), ex vivo (outside the living body, in tissue removed from an organism), and in utero (in the uterus). Ethical frameworks such as the Three Rs (replacement, reduction, refinement) govern the use of animals in in vivo research.
