The Ostrogoths (Latin: Ostrogothi) were an East Germanic people who, with their western cousins the Visigoths, formed the eastern branch of the Goths. Emerging in the Ukrainian steppe, where they built a large realm under Ermanaric in the 4th century, they were subjugated by the Huns in about 375 and marched west as part of Attila's confederation. After the Hunnic empire collapsed, the Ostrogoths settled in Pannonia and rose, under Theodoric the Great, to rule Italy for sixty years.

Theodoric and the Kingdom of Italy

In 488 the Eastern Roman emperor Zeno commissioned Theodoric to overthrow Odoacer, the Germanic king who had deposed the last western emperor. Theodoric invaded Italy, defeated Odoacer, and in 493 killed him at a banquet supposedly held to reconcile the two. From Ravenna, Theodoric ruled Italy (493–526) in formal subordination to Constantinople while preserving Roman administration, law and senatorial institutions; the Romans kept their laws and the Goths theirs, and Arian Gothic Christians and Catholic Romans worshipped separately. His court patronised Boethius and Cassiodorus, restored aqueducts and monuments, and made Italy prosperous — the mausoleum Theodoric built at Ravenna still stands.

Decline and fall

Theodoric's last years were darkened by religious tension and the execution of Boethius (524). After his death, succession disputes among his heirs gave the emperor Justinian his pretext: in 535 Byzantine forces under Belisarius invaded, beginning the ruinous twenty-year Gothic War. Despite heroic resistance under kings Totila and Teia, the Ostrogothic kingdom was destroyed by 553–554, Italy was devastated, and the Ostrogoths vanish from history as a distinct people, absorbed into the population of Italy and the Lombard kingdom that followed.
