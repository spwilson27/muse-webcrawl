Cape Cod National Seashore

The Cape Cod National Seashore is a United States National Seashore created by President John F. Kennedy on August 7, 1961. It comprises 43,607 acres (68.1 square miles) of ponds, woods, and beachfront on the Atlantic-facing eastern shore of Cape Cod, in Massachusetts, spanning the towns of Chatham, Orleans, Eastham, Wellfleet, Truro, and Provincetown.

Establishment

The seashore was the first national park area created under a model that combined federal, state, and private land within a protected boundary, allowing existing homeowners to remain while new development was restricted. Kennedy, whose family compound was at Hyannis Port, had long championed preserving the "Great Beach" described by Henry David Thoreau in his book Cape Cod.

Features

The park includes nearly 40 miles of seashore, the great dune fields of the Peaked Hill Bars Historic District, Marconi Station — where Guglielmo Marconi transmitted the first transatlantic wireless message from the United States in 1903 — and historic lifesaving stations and lighthouses including Nauset Light and Highland Light. Freshwater kettle ponds, formed by retreating glaciers, dot the interior. Visitor centres at Salt Pond (Eastham) and Province Lands (Provincetown) serve about four million visitors a year.

Nature

The seashore protects pitch pine and oak forests, heathlands, cranberry bogs, and nesting habitat for the threatened piping plover. The beaches are subject to rapid erosion, which regularly reshapes cliffs and occasionally exposes shipwrecks from the hundreds of vessels lost along this coast.
