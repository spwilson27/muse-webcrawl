Sabar

Sabar is the name both of a drum tradition of the Wolof people of Senegal and The Gambia, and of the festive dance events at which the drums are played. The sabar drum family comprises several tall, single-headed drums carved from a single piece of wood and played with one bare hand and one thin stick, producing the sharp, lightning-fast patterns that accompany dancers in short solo displays. Sabar rhythms mark neighbourhood celebrations, wrestling matches, weddings, naming ceremonies and street parties in Dakar and throughout Senegal.

Instruments and performance

The ensemble includes lead and accompaniment drums such as the gorong bamba, mbëng-mbëng, n'der and the tungoné. Each rhythm (called a bàkk) corresponds to specific dance steps, and drummers cue dancers phrase by phrase, so that a skilled dancer appears to make the drum speak. Sabar is traditionally a women's dance space in many settings, with drumming passed down through griot (géwël) families. The tradition profoundly shaped mbalax, Senegal's popular dance music popularised internationally by Youssou N'Dour, whose sound fuses sabar percussion with Western instruments.
