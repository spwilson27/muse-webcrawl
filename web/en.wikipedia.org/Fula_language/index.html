Fula language

The Fula language (also known as Fulani, Fulfulde, Pulaar, and Peul) is a Senegambian language of the Atlantic branch of the Niger–Congo family, spoken by the Fula people across West and Central Africa. It is one of the most widely dispersed languages in Africa, spoken from Senegambia in the west to Sudan in the east.

Geographic distribution

Fula is spoken across a vast belt of the Sahel and savanna, including Senegal, Guinea, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Nigeria, Cameroon, Chad, and Mauritania. In Guinea, Pular is the most widely spoken indigenous language. In northern Nigeria and Cameroon, Fulfulde serves as a regional lingua franca.

Dialects

The language exists as a dialect continuum with several major varieties, including Pulaar (Senegal and Mauritania), Pular (Guinea), Maasina Fulfulde (Mali), Nigerian Fulfulde, and Adamawa Fulfulde (Cameroon and Nigeria). Mutual intelligibility between the most distant dialects can be limited.

Writing systems

Fula has been written for centuries in an adapted Arabic script known as Ajami. Latin-based alphabets were standardized in the 20th century. In 1989, brothers Ibrahima and Abdoulaye Barry of Guinea invented the Adlam script, a right-to-left alphabetic script that has gained growing use and was added to Unicode in 2016.

Phonology and grammar

Fula is known for its large system of noun classes (roughly 20–25 depending on dialect), marked by suffixes and triggering initial consonant alternation (mutation) in the first consonant of the noun stem. The language has implosive consonants (ɓ, ɗ, ƴ) and a prenasalized consonant series. Verb extensions encode voice distinctions such as active, middle, and passive.

Status

Fula holds national-language status in several countries, including Senegal, Guinea, Mali, and Mauritania, and is used in radio broadcasting and primary education in parts of the region.
