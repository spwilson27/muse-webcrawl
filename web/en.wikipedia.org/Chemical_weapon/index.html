Chemical weapon

A chemical weapon (CW) is a specialized munition that uses chemicals formulated to inflict death or harm on humans. Chemical weapons are classified as weapons of mass destruction, distinct from conventional and nuclear weapons.

Agents are categorized by their effects: nerve agents (sarin, VX), blister agents (mustard gas), choking agents (chlorine, phosgene), blood agents, and incapacitating or riot-control agents. Large-scale use began in World War I, notably at the Second Battle of Ypres in 1915.

The Chemical Weapons Convention (1997), administered by the OPCW, prohibits the development, production, stockpiling and use of chemical weapons and requires destruction of declared stockpiles. Despite this, chemical weapons have been used in conflicts including the Iran–Iraq War and the Syrian civil war.
