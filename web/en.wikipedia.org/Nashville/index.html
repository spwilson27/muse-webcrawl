Nashville

Country: United States
State: Tennessee
Founded: 1779
Named for: Francis Nash
Nickname: Music City

Nashville is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Tennessee, and the seat of Davidson County. Located on the Cumberland River in north-central Tennessee, it had a population of roughly 690,000 at the 2020 census, with a metropolitan area of about two million. Since 1963 the city and county have shared a consolidated government.

History

Founded in 1779 as Fort Nashborough, named for Revolutionary War general Francis Nash, the settlement became a river and later rail hub. Nashville was fiercely contested in the Civil War; the Battle of Nashville in December 1864 effectively destroyed the Confederate Army of Tennessee. After the war it grew as a commercial and educational centre — home to Vanderbilt University and historically Black Fisk University, whose Jubilee Singers toured the world.

Music City

Nashville is the centre of the American country music industry, home to the Grand Ole Opry (broadcast since 1925), the Ryman Auditorium, the Country Music Hall of Fame, and the recording studios of Music Row. Its music economy extends well beyond country into rock, gospel, blues and pop.

Economy and culture

Healthcare is the city's largest industry, alongside education, tourism, finance and automotive manufacturing (Nissan's North American headquarters are in nearby Franklin). The city has a full-scale replica of the Parthenon in Centennial Park, built for the 1897 Tennessee Centennial Exposition, reflecting its "Athens of the South" nickname.
