Gigafactory Nevada (also known as Giga Nevada and originally Gigafactory 1) is a large lithium-ion battery and electric vehicle component factory near Reno, Nevada, owned and operated by Tesla, Inc. in partnership with Panasonic. When its design was announced, it was planned to be one of the largest buildings in the world by footprint and the largest battery factory, intended to reduce the cost of Tesla's batteries through economies of scale sufficient to support mass production of the Model 3.

History

Tesla announced the project in 2014 after a much-publicised competition among several states, selecting Nevada in September 2014 in return for a package of tax incentives worth about $1.3 billion over twenty years. The factory was officially opened on 29 July 2016, while still under construction. Panasonic produces battery cells (initially the 2170 format for the Model 3 and Model Y) in its portion of the plant, while Tesla assembles battery packs, drive units, and stationary storage products such as the Powerwall and Megapack. In January 2023 Tesla announced a further $3.6 billion expansion to add production of 4680 battery cells and the Tesla Semi truck. The factory is powered substantially by renewable energy, including a large rooftop solar array, in line with Tesla's goal of net-zero energy operation.

Scale

The building's footprint is roughly 1.9 million square feet (about 15 football fields) with about 5.3 million square feet of operational space on multiple floors, employing several thousand workers. Its name coined the term "gigafactory", later applied to Tesla's other large plants in New York, Shanghai, Berlin, and Texas.
