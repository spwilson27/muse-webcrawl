The Silverstone Circuit is a motor racing circuit in England, near the Northamptonshire villages of Silverstone and Whittlebury. It is the home of the British Grand Prix, which it first hosted as the 1950 British Grand Prix — the first race of the inaugural Formula One World Championship. The circuit straddles the Northamptonshire and Buckinghamshire border and is built on the site of a Royal Air Force bomber station, RAF Silverstone, which was active during the Second World War.

History

RAF Silverstone opened in 1943 as a training base for Wellington bomber crews. After the war the airfield fell into disuse, and in 1948 local enthusiasts organised an informal race around its runways and perimeter tracks. The Royal Automobile Club took over the lease and held the first British Grand Prix at the circuit in October 1948, won by Luigi Villoresi in a Maserati. Two years later, on 13 May 1950, Silverstone hosted the very first round of the new Formula One World Championship, won by Giuseppe Farina in an Alfa Romeo before King George VI.

Through the 1950s and 1960s the Grand Prix alternated between Silverstone and Aintree, and later Brands Hatch, before Silverstone became the permanent home of the race in 1987. The layout evolved steadily: the original airfield triangle gave way to a faster perimeter circuit, and chicanes and new complexes were added at Woodcote, Becketts, Bridge and Brooklands to control rising speeds.

Modern layout

A major redevelopment completed in 2011 moved the pit and paddock complex to a new building, the Silverstone Wing, between Club and Abbey corners, and made the Arena layout the Grand Prix configuration. The 5.891 km (3.660 mi) circuit has 18 corners, including the celebrated sequence of Copse, Maggotts, Becketts and Chapel, taken at very high speed, and the slower Brooklands–Luffield complex. The circuit also hosts rounds of MotoGP, the World Endurance Championship historically, the British Touring Car Championship and numerous national series.

Records and events

Silverstone remains one of the fastest circuits on the Formula One calendar, with cars sustaining very high average speeds through its sweeping corners. Away from racing, the venue hosts the Silverstone Festival (formerly the Silverstone Classic), music events and driving experiences, and is surrounded by a cluster of motorsport engineering businesses sometimes called "Motorsport Valley".
