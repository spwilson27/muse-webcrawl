Drake equation

The Drake equation is a probabilistic argument used to estimate the number of active, communicative extraterrestrial civilisations in the Milky Way galaxy. It was written by American astronomer Frank Drake in 1961 as a way to stimulate scientific dialogue at the first scientific meeting on the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI), held at Green Bank Observatory.

The equation

N = R* × fp × ne × fl × fi × fc × L, where N is the number of civilisations with which communication might be possible; R* is the average rate of star formation; fp the fraction of stars with planets; ne the number of planets per system that could support life; fl the fraction on which life actually arises; fi the fraction developing intelligence; fc the fraction releasing detectable signals; and L the length of time such civilisations release those signals.

Estimates and criticism

Estimates have ranged from a lonely uniqueness to millions of civilisations, because several factors — especially fl, fi and L — remain almost wholly uncertain. Critics note the equation is less a measurement than a way of organising ignorance. Modern exoplanet surveys have since pinned down fp near 1 and refined ne. The equation frames discussion of the Fermi paradox: if civilisations should be common, why have none been detected?
