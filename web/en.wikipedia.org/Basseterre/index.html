Basseterre

Basseterre (French for "low land") is the capital and largest city of Saint Kitts and Nevis, a twin-island federation in the Leeward Islands of the Caribbean. Located on the southwestern coast of the island of Saint Kitts, it is one of the chief commercial depots of the Leeward Islands and a historic port town founded by the French in 1627.

History

Basseterre was founded in 1627 by the French under Pierre Belain d'Esnambuc and became the capital of the French colony of Saint-Christophe. Its fine natural harbour and strategic position made it a major centre of the Caribbean sugar trade. The town was repeatedly devastated by fire, hurricanes and earthquakes, including the great fire of 1867, after which much of it was rebuilt. When Saint Kitts and Nevis gained independence from Britain in 1983, Basseterre remained the seat of government.

Geography

The city lies on Basseterre Bay on the southern coast of Saint Kitts, backed by the green hills of the island's interior and the peak of Mount Liamuiga to the northwest. The climate is tropical, with a wet season roughly from June to November.

Landmarks

Notable sites include Independence Square, laid out in 1790 for slave auctions and now a public garden; the Circus, a roundabout modelled on Piccadilly Circus with the Berkeley Memorial clock tower at its centre; St. George's Anglican Church; and the National Museum, housed in the former Treasury Building. Port Zante welcomes cruise ships, and the nearby Brimstone Hill Fortress is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Transport

Basseterre is served by Robert L. Bradshaw International Airport, a short distance northeast of the city, and by ferry services connecting Saint Kitts with neighbouring Nevis.
