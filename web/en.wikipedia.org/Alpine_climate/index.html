Alpine climate

The alpine climate is the typical climate for elevations above the tree line, where trees fail to grow due to cold. This climate is also referred to as a mountain climate or highland climate. In the Köppen climate classification, alpine climates are classed as group E together with polar climates, where no month has a mean temperature above 10 °C (50 °F).

Description

The climate becomes colder at high elevations—this characteristic is described by the lapse rate of air: air tends to get colder as it rises, since it expands. The dry adiabatic lapse rate is 10 °C per kilometre of elevation, so moving up a mountain 100 metres is roughly equivalent to moving 80 kilometres toward the pole. Alpine areas are also characterized by strong winds, intense ultraviolet radiation, rapid weather changes, and, in many regions, high precipitation on windward slopes.

Distribution

Alpine climates occur in mountain ranges worldwide, including the Alps, the Himalayas, the Andes, the Rocky Mountains, and the high volcanoes of East Africa and the tropics, where high elevation produces cold conditions even at the equator. Because the tree line varies with latitude, alpine zones begin near sea level in subpolar regions but only above 4,000 metres in parts of the tropics.
