Kamakhya Temple

LocationNilachal Hill, Guwahati, Assam, India
DeityKamakhya (a form of the goddess)
TraditionShaktism; one of the 51 Shakti Peethas
Present structureRebuilt 1565 by the Koch king Nara Narayan
StyleNilachal (bee-hive shikhara) style
Major festivalAmbubachi Mela

The Kamakhya Temple is a Hindu temple dedicated to the goddess Kamakhya, situated on Nilachal Hill in Guwahati, Assam. It is the foremost of the 51 Shakti Peethas and one of the most important centres of Tantric Shaktism in India. Unusually, the temple contains no image of the goddess: the object of worship is a natural spring flowing through a yoni-shaped cleft in the rock within a cave-like sanctum.

Mythology

According to the Kalika Purana and related texts, the temple marks the spot where the yoni (genitals) of Sati fell when Vishnu dismembered her corpse as Shiva carried it in grief. The hill itself is identified with the body of the goddess, and the site is associated with Kama, the god of love, who is said to have regained his form here — hence the name Kamakhya, "renowned goddess of desire".

History and architecture

The site is ancient, but the temples on the hill were repeatedly destroyed, including during invasions in the sixteenth century. The present main temple was rebuilt in 1565 under the Koch king Nara Narayan and his brother Chilarai. Its shikhara takes a distinctive beehive form, sculpted with panels of Hindu deities, in what is called the Nilachal style — a fusion of Nagara and local Assamese ("Saracenic" arch) elements. The complex also includes temples of the ten Mahavidyas, among them Kali, Tara, Tripura Sundari, Bhuvaneshvari, and Chhinnamasta.

Worship and festivals

Kamakhya is a major pilgrimage site, especially for Tantric practitioners. The temple's greatest festival is the Ambubachi Mela, held each monsoon (June), which celebrates the goddess's annual menstruation: the temple closes for three days, and the spring water is said to turn red. Devotees receive pieces of red cloth (rakta bastra) believed to carry the goddess's blessing. Animal sacrifice, particularly of goats and buffalo, remains part of traditional worship here.
