Fatal Accident Inquiry

A Fatal Accident Inquiry (FAI) is a judicial inquiry held in Scotland under the Inquiries into Fatal Accidents and Sudden Deaths etc. (Scotland) Act 2016 (successor to the Fatal Accidents and Sudden Deaths Inquiry (Scotland) Act 1976) to investigate the circumstances of certain deaths. Unlike the coroner's inquest in England and Wales, an FAI is held before a sheriff in the Sheriff Court, and its purpose is fact-finding rather than apportioning blame.

When an inquiry is mandatory

An inquiry must be held when a person dies:

  • As a result of an accident in the course of their employment
  • While in legal custody (for example, in prison or police custody)

The Lord Advocate may also direct a discretionary inquiry where a death was sudden, suspicious or unexplained and it is in the public interest to investigate.

Procedure and outcome

The Procurator Fiscal investigates the death and presents evidence. At the conclusion, the sheriff issues a determination covering the time, place and cause of death, any precautions that might have avoided it, and any defects in systems of work that contributed—together with recommendations to prevent future deaths.
