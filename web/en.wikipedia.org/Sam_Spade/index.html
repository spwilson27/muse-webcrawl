Samuel "Sam" Spade is a fictional character and the protagonist of Dashiell Hammett's 1930 novel The Maltese Falcon. Spade is a private detective working in San Francisco, the senior partner of the firm Spade & Archer. Hammett described him as "a blond satan": tall, heavy-boned, with a jutting jaw and yellow-grey eyes — deliberately unlike the matinee-idol looks later given to him on screen.

Character

Spade is the archetypal hard-boiled detective: cynical, unsentimental, and operating by a personal code rather than the law or conventional morality. When his partner Miles Archer is murdered, Spade barely mourns — he has been having an affair with Archer's wife — yet he insists on solving the killing because, as he explains, "when a man's partner is killed he's supposed to do something about it." In the novel's climax he turns in Brigid O'Shaughnessy, the woman he loves, for the murder, embodying the code over feeling.

Appearances

Besides The Maltese Falcon, Spade appears in three short stories collected in The Adventures of Sam Spade and Other Stories: "A Man Called Spade", "Too Many Have Lived", and "They Can Only Hang You Once". Hammett, himself a former Pinkerton detective, drew the character from his own experience and named him after himself (Samuel Dashiell Hammett).

In other media

The 1941 film adaptation directed by John Huston, starring Humphrey Bogart, fixed Spade's image in popular culture and helped define film noir. Earlier adaptations appeared in 1931 (with Ricardo Cortez) and 1936 (Satan Met a Lady, with the character renamed). The character also featured in radio dramas, notably The Adventures of Sam Spade with Howard Duff (1946–1951).
