Pennsylvania Railroad class GG1

GG1 4800, "Old Rivets", in Pennsylvania Railroad livery

The GG1 was a class of streamlined electric locomotives built for the Pennsylvania Railroad (PRR), in service in the northeastern United States. A total of 139 GG1s were built by the General Electric and Altoona Works between 1934 and 1943. They entered service with the PRR and later ran for its successors Penn Central, Conrail, Amtrak, and New Jersey Transit, until the last were retired in 1983.

Design

The GG1 ran on 11,000 volts AC supplied from overhead catenary. It had a 4-6-0-0-6-4 wheel arrangement (two sets of driving wheels in articulated frames) and twelve 385-horsepower traction motors, giving a continuous rating of about 4,620 horsepower with short-time peaks considerably higher. It was designed for speeds up to 100 mph (160 km/h). The famous streamlined body, with its smooth curves and (on many locomotives) five gold pinstripes sweeping back from the nose, was styled by industrial designer Raymond Loewy, whose refinements replaced the riveted shell of the prototype with a welded one.

Service

GG1s hauled the PRR's premier passenger trains, including the Congressional and the Broadway Limited, along the electrified Northeast Corridor between New York, Philadelphia, and Washington, and freight trains as dieselisation and newer electric locomotives arrived. One GG1, No. 4876, became famous in the 1953 Federal Express train wreck, when it crashed through the concourse of Washington Union Station; no one was killed, and the locomotive was repaired and returned to service. Sixteen GG1s survive in museums, though none are operational, in part because their transformers contained PCB-laden oil.
