Republic of China (1912–1949)

Republic of China
Capital: Beijing (1912–28), Nanjing (1928–49)
Established: 1 January 1912
Government relocated to Taipei: 1949
Key leaders: Sun Yat-sen, Yuan Shikai, Chiang Kai-shek

The Republic of China was the state that governed mainland China from 1912, when it succeeded the Qing dynasty, until 1949, when its government retreated to Taiwan during the Chinese Civil War. Founded by the Xinhai Revolution, it was Asia's first republic and is conventionally divided into the Beiyang era, the Nanjing decade, the war against Japan, and the final civil war with the Chinese Communist Party.

Founding and warlord era

Sun Yat-sen was inaugurated as provisional president in Nanjing on 1 January 1912, but power passed to the general Yuan Shikai in Beijing, whose monarchist ambitions and assassination of parliamentarians provoked revolts. After Yuan's death in 1916, China fragmented among regional warlords. Sun regrouped his Kuomintang (KMT) at Guangzhou with Soviet support and, after his death in 1925, Chiang Kai-shek led the Northern Expedition (1926–28), nominally reunifying China under the Nationalist government at Nanjing.

Nanjing decade and war

The Nanjing decade (1927–1937) brought economic modernisation, legal reform and diplomatic gains, while the KMT fought encirclement campaigns against the Communists, who survived by the Long March (1934–35). Following the Mukden Incident (1931), Japan seized Manchuria; full-scale war began in 1937. The capital fell, the government moved to Chongqing, and China — one of the principal Allies — endured enormous losses before Japan's defeat in 1945, gaining a permanent seat on the UN Security Council.

Civil war and retreat

The resumed civil war (1946–1949) turned against the Nationalists amid hyperinflation and corruption. Mao Zedong proclaimed the People's Republic of China in Beijing on 1 October 1949; in December the Republic of China government relocated to Taipei, Taiwan, where it continues today, while the period 1912–1949 denotes its rule on the mainland.
