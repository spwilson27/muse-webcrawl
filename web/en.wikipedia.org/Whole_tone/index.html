Whole tone

A whole tone (or whole step) is a musical interval spanning two semitones, equivalent to a major second. In twelve-tone equal temperament it corresponds to a frequency ratio of the twelfth root of two squared (about 1.122, or 200 cents).

The whole-tone scale

The whole-tone scale is built entirely of whole tones, dividing the octave into six equal steps; there are only two distinct whole-tone scales. Because every interval in it is the same, the scale lacks a leading tone or a strong sense of key, giving it a floating, dreamlike quality. Claude Debussy used it prominently in pieces such as Voiles, and it appears in the music of Liszt, Russian composers such as Glinka and Dargomyzhsky, and in jazz harmony.

Terminology

In British usage "tone" means whole tone and "semitone" means half step, while American usage prefers "whole step" and "half step". A tritone equals three whole tones.
