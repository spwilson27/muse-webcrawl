Osaka (Japanese: 大阪市, Ōsaka-shi) is a major city in the Kansai region of Honshu, Japan, and the capital of Osaka Prefecture. It is the third most populous city in Japan and the centre of the Keihanshin metropolitan area, the country's second-largest urban economy after Tokyo. Historically a merchant city and Japan's commercial capital, Osaka is famed for its food culture, canals and down-to-earth humour.

History

The area was the site of the 7th-century Naniwa Palace, briefly the imperial capital. Toyotomi Hideyoshi built Osaka Castle in 1583 and made the city his seat of power; after his fall, Tokugawa-era Osaka flourished as the great entrepôt for rice and goods shipped from across Japan, earning its nickname "the Nation's Kitchen". The Dōjima rice exchange pioneered futures trading in the 18th century. The modern city was founded in 1889 and was heavily bombed in 1945.

Districts and culture

Osaka's commercial heart is split between Kita (Umeda) in the north and Minami (Namba, Shinsaibashi and the neon-lit Dōtonbori canal) in the south. Local specialities include takoyaki (octopus balls), okonomiyaki and kushikatsu. The city is home to the National Bunraku Theatre, the comedy agency Yoshimoto Kōgyō, and Universal Studios Japan. Osaka hosted Expo '70 and Expo 2025.

Economy

Osaka's economy centres on commerce, services, manufacturing and biotechnology, with headquarters or major operations of firms such as Panasonic (in neighbouring Kadoma), Sharp and Takeda. Kansai International Airport, built on an artificial island in Osaka Bay, serves the region.
