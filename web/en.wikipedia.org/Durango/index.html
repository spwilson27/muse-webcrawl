Durango is a state in northwest Mexico, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Durango. With an area of about 123,000 km² it is the fourth-largest Mexican state, but one of the least densely populated. Its capital and largest city is Victoria de Durango.

Geography

Much of the state is covered by the rugged Sierra Madre Occidental, whose pine forests make Durango Mexico's leading timber-producing state. The eastern part lies in the Chihuahuan Desert, including part of the Bolsón de Mapimí depression and the "Zone of Silence" (Zona del Silencio). Durango shares with Sinaloa and Nayarit the rich mining regions for which it has been known since colonial times.

History

The Spanish founded the city of Durango in 1563, naming it after Durango in Spain's Basque Country. The region was part of the province of Nueva Vizcaya of New Spain. In the twentieth century, the forests and sets of Durango became famous as locations for Hollywood Westerns — so many John Wayne and other cowboy films were shot there that the state was called "the land of cinema" (La Tierra del Cine).

Economy and people

Forestry, mining (iron, silver, gold), agriculture and cattle ranching are the main industries. The state is home to the Tepehuano, Huichol, Cora and Mexicanero indigenous peoples, as well as a notable community of Plautdietsch-speaking Mennonites who settled in the 1920s.
