Jewish National Fund

Founded1901
HeadquartersJerusalem, Israel
FocusLand purchase/development, afforestation, water, community building

The Jewish National Fund (JNF; Hebrew: קרן קיימת לישראל, Keren Kayemet LeYisrael) is a non-profit organisation founded in 1901 to buy and develop land in Ottoman Syria (later Mandatory Palestine, then Israel) for Jewish settlement. Land purchased by the fund was held in trust for the Jewish people and leased rather than sold.

History

Proposed by Hermann Schapira and adopted at the Fifth Zionist Congress in Basel, the JNF raised money worldwide through its famous "Blue Box" collection tins and the sale of JNF stamps. Its land purchases underpinned many early kibbutzim and towns. After 1948 it acquired large tracts of state land and, under a 1961 covenant, managed much of Israel's state-owned land through the Israel Land Administration arrangement; it also became Israel's largest tree-planting organisation, claiming more than 240 million trees planted, and built reservoirs, parks, and recreation areas.

Activities and debates

Today the JNF works in afforestation and fire recovery, water infrastructure, development of the Negev and Galilee, research, and Zionist education. Its land policies and activities in the West Bank and East Jerusalem have drawn criticism from Palestinian groups and others, while supporters credit it with greening and developing the country.
