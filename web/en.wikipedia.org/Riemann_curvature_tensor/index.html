Riemann curvature tensor

In the mathematical field of differential geometry, the Riemann curvature tensor (or simply the Riemann tensor) is the most common way to express the curvature of Riemannian manifolds. It assigns a tensor to each point of a Riemannian manifold — a tensor field — that measures failure of covariant derivatives to commute.

Definition

For vector fields X, Y, Z on a Riemannian manifold with Levi-Civita connection ∇, the Riemann tensor is defined by

R(X, Y)Z = ∇X∇YZ − ∇Y∇XZ − ∇[X,Y]Z

In coordinates it is written Rρσμν, a (1,3) tensor expressible in terms of the Christoffel symbols and their derivatives. Geometrically, it measures how a vector changes when parallel transported around an infinitesimal closed loop, and it controls geodesic deviation and the relative acceleration of nearby geodesics.

Symmetries

The Riemann tensor satisfies important algebraic symmetries: antisymmetry in its first two and last two indices, symmetry under exchange of the two pairs, and the first (algebraic) Bianchi identity R(X,Y)Z + R(Y,Z)X + R(Z,X)Y = 0. It also satisfies the second (differential) Bianchi identity. In n dimensions the tensor has n²(n² − 1)/12 independent components: 1 in two dimensions, 6 in three, and 20 in four dimensions.

Contractions

Contracting the Riemann tensor gives the Ricci tensor Ric and the scalar curvature R, which appear in the Einstein field equations of general relativity, where curvature of spacetime is related to the distribution of matter and energy.
