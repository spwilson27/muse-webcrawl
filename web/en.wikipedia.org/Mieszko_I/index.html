Mieszko I (c. 930 – 25 May 992) was the first historical ruler of Poland and the founder of the first independent Polish state, the Duchy of Poland. A member of the Piast dynasty, he was the son of Siemomysł and the father of Bolesław I the Brave, the first crowned king of Poland, and of Świętosława, a queen consort of Denmark, Sweden and Norway.

Through his marriage in 965 to the Bohemian princess Dobrawa and his baptism in 966, Mieszko aligned Poland with the Latin Christian world and the culture of Western Europe. His reign is conventionally taken as the beginning of the recorded history of the Polish state.

Reign

Mieszko inherited from his father and grandfather a tribal territory in Greater Poland centred on Gniezno and Poznań. During his reign he expanded the realm considerably, conquering or subduing lands that included Silesia, Western Pomerania and probably Lesser Poland with Kraków. In 972 his forces defeated the troops of the Saxon margrave Hodo at the Battle of Cedynia, securing Polish influence in Western Pomerania.

In foreign policy Mieszko skilfully balanced relations with the Holy Roman Empire, Bohemia, Kievan Rus' and Denmark. The document known as the Dagome iudex (c. 991), in which Mieszko placed his state under the protection of the Holy See, is one of the oldest records describing the boundaries of early Poland.

Baptism and Christianisation

The baptism of Mieszko in 966 — the so-called Baptism of Poland — is regarded as one of the most important events in Polish history. It opened the way for the establishment of a church organisation, with the first Polish bishopric founded at Poznań in 968 under Bishop Jordan. Christianisation strengthened the duke's authority and integrated Poland into the political and cultural community of Latin Christendom.

Family and succession

Before his Christian marriage, Mieszko reportedly had several pagan wives. His marriage to Dobrawa of Bohemia in 965 produced Bolesław I and a daughter, Świętosława. After Dobrawa's death in 977 he married Oda of Haldensleben, daughter of Margrave Dietrich of Haldensleben, with whom he had three sons. In the Dagome iudex Mieszko named Oda and her sons as co-heirs, but after his death in 992 Bolesław expelled his stepmother and half-brothers and reunited the duchy under his sole rule.

Legacy

Mieszko I transformed a loose tribal union into a centralised state recognised by its neighbours. His adoption of Christianity determined Poland's cultural orientation for the following millennium, and he is remembered alongside his son Bolesław as a co-founder of the Polish nation.
