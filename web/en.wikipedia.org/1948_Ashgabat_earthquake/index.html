The 1948 Ashgabat earthquake occurred on 6 October 1948 (local time) near Ashgabat in the Turkmen Soviet Socialist Republic, USSR. It struck at night with a surface-wave magnitude of 7.3 and a maximum perceived intensity of X on the Medvedev–Sponheuer–Karnik scale, flattening much of the city of Ashgabat and surrounding villages. It is regarded as one of the deadliest earthquakes of the 20th century.

Damage and casualties

The earthquake struck at about 1:12 a.m., when most residents were asleep. Ashgabat was built largely of adobe and unreinforced masonry, which collapsed almost completely; nearly every building in the city was destroyed or badly damaged, including brick and reinforced concrete structures. The nearby town of Bezmein and numerous villages were also devastated. Countless people were buried under the rubble of their homes.

The official death toll was long obscured by Soviet secrecy. An official report released in 1988 by the Turkmen SSR put the number of deaths in Ashgabat and the surrounding area at 110,000, nearly 80 per cent of the city's population of about 132,000. Estimates by other sources range from about 10,000 to as many as 176,000. Among the dead was the family of Saparmyrat Nyýazow, the future president of Turkmenistan, who was orphaned by the disaster.

Response and legacy

Because of damage to communications and the remoteness of the region, news of the disaster spread slowly. Soviet troops were deployed for rescue and clearance work, and the city was rebuilt over the following decade in a new seismic-resistant style. Detailed information about the earthquake was largely suppressed in the Soviet press, and the event was only openly commemorated decades later; 6 October is now observed in Turkmenistan as a national day of remembrance. The earthquake occurred along the Kopet Dag fault system at the boundary between the Eurasian and Arabian plates, a zone that remains seismically active.
