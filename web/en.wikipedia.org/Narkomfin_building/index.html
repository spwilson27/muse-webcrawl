Narkomfin Building

Narkomfin Building
Location: Novinsky Boulevard, Moscow, Russia
Architects: Moisei Ginzburg and Ignaty Milinis
Built: 1928–1932
Style: Constructivism
Purpose: Communal housing for People's Commissariat of Finance staff

The Narkomfin Building is a landmark of Constructivist architecture in Moscow, Russia. Designed by Moisei Ginzburg and Ignaty Milinis and built between 1928 and 1932 for employees of the People's Commissariat of Finance (Narkomfin), it was an experiment in collective living intended to model everyday life in a socialist society.

Design

The long residential block, raised on pilotis in the manner Le Corbusier advocated, contains small duplex "living cells" (types F and K) with minimal private kitchens, on the theory that residents would eat, wash, and socialize in communal facilities: a separate block held a canteen, laundry, library, and gymnasium, linked by an enclosed bridge, and the roof was a garden terrace. Ginzburg called such buildings "social condensers."

Later history

As communal living fell from official favor in the 1930s, the building's shared spaces were converted to offices and print shops and the units subdivided and altered. After decades of decay the Narkomfin Building was restored in a major project completed in 2020 under the direction of the architect's grandson, Alexei Ginzburg, returning its facade and many interiors to their original appearance. It is regarded as one of the most important surviving works of the Soviet avant-garde. See also Constructivist architecture.
