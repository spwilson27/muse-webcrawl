Second Polish Republic

The Second Polish Republic (Polish: II Rzeczpospolita) was the Polish state that existed from 1918 to 1939, between the end of the First World War and the invasion of Poland that opened the Second World War. It re-established Polish independence after 123 years of partition between Russia, Prussia/Germany, and Austria.

The Second Polish Republic at its greatest interwar extent.

History

Józef Piłsudski proclaimed a Polish government in November 1918, and the new state's borders were settled by the Treaty of Versailles, local plebiscites, uprisings in Greater Poland and Silesia, and the Polish–Soviet War (1919–1921), ended by the Peace of Riga. The March Constitution of 1921 created a parliamentary republic. In May 1926 Piłsudski seized power in a coup d'état and led the Sanation regime until his death in 1935, steering Poland between Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union with agreements such as the 1932 Soviet non-aggression pact and the 1934 German declaration.

Society and economy

The republic was a multinational state: ethnic Poles formed roughly two-thirds of a population that grew to about 35 million, with large Ukrainian, Jewish, Belarusian, and German minorities. Warsaw was the capital and largest city. The state built the port of Gdynia, industrialized the Central Industrial District in the 1930s, and achieved notable cultural and scientific flourishing despite the hardships of the Great Depression.

End

Following the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, Germany invaded on 1 September 1939 and the Soviet Union invaded from the east on 17 September. The Polish government fled into exile, and the republic's territory was partitioned between the occupiers, ending the state, though a government-in-exile and underground state continued the legal continuity of the republic.
