Deltoid curve

In geometry, a deltoid curve, also known as a tricuspoid curve or Steiner curve, is a hypocycloid of three cusps. In other words, it is the roulette created by a point on the circumference of a circle as it rolls without slipping along the inside of a circle with three times its radius. It is named after the capital Greek letter delta (Δ), to which it resembles.

History

Ordinary cycloids were studied by Galileo and Mersenne as early as 1599, but cycloid-like curves were first conceived by Ole Rømer in 1674 while studying the best form for gear teeth. Leonhard Euler is credited with being the first to consider the deltoid in 1745 in connection with an optical problem.

Equations

A deltoid can be represented parametrically by x = 2r cos t + r cos 2t and y = 2r sin t − r sin 2t, where r is the radius of the rolling circle. Its total arc length is 16r, and the area enclosed is 2πr², twice the area of the rolling circle.

Properties

The deltoid is a rational curve of degree four. The evolute of a deltoid is another, similar deltoid three times as large. The curve also arises in Kakeya needle problems: it is a classical example related to the smallest area in which a unit needle can be rotated through 180 degrees.
