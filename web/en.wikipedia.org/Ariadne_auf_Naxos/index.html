Ariadne auf Naxos

Ariadne auf Naxos
Opera by Richard Strauss
Libretto: Hugo von Hofmannsthal
Premiere: 4 October 1912 (first version), Stuttgart; revised version 4 October 1916, Vienna

Ariadne auf Naxos (German: Ariadne on Naxos), Op. 60, is an opera by Richard Strauss with a German libretto by Hugo von Hofmannsthal. It is one of the best known of Strauss's operas and combines elements of commedia dell'arte with the Greek myth of Ariadne and Bacchus.

Composition history

The opera was originally conceived as a small-scale work: a play-within-a-play to follow Molière's Le Bourgeois gentilhomme, with incidental music by Strauss. This first version premiered in Stuttgart in 1912. Strauss and Hofmannsthal then revised it, replacing the Molière play with a Prologue in which a wealthy patron orders that a serious opera and a comedy troupe perform simultaneously. The revised version premiered in Vienna in 1916 and is the version usually performed today.

Roles

Principal roles include the Prima Donna/Ariadne (soprano), the Tenor/Bacchus, Zerbinetta (coloratura soprano), the Composer (mezzo-soprano, a trouser role), Harlequin (baritone), and the Music Master.

Synopsis

In the Prologue, preparations for an evening's entertainment at the house of the richest man in Vienna descend into chaos when the opera company and a troupe of comedians learn they must perform at the same time. In the Opera proper, Ariadne, abandoned by Theseus on the island of Naxos, laments her fate while the comedians try to cheer her up; the god Bacchus arrives and the two are transformed by love.

Recordings

The opera has been recorded many times, with notable performances conducted by Karl Böhm, Herbert von Karajan, and Georg Solti.
