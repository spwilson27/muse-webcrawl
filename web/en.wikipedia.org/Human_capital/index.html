Human capital

Human capital
FieldEconomics
Key proponentsTheodore Schultz, Gary Becker, Jacob Mincer
ComponentsEducation, training, health, skills

Human capital is the stock of knowledge, skills, health and other attributes embodied in individuals that contributes to their productivity and earnings. Economists treat expenditures on education, training and healthcare as investments that yield future returns, analogous to investment in physical capital. The modern theory was developed in the 1960s by Theodore Schultz, Gary Becker (Human Capital, 1964) and Jacob Mincer.

Theory

Mincer's earnings function relates wages to years of schooling and experience; Becker distinguished general training, which raises productivity everywhere, from firm-specific training. Human capital helps explain wage differences, economic growth (through externalities and innovation), and migration decisions.

Measurement and policy

Indices such as the World Bank's Human Capital Index combine survival, schooling and health measures. Policy debate covers public funding of education, student debt, credential signalling (the rival view that education mainly signals ability), and the depreciation of skills. Critics also object to reducing human qualities to economic assets.
