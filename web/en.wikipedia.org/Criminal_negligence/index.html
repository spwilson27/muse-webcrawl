Criminal negligence

Criminal negligence is a degree of carelessness so serious that it amounts to a culpable mental state in criminal law. It describes conduct in which a person ignores a known or obvious risk, or fails to exercise the care that a reasonable person would exercise in similar circumstances, to an extent that the law treats the failure as deserving punishment. Ordinary negligence suffices for liability in civil law (tort), but criminal liability generally requires a more serious departure — often described as gross negligence — from the standard of care.

Criminal negligence sits between civil negligence and recklessness on the scale of fault

Definition

To be guilty of a crime involving criminal negligence, a defendant must have acted (or failed to act where under a duty) in a way that created a high risk of death or serious bodily injury, and the failure to perceive or heed that risk must involve a gross deviation from the standard of care a reasonable person would observe. Unlike recklessness, which requires conscious disregard of a risk, negligence is inadvertent: the defendant should have been aware of the risk but was not.

Applications

Criminal negligence underpins offences such as negligent homicide and manslaughter by gross negligence. In English law, gross negligence manslaughter requires a duty of care, breach of that duty, a serious and obvious risk of death, and negligence so bad as to amount to a criminal act or omission. Medical practitioners, drivers, and employers responsible for workplace safety are among those who may face prosecution where gross failures cause death.

Distinction from recklessness

The boundary between criminal negligence and recklessness is a recurring question in criminal law theory. Recklessness (as under the Model Penal Code in the United States) involves conscious disregard of a substantial and unjustifiable risk, while negligence involves failure to be aware of such a risk that the person should have perceived. Whether inadvertent risk-taking deserves criminal punishment at all is debated by legal philosophers.
