Trotternish


The Old Man of Storr, Trotternish

Trotternish (Scottish Gaelic: Tròndairnis) is the northernmost peninsula of the Isle of Skye in the Scottish Highlands, extending about 30 km north from Portree, the island's main town, which lies at its base.

Geology and landscape

The peninsula is dominated by the Trotternish Ridge, a basalt escarpment running its full length, formed by ancient lava flows and shaped by a series of massive landslips—the largest in Britain. Famous formations include the Old Man of Storr, a towering pinnacle of rock; the Quirang, a dramatic landscape of pinnacles and plateaus; and Kilt Rock, a cliff of columnar basalt. The Storr and Quirang landslips are still slowly moving.

History and culture

Trotternish includes the township of Flodigarry, associated with Flora MacDonald, who helped Charles Edward Stuart ("Bonnie Prince Charlie") escape after the Battle of Culloden in 1746 disguised as her maid; she is buried at Kilmuir cemetery, where a tall carved cross marks her grave. The area remains strongly Gaelic-speaking and crofting-based, with settlements such as Uig serving as the ferry port for the Outer Hebrides.
