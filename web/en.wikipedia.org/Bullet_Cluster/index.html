Bullet Cluster

The Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-56) is a pair of colliding galaxy clusters whose 2006 study provided some of the strongest direct evidence for the existence of dark matter. It lies about 3.7 billion light-years away in the constellation Carina.

In the collision, the hot X-ray-emitting gas of the two clusters was slowed by ram pressure and lags behind, while the galaxies passed through largely unimpeded. Gravitational lensing showed that most of the mass resides with the galaxies, not the gas — consistent with collisionless dark matter.

The result is difficult to explain with modified-gravity theories such as MOND without additional mass, making the Bullet Cluster a landmark observation in cosmology.
