An independent contractor is a person, business, or corporation that provides goods or services under a contract or verbal agreement to another entity, without being classified as an employee of that entity. Independent contractors are self-employed: they typically control how and when they work, may serve multiple clients, invoice for their services, and are responsible for their own taxes, insurance, and business expenses.

Distinction from employees

Worker classification has major legal consequences. Employees are generally entitled to minimum-wage and overtime protection, unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, employer payroll-tax contributions, and statutory benefits, whereas independent contractors largely are not. The classification therefore determines tax withholding, liability, and regulatory obligations.

Various legal tests determine status. Under the US Internal Revenue Service's common-law approach, the central question is the degree of control the hiring party exercises over the worker's behaviour, finances, and the relationship's terms. Many states apply an "ABC test", under which a worker is an employee unless the employer proves (A) freedom from control, (B) work outside the employer's usual business, and (C) an independently established trade. The Fair Labor Standards Act uses an "economic reality" test considering the totality of circumstances.

Misclassification

Treating employees as independent contractors—deliberately or in error—is known as misclassification and exposes firms to back taxes, penalties, and liability for unpaid benefits. The issue became prominent with the rise of the gig economy in the 2010s, as platform companies such as ride-hailing and delivery services classified drivers and couriers as contractors. Litigation and legislation followed, including California's AB5 law (2019), which codified the ABC test, and Proposition 22 (2020), a ballot measure exempting app-based drivers while granting some benefits. Other jurisdictions, including the United Kingdom (with its "worker" category and IR35 rules) and the European Union, maintain their own tests and reforms.

Tax treatment

In the United States, businesses report payments to independent contractors on Form 1099-NEC, and contractors pay self-employment tax covering both employer and employee shares of Social Security and Medicare. They may deduct ordinary and necessary business expenses and often organise as sole proprietors, LLCs, or corporations.
