Figurate number

Figurate number
FieldNumber theory, recreational mathematics
ExamplesTriangular, square, pentagonal, tetrahedral numbers

A figurate number is a number that can be represented by a regular geometrical arrangement of equally spaced points — for example, the triangular numbers (1, 3, 6, 10, …) formed by arranging dots in triangles, and the square numbers (1, 4, 9, 16, …) formed by squares. The study of such numbers goes back to Pythagorean mathematics.

Polygonal numbers

Generalising triangles and squares gives the polygonal numbers: pentagonal, hexagonal, and so on. The nth k-gonal number is given by the formula

P(k, n) = ((k − 2)n² − (k − 4)n) / 2

Fermat conjectured that every positive integer is a sum of at most k k-gonal numbers — the polygonal number theorem, proved by Cauchy in 1813 (with the square case going back to Lagrange's four-square theorem).

Higher dimensions

Three-dimensional analogues include tetrahedral numbers (stacked spheres in a triangular pyramid), square pyramidal numbers, and octahedral numbers; four-dimensional analogues include the pentatope numbers. "Centered" variants count patterns built outward from a central point.

History

Hypsicles gave an early formal treatment of polygonal numbers; Nicomachus and Theon of Smyrna transmitted the subject to later ages, and Diophantus wrote a treatise On Polygonal Numbers. Figurate numbers remain a staple of elementary number theory because their geometry makes algebraic identities visible.
