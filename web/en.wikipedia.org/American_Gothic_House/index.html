American Gothic House

The American Gothic House, also known as the Dibble House, is a house in Eldon, Iowa, designed in the Carpenter Gothic style with a distinctive upper-window design. It was the backdrop of the 1930 painting American Gothic by Grant Wood, generally considered Wood's most famous work and among the most recognized images in 20th-century American art.

American Gothic House
Location: Eldon, Iowa, U.S.
Built: 1881–1882
Architectural style: Carpenter Gothic
NRHP reference: listed 1974

History

The house was built in 1881 and 1882 by Catherine and Charles Dibble. Grant Wood spotted the house during a visit to Eldon in 1930 and sketched it, using its Gothic window as the backdrop for his painting of a farmer and a woman (modelled by his sister Nan Wood Graham and his dentist, Dr. Byron McKeeby).

Museum

The house is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The adjacent American Gothic House Center, opened in 2007, serves as a visitor center and museum interpreting the house, the painting, and Grant Wood's life and work. The house itself has been preserved and is visible to visitors, with costumes and pitchforks provided for photographs re-creating the painting.
