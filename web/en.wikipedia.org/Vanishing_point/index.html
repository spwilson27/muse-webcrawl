Vanishing point

A vanishing point is a point on the image plane of a perspective drawing where the two-dimensional perspective projections of mutually parallel lines in three-dimensional space appear to converge. When the set of parallel lines is perpendicular to the picture plane, the construction is known as one-point perspective, and their vanishing point corresponds to the oculus, or eyepoint, from which the image should be viewed for correct perspective geometry.

Properties

Linear perspective is a rendering technique originally developed in Renaissance Italy — Filippo Brunelleschi and Leon Battista Alberti are credited with its early formulation. In a drawing with two-point perspective, two sets of parallel horizontal lines each have their own vanishing point on the horizon line; in three-point perspective, a third vanishing point appears above or below the horizon for vertical lines. Curvilinear perspective generalizes the idea, placing vanishing points on curved horizons.

Applications

Vanishing points are used in art and architecture for realistic depiction, in photography to describe lens geometry, and in computer vision, where detecting vanishing points helps estimate camera orientation and reconstruct three-dimensional scene structure, for example in autonomous vehicle navigation and augmented reality.
