Entanglement distillation

Entanglement distillation, also called entanglement purification, is a quantum information process that converts many weakly entangled noisy pairs of qubits into a smaller number of highly entangled pairs using local operations and classical communication.

Purpose

Noise in quantum channels degrades entanglement. Distillation protocols, pioneered by Bennett and collaborators in the 1990s, sacrifice some pairs to detect and remove errors from the rest, enabling faithful teleportation and quantum repeaters.

Methods

Recurrence protocols repeatedly combine pairs and measure subsets; hashing and breeding protocols achieve better yields on large ensembles.
