The Bronze Horseman (Russian: Медный всадник, Medny vsadnik) is an equestrian statue of Peter the Great in Senate Square, Saint Petersburg, Russia. Commissioned by Catherine the Great and created by the French sculptor Étienne Maurice Falconet, it was unveiled in 1782 and has become one of the city's most famous symbols.

Design and construction

The statue depicts Peter as a heroic leader on a rearing horse, his outstretched arm gesturing toward the Neva River. The horse tramples a serpent, representing treason and the forces Peter overcame. Work began in 1768; Falconet left Russia in 1778 before completion, and his pupil Marie-Anne Collot, who modelled the tsar's head, saw the project through. Casting the huge bronze took 12 years of effort after a first attempt failed.

The Thunder Stone

The pedestal is the Thunder Stone, reputedly the largest stone ever moved by humans. The granite monolith, found at Lakhta near the Gulf of Finland, weighed an estimated 1,500 tonnes before trimming and was transported to the city in 1768–1770 using a specially built barge, capstans and a road of bronze ball bearings — a celebrated feat of 18th-century engineering.

In culture

The statue takes its popular name from Alexander Pushkin's 1833 narrative poem The Bronze Horseman, in which the statue comes to life and pursues a poor clerk through the flooded city. The poem fixed the monument at the centre of Saint Petersburg's literary mythology.
