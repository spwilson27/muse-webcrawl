Elohim

Elohim (Hebrew: אֱלֹהִים, ʾĔlōhīm) is a Hebrew word meaning "gods" or "godhood". Although grammatically plural in form, in the Hebrew Bible it most often takes singular verbal agreement and refers to the single God of Israel; in other passages it denotes plural gods, foreign deities, or divine beings such as angels and judges. Elohim occurs more than 2,600 times in the Hebrew Bible and is the first name of God used in the text — "In the beginning Elohim created the heavens and the earth" (Genesis 1:1).

Etymology and grammar

The word is the plural of eloah, related to the common Semitic root ʾil- (compare Arabic ilāh and Ugaritic ʾil, "god"). Scholars explain its use for the one God variously as a "plural of majesty" or abstract plural, or as a survival from earlier Canaanite religion, in which El headed a pantheon. When used of other beings, the Bible applies Elohim to the gods of the nations (e.g. Chemosh in Judges 11:24), to the spirit of Samuel raised at Endor, and perhaps to members of a heavenly council (Psalm 82).

In interpretation

Source criticism of the Pentateuch labels one strand of Genesis the "Elohist" source because it characteristically uses Elohim rather than the Tetragrammaton (YHWH). In rabbinic tradition Elohim is associated with God's attribute of justice, as against the mercy connoted by YHWH. In Kabbalah the name receives mystical interpretations; in Mormon theology "Elohim" is used as a name-title of God the Father; and some modern movements, notably Raëlism, reinterpret Elohim as extraterrestrials.
