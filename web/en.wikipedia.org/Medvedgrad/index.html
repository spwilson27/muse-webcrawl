Medvedgrad

Medvedgrad (pronounced [mědʋedɡraːd]; Croatian for "bear-fort") is a medieval fortified town located about 10 km west of Zagreb, Croatia, on the southern slopes of Mount Medvednica, at an elevation of about 593 m. For defensive purposes it was built on a hill overlooking the city. During its centuries of occupation it changed hands frequently; today it is a tourist attraction and houses the Altar of the Homeland.

History

After a Mongol invasion in 1242 destroyed nearby settlements, Bishop Filip of Zagreb had Medvedgrad built between 1249 and 1254. Later it was owned by the bans of Slavonia and a succession of noble families. In 1590 a strong earthquake seriously damaged the fortress, and it was finally abandoned in 1699 after another earthquake. It remained a ruin until late in the 20th century, when restoration work began.

Altar of the Homeland

In 1994 the Altar of the Homeland (Oltar Domovine), designed by sculptor Kuzma Kovačić, was erected near the fortress. It is a ceremonial monument of stone and glass with an eternal flame, dedicated to Croatia; official state commemorations are held there.

Today

Restored towers and walls offer views over Zagreb, and the site hosts the annual Medvedgrad Music Nights and medieval re-enactment events.
