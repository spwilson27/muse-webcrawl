Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement

The Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) is a free-trade agreement between Canada and the European Union and its member states. It has been provisionally applied since 21 September 2017, eliminating 98% of the tariffs in place between the two parties.

Negotiations were concluded in August 2014, and the agreement was signed on 30 October 2016 in Brussels by Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau and the presidents of the European Council and the European Commission. The European Parliament gave its consent on 15 February 2017.

Provisions

CETA eliminates most customs duties on goods traded between Canada and the EU, opens government procurement markets at federal and sub-federal levels, liberalises trade in services, and includes provisions on investment protection, regulatory cooperation, intellectual property, and sustainable development. Certain sensitive agricultural products, such as dairy and poultry in Canada and beef and sweet corn in the EU, are subject to tariff-rate quotas rather than full liberalisation.

Investment court system

In place of traditional investor-state dispute settlement, CETA establishes a permanent Investment Court System, with a tribunal and an appellate tribunal, whose members are appointed by the parties rather than chosen by investors. The compatibility of this system with EU law was upheld by the Court of Justice of the European Union in Opinion 1/17 of 30 April 2019.

Ratification

Because CETA is a "mixed" agreement, full entry into force requires ratification by all EU member states in addition to provisional application of the parts falling under EU competence. Ratification debates in several member states have focused on the investment court and on agricultural competition.
