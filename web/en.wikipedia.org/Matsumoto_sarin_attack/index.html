Matsumoto sarin attack

The Matsumoto sarin attack was an act of domestic terrorism perpetrated on the evening of 27 June 1994 in Matsumoto, Nagano Prefecture, Japan, by members of the Aum Shinrikyo cult. Eight people were killed and more than 500 were harmed by the sarin nerve agent released from a converted refrigerator truck parked in a residential neighbourhood. The attack targeted judges who were overseeing a lawsuit concerning a real-estate dispute involving Aum.

Attack

Cult members drove a truck fitted with a heating and fan system into a parking lot near the judges' dormitory and released sarin gas. The judges were poisoned but survived, and the court case was suspended.

Investigation

Police initially focused suspicion on Yoshiyuki Kōno, a resident whose wife was left in a coma by the gas; he was hounded by media as the "Poison Gas Man" before being exonerated. Only after the far larger Tokyo subway sarin attack of March 1995 was Aum Shinrikyo identified as responsible for both attacks. Several cult members were convicted; cult leader Shoko Asahara was executed in 2018.

Aftermath

The wrongful suspicion of Kōno became a notorious example of media trial by ordeal in Japan and prompted reflection on police leaks and press conduct.
