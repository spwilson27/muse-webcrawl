De-identification

De-identification is the process used to prevent someone's personal identity from being revealed. It involves removing or masking personal identifiers—such as names, addresses, dates of birth, and identification numbers—from data sets so that the remaining information cannot reasonably be used to identify an individual.

Techniques

Common techniques include pseudonymization (replacing identifiers with codes), generalization (coarsening values, such as converting a birth date to a birth year), suppression of rare values, data masking, and statistical methods such as k-anonymity, l-diversity, and differential privacy.

Regulatory context

Under the U.S. HIPAA Privacy Rule, health data may be de-identified either by removing 18 specified identifier types (the Safe Harbor method) or by expert certification that re-identification risk is very small. The EU General Data Protection Regulation treats properly anonymized data as outside its scope, while pseudonymized data remains regulated.

Risks

De-identified data can sometimes be re-identified by linkage with external data sets, as demonstrated in studies of movie ratings, search logs, and medical records. Effective de-identification therefore balances data utility against disclosure risk.
