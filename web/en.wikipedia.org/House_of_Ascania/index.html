House of Ascania

The House of Ascania was a German noble dynasty of Frankish origin, also known as the Askanier. Its members ruled various territories in the Holy Roman Empire, most notably the Margraviate of Brandenburg, the Duchy of Saxony, and the principalities of Anhalt.

History

The dynasty takes its name from Aschersleben (in Latin, Ascania) in present-day Saxony-Anhalt. Its earliest known member was Esico, Count of Ballenstedt, in the 11th century. His descendant Albert the Bear (c. 1100–1170) became the first Margrave of Brandenburg in 1157 and greatly expanded German settlement east of the Elbe. Another branch, founded through Bernhard, Count of Anhalt, held the Duchy of Saxony and the Anhalt lands.

Legacy

The Ascanian line in Brandenburg died out in 1320, and the Saxon electoral line ended in 1422, but the Anhalt branch endured: the Dukes of Anhalt ruled until the German Revolution of 1918, and the head of the house remains a notable figure among German nobility. Catherine the Great of Russia was born a princess of Anhalt-Zerbst, making her an Ascanian by birth.
