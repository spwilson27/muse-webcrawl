Mōri Motonari

Mōri Motonari (1497 – 1571) was a prominent daimyō (feudal lord) in the western Chūgoku region of Japan during the Sengoku period of the 16th century. From a minor local lord of Aki Province, he expanded the Mōri clan to rule over much of Chūgoku, defeating the Ōūuchi and Amago clans. He is famous for the legend of the "three arrows" used to teach his sons unity.

Rise to power

Motonari became head of the Mōri clan in 1523. Manoeuvring between the powerful Ōūuchi and Amago clans, he consolidated his hold on Aki Province and survived the siege of Yoshida-Kōriyama Castle in 1540–41.

Expansion

In 1555 Motonari defeated Sue Harukata at the Battle of Itsukushima, destroying Ōūuchi power, and by 1557 the Ōūuchi were extinguished. Campaigns against the Amago ended in their surrender in 1566, leaving the Mōri dominant in Chūgoku.

Legacy

Motonari's sons Takamoto, Motoharu and Takakage continued the clan's expansion. The tale that he showed his sons that one arrow breaks easily but three together do not became one of Japan's best-known parables of unity.
