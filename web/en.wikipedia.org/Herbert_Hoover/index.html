Herbert Hoover

Office: 31st President of the United States
Term: 4 March 1929 – 4 March 1933
Born: 10 August 1874, West Branch, Iowa
Died: 20 October 1964, New York City

Herbert Clark Hoover (10 August 1874 – 20 October 1964) was an American politician and engineer who served as the 31st president of the United States from 1929 to 1933. A member of the Republican Party, he had previously served as Secretary of Commerce (1921–1928) and directed major humanitarian relief efforts during and after the First World War.

Early life and career

Born in Iowa to a Quaker family and orphaned at nine, Hoover was in the first graduating class of Stanford University (1895) and became a highly successful mining engineer with worldwide business interests. During the First World War he organised the Commission for Relief in Belgium, feeding millions in German-occupied territory, and afterwards headed the American Relief Administration, which delivered food to famine-stricken Europe, including Soviet Russia in 1921–1923. As Secretary of Commerce under Presidents Harding and Coolidge he promoted standardisation, aviation, and radio regulation.

Presidency

Hoover won the 1928 election in a landslide over Al Smith. Eight months into his term the Wall Street Crash of 1929 ushered in the Great Depression. Hoover initially relied on voluntary cooperation and limited public works, but as the crisis deepened he signed the Smoot–Hawley Tariff (1930), created the Reconstruction Finance Corporation (1932), and approved major relief and public-works legislation. His administration's handling of the Bonus Army protest in 1932 damaged him politically. Blamed for the Depression, he lost decisively to Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1932.

Later life

After long retirement Hoover was rehabilitated as an elder statesman: President Truman sent him on a food mission in 1946, and he chaired the two "Hoover Commissions" on government reorganisation (1947–49 and 1953–55). He died in 1964, aged 90. Historians once ranked him harshly for the Depression, though scholarship has since credited his humanitarian career and noted institutional constraints on his presidency.
