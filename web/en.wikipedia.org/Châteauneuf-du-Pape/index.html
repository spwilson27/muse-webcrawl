Châteauneuf-du-Pape

Region: Southern Rhône, France

Known for: Red and white wines

Châteauneuf-du-Pape is a commune and famous wine appellation in the southern Rhône Valley of France, near Avignon. Its name, “the Pope's new castle,” recalls the fourteenth-century Avignon papacy, whose summer residence stood above the village.

Wine

The appellation, among the first recognized under France's AOC system in 1936, permits a palette of grape varieties traditionally counted as thirteen (or eighteen with variants), led by Grenache, Syrah, and Mourvèdre. Many vineyards are covered in large rounded stones, galets roulés, that store heat and aid ripening.

Village

The ruined papal castle, medieval streets, and wine cellars draw visitors, and the wines are among the most prestigious and age-worthy of the Rhône.
