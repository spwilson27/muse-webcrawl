Commedia dell'arte

Commedia dell'arte was a form of professional improvised theatre that began in Italy in the sixteenth century and spread across Europe. Troupes of actors performed from a brief scenario, improvising dialogue around a set of masked stock characters and practised comic routines called lazzi.

Stock characters

The companies typically comprised the vecchi or old men — the miserly Venetian merchant Pantalone and the pedantic Doctor — the zanni or servants, including the cunning Brighella and the acrobatic, gluttonous Arlecchino (Harlequin), the boastful Capitano, and the unmasked young lovers, the innamorati. Colombina, the clever maid, bridged the worlds of masters and servants.

Practice and influence

Troupes toured constantly, performing in marketplaces and courts and adapting their material to local audiences. The form profoundly influenced European drama: Molière, the English harlequinade and pantomime, Punch and Judy, and even modern improvisational comedy all descend from it. Playwright Carlo Goldoni began replacing improvisation with scripted comedy in the eighteenth century, and the professional commedia tradition gradually faded, though its characters survive in theatre, opera, and ballet.
