The Siberian Husky is a medium-sized working sled dog breed. The breed belongs to the Spitz genetic family. It is recognizable by its thickly furred double coat, erect triangular ears, and distinctive markings, and is smaller than the similar-looking Alaskan Malamute. It is an active, energetic, resilient breed, whose ancestors lived in the extremely cold and harsh environment of the Siberian Arctic.

Siberian Huskies were originally developed by the Chukchi people of Siberia for sled pulling and companionship. They were brought to Alaska in the early 20th century and gained fame in sled dog racing and through the 1925 serum run to Nome, in which teams of sled dogs relayed diphtheria antitoxin across Alaska; the lead dog Balto became famous, while Leonhard Seppala's lead dog Togo covered the longest and most hazardous leg.

Characteristics

The Siberian Husky has a dense double coat that protects against both cold and heat, with the undercoat shed typically twice a year ("blowing the coat"). Eyes may be blue, brown, green, or amber, and heterochromia — one eye of each colour — is common. Ears are erect and triangular; the tail is well furred and carried over the back in a sickle curve. Colours range from black to pure white, often with striking facial markings.

Temperament and care

Siberian Huskies are friendly, outgoing, and alert, but are generally unsuitable as guard dogs. They are intelligent but independent and can be challenging to train; they require substantial daily exercise and secure fencing, as they are known escape artists with a strong prey drive. The breed is generally healthy, with hereditary concerns including cataracts, corneal dystrophy, progressive retinal atrophy, and hip dysplasia.

History

The Chukchi developed the breed over centuries to pull light loads over long distances on minimal food. Imported to Nome, Alaska, during the gold rush, Siberian dogs dominated the All-Alaska Sweepstakes races from 1909 onwards. The breed was recognised by the American Kennel Club in 1930. Siberian Huskies served in the United States Army's Arctic search and rescue unit during the Second World War, and teams of the breed remain prominent in mid-distance sled dog racing.

In popular culture

The breed's popularity surged after the 1995 animated film Balto and again in the 2010s, when wolf-like direwolves in television fantasy dramas led to increased demand — followed, animal welfare groups reported, by increased abandonment by owners unprepared for the breed's exercise and grooming needs.
