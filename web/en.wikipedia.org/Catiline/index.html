Catiline

Lucius Sergius Catilina (c. 108 BC – January 62 BC), known in English as Catiline, was a Roman politician and soldier best known for the Catilinarian conspiracy, an attempt to overthrow the Roman Republic and particularly the power of the aristocratic Senate.

Early career

Catiline came from the ancient but impoverished patrician Sergia gens. He served under Sulla during the civil wars and prospered under the proscriptions. He later held the praetorship (68 BC) and governed Africa as propraetor, but was charged with extortion on his return and repeatedly failed to win the consulship.

The conspiracy

After electoral defeats in 63 BC, Catiline gathered discontented nobles, veterans, and debtors in a plot to seize power. The consul Cicero exposed the conspiracy in a series of famous speeches, the Catilinarians, and the Senate passed the senatus consultum ultimum. Five conspirators in Rome were executed on Cicero's orders.

Death

Catiline fled to Etruria, where he joined the rebel army of Manlius. He was killed fighting at the Battle of Pistoria in January 62 BC. The historian Sallust's monograph Bellum Catilinae is the principal surviving account of the episode.
