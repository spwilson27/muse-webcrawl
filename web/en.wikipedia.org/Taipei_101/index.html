Taipei 101, the tallest building in the world from 2004 to 2010.

Taipei 101 (Chinese: 台北101), formerly the Taipei World Financial Center, is a 508-metre (1,667 ft) skyscraper in the Xinyi District of Taipei, Taiwan. Completed in 2004, it was the world's tallest building until surpassed by the Burj Khalifa in 2010, and it remains Taiwan's tallest building and an icon of the city. It is among the tallest buildings in the world with a LEED Platinum rating for energy efficiency.

Design

Designed by C.Y. Lee & Partners, the tower's form evokes a stalk of bamboo — a symbol of growth and prosperity — with eight stacked segments of eight floors each (eight being a lucky number in Chinese culture), flaring outward like a Chinese pagoda or an opening flower. Decorative elements include giant coin and ruyi motifs. The building houses offices, a shopping mall in its podium, restaurants, and indoor (89th floor) and outdoor (91st floor) observatories.

Engineering

Standing in a typhoon-prone, earthquake-active zone near a fault line, Taipei 101 is stabilised by a 660-tonne tuned mass damper — a giant steel pendulum suspended between the 87th and 92nd floors, the largest of its kind when installed and itself a visitor attraction. The damper reduces sway by up to 40 percent and famously swung visibly during the 2008 Sichuan earthquake. Its high-speed elevators, among the world's fastest when built, travel at up to 60.6 km/h.

Events

The tower hosts Taipei's famous New Year's Eve fireworks display, launched from its own floors, and the annual Taipei 101 Run Up stair-climbing race up 2,046 steps. Climber Alain Robert scaled the building officially in 2004.
