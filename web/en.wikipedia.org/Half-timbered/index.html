Half-timbered

Timber framing and "post-and-beam" construction are traditional methods of building with heavy timbers. The term half-timbered describes a structure built with a wooden frame whose spaces are filled with wattle and daub, brick or plaster infill, leaving the frame members exposed.

History

Half-timbered construction was widespread in medieval and early modern Europe, especially in England, France (colombage) and Germany (Fachwerk), and survives in countless historic town centres. The exposed timbers are often arranged in decorative patterns with braces, jettied upper floors and carved posts.

Characteristics

Structural members are joined with mortise and tenon joints secured by wooden pegs. The style was revived in the 19th century (Tudorbethan) and remains popular in faux form on modern suburban houses.
