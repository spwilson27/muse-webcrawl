Lepton number

In particle physics, lepton number (historically also called leptonic charge) is a conserved quantum number assigned to leptons. Leptons carry lepton number L = +1, antileptons carry L = −1, and all other particles have L = 0. In the Standard Model, total lepton number is conserved in all interactions, a consequence of the structure of the theory rather than a fundamental symmetry imposed by hand.

Flavour lepton numbers

In addition to the total lepton number, each lepton family has its own flavour lepton number: electron lepton number Le, muon lepton number Lμ and tau lepton number Lτ. In reactions involving only charged leptons and their neutrinos these were long believed to be separately conserved. The discovery of neutrino oscillation — recognised by the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics — showed that flavour lepton numbers are violated for propagating neutrinos, though total lepton number remains conserved in all observed processes.

Violating processes

Processes that would violate total lepton number, such as neutrinoless double beta decay, have never been observed. If discovered, they would prove neutrinos are Majorana particles and point to physics beyond the Standard Model; lepton number violation in the early universe is also a key ingredient of leptogenesis, a proposed explanation of the matter–antimatter asymmetry.
