Feud

Feud (blood feud)
Type: Customary vendetta
Examples: Albanian gjakmarrja, Corsican vendetta

A feud or blood feud is a long-running argument or fight between parties — often families or clans — in which killings are avenged by further killings in a retaliatory cycle. Feuds were regulated by customary law in many pre-state societies.

Customary law

Codes such as the Albanian Kanun governed who could be targeted and allowed mediation and truces (besa). Similar institutions existed in Corsica, Montenegro, Sicily and among Germanic peoples, where compensation (wergeld) could end a feud.

Famous feuds

The Hatfield–McCoy feud in the Appalachian United States (1863–1891) became a byword for inter-family violence. Blood feuds persist in a few regions today despite state prohibition.
