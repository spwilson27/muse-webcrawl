Herbert Jones, VC, OBE (14 May 1940 – 28 May 1982), known as "H", was a British Army officer who was posthumously awarded the Victoria Cross for his actions during the Battle of Goose Green in the Falklands War.

Early life and career

Born in Putney, London, and educated at Eton, Jones was commissioned into the Devonshire and Dorset Regiment before transferring to the Parachute Regiment. He served in Northern Ireland, where he was appointed OBE, and by 1982 commanded 2 PARA, the battalion that sailed south with the task force sent to retake the Falkland Islands after the Argentine invasion.

Battle of Goose Green

On 28 May 1982, 2 PARA attacked Argentine positions defending the Darwin–Goose Green isthmus. When the advance stalled under fire from a trench position on Darwin Hill, Jones led a small group in a charge up the hill; he was shot and killed while attacking the position, which was subsequently overrun. His citation credited his actions with turning the course of the battle, the first major land engagement of the war, which ended with the surrender of over a thousand Argentine troops.

Legacy

Jones's Victoria Cross is held by the National Army Museum. His widow, Sara Jones, became a prominent campaigner for war widows. Historians continue to debate whether his charge was inspired leadership or an avoidable risk, but his name remains among the most famous of the Falklands conflict.
