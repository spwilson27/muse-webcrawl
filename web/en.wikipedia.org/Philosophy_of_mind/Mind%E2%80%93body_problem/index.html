Field: Philosophy of mind
Related: Dualism, physicalism, consciousness

Mind–body problem

The mind–body problem is a philosophical problem concerning the relationship between thought and consciousness in the human mind and the body. It addresses how mental states, events, and processes relate to physical states, events, and processes, and whether the mind is in some way distinct from the body.

Dualism

René Descartes argued for substance dualism: the mind is an immaterial, thinking substance, while the body is a material, extended substance. Dualism faces the interaction problem — how an immaterial mind could causally affect a physical body — pressed forcefully by Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia in correspondence with Descartes. Variants include property dualism, parallelism, and occasionalism.

Physicalism and monism

Physicalists hold that the mind is ultimately physical, defending positions such as behaviourism, the type identity theory, functionalism, and eliminative materialism. Neutral monists and idealists offer alternative one-substance views. Despite advances in neuroscience correlating mental states with brain states, the problem of phenomenal consciousness — the "hard problem" — keeps the debate open.
