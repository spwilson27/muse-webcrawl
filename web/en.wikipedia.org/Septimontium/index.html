Septimontium

The Septimontium was a proto-urban federation of settlements on the hills of early Rome, and later the name of a festival celebrated by the inhabitants of the seven hills (montes) on 11 December. The Latin name derives from septem ("seven") and montes ("hills").

The seven hills

The hills traditionally associated with the Septimontium were not identical to the later canonical seven hills of Rome. Ancient sources list: the Palatium and Cermalus (the two peaks of the Palatine), the Velia, the Fagutal, and the three peaks of the Esquiline — the Oppius, Cispius, and the Subura area. The festival likely commemorated the gradual synoecism — the joining together — of these hilltop communities into a single city.

The festival

According to Varro and Festus, sacrifices were offered at each of the montes. Originally the Septimontium was celebrated only by the montani, the inhabitants of the hills, but in later times the observance became general throughout the city. Roman tradition held that it was not a public festival in the formal calendar but a celebration "for the people of the seven hills".
