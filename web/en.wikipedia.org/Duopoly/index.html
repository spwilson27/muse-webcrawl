Duopoly

Market structureOligopoly with two sellers
Classic modelsCournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg

A duopoly is a type of oligopoly in which two firms dominate a market for a particular product or service. Because each firm's decisions strongly affect the other, duopolies are a central case study in game theory and industrial organisation.

Models

The Cournot model (1838) assumes firms compete by choosing quantities simultaneously, producing an outcome between monopoly and perfect competition. The Bertrand model assumes price competition, which with identical products can drive prices to marginal cost even with only two firms. The Stackelberg model introduces a leader that commits to a quantity first, gaining a first-mover advantage.

Examples

Familiar near-duopolies include Boeing and Airbus in large commercial aircraft, Visa and Mastercard in payment card networks, and Coca-Cola and Pepsi in cola drinks. Regulators scrutinise duopolistic markets for tacit collusion, since only two competitors may find it easier to sustain parallel pricing without explicit agreement.

Media usage

The term is also used in broadcasting regulation, notably in the United States, where a "duopoly" refers to common ownership of two television stations in one market, permitted under FCC rules since 1999 subject to conditions.
