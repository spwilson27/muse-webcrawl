States' rights

States' rights is a political philosophy in the United States holding that the individual states have certain powers and prerogatives that the federal government may not usurp. It rests on the Tenth Amendment to the U.S. Constitution, which reserves to the states or the people all powers not delegated to the federal government.

History

The doctrine underlay the Kentucky and Virginia Resolutions of 1798, the nullification crisis of 1832–33, in which South Carolina claimed the right to nullify federal tariffs, and the Southern states' secession in 1860–61, which led to the Civil War. The war and Reconstruction amendments curtailed the doctrine's stronger claims.

Later usage

In the 20th century "states' rights" became closely associated with resistance to federal civil-rights enforcement and school desegregation, a linkage that has shaped the term's political resonance. The concept nevertheless continues to appear in debates over federalism — including education, environmental regulation, firearms, and healthcare — and in Supreme Court jurisprudence on the Commerce Clause and anti-commandeering principle.
