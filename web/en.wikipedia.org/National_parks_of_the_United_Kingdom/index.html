National parks of the United Kingdom

The national parks of the United Kingdom are fifteen designated areas of protected countryside: thirteen in England and Wales under the 1949 Act framework, and two in Scotland — Loch Lomond and The Trossachs (2002) and the Cairngorms (2003) — created under the National Parks (Scotland) Act 2000. There are no national parks in Northern Ireland, although the Mourne Mountains have long been proposed. Together the parks cover roughly 8% of the UK's land area.

Characteristics

British national parks differ from the IUCN model of wilderness parks: they are lived-in, largely privately owned cultural landscapes, classified by the IUCN as Category V protected landscapes. National Park Authorities are the local planning authorities for their areas. The Scottish parks add aims of sustainable use of natural resources and sustainable economic and social development to the conservation and recreation purposes applied in England and Wales.

History and developments

The system began with the Peak District in 1951 following the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949. Extensions to existing parks (the Lake District and Yorkshire Dales in 2016) brought park boundaries almost together. Recent reviews, including the 2019 Glover Landscapes Review, have recommended stronger nature recovery duties, and both England and Scotland have consulted on creating new parks — Scotland committed to designating at least one further park by 2026.
