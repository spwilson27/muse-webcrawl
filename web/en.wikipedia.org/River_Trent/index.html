The River Trent is the third-longest river in the United Kingdom, at 298 kilometres (185 miles). It rises in Staffordshire near Biddulph Moor and flows through the Midlands — past Stoke-on-Trent, Burton upon Trent, Nottingham, and Newark-on-Trent — before joining the Yorkshire Ouse at Trent Falls to form the Humber Estuary.

Course and tributaries

The Trent drains a catchment of about 15,300 square kilometres, encompassing much of the Midlands. Its major tributaries include the Sow, the Tame, the Derbyshire Derwent, the Soar, and the Devon. Unusually for a major English river, it flows northwards in its lower course. Burton upon Trent became a world centre of brewing thanks to its gypsum-rich water, and the cooling towers of the Trent valley power stations once gave the reach the nickname "Megawatt Valley".

Navigation and tides

The river is navigable to Burton, and large craft reach Gainsborough. Below Cromwell Lock it is tidal; the tidal bore known as the Trent Aegir can be seen at Gainsborough on high spring tides. Historically the Trent formed the boundary between northern and southern England in administration and dialect, and in the phrase "north of the Trent".
