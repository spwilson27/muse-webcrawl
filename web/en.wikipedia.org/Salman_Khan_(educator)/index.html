Salman Amin Khan (born October 11, 1976), known as Sal Khan, is an American educator and entrepreneur who founded Khan Academy, a free online education platform that produces short instructional videos and practice exercises for students around the world. He is widely regarded as a pioneer of large-scale digital learning and one of the most influential figures in modern education technology.

Early life and education

Khan was born in Metairie, Louisiana, to a family of Bengali Muslim origin; his father was from Barisal, present-day Bangladesh, and his mother from Murshidabad, India. He attended public schools in Louisiana and went on to study at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where he earned bachelor's and master's degrees in mathematics and in electrical engineering and computer science. He later completed a Master of Business Administration at Harvard Business School.

Career

After graduating, Khan worked as a hedge fund analyst at Connective Capital Management. In 2004, he began tutoring his cousin Nadia in mathematics over the telephone and later through Yahoo! Doodle images and YouTube videos. As friends and relatives asked to use the videos, Khan posted them publicly; by 2008 the demand led him to quit his job and devote himself full-time to the lessons. In 2009 he formally incorporated Khan Academy as a nonprofit organization.

Khan Academy grew rapidly with support from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, Google, and other donors, expanding from mathematics into science, history, economics, computing, and test preparation. Khan himself has recorded thousands of videos, typically narrating problems on a digital blackboard in a conversational style. The platform also developed adaptive practice software, teacher tools, and later AI-assisted tutoring experiments.

Recognition and writings

Khan has been named to Time magazine's list of the 100 most influential people in the world and has received numerous awards, including the Heinz Award in Technology, the Economy and Employment. In 2012 he published The One World Schoolhouse: Education Reimagined, arguing for free, self-paced, mastery-based learning. In 2024 he co-authored Brave New Words, exploring the role of artificial intelligence in education.
