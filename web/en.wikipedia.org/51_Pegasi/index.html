51 Pegasi

51 Pegasi (abbreviated 51 Peg), formally named Helvetios, is a Sun-like star located about 50.9 light-years from Earth in the constellation of Pegasus. In 1995 it became the first main-sequence star found to have an exoplanet in orbit around it.

The planet

The planet, 51 Pegasi b (informally "Bellerophon", formally "Dimidium"), was discovered by Swiss astronomers Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz using the radial velocity method at the Observatoire de Haute-Provence. It is a "hot Jupiter" — a gas giant of roughly half Jupiter's mass orbiting its star in just 4.2 days, far closer than Mercury orbits the Sun. The discovery was announced on 6 October 1995, and Mayor and Queloz shared the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics for it.

Stellar properties

51 Pegasi is a G-type main-sequence star slightly more massive and metal-rich than the Sun, with an estimated age of about 6 billion years. Its apparent magnitude of 5.49 makes it visible to the naked eye under dark skies.

Naming

In the International Astronomical Union's NameExoWorlds contest of 2015, the star received the name Helvetios (Latin for "the Helvetian", referring to Switzerland) and its planet the name Dimidium ("half", referring to its mass relative to Jupiter).
