Deuteronomy

Deuteronomy (Ancient Greek: Δευτερονόμιον, "second law"; Hebrew: דְּבָרִים, Devarim, "words") is the fifth and final book of the Torah and of the Old Testament. It consists largely of three speeches delivered by Moses to the Israelites on the plains of Moab, reviewing their history since the Exodus and restating the covenant law before they enter the Promised Land, and it ends with the appointment of Joshua and the death of Moses on Mount Nebo.

Contents

After a historical prologue (chapters 1–4), Moses expounds the law: the Shema ("Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one"), the Ten Commandments repeated, laws on worship at a single central sanctuary, festivals, justice, warfare, family and social life, and blessings and curses (chapter 28) attaching to obedience and disobedience. The book closes with the Song of Moses, his blessing of the tribes, and his death at the age of 120 within sight of Canaan.

Composition

Mainstream biblical scholarship associates the core of Deuteronomy with the "book of the law" found in the Temple during repairs under King Josiah of Judah (2 Kings 22, c. 622 BCE) and with Josiah's centralizing reform; the work is linked to the hypothetical Deuteronomistic History spanning Joshua through Kings. Distinctive features include its rhetorical, sermon-like style, insistence on one sanctuary, and its covenant theology, which influenced much later Jewish and Christian thought. Jesus quotes Deuteronomy more than any other book except Psalms and Isaiah, answering temptation in the Gospels with three quotations from it.
