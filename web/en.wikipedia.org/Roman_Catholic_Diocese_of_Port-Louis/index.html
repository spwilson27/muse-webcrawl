The Roman Catholic Diocese of Port-Louis (Latin: Dioecesis Portus Ludovici) is the Catholic diocese covering the island nation of Mauritius in the Indian Ocean. Its seat is St Louis Cathedral in the capital, Port Louis. The diocese serves the island's Catholic community, historically rooted among Franco-Mauritians and Creoles, in a religiously plural country with a Hindu majority.

History

Catholicism arrived with French colonisation of the Isle de France in the 18th century. After Britain took the island in 1810 the Church was reorganised; a vicariate apostolic for Mauritius was erected in 1819 (initially covering a vast Indian Ocean territory) and it became the Diocese of Port-Louis in 1847. The diocese was long led by English, Irish and French missionary clergy; the Mauritian Jean Margéot, ordained bishop in 1969, became the country's first cardinal in 1988 and a nationally respected moral figure. His successor Maurice Piat, bishop from 1993, was made a cardinal in 2016.

The diocese today

The diocese is exempt — it belongs to no metropolitan province and answers directly to Rome — and it participates in the Episcopal Conference of the Indian Ocean (CEDOI), which groups the island dioceses of the region. Rodrigues island is served separately by its own apostolic vicariate. Catholic schools and charities, including institutions founded by figures such as the beatified missionary priest Jacques-Désiré Laval — whose tomb at Sainte-Croix draws pilgrims of all faiths each September — form a major part of the diocese's public presence.
