The New York State Canal Corporation is a New York State public-benefit corporation responsible for the oversight, administration and maintenance of the New York State Canal System, which consists principally of the Erie Canal, the Oswego Canal, the Cayuga–Seneca Canal and the Champlain Canal. It also operates the Canalway Trail, a network of multi-use recreational paths along the canals.

History

The corporation was created in 1992 as a subsidiary of the New York State Thruway Authority, to which responsibility for the canal system was transferred from the state's Department of Transportation. Legislation signed in 2016 transferred the Canal Corporation to the New York Power Authority (NYPA), effective 1 January 2017, as part of a plan to revitalise the canal system and its surrounding communities.

Operations

The canal system comprises some 524 miles of waterways and 57 locks. Originally built for commercial freight, the canals today are used mainly by recreational boaters, tour boats and a small amount of cargo traffic. The corporation maintains locks, dams, bridges, and other infrastructure; sets rules for navigation; and promotes tourism and economic development along the canal corridor, which passes through much of upstate New York.
