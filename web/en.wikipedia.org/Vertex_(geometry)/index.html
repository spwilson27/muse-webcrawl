Vertex (geometry)

DefinitionCorner point where edges or curves meet
PluralVertices (or vertexes)
ExamplesPolygon corners; polyhedron corners; graph nodes

In geometry, a vertex (plural: vertices) is a point where two or more curves, lines, or edges meet. Vertices are the corner points of polygons and polyhedra, the apex of an angle, and — in graph theory and computer graphics — the points of a mesh connected by edges.

Vertices of angles and polygons

The vertex of an angle is the common endpoint of its two rays. In a polygon, each vertex is shared by exactly two edges and one interior angle. A polygon with n sides has exactly n vertices. Euler characteristic calculations for polyhedra relate the numbers of vertices (V), edges (E), and faces (F) by the formula V − E + F = 2.

Principal vertex and other uses

The highest point of a parabola or a conic section is also called its vertex. In computer graphics, a vertex is a data structure describing a point in 2D or 3D space, often with attributes such as colour, normal vector, and texture coordinates, processed by vertex shaders. In graph theory, vertices (nodes) are the fundamental units connected by edges.
