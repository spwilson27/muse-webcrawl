Engagement ring

An engagement ring, also known as a betrothal ring, is a ring indicating that the person wearing it is engaged to be married, especially in Western cultures. It is traditionally presented during a marriage proposal or soon after a proposal is accepted. In many traditions it is worn on the ring finger of the left hand, based on the ancient belief that a vein in that finger (the vena amoris) ran directly to the heart.

History

Rings as symbols of betrothal date back to ancient Rome, where a woman might receive an iron ring for everyday wear and a gold ring for public occasions. Diamond engagement rings appeared among European aristocracy from at least the 15th century; the 1477 betrothal of Archduke Maximilian of Austria to Mary of Burgundy is a famous early example. The custom became widespread only in the 20th century, boosted by the diamond company De Beers' advertising campaigns beginning in the 1930s and the slogan "A Diamond Is Forever" (1948), which helped make the diamond solitaire the standard engagement ring in many countries.

Styles and customs

The classic engagement ring is a diamond solitaire in a prong or "Tiffany" setting, but other gemstones such as sapphires, emeralds, and moissanite, and lab-grown diamonds, are also popular. Customs vary worldwide: in some countries both partners wear engagement rings, and in some traditions the engagement ring is later worn with a wedding band on the same finger. The expected expenditure on the ring varies widely; the widely marketed notion that it should cost two months' salary originated in advertising.

Legal questions

When an engagement is broken off, legal systems differ on whether the ring must be returned. Some jurisdictions treat it as a conditional gift that must be returned if the marriage does not occur, regardless of fault; others consider it an absolute gift.
