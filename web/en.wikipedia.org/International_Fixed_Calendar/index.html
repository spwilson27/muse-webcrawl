International Fixed Calendar

The International Fixed Calendar (IFC) is a proposed solar calendar reform designed by Moses B. Cotsworth, first proposed in 1902. It divides the year into 13 months of 28 days each (364 days), so that every month begins on a Sunday and has exactly four weeks.

Structure

The extra month, called Sol, is inserted between June and July. The remaining day of the year — two in leap years — is treated as an intercalary holiday ("Year Day", and "Leap Day") not assigned to any week. The IFC was famously used by the Eastman Kodak Company for internal accounting from 1928 to 1989, but proposals for international adoption failed at the League of Nations in the 1930s, partly owing to religious objections to days outside the seven-day week.
