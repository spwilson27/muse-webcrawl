Aaron T. Beck

Aaron Temkin Beck
Born: July 18, 1921, Providence, Rhode Island
Died: November 1, 2021, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania
Known for: Cognitive therapy; Beck Depression Inventory

Aaron Temkin Beck (July 18, 1921 – November 1, 2021) was an American psychiatrist who was a professor in the department of psychiatry at the University of Pennsylvania. He is regarded as the father of cognitive therapy and cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), and his pioneering methods are widely used in treating clinical depression and various anxiety disorders.

Work

Trained in psychoanalysis, Beck's research with depressed patients in the 1960s led him to identify distorted, negative automatic thoughts — about oneself, the world, and the future — as central to depression. He developed structured techniques to test and change these cognitions, and created widely used assessment tools including the Beck Depression Inventory and the Beck Anxiety Inventory.

Legacy

In 1994 Beck co-founded the Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy with his daughter Judith S. Beck. He received the Lasker-DeBakey Award in 2006 and is regarded as one of the most influential psychotherapists of all time.
