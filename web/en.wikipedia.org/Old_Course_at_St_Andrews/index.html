The Old Course at St Andrews is one of the oldest and most iconic golf courses in the world, a public links on common land in St Andrews, Fife, Scotland. Widely regarded as the "home of golf," it is held in trust by the St Andrews Links Trust and operated in partnership with the local authority. Golf is documented on the links as early as 1552, and the course is traditionally said to have hosted the game since the early 1400s.

History

The links land was granted to the burgh of St Andrews for the "playing of golf, futball, schuting" and other pastimes. In 1764 golfers reduced the course from 22 holes to 18 — a change that helped establish 18 as the standard number of holes worldwide. The course originally played the same fairways out and back, with two holes per green; the famous huge double greens, the deep pot bunkers (112 in total), and names such as the "Road Hole" (the 17th, with its notorious Road Bunker) and "Hell Bunker" emerged over centuries of play. Allan Robertson and Old Tom Morris shaped the course in the mid-19th century, widening fairways and separating greens when the one-course layout became congested.

The Open Championship

The Old Course has hosted The Open Championship 30 times, more than any other venue, beginning in 1873 when Tom Kidd won. Winners at St Andrews include Jack Nicklaus (1970, 1978), Seve Ballesteros (1984), Nick Faldo (1990), John Daly (1995), Tiger Woods (2000, 2005), Louis Oosthuizen (2010), Zach Johnson (2015) and Cameron Smith, who won the 150th Open in 2022. The stone Swilcan Bridge over the Swilcan Burn on the 18th hole, and the adjoining Valley of Sin, are among the most photographed features in sport.

Access

Unusually for a major championship venue, the Old Course is a public course on common land: residents have preferential rights, and visitors enter an advance ballot (lottery) or book guaranteed tee times. The course is closed on most Sundays, when the public traditionally walks the fairways.
