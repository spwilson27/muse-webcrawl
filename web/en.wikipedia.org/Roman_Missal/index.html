The Roman Missal (Latin: Missale Romanum) is the principal liturgical book of the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church. It contains the texts and rubrics for the celebration of the Mass, including the Order of Mass, the prayers for Sundays, feasts and seasons of the liturgical year, and rites for particular occasions.

Contents

The missal is organised around the liturgical calendar: the Proper of Time (Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter and Ordinary Time), the Proper of Saints, Commons, ritual Masses, Masses for various needs, votive Masses and Masses for the dead. It opens with the General Instruction of the Roman Missal (GIRM), which sets out the norms for celebration.

History

Early medieval sacramentaries — such as the Leonine, Gelasian and Gregorian sacramentaries — contained the priest's prayers but not readings or chants; the modern missal, combining all parts in one volume, developed in the later Middle Ages. After the Council of Trent, Pope Pius V promulgated a standardised Missale Romanum in 1570 with the bull Quo primum. Successive popes issued revised editions, the last pre-conciliar one being that of John XXIII in 1962, which remains the basis of the "Traditional Latin Mass".

Following the Second Vatican Council, Paul VI promulgated a thoroughly revised missal in 1969–1970 implementing the reforms of Sacrosanctum Concilium. A third typical edition appeared under John Paul II in 2002, and revised vernacular translations, such as the English translation of 2011, aimed at closer fidelity to the Latin text.
