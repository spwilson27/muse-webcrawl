Endorphin

Endorphins (from "endogenous morphine") are endogenous opioid neuropeptides produced by the pituitary gland and central nervous system. They bind opioid receptors, inhibiting pain perception and producing feelings of well-being.

Types and function

The best-known, beta-endorphin, is cleaved from pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC), as are ACTH and melanocyte-stimulating hormones. Endorphins are released in response to pain, stress, exercise, and other stimuli. The popular "runner's high" was long attributed to endorphins, though endocannabinoids are now thought to play a major role because endorphins cross the blood–brain barrier poorly.

History

Opioid receptors were identified in the early 1970s; John Hughes and Hans Kosterlitz isolated the enkephalins in 1975, and endorphins were characterized soon afterward.
