Nazca Plate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nazca Plate is a tectonic plate in the southeastern Pacific Ocean off the west coast of South America. It is named after the Nazca region of southern Peru. It is subducting under the South American Plate along the Peru–Chile Trench at a rate of roughly 7–8 centimetres per year, one of the fastest absolute plate motions known.

Movements and effects

The subduction of the Nazca Plate is responsible for the uplift of the Andes, the world's longest continental mountain range, and for the frequent powerful earthquakes and volcanic eruptions of western South America, including the 1960 Valdivia earthquake in Chile — the most powerful ever recorded. Hot spots carried on the plate have produced the Galápagos, Easter, and Juan Fernández islands; the subducting Nazca Ridge is thought to explain the "flat slab" segment and the inland gap in volcanism under Peru.

Boundaries

The plate is bounded by the Pacific and Cocos plates to the west and north along spreading ridges (the East Pacific Rise and Galápagos spreading centre), by the Antarctic Plate to the south along the Chile Rise, and by the South American Plate to the east. See also Plate tectonics and Andes.
