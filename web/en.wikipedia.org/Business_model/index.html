Business model

Business model
Concept in management
Describes: How an organisation creates, delivers and captures value
Related: Business Model Canvas, value proposition

A business model describes the rationale of how an organisation creates, delivers, and captures value, in economic, social, cultural or other contexts. It specifies the customers served, the value proposition offered, the activities and resources required, and how the organisation earns revenue and profit.

Components

Alexander Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas organises a model into nine blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships and cost structure. Other frameworks emphasise value networks, capabilities and financial logic.

Types

Common types include subscription models, freemium, razor-and-blades, franchising, marketplaces and platforms, advertising-supported media, and direct-to-consumer retail. Digital technology has enabled new models such as software as a service and sharing-economy platforms.

Business model innovation

Changing the business model — rather than only products or processes — can itself be a source of competitive advantage, as illustrated by low-cost airlines, online retail and streaming media displacing older industry models.
