Reduction print

A reduction print is a multicolour relief print made from a single block that is progressively carved away between colour layers. The artist prints the lightest colour first over the whole edition, then cuts away the areas to remain that colour, prints the next colour, and repeats — lightest to darkest — until the block is largely destroyed. For this reason it is nicknamed the 'suicide print': mistakes cannot be corrected and the edition cannot be reprinted.

The technique is used in linocut and woodcut and was famously developed by Pablo Picasso in the late 1950s for his linocuts, in collaboration with printer Hidalgo Arnéra.
