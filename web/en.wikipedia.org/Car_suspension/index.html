Car suspension

Car suspension is the system of tires, tire air, springs, shock absorbers and linkages that connects a vehicle to its wheels and allows relative motion between the two. Suspension systems must support both road holding and ride quality, which are at odds with each other, so design is a compromise tuned to the vehicle's purpose.

Function

The suspension keeps the tires in contact with the road, isolates the body from road irregularities, resists body roll and pitch, and maintains steering and wheel alignment geometry. Springs (coil, leaf, torsion bar, or air) support the vehicle's weight, while dampers (shock absorbers) control spring oscillation.

Types

Dependent suspensions, such as a live axle, move both wheels together; independent suspensions (MacPherson strut, double wishbone, multi-link) allow each wheel to move separately, improving ride and handling. Modern developments include semi-active and fully active suspension, adaptive dampers, and air suspension with automatic leveling.
