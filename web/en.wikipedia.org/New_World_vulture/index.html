New World vulture

New World vultures, family Cathartidae, are scavenging birds of prey native to the Americas, comprising seven species including the turkey vulture, black vulture, king vulture and the condors. They are not closely related to the superficially similar Old World vultures of Africa and Asia, representing an example of convergent evolution.

Characteristics

New World vultures have largely bald heads, hooked bills suited to tearing carrion, and broad wings for soaring. Unusually among birds, turkey vultures locate food by an acute sense of smell. They lack a syrinx and are nearly voiceless, and they practise urohidrosis — defecating on their legs — for cooling.

Species

The family includes the Andean and California condors, among the largest flying birds, the widespread turkey vulture, and the yellow-headed vultures of South American lowlands.
