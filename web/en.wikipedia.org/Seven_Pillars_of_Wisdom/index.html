Seven Pillars of Wisdom is the autobiographical account by British Army officer T. E. Lawrence ("Lawrence of Arabia") of his experiences during the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire in 1916–1918, when he served as a liaison officer with rebel forces in the Hejaz and the Levant.

Background

The title comes from the Book of Proverbs (9:1): "Wisdom hath builded her house, she hath hewn out her seven pillars." Before the war, Lawrence had planned a book about seven great cities of the Middle East; he repurposed the title for his war memoir. The book recounts guerrilla operations, including attacks on the Hejaz railway and the capture of Aqaba and Damascus.

Publication history

Lawrence drafted the work in 1919, lost much of the manuscript, and rewrote it from memory. An abridged version, Revolt in the Desert, appeared in 1927; the full text was published for general readers in 1935, the year of Lawrence's death. The book formed the basis for David Lean's 1962 film Lawrence of Arabia.
