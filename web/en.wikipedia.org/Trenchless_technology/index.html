Trenchless technology

Trenchless technology is a type of subsurface construction work that requires few trenches or no continuous trenches. It is a rapidly growing sector of the construction and civil engineering industry, used for installing, replacing, or rehabilitating underground infrastructure — pipelines, conduits, and cables — with minimal excavation and disruption at the surface.

New installation methods

  • Horizontal directional drilling (HDD) steers a drill head along a curved pilot path, enlarges the bore with reamers, and pulls the product pipe back through.
  • Microtunnelling uses a remotely controlled, laser-guided boring machine with pipe jacked behind it, allowing accurate installation below the water table.
  • Pipe ramming drives a steel casing through the ground with a percussive hammer.
  • Auger boring rotates a cutting head inside a casing, removing spoil with helical flights.
  • Impact moling displaces soil with a pneumatic piercing tool for short, small-diameter crossings.

Rehabilitation methods

Existing pipelines can be renewed without digging them up. Cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining inserts a resin-impregnated tube that hardens to form a new pipe within the old one. Pipe bursting fractures the old pipe outward while pulling a replacement pipe into the same alignment, even allowing upsizing. Other techniques include sliplining, spray and brush coatings, and grouting.

Advantages and limits

Trenchless methods reduce traffic disruption, surface reinstatement costs, and damage to landscaping and pavements, and can cross rivers, railways, and buildings. They generally require careful geotechnical investigation, skilled operators, and suitable ground conditions, and unit costs may exceed open-cut work for shallow, short installations.
