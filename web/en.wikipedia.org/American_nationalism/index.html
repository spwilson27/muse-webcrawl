American nationalism

American nationalism is a form of nationalism found in the United States. Analysts have debated whether it is best understood as civic nationalism, grounded in shared political ideals such as liberty, democracy, and the rule of law, or whether ethnic and cultural elements have also played a defining role in American identity.

History

American national identity was forged in the Revolutionary era around the "American Creed" of republican ideals. The Civil War redefined the nation as an indivisible union, while waves of immigration, westward expansion, and Manifest Destiny added expansionist and sometimes exclusionary strands, including nativist movements.

Modern debates

Scholars such as Samuel P. Huntington have argued that Anglo-Protestant culture forms the core of American identity, while others emphasize a multicultural, civic conception. National symbols — the flag, the Pledge of Allegiance, and the national anthem — remain central to expressions of American nationalism.
