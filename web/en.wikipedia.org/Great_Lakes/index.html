Surface area: 244,106 km²

Countries: Canada, United States

The Great Lakes, also called the Great Lakes of North America, are a series of large interconnected freshwater lakes in the east-central interior of North America, on the border of Canada and the United States.

Geography

From west to east: Lake Superior (largest by area), Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario. They drain via the St. Lawrence River to the Atlantic. Formed by glacial ice scouring at the end of the last Ice Age about 14,000 years ago, they hold about 21% of the world's surface fresh water.

History

Indigenous peoples including the Anishinaabe lived around the lakes for millennia, harvesting wild rice. French explorers and the fur trade arrived in the 17th century. The lakes became a major shipping route, especially after canals and the St. Lawrence Seaway (1959).

Ecology

The basin supports diverse fisheries and cities such as Chicago, Toronto, Detroit and Cleveland, alongside pollution, invasive species and climate challenges managed binationally.
