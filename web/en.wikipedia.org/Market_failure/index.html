Market failure

In neoclassical economics, market failure is a situation in which the allocation of goods and services by a free market is not Pareto efficient — another allocation could make someone better off without making anyone worse off.

Causes

Recognized sources include externalities (such as pollution), public goods that are non-excludable and non-rival, market power including monopoly, information asymmetries, and incomplete markets. Each violates assumptions of the first fundamental theorem of welfare economics.

Responses

Proposed remedies include Pigouvian taxes and subsidies, regulation, public provision, tradable permits, and assigning property rights (the Coase theorem). Economists also note government failure: interventions can create their own inefficiencies, rent-seeking, and capture.

Criticism

Public-choice and Austrian-school economists question whether real markets should be judged against an idealized benchmark, arguing that entrepreneurial discovery tends to correct failures over time.
