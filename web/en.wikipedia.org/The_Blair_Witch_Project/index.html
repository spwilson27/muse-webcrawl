The Blair Witch Project is a 1999 American psychological horror film written, directed, and edited by Daniel Myrick and Eduardo Sánchez. It is presented as "found footage": the recovered film of three student filmmakers — Heather Donahue, Michael C. Williams, and Joshua Leonard, playing fictionalised versions of themselves — who hiked into the Black Hills of Maryland in 1994 to make a documentary about the local legend of the Blair Witch, and disappeared.

Production

The film was shot over eight days in Maryland's Seneca Creek State Park on 16 mm film and consumer Hi8 video, with the three actors operating the cameras themselves. The directors fed the cast minimal information, guided them by GPS to pre-placed notes and props, and subjected them to dwindling food rations and night-time scares to provoke genuine exhaustion and fear. The initial production cost was around $60,000 (rising to a few hundred thousand dollars with post-production and marketing after acquisition by Artisan Entertainment).

Marketing and reception

The film is a landmark of internet marketing: its website, fake police reports, and a Sci-Fi Channel mockumentary (Curse of the Blair Witch) blurred fiction and reality so effectively that many early viewers believed the footage was genuine. Premiered at Sundance in 1999, it grossed nearly $250 million worldwide, making it one of the most profitable films ever relative to its budget. Critics praised its restraint and atmosphere, though some audiences disliked its shaky camerawork, which reportedly induced motion sickness. It went on to define the found-footage horror genre, paving the way for films such as Paranormal Activity and Cloverfield, and was followed by a sequel (2000), a further sequel, Blair Witch (2016), books, comics, and video games.
