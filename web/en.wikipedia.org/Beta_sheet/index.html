Beta sheet

The beta sheet (β-sheet, beta-pleated sheet) is a protein secondary structure of extended beta strands connected laterally by backbone hydrogen bonds, forming a pleated sheet. Proposed by Linus Pauling and Robert Corey in 1951 alongside the alpha helix.

Beta sheet
Strands: Parallel or antiparallel
H-bonds: Between strands
Examples: Silk fibroin, amyloid

Structure

Strands of 5–10 amino acids run parallel or antiparallel; side chains alternate above and below the pleats. Beta bulges, barrels and propellers are common motifs.

Significance

Silk fibroin is largely beta sheet, giving tensile strength. Aberrant beta-sheet aggregation forms amyloid fibrils in Alzheimer's and prion diseases.
