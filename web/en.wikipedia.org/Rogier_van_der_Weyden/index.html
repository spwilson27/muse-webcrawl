Rogier van der Weyden

Born: c. 1399/1400, Tournai, County of Hainaut
Died: 18 June 1464, Brussels, Duchy of Brabant
Movement: Early Netherlandish painting

Rogier van der Weyden (Dutch: [roːˈɣiːr vɑn dər ˈʋɛi̯də(n)]; c. 1399 or 1400 – 18 June 1464), initially known as Rogier de le Pasture, was an Early Netherlandish painter whose surviving works consist mainly of religious triptychs, altarpieces, and commissioned portraits. Along with Robert Campin and Jan van Eyck, he is considered one of the founders of Early Netherlandish painting and the most influential Northern artist of the mid-fifteenth century.

Life and career

Born in Tournai, Van der Weyden entered the workshop of Robert Campin in 1427 and became a master in 1432. By 1435 he had moved to Brussels, where he was appointed city painter. Wealthy and esteemed, he received commissions from the Burgundian court, the city of Leuven, and Italian patrons; his style profoundly influenced painting across Europe, including in France, Germany, and Spain.

Major works

His masterpiece, The Descent from the Cross (c. 1435, Museo del Prado, Madrid), packs grieving figures into a shallow gilded space with sculptural intensity. Other major works include the Beaune Altarpiece (c. 1445–50), depicting the Last Judgment for the Hospices de Beaune; the Bladelin Altarpiece; the Crucifixion Triptych; and sensitive portraits such as the Portrait of a Lady (c. 1460) in the National Gallery of Art, Washington.

Style and influence

Van der Weyden was unmatched in conveying emotional pathos through gesture, silhouette, and composition. Because no signed works survive, attributions rest on documentary and stylistic evidence, and many workshop productions blur the line between his hand and those of assistants. His compositional inventions were copied for generations.
