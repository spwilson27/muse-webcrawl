The Shanghai maglev train (Chinese: 上海磁浮示范运营线) is a magnetic levitation train line that operates in Shanghai, China. It was the first commercially operated high-speed magnetic levitation line in the world. The line connects Shanghai Pudong International Airport with Longyang Road station in Pudong, where passengers can transfer to the Shanghai Metro. Using German Transrapid technology, the train covers the roughly 30 km (19 mi) journey in about 7 minutes and 20 seconds.

History

Construction began in 2001, and commercial service began in January 2004 after trial runs the previous year. The project cost approximately 10 billion yuan (about US$1.2 billion). During a test run on 12 November 2003, a train reached 501 km/h (311 mph), a record for a commercial maglev train.

Operations

In regular service the train reaches a maximum speed of 431 km/h (268 mph), making it one of the fastest passenger trains in commercial operation. The train levitates slightly above the guideway, propelled and guided by magnetic forces, which reduces friction, noise and mechanical wear compared with conventional rail.

Extensions

A long-planned extension to Shanghai Hongqiao station, and an intercity line to Hangzhou, were studied for years but repeatedly postponed, in part because of public concern over magnetic radiation and the cost of the technology compared with conventional high-speed rail.
