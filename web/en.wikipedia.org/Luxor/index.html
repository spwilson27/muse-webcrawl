Luxor (Arabic: الأقصر, al-ʾUqṣur, lit. "the palaces") is a city in Upper Egypt and the capital of Luxor Governorate. It stands on the east and west banks of the Nile at the site of ancient Thebes, capital of Egypt during the New Kingdom. Luxor is frequently characterised as the "world's greatest open-air museum", home to the temple complexes of Karnak and Luxor Temple on the east bank and the Theban Necropolis—including the Valley of the Kings and the Valley of the Queens—on the west bank.

Luxor Temple, built largely under Amenhotep III and Ramesses II.

History

Thebes (Egyptian Waset) rose to prominence during the Middle Kingdom and became the capital of the New Kingdom empire (c. 1550–1070 BC). Its god Amun, with Mut and Khonsu, formed the Theban Triad worshipped at Karnak. After periods of decline and foreign rule, the city remained a religious centre; in the modern era tourism became its economic mainstay following the great Egyptological discoveries, including Tutankhamun's tomb in 1922 in the nearby Valley of the Kings.

Main sights

  • Karnak temple complex
  • Luxor Temple and the Avenue of Sphinxes
  • Valley of the Kings and Valley of the Queens
  • Mortuary Temple of Hatshepsut at Deir el-Bahari
  • Colossi of Memnon
  • Luxor Museum and the Mummification Museum

Climate

Luxor has a hot desert climate, with some of the hottest summer days of any city in Egypt and very little rainfall.
