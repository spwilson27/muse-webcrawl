Cayenne pepper

The cayenne pepper is a type of Capsicum annuum. It is usually a hot chili pepper used to flavor dishes. Cayenne peppers are a group of tapering, 10 to 25 cm long, generally skinny, mostly red-colored peppers, often with a curved tip and somewhat rippled skin, which hang from the bush as opposed to growing upright.

Heat

Most varieties are generally rated at 30,000 to 50,000 Scoville units, owing to their content of capsaicin. The powder is made from dried, ground fruit and is a staple spice in many cuisines, including Cajun and Creole cooking of Louisiana, and is used in hot sauces.

History

The pepper takes its name from the city of Cayenne in French Guiana. Chili peppers were cultivated in Central and South America for thousands of years before the Columbian exchange spread them worldwide, where they became integral to cuisines from Hungary to Sichuan.

Uses

Cayenne is used fresh, dried, or powdered in cooking; in traditional medicine; and as the active ingredient in some pepper sprays. It is rich in vitamin A and contains capsaicin, studied for its metabolic and analgesic effects.
