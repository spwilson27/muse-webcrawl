Geriatrics

Geriatrics, also known as geriatric medicine, is a medical specialty focused on addressing the unique health needs of older adults. It aims to promote health by preventing, diagnosing, and treating disease and disability in older adults, with an emphasis on maintaining function and quality of life rather than on curing single diseases in isolation.

Geriatric care emphasises function, independence, and quality of life.

Scope

Older patients frequently live with multiple chronic conditions simultaneously (multimorbidity) and take many medications, so geriatricians are trained in comprehensive geriatric assessment — a multidisciplinary evaluation of medical, functional, cognitive, and social domains. Characteristic "geriatric syndromes" such as frailty, falls, delirium, urinary incontinence, and pressure ulcers often matter more to outcomes than any single diagnosis. The field is distinct from gerontology, the broader scientific study of ageing itself.

History

The term was coined in 1909 by the Austrian-American physician Ignatz Nascher, by analogy with "pediatrics". The modern specialty took shape in Britain through the work of Marjory Warren in the 1930s and 1940s, who showed that systematic assessment and rehabilitation could transform the care of elderly inpatients. Geriatrics is now an established specialty worldwide, though most countries report shortages of specialists relative to their ageing populations.
