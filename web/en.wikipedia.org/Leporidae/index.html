Leporidae

Leporidae is the family of rabbits and hares, containing over 60 species of extant mammals in 11 genera. Together with the pikas (family Ochotonidae), they form the order Lagomorpha. Leporids are found worldwide, either natively or as introduced species, in environments ranging from tundra to desert and tropical forest. They are herbivores characterised by long ears, short tails, and hind legs adapted for jumping and running.

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Mammalia
Order: Lagomorpha
Family: Leporidae (Fischer de Waldheim, 1817)

Characteristics

Leporids range in size from the pygmy rabbit at about 20 cm long and 0.4 kg to hares exceeding 70 cm and 5 kg. Their dentition is distinctive: behind the main pair of upper incisors sits a second small pair of "peg teeth", giving four upper incisors in total — a trait distinguishing lagomorphs from rodents. Hind limbs are elongated and powerful, and the body is covered in soft fur. Most species are crepuscular or nocturnal and highly social or solitary depending on the genus; rabbits typically live in warrens, whereas hares are generally solitary and give birth to precocial young in open nests called forms.

Genera

The 11 extant genera include Lepus (hares and jackrabbits), Oryctolagus (the European rabbit, ancestor of all domestic rabbits), Sylvilagus (cottontails), Brachylagus (pygmy rabbit), Nesolagus (striped rabbits) and Romerolagus (volcano rabbit).
