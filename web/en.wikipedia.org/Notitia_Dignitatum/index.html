Notitia Dignitatum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Notitia Dignitatum (Latin for "The List of Offices") is a document of the late Roman Empire that details the administrative organization of the eastern and western imperial courts and the military establishment of the empire. It is one of the very few surviving documents of Roman imperial government and an unparalleled source for the structure of the late Roman army.

Contents

The Notitia is divided into two parts, for the Eastern and Western empires. For each, it lists the great officers of state — praetorian prefects, masters of the soldiers, counts, dukes, and civil governors — together with their staffs, subordinates, and areas of responsibility. The military sections list the units of the field armies (comitatenses) and frontier troops (limitanei) province by province, recording where each regiment was stationed.

Uniquely, the surviving medieval copies are illustrated: they reproduce the shield patterns (insignia) of the army units, the badges of office of officials, and schematic pictures of the fortresses and cities under each frontier commander. These images provide precious evidence about late antique art, heraldry's precursors, and military equipment.

Date and transmission

The text in its final form reflects the empire as it stood around AD 420 in the West and around 395–400 in the East, though it was probably compiled and updated over several decades by the office of the primicerius notariorum, the chief notary who tracked imperial appointments. The original is lost; the text survives through a Carolingian manuscript (the Codex Spirensis, itself lost) known from Renaissance copies, several of them illustrated.

Significance

Historians use the Notitia to reconstruct the late Roman army's order of battle, the geography of military deployment along frontiers such as the Rhine, Danube, and Saxon Shore, and the machinery of the later Roman state. Its lists of forts and units complement archaeological evidence and other sources such as the works of Ammianus Marcellinus. The shield designs it preserves have become emblematic of the late Roman military in popular culture.
