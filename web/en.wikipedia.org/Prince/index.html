Prince

Prince Rogers Nelson
Born: June 7, 1958, Minneapolis, Minnesota, U.S.
Died: April 21, 2016 (aged 57), Chanhassen, Minnesota, U.S.
Genres: Funk, R&B, rock, pop, soul

Prince Rogers Nelson (June 7, 1958 – April 21, 2016) was an American singer, songwriter, musician, record producer and actor. Widely regarded as one of the greatest musicians of his generation, he was known for his flamboyant stage presence, eclectic work across multiple genres, and wide vocal range, which included a distinctive falsetto and high-pitched screams. His music integrated funk, R&B, rock, new wave, soul, psychedelia and pop.

Career

Born and raised in Minneapolis, Prince signed with Warner Bros. Records as a teenager and released his debut album, For You, in 1978. He achieved critical and commercial success with albums including Dirty Mind (1980), Controversy (1981) and 1999 (1982). His sixth album, Purple Rain (1984) — the soundtrack to the film of the same name, in which he starred — spent 24 consecutive weeks at number one on the Billboard 200 and won the Academy Award for Best Original Song Score.

During the 1980s he was a central figure in the "Minneapolis sound" and led the bands The Revolution and later the New Power Generation. In 1993, amid a contractual dispute with Warner Bros., he changed his stage name to an unpronounceable symbol, and was often referred to as "the Artist Formerly Known as Prince"; he resumed using the name Prince in 2000. Subsequent acclaimed albums included Sign o' the Times (1987), the Batman soundtrack (1989), Diamonds and Pearls (1991) and Musicology (2004).

Death and legacy

Prince died on April 21, 2016, at his Paisley Park estate in Chanhassen, Minnesota, from an accidental overdose of fentanyl, aged 57. He sold over 100 million records worldwide, won seven Grammy Awards and an Academy Award, and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2004. Landmarks around the world were lit purple in his honour after his death.
