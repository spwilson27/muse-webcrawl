The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center (PTWC) is one of two tsunami warning centers in the United States, operated by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). Located at Ewa Beach on the island of Oahu in Hawaii, it is responsible for issuing tsunami warnings for most of the Pacific Ocean basin and for Hawaii.

History

The PTWC was founded in 1949 in response to the 1946 Aleutian Islands earthquake, whose tsunami devastated Hilo, Hawaii, killing more than 150 people. Initially called the Seismic Sea Wave Warning System, it was headquartered at the Honolulu Magnetic and Seismological Observatory before moving to Ewa Beach.

Operations

Scientists at the center monitor seismic stations worldwide in real time. When a large undersea earthquake occurs, they analyze its magnitude, depth, and location, and consult data from the DART (Deep-ocean Assessment and Reporting of Tsunamis) buoy network, which detects tsunami waves in the open ocean. Depending on the threat, the center issues warnings, watches, advisories, or information statements to emergency managers and to international partners around the Pacific Rim. After the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, the center's responsibilities temporarily expanded to the Indian Ocean and Caribbean until regional systems were established.
