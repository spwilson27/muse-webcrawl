Diana Ross

Diana Ross (born March 26, 1944) is an American singer and actress. She rose to fame as the lead singer of the vocal group The Supremes, who became Motown's most successful act during the 1960s and one of the world's best-selling girl groups of all time.

The Supremes

Born in Detroit, Michigan, Ross began singing with friends as the Primettes in 1959 and signed to Motown as the Supremes in 1961. Between 1964 and 1969 the group achieved twelve number-one singles on the Billboard Hot 100, including "Where Did Our Love Go", "Stop! In the Name of Love" and "You Can't Hurry Love".

Solo career

Ross left the Supremes in 1970 and launched a successful solo career with hits such as "Ain't No Mountain High Enough", "Touch Me in the Morning", "Love Hangover", "Upside Down" and "Endless Love" (a duet with Lionel Richie). As an actress she received an Academy Award nomination for her portrayal of Billie Holiday in Lady Sings the Blues (1972) and starred in Mahogany (1975) and The Wiz (1978).

Legacy and honours

Ross was named the "Female Entertainer of the Century" by Billboard in 1976. She received a Kennedy Center Honor in 2007, the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2012, and the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016. Her influence on subsequent generations of pop and R&B performers is widely acknowledged.
