Kambuja

Kambuja (or Kamboja) was an ancient Indo-Aryan people and kingdom of the far northwest of the Indian subcontinent, mentioned in Sanskrit and Pali literature alongside the Gandharas, Madras and other peoples of the Uttarapatha ("northern route"). Their country is generally located in the region spanning eastern Afghanistan and the Hindu Kush, and later tradition extended the name toward Central Asia; some scholars associate them with Iranian-speaking groups on the Indo-Iranian frontier.

In ancient literature

The Kambojas appear in the Mahabharata and Ramayana, in the edicts of Ashoka, and in Patanjali's Mahabhashya. They are listed among the sixteen great realms in early Buddhist sources as Kammboja. Kamboja warriors were prized as cavalrymen, and their horses were considered among the finest in ancient India. A Kamboja king, Sudakshina, fights on the Kaurava side in the Kurukshetra War of the Mahabharata.

Name and legacy

The name Kambuja is also the root of the word "Cambodia": Khmer tradition held that the Khmer Empire's founder descended from the sage Kambu Svayambhuva — hence Kambuja, "born of Kambu" — and the Sanskritised name of the Cambodian state, Kampuchea, preserves this lineage claim, though the exact historical connection between the northwestern Kambojas and Southeast Asia remains debated.
