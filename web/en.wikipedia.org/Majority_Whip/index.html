Majority Whip

The Majority Whip is a leadership position in a legislative body. In the United States Congress, the majority whip is the second- or third-ranking member of the majority party leadership in the House of Representatives and the Senate, responsible for mobilizing votes on major issues.

Role

The whip counts votes, persuades members to support the party position, ensures attendance for key votes, and communicates between the leadership and rank-and-file members. A network of deputy and regional whips assists in this work. The term derives from the "whipper-in" in fox hunting, who kept the hounds together.

House and Senate

In the House, the majority whip is the third-highest-ranking majority-party member, after the Speaker and the majority leader. In the Senate, the majority whip (also called the assistant majority leader) is second in the majority leadership. The minority party has a corresponding minority whip.

Other systems

In Westminster-style parliaments, whips enforce party discipline and issue written voting instructions also called "whips"; the government Chief Whip is a senior office.
