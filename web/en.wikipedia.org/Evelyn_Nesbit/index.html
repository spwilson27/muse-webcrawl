Evelyn Nesbit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Evelyn Nesbit (born Florence Evelyn Nesbit; December 25, 1884 or 1885 – January 17, 1967) was an American artists' model, chorus girl, and actress. She is best remembered for her involvement in the sensational 1906 murder of architect Stanford White by her husband, millionaire Harry Kendall Thaw, in what the press dubbed "The Trial of the Century".

Early career

Raised in poverty near Pittsburgh after her father's death, Nesbit moved to New York City in 1900 and became one of the most photographed models of the era, posing for painters and illustrators including Charles Dana Gibson — she is often cited as an inspiration for the "Gibson Girl". She joined the chorus of the musical Florodora and then The Wild Rose on Broadway.

Stanford White and Harry Thaw

Backstage, Nesbit was courted by the celebrated architect Stanford White, then 47, who became her patron — and, as she later testified, sexually assaulted her when she was 16 after she had been given champagne in his apartment, which contained a famous red velvet swing. Railroad heir Harry Kendall Thaw, obsessed with both Nesbit and ruining White, married her in 1905. On 25 June 1906, at a performance of Mam'zelle Champagne at Madison Square Garden (which White had designed), Thaw shot White three times in the face in front of hundreds of witnesses.

Trial and later life

Thaw's trials riveted the country; the first ended in a hung jury, and at the second (1908) he was found not guilty by reason of insanity and committed to an asylum, from which he was released in 1915. Nesbit testified for the defence, describing White's assault in testimony that made her a figure of scandal. The Thaws divorced in 1915. Nesbit went on to careers in vaudeville, silent film, and dance instruction, ran a tearoom in Hell's Kitchen during Prohibition, struggled with addiction, and published two memoirs, The Story of My Life (1914) and Prodigal Days (1934). She died in Santa Monica, California, in 1967.

Nesbit's story inspired the novel and film Ragtime and the 1955 film The Girl in the Red Velvet Swing.
