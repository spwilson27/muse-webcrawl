The San Diego Zoo is a zoo in Balboa Park, San Diego, California, housing more than 12,000 animals of more than 650 species and subspecies on 100 acres (40 ha) of land leased from the city. It is operated by the nonprofit San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance and is one of the most famous and frequently visited zoos in the world, regularly attracting over four million visitors a year.

History

The zoo grew out of exotic animal exhibitions abandoned after the 1915 Panama–California Exposition. Dr. Harry M. Wegeforth founded the Zoological Society of San Diego in 1916, and the zoo opened in Balboa Park shortly thereafter. It pioneered open-air, cageless enclosures with moats, and early director Belle Benchley became its first full-time female zoo director. In 1972 the organization opened the San Diego Wild Animal Park (now the San Diego Zoo Safari Park) in the San Pasqual Valley.

Exhibits and conservation

Major exhibits include the Elephant Odyssey, the Lost Forest (with gorillas and hippos), Panda Trek — home for decades to giant pandas under a celebrated breeding program — and the Africa Rocks complex that opened in 2017. The zoo is renowned for its botanical collection, with more than 700,000 plants, an important part of its mission of plant conservation.

Through the San Diego Zoo Institute for Conservation Research, the organization runs breeding and reintroduction programs, most famously for the California condor, which was brought back from just 22 birds in the 1980s to a growing wild population.
