The Knesset (Hebrew: הַכְּנֶסֶת, "the Assembly") is the unicameral legislature of Israel. It passes all laws, elects the president, approves the cabinet (the "government"), and supervises the work of the executive through its committees and no-confidence votes. Its 120 members are elected every four years by nationwide proportional representation with an electoral threshold of 3.25 per cent.

History

The Knesset first convened on 14 February 1949, succeeding the Assembly of Representatives of the Jewish community under the British Mandate and the Provisional State Council. Its name and size derive from the Knesset HaGedolah (Great Assembly) of the Second Temple period, which also had 120 members. Israel has no formal written constitution; the Knesset has instead enacted a series of Basic Laws that together form an evolving constitutional framework, several of which can only be amended by special majorities.

Composition and elections

The whole country forms a single constituency. Voters cast a ballot for a party list rather than an individual candidate; seats are allocated proportionally among lists passing the threshold. Because no party has ever won an outright majority, Israeli governments are always coalitions. Members of the Knesset (MKs) enjoy parliamentary immunity and elect a Speaker from among their number.

Building

The permanent Knesset building in Jerusalem's Givat Ram district, funded by the Rothschild family, was inaugurated in 1966. Its forecourt displays a large bronze menorah, a gift from the British Parliament, whose reliefs depict scenes from Jewish history. Earlier sessions were held in Tel Aviv and in Jerusalem's Froumine House.

Functions

Besides legislation, the Knesset approves the state budget, declares war (formally on the government's recommendation), and its committees — including Finance, Foreign Affairs and Defense, and the State Control Committee — conduct oversight of ministries. Legislation begins as government bills, private members' bills, or committee bills and normally passes three readings in the plenum.
