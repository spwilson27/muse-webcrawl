Genetic genealogy

Genetic genealogy is the use of genealogical DNA tests — DNA-based genetic tests — in combination with traditional genealogical methods to infer genetic relationships between individuals, find relatives, and discover personal ancestry. It involves comparing the results of tests for living or deceased individuals and, increasingly, ancient DNA.

Types of test

Three main kinds of test dominate. Autosomal DNA tests examine the 22 pairs of non-sex chromosomes and can identify relatives within roughly the last five to seven generations, reporting shared segments measured in centiMorgans. Y-DNA tests trace the direct paternal line, since the Y chromosome passes largely unchanged from father to son, and are used in surname projects. Mitochondrial DNA tests trace the direct maternal line. Results are interpreted using haplogroups, which describe shared deep ancestry.

History

Consumer genetic genealogy began around 2000 with companies such as Family Tree DNA, and expanded greatly after 2007 with autosomal testing from 23andMe and later AncestryDNA. The International Society of Genetic Genealogy (ISOGG) was founded in 2005. In 2018, investigative genetic genealogy — comparing crime-scene DNA against public databases such as GEDmatch — led to the identification of the Golden State Killer suspect, raising both public interest and privacy debate.

Ethics and privacy

Testing can reveal unexpected parentage and unknown relatives, and shared databases create privacy implications for people who never tested themselves. Several jurisdictions and database operators have since introduced consent and opt-in policies for law-enforcement matching.
