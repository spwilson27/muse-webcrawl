The Norwegian Sea (Norwegian: Norskehavet) is a marginal sea of the Arctic Ocean, situated northwest of Norway between the North Sea and the Greenland Sea. It is bounded by Norway to the east, Iceland to the west, the Faroe Islands to the south, and Jan Mayen to the north, where it merges with the Barents Sea near Bear Island.

Geography and oceanography

The sea covers about 1.38 million square kilometres with an average depth around 2,000 metres. It was formed tens of millions of years ago as Greenland and Scandinavia separated. The warm Norwegian Current brings Atlantic water northward, keeping much of the sea ice-free year-round despite its latitude, while colder Arctic water enters from the north and west. The sharp fronts between these water masses create highly productive fishing grounds.

Resources

The Norwegian Sea supports major fisheries for cod, herring, and mackerel, and its continental shelf off Norway contains significant oil and gas fields, production from which began in the 1990s. Whales and seabirds are abundant in summer.
