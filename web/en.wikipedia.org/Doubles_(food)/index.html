Doubles (food)

Doubles is a common street food in Trinidad and Tobago, of Indo-Trinidadian origin. It consists of curried chickpeas (channa) served between two pieces of fried flat bread called bara, and is topped with condiments such as cucumber, coconut chutney, tamarind sauce, and pepper sauce. It is eaten most often for breakfast but also as a late-night snack, and is one of the country's most beloved and inexpensive foods.

Components

The bara is a soft, slightly chewy flatbread made from flour, split-pea flour, yeast, turmeric (giving it a yellow colour), and spices, fried briefly in hot oil. The channa is curried chickpeas simmered with curry powder, cumin (geera), garlic, and culantro (chadon beni/shado beni). Customers order doubles with specified heat levels — "slight pepper", "medium", or "plenty pepper" — and vendors assemble them to order in seconds.

Origin and name

Doubles is generally credited to Emamool "Mamoodeen" Deen and his wife Raheman Rasulan Deen, who began selling fried channa wrapped in cone-shaped paper in Princes Town in 1936 and developed the bara-and-channa sandwich. According to a common account, customers who asked for their order to be "doubled up" with a second bara gave the food its name. The dish combines Indian culinary traditions brought by indentured labourers with Caribbean ingredients and street-vending culture.

Cultural significance

Doubles vendors are fixtures at street corners, markets, and transport hubs across Trinidad, and the food has spread with the Trinidadian diaspora to Toronto, New York, and London. It is a point of national pride and a staple at fetes and Carnival. Variations include "triples" (three baras) and aloo (potato) pies sold by the same vendors.
