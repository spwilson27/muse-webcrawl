Ebstein's anomaly

Ebstein's anomaly
Specialty: Cardiology
Type: Congenital heart defect
Affected structure: Tricuspid valve and right ventricle
Named for: Wilhelm Ebstein

Ebstein's anomaly is a rare congenital heart defect in which the septal and posterior leaflets of the tricuspid valve are displaced downward into the right ventricle, so that part of the ventricle is "atrialised". It was first described by the German physician Wilhelm Ebstein in 1866.

Signs and symptoms

Severity varies widely. Mild cases may be asymptomatic, while severe neonatal cases present with cyanosis, heart failure and cardiomegaly. Associated features include an atrial septal defect, accessory conduction pathways causing Wolff–Parkinson–White syndrome, and tricuspid regurgitation. A widely split first heart sound ("sail sound") may be heard.

Causes

The malformation arises from failure of delamination of the tricuspid valve leaflets during fetal development. Maternal lithium use in pregnancy has historically been associated with an increased risk, though the association is weaker than once thought.

Treatment

Management ranges from observation and medical therapy in mild cases to surgical repair of the valve — notably the cone reconstruction procedure — or valve replacement in severe cases.
