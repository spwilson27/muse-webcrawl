Nabu

Nabu (Akkadian: Nabû) is the Babylonian and Assyrian god of literacy, the rational arts, scribes, and wisdom. He was the son of the god Marduk and his consort Sarpanit, and the grandson of Ea. His symbols are the clay tablet and the stylus, and he rode on the horned dragon-serpent (mušḫuššu) associated with his father.

Worship

Nabu's main cult centre was the Ezida temple at Borsippa, near Babylon; another major temple stood at Nimrud (Kalhu) in Assyria. During Babylon's New Year (Akitu) festival, Nabu's statue travelled to Babylon to join Marduk, and he was credited with recording the destinies of mankind on the Tablets of Destiny each year. His worship rose sharply in the first millennium BCE, and his name became a common element in Babylonian and Assyrian personal names — as in Nebuchadnezzar ("Nabu, protect the heir").

Later tradition

Nabu appears in the Hebrew Bible as Nebo (notably in Isaiah 46). He was associated by interpreters with the classical Mercury and Apollo as a god of writing and prophecy, and elements of his cult survived into late antiquity in Mesopotamia and Syria.
