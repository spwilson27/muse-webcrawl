Politics of the Republic of Ireland

The politics of the Republic of Ireland takes place in a framework of a parliamentary representative democratic republic and of a plural multi-party system, wherein the President is head of state and the Taoiseach is head of government. Executive power is exercised by the government, legislative power is vested in both the government and the two houses of the parliament, the Oireachtas, and the judiciary is independent of the executive and the legislature. The state is governed under the Constitution of Ireland (Bunreacht na hÉireann), adopted in 1937.

President

The President of Ireland (Uachtarán na hÉireann) is elected directly by the people for a seven-year term, renewable once. The office is largely ceremonial: the President formally appoints the Taoiseach and ministers, signs bills into law, and serves as Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces, but ordinarily acts on the advice of the government. The President may, at their absolute discretion, refuse a dissolution of the Dáil to a Taoiseach who has lost majority support.

Government

The government is headed by the Taoiseach, nominated by the Dáil Éireann (the lower and more powerful house of the Oireachtas) and appointed by the President. The Tánaiste serves as deputy prime minister. Ministers are chosen by the Taoiseach from members of the Oireachtas and are collectively responsible to the Dáil.

Oireachtas

The Oireachtas consists of the President and two houses: Dáil Éireann and Seanad Éireann (the Senate). Teachtaí Dála (TDs) are elected by the single transferable vote in multi-seat constituencies. The Seanad, with 60 members elected or nominated through vocational panels, universities and Taoiseach's nominees, has only delaying powers over legislation.

Political parties

Irish politics was long dominated by two large parties descended from opposing sides of the Irish Civil War: Fianna Fáil and Fine Gael, which formed their first coalition together in 2020. Other significant parties include Sinn Féin, the Labour Party, the Green Party and the Social Democrats. No party has won an outright Dáil majority since 1977, and coalition government is the norm.

Judiciary and local government

The courts, headed by the Supreme Court, may review the constitutionality of legislation. Local government is exercised by county and city councils with comparatively limited powers, though directly elected mayors have been introduced in some cities.
