Greenlandic (Greenlandic: Kalaallisut) is an Inuit language spoken by the Greenlandic Inuit people in Greenland. It is closely related to the Inuit languages of Canada, such as Inuktitut, and is the most widely spoken of the Eskaleut languages. The standard language is based on the West Greenlandic dialect spoken around the capital, Nuuk.

Classification and dialects

Greenlandic has three main dialect groups: West Greenlandic (Kalaallisut), the standard and by far the largest; East Greenlandic (Tunumiisut), strikingly divergent in phonology; and the northern dialect Inuktun (Avanersuarmiutut) spoken around Qaanaaq, which is closer to Canadian Inuktitut. Since the Self-Government Act of 2009, Greenlandic is the sole official language of Greenland, although Danish remains widely used in administration and education.

Grammar

Greenlandic is a polysynthetic language: long, complex words are built from a root plus numerous derivational suffixes and inflectional endings, so that a single word can express the meaning of a whole sentence in languages such as English. It is ergative–absolutive in alignment and has a dual number in addition to singular and plural. Nouns inflect for eight cases, and verbs agree with both subject and object.

Writing system

The language was first written in the 18th century by Danish missionaries. A spelling reform in 1973 replaced the older system devised by Samuel Kleinschmidt (which used the special letter ĸ, "kra") with a simpler Latin orthography closer to actual pronunciation. Greenlandic makes notable use of consonant clusters and gemination.
