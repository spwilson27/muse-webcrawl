Mihal "Mike" Lazaridis (born 14 March 1961) is a Canadian businessman and philanthropist who co-founded Research In Motion (RIM) in 1984, the company that created the BlackBerry smartphone. He served as co-CEO of RIM until 2012 and is credited as the technical visionary behind the BlackBerry's secure mobile email system.

Early life and RIM

Lazaridis was born in Istanbul to Greek parents and immigrated to Canada as a child, settling in Windsor, Ontario. He dropped out of the University of Waterloo's electrical engineering program weeks before graduating to grow RIM, which he had founded with Douglas Fregin. RIM's two-way pagers evolved into the BlackBerry, launched in 1999, which dominated enterprise mobile communications for a decade.

Philanthropy and science

Lazaridis donated over $170 million to found the Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics in Waterloo (1999) and gave major gifts to the University of Waterloo, including for its Institute for Quantum Computing and the Mike & Ophelia Lazaridis Quantum-Nano Centre. In 2013 he co-founded the Quantum Valley Investments fund to commercialize quantum technologies in the Waterloo region.

Honors

He was made an Officer of the Order of Canada, elected a Fellow of the Royal Society, and received numerous honorary degrees. He stepped down from BlackBerry's board in 2013 after an unsuccessful bid, with Fairfax Financial, to take the company private.
