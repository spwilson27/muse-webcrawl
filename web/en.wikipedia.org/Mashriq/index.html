Mashriq
Arabic: ٱلْمَشْرِق, "the east"
Region: Eastern Arab world
Countries commonly included: Egypt, Sudan, Jordan, Lebanon, Palestine, Syria, Iraq, and the Arabian Peninsula states

The Mashriq (Arabic: ٱلْمَشْرِق, romanized: al-Mashriq, literally "the place of sunrise" or "the east") is the eastern part of the Arab world, in contrast to the Maghreb ("the place of sunset", "the west") in North Africa. The term is used historically, culturally and politically to group the Arabic-speaking lands east of Libya, where the sun rises relative to the western Arab lands.

Geography

Definitions vary, but the Mashriq generally encompasses Egypt, Sudan, the Levant (Jordan, Lebanon, Palestine and Syria), Iraq, and frequently the countries of the Arabian Peninsula — Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Oman, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait. The region includes the historic heartlands of ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt and the Levant, and the Fertile Crescent where agriculture and cities first developed. Mashriqi Arabic dialects, though diverse, are broadly distinguished from the Maghrebi dialects of the west.

History and usage

The east–west division dates to the early Islamic conquests, when the caliphate administered its eastern and western provinces separately, with the dividing line shifting over time. In the Ottoman period the Mashriq roughly corresponded to the empire's Arab provinces east of Egypt. In modern usage, "Mashriq" appears in political and economic groupings, such as the World Bank's regional classification, and contrasts with the Maghreb Union countries of Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya and Mauritania.
