Nephrite

Nephrite
Category: Inosilicate
Formula: Ca2(Mg,Fe)5Si8O22(OH)2
Hardness: 6.0–6.5 (Mohs)

Nephrite is a variety of the calcium, magnesium, and iron-rich amphibole minerals tremolite or actinolite. It is one of two minerals commonly called jade, the other being jadeite. Nephrite is typically green, ranging to nearly black, and is valued for its toughness in carving.

Occurrences and use

Major sources include British Columbia (Canadian jade), the Kunlun Mountains of China, Russia's Lake Baikal region, and New Zealand, where it is known to Māori as pounamu and treasured for tools, weapons, and ornaments. Nephrite has been worked since prehistory in China, Korea, and Central America.

Properties

Its interlocking fibrous crystal structure makes nephrite exceptionally tough — tougher than steel — though slightly softer than jadeite. The name derives from the Latin lapis nephriticus, from Greek for "kidney stone," reflecting a traditional belief it cured kidney ailments.
