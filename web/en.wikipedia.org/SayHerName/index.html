#SayHerName
TypeSocial movement and awareness campaign
LaunchedDecember 2014
FoundersAfrican American Policy Forum (AAPF), Kimberlé Crenshaw and colleagues
PurposeRaise visibility of Black women and girls killed or harmed by police violence

#SayHerName is a social movement and campaign created in 2014 by the African American Policy Forum (AAPF), co-founded by legal scholar Kimberlé Crenshaw, to call attention to police violence against Black women and girls in the United States — cases the founders argued received far less media coverage and public protest than those of Black men.

Origins

The AAPF launched the campaign in December 2014 and in May 2015 published the report Say Her Name: Resisting Police Brutality Against Black Women, documenting cases such as those of Tanisha Anderson, Michelle Cusseaux, and Kayla Moore. The hashtag spread widely on social media and in protests, encouraging demonstrators to speak the names of women victims aloud as a form of public memorialisation.

Development and impact

The campaign gained renewed prominence after the deaths of Sandra Bland (2015), Breonna Taylor (2020) and other women whose cases became touchstones of the broader Black Lives Matter movement; Taylor's name in particular was invoked alongside #SayHerName in the protests of 2020. The campaign has since been extended through art installations, a "Mothers of the Movement" network supporting affected families, vigils, academic work on intersectionality, and adaptations in other countries to highlight violence against women of colour by state agents.

Scholars and journalists cite #SayHerName as an example of how intersectional framing — analysing race and gender together — reshaped public discussion of police violence, and of the role of hashtags in contemporary activism.
