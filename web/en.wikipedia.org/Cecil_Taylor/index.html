Cecil Percival Taylor (March 25, 1929 – April 5, 2018) was an American pianist and poet. Classically trained, he was one of the pioneers of free jazz, known for intensely energetic, physically demanding improvisations built from dense tone clusters, complex rhythms, and percussive attack.

Career

Taylor studied at the New York College of Music and the New England Conservatory. His 1956 debut album Jazz Advance already showed a radical approach. In the 1960s, recordings for Blue Note — Unit Structures and Conquistador! — established his reputation, and his performances at the 1957 Newport Jazz Festival were landmarks of the avant-garde. Long marginalised commercially, he received a Guggenheim Fellowship and later a MacArthur "Genius" grant (1991).

Style

Taylor described his approach as constructing "unit structures" — cells of material developed in performance. His playing treated the piano as an orchestra of tuned drums, influenced by Duke Ellington, Thelonious Monk, and modern classical composers such as Bartók and Stravinsky. He also incorporated poetry and dance into performances.

Legacy

Taylor is regarded as one of the most uncompromising and influential figures in jazz history, a foundational figure of the free jazz movement alongside Ornette Coleman and Albert Ayler.
