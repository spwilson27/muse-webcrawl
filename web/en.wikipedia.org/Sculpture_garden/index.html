Sculpture garden

A sculpture garden or sculpture park is an outdoor garden or park that includes the presentation of sculpture, usually several permanently sited works in durable materials in landscaped surroundings.

Characteristics

Sculpture gardens range from collections of monumental works on the grounds of museums and estates to large rural sculpture parks where works are spread across fields, woodland, and water features. They may be devoted to a single artist's work, to a period or movement such as modernism, or to temporary exhibitions and commissioned installations. Unlike most gallery displays, outdoor sculpture is experienced in changing light and weather, and the landscape itself often forms part of the work.

Notable examples

Well-known sculpture gardens include the Hirshhorn Museum and Sculpture Garden in Washington, D.C., the Yorkshire Sculpture Park in England, the Kröller-Müller Museum's garden in the Netherlands, and the Hakone Open-Air Museum in Japan. The grounds of estates such as Storm King Art Center in New York state display large-scale works by artists including Alexander Calder, Henry Moore, and Richard Serra across extensive parkland.

History

Displaying sculpture in gardens dates to antiquity and flourished in Renaissance and Baroque formal gardens, where classical statues adorned villas and palaces. The modern sculpture park emerged in the mid-twentieth century alongside land art and the creation of monumental abstract sculpture that exceeded gallery scale.
