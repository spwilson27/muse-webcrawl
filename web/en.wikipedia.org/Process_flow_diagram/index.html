Process flow diagram

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A process flow diagram for an amine treating plant

A process flow diagram (PFD) is a diagram commonly used in chemical and process engineering to indicate the general flow of plant processes and equipment. The PFD displays the relationship between major equipment of a plant facility and does not show minor details such as piping details and designations. Another commonly used term is flowsheet.

Contents of a process flow diagram

Typically, a process flow diagram of a single unit process includes process piping, major equipment items with designations, names and numbers, control valves and process-critical valves, connections with other systems, major bypass and recirculation lines, and system design and operating values such as flow rates, pressures, temperatures and stream compositions. PFDs also present the material and energy balances that form the basis of plant design. Unlike the more detailed piping and instrumentation diagram (P&ID), a PFD does not show every pipe, instrument or minor component.

Uses

PFDs are produced early in the design of a chemical plant, after the process concept has been selected and simulated. They are used to communicate the process design to project teams, to estimate capital cost, to support hazard studies such as HAZOP, and as reference documents for plant operation and training. Computer-aided process simulation software can generate stream data that feed directly into the flowsheet.
