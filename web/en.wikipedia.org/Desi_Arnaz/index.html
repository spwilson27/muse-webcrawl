Desi Arnaz

Desi Arnaz
Born: 2 March 1917, Santiago de Cuba, Cuba
Died: 2 December 1986, Del Mar, California, US
Occupations: Actor, musician, producer, bandleader
Spouse: Lucille Ball (m. 1940; div. 1960)

Desiderio Alberto Arnaz y de Acha III (2 March 1917 – 2 December 1986), known professionally as Desi Arnaz, was a Cuban-American actor, musician, producer and bandleader. He achieved international fame playing Ricky Ricardo opposite his wife Lucille Ball in the landmark American television sitcom I Love Lucy (1951–1957).

Early life and music career

Arnaz was born in Santiago de Cuba to a wealthy family; his father was the city's youngest mayor. The family fled to Miami after the 1933 Cuban revolution. Arnaz worked his way up in show business, joining Xavier Cugat's orchestra and then forming his own band, popularising the conga dance in the United States. His signature song was "Babalu". Broadway and film work followed, including the Rodgers and Hart musical Too Many Girls (1939), where he met Lucille Ball.

I Love Lucy and Desilu

When CBS adapted Ball's radio show for television, Ball insisted Arnaz play her husband. The resulting series, I Love Lucy, became one of the most influential programmes in television history. As president of Desilu Productions, the company he co-founded with Ball, Arnaz was a pioneering television executive: he championed filming sitcoms with multiple cameras before a live studio audience, shooting on film rather than kinescope — a decision that made profitable syndicated reruns possible. Desilu went on to produce The Andy Griffith Show, The Dick Van Dyke Show, Star Trek and Mission: Impossible.

Later life

Arnaz and Ball divorced in 1960. He sold his share of Desilu to Ball and pursued horse racing and breeding interests in California. His memoir, A Book, appeared in 1976. Arnaz died of lung cancer in Del Mar, California, in 1986.
