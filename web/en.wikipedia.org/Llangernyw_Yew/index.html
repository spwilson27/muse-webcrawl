The Llangernyw Yew (Welsh: Tis Llangernyw) is an ancient yew tree (Taxus baccata) in the village of Llangernyw, Conwy, North Wales. The tree is in the churchyard of St Digain's Church and is estimated to be between 4,000 and 5,000 years old, making it one of the oldest living organisms in Europe and a contender for the oldest tree in Britain.

The Llangernyw Yew in the churchyard of St Digain's Church.

Description

The tree's core has been lost to age and decay, and the trunk has split into two large fragments, which makes dendrochronological dating impossible and its exact age a matter of estimation. Its girth at 0.3 metres from the ground is 10.75 metres (35.3 ft). The fragmentation of the main body of the tree adds considerably to the difficulty of determining its age, since yew growth rates slow dramatically in old trees and hollow trunks cannot be ring-counted.

History and legend

The churchyard is associated with a legend of the Angelystor, a spirit said to read out the names of parishioners who would die in the coming year each Halloween. The site is one of the "Fifty Great British Trees" recognised in 2002 as part of the Queen's Golden Jubilee celebrations. The yew predates the Christian church on the site and may mark a pre-Christian sacred location.
