The domestic pig (Sus scrofa domesticus), also often called the swine, hog, or simply pig when there is no need to distinguish it from other pigs, is a domesticated, omnivorous, hoofed mammal. It is named the domestic pig when distinguishing it from other members of the genus Sus. It is considered a subspecies of the wild boar or a distinct species, and was domesticated by humans around 8500 BC.

Domestication and history

Archaeological and genetic evidence shows pigs were domesticated independently in the Near East and in China from wild boar populations. From Anatolia, domestic pigs spread into Europe with early farmers; later, European breeds were transformed in the 18th and 19th centuries by crossing with Chinese pigs, producing modern commercial breeds such as the Large White, Landrace, Duroc and Hampshire. Feral pigs — escaped domesticates — are now serious invasive pests in the Americas, Australia and elsewhere.

Biology and behaviour

Pigs are highly intelligent, social animals with an excellent sense of smell and poor eyesight. Sows build nests before farrowing and nurse litters of typically 10–12 piglets. Omnivorous foragers, pigs root in soil with their cartilaginous snouts. Contrary to the stereotype, pigs wallow in mud chiefly to cool themselves, since they have few functional sweat glands. Studies rank pig cognition alongside that of dogs and chimpanzees in some tasks.

Pigs and humans

Pork is the most widely eaten meat in the world, accounting for roughly a third of global meat consumption; cured products include ham, bacon and salami. Pigskin yields leather, bristles make brushes, and pig insulin was historically used to treat diabetes. Because of their anatomical similarity to humans, pigs are important in medical research and are at the frontier of xenotransplantation — genetically modified pig hearts and kidneys have been transplanted into human patients. At the same time, religious law in Judaism and Islam forbids pork consumption, and intensive pig farming raises environmental and animal-welfare debates.
