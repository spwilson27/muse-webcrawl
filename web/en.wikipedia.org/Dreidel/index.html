Dreidel

A dreidel, also spelled dreidl or draydl (Yiddish: דריידל), is a four-sided spinning top, played during the Jewish holiday of Hanukkah. Each side bears a Hebrew letter: Nun (נ), Gimel (ג), Hei (ה) and Shin (ש), which together form an acronym for "Nes Gadol Hayah Sham" — "A great miracle happened there." In Israel the Shin is replaced by Pei (פ) for "Po", "here", since the miracle of Hanukkah occurred in the Land of Israel.

The game

Players start with an equal number of tokens — commonly chocolate coins (gelt), nuts or pennies — and put one in the "pot". Each player in turn spins the dreidel: Nun means nothing happens; Gimel, take the whole pot; Hei, take half; Shin (or Pei), put one in. A player who runs out of tokens is out; the last remaining player wins.

Origins

The dreidel descends from the European teetotum gambling top, popular in England and Germany; its letters were reinterpreted in Yiddish and Hebrew tradition. Folklore connects the game to the era of the Maccabees, claiming Jews studied Torah in secret and produced tops to appear to be merely playing when Greek patrols approached — a story generally considered a later legend. Dreidels are made in wood, plastic, metal and glass, and giant public dreidels are displayed during Hanukkah.
