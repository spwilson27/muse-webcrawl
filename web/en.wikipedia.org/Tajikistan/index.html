Tajikistan, officially the Republic of Tajikistan, is a landlocked country in Central Asia. It is bordered by Afghanistan to the south, Uzbekistan to the west and northwest, Kyrgyzstan to the north, and China to the east. Its capital and largest city is Dushanbe.

Geography

Tajikistan is the smallest nation in Central Asia by land area, covering 143,100 square kilometres. Over 90 percent of the country is mountainous; the Pamir Mountains in the east include some of the highest peaks outside the Himalayas and Karakoram, and Fedchenko Glacier is among the longest glaciers in the world outside the polar regions. The main lowland areas lie in the Fergana Valley in the north and along the Amu Darya (Panj) river in the south.

History

The region was home to ancient cultures including the Sogdians and Bactrians, and passed through Persian, Hellenistic, Arab, Mongol, and Russian rule. In the Soviet era it became the Tajik Soviet Socialist Republic in 1929. Tajikistan declared independence on 9 September 1991 as the USSR collapsed. A devastating civil war (1992–1997) between the government and a coalition of Islamist and democratic forces ended with a UN-brokered peace agreement. Emomali Rahmon has been president since 1994.

Demographics and culture

The population is around ten million, predominantly ethnic Tajiks, who speak Tajik, a variety of Persian written in the Cyrillic alphabet. Uzbeks form the largest minority. Islam, mainly Sunni of the Hanafi school, is the predominant religion. The country has a rich Persian literary heritage; the poet Rudaki is regarded as the founder of Persian-Tajik literature.

Economy

Tajikistan is one of the poorest countries in the former Soviet Union. Aluminium production, cotton, and remittances from migrant workers — mainly in Russia — are major sources of income. The country has enormous hydroelectric potential; the Rogun Dam on the Vakhsh River, under construction for decades, is designed to be the world's tallest dam.
