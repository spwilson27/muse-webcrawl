Mornay sauce

Type: Sauce
Place of origin: France
Main ingredients: Béchamel sauce, grated cheese (typically Gruyère and Parmesan), egg yolk

Mornay sauce is a béchamel sauce enriched with grated cheese, classically one of the derivative sauces of French cuisine. The cheeses used are typically Gruyère and Parmesan, though Emmental or white Cheddar are sometimes substituted, and the sauce is often finished with egg yolk and sometimes a little cream.

History and naming

The sauce is named after Philippe, duc de Mornay (1549–1623), a French Protestant writer and statesman—though the cheese-enriched béchamel is generally dated to the 19th century, and the connection to the duke is conventional rather than documented. The sauce appears in classic French culinary references and was a staple of grande cuisine.

Uses

Mornay sauce is used in dishes such as eggs Mornay, cauliflower cheese-style gratins, seafood gratins, the Kentucky Hot Brown, and the croque-monsieur (where it is spread and gratinated). It is the traditional topping for eggs and vegetables served à la Mornay, and a base for macaroni and cheese in French-influenced cooking.
