First Battle of the Masurian Lakes

Date2–16 September 1914
LocationEast Prussia, German Empire
ResultGerman victory
BelligerentsGerman Empire vs. Russian Empire
CommandersPaul von Hindenburg, Erich Ludendorff; Paul von Rennenkampf

The First Battle of the Masurian Lakes was a German offensive in the early stages of the First World War, fought from 2 to 16 September 1914. The German Eighth Army under Paul von Hindenburg and Erich Ludendorff drove the Russian First Army of Paul von Rennenkampf out of East Prussia.

Background

Following the victory at Tannenberg in late August 1914, the German Eighth Army turned north to confront Rennenkampf's First Army, which had advanced slowly and halted near the Masurian lake district. German intelligence, aided by intercepted Russian radio messages sent in the clear, revealed the Russian dispositions.

The battle

The German attack began on 7 September, striking the southern flank of the Russian position. German forces broke through near the lakes, threatening to encircle the Russian army. Rennenkampf ordered a general retreat on 11 September, and his army withdrew across the frontier in good order but with heavy losses, abandoning guns and supplies.

Aftermath

The battle ended the Russian invasion of East Prussia. Russian losses are estimated at around 125,000 men, including some 45,000 prisoners, while German losses were far lower. A Second Battle of the Masurian Lakes followed in February 1915.
