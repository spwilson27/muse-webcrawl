Climate movement

The climate movement is a global social movement of citizens, organisations and networks pressing governments and corporations to act on climate change. It grew out of the broader environmental movement and gained momentum from the late 1980s, as scientific assessments by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (established 1988) clarified the human role in global warming.

History

Early campaigning focused on the UN climate negotiations that produced the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and Copenhagen summit (2009). After 2010 the movement diversified: fossil-fuel divestment campaigns persuaded universities and pension funds to sell holdings; litigation sought to hold states and companies legally accountable; and from 2018 the youth-led school strike movement, inspired by Greta Thunberg's Fridays for Future protests, brought millions into the streets, alongside direct-action groups such as Extinction Rebellion demanding faster emissions cuts.

Goals and strategies

Common demands include keeping warming below 1.5 °C, phasing out fossil fuels, a just transition for affected workers, and climate justice for communities least responsible for emissions. Tactics range from lobbying, shareholder resolutions and electoral campaigning to marches, civil disobedience and strategic lawsuits such as Urgenda v. Netherlands.

Criticism and response

Critics question the efficacy or tactics of disruptive protest, while defenders argue the movement has shifted policy, investment and public discourse worldwide.
