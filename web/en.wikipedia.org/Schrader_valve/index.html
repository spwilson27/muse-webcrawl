Schrader valve


Image placeholder: Schrader valve

The Schrader valve (also called the American valve) is a type of pneumatic tire valve used on virtually every motor vehicle in the world today, as well as on most bicycle tires with wide rims. It was invented by August Schrader, a German-American immigrant, and patented in the United States in the 1890s by his company A. Schrader's Son.

Design

The valve consists of a hollow cylindrical stem, usually of brass, with external threads at the outer end, into which a spring-loaded valve core is screwed. The core contains a pin that, when depressed by the chuck of a pump or pressure gauge, opens the valve; internal air pressure and the spring keep it sealed otherwise. Because the pin must be depressed, Schrader valves can be opened by any probe fitting the stem, and they are robust and tolerant of dirt, though they require higher pumping effort than Presta valves on narrow bicycle rims.

Uses

Beyond tires, Schrader-type valves are used in refrigeration and air-conditioning service ports, fuel rails, air suspension systems, SCUBA buoyancy compensators, and many other pneumatic and hydraulic applications. The same basic fitting allows pressure to be checked and added with universal equipment.
