Rainbow Warrior was a former trawler, launched in 1957 as the Sir William Hardy, that became the flagship of the environmental organization Greenpeace. She was sunk in Auckland Harbour, New Zealand, on 10 July 1985 by agents of the French foreign intelligence service (DGSE), an operation that killed Greenpeace photographer Fernando Pereira and caused an international scandal.

The Rainbow Warrior on campaign in the Pacific.

Service with Greenpeace

Built in Aberdeen, Scotland, for the British Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food, the vessel was bought by Greenpeace UK in 1978 and refitted as a campaign ship, with sails added to reduce fuel use. She campaigned against whaling, seal hunting, ocean dumping of nuclear waste, and nuclear weapons testing in the Pacific. In 1985 she helped evacuate the residents of Rongelap Atoll, whose island had been contaminated by US nuclear tests, before sailing to New Zealand en route to protest French nuclear testing at Moruroa.

The bombing

On the night of 10 July 1985, two French combat divers attached limpet mines to the hull in Auckland's Waitematā Harbour. The first explosion blew a large hole in the engine room; as crew evacuated, a second mine sank the ship. Photographer Fernando Pereira, who had returned below deck for his equipment, was drowned. Two agents, Dominique Prieur and Alain Mafart, were arrested by New Zealand police and pleaded guilty to manslaughter, receiving ten-year sentences. France initially denied involvement, but a French government report and later admissions confirmed the operation, code-named Opération Satanique, had been authorized at high levels.

Aftermath

The affair damaged Franco–New Zealand relations and led to a UN-mediated settlement under which France apologized and paid compensation. The wreck was refloated, found beyond repair, and scuttled in Matauri Bay in 1987 as an artificial reef and dive site. Greenpeace acquired a successor vessel, also named Rainbow Warrior, in 1989.
