Sariputta (Pali: Sāriputta; Sanskrit: Śāriputra), born Upatissa, was one of the top disciples of the Buddha. He is considered the first of the Buddha's two chief male disciples (aggasāvaka), together with Moggallana, and was praised by the Buddha as foremost among his monks in wisdom. Sariputta played a central role in systematising and teaching the Buddha's doctrine, and in later tradition is closely associated with the Abhidhamma.

Life

Born to a Brahmin family near Rajagaha, Upatissa and his friend Kolita (later Moggallana) studied under the sceptical teacher Sañjaya Belaṭṭhaputta. Hearing a single verse of the teaching from the monk Assaji — "Of those phenomena that arise from a cause, the Tathagata has stated the cause, and also their cessation" — Upatissa attained stream-entry and sought out the Buddha at Bamboo Grove. He attained arahantship two weeks after ordination, while listening to the Buddha teach the Vedana-parivatta Sutta to the wanderer Dighanakha.

Role and teachings

Sariputta served as the Buddha's chief expositor, expounding the doctrine with analytical precision; the Majjhima Nikaya discourses Sammaditthi Sutta and others are attributed to him. Tradition credits him with requesting the Buddha to lay down the monastic code and with organising the teachings. He died at his birthplace Nalaka, having returned home to convert his mother, shortly before the Buddha's own final passing. His relics were enshrined in stupas; those later excavated near Sanchi and Satdhara were returned to India in the 20th century and are now enshrined at Sanchi.
