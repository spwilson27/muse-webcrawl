Heitor Villa-Lobos (5 March 1887 – 17 November 1959) was a Brazilian composer, conductor, cellist and classical guitarist described as "the single most significant creative figure in 20th-century Brazilian art music". One of the most prolific composers of his era, he wrote more than 2,000 works, fusing Brazilian folk and popular music with the forms and techniques of Western classical music.

Life

Villa-Lobos learned the cello from his father and absorbed the popular music of Rio de Janeiro by playing guitar in street ensembles called chorões. After his father's death he supported himself as a cinema and theatre musician and travelled through Brazil, encountering the music of the Amazon and the Northeast. His work caused a sensation at the São Paulo Week of Modern Art in 1922, and during the 1920s he spent extended periods in Paris, where his music — often marketed with jungle exoticism he alternately encouraged and resented — gained an international reputation.

Returning to Brazil under the Vargas regime in 1930, Villa-Lobos directed music education nationwide, founding conservatoires and organising mass choral singing events involving tens of thousands of schoolchildren. He continued composing and conducting internationally until his death in 1959.

Works

His best-known compositions include the nine Bachianas Brasileiras — suites blending Bachian counterpoint with Brazilian melody, the most famous being the aria of No. 5 for soprano and cellos — the fourteen Chôros, twelve symphonies, seventeen string quartets, and a landmark body of guitar music including the Five Preludes and Twelve Études, which reshaped the classical guitar repertoire.
