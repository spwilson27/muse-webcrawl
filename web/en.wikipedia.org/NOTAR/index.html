NOTAR


Labelled image: NOTAR-equipped MD 520N

NOTAR ("no tail rotor") is a helicopter system which avoids the use of a conventional tail rotor. Developed by McDonnell Douglas Helicopter Systems (through Hughes Helicopters), it consists of a fan inside the tail boom which builds up a high volume of low-pressure air exhausted through two slots along the tail boom, using the Coandă effect, plus a direct jet thruster at the tail for yaw control.

Principle

In a conventional helicopter, the tail rotor counteracts main-rotor torque and provides directional control. In the NOTAR design, air blown through slots on the right side of the tail boom adheres to the curved surface (the Coandă effect), accelerating the downwash from the main rotor on that side and generating a lateral force that counteracts torque in hover. Directional control at low speed, and additional anti-torque at higher speeds, comes from a rotating direct-jet thruster cone at the end of the boom, controlled by the pilot's pedals; in forward flight the vertical stabilizers also contribute.

Advantages and uses

Eliminating the exposed tail rotor reduces noise markedly, removes a major source of accidents to ground personnel, and reduces vulnerability to foreign-object damage — benefits valued in law enforcement, emergency medical and utility operations. NOTAR is used on the MD Helicopters MD 520N, MD 600N and the twin-engine MD Explorer (MD 900/902) series.
