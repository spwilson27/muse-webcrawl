Pomology

Pomology is the branch of horticulture and botany concerned with the study, cultivation, and improvement of fruit. The word derives from Latin pomum (fruit, apple) and -logia (study).

Scope

Pomologists study fruit-bearing trees and shrubs, including apples, pears, stone fruits, citrus, and berries. Their work covers variety identification and naming, breeding for flavour, yield, and disease resistance, orchard management, and post-harvest storage.

History

Systematic pomology developed in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, when illustrated fruit catalogues documented thousands of apple and pear varieties. In the United States, the Department of Agriculture maintained a large collection of watercolor fruit illustrations, the USDA Pomological Watercolor Collection, recording cultivars from 1886 to 1942.

Modern practice

Modern pomology applies plant physiology, genetics, and genomics to breeding programmes, and supports genebanks that preserve heritage varieties.
