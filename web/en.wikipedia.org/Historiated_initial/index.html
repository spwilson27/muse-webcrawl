Historiated initial

Historiated initial
[Image: Historiated initial from an illuminated manuscript]
MediumIlluminated manuscripts
Periodc. 9th–16th centuries
Related termsInhabited initial, illuminated initial, drop cap

A historiated initial is an enlarged initial letter at the start of a text in an illuminated manuscript that contains an identifiable picture or narrative scene — a figure, episode, or portrait — inside the bowls or compartments of the letter itself. The term derives from the Anglo-Norman historie, "story". Historiated initials are distinguished from inhabited initials, which contain figures, animals, or foliage that do not illustrate the text, and from purely decorated initials.

Use and development

Large decorated initials marked textual divisions from late antiquity, but true historiated initials flourished from the Romanesque period onward, especially in Bibles, psalters, breviaries, and books of hours. The image inside the letter usually illustrated the passage it introduced: the initial "I" of Genesis ("In principio") in Romanesque Bibles could contain the days of Creation stacked in medallions, and psalter initials often showed King David, the psalms' supposed author. In late medieval manuscripts historiated initials became major works of miniature painting, and in early printed books spaces were left for them to be added by hand before woodcut historiated initials took over.
