Spiral model

The spiral model is a risk-driven software development process model that combines elements of iterative development with the systematic, controlled aspects of the waterfall model. It was described by Barry Boehm in his 1986 paper "A Spiral Model of Software Development and Enhancement".

Phases

Each pass around the spiral proceeds through four phases: determining objectives and constraints, identifying and resolving risks (often by prototyping), development and testing, and planning the next iteration. The radius of the spiral represents cumulative cost and the angular dimension progress.

Use

The model emphasises risk analysis and is suited to large, complex, high-risk projects, though it can be expensive to apply to small projects.
