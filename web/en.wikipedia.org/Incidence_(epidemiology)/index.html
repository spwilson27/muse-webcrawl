Incidence (epidemiology)

In epidemiology, incidence is the probability of a specified medical condition occurring in a population within a specified period of time. It measures the rate at which new cases arise, and contrasts with prevalence, which measures how common a condition is at a given moment.

Incidence proportion and rate

The incidence proportion (or cumulative incidence) is the number of new cases divided by the population at risk at the start of the period. The incidence rate divides new cases by the total person-time at risk, allowing for people entering and leaving observation.

Example

If 10 new cases occur among 1,000 people followed for one year, the incidence rate is 10 per 1,000 person-years.

Uses

Comparing incidence between exposed and unexposed groups yields measures such as the incidence rate ratio, a standard way of quantifying the effect of a risk factor.
