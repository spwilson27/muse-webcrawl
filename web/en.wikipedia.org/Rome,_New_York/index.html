Rome is a city in Oneida County, New York, United States, in the Mohawk Valley region. With a population of about 32,000 at the 2020 census, it is one of two principal cities (with Utica) of the Utica–Rome metropolitan area. The city stands on the Oneida Carrying Place, an ancient portage between the Mohawk River and Wood Creek used for centuries by the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois).

History

Because the portage linked the Hudson River watershed to Lake Ontario, it was of great strategic value. The British built Fort Stanwix there in 1758; in 1777, during the American Revolutionary War, its garrison famously withstood a siege by Barry St. Leger's expedition. Construction of the Erie Canal began at Rome on 4 July 1817, and the canal transformed the settlement into a commercial centre. Rome became known as the "Copper City" for its brass and copper mills, including Revere Copper Products.

The writer Francis Bellamy, author of the United States Pledge of Allegiance, was a native of nearby Mount Morris and lived and worked in Rome. Griffiss Air Force Base, opened in 1942 on the city's outskirts, was a major employer until its realignment in the 1990s; the site now hosts the Griffiss Business and Technology Park and the Air Force Research Laboratory's information directorate.

Attractions

Fort Stanwix National Monument, a full-scale reconstruction of the Revolutionary War fort, is managed by the National Park Service in the city centre. Other sites include the Erie Canal Village museum and Delta Lake State Park.
