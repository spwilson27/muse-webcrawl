Drilling

Drilling is a cutting process that uses a drill bit to cut a hole of circular cross-section in solid materials. The drill bit is usually a rotary cutting tool, often multi-point, pressed against the workpiece and rotated at rates from hundreds to thousands of revolutions per minute, forcing the cutting edge against the material and removing chips.

Process

Drilling is commonly performed with a drill press, lathe, milling machine, or handheld power drill. Holes may be started with a centre punch or spot drill to prevent the bit from wandering. Cutting fluid is frequently used to cool the bit and workpiece, lubricate the cut, and flush away chips. Related operations include reaming, which enlarges and finishes a hole to a precise diameter; tapping, which cuts internal threads; and countersinking and counterboring, which shape the hole entrance.

Drill bits

The most common bit is the twist drill, which has two helical flutes that evacuate chips. Other types include spade bits for wood, masonry bits with carbide tips, hole saws, step drills, and diamond core drills for stone and glass. Deep-hole drilling of metal uses specialized gun drills with through-tool coolant.

Drilling in earth

On a larger scale, drilling rigs bore wells for water, oil, and natural gas, and blast holes for mining and construction. Rotary drilling, in which a bit is rotated from the surface while drilling mud circulates cuttings out of the hole, is the dominant technique; directional and horizontal drilling allow wells to reach targets far from the surface location.
