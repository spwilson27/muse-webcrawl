The Nürburgring is a 150,000-person capacity motorsports complex located in the town of Nürburg, Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany. It features a Grand Prix race track built in 1984 and the famous Nordschleife ("northern loop"), a 20.8-kilometre course opened in 1927 that runs through the forested Eifel mountains around the medieval castle of Nürburg.

History

The original circuit opened in 1927 with a total length of over 28 kilometres, comprising the Nordschleife and the shorter Südschleife. The Nordschleife became legendary as the most demanding racing circuit in the world, hosting the German Grand Prix until Niki Lauda's near-fatal crash there in 1976 ended Formula One racing on the old course on safety grounds. Grand Prix racing moved to a new, shorter circuit built in 1984. The Nordschleife remains in use for touring-car racing, the Nürburgring 24 Hours, manufacturer testing and public "tourist drives" (Touristenfahrten), and lap records on the loop are a benchmark for high-performance cars.

The Green Hell

With more than 70 corners, dramatic elevation changes and narrow, bumpy surfaces, the track was dubbed the "Green Hell" by Jackie Stewart. It remains a byword for driving difficulty and an important development venue for the motor industry, whose test centre cluster is based at the circuit.
