Michel Berger (born Michel Jean Hamburger; 28 November 1947 – 2 August 1992) was a French singer-songwriter and composer, one of the most influential figures in French popular music of the 1970s and 1980s. He is remembered for reviving the career of singer France Gall, his wife, and for co-writing with Luc Plamondon the rock opera Starmania.

Career

Son of the physician Jean Hamburger, Berger emerged during the yé-yé era and became a songwriter and artistic director at Pathé-Marconi, writing for artists such as Bourvil, Véronique Sanson and Johnny Hallyday. In 1974 he relaunched France Gall's career with "La Déclaration d'amour"; they married in 1976, and he wrote a string of her hits including "Musique", "Si, maman si", "Il jouait du piano debout", "Résiste", "Babacar" and "Ella, elle l'a".

His own recordings included the albums Beauséjour (1980) — with "La Groupie du pianiste" and "Quelques mots d'amour" — Beaurivage (1981) and Voyou (1983), and songs such as "Le Paradis blanc" and "Seras-tu là ?". The rock opera Starmania (album 1978, staged 1979), co-created with Luc Plamondon, became a landmark of francophone musical theatre; a later version, Tycoon, carried English lyrics by Tim Rice. With Plamondon he also wrote La Légende de Jimmy (1990), inspired by James Dean.

Death

Berger died of a heart attack on 2 August 1992 after a tennis match in Ramatuelle, aged 44. With his wife he founded humanitarian efforts such as Action Écoles in Africa; a retrospective interest in his work has remained strong in France.
