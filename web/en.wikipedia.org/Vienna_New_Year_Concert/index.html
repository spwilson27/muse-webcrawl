The New Year Concert of the Vienna Philharmonic (German: Neujahrskonzert der Wiener Philharmoniker) is an annual concert of classical music held on the morning of New Year's Day in Vienna, Austria, in the Golden Hall of the Musikverein. It is broadcast around the world to an estimated audience of tens of millions in some ninety countries.

Programme

The programme consists chiefly of waltzes, polkas, marches and operetta excerpts by Johann Strauss I, Johann Strauss II, Josef Strauss and Eduard Strauss, together with works by their contemporaries. The concert traditionally closes with a fixed sequence of encores: the waltz The Blue Danube by Johann Strauss II, preceded by the conductor's New Year address, and the Radetzky March by Johann Strauss I, during which the audience claps along in time, conducted by the maestro.

History

The first concert was held on 31 December 1939 under Clemens Krauss, in the dark context of Austria's annexation by Nazi Germany; from 1941 it moved to New Year's Day. Long-serving conductors of the concert include Willi Boskovsky, who led it from the concertmaster's desk from 1955 to 1979, and Lorin Maazel. Since 1987 the orchestra has invited a different internationally prominent conductor each year, among them Herbert von Karajan, Claudio Abbado, Nikolaus Harnoncourt, Riccardo Muti, Mariss Jansons, Daniel Barenboim and Franz Welser-Möst.

Tickets and broadcast

Demand for tickets vastly exceeds supply: seats are allocated by ballot held a year in advance, with prices reaching into the hundreds of euros. The concert is produced for television by ORF with Eurovision distribution; floral decorations for the hall are provided by the Vienna City Gardening Department, and the broadcast intermissions showcase Austrian scenery and culture.
