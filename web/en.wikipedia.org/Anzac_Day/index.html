Anzac Day

Observed byAustralia, New Zealand
Date25 April
First observed1916

Anzac Day is a national day of remembrance in Australia and New Zealand observed on 25 April each year. It commemorates the members of the Australian and New Zealand Army Corps (ANZAC) who landed at Gallipoli in 1915, and more broadly all Australians and New Zealanders who served and died in wars, conflicts, and peacekeeping operations.

History

On 25 April 1915, Anzac troops landed at Gallipoli on the Ottoman Turkish coast during the First World War. The campaign lasted eight months and caused heavy casualties. The anniversary of the landing was first marked in 1916 and gradually became the central focus of war remembrance in both countries.

Commemoration

Anzac Day begins with dawn services held at war memorials, recalling the time of the Gallipoli landing. Later in the morning, marches by veterans and serving personnel take place in cities and towns. The day features the laying of poppy wreaths, the playing of the Last Post, a period of silence, and the recitation of the Ode of Remembrance ("Lest we forget"). Traditional elements include rosemary sprigs, Anzac biscuits, and the game of two-up.

Significance

The Gallipoli campaign is often described as a founding moment in the national identities of Australia and New Zealand, shaping the "Anzac spirit" of endurance, mateship, and sacrifice.
