Sundance Film Festival

The Sundance Film Festival (formerly known as the Utah/US Film Festival, then the US Film and Video Festival) is an annual film festival organized by the Sundance Institute. It is the largest independent film festival in the United States, and takes place each January in Park City, Utah, Salt Lake City, and at the Sundance Resort (a ski resort near Provo, Utah). The festival consists of competitive sections for American and international dramatic and documentary films, both feature films and short films, and a group of out-of-competition sections.

History

The festival was founded in 1978 in Salt Lake City by Sterling Van Wagenen, John Earle, and Cirina Hampton Catania as the Utah/US Film Festival, intended to attract filmmakers to Utah and showcase American independent cinema. Actor Robert Redford, then Utah's most famous film resident, lent his support and his Sundance name; in 1981 the festival moved to Park City and in 1985 its management passed to the Sundance Institute Redford had founded. Under the Institute it grew into the premier launchpad for independent film.

Impact

Sundance premieres credited with launching major careers include sex, lies, and videotape, Reservoir Dogs, The Blair Witch Project, Little Miss Sunshine, Whiplash, and CODA, which went on to win the Academy Award for Best Picture. The festival remains a key market where distributors acquire independent films.
