Off-road racing

Off-road racing is a form of motorsports consisting of specially modified vehicles (including cars, trucks, motorcycles, and buggies) racing in off-road environments. Races range from short closed-course events on dirt tracks to endurance contests of hundreds or thousands of kilometres across deserts and mountains.

Major events

The most famous off-road races include the Baja 1000, held since 1967 on Mexico's Baja California peninsula, the Dakar Rally, founded in 1978 and now run in Saudi Arabia, and the Mint 400 in Nevada. Organisations such as SCORE International and Best in the Desert sanction the principal North American desert series, while rally raid events worldwide are governed by the FIA and FIM.

Vehicles

Classes range from near-stock production vehicles to unlimited Trophy Trucks with long-travel suspension, large-displacement engines and top speeds exceeding 200 km/h over rough terrain. Other popular classes include Class 1 buggies, UTVs (side-by-sides), motorcycles and quad bikes. Closed-course short-course racing features purpose-built trucks racing door-to-door on stadium-style dirt circuits.

Characteristics

Desert racing is typically point-to-point or run over long loops, with navigation, durability and pit strategy as important as outright speed. Dangers include dust, rocks, jumps known as "whoops", and the isolation of remote courses, which has historically made the discipline one of motorsport's most hazardous.
