Bivalvia

Bivalvia is a class of marine and freshwater molluscs with laterally compressed bodies enclosed by a shell of two hinged parts (valves). Bivalves include clams, oysters, mussels, scallops and cockles — more than 9,000 living species. They lack a radula and a distinct head, and most are filter feeders that strain plankton from the water with their gills.

Anatomy

The two valves are joined by a ligament and closed by adductor muscles. The mantle cavity contains large paired gills (ctenidia) used for both feeding and respiration. Many bivalves burrow into sediment with a muscular foot; others, such as oysters and mussels, attach to hard surfaces, mussels using proteinaceous byssal threads.

Ecology and distribution

Bivalves occur from the intertidal zone to abyssal depths, and in freshwater rivers and lakes. They are important ecosystem engineers: filter-feeding beds clarify water, and shell reefs provide habitat. The pearl oysters produce natural pearls as a by-product of coating intruding particles in nacre.

Human use

Bivalves are a major seafood — oysters, mussels, clams and scallops are farmed and fished worldwide — though they can accumulate toxins and pathogens from the water they filter. Their shells have been used as tools, currency and ornament throughout human history.
