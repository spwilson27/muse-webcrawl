Burns supper

A Burns supper is a celebration of the life and poetry of the Scottish poet Robert Burns (1759–1796), held on or near his birthday, 25 January — Burns Night. The suppers are held across Scotland and by Scots communities worldwide.

Order of the evening

Guests gather and the haggis is piped in by a bagpiper. The host recites Burns's "Address to a Haggis", cutting the pudding open at the dramatic moment. After the meal — traditionally haggis with neeps and tatties, washed down with Scotch whisky — come speeches: the Immortal Memory address, the Toast to the Lassies and the ladies' reply, interspersed with recitations and songs of Burns.

History

The first supper was held by Burns's friends at Alloway in 1801, five years after his death. The format they devised has remained remarkably stable ever since.
