Ágnes Keleti

Ágnes Keleti Born: 9 January 1921, Budapest, Hungary
Died: 2 January 2025 (aged 103), Budapest, Hungary
Sport: Artistic gymnastics
Olympic medals: 10 (5 gold, 3 silver, 2 bronze)
Countries: Hungary; later Israel
Ágnes Keleti, holder of ten Olympic medals.

Ágnes Keleti (9 January 1921 – 2 January 2025) was a Hungarian artistic gymnast and coach who won ten Olympic medals, including five golds, for Hungary at the 1952 Helsinki and 1956 Melbourne Games. She is among the most successful Olympic gymnasts of all time and, at her death at 103, was the oldest living Olympic champion.

Born Ágnes Klein in Budapest to a Jewish family, she began gymnastics at four and was Hungarian national champion by her teens. Expelled from her club under anti-Jewish laws, she survived the Second World War with false papers, working as a maid, while her mother and sister were saved by Raoul Wallenberg; her father and other relatives were murdered at Auschwitz. An injury kept her from the 1948 Olympics, so she made her debut at 31.

Later life

At Melbourne in 1956, competing days after the Soviet crushing of the Hungarian uprising, she won four golds, then defected and eventually settled in Israel, where she coached the national gymnastics team and taught physical education into her nineties. In later years she returned to Hungary, receiving its highest sporting honours; the gymnastics world recognised her longevity and she remained able to do the splits well past the age of 100.
