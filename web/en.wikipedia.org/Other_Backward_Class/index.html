Other Backward Class

CountryIndia
TypeGovernment classification
PurposeReservation and affirmative action

Other Backward Class (OBC) is a collective term used by the Government of India to classify castes and communities that are educationally or socially disadvantaged. OBCs, together with Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST), are entitled to reservations (quotas) in government jobs and educational institutions.

Background

The Constitution of India permits the state to make special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes. The Mandal Commission (1980) recommended 27 per cent reservation for OBCs in central government employment, a policy implemented in 1990 amid widespread debate and protest.

Creamy layer

The Supreme Court of India, in the Indra Sawhney judgment (1992), upheld OBC reservation but excluded the "creamy layer" — the relatively affluent members of listed communities — from its benefits, and capped total reservations at 50 per cent.

Lists

OBC lists are maintained both by the central government and by individual states, and differ between them. The National Commission for Backward Classes advises on inclusion and exclusion of communities.
