Restitution

In law, restitution is an equitable remedy by which a person who has been unjustly enriched at the expense of another is required to give back the benefit received, restoring the claimant to the position held before the enrichment. It is a central concept of the law of unjust enrichment and may take the form of returning specific property or paying its monetary value.

Principles

A restitution claim generally requires: (1) enrichment of the defendant, (2) at the claimant's expense, (3) an unjust factor such as mistake, duress, failure of consideration, or illegality, and (4) the absence of defences such as change of position. Restitution is gain-based rather than loss-based, distinguishing it from compensatory damages.

Contexts

Restitution arises in contract (after rescission or total failure of consideration), in criminal law (court-ordered payments to victims), and in historical justice, including the return of cultural property looted during war or colonial rule.
