The Republic of Bashkortostan, also known as Bashkiria, is a republic of Russia located between the Volga River and the Ural Mountains in Eastern Europe. Its capital and largest city is Ufa. It is the most populous republic in the Russian Federation.

Geography

Bashkortostan stretches from the western foothills of the southern Urals across rolling plains to the Kama and Belaya river basins. Mount Yamantau, the highest peak of the Southern Urals at about 1,640 metres, lies within the republic. Forests cover a large share of the territory, and the region is known for its steppe, caves such as Kapova Cave with Paleolithic paintings, and the Bashkir nature reserves.

People and history

The republic takes its name from the Bashkirs, a Turkic people historically engaged in semi-nomadic herding, beekeeping, and hunting. Bashkir lands were incorporated into the Russian state in the 16th century after the fall of Kazan, and Bashkirs repeatedly rose in revolt, including in the Pugachev rebellion. The Bashkir Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, created in 1919, was the first autonomous republic within Soviet Russia; in 1990 Bashkortostan declared state sovereignty, and in 1992 adopted its present name as a republic within the Russian Federation.

Economy

Bashkortostan is a major industrial and agricultural region, with large oil refining and petrochemical complexes around Ufa, Salavat, and Sterlitamak, machine building, and production of grain, sugar beet, and the famous Bashkir honey.
