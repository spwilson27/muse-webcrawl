Jean-Baptiste le Rond d'Alembert (1717–1783) was a French mathematician, mechanician, physicist and philosopher, and co-editor with Denis Diderot of the Encyclopédie. Abandoned as an infant on the steps of the Paris church of Saint-Jean-le-Rond (from which his name derives), he was raised by a glazier's wife and rose to become perpetual secretary of the Académie française.

Mathematics and mechanics

His Traité de dynamique (1743) introduced d'Alembert's principle, reducing dynamics problems to statics. He made foundational contributions to partial differential equations through the wave equation for a vibrating string, gave an early (incomplete) proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra, and developed the ratio test for series convergence.

The Encyclopédie

With Diderot, d'Alembert launched the Encyclopédie in 1751, writing its famous Preliminary Discourse and hundreds of mathematical and scientific articles; he withdrew in 1759 amid controversy. His salon connections and friendship with Julie de Lespinasse placed him at the centre of the Enlightenment.
