Erdos number

The Erdos number (Hungarian: Erdős-szám) describes the "collaborative distance" between mathematician Paul Erdős and another person, as measured by authorship of mathematical papers. The same principle has been applied in other fields where it keeps the name "Erdos number", such as physics, biology, and social science.

Definition

To be assigned an Erdos number, a person must co-author a research paper with a person who already has a finite Erdos number. Paul Erdős himself has an Erdos number of zero. Someone who co-authored a paper with Erdős has an Erdos number of 1, someone who co-authored with one of those people has an Erdos number of 2, and so on.

Impact outside mathematics

The concept has spread beyond mathematics. Physicists, computer scientists, and biologists also track their Erdos numbers, and analogues exist such as the Bacon number for actors, which together with the Erdos number yields the Erdos–Bacon number. A small number of people are known to have both a finite Erdos number and a finite Bacon number.

The Erdos Number Project

The Erdos Number Project, maintained at Oakland University, catalogues the collaboration graph of mathematics and computes Erdos numbers for hundreds of thousands of authors. Erdős published around 1,500 mathematical papers during his lifetime, most of them co-authored, giving him more than 500 direct collaborators with Erdos number 1.
