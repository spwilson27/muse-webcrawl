Machinist

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A machinist is a skilled tradesperson who uses machine tools to fabricate precision metal (and sometimes plastic or composite) parts. Working from engineering drawings and specifications, machinists set up and operate lathes, milling machines, grinders, drills and computer numerical control (CNC) machining centres, and measure their work with instruments such as micrometers and calipers to tolerances that may be measured in thousandths of an inch or micrometres.

Work and skills

Machinists produce parts for industries including aerospace, automotive manufacturing, energy, medical devices and scientific instruments. Their work combines mathematics (especially geometry and trigonometry), knowledge of the properties of metals and cutting tools, blueprint reading, and the selection of speeds, feeds and coolants. Related specialisms include tool and die makers, who build the dies and moulds used in mass production, and maintenance machinists, who repair industrial machinery.

Training

Training traditionally takes the form of a four-year apprenticeship combining supervised workshop practice with classroom instruction, though many machinists now enter through technical-college machining programmes. Modern machinists also learn computer-aided design and manufacturing (CAD/CAM) and the programming of CNC machines in languages such as G-code. Certification schemes, such as those of the National Institute for Metalworking Skills in the United States, validate competencies.

History

The trade emerged during the Industrial Revolution, when the construction of steam engines, textile machinery and firearms required interchangeable precision parts. Pioneers such as Henry Maudslay, inventor of the screw-cutting lathe, effectively created the modern machine-tool industry in the early nineteenth century. For much of the twentieth century machinists formed one of the largest skilled trades in industrial economies; automation has since reduced employment in routine production work while increasing demand for highly skilled CNC programmers and setup specialists.
