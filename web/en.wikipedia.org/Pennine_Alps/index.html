The Pennine Alps (French: Alpes Pennines, German: Walliser Alpen, Italian: Alpi Pennine) are a mountain range in the Western Alps. They lie in southwestern Switzerland (canton of Valais) and northwestern Italy (Piedmont and the Aosta Valley), and contain more high peaks than any other section of the Alps, including the Matterhorn and Monte Rosa, the second-highest summit in the Alps.

Geography

The range runs from the Great St Bernard Pass in the west to the Simplon Pass in the east, forming the border between Switzerland and Italy. On the Swiss side its valleys drain to the Rhône; the Italian slopes drain to the Po. The highest point is the Dufourspitze (4,634 m) of Monte Rosa. Other major four-thousanders include the Zumsteinspitze, Signalkuppe (site of the Margherita Hut, Europe's highest building), Lyskamm, the Weisshorn, the Dom—entirely in Switzerland and its highest wholly Swiss summit—and the Matterhorn (4,478 m), first climbed by Edward Whymper's party in 1865.

Passes and transport

Historic crossings include the Great St Bernard, used since Roman times and famous for its hospice and dogs; the Simplon, crossed by a Napoleonic road and since 1906 by the Simplon Tunnel; and the Theodul Pass near the Matterhorn. The Great St Bernard Tunnel (1964) provides an all-weather road link.

Tourism

Zermatt, Saas-Fee, and Verbier (in the western part of the range's foreland) are major centres for mountaineering and skiing. The Haute Route traverses the range from Chamonix to Zermatt.
