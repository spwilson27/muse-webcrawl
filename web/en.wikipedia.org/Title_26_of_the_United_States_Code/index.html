Title 26 of the United States Code

Title 26 of the United States Code is the Internal Revenue Code (IRC), the domestic portion of United States federal statutory tax law. Enacted as the Internal Revenue Code of 1954 and substantially reorganised by the Tax Reform Act of 1986, it governs federal income, payroll, estate, gift and excise taxes.

Organisation

The Code is divided into subtitles covering, among other topics: (A) income taxes; (B) estate and gift taxes; (C) employment taxes; (D) miscellaneous excise taxes; and (F) procedure and administration. It is administered by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), a bureau of the Department of the Treasury, whose interpretations appear in Treasury regulations and rulings.

History

Federal income taxation rests on the Sixteenth Amendment (1913), which authorised Congress to tax incomes without apportionment among the states. The modern code descends from the Revenue Act of 1913 and the comprehensive Internal Revenue Code of 1939.
