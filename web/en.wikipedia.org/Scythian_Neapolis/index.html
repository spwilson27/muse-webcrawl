Scythian Neapolis

Location: Simferopol, Crimea

Founded: Late 3rd century BC

Culture: Late Scythian

Scythian Neapolis (Greek: Σκυθική Νεάπολις) was an ancient settlement that existed from the end of the 3rd century BC until the 3rd century AD in the southwestern part of present-day Simferopol in Crimea. It was the capital of the Late Scythian kingdom in Crimea.

History

The city flourished under kings such as Scilurus and his son Palacus in the 2nd century BC, when the Scythians controlled much of the Crimean steppe and contested the Greek cities of the coast. Its defences included thick stone walls and a large mausoleum where Scythian royalty were buried with rich grave goods. The city was eventually destroyed during the Gothic invasions of the 3rd century AD.

Archaeology

Excavations, mainly conducted after the Second World War, uncovered defensive walls, a mausoleum with dozens of burials, residential quarters and painted crypts. Finds from the site, including gold ornaments and reliefs of King Scilurus, are held in museums including the Hermitage. The ruins are now an archaeological reserve on the outskirts of Simferopol.
