The guitar is a fretted musical instrument that typically has six strings, sounded by plucking or strumming with the fingers or a pick. It is one of the most widely played instruments in the world, central to genres from flamenco and classical music to blues, rock, jazz, country and pop.

Types

Classical guitars use nylon strings and a wide neck, played fingerstyle. Steel-string acoustic guitars produce a brighter, louder tone suited to folk and country. Electric guitars, developed in the 1930s, rely on magnetic pickups and amplification; solid-body designs such as the Fender Stratocaster and Gibson Les Paul shaped rock music. The bass guitar, usually with four strings tuned an octave below the guitar's lowest strings, anchors the rhythm section.

History

The guitar descends from earlier plucked instruments such as the lute and the Spanish vihuela. Antonio de Torres Jurado standardised the modern classical guitar in 19th-century Spain. In the 20th century, amplification and mass production made the guitar the emblematic instrument of popular music worldwide.

Playing

The left hand stops strings at frets while the right hand plucks or strums. Techniques include fingerpicking, sweep picking, tapping and slide. Standard tuning (E–A–D–G–B–E) is most common, but alternate tunings such as drop D and open G are widely used.
