Auvergne

The Chaîne des Puys, a chain of dormant volcanoes in Auvergne.

Auvergne (Occitan: Auvèrnhe or Auvèrnha) is a cultural region in central France. From 1956 to 2015 it was also an administrative region, comprising the departments of Allier, Puy-de-Dôme, Cantal, and Haute-Loire. Since 1 January 2016 it has formed part of the region of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, and its former capital was Clermont-Ferrand.

Geography

Much of Auvergne consists of the Massif Central, a high plateau of ancient volcanic uplands. The Chaîne des Puys, a north–south chain of cinder cones, lava domes, and maars including the Puy de Dôme, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2018. The Puy de Sancy, at 1,885 m, is the highest point of the Massif Central.

History

The region takes its name from the Arverni, a powerful Gallic tribe whose leader Vercingetorix resisted Julius Caesar and was defeated at the siege of Alesia in 52 BC. In the medieval period, the counts and later dukes of Auvergne ruled from Clermont; the region came under the French crown over the thirteenth to seventeenth centuries. The region is known for its Romanesque churches, its cheeses (Cantal, Saint-Nectaire, Bleu d'Auvergne, Fourme d'Ambert, Salers), and the spa towns of Vichy and La Bourboule.
