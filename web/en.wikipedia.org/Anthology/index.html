Anthology

In book publishing, an anthology is a collection of literary works chosen by a compiler; it may be a collection of plays, poems, short stories, songs or excerpts by different authors. The word comes from the Greek anthologia, "a gathering of flowers", and was first applied to the Greek Anthology, a celebrated collection of epigrams assembled in antiquity.

Types

Anthologies may gather works by many authors on a theme or genre — for example, anthologies of science fiction or of war poetry — or works by a single author. They have historically been important in preserving short works, establishing literary canons and introducing readers to unfamiliar writers. Famous examples include The Oxford Book of English Verse (1900) and the annual Best American Short Stories series, begun in 1915.

Other media

The term is also used in film and television for works made up of separate stories: an anthology film combines several short films, while an anthology series such as The Twilight Zone or Black Mirror presents a new story and cast in each episode. In music, an anthology album collects recordings from across an artist's career.
