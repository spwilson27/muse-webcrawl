Elfstedentocht

The Elfstedentocht (Dutch pronunciation: [ɛlfˈsteːdə(n)tɔxt]; West Frisian: Alvestêdetocht), or Eleven Cities Tour, is a long-distance tour skating event on natural ice, almost 200 kilometres (120 mi) long, which is held both as a speed skating match and a leisure tour in the province of Friesland in the north of the Netherlands. It leads past all eleven historical cities of the province.

The tour is held only when the natural ice along the entire course is at least 15 centimetres (6 in) thick. Because winters have become milder, the event has become increasingly rare: it has been held 15 times since 1909, most recently on 4 January 1997. When the ice is unsuitable, an alternative event, the Alternative Elfstedentocht, is held on the Weissensee in Austria.

History

The idea of the Elfstedentocht as an organized race emerged in 1909. The winner of the last edition in 1997 was Henk Angenent, who finished in 6 hours 49 minutes; the women's race was won by Klasina Seinstra. Participants who finish before midnight receive the coveted Elfsteden Cross (Elfstedenkruisje).

Route

The route starts and finishes in the provincial capital, Leeuwarden, and passes through Sneek, IJlst, Sloten, Stavoren, Hindeloopen, Workum, Bolsward, Harlingen, Franeker, and Dokkum — the eleven Frisian cities.

Cultural significance

The Elfstedentocht occupies a unique place in Dutch culture. The mere prediction of a sufficiently cold winter generates intense public excitement, known as Elfstedenkoorts ("Eleven Cities fever").
