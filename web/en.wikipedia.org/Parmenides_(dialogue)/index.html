Parmenides (dialogue)

Parmenides (Greek: Παρμενίδης) is one of the dialogues of Plato. It is widely considered one of the more challenging and enigmatic of Plato's dialogues, in which Socrates, as a young man, debates the Eleatic philosophers Parmenides and Zeno.

Overview

The dialogue takes place shortly after Zeno has presented his paradoxes in defence of Parmenides' doctrine of the One. Socrates proposes his theory of Forms as a solution, but the elderly Parmenides subjects the theory to a searching criticism, raising problems about participation, separation, and the famous "Third Man Argument." In the second half, Parmenides himself undertakes a rigorous dialectical exercise, examining the consequences of supposing that "the One is" and that "the One is not."

Characters

The speakers include Cephalus, who narrates the frame story; Pythodorus, who hosted the gathering; and in the main dialogue, Parmenides, Zeno, Socrates, and Aristoteles (a young namesake, not the later philosopher).

Interpretation

Scholars disagree over whether the dialogue is a critique of Plato's own earlier theory of Forms, a methodological exercise in dialectic, or a metaphysical inquiry into unity and plurality. Its influence on later Platonism, and especially on Neoplatonists such as Plotinus and Proclus, was enormous.
