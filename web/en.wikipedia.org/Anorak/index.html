Anorak

An anorak is a type of heavy weatherproof jacket, typically hooded and pulled on over the head rather than opening fully at the front, designed for protection against cold and wet weather. The word derives from the Greenlandic Inuit annoraaq, and traditional Inuit parkas of caribou or seal skin provided the model for modern designs.

Design

Classic anoraks are thigh- or hip-length pullovers with a short zip or lace-up placket at the neck, an attached hood, drawstrings at the hood and hem, and often a large "kangaroo" pocket across the chest. Because there is no full-length front opening, the design sheds wind more effectively than a conventional zipped jacket. Modern versions are made from synthetic waterproof-breathable fabrics or waxed cotton.

Uses

Anoraks are worn for hiking, mountaineering, sailing, skiing and military use in cold climates. A lighter, unlined windproof variant is sometimes called a windcheater or cagoule. In the 1990s the anorak also became a fashion item in Britain, strongly associated with Britpop and football-casual culture.

In slang

In British slang, "anorak" has become a humorous, mildly derogatory term for a person with an obsessive interest in a niche subject — such as trainspotting or statistics — stereotypically imagined as wearing the garment at their hobby.
