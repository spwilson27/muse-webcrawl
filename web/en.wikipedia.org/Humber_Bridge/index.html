The Humber Bridge is a single-span suspension bridge near Kingston upon Hull in England, spanning the Humber estuary between Hessle on the north bank and Barton-upon-Humber on the south. When it opened in 1981, its 1,410-metre main span made it the longest single-span suspension bridge in the world, a record it held for 17 years.

History

A crossing of the Humber had been proposed for decades to link Hull with Lincolnshire and shorten journeys that otherwise required a long detour via Goole. Construction began in 1972 under designers Freeman Fox & Partners; the bridge was opened to traffic on 24 June 1981 and officially opened by Queen Elizabeth II the following month. The cost, and the large debt incurred, kept tolls in place for decades and made the bridge a recurring political issue.

Engineering

The bridge has a total length of 2,220 metres, with side spans of 280 m (north) and 530 m (south) flanking the main span. Its concrete towers rise 155.5 m, and because of the Earth's curvature their tops are slightly further apart than their bases. The deck carries a four-lane dual carriageway with footpaths and cycle tracks on both sides.

The bridge today

The bridge is Grade I listed and carries around ten million vehicles a year. Tolls were halved in 2012 with government support for writing down the bridge board's debt. It remains one of Britain's most recognisable engineering landmarks and a popular subject for photographers of the estuary.
