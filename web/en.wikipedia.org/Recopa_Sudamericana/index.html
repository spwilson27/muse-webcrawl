The Recopa pits the champions of South America's two continental club competitions against each other.

The Recopa Sudamericana (Portuguese: Recopa Sul-Americana), known commercially as the CONMEBOL Recopa, is an annual association football competition organised by CONMEBOL between the winners of the previous season's Copa Libertadores and Copa Sudamericana, the two main continental club tournaments in South America. It is the South American equivalent of the UEFA Super Cup.

History

A predecessor competition, the Supercopa Masters, was played in 1989 between past Libertadores champions, and a single Recopa was staged in 1989, won by Nacional of Uruguay. The modern Recopa Sudamericana was revived in 2003, initially as a single match played in the United States — the 2003 final in Los Angeles saw Olimpia of Paraguay defeat San Lorenzo — before settling into a two-legged, home-and-away format from 2005 onward.

Format

The Recopa is contested over two legs, one at each club's stadium, usually early in the calendar year following the parent competitions. The team scoring more goals on aggregate wins; if the aggregate is level, the final is decided by a penalty shoot-out without extra time in most editions. The Copa Libertadores champion traditionally hosts the second leg.

Records

Argentina's Boca Juniors is the most successful club in the competition with four titles (2005, 2006, 2008 and a predecessor title in 1990 in some reckonings). Brazilian clubs have also won frequently, reflecting the countries' dominance of the Libertadores. Other notable winners include São Paulo, Internacional, River Plate, LDU Quito — the first Ecuadorian winner, in 2009 and 2010 — and Defensa y Justicia, whose 2021 victory over Palmeiras came after they had won the Sudamericana as a second-tier club.
