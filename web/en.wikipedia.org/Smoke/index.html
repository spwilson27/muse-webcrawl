Smoke

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Smoke rising from a fire

Smoke is a suspension of airborne particulates and gases emitted when a material undergoes combustion or pyrolysis, together with the quantity of air that is entrained or otherwise mixed into the mass. It is commonly an unwanted by-product of fires (including stoves, candles, internal combustion engines, oil lamps, and fireplaces), but may also be used for pest control (fumigation), communication (smoke signals), defensive and offensive capabilities in the military (smoke screen), cooking, or smoking (tobacco, cannabis, etc.).

Composition

The composition of smoke depends on the nature of the burning fuel and the conditions of combustion. Fires with high temperature contain the unburned fuel (mostly carbon) and gases, including carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, water vapour, and other products. Smoke from wood consists mainly of fine particles of carbon, tar, oils, and ash.

Health effects

Smoke inhalation is a major cause of death in victims of indoor fires. The smoke kills by a combination of thermal damage, poisoning, and pulmonary irritation caused by carbon monoxide, hydrogen cyanide, and other combustion products. Smoke particles can also damage the respiratory tract and carry carcinogens.

Visibility and measurement

Smoke obscures visibility, which can hinder occupants trying to flee a fire. Smoke density is often measured by its light-obscuring power, and detectors use optical or ionisation principles to sense it.
