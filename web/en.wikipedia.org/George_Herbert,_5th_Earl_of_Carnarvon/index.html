George Herbert, 5th Earl of Carnarvon (26 June 1866 – 5 April 1923) was an English aristocrat best known as the financial backer of the search for and excavation of the tomb of Tutankhamun in the Valley of the Kings, carried out by the archaeologist Howard Carter.

Life

Educated at Eton and Trinity College, Cambridge, Carnarvon inherited the earldom and Highclere Castle in 1890. A keen sportsman, racehorse owner and pioneering motorist — he was badly injured in a motoring accident in Germany in 1901 — he began spending winters in Egypt for his health. Fascinated by Egyptology, he obtained excavation concessions and, from 1907, employed Howard Carter. After years of modest results in Thebes, their work in the Valley of the Kings was nearly abandoned when, in November 1922, Carter found the steps leading to Tutankhamun's tomb, the richest and best-preserved pharaonic burial ever discovered.

Death and the "curse"

In March 1923 Carnarvon cut his cheek while shaving; the wound became infected, and he died in Cairo on 5 April 1923 of pneumonia following blood poisoning. Press speculation about a "pharaoh's curse" — fuelled by other deaths later associated with the tomb — became a durable legend, though historians note that most of those present at the opening lived for many years afterwards.
