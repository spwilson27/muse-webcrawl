National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics

Founded: 1915
Dissolved: 1958 (absorbed into NASA)
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Main laboratory: Langley, Hampton, Virginia

The National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA) was a United States federal agency founded on 3 March 1915 to undertake, promote and institutionalise aeronautical research. For more than four decades it conducted fundamental research that transformed American aviation, before being absorbed into the newly created NASA on 1 October 1958.

Research and achievements

NACA's laboratories — Langley (Virginia), Ames (California), Lewis (Ohio) and the flight research station at Muroc/Edwards — operated wind tunnels and flight-test programmes. Its landmark outputs include the systematic NACA airfoil series still used in wing design, the low-drag engine cowling that won the 1929 Collier Trophy, research on transonic flight and the area rule, and wartime work improving nearly every American combat aircraft. NACA pilots and engineers, including X-1 and X-15 programmes in cooperation with the Air Force and Bell, pushed through the sound barrier era.

From NACA to NASA

Following the Soviet launch of Sputnik in 1957, the National Aeronautics and Space Act of 1958 transformed NACA into NASA, adding spaceflight to its mission; its laboratories, personnel and research culture formed the core of the new agency.
