Five-year plans of China

The five-year plans of China are a series of social and economic development initiatives issued by the Chinese Communist Party and the government of the People's Republic of China since 1953. Modelled initially on the Soviet Union's five-year plans, they set national targets for economic growth, industrial structure, infrastructure, science and technology, and social policy. Although the Chinese economy has become far more market-oriented since the reform era, the plans continue to serve as central blueprints guiding state policy and investment.

Early plans

The First Five-Year Plan (1953–1957), launched with Soviet assistance, concentrated on heavy industry and collectivisation; 156 major industrial projects were built with Soviet aid, and steel and coal output rose sharply. The Second Five-Year Plan (1958–1962) was overtaken by the Great Leap Forward (1958–1960), whose radical collectivisation and backyard-steel campaigns ended in economic disaster and the Great Chinese Famine. Planning was disrupted again during the Cultural Revolution decade (1966–1976), after which Deng Xiaoping's reform and opening-up reoriented the plans toward modernisation and growth.

Reform-era plans

From the 1980s onward the plans shifted from command-style output quotas toward macroeconomic guidance, structural reform and integration with global markets. The Seventh Plan (1986–1990) emphasised reform of state enterprises; the Tenth (2001–2005) coincided with China's accession to the World Trade Organization. In 2006 the Chinese name was changed from jihua ("plan") to guihua ("guideline" or "program"), reflecting the indicative rather than mandatory character of most targets in a market economy.

Recent plans

The Thirteenth Five-Year Plan (2016–2020) targeted innovation, green development and the elimination of extreme rural poverty, a goal the government declared achieved in 2020. The Fourteenth Five-Year Plan (2021–2025) centres on "dual circulation", technological self-reliance, high-quality development, carbon peaking before 2030, and common prosperity. A Fifteenth Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) was under preparation following recommendations adopted in 2025.
