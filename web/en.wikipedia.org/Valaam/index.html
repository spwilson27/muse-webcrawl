Valaam

The Transfiguration Cathedral of Valaam Monastery.

Valaam (Russian: Валаа́м; Finnish: Valamo) is an archipelago in the northern portion of Lake Ladoga, lying within the Republic of Karelia, Russia. The total area of its more than 50 islands is about 36 square kilometres. The largest island is also called Valaam and is famous for the Valaam Monastery, a medieval Orthodox monastery that remains a major centre of pilgrimage.

History

Tradition holds that the monastery was founded by the monks Sergius and Herman, perhaps as early as the tenth or eleventh century. The islands were fought over by Sweden and Novgorod and later Russia; after the Winter War of 1939–40, Finland ceded the archipelago to the Soviet Union, and the monks evacuated to Finland, where they founded the New Valamo monastery in Heinävesi, still active today. The Soviet state used the island for institutions including a home for disabled war veterans.

Revival

In 1989 the monastery was returned to the Russian Orthodox Church, and monastic life resumed. Valaam is now one of Russia's most visited pilgrimage and tourist destinations, reachable by boat from Sortavala and Priozersk, and celebrated in the tradition of Russian Orthodox chant associated with the island's choir.
