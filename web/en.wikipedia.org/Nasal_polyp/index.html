Nasal polyp

Nasal polyps (NP) are painless, noncancerous, polypoidal growths of the lining of the nose or paranasal sinuses. They typically arise in the context of chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP), a long-standing inflammatory condition affecting an estimated 2–4% of adults.

Signs and symptoms

Polyps can cause nasal congestion and obstruction, runny nose, sneezing, postnasal drip, and a reduced or lost sense of smell (hyposmia or anosmia), together with facial pressure. They are associated with asthma, allergic rhinitis, aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (Samter's triad), and cystic fibrosis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by nasal endoscopy and CT imaging of the sinuses. First-line treatment is intranasal corticosteroid sprays; short courses of oral corticosteroids may shrink larger polyps. For severe cases, endoscopic sinus surgery (polypectomy) can remove the polyps, though recurrence is common. Biologic drugs targeting type-2 inflammation — such as dupilumab, omalizumab and mepolizumab — are approved for severe CRSwNP.
