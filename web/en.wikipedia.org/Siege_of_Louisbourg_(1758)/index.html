Siege of Louisbourg (1758)

The siege of Louisbourg was a pivotal battle of the French and Indian War (the North American theatre of the Seven Years' War) in 1758. A large British force under Major-General Jeffery Amherst, supported by a fleet under Admiral Edward Boscawen, besieged and captured the French fortress of Louisbourg on Cape Breton Island, Nova Scotia. The victory opened the St. Lawrence River to a British invasion of Canada.

Background

Louisbourg, founded in 1713, guarded the entrance to the Gulf of St. Lawrence and was France's principal naval base in North America. It had been captured by New England forces in 1745 but returned to France by the Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle in 1748. The British Prime Minister William Pitt the Elder made its capture a priority for the 1758 campaign.

The siege

The British expedition of some 14,000 soldiers and more than 150 ships arrived off Louisbourg on 2 June 1758. After delays from rough surf, troops landed at Freshwater Cove on 8 June, with a young brigadier, James Wolfe, distinguishing himself in the assault. Wolfe subsequently established batteries at Lighthouse Point that silenced the harbour defences. A naval cutting-out operation on the night of 25–26 July destroyed or captured the last French warships in the harbour. The governor, the Chevalier de Drucourt, surrendered on 26 July.

Aftermath

The fall of Louisbourg deprived France of its chief Atlantic naval base and allowed the British to sail up the St. Lawrence in 1759, leading to the capture of Quebec. The fortress was demolished in 1760 to prevent its future use. Several thousand French inhabitants of Île Royale were deported to France.
