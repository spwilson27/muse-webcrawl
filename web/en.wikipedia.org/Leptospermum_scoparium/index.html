Leptospermum scoparium

Leptospermum scoparium, commonly known as mānuka, the mānuka myrtle, New Zealand tea tree, broom tea-tree, or simply tea tree, is a species of flowering plant in the myrtle family Myrtaceae, native to New Zealand (including the Chatham Islands) and south-east Australia.

Family: Myrtaceae
Genus: Leptospermum
Species: L. scoparium
Common names: Mānuka, tea tree

Description

It is a prolific scrub-type tree, usually growing 2–5 m tall, with prickly, sharply pointed leaves and white (occasionally pink) five-petalled flowers. The wood is hard and was used by Māori for tool handles and other implements.

Uses

Mānuka is best known as the source of mānuka honey, produced by bees that collect its nectar; the honey is prized for antibacterial properties linked to the compound methylglyoxal. Mānuka oil, distilled from the leaves, is used in cosmetics and traditional remedies. Captain James Cook's crew brewed the leaves as a tea substitute, giving rise to the name "tea tree".

Ecology

The species is an early coloniser of disturbed ground and plays an important role in regenerating native forest. Cultivars with pink and red double flowers are popular garden shrubs worldwide.
