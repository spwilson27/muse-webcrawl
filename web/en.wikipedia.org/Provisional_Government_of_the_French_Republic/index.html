The Provisional Government of the French Republic (PGFR; French: Gouvernement provisoire de la République française, GPRF) was the provisional government of Free France between 3 June 1944 and 27 October 1946, following the liberation of continental France after the Second World War and preceding the establishment of the French Fourth Republic. It succeeded the French Committee of National Liberation (CFLN) and governed a France emerging from occupation, administering the transition from the Vichy regime, organizing elections, and laying the foundations of the postwar French state.

Formation and liberation

The PGFR was proclaimed in Algiers on 3 June 1944 under the chairmanship of General Charles de Gaulle, days before the Normandy landings. After the Liberation of Paris in August 1944, the government moved to the capital and was recognized by the Allies in October. It asserted the legal position that the Republic had never ceased to exist and that the Vichy regime was illegitimate. At home it presided over the épuration — the purge of collaborators — and integrated the Resistance movements into the state, incorporating the National Council of the Resistance's reform program.

Reforms

The provisional government enacted far-reaching reforms: women's suffrage (granted by ordinance in April 1944 and first exercised in 1945), the creation of the social security system (1945), the nationalization of major banks, insurance companies, coal mines, Renault, and the energy sector, and the establishment of works councils and the National School of Administration (ENA). It also restored republican legality and universal suffrage and organized elections to a Constituent Assembly in October 1945.

End

De Gaulle resigned in January 1946 over disputes about the powers of the presidency in the draft constitution. He was succeeded by Félix Gouin, then Georges Bidault, and finally Léon Blum. The constitution of the Fourth Republic was approved by referendum in October 1946, and the provisional government gave way to the new regime when Vincent Auriol was elected president in January 1947 following the constitution's entry into force on 27 October 1946.
