Siddur

A siddur is a Jewish prayer book containing a set order of daily prayers. The word derives from the Hebrew root meaning 'order'. Siddurim (plural) structure Jewish worship for weekdays, Sabbaths and festivals.

Contents

A complete siddur includes morning, afternoon and evening services, the Amidah, Shema, blessings, Psalms, the Aleinu, Kaddish and Grace After Meals, with variations between Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Mizrahi and other rites, and between Orthodox, Conservative and Reform movements.

History

The earliest surviving prayer books date from the Geonic period in Babylonia, including the siddurim of Amram Gaon and Saadia Gaon in the 9th–10th centuries. Printing spread standard editions from the 15th century onward, while modern editions add translation and commentary.
