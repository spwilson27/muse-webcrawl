Fetch (folklore)

A fetch is a supernatural double or apparition of a living person in Irish and English folklore. Seeing one's own fetch, or having it seen by others, was traditionally regarded as an omen of death — particularly if the fetch appeared in the morning, though an evening sighting might instead portend a long life.

Origins and parallels

The fetch has parallels in other traditions: the Norse fylgja, a spirit double that accompanies a person, and the German concept of the Doppelgänger. In Ireland, belief in the fetch persisted into the 19th and early 20th centuries and was recorded by folklorists and writers such as W. B. Yeats. The word "fetch" in this sense may derive from the notion that the apparition "fetches" the soul of the dying person.

In literature

The fetch appears in works by Walter Scott, Sheridan Le Fanu, and other authors of Gothic and supernatural fiction, and it influenced later treatments of the double in literature. The concept is related to bilocation reports and to the "wraith" of Scottish folklore — a spectral double of a person seen shortly before their death.
