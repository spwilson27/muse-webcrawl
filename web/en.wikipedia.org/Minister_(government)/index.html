Minister (government)

RoleHead of a government ministry
Appointed byHead of government / head of state
Senior titlePrime minister; senior minister

A minister is a politician who heads a ministry (department) of government, making and implementing decisions on policies in areas such as finance, defense, foreign affairs, health, or education. Ministers are usually members of the cabinet and are appointed by the head of government — for example a prime minister — typically from among the members of the legislature, and formally commissioned by the head of state.

The term derives from Latin minister, "servant." In parliamentary systems, ministers are collectively responsible to the legislature for government policy and individually responsible for their departments; conventions vary, but ministers may be expected to resign over major departmental failures. In presidential systems, equivalent officials are often called secretaries — such as the United States Secretary of State — and usually cannot simultaneously sit in the legislature.

Ranks and roles

Below cabinet ministers sit junior office-holders such as ministers of state, deputy ministers, and parliamentary secretaries, who assist with portfolios. A minister without portfolio sits in cabinet without a specific department. The number, titles, and remit of ministries differ by country and change as governments reorganize departments.
