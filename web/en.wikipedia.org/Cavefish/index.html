Cavefish

Cavefish or cave fish is a generic term for fresh and brackish water fish adapted to life in caves and other subterranean habitats. Related terms are subterranean fish, troglomorphic fish, troglobitic fish, stygobitic fish, phreatic fish and hypogean fish.

There are more than 200 scientifically described species of obligate cavefish found on all continents except Antarctica. Although widespread as a group, many cavefish species have very small distributions and are seriously threatened. Cavefish are members of a wide range of families and do not form a monophyletic group. Typical adaptations found in cavefish are reduced eyes and pigmentation.

Diversity

Over 200 species of obligate cavefish have been described, representing over 50 genera in 20 families; a high density of 58 species has been recorded from East and Southeast Asia alone. Their evolutionary ancestors were surface-dwelling, and the cave lineages have colonised the subterranean world independently on many occasions, making cavefish a classic case of convergent evolution.

Families

The most species-rich groups include the cyprinids (carps and minnows), the catfishes (loricariids, clariids, heptapterids and others), the amblyopsids (including the swampfish and northern cavefish), the bythitids (brotulas), and the loaches. The best-studied species is the Mexican tetra (Astyanax mexicanus), which exists in both surface and cave forms.

Adaptations

Cavefish typically show troglomorphism: loss or severe reduction of the eyes, depigmentation, elaboration of the lateral line system and other non-visual senses, and often a reduction in metabolic rate and scale cover. In the Mexican tetra, cave populations have lost functional eyes through developmental changes involving increased sonic hedgehog signalling, which suppresses eye formation while enlarging taste buds and jaw structures.

Habitat

Cavefish live in underground rivers, pools, sinkholes and aquifers, including the cenotes of Yucatán and the karst caves of China, Thailand, Brazil and the United States. Food is scarce and arrives mainly as detritus washed in from the surface or as bat guano.

Conservation

Many species are known from single caves and are listed as threatened by groundwater extraction, pollution and introduced surface species. Some, such as the Alabama cavefish (Speoplatyrhinus poulsoni), are protected by law.
