Abbas Mirza

Abbas Mirza
Born: 20 August 1789, Nava, Mazandaran
Died: 25 October 1833, Mashhad
House: Qajar dynasty
Father: Fath-Ali Shah Qajar

Abbas Mirza (Persian: عباس میرزا; 20 August 1789 – 25 October 1833) was a Qajar crown prince of Iran. He developed a reputation as a military commander during the Russo-Persian War of 1804–1813 and the Russo-Persian War of 1826–1828, as well as through the Ottoman–Persian War of 1821–1823. He is furthermore noted as an early modernizer of Persia's armed forces and institutions.

Reforms

As governor of Azerbaijan based in Tabriz, Abbas Mirza employed European instructors to train a new-model army (the Nezam-e Jadid), sent students to study in England, founded a printing press, and promoted translation of European works — reforms that anticipated later Iranian modernization efforts.

Wars and death

Despite his reforms, Persia lost its Caucasian territories to Russia under the treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828). Abbas Mirza died of illness at Mashhad in 1833, predeceasing his father; his son succeeded as Mohammad Shah.
