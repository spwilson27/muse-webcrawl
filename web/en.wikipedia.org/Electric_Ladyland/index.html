Electric Ladyland
Artist: The Jimi Hendrix Experience
Released: October 1968
Genre: Psychedelic rock, hard rock, acid rock
Length: about 75 minutes
Producer: Jimi Hendrix

Electric Ladyland is the third and final studio album by the Jimi Hendrix Experience, released as a double album in October 1968. Produced by Jimi Hendrix himself, it is widely regarded as one of the greatest rock albums of all time, showcasing Hendrix's mastery of the recording studio as well as his guitar playing.

Recording

The album was recorded at Olympic Studios in London and the Record Plant in New York, with engineers Eddie Kramer and Gary Kellgren. Hendrix's perfectionism during long, guest-filled sessions strained his relationship with producer Chas Chandler, who quit midway, and bassist Noel Redding. Contributors included Steve Winwood, Jack Casady, Al Kooper, and Buddy Miles. Hendrix made extensive use of stereo panning, phasing, backwards guitar, and the wah-wah pedal.

Music

Tracks include "Voodoo Child (Slight Return)", the extended blues jam "Voodoo Chile", a celebrated cover of Bob Dylan's "All Along the Watchtower", "Crosstown Traffic", "Gypsy Eyes", "Burning of the Midnight Lamp", and the aquatic suite "1983... (A Merman I Should Turn to Be)". The album blends psychedelic rock, blues, funk, and early heavy metal.

Reception

It was the Experience's only number-one album in the United States. The original UK cover, featuring a photograph of naked women that Hendrix disliked, caused controversy. In 2020, Rolling Stone ranked it among the top sixty albums of all time on its revised 500 greatest albums list.
