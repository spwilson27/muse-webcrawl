Inborn error of metabolism

Inborn errors of metabolism are a class of genetic disorders in which a defective gene causes a clinically significant block in a metabolic pathway. The term was coined by Archibald Garrod in 1908 from his studies of alkaptonuria, one of the first diseases understood to follow Mendelian inheritance.

Mechanism

Most such disorders result from single-gene defects that reduce or abolish the activity of an enzyme. Substances upstream of the block accumulate — sometimes to toxic levels — while products downstream become deficient.

Examples

Well-known examples include phenylketonuria (PKU), galactosaemia, maple syrup urine disease, the glycogen storage diseases and many disorders of fatty-acid oxidation.

Diagnosis and treatment

Many jurisdictions screen newborns for treatable metabolic disorders. Management may involve dietary restriction of the accumulating substrate, cofactor supplementation, enzyme replacement therapy or, in some cases, organ transplantation.
