Telluride is a town in the San Juan Mountains of southwestern Colorado, United States, and the county seat of San Miguel County. Situated in a box canyon at an elevation of about 8,750 feet (2,667 m), it is surrounded by steep forested mountains and cliffs, with Bridal Veil Falls at the canyon's head. Founded as a mining camp in the 19th century, Telluride is now known for the Telluride Ski Resort and for festivals including the Telluride Film Festival and the Telluride Bluegrass Festival.

History

The area was originally summer hunting ground of the Ute people. Gold and silver mining drew settlers in the 1870s; the settlement was first called Columbia and was renamed Telluride in 1881, after the tellurium minerals found in the region's gold ores — though telluride minerals were never actually found near the town. The Rio Grande Southern Railroad reached Telluride in 1890. In 1891, the Ames Hydroelectric Generating Plant near Telluride, promoted by L. L. Nunn, became one of the earliest commercial installations to transmit alternating current over a distance, supplying power to the Gold King Mine.

Butch Cassidy is said to have robbed his first bank, the San Miguel Valley Bank in Telluride, in June 1889. Mining declined through the 20th century, and the last major mine, Idarado, closed in 1978. The ski area opened in 1972, and tourism and second-home development transformed the town's economy.

Geography

Telluride occupies the floor of a glacial box canyon along the San Miguel River. The town is largely within a National Historic Landmark District designated in 1961 for its well-preserved Victorian-era mining architecture. The adjacent town of Mountain Village, reached by a free gondola, sits at the base of the ski resort.

Culture

The Telluride Film Festival, founded in 1974, is held every Labor Day weekend and attracts filmmakers and critics from around the world. The Telluride Bluegrass Festival, also founded in 1974, is held around the summer solstice. Other events include the Telluride Jazz Festival and Mountainfilm.
