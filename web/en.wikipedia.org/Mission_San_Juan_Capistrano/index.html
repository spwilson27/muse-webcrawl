Mission San Juan Capistrano

Mission San Juan Capistrano is a Spanish mission in San Juan Capistrano, Orange County, California. Founded on November 1, 1776, by Saint Junípero Serra, it was the seventh of the twenty-one California missions and is often called the "Jewel of the California Missions." The mission was named for Saint John of Capistrano, a 15th-century Italian theologian and warrior priest.

History

An earlier founding attempt in October 1775 was abandoned when violence erupted at Mission San Diego; the founding party buried the mission bells and cross before retreating. Returning the following year, Serra celebrated the mission's founding on All Saints' Day, 1776. The mission ministered to the Acjachemen (Juaneño) people, and its lands supported cattle ranching, farming, and trades such as tanning and ironworking — California's first iron works operated here in the 1790s.

The Great Stone Church

Construction of the Great Stone Church, built of sandstone with a vaulted ceiling and a 104-foot bell tower, began in 1797 and was completed in 1806. On December 8, 1812, a major earthquake struck during morning Mass, collapsing the nave and killing some forty worshippers. The church was never rebuilt; its dramatic ruins, including the soaring arched sanctuary, remain the mission's most famous feature.

The swallows

The mission is famous for the cliff swallows that historically returned each spring from Argentina to nest in its arches, celebrated around St. Joseph's Day (March 19) with the Fiesta de las Golondrinas. In recent decades fewer swallows have nested at the mission itself, and conservation efforts, including recorded swallow calls and artificial nests, have sought to lure them back.
