The International Energy Agency (IEA) is a Paris-based autonomous intergovernmental organisation, established in 1974 within the framework of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) in the wake of the 1973 oil crisis. Its initial mandate was to coordinate member countries' responses to major oil supply disruptions, including maintaining strategic petroleum reserves equal to at least 90 days of net imports and coordinating collective releases.

Role

The IEA has broadened its focus to the full spectrum of energy issues: energy security, energy economics, environmental awareness and engagement worldwide. It publishes influential analysis, most notably the annual World Energy Outlook, monthly oil market reports, and statistics used by governments and markets. In 2021 the IEA published its "Net Zero by 2050" roadmap, a landmark scenario for limiting global warming to 1.5 °C.

Membership and structure

Membership requires OECD membership and commitments on emergency oil stocks. The Agency has 31 member countries, plus association countries such as China, India and Brazil that participate in much of its work without full membership. It is led by an Executive Director — Fatih Birol of Turkey since 2015 — and governed by a Governing Board of member-country officials.

Collective actions

Member countries have coordinated emergency stock releases on several occasions, including during the 1991 Gulf War, after Hurricane Katrina in 2005, during the 2011 Libyan civil war, and twice in 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine — the largest releases in the Agency's history.
