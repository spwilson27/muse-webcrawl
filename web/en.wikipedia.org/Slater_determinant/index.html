Slater determinant

In quantum mechanics, a Slater determinant is an expression that describes the wave function of a multi-fermion system. It satisfies the Pauli exclusion principle: the many-body wave function changes sign under exchange of any two identical fermions and vanishes entirely when any two fermions occupy the same state.

The determinant is named for John C. Slater, who introduced it in 1929 as a means of ensuring the antisymmetry of the wave function of a many-electron system, building on the matrix mechanics work of Heisenberg. The wave function is written as the determinant of a matrix whose entries are the single-particle spin-orbitals evaluated at the coordinates of each particle.

Properties and use

Because the determinant of a matrix with two identical rows is zero, no two electrons in a Slater determinant can share the same spin-orbital — a direct expression of Pauli exclusion. A single Slater determinant is used as the approximation to the ground state in Hartree–Fock theory; more accurate treatments in quantum chemistry (configuration interaction, coupled cluster) combine many determinants to account for electron correlation.
