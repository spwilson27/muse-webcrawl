27 Club

The 27 Club is an informal cultural meme describing a group of celebrated musicians, artists and actors who died at the age of 27. The concept gained widespread attention after the deaths of several iconic rock musicians within a short period in 1969–1971, and again following the deaths of Kurt Cobain in 1994 and Amy Winehouse in 2011.

Key members

The phenomenon is most often traced to the deaths of blues guitarist Robert Johnson (1938), Rolling Stones founder Brian Jones (1969), Jimi Hendrix (1970), Janis Joplin (1970) and Jim Morrison of the Doors (1971) — all at 27, several from drug- or alcohol-related causes. Nirvana frontman Kurt Cobain, who died by suicide in 1994, and singer Amy Winehouse, who died of alcohol poisoning in 2011, cemented the idea in popular culture.

Statistical view

Studies have found no statistically significant spike in musician deaths at 27; a 2011 study in the British Medical Journal concluded that fame itself is associated with a higher risk of death in early adulthood, but that the risk does not peak specifically at 27. The "club" is generally regarded as a product of coincidence amplified by media storytelling, although it continues to be referenced in songs, films and books.
