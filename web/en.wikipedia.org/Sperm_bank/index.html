Sperm bank

A sperm bank, semen bank, or cryobank is a facility or enterprise which purchases, stores and sells human sperm. The sperm is produced and sold by men who are known as sperm donors. The sperm is purchased by or for other persons for the purpose of achieving a pregnancy or pregnancies other than by a sexual partner. Sperm sold by a sperm donor is known as donor sperm.

General

A sperm bank may be a separate service provided by a medical facility or a fertility clinic, or it may be a facility that operates mainly for the purpose of purchasing, storing and selling sperm. Sperm banks usually recruit donors, test them for infectious diseases and genetic conditions, assess their medical history and personal characteristics, and cryopreserve their semen in liquid nitrogen, where it can be stored for many years. Recipients purchase vials for use in intrauterine insemination (IUI), intracervical insemination, or in vitro fertilisation (IVF) with intracytoplasmic sperm injection.

Donors and recipients

Donors are usually required to be between about 18 and 39 years old, in good health, and to meet high standards of sperm quality; only a small fraction of applicants are accepted. Donations may be anonymous or identity-release, depending on jurisdiction and bank policy, and laws vary widely: some countries limit the number of families per donor, prohibit payment beyond expenses, or ban anonymous donation so donor-conceived people can learn their origins. Sperm banks serve heterosexual couples with male infertility, single women, and same-sex couples. Advances in home DNA testing have made long-term donor anonymity effectively impossible in many cases, generating legal and ethical debate.
