sRGB

sRGB (standard Red Green Blue) is a standard colour space for the Internet, computers, and printers created cooperatively by HP and Microsoft in 1996 and standardised by the IEC as IEC 61966-2-1:1999. It defines a gamut of colours based on the primaries of the Rec. 709 HDTV standard and a specified gamma (approximately 2.2), and it is the default colour space of the World Wide Web, most consumer cameras, displays, and operating systems.

Specification

sRGB defines red, green, and blue primaries at the Rec. 709 chromaticities, a D65 reference white, and viewing conditions typical of a home or office monitor environment. Its electro-optical transfer function is a piecewise curve: a linear segment near black joined to a power law with exponent 2.4, giving an effective gamma of about 2.2. Intensities are conventionally encoded in 8 bits per channel (24-bit "true colour").

Gamut and limitations

The sRGB gamut covers roughly 35% of the visible colours in the CIE 1931 diagram and cannot reproduce highly saturated cyans and greens found in nature and in print. Wider-gamut standards such as Adobe RGB, Display P3, and Rec. 2020 are used in professional photography and modern video. Nevertheless, because untagged images are assumed to be sRGB, the space remains the safest common denominator for web content.

Usage

Web browsers, CSS colours, and common image formats treat sRGB as the default. Digital cameras typically record JPEGs in sRGB unless set to a wider space. Colour-managed workflows convert between sRGB and device profiles using ICC profiles, with sRGB serving as a standard profile connection space for consumer devices.
