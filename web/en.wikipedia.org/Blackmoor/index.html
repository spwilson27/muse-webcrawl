Blackmoor is a fantasy role-playing game campaign setting generally associated with the game Dungeons & Dragons. It originated in the early 1970s as the personal setting of Dave Arneson, the co-creator of Dungeons & Dragons, first as a setting for Arneson's own games and later in a series of published materials.

Arneson's Blackmoor campaign began in 1971 in the Twin Cities area of Minnesota, evolving out of the Braunstein-style wargames run by David Wesely. Players explored the Castle Blackmoor dungeon, and the sessions developed many of the conventions of role-playing: individual characters, experience, hit points, and armor class among them.

Publication history

The first published Blackmoor material appeared in the 1975 Dungeons & Dragons supplement Supplement II: Blackmoor, edited by Tim Kask. It introduced the assassin and monk character classes as well as underwater adventuring rules. In the 1980s, TSR published a series of DA-series modules (DA1 Adventures in Blackmoor through DA4 The Duchy of Ten) set in the Blackmoor world, placed in the past of the Mystara setting.

Setting

Blackmoor is a barony in a pseudo-medieval world threatened by the Egg of Coot, an ancient evil entity, and by invasions from the Afridhi and other powers. The setting blends medieval fantasy with occasional science-fiction elements, such as the crashed spaceship in the City of the Gods.
