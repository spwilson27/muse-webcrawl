Closed timelike curve

In mathematical physics, a closed timelike curve (CTC) is a world line in a Lorentzian manifold of a material particle in spacetime that is "closed," returning to its starting point. Such curves would permit time travel to the past and appear in certain exact solutions of Einstein's field equations of general relativity.

History

The first solution containing a CTC was found by Willem Jacob van Stockum in 1937, describing an infinitely long rotating cylinder of dust. Kurt Gödel's 1949 rotating-universe solution also contains CTCs through every point. Later examples include the interior of a Kerr black hole, Tipler cylinders, and traversable wormholes.

Causality and paradoxes

CTCs raise problems for causality, such as the grandfather paradox. Stephen Hawking proposed the chronology protection conjecture, suggesting that the laws of physics prevent CTCs from forming. Quantum effects near a would-be time machine may destabilize it, though no complete proof exists within a theory of quantum gravity.
