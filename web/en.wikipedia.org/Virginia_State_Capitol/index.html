The Virginia State Capitol is the seat of state government of the Commonwealth of Virginia, located in Richmond on Capitol Square. Conceived by Thomas Jefferson and based on the Maison Carrée, a Roman temple in Nîmes, France, it was the first American public building designed in the Neoclassical style and has housed the Virginia General Assembly since 1788.

Design and construction

While serving as U.S. minister to France, Jefferson had a plaster model made from drawings by Charles-Louis Clérisseau, adapting the ancient temple with an Ionic portico. Construction began in 1785 and the building was occupied in 1788, though finishes continued into the 1790s. Its central rotunda contains Jean-Antoine Houdon's life-size statue of George Washington, carved from life.

History

During the American Civil War the building served as the Capitol of the Confederacy. In 1870 a balcony in the House chamber collapsed into the courtroom below, killing 62 people. Wings for the House and Senate chambers, designed by John Kevan Peebles, were added in 1904–1906, giving the building its present temple-fronted form flanked by hyphens and wings. A major restoration and underground extension were completed in 2007.

Influence

The Virginia Capitol set the pattern for American state capitols and courthouses, establishing the temple form — later crowned with domes elsewhere — as the emblem of republican government, and it is a National Historic Landmark.
