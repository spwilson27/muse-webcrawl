Imad al-Din Zengi (c. 1085–1146), often simply Zengi, was a Turkic atabeg who ruled Mosul, Aleppo, Hama, and Edessa in the early 12th century. He founded the Zengid dynasty and is remembered as the first Muslim leader to mount a sustained counter-offensive against the Crusader states, earning the titles al-Malik al-Mansur ("the Victorious King") and martyr of the faith (al-shahid).

Rise

The son of Aq Sunqur al-Hajib, a mamluk of the Seljuk sultan, Zengi rose through military service. In 1127 the Seljuk sultan Mahmud II appointed him atabeg of Mosul, and in 1128 he took control of Aleppo, uniting northern Syria and upper Mesopotamia under one ruler for the first time since the Crusades began.

Capture of Edessa

After years of campaigning against the Byzantines, the Artuqids, the Damascenes, and the Franks, Zengi besieged Edessa in late 1144. The city fell on 24 December 1144 — the first of the Crusader states to be destroyed — and the shock reverberated through Europe, prompting Pope Eugenius III to call the Second Crusade.

Death and legacy

Zengi was assassinated in his sleep in September 1146 while besieging Qal'at Ja'bar, reportedly by a eunuch servant he had threatened. His dominions were divided between his sons: Saif al-Din Ghazi took Mosul, while Nur al-Din succeeded in Aleppo and carried the counter-crusade to completion in Syria, paving the way for Saladin.
