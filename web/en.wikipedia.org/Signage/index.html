Signage is the design or use of signs and symbols to communicate a message. Signage also refers to the collective body of signs in a given environment. Signs serve many functions: they provide information (such as directions or identification of facilities), direct navigation through wayfinding systems, promote businesses, convey safety warnings and regulations, and shape the visual identity of places.

History

Signs are among the oldest forms of visual communication. In the ancient world, innkeepers and traders hung pictorial signs — a bunch of grapes for a wine shop, for example — because many customers could not read. Pub signs in Britain and trade signs across Europe developed rich pictorial traditions. The spread of mass literacy in the 19th century shifted signage toward lettering, illuminated by gas and later by neon and electric light in the 20th century.

Types

Signage includes traffic signs regulating roads; informational and directional signs in airports, hospitals, and campuses; retail fascias and window displays; advertising billboards; safety signs mandated by occupational regulations; and plaques marking historic sites. Digital signage, using electronic displays whose content can be updated remotely, has grown rapidly since the 2000s.

Design and regulation

Effective signage emphasizes legibility — appropriate typefaces, contrast, pictograms, and placement. Because signs affect public space, most jurisdictions regulate their size, illumination, and placement through planning and advertising codes; some places, such as São Paulo under its 2006 "Clean City Law", have banned most outdoor advertising altogether.
