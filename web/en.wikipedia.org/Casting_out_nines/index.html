Casting out nines

Casting out nines is a traditional method of checking arithmetic calculations, based on the fact that a number is congruent, modulo 9, to the sum of its decimal digits. Because 10 ≡ 1 (mod 9), every power of ten is also congruent to 1, so a number and the sum of its digits leave the same remainder on division by 9.

Method

To check a calculation, replace each number by its digital root-like remainder modulo 9 (obtained by repeatedly summing digits and discarding — "casting out" — any nines along the way). Perform the same operation on these small remainders. If the result does not match the remainder of the claimed answer, the calculation is certainly wrong. If it does match, the answer is probably, though not certainly, correct: errors that change a result by a multiple of 9, such as transposing digits, escape detection.

History

The technique was known to medieval mathematicians, described by writers including Fibonacci in his Liber Abaci (1202), and was widely taught in schools into the twentieth century as a quick sanity check before the era of calculators. It is a special case of checking arithmetic using modular arithmetic, analogous to check digits in modern codes.
