William Beebe

[Image: William Beebe]

Charles William Beebe (July 29, 1877 – June 4, 1962) was an American naturalist, ornithologist, marine biologist, entomologist, explorer, and author. He is remembered for his numerous expeditions with the New York Zoological Society, his deep dives in the Bathysphere, and his prolific scientific and popular writings.

Bathysphere dives

In the 1930s, with engineer Otis Barton, Beebe made a series of record-breaking descents off Bermuda in the Bathysphere, a spherical steel submersible lowered by cable. On August 15, 1934, they reached a depth of 3,028 feet (923 m), a record that stood for over a decade. Beebe's live radio descriptions of the strange deep-sea creatures he observed brought ocean exploration to a wide public.

Career and writings

Beebe was the first curator of ornithology at the Bronx Zoo and later headed the New York Zoological Society's Department of Tropical Research. He founded the research station Rancho Grande in Venezuela and wrote more than 800 articles and 24 books, including The Bird (1906), Half Mile Down (1934) and Edge of the Jungle (1921). His early work on the evolution of birds proposed a four-winged stage in their ancestry.
