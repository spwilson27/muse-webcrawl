Continuing education

Continuing education is an all-encompassing term for post-secondary learning activities and programs. The term is commonly used in the United States and Canada to describe a wide range of educational activities intended for adult learners, including degree credit courses by non-traditional students, non-degree career training, workforce training, formal personal enrichment courses (both on-campus and online), and self-directed learning. In the United Kingdom, the similar concept is often called lifelong learning or further education.

Professional continuing education

Many licensed professions require practitioners to complete continuing education in order to maintain their licenses or certifications. Physicians, nurses, pharmacists, lawyers, accountants, teachers, engineers, architects and real estate agents are common examples. Requirements are usually measured in contact hours or continuing education units (CEUs), where one CEU equals ten hours of participation in an accredited program. Professional associations, universities and private providers offer approved coursework, often through conferences, workshops and online modules.

General continuing education

Beyond professional requirements, colleges and universities operate continuing education divisions or extension schools — such as university extension programs — offering credit and non-credit courses to the general public. Community colleges play a large role in workforce retraining. Since the 2010s, massive open online courses (MOOCs) and other online platforms have greatly expanded access to adult learning.
