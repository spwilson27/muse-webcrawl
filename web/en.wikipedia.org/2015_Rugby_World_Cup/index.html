The 2015 Rugby World Cup was the eighth Rugby World Cup, held in England from 18 September to 31 October 2015. New Zealand defeated Australia 34–17 in the final at Twickenham to become the first team to win three World Cup titles and the first to retain the Webb Ellis Cup.

Pool stage

The tournament produced one of the greatest upsets in rugby history on its second day, when Japan defeated two-time champions South Africa 34–32 in Brighton — a result remembered as the "Brighton Miracle". Hosts England, drawn into a difficult pool with Australia and Wales, were eliminated in the pool stage after defeats by both, becoming the first host nation to fail to reach the knockout rounds of a World Cup.

Knockout stage

The quarter-finals saw all four southern hemisphere teams advance. In the semi-finals New Zealand beat South Africa 20–18 and Australia overcame Argentina. In the final, tries from Nehe Milner-Skudder, Ma'a Nonu and Beauden Barrett, together with the kicking of Dan Carter — named man of the match and later World Rugby Player of the Year — secured the All Blacks' victory before a crowd of 80,125.

Legacy

With a total attendance of nearly 2.5 million, the 2015 tournament was the best-attended Rugby World Cup to that date and was praised for its atmosphere and organisation. Japan's heroics helped build momentum for their hosting of the 2019 tournament.
