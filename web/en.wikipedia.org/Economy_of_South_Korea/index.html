Economy of South Korea

Currency: South Korean won (KRW)
GDP: Among the world's ten largest economies (nominal, approx.)
Major industries: Electronics, automobiles, shipbuilding, steel, petrochemicals
Exports include: Semiconductors, vehicles, displays, ships

The economy of South Korea is a highly developed mixed economy. By nominal GDP, it is one of the largest economies in the world and the fourth largest in Asia. South Korea is known for its rapid economic development from one of the poorest countries in the world in the mid-20th century to a developed, high-income economy in just a few generations — a transformation known as the "Miracle on the Han River".

History

Following the Korean War, South Korea pursued state-directed, export-oriented industrialisation through five-year plans, fostering large family-controlled conglomerates known as chaebol, such as Samsung, Hyundai, LG, and SK. The economy was severely hit by the 1997 Asian financial crisis, prompting an IMF programme and major reforms, but recovered to become a leader in high technology.

Sectors

South Korea is a world leader in semiconductors, consumer electronics, shipbuilding, automobiles, and steel. Services account for roughly 60% of GDP. The country invests heavily in research and development and education, and its cultural exports (the "Korean Wave") contribute significantly to tourism and trade.

Challenges

The economy faces demographic headwinds from one of the world's lowest fertility rates and a rapidly ageing population, high household debt, youth unemployment, and dependence on imported energy and on exports to China and the United States.
