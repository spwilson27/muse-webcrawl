Food

Food is any substance consumed by an organism for nutritional support. Food is usually of plant, animal, or fungal origin and contains essential nutrients such as carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, or minerals. The substance is ingested by an organism and assimilated by the organism's cells to provide energy, maintain life, or stimulate growth. Different species of animals have different feeding behaviours that satisfy the needs of their metabolisms and have evolved to fill a specific ecological niche within specific geographical contexts.

Nutrients and diet

Human food can be classified in various ways, either by related nutritional content or by how it is processed. Macronutrients — carbohydrate, fat and protein — supply energy, while micronutrients such as vitamins and minerals are needed in small quantities for physiological functions. Folate (vitamin B9), found in leafy green vegetables, legumes and fortified grains, is essential for DNA synthesis and cell division, and its deficiency in early pregnancy causes neural tube defects. Omnivorous humans are highly adaptable and have adopted many different diets and food cultures.

Production

Most food has its origin in plants, produced by agriculture or horticulture; meat and dairy come from animal husbandry. The food industry processes, transports and distributes food globally, while food safety regulation aims to prevent contamination and foodborne illness. Subsistence farming persists alongside industrial agriculture, and a growing share of food is produced from cell cultures and other novel technologies.

Culture

Food is central to culture and religion: cuisines define regional identity, feasts mark celebrations, fasting marks religious observance, and taboos govern what may be eaten. The study of food spans nutrition science, gastronomy, economics and anthropology.
