Salmonella

Salmonella colonies cultured from a food sample.

Salmonella is a genus of rod-shaped (bacillus) Gram-negative bacteria of the family Enterobacteriaceae. The genus comprises two species, Salmonella enterica and Salmonella bongori, with more than 2,600 serotypes recognised within S. enterica. Named after the American veterinary surgeon Daniel Elmer Salmon, salmonellae are among the most common causes of foodborne illness in the world.

Foodborne transmission

The classic picture of Salmonella infection is gastroenteritis acquired from food: undercooked poultry and eggs, raw egg dishes, unpasteurised milk, contaminated produce and contact with infected animals. Unlike norovirus — a virus that spreads explosively person-to-person and via contaminated surfaces and shellfish — Salmonella is a bacterial pathogen that multiplies in food left at warm temperatures, so a small initial contamination can become an infectious dose. Symptoms begin 6–72 hours after exposure and usually resolve within a week without antibiotics; treatment focuses on rehydration, and antibiotics are reserved for invasive disease and vulnerable patients.

Serotypes and disease spectrum

Non-typhoidal serotypes such as Enteritidis and Typhimurium usually cause self-limiting gut infection, whereas the human-adapted serotypes Typhi and Paratyphi invade beyond the intestine to cause enteric (typhoid) fever, a potentially fatal systemic illness where sanitation is poor. Invasive non-typhoidal salmonellosis is a significant cause of bloodstream infection in sub-Saharan Africa, particularly in young children and people with HIV or malaria.

Prevention

Prevention rests on the farm-to-fork chain: vaccination and biosecurity in poultry flocks, pasteurisation of milk and egg products, thorough cooking (a core temperature sufficient to kill the bacteria), separation of raw and cooked foods, refrigeration, and hand hygiene. Public health agencies track serotypes through molecular surveillance networks to detect outbreaks and recall contaminated products.
