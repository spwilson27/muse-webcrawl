Listeria is a genus of bacteria that, as of 2024, contains more than 25 recognised species. They are Gram-positive, rod-shaped, facultatively anaerobic bacteria that do not form endospores. The genus is named after the British pioneer of sterile surgery Joseph Lister. The species Listeria monocytogenes is by far the most important for human health: it is the causative agent of listeriosis, one of the most serious foodborne infections.

Biology

Listeria species are widespread in the environment — in soil, water, vegetation and the faeces of many animals. They are notable for their hardiness: L. monocytogenes grows at refrigeration temperatures (down to about 0 °C), tolerates high salt concentrations and a wide pH range, and forms biofilms on food-processing surfaces, making it a persistent hazard in food plants. The bacteria are motile by peritrichous flagella at room temperature, showing a characteristic tumbling motility.

Pathogenesis

L. monocytogenes is a facultative intracellular pathogen. After ingestion it crosses the intestinal barrier, spreads via the blood and lymph, and has a particular tropism for the liver, spleen, central nervous system and placenta. Its surface protein internalin binds host E-cadherin to enter cells, and the pore-forming toxin listeriolysin O lets it escape the phagosome; the ActA protein polymerizes host actin, propelling the bacterium within and between cells. Healthy adults usually experience only mild febrile gastroenteritis, but in pregnant women, neonates, the elderly and the immunocompromised, listeriosis can cause meningitis, septicaemia, miscarriage and stillbirth, with case-fatality rates of 20–30%.

Species

Historically the genus comprised six species, including L. innocua (non-pathogenic and commonly used as a surrogate in food safety studies), L. ivanovii (mainly an animal pathogen), L. seeligeri, L. welshimeri and L. grayi. Since 2010, genome-based surveys of the environment have added many new species, several of which have been proposed for transfer to new genera such as Murraya, Mesolisteria and Paenilisteria.
