Food safety

Food safety (or food hygiene) is the scientific discipline and set of practices concerned with handling, preparing and storing food in ways that prevent foodborne illness. The World Health Organization estimates that hundreds of millions of people fall ill each year from contaminated food, with young children bearing a large share of the burden.

Hazards

Food hazards are biological (bacteria such as Salmonella, Listeria and pathogenic E. coli, viruses such as norovirus, and parasites), chemical (pesticide residues, toxins, allergens), or physical (foreign objects such as glass or metal). Contamination can occur at any point from farm to table.

Controls

Core consumer practices are summed up as clean, separate, cook and chill: wash hands and surfaces, avoid cross-contamination, cook to safe internal temperatures, and refrigerate promptly. Industry and regulators use systems such as Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP), good manufacturing practice, traceability and recall procedures. Agencies such as the US Food and Drug Administration, the European Food Safety Authority and Codex Alimentarius set standards and guidance.
