Anencephaly is a serious birth defect in which a baby is born without parts of the brain and skull. It is a neural tube defect that occurs when the rostral (head) end of the neural tube fails to close, usually between the 23rd and 26th day of gestation, resulting in the absence of a major portion of the brain, skull and scalp. Infants born with anencephaly typically have only a brain stem and are unconscious and unable to feel; most die within hours or days of birth.

Causes and prevention

The exact cause is unknown but involves genetic and environmental factors. The most important known risk factor is insufficient folic acid (vitamin B9) before conception and during early pregnancy. Following public-health recommendations that women of childbearing age take 400 micrograms of folic acid daily, and the fortification of cereal grains with folic acid in the United States (from 1998) and many other countries, the incidence of anencephaly and spina bifida fell substantially. Women with a previously affected pregnancy are advised to take a much higher dose (4 mg) under medical supervision.

Diagnosis

Anencephaly can be diagnosed during pregnancy by ultrasound — the absent cranial vault is visible by the end of the first trimester — and by elevated maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein, or at birth by examination. Prenatal diagnosis raises difficult counselling and, in many jurisdictions, legal and ethical questions about continuation of the pregnancy.
