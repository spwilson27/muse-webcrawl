Food poisoning

Food poisoning
Other names: Foodborne illness, foodborne disease
Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, diarrhea, fever
Common causes: Bacteria, viruses, parasites, toxins
Prevention: Food safety, proper cooking and storage

Food poisoning, also called foodborne illness, is any illness resulting from the spoilage of contaminated food by pathogenic bacteria, viruses, or parasites, or from their toxins. Symptoms typically begin within hours to days of consuming contaminated food and most often resolve within a few days.

Causes

The most common causes of foodborne illness are bacteria such as Salmonella, Escherichia coli, Listeria, and Campylobacter; viruses such as norovirus; and bacterial toxins, such as staphylococcal enterotoxin. Chemical contaminants, heavy metals, and certain mushrooms can also cause food poisoning.

Symptoms

Symptoms commonly include nausea, vomiting, watery diarrhea, abdominal pain and cramps, and fever. Severe cases can cause dehydration, bloody stool, and, in rare cases, kidney failure (as in hemolytic–uremic syndrome from Shiga-toxin-producing E. coli) or death.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical; stool cultures or PCR panels can identify the organism. Treatment is mainly supportive: oral rehydration and rest. Antibiotics are reserved for specific infections.

Prevention

Prevention rests on food safety practices: adequate cooking, refrigeration, separation of raw and cooked foods, hand hygiene, and safe water. Public health surveillance systems track outbreaks and contaminated food products.
