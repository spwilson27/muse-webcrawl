Androgen receptor

The androgen receptor (AR), also known as NR3C4, is a type of nuclear receptor that is activated by binding the androgenic hormones testosterone and dihydrotestosterone. It functions as a transcription factor: once bound to its ligand, the receptor moves into the cell nucleus, binds to specific DNA sequences, and regulates the expression of genes that produce male sexual characteristics and other effects.

Structure and mechanism

The AR gene is located on the X chromosome (Xq11–12) and encodes a protein with three main domains: an N-terminal transactivation domain (which contains a variable polyglutamine tract), a DNA-binding domain, and a ligand-binding domain. In the absence of hormone, the receptor is held in the cytoplasm by heat shock proteins; ligand binding triggers a conformational change, dimerisation and nuclear translocation.

Clinical significance

Mutations that disable the receptor cause androgen insensitivity syndrome, in which genetically male (XY) individuals develop female or undervirilised external characteristics. Expansion of the polyglutamine tract causes spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy (Kennedy's disease). The receptor is also central to prostate cancer: most prostate cancers depend on androgen signalling, so androgen deprivation therapy and AR antagonists such as enzalutamide are mainstays of treatment, though resistance commonly develops.
