Hypogonadism

Hypogonadism is a medical term for decreased functional activity of the gonads — the testes in males or the ovaries in females — that may result in diminished production of sex hormones. In men it usually refers to testosterone deficiency; in women, to oestrogen deficiency. Low androgen levels are referred to as hypoandrogenism and low oestrogen as hypoestrogenism.

Classification

Primary hypogonadism results from failure of the gonads themselves, with compensatorily high pituitary gonadotropins (LH and FSH). Causes include Klinefelter syndrome, cryptorchidism, mumps orchitis, chemotherapy, and ovarian failure. Secondary (central) hypogonadism results from disease of the hypothalamus or pituitary — for example Kallmann syndrome, pituitary tumours, hyperprolactinaemia, or opioid use — with low or inappropriately normal gonadotropins.

Signs and symptoms

Before puberty, hypogonadism causes delayed or absent pubertal development and eunuchoid proportions. In adult men: reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, infertility, loss of muscle mass and body hair, fatigue, depressed mood, and osteoporosis. In women: amenorrhoea, infertility, hot flushes, and loss of bone density.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines clinical features with repeated morning measurements of testosterone or oestradiol together with LH and FSH to localise the defect. Treatment addresses the underlying cause where possible and replaces the deficient hormone — testosterone replacement therapy in men, oestrogen (with progestogen) in women; gonadotropin therapy can restore fertility when the pituitary is at fault.
