Women's Health Initiative

Launched1991
SponsorNational Institutes of Health (NHLBI)
Participants161,808 postmenopausal women
Age range50–79 years

The Women's Health Initiative (WHI) is a United States national health study sponsored by the National Institutes of Health, launched in 1991 to address major causes of death, disability, and frailty in postmenopausal women — principally cardiovascular disease, cancer, and osteoporotic fractures. Enrolling 161,808 women aged 50 to 79, it is among the largest prevention studies ever conducted in the US.

Components

The WHI consisted of a clinical trial of three interventions — hormone replacement therapy (estrogen alone, and estrogen plus progestin), dietary modification (a low-fat eating pattern), and calcium plus vitamin D supplementation — together with a large observational study. Women could enrol in one or more trial components.

Key findings

In 2002 the estrogen-plus-progestin trial arm was stopped early because the treatment increased the risks of breast cancer, coronary heart disease, stroke, and pulmonary embolism, outweighing benefits such as fewer fractures and colorectal cancers. The estrogen-alone arm was stopped in 2004 after an increased stroke risk was found. The dietary modification trial found no significant reduction in breast or colorectal cancer or cardiovascular disease, and calcium/vitamin D did not significantly reduce hip fractures overall.

Impact

The WHI results transformed medical practice: prescriptions for menopausal hormone therapy fell sharply worldwide, and subsequent research has refined the picture, suggesting that the balance of risks and benefits depends strongly on age and time since menopause (the "timing hypothesis"). Follow-up of the cohort continues to generate findings on women's ageing and health.
