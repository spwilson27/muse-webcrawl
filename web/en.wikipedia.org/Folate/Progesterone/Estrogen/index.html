TypeSteroid hormone

Estrogen (also spelled oestrogen) is a category of sex hormones responsible for the development and regulation of the female reproductive system and secondary sex characteristics. There are three major endogenous estrogens with estrogenic hormonal activity: estrone (E1), estradiol (E2), and estriol (E3). Estradiol is the most potent and prevalent estrogen during the reproductive years.

Biosynthesis

Estrogens are synthesised from androgen precursors by the enzyme aromatase. In premenopausal women the ovaries are the principal source; after menopause, estrogens are produced mainly in peripheral tissues such as adipose tissue. During pregnancy the placenta produces large quantities of estriol.

Functions

Estrogens drive the development of female secondary sexual characteristics, regulate the menstrual cycle, and maintain the endometrium. Beyond reproduction they influence bone density, cardiovascular function, fat distribution, skin, and the brain. Estrogen levels fall at menopause, contributing to symptoms such as hot flushes and to accelerated bone loss.

Medical uses

Estrogens are used in combined oral contraceptives, in hormone replacement therapy for menopausal symptoms, and in feminising hormone therapy. Long-term exposure patterns are also studied in relation to breast and endometrial cancer risk, in which the related hormone progesterone plays a balancing role in the endometrium.
