Women's Health Initiative hormone therapy study

The Women's Health Initiative (WHI) was a large United States research program launched in 1991 by the National Institutes of Health to study the causes of disease in postmenopausal women. Its randomized trials of hormone therapy — estrogen plus progestin (with progesterone-type compounds such as medroxyprogesterone acetate) and estrogen alone — reshaped medical understanding of menopausal hormone treatment.

The estrogen-plus-progestin trial

The trial randomized more than 16,000 women aged 50–79 with a uterus to conjugated equine estrogens plus medroxyprogesterone acetate or placebo. It was stopped early in 2002 because the treatment group showed increased risks of coronary heart disease, stroke, pulmonary embolism, and invasive breast cancer that outweighed benefits, which included fewer hip fractures and colorectal cancers.

Folate and related nutrition research

The WHI also examined dietary and nutritional factors, including folate status, through its observational study and dietary modification trial, contributing data on how B-vitamin intake relates to cardiovascular disease and cancer risk in older women. Results for many nutrient hypotheses were largely null, tempering enthusiasm for high-dose supplementation.

Impact

The 2002 results led to a sharp worldwide decline in hormone therapy prescriptions and revised guidelines recommending the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration for symptom relief. Later analyses suggested risks depend strongly on age and time since menopause, prompting the "timing hypothesis" debate.
