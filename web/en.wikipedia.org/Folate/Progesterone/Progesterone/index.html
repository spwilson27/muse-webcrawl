Progesterone

Chemical classPregnane steroid
FormulaC21H30O2
Produced mainly inCorpus luteum; placenta during pregnancy
ReceptorProgesterone receptor (PR)

Progesterone (P4) is an endogenous steroid and progestogen sex hormone involved in the menstrual cycle, pregnancy and embryogenesis of humans and other species. It belongs to a group of steroid hormones called progestogens and is the major progestogen in the body. Progesterone is also a crucial metabolic intermediate in the production of other endogenous steroids, including the sex hormones and corticosteroids.

Biological function

After ovulation, the corpus luteum secretes progesterone, which converts the endometrium to its secretory stage to prepare the uterus for implantation and maintains pregnancy by suppressing uterine contractility. If pregnancy does not occur, progesterone levels fall and menstruation begins. During pregnancy the placenta becomes the main source.

Biosynthesis

Progesterone is synthesized from pregnenolone, itself derived from cholesterol. It is produced in the ovaries, adrenal glands and, during pregnancy, the placenta, and in smaller amounts in the testes and brain.

Medical uses

Progesterone and synthetic progestins are used in hormonal contraception, hormone replacement therapy, prevention of preterm birth in some cases, and support of the luteal phase in assisted reproduction. The hormone was first isolated in 1934, and its name reflects its role as a "pro-gestational" steroid.

Neurosteroid activity

Progesterone's metabolite allopregnanolone acts on GABAA receptors in the brain, producing anxiolytic and sedative effects, and progesterone is regarded as a neurosteroid.
