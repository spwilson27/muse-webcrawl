Dihydrotestosterone

Dihydrotestosterone (DHT, or 5α-dihydrotestosterone), also known as androstanolone, is an endogenous androgen sex steroid and hormone. The enzyme 5α-reductase catalyses the formation of DHT from testosterone in certain tissues including the prostate gland, seminal vesicles, epididymides, skin, hair follicles, liver, and brain.

Biological role

DHT binds the androgen receptor with greater affinity and slower dissociation than testosterone, making it the more potent androgen in many tissues. It is essential for the embryonic development of the male external genitalia and prostate, and at puberty for prostate growth, facial and body hair, and sebaceous gland activity. People with 5α-reductase deficiency are born with undervirilised genitalia despite normal testosterone, demonstrating DHT's distinct role.

Clinical significance

DHT drives the enlargement of the prostate in benign prostatic hyperplasia and the follicle miniaturisation of male-pattern hair loss (androgenetic alopecia). Inhibitors of 5α-reductase — finasteride and dutasteride — lower DHT levels and are used to treat both conditions. Unlike testosterone, DHT cannot be aromatised to oestrogen, so its effects are purely androgenic.
