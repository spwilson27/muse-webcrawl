Menstruation (also known as a period, menstrual period, menstrual cycle or menses) is the regular discharge of blood and mucosal tissue from the inner lining of the uterus through the vagina. The menstrual cycle is characterized by the rise and fall of hormones. Menstruation is triggered by falling progesterone levels and is a sign that pregnancy has not occurred.

Physiology

During the follicular phase, oestrogen causes the endometrium to thicken (proliferative phase); after ovulation, progesterone from the corpus luteum makes it secretory and receptive to implantation. If no fertilized egg implants, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone and oestrogen fall, and the superficial endometrial layer breaks down and is shed over several days, accompanied by uterine cramps driven by prostaglandins. Blood loss averages about 30–40 mL; loss over 80 mL is considered heavy menstrual bleeding. The first period, menarche, usually occurs between ages 11 and 14, and periods continue until menopause at around age 51.

Symptoms and disorders

Many women experience premenstrual symptoms (breast tenderness, bloating, irritability) and cramping pain (dysmenorrhoea). Disorders include amenorrhoea (absent menstruation), oligomenorrhoea (infrequent), menorrhagia (heavy) and irregular bleeding; causes range from pregnancy and polycystic ovary syndrome to thyroid disease and eating disorders. Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) is a severe form of premenstrual syndrome affecting mood.

Management and products

Menstrual products used to absorb or collect flow include disposable pads, tampons, menstrual cups, period underwear and reusable cloth pads. Pain is commonly treated with NSAIDs such as ibuprofen, heat, or hormonal contraception. Access to affordable products and sanitation — "period poverty" — is a public-health and education issue in many countries, and several jurisdictions have removed taxes on menstrual products or made them free in schools.

Culture and taboo

Menstruation has been surrounded by cultural practices, religious rules and taboos across societies, from restrictions on entering temples or preparing food to celebrations of menarche. Attitudes have liberalized in many places, but stigma persists and can limit girls' schooling and participation in daily life.
