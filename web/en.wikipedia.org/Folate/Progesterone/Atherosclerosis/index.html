Atherosclerosis

Atherosclerosis is a chronic disease in which plaques of fat, cholesterol, calcium and other substances build up in the walls of arteries, narrowing them and restricting blood flow. It is the underlying cause of most heart attacks and many strokes, and it develops slowly over decades, often without symptoms until a plaque ruptures or an artery becomes critically narrowed.

Pathophysiology

The process begins with injury to the endothelium, the inner lining of the artery, promoted by high LDL cholesterol, high blood pressure, smoking and diabetes. LDL particles accumulate in the wall and become oxidised, triggering inflammation; monocytes enter, become macrophages, ingest lipid and form foam cells, producing a fatty streak. Smooth muscle cells then form a fibrous cap over the lipid core. If the cap ruptures, a blood clot can form suddenly and block the artery.

Risk factors and prevention

Major risk factors include raised LDL cholesterol, hypertension, smoking, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, age and family history. Prevention and treatment centre on lifestyle measures — not smoking, a diet low in saturated fat, exercise, weight control — and medicines such as statins, antihypertensives and antiplatelet drugs. Advanced disease may require angioplasty with stenting or bypass surgery.
