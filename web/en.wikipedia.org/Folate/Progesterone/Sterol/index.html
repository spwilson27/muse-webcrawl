Sterol

Sterols are a subgroup of steroids characterised by a hydroxyl group at the 3-position of the A-ring of the characteristic four-ring steroid structure, together with a hydrocarbon side chain. They are important components of the cell membranes of eukaryotes, where they regulate membrane fluidity and permeability.

Types

The most familiar sterol is cholesterol, the principal sterol of animals, which is also the precursor of steroid hormones, vitamin D and bile acids. Plants instead contain phytosterols such as β-sitosterol, campesterol and stigmasterol, which differ in their side chains and can modestly reduce cholesterol absorption from the gut. The main fungal sterol is ergosterol, the target of antifungal drugs such as amphotericin B and the azoles, and the precursor converted by ultraviolet light to vitamin D2.

Biochemistry

Sterols are synthesised from acetyl-CoA via the mevalonate pathway, through squalene and lanosterol. In the membrane, the rigid ring system and small polar hydroxyl group allow sterols to pack among phospholipid fatty-acid chains, buffering membrane fluidity against temperature changes. Sterols also serve as signalling molecules and are organised with sphingolipids in membrane "rafts".
