Menstrual cycle

Hormonal and ovarian changes through a typical 28-day cycle.

The menstrual cycle is a series of natural changes in hormone production and the structures of the uterus and ovaries of the female reproductive system that makes pregnancy possible. The ovarian cycle controls the production and release of eggs and the cyclic release of estrogen and progesterone. The uterine cycle governs the preparation and maintenance of the lining of the uterus (womb) to receive an embryo. These cycles are concurrent and coordinated and normally last between 21 and 35 days in an adult woman, with a median length of 28 days. The menstrual cycle is counted from the first day of menstrual bleeding (menstruation), which is the discharge of the uterine lining when pregnancy has not occurred.

Phases

  • Menstruation and the follicular phase — falling hormone levels trigger shedding of the endometrium over roughly 3–7 days, while follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) recruits a cohort of ovarian follicles; one dominant follicle matures as estrogen rises and rebuilds the uterine lining.
  • Ovulation — around the middle of the cycle, a surge of luteinising hormone (LH) triggers release of the mature egg, which remains viable for about a day. This is the fertile window.
  • Luteal phase — the emptied follicle becomes the corpus luteum and secretes progesterone, which makes the endometrium secretory and receptive. Without fertilisation, the corpus luteum degenerates, hormones fall, and menstruation restarts the cycle.

Regulation and variation

The cycle is driven by the hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis: GnRH from the hypothalamus stimulates FSH and LH from the pituitary, which act on the ovaries, whose steroid hormones feed back to regulate the system. Cycle length and symptoms vary widely between individuals and across life — irregular in adolescence and perimenopause, suppressed during pregnancy and lactation, and modifiable by stress, illness, energy balance and hormonal contraception. Folate status matters for those planning pregnancy, since periconceptional folic acid prevents neural tube defects.
