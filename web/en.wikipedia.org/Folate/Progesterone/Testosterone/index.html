Testosterone

FormulaC19H28O2
ClassAndrogen; anabolic steroid
Produced inTestes (Leydig cells); ovaries; adrenal glands
Regulated byLuteinizing hormone (LH)

Testosterone is the principal male sex hormone and an anabolic steroid. In male humans, testosterone plays a key role in the development of male reproductive tissues such as the testes and prostate, as well as promoting secondary sexual characteristics such as increased muscle and bone mass and the growth of body hair. In both sexes it influences mood, libido, and general health, and it is produced in far smaller amounts in females.

Biosynthesis and physiology

Testosterone is synthesised from cholesterol, mainly in the Leydig cells of the testes under the control of luteinizing hormone from the pituitary gland, with smaller amounts produced by the ovaries and adrenal cortex. In target tissues it may be converted to the more potent dihydrotestosterone (DHT) by 5α-reductase, or aromatised to estradiol. Levels rise sharply at puberty, peak in early adulthood, and decline gradually with age.

Effects

Before birth, testosterone drives male sexual differentiation. At puberty it causes deepening of the voice, growth of facial and body hair, increased muscle mass and strength, and maturation of the genitalia. It also supports red blood cell production and bone density. Insufficient testosterone (hypogonadism) can cause infertility, reduced libido, fatigue, and loss of muscle and bone mass.

Medical use

As a medication, testosterone is used in replacement therapy for male hypogonadism and in gender-affirming care. It is also misused as a performance-enhancing drug in sport, where it is prohibited as an anabolic steroid. Testosterone was first isolated in 1935 and approved for medical use in the United States in 1939.
