Hormone replacement therapy

Hormone therapy is delivered as tablets, patches, gels and implants.

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT), also known as menopausal hormone therapy or postmenopausal hormone therapy, is a form of hormone therapy used to treat symptoms associated with female menopause. These symptoms can include hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness, disturbed sleep and loss of bone density leading to osteoporosis. HRT supplies estrogen, and in women with a uterus, a progestogen (such as progesterone) to protect the endometrium from estrogen-driven overgrowth.

UsesMenopausal symptoms; prevention of osteoporosis
HormonesEstrogen ± progestogen (e.g. progesterone)
RoutesOral, transdermal patch/gel, vaginal, implant
Key risksVenous thromboembolism; breast cancer (combined, long-term)

Forms

Estrogen may be taken alone by women who have had a hysterectomy; women with an intact uterus take combined therapy. Progesterone — either micronised natural progesterone or a synthetic progestin — is added for at least part of each cycle or continuously, because unopposed estrogen increases the risk of endometrial cancer. Transdermal estrogen (patches, gels, sprays) avoids first-pass liver metabolism and carries a lower risk of blood clots than oral estrogen. Low-dose vaginal estrogen treats local genitourinary symptoms with minimal systemic absorption.

Benefits and risks

HRT is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats and prevents osteoporotic fractures. The large Women's Health Initiative trials (reported from 2002) found that combined oral therapy increased risks of breast cancer, stroke and venous thromboembolism in older women, leading to a sharp fall in prescribing. Subsequent analyses emphasised the "timing hypothesis": for healthy women under 60 or within ten years of menopause, benefits generally outweigh risks, and modern guidelines support individualised, shared decision-making with periodic review.

Other uses of hormone replacement

The term also covers testosterone replacement in hypogonadal men, thyroid hormone replacement in hypothyroidism, and gender-affirming hormone therapy. All share the principle of restoring physiological hormone levels lost through gland failure, surgery, or the menopause.
