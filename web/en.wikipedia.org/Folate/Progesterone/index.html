Progesterone

Progesterone is an endogenous steroid and progestogen sex hormone involved in the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, and embryogenesis of humans and other species. It belongs to a group of steroid hormones called the progestogens and is the major progestogen in the body. Progesterone has a variety of important functions: it converts the endometrium to its secretory stage to prepare the uterus for implantation, decreases the maternal immune response to allow for the acceptance of a pregnancy, and decreases uterine contractility.

Biosynthesis and function

Progesterone is synthesised from cholesterol via pregnenolone, chiefly in the corpus luteum after ovulation and, during pregnancy, in large quantities by the placenta. In the luteal phase it transforms the estrogen-primed endometrium into a secretory lining able to receive an embryo, maintains pregnancy by suppressing uterine contractions, and prepares the breasts for lactation. It also raises basal body temperature, the sign used in fertility-awareness methods.

Medical use

As a medication, progesterone (usually micronized) is used in hormone replacement therapy, in luteal support during assisted reproduction, and to prevent preterm birth in some pregnancies. Synthetic progestins derived from or modelled on progesterone are components of hormonal contraceptives.
