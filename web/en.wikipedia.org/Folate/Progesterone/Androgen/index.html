Androgen

An androgen (from Greek andr-, "man") is any natural or synthetic steroid hormone that regulates the development and maintenance of male characteristics in vertebrates by binding to androgen receptors. The primary and most well-known androgen is testosterone; others include dihydrotestosterone (DHT) and androstenedione.

Function

Androgens are essential for male sexual differentiation in the embryo, the development of male secondary sexual characteristics at puberty — deepened voice, facial and body hair, increased muscle mass — and the maintenance of libido, spermatogenesis, and bone density in adult life. In females, androgens produced by the ovaries and adrenal glands serve as precursors for oestrogens and contribute to libido and bone health.

Biosynthesis

Androgens are synthesised from cholesterol in the testes, ovaries, and adrenal glands. In the testes, Leydig cells convert cholesterol through pregnenolone and dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) intermediates to testosterone, some of which is converted in target tissues to the more potent DHT by the enzyme 5α-reductase.

Medical use and misuse

Synthetic androgens are prescribed for male hypogonadism, delayed puberty, and certain wasting conditions. Anabolic-androgenic steroids are also misused for performance enhancement, with risks including cardiovascular disease, liver damage, infertility, and psychiatric effects. Antiandrogens, which block androgen action, are used in prostate cancer treatment and other conditions.
