Cholesterol

Cholesterol is an organic molecule. It is a sterol (or modified steroid), a type of lipid. Cholesterol is biosynthesised by all animal cells and is an essential structural component of animal cell membranes. It also serves as a precursor for the biosynthesis of steroid hormones, bile acids, and vitamin D.

Function

Cholesterol modulates membrane fluidity and forms lipid rafts that organise signalling proteins. In specialised tissues it is the starting material for cortisol, aldosterone, sex hormones such as testosterone and progesterone, and the bile salts that emulsify dietary fat in digestion.

Transport in blood

Because cholesterol is insoluble in water, it circulates packaged in lipoproteins. Low-density lipoprotein (LDL) carries cholesterol from the liver to the tissues, and elevated LDL cholesterol is a major risk factor for atherosclerosis and cardiovascular disease — hence its label "bad cholesterol." High-density lipoprotein (HDL) returns cholesterol to the liver for excretion or recycling and is associated with lower risk.

Synthesis and regulation

Most cells can synthesise cholesterol, chiefly in the liver, through the mevalonate pathway whose rate-limiting enzyme is HMG-CoA reductase — the target of statin drugs. Dietary intake contributes a minority of the body's cholesterol; synthesis is down-regulated when dietary intake rises. Genetic defects such as familial hypercholesterolaemia, caused by mutations in the LDL receptor, produce very high blood levels and premature heart disease.

Measurement and management

Blood cholesterol is measured in a lipid panel, typically reported as total, LDL, and HDL cholesterol plus triglycerides. Management emphasises diet, exercise, weight control, and, where indicated, medication.
