SpecialtyMedical genetics, paediatrics
TypesSpina bifida, anencephaly, encephalocele, iniencephaly

Neural tube defects (NTDs) are a group of birth defects in which an opening in the spinal cord or brain remains from early development. In the third week of gestation the neural tube forms and closes; failure of closure produces defects ranging from the lethal anencephaly (failure at the cranial end) to spina bifida (failure along the spine), together with rarer conditions such as encephalocele and iniencephaly. Neural tube defects are among the most common of all birth defects.

Causes

NTDs are multifactorial: folate status is the best-established factor, and periconceptional folic acid supplementation reduces their occurrence by roughly 50–70 percent. Other risk factors include certain genetic variants affecting folate metabolism (such as MTHFR polymorphisms), maternal diabetes and obesity, exposure to the anticonvulsant valproic acid, and hyperthermia in early pregnancy. A woman who has had one affected pregnancy has a markedly increased recurrence risk and is advised high-dose folic acid.

Diagnosis and treatment

Screening combines maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein measurement with detailed ultrasound; amniocentesis can confirm. Spina bifida is treated surgically — in some cases by fetal surgery before birth, which the 2011 MOMS trial showed reduces the need for shunting and improves motor outcomes — followed by lifelong multidisciplinary care for mobility, bladder and bowel function, and associated hydrocephalus. Anencephaly has no treatment and is fatal.

Epidemiology

Rates have fallen where folic-acid fortification of staple foods is mandatory; historically high rates were reported in Ireland, Wales and northern China. Worldwide, an estimated 300,000 or more births per year are affected.
