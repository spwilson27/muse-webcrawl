Haemolytic–uremic syndrome

SpecialtyNephrology, haematology
SymptomsBloody diarrhoea, reduced urination, pallor, fatigue
HallmarksMicroangiopathic haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, acute kidney injury
Main causeShiga toxin-producing E. coli (STEC)

Haemolytic–uremic syndrome (HUS) is a group of blood disorders characterised by low red blood cells (haemolytic anaemia), acute kidney failure (uraemia), and a low platelet count (thrombocytopenia). Initial symptoms typically include bloody diarrhoea, abdominal pain, vomiting, and fever, followed as the condition progresses by signs of kidney failure such as reduced urine output and swelling.

Causes

The most common cause, especially in children, is infection with Shiga toxin-producing Escherichia coli (STEC), including serotype O157:H7, usually acquired from undercooked meat, unpasteurised dairy products, or contaminated produce. "Atypical" HUS arises from dysregulation of the complement system, often genetic, and may be triggered by infections, pregnancy, or drugs. Streptococcus pneumoniae infection is another recognised cause.

Pathophysiology

Shiga toxins damage endothelial cells lining small blood vessels, particularly in the kidneys, triggering platelet aggregation and the formation of microthrombi. Red blood cells are sheared as they pass through the narrowed vessels, producing schistocytes, while consumption of platelets causes bleeding tendency and glomerular damage causes kidney failure.

Treatment and prognosis

Treatment is primarily supportive: careful fluid management, blood transfusion where needed, and dialysis for kidney failure. Antibiotics are generally avoided in STEC-HUS because they may increase toxin release. Atypical HUS is treated with the complement inhibitor eculizumab, which has greatly improved outcomes. Most children with typical HUS recover, though a minority develop long-term kidney damage.
