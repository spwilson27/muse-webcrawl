Infection

An infection is the invasion of an organism's tissues by disease-causing agents — microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites or prions — their multiplication, and the reaction of the host's tissues to them and to the toxins they produce. An illness caused by infection is an infectious disease. Infections may be asymptomatic (subclinical) or produce symptoms ranging from mild to life-threatening.

Transmission

Infectious agents spread by direct contact, droplets and aerosols, contaminated food and water, insect vectors, contact with animals (zoonoses), and from mother to child. The chain of infection links a pathogen, a reservoir, a portal of exit, a mode of transmission, a portal of entry and a susceptible host; breaking any link can prevent spread.

Host defence and treatment

The immune system defends against infection through barriers such as skin, innate responses including inflammation and phagocytes, and adaptive immunity producing antibodies and T cells — the basis of vaccination. Treatment depends on the agent: antibiotics for bacteria, antivirals for some viruses, antifungals and antiparasitics for their respective targets, plus supportive care. Hygiene, sanitation, safe water, vector control and immunisation remain the foundations of prevention.
