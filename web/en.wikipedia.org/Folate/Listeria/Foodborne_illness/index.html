Foodborne illness (also foodborne disease and food poisoning) is any illness resulting from the spoilage or contamination of food by pathogenic bacteria, viruses, parasites, or toxins. Contamination can occur at any stage of the food supply chain — production, processing, distribution, storage and preparation. In addition to acute gastrointestinal illness, some foodborne infections cause serious long-term sequelae, including reactive arthritis, Guillain–Barré syndrome after Campylobacter infection, and haemolytic uraemic syndrome after infection with Shiga toxin–producing E. coli.

Burden of disease

In 2015 the World Health Organization's Foodborne Disease Burden Epidemiology Reference Group published the first global estimates: about 1 in 10 people fall ill each year from contaminated food, with the heaviest burden in Africa and South-East Asia and among children under five, who account for roughly 40% of the disease burden. In the United States, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention estimates about 48 million illnesses, 128,000 hospitalizations and 3,000 deaths annually.

Common pathogens

Norovirus causes the greatest number of cases and spreads readily via contaminated food handled by infected workers, shellfish, and fresh produce. Salmonella (from eggs, poultry, and produce), Campylobacter (mainly poultry), Clostridium perfringens (bulk-cooked meats held at improper temperatures), and Staphylococcus aureus are leading bacterial causes. Listeria monocytogenes is comparatively rare but has a high fatality rate, particularly in pregnant women, newborns and the immunocompromised, and is associated with deli meats and unpasteurized dairy products. Typhoid fever, caused by Salmonella enterica serotype Typhi, is a systemic food- and waterborne disease still common in regions with poor sanitation.

Surveillance and control

Public health agencies track foodborne illness through outbreak reporting, laboratory networks such as PulseNet that use whole-genome sequencing to link cases, and active surveillance programs. Control measures include farm-level biosecurity, slaughterhouse hygiene, pasteurization of milk and juice, irradiation, HACCP-based process control, product recalls, and consumer education on the four steps of clean, separate, cook and chill.

Notable outbreaks

Large recorded outbreaks include the 1993 Jack in the Box E. coli O157:H7 outbreak in the United States, which prompted major reforms in meat inspection; the 2011 German E. coli O104:H4 outbreak linked to fenugreek sprouts, with about 4,000 cases and over 50 deaths; and the 2017–18 South African listeriosis outbreak, the largest ever recorded, traced to processed meats.
