Progesterone

Progesterone, an endogenous steroid and progestogen sex hormone.

Progesterone (P4) is an endogenous steroid and progestogen sex hormone involved in the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, and embryogenesis of humans and other species. It belongs to a group of steroid hormones called the progestogens and is the major progestogen in the body. Progesterone has a variety of important functions: it is also a crucial metabolic intermediate in the production of other endogenous steroids, including the sex hormones and the corticosteroids, and it plays an important role in brain function as a neurosteroid.

Biological functions

Progesterone is produced mainly by the corpus luteum of the ovary after ovulation, and in much larger amounts by the placenta during pregnancy. Its central role is to prepare and maintain the endometrium (uterine lining) for implantation of a fertilised egg and to sustain early pregnancy: it suppresses uterine contractions, modulates the maternal immune response, and supports development of the mammary glands in preparation for lactation, while preventing lactation before birth. If pregnancy does not occur, progesterone levels fall and menstruation follows.

Beyond reproduction, progesterone acts in the brain as a neurosteroid — its metabolite allopregnanolone has sedative and anxiolytic effects — and it is a biosynthetic precursor of testosterone, estrogens, cortisol and aldosterone.

Medical use

Progesterone and synthetic progestins are used in hormonal contraception, in hormone replacement therapy (to protect the endometrium against unopposed estrogen), in fertility treatment to support the luteal phase, and in the prevention of preterm birth in certain pregnancies. Routes of administration include oral, vaginal, and intramuscular injection in oil.

Chemistry and history

Progesterone was isolated in 1934, and its structure determined soon after; it was the first progestational hormone identified. Its name derives from "progestational steroidal ketone". Industrial synthesis from plant sterols such as diosgenin from Mexican yams made progestins cheaply available and enabled the development of the combined oral contraceptive pill.
