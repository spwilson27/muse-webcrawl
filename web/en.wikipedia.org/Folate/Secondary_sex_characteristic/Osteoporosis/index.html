Osteoporosis

Osteoporosis is a systemic skeletal disorder characterised by low bone mass, micro-architectural deterioration of bone tissue leading to more porous bone, and consequent increase in fracture risk. It is the most common reason for a broken bone among the elderly. Bones that commonly break include the vertebrae, the bones of the forearm, and the hip. Until a broken bone occurs there are typically no symptoms.

Causes and risk factors

Bone is continually remodelled, with old bone resorbed and new bone formed; osteoporosis develops when resorption outpaces formation. Risk increases with age, and in women bone loss accelerates after menopause as estrogen levels fall, since estrogen normally restrains osteoclast activity. Other risk factors include low body weight, smoking, alcohol use, glucocorticoid medication, rheumatoid arthritis, and a family history of hip fracture.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually made by measuring bone mineral density with dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA); a T-score of −2.5 or lower defines osteoporosis. Prevention and treatment include adequate calcium and vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise, fall prevention, and medications such as bisphosphonates, which inhibit bone resorption, as well as anabolic agents and, in selected postmenopausal women, hormone replacement therapy.
