Puberty

Puberty is the process of physical changes through which a child's body matures into an adult body capable of sexual reproduction. It is initiated by hormonal signals from the brain to the gonads: the ovaries in a girl, the testes in a boy. In response, the gonads produce hormones that stimulate libido and the growth, function, and transformation of the brain, bones, muscle, blood, skin, hair, breasts, and sex organs.

Hormonal control

Puberty begins when the hypothalamus increases secretion of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), prompting the pituitary gland to release luteinizing hormone and follicle-stimulating hormone. These stimulate the gonads to produce the sex hormones — testosterone in boys and estrogen (estradiol) in girls — which drive the appearance of secondary sex characteristics.

Physical changes

In girls, puberty typically begins between ages 8 and 13, with breast development (thelarche), growth of pubic hair, a growth spurt, and the onset of menstruation (menarche). In boys, beginning typically between ages 9 and 14, the first sign is enlargement of the testes, followed by growth of the penis, deepening of the voice, increased muscle mass, and facial hair. Physical growth accelerates in the form of a growth spurt that ends with the fusion of the epiphyseal plates of the long bones.

Variations

Puberty that begins unusually early is called precocious puberty; delayed puberty may reflect chronic illness, undernutrition, or endocrine disorders. The average age of puberty, particularly menarche, has declined over the past two centuries, a trend attributed mainly to improved nutrition and health.
