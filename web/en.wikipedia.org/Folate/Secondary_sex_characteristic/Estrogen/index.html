Estrogen

Estrogen (also spelled oestrogen in British English) is a category of sex hormone responsible for the development and regulation of the female reproductive system and secondary sex characteristics. There are three major endogenous estrogens that have estrogenic hormonal activity: estrone (E1), estradiol (E2), and estriol (E3). Estradiol, an estrane, is the most potent and prevalent.

Biological function

Estrogens are synthesized from androgens by the enzyme aromatase, chiefly in the ovaries, and during pregnancy in the placenta. Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) stimulates estrogen production. Estrogens promote the development of female secondary sex characteristics such as breast development, drive the proliferative phase of the menstrual cycle, and act on many tissues beyond the reproductive system — including bone, where they restrain resorption; their decline after menopause contributes to osteoporosis.

Medical uses

Estrogens are used in hormonal contraception, as in the combined oral contraceptive pill, in hormone replacement therapy for menopausal symptoms, and in feminizing hormone therapy. They are also used in the treatment of certain hormone-sensitive cancers, while anti-estrogen drugs are used in breast cancer therapy.
