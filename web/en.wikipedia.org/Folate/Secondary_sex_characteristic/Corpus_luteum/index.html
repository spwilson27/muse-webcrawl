Corpus luteum

The corpus luteum (Latin for "yellow body"; plural corpora lutea) is a temporary endocrine structure in female ovaries involved in the production of relatively high levels of progesterone, and moderate levels of estradiol and inhibin A. It is the remains of the ovarian follicle that has released a mature ovum (egg) during a previous ovulation.

Development and function

After ovulation, the ruptured follicle is transformed under the influence of luteinizing hormone: its granulosa and theca cells enlarge, accumulate yellow pigment (lutein), and begin secreting progesterone. Progesterone prepares the endometrium for implantation of a fertilised egg and maintains early pregnancy. The corpus luteum also produces estrogen in moderate amounts.

Fate

If fertilisation does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates after about 10–14 days into a fibrous scar called the corpus albicans, progesterone levels fall, and menstruation begins. If pregnancy occurs, human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) secreted by the developing embryo "rescues" the corpus luteum, which continues producing progesterone until the placenta takes over later in the first trimester.
