Androgen

An androgen is any natural or synthetic steroid hormone that regulates the development and maintenance of male characteristics by binding to androgen receptors. The principal androgens are testosterone and dihydrotestosterone (DHT); others include dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) and androstenedione. Androgens are produced mainly in the testes, with smaller amounts from the adrenal glands and ovaries, and they are present in both sexes.

Biological functions

Androgens drive the development of the male reproductive system before birth, the changes of male puberty (deepening of the voice, growth of facial and body hair, increased muscle mass), and the maintenance of libido, bone density and red blood cell production in adults. DHT, converted from testosterone by the enzyme 5α-reductase, is especially important for the prostate and hair follicles.

Medical uses

Testosterone replacement therapy is used to treat male hypogonadism. Anabolic steroids — synthetic androgen derivatives — are used for some wasting and anaemia conditions but are widely misused for performance enhancement, with risks including cardiovascular disease, liver damage, infertility and mood disturbance. Antiandrogens, which block androgen action, are used in prostate cancer, hirsutism and gender-affirming care.
