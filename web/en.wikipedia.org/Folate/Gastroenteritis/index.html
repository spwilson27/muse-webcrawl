Gastroenteritis

Gastroenteritis is inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract — the stomach and intestines — typically caused by infection. It is characterised by diarrhoea, vomiting, abdominal pain and cramping, sometimes with fever. It is often called "stomach flu", although it is unrelated to influenza.

Causes

Viruses cause the majority of cases: norovirus is the leading cause in adults, and rotavirus historically the leading cause of severe disease in young children, now reduced where vaccination is routine. Bacterial causes include Salmonella, Campylobacter, Shigella and toxin-producing E. coli; parasites such as Giardia account for a smaller share. Transmission is usually faecal–oral, via contaminated food or water or person-to-person contact.

Treatment

Most cases resolve without specific treatment. The mainstay of care is rehydration — oral rehydration solution for mild to moderate dehydration, intravenous fluids for severe cases. Antibiotics are reserved for certain bacterial infections. Globally, diarrhoeal disease remains a major cause of death in children under five, largely preventable through clean water, sanitation, hygiene and rotavirus vaccination.
