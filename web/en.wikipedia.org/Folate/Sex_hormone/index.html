Sex hormone

Sex hormones, also known as sex steroids, gonadocorticoids and gonadal steroids, are steroid hormones that interact with vertebrate steroid hormone receptors and regulate sexual development and reproduction. The natural sex hormones are made by the gonads (ovaries or testes), by adrenal glands, or by conversion from other sex steroids in other tissues such as the liver or fat.

Types and roles

Androgens such as testosterone drive male sexual development and maintain muscle, bone and libido in both sexes. Estrogens, principally estradiol, drive female development, regulate the menstrual cycle, and affect bone, cardiovascular and brain function. Progestogens, principally progesterone, prepare and maintain the uterus for pregnancy. Sex hormones also shape the secondary sex characteristics that emerge at puberty.

Regulation and medical use

Secretion is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis: GnRH pulses stimulate pituitary LH and FSH, which act on the gonads, whose steroid output feeds back on the brain and pituitary. Synthetic sex hormones are used in hormonal contraception, hormone replacement therapy, fertility treatment, cancer therapy and gender-affirming care.
