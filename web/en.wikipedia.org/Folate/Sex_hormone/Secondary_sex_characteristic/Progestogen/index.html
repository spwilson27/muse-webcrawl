Progestogen

Progestogens, also sometimes written progestagens or gestagens, are a class of natural or synthetic steroid hormones that bind to and activate the progesterone receptor. The major natural progestogen is progesterone, which is produced by the corpus luteum of the ovary after ovulation and in large amounts by the placenta during pregnancy. Synthetic progestogens are called progestins.

Biological functions

Progesterone transforms the estrogen-primed endometrium into a secretory lining suitable for implantation, maintains pregnancy by suppressing uterine contractility, and prepares the breasts for lactation. Its rise after ovulation also raises basal body temperature, a change used in fertility awareness methods.

Medical uses

Progestogens are used in hormonal contraception — alone in the progestogen-only pill, implants, injections, and hormonal intrauterine devices, and combined with estrogen in the combined oral contraceptive pill. In hormone replacement therapy they protect the endometrium. They are also used to treat endometriosis, abnormal uterine bleeding, and in assisted reproduction.
