Estrogen

Estrogen (also spelled oestrogen) is the primary female sex hormone, a category of steroid hormones responsible for the development and regulation of the female reproductive system and secondary sex characteristics. The three major endogenous estrogens are estrone (E1), estradiol (E2), and estriol (E3); estradiol predominates during the reproductive years.

Synthesis and function

Estrogens are produced mainly by the ovaries from androgen precursors via the enzyme aromatase. They stimulate development of the endometrium, breasts, and female secondary sex characteristics at puberty, regulate the menstrual cycle in concert with progesterone, and influence bone density, lipid metabolism, and the cardiovascular system.

Clinical use

Estrogens are components of combined hormonal contraceptives and of hormone replacement therapy. Low estrogen after menopause contributes to hot flushes and bone loss, while excess exposure is a risk factor for some hormone-sensitive cancers.
