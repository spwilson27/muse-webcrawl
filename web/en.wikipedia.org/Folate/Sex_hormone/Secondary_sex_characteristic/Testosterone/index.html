Testosterone

Testosterone is the primary male sex hormone and an anabolic steroid. In humans, testosterone plays a key role in the development of male reproductive tissues such as the testes and prostate, as well as promoting secondary sex characteristics such as increased muscle and bone mass and the growth of body hair. It is produced mainly by the Leydig cells of the testes under the control of luteinizing hormone, and in smaller amounts by the ovaries and adrenal glands.

Biological effects

During puberty, rising testosterone drives the male growth spurt, deepening of the voice, enlargement of the genitalia, and spermatogenesis. In adult men it maintains libido, muscle mass, bone density, and red blood cell production. Testosterone can be converted to dihydrotestosterone, a more potent androgen, or aromatised to estradiol, an estrogen.

Medical use and misuse

Testosterone replacement therapy is used for men with hypogonadism. Because of its anabolic effects, testosterone and related steroids are misused in sport to enhance performance, a practice banned by sporting bodies and associated with cardiovascular, hepatic, and psychiatric adverse effects.
