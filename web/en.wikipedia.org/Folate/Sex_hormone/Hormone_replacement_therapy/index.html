Hormone replacement therapy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT), also known as menopausal hormone therapy or postmenopausal hormone therapy, is a form of hormone therapy used to treat symptoms associated with female menopause. These symptoms can include hot flashes, vaginal atrophy, accelerated skin aging, vaginal dryness, decreased muscle mass, sexual dysfunction, and bone loss (osteoporosis).

The main hormonal medications used in HRT for menopause are estrogens and progestogens; for women who have had a hysterectomy, estrogen alone is usually given, while women with an intact uterus receive a progestogen as well to protect against endometrial hyperplasia and cancer. Androgens such as testosterone are sometimes added.

Uses

Approved uses include the treatment of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes and night sweats), the prevention of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women at high risk of fracture, the treatment of vulvovaginal atrophy and genitourinary syndrome of menopause, and hormone replacement in women who experience premature menopause or primary ovarian insufficiency.

Risks and benefits

Combined estrogen–progestogen therapy is associated with a small increased risk of breast cancer, venous thromboembolism, and stroke, while it reduces the risk of hip fracture and colorectal cancer. Estrogen-only therapy increases the risk of endometrial cancer in women with a uterus. The balance of benefits and risks is most favourable for women who begin therapy before age 60 or within ten years of menopause. Large trials such as the Women's Health Initiative (2002) substantially reshaped prescribing practice.

Forms

HRT preparations include oral tablets, transdermal patches and gels, vaginal creams, rings and pessaries, and subcutaneous implants. Commonly used estrogens include estradiol and conjugated equine estrogens; progestogens include medroxyprogesterone acetate, norethisterone, and micronized progesterone. Low-dose vaginal estrogen is used for local symptoms with minimal systemic absorption.

Other hormone therapies

The term HRT is also used for testosterone replacement in hypogonadal men, thyroid hormone replacement, and gender-affirming hormone therapy. The replacement of declining sex hormone levels is the common principle.
