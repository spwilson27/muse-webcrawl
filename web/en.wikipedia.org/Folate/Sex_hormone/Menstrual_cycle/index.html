Menstrual cycle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The menstrual cycle is a series of natural changes in hormone production and the structures of the uterus and ovaries of the female reproductive system that make pregnancy possible. The ovarian cycle controls the production and release of eggs and the cyclic release of estrogen and progesterone. The uterine cycle governs the preparation and maintenance of the lining of the uterus (womb) to receive an embryo.

Up to 80% of women report having some symptoms during the one to two weeks prior to menstruation. The cycle, counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next, typically lasts 21 to 35 days in adults, with a median of 28 days. The first period, menarche, usually begins between ages 12 and 15; cycles cease at menopause, which usually occurs between 45 and 55 years of age.

Phases

The cycle is divided into the follicular phase, ovulation, and the luteal phase in the ovaries, and into menstruation, the proliferative phase, and the secretory phase in the uterus.

Follicular phase

Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) stimulates ovarian follicles to mature; the dominant follicle secretes increasing amounts of estradiol, which causes the endometrium to thicken (the proliferative phase).

Ovulation

A surge of luteinizing hormone (LH), triggered by high estrogen, causes the dominant follicle to rupture and release a mature oocyte, usually around day 14 of a 28-day cycle.

Luteal phase

The ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone converts the endometrium into a secretory lining suitable for implantation. If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates, hormone levels fall, and the lining is shed as menstruation.

Hormonal control

The cycle is regulated by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis. Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) from the hypothalamus controls pituitary release of LH and FSH; ovarian sex hormones feed back to modulate this system. Hormonal contraceptives disrupt the cycle by suppressing ovulation.

Disorders

Disorders include dysmenorrhea (painful periods), amenorrhea (absent periods), menorrhagia (heavy bleeding), premenstrual syndrome (PMS), and polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), a common cause of irregular cycles.
