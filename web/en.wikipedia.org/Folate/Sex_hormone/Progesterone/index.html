Progesterone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Progesterone (P4) is an endogenous steroid and progestogen sex hormone involved in the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, and embryogenesis of humans and other species. It belongs to a group of steroid hormones called the progestogens and is the major progestogen in the body.

Progesterone is produced in the ovaries (by the corpus luteum after ovulation), the adrenal glands, and, during pregnancy, the placenta. It is also an important metabolic intermediate in the production of other endogenous steroids, including the sex hormones and the corticosteroids, and plays an important role in brain function as a neurosteroid.

Biological function

Progesterone's primary function is to prepare the endometrium for implantation of a fertilized egg and to maintain pregnancy. After ovulation, progesterone from the corpus luteum transforms the estrogen-primed endometrium into a secretory tissue, reduces uterine contractility, and thickens cervical mucus. If pregnancy occurs, progesterone production is maintained first by the corpus luteum and then by the placenta; it suppresses further ovulation and supports gestation. Progesterone also promotes the development of the mammary glands, modulates immune function, and raises basal body temperature.

Mechanism of action

Progesterone acts through the nuclear progesterone receptors PR-A and PR-B, ligand-activated transcription factors that regulate gene expression in target tissues such as the uterus, breast, and brain. It also binds membrane progesterone receptors and is metabolized to allopregnanolone, a potent modulator of GABA-A receptors with sedative and anxiolytic effects.

Medical use

Progesterone is used as a medication, for instance in hormone replacement therapy, in fertility treatment and luteal support, in the prevention of preterm birth in certain pregnancies, and (as synthetic progestins) in hormonal contraception. It is available in oral, vaginal, and injectable formulations; oral micronized progesterone is widely used.

History

The hormone's function in maintaining pregnancy was demonstrated in the 1920s; it was isolated and its structure determined in 1934, when the name "progesterone" (from "progestational steroidal ketone") was proposed.
