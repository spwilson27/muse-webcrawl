Menopause

Menopause is the time in a woman's life when menstrual periods permanently stop and she is no longer able to bear children. It is diagnosed after twelve months without a period and usually occurs between the ages of 45 and 55, with an average around 51 in developed countries. It results from the loss of ovarian follicular activity and a decline in oestrogen production.

Signs and symptoms

The transition, called perimenopause, may last several years and can involve irregular periods, hot flushes, night sweats, sleep disturbance, vaginal dryness and changes in mood. After menopause, lower oestrogen increases the risk of osteoporosis and cardiovascular disease.

Management

Many women need no treatment. Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) using oestrogen, usually combined with a progestogen when the uterus is present, is effective for hot flushes and protects bone, but carries small increased risks of breast cancer and thrombosis that must be weighed individually. Non-hormonal options include certain antidepressants and gabapentin for flushes, lubricants for vaginal symptoms, and lifestyle measures and calcium, vitamin D and exercise for bone health.

Society and culture

Attitudes to menopause vary widely across cultures, from a marker of ageing to a liberating stage of life. Medicalisation of menopause and the popularity of hormone therapy have fluctuated since the mid-20th century, notably after the Women's Health Initiative results in 2002 reshaped understanding of HRT risks and benefits.
