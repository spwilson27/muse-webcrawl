Animal cell

TypeEukaryotic cell
Typical size10–30 μm
Key organellesNucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes
Distinguishing featuresNo cell wall or chloroplasts; small vacuoles; centrosomes

An animal cell is the type of eukaryotic cell that makes up the tissues of animals. Animal cells are enclosed by a plasma membrane, contain a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles, and differ from plant cells in lacking a rigid cell wall and chloroplasts.

Structure

The cell membrane regulates the passage of substances and mediates communication. Within the cytoplasm, the nucleus houses DNA and directs protein synthesis; mitochondria generate most of the cell's ATP; the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus synthesize, modify and ship proteins and lipids; lysosomes digest waste and worn-out components; and the cytoskeleton gives shape and enables movement. Animal cells also possess centrosomes with centrioles, important in cell division.

Cell types and organization

Animals have many specialized cell types — muscle cells, neurons, epithelial cells, blood cells and others — organized into tissues, organs and organ systems. Cells reproduce by mitosis, and in multicellular development they differentiate from stem cells into their specialized forms.

Comparison with plant cells

Unlike plant cells, animal cells have no chloroplasts or cellulose walls, have only small vacuoles, and can change shape readily; many are motile or part of motile tissues, supporting the movement described in animal locomotion.
