Grand Rapids, Michigan

Downtown Grand Rapids on the Grand River

Grand Rapids is a city in and the county seat of Kent County, Michigan, United States. At the 2020 census, the city had a population of 198,917, making it the second-most populous city in Michigan, and its metropolitan area exceeds one million residents. It lies on the Grand River about 30 miles east of Lake Michigan.

History

The area was home to Ottawa peoples for centuries; the name derives from the rapids on the Grand River. French fur trader Louis Campau established a trading post in 1826, and the settlement grew with the gypsum and lumber trades. In the late 19th century Grand Rapids became famous as "Furniture City" for its fine wood furniture industry, showcased at world exhibitions. Though furniture production declined in the late 20th century, office furniture makers such as Steelcase, Herman Miller, and Haworth remain based in the region.

Economy and culture

Modern Grand Rapids is a centre for healthcare — anchored by the Medical Mile along Michigan Street — education, and craft brewing, styling itself "Beer City USA." It is the hometown of President Gerald R. Ford, whose presidential museum and burial site are in the city. The annual ArtPrize competition, launched in 2009, is one of the world's largest open art contests. The Frederik Meijer Gardens & Sculpture Park is a leading regional attraction.
