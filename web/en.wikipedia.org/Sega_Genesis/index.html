The Sega Genesis, known as the Mega Drive outside North America, is a 16-bit home video game console developed and sold by Sega. Released in Japan in 1988 and in North America in 1989, it was Sega's third console and the successor to the Master System.

History

Built around the Motorola 68000 processor, the Genesis competed directly with Nintendo's Super Nintendo Entertainment System in the early 1990s "console wars." Its marketing, including the slogan "Genesis does what Nintendon't," and its mascot Sonic the Hedgehog helped it achieve major success, particularly in North America and Europe.

Games and add-ons

The console's library of more than 800 games includes Sonic the Hedgehog, Streets of Rage, Madden NFL, and Mortal Kombat. Sega released add-ons including the Sega CD and the 32X, which proved less successful. The Genesis was discontinued in 1997 but remains influential in gaming culture.
