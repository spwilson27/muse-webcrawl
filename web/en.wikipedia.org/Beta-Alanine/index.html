β-Alanine

IUPAC name3-Aminopropanoic acid
FormulaC3H7NO2
ClassificationNon-proteinogenic amino acid
RolePrecursor of carnosine

β-Alanine (3-aminopropanoic acid) is a naturally occurring beta amino acid in which the amino group is attached to the β-carbon rather than the α-carbon found in the standard amino acid alanine. It is not used in the biosynthesis of proteins.

Biochemistry

β-Alanine is a component of the dipeptide carnosine and of pantothenic acid (vitamin B5), and hence of coenzyme A. In muscle, carnosine acts as an intracellular buffer during high-intensity exercise, and β-alanine availability is the rate-limiting factor in its synthesis.

Sports supplementation

Beta-alanine is a popular sports supplement intended to raise muscle carnosine levels and delay fatigue in efforts lasting roughly one to four minutes. Supplemental doses commonly cause a harmless tingling sensation called paraesthesia.
