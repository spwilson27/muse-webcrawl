Knafeh

Knafeh

CourseDessert
RegionLevant (Palestine, Jordan, Syria, Lebanon)
Main ingredientsShredded phyllo (kataifi) or semolina dough, white cheese, sugar syrup, pistachio

Knafeh (Arabic: كنافة) is a traditional Middle Eastern dessert made with spun pastry dough called kataifi (or a fine semolina dough), layered with a mild white cheese such as Nabulsi or akkawi, baked until crisp, soaked in sweet sugar-based syrup (attar) flavoured with rose or orange-blossom water, and finished with ground pistachios. It is especially associated with the Palestinian city of Nablus and is eaten across the Levant and the wider Middle East.

Varieties and preparation

Two main styles of the pastry base are distinguished: khishneh ("rough"), made with shredded kataifi strands, and na’meh ("fine"), made with a smooth semolina dough, the latter typical of the famous Nablus knafeh served on a large round tray (sanieh) and cut into squares. The cheese layer is desalted before baking; the finished dessert is served hot so the cheese stretches, often with a sprinkling of crushed pistachio and sometimes a scoop of clotted cream (ashta).

Cultural significance

Knafeh is a celebratory food, served at weddings, graduations and during Ramadan evenings. Nablus holds records for the largest plate of knafeh, and the dessert’s origin — variously claimed by Palestinian, Syrian, Lebanese and Egyptian cities — is a matter of regional pride and friendly (and sometimes not so friendly) dispute, amplified by its popularity in the Palestinian diaspora.
