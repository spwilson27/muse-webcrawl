Saint Lucian cuisine

Saint Lucian cuisine refers to the food traditions of the Caribbean island nation of Saint Lucia. It draws on West African, French Creole, East Indian and British influences, reflecting the island's colonial history and its Kwéyòl-speaking culture.

National dish

The national dish is green figs and saltfish: unripe green bananas ("figs" in local usage) boiled and served with stewed salted cod, garnished with cucumber salad, avocado and a sauce of onions, peppers and mayonnaise. It is traditionally eaten on weekends and at cultural celebrations such as Jounen Kwéyòl (Creole Day).

Other dishes

Bouyon is a hearty one-pot soup of red beans, dumplings, ground provisions (yam, dasheen, sweet potato), salted meat and coconut milk. Accra are saltfish fritters seasoned with herbs and pepper. Callaloo soup, made from dasheen leaves, okra and coconut milk, is a Sunday staple. Rotis — flatbreads wrapped around curried chicken, goat or vegetables — reflect East Indian heritage. Fried bakes (floats) accompany many meals, and cocoa tea, made from locally grown cocoa sticks grated into spiced hot milk, is a beloved breakfast drink.

Seafood

The Friday-night street parties at Gros Islet and Anse la Raye are famous for grilled fish, lambi (conch), lobster and dorado (mahi-mahi), seasoned with local herbs and served with garlic sauce.

Produce

Saint Lucia was historically a major banana exporter. Breadfruit, mango, soursop, plantain, christophene (chayote) and dasheen are widely grown. Hot pepper sauce, often made with Scotch bonnet peppers, accompanies most meals.
