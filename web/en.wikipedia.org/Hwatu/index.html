Hwatu

Hwatu (Korean: 화투, "flower cards") is a playing card deck of Korean origin, adapted from the Japanese hanafuda cards and used for several popular Korean games, most notably Go-Stop (고스톱) and Seotda. The deck consists of 48 cards divided into twelve suits of four, one for each month of the year, each suit decorated with flowers or plants associated with that month.

The deck

The twelve months are represented by: pine (January), plum blossom (February), cherry blossom (March), black bush clover (April), iris (May), peony (June), red bush clover (July), pampas grass (August), chrysanthemum (September), maple (October), paulownia (November), and willow (December). Cards within a suit are ranked as kwang (bright, 20 points), yeol (animal/ribbon tier, 10 points), tti (ribbon, 5 points), and pi (junk, 1 point).

Games

In Go-Stop, two or three players match cards from their hands with cards face-up on the table to capture scoring combinations, choosing after each scoring play whether to "go" for more points or "stop" and collect. Hwatu games are a fixture of Korean family gatherings, especially during the Lunar New Year and Chuseok holidays, though they are also associated with gambling.
