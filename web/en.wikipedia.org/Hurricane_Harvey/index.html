Hurricane Harvey was a devastating tropical cyclone that struck Texas and Louisiana in August 2017. It was the first major hurricane to make landfall in the United States since Wilma in 2005, ending a record 12-year span, and it tied Hurricane Katrina as the costliest U.S. tropical cyclone on record.

Storm history and flooding

Harvey rapidly intensified in the Gulf of Mexico and made landfall near Rockport, Texas, as a Category 4 hurricane on 25 August 2017. It then stalled over southeast Texas, meandering for days and drawing moisture from the Gulf. The Houston metropolitan area received extraordinary rainfall — a U.S. tropical-cyclone record of 60.58 inches (1,539 mm) near Nederland, Texas — causing catastrophic flooding that inundated hundreds of thousands of homes, displaced more than 30,000 people and prompted over 17,000 rescues.

Aftermath

Damage was estimated at about $125 billion, mostly from flooding in the Houston area. The storm damaged refineries and chemical plants, including the Arkema plant fires, and recovery and rebuilding continued for years. The name Harvey was retired by the World Meteorological Organization in 2018 and replaced with Harold.
