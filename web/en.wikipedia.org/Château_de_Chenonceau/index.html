Château de Chenonceau

The Château de Chenonceau is a French château spanning the River Cher, near the small village of Chenonceaux in the Indre-et-Loire department of the Loire Valley. It is one of the best-known châteaux of the Loire Valley and is often called the "Château des Dames" (Ladies' Castle) for the series of remarkable women who built, extended, and preserved it.

History

The original château was built between 1513 and 1522 by Katherine Briçonnet, wife of the royal tax collector Thomas Bohier. King Henry II later gave Chenonceau to his mistress, Diane de Poitiers, who built the arched bridge across the Cher. After Henry's death in 1559, his widow, Catherine de' Medici, took the château from Diane and added the famous gallery over the bridge, turning it into a grand ballroom, and staged lavish festivals there.

Later history

The château was preserved during the French Revolution by Louise Dupin, who hosted Enlightenment figures such as Rousseau. In the First World War the gallery served as a hospital ward; in the Second World War the river marked the line between occupied and Vichy France, and the gallery served as an escape route for the Resistance. It was restored by the Menier family, who purchased it in 1913.

Architecture and gardens

The château combines late Gothic and early Renaissance forms, with a rectangular corps de logis, a five-arched bridge, and a two-storey gallery 60 metres long. The gardens of Diane de Poitiers and Catherine de' Medici, on opposite banks, are maintained in their historic designs.
