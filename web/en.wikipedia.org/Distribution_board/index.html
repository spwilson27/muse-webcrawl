Distribution board

A distribution board (also known as a panelboard, breaker panel, or consumer unit) is a component of an electricity supply system that divides an electrical power feed into subsidiary circuits while providing a protective fuse or circuit breaker for each circuit in a common enclosure.

Design

A distribution board typically contains a main switch for isolating the installation, busbars that distribute power, and rows of protective devices — miniature circuit breakers (MCBs), fuses, or residual-current devices (RCDs, also called ground fault interrupters) — each feeding a final circuit for lighting, sockets or fixed appliances. In North American practice, the equivalent "load center" usually has a main breaker and two vertical rows of branch-circuit breakers fed from split-phase busbars.

Terminology

In the United Kingdom the domestic version is called a consumer unit; larger commercial and industrial versions are distribution boards, often fed by a switchboard. Electrical codes such as the UK's Wiring Regulations (BS 7671) and the U.S. National Electrical Code govern their installation, protection and enclosure requirements.
