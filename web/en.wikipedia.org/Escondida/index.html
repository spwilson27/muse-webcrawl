Escondida

The Escondida open pit in northern Chile

The Escondida mine is a copper mine complex in the Atacama Desert of the Antofagasta Region of northern Chile, about 170 kilometres southeast of Antofagasta at an elevation of roughly 3,100 metres. It is the world's largest copper-producing mine, accounting for a significant share of global copper output.

History

The Escondida ("hidden") porphyry copper deposit was discovered in 1981, following exploration of the region by a joint venture. Production began in 1990. The mine is operated by Minera Escondida Ltda. and owned by BHP (57.5%), Rio Tinto (30%), and the JECO consortium (12.5%).

Operations

The complex comprises two open pits, Escondida and Escondida Norte, together with concentrator plants producing copper concentrate and a leaching operation producing copper cathodes. A desalination plant on the coast at Coloso supplies water, which is pumped to the mine, reducing reliance on continental water in one of the driest regions on Earth. Escondida has repeatedly expanded capacity, and its workforce is one of the largest in Chilean mining. Labour disputes at the mine — including the 2017 strike of 44 days, the longest in Chilean private mining history — can move world copper prices.
