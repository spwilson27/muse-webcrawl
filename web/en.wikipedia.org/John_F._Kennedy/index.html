John F. Kennedy

35th President of the United States
In office: 20 January 1961 – 22 November 1963
Born: 29 May 1917, Brookline, Massachusetts
Died: 22 November 1963 (aged 46), Dallas, Texas
Party: Democratic
Spouse: Jacqueline Bouvier

John Fitzgerald Kennedy (May 29, 1917 – November 22, 1963), often referred to by his initials JFK, was an American politician who served as the 35th president of the United States from 1961 until his assassination in 1963.

Early life and naval service

Kennedy was born into the prominent Kennedy family in Brookline, Massachusetts, the second son of Joseph P. Kennedy Sr. and Rose Fitzgerald. After graduating from Harvard, he commanded the patrol torpedo boat PT-109 in the Pacific during the Second World War; when the boat was rammed and sunk in 1943, Kennedy's efforts to save his crew made him a war hero.

Congressional career

Kennedy represented Massachusetts in the U.S. House of Representatives (1947–1953) and the Senate (1953–1960). His book Profiles in Courage won the Pulitzer Prize in 1957. In 1960 he narrowly defeated Richard Nixon in one of the closest presidential elections in American history, becoming the youngest person elected president and the first Catholic.

Presidency

Kennedy's administration faced the failed Bay of Pigs invasion, the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962, the building of the Berlin Wall, and the escalation of American involvement in Vietnam. Domestically he launched the New Frontier programme, established the Peace Corps, committed the United States to landing on the Moon through the Apollo programme, and moved toward the civil rights legislation completed after his death.

Assassination

On 22 November 1963, Kennedy was shot and killed while riding in a motorcade in Dallas, Texas. Lee Harvey Oswald was arrested for the murder but was himself shot two days later by Jack Ruby. The Warren Commission concluded Oswald acted alone, a finding that has remained the subject of public debate.
