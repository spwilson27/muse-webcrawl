Bonang

Classification: Percussion, idiophone

Origin: Java, Indonesia

Related instruments: Gong, kenong, kethuk

Ensemble: Gamelan

The bonang is a musical instrument of the Indonesian gamelan, consisting of a set of small kettle gongs resting on cords in a wooden frame (the rancakan). In the central Javanese gamelan it is one of the most important elaborating instruments, leading the ensemble and cueing other players. A full set comprises ten to fourteen kettles arranged in two rows, according to their pitch.

Construction

The kettles, cast from bronze, have a raised central boss (the pencu) which is struck with a pair of padded sticks (tabuh). Gamelan sets are tuned to one of two scale systems, the five-tone sléndro or the seven-tone pélog; pélog bonang often have interchangeable kettles to suit different subsets of the scale.

Types and role

Two sizes are used in Javanese gamelan: the larger bonang barung, playing in the middle register, and the bonang panerus, pitched an octave higher and playing patterns at double speed. The bonang player anticipates and outlines the core melody (balungan), using interlocking patterns, and in pieces with vocal or soft dynamics may lead transitions between sections. Its patterns also introduce pieces and signal changes of tempo or structure to the rest of the ensemble.

Use elsewhere

Instruments of the bonang type appear in other gamelan traditions of Java, Sunda, and Bali under different names, and kettle-gong rows of similar design feature in the kulintang ensembles of the southern Philippines and maritime Southeast Asia.
