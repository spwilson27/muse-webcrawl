ERCC8
Also known as: CSA (Cockayne syndrome protein A)
Gene location: Chromosome 5 (human)
Function: Transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair
Associated disease: Cockayne syndrome type A

ERCC8 (excision repair cross-complementation group 8) is a human gene that encodes the DNA excision repair protein ERCC-8, also known as Cockayne syndrome protein A (CSA). Together with CSB, the product of ERCC6, CSA is essential for transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair.

CSA (ERCC8) works with CSB to repair transcription-blocking DNA lesions.

Function

CSA is a WD repeat protein that forms part of an E3 ubiquitin ligase complex (with DDB1, CUL4A and ROC1). After CSB detects RNA polymerase II stalled at a DNA lesion, CSA's ubiquitin ligase activity helps remodel the stalled complex, ubiquitinating CSB and other substrates so that repair factors can access the damage and transcription can restart.

Clinical significance

Mutations in ERCC8 cause Cockayne syndrome type A, a rare autosomal recessive segmental progeroid disorder marked by severe growth retardation, sensitivity to sunlight, hearing loss, and progressive neurological deterioration. The gene was identified through complementation studies of cells from Cockayne syndrome patients.
