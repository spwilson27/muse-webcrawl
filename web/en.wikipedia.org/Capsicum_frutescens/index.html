Capsicum frutescens is a wild chili pepper having genetic proximity to the cultivated pepper Capsicum chinense. Peppers of this species are generally small, erect, and pungent, growing on bushy plants. The species is native to the Americas and is now grown worldwide in tropical and subtropical regions.

Cultivars

The best-known cultivar is Tabasco, the basis of Tabasco sauce, produced in Louisiana. Others include the Brazilian malagueta, African piri piri, and the siling labuyo of the Philippines. Fruits typically ripen from green to red and are harvested for sauces, drying, and ornamental use.

Cultivation

Plants are perennial in frost-free climates but often grown as annuals elsewhere. They prefer full sun, warm temperatures, and well-drained soil.
