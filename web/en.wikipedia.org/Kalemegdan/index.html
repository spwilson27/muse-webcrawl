Kalemegdan (Serbian: Калемегдан) is an urban area of Belgrade, the capital of Serbia, comprising the Belgrade Fortress and the surrounding Kalemegdan Park on a 125-metre-high cliff at the junction of the River Sava and the Danube. The name derives from the Turkish kale meydanı, "fortress field" or "battlefield".

History

The site has been fortified since antiquity, when the Celtic and then Roman settlement of Singidunum stood here. The fortress was repeatedly rebuilt by Byzantines, medieval Serbian rulers such as Despot Stefan Lazarević (who made Belgrade his capital in the early 15th century), Hungarians, and Ottomans, and later modified under Austrian occupation in the 18th century. It witnessed numerous sieges as a frontier stronghold between empires.

Kalemegdan Park

After Serbia gained control of the fortress in 1867, the grounds outside the walls were gradually converted into a public park, laid out in stages from the late 19th century and expanded in the 1930s. The park contains monuments, fountains, the Belgrade Zoo (founded 1936), sports courts, and promenades with views over the rivers and New Belgrade.

Landmarks

Within the fortress and park stand the Victor monument (Pobednik, 1928), a symbol of the city; the Military Museum; the Nebojša Tower; the Clock Tower and Gate; Roman well; the churches of Ružica and St. Petka; and the tomb of Ottoman grand vizier Silahdar Damat Ali Pasha. Kalemegdan is one of the most visited tourist attractions in Belgrade.
