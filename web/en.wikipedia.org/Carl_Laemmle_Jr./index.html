Carl Laemmle Jr. (born Julius Laemmle; April 28, 1908 – September 24, 1979) was an American film producer and the son of Universal Pictures founder Carl Laemmle. As head of production at Universal from 1929 to 1936, he oversaw an influential slate of films, including the studio's celebrated early horror cycle.

Laemmle Jr. ran production at Universal during its classic horror era.

Early life

Laemmle was born in Chicago in 1908. His father, a German Jewish immigrant, founded the Independent Moving Pictures Company in 1909 and co-founded Universal Pictures in 1912. The younger Laemmle joined the studio as a teenager and rose quickly through its ranks.

Career at Universal

Given control of production as a twenty-first birthday present in 1929, Laemmle Jr. pushed Universal toward more prestigious and ambitious pictures. His productions included All Quiet on the Western Front (1930), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture, as well as Dracula (1931), Frankenstein (1931), The Mummy (1932), The Invisible Man (1933), and Bride of Frankenstein (1935). These films launched the Universal Monsters cycle and made stars of Boris Karloff and Bela Lugosi.

His fondness for expensive productions contributed to the studio's financial difficulties during the Great Depression. After the Laemmle family lost control of Universal in 1936, he left the studio and largely retired from film production.

Later life

Laemmle Jr. lived in Beverly Hills and died of a stroke on 24 September 1979 — the same date on which his father had died forty years earlier, in 1939.
