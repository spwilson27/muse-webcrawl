Endel Tulving

Endel Tulving (May 26, 1927 – September 11, 2023) was an Estonian-born Canadian experimental psychologist and cognitive neuroscientist whose research on human memory reshaped the field. He is best known for distinguishing episodic memory — memory of personally experienced events — from semantic memory — general knowledge of facts and concepts — and for concepts including encoding specificity and 'autonoetic' consciousness.

Career

Born in Petseri, Estonia (now Pechory, Russia), Tulving fled the Soviet occupation as a teenager and emigrated to Canada in 1949. He studied at the University of Toronto, took his doctorate at Harvard University, and spent most of his career at Toronto and Yale, later holding a visiting chair at the Rotman Research Institute. His 1972 chapter formalising the episodic/semantic distinction, and his 1983 book Elements of Episodic Memory, are landmarks of memory research.

Concepts

Tulving proposed the encoding specificity principle (with Donald Thomson), the idea of ecphory in retrieval, and a taxonomy linking memory systems to forms of consciousness — anoetic, noetic and autonoetic. His work on a patient known as K.C., who lost episodic memory while retaining semantic knowledge, was influential clinical evidence for separate memory systems. He received many honours, including the Gairdner Foundation International Award.
