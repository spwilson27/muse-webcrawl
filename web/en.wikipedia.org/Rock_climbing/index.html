Rock climbing

Rock climbing is a sport and recreational activity in which participants climb up, down, or across natural rock formations or artificial climbing walls. It developed as a component of mountaineering in the late nineteenth century and has since grown into a family of distinct disciplines, including sport climbing, trad climbing, bouldering, and free soloing.

Disciplines

In top-rope climbing, the rope runs through an anchor above the climber, minimising falls. Lead climbing requires the climber to clip the rope into protection while ascending. Traditional (trad) climbing uses removable gear placed in cracks, while sport climbing relies on fixed bolts. Bouldering tackles short, difficult "problems" close to the ground over crash pads, and free soloing dispenses with ropes entirely. Competition climbing, emphasising speed, lead, and boulder formats, debuted at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games.

Equipment and grading

Essential equipment includes climbing shoes, a harness, belay devices, carabiners, quickdraws or traditional protection, helmets, and chalk for grip. Difficulty is expressed in grading systems such as the Yosemite Decimal System in North America and French grades in sport climbing.

History

Rock climbing emerged in the Lake District, Wales, and the Elbe Sandstone Mountains in the 1880s–90s, spread to the Alps and the United States, and was transformed after the Second World War by the big-wall ascents of Yosemite Valley. From the 1980s, sport climbing and indoor gyms made the activity widely accessible.
