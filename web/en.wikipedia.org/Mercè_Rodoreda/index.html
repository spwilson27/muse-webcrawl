Mercè Rodoreda i Gurguí (10 October 1908 – 13 April 1983) is widely regarded as the most important Catalan novelist of the twentieth century. Her masterpiece, La plaça del Diamant (1962; translated as The Time of the Doves and In Diamond Square), is the most celebrated Catalan novel of all time and has been translated into more than thirty languages. Gabriel García Márquez called it the most beautiful novel published in Spain since the Civil War.

Life

Rodoreda was born in the Sant Gervasi district of Barcelona. She married young, began publishing fiction and journalism in the 1930s, and worked for the Catalan government's propaganda services during the Spanish Civil War. After the Republican defeat in 1939 she went into exile, living precariously in France — including during the Nazi occupation, which she survived sewing and writing in Bordeaux and Paris — before settling in Geneva, Switzerland, where she worked as a translator for international organisations and wrote her mature fiction. She returned to Catalonia in the 1970s, settling in Romanyà de la Selva, and died in Girona in 1983.

Work

Her novels trace the inner lives of women against the upheavals of war and exile, in a deceptively simple, lyrical prose dense with symbolism — flowers, gardens, doves, and mirrors recur throughout her work.

  • Aloma (1938; revised 1969)
  • La plaça del Diamant (1962) — the story of Natàlia, "Colometa", from the Republic through the Civil War and its aftermath.
  • El carrer de les Camèlies (1966)
  • Jardí vora el mar (1967)
  • Mirall trencat (1974; A Broken Mirror) — a family saga spanning three generations.
  • Quanta, quanta guerra... (1980)
  • La mort i la primavera (1986, posthumous)

She also wrote celebrated short stories, including the collection Vint-i-dos contes (1958). In 1980 she received the Premi d'Honor de les Lletres Catalanes.
