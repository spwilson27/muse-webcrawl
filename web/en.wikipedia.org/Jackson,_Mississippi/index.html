Jackson, Mississippi

Jackson
State capital of Mississippi, USA
Counties: Hinds, Madison, Rankin
Population: approx. 153,000 (2020)
Nickname: "The City with Soul"

Jackson is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Mississippi. It is one of two county seats of Hinds County, and sits on the Pearl River in the west-central part of the state. Jackson is the anchor of the Jackson metropolitan statistical area, the largest metro area in Mississippi entirely within the state.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Choctaw people. Founded in 1821 as the new state capital and named for General Andrew Jackson, the city was laid out on the site of a trading post known as LeFleur's Bluff. During the American Civil War, Jackson was captured and largely destroyed by Union forces under General William Tecumseh Sherman in 1863. It grew as a railway and manufacturing centre in the 20th century and was a major site of the civil rights movement, including the 1961 Freedom Riders arrests and the 1963 assassination of Medgar Evers.

Geography and climate

Jackson sits atop the extinct Jackson Volcano. It has a humid subtropical climate with long, hot summers and short, mild winters. The city suffered a severe water crisis in 2022 when flooding disabled its main water treatment plant.

Culture and economy

Known as "The City with Soul", Jackson is noted for gospel, blues and soul music. Landmarks include the Mississippi State Capitol, the Mississippi Civil Rights Museum, and the home of writer Eudora Welty. Jackson State University and the University of Mississippi Medical Center are major institutions.
