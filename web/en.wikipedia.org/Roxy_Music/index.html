Roxy Music

Roxy Music are an English rock band formed in 1970 in London by Bryan Ferry and bassist Graham Simpson. One of the most influential groups of their era, Roxy Music blended art-school sophistication, glamour, and irony with rock, electronic experimentation, and pop, exerting a lasting influence on punk, new wave, and subsequent British pop. The classic line-up included Ferry (vocals, keyboards), Brian Eno (synthesiser and treatments, until 1973), Andy Mackay (saxophone and oboe), Phil Manzanera (guitar), and Paul Thompson (drums).

Career

The band's self-titled debut album Roxy Music (1972) and the single "Virginia Plain" announced a strikingly original sound. After For Your Pleasure (1973), Eno left following disagreements with Ferry and was replaced by Eddie Jobson. Subsequent albums Stranded (1973), Country Life (1974), and Siren (1975) — the last containing the hit "Love Is the Drug" — consolidated their success in Britain and growing recognition in the United States.

After a hiatus in which Ferry pursued a successful solo career, Roxy Music returned with the sleeker, dance-influenced Manifesto (1979), Flesh and Blood (1980), and their most commercially successful album, Avalon (1982), which included the singles "More Than This" and the title track and became a landmark of sophisticated 1980s pop. The band disbanded in 1983 and reunited for tours in 2001 and occasionally thereafter, including a 50th-anniversary tour in 2022.

Style and influence

Roxy Music's art-conscious image — retro-glamorous album covers, stylised stage dress, and Ferry's crooning delivery — set them apart from contemporaries and anticipated the visual self-consciousness of new wave and the New Romantics. Their influence has been cited by artists across punk, post-punk, dance, and pop. Roxy Music were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019.

Discography

Studio albums: Roxy Music (1972), For Your Pleasure (1973), Stranded (1973), Country Life (1974), Siren (1975), Manifesto (1979), Flesh and Blood (1980), Avalon (1982).
