Spam (electronic)

Spam is the use of electronic messaging systems to send unwanted messages (spam) to large numbers of recipients for the purpose of commercial advertising, non-commercial proselytizing, or malicious ends such as phishing and malware distribution. The most widely recognised form is email spam, but the term applies to instant messaging, social media, text messages, and other media.

Etymology

The term derives from a 1970 Monty Python sketch in which the name of the canned meat product Spam is repeated insistently, drowning out other conversation — analogous to unwanted messages flooding inboxes.

History

The first major commercial spam is often dated to 1994, when immigration lawyers Canter and Siegel posted an advertisement for the US green card lottery to thousands of Usenet newsgroups. As the commercial internet grew, spam volumes grew enormously, at times constituting the majority of all email traffic.

Fighting spam

Countermeasures include content and reputation-based filtering, authentication protocols such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, blacklists of known spam sources, and legal measures such as the US CAN-SPAM Act of 2003 and the EU's ePrivacy rules. Much spam is distributed through botnets of compromised computers.
