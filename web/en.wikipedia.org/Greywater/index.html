Greywater

SourcesBaths, showers, hand basins, washing machines
ExcludedToilet waste (blackwater); often kitchen water
Common reusesGarden irrigation, toilet flushing, groundwater recharge
Share of household wastewaterRoughly 50–80%

Greywater (or gray water, sullage) refers to domestic wastewater generated in households or office buildings from streams without serious faecal contamination — that is, the water from baths, showers, hand-washing basins and clothes-washing machines. It is distinguished from blackwater, which contains sewage from toilets. Some definitions also classify kitchen wastewater as greywater, while others exclude it because of its grease and food content.

Reuse

Greywater accounts for roughly half to four-fifths of household wastewater. Reusing it for garden irrigation or toilet flushing can substantially reduce demand for potable water, which is why greywater systems are encouraged in water-stressed regions such as Australia, the Middle East and the western United States. Simple systems divert laundry or shower water directly to subsurface garden irrigation; more elaborate systems filter, disinfect and store water for flushing toilets or washing.

Health and environmental considerations

Greywater is not sterile: it contains soaps and detergents, pathogens from skin and washing, and elevated levels of salts, boron and nutrients that can damage soil and plants if misapplied. It deteriorates quickly in storage, turning anaerobic within a day or two, so most regulations require that it be used promptly, applied below the soil surface, and never sprayed or used on edible crops eaten raw. Legal frameworks vary widely: some jurisdictions actively permit and standardise household greywater reuse, while others treat all wastewater alike.
