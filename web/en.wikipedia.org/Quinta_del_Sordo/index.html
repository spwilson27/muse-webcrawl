Quinta del Sordo

Quinta del Sordo
Location: Madrid, Spain
Built: early 19th century
Known for: Black Paintings by Francisco Goya

The Quinta del Sordo ("Villa of the Deaf Man") was the name given to a villa on the outskirts of Madrid, on the banks of the Manzanares river, where the Spanish artist Francisco Goya lived from 1819 to 1823, in self-imposed exile during the political turmoil that followed the restoration of Ferdinand VII.

History

The house took its name not from Goya himself — though he was by then profoundly deaf, following an illness in the 1790s — but from a previous owner who had also been deaf. Goya purchased the property in February 1819 and lived there with his housekeeper and companion Leocadia Weiss.

It was in this house that Goya, then in his seventies, painted the fourteen works now known as the Black Paintings (Pinturas negras), executed directly in oil on the plaster walls of two rooms. These dark, unsettling images — including Saturn Devouring His Son, Witches' Sabbath and The Dog — were never intended for public display and were not given titles by the artist.

The Black Paintings

After Goya's departure for Bordeaux in 1823, the house passed through several owners. In the 1870s the French banker Frédéric Émile d'Erlanger had the paintings transferred from the walls to canvas, a process that caused some damage and loss, and donated them to the state. They are now among the most famous holdings of the Museo del Prado in Madrid.

Demolition

The Quinta del Sordo itself was demolished around 1909, so the original setting of the Black Paintings is lost; it is known only from photographs taken before the transfer of the murals.
