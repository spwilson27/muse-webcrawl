Second United Front

The Second United Front was the alliance between the Chinese Nationalist Party (Kuomintang) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), formed in 1937 to resist the Imperial Japanese invasion of China during the Second Sino-Japanese War. It followed the earlier First United Front of the 1920s and, despite formal unity, was marked by deep mutual suspicion and periodic armed clashes.

Formation

After years of civil war and Communist encirclement campaigns, the Xi'an Incident of December 1936 — in which Chiang Kai-shek was detained by his own generals — forced negotiations. Following the Marco Polo Bridge Incident in July 1937, the two parties announced cooperation; the Red Army was nominally reorganised as the Eighth Route Army and New Fourth Army under Nationalist command.

Wartime relations

The Communists largely practised guerrilla warfare behind Japanese lines and expanded their base areas, while Nationalist armies bore the brunt of conventional battles. Friction culminated in the New Fourth Army Incident of January 1941, after which cooperation was nominal only. When Japan surrendered in 1945, the two sides raced to occupy territory, and full-scale civil war resumed in 1946.
