Ponte Fabricio

The Ponte Fabricio, also known as the Pons Fabricius or Ponte dei Quattro Capi, is the oldest Roman bridge in Rome, Italy, still existing in its original state. Built in 62 BC, it spans half of the Tiber River, from the Campus Martius on the east side to Tiber Island in the middle. (The Ponte Cestio crosses the western half of the river from the island to Trastevere.)

History

According to the historian Dio Cassius, the bridge was built in 62 BC by Lucius Fabricius, the curator of roads (curator viarum), to replace an earlier wooden bridge destroyed by fire. Fabricius's name is inscribed four times on the bridge in large letters. A smaller inscription records a restoration under the emperor Probus in the 3rd century AD. The bridge has survived largely intact, in continuous use for over two thousand years, though the flooding of the Tiber in 1450 and 1557 caused damage to the river embankments nearby.

Description

The bridge is built of tuff and peperino faced with travertine, with two wide semicircular arches of about 24.5 metres span supported on a central pier. A small arch in the pier allows floodwater to pass and reduces pressure on the structure. In the Middle Ages it was known as the pons Judaeorum because of its proximity to the Jewish Ghetto. Its later name, Ponte dei Quattro Capi ("Bridge of the Four Heads"), comes from two ancient herms with four heads set on the parapet at the eastern end, said to represent Janus or, in legend, rival restorers of the bridge.
