Paul Kagame

Paul Kagame (born 23 October 1957) is a Rwandan politician and former military officer who has served as President of Rwanda since 2000. He previously commanded the Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF), the rebel force whose military victory ended the Rwandan genocide in 1994. Kagame is widely credited with leading Rwanda's post-genocide recovery and economic development, though his government has also been criticised for authoritarian practices and restrictions on political freedom.

Early life and exile

Kagame was born in Tambwe, in Rwanda's Southern Province, the youngest of six children. In 1960, during the period of violence surrounding the Rwandan revolution, his family fled to neighbouring Uganda, where he grew up in refugee camps and attended school in Kampala. He later studied at Makerere University but did not complete a degree there.

Military career

In 1979 Kagame joined Yoweri Museveni's rebel forces in Uganda, becoming a founding member of the National Resistance Army (NRA). He rose to head military intelligence in the NRA. In 1990 he was studying at the United States Army Command and General Staff College at Fort Leavenworth, Kansas, when the RPF invaded Rwanda from Uganda. After the death of RPF commander Fred Rwigyema, Kagame returned to take command of the rebel movement, leading it through the Rwandan Civil War and to victory in July 1994, ending the genocide against the Tutsi.

Presidency

Kagame served as Vice President and Minister of Defence from 1994 to 2000, during which time he was widely regarded as the country's de facto leader. He became president in 2000 after Pasteur Bizimungu resigned, and has since won elections in 2003, 2010, 2017 and 2024. A 2015 constitutional referendum allowed him to serve additional terms. Under his leadership Rwanda has achieved strong economic growth, expanded healthcare and education, and become a regional technology hub, though critics cite limits on press freedom and political opposition. Kagame also served as Chairperson of the African Union in 2018.
