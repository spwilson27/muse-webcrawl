Nieuw Amsterdam is a town and commune in Suriname, the capital of the Commewijne District. It lies at the confluence of the Suriname River and the Commewijne River, opposite the national capital Paramaribo, and is connected to it by the Jules Wijdenbosch Bridge, opened in 2000.

The town grew around Fort Nieuw-Amsterdam, a five-pointed earthen fortress built between 1734 and 1747 to defend the colony's plantations against raids by maroons—escaped enslaved people—from the interior, as well as foreign attack. The fort is now an open-air museum.

History

During the Second World War, American troops were stationed at the fort and its guns protected the bauxite supply routes vital to aluminium production. The district around Nieuw Amsterdam was historically a plantation area growing sugar, coffee, cotton and cacao, and the town today is home to a diverse population descended from Dutch, African, Indian, Javanese and Chinese settlers and labourers.
