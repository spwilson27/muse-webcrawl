Louis, Grand Condé

Louis II de Bourbon, Prince of Condé (8 September 1621 – 11 December 1686), known as le Grand Condé, was a French general and the most famous member of the Condé branch of the House of Bourbon. He first won renown at the Battle of Rocroi (1643), aged 21, where his victory over the Spanish tercios ended Spain's military dominance in Europe.

During the Fronde civil wars Condé rebelled against the crown and later served Spain against France, before being pardoned and restored. He went on to command in the Dutch War, winning at Seneffe (1674) and conquering Franche-Comté. Widely regarded as one of the great commanders of the 17th century, he spent his last years at Chantilly as a patron of the arts.
