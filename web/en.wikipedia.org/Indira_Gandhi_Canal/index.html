Indira Gandhi Canal

The Indira Gandhi Canal, originally called the Rajasthan Canal, is the largest canal in India and one of the largest irrigation projects in the world. It carries water from the Harike Barrage on the Sutlej River in Punjab some 650 kilometres southwest through the Thar Desert of Rajasthan, providing irrigation and drinking water to the arid districts of Bikaner, Jaisalmer, and beyond.

History

The idea of bringing Himalayan river water to the Rajasthan desert was conceived by hydraulic engineer Kanwar Sain in 1948. Construction of the canal began in 1958, and the project was renamed the Indira Gandhi Canal in 1984 following the assassination of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi. The system includes a 204-kilometre feeder canal and a 445-kilometre main canal, with thousands of kilometres of distributaries.

Impact

The canal has brought over a million hectares of desert under cultivation, enabling crops of cotton, groundnut, wheat, and mustard in a region once considered wasteland, and supplies drinking water to millions of people and livestock. It has also raised concerns about waterlogging and soil salinity in command areas, displacement of desert ecosystems and pastoral livelihoods, and the dependency of the region on inter-state water sharing under the Indus Waters Treaty framework.
