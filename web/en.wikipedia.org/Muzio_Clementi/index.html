Muzio Clementi

Muzio Filippo Vincenzo Francesco Saverio Clementi (23 January 1752 – 10 March 1832) was an Italian-born British composer, virtuoso pianist, pedagogue, conductor, music publisher, editor, and piano manufacturer, who was mostly active in England. He is best known for his piano sonatinas and his collection of piano studies, Gradus ad Parnassum.

Born: 23 January 1752, Rome, Papal States

Died: 10 March 1832, Evesham, England

Occupations: Composer, pianist, publisher, piano maker

Life

Born in Rome, Clementi was sponsored as a boy by the English writer Peter Beckford, who brought him to England in 1766 to be educated at his estate in Dorset. Clementi made his London debut as a harpsichordist in 1775 and soon became one of the city's most sought-after keyboard performers and teachers. A celebrated musical contest with Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart in Vienna in 1781, before Emperor Joseph II, ended in a draw.

In later life Clementi turned increasingly to business: he founded a music publishing house and a piano factory in London, championed the works of Ludwig van Beethoven in England, and toured Europe selling his instruments. He died at Evesham in 1832 and was buried in Westminster Abbey.

Works and legacy

Clementi composed more than a hundred piano sonatas, which influenced Beethoven, as well as symphonies, chamber music, and songs. His three-volume Gradus ad Parnassum (1817–1826) remains a cornerstone of piano pedagogy, and his Introduction to the Art of Playing on the Piano Forte (1801) was a standard method book. His Op. 36 sonatinas are still played by piano students worldwide.
