Wang Shimin (1592–1680) was a Chinese landscape painter of the late Ming and early Qing dynasties. He is counted as one of the "Four Wangs," the dominant orthodox-school landscape painters of the early Qing, and was the grandfather of Wang Yuanqi and the teacher of Wang Hui — the other two members of the group besides Wang Jian.

Life and art

Wang came from a prominent official family of Taicang in Jiangsu; his grandfather Wang Xijue had been a Grand Secretary under the Wanli Emperor. As a young man Wang Shimin studied with the great painter and theorist Dong Qichang, who shaped his commitment to the literati tradition. He held a sinecure post under the Ming, and after the fall of the dynasty in 1644 retired to his estate to paint.

Style

Wang's landscapes deliberately rework the styles of Song and Yuan masters, above all Huang Gongwang, whose dry-brush, layered mountain forms he cultivated in album leaves and handscrolls of cool, detached refinement. Works such as his long handscrolls after Huang Gongwang exemplify the orthodox school's reverence for antiquity. Despite living to the age of 88, largely as a Ming loyalist recluse, his authority over the next two generations of court and professional painters was immense.
