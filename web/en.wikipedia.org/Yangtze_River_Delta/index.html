Yangtze River Delta

CountryChina
Core cityShanghai
Area~211,700 km² (economic region)
PopulationOver 150 million

The Yangtze River Delta is a triangle-shaped megalopolis in East China where the Yangtze River flows into the East China Sea. Centred on Shanghai, it encompasses southern Jiangsu, northern Zhejiang, and parts of Anhui, and is one of the most economically productive and densely urbanised regions in the world.

Geography

The delta is a low-lying alluvial plain formed by sediment carried by the Yangtze, laced with rivers, canals, and lakes such as Taihu. Historic water towns like Suzhou and Hangzhou grew along the Grand Canal and the region's silk and rice trades.

Economy

The delta region generates roughly a fifth to a quarter of China's GDP. It is a global centre for manufacturing, finance, shipping, and technology, anchored by Shanghai's port — among the busiest container ports in the world — and by dense high-speed rail links among its cities.

Integration

China's Yangtze River Delta integration strategy, elevated to a national plan in 2018, aims to coordinate infrastructure, industry, and environmental policy across Shanghai and the provinces of Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and Anhui.
