Louis the German

Louis the German (c. 806/810 – 28 August 876), also known as Louis II, was a grandson of Charlemagne and the first king of East Francia, which he ruled from 843 until his death. His long reign is often treated as the beginning of the separate history of the Kingdom of Germany.

Rise to kingship

Louis was the third son of Emperor Louis the Pious, who assigned him Bavaria in childhood. During the civil wars of the 830s Louis rebelled repeatedly against his father over his share of the empire. After Louis the Pious died in 840, Louis the German allied with his half-brother Charles the Bald against their elder brother Lothair I, defeating him at Fontenoy (841) and confirming their alliance in the Oaths of Strasbourg (842). The Treaty of Verdun (843) divided the Carolingian Empire into three, giving Louis the lands east of the Rhine.

Reign

As king of East Francia, Louis defended his eastern frontiers against Moravians and Slavs, intervened in West Frankish affairs, and attempted — unsuccessfully — to claim a share of Lotharingia after Lothair II's death in 869, obtaining only the eastern part by the Treaty of Meerssen (870). He faced rebellions by his own sons, notably Carloman, and campaigned in Italy on behalf of his nephew Louis II. He died at Frankfurt in 876 and was buried at Lorsch Abbey.

Legacy

Though his kingdom was divided among his three sons, his reign consolidated a distinct East Frankish polity. The nickname "the German" (Latin Germanicus) was applied to him by later historians to mark him as the founder-king of the German kingdom.
