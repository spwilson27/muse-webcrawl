Miniature wargaming

Miniature wargaming is a form of wargaming in which military units are represented by miniature physical models on a model battlefield. Players move figures representing soldiers, vehicles, and artillery across terrain, using rules and usually dice to resolve movement and combat.

History

The hobby traces to the Prussian staff game Kriegsspiel of the 19th century and to H. G. Wells's Little Wars (1913), the first published recreational ruleset. It expanded greatly after the Second World War, and fantasy settings were popularised by Games Workshop's Warhammer (1983) and Warhammer 40,000 (1987).

Scales and play

Miniatures are made at scales such as 6 mm, 15 mm, and 28 mm. The hobby combines collecting, model-making, and painting with gameplay. Games range from mass-battle systems with hundreds of figures to skirmish games with a handful, and cover historical, science-fiction, and fantasy settings. Players typically measure movement with rulers and use dice rolls modified by unit statistics to resolve shooting and melee.
