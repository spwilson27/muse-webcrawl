PublisherNintendo

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is a 1998 action-adventure game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo 64. It is the first game in The Legend of Zelda series rendered in 3D graphics, and is frequently cited as one of the greatest video games of all time.

Gameplay

The player controls Link in the kingdom of Hyrule, exploring an open overworld and completing dungeons. Ocarina of Time introduced Z-targeting, a lock-on targeting system that became standard in 3D action games, as well as context-sensitive buttons. Central to the game is the Ocarina of Time, a magical instrument whose melodies — learned throughout the adventure — warp Link through space, change the weather and open sealed doors.

Plot

Link, a young boy of the Kokiri forest, is tasked with stopping the Gerudo king Ganondorf from seizing the Triforce, an omnipotent relic. Drawing the Master Sword from its pedestal, Link is sealed away for seven years and awakens as an adult in a Hyrule conquered by Ganondorf. Travelling back and forth between his childhood and adulthood, Link awakens the Seven Sages, defeats Ganondorf in his demonic Ganon form, and Princess Zelda sends him back to relive his lost childhood, splitting the series timeline.

Development and music

Development spanned roughly three years with a large team working in parallel on the world, dungeons and the game's many systems. Composer Koji Kondo's score made the ocarina itself a musical instrument the player performs on, with melodies such as "Zelda's Lullaby" and the "Minuet of Forest" integral to progress.

Reception and legacy

The game was a critical and commercial triumph, selling over seven million copies on the Nintendo 64. It holds a near-perfect aggregate score on Metacritic and has been ranked first on numerous "greatest games" lists. A remake, Ocarina of Time 3D, was released for the Nintendo 3DS in 2011; an expanded randomizer and fan projects, and the 2022 decompilation project Ship of Harkinian, have kept it in active play.
