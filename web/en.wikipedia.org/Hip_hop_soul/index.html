Hip hop soul

Hip hop soul is a subgenre of contemporary R&B that fuses R&B and gospel-style singing with hip hop production and beats. The genre emerged in the early 1990s as producers began combining the rhythms of hip hop with soulful vocal performances, and it became one of the dominant sounds of the decade.

History

The sound was pioneered by producer and executive Sean Combs and popularised by Mary J. Blige, whose 1992 debut album What's the 411? earned her the title "Queen of Hip Hop Soul". Producers such as Teddy Riley, Jermaine Dupri, and Dave "Jam" Hall shaped a style built on sampled loops, hard drum programming, and melismatic vocals. Artists including Jodeci, Boyz II Men, TLC, Aaliyah, and Faith Evans carried the genre through the mid-1990s, and it later fed into the neo soul movement and the mainstream R&B of the 2000s.

Characteristics

Hip hop soul tracks typically pair sung verses and choruses with hip hop drum patterns, sometimes interpolating rap verses. Lyrically the genre favoured streetwise realism over the polished romance of earlier R&B, an approach that influenced the genre's fashion and image as well as its sound.
