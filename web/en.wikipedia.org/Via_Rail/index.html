Via Rail Canada Inc. (reporting mark VIA) (/ˈviːə/), operating as Via Rail or Via, is a Canadian Crown corporation that is mandated to operate intercity passenger rail service in Canada. It receives operating subsidies from the Government of Canada to support the public service obligation of providing rail transportation to citizens.

Via Rail was created in 1977, initially as a subsidiary of Canadian National Railway (CN), and became an independent Crown corporation in 1978, taking over most passenger services from CN and Canadian Pacific Railway. Cutbacks in 1981 and especially 1990 sharply reduced the network, but the core services survive: frequent trains in the Quebec City–Windsor Corridor, where most ridership is concentrated, and long-distance trains such as The Canadian between Toronto and Vancouver and The Ocean between Montreal and Halifax, as well as regional services to remote communities.

Services

The Corridor network links Quebec City, Montreal, Ottawa, Toronto, London, and Windsor. Long-distance trains include The Canadian, the flagship transcontinental train using stainless-steel Budd cars from the 1950s, and The Ocean, in operation since 1904. Other routes serve northern Manitoba (to Churchill), northern Ontario, the Gaspé Peninsula (suspended), and British Columbia's Skeena route to Prince Rupert. The government has studied high-frequency rail in the Corridor for years; in the 2020s, procurement advanced for a dedicated high-frequency/high-speed project between Toronto and Quebec City ("Alto").

Fleet

Via's fleet has included LRC (Light, Rapid, Comfortable) cars, HEP stainless-steel cars inherited from CP, Renaissance cars purchased from the United Kingdom, and F40PH and P42DC locomotives. Beginning in 2022, new Siemens Charger locomotives and Venture trainsets entered Corridor service, replacing the oldest equipment.

Challenges

Nearly all Via trains run on track owned by freight railways, principally CN, so freight priority often causes delays. Successive governments have debated the corporation's funding, structure, and the absence of enabling legislation defining its mandate, unlike Amtrak in the United States.
