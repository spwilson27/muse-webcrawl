Emperor Jimmu

Reign11 February 660 BC – 9 April 585 BC (traditional)
PredecessorNone (dynasty founder)
SuccessorEmperor Suizei
Posthumous nameJimmu Tennō

Emperor Jimmu (Japanese: 神武天皇, Jimmu Tennō) was the legendary first emperor of Japan according to the Nihon Shoki and Kojiki, the early chronicles compiled in the 8th century AD. His accession is traditionally dated to 11 February 660 BC, a date commemorated in modern Japan as National Foundation Day (Kenkoku Kinen no Hi). Jimmu is said to have been a descendant of the sun goddess Amaterasu through her grandson Ninigi.

Emperor Jimmu on his legendary eastern expedition

The eastern expedition

According to the chronicles, Jimmu — born Kamuyamato Iwarebiko — set out from Hyūga in southern Kyushu with his brothers to conquer the Yamato plain in central Honshu. After an initial defeat in which his elder brother died, Jimmu regrouped, travelled around the Kii Peninsula, and — guided by the three-legged crow Yatagarasu sent by the god Takemikazuchi — defeated the chieftain Nagasunehiko and established his capital at Kashihara in Yamato. There he was enthroned as Japan's first emperor.

Historicity

Modern historians regard Jimmu as a legendary figure; there is no contemporary evidence for his existence, and the early emperors in the chronicles are considered mythical. The chronology of the Nihon Shoki was constructed retrospectively, and the 660 BC date was derived from cosmological calculations. Nevertheless, the myth of Jimmu's founding of an unbroken imperial line became central to Japanese national ideology, especially during the Meiji period and the era of State Shinto.

Commemoration

Jimmu's supposed tomb (misasagi) is maintained by the Imperial Household Agency near Kashihara Shrine in Nara Prefecture, where he is enshrined as a kami. The year 1940 was celebrated as the 2600th anniversary of his accession. National Foundation Day, abolished after the Second World War, was reinstated in 1966 on the traditional accession date.
