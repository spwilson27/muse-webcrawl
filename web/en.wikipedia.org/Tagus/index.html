Tagus

The Tagus (Spanish: Tajo; Portuguese: Tejo) is the longest river in the Iberian Peninsula, at 1,007 km (626 mi). It rises in the Sierra de Albarracín of eastern Spain, flows westward through Toledo and across Portugal, and empties into the Atlantic Ocean at Lisbon.

Course

From its source near Frías de Albarracín, the Tajo cuts deep gorges through central Spain, passes Aranjuez and the historic clifftop city of Toledo, and is impounded by large reservoirs including Entrepeñas and Alcántara. Entering Portugal, it widens at the Lezíria floodplain and forms a broad estuary — the Mar da Palha — before passing under Lisbon's 25 de Abril and Vasco da Gama bridges to the sea.

History and economy

The river was famous in antiquity for gold-bearing sands and has defined settlement in Iberia for millennia. Today it supplies irrigation, hydroelectric power and drinking water; the Tagus–Segura water transfer diverts water to Spain's arid southeast, a long-standing source of political controversy. The estuary's nature reserve protects important wetland bird habitats.

Bridges

Notable crossings include Toledo's medieval Alcántara and San Martín bridges, the iron bridge at Fão-era engineering sites, and at Lisbon two of Europe's longest bridges — Vasco da Gama (17.2 km) and the 25 de Abril suspension bridge.
