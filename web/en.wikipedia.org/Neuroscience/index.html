Neuroscience

Neuroscience is the scientific study of the nervous system, its structures, function, development, genetics, biochemistry, physiology, pharmacology and pathology. Traditionally considered a branch of biology, it is now an interdisciplinary field drawing on psychology, computer science, statistics, physics, chemistry, medicine and philosophy. The scope of neuroscience has broadened to include molecular, cellular, developmental, structural, functional, evolutionary, computational and medical aspects of the nervous system.

Branches

Major branches include molecular and cellular neuroscience, studying neurons and their molecules; systems neuroscience, describing how neural circuits analyse sensory information and control behaviour; cognitive neuroscience, addressing the neural mechanisms of perception, memory, language and consciousness; behavioural neuroscience; developmental neuroscience; computational neuroscience, which models neural systems mathematically; and clinical neuroscience, concerned with disorders of the nervous system.

History

The study of the nervous system dates to ancient Egypt and Greece, but modern neuroscience began in the late 19th century with the neuron doctrine of Santiago Ramón y Cajal, who showed the nervous system is made of discrete cells, and the anatomical work of Camillo Golgi, with whom he shared the 1906 Nobel Prize. In the 20th century, Hodgkin and Huxley's description of the action potential, the discovery of chemical neurotransmission, and Hebb's theory of synaptic plasticity laid foundational principles. From the 1990s, neuroimaging techniques such as PET and fMRI transformed the study of the living human brain, and large-scale initiatives such as the US BRAIN Initiative (2013) have sought to map neural circuits comprehensively.

Methods

Neuroscientists use techniques including electrophysiology (single-unit recording, EEG), optogenetics, calcium imaging, patch-clamp recording, electron microscopy, genetic manipulation of model organisms such as Drosophila, zebrafish and mice, and computational modelling. The Society for Neuroscience, founded in 1969, is the field's largest professional organisation, with tens of thousands of members.
