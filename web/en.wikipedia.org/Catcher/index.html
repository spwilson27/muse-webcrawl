Catcher

Catcher is a position in baseball and softball. The catcher crouches behind home plate, in front of the umpire, and receives the ball from the pitcher. Catchers are fielders who take part in every pitch of the game, and they are involved in more plays than any player other than the pitcher. The position is designated as number 2 for scoring purposes.

Role and responsibilities

The catcher is a key defensive player. Working with the pitcher, the catcher calls pitches by giving signals, blocks balls thrown in the dirt, catches foul tips and pop-ups, and attempts to throw out baserunners who try to steal bases. Because the catcher must handle powerful pitches and is exposed to foul balls and swinging bats, the position requires protective equipment, including a mask, chest protector, shin guards, and a specialized mitt.

Skills

Catchers need quick reflexes, a strong and accurate throwing arm, and the ability to receive and "frame" pitches so that borderline pitches appear to be strikes. Many catchers are also valued for their game-calling ability and leadership, and are sometimes described as "field generals" because they face the entire field and can direct their teammates.

History

In the early days of baseball, catchers stood far behind the batter and caught the ball on the bounce. Over the nineteenth century the position moved closer to the plate, and protective gear such as the mask (introduced in the 1870s) and the padded glove allowed catchers to take their modern position directly behind home plate.
