The Premier of the People's Republic of China, officially the Premier of the State Council of the People's Republic of China, is the head of government of China and the leader of the State Council. The premier is one of the top posts of the Chinese political system and is usually considered the second- or third-most powerful figure in the leadership hierarchy of the Chinese Communist Party.

Role and selection

The premier heads the State Council, China's chief administrative authority, and directs the work of ministries and commissions, with particular responsibility for the economy and domestic administration. The premier is nominated by the president and confirmed by the National People's Congress, serving a five-year term renewable once consecutively.

History and holders

Zhou Enlai served as the first premier from the founding of the People's Republic in 1949 until his death in 1976. Later premiers include Hua Guofeng, Zhao Ziyang, Li Peng, Zhu Rongji, Wen Jiabao, and Li Keqiang. Since March 2023, the premier has been Li Qiang.
