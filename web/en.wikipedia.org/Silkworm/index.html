Silkworm

The silkworm is the larva of the domestic silk moth Bombyx mori, an insect of enormous economic importance as the producer of silk. Domesticated in China over 5,000 years ago from the wild silk moth Bombyx mandarina, it is now entirely dependent on human care and cannot survive in the wild.

Life cycle

Female moths lay several hundred eggs. The larvae feed voraciously on mulberry leaves for about a month, moulting four times. They then spin a cocoon of a single continuous silk thread up to about a kilometre long; inside, the larva pupates. In sericulture, most pupae are killed with heat so the thread can be unwound intact, while some are allowed to emerge as moths for breeding.

Sericulture

Silk farming spread from China along the Silk Road to Korea, Japan, India, and eventually Europe. China and India remain the largest producers. Silkworms are also a model organism in genetics research and are raised as food for reptiles and, in some cuisines, for people.
