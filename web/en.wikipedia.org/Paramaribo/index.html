Paramaribo

Paramaribo (Dutch pronunciation: [ˌpaːraːˈmaːriboː], nicknamed Par'bo) is the capital and largest city of Suriname, located on the banks of the Suriname River in the Paramaribo District. Its population was about 240,000 at the 2012 census — roughly half of Suriname's total population. The historic inner city has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2002, noted for its distinctive wooden Dutch colonial architecture.

History

The name Paramaribo is probably derived from a native village called Parmurbo. The Dutch established a trading post in 1613; the English under Lord Francis Willoughby founded a colony in 1650, and in 1667 Suriname was ceded to the Netherlands under the Treaty of Breda. Paramaribo developed as the port for sugar, coffee and cotton plantations worked by enslaved Africans, and after emancipation in 1863 by indentured labourers from India, Java and China — the origin of the city's remarkable ethnic diversity. Suriname became independent from the Netherlands on 25 November 1975. In the 1980s the city saw the turbulence of the military regime of Dési Bouterse, including the December murders of 1982.

Geography and climate

Paramaribo lies on the Guiana Shield's coastal plain about 15 km inland from the Atlantic Ocean, on the left bank of the Suriname River. It has a tropical rainforest climate with a long wet season; temperatures hover around 30 °C year-round. Much of the city is barely above sea level and is protected by dikes and drainage canals.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the white wooden Saint Peter and Paul Cathedral — among the largest wooden structures in the Americas — the 17th-century Fort Zeelandia, the Presidential Palace on Onafhankelijkheidsplein (Independence Square), the Neveh Shalom Synagogue standing beside the Keizerstraat Mosque, the Palmentuin palm garden, and the Jules Wijdenbosch Bridge (opened 2000) across the Suriname River to Meerzorg.

Demographics and culture

The city's population includes Creoles, Hindustani (East Indians), Javanese, Maroons, Chinese, Europeans, Lebanese and Indigenous peoples. Dutch is the official language, while Sranan Tongo serves as lingua franca; Hindi, Javanese, Sarnami and other languages are widely spoken. Places of worship of nearly every world religion stand within a few streets of one another.
