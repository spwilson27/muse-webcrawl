Queso Oaxaca

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Queso Oaxaca, also known as quesillo, is a white, semi-hard Mexican cheese made from cow's milk, originating in the state of Oaxaca. It belongs to the pasta filata family of stretched-curd cheeses, like Italian mozzarella: the curd is stretched and kneaded in hot water, then pulled into long ribbons that are rolled up into a ball resembling a ball of yarn.

Production and use

According to tradition, quesillo was developed in the municipality of Reyes Etla by Dominican monks or, in a popular tale, by a young girl who accidentally over-heated the curd. The cheese has a mild, slightly salty, buttery flavour and melts exceptionally well; it is a defining ingredient of tlayudas, quesadillas (in Oaxaca, folded with squash blossoms or huitlacoche), and is eaten pulled apart as a snack, often with chapulines or salsa.

Recognition

Oaxaca cheese is one of Mexico's most popular cheeses and is produced throughout the country; in 2020 it ranked among the world's best cheeses in international taste rankings, drawing attention to the original quesillo of Oaxaca, whose producers seek denominación de origen protection.
