Anglin brothers

The Anglin brothers, John William Anglin (May 2, 1930 – disappeared June 11, 1962) and Clarence Anglin (May 11, 1931 – disappeared June 11, 1962), were American bank robbers who are best known as two of the three men who escaped from Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary on June 11, 1962, together with Frank Morris. In one of the most famous prison breaks in American history, the trio left dummy heads in their bunks, slipped through widened ventilation holes into a utility corridor, and vanished into San Francisco Bay on an improvised raft. Whether they survived has never been conclusively established: the FBI closed its case in 1979 concluding they probably drowned, while the U.S. Marshals Service has kept the case open.

Early life and crimes

John and Clarence Anglin were born into a family of fourteen children of seasonal farm workers in Georgia and grew up in Florida, becoming unusually strong swimmers in the cold waters of Lake Michigan during family picking trips north. After a string of burglaries, the brothers were convicted of robbing a bank in Columbia, Alabama, in 1958 — reportedly using a toy gun — and received long federal sentences. Repeated escape attempts from Leavenworth and Atlanta led to their transfer to Alcatraz, Clarence arriving in January 1961 and John in the same period.

The 1962 escape

At Alcatraz the brothers joined Frank Morris and Allen West in an elaborate plot. Over months they widened the ventilation holes in their cell walls with improvised tools, fashioned papier-mâché dummy heads with real hair to fool night counts, and assembled a raft and life vests from more than fifty stolen raincoats. On the night of June 11, 1962, Morris and the Anglins climbed the utility corridor to the roof, descended to the shore and launched the raft; West failed to get out of his cell in time. The men were never seen again. A paddle and personal effects were later recovered in the bay, and in 1963 a body in prison-like clothing found near the Golden Gate could not be identified.

Theories and investigation

The official view — that the raft failed and the men drowned in the cold, current-swept bay — has been challenged by the Anglin family, who produced photographs and Christmas cards they believed showed the brothers alive in Brazil in the 1970s, and by a 2015 television investigation presenting a photograph allegedly of the brothers in Brazil in 1975. A letter purporting to be from John Anglin, received by San Francisco police in 2013 and publicised in 2018, claimed all three survived but has not been authenticated. The U.S. Marshals' warrants for the men remain technically active until their 100th birthdays.

In popular culture

The escape was dramatised in the 1979 film Escape from Alcatraz, starring Clint Eastwood as Morris, with Fred Ward and Jack Thibeau as John and Clarence Anglin.
