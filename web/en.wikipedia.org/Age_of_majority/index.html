Age of majority

The age of majority is the threshold of legal adulthood as recognised or declared in law. It is the moment when a person ceases to be considered a minor and assumes legal control over their persons, actions, and decisions, terminating the control and legal responsibilities of their parents or guardians. Most countries set the age of majority at 18, though it ranges from as low as 15 to as high as 21.

Legal consequences

Reaching the age of majority typically confers the ability to enter binding contracts, marry without parental consent, vote (where the voting age coincides), make a will, consent to medical treatment, and be tried as an adult. Some rights are tied to separate ages: legal drinking ages, driving ages, ages of consent, and ages of candidacy frequently differ from the age of majority.

History

In Roman law, majority was reached at 25, with younger adults protected by the lex Laetoria. Medieval England set majority at 21 for men holding knight's fees. The United Kingdom reduced the age from 21 to 18 by the Family Law Reform Act 1969. In the United States, the age fell from 21 to 18 in most states during the Vietnam War era — driven by the argument that those old enough to be conscripted should be adults in law — alongside the Twenty-sixth Amendment (1971), which set the voting age at 18.

Variations

A few jurisdictions retain 21 (for example, Singapore and several US states for specific purposes such as alcohol purchase), while others use 19 or 20. Some religious traditions mark adulthood earlier through ceremonies such as the bar and bat mitzvah at 13, though these carry no civil legal effect. Minors may also attain adult status early through emancipation, marriage, or military service.
