Nosocomial infection

Other namesHealthcare-associated infection (HAI), hospital-acquired infection
Common sitesUrinary tract, surgical wounds, lungs, bloodstream
Common pathogensStaphylococcus aureus (incl. MRSA), E. coli, Pseudomonas, Clostridioides difficile
PreventionHand hygiene, aseptic technique, device care bundles

A nosocomial infection, also called a healthcare-associated infection (HAI), is an infection that a patient acquires during the course of receiving treatment in a hospital or other healthcare facility, and that was not present or incubating at the time of admission. The term derives from the Greek nosokomeion, meaning hospital.

Types and causes

The most frequent types are urinary tract infections associated with catheters, surgical site infections, ventilator-associated pneumonia, and central line–associated bloodstream infections. Causative organisms are often bacteria that thrive in healthcare environments and may be resistant to multiple antibiotics, including methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), vancomycin-resistant enterococci, and carbapenem-resistant Gram-negative bacilli. Risk factors include invasive devices, surgery, immunosuppression, and prolonged stays.

Prevention and impact

HAIs affect millions of patients worldwide each year and add substantially to morbidity, mortality, and healthcare costs. Prevention rests on hand hygiene, sterile technique, environmental cleaning, antimicrobial stewardship, surveillance, and care "bundles" for devices. Organisations such as the WHO and the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention publish guidelines and track infection rates as hospital quality indicators.
