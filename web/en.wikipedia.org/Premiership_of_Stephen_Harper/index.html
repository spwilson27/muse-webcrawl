Premiership of Stephen Harper

Stephen Harper
Prime Minister of Canada: 6 February 2006 – 4 November 2015
Party: Conservative Party of Canada
Predecessor: Paul Martin
Successor: Justin Trudeau

Harper led Conservative governments for nearly a decade, 2006–2015.

The premiership of Stephen Harper spanned from 6 February 2006 to 4 November 2015, during which Stephen Harper served as the 22nd prime minister of Canada. Harper led the Conservative Party to two minority governments (2006 and 2008) and a majority government in 2011, governing nearly ten years.

Policies and events

His government cut the Goods and Services Tax from 7% to 5%, introduced income splitting for families with children (later a family tax cut), passed the Federal Accountability Act, and formally apologised in 2008 for the residential school system. Canada weathered the 2008 financial crisis with no bank failures, aided by conservative banking regulation, though deficits returned. Abroad, Harper extended Canada's combat mission in Afghanistan to 2011, strongly supported Israel, withdrew Canada from the Kyoto Protocol in 2011, and pursued trade deals including the Canada–EU agreement (CETA) and the Trans-Pacific Partnership. Controversies included the 2008 prorogation crisis, the Senate expenses scandal, and criticism of environmental policy and the "muzzling" of government scientists. The Conservatives lost the 2015 election to Justin Trudeau's Liberals, and Harper resigned as party leader.
