Régine Chassagne (born 19 August 1976) is a Canadian singer and multi-instrumentalist, best known as a founding member of the indie rock band Arcade Fire, alongside her husband Win Butler.

Early life

Chassagne was born in Montreal, Quebec, to Haitian immigrant parents who had fled the regime of François "Papa Doc" Duvalier. She grew up in Saint-Lambert, on the south shore of Montreal. She studied jazz voice at McGill University, graduating with a degree in communication studies, and sang in a medieval ensemble and with an Afro-Cuban group while at university.

Arcade Fire

Chassagne met Win Butler after he saw her performing at an art exhibition at Concordia University, and the two formed Arcade Fire in Montreal in the early 2000s. Known for her energetic stage presence and her playing of instruments including the accordion, hurdy-gurdy, keyboards, xylophone and drums, she sings lead vocals on songs such as "Haiti", "In the Backseat", "Sprawl II (Mountains Beyond Mountains)" and "Reflektor" (with Butler). The band's albums include Funeral (2004), Neon Bible (2007), The Suburbs (2010), which won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year, Reflektor (2013) and Everything Now (2017).

Haiti and philanthropy

Chassagne's Haitian heritage has been a recurring influence on Arcade Fire's music, notably the song "Haiti" on Funeral, written about her family's history. She has been active in raising awareness and funds for Haiti, co-founding the charity Kanpe, which works with communities in Haiti's Central Plateau on health, agriculture, housing and financial independence, and has collaborated with organisations such as Partners in Health.
