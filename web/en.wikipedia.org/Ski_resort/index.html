A ski resort is a resort developed for skiing, snowboarding, and other winter sports. In Europe and America, most ski resorts are towns or villages in or near mountains, with the ski area developed around them. Ski areas are marked by pistes (trails) of graded difficulty served by ski lifts — chairlifts, gondolas, cable cars, and surface lifts — and supported by snowmaking, grooming, ski patrol, lodging, and après-ski amenities.

History

Downhill skiing as recreation developed in the Alps and Scandinavia in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, aided by mountain railways and the first mechanical lifts in the 1930s. Purpose-built resorts expanded rapidly after World War II — Sun Valley in Idaho (opened 1936) pioneered the destination resort model in North America, while large interlinked areas grew in France, Austria, and Switzerland. The Winter Olympics repeatedly showcased resort towns such as St. Moritz, Innsbruck, and Cortina d'Ampezzo.

Operations

Resorts sell lift passes, operate ski schools and rental shops, and groom pistes nightly with snowcats. Snowmaking supplements natural snowfall and extends seasons. Many resorts have diversified into year-round tourism with mountain biking, hiking, and festivals in summer. Large corporate operators such as Vail Resorts and Alterra Mountain Company run multi-resort passes (Epic Pass and Ikon Pass).

Environmental and economic issues

Ski resorts are important employers in mountain regions but face concerns over water use for snowmaking, habitat fragmentation, and real-estate development. Climate change is shortening seasons and raising snow lines, pushing resorts toward higher elevations, more snowmaking, and summer diversification.
