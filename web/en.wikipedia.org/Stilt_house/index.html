Stilt house

Stilt houses (also called pile dwellings or lake dwellings) are houses raised above the ground or water on wooden or concrete piles called stilts. They are built primarily to protect against flooding, but the elevation can also guard against pests and vermin, increase ventilation in hot climates, and provide storage space beneath the living area.

Distribution

Stilt houses are found across Southeast Asia, Oceania, West Africa, and parts of the Americas. The Malay and Indonesian rumah panggung, the floating and stilted villages of Cambodia's Tonlé Sap lake, the palafitos of Venezuela (which gave the country its name, "little Venice"), and raised cottages in flood-prone areas of the southern United States are all examples. In the Arctic, homes are raised on pilings to prevent the melting of permafrost.

History

Pile dwellings were widespread in Neolithic and Bronze Age Europe around the Alpine lakes; the Prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, comprising 111 sites in six countries. Their waterlogged remains preserve wood, textiles, and food remains exceptionally well.

Modern use

Contemporary architects adapt the stilt principle for flood-resilient housing in coastal regions, and building codes in hurricane- and typhoon-prone areas often require new homes to be elevated on piers above base flood elevation.
