Cyrene was an ancient Greek city in present-day Libya, the oldest and most important of the five Greek cities in the region (the Pentapolis), which gave eastern Libya its classical name, Cyrenaica. Its ruins near the modern village of Shahhat are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

According to tradition, Cyrene was founded around 630 BC by colonists from the island of Thera led by Battus, whose dynasty ruled for eight generations. The city grew wealthy exporting grain, wool, and the medicinal plant silphium, which became its emblem. Under Ptolemaic and then Roman rule it remained a major centre; the philosopher Aristippus founded the Cyrenaic school of philosophy there, and the mathematician Eratosthenes was a native. Cyrene declined after earthquakes and raids in late antiquity.

Archaeology

Extensive remains include the Temple of Zeus, the sanctuary of Apollo, a Greek theatre later converted to a Roman amphitheatre, temples, baths, and a large necropolis.
