Blower door

A blower door is a machine used to measure the airtightness of buildings. It consists of a powerful variable-speed fan mounted in a frame that temporarily seals an exterior doorway, together with a pressure gauge (manometer). By pressurising or depressurising the building to a standard pressure (typically 50 pascals), the test quantifies air leakage.

Use

Results are commonly expressed as air changes per hour at 50 Pa (ACH50) or as leakage area. Blower door tests are required by many energy codes and green-building programmes, and are used in energy audits to find leaks around windows, penetrations and junctions, often combined with infrared thermography or a smoke pencil to locate leak sites.

History

Early door-fan tests were developed in Sweden in the 1970s; Harold Orr and others in Canada and the United States developed the modern calibrated blower door, commercialised by companies such as the Minneapolis Blower Door and Retrotec.
