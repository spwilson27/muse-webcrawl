Expo Line (TransLink)

The Expo Line is the oldest line of the SkyTrain rapid transit system in Metro Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. Operated by TransLink's subsidiary BC Rapid Transit Company, it runs from Waterfront station in downtown Vancouver through Burnaby and New Westminster, branching to King George station in Surrey and to Production Way–University station in Burnaby.

History

The line opened in stages beginning in December 1985, built to showcase driverless train technology during Expo 86, Vancouver's world exposition — the source of its later name. It was originally simply called the SkyTrain; when the Millennium Line opened in 2002, the original route was renamed the Expo Line. Extensions carried the line across the Fraser River into Surrey in the early 1990s, and the Evergreen Extension, opened in 2016, was initially operated as part of the Expo Line before being reassigned to the Millennium Line. A further extension from King George to Langley City is under construction.

Route and operations

Trains are fully automated, using Bombardier's Innovia Metro technology with linear induction motors, and run on mostly elevated guideway with underground sections downtown and in New Westminster. The line shares its downtown segment with the later Millennium Line services historically and connects with the Canada Line and West Coast Express at Waterfront. Stations such as Commercial–Broadway and Metrotown are among the busiest in the system.
