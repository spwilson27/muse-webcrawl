Acanthus (plant)

Acanthus is a genus of about 30 species of flowering plants in the family Acanthaceae, native to tropical and warm temperate regions, with the highest species diversity in the Mediterranean Basin and Asia. This flowering plant is commonly known as bear's breeches, sea dock, bear's foot plant, sea holly, and oyster plant.

The genus name derives from the Greek word ákanthos (ἄκανθος), meaning "thorny", referring to the spiny leaves of some species. Acanthus leaves were the model for the ornament on Corinthian capitals in classical Greek and Roman architecture, one of the most enduring motifs in Western decorative art.

Description

Acanthus species are herbaceous perennial plants or subshrubs with large, glossy, lobed or spiny leaves, often forming bold architectural clumps. The flowers are borne on tall erect spikes and have a single prominent bract that is often spiny and coloured white, pink or purple, with two-lipped tubular petals.

Cultivation

Several species are grown as ornamental garden plants, notably Acanthus mollis (bear's breeches), with large glossy evergreen leaves, and Acanthus spinosus, the spiny bear's breeches. They are valued for shade tolerance and bold foliage but can spread vigorously in favourable climates.
