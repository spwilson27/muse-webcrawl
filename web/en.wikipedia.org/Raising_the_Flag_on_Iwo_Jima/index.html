Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima

Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima
Photograph by Joe Rosenthal, 23 February 1945
Won the Pulitzer Prize for Photography

Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima is a photograph of six United States Marine Corps personnel raising the U.S. flag atop Mount Suribachi during the Battle of Iwo Jima in the final stages of the Pacific War. Taken by Joe Rosenthal of the Associated Press on February 23, 1945, the photograph was published in Sunday newspapers across the United States and became one of the most reproduced images of the war.

Background

The pictured raising was actually the second flag-raising on Suribachi that day: a smaller flag planted earlier was replaced by a larger battle ensign. Rosenthal's frame captured the replacement. Three of the six flag-raisers were killed in action later in the battle. The identities were investigated repeatedly, and corrections were announced by the Marine Corps in 2016 and 2019 based on photographic evidence.

Legacy

The image anchored the Seventh War Loan drive, won the 1945 Pulitzer Prize for Photography, and served as the model for the Marine Corps War Memorial in Arlington, Virginia (dedicated 1954). It inspired the films Sands of Iwo Jima (1949) and Clint Eastwood's Flags of Our Fathers (2006).
