Human

Humans (Homo sapiens, Latin for "wise man") are the most common and widespread species of primate, and the last surviving species of the genus Homo. They are great apes characterized by bipedalism, opposable thumbs, and a large, complex brain that enables advanced cognitive skills, language, and the creation of complex societies and technologies. The human population surpassed 8 billion in November 2022.

Evolution and origins

Humans evolved in Africa. The lineage leading to Homo diverged from that of chimpanzees roughly 5–7 million years ago; early hominins such as Australopithecus walked upright while retaining small brains. The genus Homo appeared by about 2.3–2.8 million years ago, and anatomically modern humans are documented at Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, from about 300,000 years ago. Beginning around 70,000–60,000 years ago, humans spread out of Africa across Eurasia, Oceania and eventually the Americas, interbreeding to a limited degree with archaic relatives such as Neanderthals and Denisovans.

Anatomy and biology

Humans are obligate bipeds: the skeleton is adapted for upright walking, with an S-shaped spine, a short broad pelvis, and arched feet. Adult height averages about 171 cm for men and 159 cm for women globally, with wide variation. The human brain, averaging about 1,350 cm³, is large relative to body size, especially the cerebral cortex. Humans have a long lifespan (global life expectancy around 73 years), an extended childhood, and relatively hairless skin with abundant sweat glands for thermoregulation.

Mind and behavior

Humans possess language with open-ended syntax and semantics, abstract reasoning, self-awareness and theory of mind. These capacities underpin cumulative culture: knowledge, technology, art, religion, law and institutions transmitted across generations. Humans are highly social, living in groups ranging from families to nation-states of hundreds of millions.

Civilization and impact

From about 10,000 BC, the development of agriculture led to settled societies, cities, states and writing. Industrialization from the 18th century transformed energy use, population growth and the environment. Humans now dominate most terrestrial ecosystems, and their activities drive climate change and an elevated rate of species extinction, leading many scientists to call the present era the Anthropocene. Humanity has also ventured beyond Earth: since 1961 humans have flown in space, and in 1969 landed on the Moon.

Variation

Genetic variation among humans is small — any two individuals differ at roughly 0.1% of their DNA — and most variation occurs within, rather than between, geographic populations. Visible traits such as skin color, adapted to ultraviolet radiation levels, vary clinally across the globe.
