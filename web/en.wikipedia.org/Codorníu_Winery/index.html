Codorníu Winery

Codorníu is one of Spain's oldest family-owned wine companies, founded in 1551 in Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, Catalonia. It is credited with creating cava, the Spanish sparkling wine made by the traditional method, in 1872, when Josep Raventós brought techniques learned in Champagne back to the Penedès region.

History

The Codorníu family has grown vines since the 16th century. The marriage of Anna Codorníu to Miguel Raventós in 1659 united the two winemaking families, and Anna's name is honoured in one of the company's best-known cavas. Manuel Raventós led the firm through the phylloxera crisis and expanded its cellars dramatically.

The winery building

The Codorníu cellars at Sant Sadurní d'Anoia were designed by the modernista architect Josep Puig i Cadafalch and built between 1902 and 1915. They include some 30 kilometres of underground galleries and have been declared a national historic-artistic monument. The site is a major centre of wine tourism.
