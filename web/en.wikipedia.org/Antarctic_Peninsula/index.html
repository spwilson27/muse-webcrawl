Antarctic Peninsula

The Antarctic Peninsula, known as O'Higgins Land in Chile, Tierra de San Martín in Argentina and originally as Graham Land in the United Kingdom and the Palmer Peninsula in the United States, is the northernmost part of mainland Antarctica. It extends from a line between Cape Adams and a point south of Eklund Islands toward the South Pole, jutting some 1,300 km toward the southern tip of South America.

Geography and climate

The peninsula is highly mountainous, its peaks being considered continuations of the Andes. It has the mildest climate in Antarctica, with temperatures on the northern coast sometimes rising above freezing in summer, and supports tundra vegetation of mosses, lichens and grasses. The surrounding waters and islands host penguins, seals and whales.

Research and claims

The peninsula and its offshore islands host the highest concentration of research stations on the continent, operated by Argentina, Chile, the United Kingdom, the United States and many other Antarctic Treaty nations. Territorial claims by Argentina, Chile and the UK overlap here; all are held in abeyance under the Antarctic Treaty System.
