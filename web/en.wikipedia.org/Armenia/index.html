Armenia

Armenia, officially the Republic of Armenia, is a landlocked country in the Armenian Highlands of West Asia, in the South Caucasus region. It is bordered by Turkey to the west, Georgia to the north, Azerbaijan to the east, and Iran to the south. The capital and largest city is Yerevan. Armenia has a population of roughly 2.8 million and covers 29,743 square kilometres.

History

Armenia is one of the oldest centres of civilization in its region: the kingdom of Urartu flourished in the highlands in the 1st millennium BC, and a distinct Armenian kingdom emerged under the Orontid and Artaxiad dynasties. In AD 301 Armenia became the first state to adopt Christianity as its official religion. The Armenian alphabet was created by Mesrop Mashtots around AD 405. Over the centuries the country was ruled or contested by Persian, Byzantine, Arab, Mongol, Ottoman and Russian empires. Eastern Armenia joined the Russian Empire in the 19th century; after a brief independent republic in 1918–1920 it became a Soviet republic. During the First World War, Armenians in the Ottoman Empire suffered the Armenian genocide, in which around a million or more perished. Armenia declared independence from the Soviet Union on 21 September 1991.

Geography

The country is mountainous, with an average elevation of about 1,800 metres; its highest peak is Mount Aragats (4,090 m). Lake Sevan, one of Eurasia's largest high-altitude lakes, lies in the east. Mount Ararat, Armenia's national symbol, stands across the border in Turkey.

Politics and economy

Since constitutional reforms in 2015 Armenia is a parliamentary republic; the president is head of state and the prime minister head of government. The economy is driven by services, mining (copper, gold, molybdenum), agriculture and a growing information-technology sector, with a large and influential worldwide diaspora. Armenia's relations with Azerbaijan have been dominated by the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict; following Azerbaijan's 2023 offensive, the self-declared Republic of Artsakh was dissolved and nearly its entire Armenian population fled to Armenia.
