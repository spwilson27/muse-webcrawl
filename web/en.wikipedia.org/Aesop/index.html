Aesop

Aesop (c. 620 – 564 BCE) was a Greek fabulist and storyteller credited with a collection of fables now known as Aesop's Fables. According to tradition he was a slave living in ancient Greece whose brief, moral animal tales have influenced storytelling for over two millennia.

Life

Little is known reliably about Aesop's life. Herodotus mentions a slave named Aesop on Samos, and later tradition made him a clever, ugly slave who won his freedom through his wit. A fictional biography, the Aesop Romance, embellished his story in later centuries.

Fables

Famous fables attributed to him include "The Tortoise and the Hare", "The Boy Who Cried Wolf", "The Fox and the Grapes" (the origin of "sour grapes") and "The Ant and the Grasshopper". The fables were transmitted orally for centuries before being written down, notably in verse by Babrius and in prose collections.
