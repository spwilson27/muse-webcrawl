Weapon dance

A weapon dance is a folk dance performed with weapons — such as swords, sticks, staves, shields, or spears — either as stylized combat, as martial training, or as ceremonial display. Such dances are found in cultures across the world, from ancient times to the present.

Examples

Famous examples include the Greek pyrrhichios described by Plato and Xenophon; English morris dances with sticks and handkerchiefs and the rapper and longsword dances of northern England; the Scottish sword dance; the Māori haka and taiaha routines; the Chinese sword and spear dances; stick dances of the Middle East such as tahtib in Egypt; and the Yemeni dagger dance. Many incorporate mock battles, leaps over crossed blades, or feats of dexterity.

Functions

Historically, weapon dances served to train warriors, celebrate victories, honour ancestors or gods, and mark rites of passage. Today they are preserved mostly as folk performance and cultural heritage.
