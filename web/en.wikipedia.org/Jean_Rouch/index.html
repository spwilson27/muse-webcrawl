Jean Rouch (31 May 1917 – 18 February 2004) was a French filmmaker and anthropologist. He is regarded as a pioneer of visual anthropology and the father of Nigerien cinema, and his concept of cinéma vérité — elaborated in works such as Chronique d'un été (Chronicle of a Summer, 1961), co-directed with sociologist Edgar Morin — profoundly influenced the French New Wave and documentary practice worldwide.

Early life and Africa

Rouch trained as a civil engineer and was sent to Niger in 1941 to supervise road-building. There he became fascinated by Songhai culture and ritual, studied ethnology in Paris under Marcel Mauss and Marcel Griaule, and began filming with a hand-held 16mm camera. His early ethnographic films, shot among the Songhai and Dogon peoples, recorded possession rituals, hunts and migrations.

Ethnofiction and shared anthropology

Rouch developed "ethnofiction," in which his subjects improvised versions of their own lives: Moi, un noir (1958) follows young Nigerian migrants in Abidjan narrating their dreams; La Pyramide humaine (1961) and Jaguar (1967) further blurred documentary and fiction. He advocated "shared anthropology," screening films back to their subjects and treating the camera as a catalyst — his "ciné-transe" — rather than a neutral observer. His film Les Maîtres fous (1955), depicting the Hauka possession movement in the Gold Coast, remains both influential and controversial.

Cinéma vérité

Translating Dziga Vertov's kino-pravda, Rouch's cinéma vérité used lightweight synchronous-sound equipment to provoke rather than merely record reality. Chronique d'un été (1961) asked Parisians "Are you happy?" and won a prize at Cannes, shaping direct cinema and the work of directors such as Jean-Luc Godard.

Institutions and legacy

Rouch founded the Comité du film ethnographique at the Musée de l'Homme and taught generations of African filmmakers, including Oumarou Ganda and Safi Ndiaye. He made over 100 films during six decades of work in West Africa. He died in a car accident in Niger in 2004 while attending a film festival, and was buried there with honours.
