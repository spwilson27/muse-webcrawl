Cambridge University Press

Cambridge University Press (CUP) is the university press of the University of Cambridge. Granted letters patent by King Henry VIII in 1534, it is the world's oldest university press and the oldest continuously operating publisher. It is also the King's Printer. Since 2021 it has operated jointly with Cambridge Assessment as Cambridge University Press and Assessment.

History

The Press began printing in 1584 and has published continuously since. Early landmarks included the King James Bible (1611, printed under privilege) and works by John Milton, Isaac Newton, and later Bertrand Russell and Stephen Hawking, whose A Brief History of Time (1988) became a global bestseller. In the 19th century the Press printed for the British and Foreign Bible Society and expanded into educational publishing.

Publishing

CUP publishes more than 300 peer-reviewed academic journals and thousands of new book titles each year across the humanities, social sciences, science, technology, and medicine. It is a major publisher of English Language Teaching (ELT) materials, used by millions of learners worldwide, and of Bibles in the King James and other versions.

Organization

The Press is governed by the Press Syndicate, a committee of University of Cambridge academics. It operates offices in over 40 countries and employs several thousand people. In 2021 Cambridge University Press merged organizationally with Cambridge Assessment to form Cambridge University Press and Assessment.
