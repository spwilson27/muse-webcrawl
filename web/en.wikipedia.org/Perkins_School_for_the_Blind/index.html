Perkins School for the Blind

Established: 1829
Location: Watertown, Massachusetts, US
Type: Private school for blind and deafblind students
First director: Samuel Gridley Howe

Perkins School for the Blind is a private school in Watertown, Massachusetts, for students who are blind and deafblind. Founded in 1829, it is the oldest school for the blind in the United States and is famous as the school attended by Helen Keller and taught by Anne Sullivan.

History

The school was incorporated in 1829 as the New England Asylum for the Blind, inspired by the world's first school for the blind in Paris, and opened in Boston in 1832 under its first director, Samuel Gridley Howe. It was renamed for Thomas Handasyd Perkins, a wealthy Boston merchant who donated his mansion for its use and left it a major bequest. Howe made the school famous through his education of Laura Bridgman, a deafblind girl, using raised-print books produced by the school's own Perkins Brailler-era printing operation (Perkins used Boston line letter before standard braille prevailed).

In 1887 Howe's successor, Michael Anagnos, sent graduate Anne Sullivan to Alabama to teach the young Helen Keller, who later attended Perkins herself. The school moved to its present campus in Watertown in 1912, built around a distinctive tower that remains its symbol.

Programmes

Perkins today operates day and residential programmes from early childhood through secondary school, a deafblind programme, and international work through Perkins International, which supports education for blind children in dozens of countries. Its Howe Press produces the Perkins Brailler, the standard manual braille writer, first produced in 1951, and the school library maintains one of the largest collections of materials on blindness.
