Hungry ghost

Hungry ghost
Sanskrit: preta; Chinese: èguǐ (餓鬼); Japanese: gaki
Realm: One of the six realms of rebirth in Buddhism
Traits: Enormous belly, needle-thin neck, insatiable hunger and thirst

Hungry ghosts are beings in Buddhist, Hindu and related traditions who suffer from insatiable hunger and thirst as a result of greed, jealousy and miserliness in past lives. In Sanskrit they are called preta; the term is translated into Chinese as “hungry ghost”, which passed into English usage.

Depiction

Hungry ghosts are typically portrayed with huge distended bellies and throats as thin as needles, so that food and drink cannot satisfy them; in some texts food turns to fire or filth in their mouths. Their realm is one of the six realms of samsara, below the human realm. Rebirth there results from intense greed and attachment.

Observances

In Chinese Buddhism and folk religion, the seventh lunar month is Ghost Month, culminating in the Ghost Festival (Zhongyuan), when families offer food, incense and paper goods to ancestors and wandering hungry ghosts. The Japanese equivalent is Obon, and the Vietnamese Vu Lan. Ritual feeding of hungry ghosts is also dramatised in the story of the monk Mulian rescuing his mother, a classic tale of filial piety.

In Buddhist teaching

The figure of the hungry ghost is used as a metaphor for addiction and craving that can never be satisfied. Ceremonies such as the Tibetan chöd and the Japanese segaki ritual aim to feed and liberate these beings. In the Tibetan Book of the Dead, pretas appear among the forms consciousness may take between lives.
