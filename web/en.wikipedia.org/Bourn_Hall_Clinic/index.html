Bourn Hall Clinic

Bourn Hall Clinic is a fertility treatment center at Bourn, near Cambridge, England. It was founded in 1980 by the IVF pioneers Patrick Steptoe and Robert Edwards, together with embryologist Jean Purdy, and is widely described as the world's first IVF clinic.

History

Steptoe and Edwards had achieved the birth of Louise Brown, the first baby conceived by in vitro fertilisation, in 1978. They established the clinic at Bourn Hall, a Jacobean manor house, to continue their research and offer treatment, moving there after difficulties securing funding and premises elsewhere. Jean Purdy, who ran the laboratory, is now recognized as a co-founder whose role was long overlooked.

Activity

The clinic provides IVF, ICSI, egg and sperm donation, fertility preservation, and related services, and has contributed to the births of many thousands of babies. It remains part of a network of fertility clinics in the east of England. Robert Edwards received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2010 for the development of IVF.
