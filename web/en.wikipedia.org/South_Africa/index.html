South Africa

Republic of South Africa
Capitals: Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative), Bloemfontein (judicial)
Largest city: Johannesburg
Population: ~60 million
Official languages: 12, incl. Zulu, Xhosa, Afrikaans, English

South Africa, officially the Republic of South Africa, is the southernmost country in Africa. It is bounded by Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, and Eswatini, and it completely surrounds Lesotho. With coasts on the Atlantic and Indian Oceans, it is the continent's most industrialised and one of its largest economies.

History

The region has some of the oldest evidence of human habitation on Earth. Khoisan peoples lived there for millennia before Bantu-speaking peoples migrated into the east. Dutch settlers founded Cape Town in 1652; the British seized the Cape in 1806, prompting the Boer Great Trek inland and the founding of republics later fought over in the Anglo-Boer Wars. The Union of South Africa was formed in 1910.

From 1948 the ruling National Party instituted apartheid, a system of racial segregation and discrimination. Resistance, led by the African National Congress, and international pressure culminated in negotiations, the release of Nelson Mandela in 1990, and the first universal-suffrage elections in 1994, which made Mandela president.

Geography and society

The interior high plateau (Highveld) is ringed by the Great Escarpment, including the Drakensberg mountains; the Cape Floristic Region is a global biodiversity hotspot. South Africa is a multi-ethnic society with 12 official languages (South African Sign Language was added in 2023) and is often called the "Rainbow Nation". Its economy centres on mining (gold, platinum, diamonds), finance, manufacturing, and tourism, centred on Johannesburg, while unemployment and inequality remain severe challenges.
