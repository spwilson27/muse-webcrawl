Planctomycetota

Planctomycetota is a phylum of aquatic bacteria found in marine, freshwater and terrestrial environments. Members were first described in the 1920s, when they were mistaken for fungi (the name means "fungus-like wanderer"), and were recognised as bacteria only later.

Cell biology

Planctomycetes are notable among bacteria for their complex internal membrane organisation and unusual life cycles involving budding reproduction and stalked, sessile and free-swimming cell types. Some were long thought to possess membrane-bound compartments resembling a nucleus, though interpretation of their cell plan remains debated.

Anammox

Members of the group include the anammox bacteria, which carry out anaerobic ammonium oxidation inside a specialised organelle, the anammoxosome. Anammox is a major component of the marine nitrogen cycle and is exploited in wastewater treatment to remove nitrogen efficiently.
