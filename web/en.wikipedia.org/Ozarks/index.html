Ozarks

LocationMissouri, Arkansas, Oklahoma, Kansas, Illinois
Highest pointBuffalo Lookout, 2,561 ft (781 m)
AreaAbout 47,000 sq mi (122,000 km²)

The Ozarks, also known as the Ozark Mountains, Ozark Highlands or Ozark Plateau, are a physiographic region in the United States, spanning northern Arkansas, southern Missouri, northeastern Oklahoma, the far southeastern corner of Kansas, and a small part of southern Illinois. The Ozarks cover a significant portion of northern Arkansas and most of the southern half of Missouri, extending from Interstate 40 in central Arkansas to Interstate 70 in central Missouri.

The region is a broad, deeply dissected plateau of ancient sedimentary rock, uplifted during the Ouachita orogeny. Its rugged hills, clear spring-fed streams and extensive karst topography — including caves, sinkholes and large springs — give the Ozarks a distinctive landscape. The Boston Mountains of Arkansas form the highest part of the range.

Name

The name "Ozarks" is generally believed to derive from the French aux Arcs, a shortened form of aux Arkansas ("to the Arkansas [country]"), used by French explorers and traders travelling up the Mississippi and Arkansas rivers. Another theory connects it to the French arc for the natural bridges and rock arches found in the region.

Geology and geography

Unlike true mountain ranges built by folding and faulting, the Ozarks are an erosional plateau: the Salem Plateau, Springfield Plateau and Boston Mountains are remnants of Paleozoic limestone, dolomite, sandstone and chert uplifted and then carved by streams. The Saint Francois Mountains in southeastern Missouri expose much older Precambrian igneous rock, among the oldest in North America. The region contains some of the largest springs in the United States, including Big Spring and Greer Spring in Missouri.

Culture and economy

The Ozarks have a distinct regional culture shaped by Scots-Irish settlement in the late 18th and 19th centuries, reflected in traditional music, crafts and speech. Tourism is a major industry, centred on lakes created by damming the White River system — including Table Rock Lake, Lake of the Ozarks and Beaver Lake — and on the entertainment town of Branson, Missouri. Springfield, Missouri, and Fayetteville–Springdale, Arkansas, are the largest metropolitan areas in the region.
