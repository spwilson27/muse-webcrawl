Etymology"Rich father" (Latin dives, wealthy)

Dis Pater (Latin: Dīs Pater, "rich father"), or simply Dis, is a Roman god of the underworld and of the riches hidden in the earth — mineral wealth, fertile soil, and buried treasure. His name is a calque of the Greek Plouton ("the wealthy one"), and he became identified with the Greek god Hades and with the Roman Pluto. His consort was Proserpina, corresponding to the Greek Persephone.

Cult

Dis Pater and Proserpina were worshipped together, notably at an underground altar in the Campus Martius in Rome known as the Tarentum. The Ludi Saeculares (Secular Games) were originally celebrated in their honour with nocturnal sacrifices of black animals, before being reorganised by Augustus in 17 BC. In 249 BC, during the First Punic War, the Romans vowed games to Dis and Proserpina on the advice of the Sibylline Books.

In Celtic lands

Julius Caesar, in his Commentarii de Bello Gallico, reports that the Gauls claimed descent from Dis Pater — an example of interpretatio romana, in which a Celtic ancestor god of the dead was equated with the Roman underworld deity. Dis Pater also appears on inscriptions in the Roman provinces, sometimes alongside local gods of the dead.

Legacy

The name survives in literary usage, where "Dis" became a poetic name for the underworld itself, as in Dante's Inferno, where the city of Dis forms the boundary of lower Hell.
