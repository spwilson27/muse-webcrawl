Skunk Works

Skunk Works is an official pseudonym for Lockheed Martin's Advanced Development Programs (ADP), formerly called the Lockheed Advanced Development Projects. It is responsible for a number of famous aircraft designs, including the U-2, the Lockheed SR-71 Blackbird, the Lockheed F-117 Nighthawk, the Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor, and the Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II. The name "Skunk Works" has become a generic term for any small, autonomous group working on advanced or secret projects.

History

The Skunk Works was founded in 1943 in Burbank, California, when the US Army Air Forces asked Lockheed to build a jet fighter around the British Goblin engine. Under Clarence "Kelly" Johnson, the team delivered the XP-80 Shooting Star prototype in only 143 days, working in a rented circus tent next to a plastics factory whose smell inspired the name, borrowed from the "Skonk Works" in the Li'l Abner comic strip. Johnson ran the group under his "14 rules" of management, emphasizing small teams, trust, and minimal bureaucracy.

Notable projects

Later leaders included Ben Rich, who oversaw development of the F-117, the first production stealth aircraft, using the faceted design derived from the Have Blue demonstrator. Other projects include the A-12 and SR-71 reconnaissance aircraft, the Sea Shadow stealth ship, and numerous unmanned aerial vehicles. The Skunk Works is now based in Palmdale, California.
