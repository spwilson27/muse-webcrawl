Slavic paganism

Slavic paganism is the historical pre-Christian religion of the Slavic peoples, practised from at least the early medieval period until their gradual Christianisation between the 9th and 13th centuries. It was a polytheistic, animistic faith centred on nature worship, ancestor veneration and seasonal ritual.

Slavic paganism

Type: Polytheistic indigenous religion
Attested: 6th–13th centuries
Modern revival: Rodnovery

Sources

Early accounts include Procopius, who reported that the 6th-century Slavs worshipped a single god of lightning, rivers and spirits; the Primary Chronicle, which lists the gods of Vladimir's pantheon; and German chronicles by Thietmar of Merseburg and Saxo Grammaticus describing West Slavic temples at Rethra and Arkona.

Decline and revival

Christianisation proceeded unevenly: violent suppression in some regions, gradual absorption in others, with pagan uprisings recorded in Poland (1030s) and Kievan Rus' (11th century). Since the 20th century a reconstructed form of the faith, known as Rodnovery or Slavic Native Faith, has been practised by modern pagan movements across Slavic countries.
