Andragogy

Andragogy refers to methods and principles used in adult education. The word comes from the Greek andr- ("adult man") and agogos ("leader of"), and is contrasted with pedagogy, the teaching of children. The term was first coined by the German educator Alexander Kapp in 1833, and was later developed into a theory of adult education by the American educator Malcolm Knowles.

Knowles' assumptions

Knowles proposed that adult learners differ from child learners in several key respects:

  • Self-concept — adults move from dependence toward self-direction.
  • Experience — adults accumulate a reservoir of experience that is a resource for learning.
  • Readiness to learn — adults are most ready to learn things connected to their social roles.
  • Orientation to learning — adults are problem-centred rather than subject-centred, seeking immediate application.
  • Motivation — adults are motivated more by internal than external factors.
  • Need to know — adults need to know why they should learn something.

Application and criticism

Andragogy has been widely influential in adult and continuing education, professional training, and human resource development, encouraging facilitators to involve learners in planning and evaluating their instruction. Critics note that the assumptions describe an idealized learner, that children can also be self-directed and adults dependent, and that the framework is better understood as a set of principles on a continuum than as a strict dichotomy.
