Orhan

Orhan Ghazi (Ottoman Turkish: اورخان غازی; Turkish: Orhan Gazi; c. 1281 – March 1362) was the second bey of the Ottoman Empire from 1323/4 to 1362, succeeding his father Osman I. In his youth he fought alongside Osman, and during his reign the small frontier principality in north-western Anatolia was transformed into a structured state: he conquered Bursa (1326), Nicaea (1331) and Nicomedia (1337), absorbed the neighbouring beylik of Karesi, and established the first Ottoman foothold in Europe at Gallipoli (1354).

Reign

Orhan's victory over a Byzantine relief force at Pelekanon (1329) opened the way to the fall of Nicaea and Nicomedia, completing the conquest of Bithynia. He made Bursa his capital, issued the first Ottoman silver coins (akçe), organised a standing infantry (the yaya and later the nucleus of the Janissary institution is usually dated to his and Murad's reforms), and appointed his brother Alaeddin as vizier — often credited with early administrative organisation. Diplomatically, Orhan allied with the Byzantine emperor John VI Kantakouzenos, marrying his daughter Theodora in 1346 and supplying troops for John's civil wars; these interventions gave Ottoman forces their route into Thrace, where Orhan's son Süleyman Pasha seized Gallipoli in 1354 after an earthquake damaged its walls.

Family and succession

Orhan's consort Nilüfer Hatun, of Byzantine origin, was the mother of Murad I. His eldest son Süleyman Pasha, leader of the Thracian expansion, predeceased him in 1357 after a hunting accident, and another son, Halil, was once ransomed from Genoese pirates in a celebrated diplomatic episode. On Orhan's death in 1362 the throne passed to Murad.

Legacy

Orhan is remembered as the true organiser of the Ottoman state: his reign saw its first capital, coinage, religious endowments (he built mosques, madrasas and soup kitchens in Bursa and Iznik) and its first European territories. The Orhan Gazi district of Bursa preserves his name, and his tomb stands in Bursa beside that of Osman.
