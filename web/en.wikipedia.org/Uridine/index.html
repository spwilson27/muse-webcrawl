Uridine

Uridine is a nucleoside consisting of the pyrimidine base uracil attached to a ribose sugar. It is a building block of RNA and a precursor to uridine phosphates such as UDP and UTP.

Biochemistry

Uridine is formed when uracil links via a beta-N1-glycosidic bond to ribose. In RNA it pairs with adenine, replacing thymidine found in DNA.

Derivatives

Phosphorylated forms — UMP, UDP and UTP — participate in glycogen synthesis, membrane lipid production and glycosylation reactions.

Sources

Uridine occurs in foods such as beer, broccoli and organ meats, and is studied for roles in brain and metabolic health.
