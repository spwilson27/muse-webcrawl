Preventive detention

Preventive detention is an imprisonment that is putatively justified for non-punitive purposes, most often to prevent a person from committing further crimes or from disrupting the public order.

Types

Forms of preventive detention include pre-trial detention (remand), detention of persons considered a danger to themselves or others, immigration detention, and post-sentence preventive detention of offenders assessed as high-risk, as practised in countries such as Germany (Sicherungsverwahrung) and in some civil commitment schemes in the United States.

Criticism

Critics argue that preventive detention can conflict with the presumption of innocence and the prohibition of punishment without trial, and international human rights law restricts its use.
