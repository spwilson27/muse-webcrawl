Marshal of the Royal Air Force Sir Arthur Travers Harris, 1st Baronet (13 April 1892 – 5 April 1984), commonly known as "Bomber" Harris and, within the RAF, as "Butcher" Harris, was Air Officer Commanding-in-Chief of RAF Bomber Command during the height of the Anglo-American strategic bombing campaign against Nazi Germany in the Second World War.

Career

Harris emigrated to Southern Rhodesia as a teenager and served in the First World War, first as a soldier in Africa and then as a pilot in the Royal Flying Corps. Between the wars he held commands in India, Iraq, and Palestine and rose to senior staff posts, becoming head of Bomber Command in February 1942.

Bomber Command

Harris was the leading British advocate and executor of area bombing — night attacks on whole German cities aimed at destroying industry and breaking morale. Under his command, Bomber Command mounted the thousand-bomber raid on Cologne (1942), the Battle of the Ruhr, the firestorm raid on Hamburg (1943), the Battle of Berlin, and, with the USAAF, took part in the bombing of Dresden in February 1945. His methods remain intensely controversial because of the very high civilian death toll and the losses among his own crews, some 55,573 of whom were killed.

Later life

Harris never received the peerage customary for commanders of his rank and long campaigned for a campaign medal for his crews. He retired to South Africa for a period, returned to England, and died in 1984. A statue of Harris unveiled in London in 1992 drew public protest.
