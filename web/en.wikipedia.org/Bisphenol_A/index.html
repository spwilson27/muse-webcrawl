Bisphenol A

Bisphenol A (BPA) is an organic synthetic compound with the chemical formula (CH3)2C(C6H4OH)2. It belongs to the group of diphenylmethane derivatives and bisphenols. BPA is a colourless solid that is used primarily to make polycarbonate plastics and epoxy resins.

Uses

Polycarbonates made with BPA are used in water bottles, sports equipment, medical devices, CDs and DVDs, and household electronics. Epoxy resins containing BPA line many food and beverage cans and are used in floorings, adhesives, and protective coatings. BPA is also used as a colour developer in thermal paper receipts.

Health concerns

BPA can mimic estrogen and bind to estrogen receptors, making it an endocrine disruptor. Because it can leach from containers into food and drink, regulators in many countries have banned its use in baby bottles and restricted it in food-contact materials, and manufacturers have introduced substitutes such as bisphenol S — which has raised similar concerns. Human exposure is widespread; BPA is detectable in the urine of most people sampled, though it is metabolised and excreted relatively quickly.

History

BPA was first synthesised in 1891 by the Russian chemist Aleksandr Dianin. Its estrogenic properties were recognised in the 1930s, and its use in plastics began in the 1950s.
