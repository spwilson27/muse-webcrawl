The Industrial Areas Foundation (IAF) is the largest network of local faith and community-based organizations in the United States. Founded in Chicago in 1940 by Saul Alinsky, working with figures such as Bishop Bernard Sheil and businessman Marshall Field III, it pioneered the model of broad-based community organizing built on local institutions like congregations, schools, and unions.

History

The IAF grew out of Alinsky's Back of the Yards organizing in Chicago's stockyard neighbourhood (1939). For decades Alinsky personally trained organizers and seeded affiliates across the country. After his death in 1972, Edward T. Chambers, a former seminarian whom Alinsky had recruited, became executive director and led the IAF for nearly four decades, reshaping it around permanent local organizations funded by member dues rather than foundation grants. Ernesto Cortés Jr., organizer of COPS in San Antonio, became another influential leader in its western and southwestern affiliates.

Method

IAF organizations are broad-based coalitions of dues-paying member institutions. Organizers conduct thousands of one-to-one "relational meetings" and house meetings to identify local concerns and leaders, then build public campaigns—neighbourhood "actions" with officials—around issues chosen by members, such as living wages, school reform, affordable housing, and infrastructure. The IAF famously insists on training lay leaders rather than depending on charismatic individuals.

Notable affiliates and achievements

Affiliates include Communities Organized for Public Service (COPS) in San Antonio, Baltimoreans United in Leadership Development (BUILD), the Metro Alliance in Texas, and the Greater Boston Interfaith Organization. IAF organizations have been credited with major local victories in drainage and infrastructure investment, education reform, and the Nehemiah affordable housing programme in East Brooklyn.
