Śūnyatā

Śūnyatā (Sanskrit: शून्यता), usually translated as "emptiness" or "voidness", is a central concept in Buddhism, particularly in the Mahāyāna tradition, denoting that all phenomena are empty of inherent, independent existence (svabhāva).

Doctrine

The philosopher Nāgārjuna (c. 2nd century CE) gave the doctrine its classic Madhyamaka formulation, identifying emptiness with dependent origination (pratītya-samutpāda): because things arise only in dependence on causes, conditions and conceptual designation, none possesses a fixed self-nature. Emptiness itself is also empty — it is not a void or absolute underlying reality.

Development

Early Buddhist texts already described phenomena as empty of a self (ātman). The Prajñāpāramitā sutras extended the analysis to all dharmas, and the Heart Sutra condensed it in the famous line "form is emptiness, emptiness is form". The concept later informed Yogācāra, Tiantai, Huayan and Zen interpretations in distinct ways.
