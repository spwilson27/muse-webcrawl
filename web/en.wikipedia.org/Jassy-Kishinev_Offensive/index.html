Jassy–Kishinev Offensive

Second Jassy–Kishinev Offensive
Date20–29 August 1944
LocationEastern Romania and Moldavia
BelligerentsSoviet Union vs Germany and Romania
ResultDecisive Soviet victory; Romania changes sides

The Jassy–Kishinev Offensive, named after the cities of Iași (Jassy) and Chișinău (Kishinev), was a Soviet strategic offensive of August 1944 in which the Red Army destroyed the German–Romanian Army Group South Ukraine and knocked Romania out of the Axis. It was one of the most complete and rapid victories of the Second World War and had immediate political consequences across the Balkans.

Background

A first Soviet attempt on this axis had stalled in the spring of 1944. For the renewed offensive, Marshal Stalin assigned the 2nd Ukrainian Front (General Rodion Malinovsky) and 3rd Ukrainian Front (General Fyodor Tolbukhin), totalling roughly 1.3 million men, some 16,000 guns and mortars, and over 1,800 tanks and self-propelled guns, against Army Group South Ukraine under General Johannes Friessner — German Sixth and Eighth Armies plus Romanian Third and Fourth Armies, weaker in numbers and armour and holding an overextended salient.

The offensive

Launched on 20 August 1944 behind massive artillery preparations, Soviet forces broke through the Romanian-held sectors on both sides of the salient and drove deep armoured wedges toward the Prut crossings. The pincers closed near Huşi within days, encircling most of the German Sixth Army — re-formed after Stalingrad and destroyed a second time — along with elements of the Eighth Army. By 29 August organised Axis resistance had collapsed; Soviet losses were comparatively light, while Axis casualties numbered in the hundreds of thousands, including some 200,000 or more taken prisoner.

Consequences

On 23 August, as the front disintegrated, King Michael of Romania staged a coup, arrested the dictator Ion Antonescu, and declared Romania's defection to the Allies; Romanian troops turned against their former German allies. The Soviet victory opened the Balkans: Bulgaria fell under Soviet domination within weeks, and the Red Army advanced toward Hungary and Yugoslavia, while Germany lost the Ploiești oilfields — its last major natural source of oil.
