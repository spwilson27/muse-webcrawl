Columbia-class submarine

The Columbia-class submarine is a class of nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines under construction for the United States Navy to replace the Ohio-class. Twelve boats are planned, with the lead ship USS District of Columbia (SSBN-826) intended to enter service in the early 2030s.

Design

At about 20,800 tonnes submerged and 171 metres long, the class will be the largest submarines ever built for the US Navy. Each carries 16 Trident II D5 missiles in a Common Missile Compartment shared with the Royal Navy's Dreadnought-class. An electric-drive propulsion system and a life-of-ship reactor core — requiring no mid-life refuelling — are intended to maximise patrol availability.

Construction

General Dynamics Electric Boat is the prime contractor, with Newport News Shipbuilding as major subcontractor, building modules in Virginia and Rhode Island. Construction of the lead boat began in 2020–2022. The programme is the Navy's top acquisition priority.

Role

The class will form the sea-based leg of the US nuclear triad through the 2080s, each boat planned for a 42-year service life.
