Moïse Tshombe

Died29 June 1969 (aged 49), Algiers, Algeria

Moïse Kapenda Tshombe (10 November 1919 – 29 June 1969) was a Congolese businessman and politician. He served as the president of the secessionist State of Katanga from 1960 to 1963 and as prime minister of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from 1964 to 1965.

Katanga secession

Tshombe founded the CONAKAT party, which won control of the provincial government of Katanga, the Congo's mineral-rich southern province, shortly before independence from Belgium on 30 June 1960. Eleven days later, on 11 July 1960, he proclaimed Katanga's secession, a move backed by Belgian mining interests (notably the Union Minière du Haut-Katanga) and Belgian officers. The secession precipitated the Congo Crisis; the United Nations deployed a large peacekeeping force (ONUC), which, after fierce fighting in 1961–1963, forcibly ended the secession in January 1963, and Tshombe fled into exile.

During the crisis, Congo's first prime minister, Patrice Lumumba, was murdered in Katanga in January 1961 after being transferred there by his Congolese captors; Tshombe's exact role in the killing has remained a subject of investigation and controversy.

Prime minister and exile

Recalled from exile in 1964 to head a government of national reconciliation amid the Simba rebellion, Tshombe served as prime minister and relied on white mercenaries to defeat the rebels. President Joseph Kasa-Vubu dismissed him in October 1965, weeks before Joseph Mobutu seized power. Tshombe again fled to Europe. In 1967 he was sentenced to death in absentia for treason; that June, the aircraft carrying him over the Mediterranean was hijacked to Algeria, where he was held under house arrest in Algiers until his death, officially of a heart attack, on 29 June 1969.
