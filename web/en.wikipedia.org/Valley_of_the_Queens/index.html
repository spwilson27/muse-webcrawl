Valley of the Queens

The Valley of the Queens is a site in Egypt, where the wives of pharaohs were buried in ancient times. It was known then as Ta-Set-Neferu, meaning "the place of beauty". It was most famous for the tomb of Queen Nefertari (QV66), the wife of Ramesses II, which is one of the most richly decorated and best-preserved tombs in Egypt.

Located on the west bank of the Nile near Thebes (modern Luxor), close to the Valley of the Kings, the valley was used for burials during the New Kingdom. Besides queens, princes, princesses, and high officials were buried here. Workers for the necropolis lived in the nearby village of Deir el-Medina.

Tomb of Nefertari (QV66)

Discovered by Ernesto Schiaparelli in 1904, Nefertari's tomb is renowned for its vivid paintings of the queen's journey through the afterlife, guided by gods including Osiris, Isis, and Hathor. The tomb suffered severe salt damage and was closed for decades; after a long Getty Conservation Institute restoration, it reopened to limited visitor numbers.

Other notable tombs

Other tombs include those of Queen Titi (QV52), Prince Amunherkhepeshef (QV55), a son of Ramesses III, and Khaemwaset (QV44). Many tombs were reused in the Roman and Coptic periods.
