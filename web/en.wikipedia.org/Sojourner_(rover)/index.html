Sojourner is a robotic Mars rover that landed in Ares Vallis on 4 July 1997, as part of the Mars Pathfinder mission. It was the first wheeled vehicle to rove on a planet other than Earth. The rover was named after the nineteenth-century abolitionist and women's rights activist Sojourner Truth, following a student essay contest.

Design

The small rover measured about 0.65 m long and weighed 10.6 kg. It was equipped with an Alpha Proton X-ray Spectrometer (APXS) to determine the chemical composition of rocks and soil, three cameras, and a "rocker-bogie" six-wheel suspension that let it climb over obstacles roughly one and a half wheel diameters high. Power came from a gallium arsenide solar panel supplemented by a lithium battery.

Mission

Sojourner left its lander on 6 July 1997 and spent the following weeks analysing rocks given names such as "Barnacle Bill", "Yogi" and "Moe". It found that Martian rocks were strikingly similar in composition to terrestrial basalts and gathered evidence that Mars had once been warm and wet. Designed to operate for seven sols (Martian days), the rover lasted almost twelve times that long; communications were lost on 27 September 1997, most likely because of a failed battery in the lander.

Legacy

Sojourner's success demonstrated that small, low-cost rovers were practical and paved the way for the larger Spirit and Opportunity rovers, the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers that followed.
