Drumcree conflict

The Drumcree conflict or Drumcree standoff is a dispute over yearly Orange Order parades in Portadown, County Armagh, Northern Ireland. The conflict centres on the Order's traditional route for its church parade to Drumcree Church, which passes along the Garvaghy Road through a mainly Catholic nationalist area whose residents object to the parade.

Background

The Drumcree church parade dates to 1807. Tensions intensified during the Troubles, and the dispute became one of Northern Ireland's most volatile annual flashpoints in the 1990s. In 1995 and 1996, large standoffs and widespread loyalist protests occurred when police blocked and then permitted the parade; in 1996 the violence that spread across Northern Ireland led to several deaths, including the killing of Catholic taxi driver Michael McGoldrick.

Parades Commission era

From 1998, the newly created Parades Commission banned the parade from the Garvaghy Road, and it has been rerouted or stopped each year since. That year the stand-off also coincided with the Quinn brothers' deaths in a loyalist arson attack in Ballymoney. The Orange Order maintained a long-running protest camp at Drumcree, and although the dispute has largely faded from headlines, the parade remains prohibited from the contested route and the standoff has never been formally resolved.
