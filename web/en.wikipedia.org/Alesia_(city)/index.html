Alesia (city)

Alesia was the capital city of the Mandubii, a Gallic tribe allied with the Aedui. It was the site of the decisive Battle of Alesia in 52 BC, in which Julius Caesar defeated the Gallic coalition led by Vercingetorix, effectively ending organized Gallic resistance to Roman rule in the Gallic Wars.

Battle of Alesia

After his defeat at Gergovia, Caesar pursued Vercingetorix, who took refuge in the hilltop oppidum of Alesia. Caesar surrounded the town with elaborate siege works: a circumvallation of about 16 km facing inward and a contravallation of about 21 km facing outward against a Gallic relief army. Starved and defeated, Vercingetorix surrendered, reportedly riding out to throw his weapons at Caesar's feet.

Site identification

Excavations commissioned by Napoleon III in the 1860s, under the direction of Eugène Stoffel, identified the site with modern Alise-Sainte-Reine on Mont Auxois. A monumental statue of Vercingetorix was erected there in 1865. Although a minority of scholars once proposed alternative sites, Alise-Sainte-Reine remains the accepted location, supported by archaeology. The MuséoParc Alésia opened on the site in 2012.

Roman period

After the conquest, Alesia continued as a Gallo-Roman town known for metalworking, particularly bronze and brass goods, before declining in the early Middle Ages.
