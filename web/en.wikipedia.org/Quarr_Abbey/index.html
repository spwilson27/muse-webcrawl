Quarr Abbey

Quarr Abbey is a Catholic Benedictine monastery on the Isle of Wight, England, situated between the villages of Binstead and Fishbourne on the island's north-east coast, a short distance west of Ryde. It belongs to the Solesmes Congregation of the Benedictine Confederation.


The abbey church, completed in 1912

History

The present community descends from monks of the Abbey of Saint-Pierre de Solesmes in France who, expelled by the French anti-clerical laws of 1901, settled first at Appuldurcombe House and then purchased the Quarr estate. The new monastery was designed by Dom Paul Bellot, himself a monk of the community and a pioneer of modern brick ecclesiastical architecture, and built between 1907 and 1914. Its church, consecrated in 1912, is regarded as one of the most important religious buildings of the 20th century in England and is listed Grade I.

The name Quarr derives from the quarries nearby that supplied stone for medieval buildings on both sides of the Solent. The site adjoins the ruins of the earlier Quarr Abbey, a Cistercian house founded in 1132 by Baldwin de Redvers, which was suppressed in the Dissolution of the Monasteries under Henry VIII; its surviving ruins include parts of the precinct wall and monastic buildings.

Present day

The community follows the Rule of Saint Benedict, singing the Divine Office in Latin with Gregorian chant, and maintains a guesthouse, gardens, a farm, an art gallery and a bookshop. The abbey is known for its tea shop and for produce from its gardens and pigs, and it welcomes visitors and retreatants throughout the year.
