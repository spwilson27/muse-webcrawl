Heidelberg University

Heidelberg University, officially the Ruprecht Karl University of Heidelberg (German: Ruprecht-Karls-Universität Heidelberg), is a public research university in Heidelberg, Baden-Württemberg, Germany. Founded in 1386 by Rupert I, Elector Palatine, under a charter from Pope Urban VI, it is Germany's oldest university and one of the world's oldest surviving universities.

History

Modelled on the University of Paris, the university began with faculties of theology, law, medicine, and philosophy. In the 16th century it became a centre of the Reformation under the Palatine electors; the Heidelberg Catechism of 1563 was written by its theologians. The university suffered during the Thirty Years' War and the wars of Louis XIV, and was re-founded in 1803 by Karl Friedrich of Baden, whose name was added to that of the founder. In the 19th century it became famous for its research in law, chemistry (Robert Bunsen), physics (Gustav Kirchhoff, Hermann von Helmholtz), and history.

Academics

Today the university comprises twelve faculties and enrolls around 30,000 students. It is a member of the League of European Research Universities and the German Universities Excellence Initiative. Associated researchers have won numerous Nobel Prizes, including in physics, chemistry, and medicine. The university library holds the Codex Manesse, the most comprehensive surviving source of medieval German love poetry (Minnesang).
