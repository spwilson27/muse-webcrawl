Gaetano Donizetti

Born: November 29, 1797, Bergamo, Italy
Died: April 8, 1848, Bergamo, Italy
Occupation: Composer
Known for: Lucia di Lammermoor, L'elisir d'amore, Don Pasquale

Domenico Gaetano Maria Donizetti (29 November 1797 – 8 April 1848) was an Italian opera composer. Along with Gioachino Rossini and Vincenzo Bellini, he was a leading composer of the bel canto opera style during the first half of the 19th century and a probable influence on other composers such as Giuseppe Verdi. He composed nearly 70 operas, often at remarkable speed.

Life and career

Born into a poor family in Bergamo, Donizetti was taken under the wing of the composer Simon Mayr, who gave him a free musical education. After early sacred and chamber works, his opera career took off in Naples in the 1820s. His breakthrough came with Anna Bolena (1830), and he soon dominated Italian stages with works such as the comedy L'elisir d'amore (1832) and the tragedy Lucia di Lammermoor (1835), famous for its "mad scene."

Donizetti also worked successfully in Paris, where he composed La fille du régiment (1840) and the grand opera La favorite (1840), and in Vienna. His late comic masterpiece Don Pasquale premiered in Paris in 1843.

Final years and legacy

In the mid-1840s Donizetti's health collapsed, probably from syphilis, and he suffered severe mental deterioration. He died in Bergamo in 1848. After a long period in which most of his operas were neglected, the bel canto revival of the mid-20th century — championed by singers such as Maria Callas and Joan Sutherland — restored works like Lucia di Lammermoor, Maria Stuarda, and Roberto Devereux to the standard repertory.
