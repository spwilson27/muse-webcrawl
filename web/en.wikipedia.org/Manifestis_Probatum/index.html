Manifestis Probatum

The bull of 1179 recognizing Portugal

Manifestis Probatum is a papal bull issued by Pope Alexander III on 23 May 1179 by which the Holy See formally recognized Afonso Henriques as King of Portugal and Portugal as an independent kingdom under papal protection. It is considered one of the foundational documents of Portuguese statehood.

Background

Afonso Henriques had proclaimed himself King of Portugal after the Battle of Ourique in 1139 and had been recognized by Alfonso VII of León in the Treaty of Zamora (1143) as king of an independent realm. Papal recognition, however, was not immediately forthcoming. In 1144 Afonso offered Portugal as a vassal of the Holy See and pledged an annual tribute, and in the bull Devotionem Tuam (1144) Pope Lucius II acknowledged his devotion but addressed him only as duke (dux).

The bull

After decades of negotiation and the consolidation of Portuguese independence — including the conquest of Lisbon in 1147 with the help of crusaders — Alexander III finally issued Manifestis Probatum. The bull's opening words ("it is clearly demonstrated…") refer to Afonso's proven deeds in the service of the Church against the enemies of the Christian faith. The Pope granted Afonso and his heirs the royal title and confirmed the territorial integrity of the kingdom, in return for an annual census of two marks of gold.

"By the authority of the omnipotent God... we concede and confirm to you and your heirs the kingdom of Portugal with the full honour of royalty."

Legacy

The bull is preserved in the Torre do Tombo national archive in Lisbon. Together with the Treaty of Zamora, it marks the definitive diplomatic recognition of Portugal as an independent kingdom, the start of the Afonsine dynasty's legitimacy in the eyes of Latin Christendom.
