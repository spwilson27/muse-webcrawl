Haru no Umi

Haru no Umi (Japanese: 春の海, "The Sea in Spring") is a piece of Japanese music composed in 1929 by the koto player and composer Michio Miyagi (1894–1956). Written for koto and shakuhachi, it is Miyagi's most famous work and one of the best-known pieces of modern Japanese traditional music, frequently heard in Japan during the New Year season.

Haru no Umi
Composer: Michio Miyagi
Composed: 1929
Instrumentation: Koto and shakuhachi
Genre: Shinkyoku (new music for traditional instruments)

Background

Miyagi, blind from childhood, drew inspiration from the scenery of the Seto Inland Sea at Tomonoura, which he had visited as a child. He recalled the view through the sounds of waves, seagulls, and boats, and composed the piece in 1929, shortly before presenting it in concerts the following year.

Music

The duet evokes waves lapping at the shore, the cries of gulls, and the creak of oars. The shakuhachi imitates the sounds of birds and water while the koto provides rippling figuration. Haru no Umi helped establish the genre of shinkyoku and has been arranged for many other instruments, including violin and piano.
