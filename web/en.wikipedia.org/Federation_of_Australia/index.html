Federation of Australia

The Federation of Australia was the process by which the six separate British self-governing colonies of Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria, Tasmania, South Australia and Western Australia agreed to unite and form the Commonwealth of Australia as a dominion of the British Empire. It came into effect on 1 January 1901, when the Australian Constitution took effect and Edmund Barton became the first Prime Minister of Australia.

The opening of the Parliament of the Commonwealth of Australia, Melbourne, 1901.

Background

By the 1880s the six colonies, though separated by vast distances, shared language, law and British institutions, and faced common concerns about defence, immigration restriction, trade barriers between colonies, and the need for a uniform approach to matters such as railways and rivers. The Federal Council of Australasia, created in 1885, was a weak coordinating body without executive power. A popular federation movement gathered strength in the 1890s, driven by figures such as Henry Parkes, whose "Tenterfield Oration" of 1889 called for a convention to draft a federal constitution, and by organisations such as the Australian Natives' Association.

Conventions and referendums

Conventions met in Sydney in 1891 and in Adelaide, Sydney and Melbourne in 1897–98; the second convention, with delegates elected by popular vote in four colonies, produced a draft constitution drawing on the United States, Swiss and Canadian models. Referendums in 1898 failed in New South Wales, but after amendments — including a promise that the federal capital would be located in New South Wales at least 100 miles from Sydney — referendums in 1899 and 1900 approved the constitution in all six colonies. The Commonwealth of Australia Constitution Act was passed by the British Parliament and received royal assent in July 1900.

The new Commonwealth

The Constitution established a federal parliament with a House of Representatives based on population and a Senate in which each state had equal representation, a High Court, and a division of powers between the Commonwealth and the continuing states. The first federal elections were held in March 1901, and the first Parliament was opened in Melbourne's Royal Exhibition Building on 9 May 1901 by the Duke of Cornwall and York, the future King George V.
