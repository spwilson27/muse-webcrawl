San Lorenzo Tenochtitlán

LocationVeracruz, Mexico
CultureOlmec
Periodc. 1200–900 BCE (peak)
Known forOlmec colossal heads

San Lorenzo Tenochtitlán is the collective name for a group of three related archaeological sites — San Lorenzo, Tenochtitlán, and Potrero Nuevo — in the Mexican state of Veracruz. San Lorenzo was the earliest major Olmec centre and one of the first large ceremonial complexes in Mesoamerica.

Colossal heads

The site is famous for its ten colossal stone heads, carved from basalt boulders transported from the Tuxtla Mountains nearly 100 kilometres away. The heads, up to about 3 metres tall, are thought to portray individual rulers.

Decline

San Lorenzo flourished between roughly 1200 and 900 BCE, after which its monuments were defaced or buried and the centre of Olmec power shifted east to La Venta.
