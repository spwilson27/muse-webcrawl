Zoom, stylized as zoom, is an American communications technology platform and proprietary videotelephony software program developed by Zoom Video Communications. It provides videotelephony and online chat services through a cloud-based peer-to-peer software platform, and is used for teleconferencing, telecommuting, distance education and social relations.

A Zoom meeting in progress, showing the gallery view of participants.

History

Zoom was founded in 2011 by Eric Yuan, a former Cisco Webex engineer and executive. The service's first version supported conferences with up to 15 video participants; by 2013 it had reached one million users. Zoom's business strategy focuses on providing an easier-to-use product than competitors, with a freemium model in which group meetings on the free tier are limited to 40 minutes.

Growth during the COVID-19 pandemic

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Zoom saw a major increase in usage for remote work, distance education and online social relations. By April 2020 the platform reported 300 million daily meeting participants. The surge also drew scrutiny of the company's security and privacy practices — a phenomenon dubbed "Zoombombing" for disruptions of meetings by uninvited guests — prompting Zoom to acquire the secure messaging company Keybase and add end-to-end encryption options.

Features

Zoom offers one-on-one and group video meetings, screen sharing, in-meeting chat, breakout rooms, virtual backgrounds, recording and transcription. Related products include Zoom Phone, Zoom Events, Zoom Rooms and the Zoom AI Companion assistant.
