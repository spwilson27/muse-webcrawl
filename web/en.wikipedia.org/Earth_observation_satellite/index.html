An Earth observation satellite (or Earth remote sensing satellite) is a satellite used or designed for Earth observation (EO) from orbit, including spy satellites and similar ones intended for non-military uses such as environmental monitoring, meteorology, oceanography, cartography, agriculture, and disaster management. Most fly in Sun-synchronous low Earth orbit so they pass over each point at the same local solar time, or in geostationary orbit for continuous weather coverage.

History

The first photographs of Earth from space were taken on sub-orbital V-2 rockets in the late 1940s; Sputnik 1 (1957) began the satellite era. TIROS-1 (1960) returned the first television images of Earth from space, founding satellite meteorology. The U.S. military CORONA programme (1960–1972) flew film-return reconnaissance satellites whose imagery was declassified in 1995. Landsat 1 (1972) opened civilian multispectral land imaging, whose free and open archive since 2008 underpins global change science.

Sensors

EO satellites carry passive sensors (multispectral and hyperspectral imagers recording reflected sunlight, thermal infrared radiometers) and active sensors that illuminate the surface: synthetic-aperture radar (SAR) can image through clouds and at night, altimeters measure sea-surface height and ice topography, and lidar (laser altimetry) maps terrain and forest structure. Geostationary weather satellites (GOES, Meteosat, Himawari) monitor cloud systems continuously, while low-orbit sounders profile atmospheric temperature and composition.

Applications

  • Weather and climate — numerical weather prediction assimilates satellite data; long records track sea level, ice, and greenhouse gases.
  • Agriculture — crop mapping, yield forecasting, and precision agriculture via vegetation indices such as NDVI.
  • Disasters — flood mapping, wildfire detection, oil-spill monitoring, and the International Charter "Space and Major Disasters."
  • Security — reconnaissance and arms-treaty verification.
  • Cartography and geodesy — global elevation models and plate-tectonic measurements.

Major programmes

Notable systems include Landsat and GOES (U.S.), NOAA polar orbiters, the European Copernicus programme's Sentinel fleet (Sentinel-1 radar, Sentinel-2 optical), EUMETSAT's Meteosat, Japan's Himawari and ALOS series, India's IRS/Cartosat and INSAT, and China's Gaofen and Fengyun constellations. Commercial providers such as Planet Labs and Maxar now supply daily high-resolution imagery.
