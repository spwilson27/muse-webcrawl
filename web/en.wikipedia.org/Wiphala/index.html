Wiphala

The wiphala is a square emblem commonly used as a flag to represent some native peoples of the central Andes. The symbol is a 7×7 square patchwork of seven colours, said to derive from an Inca banner. The colours of the rainbow are used in a specific diagonal arrangement: red, orange, yellow, white, green, blue, and violet.

Use in Bolivia

Under the 2009 Constitution, the wiphala is a national symbol of Bolivia, flown alongside the red-yellow-green tricolour, and it is particularly associated with the Quechua, Aymara, and Guaraní peoples. In 2010 the Bolivian armed forces adopted a wiphala patch on their uniforms.

History

Rainbow banners appear on some pre-Columbian artefacts such as Tiwanaku ceramics, though the direct descent of the modern wiphala from Inca usage is debated by historians. The modern emblem was popularised by Indigenous movements in the 1970s, notably by the Aymara activist Germán Choquehuanca.
