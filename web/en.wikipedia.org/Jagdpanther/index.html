The Jagdpanther (German for "hunting panther"), officially the Sd.Kfz. 173, was a tank destroyer built by Germany during World War II. It combined the chassis of the Panther tank with the powerful 8.8 cm Pak 43 anti-tank gun in a fixed, sloped casemate.

Design

Ordered in 1942, the Jagdpanther carried the same long-barrelled 88 mm gun used in the Tiger II, mounted in a heavily sloped superstructure with frontal armour up to 80 mm thick. Its low profile, mobility, and firepower made it one of the most effective tank destroyers of the war.

Service history

Production began in January 1944 and about 415 vehicles were built before the war ended. Jagdpanthers served in heavy anti-tank battalions on both the Eastern and Western Fronts, seeing action in Normandy, the Ardennes offensive, and the defence of Germany.

Survivors

Several examples survive in museums, including running vehicles; a Jagdpanther captured in 1944 is displayed at the Imperial War Museum in London.
