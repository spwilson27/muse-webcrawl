Cord-cutting

Cord-cutting is the practice of cancelling subscriptions to cable or satellite television services in favour of programming delivered over the internet, through streaming platforms, or received free over the air. Subscribers who do so are called cord-cutters; those who have never subscribed to pay television are cord-nevers, and those who reduce their packages are cord-shavers.

Development

The trend emerged in the late 2000s as streaming services such as Netflix and Hulu, and devices from Roku and Apple TV, made large catalogues available on demand at a fraction of typical cable bills. Rising cable prices, bundled channels many viewers never watched, and the growth of original streaming content accelerated defections through the 2010s. Major sports and news programming initially anchored pay-TV subscriptions, but live streaming bundles (such as YouTube TV and Hulu + Live TV) and direct-to-consumer sports services weakened that hold.

Industry impact

Pay-TV households in the United States peaked around 2010–2012 at roughly 100 million and declined steadily thereafter, with streaming households overtaking them in the early 2020s. Cable networks lost advertising and affiliate revenue, media companies launched their own streaming services, and broadcasters promoted free over-the-air reception with digital antennas — a behaviour sometimes called cord-revival or antenna use as part of a streaming-centred bundle.
