Komitas


Komitas (1869–1935), Armenian composer and ethnomusicologist.

Komitas (Armenian: Կոմիտաս), born Soghomon Soghomonian (26 September 1869 – 22 October 1935), was an Armenian priest, musicologist, composer, arranger, singer and choirmaster, regarded as the founder of the Armenian national school of music and one of the pioneers of ethnomusicology.

Life and work

Born in Kütahya in the Ottoman Empire, Komitas was orphaned young and educated at the Gevorgian Seminary in Etchmiadzin, where he was ordained a priest and took the name Komitas after a 7th-century hymn writer. He studied in Berlin and travelled widely, collecting and transcribing thousands of Armenian folk songs and liturgical melodies, and presenting Armenian music to European audiences through lectures and his choir's concerts.

His compositions and arrangements — including sacred works and choral settings of folk songs such as "Krunk" (The Crane) — synthesized folk material with Western classical technique.

Later life and legacy

Komitas was arrested and deported in April 1915 at the onset of the Armenian genocide. Though released through interventions, he suffered a severe mental breakdown and spent the rest of his life in psychiatric hospitals, dying in Paris in 1935. His ashes were later transferred to Yerevan, where he is buried in the Komitas Pantheon. The Yerevan State Conservatory bears his name, and his manuscripts are preserved at the Komitas Museum-Institute.
