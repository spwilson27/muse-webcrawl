Sundiata Keita

Sundiata Keita (c. 1217 – c. 1255), also known as Sundiata Keïta, Sunjata, or the "Lion King," was the founder of the Mali Empire in West Africa. His life is celebrated in the Epic of Sundiata, an oral tradition preserved and performed by Mandinka griots (jeliw) for centuries.

Life and legend

According to the epic, Sundiata was the son of Naré Maghann Konaté, king of the Mandinka people, and Sogolon Condé, a hunchbacked woman of great spiritual power. Born unable to walk until age seven, the young prince was exiled with his mother after his father's death, when a rival queen seized power for her own son.

Founding of the empire

After years in exile, Sundiata returned to liberate his homeland from the Sosso king Soumaoro Kanté, a sorcerer-tyrant who had conquered the Mandinka. At the Battle of Kirina (c. 1235), Sundiata defeated Soumaoro, and he went on to unite the Mandinka chiefdoms. At a great assembly at Kouroukan Fouga, he is said to have promulgated a constitution dividing offices and lands among the clans — the foundation charter of the Mali Empire, often called the Manden Charter.

Sundiata established his capital at Niani and made Mali a center of agriculture, trade in gold and salt, and Islamic learning. He died around 1255, in some versions drowning in the Sankarani River. His successors, including Mansa Musa, expanded Mali into one of the largest and wealthiest empires of the medieval world.
