Bullion

Bullion is non-ferrous metal that has been refined to a high standard of elemental purity. The term is usually applied to bulk metal used by large industrial users and investors rather than to finished products. Precious metal bullion — gold, silver, platinum, and palladium — is traded on world markets and valued by its mass and purity rather than by any face value.

Forms

Bullion is cast or minted into bars, ingots, and coins. Bullion bars bear stamps showing weight, fineness, and the refiner's mark, and bars from refiners accredited by the London Bullion Market Association's Good Delivery list are accepted in settlement of loco London trades. Bullion coins such as the American Gold Eagle, Canadian Maple Leaf, and South African Krugerrand are minted by governments and sold at a small premium over the metal's spot price.

Markets

London is the center of the world's wholesale bullion market, with prices fixed by auction benchmarks; the COMEX division of the New York Mercantile Exchange is the principal futures market. Central banks hold gold bullion as part of their foreign reserves, and investors hold bullion directly or through exchange-traded funds as a store of value and hedge.

Purity

Purity is expressed in parts per thousand: bullion gold of 999.9 fineness is termed "four nines fine". Good Delivery gold bars must be at least 995 fine and weigh about 400 troy ounces.
