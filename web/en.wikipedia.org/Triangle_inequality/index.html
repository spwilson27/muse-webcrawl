Triangle inequality
For any triangle, the sum of the lengths of any two sides is greater than the length of the remaining side

In mathematics, the triangle inequality states that for any triangle, the sum of the lengths of any two sides must be greater than or equal to the length of the remaining side. In Euclidean geometry, for vertices x, y and z: |x − z| ≤ |x − y| + |y − z|.

The inequality generalises far beyond triangles. In normed vector spaces it takes the form ‖x + y‖ ≤ ‖x‖ + ‖y‖, and in metric spaces the requirement d(x,z) ≤ d(x,y) + d(y,z) is one of the defining axioms of a metric.

Consequences

A degenerate case, equality, occurs when the three points are collinear. The reverse triangle inequality gives a lower bound: ||x| − |y|| ≤ |x − y|.
