Rack and pinion

A rack and pinion gear arrangement: a circular pinion meshes with a linear rack.

A rack and pinion is a type of linear actuator that comprises a circular gear (the pinion) engaging a linear gear bar (the rack). Together, they convert between rotational motion and linear motion. Rotating the pinion causes the rack to be driven in a line; conversely, moving the rack linearly causes the pinion to rotate.

Applications

The most familiar application is in automobile steering systems, where the steering wheel rotates a pinion that drives a rack connected to the tie rods, turning the wheels. Rack and pinion mechanisms are also used in machine tools, in rack railways (where a pinion on the locomotive engages a rack rail between the running rails to climb steep gradients), in drill presses, and in various actuators.

Gear geometry

The teeth of the rack are straight and perpendicular to the direction of travel, effectively forming a gear of infinite radius. The pinion's teeth are usually involute or cycloidal in profile. Because the rack is straight, it can be cut with straight-sided tools, making racks relatively easy to manufacture.
