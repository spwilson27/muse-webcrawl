Green Mountain Club

The Long Trail, built and maintained by the Green Mountain Club.

The Green Mountain Club (GMC) is a non-profit membership organisation dedicated to maintaining and protecting Vermont's Long Trail, the oldest long-distance hiking trail in the United States. Founded on 11 March 1910 in Burlington, Vermont, largely through the efforts of schoolteacher James P. Taylor, the club today maintains about 500 miles (800 km) of trails and some 70 shelters and campsites, supported by around 10,000 members, local sections, and seasonal caretakers and trail crews.

The Long Trail

The Long Trail runs 272 miles (438 km) along the main ridge of the Green Mountains from the Massachusetts border to the Canadian border, crossing Vermont's highest peaks including Mount Mansfield and Camel's Hump. Built between 1910 and 1930, it inspired Benton MacKaye's conception of the Appalachian Trail, with which it coincides for about 100 miles in southern Vermont. The club publishes the Long Trail Guide and waterproof maps, staffs summit caretakers on fragile alpine peaks, and works with the state of Vermont and the Green Mountain National Forest to protect the trail corridor.
