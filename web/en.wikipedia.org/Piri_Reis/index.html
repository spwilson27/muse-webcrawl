Piri Reis

Piri Reis (Ottoman Turkish: پيري رئيس‎; c. 1465/70 – 1553) was an Ottoman admiral, geographer and cartographer. He is known primarily for the Piri Reis map, the oldest known surviving map of the world showing the Americas, and for his Kitab-ı Bahriye (Book on Navigation), one of the most famous cartographical works of the period.

Biography

Piri Reis was born in Gallipoli on the Aegean coast of the Ottoman Empire. He took to a life at sea early, engaging, together with his uncle Kemal Reis, in piracy and later in naval service for the Ottoman Empire. He took part in numerous naval wars against the Spanish, Genoese and Venetians, including the First Battle of Lepanto in 1499.

When his uncle Kemal Reis died in 1511, Piri returned to Gallipoli and began work on his world map, completing it in 1513. The map was presented to Sultan Selim I in 1517 in Cairo. In 1521 he published the first version of the Kitab-ı Bahriye, a detailed atlas and guide to the Mediterranean coasts; a more elaborate, illustrated version followed in 1526, dedicated to Suleiman the Magnificent.

Later career and death

In his later years Piri Reis served as admiral of the Ottoman fleet in the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean. In 1552 his fleet failed in its attempt to take the island of Hormuz from the Portuguese, and after lifting the siege and abandoning his damaged fleet at Basra, he made his way overland to Egypt. For this failure he was executed at Cairo in 1553, at an advanced age.

Legacy

The Piri Reis map, a fragment of which survives in the library of the Topkapı Palace, is celebrated for its depiction of the Atlantic coasts. Several Turkish naval vessels and a research vessel have been named after him, and his portrait appeared on the Turkish 10 lira banknote.
