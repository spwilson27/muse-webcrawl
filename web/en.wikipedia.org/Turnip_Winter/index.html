Turnip Winter

The Turnip Winter (German: Steckrübenwinter) was a period of severe food shortage in the German Empire during the winter of 1916–17, in the middle of the First World War. The potato harvest of autumn 1916 failed badly, and the turnip — normally used as animal fodder — became a staple substitute in bread, soup, coffee and jam. The crisis was compounded by the British naval blockade of Germany, which severely restricted food imports, and by an exceptionally cold winter.

Background

By 1916, German agriculture was suffering from shortages of labour, horses and fertiliser as resources were diverted to the war effort. The potato blight and wet weather destroyed much of the 1916 potato crop. The Imperial Food Office attempted rationing and price controls, but supplies collapsed further in the winter, and turnips filled the gap on civilian tables.

Impact

Malnutrition and hunger spread widely; civilian mortality rose, and it is estimated that hundreds of thousands of excess deaths in Germany during the war were attributable to the effects of the blockade and food scarcity. The Turnip Winter badly damaged home-front morale and contributed to growing unrest, strikes and the erosion of support for the war, feeding into the revolutionary atmosphere of 1918.
