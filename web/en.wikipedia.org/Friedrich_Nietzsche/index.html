Friedrich Nietzsche

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Friedrich Wilhelm Nietzsche (15 October 1844 – 25 August 1900) was a German philosopher, cultural critic, and philologist whose work has exerted a profound influence on modern intellectual history. His writings challenged traditional morality and religion, introduced ideas such as the Übermensch, the will to power, eternal recurrence, and the declaration that "God is dead", and developed the method of genealogy and the outlook of perspectivism.

Life

Nietzsche was born in Röcken, Saxony, the son of a Lutheran pastor who died when Friedrich was four. A brilliant student of classical philology at Bonn and Leipzig, he was appointed professor at the University of Basel at the age of 24, before completing his doctorate. His first book, The Birth of Tragedy (1872), interpreted Greek tragedy through the tension between the Apollonian and Dionysian principles; it grew out of his early enthusiasm for the music of Richard Wagner, with whom he later broke.

Plagued by ill health — migraines, failing eyesight, and digestive illness — Nietzsche retired from Basel in 1879 and spent a decade wandering between the Swiss Alps (above all Sils Maria in the Engadin), the Italian Riviera, and Turin, producing the works of his maturity in a distinctive aphoristic style: Human, All Too Human (1878), The Gay Science (1882), Thus Spoke Zarathustra (1883–85), Beyond Good and Evil (1886), and On the Genealogy of Morality (1887).

Breakdown and death

In January 1889 Nietzsche suffered a mental collapse in Turin — legend holds that he threw his arms around a horse being beaten in the street — and he lived his remaining eleven years in the care of his mother and then his sister, Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche, publishing nothing further. He died in Weimar in 1900.

Philosophy

Nietzsche diagnosed European culture as falling into nihilism following the decline of Christian belief: the "death of God" removed the traditional foundation of values. In response he called for a revaluation of all values, contrasting the life-denying "slave morality" of resentment with the self-affirming creativity of the Übermensch (overman). His thought experiment of eternal recurrence asks whether one could affirm one's life so completely as to will its exact repetition forever.

Influence

Largely unrecognised in his lifetime, Nietzsche's reputation grew rapidly after his death. He influenced existentialism, psychoanalysis, postmodernism, and writers from Thomas Mann to Michel Foucault. His sister's editing of his notebooks and her later association with National Socialism long distorted his reception; modern scholarship emphasises his opposition to antisemitism and German nationalism.
