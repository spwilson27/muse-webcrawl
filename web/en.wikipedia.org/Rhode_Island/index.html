Rhode Island

Rhode Island is a state in the New England region of the Northeastern United States. It borders Connecticut to its west, Massachusetts to its north and east, and the Atlantic Ocean to its south via Rhode Island Sound and Block Island Sound. It is the smallest U.S. state by area.

Rhode Island was one of the original Thirteen Colonies and was the last of them to ratify the United States Constitution, on May 29, 1790. Its official name is simply the State of Rhode Island; until a 2020 referendum it was the State of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations, the longest state name in the country. The state is named after the island of the same name in Narragansett Bay, which itself may have been named for the Greek island of Rhodes.

History

The colony was founded in 1636 by Roger Williams, who had been banished from the Massachusetts Bay Colony for his religious views, at Providence on land purchased from the Narragansett people. Rhode Island became known for its religious tolerance and independent spirit; it was the first colony to formally renounce allegiance to the British Crown, on May 4, 1776.

Geography

Rhode Island covers an area of about 1,214 square miles (3,144 km²), of which about 14 percent is water. Narragansett Bay is a major feature of the state's topography, containing more than 30 islands, the largest of which is Aquidneck Island, home to Newport, Middletown and Portsmouth.

Economy

Historically a textile and manufacturing centre, the state's economy today is dominated by services, notably health care, education, tourism and financial services. Hasbro, the toy company, is headquartered in Pawtucket, and CVS Health in Woonsocket.
