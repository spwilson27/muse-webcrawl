National debt

National debt, also called government debt, public debt or sovereign debt, is the total financial obligations owed by a country's government to creditors. It accumulates from annual budget deficits, when government spending exceeds revenue, and is typically financed by issuing securities such as government bonds, bills and notes bought by domestic and foreign investors, banks and other governments.

Measurement

National debt is commonly expressed as an absolute figure and as a percentage of gross domestic product (GDP), the debt-to-GDP ratio, which allows comparison across countries and over time. Gross debt counts all government liabilities; net debt subtracts government financial assets. Debt held by the public excludes intragovernmental holdings such as social security trust funds.

History and levels

Modern national debts emerged with the financial revolutions in the Netherlands and England in the seventeenth century. Debt levels typically spike in wartime and crises: after the Second World War the United States and United Kingdom both carried debts above 100% of GDP, ratios that fell over subsequent decades. Japan has sustained the highest ratio among advanced economies, exceeding 250% of GDP, while many governments' debts rose sharply after the 2008 financial crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic.

Economics

Economists debate the burdens of public debt: interest payments consume revenue, high debt may crowd out private investment or raise borrowing costs, and excessive foreign-currency debt has triggered sovereign defaults. Others note that debt denominated in a government's own currency, held domestically, and spent on productive investment is more sustainable, and that safe government bonds underpin the financial system.
