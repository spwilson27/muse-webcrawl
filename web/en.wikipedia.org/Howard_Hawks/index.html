Howard Hawks

Howard Winchester Hawks (May 30, 1896 – December 26, 1977) was an American film director, producer, and screenwriter of the classic Hollywood era. He is ranked among the greatest and most influential filmmakers in the history of cinema, noted for his mastery across genres including westerns, gangster films, war films, musicals, and screwball comedies.

Career

Hawks began in the silent era, working in the property department at Famous Players-Lasky before directing his first feature, The Road to Glory (1926). He rose to prominence with films such as Scarface (1932), Bringing Up Baby (1938), Only Angels Have Wings (1939), His Girl Friday (1940), Sergeant York (1941), To Have and Have Not (1944), The Big Sleep (1946), Red River (1948), Gentlemen Prefer Blondes (1953), and Rio Bravo (1959).

Style

Hawks's films are characterised by fast, overlapping dialogue, strong ensemble groupings, and themes of professional competence, camaraderie, and moral codes within groups. His heroines — the so-called "Hawksian woman" — are typically tough, witty, and able to hold their own in male company. French critics of the Cahiers du Cinéma championed him as a model auteur, and his influence runs through directors from John Carpenter to Quentin Tarantino.

Honours

Despite being nominated only once for a competitive Academy Award (for Sergeant York), Hawks received an Honorary Academy Award in 1975, presented with the citation recognising him as "a giant of American cinema". His films Scarface, Bringing Up Baby, Only Angels Have Wings, His Girl Friday, Sergeant York, The Big Sleep, Red River, and Rio Bravo have been inducted into the National Film Registry.
