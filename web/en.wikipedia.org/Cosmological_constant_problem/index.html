Cosmological constant problem

The cosmological constant problem is the disagreement between the observed value of the cosmological constant Λ (the energy density of empty space, or dark energy) and the huge value predicted by quantum field theory for vacuum energy.

The discrepancy

Naive estimates of zero-point energy give a vacuum density up to 120 orders of magnitude larger than observed — often called the worst prediction in physics. Einstein introduced Λ in 1917; its small non-zero value was measured in 1998 from the accelerating expansion of the universe.

Proposed solutions

Ideas include supersymmetry, anthropic selection within a multiverse of varying Λ, modified gravity, and dynamical dark energy (quintessence). No explanation commands consensus.
