List of rabbit breeds

Domestic rabbits, all descended from the European rabbit (Oryctolagus cuniculus), have been developed into hundreds of varieties for fur, meat, wool, showing and companionship. More than 300 varieties are recognised somewhere in the world; national bodies such as the American Rabbit Breeders Association (ARBA, recognising around 50 breeds) and the British Rabbit Council (over 70 breeds and hundreds of varieties) maintain standards. Breeds vary enormously in size — from the ~1 kg Netherland Dwarf to the Flemish Giant, which can exceed 10 kg.

Selected breeds

  • Angora breeds (English, French, German, Satin, Giant) — kept for long, soft wool harvested for fibre.
  • Rex and Mini Rex — plush, velvety fur caused by a recessive mutation; the Satin has a distinctive sheen.
  • Holland Lop, Mini Lop, English Lop, French Lop and Dwarf Lop — lop-eared breeds popular as pets.
  • Netherland Dwarf and Polish — small breeds; the Lionhead has a mane of longer wool.
  • Flemish Giant, Continental Giant, Checkered Giant — giant breeds; New Zealand, Californian and Palomino — classic commercial meat breeds.
  • Dutch — with its characteristic white blaze and saddle; English Spot, Dalmatian Rex and Rhinelander — marked breeds; Himalayan — colourpoint pattern; Silver Fox, Champagne d'Argent and Creme d'Argent — silvered fur breeds; Harlequin — alternating bands of two colours; Belgian Hare — a rabbit bred to resemble a hare; Jersey Wooly — a small wool breed.

Classification

ARBA groups breeds by body type — commercial, cylindrical, compact, arched and semi-arch — and by fur type (normal, rex, satin and wool). Show judging follows published standards of points for type, fur, colour and condition. Many older utility breeds are now rare and are monitored by livestock conservancies.
