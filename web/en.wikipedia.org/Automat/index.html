Automat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The vending wall of an automat

An automat is a fast food restaurant where simple foods and drinks are served by vending machines. Customers insert coins (or, in modern versions, pay by card) to open small glass-doored compartments containing individual portions of food, which staff behind the wall replenish.

History

The format began in Berlin with the Quisisana company's automat restaurants in the 1890s and spread across Europe and North America. In the United States, Horn & Hardart opened the first New York automat in 1912 and became the best-known operator, famous for fresh food at low prices, gleaming chrome and glass interiors, and coffee. Automats democratised eating out: they required no interaction with waiters and were popular with office workers, immigrants, and artists.

Decline and revivals

Fast-food chains with counter service displaced automats in the later 20th century; the last Horn & Hardart automat in New York closed in 1991. In the Netherlands, FEBO automats (automatiek) remain common for snacks such as croquettes. The concept has seen periodic revivals, including automated, contactless restaurants in the 2010s and 2020s.

Cultural references

Automats feature prominently in Edward Hopper's painting Automat (1927) and in numerous films and photographs evoking mid-century urban life.
