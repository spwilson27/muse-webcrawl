Integral

In mathematics, an integral is the continuous analog of a sum, and is used to calculate areas, volumes, and their generalisations. Integration, the process of computing an integral, is one of the two fundamental operations of calculus, the other being differentiation. Integration was initially used to solve problems in mathematics and physics, such as finding the area under a curve, or determining displacement from velocity. Usage of integration expanded to a wide variety of scientific fields thereafter.

Definitions

A definite integral computes the signed area of the region in the plane bounded by the graph of a given function between two points on the real line. Conventionally, areas above the horizontal axis are treated as positive and areas below as negative. An indefinite integral of a function is an antiderivative — a function whose derivative is the original. The fundamental theorem of calculus relates the two: if F is an antiderivative of f, then the definite integral of f from a to b equals F(b) − F(a).

History

Methods of computing areas and volumes date to ancient Greek mathematics, notably the method of exhaustion of Eudoxus and Archimedes. Systematic development came in the seventeenth century with the independent work of Isaac Newton and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, who introduced the integral sign ∫ (an elongated S, for Latin summa). Bernhard Riemann gave the first rigorous definition in the nineteenth century; Henri Lebesgue's more general integral extended the theory to a broader class of functions.

Applications and extensions

Integrals appear throughout physics and engineering — computing work, flux, probability, and centres of mass — and in economics and statistics. Extensions include line integrals and surface integrals over curves and surfaces, multiple integrals over regions of higher-dimensional space, and improper integrals over unbounded domains. Many integrals cannot be evaluated in closed form and are computed numerically using methods such as Simpson's rule and Gaussian quadrature.
