The House of Dunkeld (in Scottish Gaelic Dùn Chailleann) is a historiographical label for the Scottish royal dynasty that ruled the Kingdom of Scotland from 1034 to 1040 and again from 1058 to 1290, taking its name from Dunkeld, an early ecclesiastical and political centre.

History

The dynasty began with Duncan I, grandson of Malcolm II through his daughter Bethóc and Crínán, lay abbot of Dunkeld. Duncan was killed by Macbeth in 1040; after Macbeth and Lulach, Duncan's son Malcolm III ("Canmore") seized the throne in 1058. His marriage to Saint Margaret began a line including Edgar, Alexander I and David I, under whom Scotland was transformed by feudal, ecclesiastical and burghal reforms — the "Davidian Revolution".

End of the line

The dynasty continued through William the Lion, Alexander II and Alexander III. After Alexander III's accidental death in 1286 and the death of his granddaughter Margaret, Maid of Norway, in 1290, the succession crisis opened the way to the Competitors, Edward I's intervention and the Wars of Scottish Independence.
