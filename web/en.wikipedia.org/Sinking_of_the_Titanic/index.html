Sinking of the Titanic

Date14–15 April 1912
LocationNorth Atlantic Ocean, about 400 miles south of Newfoundland
CauseCollision with an iceberg
DeathsAbout 1,500
SurvivorsAbout 710

The British ocean liner RMS Titanic sank in the early hours of 15 April 1912 after striking an iceberg during her maiden voyage from Southampton to New York City. Of the roughly 2,224 people aboard, more than 1,500 died, making it one of the deadliest peacetime maritime disasters in history.

Background

Titanic, operated by the White Star Line and built by Harland and Wolff in Belfast, was the largest ship afloat at the time. She was widely publicised as virtually unsinkable: her hull was divided into sixteen watertight compartments, and she could remain afloat with any four flooded. She carried lifeboats for only about half of those aboard, which was legal under the regulations of the day.

Collision and sinking

At 23:40 ship's time on 14 April, lookouts spotted an iceberg directly ahead. The ship turned, but the berg scraped along her starboard side, buckling hull plates and opening five — by some accounts six — compartments to the sea. As the bow sank, the ship's stern rose; at about 02:20 she broke in two and foundered. Many lifeboats were launched partly empty, and most of those in the water died of hypothermia within minutes.

Rescue

The Cunard liner RMS Carpathia answered Titanic's distress calls and arrived around 04:00, rescuing about 710 survivors from the lifeboats. The nearby SS Californian, which had not responded, became the subject of enduring controversy.

Aftermath

Inquiries in Britain and the United States led to sweeping reforms: the International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) of 1914 required lifeboat capacity for everyone aboard, 24-hour radio watches, and established the International Ice Patrol. The wreck was located in 1985 by an expedition led by Robert Ballard, resting in two pieces about 3,800 metres down. The disaster remains a fixture of popular culture, most famously through James Cameron's 1997 film Titanic.
