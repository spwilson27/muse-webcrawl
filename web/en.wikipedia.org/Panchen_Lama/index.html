The Panchen Lama is a tulku (reincarnate lama) of the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism and, after the Dalai Lama, the most senior figure in that tradition. The title Panchen derives from the Sanskrit paṇḍita ("scholar") and Tibetan chenpo ("great"): "Great Scholar". Panchen Lamas are considered emanations of Amitābha, the Buddha of Infinite Light, while Dalai Lamas are emanations of Avalokiteśvara; traditionally each has a role in recognising the other's reincarnation.

History

The lineage was established when the 5th Dalai Lama bestowed the title on his teacher, Lobsang Chökyi Gyaltsen, abbot of Tashi Lhunpo Monastery in Shigatse, and recognised three earlier abbots as his predecessors. Tashi Lhunpo, founded in 1447 by the 1st Dalai Lama, remained the Panchen Lamas' seat. Later holders played major political roles: the 9th Panchen Lama fled to Mongolia and China in 1923 after disputes with the 13th Dalai Lama, while the 10th Panchen Lama, Choekyi Gyaltsen (1938–1989), remained in Tibet after 1959, was imprisoned for much of the Cultural Revolution era after criticising Chinese policies in his "70,000 Character Petition" of 1962, and spent his later years working — controversially for many exile Tibetans — within the Chinese system.

The disputed 11th Panchen Lama

After the 10th Panchen Lama's death in 1989, the Dalai Lama recognised a six-year-old boy, Gedhun Choekyi Nyima, as the 11th Panchen Lama in May 1995. Days later the child and his family were taken into Chinese custody and have not been seen publicly since — he is sometimes called "the world's youngest political prisoner". The Chinese government installed another boy, Gyaltsen Norbu, through a state-managed selection, and he serves in official roles in China while being rejected by most Tibetans in exile. The dispute is widely seen as a rehearsal for a future contested succession of the Dalai Lama himself.
