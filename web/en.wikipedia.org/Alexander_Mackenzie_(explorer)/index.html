Sir Alexander Mackenzie (1764 – 12 March 1820) was a Scottish explorer and fur trader known for accomplishing the first recorded crossing of continental North America by a European north of Mexico, reaching the Pacific in 1793 — more than a decade before the Lewis and Clark Expedition.

Early life and fur trade

Born in Stornoway on the Isle of Lewis, Mackenzie emigrated to New York and then Montreal, entering the fur trade. He joined the North West Company and in 1788 helped found Fort Chipewyan on Lake Athabasca, in present-day Alberta.

Expeditions

In 1789, seeking a route to the Pacific, Mackenzie descended the great river that now bears his name — the Mackenzie River, Canada's longest — only to reach the Arctic Ocean, calling it in disappointment the "River of Disappointment". In 1792–93 he tried again, ascending the Peace River, crossing the Rocky Mountains and descending the Bella Coola River to the Pacific coast near present-day Bella Coola, British Columbia, on 20 July 1793. There he inscribed on a rock: "Alexander Mackenzie, from Canada, by land, the twenty-second of July, one thousand seven hundred and ninety-three." The journey covered some 3,700 km across unmapped territory.

Later life

Mackenzie published his Voyages from Montreal in 1801 and was knighted in 1802. He served briefly in the Legislative Assembly of Lower Canada, then returned to Scotland, where he died in 1820. The Mackenzie River, Mackenzie Bay and numerous Canadian places commemorate him.
