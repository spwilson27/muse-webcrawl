Siege of Fort Detroit

The siege of Fort Detroit was an ultimately unsuccessful attempt by North American Indians to capture Fort Detroit during Pontiac's War. The siege was led primarily by Ottawa chief Pontiac, beginning on May 7, 1763, and was lifted on October 31, 1763.

Background

After the Seven Years' War, British policies toward the Native nations of the Great Lakes — ending gift-giving and restricting trade — provoked resistance. Pontiac, aware of the British garrison's small size, planned to enter the fort under pretense of a council and seize it from within.

The siege

The plot was betrayed and the fort's commander, Major Henry Gladwin, was prepared. Pontiac instead laid siege, ambushing supply parties and defeating a relief column at the Battle of Bloody Run on July 31, 1763, while the armed schooner Huron kept the river route open and supplied the garrison.

End of the siege

As French support failed to materialise and his warriors dispersed for the winter hunt, Pontiac lifted the siege in late October 1763 and withdrew to the Maumee River.
