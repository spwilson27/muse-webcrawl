Thomas Crombie Schelling (14 April 1921 – 13 December 2016) was an American economist and professor of foreign policy, national security and arms control. He shared the 2005 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Robert Aumann "for having enhanced our understanding of conflict and cooperation through game-theory analysis".

Work

His classic book The Strategy of Conflict (1960) recast military strategy as bargaining, introducing concepts — many now standard — such as credible commitment, deterrence, compellence, brinkmanship, and the "focal point" (Schelling point): the solution people converge on in coordinating without communication. He advised US administrations on nuclear strategy and arms control, and was associated with the RAND Corporation and Harvard's Kennedy School.

In Micromotives and Macrobehavior (1978) he showed how mild individual preferences can produce large-scale patterns, famously demonstrating racial segregation in his checkerboard model even when no individual seeks a strongly segregated outcome. He also wrote influentially on self-command, addiction, and climate-change policy.
