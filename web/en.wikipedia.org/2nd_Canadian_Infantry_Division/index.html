2nd Canadian Infantry Division

The 2nd Canadian Infantry Division was an infantry division of the Canadian Army raised for service during the Second World War. It was mobilised in September 1939, shortly before Canada's declaration of war, and was initially recruited across Canada before concentrating in England in 1940.

Second World War

The division's first major action was the ill-fated Dieppe Raid of 19 August 1942 (Operation Jubilee), in which the division provided the bulk of the assault force and suffered very heavy casualties, with over 900 men killed and nearly 1,900 taken prisoner. Rebuilt in England, the division landed in Normandy in July 1944 as part of II Canadian Corps and fought through the battles of the Falaise Pocket, the pursuit across France, and the gruelling Battle of the Scheldt in the autumn of 1944, which opened the port of Antwerp to Allied shipping.

In early 1945 the division took part in Operation Veritable, the advance into the Rhineland, and in the liberation of the northern Netherlands, ending the war in northern Germany. The division was disbanded in 1945 following the end of hostilities in Europe.

Postwar

The division's lineage was perpetuated in the Canadian Militia, and its headquarters was reactivated in later decades as a formation headquarters. In 2013, Land Force Quebec Area was redesignated as the 2nd Canadian Division, restoring the formation's name to the Canadian Army's order of battle, though as a regional command rather than a field division.
