Flat-coated Retriever

OriginEngland
Height56–62 cm (22–24 in)
CoatDense, flat, glossy; black or liver
Life span8–14 years

The Flat-coated Retriever is a breed of gundog from England, developed as a dual-purpose retriever of game on land and from water. It is recognised by its sleek black or liver-coloured coat, its long, moulded head, and its famously cheerful, puppy-like temperament that persists well into old age, earning it the nickname "the Peter Pan of dogs".

History

The breed was developed in the mid-19th century from crosses that likely included the St. John's water dog, setter-type dogs, and collie-type herding dogs. It was one of the most popular retrievers in Britain in the late 19th and early 20th centuries before being eclipsed in popularity by the Labrador Retriever and Golden Retriever.

Characteristics

Flat-coats are intelligent, outgoing, and eager to please, making them capable gundogs and family companions. They require substantial exercise and mental stimulation. The breed has a relatively high incidence of certain cancers, including histiocytic sarcoma, which breed organisations actively monitor.
