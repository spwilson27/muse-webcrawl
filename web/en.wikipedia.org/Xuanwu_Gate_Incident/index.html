Xuanwu Gate Incident

The Xuanwu Gate Incident (玄武門之變) was a palace coup at the start of China's Tang dynasty, occurring on 2 July 626 at the Xuanwu Gate, the northern gate of the Taiji Palace in Chang'an. Prince Li Shimin ambushed and killed his elder brother, Crown Prince Li Jiancheng, and his younger brother Li Yuanji, clearing his path to the throne.

Background

Li Yuan, Emperor Gaozu, founded the Tang in 618 with the help of his sons, who commanded armies in the dynasty's wars of unification. Li Jiancheng, the eldest, was crown prince and a capable commander-politician; Li Shimin, the second son, was the dynasty's most brilliant general, victor at Hulao (621) over two rival claimants. Their rivalry intensified after unification: the crown prince allied with Li Yuanji against Shimin, and each faction schemed for the emperor's favour, amid mutual accusations of assassination plots and treason.

The coup

After Shimin accused his brothers of misconduct with palace concubines and secured a summons to court, he laid an ambush at the Xuanwu Gate with trusted guards, having won over the gate commander Chang He. When Jiancheng and Yuanji rode in and sensed trouble, Shimin shot Jiancheng dead with an arrow; his officer Yuchi Gong killed Yuanji. Shimin's men then presented themselves to Gaozu, who was boating on a palace lake, and reported the deed as righteous suppression of a plot.

Aftermath

Within days Gaozu named Li Shimin crown prince; two months later, on 4 September 626, Gaozu abdicated and Shimin ascended as Emperor Taizong, whose reign (626–649) became a byword for good government. The sons of Jiancheng and Yuanji were executed, though Taizong later adopted some of their daughters' lines into favour and employed Jiancheng's adviser Wei Zheng, who became his famously outspoken chancellor. Historians have long debated how Taizong's pressure on court chroniclers shaped the surviving record of the incident.
