Hydronephrosis

Hydronephrosis
Swelling of a kidney due to a build-up of urine, caused by obstruction of urinary outflow; may be unilateral or bilateral.

Hydronephrosis is the swelling of a kidney due to a build-up of urine. It occurs when urine cannot drain from the kidney to the bladder because of a blockage or obstruction. If left untreated, the rising pressure can progressively destroy kidney tissue (atrophy) and lead to loss of renal function.

Causes

Obstruction can occur anywhere along the urinary tract. In adults, common causes include kidney stones, tumours, blood clots, benign prostatic hyperplasia and pregnancy. In children, congenital abnormalities such as ureteropelvic junction obstruction and posterior urethral valves predominate. Hydronephrosis found on prenatal ultrasound is one of the most common fetal anomalies detected.

Signs and diagnosis

Symptoms depend on the speed of onset: acute obstruction typically causes severe flank pain (renal colic), nausea and reduced urine output, whereas chronic obstruction may be nearly painless while damage accumulates. Diagnosis is usually made by ultrasound, which shows dilation of the renal pelvis and calyces; CT urography and nuclear medicine renograms identify the site and functional impact of obstruction. Severity is graded, commonly by the Society for Fetal Urology system.

Treatment

Treatment aims to relieve the obstruction and treat its cause: ureteral stents or percutaneous nephrostomy drain the kidney in emergencies (especially when infection is present, a urological emergency), while definitive management may involve stone removal, surgery to correct anatomical blockages, or treatment of prostatic enlargement. Unlike fluid-depleted states such as hypovolemia, the kidneys here are damaged by pressure from behind the obstruction, not by low circulating volume.
