Tel Dan stele

The Tel Dan stele is a fragmentary basalt stele containing a Canaanite inscription in Old Aramaic, dating to the 9th century BCE. It is famous for containing the phrase "House of David" (bytdwd), the first historical evidence of King David found outside the Hebrew Bible.

Discovery

The fragments were discovered by archaeologist Avraham Biran and his team at Tel Dan in northern Israel: the main fragment (A) in 1993, and two smaller fragments (B1 and B2) in 1994. The stele fragments are now displayed in the Israel Museum in Jerusalem.

Inscription

The inscription commemorates a victory of an Aramean king — usually identified as Hazael of Aram-Damascus — over the king of Israel and the king of the "House of David". It is written in Aramaic in a monumental square script and is one of the oldest extrabiblical references to the Davidic dynasty.

Interpretation

Most scholars accept the reading bytdwd as "House of David", making the stele a key piece of evidence in debates over the historicity of the biblical united monarchy. A minority of scholars have proposed alternative readings of the phrase.
