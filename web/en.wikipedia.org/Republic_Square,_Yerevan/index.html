Republic Square, Yerevan

Republic Square, with the History Museum and ministries arranged around its oval.

Republic Square (Armenian: Հանրապետության հրապարակ, Hanrapetutyan hraparak) is the central town square of Yerevan, the capital of Armenia. Designed by architect Alexander Tamanian as part of his 1924 master plan for the city, the square consists of two sections: an oval roundabout with a central pool and musical "singing" fountains, and a trapezoid-shaped section. Five monumental buildings of pink tuff stone, decorated with traditional Armenian carving, surround it, housing the History Museum of Armenia, the National Gallery, and several government ministries.

History

Known as Lenin Square during the Soviet era, when a statue of Lenin stood at its head, the square was renamed after Armenia's independence in 1991 and the statue was removed. It has since been the focal point of national celebrations, military parades, and large public gatherings, including the mass protests of the 2018 Velvet Revolution. The singing fountains, installed in the Soviet period and later renovated, perform nightly shows of water, light, and music during the warmer months.

Architecture

The ensemble is considered a masterpiece of Armenian neoclassical architecture, uniting modern reinforced-concrete construction with ornament drawn from medieval Armenian churches and manuscripts. The clock tower of the government house is one of the most recognisable landmarks of the city.
