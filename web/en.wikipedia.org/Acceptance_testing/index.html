Acceptance testing

In engineering and its various subdisciplines, acceptance testing is a test conducted to determine if the requirements of a specification or contract are met. It may involve chemical tests, physical tests, or performance tests. In software engineering, acceptance testing by the system provider is often distinguished as user acceptance testing (UAT) when performed by the customer, and may also be structured as alpha and beta testing phases.

In software testing, the International Software Testing Qualifications Board (ISTQB) defines acceptance testing as formal testing with respect to user needs, requirements, and business processes conducted to determine whether a system satisfies the acceptance criteria and to enable the user, customers or other authorised entity to determine whether to accept the system.

Overview

Acceptance testing is generally the final phase of testing before a system is released. Whereas unit, integration and system testing are concerned with finding defects (verification — "building the product right"), acceptance testing is a form of validation: determining whether the product that was built is the right product for its intended users. Test cases are typically derived from acceptance criteria agreed with the customer or from real-world business scenarios.

Types

  • User acceptance testing (UAT) – performed by end users or clients to verify the system handles required real-world tasks.
  • Business acceptance testing (BAT) – checks the system against business processes and scenarios.
  • Contract acceptance testing – verifies compliance with the criteria in a development contract.
  • Regulation acceptance testing – checks conformity with applicable laws and regulations.
  • Alpha and beta testing – acceptance testing conducted at the developer's site (alpha) or by a limited external user group (beta) before general release.
