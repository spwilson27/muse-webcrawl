Palm Pre

Manufacturer: Palm, Inc.
Released: June 2009
Operating system: webOS
Form factor: Slider with QWERTY keyboard

The Palm Pre, styled as palm prē, is a multitasking smartphone that was designed and marketed by Palm with a multi-touch screen and a sliding keyboard. The smartphone was the first to use Palm's Linux-based mobile operating system, webOS. The Pre was introduced at the Consumer Electronics Show in January 2009 and was released in the United States on 6 June 2009, initially exclusively on the Sprint network.

Features

The Pre introduced webOS's card-based multitasking interface, in which each open application is represented by a card that users flick between and throw off the screen to close. Its "Synergy" feature unified contacts and calendars from sources such as Google, Facebook, and Exchange. The phone had a 3.1-inch capacitive touchscreen, a slide-out portrait QWERTY keyboard, a 3-megapixel camera, 8 GB of storage, GPS, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth. It also launched the Touchstone, an inductive charging dock that charged the phone wirelessly — a novelty at the time.

Reception and legacy

The Pre was widely praised as the first credible rival to the iPhone, and webOS was admired for its elegant interface. However, sales were hampered by Sprint's smaller subscriber base, build-quality complaints, and a slow application catalogue. Follow-ups included the smaller Pixi and the Pre Plus and Pre 2. Palm was acquired by Hewlett-Packard in 2010 for $1.2 billion; HP discontinued webOS hardware in 2011. Elements of webOS design — cards, gestures, and swipe-to-dismiss notifications — influenced later mobile operating systems, and webOS survives in LG smart televisions.
