Enschede fireworks disaster

Date13 May 2000
LocationRoombeek, Enschede, Netherlands
Deaths23 (including four firefighters)
InjuriesAbout 950
DamageSome 400 houses destroyed; about €450 million

The Enschede fireworks disaster was a catastrophic fireworks explosion at the S.E. Fireworks depot in the Dutch city of Enschede on 13 May 2000. The explosion killed 23 people, including four firefighters, injured about 950, and destroyed much of the residential neighbourhood of Roombeek.

The explosion

A fire started inside the warehouse complex on a Saturday afternoon and spread to illegally stored containers of fireworks. Two major explosions followed; the largest, at about 15:35, involved an estimated 100 tonnes of fireworks and was felt kilometres away, registering on seismographs across the region. Windows shattered across the city and a mushroom cloud rose over Roombeek.

Investigation

Investigations found that the company had stored far more fireworks — and of heavier classes — than its permits allowed, in sea containers placed on the site rather than in the required bunkers. The two company directors were convicted of violating environmental and fireworks regulations and of culpable arson, though the immediate cause of the initial fire was never definitively established. Official inquiries also criticised municipal and national authorities for lax licensing and enforcement.

Aftermath

The disaster prompted a complete overhaul of Dutch fireworks regulations, including stricter storage classifications, larger safety distances and tighter import rules. Roombeek was rebuilt as a model residential district, designed under the supervision of architect Pi de Bruijn with extensive resident participation. A monument in the rebuilt neighbourhood commemorates the victims, and the disaster remains the worst fireworks accident in Dutch history.
