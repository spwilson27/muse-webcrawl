Skolt Sámi language

Skolt Sámi (nuõrttsääʹmǩiõll) is a Sámi language spoken by the Skolt Sámi people, an Eastern Sámi group indigenous to the border region of Finland, Russia and Norway. It belongs to the Eastern Sámi branch of the Uralic language family and is closely related to Akkala Sámi (now extinct) and Kildin Sámi.

Skolt Sámi is spoken by a few hundred people, mainly in the villages of Sevettijärvi, Keväjärvi and Nellim in the Finnish municipality of Inari, to which Skolt families were resettled after Finland ceded Petsamo to the Soviet Union in 1944. A small number of speakers also live in Norway (Neiden) and Russia. It is classified as severely endangered, although language nest preschools and school teaching aim at revitalisation.

Writing system

Skolt Sámi is written in an extended Latin alphabet standardised in 1973, using letters such as ʹ (palatalisation mark), ǩ, ǧ and ä. Until the 1970s it was mainly written, when written at all, in Cyrillic among Orthodox Skolts in Russia. Its phonology is notable for a three-way length distinction interacting with consonant gradation.
