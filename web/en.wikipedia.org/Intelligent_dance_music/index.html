Intelligent dance music (commonly abbreviated as IDM) is a style of electronic music originating in the early 1990s, defined by idiosyncratic experimentation rather than specific genre traits. It emerged from the culture of rave and club music — including techno, ambient and breakbeat — but departed from dancefloor functionality toward home listening, with complex rhythms, unusual timbres and abstract structures.

History

The term gained currency around Warp Records' 1992 compilation Artificial Intelligence, marketed as "electronic listening music", which featured Aphex Twin (as the Dice Man), Autechre, the Black Dog, Richie Hawtin's F.U.S.E., and B12. The phrase "intelligent techno" appeared in press coverage, and an Internet mailing list called the IDM list was founded in 1993 by Alan Parry. Artists associated with the style include Aphex Twin, Autechre, Boards of Canada, Squarepusher, µ-Ziq, Plaid, and Venetian Snares, on labels such as Warp, Rephlex, Skam and Ninja Tune.

Characteristics

IDM tracks often use irregular time signatures, glitchy or fractured beats, intricate drum programming, and melodic material ranging from ambient washes to dissonant synthesis. Richard D. James (Aphex Twin) preferred the term "braindance" for the Rephlex label's output.

Criticism of the term

Many artists dislike the label, arguing that it implies other dance music is "unintelligent". Aphex Twin commented that the term was only ever meant as a joke; others have seen in it an elitism or class divide within electronic music. Nevertheless, "IDM" remains in widespread critical and commercial use as a loose category for experimental electronic music with dance-music roots.
