Vichy France

Vichy France (French: Régime de Vichy), officially the French State (État français), was the French regime that governed the southern, "free" zone of metropolitan France and the French colonial empire from 10 July 1940 to 9 August 1944, after the Fall of France in World War II.

Establishment

After the German invasion, Marshal Philippe Pétain negotiated an armistice and the National Assembly granted him full powers at Vichy on 10 July 1940, ending the Third Republic. The regime pursued the "National Revolution" — a conservative, authoritarian program — and collaborated with Nazi Germany, including the deportation of Jews from France.

Fall

Germany occupied the whole of metropolitan France in November 1942 (Case Anton). Vichy's legitimacy collapsed; Pétain's government fled to Sigmaringen as the Allies liberated France, and the Provisional Government declared the regime illegitimate. Pétain was later tried for treason.
