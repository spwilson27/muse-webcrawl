Lapulapu

Lapulapu (fl. 1521), also spelled Lapu-Lapu, was a datu (chief) of Mactan Island in the Visayas, Philippines. He is regarded as the first Filipino national hero for his resistance to Spanish colonisation: on 27 April 1521 his forces defeated a Spanish expedition led by Ferdinand Magellan at the Battle of Mactan, in which Magellan was killed.

Life and legend

Little is documented about his life beyond Antonio Pigafetta's account of the voyage. Lapulapu refused to submit to Rajah Humabon of Cebu and the Spanish, and fought the landing party in shallow water where Spanish boats and armour were disadvantaged. Later folklore gave him the name Kali Pulaka and other legendary details.

Legacy

The city of Lapu-Lapu on Mactan is named for him; his image has appeared on Philippine currency and stamps, and a large shrine and statue stand at Punta Engaño.
