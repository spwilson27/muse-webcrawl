House of Windsor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The House of Windsor is the reigning royal house of the United Kingdom and the other Commonwealth realms. It was founded on 17 July 1917, when King George V changed the name of the British royal house from the German-sounding House of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha to the more English-sounding Windsor, after the castle of that name, in the midst of the First World War.

Founded: 17 July 1917
Founder: George V
Parent house: House of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha (House of Wettin)
Current head: Charles III

Origins

George V was a grandson of Queen Victoria and Prince Albert of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha. Anti-German feeling during the First World War — sharpened when Gotha bombers raided London — led the king to relinquish German titles for himself and his family and to adopt Windsor as the family name by royal proclamation.

Monarchs

The Windsor monarchs have been George V (1910–1936), Edward VIII (1936, who abdicated), George VI (1936–1952), Elizabeth II (1952–2022), the longest-reigning British monarch, and Charles III (from 2022). In 1952 Elizabeth II confirmed that she and her descendants would continue to bear the name Windsor, and in 1960 the surname Mountbatten-Windsor was adopted for male-line descendants without royal styles.
