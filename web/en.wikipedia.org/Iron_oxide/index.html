Iron oxide

Iron oxides
Common formsFeO (wüstite), Fe2O3 (hematite), Fe3O4 (magnetite), FeO(OH) (goethite)
Known compounds16 oxides and oxyhydroxides
Main usesIron ore, pigments, catalysts, polishing (rouge)

Iron oxides are chemical compounds composed of iron and oxygen. Sixteen oxides and oxyhydroxides of iron are known, the best known of which is rust, a form of iron(III) oxide. Iron oxides and oxyhydroxides are widespread in nature and play an important role in many geological and biological processes. They are used as iron ores, pigments, catalysts, and in thermite, and occur in haemoglobin.

Principal oxides

Iron(II) oxide (FeO, wüstite) is a black, non-stoichiometric oxide. Iron(III) oxide (Fe2O3) occurs as the mineral hematite, the principal ore of iron, and as maghemite; its hydrated forms constitute rust. Iron(II,III) oxide (Fe3O4, magnetite, or lodestone) is ferrimagnetic and was used in early compasses. Oxyhydroxides include goethite (α-FeO(OH)), the main component of ochre pigments and of limonite, and ferrihydrite, common in soils.

Occurrence

Iron oxides give many rocks and soils their red, yellow and brown colours; the banded iron formations laid down in ancient oceans are major iron ores. The red colour of Mars comes from iron(III) oxide dust on its surface. In biology, organisms store iron as ferrihydrite in the protein ferritin, and magnetotactic bacteria grow chains of magnetite crystals.

Uses

Mined iron oxides are smelted to produce iron and steel. Synthetic iron oxide pigments (reds, yellows, browns and blacks) are used in paints, concrete, cosmetics and food colouring (E172). Finely divided hematite ("jeweller's rouge") polishes metal and optics; magnetite is used in magnetic recording, ferrofluids and as a catalyst in the Haber process and water-gas shift reaction.
