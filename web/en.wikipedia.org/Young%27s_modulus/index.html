Young's modulus (symbol E), also known as the elastic modulus, is a mechanical property that measures the stiffness of a solid material. It defines the relationship between tensile stress and tensile strain in the linear elastic region of a material's deformation, and is named after the British scientist Thomas Young.

Definition

Young's modulus is the ratio of stress (force per unit area) to strain (proportional deformation) along an axis: E = σ/ε. Materials with a high Young's modulus, such as steel (about 200 GPa) or diamond, deform very little under load, whereas rubber (around 0.01–0.1 GPa) deforms greatly.

Uses

The property is fundamental in engineering calculations of deflection of beams, extension of springs and wires, and in materials selection. It is measured in standardised tensile tests and by ultrasonic techniques.
