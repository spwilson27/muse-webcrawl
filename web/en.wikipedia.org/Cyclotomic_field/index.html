Cyclotomic field

In number theory, a cyclotomic field is a number field obtained by adjoining a complex primitive root of unity to the field of rational numbers. For a positive integer n, the n-th cyclotomic field Q(ζn) is generated by a primitive n-th root of unity ζn = e2πi/n.

Properties

The degree of Q(ζn) over Q is φ(n), where φ is Euler's totient function, since the minimal polynomial of ζn is the n-th cyclotomic polynomial Φn(x). Cyclotomic fields are Galois over Q with abelian Galois group isomorphic to (Z/nZ)×. By the Kronecker–Weber theorem, every finite abelian extension of Q is contained in some cyclotomic field.

History and applications

Cyclotomic fields were studied by Kummer in his work on Fermat's Last Theorem, in which he introduced ideal numbers after finding that unique factorisation can fail in cyclotomic integer rings; a prime p for which it holds is called a regular prime. Cyclotomic fields also underlie the theory of Gauss sums, class field theory, and Iwasawa theory, and they furnish explicit constructions in algebraic number theory.
