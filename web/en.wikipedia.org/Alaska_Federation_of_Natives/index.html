The Alaska Federation of Natives (AFN) is the largest statewide Native organization in Alaska, representing Alaska Native tribal governments, regional and village corporations, and other Native organizations. It was founded in 1966 to address Alaska Native land claims.

History

AFN was formed in October 1966, when leaders from across Alaska gathered in Anchorage to pursue a unified settlement of aboriginal land claims. Its advocacy was central to the passage of the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act (ANCSA) in 1971, the largest land claims settlement in United States history, which conveyed about 44 million acres and nearly $1 billion to Alaska Native corporations.

Annual convention

AFN's annual convention, held each autumn, is one of the largest gatherings of Indigenous people in the United States, drawing thousands of delegates to discuss subsistence rights, tribal sovereignty, health, and economic issues.
