Minos (Greek: Μίνως) was a legendary king of Crete, son of Zeus and Europa. He is best known as the ruler for whom Daedalus built the Labyrinth to imprison the Minotaur, and for the tribute of Athenian youths exacted until Theseus slew the monster. After death, Minos became one of the three judges of the dead in Hades, alongside his brother Rhadamanthus and Aeacus.

Mythology

Minos contested the Cretan throne with his brothers and, to prove divine favour, prayed to Poseidon for a bull from the sea as a sign, vowing to sacrifice it. Poseidon's magnificent bull emerged, but Minos kept it and sacrificed another; in revenge the god made Minos's wife Pasiphaë fall in love with the bull, producing the Minotaur. Minos later warred on Athens after his son Androgeos was killed there, imposing a tribute of seven youths and seven maidens. When his master craftsman Daedalus aided Theseus's escape with Ariadne, Minos imprisoned Daedalus and his son Icarus in the Labyrinth.

Historical background

Greek writers such as Thucydides treated Minos as the first ruler to command a navy and suppress piracy in the Aegean. Arthur Evans named the Bronze Age civilisation of Crete "Minoan" after him, and its palace at Knossos — with its maze-like plan — may underlie the Labyrinth legend.
