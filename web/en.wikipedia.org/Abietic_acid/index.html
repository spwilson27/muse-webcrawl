Abietic acid

Molecular formula: C20H30O2

Molar mass: 302.46 g/mol

Appearance: Yellow resinous solid

Source: Pine rosin (colophony)

Abietic acid (from the Latin Abies, "fir") is an organic compound, a diterpene resin acid that is the principal component of pine rosin and the most abundant of the closely related abietane acids. It was first isolated from pine resin and named after the fir genus Abies.

Properties and occurrence

Abietic acid is a yellow, resinous, nearly odourless solid that is insoluble in water but soluble in alcohols, acetone and ethers. In living pines it is dissolved in oleoresin and helps seal wounds against insects and fungi; on exposure to air and heat it isomerises readily to a mixture of related acids, which is why commercial "rosin acid" is a mixture.

Uses and safety

Abietic acid and its derivatives are used in varnishes, lacquers, soaps, paper sizing and soldering fluxes. Hydrogenated and disproportionated forms are used as emulsifiers in the production of synthetic rubber. The acid is a mild skin irritant, and its oxidation products — formed when rosin is heated — are a recognised cause of contact allergy and occupational asthma among solderers and violinists handling rosin.
