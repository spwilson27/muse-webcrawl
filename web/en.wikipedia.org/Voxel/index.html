Voxel

A voxel is a value on a regular grid in three-dimensional space — the 3D analogue of a pixel. The word is a blend of "volume" and "pixel" (with el for "element"). Each voxel represents a sample of some quantity — density, colour, opacity — within a small cubic cell of a volumetric dataset, and the position of the voxel is implicit in its position in the grid.

Uses

Voxels are fundamental to medical and scientific imaging: CT, MRI and PET scanners produce volumetric data naturally stored as voxel grids, which volume-rendering techniques display as 3D images. They are also used in fluid and physics simulation, microscopy, and 3D printing. In video games, voxel engines render destructible terrain and block-built worlds — most famously Minecraft, in which the world is made of meter-scale cubic blocks — while heightmap-based "voxel terrain" was used in earlier titles such as Comanche and Delta Force.

Data structures

A raw voxel grid of resolution n³ grows cubically in memory, so large volumes are stored with compression, sparse voxel octrees that skip empty space, or brick-based streaming. Rendering techniques include ray casting, splatting, and conversion to polygon meshes using algorithms such as marching cubes.
