Rare-earth magnet

A rare-earth magnet is a strong permanent magnet made from alloys of rare-earth elements. Developed in the 1970s and 1980s, rare-earth magnets are the strongest type of permanent magnets made, producing significantly stronger magnetic fields than other types such as ferrite or alnico magnets.

Types

The two main types are samarium–cobalt (SmCo) magnets, developed first, and neodymium (NdFeB) magnets, developed by General Motors and Sumitomo in 1982–84. Neodymium magnets are stronger and cheaper but more vulnerable to corrosion and demagnetise at lower temperatures; samarium–cobalt magnets retain performance at high temperatures and resist corrosion, at higher cost.

Applications

Their high field strength allows smaller, lighter motors and generators. Applications include electric-vehicle traction motors, wind-turbine generators, hard disk drives, headphones, magnetic resonance imaging components, cordless tools and high-performance servomotors.

Hazards and supply

Large rare-earth magnets can pinch or shatter violently, and swallowed small magnets are a serious hazard to children. Because rare-earth mining and processing are concentrated in few countries, notably China, supply security for these magnets is a recurring strategic concern.
