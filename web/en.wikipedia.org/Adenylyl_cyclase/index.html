Adenylyl cyclase

Adenylyl cyclase (also known as adenylate cyclase or adenyl cyclase; EC 4.6.1.1) is an enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) to 3′,5′-cyclic AMP (cAMP) and pyrophosphate. cAMP is a crucial second messenger in signal transduction pathways.

Classes and regulation

Several classes of adenylyl cyclases exist. The class III enzymes found in eukaryotes are typically membrane proteins with twelve transmembrane segments and two cytosolic catalytic domains. In mammals there are nine membrane-bound isoforms (AC1–AC9) plus a soluble form (sAC). They are activated or inhibited by G proteins downstream of G protein-coupled receptors: Gαs stimulates, while Gαi inhibits. Calcium, calmodulin, and protein kinases further tune activity.

Biological importance

Through cAMP, adenylyl cyclases mediate the effects of many hormones and neurotransmitters, including adrenaline, glucagon, and dopamine. Bacterial toxins exploit the enzyme: cholera toxin locks Gαs in its active state, and pertussis toxin disables Gαi.
