Thomas Edward Yorke (born 7 October 1968) is an English musician who is the singer and main songwriter of the rock band Radiohead. A multi-instrumentalist, he mainly plays guitar and keyboards and is noted for his falsetto singing voice. Rolling Stone has described him as one of the greatest and most influential singers of his generation.

Life and career

Yorke was born in Wellingborough, Northamptonshire, and grew up in Oxfordshire. He was born with a paralysed left eye and underwent several operations in childhood, leaving him with a drooping eyelid (ptosis). At Abingdon School he formed a band, On a Friday, with schoolmates including Jonny and Colin Greenwood, Ed O'Brien and Philip Selway. After studying English and Fine Art at the University of Exeter, the band signed to Parlophone as Radiohead and achieved international success with the single "Creep" (1992) and albums including The Bends (1995), OK Computer (1997), Kid A (2000) and In Rainbows (2007).

Solo and other projects

Yorke's first solo album, The Eraser (2006), an electronic work produced by Nigel Godrich, was nominated for the Mercury Prize. He formed the supergroup Atoms for Peace with Godrich, Flea, Joey Waronker and Mauro Refosco to perform the songs live, releasing the album Amok in 2013. His second solo album, Tomorrow's Modern Boxes (2014), was released via BitTorrent and downloaded millions of times; his third, Anima (2019), was accompanied by a short film directed by Paul Thomas Anderson and reached number one in the UK. Yorke has also composed film music, including the score for Suspiria (2018). In 2021 he formed the band the Smile with Jonny Greenwood and drummer Tom Skinner, releasing the albums A Light for Attracting Attention (2022) and Wall of Eyes (2024). Radiohead were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019.

Activism and views

Yorke is a prominent campaigner on climate change and human rights, has criticised the music industry's treatment of artists and streaming royalties, and was a public supporter of campaigns such as Friends of the Earth's "The Big Ask". His lyrics frequently address technology, alienation, politics and environmental anxiety.
