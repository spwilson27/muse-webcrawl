Mount Rainier National Park

Mount Rainier National Park is a national park in Washington state, established on March 2, 1899 as the fifth national park in the United States. It encompasses 236,381 acres including all of Mount Rainier, a 14,410-foot (4,392 m) stratovolcano and the most heavily glaciated peak in the contiguous United States.

Features

The park contains 25 major glaciers, old-growth forest, subalpine wildflower meadows at Paradise and Sunrise, and some 418 km of trails including the 93-mile Wonderland Trail encircling the mountain. Historic districts include Longmire and the rustic "NPS Rustic" architecture of Paradise Inn (1917). Around two million visitors come each year; several thousand attempt the summit climb. The volcano is considered among the most dangerous in the world because of the lahar risk to the populated Puyallup River valley below.
