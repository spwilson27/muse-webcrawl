Merchants of death

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"Merchants of death" is an epithet applied to arms manufacturers and dealers accused of profiting from war and of encouraging conflict to increase sales. The phrase was popularized by the 1934 book Merchants of Death by H. C. Engelbrecht and F. C. Hanighen, an exposé of the international armaments industry published as the world moved toward another war.

The Nye Committee

The book and the wider "merchants of death" thesis fed into the work of the United States Senate's Nye Committee (1934–1936), chaired by Gerald Nye, which investigated the munitions industry and the financial interests that had drawn America into the First World War. The committee's hearings strengthened American isolationism and supported the Neutrality Acts of the 1930s.

Later usage

The phrase has since been applied to various industries — including later arms traders, and by extension, critics have used it for tobacco and other manufacturers whose products are linked to mass death. It remains a potent slogan in debates over the arms trade and the military–industrial complex.
