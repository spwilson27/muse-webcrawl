Galley proof

In printing and publication, a galley proof is a preliminary version of a publication meant for review by authors, editors, and proofreaders. Galley proofs may be uncut and unbound, or in some cases electronic. The term derives from the metal trays — galleys — used in letterpress printing to hold type after it had been set but before the space on the page was finalised.

Purpose

The primary purpose of a galley proof is to identify and correct errors in typesetting, spelling, grammar, and punctuation before the work is committed to its final format. Because corrections at the galley stage are relatively cheap compared to changes after plates or final layouts are made, galleys were traditionally checked with great care. In modern publishing the same role is filled by PDF proofs, and an advance reading copy or "bound galley" is used for reviewers and publicity.

Terminology

Related proof stages include page proofs, which show text arranged in its final pages, and press proofs. In scientific publishing, authors typically receive galley proofs of accepted articles and must approve corrections within a short deadline.
