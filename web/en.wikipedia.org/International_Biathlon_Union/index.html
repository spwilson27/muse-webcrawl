The International Biathlon Union (IBU) is the international governing body of biathlon, the winter sport combining cross-country skiing and rifle shooting. Its headquarters are in Salzburg, Austria. The IBU organises the Biathlon World Cup, the Biathlon World Championships and other international competitions, and it sets and enforces the rules of the sport.

History

Biathlon was governed from 1948 by the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM), later the UIPMB, which administered both modern pentathlon and biathlon. On 2 July 1993 the biathlon branch split off at a congress in London and formed the independent International Biathlon Union. The IBU was recognised by the International Olympic Committee, and biathlon has been an Olympic sport since 1960 (with women's events added in 1992).

Competitions

The IBU's principal events include the annual Biathlon World Cup circuit, the Biathlon World Championships (held in non-Olympic years), the second-tier IBU Cup, junior and youth championships, and summer biathlon events on roller skis. Event formats include the sprint, pursuit, individual, mass start and relay races.

Controversies

The federation faced a major governance crisis in 2018, when Austrian police raided its headquarters and its long-serving leadership was accused of corruption and of covering up doping cases, leading to resignations and reforms. Russian and Belarusian athletes were excluded from IBU events following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.
