Non-communicable disease

A non-communicable disease (NCD) is a disease that is not transmissible from person to person. NCDs are typically long in duration and progress slowly, resulting from a combination of genetic, physiological, environmental, and behavioural factors.

Major types and burden

The four main groups are cardiovascular diseases, cancers, chronic respiratory diseases, and diabetes. According to the World Health Organization, NCDs cause about 41 million deaths each year—roughly three-quarters of all deaths globally—with the majority of premature NCD deaths occurring in low- and middle-income countries.

Risk factors and prevention

Shared modifiable risk factors include tobacco use, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, harmful use of alcohol, and air pollution. Prevention strategies focus on reducing these risks, alongside early detection and affordable treatment. The United Nations and WHO have adopted global targets, including reducing premature NCD mortality by one third by 2030 under the Sustainable Development Goals.
