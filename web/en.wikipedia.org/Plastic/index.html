Plastics are a wide range of synthetic or semisynthetic materials that use polymers as a main ingredient. Their plasticity makes it possible for plastics to be moulded, extruded or pressed into solid objects of various shapes. This adaptability, plus a wide range of other properties, such as being lightweight, durable, flexible, and inexpensive to produce, has led to their widespread use. Plastics typically are made through human industrial processes; most modern plastics are derived from fossil fuel-based chemicals like natural gas or petroleum, although recent industrial methods use variants made from renewable materials, such as corn or cotton derivatives.

History

Natural plastics such as shellac and gutta-percha were used for centuries. In 1862 Alexander Parkes demonstrated Parkesine, an early synthetic polymer derived from cellulose. Bakelite, invented by Leo Baekeland in 1907, was the first fully synthetic plastic. The 1930s–1940s saw the commercialization of polystyrene, PVC, nylon (Wallace Carothers at DuPont), polyethylene, and PTFE (Teflon). Mass production after the Second World War transformed manufacturing, packaging, and consumer goods.

Types and uses

Common commodity plastics include polyethylene (bags, bottles), polypropylene (containers, fibres), polyvinyl chloride (pipes, insulation), polystyrene (foam packaging), polyethylene terephthalate (drink bottles), and polymethyl methacrylate (acrylic glass). Engineering plastics such as polycarbonate, ABS, and polyamides serve in automotive, electronics, and construction. Plastics are classed as thermoplastics, which soften on heating and can be remoulded, and thermosets, which cure irreversibly. Additives — plasticizers, flame retardants, stabilizers, colorants — tailor properties but can raise health and environmental concerns.

Environmental issues

Because most plastics degrade extremely slowly, plastic pollution has accumulated in landfills and oceans; microplastics have been detected throughout ecosystems and food webs. Only a minority of plastic waste is recycled — globally roughly 9 percent of all plastic ever made had been recycled as of the mid-2010s — the rest being incinerated, landfilled, or released into the environment. Responses include improved recycling, biodegradable and bio-based plastics, product redesign, deposit schemes, and international negotiations toward a global plastics treaty.
