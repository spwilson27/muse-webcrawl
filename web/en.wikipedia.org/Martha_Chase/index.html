Martha Chase

Martha Cowles Chase (November 30, 1927 – August 8, 2003) was an American geneticist who in 1952, with Alfred Hershey, conducted the Hershey–Chase experiments, which helped to confirm that DNA is the genetic material of life.

Early life and career

Chase was born in Cleveland Heights, Ohio. She graduated from the College of Wooster in 1950 and worked as a research assistant at Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, where she collaborated with Alfred Hershey. She later earned a PhD from the University of Southern California in 1964.

Hershey–Chase experiments

In 1952 Chase and Hershey used bacteriophages labelled with radioactive phosphorus and sulfur to show that DNA, not protein, entered bacterial cells during infection and carried the genetic information. The experiments provided strong evidence that DNA is the hereditary material. Hershey shared the 1969 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, while Chase's contribution was not similarly honoured.
