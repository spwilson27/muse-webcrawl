Failure rate

Failure rate is the frequency with which an engineered system or component fails, expressed in failures per unit of time. It is usually denoted by the Greek letter λ (lambda) and is of central importance in reliability engineering and safety engineering. A common unit is the FIT (failures in time), one failure per billion device-hours.

The bathtub curve

For many systems the failure rate over life follows a "bathtub curve": a high but decreasing rate during burn-in (early failures from defects), a long low and roughly constant rate during useful life (random failures), and a rising rate in wear-out. The failure rate is the hazard function of the lifetime distribution; for the exponential distribution it is constant, giving a mean time between failures (MTBF) equal to 1/λ.

Measurement

Failure rates are estimated from life testing, field data and prediction handbooks such as MIL-HDBK-217. For systems of components, rates combine according to the system architecture — for series systems the overall rate is approximately the sum of component rates.
