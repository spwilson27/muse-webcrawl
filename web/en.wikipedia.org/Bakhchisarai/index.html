Bakhchisarai (Ukrainian and Russian: Бахчисарай; Crimean Tatar: Bağçasaray) is a town in central Crimea, best known as the former capital of the Crimean Khanate and the site of the Khan's Palace.

History

The town was founded in 1532 by Khan Sahib I Giray, who built the Hansaray palace complex. Bakhchisarai remained the political center of the Crimean Khanate until the Russian annexation of Crimea in 1783. In 1944 the Crimean Tatar population was deported to Central Asia; Tatars returned in large numbers after 1989.

Khan's Palace

The Hansaray, built in the 16th century, combines Ottoman, Persian and Italian architectural elements and contains the famous Fountain of Tears, immortalized in Alexander Pushkin's poem "The Fountain of Bakhchisarai". Nearby are the cliff monastery of Uspensky and the cave city of Çufut Qale.
