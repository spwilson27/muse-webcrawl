Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-15

MiG-15 (NATO: Fagot)
Role: Fighter aircraft
Manufacturer: Mikoyan-Gurevich
First flight: 30 December 1947
Introduction: 1949
Number built: over 13,000 (plus licence production)

The Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-15 (NATO reporting name: Fagot) is a jet fighter aircraft developed by Mikoyan-Gurevich for the Soviet Union. It was one of the first successful swept-wing jet fighters and achieved fame in the skies over Korea, where it outclassed most straight-wing Western aircraft.

Design and development

The MiG-15 was powered by the Klimov VK-1 turbojet, a development of the Rolls-Royce Nene engine sold to the USSR. Its 35-degree swept wing and tailplane gave it transonic performance, with a maximum speed of about 1,075 km/h (668 mph). Armament typically consisted of two 23 mm cannons and one 37 mm cannon, optimised for destroying heavy bombers — a battery that was powerful but slow-firing and ill-suited to dogfighting.

Operational history

The type's combat debut came in the Korean War in November 1950, flown by Soviet, Chinese and North Korean pilots in the corridor known as "MiG Alley." Its only real peer was the North American F-86 Sabre. A defection in 1953 brought an intact MiG-15 into American hands under Operation Moolah. The MiG-15 also saw action in the Suez Crisis and numerous Cold War conflicts, and the two-seat MiG-15UTI served for decades as a jet trainer.

Production

Built in the USSR and under licence in Czechoslovakia, Poland and China (as the Shenyang J-2), total production is often estimated above 18,000 aircraft, making it one of the most heavily produced jet aircraft in history.
