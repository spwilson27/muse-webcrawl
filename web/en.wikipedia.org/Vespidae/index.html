The Vespidae are a large, diverse family of wasps, with nearly 5,000 described species worldwide. The family includes almost all of the familiar social wasps — paper wasps, yellowjackets, and hornets — as well as many solitary species such as potter and mason wasps.

Subfamilies and behaviour

The social subfamilies are the Polistinae (paper wasps), Vespinae (yellowjackets and hornets), and Stenogastrinae (hover wasps of Southeast Asia). Social vespids build nests of wood fibre chewed into paper, live in colonies headed by a queen, and can sting repeatedly in defence of the nest. Solitary subfamilies such as the Eumeninae (potter wasps) build mud nests and provision them with paralysed prey for their larvae.

Biology

Most vespids are predators of other insects, making them important controllers of pest species, though adults also feed on nectar and contribute to pollination. Vespid wings fold longitudinally at rest, a distinctive trait of the family. Some species, notably the German and common yellowjackets, have become invasive pests outside their native ranges.
