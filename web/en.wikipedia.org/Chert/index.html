Chert

Chert is a hard, fine-grained sedimentary rock composed of microcrystalline or cryptocrystalline quartz (silica, SiO2). It occurs as nodules, concretions and layered beds within limestone, dolomite and other sedimentary rocks, and ranges in colour from white and grey to brown, red and black.

Formation

Chert forms principally by the accumulation and diagenesis of siliceous organisms such as diatoms, radiolarians and sponges on the sea floor, or by the replacement of carbonate rock by silica carried in groundwater. Precambrian banded iron formations contain thick beds of chert, and famous fossil-bearing deposits such as the Rhynie chert of Scotland and the Gunflint chert of Canada preserve some of the oldest evidence of life on Earth.

Uses

Because it fractures conchoidally and produces sharp edges, chert was one of the principal materials for stone tools throughout prehistory. The variety known as flint was used for arrowheads, knives and scrapers and later for striking sparks in flintlock firearms. Today chert is used mainly as a construction aggregate and road material.
