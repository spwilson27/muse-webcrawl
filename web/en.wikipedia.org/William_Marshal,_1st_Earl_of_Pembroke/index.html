William Marshal, 1st Earl of Pembroke

William Marshal, 1st Earl of Pembroke (c. 1146 – 14 May 1219), also called William the Marshal, was an Anglo-Norman soldier and statesman widely celebrated as the greatest knight of the Middle Ages. Rising from landless younger son to regent of England, he served four kings: Henry II, Richard I, John, and Henry III.

Life

As a young knight-errant, Marshal made his fortune on the tournament circuit of France, where he was reputed never to have been defeated. He served Eleanor of Aquitaine and her son Henry the Young King, was with Henry II at his death in 1189, and by marriage to the heiress Isabel de Clare acquired the earldom of Pembroke and great estates in Wales and Ireland, founding towns and castles including Kilkenny and the rebuilt Chepstow. Loyal to King John to the end, he was made regent for the nine-year-old Henry III in 1216, reissued Magna Carta under his own seal, and personally led the royalist army — at over 70 years of age — to victory at the Battle of Lincoln in 1217, saving the young king's throne.

Marshal died at Caversham in 1219 and was buried in the Temple Church, London. His biography, L'Histoire de Guillaume le Maréchal, commissioned by his son, is the earliest surviving biography of a medieval knight.
