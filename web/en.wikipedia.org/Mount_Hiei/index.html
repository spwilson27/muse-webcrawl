Mount Hiei

Mount Hiei (比叡山, Hiei-zan)
Elevation: 848 m
Location: border of Kyoto and Shiga prefectures, Japan
Known for: Enryaku-ji, head temple of Tendai Buddhism

Mount Hiei is a mountain to the northeast of Kyoto, on the border between Kyoto and Shiga prefectures in Japan. It is home to Enryaku-ji, the head temple of the Tendai school of Japanese Buddhism, founded by the monk Saichō in 788. Because it stands northeast of the imperial capital — the direction from which malign influences were believed to come — the mountain and its temple were regarded as spiritual protectors of Kyoto and the state.

History

Enryaku-ji grew into one of the most powerful institutions in medieval Japan: many founders of later Japanese Buddhist schools, including Hōnen, Shinran, Dōgen, Eisai and Nichiren, trained there. Its warrior monks (sōhei) wielded enough military power to threaten the capital, provoking repeated conflicts. In 1571 the warlord Oda Nobunaga, exasperated by the temple's support for his enemies, burned Enryaku-ji to the ground and massacred its inhabitants; the temple was rebuilt under the Tokugawa shogunate. Mount Hiei is also famous for the "marathon monks" (kaihōgyō) of the Tendai tradition, whose thousand-day walking practice around the mountain is among the most demanding ascetic feats in Buddhism. The mountain is accessible by cable cars from both the Kyoto (Eizan) and Shiga (Sakamoto) sides.
