Patient safety

Patient safety is a discipline within health care concerned with preventing and reducing risks, errors, and harm that occur to patients during the provision of care. It emerged as a distinct field after reports in the late 1990s — notably the Institute of Medicine's To Err Is Human (1999) — estimated that tens of thousands of hospital deaths each year in the United States resulted from preventable medical errors.

Sources of harm

Common safety problems include medication errors, healthcare-associated infections, surgical complications, diagnostic errors, falls, pressure injuries, and errors in transitions of care. Research emphasises that most harm arises from failures of systems and processes rather than from individual incompetence.

Interventions

  • Surgical safety checklists, such as the WHO Safe Surgery Checklist
  • Computerised physician order entry and barcode medication administration
  • Standardised handovers and communication tools (e.g. SBAR)
  • Incident reporting systems and root-cause analysis
  • Hand hygiene and infection-control bundles

Organisations

The World Health Organization coordinates global patient-safety initiatives, including World Patient Safety Day (17 September). National bodies such as the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality in the US promote safety culture and measurement.
