Peter Grimes

The Suffolk coast at Aldeburgh, the setting of the opera.

Peter Grimes, Op. 33, is an opera in three acts by Benjamin Britten, with a libretto by Montagu Slater based on the narrative poem "Peter Grimes" from George Crabbe's collection The Borough (1810). It premiered on 7 June 1945 at Sadler's Wells Theatre in London, conducted by Reginald Goodall with Peter Pears in the title role, and its success re-established British opera on the international stage.

Synopsis

In a bleak fishing village on the Suffolk coast, the fisherman Peter Grimes is hauled before an inquest after his apprentice dies at sea. Though the death is ruled accidental, the Borough turns against him. Grimes hopes to win respectability through wealth and marriage to the widowed schoolmistress Ellen Orford, and takes on a new workhouse apprentice, John. Ignoring Ellen's concern over the boy's bruises, Grimes drives him relentlessly; when the boy falls from the cliff hut to his death while setting out to sea, Grimes is destroyed. The village manhunt closes in, and the retired sea captain Balstrode advises Grimes to sail out and sink his boat. The opera ends with the village resuming its daily round, indifferent, as Grimes's boat goes down offshore.

Music

The score is celebrated for its vivid orchestral depiction of the sea. The four "Sea Interludes" — "Dawn", "Sunday Morning", "Moonlight", and the storm "Passacaglia" sequence among them — are frequently performed as a concert suite. Britten uses the interludes to reflect both the sea's changing moods and Grimes's inner state, setting the outsider-protagonist against the hypocritical chorus of villagers.

Reception and legacy

Peter Grimes is regarded as one of the greatest operas of the 20th century and a landmark of English music. The role of Grimes has been associated with Peter Pears, for whom it was written, and later with tenors including Jon Vickers. The opera is set in Aldeburgh, Britten's home town, where a sculpture honoring the work stands on the beach and where the Aldeburgh Festival, founded by Britten and Pears, continues to perform it.
