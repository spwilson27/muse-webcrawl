The Ritz London

The Ritz London

Location: 150 Piccadilly, London

Opened: 1906

Owner: The Barclay brothers' successors

Style: Louis XVI neoclassical

The Ritz London is a luxury hotel in London, situated at 150 Piccadilly in Mayfair, overlooking Green Park. Opened in 1906 by the Swiss hotelier César Ritz, it is widely regarded as one of the great hotels of the world and a byword for luxury, giving the English language the words "ritzy" and "to put on the Ritz".

History

César Ritz, who had earlier established the Hôtel Ritz Paris, planned a London counterpart built to the same lavish standards. The hotel was designed by the architects Mewès and Davis in the French château style with Louis XVI interiors. It opened on 24 May 1906. During the Second World War it became a meeting place for politicians and royalty in exile. In 1995 the hotel was bought by the Barclay brothers for £80 million, and it was later sold to a Qatari investor in 2020.

Facilities

The hotel has 136 rooms and suites. Its Palm Court is famous for its afternoon tea, served in an opulent Louis XVI setting; the Ritz Restaurant, with its garland chandeliers and painted ceiling, holds a Michelin star. The Rivoli Bar is decorated in Art Deco style.

Cultural references

The hotel features in the Irving Berlin song "Puttin' On the Ritz" and has hosted guests from Charlie Chaplin to Winston Churchill. Afternoon tea at the Ritz remains a quintessential London experience, with a formal dress code.
