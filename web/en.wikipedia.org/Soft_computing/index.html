Soft computing

Soft computing is an approach to computation that tolerates imprecision, uncertainty, partial truth, and approximation in order to achieve tractable, robust, low-cost solutions, in contrast to "hard" (conventional) computing, which demands precise models and exact answers. The term was introduced by Lotfi A. Zadeh, founder of fuzzy logic, who described soft computing as a partnership of complementary methodologies unified by the guiding principle of exploiting tolerance for imprecision.

Components

The principal constituents are fuzzy logic (reasoning with degrees of truth and linguistic variables), neurocomputing (artificial neural networks that learn from data), evolutionary computation (genetic algorithms and related population-based search), and probabilistic reasoning (including Bayesian networks and chaos theory in some accounts). Hybrids such as neuro-fuzzy systems (e.g., ANFIS) and evolutionary neural networks combine these strengths: learning ability, knowledge representation through fuzzy rules, and optimization.

Applications

Soft computing techniques have been applied in control systems (the famous Sendai subway fuzzy controller, appliance control in rice cookers and washing machines, camera autofocus), pattern recognition and handwriting recognition, consumer electronics, robotics, decision support, data mining, and forecasting. Zadeh argued that soft computing underlies much of machine intelligence as systems move from precise numerical computation toward perception-based, human-like reasoning — a perspective reflected in the field's journals and the World Congress on Computational Intelligence.
