9-j symbol

In physics, the 9-j symbol is a quantity used in quantum mechanics to describe the coupling of four angular momenta. It generalizes the 6-j symbol and arises when coupling three angular momenta in two different orders, or when four angular momentum eigenstates are combined.

Definition

The 9-j symbol is written as a 3×3 array enclosed in braces, containing nine angular momentum quantum numbers j1 through j9. It is related to a sum over a product of three 6-j symbols, and can also be expressed in terms of Wigner 3-j symbols.

Properties

The 9-j symbol is invariant under reflection of the array in either diagonal and under even permutations of its rows or columns; odd permutations introduce a phase factor. Interchanging two rows or two columns multiplies the symbol by (−1)S, where S is the sum of all nine entries.

Applications

9-j symbols appear in atomic and nuclear spectroscopy, in the theory of the fractional parentage coefficients, and in recoupling calculations for multi-particle states.
