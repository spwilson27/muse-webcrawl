Science fiction studies

Science fiction studies is the academic discipline that studies science fiction literature, film, television, and related media. It emerged as a recognised scholarly field in the second half of the 20th century, building on both fan criticism and literary scholarship.

History and key figures

Early critical work appeared in fan and semi-professional venues, while landmark academic studies include J. O. Bailey's Pilgrims Through Space and Time (1947) and Kingsley Amis's New Maps of Hell (1960). Darko Suvin's Metamorphoses of Science Fiction (1979) supplied an influential definition of the genre around the concept of "cognitive estrangement" and the novum. Other major scholars include Fredric Jameson, Carl Freedman, Istvan Csicsery-Ronay Jr., and Veronica Hollinger.

Journals and institutions

The field's principal journals are Science Fiction Studies (founded 1973), Extrapolation (founded 1959, the oldest journal in the field), and Foundation: The International Review of Science Fiction (founded 1972). The Science Fiction Research Association (founded 1970) is the principal professional body. Universities now offer dedicated courses and degrees, and major research collections of SF manuscripts and fanzines are held by several university libraries.
