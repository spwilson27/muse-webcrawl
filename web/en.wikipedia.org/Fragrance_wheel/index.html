Fragrance wheel

The fragrance wheel is a circular diagram used in perfumery to classify scents into families and show the relationships between them. It was created in 1983 by perfumer Michael Edwards and has become a standard reference tool in the fragrance industry.

Structure

Edwards' wheel organizes fragrances into four main families arranged around a circle: Floral, Fresh, Woody (originally called Oriental's neighbours in early versions), and Oriental (now often called Amber). Between these poles lie blended subfamilies such as Soft Floral, Floral Oriental, Soft Oriental, Woody Oriental, Mossy Woods (Chypre), Dry Woods, Citrus, Green, and Water. Subfamilies adjacent on the wheel tend to blend well, while those opposite one another contrast most strongly.

History and revisions

Before Edwards' system, fragrance classification was inconsistent across houses and retailers. The wheel first appeared in his Fragrance Manual and has been revised several times, notably in 1992 and 1998, with terminology updated over the years — for example, "Oriental" was renamed "Amber" in the 2021 edition to retire outdated terminology. The Fragrances of the World database, built on the wheel, classifies thousands of perfumes launched each year.

Use

Retailers use the wheel to guide customers toward fragrances similar to ones they already enjoy, and perfumers use it as a shared vocabulary for describing scent character.
