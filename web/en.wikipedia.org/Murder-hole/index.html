Murder hole

A murder hole above a castle gateway

A murder hole or meurtrière is a hole in the ceiling of a gateway or passageway in a fortification through which defenders could shoot, throw, or pour harmful substances on attackers below. They were typically located above the entrance, between the portcullis and the inner gate, trapping attackers in a confined space.

Use

Through murder holes defenders could drop stones, boiling water (and, contrary to popular myth, rarely boiling oil, which was expensive), hot sand, quicklime, or arrows onto enemies who had breached the outer gate. The attacker, confined in the passage, had little protection or ability to retaliate upward.

Relation to machicolations

Murder holes are the interior counterpart of machicolations — openings in the floors of projecting galleries on the exterior of castle walls through which missiles could be dropped on attackers at the base of the wall.
