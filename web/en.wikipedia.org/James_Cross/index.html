James Richard Cross CMG (29 September 1921 – 6 January 2021) was a British diplomat who served as the British Trade Commissioner in Montreal. He is best known for having been kidnapped by the Front de libération du Québec (FLQ) during Canada's October Crisis of 1970.

Early life and career

Cross was born in Nenagh, Ireland, to English parents and was educated at the Royal Military Academy, Woolwich. He served in the British Army during the Second World War, rising to the rank of lieutenant colonel; he fought in North Africa and at Monte Cassino and ended the war involved in the liberation of concentration camps. After the war he joined the British diplomatic service, serving in India, Germany and elsewhere before his posting to Montreal in 1968.

Kidnapping

On 5 October 1970, members of the FLQ's Libération cell, disguised as delivery men, abducted Cross from his Montreal home. He was held for 59 days, chained and guarded, while the FLQ issued demands including the release of "political prisoners" and a ransom. Five days after Cross's abduction, another cell kidnapped Quebec labour minister Pierre Laporte, who was later murdered. Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau invoked the War Measures Act.

On 3 December 1970, police located the house where Cross was being held in Montreal North. His release was negotiated in exchange for safe passage to Cuba for his captors. Cross was freed unharmed, apart from weight loss and stress.

Later life

Cross returned to Britain and worked in government and academic administration; he was appointed a Companion of the Order of St Michael and St George (CMG). He wrote a memoir of his captivity and returned to Canada in 1991 for a CBC documentary. He died on 6 January 2021 at his home in Seaford, East Sussex, aged 99, having contracted COVID-19 during the pandemic.
