Sinosauropteryx (meaning "Chinese reptilian wing") is a compsognathid dinosaur genus. Described in 1996, it was the first dinosaur taxon outside of Avialae (birds and their immediate relatives) to be found with evidence of feathers. It was covered with very simple filamentous down-like feathers. Structures that indicate colouration have been preserved in some of its feathers, which makes Sinosauropteryx the first dinosaur for which colouration has been estimated from fossil evidence.

Description

Sinosauropteryx was a small theropod, about 1 metre long including its very long tail, with short forelimbs and long hind legs. Fossils come from the Yixian Formation of Liaoning, China, dating to the Barremian–Aptian stages of the Early Cretaceous. Preserved stomach contents include small mammals and lizards.

Feathers and colour

The filaments surrounding the body were interpreted as primitive feathers composed of simple branched filaments, comparable to stage I–II feathers in modern birds. In 2010, analysis of fossil melanosomes indicated that the tail carried alternating bands of chestnut (rufous) and white, and that the body was countershaded with a darker back and lighter belly — an early landmark in palaeo-colour reconstruction. Some workers have questioned whether the filaments represent feathers or degraded collagen, but the melanosome evidence supports the feather interpretation.
