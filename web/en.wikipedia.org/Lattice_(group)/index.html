Lattice (group)

In geometry and group theory, a lattice is a discrete subgroup of Euclidean space that spans the space. More precisely, a lattice in ℝⁿ is a subgroup of the form ℤb₁ + ⋯ + ℤbₙ, where b₁, …, bₙ is a basis of ℝⁿ. Lattices are infinite periodic arrangements of points, generalising the integer grid.

Examples

The integer lattice ℤⁿ is the simplest example. In two dimensions, lattices include square, rectangular, and hexagonal (triangular) arrangements. In crystallography, the 14 Bravais lattices classify all lattice types in three dimensions by symmetry.

Fundamental domain and covolume

Dividing space by the translation symmetries of a lattice gives a quotient torus ℝⁿ/L whose volume, called the covolume or determinant of the lattice, equals the volume of a fundamental parallelepiped. Different bases can generate the same lattice; they are related by unimodular transformations (matrices in GL(n, ℤ) with determinant ±1).

Applications

Lattices are central to crystallography, sphere packing, coding theory, and number theory. Shortest-vector and closest-vector problems on lattices underpin lattice-based cryptography, a leading family of post-quantum cryptographic schemes. Minkowski's theorem on convex bodies is a foundational result of the geometry of numbers.
