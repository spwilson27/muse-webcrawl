Violin technique
Covers: posture, left-hand positions and shifting, vibrato, bowing (détaché, legato, staccato, spiccato, martelé), double stops, harmonics, pizzicato

Violin technique comprises the physical methods used to play the violin: holding the instrument and bow, stopping the strings with the left hand, and drawing or striking the strings with the bow to produce the range of articulations demanded by the repertoire.

Left hand

The left hand stops the strings in a series of positions along the fingerboard; shifting moves the hand between positions. Vibrato — a regular oscillation of the stopping finger, from the wrist, arm or finger — warms the tone. Advanced techniques include double stops (two strings at once), chords, harmonics, left-hand pizzicato and glissando.

Bowing

Most expression comes from the bow. Basic strokes include legato (smooth, connected), détaché (separate strokes), staccato (short, stopped), martelé (hammered), and off-the-string strokes such as spiccato and sautillé. Col legno (striking with the wood), sul ponticello (bowing near the bridge) and sul tasto (over the fingerboard) colour the sound. Pizzicato — plucking with the right hand — is used when the bow is set aside.
