The Russian Navy (Russian: Военно-морской флот, Voyenno-morskoy flot) is the naval arm of the Russian Armed Forces. Its origins lie in the Imperial Russian Navy founded by Peter the Great in 1696; the present service was formed in 1992 from the remnants of the Soviet Navy after the dissolution of the USSR.

Organisation

The navy comprises surface forces, submarine forces, naval aviation, coastal troops (including naval infantry) and support services. Operationally it is divided into four fleets — the Northern Fleet (headquartered at Severomorsk), the Pacific Fleet (Vladivostok), the Baltic Fleet (Kaliningrad/Baltiysk) and the Black Sea Fleet (Sevastopol) — plus the Caspian Flotilla.

History

Peter the Great built Russia's first regular navy to contest Swedish power in the Baltic. The Imperial Navy later fought in the Russo-Turkish wars, the Crimean War and the Russo-Japanese War, where its Pacific and Baltic fleets were destroyed at Tsushima in 1905. The Soviet Navy expanded into a global blue-water force during the Cold War, centred on nuclear submarines and anti-carrier missile forces. After 1991, funding collapse shrank the fleet drastically; a rearmament programme from the 2010s emphasised Borei-class ballistic missile submarines and Kalibr-armed frigates and corvettes.

Role in the war in Ukraine

Following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, the Black Sea Fleet suffered notable losses to Ukrainian missiles and uncrewed surface vessels, including the sinking of its flagship, the cruiser Moskva, in April 2022, and subsequently dispersed much of its strength from Sevastopol to Novorossiysk.
