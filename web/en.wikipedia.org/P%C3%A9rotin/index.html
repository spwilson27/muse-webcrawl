Pérotin

Floruitc. 1200
OriginParis, France
EraNotre Dame school; Ars antiqua

Pérotin (fl. c. 1200), also called Perotinus Magnus or Pérotin the Great, was a European composer of the late 12th and early 13th centuries, associated with the Notre Dame school of polyphony in Paris. Almost nothing is known of his life; he is identified principally through the writings of the music theorist known as Anonymous IV.

Works

Anonymous IV credited Pérotin with revising the Magnus liber organi of Léonin and composing organa in three and four voices — the first known four-voice polyphonic compositions in Western music. His surviving works include the quadrupla Viderunt omnes and Sederunt principes, written for Christmas and Saint Stephen's Day, along with three-voice organa and conductus such as Beata viscera.

Style and significance

Pérotin's music uses modal rhythm and extensive discant passages in which voices move together in measured counterpoint over a sustained chant tenor. His expansion of organum to three and four voices was a landmark in the development of Western polyphony.
