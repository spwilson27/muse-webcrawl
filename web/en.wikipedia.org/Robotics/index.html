Robotics

Robotics is an interdisciplinary branch of technology and science including mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, information engineering, computer science, and others. Robotics deals with the design, construction, operation, and use of robots, as well as computer systems for their control, sensory feedback, and information processing.

These technologies are used to develop machines that can substitute for humans in hazardous environments or manufacturing processes, or resemble humans in appearance, behavior, or cognition. The concept of creating machines that can operate autonomously dates back to classical times, but research into the functionality and potential uses of robots did not grow substantially until the 20th century.

History

In 1948 and 1949, William Grey Walter, a neurologist and roboticist at the Burden Neurological Institute at Bristol, England, created the first electronic autonomous robots, named Elmer and Elsie. In 1954, George Devol invented the first digitally operated and programmable robot, the Unimate, which laid the foundation of the modern robotics industry. The field took its name from "robot", a word coined in Karel Čapek's 1920 play R.U.R.; Isaac Asimov popularized the term "robotics" in his science fiction stories of the 1940s and formulated the Three Laws of Robotics.

Components

Robotic systems are composed of a mechanical structure, actuators that produce motion, sensors that measure the environment and the robot's state, and a control system that coordinates them. Power sources include electric batteries, hydraulic systems, and pneumatic systems. End effectors—grippers, tools, or hands—interact with the world.

Fields and applications

Robotics research areas include kinematics and dynamics, control theory, machine vision, artificial intelligence, human–robot interaction, swarm robotics, and soft robotics. Applications span manufacturing, medicine, agriculture, logistics, space exploration, military operations, and domestic services. See also robot and robot navigation.

Education and competitions

Robotics is taught in schools and universities worldwide, and competitions such as FIRST Robotics, RoboCup, and the DARPA Robotics Challenge have promoted advances in autonomous systems and public interest in the field.
