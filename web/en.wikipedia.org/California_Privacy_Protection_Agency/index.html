California Privacy Protection Agency

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) is a state agency charged with implementing and enforcing the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA). Established by voter approval of Proposition 24 in November 2020, it is the first dedicated privacy regulator in the United States.

Structure

The agency is governed by a five-member board appointed by the governor, attorney general, Senate Rules Committee, and Assembly Speaker. Its first executive director, Ashkan Soltani, was appointed in 2021.

Powers

The CPPA writes regulations, conducts audits, and brings administrative enforcement actions, powers largely transferred from the Attorney General on 1 July 2023. It has issued rules on opt-out preference signals, cybersecurity audits, automated decision-making, and risk assessments, and levied settlements over data practices and data-broker registration.

Significance

As a specialised regulator akin to European data protection authorities, the agency's actions influence privacy practice nationally, since many companies apply California rules across the US.
