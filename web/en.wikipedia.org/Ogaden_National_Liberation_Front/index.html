Ogaden National Liberation Front

Ogaden National Liberation Front (ONLF)
Founded: 1984
Active regions: Somali Region, Ethiopia (Ogaden)
Ideology: Somali self-determination, Ogaden nationalism
Status: Peace agreement with Ethiopia signed 2018

The Ogaden National Liberation Front (ONLF; Somali: Jabhada Wadaniga Xoreynta Ogaadeenya) is a social and political movement seeking self-determination for ethnic Somalis in the Ogaden (Somali Region) of eastern Ethiopia. Founded in 1984, it waged an intermittent insurgency against the Ethiopian government for over three decades.

History

The ONLF was founded by former members and sympathisers of the Western Somali Liberation Front following Somalia's defeat in the Ogaden War of 1977–78. It began armed operations in 1994 after the ruling coalition in the Somali Region fell out with Addis Ababa, and the Ethiopian government designated it a terrorist organisation, as did critics who blamed it for attacks including the 2007 raid on an oil exploration site at Abole, which killed 74 people and triggered a major military counterinsurgency.

Peace process

After the reformist government of Abiy Ahmed came to power in 2018, the ONLF was removed from Ethiopia's terrorist list and declared a ceasefire. In October 2018 it signed a peace agreement with the Ethiopian government at Asmara, Eritrea, ending the insurgency and allowing the movement to pursue its aims politically; its fighters were subsequently demobilised.
