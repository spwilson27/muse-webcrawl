John Tracy Kidder (born November 12, 1945) is an American writer of literary nonfiction. He received the Pulitzer Prize for General Nonfiction, the National Book Award, and the Robert F. Kennedy Award for his 1981 book The Soul of a New Machine, an account of engineers racing to design a new computer at Data General Corporation.

Career

Kidder graduated from Harvard University and served in the United States Navy in Vietnam. His immersive, character-driven reporting follows subjects over long periods. House (1985) documents the building of a house; Among Schoolchildren (1989) follows a fifth-grade teacher; Mountains Beyond Mountains (2003) profiles physician Paul Farmer and his work with Partners In Health in Haiti and elsewhere. Later works include Strength in What Remains (2009) and Rough Sleepers (2023), about a doctor caring for homeless people in Boston.

Style

Kidder is associated with the New Journalism and creative nonfiction movements, combining novelistic scene-setting with rigorous factual reporting.
