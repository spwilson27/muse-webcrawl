Mary Ashford

Mary Ashford (1797–1817) was an English domestic servant whose murder near Erdington, Warwickshire, became one of the most notorious criminal cases of the Regency era, and led to the last successful use of a medieval legal procedure in England.

The case

On 27 May 1817 Ashford attended a dance with Abraham Thornton, a bricklayer. She was found drowned in a pit the following morning. Thornton was tried for murder and acquitted by a jury in only six minutes, a verdict that caused public outrage.

Appeal of murder and trial by battle

Ashford's brother William brought a private "appeal of murder" against Thornton. When the case came before the Court of King's Bench in 1818, Thornton claimed his right to wager of battle — trial by combat — and threw down his gauntlet in court. The court upheld the right, the Ashford family declined the combat, and Thornton was discharged. Parliament abolished appeals of murder and trial by battle in 1819.
