Robert Y. Hayne

Born: Robert Young Hayne, 10 November 1791, St. Paul's Parish, South Carolina, U.S.
Died: 24 September 1839, Asheville, North Carolina
Offices: U.S. Senator (1823–1832); Governor of South Carolina (1832–1834); Mayor of Charleston

Robert Young Hayne (November 10, 1791 – September 24, 1839) was an American lawyer and statesman from South Carolina. He served as a United States senator, as the 54th governor of South Carolina, and as mayor of Charleston. He is best remembered for the Webster–Hayne debate of 1830, in which he defended states' rights and nullification against Daniel Webster's defence of the Union.

Early career

Hayne studied law and was admitted to the bar in 1812. He served in the South Carolina House of Representatives, became state attorney general in 1818, and was elected to the U.S. Senate in 1823. In the Senate he emerged as a leading spokesman for the South, advocating low tariffs and states' rights.

Webster–Hayne debate

In January 1830, a resolution by Senator Samuel Foot of Connecticut led to a famous debate on the nature of the Union. Hayne argued that the Constitution was a compact among sovereign states, which retained the right to nullify unconstitutional federal laws. Webster's reply, concluding with "Liberty and Union, now and forever, one and inseparable," became one of the most celebrated speeches in American history.

Nullification crisis

Hayne resigned from the Senate in 1832 to become governor of South Carolina during the Nullification Crisis, when the state declared the federal tariffs of 1828 and 1832 null and void. As governor and major general of state forces, he prepared to defend the state against federal coercion, though the crisis was defused by Henry Clay's compromise tariff of 1833. He later served as mayor of Charleston and promoted railroad development in the South before his death in 1839.
