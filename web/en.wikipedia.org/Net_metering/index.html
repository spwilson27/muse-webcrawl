Net metering

Net metering (or net energy metering, NEM) is an electricity billing mechanism that allows consumers who generate some or all of their own electricity — commonly with rooftop solar panels — to use that electricity anytime, and to export surplus generation to the grid in exchange for credits.

How it works

A bidirectional meter records both consumption and export. Customers are billed only for their net energy usage over the billing period; excess credits may roll over or be compensated at rates set by regulation.

Policy debate

Net metering has been credited with accelerating distributed solar adoption but debated over cost-shifting to non-solar customers. Many jurisdictions have moved to successor schemes (net billing) that compensate exports at lower, avoided-cost or time-varying rates; California's NEM 3.0 decision of 2022 is a prominent example.
