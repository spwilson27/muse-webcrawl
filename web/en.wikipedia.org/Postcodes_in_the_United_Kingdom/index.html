Postcodes in the United Kingdom

Postcodes in the United Kingdom, known as post codes or postal codes, are alphanumeric codes used by Royal Mail to route and sort mail. A full postcode identifies a small group of addresses — on average about 15 properties — and consists of an outward code and an inward code separated by a space, for example SW1A 1AA (Buckingham Palace).

Format

The outward code (outcode) has two to four characters: a postcode area of one or two letters (e.g. SW for south-west London, EH for Edinburgh) followed by a district number or number-and-letter. The inward code (incode) has three characters: a digit followed by two letters identifying the sector and the individual unit. Valid formats include A9 9AA, A99 9AA, AA9 9AA, AA99 9AA, A9A 9AA, and AA9A 9AA. The special code GIR 0AA was used for the Girobank.

History

London was divided into postal districts in 1857–58 (EC, WC, N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW), and other large cities followed. The modern national postcode system was developed following experiments in Norwich in 1959, rolled out by the Post Office under Tony Benn's tenure, and completed across the country by 1974. There are 124 postcode areas in the UK.

Uses beyond mail

Postcodes are widely used for navigation, insurance rating, credit checks, demographic analysis, and statistical geography; the Office for National Statistics maintains hierarchies linking postcodes to administrative areas. The full address database, the Postcode Address File (PAF), is maintained by Royal Mail and licensed commercially; the simpler Code-Point product provides coordinates for each postcode.
