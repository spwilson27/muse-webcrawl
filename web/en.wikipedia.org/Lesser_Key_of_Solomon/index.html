Lesser Key of Solomon

The Lesser Key of Solomon, also known as the Lemegeton or Clavicula Salomonis Regis, is an anonymous grimoire (book of magic) compiled in the mid-17th century from materials dating back several centuries. It describes the summoning and control of spirits and is attributed in legend to King Solomon.

Contents

The first and most famous book, the Ars Goetia, catalogues 72 demons said to have been commanded by Solomon, giving their ranks, seals, appearances and powers. The remaining books deal with aerial spirits, angels of the hours, and the Almadel, a wax altar used to contact angels.

Influence

The Lesser Key became one of the most influential texts of Western ceremonial magic. Aleister Crowley published an English edition of the Goetia in 1904, and the demon catalogue continues to appear in occult works, games and popular fiction.
