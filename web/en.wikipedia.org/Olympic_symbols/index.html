The Olympic symbols are the icons, flags, emblems, and rituals used by the International Olympic Committee to represent the Olympic Games and the Olympic Movement. The best known is the Olympic rings emblem.

The rings

Designed by Pierre de Coubertin in 1913, the symbol consists of five interlaced rings — blue, yellow, black, green, and red — on a white field. Coubertin intended the six colours (including white) to represent the colours appearing on the flags of all competing nations at the time, and the rings to symbolize the inhabited continents and the universality of Olympism. Contrary to popular belief, no individual ring colour is officially assigned to a specific continent.

Flag, flame, and torch relay

The Olympic flag was first flown at the 1920 Antwerp Games. The Olympic flame, kindled at Olympia in Greece using a parabolic mirror, is carried in a torch relay to the host city and lights the stadium cauldron for the duration of the Games — a ritual introduced at Amsterdam 1928 (flame) and Berlin 1936 (relay).

Motto, creed, and anthem

The Olympic motto is Citius, Altius, Fortius ("Faster, Higher, Stronger"), amended in 2021 to add Communiter ("Together"). The Olympic Hymn, composed by Spyridon Samaras with lyrics by Kostis Palamas for the 1896 Games, is played at ceremonies. Mascots, medals, and pictograms for each edition form further familiar symbols.
