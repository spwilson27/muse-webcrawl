Language contact

Language contact occurs when speakers of two or more languages or varieties interact with and influence each other. The study of language contact is a branch of sociolinguistics and historical linguistics. Outcomes range from simple borrowing of words to the creation of entirely new languages.

Outcomes

Common outcomes include loanwords (lexical borrowing), borrowing of grammatical features, code-switching and code-mixing, language shift, and the formation of contact languages: pidgins (simplified codes for limited communication), creoles (pidgins acquired by children as first languages), and mixed languages. Extensive contact within a region can create a Sprachbund — a linguistic area such as the Balkans, whose languages share features not inherited from a common ancestor.

Factors

The intensity of contact, relative prestige and numbers of speakers, and social attitudes determine how much influence flows between languages. Language contact is a major driver of language change and also a cause of language endangerment, when speakers shift to a dominant language.

Examples

The Norman French influence on English after 1066, the extensive Turkic, Persian and Arabic vocabulary in languages of the Balkans and South Asia, and the emergence of creoles in the Caribbean and Pacific are classic examples.
