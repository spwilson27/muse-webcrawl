The Plymouth Company was an English joint-stock company founded in 1606 by James I as part of the Virginia Company, chartered with the purpose of establishing settlements on the coast of North America. It is distinct from the later automobile manufacturer of the same name, and from the Plymouth Colony founded in 1620, which despite the similarity of names was organised under a separate patent.

Charter

Under the Charter of 1606, the Virginia Company was divided into two branches. The London Company (the Virginia Company of London) received rights between latitudes 34° and 41° N, while the Plymouth Company, backed by merchants of Plymouth, Bristol and Exeter and promoted by Sir Ferdinando Gorges and Chief Justice Sir John Popham, received the territory between 38° and 45° N — roughly the coast from Long Island to Maine — with the two zones overlapping between 38° and 41° on condition that neither settle within 100 miles of the other.

The Popham Colony

In 1607 the Plymouth Company dispatched two ships, the Gift of God and the Mary and John, carrying about 120 colonists under George Popham and Raleigh Gilbert to the mouth of the Kennebec River in present-day Maine, where they built Fort St. George — the Popham Colony. The settlers constructed the pinnace Virginia, the first English ocean-going vessel built in the Americas. But after a brutal winter, the death of George Popham, and news that Gilbert had inherited an estate in England, the colonists abandoned the settlement and sailed home in 1608 — the same year the company's other 1607 expedition under Captain Henry Challons had already been captured by the Spanish.

Dissolution and successors

The failure of Popham and the death of its patron Sir John Popham left the Plymouth Company dormant after about 1609, though its claims were not extinguished. Gorges revived the enterprise in 1620 as the Council for New England, chartered by James I with territory between 40° and 48° N. The Council issued the patents under which the later New England colonies — including, via the Bradford patent, Plymouth Colony, and the Massachusetts Bay Colony — were settled, before surrendering its charter in 1635.
