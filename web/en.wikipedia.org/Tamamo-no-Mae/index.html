Tamamo-no-Mae

Tamamo-no-Mae (玉藻前, also 玉藻の前) is a legendary figure in Japanese mythology. She is a courtesan who served the Emperor Konoe in the 12th century and, in the most famous version of the tale, is revealed to be a nine-tailed fox (kitsune) who had worked her way through the courts of China and India causing the downfall of kings for centuries. She is counted among the Three Great Evil Yokai of Japan, along with Shuten-dōji and Emperor Sutoku.

Legend

According to the otogizōshi versions of the story, the beautiful and learned Tamamo-no-Mae so enchanted Emperor Konoe that he fell gravely ill. An astrologer, Abe no Yasuchika, divined that a fox spirit was the cause. When Tamamo-no-Mae was exposed, she fled the capital and transformed into her true form, a golden nine-tailed fox, escaping to the plains of Nasu.

The emperor dispatched warriors — in the tale, Miura-no-suke and Kazusa-no-suke — who hunted the fox. In her final moments she repented and begged for her life, but was killed. Her body turned into a poisonous stone, the Sesshō-seki ("Killing Stone"), which killed anyone who approached it, until the monk Gennō split it in the 14th century and pacified her spirit.

Earlier cycles

The story synthesizes earlier fox-woman legends from the continent: in China she was identified with Daji, the consort blamed for the fall of the Shang dynasty, and with Lady Huayang in India. Japanese texts of the Muromachi period assembled her biography into a single career of dynastic destruction spanning three countries.

In popular culture

Tamamo-no-Mae appears in the Noh play Sesshōseki and the kabuki and puppet play Tamamo no Mae Asahi no Tamoto. She is a popular character in modern anime, manga, and games, including the Fate series, where she appears as the Caster-class servant Tamamo-no-Mae.
