Metropolitan cities of Italy

The metropolitan cities of Italy (Italian: città metropolitane d'Italia) are administrative divisions of Italy, operative since 2015, established by law in 2014 to replace the provinces corresponding to the country's largest urban areas. There are fifteen metropolitan cities, including Rome Capital, Milan, Naples, Turin, Palermo, Bari, Catania, Florence, Bologna, Genoa, Venice, Messina, Reggio Calabria and Cagliari.

Government

Each metropolitan city is governed by a metropolitan mayor — by law the mayor of the chief municipality — a metropolitan council elected by local mayors and councillors, and a metropolitan conference. Their functions include strategic development planning, mobility and transport, economic development and general territorial planning.

History

The concept was first introduced by a 1990 reform of local government but remained unimplemented until the Delrio law of 2014, which abolished elected provincial governments in the designated areas and brought the metropolitan cities into operation from 1 January 2015.
