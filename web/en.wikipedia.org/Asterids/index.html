Asterids


Asterids comprise roughly a third of all flowering plant species.

The asterids are a large clade of flowering plants (angiosperms) comprising about 80,000 species, roughly a third of all angiosperms. The group is recognised in the APG classification systems and includes many familiar plants: the aster family, coffee, tomatoes and potatoes, mint, olives, and the heathers.

Characteristics

Asterids are characterised by flowers whose petals are fused into a tube (sympetaly), typically with the stamens equal in number to the petal lobes or fewer, a single integument around the ovule (unitegmic ovules) and tenuinucellate ovules. Iridoid compounds are common chemical defences in the group.

Classification

In the APG IV system the asterids include the orders Cornales and Ericales as basal branches, followed by the lamiids (including Lamiales, Gentianales and Solanales) and the campanulids (including Asterales, Apiales and Dipsacales). Together with the rosids, the asterids form the core eudicots' largest subclades.
