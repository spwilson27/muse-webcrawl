Battle of Dyrrhachium (48 BC)

Battle of Dyrrhachium
Date: 10 July 48 BC
Location: Dyrrhachium (modern Durrës, Albania)
Result: Pompeian victory
Part of Caesar's Civil War

The Battle of Dyrrhachium was fought on 10 July 48 BC during Caesar's Civil War, near Dyrrhachium in Illyria. Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus (Pompey) broke through the fortified lines with which Julius Caesar had attempted to blockade him, inflicting a defeat that forced Caesar to abandon the blockade and retreat into Thessaly.

Background

Early in 48 BC Caesar crossed the Adriatic with part of his army and moved against Pompey's base at Dyrrhachium. Outnumbered, but unwilling to withdraw, Caesar built a line of circumvallation some 15 miles long around Pompey's camp on the coast, hoping to cut off forage and force battle on his own terms. Pompey, supplied by sea, built a counter-line within, and both armies suffered — Caesar's men from real hunger, reduced at one point to making bread from roots.

The battle

Alerted by two Gallic deserters to a weak, unfinished stretch of Caesar's coastal works, Pompey attacked there with legions while his fleet landed troops behind the lines. The Caesarian defences collapsed locally; Caesar's counterattack miscarried amid confusion, and his army was driven back with losses of about 1,000 men and many standards. Caesar himself famously remarked that the war would have ended that day had Pompey known how to use his victory.

Aftermath

Caesar abandoned the blockade and conducted a skilful retreat into Thessaly, drawing Pompey after him. Within a month the armies met again at the Battle of Pharsalus, where Caesar won the decisive victory.
