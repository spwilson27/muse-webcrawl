Mausoleum at Halicarnassus

Mausoleum at Halicarnassus
Location: Halicarnassus (modern Bodrum, Turkey)
Built: c. 351 BC
Architects: Satyros and Pythius of Priene
Height: approximately 45 m
Built for: Mausolus and Artemisia II of Caria

The Mausoleum at Halicarnassus was a tomb built around 351 BC at Halicarnassus for Mausolus, a satrap of the Persian Empire and ruler of Caria, and his sister-wife Artemisia II. It was counted among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and the name "mausoleum" has since become the generic word for a grand tomb.

Design

The structure, designed by the Greek architects Satyros and Pythius, combined Lycian, Egyptian, and Greek elements: a high rectangular podium, a colonnade of 36 Ionic columns, a stepped pyramid roof, and a crowning four-horse chariot (quadriga) carrying statues of Mausolus and Artemisia. Leading sculptors — Scopas, Bryaxis, Leochares, and Timotheus — each carved one side. At roughly 45 metres tall and lavishly decorated with relief friezes, it astonished visitors for centuries.

Destruction and excavation

The Mausoleum stood for some 16 centuries until earthquakes between the 12th and 15th centuries ruined it; the Knights of St John quarried its stone for Bodrum Castle. Excavations by Charles Newton in 1856–57 recovered the great friezes and statues, now chiefly in the British Museum; Danish excavations in the 1960s–70s refined knowledge of the site.
