FormWaltz

"The Blue Danube" (German: An der schönen blauen Donau, Op. 314) is a waltz by the Austrian composer Johann Strauss II, composed in 1866 and first performed in 1867. It is one of the most famous pieces of classical music and is regarded as an unofficial national anthem of Austria.

History

Strauss was commissioned to write a choral work for the Vienna Men's Choral Association, with words by the poet Josef Weyl that satirised Austria's defeat in the Austro-Prussian War. The choral premiere in February 1867 was only a moderate success, but the orchestral version, performed later that year at the Paris World's Fair, was a triumph, and the piece quickly became popular around the world.

Music

The waltz opens with a gentle introduction evoking the river at dawn, followed by a chain of five waltz sections and a coda that recalls the opening themes. Despite its title, the Danube at Vienna is not notably blue; the name comes from a poem by Karl Isidor Beck.

In popular culture

The Blue Danube is traditionally performed at the Vienna New Year's Concert and was famously used in Stanley Kubrick's film 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), accompanying a docking space station.
