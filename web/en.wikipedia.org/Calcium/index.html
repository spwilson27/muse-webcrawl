Calcium

Calcium
Symbol: Ca
Atomic number: 20
Group: alkaline earth metal
Melting point: 842 °C
Density: 1.55 g/cm³

Calcium is a chemical element; it has the symbol Ca and atomic number 20. As an alkaline earth metal, calcium is a reactive metal that forms a dark oxide-nitride layer when exposed to air. Its physical and chemical properties are most similar to the heavier elements strontium and barium. It is the fifth most abundant element in Earth's crust and the third most abundant metal, after iron and aluminium.

Properties

Calcium is a moderately soft, silvery metal that tarnishes rapidly in air. It reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide and hydrogen gas. Its chemistry is dominated by the +2 oxidation state; calcium compounds are typically ionic salts such as calcium carbonate (limestone, chalk, marble), calcium sulfate (gypsum) and calcium fluoride (fluorite).

History

Lime was used as a building material since prehistoric times, but calcium was not isolated as an element until 1808, when Humphry Davy obtained it by electrolysis of a mixture of lime and mercuric oxide. The name derives from the Latin calx, "lime".

Biological role

Calcium is essential for living organisms. Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) are a universal intracellular messenger controlling muscle contraction, neurotransmitter release, enzyme activity and fertilisation. In vertebrates, about 99% of the body's calcium is stored in bones and teeth as hydroxyapatite, giving them strength and structure. Blood calcium levels are tightly regulated by parathyroid hormone, calcitonin and vitamin D. Dairy products, leafy greens and fortified foods are common dietary sources; deficiency can contribute to osteoporosis.

Applications

Calcium metal is used as a reducing agent in the production of other metals such as thorium and uranium, and as an alloying agent. Its compounds are far more widely used: in cement and mortar, as a soil conditioner, in water treatment, in the manufacture of steel and glass, and as dietary supplements.
