Sophia Palaiologina

Sophia Fominichna Palaiologina (c. 1440/1449 – 7 April 1503), also known as Zoe Palaiologina, was a Byzantine princess of the Palaiologos dynasty and, by her marriage to Ivan III, Grand Princess of Moscow. She was a niece of Constantine XI, the last Byzantine emperor, and the daughter of Thomas Palaiologos, Despot of the Morea.

Early life and marriage

After the fall of Constantinople in 1453, Sophia's family fled to Italy, where she was raised in Rome under papal protection. In 1472 she travelled to Moscow and married Grand Prince Ivan III in a union promoted by Pope Paul II, in part in the hope of drawing Russia toward union with Rome. The marriage instead strengthened Moscow's claim to inherit the Byzantine imperial legacy; tradition credits Sophia's retinue with bringing Italian architects and Byzantine court ceremonial to the Kremlin, contributing to the ideology of Moscow as the "Third Rome."

Role at court

Sophia bore several children, including Vasily III, who succeeded Ivan III. Court factions formed around Sophia and her son versus Ivan's grandson Dmitry; by 1499 Vasily had been named grand prince and co-ruler, and he became sole ruler in 1505. Sophia died in Moscow in 1503 and was buried in the Ascension Convent of the Kremlin. A forensic facial reconstruction of her skull was produced in the twentieth century from her remains.
