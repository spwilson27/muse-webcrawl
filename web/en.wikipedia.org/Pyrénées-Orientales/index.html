Pyrénées-Orientales

Pyrénées-Orientales (French: [piʁene ɔʁjɑ̃tal]; Catalan: Pirineus Orientals) is a department of France located on the Mediterranean coast at the eastern end of the Pyrenees. It corresponds roughly to the former province of Roussillon together with the Cerdanya and Fenouillèdes, and forms part of the region of Occitanie. Its prefecture and largest city is Perpignan. The department is numbered 66 in the French departmental system.

History

Most of the department was Spanish territory until the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659 transferred Roussillon and part of the Cerdanya to France. The department itself was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790. Catalan language and culture remain strong, and the area is often called Northern Catalonia by Catalan speakers; the department also includes the Spanish enclave of Llívia nearby.

Geography

The department divides into the fertile Roussillon plain along the coast, the Pyrenean massif to the west and south culminating in Pic Carlit, and the Corbières hills in the north. The Canigó (2,784 m) is a symbol of Catalan identity. Coastal resorts include Collioure and Argelès-sur-Mer; inland are the fortified town of Villefranche-de-Conflent and the abbey of Saint-Martin-du-Canigou.
