Norodom Sihanouk, Cambodia's dominant political figure for much of the 20th century.

Norodom Sihanouk (31 October 1922 – 15 October 2012) was a Cambodian statesman, politician, and filmmaker who led Cambodia in various capacities throughout his long political career. He was King of Cambodia from 1941 to 1955 and again from 1993 to 2004, and served as the country's head of state under several titles — including prime minister, president of the State Presidium, and chairman of the Supreme National Council — between 1953 and 1970 and during the transitions of the 1990s. The Guinness Book of Records identifies him as the politician who served the world's greatest variety of political offices.

Early life and first reign

Sihanouk was born in Phnom Penh, the only son of Prince Norodom Suramarit and Princess Sisowath Kossamak. Educated in Saigon and at the French cavalry school at Saumur, he was selected by the French colonial authorities to succeed his grandfather, King Sisowath Monivong, and was crowned in September 1941 at the age of eighteen. The French assumed the young king would be pliable, but he progressively asserted Cambodian interests.

Independence and the Sangkum era

In 1953 Sihanouk launched a "royal crusade for independence," refusing to return to Cambodia until France conceded full sovereignty; independence was proclaimed on 9 November 1953. In 1955 he abdicated in favour of his father and founded the Sangkum Reastr Niyum political movement, winning power as prime minister. After his father's death in 1960 he became head of state with the title of Prince. His rule combined Buddhist socialism, neutrality in the Cold War, and extensive development programmes, but also increasing authoritarianism and suppression of dissent.

Overthrow, exile, and the Khmer Rouge

Sihanouk was deposed on 18 March 1970 by his prime minister Lon Nol while travelling abroad. From exile in Beijing he formed a government-in-exile in alliance with the Khmer Rouge, lending them his prestige and contributing to their growth. After the Khmer Rouge seized power in April 1975 he returned as head of state of Democratic Kampuchea, but was placed under house arrest and resigned in April 1976; many members of his family were killed during the regime, which caused up to two million deaths.

Later years

Following Vietnam's 1978 invasion, Sihanouk again led a resistance coalition from exile, and played a central role in the negotiations leading to the 1991 Paris Peace Agreements. He returned to Cambodia in November 1991 and was re-elected king by the Throne Council in 1993 under the restored constitutional monarchy, reigning alongside bitter rival co-premiers. He abdicated on 7 October 2004, citing age and health, and was succeeded by his son Norodom Sihamoni. He retained the title "King Father" and died in Beijing on 15 October 2012; his cremation in Phnom Penh in February 2013 drew enormous crowds.
