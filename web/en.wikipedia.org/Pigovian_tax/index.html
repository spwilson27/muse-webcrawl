Pigovian Tax

A Pigovian tax (also spelled Pigouvian) is a tax on any market activity that generates negative externalities — costs borne by third parties not involved in the transaction. Named after economist Arthur Cecil Pigou, who developed the concept in The Economics of Welfare (1920), the tax is intended to correct an inefficient market outcome by pricing the externality: ideally the tax equals the marginal external damage.

Examples and debate

Carbon taxes, congestion charges, and taxes on tobacco and alcohol are commonly cited as Pigovian measures. A symmetric concept, the Pigovian subsidy, encourages activities with positive externalities. Critics note the difficulty of measuring external damages and raise distributional concerns, while supporters argue such taxes can yield a "double dividend" by reducing harm and raising revenue efficiently.
