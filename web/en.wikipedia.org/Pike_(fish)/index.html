Pike (Fish)

The pikes are predatory freshwater fishes of the genus Esox, the only living genus in the family Esocidae. Species include the northern pike (Esox lucius), widespread across the Northern Hemisphere; the muskellunge, the largest member; and the chain, grass, and redfin pickerels of North America, along with the Amur pike and the southern and Aquitanian pikes.

Biology

Pikes have elongated bodies, duckbill-shaped snouts lined with sharp teeth, and dorsal and anal fins set far back for burst acceleration. They are ambush predators of fish, frogs, and even small waterbirds and mammals, typically lying motionless in vegetation before striking. Northern pike commonly reach about a meter in length. Pikes are valued game fish and are also taken for food, though their many fine bones complicate preparation.
