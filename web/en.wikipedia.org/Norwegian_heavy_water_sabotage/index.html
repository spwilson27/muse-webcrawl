Norwegian heavy water sabotage

Date1940–1944
LocationVemork, Telemark, Norway
TargetHeavy water production at Norsk Hydro's Vemork plant
ParticipantsNorwegian resistance, British SOE
OutcomeProduction halted; stocks destroyed

The Norwegian heavy water sabotage was a series of operations during the Second World War undertaken by Norwegian resistance commandos, supported by the British Special Operations Executive, to prevent Nazi Germany from obtaining heavy water produced at Norsk Hydro's Vemork plant near Rjukan in Telemark. Heavy water (deuterium oxide) could serve as a moderator in a nuclear reactor, and the Allies feared it might contribute to a German atomic bomb programme.

Operations

In October 1942 the advance party Operation Grouse parachuted onto the Hardangervidda plateau. Operation Freshman, a glider-borne British engineer raid in November 1942, ended in disaster with the surviving commandos captured and executed. In February 1943, Operation Gunnerside — six Norwegian commandos under Joachim Rønneberg joining the Grouse team — infiltrated the plant and destroyed the heavy water electrolysis cells and some 500 kg of heavy water, without firing a shot or suffering casualties. After production resumed, American bombers raided the plant in November 1943. In February 1944, Knut Haukelid's team sank the ferry SF Hydro on Lake Tinn, drowning the shipment of heavy water bound for Germany.

Legacy

The operations are regarded as among the most successful sabotage actions of the war. They were dramatised in the film The Heroes of Telemark (1965) and the 2015 television series The Heavy Water War. Historians note the German nuclear programme was in any case far from a weapon, but this was not known to Allied planners at the time.
