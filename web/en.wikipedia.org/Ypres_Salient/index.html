Ypres Salient

Ypres Salient
LocationAround Ypres, West Flanders, Belgium
Period1914–1918
Major battlesFirst, Second, Third, Fourth, Fifth Ypres

The Ypres Salient was the area around the Belgian town of Ypres in which the front line bulged into German-held territory during the First World War. Held by British and Commonwealth forces for most of the war, the salient was one of the most contested and deadly sectors of the Western Front.

Battles

Fighting around the salient began with the First Battle of Ypres in the autumn of 1914. The Second Battle of Ypres in 1915 saw the first large-scale use of poison gas on the Western Front. The Third Battle of Ypres in 1917, commonly known as Passchendaele, became notorious for mud and enormous casualties. Further battles followed in 1918 as the front finally moved.

Legacy

Hundreds of thousands of soldiers died in the salient. The area is now marked by numerous Commonwealth War Graves cemeteries, including Tyne Cot, and by the Menin Gate memorial in Ypres, where the Last Post is sounded daily in commemoration.
