The Iranian Revolution (Persian: انقلاب ایران), also known as the Islamic Revolution, was a series of events that culminated in the overthrow of the Pahlavi dynasty in 1979 and the establishment of the Islamic Republic of Iran under Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini.

Causes

Discontent with Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi's authoritarian rule, the repression of his SAVAK secret police, resentment of corruption and inequality amid the oil boom, Western cultural influence, and the alienation of the Shia clergy from the Shah's secularizing White Revolution all fed opposition. A broad coalition — Islamists, liberals, communists and bazaar merchants — united against the monarchy, but Khomeini's network, directing the movement from exile in Iraq and then France, proved best organized.

Course of events

Protests began in January 1978 in Qom and spread through a cycle of demonstrations and mourning ceremonies. On "Black Friday" (8 September 1978) troops fired on demonstrators in Tehran's Jaleh Square, killing many and radicalizing the movement. Strikes, including in the oil fields, paralysed the economy in the autumn. The Shah left Iran on 16 January 1979; Khomeini returned triumphantly on 1 February, and after the armed forces declared neutrality the monarchy collapsed on 11 February 1979.

Consolidation and aftermath

A referendum on 30–31 March 1979 approved an Islamic Republic, proclaimed on 1 April. A new constitution established rule by the Islamic jurist (velayat-e faqih) with Khomeini as Supreme Leader. Revolutionary courts executed former officials, former allies of the movement were suppressed, and in November 1979 students seized the US embassy in Tehran, beginning the 444-day Iran hostage crisis. In September 1980 Iraq invaded, starting the eight-year Iran–Iraq War.
