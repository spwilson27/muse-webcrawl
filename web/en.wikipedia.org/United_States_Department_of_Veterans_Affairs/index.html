The United States Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is a Cabinet-level executive branch department of the U.S. federal government charged with providing lifelong services to military veterans, including health care, disability compensation, education assistance, home loans, pensions, and burial and memorial services. It is the second-largest department in the federal government by number of employees, after the Department of Defense.

History

Federal benefits for veterans date to the Revolutionary and Civil Wars; President Abraham Lincoln's second inaugural address in 1865 expressed the commitment "to care for him who shall have borne the battle and for his widow, and his orphan", which became the VA's motto. In 1930, President Herbert Hoover signed an executive order consolidating scattered veterans' bureaus into the Veterans Administration.

The Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944 — the G.I. Bill — vastly expanded benefits, funding education, home loans, and unemployment support for returning Second World War veterans. On 15 March 1989, the Veterans Administration was elevated to cabinet status as the Department of Veterans Affairs under President Ronald Reagan's 1988 legislation; Edward Derwinski became its first Secretary.

Organization

The department operates through three main administrations:

  • Veterans Health Administration (VHA) — the largest integrated health care system in the United States, with over 1,300 facilities including 171 medical centers serving about 9 million enrolled veterans annually
  • Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA) — administers disability compensation, pensions, education benefits (including the Post-9/11 GI Bill), vocational rehabilitation, and home loan guarantees
  • National Cemetery Administration (NCA) — operates and maintains more than 150 national cemeteries

Issues

The VA has periodically faced criticism over disability claims backlogs and health care access. In 2014, revelations that some VA medical centers kept unofficial waiting lists and falsified wait-time data led to the resignation of Secretary Eric Shinseki and passage of the Veterans Access, Choice, and Accountability Act, expanding veterans' ability to receive care in the community — a policy extended by the VA MISSION Act of 2018.
