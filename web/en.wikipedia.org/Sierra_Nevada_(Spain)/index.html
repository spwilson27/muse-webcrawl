Sierra Nevada (Spain)

Sierra Nevada
Location: Andalusia, Spain
Highest point: Mulhacén, 3,479 m (11,414 ft)
Protected: Sierra Nevada National Park and Natural Park
Designation: UNESCO Biosphere Reserve (1986)

The Sierra Nevada (Spanish for "snowy mountain range") is a mountain range in the Andalusia region of southern Spain, in the provinces of Granada and Almería. It contains the highest point of continental Spain, Mulhacén, at 3,479 metres (11,414 ft) above sea level.

Geography

The range runs roughly east–west for about 80 km. Notable peaks besides Mulhacén include Veleta (3,398 m) and Alcazaba (3,371 m). The northern slopes drain toward the Genil River near Granada, while the southern slopes form the valleys of the Alpujarras. The mountains were shaped partly by glaciers, which left cirques, U-shaped valleys and small glacial lakes.

National park

The core of the range is protected as the Sierra Nevada National Park, declared in 1999, surrounded by a larger natural park; together they cover more than 2,000 square kilometres. The whole massif was declared a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve in 1986. Because of its range of altitudes and its position as a glacial refuge, the Sierra Nevada holds exceptional plant diversity, with many endemic species found nowhere else.

Ski resort and astronomy

The Sierra Nevada Ski Station, built on the northern slopes of Veleta and served from Granada, is one of Europe's southernmost ski resorts and hosted the 1996 Alpine World Ski Championships. The high, dry air also favours astronomy: the Sierra Nevada Observatory and the IRAM 30 m radio telescope stand on the northern slopes.
