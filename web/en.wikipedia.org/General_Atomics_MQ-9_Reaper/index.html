General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper

The General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper (formerly the Predator B) is an unmanned aerial vehicle developed by General Atomics Aeronautical Systems primarily for the United States Air Force. Capable of remotely controlled or autonomous flight, the Reaper is used mainly for intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance and as a hunter-killer platform employing precision-guided munitions.

Design

The MQ-9 is larger and more powerful than its predecessor, the MQ-1 Predator: it is about 11 m long with a 20 m wingspan, powered by a Honeywell turboprop of roughly 950 shaft horsepower, and can fly above 40,000 ft with endurance exceeding 27 hours. It is flown by a pilot and sensor operator from a ground control station via satellite link.

Armament and sensors

The Reaper carries a multispectral targeting system and can employ up to about 1,700 kg of ordnance, including AGM-114 Hellfire missiles, GBU-12 Paveway II laser-guided bombs, GBU-38 JDAMs, and AIM-9X missiles in trials. Its armament distinguished it from the originally reconnaissance-only Predator.

Service

Entering USAF service in 2007, the Reaper has flown extensively in Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, and elsewhere, and is operated or ordered by several allied air forces, including the United Kingdom (Protector RG1 variant), France, Italy, and India. It has also been adapted for maritime patrol (SeaGuardian) and for civil roles such as disaster monitoring. Its use for targeted killings has drawn legal and ethical debate, and its vulnerability to modern air defences has prompted planning for more survivable successors.
