Centralia is a borough and near-ghost town in Columbia County, Pennsylvania, United States. It is part of northeastern Pennsylvania's Coal Region, and its population has declined from 1,017 residents in 1980 to five in the 2020 census. The community has been consumed by an underground mine fire that has burned beneath the town since 1962, when a fire in the borough landfill ignited an exposed coal seam in a former strip mine pit.

The mine fire

The Centralia mine fire began in May 1962, most likely when burning trash in the town's landfill — itself located in an abandoned strip-mine pit — ignited the underlying anthracite coal. Early efforts to extinguish or contain the fire failed, and it spread through the labyrinth of abandoned mine workings beneath the borough. By the late 1970s, residents reported health effects from carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide seeping into homes, subsidence, and ground hot enough to steam.

Public attention peaked in 1981, when 12-year-old Todd Domboski fell into a sinkhole that suddenly opened in his grandmother's backyard; he was pulled out alive. In 1984, Congress appropriated $42 million for relocation assistance, and most residents accepted buyouts and left. Buildings were demolished, and in 1992 Pennsylvania governor Robert P. Casey invoked eminent domain over all properties in the borough. The U.S. Postal Service revoked Centralia's ZIP code in 2002.

Present day

A handful of residents won a 2013 settlement allowing them to remain in their homes for the rest of their lives. The fire, which has burned for more than sixty years, is expected to continue for decades or centuries more; the similar fire at Burning Mountain in Australia has burned for an estimated 6,000 years. Centralia has become a famous example of an environmental ghost town and inspired the fictional town of Silent Hill in the film adaptation of the video game series. The abandoned section of Pennsylvania Route 61, closed in 1994 after the fire buckled and cracked its surface, became known as the Graffiti Highway before being covered with dirt berms in 2020.
