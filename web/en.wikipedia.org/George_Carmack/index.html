George Washington Carmack (1860–1922) was an American prospector credited, together with his Tagish relatives Skookum Jim and Dawson Charlie, with the gold discovery on Bonanza Creek in August 1896 that set off the Klondike Gold Rush.

Life in the Yukon

Carmack deserted from the United States Marine Corps in California and drifted north, eventually settling in the Yukon country, where he fished, traded, and prospected. He married Shaaw Tláa (Kate Carmack), a Tagish woman, and lived largely within Tagish society, a way of life that the searching parties of prospectors jokingly called "squaw man" existence.

The discovery

In August 1896, following a suggestion from the prospector Robert Henderson, Carmack's party travelled to Rabbit Creek, a tributary of the Klondike River. On August 16 (or 17), gold was found in the creek gravel; Carmack staked the discovery claim the next day and registered it at Fortymile, renaming the stream Bonanza Creek. Whether Carmack himself, Skookum Jim, or Dawson Charlie first spotted the gold remains disputed, and Tagish oral tradition credits Skookum Jim. When news reached the outside world in 1897, some 100,000 fortune-seekers set out for the Klondike.

Later life

Carmack's claims made him wealthy, though he lost and remade money in later business ventures in Seattle and California. He died in Vancouver in 1922.
