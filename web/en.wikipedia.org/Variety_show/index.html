Variety show

A variety show, also known as a variety arts or variety entertainment programme, is entertainment made up of a variety of acts including musical performances, comedy sketches, magic, acrobatics, juggling, and ventriloquism. It is normally introduced by a host or master of ceremonies. The variety format descends from music hall and vaudeville in the English-speaking world and from the café-concert and revue traditions elsewhere.

History

Variety theatre flourished in Britain as music hall and in America as vaudeville from the mid-19th century until the 1930s. The format transferred successfully to radio and then television: landmark television variety shows include The Ed Sullivan Show (1948–1971), which introduced acts such as Elvis Presley and The Beatles to American audiences, The Carol Burnett Show, and sketch-and-music series in many other countries. In South Korea and Japan, variety programmes remain a dominant television genre, mixing games, talk, and comedy.

Format

Episodes typically string together short, self-contained acts with little narrative continuity, allowing viewers to join at any point. Modern descendants include talent shows, late-night talk shows with musical guests, and sketch comedy series.
