Bone marrow

Bone marrow is the soft, spongy tissue found inside bones. It is the primary site of haematopoiesis — the production of new blood cells — in adult humans. In an average adult, bone marrow accounts for roughly 4% of total body mass (about 2.6 kg), and it produces on the order of 500 billion blood cells per day.

Types

Two types of marrow are recognised. Red marrow consists mainly of haematopoietic tissue and produces red blood cells, platelets and most white blood cells. Yellow marrow consists mainly of fat cells (adipocytes) and is found in the hollow centres of long bones; in emergencies such as severe blood loss it can revert to red marrow. At birth all marrow is red; with age, much of it converts to yellow marrow, so that in adults red marrow is confined mainly to the flat bones (pelvis, sternum, cranium, ribs, vertebrae) and the ends of the long bones.

Function and stem cells

Marrow contains haematopoietic stem cells, which give rise to all blood lineages: red blood cells (erythrocytes) that carry oxygen, platelets (thrombocytes) that enable clotting, and the white blood cells of the immune system, including neutrophils, lymphocytes and monocytes. It also contains mesenchymal stem cells, which can generate bone, cartilage and fat. The marrow is where B lymphocytes mature in mammals, and it filters out old or defective red blood cells from circulation.

Clinical significance

Diseases of the marrow include leukaemias, lymphomas, multiple myeloma and aplastic anaemia. Bone marrow examination, by aspiration or biopsy (often from the pelvis), is a key diagnostic tool. Bone marrow transplantation — more precisely haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation — is used to treat these diseases: a patient's diseased marrow is destroyed by chemotherapy or radiation and replaced with healthy stem cells from a matched donor, from the patient's own stored cells, or from peripheral blood or umbilical cord blood. The first successful allogeneic marrow transplant was performed in 1956 by E. Donnall Thomas, who later received the Nobel Prize.
