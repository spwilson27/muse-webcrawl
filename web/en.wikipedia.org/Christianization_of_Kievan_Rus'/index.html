Christianization of Kievan Rus'

The Christianization of Kievan Rus' took place in several stages. In 867, Patriarch Photius of Constantinople announced that the Rus' had accepted a bishop from Byzantium. The decisive event, however, came in 988, when Prince Vladimir of Kiev (later Saint Vladimir) converted to Orthodox Christianity and ordered the mass baptism of his subjects, an event traditionally regarded as the baptism of Rus'.

Background

Slavic paganism, centred on deities such as Perun, was the dominant religion of the early Rus'. Missionaries from Byzantium, and later from Rome, had intermittent contact with the region; Princess Olga of Kiev, Vladimir's grandmother, had herself converted to Christianity around 955, though her son Svyatoslav remained pagan.

The conversion of Vladimir

According to the Primary Chronicle, Vladimir sent envoys to investigate Islam, Judaism, Latin Christianity and Byzantine Orthodoxy, and chose Orthodoxy after his emissaries reported being overwhelmed by the beauty of the liturgy in Hagia Sophia. Vladimir's conversion was also bound up with diplomacy: he seized the Byzantine city of Chersonesus and married Anna, sister of the emperors Basil II and Constantine VIII, receiving baptism in 988. On returning to Kiev he destroyed pagan idols — the statue of Perun was dragged through the streets and thrown into the Dnieper — and commanded the population to be baptised in the river.

Consequences

Christianization tied Kievan Rus' to the Byzantine cultural world, bringing Church Slavonic writing, Byzantine canon law, church architecture and iconography. The process was gradual: pagan practices persisted in rural areas for centuries, and resistance flared in cities such as Novgorod and Suzdal. Nevertheless, 988 is commemorated in Russia, Ukraine and Belarus as the founding moment of their shared Christian heritage.
