Gurdwara

A gurdwara, the Sikh place of worship and assembly.

A gurdwara (Punjabi: ਗੁਰਦੁਆਰਾ, gurdu'ārā, meaning "door to the Guru") is a place of assembly and worship for Sikhs. It is also a centre for community life, learning, and charitable service. The most prominent gurdwara is the Golden Temple (Harmandir Sahib) in Amritsar, Punjab, India.

Features

A gurdwara centres on the Darbar Sahib, the hall where the Guru Granth Sahib, the Sikh holy scripture, is placed on a throne (manji sahib) beneath a canopy. Congregational singing of hymns (kirtan) and readings from the scripture form the core of worship. Outside, a tall flagpole flying the Sikh flag, the Nishan Sahib, marks the building.

Langar

Every gurdwara maintains a langar, a community kitchen that serves free vegetarian meals to all visitors regardless of faith, caste, or background. The institution embodies the Sikh principles of seva (selfless service) and equality; volunteers prepare and serve the food, and diners sit together on the floor in rows.

Historical development

The first gurdwara was built by Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, at Kartarpur in the early 16th century, where he gathered a community of followers. His successors expanded the institution, and congregations (sangats) formed around gurdwaras across Punjab and, with the Sikh diaspora, worldwide.

Customs

Visitors cover their heads, remove their shoes, and wash their hands (and in some gurdwaras their feet) before entering the prayer hall. Alcohol, tobacco, and meat are not permitted on the premises. All are welcome, and offerings of food or money support the langar and community services.
