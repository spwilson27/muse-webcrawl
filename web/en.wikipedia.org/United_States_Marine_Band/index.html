The United States Marine Band is the premier band of the United States Marine Corps. Established by act of Congress on July 11, 1798, it is the oldest continuously active professional musical organization in the United States. Its primary mission is unique: to provide music for the President of the United States and the Commandant of the Marine Corps; President John Adams invited the band to make its White House debut on New Year's Day, 1801. Because of this mission, the band has earned the title "The President's Own", bestowed by President Thomas Jefferson.

History

The band performed for every presidential inauguration from Jefferson onward, and accompanies state dinners, funerals, and ceremonies of honour. Under its 17th director, John Philip Sousa (1880–1892), the band rose to international fame; Sousa transformed it into one of the world's finest wind bands, made its first sound recordings in 1890, and led its first concert tour in 1891 with President Benjamin Harrison's permission — a tradition of national concert tours that continues annually. The band's members are enlistment-exempt from military duties other than music; musicians audition directly into the ensemble as staff sergeants.

Notable music

The Marine Band is inseparably linked with the march repertoire. It performs Sousa's "Semper Fidelis" (the official march of the Marine Corps), "The Washington Post", and "The Liberty Bell". The band also popularised "The Star-Spangled Banner" and provides the musical escort music — including "Hail to the Chief" — for presidential appearances.

Organisation

The organisation consists of the Marine Band proper (about 130 musicians), the Marine Chamber Orchestra, and smaller ensembles. Its director is appointed by the Commandant. The band rehearses and performs at Marine Barracks Washington, the "Oldest Post of the Corps", and in the John Philip Sousa Band Hall.
