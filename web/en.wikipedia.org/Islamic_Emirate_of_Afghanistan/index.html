The Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan is the name used by the Taliban for the state of Afghanistan. An Islamic emirate governed by the Taliban first ruled most of Afghanistan from 1996 to 2001, and the name was restored after the Taliban retook Kabul on 15 August 2021 during the final phase of the War in Afghanistan, following the withdrawal of United States and allied forces and the collapse of the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan.

First emirate (1996–2001)

The Taliban, a movement of religious students led by Mullah Mohammed Omar, emerged in Kandahar in 1994 amid the Afghan civil war and captured Kabul in September 1996. The Islamic Emirate was recognized by only three countries — Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates — while the United Nations continued to recognize the Islamic State of Afghanistan, whose forces (the Northern Alliance) held the northeast. The regime imposed a strict interpretation of Sharia law, severely restricting women's education and employment and destroying the Buddhas of Bamiyan in March 2001. After the September 11 attacks, a US-led invasion toppled the emirate in late 2001 for sheltering al-Qaeda.

Return to power (2021–present)

In February 2020 the United States signed the Doha Agreement with the Taliban, committing to withdraw its forces. As the withdrawal proceeded in 2021, the Taliban launched a nationwide offensive; provincial capitals fell in rapid succession in August, and President Ashraf Ghani fled as Taliban fighters entered Kabul on 15 August 2021. The Taliban proclaimed the restoration of the Islamic Emirate and formed a caretaker government in September 2021, with Hibatullah Akhundzada as supreme leader (Amir al-Mu'minin) and ministers drawn almost entirely from the Taliban's old guard.

Government and policy

The emirate is ruled through the supreme leader in Kandahar, whose decrees are implemented by a cabinet based in Kabul. The regime has progressively restricted the rights of women and girls: secondary schools for girls — promised to reopen — remained closed, a university ban followed in December 2022, and most forms of female employment, including work for NGOs and the UN, were prohibited, making Afghanistan the only country in the world banning girls from secondary education. The government also enforces dress codes, segregation, and punishments under its interpretation of Islamic law, and suppresses critical media.

International status

No country formally recognized the Islamic Emirate in the years immediately after 2021, although several states — including China, Russia, Pakistan, Iran, and Central Asian governments — maintained embassies and working relations with it, and China accepted a Taliban ambassador's credentials in early 2024. The regime seeks Afghanistan's UN seat and the release of frozen central-bank reserves held abroad, while Western governments condition recognition on human rights, particularly for women. Armed resistance by the National Resistance Front and attacks by Islamic State – Khorasan Province continue, alongside a severe humanitarian and economic crisis.
