Jane Addams

Laura Jane Addams (September 6, 1860 – May 21, 1935) was an American settlement activist, reformer, social worker, sociologist and author. In 1889 she co-founded Hull House in Chicago, one of the first settlement houses in the United States, and in 1931 she became the first American woman to be awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.

Born: September 6, 1860, Cedarville, Illinois, US

Died: May 21, 1935, Chicago, Illinois, US

Known for: Hull House, social work, peace activism

Awards: Nobel Peace Prize (1931)

Hull House

Inspired by Toynbee Hall in London, Addams and Ellen Gates Starr opened Hull House in a mansion built by Charles Hull on Chicago's Near West Side. It offered kindergarten, adult education, art, music and theatre programmes, and served as a centre for social research and advocacy on child labour, factory safety, sanitation and immigrant rights. Addams's writings, including Twenty Years at Hull-House (1910), made her one of the most famous women in America.

Peace work

Addams opposed American entry into the First World War and chaired the Women's International League for Peace and Freedom, founded in 1919. Her pacifism made her a target of official suspicion in the 1920s, but she was later recognised with the Nobel Peace Prize, shared with Nicholas Murray Butler.
