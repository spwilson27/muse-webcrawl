Albuminuria

DefinitionAbnormal presence of albumin in the urine
NormalUrine albumin–creatinine ratio (ACR) < 30 mg/g
Moderately increasedACR 30–300 mg/g (formerly "microalbuminuria")
Severely increasedACR > 300 mg/g (formerly "macroalbuminuria")
Associated withDiabetic kidney disease, hypertension, chronic kidney disease

Albuminuria is a pathological condition in which the plasma protein albumin is present in the urine in abnormal quantities. Healthy kidneys retain albumin in the blood, so its appearance in urine — usually from damage to the glomerular filtration barrier — is an early and sensitive marker of kidney disease and also predicts cardiovascular events.

Measurement and categories

Albuminuria is best measured as the albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) on a spot urine sample, which corrects for urine concentration. Current KDIGO categories are A1 (normal to mildly increased, ACR < 30 mg/g), A2 (moderately increased, 30–300 mg/g, historically called microalbuminuria) and A3 (severely increased, > 300 mg/g, formerly macroalbuminuria). ACR categories, together with estimated GFR, define the staging of chronic kidney disease.

Causes and management

The commonest causes are diabetes mellitus and hypertension; transient albuminuria can follow fever, exercise or urinary infection. Management targets the cause — blood-pressure and glucose control — and drugs that block the renin–angiotensin system (ACE inhibitors, ARBs) and SGLT2 inhibitors reduce albuminuria and slow progression of kidney disease. Screening is recommended for people with diabetes and other high-risk groups.
