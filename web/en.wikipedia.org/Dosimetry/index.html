Dosimetry


A personal dosimeter used to monitor occupational radiation exposure.

Dosimetry is the measurement and calculation of radiation dose absorbed by a substance or tissue. It is fundamental to radiation protection, radiation therapy, nuclear medicine, and occupational safety in environments with ionising radiation.

Quantities and units

The primary quantity is the absorbed dose, measured in gray (Gy), which is the energy deposited per unit mass. Accounting for the biological effectiveness of different radiation types gives the equivalent dose, measured in sievert (Sv). Corrections for the sensitivity of different organs produce the effective dose, the standard for setting exposure limits.

Devices

Personal dosimeters include film badges, thermoluminescent dosimeters (TLDs), optically stimulated luminescence devices, and direct-reading electronic dosimeters. Calibration is performed against national standards, and results feed into exposure records that regulators require for radiation workers.
