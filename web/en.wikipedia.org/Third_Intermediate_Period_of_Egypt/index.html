The Third Intermediate Period of ancient Egypt began with the death of Pharaoh Ramesses XI in 1070 BC and the end of the New Kingdom, and lasted until about 664 BC. It was a time of political fragmentation, with power divided between rulers in the north and the High Priests of Amun at Thebes, followed by kings of Libyan origin.

Overview

During the 21st Dynasty the country was effectively split between Tanis in the Delta and Thebes in Upper Egypt. The 22nd Dynasty, founded by Shoshenq I of Libyan descent, briefly reunified Egypt and campaigned in the Levant, but the kingdom again splintered among rival dynasties (the 23rd and 24th) and local rulers.

Kushite period

In the late 8th century BC, kings of Kush from Nubia — beginning with Piye — conquered Egypt and ruled as the 25th Dynasty, reviving monumental building and Egyptian artistic traditions. Their rule ended amid conflict with Assyria, whose invasions in the 660s BC ushered in the Late Period.
