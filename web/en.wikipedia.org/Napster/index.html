Napster was a peer-to-peer file sharing application primarily associated with sharing MP3 audio files. Developed by Shawn Fanning and Sean Parker and released in June 1999, it allowed users to search a central index of files stored on other users' computers and download them directly. At its peak in early 2001 it had around 80 million registered users, and it is credited with transforming how music was distributed and consumed.

Legal challenges and shutdown

The service provoked immediate opposition from the recording industry. The RIAA sued in December 1999, the band Metallica sued in 2000 after an unreleased track leaked, and Dr. Dre filed a similar suit. In 2001 the Ninth Circuit largely upheld an injunction in A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc., and Napster shut down its file-sharing network in July 2001. The company filed for bankruptcy in 2002.

Legacy and later brand

Napster's assets and name were acquired by Roxio and relaunched as a legal paid download and subscription service; the brand later passed through Best Buy and Rhapsody, and the streaming service Rhapsody rebranded as Napster in 2016. Although short-lived, the original Napster forced the music industry toward licensed digital distribution, paving the way for the iTunes Store and, later, streaming services such as Spotify.
