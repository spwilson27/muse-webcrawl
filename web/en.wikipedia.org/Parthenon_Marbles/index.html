The Parthenon Marbles (also known as the Elgin Marbles) are a collection of Classical Greek marble sculptures made under the supervision of the architect and sculptor Phidias and his assistants. They were originally part of the Parthenon temple and other buildings on the Acropolis of Athens. The collection principally consists of about half of the surviving sculptures of the Parthenon: sections of the frieze, metopes and pedimental figures, together with a caryatid and a column from the nearby Erechtheion.

Removal

Between 1801 and 1812, agents of Thomas Bruce, 7th Earl of Elgin, then British ambassador to the Ottoman Empire, removed the sculptures from the Acropolis — Athens was then under Ottoman rule — claiming permission under an Ottoman firman whose exact terms remain disputed (the original document is lost; only an Italian translation survives). The removal was controversial even at the time: Lord Byron denounced it in verse. Financially ruined, Elgin sold the marbles to the British government in 1816 for £35,000, and they were placed in the British Museum, where a select parliamentary committee upheld the legality of the purchase.

The restitution debate

Greece has formally sought the marbles' return since independence, most prominently under culture minister Melina Mercouri in the 1980s. Greek arguments rest on the doubtful legality of the Ottoman permission, the sculptures' status as an integral part of a single monument, and their national significance; the Acropolis Museum, opened in 2009, displays the surviving Athens-held pieces with spaces left for those in London. The British Museum and UK government have argued that the museum acquired the marbles legally, that they are held for a world audience under the British Museum Act 1963 (which generally bars disposal of collection items), and that they form part of a universal museum. Opinion polls in Britain have repeatedly shown majority support for return, and in the 2020s discussions of a long-term loan arrangement were reported, though no final agreement had been reached. Fragments held elsewhere have been returned: the Vatican Museums loaned three pieces in 2023 and a museum in Palermo permanently returned a fragment in 2022.
