South Williamsport, Pennsylvania

South Williamsport is a borough in Lycoming County, Pennsylvania, United States. It lies on the south bank of the West Branch Susquehanna River, directly across from the city of Williamsport. The population was about 6,300 at recent censuses.

Little League Baseball

South Williamsport is world-famous as the home of the Little League World Series, held every August since 1947 at the Little League International headquarters complex. Games are played at Howard J. Lamade Stadium (opened 1959) and the adjacent Volunteer Stadium, drawing tens of thousands of spectators and international television coverage. The Peter J. McGovern Little League Museum is also located in the borough.

History

The borough grew with the 19th-century lumber boom that made neighbouring Williamsport briefly one of the wealthiest cities per capita in America. It was incorporated in 1886 from parts of Armstrong Township. Bald Eagle Mountain rises directly behind the borough, which has repeatedly contended with flooding from the Susquehanna.
