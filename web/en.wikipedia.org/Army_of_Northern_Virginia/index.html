Army of Northern Virginia

The Army of Northern Virginia was the principal Confederate field army in the Eastern Theater of the American Civil War. Commanded for most of its existence by General Robert E. Lee, it fought the Union Army of the Potomac from 1861 until its surrender at Appomattox Court House on 9 April 1865.

Organisation

The army was organised into infantry corps led over time by commanders including Stonewall Jackson, James Longstreet, Richard S. Ewell, and A. P. Hill, supported by J. E. B. Stuart's cavalry and a strong artillery arm. Its peak strength was roughly 90,000 men around the Chancellorsville campaign of 1863.

Major campaigns

Under Lee the army won the Seven Days Battles, Second Bull Run, Fredericksburg, and Chancellorsville, and invaded the North twice, campaigns ending at Antietam (1862) and Gettysburg (1863). From 1864 Ulysses S. Grant's Overland Campaign and the siege of Petersburg ground the army down until Lee's retreat westward ended in surrender.

Legacy

The Army of Northern Virginia became the most famous Confederate army of the war, and its battle flag became an enduring, and controversial, symbol.
