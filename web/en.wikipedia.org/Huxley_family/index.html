Huxley family

The Huxley family is a British family whose members have distinguished themselves in science, medicine, literature, and the arts over several generations. The family's patriarch was Thomas Henry Huxley (1825–1895), the biologist famed as "Darwin's Bulldog" for his advocacy of Charles Darwin's theory of evolution.

Notable members

  • Thomas Henry Huxley (1825–1895) — biologist and comparative anatomist; coined the term "agnosticism"; grandfather of Aldous and Julian Huxley.
  • Leonard Huxley (1860–1933) — writer, editor, and schoolmaster, son of Thomas Henry, father of Aldous, Julian, and Noel.
  • Julian Huxley (1887–1975) — evolutionary biologist, first director of UNESCO, and a founder of the World Wildlife Fund.
  • Aldous Huxley (1894–1963) — novelist, author of Brave New World (1932) and The Doors of Perception (1954).
  • Andrew Huxley (1917–2012) — physiologist, half-brother of Aldous and Julian, who shared the 1963 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for work on nerve impulses.
  • Hugh Esmor Huxley (1924–2013) — molecular biologist known for the sliding filament theory of muscle contraction (a more distant relation in the family's scientific line).

Legacy

The Huxleys are often cited as a leading example of a multi-generational intellectual dynasty, linked by marriage to other prominent families including the Arnolds — Thomas Henry Huxley's wife's family connection and Leonard's marriage to Julia Arnold, niece of the poet Matthew Arnold.
