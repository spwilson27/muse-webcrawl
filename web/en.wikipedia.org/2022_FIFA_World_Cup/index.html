2022 FIFA World Cup

The 2022 FIFA World Cup was the 22nd FIFA World Cup, held in Qatar from 20 November to 18 December 2022 — the first World Cup staged in the Arab and Muslim world, and the first held in the Northern Hemisphere winter, scheduled to avoid Qatar's extreme summer heat. Argentina won their third title, defeating defending champions France 4–2 on penalties after a 3–3 draw in a final widely described as one of the greatest ever played; captain Lionel Messi was named player of the tournament.

Lusail Stadium, venue of the final.

Tournament summary

Thirty-two teams played in eight stadiums in and around Doha, the most compact World Cup ever; total attendance was about 3.4 million. Morocco became the first African and Arab team to reach a semi-final, finishing fourth, while Croatia took third. Kylian Mbappé won the Golden Boot with eight goals, including a hat-trick in the final — only the second hat-trick in a World Cup final, after Geoff Hurst in 1966. Messi, playing in his fifth and final World Cup, scored seven goals and broke the record for most World Cup appearances (26). Notable shocks included Saudi Arabia's 2–1 win over Argentina, Germany's group-stage elimination, and Japan's wins over Germany and Spain.

The final

At Lusail Stadium on Qatar National Day, Argentina led 2–0 through Messi's penalty and Ángel Di María, until Mbappé scored twice in 97 seconds late in the second half. Messi restored the lead in extra time, Mbappé equalised again from the penalty spot, and in the shootout Argentine goalkeeper Emiliano Martínez's save from Kingsley Coman helped Argentina win 4–2.

Controversies

The tournament was shadowed by criticism of Qatar's human-rights record, including the treatment and deaths of migrant workers who built the infrastructure, laws criminalising homosexuality, and allegations of bribery in FIFA's 2010 hosting vote. FIFA's threats of sporting sanctions prompted several European captains to abandon the "OneLove" armband. Qatar's reported spending of over $220 billion on tournament-related infrastructure made it by far the most expensive World Cup in history.
