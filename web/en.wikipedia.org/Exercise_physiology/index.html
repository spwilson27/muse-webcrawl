Exercise physiology

Exercise physiology
FieldPhysiology; sports science
FocusBody's responses and adaptations to physical activity

Exercise physiology is the study of the acute responses and chronic adaptations of the human body to physical activity and exercise. It draws on anatomy, biochemistry and physiology to understand how the cardiovascular, respiratory, muscular and metabolic systems respond to exertion.

Scope

Exercise physiologists study energy metabolism during activity, including the roles of ATP, phosphocreatine, glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation; oxygen uptake and the measurement of VO₂ max; muscle fatigue; thermoregulation; and the effects of altitude, heat and other environmental stresses. Long-term training adaptations include increased stroke volume, capillarisation and mitochondrial density in muscle.

Applications

The discipline underpins sports science and athletic training, cardiac and pulmonary rehabilitation, occupational health, and exercise prescription for conditions such as diabetes, obesity and cardiovascular disease. Clinical exercise physiologists work in hospitals and health services, while others support elite sport and military performance.

History

Modern exercise physiology emerged in the early twentieth century, with landmark work at institutions such as the Harvard Fatigue Laboratory (1927–1947), and developed rapidly with the study of endurance performance and the fitness boom of the later twentieth century.
