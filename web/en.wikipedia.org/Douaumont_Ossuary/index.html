The Douaumont Ossuary (French: Ossuaire de Douaumont) is a memorial containing the skeletal remains of soldiers who died on the battlefield during the Battle of Verdun in the First World War. It stands within the Verdun battlefield, near the destroyed village of Douaumont and the fort of the same name, in northeastern France.

The memorial

The ossuary, some 137 metres long with a 46-metre tower (the "Lantern of the Dead"), houses the remains of at least 130,000 unidentified French and German soldiers in alcoves corresponding to sectors of the battlefield; small windows allow visitors to see the bones within. In front of the ossuary lies France's largest military cemetery of the First World War, with 16,142 graves, including Muslim and Jewish sections. The initiative for the memorial came from the Bishop of Verdun, Charles Ginisty; construction began in 1920 and the ossuary was officially inaugurated on 7 August 1932 by President Albert Lebrun.

Symbol of reconciliation

In September 1984 the ossuary was the site of a famous act of Franco-German reconciliation, when French President François Mitterrand and German Chancellor Helmut Kohl stood hand in hand during a commemoration ceremony — an enduring image of European unity.
