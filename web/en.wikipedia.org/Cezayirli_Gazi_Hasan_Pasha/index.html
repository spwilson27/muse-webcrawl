Cezayirli Gazi Hasan Pasha (1713 – 19 March 1790) was an Ottoman grand vizier, admiral (Kapudan Pasha) and general of Georgian origin. His epithet Cezayirli means "from Algiers" (Turkish: Cezayir), reflecting his early career in the Regency of Algiers, and Gazi ("the warrior") honored his military service. He is famous for having been accompanied everywhere by a lion he received as a gift and kept as a pet.

Career

Born in 1713 and sold as a slave in his youth, Hasan rose through service in Algiers and in the Ottoman navy. He distinguished himself in the Russo-Turkish War of 1768–1774: after the destruction of the Ottoman fleet at the Battle of Chesma (1770), he rebuilt Ottoman naval power and became Kapudan Pasha, reforming the fleet, founding a naval engineering school in Istanbul, and building fortifications and dockyards. He suppressed rebellions in Egypt and Syria, restoring Ottoman authority there in the 1780s.

Grand vizierate and death

During the Russo-Turkish War of 1787–1792 Hasan Pasha campaigned on the Danube front. Sultan Selim III appointed him Grand Vizier in 1789 after the fall of Ochakov. Despite his energy, the Ottoman armies suffered defeats, including at Rymnik. He died at Shumen (Şumnu) on 19 March 1790, amid rumors — never substantiated — that he had been poisoned.

Legacy

Hasan Pasha is remembered as one of the last great Ottoman soldier-statesmen of the 18th century; the Hasan Pasha barracks and dockyard district of Kasımpaşa in Istanbul, the Algerians' mosque he endowed, and several fountains and public works are associated with his name. His lion became a famous anecdote of Ottoman court life, recounted by European travelers of the time.
