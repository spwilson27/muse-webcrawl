The Darvaza gas crater (Turkmen: Jähennem derwezesi, "Gate of Hell"), also known as the Door to Hell or Gates of Hell, is a burning natural gas field collapsed into a cavern near Darvaza, Turkmenistan. The crater is located in the Karakum Desert, about 260 kilometres (160 mi) north of the capital, Ashgabat, near the village of Derweze. Its glow is visible for kilometres around at night, and it has become one of Turkmenistan's best-known tourist attractions.

History

The crater formed in 1971 during Soviet gas exploration. Geologists drilling for natural gas tapped into a large underground cavern filled with gas; the ground beneath the drilling rig collapsed, swallowing the rig and creating a crater roughly 69 metres across and 30 metres deep. Fearing the release of poisonous methane gas, the geologists decided to burn the gas off, expecting it to be exhausted within a few weeks. Instead, fed by the rich gas deposits of the region, the fire has burned continuously ever since.

The exact circumstances were not formally documented at the time, and no records of deaths or injuries from the collapse have been published.

Tourism and expeditions

The site, unmarked for decades, has drawn adventurous travellers; a glowing pit in an otherwise empty desert, it is often reached by off-road tours from Ashgabat. In November 2013, explorer George Kourounis became the first person known to descend to the bottom of the crater, wearing a heat-reflective suit and custom harness, collecting soil samples for scientific analysis of extremophile bacteria as part of a National Geographic expedition. The samples revealed bacteria thriving at the crater floor despite the extreme temperatures.

Proposed extinguishing

In April 2010, President Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow visited the site and ordered that the crater be closed, citing its effect on the development of other gas fields in the area, but no closure took place. In January 2022, he again instructed officials to find a way to extinguish the fire, citing economic losses of valuable gas and environmental harm. In June 2025, Turkmen officials reported that the fire had diminished significantly after engineers reduced the flow of gas feeding it, with flames reduced to a fraction of their former size.
