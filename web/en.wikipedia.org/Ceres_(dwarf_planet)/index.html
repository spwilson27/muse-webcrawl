Ceres (dwarf planet)

Ceres (minor-planet designation: 1 Ceres) is a dwarf planet in the middle main asteroid belt between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. It was the first asteroid discovered, on 1 January 1801 by Giuseppe Piazzi at Palermo Astronomical Observatory in Sicily, and announced as a new planet. Ceres was later classified as an asteroid and then a dwarf planet (the only one in the inner Solar System). Ceres's small size means that even at its brightest it is too dim to see with the naked eye, except under extremely dark skies.

Discovery and naming

Piazzi spotted Ceres while compiling a star catalogue; the object moved against the stars like a planet predicted by the Titius–Bode law to lie between Mars and Jupiter, but after it was lost in the Sun's glare the young Carl Friedrich Gauss computed its orbit from few observations, enabling its recovery — a celebrated triumph of celestial mechanics. Piazzi named it for Ceres, the Roman goddess of agriculture and patron of Sicily. For half a century it was counted a planet, until the flood of asteroid discoveries demoted the whole class.

Physical characteristics

Ceres is an oblate spheroid about 940 km across, differentiated into a rocky core and an icy mantle, possibly with remnants of a subsurface brine ocean. Its dark, carbonaceous surface features craters, the cryovolcanic dome Ahuna Mons, and spectacular bright spots (faculae) in Occator crater composed mainly of sodium carbonate salts deposited by briny water reaching the surface. Gravity measurements by Dawn revealed a muddy, porous interior. Traces of water vapour were detected around Ceres by the Herschel Space Observatory in 2014.

Exploration

NASA's Dawn spacecraft, after orbiting the asteroid Vesta in 2011–2012, arrived at Ceres in March 2015 — the first mission to orbit two extraterrestrial bodies and the first to visit a dwarf planet (arriving months before New Horizons reached Pluto). Dawn mapped Ceres in detail until its fuel ran out in November 2018, and remains in orbit around it.

Classification

In 2006 the International Astronomical Union defined "dwarf planet"; Ceres qualified as a body in hydrostatic equilibrium that has not cleared its orbital neighbourhood, moving it from the asteroid category while it remains designated "1 Ceres".
