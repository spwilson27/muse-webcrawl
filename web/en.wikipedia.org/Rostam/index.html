Rostam

Rostam (Persian: رستم) is a legendary hero in Persian mythology, the son of Zāl and Rudaba, and the central figure of Ferdowsi's epic poem the Shahnameh (Book of Kings). He is the greatest of the paladins of Iranian legend, a champion who defends Iran from its enemies across a lifespan of centuries, and is famed for his immense strength, his loyal horse Rakhsh, and his tragic duel with his own son, Sohrab.

Birth and youth

Rostam's birth was extraordinary: because of his enormous size, his mother Rudaba could deliver him only with the aid of the mythical bird Simurgh, which instructed that the child be delivered by a form of caesarean section. Rostam grew rapidly and as a boy already performed heroic feats, taming the wild stallion Rakhsh and slaying a maddened white elephant single-handedly.

The Seven Labours

Rostam's most famous adventures are his Seven Labours (Haft Khān), undertaken to rescue King Kay Kavus, who had been captured by demons (divs) in Mazandaran. Along the way Rostam overcame a lion, a desert, a dragon, a sorceress, the demon Olād, the div Arzhang, and finally the White Demon (Div-e Sepid), whose blood restored the king's sight.

Rostam and Sohrab

The most poignant episode of the cycle is the tragedy of Rostam and Sohrab. During an earlier journey, Rostam had fathered a son, Sohrab, with the princess Tahmina in Turan. Raised without knowledge of his father, Sohrab became a mighty warrior and marched against Iran hoping to find him. The two met in single combat, neither knowing the other's identity. After a long struggle Rostam fatally wounded Sohrab and only then discovered, by a token on the young man's arm, that he had killed his own son. The story is one of the most celebrated tragedies in Persian literature.

Death

Rostam met his end through treachery: his jealous half-brother Shaghad lured him into a pit filled with poisoned spears and blades, into which Rostam and Rakhsh fell. With his last strength Rostam shot an arrow through a tree and killed Shaghad before dying.

Cultural significance

Rostam is an enduring national hero of Iran and the Persian-speaking world, appearing in countless stories, paintings, coffeehouse performances, and modern adaptations. His epithet "Tahamtan" ("strong-bodied") and his tiger-skin garment, the babr-e bayān, are instantly recognisable symbols.
