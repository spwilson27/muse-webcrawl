Speedway Grand Prix

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Riders slide through the turns on brakeless motorcycles

The Speedway Grand Prix (SGP) is a series of motorcycle speedway events that together decide the annual FIM Speedway World Championship. Introduced in 1995, replacing the previous one-off World Final, the series consists of a number of rounds held across Europe (and occasionally beyond), in which 16 permanent riders accumulate championship points.

Format

Each Grand Prix features 16 riders contesting 20 qualifying heats, in which every rider races against each other rider once. The top eight progress to two semi-finals, and the top two from each semi-final advance to the final. Championship points are awarded based on finishing position in each round, and the rider with the highest cumulative total at the end of the season is crowned world champion.

History

The Grand Prix system was adopted from 1995; the first SGP world champion was Denmark's Hans Nielsen. Successful riders of the series include Tony Rickardsson of Sweden (six world titles), Nicki Pedersen of Denmark, Jason Crump of Australia, Greg Hancock of the United States, Tai Woffinden of Great Britain, and Poland's Bartosz Zmarzlik, a multiple champion of the 2020s.
