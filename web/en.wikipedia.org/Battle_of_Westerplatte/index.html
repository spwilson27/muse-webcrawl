Battle of Westerplatte

Date1–7 September 1939
LocationWesterplatte peninsula, Free City of Danzig
ResultGerman victory
DefendersPolish garrison of about 200 men

The Battle of Westerplatte was the first battle of the German invasion of Poland and thus the opening engagement of the Second World War in Europe. A small Polish garrison held the Military Transit Depot on the Westerplatte peninsula against overwhelming German attacks for seven days.

The attack

At 04:45 on 1 September 1939, the German battleship Schleswig-Holstein opened fire on the depot, beginning the assault. The roughly 200 Polish defenders, commanded by Major Henryk Sucharski, repelled repeated attacks by naval infantry, dive bombers, and artillery despite being vastly outnumbered.

Surrender and legacy

The garrison capitulated on 7 September, having held out far longer than expected. The defence of Westerplatte became a powerful symbol of Polish resistance, and the site is now a national memorial.
