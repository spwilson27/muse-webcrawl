Trading card game

A trading card game (TCG), also called a collectible card game (CCG), is a type of card game that mixes strategic deck building elements with features of trading cards. Players construct their own decks from a large pool of cards published in sets, and play matches against other players whose decks may be entirely different.

History

The genre began in 1993 with Magic: The Gathering, designed by Richard Garfield and published by Wizards of the Coast. Its success was followed by games such as the Pokémon Trading Card Game (1996) and Yu-Gi-Oh! (1999), which became global phenomena. Cards are sold in randomised booster packs, encouraging collecting and trading on a secondary market where rare cards can command high prices.

Gameplay

Most TCGs share common features: cards represent creatures, spells, items or resources; players typically draw cards, pay resource costs, and reduce an opponent's life total or achieve a game-specific victory condition. Deck construction — choosing which cards to include, usually within limits such as a minimum deck size and a maximum number of copies — is considered a core skill of the genre.
