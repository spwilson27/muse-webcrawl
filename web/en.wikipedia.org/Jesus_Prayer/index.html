Jesus Prayer

The Jesus Prayer, also known as the Prayer of the Heart, is a short formulaic prayer esteemed and advocated especially within Eastern Christianity and Catholicism: "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner." The prayer is most often repeated continuously as part of the eremitic practice known as hesychasm, using a prayer rope (komboskini or chotki) to count repetitions.

Origins

The prayer's roots lie in the Egyptian desert monasticism of the early Church and the biblical models of the publican's plea ("God, be merciful to me a sinner", Luke 18:13) and the blind man's cry to Jesus. Evagrius Pontus and Diadochos of Photiki wrote of brief invocations of the name of Jesus, and John Climacus recommended uniting prayer with breathing. The practice was systematised by the hesychast monks of Mount Athos in the 14th century, whose theology was defended by Gregory Palamas, and spread through the anthology known as the Philokalia.

Practice

Practitioners seek, through ceaseless repetition coordinated with the breath and the heartbeat, to make the prayer "self-acting" in the heart, fulfilling the injunction to "pray without ceasing" (1 Thessalonians 5:17). The anonymous 19th-century Russian spiritual classic The Way of a Pilgrim popularised the practice in the West. A shorter core, "Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy on me", is also used, as is invocation of the name "Jesus" alone.
