The history of Hungary before the Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin in the late 9th century spans the Roman province of Pannonia and successive Hunnic, Avar and Slavic states. The Magyars, a Finno-Ugric people from the Eurasian steppe, conquered the basin around 895 under Árpád, whose dynasty founded the Christian Kingdom of Hungary when Stephen I was crowned in 1000/1001.

Medieval kingdom

Medieval Hungary became a major European power: it repelled (after devastating invasion in 1241–42) the Mongols, expanded under the Angevin kings, and under Matthias Corvinus (1458–1490) was a Renaissance centre whose Bibliotheca Corviniana was among Europe's great libraries. John Hunyadi's victory over the Ottomans at Belgrade (1456) checked Ottoman expansion for decades, but defeat at Mohács in 1526 — where King Louis II died — shattered the kingdom.

Partition and Habsburg rule

For 150 years Hungary was divided three ways: the centre under Ottoman rule, the east as the Principality of Transylvania, and the west and north under the Habsburgs. Habsburg reconquest (1686–1699) reunited the country but as part of the Austrian monarchy; Rákóczi's War of Independence (1703–1711) failed, and Hungary remained a Habsburg crownland. The Reform Era, led by István Széchenyi and Lajos Kossuth, culminated in the Revolution and War of Independence of 1848–49, crushed with Russian help, and then the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, which made Hungary an equal partner in the Dual Monarchy.

20th century

After the First World War the Treaty of Trianon (1920) stripped Hungary of about two-thirds of its territory and population — a lasting national trauma. Interwar Hungary under Regent Miklós Horthy aligned with Nazi Germany, regained some territories, and in 1944 was occupied by Germany; most of Hungary's Jewish population was deported and murdered in the Holocaust. After 1945 Hungary became a communist satellite of the USSR; the 1956 Revolution was crushed by Soviet invasion. In 1989 Hungary helped open the Iron Curtain and transitioned to democracy, joining NATO in 1999 and the European Union in 2004.
