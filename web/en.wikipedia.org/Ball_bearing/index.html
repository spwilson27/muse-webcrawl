Ball bearing

A ball bearing is a type of rolling-element bearing that uses balls to maintain the separation between the bearing races. The purpose of a ball bearing is to reduce rotational friction and support radial and axial loads.

Design

A typical ball bearing consists of an inner race, an outer race, a set of balls, and usually a cage that spaces the balls apart. Most ball bearings support both radial and axial loads to some degree. Common designs include deep-groove, angular-contact, self-aligning, and thrust ball bearings. Balls and races are typically made of high-carbon chromium steel (such as AISI 52100), sometimes stainless steel or ceramic for specialized applications.

History

Rolling elements were known in antiquity; the modern ball bearing took shape in the 18th and 19th centuries, with Philip Vaughan's 1794 patent for an iron ball bearing for carriage axles and Jules Suriray's 1869 patent for bicycle bearings. Companies such as SKF, founded in 1907 by Sven Wingquist (inventor of the self-aligning ball bearing), industrialized their manufacture.
