Coming of age

Coming of age is the transition from childhood to adulthood, marked in societies by changes in legal status, social expectations and often by formal rites of passage. The term also names a literary and film genre — rooted in the Bildungsroman — that follows a young protagonist's growth into maturity and self-knowledge.

Rites of passage

Many cultures formalise the transition with ceremonies. Examples include the Jewish bar and bat mitzvah at age 13 or 12; Christian confirmation; the Latin American quinceañera for a girl's fifteenth birthday; Japan's Coming of Age Day (Seijin no Hi) for those who have turned 20 (the age of majority in Japan was lowered to 18 in 2022); Hindu upanayana; and debutante balls and "sweet sixteen" parties in the United States. Traditional initiation ceremonies involving seclusion, instruction or ordeals are found in many Indigenous societies.

Legal markers

Modern states mark adulthood through the age of majority — most commonly 18 — at which a person gains the right to vote, sign contracts and be treated as an adult in criminal law. Related thresholds include ages of consent, minimum drinking and driving ages and school-leaving ages, which vary between jurisdictions and do not always coincide.

As a genre

Coming-of-age stories focus on the protagonist's personal growth, loss of innocence and negotiation of identity. Classic literary examples include The Catcher in the Rye and To Kill a Mockingbird; in film, works such as Stand by Me, Boyhood and Lady Bird exemplify the genre. Anthropologists, following Arnold van Gennep's The Rites of Passage (1909), analyse such transitions as separation, liminality and incorporation.
