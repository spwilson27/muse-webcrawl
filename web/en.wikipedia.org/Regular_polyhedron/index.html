Regular polyhedron

A regular polyhedron is a polyhedron whose symmetry group acts transitively on its flags. A regular polyhedron is highly symmetrical, being all of edge-transitive, vertex-transitive, and face-transitive. In classical contexts, many — though not all — authors require that the faces be congruent regular polygons; under this definition there are exactly five convex regular polyhedra, the Platonic solids, known since antiquity.

Euclid proved in the Elements that these five are the only convex regular polyhedra: at each vertex the face angles must sum to less than 360°, which severely restricts the possible combinations of regular polygonal faces meeting at a point.

Non-convex regular polyhedra

If self-intersecting (star) polyhedra are admitted, four further regular polyhedra appear: the Kepler–Poinsot solids — the small stellated dodecahedron, great dodecahedron, great stellated dodecahedron, and great icosahedron. Together with the Platonic solids they form the nine finite regular polyhedra in the broadest classical sense.

Higher dimensions and generalisations

The concept generalises to regular polytopes in any dimension, classified by Schläfli, and to regular maps and tilings. In the plane the analogous objects are the regular polygons, and regular polyhedra have analogues among the uniform tilings of the sphere.
