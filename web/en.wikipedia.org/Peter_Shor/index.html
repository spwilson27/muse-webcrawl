Peter Shor

Peter Williston Shor (born August 14, 1959) is an American professor of applied mathematics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). He is best known for Shor's algorithm, a quantum algorithm for factoring integers exponentially faster than the best known classical algorithm, which launched the modern era of interest in quantum computing.

Career

Shor studied at Caltech and earned his PhD in applied mathematics from MIT in 1985. After a postdoctoral position at Berkeley, he joined AT&T Bell Laboratories, where in 1994 he published his factoring algorithm, showing that a quantum computer could break widely used public-key cryptosystems such as RSA. He also devised a quantum algorithm for the discrete logarithm problem.

Quantum error correction

In 1995 Shor demonstrated the first quantum error correction code — the nine-qubit Shor code — proving that, despite decoherence, arbitrarily long quantum computations are possible in principle. This foundational result, alongside work on fault-tolerant quantum computation, underpins the field's theoretical framework.

Honours

Shor has received the Nevanlinna Prize (1998), a MacArthur Fellowship ("genius grant", 1999), the Gödel Prize (1999), and the Dirac Medal, and shared the 2023 Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics with David Deutsch, Charles Bennett and Gilles Brassard for foundational work in quantum information.
