Base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS)
FieldInternational taxation
Key bodyOECD / G20 BEPS Project
Action plan15 actions (2013–2015)
Follow-upGlobal minimum tax (Pillar Two), 15%

Base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS) refers to tax planning strategies used by multinational enterprises that exploit gaps and mismatches in tax rules to artificially shift profits to low- or no-tax locations where the enterprise has little or no economic activity, thereby eroding the tax bases of higher-tax jurisdictions.

The OECD/G20 BEPS Project

In 2013 the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), mandated by the G20, launched a project to address BEPS. In October 2015 it delivered a package of 15 actions covering the coherence of corporate tax rules, the substance of transactions, transfer pricing, treaty abuse, permanent establishment definitions, harmful tax practices, and transparency measures such as country-by-country reporting of multinationals' activities and taxes paid.

Implementation

More than 135 jurisdictions joined the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework to implement the BEPS minimum standards: countering harmful tax practices, preventing treaty abuse, country-by-country reporting, and improving dispute resolution. A multilateral instrument (MLI), signed in 2017, allowed countries to modify thousands of bilateral tax treaties simultaneously.

Two-pillar solution

In October 2021 the Inclusive Framework agreed a two-pillar solution to address the tax challenges of digitalization: Pillar One reallocates some taxing rights over the largest multinationals to market jurisdictions, while Pillar Two establishes a 15% global minimum corporate tax, which many jurisdictions began implementing from 2024.
