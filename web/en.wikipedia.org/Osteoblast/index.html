Osteoblasts (from the Greek osteon, "bone", and blastos, "germ") are cells with a single nucleus that synthesize bone. In the process of bone formation, osteoblasts function in groups of connected cells called osteons; individual cells cannot make bone alone. They are found on the surface of new bone, where they produce the organic matrix, or osteoid, which subsequently mineralises to form mature bone tissue.

Development

Osteoblasts derive from mesenchymal stem cells, whose commitment to the osteoblast lineage is driven principally by the transcription factors Runx2 and osterix (Sp7), under the influence of the Wnt/β-catenin signalling pathway and bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs). Commitment can proceed through the osteochondral route shared with chondrocytes. Mutations in Runx2 cause cleidocranial dysplasia, a disorder of defective bone formation.

Function

Active osteoblasts are cuboidal cells rich in rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, reflecting their secretory role. They secrete osteoid composed mainly of type I collagen along with non-collagenous proteins such as osteocalcin, osteopontin, and bone sialoprotein, and they release alkaline phosphatase and matrix vesicles that initiate mineralisation with hydroxyapatite crystals. Osteoblasts also regulate the bone-resorbing osteoclasts through the RANKL/OPG signalling system: they express RANKL, which activates osteoclast formation, and its decoy receptor osteoprotegerin, which inhibits it.

Fates

As bone formation proceeds, an osteoblast may become trapped within the matrix it has secreted, differentiating into an osteocyte housed in a lacuna; flatten into an inactive bone-lining cell on the bone surface; or undergo apoptosis. Osteocytes, connected by long canalicular processes, form a mechanosensory network that orchestrates the balance of formation and resorption during bone remodelling.

Clinical relevance

The balance between osteoblast-mediated formation and osteoclast-mediated resorption determines bone mass. Excess resorption relative to formation underlies osteoporosis; anabolic osteoporosis drugs such as teriparatide (parathyroid hormone analogue) and the sclerostin inhibitor romosozumab act by stimulating osteoblast activity. Defects in osteoblast collagen production cause osteogenesis imperfecta ("brittle bone disease"), and malignant transformation of osteoblast-lineage cells gives rise to osteosarcoma, the commonest primary bone cancer of adolescents.
