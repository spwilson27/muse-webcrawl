Suica

The Suica is a rechargeable contactless smart card, electronic money card that can be used as a fare card on train lines in Japan. Launched on 18 November 2001 by the East Japan Railway Company (JR East), it is used across the Greater Tokyo Area and other regions, and since nationwide interoperability began in 2013 it works on most major Japanese transit IC systems, including PASMO, ICOCA, and Kitaca.

Suica is based on Sony's FeliCa near-field communication technology. The name is an abbreviation of "Super Urban Intelligent Card" and also plays on suika, the Japanese word for watermelon, and the phrase sui-sui, meaning "smoothly". Its mascot is a penguin. Beyond transport, Suica functions as electronic money at convenience stores, vending machines, and shops. Mobile Suica, launched in 2006 for mobile phones and later Apple Pay and Google Pay, allows the card to be carried on a smartphone or smartwatch.

Types

Cards include standard anonymous Suica, personalized My Suica with the holder's name (reissuable if lost), and commuter passes. Since 2021 JR East has promoted the mascot penguin as one of Japan's best-known brand characters.
