Grandmaster (chess)

Grandmaster (GM) is the highest title awarded to chess players by the world chess federation FIDE, below only the World Champion. Once achieved, the title is held for life (though it can be revoked for cheating or fraud). As of recent counts there are roughly 1,800 living and deceased grandmasters in total, with about 2,000 having held the title historically; only a few dozen are women, since women's results qualify under the same criteria for the open GM title.

Awarded by: FIDE

Requirements: Elo rating of 2500+ and three GM norms (or qualifying achievements)

First awarded: 1950 (27 players)

Youngest ever: Abhimanyu Mishra (12 years, 4 months, 2021)

Qualification

The standard route requires achieving an Elo rating of at least 2500 at some point and scoring three "norms" — performances at a rating level of about 2600 in qualifying tournaments with international opposition and titled players. Norms must total at least 27 games. Alternative paths include winning certain events outright, such as the Women's World Championship, the World Junior Championship, or qualifying for the World Cup quarterfinals; the winner of the Women's World Championship or a player reaching the final of the women's Grand Prix can earn the title directly under FIDE regulations.

History

The term "grandmaster" was used informally in the 19th and early 20th centuries; Tsar Nicholas II is said to have bestowed it on the five finalists of St. Petersburg 1914 (Lasker, Capablanca, Alekhine, Tarrasch, Marshall). FIDE formalized the title in 1950, awarding it to 27 of the world's strongest players. The Elo system, introduced later, made criteria quantitative. Sergey Karjakin long held the record as youngest grandmaster (12 years 7 months, 2002) until Abhimanyu Mishra broke it in 2021. Nona Gaprindashvili was the first woman to earn the (open) grandmaster title, in 1978; Judit Polgár remains the strongest female player in history by rating.

Other titles

Below Grandmaster, FIDE awards International Master (IM, 2400 + norms), FIDE Master (FM, 2300), and Candidate Master (CM, 2200), as well as women-specific titles (WGM, WIM, WFM, WCM) with lower thresholds. "Super-grandmaster" is an informal label for the elite, typically rated above 2700.
