Arthur Andersen

Arthur Andersen LLP was an American holding company based in Chicago, formerly one of the "Big Five" accounting firms, providing audit, tax, and consulting services to large corporations. The firm collapsed in 2002 in the wake of the Enron scandal.

History

The firm was founded in 1913 by Arthur E. Andersen, a Northwestern University professor, as Andersen, DeLany & Co. It built a reputation for integrity — Andersen famously refused railroad client pressure over accounting in its early years — and grew into a global partnership. Its consulting arm, Andersen Consulting, split off in 2000 to become Accenture after a bitter arbitration.

Enron scandal and collapse

Arthur Andersen was Enron's auditor and was convicted in June 2002 of obstruction of justice for destroying Enron-related documents. The conviction, though unanimously overturned by the US Supreme Court in 2005 because of faulty jury instructions, came too late: clients had fled, and the firm had surrendered its audit licences and effectively ceased operating, with tens of thousands of jobs lost worldwide.
