Summit of the Americas

The Summits of the Americas are a series of international summit meetings bringing together the leaders of countries in the Americas — North, Central, and South America and the Caribbean — to discuss common policy issues. They are administered in connection with the Organization of American States (OAS).

History

The first summit was held in Miami, United States, in December 1994, convened by President Bill Clinton; it launched negotiations for a Free Trade Area of the Americas (FTAA) and a hemispheric agenda on democracy, trade, and development. Subsequent summits were held in Santiago (1998), Quebec City (2001), Mar del Plata (2005) — where the FTAA project effectively collapsed amid protests — Port of Spain (2009), Cartagena (2012), Panama City (2015, the first attended by Cuba), Lima (2018), and Los Angeles (2022). Cuba's participation, the exclusion of governments deemed undemocratic (Venezuela, Nicaragua, and Cuba were not invited to Los Angeles in 2022, prompting a partial boycott), and drug policy have been recurring points of controversy.

Purpose

Each summit produces a declaration and plan of action on themes such as democratic governance, migration, climate change, health, and digital transformation, with follow-up through the OAS and the Summit Implementation Review Group.
