Wigan is a town in Greater Manchester, England, on the River Douglas. The town is midway between Manchester and Liverpool. It forms part of the Metropolitan Borough of Wigan and has a long history as a coal-mining and mill town, later becoming known for manufacturing and for its sporting clubs.

History

Wigan is believed to occupy the site of the Roman settlement Coccium. It received a royal charter as a borough in 1246. During the Industrial Revolution the town expanded rapidly on the back of the Lancashire coalfield and its cotton mills; the Leeds and Liverpool Canal and the railways made it a transport hub, and Wigan Pier — a coal-loading wharf on the canal — became famous through George Orwell's 1937 book The Road to Wigan Pier, which documented working-class life in the town.

Culture and sport

Wigan is renowned for rugby league: Wigan Warriors are among the most successful clubs in the sport's history. Wigan Athletic, the town's football club, won the FA Cup in 2013. The town also gave its name to Wigan Casino, the celebrated northern soul venue of the 1970s, and is noted for local foods including the "Wigan kebab" (a meat pie in a barm cake) and Uncle Joe's Mint Balls.
