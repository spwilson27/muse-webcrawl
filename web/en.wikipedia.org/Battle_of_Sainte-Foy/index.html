The Battle of Sainte-Foy, sometimes called the Battle of Quebec, was fought on 28 April 1760 near Quebec City during the Seven Years' War. A French force under the Chevalier de Lévis defeated the British garrison army of James Murray, in the last French victory in Canada — though it failed to retake the city.

Background

After the fall of Quebec in September 1759, the British garrison under Murray endured a harsh winter. In spring 1760 Lévis marched from Montreal with some 7,000 men to recapture the city before British reinforcements could arrive by river. Murray, with roughly 3,800 men, marched out to meet him on the Plains of Abraham and the heights near Sainte-Foy.

The battle

Unlike the previous September, the French were able to deploy their full line. After initial British success, French numbers told; Murray's troops, their artillery mired in melting snow, were outflanked and driven back into Quebec with heavy casualties on both sides — proportionally bloodier than the 1759 battle.

Aftermath

Lévis besieged Quebec but lacked heavy guns and supplies. When British warships arrived in May, he withdrew to Montreal, which surrendered in September 1760, completing the British conquest of New France.
