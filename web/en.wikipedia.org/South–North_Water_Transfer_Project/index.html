South–North Water Transfer Project
Country: China
Construction began: 2002
Planned transfer: ~44.8 billion m³/year

The South–North Water Transfer Project (Chinese: 南水北调工程) is a multi-decade infrastructure project in China intended to channel fresh water from the water-rich south to the arid north, including Beijing and Tianjin. It is among the largest engineering projects ever undertaken, with projected costs exceeding those of the Three Gorges Dam.

Routes

Three routes were planned. The Eastern Route, opened in 2013, upgrades the Grand Canal to pump Yangtze water northward into Shandong and Tianjin. The Central Route, opened in December 2014, carries water by gravity from the enlarged Danjiangkou Reservoir on the Han River through a 1,400 km canal to Beijing. A far more difficult Western Route, which would tunnel water from Yangtze tributaries through high mountains to the Yellow River headwaters, remains unbuilt.

Impacts

The project required the resettlement of hundreds of thousands of people near Danjiangkou, raised concerns about water quality, subsidence in over-pumped northern aquifers, and the ecology of donor rivers. By the 2020s the transferred water had become a major source of supply for Beijing, allowing groundwater levels there to recover.
