Battle of Ceresole

The Battle of Ceresole was fought on 11 April 1544 during the Italian War of 1542–1546, near Ceresole d'Alba in Piedmont, between a French army under François de Bourbon, Count of Enghien, and an Imperial-Spanish army commanded by Alfonso d'Avalos. It was one of the few pitched battles of the Italian Wars in this period and ended in a French victory.

Background

France aimed to relieve the fortress of Carignano, besieged by d'Avalos. Enghien, eager for battle, received permission from Francis I and positioned his army — French infantry, Gascon and Italian troops, Swiss, and heavy cavalry of the gendarmerie — on the ridges near Ceresole. D'Avalos advanced to attack with Spanish veterans and landsknechts.

The battle

The fight opened with a prolonged skirmish of arquebusiers. In the centre, two great bodies of landsknechts clashed in a brutal pike fight won by the Imperial troops, but French and Swiss infantry defeated the Spanish on one flank while Enghien's cavalry routed the Imperial horse and then fell on the landsknechts' rear. The Imperial army collapsed, suffering perhaps 5,000–6,000 dead and many prisoners; French losses were far lighter.

Aftermath

Despite the victory, the French could not exploit it: Francis I needed troops against the English and Imperial threats nearer home, and Carignano's surrender shortly afterwards limited the gain. The battle is noted for its unusually intense musketry skirmish, a sign of the growing role of firearms.
