Toumani Diabaté (10 August 1965 – 19 July 2024) was a Malian kora player. He was widely regarded as the world's leading exponent of the instrument, a 21-string bridge-harp of West Africa, and he brought it to international audiences through solo recordings and collaborations across genres. He came from a long line of griots — hereditary musicians and oral historians — tracing his family's kora tradition back many generations; his father, Sidiki Diabaté, was himself a celebrated kora player.

Career

Diabaté made his recording debut in 1987 with Kaira, the first solo kora album, recorded in a single afternoon in London. He went on to lead the Symmetric Orchestra, which combined traditional Mande instruments with Western ones, and to record acclaimed duo albums with the Malian guitarist Ali Farka Touré — In the Heart of the Moon (2005) and Ali and Toumani (2010), both of which won Grammy Awards. His other notable collaborations include Songhai with the Spanish flamenco group Ketama, work with the American blues musician Taj Mahal, Björk, Béla Fleck, and Damon Albarn.

Legacy

Despite childhood polio that left him walking with a crutch, Diabaté toured the world for decades and trained a new generation of players, including his son Sidiki Diabaté, a leading figure in Malian popular music. He died in Bamako in July 2024 after a short illness and was mourned internationally as an ambassador of Malian culture.
