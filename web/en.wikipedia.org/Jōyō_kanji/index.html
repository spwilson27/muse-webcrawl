Jōyō kanji

Jōyō kanji (常用漢字, "regular-use kanji")
Count: 2,136 characters
Issued by: Japanese Ministry of Education
Current list: 2010 revision

The jōyō kanji (常用漢字, literally "regular-use Chinese characters") is the official list of kanji characters and their authorized readings published by the Japanese Ministry of Education. The current list, promulgated in 2010, contains 2,136 characters and serves as the standard for kanji used in official documents, newspapers, broadcasting, and education in Japan.

History

The jōyō list succeeded the postwar tōyō kanji ("kanji for current use") list of 1,850 characters issued in 1946. In 1981 the cabinet adopted the first jōyō kanji list of 1,945 characters, reframed as a guideline rather than a strict limit. The 2010 revision added 196 characters — many common in proper names and everyday words, such as 俺, 岡, and 韓 — and removed five, bringing the total to 2,136, while also revising some readings.

Education

Of the 2,136 characters, 1,026 — the kyōiku kanji — are taught in elementary school, distributed across the six grades; the remainder are taught in junior high school. By the end of compulsory education, Japanese students are expected to be able to read all jōyō kanji and write most of them. The list also defines the permitted on'yomi and kun'yomi readings for each character, with readings outside the list typically annotated with furigana in published texts.
