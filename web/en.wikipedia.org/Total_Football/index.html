Total Football

Total Football (Dutch: totaalvoetbal) is a tactical system in association football in which any outfield player can take over the role of any other player in a team. Because no player is fixed in a predetermined role, the team's structure is maintained by players fluidly filling each other's positions — demanding exceptional technique, awareness and fitness.

History

The system has roots in Jack Reynolds's Ajax sides of the 1910s–1940s and Gusztáv Sebes's Hungary team of the 1950s, but it was perfected in the 1970s by Rinus Michels at Ajax and with the Netherlands national team. Inspired by his captain and star, Johan Cruyff, Ajax won three consecutive European Cups (1971–1973), and the Netherlands reached the 1974 World Cup final, losing to West Germany despite being widely admired as the tournament's finest side.

Principles

Total Football relies on constant movement, quick passing, pressing the opposition high up the pitch, and an aggressive offside trap. Cruyff later imported its philosophy to FC Barcelona as coach, where it evolved into the possession-centred style known as tiki-taka under his disciple Pep Guardiola, shaping modern football worldwide.
