Meiji Shrine

Meiji Shrine (明治神宮, Meiji Jingū) is a Shinto shrine in Shibuya, Tokyo, dedicated to the deified spirits of Emperor Meiji and his wife, Empress Shōken. Completed in 1920, eight years after the emperor's death, it stands in a 70-hectare forest of some 120,000 trees donated from all over Japan, adjacent to Yoyogi Park.

LocationYoyogi Kamizono-chō, Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan
Festival3 November (Reitaisai)
Established1 November 1920
DeitiesEmperor Meiji, Empress Shōken

History

After Emperor Meiji died in 1912, the Imperial Diet passed a resolution to commemorate his role in the Meiji Restoration. An iris garden in Tokyo that the emperor and empress had visited was chosen as the site. Construction began in 1915 under architect Itō Chūta, and the shrine was formally dedicated on 1 November 1920. The original buildings were destroyed in air raids during the Second World War; the present structures were rebuilt with public donations and completed in October 1958.

Grounds

The shrine precinct is divided into the Naien (inner precinct), containing the shrine buildings, and the Gaien (outer precinct), which includes the Meiji Memorial Picture Gallery and sports facilities such as the National Stadium. The forest surrounding the shrine was planted by volunteers with trees donated from every prefecture and from Japan's overseas territories of the time; it was designed as an artificial forest intended to mature into a self-sustaining ecosystem.

Worship and events

Meiji Shrine is one of Japan's most visited shrines, receiving millions of visitors for hatsumōde (the first shrine visit of the New Year). Traditional Shinto weddings are frequently held there, and the annual grand festival on 3 November features processions, bugaku dance and martial arts demonstrations.
