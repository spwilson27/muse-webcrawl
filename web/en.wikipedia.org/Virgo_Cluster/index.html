Virgo Cluster

The Virgo Cluster is a large cluster of galaxies whose center is about 53.8 million light-years (16.5 megaparsecs) away in the constellation Virgo. It contains roughly 1,300, and possibly up to 2,000, member galaxies, and forms the heart of the larger Virgo Supercluster, of which the Local Group — containing the Milky Way — is an outlying member.

Characteristics

The cluster is an irregular assembly of at least three subclusters centered on the giant elliptical galaxies M87, M86 and M49, which are still in the process of merging. Its spiral galaxies show a marked deficiency of neutral hydrogen compared with isolated spirals, an effect attributed to ram-pressure stripping as they plow through the hot intracluster medium — a plasma that emits X-rays and contains more mass between the galaxies than in all the cluster's stars combined.

Notable members

The Virgo Cluster is rich in Messier objects, since Charles Messier catalogued many of its brightest galaxies. M87 is famous for the relativistic jet emerging from its supermassive black hole — the first black hole ever directly imaged, by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2019. Other notable members include the Sombrero Galaxy (M104, sometimes assigned to the cluster's outskirts), M84, M86, M49, M58, M59, M60, M61 and the interacting Eyes Galaxies (NGC 4435 and NGC 4438). Markarian's Chain is a striking arc of galaxies in the cluster's core.
