Television show

A television show, also called a television program (or simply a TV show), is any content produced for viewing on a television set or, increasingly, on streaming platforms, which is broadcast via over-the-air, satellite, cable or internet distribution.

Formats

Television programs include scripted genres — drama series, sitcoms, soap operas, miniseries, anthology series, telenovelas and television films — and unscripted genres such as news, talk shows, game shows, reality television, sports broadcasts, documentaries and variety shows. Scripted series are usually divided into seasons (series in British usage) and episodes.

Production and distribution

Programs are produced by production companies and broadcasters, financed through advertising, subscriptions, license fees or public funding, and distributed through broadcast schedules, syndication and on-demand streaming. Standard episode lengths historically matched broadcast slots of 30 or 60 minutes including advertising.

History

Regular television broadcasting began in the 1930s and expanded rapidly after World War II. The medium passed through the network era, the multi-channel transition of cable and satellite, and the current era of global streaming platforms, which has changed commissioning, binge-watching habits and international distribution.
