Gongfu tea ceremony

OriginChaozhou (Teochew) region, Guangdong, China
Typical teasOolong teas (e.g. Tieguanyin, Phoenix Dancong)
VesselsSmall teapot or gaiwan, fairness pitcher, aroma and tasting cups

The gongfu tea ceremony (Chinese: gōngfū chá, "tea made with skill/effort") is a Chinese method of brewing tea with considerable ceremony, originating in the Chaozhou region of Guangdong and closely associated with Fujian and Taiwan. It emphasises small vessels, a high leaf-to-water ratio, and many short infusions to bring out the full flavour of oolong and other teas.

Procedure

The brewer warms the vessels with hot water, adds a generous amount of leaf to a small clay teapot or gaiwan, and rinses the leaves briefly. Successive infusions are steeped for only tens of seconds; the liquor is decanted into a fairness pitcher (gong dao bei) to equalise strength, then poured into tiny tasting cups, sometimes via aroma cups that showcase the tea's fragrance. A single portion of leaves may be brewed five to ten times or more.

Equipment and setting

A typical set comprises a brewing tray to catch spilled water, a small Yixing clay teapot or porcelain gaiwan, a kettle, and matched cups. The unhurried, hospitable ritual is a centrepiece of Teochew social life and is now practised by tea enthusiasts worldwide. Gongfu brewing influenced, and contrasts with, Japanese and Korean tea traditions and with Western-style large-pot brewing.
