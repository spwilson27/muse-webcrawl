Bubble gum

Bubble gum is a type of chewing gum designed to be inflated out of the mouth as a bubble. It typically contains a gum base with higher elasticity than ordinary chewing gum, plus sweeteners and flavourings, traditionally pink in colour.

Chewing gum dates to antiquity, but bubble gum was invented in 1928 by Walter Diemer, an accountant at the Fleer Chewing Gum Company, whose recipe became Dubble Bubble. Its pink colour came from the only dye available to him.

Bubble gum became a cultural phenomenon through bubble-blowing contests, comic strips included in wrappers, and its association with baseball cards. Sugar-free versions use sweeteners such as xylitol and sorbitol.
