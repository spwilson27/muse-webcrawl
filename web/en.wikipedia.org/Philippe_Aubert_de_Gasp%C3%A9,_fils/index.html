Philippe-Joseph Aubert de Gaspé (1814 – 7 March 1841), known as Philippe Aubert de Gaspé, fils (the son), was a French Canadian writer and journalist. The eldest son of the seigneur and author Philippe Aubert de Gaspé, he is remembered as the author of the first French-Canadian novel.

Life and work

Educated at the Séminaire de Québec, Aubert de Gaspé fils worked as a journalist and legislative reporter for newspapers including Le Canadien and the Quebec Mercury. In 1837 he published L'Influence d'un livre (The Influence of a Book), a historical novel of adventure set in the Gaspé region, generally regarded as the first novel written in French Canada. After a scandal in the Legislative Assembly, where he was accused of leaving a malodorous substance, he was exiled from Quebec and settled in Halifax, where he died in 1841, aged 26.
