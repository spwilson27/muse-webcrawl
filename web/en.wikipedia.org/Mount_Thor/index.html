Mount Thor

Elevation1,675 m (5,495 ft)
LocationAuyuittuq National Park, Baffin Island, Nunavut, Canada
Notable featureGreatest purely vertical drop on Earth: 1,250 m (4,100 ft)

Mount Thor, officially Thor Peak, is a mountain with an elevation of 1,675 metres (5,495 ft) located in Auyuittuq National Park, on Baffin Island, Nunavut, Canada. The mountain is located 46 km (29 mi) northeast of Pangnirtung and features Earth's greatest vertical drop of 1,250 m (4,100 ft), at an average angle of 105 degrees: the granite west face overhangs as it falls. Despite its remoteness, this feature makes the mountain a popular rock climbing site. It is named after Thor, the Norse thunder god.

Geography

Mount Thor is part of the Baffin Mountains, which form part of the Arctic Cordillera. It rises above Akshayuk Pass, an ancient glacial valley that runs through the park, and stands near Mount Asgard, another famous Baffin Island peak. The mountain is composed of granite, carved by glaciers into dramatic spires and walls.

Climbing

The west face was first climbed in 1985 by an American team. The mountain has since attracted big-wall climbers and BASE jumpers drawn by its uninterrupted vertical drop. In 2006, a team set a record for the longest successful BASE jump descent profile from the cliffs. Access is typically via Pangnirtung or Qikiqtarjuaq, followed by a long approach up Akshayuk Pass.
