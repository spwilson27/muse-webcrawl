Focal adhesion

Focal adhesion
Cell junction: Cell–extracellular matrix
Key proteins: Integrin, talin, vinculin, paxillin
Key kinase: Focal adhesion kinase (FAK)
Function: Adhesion, signaling, mechanosensing

In cell biology, focal adhesions (also cell–matrix adhesions or FAs) are large macromolecular assemblies through which mechanical force and regulatory signals are transmitted between the extracellular matrix (ECM) and an interacting cell.

Structure and composition

Focal adhesions consist of integrin receptors spanning the plasma membrane, linked through adaptor proteins such as talin, vinculin, and paxillin to the actin cytoskeleton. They are dynamic structures that assemble and disassemble as cells migrate.

Functions

FAs anchor cells to their substrate, transmit traction forces during cell migration, and act as signaling hubs involving focal adhesion kinase (FAK) and Src-family kinases. They sense matrix stiffness (mechanotransduction) and influence proliferation, differentiation, and survival.
