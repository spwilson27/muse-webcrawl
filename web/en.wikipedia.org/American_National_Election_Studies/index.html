The American National Election Studies (ANES) are academically run national surveys of voters in the United States, conducted since 1948. The study has been based at the University of Michigan since its inception, and since 2005 has been a partnership between the University of Michigan and Stanford University. It is funded by the National Science Foundation.

Design

The ANES surveys voters just before and just after each presidential election (and in many midterm years), asking questions about voting behaviour, public opinion, and political participation. The pre-election/post-election panel design allows researchers to study campaign effects and changes in attitudes. Core content includes party identification, ideological self-placement, evaluations of candidates and parties, issue positions, political efficacy, trust in government, and the famous "feeling thermometer" ratings of political figures and groups.

Influence

ANES data underlie much of modern American electoral research. The landmark book The American Voter (1960), based on the early studies, shaped the field's understanding of party identification and voter decision-making. The cumulative Time Series data file allows analysis of American public opinion across more than seven decades, and the data are freely available to researchers.
