Bildungsroman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In literary criticism, a Bildungsroman (German: [ˈbɪldʊŋs.ʁoˌmaːn], plural Bildungsromane) is a literary genre that focuses on the psychological and moral growth of the protagonist from childhood to adulthood (coming of age), in which character change is important. The term comes from the German words Bildung ("education", "formation") and Roman ("novel").

History

The genre arose in Germany in the late 18th century; its foundational text is Goethe's Wilhelm Meister's Apprenticeship (1795–96). The term was coined by the philologist Karl Morgenstern in 1819. Related forms include the Erziehungsroman (novel of upbringing) and the Künstlerroman (novel of the artist's formation).

Plot structure

A typical Bildungsroman follows a sensitive young protagonist who leaves home, undergoes a series of trials, temptations, and mentors, suffers disillusionment, and finally reaches a mature accommodation with society. The arc is one of formation rather than mere adventure: events matter for the change they work in the hero.

Examples

Notable examples include Jane Eyre by Charlotte Brontë, Great Expectations by Charles Dickens, A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man by James Joyce, The Magic Mountain by Thomas Mann, The Catcher in the Rye by J. D. Salinger, and To Kill a Mockingbird by Harper Lee. The form remains central to modern young-adult fiction.
