Osmolality


Unit: osmoles per kilogram of solvent (Osm/kg)
Related measure: Osmolarity (per litre of solution)

Osmolality is a measure of the concentration of solute in a solution, expressed as the number of osmoles of solute particles per kilogram of solvent. It is one of the colligative properties of solutions, depending on the number of dissolved particles rather than their identity. The closely related osmolarity is expressed per litre of solution.

Measurement

Osmolality is most commonly measured by freezing-point depression osmometry: dissolved particles lower the freezing point of a solvent by a predictable amount (about 1.86 °C per osmole per kilogram of water), allowing an instrument to compute osmolality precisely. Vapour-pressure osmometers are used for small samples. Because it is based on mass of solvent rather than volume, osmolality is independent of temperature and pressure.

Clinical significance

In medicine, plasma osmolality normally ranges from about 275 to 295 mOsm/kg and is tightly regulated by antidiuretic hormone (ADH) and thirst, since cells are sensitive to water shifts driven by osmotic gradients. Comparing measured plasma osmolality with a value calculated from sodium, glucose and urea gives the osmolal gap, which can reveal the presence of unmeasured solutes such as toxic alcohols. urine osmolality helps evaluate disorders of water balance such as diabetes insipidus and the syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion.
