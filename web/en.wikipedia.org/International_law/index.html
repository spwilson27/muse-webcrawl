International law (also known as public international law and the law of nations) is the set of rules, norms and standards that govern relations between states and other actors on the international stage. It establishes normative guidelines and a common conceptual framework for states across a broad range of domains, including war and diplomacy, economic relations, human rights and the management of the global commons.

Sources

Article 38 of the Statute of the International Court of Justice lists the recognised sources: international conventions (treaties), international custom as evidence of a general practice accepted as law, general principles of law recognised by civilised nations, and, as subsidiary means, judicial decisions and scholarly writings. Customary international law arises from consistent state practice undertaken from a sense of legal obligation (opinio juris). Certain peremptory norms (jus cogens), such as the prohibitions of genocide, slavery and aggression, bind all states without exception.

History

Although agreements between polities date to antiquity, modern international law is usually traced to early modern Europe and to Hugo Grotius, whose De jure belli ac pacis (1625) systematised the law of war and peace. The Peace of Westphalia (1648) entrenched state sovereignty; the nineteenth century added congress diplomacy and humanitarian conventions; and the twentieth century brought the League of Nations, the United Nations Charter's prohibition on the use of force, and an explosion of treaty regimes.

Branches and institutions

Major fields include the law of treaties, the law of the sea (codified in UNCLOS), international humanitarian law (the Geneva Conventions), international criminal law (enforced by the International Criminal Court), international human rights law, international trade law (the WTO) and international environmental law. Unlike domestic legal systems, international law lacks a central legislature and compulsory enforcement, relying instead on reciprocity, reputation, diplomacy and — where states consent — adjudication.
