Lateral geniculate nucleus

The lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN; also called the lateral geniculate body or lateral geniculate complex) is a relay center in the thalamus for the visual pathway. It receives information directly from the retina via retinal ganglion cells through the optic nerve, optic chiasm, and optic tract, and sends projections to the primary visual cortex (V1) through the optic radiation.

Structure

In primates, the LGN is a small, ovoid, layered structure containing six principal layers separated by interlaminar zones. Layers 1 and 2 are magnocellular layers (large cells, fast, motion- and brightness-sensitive), and layers 3–6 are parvocellular layers (small cells, color- and detail-sensitive); between the layers lie tiny koniocellular cells. Inputs are eye-specific: the contralateral eye projects to layers 1, 4, and 6, the ipsilateral eye to layers 2, 3, and 5, keeping the two eyes' signals segregated until cortex. Each LGN contains a complete retinotopic map of the contralateral visual hemifield.

Function

Although it relays retinal signals to cortex, the LGN is not a passive relay: only a minority of its synaptic input comes from the retina, the rest arriving as feedback from visual cortex, the thalamic reticular nucleus, and brainstem modulatory systems. This feedback is thought to gate visual information according to attention and arousal. Damage to the LGN produces visual field defects corresponding to the retinotopic map.
