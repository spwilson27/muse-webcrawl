Beam axle

A beam axle, also called a rigid axle or solid axle, is a suspension design in which the wheels on each side are connected by a single rigid beam, so that movement of one wheel directly affects the other. It is one of the oldest and simplest axle designs.

Characteristics

Beam axles are strong, simple, and maintain constant wheel alignment under load, which makes them well suited to heavy vehicles, trucks, and off-road vehicles. Front beam axles on driven four-wheel-drive vehicles are often called "live axles" when they also transmit power. Their disadvantages are high unsprung mass and poorer ride comfort and handling compared with independent suspension.

Use today

While largely replaced by independent suspension in passenger cars, beam axles remain common at the rear of pickup trucks and in the heavy commercial vehicle sector, and are prized by off-road enthusiasts for articulation and durability.
