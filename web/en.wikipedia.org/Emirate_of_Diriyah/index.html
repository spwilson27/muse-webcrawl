Emirate of Diriyah

Capital: Diriyah

Existed: 1744–1818

Also known as: First Saudi state

The Emirate of Diriyah, also known as the first Saudi state, was a state in the Arabian Peninsula established in 1744 when Muhammad ibn Saud, emir of Diriyah, and the religious scholar Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab concluded the Diriyah pact, joining political power with a reformist religious movement. From their capital at Diriyah the state expanded to rule most of the Arabian Peninsula.

Expansion

Under Muhammad ibn Saud and his successors Abd al-Aziz and Saud ibn Abd al-Aziz, the emirate conquered Riyadh, the Najd, and eventually al-Hasa, the Hijaz, Mecca (1803) and Medina (1805). Its expansion brought it into conflict with the Ottoman Empire, whose nominal authority in the Hijaz it displaced.

Fall

In response, the Ottoman sultan directed Muhammad Ali of Egypt to reconquer Arabia. Egyptian forces under Ibrahim Pasha advanced across the peninsula, and after a siege of several months Diriyah fell in 1818. The ruler Abdullah ibn Saud was sent to Constantinople and executed, the city was largely destroyed, and the first Saudi state came to an end. A second Saudi state, the Emirate of Najd, was established by Turki ibn Abdullah in 1824.

Legacy

The ruins of Diriyah, including the At-Turaif district, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, survive as a symbol of the Saudi dynasty's origins.
