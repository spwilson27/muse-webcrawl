California Cooperative Oceanic Fisheries Investigations

The California Cooperative Oceanic Fisheries Investigations (CalCOFI) is an oceanographic and marine ecosystem research program off the coast of California that studies the marine environment in the context of fisheries. Founded in 1949, it is one of the world's longest-running and most comprehensive ocean monitoring programs.

History

CalCOFI was established in response to the collapse of the California sardine fishery in the 1940s, which had devastated the canning industry centred on Cannery Row in Monterey. State and federal agencies and the Scripps Institution of Oceanography joined in a cooperative program — originally the California Cooperative Sardine Research Program — to understand the fluctuations of sardine populations and their environment.

Program

Partners include the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, the NOAA Southwest Fisheries Science Center, and the California Department of Fish and Wildlife. Research vessels conduct quarterly cruises over a fixed grid of stations off Southern and Central California, measuring temperature, salinity, oxygen, nutrients, and chlorophyll, and sampling fish eggs, larvae, and plankton. The resulting time series — spanning more than seven decades — documents the effects of El Niño events, decadal regime shifts between sardine- and anchovy-dominated states, and long-term climate change on the California Current ecosystem.

Legacy

CalCOFI's data underpin West Coast fisheries management and assessments of marine protected areas. The program's annual conference and reports remain central references for California Current oceanography.
