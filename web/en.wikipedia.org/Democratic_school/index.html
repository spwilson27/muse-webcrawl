Democratic school

Students participating in a democratic school meeting

A democratic school is a school in which students and staff participate in decision-making on an equal or near-equal basis, typically through school meetings where each person has one vote. Democratic schools emphasize student self-direction and democratic governance as preparation for life in a democracy.

Examples

The best-known examples are Summerhill School, founded by A. S. Neill in England in 1921, and the Sudbury Valley School, founded in 1968 in Massachusetts, whose model ("Sudbury schools") has been replicated internationally.

Features

Common features include optional classes, mixed-age groups, conflict resolution by school meeting or judicial committees, and no compulsory curriculum or testing, though details vary widely.
