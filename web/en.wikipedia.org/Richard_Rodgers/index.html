Richard Rodgers

Richard Charles Rodgers (28 June 1902 – 30 December 1979) was an American composer of more than 900 songs and 43 Broadway musicals, best known for his partnerships with lyricist Lorenz Hart and later Oscar Hammerstein II. He was the first person to win all four top American entertainment awards — Emmy, Grammy, Oscar, and Tony (EGOT) — and also won a Pulitzer Prize.

Rodgers and Hart

With Lorenz Hart, Rodgers wrote witty, sophisticated musicals and standards in the 1920s and 1930s, including A Connecticut Yankee, On Your Toes, Babes in Arms, and Pal Joey, producing songs such as "My Funny Valentine", "The Lady Is a Tramp", and "Bewitched, Bothered and Bewildered".

Rodgers and Hammerstein

After Hart's death, Rodgers teamed with Oscar Hammerstein II to reshape the American musical with Oklahoma! (1943), Carousel, South Pacific, The King and I, and The Sound of Music (1959), integrating song, story, and dance in works that won numerous Tony Awards and Pulitzer Prizes.

Legacy

Rodgers's melodies are cornerstones of the Great American Songbook, and the Richard Rodgers Theatre on Broadway bears his name.
