Pamplona

Pamplona is the historical capital city of the Chartered Community of Navarre, in Spain. It is world-famous for the annual festival of San Fermín, held each July, whose centrepiece is the running of the bulls popularised internationally by Ernest Hemingway's 1926 novel The Sun Also Rises.

History

The city is traditionally said to have been founded by the Roman general Pompey in 74 BC as Pompaelo. It later served as the capital of the Kingdom of Navarre and was an important stop on the Camino de Santiago pilgrimage route.

San Fermín festival

From 6 to 14 July, the city hosts the San Fermín festival. Each morning at 8 a.m. bulls are released to run through the old town's narrow streets to the bullring, chased by hundreds of runners dressed in white with red neckerchiefs.

Geography

Pamplona sits on a plateau in northern Spain, surrounded by hills, and is crossed by the Arga River. Its climate is transitional between oceanic and Mediterranean influences.
