Veneto

Veneto (Venetian: Vèneto) is one of the twenty regions of Italy, in the northeast of the country. Its capital and largest city is Venice. With a population of roughly 4.9 million, Veneto is one of Italy's wealthiest and most industrialised regions and among its most visited, thanks to Venice, Verona, Padua, the Dolomites, Lake Garda and the Prosecco hills.

Geography

Veneto stretches from the Austrian border in the Dolomites south to the Po delta and the Adriatic Sea. Its landscape descends from alpine peaks through foothills and the Venetian plain, crossed by the Po, Adige, Brenta and Piave rivers, to the Venetian Lagoon.

History

The region takes its name from the ancient Veneti. It was Romanised in the 2nd–1st centuries BC, and from the Middle Ages much of it fell under the Republic of Venice, whose mainland empire (Terraferma) lasted until 1797. After Napoleonic and Austrian rule, Veneto joined the Kingdom of Italy in 1866. Venetian, a Romance language distinct from standard Italian, remains widely spoken.

Economy

Once a region of mass emigration, Veneto became an engine of Italy's post-war economic miracle through networks of small and medium enterprises — eyewear (Luxottica in Agordo), sportswear, machinery, furniture and wines such as Prosecco, Valpolicella and Soave. Tourism centred on Venice contributes heavily.
