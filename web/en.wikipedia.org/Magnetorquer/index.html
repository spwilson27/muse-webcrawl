Magnetorquer

A magnetorquer or magnetic torquer (also called a torque rod) is a satellite system for attitude control, detumbling, and stabilization, built from electromagnetic coils. The magnetorquer creates a magnetic dipole that interfaces with an ambient magnetic field — usually Earth's — so that counter-forces produced provide useful torque to the spacecraft without consuming propellant.

Principle

When electric current flows through the coils, the device produces a magnetic dipole moment m; in an external field B the resulting torque is τ = m × B. Because the torque is always perpendicular to the local magnetic field, magnetorquers cannot produce torque about the field direction, so they are normally used together with reaction wheels or thrusters, and they only work where a usable planetary magnetic field exists — effectively low Earth orbit in most cases.

Design

Torquers take two main forms: cylindrical torque rods, in which wire is wound around a ferromagnetic core, and flat air-core coils (torque coils) printed or wound onto panels, which avoid residual magnetisation of a core but produce weaker dipoles. A complete system uses three mutually perpendicular units to control all axes. Power consumption is modest, and there are no moving parts, making magnetorquers highly reliable and very popular on CubeSats and other small satellites.

Uses

Primary uses include detumbling a spacecraft after separation from its launcher, unloading (desaturating) momentum built up in reaction wheels, and coarse pointing. The magnetic field also interacts with magnetorquers as a disturbance: satellites without them can experience small attitude perturbations from residual dipole moments of their own components.
