Malik ibn Anas

Malik ibn Anas (Arabic: مَالِك بن أَنَس; c. 711–795 CE), commonly known as Imam Malik, was an Arab Muslim scholar, jurist, traditionist and theologian. Born in Medina, he founded the Maliki school of Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh), one of the four canonical schools of Sunni Islam. His best-known work, Al-Muwatta ("The Well-Trodden Path"), is one of the earliest surviving collections combining hadith and legal doctrine, and is sometimes regarded as the earliest surviving book of Islamic law.

Life

Malik was born in Medina to a family originally from Yemen. He memorised the Qur'an as a boy and studied under many of the city's leading scholars, including Nafi Mawla Ibn Umar, Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri, and Ja'far al-Sadiq. He spent his entire life in Medina, teaching in the Prophet's Mosque and issuing legal opinions. His reliance on the practice (ʿamal) of the people of Medina as a source of law distinguished his method. Under the Abbasid caliph al-Mansur he was flogged for a legal opinion considered politically inconvenient — an episode that enhanced his reputation for integrity. Among his students was al-Shafi'i, founder of the Shafi'i school.

Al-Muwatta

Malik compiled the Muwatta over some forty years, reportedly refining it by presenting it to the jurists of Medina. It contains roughly 1,900 traditions and rulings arranged by legal topic, combining Prophetic hadith, opinions of the Companions and Successors, and Malik's own judgements. It survives in several recensions, the best known being that of Yahya ibn Yahya al-Laythi, through which the Maliki school spread to North Africa and Islamic Spain.

Legacy

The Maliki school today predominates in North and West Africa and parts of the Arabian Peninsula. Malik is revered across Sunni Islam as one of the great imams of jurisprudence, and al-Shafi'i is reported to have said that when scholars are mentioned, Malik is the star. He is buried in Medina's Al-Baqi' cemetery.
