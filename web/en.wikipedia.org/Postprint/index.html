Postprint

A postprint is the accepted manuscript after peer review, before publisher typesetting.

A postprint is a digital draft of a research journal article after it has been peer reviewed and accepted for publication, but before it has been typeset and formatted by the journal's publisher. The postprint — also called the author's accepted manuscript (AAM) — incorporates revisions made in response to reviewers' comments, so its intellectual content is essentially identical to the published version, differing mainly in layout, pagination, and copy-editing.

Role in open access

Postprints are central to "green" open access: many publishers permit authors to deposit the postprint in an institutional or subject repository (such as arXiv), sometimes after an embargo period, even when the published version of record remains behind a paywall. Depositing a postprint is called self-archiving. Policies vary by publisher and are tracked in databases such as SHERPA/RoMEO.

Terminology

The postprint stands between the preprint — the draft submitted to a journal, before peer review — and the version of record, the publisher's formatted final version. Terminology is not fully standardised: some sources use "postprint" for any version after acceptance, including proofs. The NISO/ALPSP journal article versions framework distinguishes the Author's Original, the Submitted Manuscript Under Review, the Accepted Manuscript, the Proof, and the Version of Record.
