Lee Strasberg, the influential American acting teacher associated with method acting.

Lee Strasberg (born Israel Lee Strassberg; November 17, 1901 – February 17, 1982) was an American actor and theatre director, widely regarded as the foremost American exponent of method acting. Born in Austria-Hungary, he emigrated to the United States as a child and grew up on New York's Lower East Side.

Career

Strasberg studied at the American Laboratory Theatre under Richard Boleslawski and Maria Ouspenskaya, who had been trained in the system of Konstantin Stanislavski. In 1931 he co-founded the Group Theatre with Harold Clurman and Cheryl Crawford, an ensemble that revolutionised American theatre with productions such as Clifford Odets's Waiting for Lefty and Awake and Sing!

In 1949 Strasberg began teaching at the Actors Studio, becoming its artistic director in 1951, a position he held until his death. His students and Studio members included Marilyn Monroe, James Dean, Marlon Brando, Al Pacino, Robert De Niro, Paul Newman and Dustin Hoffman. In 1969 he founded the Lee Strasberg Theatre and Film Institute in New York, with a second school in Los Angeles.

The Method

Strasberg's teaching emphasised relaxation, concentration, sense memory and affective (emotional) memory, tools intended to help actors draw on their own experiences to create truthful performances. His approach was adapted from Stanislavski's early work and differed in emphasis from the versions taught by Stella Adler and Sanford Meisner, both former Group Theatre colleagues.

Acting roles

Though primarily a teacher, Strasberg acted occasionally. His most famous screen role was as the gangster Hyman Roth in The Godfather Part II (1974), for which he was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor.

Death and legacy

Strasberg died of a heart attack in New York City on February 17, 1982, aged 80. His papers are held by the Library of Congress, and the Lee Strasberg Theatre and Film Institute continues to train actors in his method.
