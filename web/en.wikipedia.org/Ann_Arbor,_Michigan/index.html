Ann Arbor is a city in the U.S. state of Michigan and the county seat of Washtenaw County. It lies on the Huron River about 35 miles (56 km) west of Detroit. With a population of about 124,000 in 2020, it is the sixth-largest city in Michigan. The city is best known as the home of the University of Michigan, founded there in 1837, which is the city's largest employer and a dominant cultural influence.

History

Ann Arbor was founded in 1824 by land speculators John Allen and Elisha Walker Rumsey. Tradition holds that the name combined the first name of both men's wives (both named Ann) with "arbor" for the stands of bur oak trees. The community became the county seat in 1827 and was incorporated as a village in 1833 and as a city in 1851. The arrival of the University of Michigan in 1837 and the Michigan Central Railroad in 1839 spurred growth.

Economy and culture

Higher education, health care, research and technology dominate the economy. The city hosts the Ann Arbor Art Fair, one of the largest outdoor art fairs in the United States, and the Ann Arbor Film Festival. It is known for its tree canopy, earning the nickname "Tree Town", and for a politically progressive civic culture shaped by the university.

Geography

The city covers about 29 square miles (75 km2). The Huron River winds through Ann Arbor, with parks and canoe liveries along its banks, and the Nichols Arboretum ("the Arb") adjoins the university's central campus.
