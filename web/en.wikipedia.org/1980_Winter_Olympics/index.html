The 1980 Winter Olympics, officially the XIII Olympic Winter Games, were held from 13 to 24 February 1980 in Lake Placid, New York, United States. Lake Placid had previously hosted the 1932 Winter Olympics, making it the first city to host the Winter Games twice. The Games are best remembered for the "Miracle on Ice", in which the young United States ice hockey team defeated the heavily favoured Soviet Union.

Highlights

American speed skater Eric Heiden won all five men's speed skating events, a feat unmatched in Olympic history, setting one world record and four Olympic records. In ice hockey, the United States team of amateur and college players, coached by Herb Brooks, beat the Soviet Union 4–3 on 22 February in the medal round and went on to defeat Finland for the gold medal. The Soviet Union topped the overall medal table.

Sports

The programme comprised 38 events in six sports: alpine skiing, biathlon, bobsleigh, figure skating, ice hockey, luge, Nordic skiing (cross-country skiing, Nordic combined and ski jumping), and speed skating. Artificial snow was used for the first time at a Winter Olympics.

Medal table

The Soviet Union led the medal standings with 10 gold, 6 silver and 6 bronze medals, followed by East Germany and the United States. A total of 37 nations participated, and Liechtenstein won its first Olympic medals through alpine skier Hanni Wenzel.
