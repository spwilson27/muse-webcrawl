The Guadalquivir is the fifth-longest river in the Iberian Peninsula and the longest river entirely within Spain. It flows 657 kilometres westward across Andalusia, from its source in the Sierra de Cazorla in Jaén province to the Gulf of Cádiz at Sanlúcar de Barrameda. Its name derives from the Arabic al-wādī al-kabīr, "the great river"; the Romans knew it as the Baetis, giving their province of Hispania Baetica its name.

Course

Rising in the Sierra de Cazorla, the river flows west through Jaén and Córdoba, where the Roman bridge and the Great Mosque-Cathedral overlook it, then southwest to Seville. Beyond Seville it crosses the marshlands of the Marismas del Guadalquivir, beside Doñana National Park, before reaching the Atlantic at Sanlúcar de Barrameda. Major tributaries include the Genil, the Guadalimar and the Guadiana Menor.

Navigation and history

The Guadalquivir is navigable inland as far as Seville, making that city Spain's only inland river port. During the 16th and 17th centuries Seville held a monopoly on trade with Spanish America, and treasure fleets sailed up the river with silver from the New World. The river featured in the expeditions of Columbus and Magellan, whose fleet departed from Sanlúcar in 1519.
