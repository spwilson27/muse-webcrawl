Unburnable carbon

Unburnable carbon is the fossil fuel energy resources that cannot be burned if the world is to adhere to a given carbon budget and limit global warming to an agreed maximum, commonly 1.5 °C or 2 °C above pre-industrial levels. The concept implies that a large fraction of proven coal, oil, and gas reserves must remain in the ground, creating financial risks of stranded assets for fossil fuel companies and their investors.

Origin of the concept

The term gained prominence through analyses by the Carbon Tracker Initiative, whose 2011 "Unburnable Carbon" report compared global reserves with the carbon budget for an 80% chance of limiting warming to 2 °C. A widely cited 2015 study in Nature by McGlade and Ekins estimated that globally a third of oil reserves, half of gas reserves, and over 80% of coal reserves should remain unused between 2010 and 2050 to meet the 2 °C target.

Financial implications

If climate policy restricts combustion, demand for fossil fuels may fall, leaving reserves and infrastructure as stranded assets and potentially deflating a "carbon bubble" in financial markets. Central banks and regulators have studied these risks, and investor campaigns have pressed companies to disclose their exposure.
