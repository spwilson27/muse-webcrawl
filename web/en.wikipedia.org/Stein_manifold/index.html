Stein manifold

In the theory of several complex variables and complex geometry, a Stein manifold is a complex manifold of complex dimension n that has enough holomorphic functions to "separate points" and provide local coordinates. Stein manifolds are the complex analogues of affine varieties and affine schemes in algebraic geometry. They are named after Karl Stein, who introduced the concept in 1951.

Definition

A Stein manifold X is a complex manifold that is holomorphically convex, holomorphically separable (holomorphic functions separate points), and on which holomorphic functions give local coordinates at every point. Equivalently, a connected complex manifold is Stein if the higher cohomology groups of every coherent analytic sheaf on it vanish — the content of Cartan's Theorem B.

Examples

Complex Euclidean space ℂn and every domain of holomorphy in ℂn are Stein. Every noncompact Riemann surface is Stein (the Behnke–Stein theorem). A deep result of Grauert and Narasimhan implies that every Stein manifold of dimension n can be properly embedded as a closed complex submanifold of ℂ2n+1. By contrast, no compact complex manifold of positive dimension is Stein, since its holomorphic functions must be constant.

Properties and significance

On Stein manifolds the Oka principle applies: topological and holomorphic problems tend to have the same answer, so that, for example, holomorphic vector bundles are classified by topology. Stein manifolds thus form a flexible class intermediate between affine algebraic varieties and arbitrary complex manifolds, and they are central to modern Oka theory.
