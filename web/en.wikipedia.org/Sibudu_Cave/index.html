Sibudu Cave

Location: KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa
Period: Middle Stone Age
Dates: c. 77,000–38,000 BP

Sibudu Cave is a rock shelter in a sandstone cliff in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, about 40 km north of Durban. It contains an important sequence of Middle Stone Age deposits dating from roughly 77,000 to 38,000 years ago.

Discoveries

Excavations directed by Lyn Wadley from 1998 revealed early evidence of modern human behaviour: bone arrowheads and other bone tools, shell beads, ochre processing, and carefully maintained bedding of sedges and medicinal leaves, the oldest known bedding in the world. Sibudu also yielded early evidence of compound adhesives and, around 64,000 years ago, a possible snare or trap technology.

Significance

Together with sites such as Blombos Cave, Sibudu shows that anatomically modern humans in southern Africa developed sophisticated technology and symbolic behaviour tens of thousands of years earlier than once thought.
