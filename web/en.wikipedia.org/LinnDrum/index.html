LinnDrum

The LinnDrum, sometimes referred to as the LM-2, was a drum machine manufactured by Linn Electronics between 1982 and 1985. About 5,000 units were sold. Along with its predecessor, the Linn LM-1, it was one of the first drum machines to use samples of acoustic drums, and one of the best-selling drum machines of its era.

Features

The LinnDrum had fifteen 8-bit digital samples of real drums: bass, snare, rimshot, hi-hat, two crash cymbals, two ride cymbals, low and high toms, congas and other percussive sounds. It featured a built-in sequencer for recording patterns, swing timing, separate outputs for each sound, and tuning controls for the snare and toms. Unlike the LM-1, the LinnDrum's cymbal sounds used longer samples stored on EPROM chips. Retailing initially at around US$2,995, it was considerably cheaper than the LM-1.

Use

The LinnDrum's crisp, punchy sampled sounds defined much of 1980s pop and R&B production. It was used extensively by artists including Prince, Michael Jackson, Madonna, and producers such as Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, and its snare and clap sounds remain staples of sample libraries.
