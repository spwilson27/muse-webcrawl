Cardiac catheterization

SpecialtyCardiology
PurposeDiagnosis and treatment of heart conditions
ApproachRadial or femoral artery access

Cardiac catheterization (heart cath) is the insertion of a catheter into a chamber or vessel of the heart, performed for both diagnostic and interventional purposes. It is a common procedure used to diagnose and treat cardiovascular conditions, and a subset, coronary angiography, visualizes the coronary arteries using iodinated contrast and X-ray imaging.

Indications

Cardiac catheterization is used to evaluate coronary artery disease, valvular heart disease, congenital heart defects, and heart failure; to measure pressures and oxygen levels within the heart; and to perform interventions such as percutaneous coronary intervention (angioplasty and stenting), valvuloplasty, and closure of septal defects.

Procedure

Access is gained most often through the radial artery at the wrist or the femoral artery in the groin. Under local anesthesia and fluoroscopic guidance, catheters are advanced to the heart, contrast is injected to image the coronary arteries (angiography), and pressure waveforms are recorded. Right-heart catheterization uses venous access to measure pulmonary pressures.

Risks

Complications are uncommon but include bleeding or hematoma at the access site, contrast-induced kidney injury, allergic reactions, arrhythmia, stroke, and, rarely, coronary dissection or myocardial infarction.

History

Werner Forssmann performed the first human cardiac catheterization on himself in 1929, work that later earned him a share of the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
