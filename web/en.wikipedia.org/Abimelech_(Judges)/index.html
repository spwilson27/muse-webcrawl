Abimelech (Judges)

PredecessorGideon (as judge)

Abimelech (Hebrew: אֲבִימֶלֶךְ, ʾĂḇīmeleḵ, "my father is king") was a son of the judge Gideon by a concubine from Shechem. According to chapter 9 of the biblical Book of Judges, after Gideon's death Abimelech persuaded the leaders of Shechem to make him king and used money from the temple of Baal-berith to hire mercenaries, with whom he murdered his seventy brothers on one stone. Only the youngest, Jotham, escaped.

Jotham's fable

From Mount Gerizim, Jotham proclaimed a fable in which the trees seek a king: the olive, fig and vine refuse, and only the bramble accepts, threatening that fire may come out of it and devour the cedars of Lebanon. The fable is read as a warning against Abimelech's kingship and against Shechem.

Downfall

After ruling three years, conflict broke out between Abimelech and Shechem; he destroyed the city and sowed it with salt, and burned the tower of Shechem with about a thousand people inside. Marching on Thebez, he besieged its tower, but as he approached to set it on fire a woman dropped an upper millstone on his head, crushing his skull. Abimelech ordered his armour-bearer to kill him with his sword so that it would not be said that a woman had slain him.
