Salerno is a city and port in Campania, south-western Italy, on the Gulf of Salerno of the Tyrrhenian Sea. Founded as the Roman colony of Salernum in 197 BCE, it became an important Lombard and then Norman principality, and was famous throughout medieval Europe for the Schola Medica Salernitana, often regarded as the first medical school of the West.

History

After Roman beginnings and Byzantine rule, Salerno became the capital of an independent Lombard principality in 849. It flourished commercially, trading with the Islamic world and Byzantium. The Norman Robert Guiscard captured the city in 1076–77, making it a principal residence of his duchy; his son Roger Borsa ruled from and died in the city. Under the Hohenstaufen and Angevins Salerno declined relative to Naples, but its medical school retained prestige into the early modern period before being suppressed under Napoleon in 1811.

In September 1943 Salerno was the site of Operation Avalanche, the main Allied landing in mainland Italy, and served briefly as seat of the Italian government of Pietro Badoglio in 1944 ("Salerno capital").

Schola Medica Salernitana

The medical school of Salerno, emerging by the 9th–10th centuries, synthesised Greek, Latin, Arabic and Jewish medical learning. Constantine the African translated Arabic medical texts there in the 11th century. Its famous health handbook, the Regimen sanitatis Salernitanum, circulated as verse throughout Europe, and the school was noted for admitting women practitioners — the "ladies of Salerno", among them Trota of Salerno.

Sights

Salerno Cathedral (Duomo), consecrated in 1084, houses the relics of Saint Matthew and the tomb of Pope Gregory VII, who died in exile in the city in 1085. The medieval old town, the Lombard-Norman castle of Arechi above the city, and the seafront promenade are landmarks. Salerno is the eastern gateway to the Amalfi Coast and a ferry port for Capri and the Cilento.
