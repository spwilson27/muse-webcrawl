Monarchy of Spain

Incumbent: Felipe VI (since 19 June 2014)

Heir: Leonor, Princess of Asturias

Residences: Royal Palace of Madrid (official); Zarzuela Palace (private)

Constitutional basis: Spanish Constitution of 1978, Title II

The monarchy of Spain (Spanish: Monarquía Española) is the constitutional form of government of Spain. It consists of a hereditary monarch who reigns as the country's head of state under the Spanish Constitution of 1978. The monarch is addressed as His Majesty the King of Spain (Su Majestad el Rey). The current king is Felipe VI, who ascended the throne on 19 June 2014 following the abdication of his father, Juan Carlos I.

Constitutional role

Under Title II of the 1978 Constitution, the King is Head of State, the symbol of the unity and permanence of the Spanish state, and the commander-in-chief of the Spanish Armed Forces. His functions include sanctioning and promulgating laws, calling and dissolving the Cortes Generales, calling referendums, proposing and appointing the Prime Minister (President of the Government), accrediting ambassadors, and representing Spain in international relations — particularly with the countries of the Ibero-American community. The King's acts must be countersigned by the government, which bears political responsibility for them.

History

The Spanish monarchy traces its origins to the Visigothic Kingdom and, in its recognisably Spanish form, to the dynastic union of the Crowns of Castile and Aragon under Isabella I and Ferdinand II in the late 15th century. The Habsburg and then Bourbon dynasties ruled Spain for centuries, broken by the First and Second Republics and the Franco dictatorship. Juan Carlos I, designated successor by Franco, oversaw Spain's transition to democracy after 1975 and was instrumental in defeating the attempted coup of 23 February 1981.

Succession

Succession follows male-preference primogeniture among the descendants of Felipe VI. The heir apparent is Leonor, Princess of Asturias, the King's elder daughter. The royal household is known as the Casa de Su Majestad el Rey.
