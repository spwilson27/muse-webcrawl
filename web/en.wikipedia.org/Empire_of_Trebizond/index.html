The Empire of Trebizond, or the Trapezuntine Empire, was a monarchy and one of three Byzantine successor states formed after the Fourth Crusade broke up the Byzantine Empire in 1204. Centred on Trebizond (modern Trabzon) on the south-eastern Black Sea coast of Anatolia, it survived — alone of the successor states — until 1461, outliving the restored Byzantine Empire by eight years.

History

The empire was founded in April 1204 by Alexios I Megas Komnenos, a grandson of Emperor Andronikos I Komnenos, who seized Trebizond with Georgian help just weeks before Constantinople fell to the Crusaders. Its emperors, styling themselves "Grand Komnenoi", at first controlled the Black Sea coast from Sinope to the Georgian frontier and contested the imperial inheritance with Nicaea and Epirus; though they lost the struggle, Trebizond's wealth as the western terminus of Silk Road trade routes sustained the court for over two centuries.

Culture and decline

Trebizond's court was famed for its wealth, its beautiful princesses — sought in marriage by Byzantine emperors and Turkish and Mongol rulers alike — and its scholarship, notably the astronomer Gregory Chioniades and the future cardinal Bessarion. Under Manuel I and John II the empire became a tributary but prosperous client of the Seljuks and then the Mongols. It survived the fall of Constantinople in 1453 only briefly: the last emperor, David Megas Komnenos, surrendered to the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II on 15 August 1461.
