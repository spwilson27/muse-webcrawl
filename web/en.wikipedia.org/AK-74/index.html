AK-74

The AK-74 is an assault rifle designed by Mikhail Kalashnikov in the Soviet Union and adopted in 1974. It is the successor to the AKM, redesigned for the smaller, high-velocity 5.45×39mm cartridge in response to the American 5.56mm M16.

Design

Like earlier Kalashnikov rifles, the AK-74 uses a long-stroke gas piston and rotating bolt. Its most visible features are a large multi-chamber muzzle brake that significantly reduces recoil and muzzle rise, and a double-column magazine. The 5.45×39mm cartridge's light bullet and flat trajectory made the rifle more controllable in automatic fire than its 7.62mm predecessors. Early rifles had wooden furniture; later production used plum and black polymer.

Variants and use

Variants include the folding-stock AKS-74, the short AKS-74U carbine, and the modernised AK-74M, which became the standard Russian service rifle before the AK-12. The AK-74 was first used in combat in the Soviet–Afghan War and remains in widespread service across former Soviet states and beyond.
