Visual cycle

Visual cycle
ProcessRegeneration of 11-cis retinal
LocationRetina / retinal pigment epithelium
Key enzymeRPE65

The visual cycle is the biological conversion of a subset of retinal molecules — a process that forms part of phototransduction and replenishes the chromophore needed for vision. When a photon is absorbed by a visual pigment such as rhodopsin, its 11-cis retinal chromophore is isomerized to all-trans retinal. The visual cycle converts all-trans retinal back to 11-cis retinal so that photoreceptors can respond to light again.

Steps

All-trans retinal is reduced to all-trans retinol in the photoreceptor outer segment and transported to the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), where it is esterified by lecithin retinol acyltransferase (LRAT). The enzyme RPE65 then performs the key isomerohydrolase step, producing 11-cis retinol, which is oxidized to 11-cis retinal and shipped back to the photoreceptors to recombine with opsin.

Clinical significance

Mutations in RPE65 cause Leber congenital amaurosis and forms of retinitis pigmentosa. In 2017, voretigene neparvovec (Luxturna), a gene therapy delivering a normal copy of RPE65, became the first gene therapy for an inherited retinal disease approved in the United States. A parallel cycle in Müller glia is thought to supply cones with chromophore during bright daylight.
