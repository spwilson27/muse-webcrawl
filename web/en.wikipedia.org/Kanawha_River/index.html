Kanawha River

Kanawha River
State: West Virginia, USA
Length: about 156 km (97 mi)
Mouth: Ohio River at Point Pleasant

The Kanawha River is a tributary of the Ohio River in the U.S. state of West Virginia. Formed at Gauley Bridge by the confluence of the New River and the Gauley River, it flows about 156 kilometres (97 miles) northwest to join the Ohio at Point Pleasant. Its valley, passing through the state capital Charleston, is West Virginia's most industrialized and populous corridor.

History

The valley was long used by Native American peoples, and its salt springs near present-day Malden attracted early European settlers; salt production boomed in the 19th century and supplied much of the young United States. The river figured in Civil War campaigns in western Virginia. From the early 20th century the Kanawha valley became a centre of the American chemical industry, with large plants around Charleston, South Charleston, and Belle — an area dubbed "Chemical Valley".

Navigation

A system of locks and dams completed in the 1930s made the river navigable year-round, carrying coal and chemical products by barge. The Kanawha is also noted for its sport fishing. In 2014 a chemical spill above Charleston's water intake on the tributary Elk River temporarily cut drinking water for some 300,000 people.
