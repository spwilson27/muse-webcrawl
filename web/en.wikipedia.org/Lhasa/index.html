Lhasa

Lhasa
Country: China
Region: Tibet Autonomous Region
Elevation: 3,656 m
Population: ~870,000 (prefecture-level)

Lhasa is the capital and largest city of the Tibet Autonomous Region of China and the traditional seat of the Dalai Lama. It lies in a valley on the north bank of the Lhasa River (Kyi Chu), a tributary of the Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra), at an elevation of about 3,656 metres, making it one of the highest cities in the world.

History

Lhasa rose to prominence in the 7th century under Songtsen Gampo, founder of the Tibetan Empire, who is credited with founding the Jokhang Temple, the most sacred temple in Tibetan Buddhism. In the 17th century the 5th Dalai Lama, Lozang Gyatso, made Lhasa the capital and built the Potala Palace on Red Hill. Tibet came under Chinese administration in the 1950s, and the 14th Dalai Lama fled to India in 1959.

Landmarks

The Potala Palace, Jokhang Temple, and Norbulingka form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Barkhor, a pilgrim circuit around the Jokhang, is lined with shops and stalls. Great monasteries of the Gelug school — Drepung, Sera, and Ganden — stand in and near the city.

Geography and transport

Lhasa has a cool highland climate with intense sunshine. The Qinghai–Tibet railway, opened in 2006, links the city to Xining and the rest of China; Lhasa Gonggar Airport serves domestic flights.
