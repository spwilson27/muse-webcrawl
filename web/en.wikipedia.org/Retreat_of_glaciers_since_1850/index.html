Retreat of glaciers since 1850

The retreat of glaciers since 1850 is well-documented worldwide. Mountain glaciers have been shrinking and thinning since the end of the Little Ice Age around 1850, driven first by natural climate variability and black-carbon deposition, and increasingly by anthropogenic global warming. The trend accelerated markedly in the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

Evidence

The World Glacier Monitoring Service compiles mass-balance records showing predominantly negative balances since the 1980s. Repeat photography provides striking visual evidence: glaciers such as the Rhône Glacier (Switzerland), Pasterze (Austria), and those of Glacier National Park (USA) have retreated kilometres from their 19th-century positions; the park's ~150 glaciers of 1850 had fallen to about 25 by the 2010s.

Causes

Glacier mass balance responds to temperature and precipitation. Global temperature rise, especially amplified at high altitude and latitude, is the dominant cause of recent retreat; soot darkening ice surfaces and changes in snowfall also contribute.

Impacts

Glacier melt is a major contributor to sea-level rise and affects seasonal water supplies for hundreds of millions of people dependent on meltwater-fed rivers. Continued retreat risks "peak water" followed by declining flows in regions such as the Andes, Central Asia and the Himalayas.
