Kashgar

Kashgar (Uyghur: قەشقەر, Qeshqer; Chinese: 喀什, Kāshí) is an oasis city in the Tarim Basin region of southern Xinjiang, China. It is the administrative seat of Kashgar Prefecture. Historically a key junction of the Silk Road, it stands west of the Taklamakan Desert near the borders with Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Afghanistan and Pakistan.

History

Kashgar was contested among Chinese dynasties, Tibetan, Turkic and Mongol powers; it was a capital of the Karakhanid Khanate and later came under Qing rule in the 18th century. It was the centre of Yaqub Beg's Kashgaria (1865–1877) and a listening post of the British and Russian consulates during the Great Game. Marco Polo described its orchards and Nestorian Christians.

Culture and sights

The Id Kah Mosque (1442), China's largest, the tomb of Islamic scholar Yusuf Khass Hajib, the Abakh Khoja Tomb, and the reconstructed mud-brick Old City define its Uyghur character. Its markets and caravan crossroads — north to the Torugart Pass, west over the Irkeshtam, south via the Karakoram Highway — remain central to regional trade.
