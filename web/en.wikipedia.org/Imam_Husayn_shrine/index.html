Imam Husayn shrine

Location: Karbala, Iraq
Contains: Tomb of Husayn ibn Ali
Significance: Major Shia pilgrimage site

The Imam Husayn shrine (Arabic: مقام الامام الحسين) in Karbala, Iraq, is the mosque and burial site of Husayn ibn Ali, the third Shia Imam and grandson of the Prophet Muhammad, who was killed at the Battle of Karbala in 680 CE. It is one of the holiest places in Shia Islam.

History

A shrine has stood over Husayn's grave since shortly after his death, though it was demolished and rebuilt several times, notably by the Abbasid caliph al-Mutawakkil in the ninth century and, in its present grand form, under successive Persian and Iraqi patrons. The complex is crowned by a gilded dome and two tall minarets. The shrine stands near the shrine of Husayn's half-brother Abbas across a broad plaza known as Bayn al-Haramayn.

Pilgrimage

Millions of pilgrims visit Karbala each year, especially during Ashura, which commemorates Husayn's martyrdom, and Arbaeen, forty days later — among the largest annual gatherings in the world. Pilgrims also visit his shrine during Sha'ban and other observances.
