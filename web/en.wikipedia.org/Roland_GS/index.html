Roland GS

Roland GS is a specification and series of extended MIDI modules developed by the Roland Corporation, introduced in 1991 with the Roland SC-55 Sound Canvas module. GS extended the General MIDI standard by adding many more instrument sounds (variations), drum kits, effects such as reverb and chorus, and finer control over parameters such as filter cutoff, resonance, envelope attack and release, and vibrato.

Overview

General MIDI guaranteed only 128 melodic instruments and one drum kit. GS used bank-select messages to address additional "variation" tones stored in capital and sub-capital banks, eventually offering hundreds of sounds, plus multiple drum kits. GS also defined standard system-exclusive messages for effects and master tuning, and supported features such as assigning any channel to drums (two drum channels being a GS hallmark).

Hardware

The Sound Canvas line — SC-55, SC-55mkII, SC-88, SC-88 Pro, SC-8850 and others — made GS a de facto standard for computer game music in the early and mid-1990s, since many game soundtracks were composed on an SC-55. Yamaha's competing XG format offered similar extensions. GS later appeared in software form in the Virtual Sound Canvas (VSC).

Legacy

As audio recording and sample libraries became cheap, hardware MIDI modules declined, but GS remains historically important as the first widely adopted extension of General MIDI, and its logo appeared on a large range of Roland keyboards, modules, and pianos.
