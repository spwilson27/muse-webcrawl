Kama (river)

The Kama (Russian: Ка́ма; Tatar: Чулман, Çulman) is a major river in European Russia, the longest left tributary of the Volga and, by discharge, the largest of its tributaries — it even carries more water than the Volga itself above their confluence. The Kama is 1,805 kilometres (1,122 mi) long, with a drainage basin of about 507,000 km².

Course

The river rises in the Udmurt Republic near the village of Kuliga, west of the Ural Mountains, and flows north, then east and south in a great arc through Kirov Oblast and Perm Krai — passing the city of Perm — before turning southwest through Tatarstan. It joins the Volga at the Kuybyshev Reservoir south of Kazan. Major tributaries include the Vyatka, the Belaya, the Ik and the Chusovaya.

Hydroelectric development

Three large hydroelectric dams impound the Kama: the Kama Dam at Perm (forming the Kama Reservoir), the Votkinsk Dam at Chaykovsky, and the Nizhnekamsk Dam near Naberezhnye Chelny. Together with the Volga cascade they made the river navigable along most of its length and linked it to Russia's Unified Deep Water System, connecting the White, Baltic, Black, Azov and Caspian seas.

History

The Kama basin was an important medieval trade route; the fur-trading lands of "Great Perm" drew Novgorod and later Muscovy eastward. Industrial cities such as Berezniki, Solikamsk and Naberezhnye Chelny (home of the Kamaz truck works) grew along its banks.
