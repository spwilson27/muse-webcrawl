Dentist

A dentist, also known as a dental surgeon, is a health care professional who specializes in dentistry, the branch of medicine focused on the teeth, gums and mouth. Dentists diagnose and treat diseases, injuries and malformations of the teeth and gums, and carry out procedures such as fillings, crowns, extractions, root canal treatment and preventive care.

Education and licensing

In most countries, dentists must complete a university degree in dentistry (such as the DDS, DMD or BDS) followed by licensing examinations and registration with a professional regulator before practising. Many dentists complete additional postgraduate training to specialise in fields such as orthodontics, oral surgery, periodontics, endodontics, prosthodontics or paediatric dentistry.

History

Dentistry is among the oldest medical professions, with evidence of dental work dating back thousands of years. Pierre Fauchard, an 18th-century French surgeon often called the father of modern dentistry, published the first complete scientific description of dental practice in 1728.

Practice

The dentist's supporting team — dental hygienists, dental nurses, technicians and therapists — aids in providing oral health services. Most dentists work in private practice, though many also work in hospitals, universities and public health services.
