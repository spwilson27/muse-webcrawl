Earth science

Earth science studies the planet's interacting spheres

Earth science or geoscience includes all fields of natural science related to the planet Earth. It is the study of the solid earth, its waters and the air that envelops it, and increasingly of the interactions between them and life — the geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere. Earth science draws on physics, chemistry, biology, mathematics and geography to build a quantitative understanding of how the Earth system works and evolves.

Branches

Major branches include geology (the structure and history of the solid Earth, from mineralogy and petrology to tectonics and paleontology), geophysics and geochemistry; oceanography and hydrology for the waters; atmospheric sciences, meteorology and climatology for the air; soil science (pedology); glaciology; and geodesy. Planetary science extends the same methods to other bodies of the Solar System. Interdisciplinary fields such as biogeochemistry, geobiology and Earth system science integrate these perspectives.

Methods and applications

Earth scientists use fieldwork, remote sensing from satellites and aircraft, seismic and other geophysical imaging, laboratory experiments, and modelling. Applications include locating energy and mineral resources, groundwater management, natural-hazard assessment for earthquakes, volcanoes, floods and landslides, environmental protection, and understanding and projecting climate change.
