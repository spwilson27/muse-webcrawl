North Korea, officially the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK), is a country in East Asia constituting the northern part of the Korean Peninsula. It borders China and Russia to the north and South Korea to the south at the Korean Demilitarized Zone. Its capital and largest city is Pyongyang.

History

The peninsula was divided at the 38th parallel after the Second World War, with Soviet occupation in the north. The DPRK was proclaimed in 1948 under Kim Il Sung. In June 1950 North Korea invaded the South, beginning the Korean War; three years of fighting ended in an armistice in 1953, but no peace treaty, so the two Koreas remain technically at war. Kim Il Sung consolidated power around the ideology of Juche (self-reliance) and a hereditary leadership succession passed to Kim Jong Il (1994) and Kim Jong Un (2011).

Politics

North Korea is a one-party state under the Workers' Party of Korea and is widely described as a totalitarian dictatorship with an elaborate cult of personality around the Kim family. A UN Commission of Inquiry in 2014 reported systematic human-rights abuses. Society is organised through the songbun classification system.

Economy and military

The economy is centrally planned, though informal markets have grown since the famine of the 1990s. The country maintains one of the world's largest standing armies under the songun (military-first) policy and has developed nuclear weapons and ballistic missiles, drawing extensive international sanctions.
