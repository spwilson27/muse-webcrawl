Travel document

Passports are the most common form of travel document used for international journeys.

A travel document is an identity document issued by a state or an international organisation that enables the bearer to travel internationally and to be identified at borders. The most widely used travel document is the passport, but the category also includes refugee travel documents, laissez-passer, seaman's books, identity cards accepted for travel, and various emergency documents.

Types

  • Passport — issued to a state's own nationals; modern examples are usually biometric (e-passports) conforming to ICAO standards for machine-readable travel documents.
  • Refugee travel document — issued under the 1951 Refugee Convention to persons recognised as refugees who cannot obtain a passport from their country of origin.
  • Convention travel document for stateless persons — the equivalent document under the 1954 Statelessness Convention.
  • Laissez-passer — a one-way or limited document, for example the United Nations laissez-passer issued to UN officials, or emergency documents issued by consulates to citizens who have lost their passport abroad.
  • Identity cards — within some regions, such as the European Union, national identity cards serve as travel documents; similar arrangements include passport cards and enhanced driving licences in North America.
  • Crew documents — seafarers and airline crew travel on specialised identity documents under ILO and ICAO conventions.

Functions

Travel documents certify identity and nationality, request safe passage and assistance for the bearer, and provide space for visas, entry and exit stamps and endorsements. States generally require travellers to hold a document valid for the duration of stay, often with six months' validity beyond the travel dates. Visas, where required, are separate authorisations placed in or linked to the travel document.

History

Safe-conduct letters are ancient — a Persian example is described in the Book of Nehemiah (c. 450 BC). The modern passport system standardised after the First World War, when the League of Nations conferences of 1920 and 1926 established uniform passport formats, a model later taken over by the International Civil Aviation Organization.
