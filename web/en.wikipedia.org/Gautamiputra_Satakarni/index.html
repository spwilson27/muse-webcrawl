Gautamiputra Satakarni

Gautamiputra Satakarni (reigned c. 106–130 CE, dates vary by source) was a ruler of the Satavahana Empire in the Deccan region of ancient India. He is widely regarded as the greatest Satavahana king: he restored the dynasty's fortunes after a period of decline, defeating the Western Kshatrapas (Sakas) and extending Satavahana power across much of central and western India. His name is a metronymic meaning "Satakarni, son of Gautami," after his mother Gautami Balashri.

Reign and achievements

Inscriptions of his mother, notably the Nashik prashasti, praise Gautamiputra as the destroyer of the Sakas, Yavanas, and Pahlavas, and as the king who "rooted out" the Kshaharata dynasty of the satrap Nahapana — a victory corroborated by a hoard of Nahapana's coins restruck in Gautamiputra's name found at Jogalthambi. At its height his empire stretched from the western coast and Gujarat–Malwa region across the Deccan to the eastern coast. He is also remembered in inscriptions as a patron of Brahmins and of Buddhist monks, to whom he granted land and cave dwellings at Nashik and Karle.

Legacy

Gautamiputra was succeeded by his son Vasishthiputra Pulumavi. His reign marks the high point of Satavahana power and of the Deccan's overland and maritime trade with the Roman world. He remains a celebrated figure in the history of Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh, commemorated in literature and in the 2017 Telugu film Gautamiputra Satakarni.
