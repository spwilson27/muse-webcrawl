Foxton Locks

Foxton Locks are two staircases, each of five canal locks, on the Leicester line of the Grand Union Canal near the village of Foxton in Leicestershire, England. Built in 1810–1814 as part of the Grand Union Canal, the flight raises boats by about 75 feet (23 m) and is the largest staircase lock flight on the English canal system of its type.

Description

A staircase lock arrangement means that the upper gates of one lock serve as the lower gates of the next, so boats pass directly from chamber to chamber. Side ponds beside each chamber conserve water during operation. Traversing the flight typically takes around 45 minutes and is supervised by lock keepers during the boating season.

Inclined plane

In 1900 a steam-powered inclined plane was opened beside the locks to speed the passage of barges, hauling caissons of water carrying boats up and down the hill. It proved uneconomical and closed in 1911; its remains, including the boiler house site, are protected as a scheduled monument. The locks are a popular visitor attraction, with a museum at the site.
