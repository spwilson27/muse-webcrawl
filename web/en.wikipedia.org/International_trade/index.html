International trade

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

International trade is the exchange of capital, goods, and services across international borders or territories. In most countries, such trade represents a significant share of gross domestic product (GDP). Trade allows countries to specialize according to comparative advantage, expanding the variety and lowering the prices of goods and services available to consumers, while exposing domestic producers to international competition.

Theory

Classical theory begins with Adam Smith's principle of absolute advantage and David Ricardo's theory of comparative advantage (1817), which shows that countries benefit from trade even when one is more productive at everything. The Heckscher–Ohlin model explains trade by countries' endowments of land, labour, and capital; the Stolper–Samuelson theorem describes how trade redistributes income within countries. New trade theory (associated with Paul Krugman) adds economies of scale and product differentiation to explain intra-industry trade between similar economies, and gravity models successfully predict trade flows from countries' sizes and distance.

History and institutions

Long-distance trade dates to ancient caravan and maritime routes, such as the Silk Road. The modern system developed with industrialization, mercantilism's decline, and the repeal of Britain's Corn Laws (1846). After the protectionism of the 1930s, the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT, 1947) progressively lowered tariffs; its successor, the World Trade Organization (WTO, founded 1995), provides rules and dispute settlement for trade in goods, services, and intellectual property. Regional arrangements — the European Union, USMCA, ASEAN, and CPTPP — further liberalize trade among members.

Policy and debate

Governments regulate trade with tariffs, quotas, subsidies, and technical standards, balancing gains from trade against adjustment costs, strategic industries, and national security. The difference between a country's exports and imports is its balance of trade. World merchandise trade is measured in the tens of trillions of U.S. dollars annually, and services (tourism, finance, software, transport) form a growing share.
