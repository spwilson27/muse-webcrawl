Women in the United States House of Representatives

Women in the United States House of Representatives have served since 1917, when Jeannette Rankin of Montana took her seat as the first woman in Congress — three years before the Nineteenth Amendment guaranteed women the vote nationwide. Since then, several hundred women have served as representatives, delegates, or resident commissioners.

Early history

Rankin, a Republican and lifelong pacifist, famously voted against U.S. entry into both world wars. Many early women members were widows appointed or elected to succeed their husbands — the so-called "widow's succession." Alice Mary Robertson, Rebecca Latimer Felton (in the Senate), and others followed; Mary Teresa Norton became the first Democratic woman and the first to chair a major committee.

Growth

Numbers grew slowly until the "Year of the Woman" in 1992, when 24 new women were elected to the House. Milestones since include Nancy Pelosi becoming the first woman Speaker of the House (2007–2011, 2019–2023), and record numbers of women elected in 2018 and 2020, when women held roughly a quarter of House seats — still well below their share of the U.S. population.

Representation

Women in the House have included members of both parties and diverse racial and ethnic backgrounds, though women of colour were long rare: Patsy Mink (1965) was the first woman of colour in the House, and Shirley Chisholm (1969) the first Black woman.
