The FTSE 100 is the principal benchmark for large UK-listed shares.

The FTSE 100 Index, also called the FTSE 100, FTSE, or colloquially the "Footsie", is a stock market index of the 100 companies listed on the London Stock Exchange with the largest market capitalisation. It is maintained by FTSE Russell, a subsidiary of the London Stock Exchange Group, and is regarded as the leading gauge of the performance of large British-listed companies.

Composition

Constituents are reviewed quarterly; companies ranked in the top 90 are guaranteed inclusion while those falling below 110 are removed, with the band between subject to rules to reduce turnover. The index is weighted by free-float-adjusted market capitalisation, so the largest companies — historically energy majors, banks, miners, and pharmaceutical firms such as Shell, HSBC, AstraZeneca, and BP — dominate its movements. Together the constituents represent roughly four-fifths of the total value of the London market.

Characteristics

The index began on 3 January 1984 at a base level of 1,000 points. Because its largest companies earn the majority of their revenues abroad, the index is only loosely tied to the UK domestic economy and tends to rise when sterling weakens, since foreign earnings translate into more pounds. Related indices include the FTSE 250 (mid-caps) and the broader FTSE All-Share. See also London Stock Exchange and FTSE 250 Index.
