Turpan

Turpan (also spelled Turfan) is a prefecture-level city in Xinjiang, China, located in an oasis within one of the world's deepest depressions. Renowned for extreme summer heat, dried grapes, and the ancient karez irrigation system, it was a major stop on the northern route of the Silk Road.

Geography and climate

The Turpan Depression descends to Ayding Lake, about 154 metres below sea level, China's lowest point. Summers are among the hottest in China, while winters are cold; rainfall is negligible, and agriculture depends on meltwater delivered by underground karez channels.

History

Nearby ruins include Jiaohe and Gaochang (Qocho), capitals of early kingdoms, and the Bezeklik grottoes with Buddhist murals. Manichaean and Buddhist manuscripts recovered at Turfan by expeditions such as those of Aurel Stein and Albert von Le Coq transformed the study of Central Asian religions.

Economy

Turpan is famous for seedless white grapes and raisins, melons, and increasingly for wine production and tourism.
