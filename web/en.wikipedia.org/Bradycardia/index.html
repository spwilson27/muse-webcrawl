Bradycardia

Bradycardia is a heart rate that is slower than normal — conventionally below 60 beats per minute in a resting adult. It may be physiological, as in trained athletes or during sleep, or pathological, arising from dysfunction of the heart's conduction system, medications such as beta blockers, hypothyroidism, or increased intracranial pressure.

Types include sinus bradycardia and heart block. Many people with bradycardia have no symptoms; when the rate is too slow to maintain adequate circulation it can cause fatigue, dizziness, fainting, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

Symptomatic bradycardia may be treated acutely with atropine and definitively with an artificial pacemaker. The opposite condition, an abnormally fast heart rate, is tachycardia.
