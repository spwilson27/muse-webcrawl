Passkey (authentication)

A passkey is a digital credential based on the FIDO2/WebAuthn standards that allows users to sign in to websites and apps without a password. Passkeys use public-key cryptography: the private key stays on the user's device, protected by biometrics, a PIN, or a device unlock, while the service stores only the public key.

How it works

When a user registers, their device creates a unique key pair for that service. Signing in requires approving a cryptographic challenge with the private key. Because credentials are bound to the legitimate site's domain, passkeys are inherently resistant to phishing, and there is no shared secret that can leak from a server breach.

Syncing and adoption

Major platform providers—including Apple, Google, and Microsoft—support passkeys that sync across a user's devices through their accounts, while hardware security keys can store non-syncable passkeys. Adoption has grown since 2022 as major services have added passkey sign-in.

Advantages and limits

Passkeys combine strong security with convenience, but account recovery, cross-platform use, and access to one's devices remain practical considerations for users and service operators.
