Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank

Abbreviation: AIIB

Formation: 25 December 2015

Headquarters: Beijing, China

Membership: 100+ approved members

President: Jin Liqun

Authorised capital: US$100 billion

The Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) is a multilateral development bank that finances infrastructure in Asia and beyond. Proposed by China in 2013 and launched in January 2016, it has grown from 57 founding members to more than 100 approved members worldwide, including most major economies except the United States and Japan. China is the largest shareholder, with about 26.6 percent of voting power, followed by India and Russia.

Operations

The AIIB finances transport, energy, water, telecommunications and urban projects, frequently co-financing with the World Bank and Asian Development Bank. It has committed tens of billions of dollars across several hundred projects, emphasises "lean, clean and green" governance, and holds triple-A credit ratings from the major agencies. Its project portfolio increasingly stresses climate finance and cross-border connectivity, complementing China's Belt and Road Initiative while formally remaining independent of it.

Governance and criticism

The bank's president, Jin Liqun, was re-elected for a second term in 2020. Western officials initially worried the AIIB would undercut existing institutions' environmental and governance standards; in practice it has largely aligned with peer practices. In 2023 Canada froze ties after a senior communications official resigned alleging Communist Party interference, an accusation the bank's internal review rejected.
