Knightia

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Actinopterygii
Order: Alepocephaliformes / Clupeiformes (historically)
Family: Clupeidae (in older classifications)
Genus: Knightia

Knightia is an extinct genus of clupeid bony fish that lived in the freshwater lakes and rivers of North America during the Eocene epoch, roughly 53.5 to 48.5 million years ago. It is one of the best-known fossils from the Green River Formation of Wyoming, Colorado and Utah.

Description

Knightia was a small schooling herring-like fish, typically about 10 centimetres (4 in) long, though specimens can reach 25 centimetres. It had a streamlined body, a deeply forked tail fin, and a single dorsal fin. It fed on plankton, algae and small invertebrates.

Fossils and significance

Thousands of Knightia fossils have been collected from Fossil Lake deposits, including mass-mortality slabs showing whole schools. Because of its abundance it is the official state fossil of Wyoming and one of the most commonly traded fossils in the world. Species include Knightia eocaena and Knightia alta. The genus was named by David Starr Jordan in 1907 in honour of Wilbur C. Knight, a University of Wyoming geologist.
