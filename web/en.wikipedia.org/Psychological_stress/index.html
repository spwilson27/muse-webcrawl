Psychological stress

Psychological stress is the feeling of strain and pressure that arises when environmental demands are appraised as exceeding a person's resources for coping.

In psychology, stress is a feeling of emotional strain and pressure. It is a type of psychological pain. Small amounts of stress may be beneficial, improving performance and motivation (eustress), but excessive or chronic stress can increase the risk of anxiety, depression, cardiovascular disease, and other health problems.

Models of stress

Hans Selye described the general adaptation syndrome, the body's three-stage response to demands: alarm, resistance, and exhaustion. Richard Lazarus's transactional model emphasises appraisal: stress arises not from events themselves but from a person's judgment that demands exceed their coping resources. The Holmes and Rahe stress scale ranks life events, such as bereavement or divorce, by the readjustment they require.

Physiology

The stress response is mediated by the sympathetic nervous system and the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis, releasing adrenaline and cortisol. Acute activation prepares the body for "fight or flight", but prolonged activation contributes to immune suppression, metabolic disturbance, and wear and tear described as allostatic load.

Coping and management

Coping strategies include problem-focused efforts to change the stressor and emotion-focused efforts to regulate the response. Evidence-based stress management techniques include exercise, sleep hygiene, relaxation training, cognitive behavioural therapy, mindfulness practice, and building social support.
