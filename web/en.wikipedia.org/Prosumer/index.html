Prosumer

Prosumer is a portmanteau of the words producer and consumer. The term describes an individual or organization that both consumes and produces a good or service. It was anticipated by Marshall McLuhan and developed by futurist Alvin Toffler in his book The Third Wave (1980), which argued that mass customization and interactive media would blur the industrial-era division between producers and consumers. The concept has since been applied in several distinct domains.

Uses of the term

In energy, prosumers are households or businesses that generate electricity — typically with rooftop solar panels — consuming part of it and feeding the surplus into the grid, a role enabled by net metering and smart grids. In media and the internet, the term describes users who create content (videos, reviews, open-source software, social media posts) that others consume, a dynamic central to Web 2.0. In marketing and equipment, "prosumer" denotes products positioned between consumer and professional grade — such as advanced cameras or audio equipment — and the enthusiastic buyers who seek them. Economists studying self-service and co-production also use the word for customers who perform labour once done by employees, from assembling furniture to designing products online.

Analysis

Scholars debate whether prosumption empowers consumers or extracts unpaid labour from them. Writers such as George Ritzer analyse modern prosumption — especially on digital platforms — as a system in which users generate value and data for firms while experiencing their activity as leisure. Others emphasize genuine gains: distributed energy prosumers reduce transmission losses and emissions, and participatory media can democratize cultural production.
