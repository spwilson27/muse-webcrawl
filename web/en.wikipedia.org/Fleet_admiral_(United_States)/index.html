Fleet admiral (United States)

Fleet admiral (abbreviated FADM) is a five-star flag officer rank in the United States Navy. It ranks immediately above admiral and is equivalent to the Army's rank of general of the army and (in modern terms) to the Air Force's general of the Air Force. The rank has been held only by four officers, all during and after the Second World War, and has not been awarded since 1946.

History

Following the establishment of the five-star general of the army rank, Congress created the grade of fleet admiral on 14 December 1944 to give the Navy's senior commanders parity with Allied field marshals and admirals of the fleet, and with US Army five-star generals. The first appointments, in seniority order, were William D. Leahy (15 December 1944), Ernest J. King (17 December 1944) and Chester W. Nimitz (19 December 1944). William F. Halsey Jr. was promoted to fleet admiral on 11 December 1945. Fleet admirals remained on active duty for life with full pay.

Earlier and related grades

The Navy had earlier created the singular rank of Admiral of the Navy for George Dewey in 1903, a grade senior to fleet admiral that lapsed on his death in 1917. During the Second World War, Leahy served as chief of staff to the commander in chief (President Roosevelt), King as chief of naval operations and commander in chief, US Fleet, and Nimitz as commander in chief, Pacific Fleet and Pacific Ocean Areas; Halsey commanded the Third Fleet.

Insignia and status

The insignia of a fleet admiral consists of five silver stars arranged in a circle, and the sleeve and shoulder markings of an admiral with five-star devices. In the order of precedence, fleet admiral is senior to all other US Navy ranks except Admiral of the Navy. Since the death of Nimitz in 1966, no officer has held the grade, and plans discussed during the war for a six-star "general of the armies of the United States" equivalent for the Navy were never enacted.
