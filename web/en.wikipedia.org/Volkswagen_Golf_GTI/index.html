Volkswagen Golf GTI

Volkswagen Golf GTI
Manufacturer: Volkswagen
Production: 1976–present
Class: Hot hatch / compact
Layout: Front-engine, front-wheel drive

The Volkswagen Golf GTI is a high-performance version of the Volkswagen Golf compact hatchback, first introduced in June 1976 at the Frankfurt Motor Show. It is widely credited with popularising the "hot hatch" concept, combining the practicality of a small family car with the performance of a sports car.

History

The original Mk1 GTI was developed in secret by a small team of Volkswagen engineers led by Anton Konrad. It used a 1.6-litre fuel-injected engine producing 110 PS (81 kW), giving the car a top speed of around 182 km/h (113 mph). Its distinctive styling cues included a red stripe around the grille, black plastic wheel-arch extensions and a golf-ball gear knob.

Generations

The GTI badge has been carried through every generation of the Golf, from the Mk1 of 1976 to the current Mk8. Later models added turbocharging, larger engines and dual-clutch DSG gearboxes, while retaining front-wheel drive and the characteristic tartan "Clark" plaid seat upholstery.

Legacy

The Golf GTI is frequently cited as one of the most influential cars of the twentieth century. Its success prompted rival manufacturers to launch their own hot hatches, including the Peugeot 205 GTi and the Ford Escort XR3i.
