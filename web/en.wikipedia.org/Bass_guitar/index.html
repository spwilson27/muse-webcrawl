Bass guitar

The bass guitar, electric bass or simply bass, is the lowest-pitched member of the guitar family. It is a plucked string instrument similar in appearance to an electric guitar but with a longer neck and scale length, typically four strings tuned an octave below the guitar's lowest four strings.

History

The modern electric bass was popularised by Leo Fender's Precision Bass in 1951, replacing the cumbersome double bass in popular music.

Technique

Bassists play fingerstyle, with a pick, or with slap and pop techniques, providing rhythm and harmony foundations in ensembles.

Role

The bass links the rhythmic and harmonic parts of a band and is central to rock, pop, jazz, funk and many other genres.
