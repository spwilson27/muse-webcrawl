Ai Weiwei

Born: 28 August 1957, Beijing, China
Known for: Conceptual art, installation, architecture, activism

Ai Weiwei (born 28 August 1957) is a Chinese contemporary artist, documentary filmmaker, and activist. One of the most prominent Chinese artists internationally, his work spans sculpture, installation, photography, film, and architecture, often engaging with Chinese cultural history, craftsmanship, and questions of human rights and freedom of expression. He is the son of the poet Ai Qing.

Early life and education

Ai was born in Beijing. During the Anti-Rightist Campaign his father was denounced and the family was exiled, first to Heilongjiang and then to Shihezi, Xinjiang. After the Cultural Revolution the family returned to Beijing in 1976. Ai enrolled at the Beijing Film Academy in 1978 and co-founded the avant-garde Stars Group. In 1981 he moved to the United States, studying in Philadelphia and New York, where he encountered the work of Marcel Duchamp, Andy Warhol, and Jasper Johns. He returned to China in 1993.

Work

Ai's works include Dropping a Han Dynasty Urn (1995), the photographic triptych in which he lets an ancient urn shatter; Fairytale (2007), for which he brought 1,001 Chinese citizens to Kassel for documenta 12; and Sunflower Seeds (2010) at Tate Modern, comprising over 100 million hand-painted porcelain seeds made by craftspeople in Jingdezhen. He was an artistic consultant on the Beijing National Stadium (the "Bird's Nest") for the 2008 Olympics. Following the 2008 Sichuan earthquake he led a citizens' investigation into student deaths in collapsed schools.

Detention and exile

In April 2011 Ai was detained at Beijing airport and held for 81 days without charge, an event that drew international protest; authorities later pursued a tax case against his company. His passport was returned in 2015, after which he settled in Berlin and later in Portugal and the United Kingdom. His documentary films include Human Flow (2017), about global migration.
