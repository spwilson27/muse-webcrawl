Iberian lynx

Scientific name: Lynx pardinus
Range: Southwestern Iberian Peninsula
Diet: Mainly European rabbits
Conservation status: Vulnerable (IUCN, since 2024)

The Iberian lynx (Lynx pardinus) is one of the four extant species of lynx, endemic to the Iberian Peninsula in southwestern Europe. It is distinguished by its spotted coat, prominent ear tufts, cheek whiskers or "beard", and short tail with a black tip. Adults weigh roughly 10–13 kg.

Decline

Once widespread across Spain and Portugal, the species collapsed during the 20th century through habitat loss, road kills, persecution, and above all the crash of its staple prey, the European rabbit, after myxomatosis and rabbit haemorrhagic disease. By 2002 fewer than 100 individuals survived in two isolated populations in Andalusia, making it the world's most threatened cat.

Recovery

Captive breeding (beginning in 2003), habitat restoration, rabbit management, and reintroductions in Extremadura, Castilla-La Mancha, Murcia, and Portugal's Guadiana valley rebuilt the population to more than 2,000 animals by the early 2020s. The IUCN downlisted it from Critically Endangered to Endangered in 2015 and to Vulnerable in 2024 — a widely cited conservation success.
