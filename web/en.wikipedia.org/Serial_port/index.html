Serial port

A serial port is a serial communication interface through which information transfers in or out sequentially one bit at a time, in contrast to a parallel port, which transfers multiple bits simultaneously over separate channels.

Standards and hardware

Historically, personal computers used serial ports conforming to the RS-232 standard, typically with DE-9 connectors, to connect modems, mice, printers, and terminals. Data rates, handshaking signals, and asynchronous framing with start and stop bits were configured through UART chips.

Legacy and successors

Serial ports have largely disappeared from consumer machines, displaced by USB, but RS-232 and related serial interfaces (RS-485, TTL-level UARTs) remain common in industrial equipment, networking consoles, embedded systems, and scientific instruments.
