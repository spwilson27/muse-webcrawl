Disko Bay (Greenlandic: Qeqertarsuup tunua; Danish: Diskobugten) is a large bay on the western coast of Greenland in northeastern Baffin Bay. The bay constitutes a wide southeastern inlet of Baffin Bay and is notable for the large numbers of icebergs calved from the Jakobshavn Glacier (Sermeq Kujalleq), one of the fastest-moving glaciers in the world.

Geography

Disko Bay is roughly 150 kilometres long and 100 kilometres wide. The bay is dotted with islands from the Aasiaat archipelago, and Disko Island lies at its northern entrance. The Ilulissat Icefjord, at the bay's eastern shore, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004.

History

The area has been inhabited for millennia by Inuit cultures, including the Saqqaq, Dorset and Thule peoples. Norse explorer Erik the Red is believed to have visited the bay around 982–985. From the 17th century, Dutch and English whalers frequented the bay, and the settlement of Christianshaab (now Qasigiannguit) was founded in 1734. Jakobshavn (now Ilulissat) was founded in 1741 and became a centre of the Greenland trade.

Economy and wildlife

The main towns are Ilulissat, Aasiaat, Qasigiannguit and Qeqertarsuaq. Fishing for Greenland halibut and shrimp is the economic mainstay, supplemented by growing tourism drawn by the icefjord. The bay's waters support humpback, fin and minke whales, seals and rich seabird colonies.
