The 3DO Interactive Multiplayer is a home video game console platform developed by The 3DO Company, founded by Electronic Arts co-founder Trip Hawkins. Unlike rival systems, the 3DO was not manufactured by its designer; instead, the specification was licensed to companies such as Panasonic, GoldStar, and Sanyo, which built their own versions.

Launch and market

The first model, Panasonic's FZ-1, launched in North America in October 1993 at $699 — far more expensive than the Super Nintendo and Sega Genesis, and a major factor in the platform's failure despite advanced hardware and CD-ROM media. Time magazine nevertheless named it the 1993 Product of the Year. The library included ports of laserdisc games such as Dragon's Lair, the acclaimed strategy title Star Control II adaptations, and the debut of series such as Need for Speed and Gex.

Legacy

Lifetime sales reached only about two million units, and the platform was discontinued in 1996. The 3DO Company pivoted to third-party software publishing before going bankrupt in 2003. The console is remembered as an ambitious but overpriced preview of the multimedia era of gaming.
