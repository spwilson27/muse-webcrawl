Dyspnea

Dyspnea
Also calledShortness of breath, breathlessness
FieldPulmonology, cardiology
TypeSymptom (subjective sensation)

Dyspnea (from Greek dys-, difficult, and pnoia, breathing) is the subjective sensation of breathing discomfort or shortness of breath. The American Thoracic Society defines it as "a subjective experience of breathing discomfort that consists of qualitatively distinct sensations that vary in intensity". It is one of the most common symptoms bringing patients to medical care and a cardinal feature of many cardiac, pulmonary, neuromuscular, and metabolic disorders.

Causes

Common causes include asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), pneumonia, heart failure, pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax, anemia, and anxiety or panic attacks. Related terms describe specific patterns: orthopnea (breathlessness when lying flat, typical of heart failure), platypnea (breathlessness when upright), and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (sudden night-time attacks).

Mechanisms

Dyspnea is thought to arise from a mismatch between the brain's motor command to the respiratory muscles (efferent signals, with corollary discharge to sensory cortex) and the afferent feedback received from lungs, airways, chest wall, and chemoreceptors. Qualitatively different sensations — air hunger, work or effort of breathing, and chest tightness — have distinct physiological correlates.

Assessment and management

Severity is commonly rated with the Borg scale or visual analogue scales, and functional impact with the mMRC (modified Medical Research Council) dyspnea scale. Treatment targets the underlying cause; for refractory breathlessness in advanced disease, opioids, airflow from a fan, and pulmonary rehabilitation provide relief.
