Tripolitania

Ruins of Leptis Magna, one of the three cities of Tripolitania

Tripolitania (Arabic: طرابلس, Ṭarābulus; Berber languages: Ṭrables), formerly also Tripolitana, is a historic region and former province of Libya. Its name, from the Greek Tripolis, refers to the three ancient cities of Oea, Sabratha, and Leptis Magna. Tripolitania was the western of Libya's three traditional provinces, alongside Cyrenaica in the east and Fezzan in the south-west.

Geography

The region occupies Libya's north-western coast along the Mediterranean Sea and extends south to the Sahara. It includes the coastal plain of Jafara, the Jebel Nafusa mountains, and the modern capital Tripoli, successor to ancient Oea. It is the most populous of Libya's regions, and its main cities include Tripoli, Misrata, Zawiya, and Khoms.

History

The three cities were founded by the Phoenicians and later enriched under Roman rule; Leptis Magna became one of the great cities of the Roman Empire and the birthplace of the emperor Septimius Severus. The area formed part of the Roman province of Africa and later of the Byzantine and Arab worlds. It passed under Ottoman rule in the 16th century as the Eyalet (later Vilayet) of Tripolitania.

In 1911 Italy seized the region from the Ottomans in the Italo-Turkish War, and Tripolitania became an Italian colony, united with Cyrenaica in 1934 as Italian Libya. After the Second World War it was administered by Britain until Libyan independence in 1951. In the federal Kingdom of Libya, Tripolitania was one of three constituent provinces until federalism was abolished in 1963.
