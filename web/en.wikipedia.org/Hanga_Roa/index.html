Hanga Roa (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈaŋɡa ˈro.a]; Rapa Nui: Haŋa Roa, "wide bay") is the main town and capital of Easter Island (Rapa Nui), a Chilean special territory in the south-eastern Pacific Ocean. Located on the sheltered south-western coast of the island, between the extinct volcanoes of Terevaka and Rano Kau, it is home to the great majority of the island's population and is its only town.

Geography and layout

The town spreads inland from the small harbour cove of Caleta Hanga Roa, where the island's fishing boats are moored and beside which stands a moai on the Ahu Tautira platform. Its two main streets are Avenida Atamu Tekena, the principal thoroughfare with most shops, hotels and restaurants, and Avenida Te Pito o Te Henua. Houses are dispersed among gardens and small fields, giving the town a low-density, village character.

History

Hanga Roa grew on the site where, under the Chilean administration and the Williamson-Balfour sheep ranching concession, the native Rapa Nui population was confined behind a boundary wall for much of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, while the rest of the island was used as ranch land. Restrictions on movement were lifted in the 1960s, and the construction of Mataveri airport in 1967 — later extended with NASA involvement as an emergency Space Shuttle landing strip — connected the island to regular air service and tourism.

Facilities

Hanga Roa contains the island's hospital, schools, post office, Catholic church (Iglesia de la Santa Cruz, whose carved wooden figures blend Christian and Rapa Nui iconography), the Museo Antropológico Padre Sebastián Englert, a football stadium, and the island's cemetery at Tahai. Mataveri International Airport, just south of the town, receives flights from Santiago and (historically) Tahiti, and is the island's principal link to the outside world. Most tourism services — hotels, guesthouses, dive shops, car rentals and tour agencies for visiting the moai sites of Rapa Nui National Park — are based in the town.
