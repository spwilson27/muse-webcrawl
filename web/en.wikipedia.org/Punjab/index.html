Punjab

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Punjab ( /pʌnˈdʒɑːb/ ; Punjabi: ਪੰਜਾਬ; Urdu: پنجاب) is a geographical, historical and cultural region in South Asia, straddling modern-day Pakistan and India. The name derives from the Persian words panj (five) and āb (water), referring to the five major rivers of the region: the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej, all tributaries of the Indus.

Geography

The Punjab is a fertile alluvial plain bounded by the Himalayas to the northeast and the Thar Desert to the south. Its canal-irrigated agriculture — built out extensively under British rule — made it the breadbasket of both Pakistan and India, producing wheat, cotton, rice and sugarcane. Major cities include Lahore, Faisalabad and Rawalpindi in Pakistan, and Amritsar, Ludhiana and Chandigarh in India.

History

The region was a heartland of the Indus Valley Civilisation, and later a crossroads of empires: Achaemenid, Macedonian under Alexander the Great, Mauryan, Mughal and British. In the fifteenth century Guru Nanak founded Sikhism in Punjab; in the early nineteenth century Maharaja Ranjit Singh built a Sikh Empire with its capital at Lahore, annexed by the British in 1849 after the Anglo-Sikh wars.

The Partition of India in 1947 split Punjab between India and Pakistan along the Radcliffe Line, accompanied by mass migration and communal violence in which hundreds of thousands died. The Indian portion was further divided in 1966 into the states of Punjab and Haryana, with Chandigarh as a shared capital.

Demographics and culture

Punjabi is the region's principal language, written in Gurmukhi script in India and Shahmukhi in Pakistan. The population is majority Muslim in Pakistani Punjab and majority Sikh and Hindu in Indian Punjab. Punjabi culture is known for its music and dance (bhangra), cuisine (tandoori dishes, makki di roti and sarson da saag), and the Sikh institution of langar, the free community kitchen exemplified at the Golden Temple in Amritsar.
