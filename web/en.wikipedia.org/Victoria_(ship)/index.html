Victoria (ship)

Victoria was a Spanish carrack and the first ship to circumnavigate the world. She was one of the five ships of Ferdinand Magellan's expedition of 1519, and the only one to complete the voyage, returning to Spain in 1522 under the command of Juan Sebastián Elcano after Magellan's death in the Philippines.

The Magellan expedition

Victoria was built at Ondarroa in the Basque Country and named after the church of Santa María de la Victoria in Seville. The fleet — Victoria, Trinidad, San Antonio, Concepción, and Santiago — sailed from Spain in August 1519 seeking a western route to the Spice Islands. After crossing the Atlantic, wintering in Patagonia, and transiting the strait that now bears Magellan's name (during which San Antonio deserted and Santiago was wrecked), three ships crossed the Pacific. Magellan was killed at the Battle of Mactan in April 1521, and Concepción was burned for lack of crew.

The circumnavigation

Trinidad and Victoria reached the Moluccas and loaded cloves. Trinidad, leaking, stayed behind and was later captured by the Portuguese; Victoria, commanded by Elcano, sailed alone across the Indian Ocean and around the Cape of Good Hope. Starving and battered, she reached Sanlúcar de Barrameda on 6 September 1522 with only 18 of her original crew — completing the first circumnavigation and proving the world's oceans were connected. Aboard was the chronicler Antonio Pigafetta, whose account is the main record of the voyage.

Later history and legacy

Victoria made two further voyages to the Caribbean before being lost with all hands around 1570. A full-scale replica built for Expo '92 in Seville later repeated the circumnavigation (2004–2006), and another replica is exhibited at the Museo Nao Victoria in Punta Arenas, Chile.
