Pátzcuaro (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈpatskwaɾo]) is a city and municipality in the Mexican state of Michoacán. It sits on the southern shore of Lake Pátzcuaro, one of Mexico's highest lakes, at an elevation of about 2,140 metres. The city was designated a Pueblo Mágico ("Magical Town") by the Mexican government in recognition of its cultural and historical significance and is renowned for its well-preserved colonial architecture and Purépecha heritage.

History

Pátzcuaro was founded around 1320 as a ceremonial centre of the Purépecha (Tarascan) state and served as one of its capitals before the Spanish conquest. After the conquest, Vasco de Quiroga, first bishop of Michoacán, made it the seat of his diocese in 1538 and founded schools and hospitals; the diocesan seat was later moved to Valladolid (now Morelia). The city retains a strong indigenous Purépecha character alongside its colonial churches, plazas, and red-tiled roofs.

Culture

Pátzcuaro is famous throughout Mexico for its Day of the Dead (Día de Muertos) celebrations, particularly the all-night vigils held by Purépecha fishing communities on the island of Janitzio and in cemeteries around the lake, where families gather at graves with candles, marigolds, and offerings. The region is also known for its traditional butterfly-net fishermen on Lake Pátzcuaro, copperwork, lacquerware, and regional cuisine such as corundas and charales.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Plaza Vasco de Quiroga (Plaza Grande), one of the largest and most beautiful plazas in Mexico; the Basílica de Nuestra Señora de la Salud, housing the revered image of the Virgin of Health; the former Jesuit college and the church of La Compañía; and the Casa de los Once Patios crafts centre. The lake's islands include Janitzio, crowned by a monumental statue of José María Morelos, and Yunuén and Pacanda.
