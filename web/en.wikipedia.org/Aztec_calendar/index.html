Aztec calendar

The Aztec sun stone, now in the National Museum of Anthropology, Mexico City.

The Aztec or Mexica calendar is the calendrical system used by the Aztecs as well as other Pre-Columbian peoples of central Mexico. It is one of the Mesoamerican calendars, sharing the basic structure of calendars from throughout the region.

The two cycles

The calendar combines two cycles. The xiuhpōhualli ("year count") is a 365-day solar calendar of eighteen months (veintenas) of twenty days each, plus a five-day period called nēmontēmi. The tōnalpōhualli ("day count") is a 260-day ritual cycle combining twenty day signs with thirteen numbers, used for divination and recorded in codices called tonalamatl. The two cycles interlock to form a 52-year "calendar round," at whose completion the New Fire ceremony was held to ward off the destruction of the world.

The sun stone

The famous Aztec sun stone (Piedra del Sol), a massive basalt sculpture unearthed in Mexico City in 1790, depicts the calendrical system and the Mexica cosmogony of the Five Suns; it is thought to have served as a ceremonial platform rather than a working calendar.
