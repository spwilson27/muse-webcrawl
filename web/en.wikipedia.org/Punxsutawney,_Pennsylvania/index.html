Punxsutawney is a borough in Jefferson County, Pennsylvania, United States. It is best known worldwide as the home of Punxsutawney Phil, the groundhog at the centre of the annual Groundhog Day celebration held every 2 February at Gobbler's Knob.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Lenape (Delaware) people; the name Punxsutawney derives from a Lenape phrase referring to the ponksad — biting sandflies — of the locality. The borough was settled by Europeans in the early 19th century and grew with the timber and coal industries of the region.

Groundhog Day

According to tradition, German settlers brought with them the custom of predicting the arrival of spring from the behaviour of a hibernating animal. The Punxsutawney Groundhog Club, founded in 1899, stages the annual ceremony in which Phil emerges from his burrow: if he sees his shadow, six more weeks of winter are predicted; if not, spring will arrive early. The 1993 film Groundhog Day, though set in Punxsutawney, greatly increased the town's fame and visitor numbers.

Geography and economy

Punxsutawney lies in the Mahoning Creek valley in western Pennsylvania, roughly 80 miles northeast of Pittsburgh. Its economy historically rested on coal mining, railroads, and manufacturing, and the town remains a local commercial centre for surrounding rural communities.
