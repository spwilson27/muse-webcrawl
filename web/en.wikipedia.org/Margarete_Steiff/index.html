Margarete Steiff

Apollonia Margarete Steiff (24 July 1847 – 9 May 1909) was a German seamstress and founder of the Steiff company, famous for its stuffed toys. Struck by polio as a child, she was left with paralysed legs and pain in her right arm, yet built one of the world's best-known toy manufacturers from a small felt-goods workshop in Giengen an der Brenz.

Early life and business

Steiff learned sewing against medical advice and, with her sisters, opened a dressmaking business. In 1877 she founded a felt-goods business. In 1879 she found a pattern for a toy elephant in a magazine and made felt elephants as pincushions; children began playing with them, and she expanded into other stuffed animals. By the 1890s the company employed many homeworkers in the region and was exporting widely.

The teddy bear

In 1902 her nephew Richard Steiff designed a jointed bear with movable arms and legs, model "55 PB". Shown at the Leipzig Toy Fair in 1903, it was ordered in quantity by an American buyer, coinciding with the "Teddy" craze in the United States named after President Theodore Roosevelt. Production exploded: in 1907 alone the company made nearly a million bears. Steiff introduced the famous "button in ear" trademark in 1904 to protect against imitations.

Legacy

Margarete Steiff died of pneumonia in 1909. The company bearing her name continues to produce high-quality stuffed animals, and vintage Steiff bears are among the most valuable antique toys. Her life story has been the subject of a German television film.
