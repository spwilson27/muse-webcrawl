Automotive industry in Germany

The automotive industry in Germany is one of the largest employers and export sectors in the country. Germany is widely regarded as the birthplace of the automobile — Karl Benz patented the first practical car in 1886 — and remains a leading producer of premium vehicles.

Major manufacturers

The principal manufacturers are the Volkswagen Group (including Audi and Porsche), Mercedes-Benz Group, and BMW, together with the German operations of Ford and Opel (Stellantis). A large supplier sector includes Bosch, Continental, ZF Friedrichshafen, and Schaeffler. German brands dominate the global premium car market.

Economic significance

The industry employs several hundred thousand people directly and many more in supply chains, and accounts for a substantial share of German exports and of private research and development spending. Major production centers include Wolfsburg, Stuttgart, Munich, Ingolstadt, and Leipzig.

Challenges

The sector faces the transition to electric vehicles, competition from Chinese and American manufacturers, software development demands, and the legacy of the 2015 diesel emissions scandal.
