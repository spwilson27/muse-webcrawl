Delphi method

Delphi method
Developed atRAND Corporation
DevelopersOlaf Helmer, Norman Dalkey, Nicholas Rescher
Period1950s–1960s
PurposeStructured forecasting and consensus building

The Delphi method is a structured communication technique and forecasting method based on the results of several rounds of questionnaires sent to a panel of experts. It was developed at the RAND Corporation in the 1950s, initially for Cold War military forecasting, and takes its name from the Oracle of Delphi.

Procedure

A facilitator selects a panel of experts who answer questionnaires anonymously in two or more rounds. After each round, the facilitator circulates a summary of the group's responses and the reasons given, and experts are encouraged to revise their earlier answers. Anonymity is intended to reduce the effects of dominant personalities and groupthink. Rounds continue until a predefined stopping criterion — often a measure of consensus such as the interquartile range — is reached.

Applications

The method is widely used in business forecasting, technology foresight, health research (for example to develop clinical guidelines and core outcome sets), education and policy analysis. Variants include the real-time Delphi, conducted online without waiting between rounds, and the policy Delphi, which seeks to expose positions rather than force consensus.

Criticism

Critics note that results depend heavily on panel selection, that consensus does not guarantee accuracy, and that the method lacks the theoretical grounding of statistical sampling.
