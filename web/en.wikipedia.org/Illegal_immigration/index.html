Illegal immigration

Illegal immigration is the migration of people into a country in violation of the immigration laws of that country, or the continuous residence in a country of an individual who is not a citizen and does not have the legal right to be there. Illegal immigration tends to refer primarily to the phenomenon of people entering or remaining in a country for economic purposes, as distinct from refugees and asylum seekers fleeing persecution, though the categories may overlap.

Terminology

People who immigrate illegally are referred to by various terms. "Illegal immigrant" and "illegal alien" are used in legal and governmental contexts in some countries, while advocacy groups, media organizations, and international bodies increasingly use terms such as "undocumented immigrant" or "irregular migrant," arguing that a person cannot be illegal and that the alternatives are less dehumanizing.

Causes

Migration, including unauthorized migration, is generally understood as being driven by push factors in countries of origin (poverty, violence, unemployment, political instability) and pull factors in destination countries (labor demand, higher wages, family reunification, established migrant networks). Restrictive immigration policies combined with strong labor demand often create conditions for unauthorized entry or visa overstays.

Methods

Unauthorized migrants may enter a country by crossing a border without inspection, by using fraudulent documents, or — commonly in developed countries — by entering legally on a temporary visa and overstaying its term. Some are smuggled by organized networks, and a smaller number are victims of human trafficking.

Effects and debates

The economic and social effects of illegal immigration are widely debated. Research commonly finds modest overall effects on native wages, with more pronounced competition for low-skilled native workers, alongside fiscal effects that vary by level of government and the migrant's characteristics. Unauthorized immigrants often face exploitation, limited access to services, and risk of deportation. Policy responses include border enforcement, employer sanctions, legalization programs, and guest-worker schemes.

By country

Estimates of unauthorized populations vary by country. In the United States, the unauthorized immigrant population was estimated at roughly 11 million in the 2010s and 2020s. European countries, Gulf states, and other destination regions each have distinct patterns and legal frameworks.
