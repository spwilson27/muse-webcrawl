The Special Supplemental Food Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC) is a federal assistance program of the Food and Nutrition Service (FNS) of the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) for healthcare and nutrition of low-income pregnant women, breastfeeding women, and children under the age of five. Their mission is to be a partner with other services that are key to childhood and family well-being.

The program provides federal grants to states for supplemental foods, health care referrals, and nutrition education for low-income pregnant, breastfeeding, and non-breastfeeding postpartum women, and to infants and children up to age five who are found to be at nutritional risk.

History

WIC was established as a pilot program in 1972 and made permanent in 1974 under amendments to the Child Nutrition Act of 1966, following studies showing that inadequate nutrition during pregnancy and early childhood contributed to infant mortality and developmental problems. The program grew steadily and today serves roughly half of all infants born in the United States.

Eligibility and benefits

Applicants must meet income guidelines (generally at or below 185% of the federal poverty level, or participation in programs such as SNAP or Medicaid), state residency requirements, and be determined to be at "nutrition risk" by a health professional. Benefits are issued as electronic benefit transfer (EBT) cards or paper vouchers redeemable for specific nutritious foods such as milk, eggs, cheese, infant formula, cereals, fruits and vegetables, and peanut butter. Participants also receive nutrition education, breastfeeding support, and referrals to healthcare and social services.

Impact

Research has linked WIC participation with improved birth outcomes, including reduced rates of low birth weight and preterm delivery, improved childhood immunization rates, and cost savings in Medicaid spending.
