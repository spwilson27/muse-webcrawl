Project Kuiper

Project Kuiper is a satellite internet constellation being developed by Amazon through its subsidiary Kuiper Systems LLC. The project aims to deploy a network of satellites in low Earth orbit (LEO) to provide low-latency, high-speed broadband internet connectivity to underserved and unserved communities around the world, competing with services such as SpaceX's Starlink.

OperatorKuiper Systems LLC (Amazon)
PurposeSatellite broadband internet
Planned satellites3,236
OrbitLow Earth orbit (590–630 km)
First prototypesOctober 2023 (KuiperSat-1 and KuiperSat-2)

History

Amazon announced Project Kuiper in 2019, and in July 2020 the U.S. Federal Communications Commission (FCC) authorised the deployment of 3,236 satellites. Under the FCC licence, Amazon must deploy half of the constellation by July 2026 and the remainder by July 2029. Amazon committed more than $10 billion to the project and secured one of the largest commercial launch procurements in history, with contracts for up to 92 launches aboard United Launch Alliance's Vulcan Centaur, Arianespace's Ariane 6, Blue Origin's New Glenn, and SpaceX's Falcon 9.

Deployment

Two prototype satellites, KuiperSat-1 and KuiperSat-2, were launched aboard a ULA Atlas V rocket on 6 October 2023 and successfully tested the system end-to-end before being deorbited. Production satellite launches began in April 2025 aboard Atlas V, with Amazon planning to begin customer service once several hundred satellites are in orbit.

Technology

The satellites operate in Ka-band and use optical inter-satellite links, phased-array antennas, and onboard processing. Amazon has designed compact customer terminals, including a small flat antenna about 7 inches square intended for lower-cost service, and has said the satellites are designed to burn up on re-entry to reduce orbital debris.
