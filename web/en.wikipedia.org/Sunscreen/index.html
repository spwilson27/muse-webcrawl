Sunscreen

Sunscreen, also known as sunblock or sun cream, is a topical product for the skin that helps protect against sunburn and skin cancer by absorbing, scattering, or reflecting ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the Sun.

How it works

Organic (chemical) filters such as avobenzone and octocrylene absorb UV radiation and convert it to heat, while inorganic (physical) filters such as zinc oxide and titanium dioxide both scatter and absorb UV. A product's sun protection factor (SPF) measures protection primarily against UVB rays: SPF 30, correctly applied, blocks about 97 per cent of UVB. "Broad spectrum" products also protect against UVA.

Use and effectiveness

Regular daily use of sunscreen has been shown in randomised trials to reduce the incidence of squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma and to slow skin photoaging. Dermatologists recommend generous application and reapplication every two hours outdoors, since typical users apply far less than the amount used in SPF testing.

Regulation

Sunscreens are regulated as over-the-counter drugs in the United States and as cosmetics in the European Union, which permits a wider range of approved UV filters.
