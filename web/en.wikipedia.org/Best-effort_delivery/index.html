Best-effort delivery

Best-effort delivery describes a network service in which the network does not provide any guarantee that data is delivered, or that delivery meets any quality-of-service (QoS) standard. In a best-effort network all users obtain best-effort service, which can be adversely affected by variable network load and congestion.

Characteristics

Best-effort delivery provides no guarantees regarding delivery, ordering, duplication, delay or bandwidth. The Internet Protocol (IP) is the best-known example: IP makes its best attempt to deliver packets, but packets may be lost, duplicated, delayed or delivered out of order.

Comparison with reliable delivery

Protocols such as TCP build reliable, ordered delivery on top of best-effort IP by using acknowledgements, retransmission and sequencing. UDP, by contrast, exposes the underlying best-effort semantics directly to applications, favouring low latency over reliability.

Applications

Best-effort service is adequate for many applications, including much web traffic and streaming media, and its simplicity is a key reason for the scalability of the Internet.
