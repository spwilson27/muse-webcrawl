Corpo Truppe Volontarie

The Corpo Truppe Volontarie (Corps of Volunteer Troops, CTV) was a Fascist Italian expeditionary force sent to Spain to support the Nationalist forces under Francisco Franco during the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939). At its peak it numbered roughly 50,000 men, organised in divisions including regular army units, Fascist Blackshirt militia divisions, and mixed Italian-Spanish "Frecce" (Arrows) brigades.

History

Italian intervention began in July 1936 with aircraft that ferried Franco's Army of Africa from Morocco to mainland Spain. Ground forces followed, and the CTV was formally constituted in early 1937 under General Mario Roatta. Its early operations were successful at Málaga (February 1937), but in March 1937 the CTV suffered a humiliating defeat at the Battle of Guadalajara, where Italian forces were routed by the Spanish Republican Army including Italian anti-fascist volunteers of the Garibaldi Battalion. The defeat was a propaganda disaster for Mussolini, and Roatta was replaced.

Under subsequent commanders (Ettore Bastico, Mario Berti, and finally Gastone Gambara), the CTV fought in the north — at Santander in 1937 — and in the Aragon Offensive and the final Catalonia Offensive of 1938–39. Italian aircraft of the Aviazione Legionaria, operating alongside the CTV, conducted bombing campaigns including the raids on Barcelona in March 1938.

Withdrawal and legacy

After Franco's victory in April 1939, the CTV paraded in Madrid and was repatriated. Italian intervention, together with German support, was a decisive rehearsal for the Axis in the Second World War, but its cost deepened Italy's military and financial weakness on the eve of that war.
