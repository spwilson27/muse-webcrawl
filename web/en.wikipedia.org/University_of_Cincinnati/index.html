University of Cincinnati

Type: Public research university
Established: 1819
Location: Cincinnati, Ohio, U.S.
Students: ~53,000

The University of Cincinnati (UC) is a public research university in Cincinnati, Ohio. Founded in 1819 as Cincinnati College, it is the second-oldest institution of higher education in the city and one of the oldest municipal universities in the United States. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity."

Cooperative education

UC is credited with inventing cooperative education: in 1906 engineering dean Herman Schneider introduced the co-op model, alternating classroom study with paid, major-related work. The programme remains one of the largest in the world and anchors the university's identity.

Campus and academics

The Uptown campus features buildings by noted architects including Frank Gehry, Michael Graves and Thom Mayne. UC's College-Conservatory of Music and its medical centre — where Albert Sabin developed the oral polio vaccine — are nationally prominent. The Cincinnati Bearcats compete in the Big 12 Conference.
