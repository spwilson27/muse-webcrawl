Gastroenterology is the branch of medicine concerned with the digestive system and its disorders. It covers the oesophagus, stomach, small and large intestines, rectum, pancreas, gallbladder, bile ducts and liver. A specialist is a gastroenterologist; hepatology, the study of the liver, is often treated as a related subspecialty.

Diagnosis

Evaluation combines history, blood and stool tests, imaging and endoscopic procedures. Upper endoscopy inspects the oesophagus, stomach and duodenum; colonoscopy examines the colon and can remove polyps. Capsule endoscopy and endoscopic ultrasound extend visualisation to harder-to-reach areas.

Conditions

Common conditions include gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, peptic ulcers, coeliac disease, irritable bowel syndrome, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, pancreatitis, gallstones, viral hepatitis and gastrointestinal cancers.

Treatment

Treatment ranges from diet and medicines such as acid suppression, anti-inflammatory and biologic drugs to endoscopic therapy, stenting, polypectomy and referral for surgery. Screening colonoscopy is a major preventive tool against colorectal cancer.
