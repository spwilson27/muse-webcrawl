Peridotite

Peridotite is a dense, coarse-grained ultramafic igneous rock consisting mostly of the silicate minerals olivine and pyroxene. It is the dominant rock type of the Earth's upper mantle above a depth of about 400 km.

Types

Varieties include dunite (over 90% olivine), harzburgite, lherzolite (the probable source rock of basaltic magmas), and wehrlite. Peridotite brought to the surface in xenoliths, ophiolites, and kimberlite pipes provides direct samples of the mantle.

Significance

When exposed to water, peridotite can alter to serpentinite. Reaction of peridotite with CO2 can form stable carbonate minerals, a process studied for carbon sequestration; natural examples occur in the Samail Ophiolite of Oman.
