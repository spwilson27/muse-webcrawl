Steyr Arms (German: Steyr Waffen) is an Austrian firearms manufacturer based in Steyr, Austria. Founded by Josef Werndl in 1864, the company became one of Europe's largest arms producers as the Österreichische Waffenfabrik-Gesellschaft (ŒWG), employing more than 15,000 workers during the First World War. It later formed part of the Steyr-Daimler-Puch conglomerate before becoming an independent firm.

History

Werndl's firm rose to prominence with the Werndl–Holub breech-loading rifle adopted by the Austro-Hungarian army in 1867. The company went on to manufacture the Mannlicher straight-pull bolt-action rifles, including the widely exported M1895, and the Mannlicher–Schönauer sporting rifle. In the twentieth century it produced military arms through both world wars and, after Austria's postwar rearmament, the StG 58.

Modern products

Steyr is best known today for the Steyr AUG (Armee-Universal-Gewehr), a bullpup assault rifle adopted by the Austrian Bundesheer in 1977 and exported worldwide, recognizable by its integrated telescopic sight. Other notable products include the Steyr Scout lightweight bolt-action rifle, developed with input from Jeff Cooper; the HS .50 heavy sniper rifle; and a line of pistols including the M series. The company also manufactures high-quality hunting rifles in the Mannlicher tradition.
