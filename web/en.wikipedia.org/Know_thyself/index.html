Know thyself

"Know thyself" (Greek: γνῶθι σεαυτόν, gnōthi seauton) is a philosophical maxim that was inscribed upon the Temple of Apollo in the ancient Greek precinct of Delphi. It is one of the Delphic maxims and was attributed in antiquity to several sages, including Thales and Chilon of Sparta.

Meaning

The maxim has been interpreted in many ways: as a warning against hubris, as advice to know one's limits and place in the world, and as a call to self-examination. In Plato's dialogues, Socrates treats self-knowledge as the foundation of philosophy and of the care of the soul.

Influence

The phrase became a cornerstone of Western philosophy, echoed by thinkers from Heraclitus and Pythagoras to Descartes, and remains a byword for introspection and self-understanding.
