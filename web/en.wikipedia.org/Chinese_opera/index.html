Chinese opera
Chinese: 戲曲 (xìqǔ)
Elements: Singing, speech, acting, acrobatics, martial arts
Major form: Peking opera (Beijing opera)

Chinese opera (xiqu) is a popular form of drama and musical theatre in China with roots going back to the early periods of Chinese history. It combines music, vocal performance, mime, dance, acrobatics and stylised acting.

History

Precursors included entertainment in the Tang dynasty's Pear Garden and Song dynasty variety plays. Mature forms developed in the Yuan dynasty zaju and Ming dynasty kunqu, one of the oldest surviving forms.

Regional forms

There are hundreds of regional genres. Peking opera, which arose in the late 18th–19th centuries, is the most widely known, alongside Cantonese opera, Sichuan opera (famous for face-changing, bian lian), Kunqu and Yue opera.

Performance

Roles fall into sheng (male), dan (female), jing (painted-face) and chou (clown) types. Elaborate makeup and costumes signal character, while sparse stage props and symbolic movement evoke setting.
