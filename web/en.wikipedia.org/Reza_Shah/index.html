Reza Shah Pahlavi (15 March 1878 – 26 July 1944) was the founder of the Pahlavi dynasty and Shah of Iran from 1925 until his forced abdication in 1941. Rising from the ranks of the Persian Cossack Brigade, he seized power in a 1921 coup and became prime minister before deposing the Qajar dynasty and taking the throne.

Reign

Reza Shah pursued an ambitious programme of modernisation: he built roads and the Trans-Iranian Railway, founded the University of Tehran, expanded secular education, reformed the judiciary, and promoted industrialisation. His secularising policies, including the 1936 ban on the hijab (Kashf-e hijab), and his centralisation of power brought him into conflict with clerics and tribal leaders.

Abdication and death

During the Second World War, the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran in August 1941 forced Reza Shah to abdicate in favour of his son, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi. He died in exile in Johannesburg, South Africa, in 1944.
