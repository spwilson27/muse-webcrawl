Watts riots

DateAugust 11–16, 1965
LocationWatts, Los Angeles, California
Deaths34
ArrestsMore than 3,400

The Watts riots, sometimes called the Watts Rebellion, were a large-scale series of riots that took place in the Watts neighborhood of Los Angeles from August 11 to 16, 1965. The unrest followed the arrest of Marquette Frye, a young African American motorist, by a white California Highway Patrol officer, an incident that drew an angry crowd.

Background

Watts was a predominantly Black neighborhood marked by high unemployment, poor housing, underfunded schools, and longstanding resentment of aggressive policing. Restrictive housing covenants had concentrated Black residents in areas like Watts even as Los Angeles's population boomed.

Events

Over six days, rioting and looting spread across a 46-square-mile area. The California National Guard was mobilized, and a curfew imposed. By the end, 34 people had been killed, more than 1,000 injured, over 3,400 arrested, and roughly 0 million in property damaged.

Aftermath

The McCone Commission investigated the riots and cited jobs, education, and police relations as root causes. The events became a landmark in the American civil rights era and prompted debate about urban inequality nationwide.
