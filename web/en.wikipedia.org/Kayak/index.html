Kayak

A kayak is a small, narrow human-powered watercraft typically propelled by means of a double-bladed paddle. The traditional kayak has a covered deck and one or more cockpits, each seating one paddler. Kayaks were originally developed by the indigenous peoples of the Arctic regions — including the Inuit, Aleut and Yup'ik — for hunting on lakes, rivers and coastal waters.

History

The word kayak derives from the Greenlandic qajaq. Traditional kayaks were built from driftwood and stitched sealskin stretched over a wooden or whalebone frame, and the paddler wore a waterproof tuilik. Kayaking entered the Olympic Games in 1936.

Types

Modern kayaks are made of plastic, fibreglass or composites and come in many forms: sea kayaks for touring, whitewater kayaks, recreational sit-on-top kayaks, racing kayaks and surf kayaks. The cockpit may be covered by a spray deck (sprayskirt) to keep out water. Unlike the canoe, which is paddled with a single-bladed paddle and usually open, the kayak is paddled seated with legs extended forward.
