Sex Pistols

The Sex Pistols were the defining band of the British punk rock movement.

The Sex Pistols were an English punk rock band formed in London in 1975. Although short-lived in their original incarnation, lasting only two and a half years, they are regarded as one of the most influential acts in the history of popular music, initiating the punk movement in the United Kingdom and inspiring many later punk and alternative rock musicians.

History

The band was assembled by manager Malcolm McLaren around his King's Road clothing shop SEX. Singer John Lydon — dubbed "Johnny Rotten" — joined guitarist Steve Jones, drummer Paul Cook, and bassist Glen Matlock in 1975. Their confrontational live shows and the swearing incident on Bill Grundy's Today programme in December 1976 made them national notoriety.

Matlock was replaced in 1977 by Sid Vicious, who could barely play bass but embodied the punk image. Signed and dropped in quick succession by EMI and A&M, the band released its only studio album, Never Mind the Bollocks, Here's the Sex Pistols, on Virgin Records in October 1977. Singles "Anarchy in the U.K.", "God Save the Queen", "Pretty Vacant", and "Holidays in the Sun" became punk anthems; "God Save the Queen", released during the Silver Jubilee, was widely seen as an attack on the monarchy and reached number two in the UK (amid claims the chart was rigged to keep it from number one).

Breakup and legacy

A chaotic tour of the United States ended in January 1978 with Lydon quitting on stage in San Francisco, famously asking, "Ever get the feeling you've been cheated?" Vicious died of a heroin overdose in February 1979 while awaiting trial for the death of his girlfriend Nancy Spungen. The remaining members continued briefly under McLaren's direction with the film and album The Great Rock 'n' Roll Swindle.

The Pistols reunited for lucrative tours in 1996, 2002–2003, and 2007–2008. Despite recording only one studio album, their stripped-down, aggressive sound and anti-establishment stance permanently changed British music and fashion, and they were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2006 — an honour they characteristically refused.
