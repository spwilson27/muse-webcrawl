The Crow language (native name Apsáalooke) is a Missouri River Siouan language spoken by the Crow people (Apsáalooke) of Montana. It is closely related to Hidatsa, the other member of the Missouri River branch of the Siouan family.

History

Crow and Hidatsa diverged as their speakers separated several centuries ago. In the modern era the number of fluent speakers declined sharply under pressure from English, but the language remains one of the more widely spoken Indigenous languages of the northern Plains, with several thousand speakers and active revitalisation efforts.

Phonology and grammar

Crow has a small consonant inventory, contrastive vowel length and nasal vowels, and pitch accent. It is a head-marking, polysynthetic language in which verbs carry pronominal prefixes and a rich set of derivational affixes; word order is generally subject–object–verb.

Revitalisation

The Crow Tribe has supported language nests, school programmes and dictionary projects. Crow is taught at Little Big Horn College, and a Crow dictionary and grammar have been published to support learners.
