Kęstutis

Kęstutis (Lithuanian pronunciation; c. 1297 – 3 or 15 August 1382) was the Duke of Trakai and co-ruler of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania with his brother Algirdas from 1345 to 1377. He was the father of Vytautas the Great and the grandfather's generation of the Gediminid dynasty.

Life

Son of Grand Duke Gediminas, Kęstutis governed western Lithuania — Samogitia and Trakai — and bore the brunt of the long war against the Teutonic Order, fighting dozens of campaigns and raids.

Struggle with Jogaila

After Algirdas's death in 1377, Kęstutis recognised his nephew Jogaila as Grand Duke, but Jogaila's secret Treaty of Dovydiškės (1380) with the Teutonic Order alienated him. In 1381 Kęstutis seized Vilnius and became Grand Duke. Jogaila recaptured power in 1382; Kęstutis was imprisoned in Kreva Castle and died there, probably murdered, days later. His son Vytautas escaped and continued the civil war.

Legacy

Kęstutis is remembered as a defender of pagan Lithuania and father of Vytautas. His wife Birutė, a former priestess (vaidilutė) of Palanga, became a folk figure.
