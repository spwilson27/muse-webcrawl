Miami Beach, Florida

Miami Beach is a coastal resort city in Miami-Dade County, Florida, United States. Located on a barrier island between Biscayne Bay and the Atlantic Ocean, across the bay from the city of Miami, it was incorporated on 26 March 1915. Its population was about 82,890 at the 2020 census.

History

The island was developed in the 1910s and 1920s by entrepreneurs including Carl G. Fisher, who promoted it as a winter resort. Miami Beach boomed during the Florida land boom and again after World War II, becoming a major destination for Jewish retirees and vacationers, and later for Cuban and other Latin American immigrant communities.

South Beach and Art Deco

The southern part of the city, South Beach (SoBe), is famous for its pastel-coloured Art Deco hotels along Ocean Drive; the Miami Beach Architectural District contains the largest collection of Art Deco architecture in the world, with some 800 preserved buildings constructed mainly in the 1930s and 1940s. South Beach is a major centre of fashion, nightlife, and events such as Art Basel Miami Beach.

Climate challenges

Situated barely above sea level, Miami Beach is highly vulnerable to sea-level rise and "sunny day" flooding, and has invested heavily in raised roads, pumps, and seawalls as adaptation measures.
