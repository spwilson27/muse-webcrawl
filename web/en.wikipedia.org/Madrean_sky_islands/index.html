Madrean sky islands

The Madrean sky islands are enclaves of Madrean pine–oak woodlands found at higher elevations in a complex of small mountain ranges in southern and southeastern Arizona, southwestern New Mexico, and northwestern Mexico. Surrounded at lower elevations by the Sonoran and Chihuahuan deserts, these isolated "islands" of forest support distinctive communities of plants and animals.

Geography

Some forty ranges are usually counted, including the Santa Catalinas, Santa Ritas, Huachucas, Chiricahuas, and Pinaleños in Arizona, and related ranges extending into Sonora and Chihuahua. Rising abruptly from desert grassland and scrub, the ranges pass through oak woodland into pine and mixed-conifer forest at their summits, the highest exceeding 3,200 metres.

Biology

The sky islands are a meeting ground of species from the Rocky Mountains to the north and the Sierra Madre Occidental to the south, together with desert species at lower elevations. This mixture, and the isolation of populations since the climate warmed after the last glacial period, has produced high biodiversity and many endemic or disjunct populations, from Mexican jays and trogons to rare salamanders and snails. The region is a noted destination for birdwatchers.

Conservation

Fire suppression, groundwater pumping, border infrastructure, and climate change threaten the connectivity and habitats of the sky islands. Conservation groups work to protect wildlife corridors between the ranges so that species such as jaguars and ocelots can move through the region.
