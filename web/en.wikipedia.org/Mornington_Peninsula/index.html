Mornington Peninsula

Location: Victoria, Australia
Population: approx. 165,000 (Shire of Mornington Peninsula)
Major towns: Mornington, Frankston, Rosebud, Dromana, Sorrento, Portsea

The Mornington Peninsula is a peninsula located south-east of Melbourne, Victoria, Australia. It is surrounded by Port Phillip Bay to the west, Western Port Bay to the east, and Bass Strait to the south, and is connected to the mainland in the north. Much of the peninsula is administered by the Shire of Mornington Peninsula, with the city of Frankston at its northern gateway.

Geography and communities

The peninsula is roughly 40–50 km long. Its western shoreline on Port Phillip features calm bay beaches and towns such as Mornington, Mount Martha, Dromana, Rosebud, Rye, Sorrento, and Portsea, while the Bass Strait coast is known for surf beaches and rugged scenery around Flinders and Cape Schanck. The hinterland around Red Hill and Main Ridge is agricultural, noted for vineyards, orchards, and olive groves.

History and attractions

The area is the traditional land of the Boonwurrung people. European settlement followed Melbourne's founding in the 1830s, and the peninsula became a favoured holiday destination in the 19th and 20th centuries. Notable events include the disappearance of Prime Minister Harold Holt while swimming at Cheviot Beach near Portsea in 1967. Attractions include the Point Nepean National Park and Quarantine Station, the Ashcombe Maze, hot springs at Fingal (Peninsula Hot Springs), the Enchanted Adventure Garden at Arthurs Seat with its chairlift (Eagle), and ferry services from Sorrento to Queenscliff across the bay entrance, known as "The Rip".

Wine region

The Mornington Peninsula is a recognised wine region, specialising in cool-climate Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, with dozens of cellar doors concentrated around Red Hill and Merricks.
