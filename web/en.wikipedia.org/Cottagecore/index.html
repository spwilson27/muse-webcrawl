Cottagecore

Cottagecore is an internet aesthetic and lifestyle movement that romanticises simple, rural and agricultural life. Popularised on social media platforms such as Tumblr, TikTok and Instagram in the late 2010s and early 2020s, it idealises a harmonious life in nature, emphasising traditional skills such as baking, gardening, knitting and foraging.

Aesthetic and fashion

The visual style centres on flowery meadows, cottages, farm animals and domestic crafts. Clothing associated with the movement includes long flowing dresses, puff sleeves, lace, gingham, floral prints and straw hats. Interiors favour vintage furniture, dried flowers, houseplants and handmade objects.

Popularity

Cottagecore grew significantly during the COVID-19 pandemic, when lockdowns, urban stress and remote work intensified interest in self-sufficiency and countryside escapes. Searches and social-media hashtags for cottagecore surged in 2020, and the aesthetic influenced fashion collections, publishing and music — Taylor Swift's albums Folklore and Evermore (2020) are frequently associated with it.

Criticism and related movements

Commentators have criticised cottagecore for idealising a pre-industrial past, ignoring the hardships of historical rural life and sometimes echoing conservative or colonial imagery; others celebrate it as a queer-friendly, anti-consumerist and environmentally minded subculture. Related aesthetics include grandmacore, farmcore and fairycore.
