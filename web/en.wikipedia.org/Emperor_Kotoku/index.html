Emperor Kōtoku

Emperor Kōtoku (596 – 24 November 654) was the 36th emperor of Japan according to the traditional order of succession. His reign, 645 to 654, began after the Isshi Incident and is associated with the Taika Reforms.

Reign

Born Prince Karu, he ascended the throne after Empress Kōgyoku abdicated following the assassination of Soga no Iruka. Working with Prince Naka no Ōe (later Emperor Tenji) and Nakatomi no Kamatari, his court proclaimed reforms modeled on Tang China: centralization, land redistribution in principle, new administrative districts, and the era name Taika, the first Japanese nengō. He moved the capital to Naniwa (present-day Osaka).

Later years

Relations with the crown prince cooled; when the court returned to Asuka, Kōtoku remained in Naniwa and died there in 654.
