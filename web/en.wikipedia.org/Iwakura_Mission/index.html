Iwakura Mission

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Iwakura Mission or Iwakura Embassy (Japanese: 岩倉使節団) was a Japanese diplomatic voyage to the United States and Europe conducted between 1871 and 1873 by leading statesmen of the Meiji government. Headed by Iwakura Tomomi, it included figures such as Ōkubo Toshimichi, Kido Takayoshi, and Itō Hirobumi, together with students sent abroad to study.

Purpose and itinerary

The mission's aims were to renegotiate the unequal treaties, to observe Western institutions, industry, and education, and to gather knowledge for Japan's modernization. Although treaty revision was refused, the mission's extensive observations — recorded by Kume Kunitake in a detailed account — deeply influenced Meiji policy, confirming the government's commitment to rapid industrialization and centralized reform.

Significance

The mission is regarded as one of the most important episodes of the Meiji Restoration: nearly half the senior government was abroad for almost two years, and its findings shaped Japanese law, education, and military organization for a generation.
