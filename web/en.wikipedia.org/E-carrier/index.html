The E-carrier is a member of the series of carrier systems for digital transmission of many simultaneous telephone calls by time-division multiplexing, standardized by the ITU-T and used in Europe and most of the world outside North America and Japan, where the related T-carrier system is used instead. The basic unit, E1, carries 2.048 Mbit/s.

E1

An E1 frame consists of 32 time slots of 64 kbit/s each, transmitted 8,000 times per second. Thirty slots typically carry voice or data, while time slot 0 provides framing and alarms and time slot 16 historically carried signalling (later often freed for data with channel-associated signalling replaced by common-channel signalling). E1 lines were widely used for ISDN primary rate access and for connecting PBXs and cellular base stations.

Hierarchy

Higher levels multiplex four streams of the level below using bit interleaving with justification (stuffing) bits, forming the plesiochronous digital hierarchy (PDH): four E1s make an E2, four E2s an E3, and so on. PDH was largely superseded by the synchronous SDH/SONET hierarchies and later by packet-based transport, but E1 interfaces remain common in legacy telephony.

Comparison with T-carrier

The North American T1 carries 24 channels at 1.544 Mbit/s using 8-bit samples with a framing bit. Interworking between the two systems requires transcoding between A-law (E-carrier) and μ-law (T-carrier) companding and conversion of signalling formats.
