The Jurassic is a geologic period and stratigraphic system that spanned from the end of the Triassic Period, about 201.4 million years ago, to the beginning of the Cretaceous Period, approximately 145 million years ago. It is the middle period of the Mesozoic Era and is named after the Jura Mountains of the European Alps, where limestone strata from the period were first identified.

Subdivisions

The Jurassic is divided into three epochs: the Early Jurassic (201.4–174.7 Ma), the Middle Jurassic (174.7–161.5 Ma) and the Late Jurassic (161.5–145 Ma). The period began with the Triassic–Jurassic extinction event, one of the major mass extinctions of the Phanerozoic.

Geography and climate

During the Jurassic, the supercontinent Pangaea continued to break apart into Laurasia and Gondwana, and the Atlantic Ocean began to open. Sea levels rose, flooding continental interiors with shallow epicontinental seas. The climate was generally warm, with no polar ice caps, and lush vegetation extended to high latitudes.

Life

Dinosaurs became the dominant land vertebrates: giant sauropods such as Diplodocus and Brachiosaurus, predators such as Allosaurus, and plated stegosaurs all flourished. The first birds, including Archaeopteryx, evolved from small theropod dinosaurs in the Late Jurassic. Mammals remained mostly small and nocturnal. In the oceans, ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs and ammonites were abundant.
