Six Gallery reading

The Six Gallery reading was a poetry reading held on October 7, 1955, at the Six Gallery in San Francisco, California. It is widely regarded as the public birth of the Beat Generation and as a landmark of the San Francisco Renaissance. Five young poets read, before an audience that included Jack Kerouac, Neal Cassady, and other figures of the emerging counterculture.

Background

The Six Gallery, located at 3119 Fillmore Street, was a cooperative art gallery run by a group of young artists in a building that had formerly been an auto-repair shop — the same space that had hosted the King Ubu Gallery. Poet Kenneth Rexroth, a central figure of the San Francisco literary scene, agreed to serve as master of ceremonies for an evening of poetry.

The event

The five poets who read were Philip Lamantia, Michael McClure, Philip Whalen, Allen Ginsberg, and Gary Snyder. Lamantia read poems by the late John Hoffman; McClure read “Point Lobos: Animism” and “For the Death of 100 Whales”; Whalen read “Plus Ca Change”; Snyder read “A Berry Feast.” The climax of the evening was Ginsberg's first public reading of the first part of Howl, which moved the audience and had Kerouac reportedly shouting encouragement from the crowd. Ginsberg later recalled that at the end “everybody was absolutely glowing.”

Legacy

The reading drew attention to the young poets and led Lawrence Ferlinghetti of City Lights Books to offer to publish Howl, resulting in Howl and Other Poems (1956) and the famous obscenity trial of 1957. The event is fictionalized in Kerouac's novel The Dharma Bums, where it appears as the “Gallery Six” reading.
