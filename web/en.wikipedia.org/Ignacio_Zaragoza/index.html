Ignacio Zaragoza

General Ignacio Zaragoza (1829–1862).

Ignacio Zaragoza Seguín (March 24, 1829 – September 8, 1862) was a Mexican general and politician, celebrated as the hero of the Battle of Puebla on May 5, 1862, when his outnumbered army defeated the invading French forces of Napoleon III. The victory is commemorated annually as Cinco de Mayo.

Early life and career

Zaragoza was born in Bahía del Espíritu Santo (present-day Goliad, Texas), then part of the Mexican state of Coahuila y Tejas, and his family moved south after Texas independence. He joined the military during the Reform War, fighting on the liberal side under Benito Juárez, and briefly served as Secretary of War in 1861 before taking command of the Army of the East against the French intervention.

Battle of Puebla and death

At Puebla, Zaragoza's roughly 4,500 troops, defending the forts of Loreto and Guadalupe, repelled repeated assaults by some 6,000 French soldiers under General Charles de Lorencez, then regarded as among the finest troops in the world. Zaragoza telegraphed Juárez the famous report that "the arms of the nation have been covered with glory." Only four months later, on campaign near Puebla, he contracted typhoid fever and died at the age of 33. Puebla's capital was renamed Puebla de Zaragoza in his honor, and he is remembered as a national hero of Mexico.
