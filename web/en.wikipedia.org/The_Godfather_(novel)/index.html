The Godfather is a crime novel by American author Mario Puzo, first published in 1969 by G. P. Putnam's Sons. The novel details the story of a Sicilian Mafia family based in New York City and Long Island, headed by the patriarch Vito Corleone. The novel covers the years 1945 to 1955 and includes the backstory of Vito Corleone from early childhood to adulthood in Sicily and New York.

Plot summary

The story begins in 1945 at the wedding of Connie Corleone, daughter of Don Vito Corleone, head of the most powerful of New York's Five Families. When the Don declines to enter the narcotics trade proposed by Virgil "The Turk" Sollozzo, an assassination attempt leaves him gravely wounded, drawing his youngest son Michael — a decorated war veteran who had kept his distance from the family business — into the criminal underworld. Michael murders Sollozzo and a corrupt police captain, flees to Sicily, and eventually returns to succeed his father. The novel climaxes with Michael consolidating power by eliminating the heads of the rival families.

Characters

Principal characters include Vito Corleone, his sons Sonny, Fredo, and Michael, his adopted son and consigliere Tom Hagen, his daughter Connie, capos Peter Clemenza and Salvatore Tessio, and singer Johnny Fontane, whose subplot involves securing a film role through the Don's intervention.

Reception and legacy

The novel remained on The New York Times Best Seller list for 67 weeks and sold over nine million copies in two years; it has since sold tens of millions worldwide. The word "godfather" in its Mafia sense entered the English language through the book. Puzo adapted the novel with director Francis Ford Coppola into the Academy Award-winning film The Godfather (1972), followed by sequels in 1974 and 1990.

Sequels and related works

Puzo's later novel The Sicilian (1984) features Michael Corleone in a supporting role. After Puzo's death, authorized sequels by Mark Winegardner (The Godfather Returns and The Godfather's Revenge) and a prequel by Ed Falco (The Family Corleone) expanded the saga.
