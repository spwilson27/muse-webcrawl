Radiculopathy

Radiculopathy

Compression of a spinal nerve root produces radicular symptoms.

Radiculopathy is a set of conditions in which one or more spinal nerve roots are affected, typically by compression or irritation near where the root exits the spinal column. Because each nerve root supplies a characteristic strip of skin (dermatome) and set of muscles (myotome), radiculopathy produces pain, numbness, paraesthesia or weakness in the corresponding distribution. Common examples include cervical radiculopathy in the arm and lumbosacral radiculopathy — sciatica — in the leg.

Causes

The most common cause is a herniated intervertebral disc pressing on the root, followed by degenerative foraminal stenosis from facet arthropathy and osteophytes, especially in older patients. Less frequent causes include tumours, infection, diabetes-related root injury and trauma.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the history and neurological examination — testing dermatomes, myotomes and reflexes — supplemented by MRI and, where needed, electrodiagnostic studies (EMG/nerve conduction). Most cases improve with conservative management: activity modification, physiotherapy and anti-inflammatory medication. Epidural steroid injections may give short-term relief. Surgery such as discectomy or foraminotomy is reserved for progressive weakness, cauda equina syndrome, or pain refractory to conservative care.
