Brâncovenesc style

RegionWallachia (present-day Romania)
PeriodLate 17th – early 18th century
Named afterPrince Constantin Brâncoveanu

The Brâncovenesc style, also known as the Brâncoveanu style, is an architectural and decorative style that flourished in Wallachia during the reign of Prince Constantin Brâncoveanu (1688–1714). It is regarded as the first distinctly Romanian national style, synthesising local Byzantine and post-Byzantine traditions with Ottoman, Venetian, and Baroque influences.

Characteristics

Brâncovenesc buildings feature richly carved stone ornament, especially floral and vegetal motifs around windows, doors, and columns; open loggias and arcaded verandas; balustrades of carved stone; and steeply pitched roofs. Churches typically combine a Byzantine plan with elaborate exterior decoration, while palaces and monasteries show elegant arcades and sculpted capitals.

Notable examples

The masterpiece of the style is the Horezu Monastery (founded 1690–1693), a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other examples include the Mogoșoaia Palace, the Antim Monastery and Stavropoleos Church in Bucharest, the Brâncoveanu palace at Potlogi, and numerous churches built or restored under the prince's patronage.

Legacy

After Brâncoveanu's execution by the Ottomans in 1714 the style faded, but it was revived in the late 19th and early 20th centuries within the Neo-Romanian (Neo-Brâncovenesc) movement led by architects such as Ion Mincu, which shaped much of modern Bucharest's domestic architecture.
