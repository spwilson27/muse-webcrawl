G. Olof Thorin (28 February 1912 – 14 February 2004) was a Swedish mathematician working in functional analysis and harmonic analysis, best known for the Riesz–Thorin theorem, one of the foundational results of interpolation theory for linear operators.

Work

Thorin proved his interpolation theorem in his 1948 doctoral dissertation at Lund University, giving a proof of Marcel Riesz's earlier convexity theorem for Lp spaces based on complex function theory — the method now known as the complex interpolation method. The theorem states that the operator norm of a linear map between Lp spaces varies log-convexly with 1/p.

Career

Much of Thorin's career was spent outside academia, working in insurance and Swedish government statistics, though he later returned to research and teaching. His interpolation result became a standard tool in harmonic analysis and partial differential equations.
