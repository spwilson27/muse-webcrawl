Mars (mythology)

In ancient Roman religion and mythology, Mars (Latin: Mārs) was the god of war and also an agricultural guardian, a combination characteristic of early Rome. He was the son of Jupiter and Juno, and was pre-eminent among the Roman army's military gods. Most of his festivals were held in March, the month named for him (Latin Martius), and in October, which began and ended the season for military campaigning and agriculture.

Origins and character

Mars combined a warlike nature with agricultural functions. As Mars Sylvanus and Mars Gradivus he protected soldiers in battle and farmers in the fields. Under the influence of Greek culture, Mars was identified with the Greek god Ares, but the character and dignity of the two deities differed fundamentally: where Ares was seen as destructive and destabilising, Mars represented military power as a way to secure peace and was regarded as the father (pater) of the Roman people.

Mars and the founding of Rome

In Roman mythology, Mars was the father of Romulus and Remus, the twin founders of Rome, by the Vestal Virgin Rhea Silvia. Their story linked the Roman people to divine ancestry and underpinned the city's self-image. The Campus Martius ("Field of Mars") outside the city walls was named for him and was used for military training and assemblies.

Cult and festivals

Mars was one of the most important deities of the Roman state. His priest, the Flamen Martialis, was among the senior flamines. Festivals included the Equirria (horse races), the Quinquatrus, and the Armilustrium. Augustus promoted Mars Ultor ("Mars the Avenger") with a great temple in his new forum. The planet Mars and the month of March both bear his name.
