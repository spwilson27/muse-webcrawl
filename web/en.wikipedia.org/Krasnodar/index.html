Krasnodar is a city in southern Russia, the administrative centre of Krasnodar Krai, located on the Kuban River about 120 km northeast of the Black Sea port of Novorossiysk. It is the largest city of the North Caucasus region and one of Russia's fastest-growing cities.

History

The city was founded in 1793 as Yekaterinodar ("Catherine's gift"), a fortress of the Black Sea Cossacks on land granted by Catherine the Great. It grew as a trading and military centre of the Kuban Cossack Host. After the October Revolution, in 1920 it was renamed Krasnodar ("gift of the Reds"). The city was occupied by German forces in 1942–43 and heavily damaged before liberation in February 1943; the first major public war-crimes trial of Nazi collaborators was held there in July 1943.

Economy and transport

Krasnodar is a hub for agriculture, food processing, machinery and oil refining in Russia's fertile Kuban steppe. It is served by Krasnodar International Airport and a major railway junction. The city's football club, FC Krasnodar, plays in a distinctive modern stadium opened in 2016.

Climate

Krasnodar has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers and mild, changeable winters, and it is among the sunniest large cities in Russia.
