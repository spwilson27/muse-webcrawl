The Canada–United States border is the longest international border in the world, stretching 8,891 kilometres (5,525 miles) between Canada and the United States, including boundaries through the Great Lakes, the St. Lawrence River and the Alaska panhandle. The land border comprises two sections: Canada's border with the contiguous United States along the 49th parallel and through the Great Lakes, and the border between Alaska and Yukon/British Columbia.

History

The boundary was established and adjusted by a series of treaties beginning with the Treaty of Paris (1783), and including the Convention of 1818, the Webster–Ashburton Treaty (1842), the Oregon Treaty (1846) and the Alaska boundary settlement of 1903. The International Boundary Commission, created in 1925, maintains the border line, including the cleared vista cut through forests along the land boundary.

Crossings and security

There are more than 100 land ports of entry, and the two countries share the world's largest bilateral trading relationship. Security cooperation intensified after the September 11 attacks, with measures such as the Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative and pre-clearance arrangements, while the border remains largely undefended militarily — a fact often celebrated in the phrase "the world's longest undefended border".
