Battle of Aughrim

Date12 July 1691 (Old Style)
LocationAughrim, County Galway, Ireland
ResultDecisive Williamite victory
BelligerentsWilliamites vs Jacobites

The Battle of Aughrim was the decisive battle of the Williamite War in Ireland, fought on 12 July 1691 (Old Style) near the village of Aughrim in County Galway. The forces of William III, commanded by the Dutch general Godert de Ginkel, inflicted a crushing defeat on the Jacobite army of James II led by the Marquis de Saint-Ruth. It is generally regarded as the bloodiest battle ever fought on Irish soil, with perhaps 7,000 men killed.

St Ruth drew up his army in a strong position on Kilcommodon Hill, protected by bog in front, and held off Williamite attacks for much of the day. The battle turned late in the evening: Williamite cavalry found a way through the bog along a causeway on the Jacobite left, and St Ruth was decapitated by a cannonball just as he was organising a counterattack. Leaderless, the Jacobite army collapsed; thousands were cut down in the pursuit, which continued into the darkness, and vast quantities of weapons and supplies were captured.

Aughrim effectively ended Jacobite resistance in Ireland: Galway surrendered soon after, and the remaining Jacobite army withdrew to Limerick, whose capitulation by the Treaty of Limerick in October 1691 ended the war. The battle was celebrated in Protestant and Orange tradition for over two centuries (its anniversary later merged with that of the Boyne on 12 July), while remaining comparatively little known in the modern Republic of Ireland.
