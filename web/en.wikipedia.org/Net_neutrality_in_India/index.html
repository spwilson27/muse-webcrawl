Net neutrality in India

Net neutrality in India refers to the debate and regulatory settlement over whether internet service providers in India must treat all data equally. India adopted some of the world's strongest net neutrality rules following a mass public campaign in 2015–2016.

Background

The debate ignited in 2014–2015 over Bharti Airtel's proposed charge for VoIP calls and the launch of zero-rating schemes such as Airtel Zero and Facebook's Free Basics (Internet.org), which offered free access to selected services. The SaveTheInternet campaign drew over a million comments to the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI).

Regulation

In February 2016, TRAI's Prohibition of Discriminatory Tariffs for Data Services Regulations banned zero-rating and differential pricing. In 2018, based on TRAI recommendations, the Department of Telecommunications approved net neutrality principles prohibiting blocking, throttling, and paid prioritisation, with exceptions for specialised and critical services.
