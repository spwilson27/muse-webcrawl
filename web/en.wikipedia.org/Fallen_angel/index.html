Fallen angel

ConceptAngel expelled from heaven for rebellion against God
ReligionsChristianity, Islam, Judaism (apocryphal traditions)
Chief figureSatan / Lucifer
Key textsBook of Enoch, Revelation 12, Isaiah 14 (interpreted), Paradise Lost

A fallen angel is an angel that has been exiled or banished from Heaven. The term originates in Abrahamic religious traditions for angels who rebelled against God — typically through pride — and were cast down, often becoming demons who tempt humanity to sin. The most famous fallen angel is Satan, identified in later tradition with Lucifer, the "light-bringer" of Isaiah 14:12.

Origins

The Hebrew Bible contains no fully developed doctrine of fallen angels, though passages such as Isaiah 14, Ezekiel 28, and the story of the "sons of God" and the Nephilim in Genesis 6 were later read in that light. The fullest early account is the Second Temple Jewish Book of Enoch (1 Enoch), in which the Watchers — angels led by Samyaza and Azazel — descend to earth, take human wives, father giants, and teach forbidden arts, for which they are bound and judged. 2 Enoch and the Book of Jubilees develop related traditions.

Christian theology

In the New Testament, Revelation 12 describes a war in heaven in which Michael and his angels defeat the dragon (Satan) and his angels, who are thrown down to earth; Luke 10:18 has Jesus say, "I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven." Church Fathers such as Origen and Augustine systematized the idea of a primordial angelic fall motivated by pride. In Dante's Inferno and especially Milton's Paradise Lost (1667), the fall of Lucifer and his host — who become the devils of Pandemonium — was given its most influential literary form.

Islam and other traditions

In Islam, Iblis (Shaitan) is expelled for refusing God's command to bow before Adam; Islamic theology generally classifies Iblis as a jinn rather than an angel, since angels in Islam do not possess free will to disobey. Fallen angels remain a staple of art, literature, film, and popular culture, from Cabanel's painting L'Ange déchu to modern television and fantasy fiction.
