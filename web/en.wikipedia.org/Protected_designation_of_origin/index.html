Protected designation of origin

Protected designation of origin (PDO) is the highest level of a type of geographical indication under European Union law, identifying a product whose quality or characteristics are essentially or exclusively due to its geographical environment and whose production, processing, and preparation all take place within the designated area.

Scheme

The PDO scheme is part of the EU's system of geographical indications, alongside Protected Geographical Indication (PGI) and Traditional Speciality Guaranteed (TSG). Registration gives producers legal protection against imitation and misuse of the name throughout the EU and, through trade agreements, in many other countries.

Examples

  • Parmigiano-Reggiano — hard cheese from a defined area of northern Italy
  • Champagne — sparkling wine from the Champagne region of France
  • Feta — brined cheese from Greece
  • Prosciutto di Parma — cured ham from Parma
  • Melton Mowbray pork pies and Stilton cheese from England

PDO versus PGI

For a PDO, every stage of production must occur in the specified region and the product's qualities must be fundamentally linked to that place. A PGI requires only one stage of production in the region and a quality, reputation, or characteristic attributable to it.
