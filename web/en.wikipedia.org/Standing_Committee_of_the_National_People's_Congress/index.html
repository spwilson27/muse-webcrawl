Standing Committee of the National People's Congress

The Standing Committee meets in the Great Hall of the People, Beijing.

The Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPCSC) is the permanent body of the National People's Congress (NPC), the national legislature of the People's Republic of China. Because the NPC itself meets only once a year for a brief session, the Standing Committee exercises most of the NPC's powers when the full body is not in session.

Functions

Under the Constitution of China, the Standing Committee interprets the constitution and laws, enacts and amends most legislation, supervises the work of the State Council, the Central Military Commission, the National Supervisory Commission, the Supreme People's Court, and the Supreme People's Procuratorate, and decides on the appointment and removal of many senior officials. It also has the power to interpret the Basic Laws of Hong Kong and Macau.

Composition

The Standing Committee consists of a chairperson, vice-chairpersons, a secretary-general, and roughly 150 to 175 members, all elected by the NPC from among its deputies for five-year terms. It typically meets every two months. Its chairperson is customarily ranked among the top leaders of the Chinese Communist Party and presides over the committee's work along with the Council of Chairpersons.
