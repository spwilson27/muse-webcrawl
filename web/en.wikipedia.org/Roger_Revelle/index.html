Roger Revelle

BornRoger Randall Dougan Revelle, 7 March 1909, Seattle, Washington, U.S.
Died15 July 1991 (aged 82), San Diego, California, U.S.
FieldsOceanography, geophysics

Roger Randall Dougan Revelle (March 7, 1909 – July 15, 1991) was an American oceanographer and geophysicist who was a central figure in the growth of oceanography after the Second World War and one of the first scientists to study and publicly warn about anthropogenic global warming. He directed the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, played a leading role in creating the University of California, San Diego, and helped design the International Geophysical Year.

Early life and career

Revelle grew up in Southern California and studied at Pomona College before earning his doctorate in oceanography from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1936, working at Scripps under Sverdrup-era oceanographers. During the Second World War he headed the oceanographic section of the U.S. Navy's Bureau of Ships, greatly expanding naval ocean research. After the war he returned to Scripps, becoming its director in 1950, and led major expeditions — including the Midpac and Capricorn expeditions — whose seafloor measurements contributed to the emerging theory of plate tectonics.

Carbon dioxide and climate

In 1957, Revelle and Hans Suess published a landmark paper arguing that the oceans would absorb excess carbon dioxide from fossil fuels far more slowly than had been assumed, famously describing humanity's fossil-fuel burning as a "large-scale geophysical experiment" whose consequences could be significant. Revelle was instrumental in establishing the Mauna Loa carbon dioxide monitoring program under Charles David Keeling, which produced the famous Keeling Curve. He chaired the panel whose 1965 report to the U.S. President's Science Advisory Committee was the first official government acknowledgment of the climate risk from CO2. Former vice president Al Gore, who took Revelle's course at Harvard, called him an important early mentor on global warming.

University building

As the University of California's chief advocate for a San Diego campus, Revelle campaigned for the creation of UC San Diego (founded 1960) and served as dean of research and first dean of its school of science and engineering; the campus's first college is named Revelle College in his honor. He later moved to Harvard, where he directed the Center for Population Studies and taught the influential undergraduate course "Natural Sciences 118".

Honors

Revelle received the National Medal of Science in 1990. The research vessel R/V Roger Revelle, operated by Scripps, and the Revelle Medal of the University of California, San Diego, bear his name.
