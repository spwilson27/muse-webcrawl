Data curation

Data curation is the organisation and integration of data collected from various sources, involving annotation, publication and presentation of the data such that its value is maintained over time and it remains available for reuse and preservation. It includes all the processes needed for principled and controlled data creation, maintenance and management, together with the capacity to add value to data.

Activities

Curation activities span the data lifecycle: appraising and selecting data, adding documentation and metadata, checking quality and provenance, migrating formats, and depositing datasets in trusted repositories with persistent identifiers such as DOIs. The FAIR principles — that data should be Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable — provide a widely adopted framework for curation practice.

Context

The term developed in library and information science and in scientific data management, where funders and journals increasingly require researchers to share data. Professional roles such as data curator and data steward, and institutional data repositories, have grown alongside open-science policies.
