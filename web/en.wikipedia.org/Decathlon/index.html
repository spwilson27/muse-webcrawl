Decathlon


A decathlete competing in the javelin throw

The decathlon is a combined event in athletics consisting of ten track and field events held over two consecutive days. The word derives from the Greek deka (ten) and athlos (contest). Points are awarded for each event according to scoring tables, and the winner is the athlete with the highest total.

Events

On day one athletes contest the 100 metres, long jump, shot put, high jump, and 400 metres. Day two comprises the 110 metres hurdles, discus throw, pole vault, javelin throw, and 1500 metres. Performances are scored against benchmark tables originally devised in 1912 and revised several times since.

History

The decathlon developed from the ancient Greek pentathlon and the American all-around championships. It was introduced at the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, where Jim Thorpe won and was famously told by King Gustav V, "You, sir, are the world's greatest athlete." The men's decathlon is contested at the Olympic Games and World Championships; the women's equivalent is usually the heptathlon, although a women's decathlon is increasingly contested. The world record is held by Kevin Mayer of France, with 9,126 points set in 2018.
