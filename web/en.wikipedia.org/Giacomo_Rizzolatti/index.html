Giacomo Rizzolatti (born 1937) is an Italian neurophysiologist best known for leading the University of Parma research team that discovered mirror neurons in the early 1990s. Mirror neurons are brain cells that fire both when an animal performs an action and when it observes another individual performing the same action; their discovery has had wide influence on the study of imitation, language evolution, empathy, and social cognition.

Career

Rizzolatti studied medicine at the University of Padua and spent a postdoctoral period at McGill University in Montreal before joining the University of Parma, where he became professor of human physiology and directed research on the motor system. His group studied single neurons in area F5 of the macaque monkey premotor cortex. During experiments in which monkeys grasped objects, the team noticed that some neurons also discharged when the monkey merely watched an experimenter perform the same grasping movement. Published papers by di Pellegrino, Fadiga, Fogassi, Gallese, and Rizzolatti in 1992 and 1996 established the phenomenon.

Impact and honours

Rizzolatti has argued that the mirror system underlies action understanding and may be a precursor of human language and empathy; deficiencies in the system have been hypothesised, controversially, to play a role in autism. He has received numerous honours, including the Golgi Prize, the Feltrinelli Prize, the Grawemeyer Award in Psychology (2007), and the Brain Prize (2014, shared with Stanislas Dehaene and Trevor Robbins). He is a member of the Academia Europaea and a foreign associate of the French and American academies of science.
