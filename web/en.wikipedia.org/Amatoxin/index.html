Amatoxin

Amatoxins are a group of toxic bicyclic octapeptides found in several mushroom genera, most notably in the death cap Amanita phalloides and other Amanita species, as well as in some Galerina, Lepiota and Conocybe species. The principal member is α-amanitin. Amatoxins are responsible for most fatal cases of mushroom poisoning worldwide.

Mechanism

Amatoxins bind to and inhibit RNA polymerase II, halting messenger RNA synthesis and thus protein production in cells. Hepatocytes are particularly vulnerable because the toxin is efficiently taken up by the liver and recirculated via the enterohepatic circulation. The compounds are heat-stable and are not destroyed by cooking.

Poisoning course and treatment

Poisoning typically unfolds in phases: a latent period of 6–24 hours, then a severe gastrointestinal phase of vomiting, abdominal pain and watery diarrhoea, an apparent recovery, and finally hepatic and renal failure 2–5 days after ingestion, which may require liver transplantation. Treatment is supportive and may include silibinin (an extract of milk thistle) or high-dose penicillin G to block hepatic uptake, and N-acetylcysteine. A single death cap can contain enough toxin to kill an adult; the lethal dose of α-amanitin is around 0.1 mg/kg body weight.
