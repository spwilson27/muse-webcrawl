Stan Lee, co-creator of many of Marvel's best-known characters.

Stan Lee (born Stanley Martin Lieber; 28 December 1922 – 12 November 2018) was an American comic book writer, editor, publisher, and producer. He rose through the ranks of a family-run business called Timely Comics, which would later become Marvel Comics. In collaboration with others at Marvel — particularly co-writers and artists Jack Kirby and Steve Ditko — he co-created iconic characters, including superheroes Spider-Man, the X-Men, Iron Man, Thor, the Hulk, the Fantastic Four, Black Panther, Daredevil, and Doctor Strange, and popularised the flawed, human superhero in contrast to earlier idealised archetypes.

The Marvel method

In the early 1960s, tasked with creating a superhero team to rival DC's Justice League, Lee and Kirby launched The Fantastic Four (1961), whose characters argued, worried about money, and lacked secret identities. Lee developed the "Marvel method" of production: he would supply a plot outline, the artist would draw the story, and Lee would then add dialogue and captions. With Ditko he created Spider-Man (1962), a teenage hero with everyday problems, and Doctor Strange; with Kirby, the Hulk, Thor, Iron Man, the X-Men, and Black Panther — the first black superhero in mainstream American comics. Lee's alliterative character names (Peter Parker, Bruce Banner) and his chatty editorial voice — including the sign-off "Excelsior!" — cultivated a loyal fan community.

Later career

Lee became publisher of Marvel in 1972 and its public face for decades. He moved to California in the 1980s to develop Marvel's film and television properties; the runaway success of the Marvel Cinematic Universe from 2008 onward made his creations central to global popular culture, and Lee made celebrated cameo appearances in nearly every Marvel film until his death. He also founded POW! Entertainment and championed comics literacy through the Stan Lee Foundation. Disputes about credit and compensation between Lee and collaborators such as Kirby have been extensively discussed; the Kirby estate's litigation with Marvel settled in 2014.

Awards and death

Lee received the National Medal of Arts in 2008 and was inducted into the Will Eisner Comic Book Hall of Fame. He died on 12 November 2018 in Los Angeles, aged 95, shortly before the release of films featuring his characters that grossed billions of dollars worldwide.
