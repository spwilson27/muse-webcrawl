Lin Fengmian (Chinese: 林風眠; 22 November 1900 – 5 August 1991) was a Chinese painter and is considered a pioneer of modern Chinese painting for blending Chinese and Western techniques.

Life

Born in Meizhou, Guangdong, Lin went to France in 1919, studying at Dijon and Paris, where he was influenced by Fauvism and Expressionism. Returning to China in 1925, he became president of the Beijing Academy of Fine Arts and in 1928 founded the Hangzhou National Academy of Art, where he taught artists such as Zao Wou-Ki and Chu Teh-Chun.

Work

Lin developed a distinctive style using ink and colour on paper in a square format, with lyrical subjects — autumn scenes, egrets, opera figures and opera-lit landscapes. He suffered persecution during the Cultural Revolution and destroyed many of his own works; he moved to Hong Kong in 1977 and died there in 1991.
