Mary in Islam

Mary (Arabic: Maryam) is held in the highest esteem in Islam and is regarded as one of the most righteous and exalted women in the faith. She is the only woman named in the Quran, and the nineteenth chapter of the Quran is named Surah Maryam after her — a distinction with no counterpart in the Bible. Muslims believe she was miraculously pregnant with Jesus (Isa) while remaining a virgin.

Quranic account

According to the Quran, Mary was born to Imran and his wife, who dedicated her unborn child to God's service; she was raised in the sanctuary under the care of Zechariah (Zakariya). The angel Gabriel announced that she would bear a son, Jesus, by God's word alone. She withdrew to a remote place, gave birth beneath a palm tree, and later returned with the infant, who spoke from the cradle to defend her innocence.

Status and veneration

The Prophet Muhammad is reported to have counted Mary among the best women of all time, alongside Asiya, Khadija and Fatima. In Islamic teaching, Jesus is honoured as a prophet and Messiah but not as the son of God, and Mary is not worshipped; nevertheless she is a major figure of devotion and a bridge in Muslim–Christian dialogue, with shrines such as the House of the Virgin Mary near Ephesus visited by members of both faiths.
