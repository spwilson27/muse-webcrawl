Bahubali

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bahubali (Sanskrit: बाहुबली, "one with strong arms"), also called Gommateshwara, is a highly revered figure in Jainism. He was the son of Rishabhanatha (the first tirthankara of Jainism) and the younger brother of Bharata Chakravartin. Bahubali is celebrated for attaining liberation (moksha) through a year of motionless meditation in the standing kayotsarga posture, and is a central symbol of renunciation, non-violence and self-control.

Legend

According to Jain texts such as the Adi Purana, after Rishabhanatha divided his kingdom among his hundred sons, Bharata demanded submission from his brothers. Bahubali defeated Bharata in three ritual contests — staring, water-fighting, and wrestling — but, disgusted by the violence and his own pride, renounced his kingdom instead of killing his brother. He meditated standing for a year, so still that creepers grew around his legs and anthills rose at his feet, yet liberation eluded him while the resentment of having stood on his brother's land remained; when Bharata came and worshipped him, the last trace of ego dissolved and Bahubali attained omniscience (kevala jnana) and then moksha, becoming the first human of the present cosmic cycle to be liberated.

Worship

The most famous image of Bahubali is the 57-foot (17 m) monolithic statue at Shravanabelagola in Karnataka, erected around 981 CE under the Western Ganga minister Chavundaraya; it is among the largest free-standing monoliths in the world and is anointed in the Mahamastakabhisheka festival held every twelve years, when the statue is bathed in milk, saffron, and other offerings. Other colossal statues stand at Karkala, Dharmasthala, and Venur.
