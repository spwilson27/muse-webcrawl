D-pad

A D-pad (short for directional pad) is a flat, usually thumb-operated control found on video game controllers, remote controls and some handheld devices. It consists of a cross-shaped pivoting pad that can be pressed in four directions — up, down, left and right — with diagonal inputs produced by pressing two directions at once.

History

The modern D-pad was designed by Nintendo engineer Gunpei Yokoi for the Game & Watch handheld Donkey Kong in 1982, as a compact alternative to a joystick. Nintendo patented the design and used it on the Nintendo Entertainment System controller in 1983/1985, where it became the standard means of moving game characters in two dimensions.

Design variants

Because of Nintendo's patent, rival manufacturers used variations: circular or disc-shaped pads, plus-sign pads with different pivoting mechanisms, and segmented designs. Sega and Sony used dish-like pads on their controllers, while Microsoft's Xbox 360 controller used an eight-way circular pad that proved unpopular for precise inputs and was redesigned in later models.

Usage beyond games

D-pads appear on television and media-player remotes for menu navigation, on portable media players, and on some mobile phones and cameras. In games, the D-pad is now often secondary to the analog stick but remains preferred for fighting games, platformers and menu navigation thanks to its crisp, discrete inputs.
