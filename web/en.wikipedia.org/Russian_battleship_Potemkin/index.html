The Russian battleship Potemkin (Russian: Князь Потёмкин Таврический, Kniaz Potemkin Tavricheskiy, "Prince Potemkin of Taurida") was a pre-dreadnought battleship built for the Imperial Russian Navy's Black Sea Fleet. She became famous for the mutiny of her crew in June 1905, one of the emblematic events of the Russian Revolution of 1905.

The mutiny

On 14 June 1905 (O.S.), during gunnery exercises near Tendra Island, sailors refused borscht made with maggot-infested meat. When the ship's commander threatened to shoot the protesting men gathered under a tarpaulin, the crew rose under the leadership of Afanasy Matushenko, killed several officers, and sailed to Odessa, where a general strike was in progress. The funeral of the slain sailor Grigory Vakulenchuk sparked further unrest, and troops massacred civilians on the Odessa Steps — a scene immortalised in Sergei Eisenstein's 1925 film Battleship Potemkin. After cruising the Black Sea seeking support, the mutineers surrendered the ship to Romanian authorities at Constanța in exchange for asylum.

Later service

Returned to Russia, the ship was renamed Panteleimon. She served in the Black Sea during the First World War, bombarding Ottoman positions and fighting inconclusive actions against German battlecruisers. Renamed Borets za Svobodu ("Fighter for Freedom") in 1917, she was scuttled and salvaged amid the Civil War and was broken up in the early 1920s. Her ensign and relics are preserved in museums in Saint Petersburg.
