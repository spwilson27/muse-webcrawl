Yersinia pestis

KingdomBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota
GenusYersinia
SpeciesY. pestis
Discovered1894, Alexandre Yersin

Yersinia pestis is a gram-negative, non-motile, coccobacillus bacterium without spores. It is a facultative anaerobic organism that can infect humans via the Oriental rat flea (Xenopsylla cheopis). It causes the disease plague, which caused the First plague pandemic, the Black Death, and the third plague pandemic.

The bacterium was discovered in 1894 by Alexandre Yersin, a Swiss/French physician and bacteriologist from the Pasteur Institute, during an epidemic of plague in Hong Kong. Shibasaburo Kitasato independently identified a bacterium he also thought to be causative, but Yersin's organism was confirmed as the true agent.

Genomics

The complete genome sequence of a strain of Y. pestis was published in 2001. Ancient DNA recovered from victims of the Black Death and the Plague of Justinian has confirmed Y. pestis as the cause of both catastrophes, resolving a long scholarly debate.

Transmission

The natural cycle involves wild rodents and their fleas. Humans are incidental hosts, infected by flea bites (bubonic plague), by inhalation of droplets (pneumonic plague), or through contact with infected tissues.
