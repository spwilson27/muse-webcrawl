Pandemic

A pandemic is an epidemic of an infectious disease that has spread across a large region, for instance multiple continents or worldwide, affecting a substantial number of individuals. A widespread endemic disease with a stable number of infected individuals is not a pandemic. Widespread endemic diseases such as seasonal influenza are generally excluded as they occur simultaneously in large regions rather than being spread worldwide.

Throughout human history, there have been a number of pandemics of diseases such as smallpox. The Black Death, caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, killed an estimated 75–200 million people in the 14th century and is often regarded as the most fatal pandemic recorded in human history. The most recent pandemic was the COVID-19 pandemic, declared by the World Health Organization in March 2020.

Definition

The term "pandemic" did not enter standard medical use until the 19th century. Definitions generally require spread across international boundaries and wide geographic extent, though the precise criteria used by the WHO have been debated.

Notable pandemics

  • Plague of Justinian (541–549)
  • Black Death (1346–1353)
  • Cholera pandemics (1817–1924)
  • 1889–1890 flu pandemic
  • Spanish flu (1918–1920)
  • Asian flu (1957–1958)
  • Hong Kong flu (1968–1969)
  • HIV/AIDS pandemic (1981–present)
  • COVID-19 pandemic (2019–2023)

Management

Pandemic preparedness involves surveillance, vaccine development, stockpiling of medical supplies, and public-health communication. Quarantine, social distancing, and travel restrictions are traditional non-pharmaceutical interventions.
