Samuel Pepys (23 February 1633 – 26 May 1703) was an English diarist and naval administrator. He served as administrator of the Royal Navy and Member of Parliament, and is most famous for the diary he kept for a decade while still a young man.

Pepys's diary provides a first-hand account of the major events of the 1660s, including the Restoration of Charles II, the Second Anglo-Dutch War, the Great Plague of London of 1665–66, and the Great Fire of London of 1666. His vivid entries on the plague — recording rising death tolls, deserted streets, and his own fears — remain a key eyewitness source for the last major outbreak of bubonic plague in England, an aftershock of the pandemic era begun by the Black Death.

The diary

Written in shorthand from 1 January 1660 to 31 May 1669, the diary was first published in abridged form in 1825. The complete, unexpurgated transcription by Robert Latham and William Matthews appeared between 1970 and 1983.

Later life

Pepys rose to become Chief Secretary to the Admiralty. He was imprisoned in the Tower of London in 1679 on suspicion of treason during the Popish Plot, but was released. He was a Fellow and later President of the Royal Society.
