Pasteur Institute

Founded1887
FounderLouis Pasteur
HeadquartersParis, France
TypeNon-profit research institute
FocusMicrobiology, infectious disease, immunology

The Pasteur Institute (French: Institut Pasteur) is a French non-profit private foundation dedicated to the study of biology, micro-organisms, diseases, and vaccines. It is named after Louis Pasteur, who made some of the greatest breakthroughs in modern medicine, including pasteurization and vaccines for anthrax and rabies. The institute was founded on June 4, 1887, and inaugurated on November 14, 1888.

For over a century, the Pasteur Institute has been at the forefront of the battle against infectious disease. Its researchers identified the plague bacillus Yersinia pestis — Alexandre Yersin, working in Hong Kong in 1894, isolated the bacterium responsible for the bubonic plague, the disease of the Black Death. Scientists at the institute also isolated HIV, the virus causing AIDS, in 1983.

History

Pasteur, aged 62 and having survived a stroke, created the institute using donations raised internationally, including funds from the successful treatment of rabies patients such as Joseph Meister in 1885. Ten of its scientists have received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Pasteur Network

The Pasteur Network comprises over 30 institutes worldwide, largely in developing countries, devoted to medical research and public health surveillance.
