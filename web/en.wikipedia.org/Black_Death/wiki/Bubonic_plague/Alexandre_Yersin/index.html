Alexandre Yersin

Alexandre Émile John Yersin (22 September 1863 – 1 March 1943) was a Swiss-French physician and bacteriologist, remembered as the discoverer of the plague bacillus, Yersinia pestis, which was named in his honour.

Life and work

Born in Aubonne, Switzerland, Yersin studied medicine in Lausanne, Marburg, and Paris, where he joined the Pasteur Institute and worked with Émile Roux on diphtheria toxin. In 1894 he was sent to Hong Kong during an outbreak of bubonic plague; working in a small hut laboratory, he isolated the causative bacterium from buboes of plague victims — one of the great feats of early bacteriology, achieved in competition with Kitasato Shibasaburō's team.

Vietnam

Yersin spent most of his later life in French Indochina. He founded the Pasteur Institute in Nha Trang, introduced rubber trees and quinine-producing cinchona to Vietnam, explored the Da Lat plateau, and is still affectionately remembered in Vietnam as "Ông Năm". He died in Nha Trang in 1943.
