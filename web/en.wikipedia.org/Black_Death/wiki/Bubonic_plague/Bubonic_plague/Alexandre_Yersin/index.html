Alexandre Emile John Yersin (22 September 1863 – 1 March 1943) was a Swiss-French physician and bacteriologist. He is remembered for his work as a pioneer in microbiology and as the discoverer of the bacillus responsible for the bubonic plague, which was named in his honour: Yersinia pestis.

Discovery of the plague bacillus

In 1894, during an outbreak in Hong Kong, Yersin isolated the plague bacterium from the buboes of victims, working from a small laboratory hut after being denied cooperation by the colonial authorities. He demonstrated that the same bacillus was present in rodents and argued that rats were the principal vehicle of transmission of the disease that had caused the Black Death centuries earlier.

Later career

Yersin founded the Pasteur Institute of Nha Trang in Vietnam, pioneered the cultivation of rubber and quinine trees there, and became a beloved figure known locally as "Ông Năm" (Mr. Nam). The bacterial genus Yersinia was named for him.
