Renaissance

The Renaissance was a period of European history spanning roughly the fourteenth to the seventeenth century, marking the transition from the Middle Ages to modernity. The word means "rebirth": a revival of learning based on classical sources, a new naturalism in art, and the cultural movement of humanism.

Origins

The movement began in Italy, especially Florence, where wealth from trade and banking — above all the Medici — funded artists, scholars, and architects. Thinkers such as Petrarch looked back to ancient Rome; artists from Giotto to Masaccio developed perspective and realistic depiction, culminating in Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Raphael.

Spread and impact

The printing press carried Renaissance ideas across Europe, fuelling developments in science, exploration, literature, and music, and contributing to the Reformation and the Scientific Revolution. Historians debate the period's boundaries and how sharply it broke with the medieval world, but its art, architecture, and ideals remain foundational to Western culture.
