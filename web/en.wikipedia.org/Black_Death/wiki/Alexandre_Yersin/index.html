Alexandre Yersin

Alexandre Émile Jean Yersin (22 September 1863 – 1 March 1943) was a Swiss-French physician and bacteriologist. He is remembered as the discoverer of the bacillus responsible for the bubonic plague, Yersinia pestis, which was named after him. Working in Hong Kong during the 1894 plague outbreak, Yersin isolated the bacterium from buboes at roughly the same time as Kitasato Shibasaburō, though Yersin's description is generally credited as definitive.

Career

Yersin studied medicine in Lausanne, Marburg, and Paris, and worked at the Pasteur Institute with Émile Roux on diphtheria toxin. He later joined the French colonial health service and traveled to Indochina, where he explored the interior of Vietnam and founded the Pasteur Institute of Nha Trang in 1895. There he produced plague serum and conducted wide-ranging research in microbiology, agriculture, and astronomy.

Legacy

Yersin introduced rubber trees and quinine-producing cinchona to Vietnam and founded the medical school in Hanoi. He died in Nha Trang in 1943 and remains a revered figure in Vietnam, where streets and schools bear his name. The plague bacillus genus Yersinia commemorates his discovery.
