Public health

Public health is "the science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life and promoting health through the organized efforts of society" — a classic definition by Charles-Edward Amory Winslow. Unlike clinical medicine, which treats individuals, public health focuses on whole populations.

Scope

Public health activities include surveillance of disease, vaccination programmes, sanitation and clean water, control of infectious outbreaks through contact tracing, isolation and quarantine, health education, and policies addressing the social determinants of health such as housing, nutrition, and air quality.

History

Quarantine was practised against plague in medieval ports, and sanitary reform transformed nineteenth-century cities. John Snow's investigation of the 1854 Broad Street cholera outbreak in London is a founding episode of epidemiology. The twentieth century brought national public health agencies, the World Health Organization (founded 1948), and triumphs such as the eradication of smallpox in 1980.

Challenges

Modern challenges include pandemics, antimicrobial resistance, the burden of chronic disease, health inequalities, and the health effects of climate change.
