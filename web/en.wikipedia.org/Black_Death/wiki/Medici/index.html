Medici

The House of Medici was an Italian banking family and political dynasty that rose to prominence in Florence during the early fifteenth century. Through the Medici Bank — then the largest in Europe — and their wealth, they became de facto rulers of Florence and later hereditary Dukes of Florence and Grand Dukes of Tuscany.

Rise and rule

Giovanni di Bicci founded the family fortune; his son Cosimo the Elder took control of Florence in 1434. Cosimo's grandson Lorenzo the Magnificent made Florence a centre of Renaissance culture. The family produced four popes (Leo X, Clement VII, Pius IV, and Leo XI), two queens of France (Catherine and Marie de' Medici), and ruled Tuscany until the line died out with Gian Gastone in 1737.

Patronage

The Medici were the great patrons of the Italian Renaissance, funding artists and thinkers including Brunelleschi, Botticelli, Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Galileo. Their commissions — from the dome of Florence Cathedral to the Uffizi — reshaped Western art, architecture, and science.
