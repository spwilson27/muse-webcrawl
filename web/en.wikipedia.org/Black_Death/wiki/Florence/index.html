Florence

Florence (Italian: Firenze) is the capital city of the Italian region of Tuscany and the most populous city in the region. Founded as a Roman settlement, Florence became a medieval banking and trading powerhouse and the birthplace of the Renaissance, producing figures such as Dante, Petrarch, Brunelleschi, Leonardo da Vinci, and Michelangelo. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Florence grew wealthy through the wool trade and banking families such as the Medici, who ruled the city for much of the period from the fifteenth to the eighteenth century and patronized artists including Botticelli and Michelangelo. In 1348 the Black Death struck Florence with devastating force, killing a large fraction — possibly half or more — of its population; Giovanni Boccaccio's Decameron, framed as stories told by young people fleeing the plague, is the era's most famous literary monument. Florence later served as capital of the newly unified Kingdom of Italy from 1865 to 1871.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore with Brunelleschi's dome, the Uffizi Gallery, the Ponte Vecchio, the Palazzo Vecchio, and the Basilica of Santa Croce. Florence attracts millions of tourists yearly and remains a centre for art, fashion, and scholarship.

Economy

Tourism, fashion (including houses such as Gucci and Ferragamo, both founded in Florence), wine, and manufacturing anchor the modern economy.
