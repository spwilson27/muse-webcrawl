The second plague pandemic was a major series of epidemics of plague that began with the Black Death, which reached Europe in 1346, and recurred repeatedly across Europe, the Mediterranean, and the Near East for more than four centuries.

Course

After the initial catastrophe of 1346–1353, which killed perhaps a third to a half of Europe's population, plague returned in waves roughly every decade or generation: the pestis secunda of 1361–62, severe outbreaks in Italy in 1629–31, the Great Plague of London in 1665–66, the Great Northern War plague (1702–1714), and the last major western European outbreak at Marseille in 1720. Plague persisted longer in the Ottoman world and eastern Europe, with outbreaks into the early 19th century.

Responses and effects

The recurring epidemics prompted the first systematic public-health measures, including quarantine (pioneered at Ragusa in 1377), lazarettos, health boards, and bills of mortality. The pandemic's demographic and social shocks are credited with reshaping labor relations, religious life, art, and urban governance across Europe.
