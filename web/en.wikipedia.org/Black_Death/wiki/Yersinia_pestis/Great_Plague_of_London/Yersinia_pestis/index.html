Yersinia pestis

GenusYersinia
SpeciesY. pestis
DiseasesBubonic, septicemic and pneumonic plague

Yersinia pestis is the causative agent of plague. During the Great Plague of London (1665–1666), the last great plague epidemic in England, the bacterium spread through the flea-infested rat population of the crowded city, killing roughly a quarter of London's inhabitants.

Evidence

In 2016, researchers analysing DNA from teeth of victims buried in a plague pit at Liverpool Street in London confirmed the presence of Y. pestis, settling the question of whether the Great Plague was caused by the same organism as the Black Death three centuries earlier.

Transmission cycle

The enzootic cycle maintains the bacterium among wild rodents and fleas; when infected rats die, their fleas seek new hosts, including humans, driving epizootic outbreaks that can spill over into urban epidemics.

Treatment

Streptomycin, gentamicin, and doxycycline are effective when given early. There is no widely available modern vaccine.
