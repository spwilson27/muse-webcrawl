Great Plague of London

DiseaseBubonic plague
Dates1665–1666
Deaths~100,000 (est.)
BacteriumYersinia pestis

The Great Plague of London, lasting from 1665 to 1666, was the last major epidemic of the bubonic plague to occur in England. It happened within the centuries-long Second plague pandemic, a period of intermittent bubonic plague epidemics that originated in China in the 1330s and included the Black Death of 1348.

The plague killed an estimated 100,000 people — almost a quarter of London's population — in 18 months. Samuel Pepys recorded the progress of the epidemic in his diary with mounting alarm; Daniel Defoe later gave a semi-fictional account in A Journal of the Plague Year (1722).

Course of the epidemic

Weekly Bills of Mortality tracked the toll. At the peak in September 1665, more than 7,000 Londoners died in a single week. King Charles II and his court fled to Oxford, theatres and public gatherings were closed, and the dead were buried in plague pits.

Aftermath

The epidemic subsided during 1666, the year of the Great Fire of London, which destroyed much of the medieval city within the walls. Although popular tradition credits the fire with ending the plague, the outbreak had already begun to decline.
