Black Death

The Black Death (1346–1353) was the catastrophic outbreak of plague caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, the second and greatest pandemic of plague after the Plague of Justinian. DNA recovered from plague pits has confirmed Y. pestis as the responsible pathogen.

The pathogen's journey

Yersinia pestis circulates among wild rodents in Central Asia. Carried by fleas along caravan and shipping routes — notably through the siege of Caffa and Genoese trading ports — it reached the Mediterranean in 1347 and within six years had spread across Europe, the Middle East, and North Africa, exploiting ships, armies, and trade networks.

Mortality and aftermath

Estimates suggest 75–200 million deaths; Europe may have lost a third to three-fifths of its people. Recurrences of plague, sustained by reservoirs the original pandemic established, struck Europe repeatedly until the eighteenth century and reached London in the Great Plague of 1665–66.
