Yersinia pestis

GenusYersinia
SpeciesY. pestis
ShapeCoccobacillus
Identified1894

Yersinia pestis is the bacterium that causes plague, including the bubonic plague of the Black Death and the Great Plague of London. It is transmitted to humans primarily through the bite of an infected flea, most famously the Oriental rat flea (Xenopsylla cheopis), which feeds on rodents.

Identification

The organism was identified in Hong Kong in 1894 by Alexandre Yersin of the Pasteur Institute. It had evolved relatively recently from the enteric pathogen Yersinia pseudotuberculosis, acquiring plasmids that enable its flea-borne lifestyle.

Virulence

Y. pestis blocks the flea's gut, causing the starved insect to bite repeatedly and regurgitate bacteria into new hosts. In humans it evades the immune system and multiplies in the lymph nodes, producing the characteristic buboes of bubonic plague. Ancient DNA studies of plague victims have confirmed its role in pandemics spanning more than a thousand years.

Modern significance

Plague is now treatable with antibiotics, but Y. pestis persists in rodent reservoirs on every continent except Australia and Antarctica, and it is listed as a potential biological weapon agent.
