Great Plague of London

BacteriumYersinia pestis
LocationLondon, England
Dates1665–1666
Deaths~100,000

The Great Plague of London (1665–1666) was the final major outbreak of plague in the British Isles, part of the long second plague pandemic that began with the Black Death. The epidemic was caused by Yersinia pestis, transmitted by fleas living on black rats in the teeming, unsanitary streets of the capital.

Course

The first cases appeared in the parish of St Giles-in-the-Fields in late 1664. By the summer of 1665 the epidemic was raging; the royal court, Parliament, and anyone who could afford to flee left the city. Watchmen shut up infected houses, marking them with a red cross and the words "Lord have mercy upon us".

Records

The diarist Samuel Pepys and the later account by Daniel Defoe provide the best-known descriptions. Official Bills of Mortality put the death toll at 68,596, but the true figure is estimated near 100,000.

Decline

Cases fell during the winter of 1665–66. The Great Fire of September 1666 destroyed the most plague-ridden slums, though historians debate how much this contributed to the epidemic's end.
