Second plague pandemic

The second plague pandemic was a major series of epidemics of plague caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, beginning with the Black Death, which reached Europe, West Asia, and North Africa in the 1340s, and recurring in waves over the following four centuries. It followed the first pandemic, the Plague of Justinian (541–549), and preceded the third pandemic, which began in China in the nineteenth century.

The Black Death

The pandemic's first and deadliest wave swept from Central Asia along trade routes to the Black Sea and Mediterranean by 1347, then across Europe, killing an estimated 30 to 60 percent of Europe's population and tens of millions more across Asia and Africa. Ancient DNA confirms Y. pestis in Black Death burials, and recent research points to the region around Lake Issyk-Kul in modern Kyrgyzstan as a source of the strain that spread westward.

Recurrences

Plague returned repeatedly — roughly every decade in some regions — including the Great Plague of London (1665–66), the Great Plague of Marseille (1720–22), and outbreaks in the Ottoman Empire and Russia into the nineteenth century. The long tail of the pandemic reshaped European demography, labour relations, medicine, and public health, giving rise to institutions such as quarantine and permanent health boards.
