Plague (disease)

CauseYersinia pestis
TransmissionFlea bites, contact, droplets
FormsBubonic, septicemic, pneumonic
TreatmentAntibiotics

Plague is an infectious disease caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis. Symptoms include fever, headache, vomiting, and swollen, painful lymph nodes (buboes). There are three forms of plague: bubonic, septicemic, and pneumonic, depending on the route of infection and the part of the body involved.

Plague is typically transmitted between animals and humans by the bite of infected fleas, direct contact with infected tissue, or inhalation of infective respiratory droplets. It has been responsible for three great pandemics: the Plague of Justinian in the 6th century, the Black Death in the 14th century, and the third plague pandemic beginning in 19th-century China.

Signs and symptoms

Bubonic plague is characterized by the sudden onset of fever and the appearance of buboes in the groin, armpits, or neck. Septicemic plague occurs when the bacteria enter the bloodstream, and pneumonic plague results from infection of the lungs and can spread directly between people.

Prevention and treatment

Early treatment with antibiotics such as streptomycin and gentamicin is effective. Control of rodent populations and flea vectors remains the mainstay of prevention in endemic areas.
