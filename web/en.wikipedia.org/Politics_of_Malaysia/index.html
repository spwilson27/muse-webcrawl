Politics of Malaysia

SystemFederal parliamentary constitutional elective monarchy
Head of stateYang di-Pertuan Agong (King), elected every five years
Head of governmentPrime Minister
LegislatureParliament (Dewan Rakyat and Dewan Negara)

The politics of Malaysia takes place in the framework of a federal representative democratic constitutional monarchy, in which the Yang di-Pertuan Agong is head of state and the Prime Minister of Malaysia is the head of government. Uniquely, the monarch is elected for a five-year term by and among the hereditary rulers of nine Malay states.

Political history

The Alliance coalition, later expanded as Barisan Nasional (BN) and dominated by the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO), governed from independence in 1957 until 2018. Politics was long structured by ethnic coalitions representing Malays, Chinese, and Indians, and by affirmative-action policies such as the New Economic Policy favouring bumiputera (Malays and other indigenous groups).

Change of government

The 2018 general election brought the first change of government in Malaysian history, when the Pakatan Harapan coalition led by Mahathir Mohamad defeated BN amid the 1MDB scandal. The government's collapse in the 2020 "Sheraton Move" ushered in a period of instability and coalition governments; after the hung parliament of 2022, Anwar Ibrahim formed a unity government.

Institutions

Malaysia's thirteen states retain their own governments; Sabah and Sarawak enjoy special autonomy under the Malaysia Agreement 1963. The judiciary, headed by the Federal Court, was substantially stripped of power by a 1988 constitutional amendment, a subject of continuing reform debate.
