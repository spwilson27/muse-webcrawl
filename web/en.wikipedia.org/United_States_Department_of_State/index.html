United States Department of State

The United States Department of State (DOS) is an executive department of the U.S. federal government responsible for the country's foreign policy and relations. Equivalent to the ministry of foreign affairs of other nations, its primary duties are advising the U.S. president on international relations, administering diplomatic missions, negotiating international treaties and agreements, and representing the U.S. at the United Nations.

History

The Department of State was the first federal executive department established under the new Constitution. Congress created the Department of Foreign Affairs on July 21, 1789, and President George Washington signed it into law on July 27. In September 1789 the name was changed to the Department of State and it was given additional domestic duties, including custody of the Great Seal of the United States and responsibility for the census and the mint. Most of its domestic functions were gradually transferred to other departments during the 19th century. Thomas Jefferson was the first secretary of state.

Duties

The department operates the diplomatic missions of the United States abroad and implements the foreign policy of the United States. It issues passports to American citizens, provides services to Americans traveling and living abroad, protects American commercial interests overseas, and manages the U.S. Foreign Service. The secretary of state, appointed by the president with the advice and consent of the Senate, is the president's chief foreign affairs adviser and the highest-ranking member of the Cabinet.
