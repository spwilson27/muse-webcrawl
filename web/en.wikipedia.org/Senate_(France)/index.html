Senate (France)

The Senate (French: Sénat) is the upper house of the French Parliament, which also includes the National Assembly, the lower house. Together with the National Assembly, it constitutes the legislature of France. The Senate is seated in the Luxembourg Palace in Paris.

Composition

As of the most recent elections, the Senate has 348 members (senators). Senators are elected indirectly by an electoral college of roughly 150,000 "grand electors" (grands électeurs), consisting mainly of local elected officials such as mayors, regional councillors, and deputies. Senators serve six-year terms, with half of the seats renewed every three years. Twelve senators represent French citizens living abroad.

Role and powers

The Senate examines, debates, and votes on legislation. Most bills must be passed in identical terms by both houses to become law, though in case of persistent disagreement the National Assembly has the final say, except for constitutional revisions and certain organic laws, for which the Senate holds a stronger position. The Senate also represents France's territorial communities (communes, departments, and regions), a role enshrined in the Constitution.

The President of the Senate is second in the order of precedence of the French state and serves as acting President of the Republic if the presidency falls vacant.

History

An upper house has existed in France under various forms since 1795, including the Council of Ancients, the Conservative Senate of Napoleon, and the Chamber of Peers. The modern Senate dates from the founding of the Fifth Republic in 1958, succeeding the Council of the Republic of the Fourth Republic.
