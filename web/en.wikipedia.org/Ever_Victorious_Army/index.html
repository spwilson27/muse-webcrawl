Ever Victorious Army

The Ever Victorious Army (Chinese: 常勝軍, cháng shèng jūn) was a small army that fought for the Qing dynasty during the Taiping Rebellion (1850–1864). It was commanded, in its famous final form, by Charles George Gordon, the British officer who became known as "Chinese Gordon". Although numerically small—rarely more than about 4,000–5,000 men—it was one of the most effective forces in the suppression of the rebellion around Shanghai.

History

The army was founded in 1860 by the American adventurer Frederick Townsend Ward, who raised and drilled Chinese soldiers with Western weapons, financed by the Shanghai merchants threatened by the Taiping. After Ward's death in battle in September 1862, command passed briefly to Henry Andres Burgevine, whose dismissal followed a dispute with Qing officials.

Gordon's command

In March 1863 the British officer Charles George Gordon took command. Gordon reorganised the force, enforced discipline, and led it in a series of successful campaigns, including the capture of Suzhou (Soochow) in December 1863 and Changzhou in May 1864. His successes earned him the nickname "Chinese Gordon" and made him a celebrity in Victorian Britain.

Disbandment

The army was disbanded in May 1864, shortly before the fall of Nanjing ended the Taiping Rebellion. Gordon returned to Britain as a national hero; he later served as Governor-General of the Sudan and was killed at Khartoum in 1885.
