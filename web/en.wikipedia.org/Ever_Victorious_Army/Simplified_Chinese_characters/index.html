Simplified Chinese characters

Simplified Chinese characters (简化字; jiǎnhuàzì) are standardized Chinese characters prescribed by the government of the People's Republic of China for use in mainland China, and are also official in Singapore and Malaysia. They were developed beginning in the 1950s as part of a programme to increase literacy by reducing the number of strokes in commonly used characters.

History

Proposals to simplify characters were discussed in the early twentieth century, and the Republic of China published a first list of 324 simplified characters in 1935, later withdrawn. The People's Republic promulgated the Chinese Character Simplification Scheme in 1956, followed by a General List of Simplified Characters in 1964 (revised 1986), covering about 2,200 characters. A second-round simplification introduced in 1977 proved unpopular and was withdrawn in 1986.

Methods of simplification

Simplification draws on longstanding cursive and shorthand forms, reduces stroke counts, replaces complex components with simpler ones, and merges some distinct traditional characters into one simplified character. Well-known examples:

Usage and debate

Traditional characters remain standard in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau. Supporters argue simplification raised literacy; critics argue it severs readers from classical texts and merges characters in ways that create ambiguity. Most native readers can recognize the other set with modest exposure. See also Radical (Chinese characters).
