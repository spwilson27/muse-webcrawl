Puyi (7 February 1906 – 17 October 1967) was the last emperor of China, reigning as the Xuantong Emperor of the Qing dynasty from 1908 until his abdication in 1912. Chosen as a toddler by the dying Empress Dowager Cixi, he was restored briefly to the throne in 1917 during the warlord Zhang Xun's monarchist coup, and later became the chief executive and then "emperor" (Kangde) of the Japanese puppet state of Manchukuo from 1932/1934 until 1945.

Early life and first reign

Puyi was the eldest son of Zaifeng, Prince Chun. After the death of the Guangxu Emperor in November 1908, the two-year-old Puyi was taken into the Forbidden City and crowned emperor. His father served as regent. Following the Xinhai Revolution of 1911, the imperial court negotiated terms of abdication, and on 12 February 1912 the boy emperor formally abdicated, ending over two thousand years of imperial rule in China. Under the Articles of Favourable Treatment he retained his title and residence in the Forbidden City.

Restoration and expulsion

In July 1917 the warlord Zhang Xun restored Puyi to the throne for twelve days before republican forces overthrew the restoration. In 1924 the warlord Feng Yuxiang expelled Puyi from the Forbidden City, and he took refuge in the Japanese concession in Tianjin, where he lived under Japanese protection and plotted a restoration.

Manchukuo

After Japan's invasion of Manchuria in 1931, Puyi agreed to head the new state of Manchukuo, becoming Chief Executive in 1932 and Emperor Kangde in 1934. He later admitted he had little real power and served as a figurehead for Japanese rule. He was captured by Soviet forces in August 1945 and held in Siberia.

Later life

Extradited to the People's Republic of China in 1950, Puyi spent nearly a decade in the Fushun War Criminals Management Centre undergoing re-education. Pardoned in 1959, he worked as a gardener at the Beijing Botanical Garden and later as a researcher, and wrote an autobiography, From Emperor to Citizen. He died of cancer in Beijing in 1967.
