Radical (Chinese characters)

A radical (Chinese: 部首; pinyin: bùshǒu; literally "section header") is a graphical component of a Chinese character under which the character is traditionally listed in a dictionary. Radicals usually serve as, or resemble, a semantic component of the character, giving a hint to its general category of meaning, while the remainder of the character often supplies a phonetic hint to its pronunciation.

History

The system originates with the dictionary Shuowen Jiezi (c. 100 CE) compiled by Xu Shen, which classified characters under 540 section headers. The number was gradually reduced by later lexicographers. The Kangxi Dictionary of 1716 standardized a set of 214 radicals, still widely used today in traditional-character dictionaries and in the Unihan database. Modern mainland Chinese dictionaries use a revised set of 201 principal components.

Function

To look up a character, one identifies its radical, counts the additional strokes in the remainder of the character, and searches the dictionary section for that radical ordered by residual stroke count. For example, the character 湖 ("lake") is indexed under the water radical 氵(水) with nine additional strokes.

Position and variants

Radicals may appear in different positions within a character — left, right, top, bottom, or enclosing — and many have variant forms depending on position, such as 人 appearing as 亻on the left, or 水 appearing as 氵. See also Simplified Chinese characters.
