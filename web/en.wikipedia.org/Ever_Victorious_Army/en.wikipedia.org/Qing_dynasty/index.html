The Qing dynasty, officially the Great Qing, was a Manchu-led imperial dynasty of China and the last imperial dynasty in Chinese history. Founded by the Jurchen Aisin-Gioro clan in Manchuria, it entered Beijing in 1644 as the Ming dynasty collapsed and ruled China proper for nearly three centuries, until the abdication of the last emperor, Puyi, in 1912 following the Xinhai Revolution.

Expansion

Under the Kangxi, Yongzheng and Qianlong emperors the dynasty reached its apex, conquering Taiwan, Tibet, Xinjiang and Mongolia and roughly tripling the territory controlled by the Ming. China's population grew to about 400 million by 1850, and the empire was one of the largest in world history.

Decline

The 19th century brought the Opium Wars with Britain and France, unequal treaties, and massive internal rebellions, most destructively the Taiping Rebellion (1850–1864), which the dynasty suppressed with regional armies and foreign-officered forces such as the Ever Victorious Army. Self-strengthening reforms failed to match Japan's modernisation; defeat in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) and the Boxer Rebellion (1899–1901) deepened the crisis.

Fall

The Xinhai Revolution of 1911 led to the abdication of Puyi on 12 February 1912, ending over two thousand years of imperial rule in China.
