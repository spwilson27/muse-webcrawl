The Jacobite rising of 1745, known in Scotland as "the Forty-five", was an attempt by Charles Edward Stuart — often called Bonnie Prince Charlie — to restore his father, James Francis Edward Stuart, to the British throne. It was the last of the Jacobite risings.

The rising

Charles landed in the Scottish Highlands in July 1745 with minimal French support and raised his father's standard at Glenfinnan on 19 August. Gathering Highland clan support, the Jacobites captured Edinburgh and defeated a government army at the Battle of Prestonpans in September. In November they marched into England, reaching Derby on 4 December — a high point that caused panic in London. With promised English Jacobite and French support failing to materialize, the Jacobite council voted to retreat to Scotland.

Culloden and aftermath

Pursued by the Duke of Cumberland's army, the Jacobites made a final stand at the Battle of Culloden on 16 April 1746, where they were decisively defeated in the last pitched battle fought on British soil. Charles escaped to France after months as a fugitive in the Highlands. The British government responded with harsh repression: the clan system was dismantled, Highland dress and weapons were proscribed, and heritable jurisdictions abolished, ending the Jacobite threat permanently.
