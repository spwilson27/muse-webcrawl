Old Livonia

Old Livonia (German: Alt-Livland; Latin: Terra Mariana, "Land of Mary") was a confederation of states in the eastern Baltic region, roughly covering modern Estonia and Latvia, from the early thirteenth century until its collapse during the Livonian War in 1561. It arose from the Northern Crusades, in which German crusaders, led by Bishop Albert of Riga and the Livonian Brothers of the Sword, conquered and Christianized the lands of the Livs, Latgalians, Estonians, and other Baltic and Finnic peoples.

Structure

The confederation comprised five ecclesiastical and military states: the Livonian branch of the Teutonic Order, the Archbishopric of Riga, and the bishoprics of Dorpat, Ösel–Wiek, and Courland. Power was contested for centuries between the Order, the bishops, and the rising Hanseatic city of Riga. The indigenous population was reduced to serfdom under a German-speaking landowning aristocracy whose descendants, the Baltic Germans, dominated the region into the twentieth century.

Collapse

Overextended and divided, Old Livonia disintegrated when Ivan IV of Russia invaded in 1558, opening the Livonian War. By 1561 the southern lands had submitted to Poland–Lithuania as the Duchy of Courland and Semigallia and the Duchy of Livonia, while northern Estonia went to Sweden and Ösel to Denmark.
