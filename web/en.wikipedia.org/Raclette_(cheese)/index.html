Raclette is a semi-hard cheese made from cow's milk, originating in the Swiss canton of Valais and also produced in the French regions of Savoie and Haute-Savoie. It is best known for the dish of the same name, in which the cut surface of the cheese is heated and the melting layer is scraped (from the French racler, "to scrape") onto boiled potatoes.

History

Cheese melting by fire in the Valais is documented as early as the medieval period, when herdsmen would place a wheel of cheese near an open fire and scrape off the softened surface. Raclette du Valais received protected designation of origin (AOP) status in Switzerland in 2003. French Raclette de Savoie received European PGI protection in 2017.

Production

Raclette is made from raw or pasteurised cow's milk formed into wheels of roughly 6 kg. The wheels are brined and aged for three to six months, developing a brownish-orange washed rind and a pale ivory paste with few small holes. The cheese melts evenly without becoming stringy or oily, a property prized in the traditional dish.

The dish

Traditional raclette is served with boiled potatoes in their skins, cornichons, pickled onions, and sometimes cured meats and a crisp white wine such as Fendant. Modern tabletop raclette grills allow diners to melt individual portions in small pans called coupelles.
