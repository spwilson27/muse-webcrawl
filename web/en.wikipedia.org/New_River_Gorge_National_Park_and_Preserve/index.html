New River Gorge National Park and Preserve is an American national park and preserve in southern West Virginia, protecting 53 miles (85 km) of the New River and its rugged sandstone gorge. Established as a National River in 1978, it was redesignated as the 63rd national park by the Consolidated Appropriations Act signed on 27 December 2020, making it the newest American national park and the first in West Virginia. About 65,000 acres are managed as a national park and roughly 7,000 as a preserve where hunting is permitted.

Features and recreation

The park's centrepiece is the New River Gorge Bridge (1977), a 3,030-foot steel arch bridge carrying US 19 some 876 feet above the river; each October, Bridge Day permits BASE jumping and rappelling from it. The New River — ironically among the oldest rivers in North America geologically — offers premier whitewater rafting, especially on the Lower New. More than 1,400 established rock-climbing routes on hard Nuttall sandstone cliffs, extensive hiking and mountain-biking trails, and historic coal towns such as Thurmond draw over a million visitors annually.

Ecology and history

The gorge's mixed mesophytic forest is among the most biodiverse in the Appalachians, harbouring rare species including the Virginia big-eared bat and Allegheny woodrat. The valley was a major coal-mining and railroad corridor from the 1870s; abandoned mines, coke ovens, and company towns survive as historic sites within the park.
