Battle of Grandson

The Battle of Grandson, fought on 2 March 1476 during the Burgundian Wars, was a major victory of the Old Swiss Confederacy over Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy. Though relatively few men were killed in the battle itself, the Burgundian army abandoned its entire artillery train and Charles's magnificent camp treasure to the Swiss.

Background

Charles the Bold, seeking to expand his domains, invaded Swiss-allied territory and captured the town of Grandson on Lake Neuchâtel, hanging its Swiss garrison of 412 men in defiance of the laws of war. The Swiss Confederacy raised an army of some 18,000 men to relieve the place and punish the outrage.

The battle

On 2 March the Swiss advance guard emerged from the woods near Concise into view of the Burgundian army. Charles manoeuvred his mounted men-at-arms and artillery to engage, but a repositioning of his forces was mistaken for a retreat; as the main Swiss battle divisions came into view, panic spread through the Burgundian ranks and the whole army fled, abandoning over 400 guns — then the finest artillery park in Europe — and the ducal treasury, jewels and tapestries.

Aftermath

The booty of Grandson became legendary, though much of it was sold cheaply by soldiers unaware of its value. The defeat humiliated Charles but did not end the war: he rebuilt an army and was beaten again at Morat three months later and killed at Nancy in January 1477.
