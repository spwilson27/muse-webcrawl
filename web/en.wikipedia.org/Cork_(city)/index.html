Cork (city)

Cork (Irish: Corcaigh, from corcach, meaning "marsh") is a city in southwestern Ireland, in the province of Munster. It is the second-largest city in the Republic of Ireland and the third-largest city on the island of Ireland, after Dublin and Belfast. The city is built on the River Lee, which splits into two channels at the western end of the city; the city centre is formed on an island between these channels.

History

Cork was originally a monastic settlement founded by Saint Finbarr in the 6th century. It became a trading port under Viking occupation in the 10th century, and its city charter was granted by King John in 1185. During the Irish War of Independence, the city centre was burned by British forces in an event known as the "Burning of Cork" in December 1920, and Cork was the scene of intense fighting during the Irish Civil War.

Geography

The River Lee flows through the city, and Cork Harbour, on the coast, is one of the largest natural harbours in the world. The city centre sits on an island in the river between the two main channels. Notable suburbs include Douglas, Blackrock, Ballincollig and Glanmire.

Culture

Cork was the European Capital of Culture in 2005. The city is known for the English Market, a covered food market dating to 1788, the Shandon Bells at St Anne's Church, and Cork Opera House. University College Cork, founded in 1845, is a major institution of the city.

Economy

The Cork region hosts many pharmaceutical and technology companies, with Apple operating its European headquarters in the city. Cork Airport serves the region, and the Port of Cork handles both freight and ferry traffic.
