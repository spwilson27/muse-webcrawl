Fandom

A fandom is a subculture of fans sharing a common interest in a work, franchise, celebrity or hobby.

A fandom is a subculture composed of fans characterised by a feeling of empathy and camaraderie with others who share a common interest. Fans typically are interested in even minor details of the object(s) of their fandom and spend a significant portion of their time and energy involved with it — often as part of a network of like-minded fans, distinguishing fandom from those with only a casual interest.

Activities

Common fandom activities include attending fan conventions, publishing fanzines and webzines, writing fan fiction, producing fan art and fan films, collecting memorabilia, cosplay, and participating in online communities and forums. Some fandoms develop their own terminology, rituals and creative conventions.

History

Organised science fiction fandom dates to the 1930s, with fanzines and the first World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) in 1939. Media fandom expanded with Star Trek in the 1960s–70s, aided by fanzine networks largely run by women. The internet from the 1990s onward transformed fandom, enabling instant global communities, archives such as Archive of Our Own, and direct interaction with creators.

Notable fandoms

Large, named fandoms include Trekkies (Star Trek), Whovians (Doctor Who), Potterheads (Harry Potter), the Brony fandom (My Little Pony), sports supporters' groups, and music fandoms such as K-pop fan communities.

Scholarship

Fan studies emerged as an academic field with works such as Henry Jenkins's Textual Poachers (1992), which reframed fans as active, creative participants ("participatory culture") rather than passive consumers.
