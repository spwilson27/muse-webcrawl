Hadewijch

Hadewijch (sometimes referred to as Hadewijch of Brabant or Hadewijch of Antwerp) was a 13th-century poet and mystic from the Low Countries, probably from the Duchy of Brabant. She is one of the earliest known authors writing in Middle Dutch and a central figure of the northern European tradition of bridal mysticism.

Life and works

Almost nothing certain is known of her life; she probably lived in the first half of the 13th century and was associated with the Beguine movement — communities of religious women who lived devout lives without formal monastic vows. Her surviving writings comprise the Book of Visions (Visioenen), forty-five stanzaic poems (strofische gedichten), and a collection of letters, all centred on the experience of Minne — Love, conceived as both God and the soul's consuming longing for union with God.

Legacy

Hadewijch's daring, courtly, and often paradoxical language influenced later mystics including Jan van Ruusbroec and Meister Eckhart's tradition. Rediscovered by scholars in the 19th century, she is now regarded as one of the greatest writers of medieval Dutch literature and a major voice in the history of Christian mysticism.
