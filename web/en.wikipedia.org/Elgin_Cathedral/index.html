Elgin Cathedral

Elgin Cathedral is a historic ruin in Elgin, Moray, north-east Scotland. The cathedral, dedicated to the Holy Trinity, was established in 1224 and was the seat of the bishops of Moray. It is sometimes known as the "Lantern of the North".

History

The cathedral was founded on land granted by King Alexander II. It suffered devastating fires, most famously in 1390 when Alexander Stewart, the "Wolf of Badenoch", burned it after being excommunicated by the bishop. It was rebuilt each time, gaining an octagonal chapter house and a grand west front flanked by two towers.

Decline

After the Scottish Reformation of 1560 the cathedral fell out of use; its lead roof was stripped in 1567–68, and the central tower collapsed in 1711. The ruins were cleared and conserved in the 19th century, an effort led by the shoemaker and antiquarian John Shanks.

Today

The remains include the twin-towered west front, the chapter house, and many carved monuments and effigies of bishops. The site is cared for by Historic Environment Scotland and is open to visitors.
