1959 Tibetan uprising

Date10–23 March 1959
LocationLhasa, Tibet
ResultUprising suppressed; the 14th Dalai Lama fled to India

The 1959 Tibetan uprising began on 10 March 1959, when a revolt erupted in Lhasa, the capital of Tibet, amid fears for the safety of the 14th Dalai Lama and growing resistance to the rule of the People's Republic of China, which had controlled the region since the Seventeen Point Agreement of 1951.

Events

Tens of thousands of Tibetans surrounded the Dalai Lama's Potala Palace to protect him after rumours spread about a Chinese plan to arrest him. Fighting broke out between Tibetan rebels and the People's Liberation Army, and on 17 March the Dalai Lama escaped from Lhasa, reaching exile in India at the end of the month.

Aftermath

The uprising was suppressed by 23 March. The Tibetan government was dissolved, and tens of thousands of Tibetans followed the Dalai Lama into exile, principally in India, where a government-in-exile was established at Dharamshala.
