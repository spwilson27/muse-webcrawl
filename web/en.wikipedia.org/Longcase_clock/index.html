Longcase clock

A longcase clock, also known as a grandfather clock (or floor clock), is a tall, freestanding, weight-driven pendulum clock whose pendulum is held inside the tower or trunk of the case. Typically 1.8–2.3 metres tall, it has an hour hand, a minute hand, and often seconds and date displays on an ornate dial.

History

The type emerged around 1660–1670 after the application of the pendulum (1656, by Christiaan Huygens) and the anchor escapement (c. 1670), which allowed long pendulums — often a seconds pendulum about one metre long — beating once per second, with great accuracy for the era. Longcases housed the long pendulum and weights protectively.

Name and culture

The name “grandfather clock” was popularised by Henry Clay Work's 1876 song “My Grandfather's Clock”, about a clock at the George Hotel in Piercebridge, Yorkshire, said to have stopped when its owner died.
