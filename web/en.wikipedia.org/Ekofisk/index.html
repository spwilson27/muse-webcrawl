Ekofisk is an oil field in block 2/4 of the Norwegian sector of the North Sea, about 320 kilometres southwest of Stavanger. Discovered in 1969, it was the first major oil discovery in Norwegian waters and launched Norway's petroleum era. The field is operated by ConocoPhillips on behalf of the Petoro-led licence group.

History

Phillips Petroleum discovered Ekofisk in late 1969, weeks after finding the smaller Cod field; production began in June 1971, initially loaded onto tankers before pipelines to Teesside (oil) and Emden (gas) opened in the mid-1970s. The reservoir is fractured Upper Cretaceous and Danian chalk, which initially gave very high well rates.

Subsidence and redevelopment

Pressure depletion compacted the chalk reservoir, causing the seabed above the field to subside by several metres. In 1987 the platforms were jacked up by about six metres in a famous engineering operation, and water injection — later expanded into a major voidage-replacement programme — slowed the subsidence and greatly improved recovery. Successive redevelopments, including Ekofisk II (1998) and new platforms such as Ekofisk Z and the Eldfisk II project, are expected to extend production toward 2050.

Incidents

In April 1977 the Bravo platform on the field suffered a blowout that released an estimated 80,000–126,000 barrels of oil over eight days — Norway's largest oil spill and a landmark in offshore safety regulation. In 1980 the Alexander L. Kielland flotel capsized near the field, killing 123 people.
