Kaveri

The Kaveri (also spelled Cauvery) is a major river in southern India. Rising at Talakaveri in the Brahmagiri Range of the Western Ghats in Karnataka, it flows about 800 kilometres (500 mi) southeast through Karnataka and Tamil Nadu to the Bay of Bengal at Poompuhar. It is one of the seven sacred rivers of Hinduism.

Course

The Kaveri flows through the Deccan Plateau, forming the Shivanasamudra Falls and the island towns of Srirangapatna and Sivasamudram. At Hogenakkal Falls it enters Tamil Nadu, where it passes the Mettur Dam (Stanley Reservoir), Erode, Tiruchirappalli, and Thanjavur before splitting into a broad delta. Major tributaries include the Hemavati, Arkavati, Shimsha, Kabini, Bhavani, Noyyal, and Amaravati.

Irrigation and power

The river supports extensive agriculture, especially the rice-growing Kaveri delta, and major dams include the Krishna Raja Sagara (KRS) in Karnataka and Mettur in Tamil Nadu. The falls at Shivanasamudra powered one of Asia's earliest hydroelectric stations (1902), supplying the Kolar Gold Fields and Bengaluru.

Dispute

Sharing of the Kaveri's waters between Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Puducherry has been disputed since the Madras–Mysore agreements of 1892 and 1924. The Kaveri Water Disputes Tribunal gave its final award in 2007, modified by the Supreme Court of India in 2018, and the Cauvery Water Management Authority now oversees implementation.
