Google Maps

Google Maps is a web mapping platform and consumer application offered by Google. It provides satellite imagery, aerial photography, street maps, 360° interactive panoramic views of streets (Street View), real-time traffic conditions, and route planning for travelling by foot, car, bicycle, air, and public transport. Launched in February 2005, it became one of the world's most widely used mapping services, with over a billion monthly users.

History

Google Maps began as a C++ desktop program developed by brothers Lars and Jens Rasmussen at the Sydney-based company Where 2 Technologies. Google acquired the company in October 2004 and converted the application into a web application, launching Google Maps on February 8, 2005. The service pioneered the draggable, tiled "slippy map" interface, and its unofficially reverse-engineered API sparked a wave of map "mashups" before an official API was released in June 2005.

Features

The service combines road maps and satellite/aerial imagery that users can toggle between. Street View, launched in 2007, provides ground-level panoramic photography collected by camera-equipped cars, trekkers, and boats. Directions include live traffic overlays and estimated arrival times; public-transit directions are offered in thousands of cities. Business listings drawn from Google Business Profiles provide hours, reviews, and photographs. Offline maps, indoor maps of airports and malls, and augmented-reality walking navigation ("Live View") were added in later years.

Data and coverage

Google sources map data from a combination of its own collection, government and commercial providers, and user contributions through programs such as Local Guides. Map data for some regions, notably China, is provided by local partners under licensing requirements. The service has been criticised over privacy in Street View imagery, depiction of disputed international borders (which vary by the viewer's country), and the business practices around its API pricing.

Related products

Google Earth, launched in 2005 from the acquisition of Keyhole, offers a three-dimensional globe. Waze, acquired by Google in 2013, operates as a complementary crowdsourced navigation app. In 2018 Google consolidated its mapping APIs into the Google Maps Platform for commercial developers.
