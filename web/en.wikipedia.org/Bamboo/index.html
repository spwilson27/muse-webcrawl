Bamboos are a diverse group of evergreen perennial flowering plants in the subfamily Bambusoideae of the grass family Poaceae. Giant bamboos are the largest members of the grass family, with some species growing more than a metre per day and reaching over 30 metres in height.

Description

Bamboo stems, called culms, are typically hollow and grow from rhizomes either in clumps or as running runners. Many species flower only at long intervals of decades, with synchronous mass flowering over wide areas followed by the death of the parent plants.

Uses

Bamboo is used as a building material and scaffolding, for furniture, textiles, paper, musical instruments and as food—both bamboo shoots for humans and foliage for animals, famously the giant panda, whose diet is almost entirely bamboo. In East and Southeast Asia bamboo holds deep cultural significance symbolizing resilience and longevity.

Ecology

Bamboo forests support specialized species and sequester carbon rapidly, though running species can become invasive outside their native ranges.
