VO2 max

Measuring oxygen consumption during graded exercise.

V̇O2 max (also maximal oxygen consumption, maximal oxygen uptake or maximal aerobic capacity) is the maximum rate of oxygen consumption attainable during physical exertion. It is measured in millilitres of oxygen used per kilogram of body weight per minute (mL/(kg·min)) and is regarded as the best single indicator of cardiorespiratory fitness and aerobic endurance.

Measurement

The classic measurement uses a graded exercise test on a treadmill or cycle ergometer while expired air is analysed; VO2 max is reached when oxygen consumption plateaus despite increasing workload. Submaximal field tests, such as the multi-stage fitness ("beep") test and Cooper test, estimate the value without laboratory equipment.

Values and determinants

Untrained young adults typically record values around 35–45 mL/(kg·min), while elite endurance athletes may exceed 70; the highest values, above 90, have been recorded in elite cross-country skiers and cyclists. VO2 max depends on cardiac output, blood oxygen-carrying capacity, and muscular oxygen extraction; it declines with age but responds substantially to endurance training, typically improving 15–20 percent in sedentary people.
