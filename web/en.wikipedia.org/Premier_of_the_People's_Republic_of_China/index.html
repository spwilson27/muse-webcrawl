Premier of the People's Republic of China

Premier of the State Council of the People's Republic of China
Incumbent: Li Qiang (since 11 March 2023)
Appointer: President, with approval of the National People's Congress
Term: Five years, renewable once
First holder: Zhou Enlai (1949)

The premier heads the State Council, China's chief administrative authority.

The premier of the People's Republic of China, officially the Premier of the State Council, is the head of government of China and the leader of the State Council, the country's chief administrative authority. The premier is nominated by the president and confirmed by the National People's Congress, serves a five-year term renewable once, and is typically ranked second or third in the hierarchy of the Chinese Communist Party, sitting on the Politburo Standing Committee.

Role and history

The premier directs the work of the State Council, oversees the ministries and the economy, and traditionally takes principal responsibility for economic policy, while the CCP general secretary (usually also president) leads the party and military. The first premier was Zhou Enlai, who served from the founding of the PRC in 1949 until his death in 1976. Successors included Hua Guofeng, Zhao Ziyang, Li Peng, Zhu Rongji, Wen Jiabao and Li Keqiang. Li Qiang became premier in March 2023.
