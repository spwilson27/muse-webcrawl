Séré de Rivières system

The Séré de Rivières system was an ensemble of fortifications built across France in the late nineteenth century, named after its designer, General Raymond Adolphe Séré de Rivières. It was France's response to the loss of Alsace-Lorraine in the Franco-Prussian War of 1870–71.

Design

The system consisted of rings of detached polygonal forts around strategic cities (Verdun, Toul, Épinal, Belfort) and barrier forts along the eastern frontier, built of masonry and later reinforced with concrete and steel cupolas. Some 166 forts and hundreds of batteries were constructed between 1874 and the First World War.

Legacy

Although partly superseded by advances in artillery, the Séré de Rivières forts played a major role at the Battle of Verdun in 1916, where Fort Douaumont and Fort Vaux became famous. The system influenced the later Maginot Line.
