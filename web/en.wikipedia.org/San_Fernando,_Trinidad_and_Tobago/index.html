San Fernando, officially the City of San Fernando, is the most populous city and second most populous municipality in Trinidad and Tobago, after Chaguanas. Located in the southwestern part of the island of Trinidad, it is nicknamed "Sando" and was promoted to city status on 19 November 1988.

History

The area was originally a settlement of the Naparima Amerindians. It grew with the sugar industry of the Naparima plain and later with Trinidad's petroleum industry; the nearby Pointe-à-Pierre refinery shaped its economy through the twentieth century.

Geography

San Fernando lies on the Gulf of Paria coast, dominated by San Fernando Hill, a landmark limestone outcrop in the centre of the city that is now a recreational park.

Culture

The city is known for its Carnival celebrations, calypso traditions, and landmarks including the San Fernando General Hospital, Skinner Park and the waterfront King's Wharf.
