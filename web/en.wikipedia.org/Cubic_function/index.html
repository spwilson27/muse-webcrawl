Cubic function

In mathematics, a cubic function is a polynomial function of degree three, of the form f(x) = ax³ + bx² + cx + d, where a ≠ 0. Its graph, a cubic curve, always has an inflection point with rotational symmetry of order 2 about that point, and it may cross the x-axis in one or three real places.

Roots

Setting f(x) = 0 gives a cubic equation. Unlike quadratic equations, whose solutions were known in antiquity, the general algebraic solution of the cubic was a Renaissance achievement: Scipione del Ferro found a method for depressed cubics around 1515, Niccolò Tartaglia rediscovered it, and Gerolamo Cardano published the general solution in his Ars Magna (1545), together with his student Lodovico Ferrari's solution of the quartic. The discriminant of the cubic determines whether it has three distinct real roots, a multiple root, or one real root and a pair of non-real complex conjugate roots.

Cardano's formula

By substituting x = t − b/(3a), any cubic can be reduced to the depressed cubic t³ + pt + q = 0, solvable by extracting cube roots of expressions involving √(q²/4 + p³/27). In the case of three real roots — the casus irreducibilis — the formula necessarily passes through complex numbers, a difficulty that helped motivate the acceptance of imaginary numbers, developed further by Rafael Bombelli.

Graph and critical points

The derivative of a cubic is a quadratic; when it has two distinct real roots, the cubic has a local maximum and a local minimum. Every cubic's graph is symmetric under a half-turn about its inflection point. Cubic functions and cubic Bézier curves are also widely used in interpolation, computer graphics, and engineering.
