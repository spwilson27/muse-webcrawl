Mayerling incident

The Mayerling incident is the series of events surrounding the apparent murder–suicide of Crown Prince Rudolf of Austria (21 August 1858 – 30 January 1889) and his lover, Baroness Mary Vetsera (19 March 1871 – 30 January 1889). Rudolf, the only son of Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria-Hungary and Empress Elisabeth, was heir apparent to the imperial throne. Their bodies were found in the Imperial hunting lodge at Mayerling in the Vienna Woods on the morning of 30 January 1889.

Background

Rudolf's marriage to Princess Stéphanie of Belgium was unhappy, and his liberal political views clashed with his father's conservatism; he was increasingly despondent, ill, and involved in romantic liaisons. Mary Vetsera was a 17-year-old baroness who had become infatuated with the prince; letters discovered in 2015, written by Vetsera shortly before her death, indicate she wished to die with him out of love.

Events and cover-up

Rudolf shot Vetsera and then himself. The imperial court initially announced that Rudolf had died of a heart attack or apoplexy; the scandal was managed through a papal dispensation allowing his Catholic burial, on the ground of mental derangement. Vetsera's body was smuggled out and buried secretly at Heiligenkreuz Abbey. The lodge at Mayerling was demolished and a Carmelite convent built on the site.

Legacy

The deaths deprived Franz Joseph of his direct male heir; the succession eventually passed through Archduke Franz Ferdinand, whose assassination in 1914 triggered World War I. The incident has inspired numerous books, ballets, films — including the 1968 film Mayerling — and persistent conspiracy theories, though historians broadly accept the murder–suicide account, which was strengthened by the discovery of Vetsera's farewell letters in a Vienna bank vault in 2015.
