Hearts and minds

Hearts and minds is a phrase and strategy referring to winning a conflict not by defeating an opponent militarily but by securing the allegiance, sympathy and cooperation of the population — "winning hearts" through emotional or moral appeal and "minds" through intellectual persuasion. The concept is central to counterinsurgency doctrine and has also spread into politics, marketing and public diplomacy.

Origins

John Adams wrote in 1818 that the American Revolution "was in the minds and hearts of the people". The phrase entered military usage during the Malayan Emergency (1948–1960), when British High Commissioner Gerald Templer declared in 1952 that the answer to the insurgency lay "not in pouring more troops into the jungle, but in the hearts and minds of the people". British practice combined security with resettlement into "New Villages", political concessions and a promise of independence for Malaya.

Vietnam and after

The United States adopted the language during the Vietnam War, where "winning hearts and minds" (sometimes abbreviated WHAM) described pacification programmes combining security, aid and propaganda; President Lyndon B. Johnson spoke of winning "the hearts and minds" of the Vietnamese people. The phrase became associated in public memory with the gap between stated ideals and the war's destruction, and the 1974 documentary Hearts and Minds took its title from it. After 2001 the concept was revived in Iraq and Afghanistan, where counterinsurgency manuals such as the U.S. Army's FM 3-24 (2006) emphasised protecting civilians, governance and legitimacy over enemy body counts.

Criticism

Critics argue the phrase can mask coercion — Malayan resettlement involved forced relocation of half a million people — and that it treats populations as targets of persuasion rather than political actors. Some analysts also note a persistent tension between short-term security operations and long-term goodwill. In civilian usage the phrase now appears in contexts from election campaigns to corporate change management.
