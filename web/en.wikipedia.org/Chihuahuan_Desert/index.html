Chihuahuan Desert

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Chihuahuan Desert (Spanish: Desierto de Chihuahua) is a desert ecoregion in Northern Mexico and the southwestern United States. It occupies much of the Mexican states of Chihuahua, Coahuila, Nuevo León, Durango, Zacatecas, and San Luis Potosí, and parts of southeastern Arizona, southern New Mexico, and Texas west of the Pecos River in the United States.

Geography and climate

The desert lies between the Sierra Madre Occidental to the west and the Sierra Madre Oriental to the east, and is largely a high-elevation desert, which makes it cooler and wetter than the Sonoran Desert to the west. Most rainfall arrives during summer monsoons, generally between June and early October.

Ecology

The Chihuahuan Desert is considered one of the most biologically diverse arid regions in the world. It is a major centre of cactus diversity, and its numerous isolated mountain ranges — "sky islands" — and endorheic basins support many endemic species. Characteristic animals include the pronghorn, mule deer, kit fox, and the Bolson tortoise.

Protected areas

Major protected areas include Big Bend National Park in Texas, White Sands National Park and Carlsbad Caverns in New Mexico, and the Cuatro Ciénegas basin and Mapimí Biosphere Reserve in Mexico.
