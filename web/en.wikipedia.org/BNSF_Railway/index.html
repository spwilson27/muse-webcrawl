BNSF Railway

A BNSF intermodal freight train in the western United States.

BNSF Railway (reporting mark BNSF) is the largest freight railroad in the United States. One of six North American Class I railroads, BNSF has 60,000 employees, 32,500 miles (52,300 km) of track in 28 states, and nearly 8,000 locomotives. It was formed on December 31, 1996, as the Burlington Northern and Santa Fe Railway from the merger of the Burlington Northern Railroad and the Santa Fe Railway.

History and ownership

The company's heritage lines include the Great Northern Railway, the Northern Pacific Railway, the Chicago, Burlington and Quincy Railroad, and the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway — some of the most storied names in American railroading. In 2010, Berkshire Hathaway acquired the remaining shares of BNSF's parent company in a deal valued at about $44 billion, described by Warren Buffett as an "all-in wager on the economic future of the United States." BNSF's headquarters are in Fort Worth, Texas.

Operations

BNSF is a major hauler of intermodal containers and trailers, coal from the Powder River Basin, grain, and consumer goods, and has competed with Union Pacific Railroad for western U.S. freight traffic. Its southern transcon route between Chicago and Los Angeles is among the busiest freight lines in the world.
