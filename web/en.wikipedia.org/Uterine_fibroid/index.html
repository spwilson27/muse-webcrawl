Uterine fibroid

Fibroids in the wall of the uterus.

Uterine fibroids, also known as uterine leiomyomas or fibroids, are benign smooth-muscle tumours that originate from the myometrium of the uterus. They are extremely common: by age 50, a majority of women have at least one. Most fibroids cause no symptoms and are discovered incidentally on imaging or pelvic examination.

Signs and symptoms

When symptomatic, fibroids can cause heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding (sometimes leading to anaemia), pelvic pressure or pain, urinary frequency from pressure on the bladder, constipation, and difficulties with fertility or pregnancy. Fibroids are classified by location as subserosal (projecting from the outer surface), intramural (within the muscle wall), or submucosal (bulging into the uterine cavity); submucosal fibroids are most associated with bleeding.

Treatment

Management ranges from watchful waiting and medication — including hormonal therapies and gonadotropin-releasing hormone analogues — to procedures such as uterine artery embolisation, focused ultrasound, myomectomy (surgical removal preserving the uterus), and hysterectomy. Fibroids typically shrink after the menopause, as their growth is influenced by oestrogen and progesterone.
