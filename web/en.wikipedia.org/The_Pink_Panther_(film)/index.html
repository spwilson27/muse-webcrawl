The Pink Panther (film)

The Pink Panther is a 1963 American comedy film produced and directed by Blake Edwards and written by Edwards and Maurice Richlin. It stars David Niven as the gentleman jewel thief Sir Charles Lytton and Peter Sellers as the bumbling French detective Inspector Jacques Clouseau, who pursues him. The title refers to the "Pink Panther" diamond at the centre of the plot, so named for a flaw resembling a leaping panther.

Plot

Princess Dala of Lugash owns the legendary Pink Panther diamond. At a Swiss ski resort, the notorious thief known as "the Phantom" — Sir Charles Lytton — plots to steal it, while his wife Simone secretly aids him and Inspector Clouseau blunders through the investigation, unaware his own wife is involved. A fancy-dress ball heist and a chaotic car chase resolve the caper with Clouseau framed and jailed, vowing revenge.

Legacy

Although Niven was intended as the star, Sellers's Clouseau stole the film, launching a long-running franchise of sequels. Henry Mancini's slinky jazz theme and the animated Pink Panther character created for the credits — who became a cartoon star in his own right — are among the film's most enduring contributions.
