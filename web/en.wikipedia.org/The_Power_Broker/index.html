The Power Broker

The Power Broker: Robert Moses and the Fall of New York is a 1974 biography by Robert Caro. The 1,246-page book won the Pulitzer Prize for Biography and is considered one of the greatest works of 20th-century nonfiction.

Subject

Caro chronicles how Robert Moses, never elected to major office, amassed extraordinary power through public authorities (notably the Triborough Bridge and Tunnel Authority) to reshape New York: building 13 bridges, hundreds of miles of expressways, Jones Beach, Lincoln Center and the UN headquarters site — while displacing hundreds of thousands of residents and starving mass transit. His rare defeat came against Jane Jacobs over the Lower Manhattan Expressway.

Legacy

The book reshaped understanding of urban power and has influenced generations of journalists and policymakers. See also The Lean Startup.
