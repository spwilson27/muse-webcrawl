Shadow education

Shadow education is fee-charging private supplementary tutoring in academic subjects.

Shadow education is a term used in education research for private supplementary tutoring in academic subjects, provided for a fee outside mainstream schooling. The metaphor of a "shadow" was popularised by the scholar Mark Bray: the tutoring system mimics the mainstream school system — as the curriculum changes, so does the shadow; as the mainstream gains importance through examinations, the shadow grows with it.

Scope

Shadow education is a global phenomenon, particularly intensive in East and South Asia — for example the juku of Japan, hagwon of South Korea, and buxiban of Taiwan — and in parts of Eastern Europe, the Middle East and Africa. It ranges from one-to-one tutoring in students' homes to large commercial cram schools and online platforms. Expenditure on it constitutes a substantial share of household education budgets in many countries.

Effects and policy

Researchers note both positive and negative implications: tutoring may raise achievement and help struggling students, but it can also exacerbate social inequalities (because wealthier families can buy more and better tutoring), distort classroom teaching, create heavy workloads for students, and generate conflicts of interest when schoolteachers tutor their own pupils privately. Governments have responded in different ways, from laissez-faire approaches to regulation or outright bans, such as South Korea's 1980 prohibition (later struck down) and China's 2021 "double reduction" policy restricting for-profit tutoring in core school subjects.
