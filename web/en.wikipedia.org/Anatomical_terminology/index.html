Anatomical terminology

Anatomical terminology is a form of scientific terminology used by anatomists, zoologists, and health professionals such as doctors, physicians, and pharmacists. Anatomical terminology uses many unique terms, suffixes, and prefixes deriving from Ancient Greek and Latin. These terms can be confusing to those unfamiliar with them, but can be more precise, reducing ambiguity and errors. Also, since these anatomical terms are not used in everyday conversation, their meanings are less likely to change and less likely to be misinterpreted.

Standard anatomical position

To avoid confusion, anatomical descriptions are based on the standard anatomical position: the body upright, facing forward, with the arms at the sides and the palms facing forward. Terms of relative location describe the position of one structure with respect to another.

Key directional terms

  • Anterior and posterior — toward the front and toward the back of the body.
  • Superior and inferior — toward the head and toward the feet.
  • Medial and lateral — toward the midline and away from the midline.
  • Proximal and distal — nearer to and farther from the point of attachment or origin.
  • Superficial and deep — nearer to and farther from the surface of the body.

Planes and axes

Three main planes divide the body: the sagittal plane (left–right divisions), the coronal or frontal plane (front–back divisions), and the transverse or axial plane (upper–lower divisions). Movements such as flexion, extension, abduction, and rotation are described relative to these planes and to the joints involved.

Standardization

The international standard reference is the Terminologia Anatomica, first published in 1998 by the Federative Committee on Anatomical Terminology, which superseded the earlier Nomina Anatomica. For animals, a parallel standard, the Nomina Anatomica Veterinaria, is used.
