The trumpeters are a family, Psophiidae, of birds restricted to the humid forests of the Amazon and Guiana Shield in South America. They are named for their loud, trumpeting calls. The genus Psophia includes the grey-winged, pale-winged and dark-winged trumpeters, sometimes split into additional species.

Description and behaviour

Trumpeters are gregarious, ground-dwelling birds about 43–53 cm long with rounded bodies, long necks and legs, and short, chicken-like bills. They fly weakly and roost in trees. Groups wander the forest floor feeding on fruit and invertebrates.

Relationship with humans

Trumpeters tame readily and have been kept by indigenous peoples as watchdogs and snake catchers on farms, warning of intruders with their booming calls.
