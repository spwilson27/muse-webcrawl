Drahomíra

Drahomíra in a 19th-century illustration.

Drahomíra (died after 935) was a Bohemian duchess, wife of Duke Vratislaus I of Bohemia and mother of Wenceslaus I (Saint Wenceslaus) and Boleslaus I. After her husband's death in 921 she acted as regent for the young Wenceslaus, in rivalry with her Christian mother-in-law Ludmila.

Life and legend

Drahomíra came from the pagan Slavic Hevelli tribe near Brandenburg. Christian legend, written from the perspective of Wenceslaus's cult, portrayed her as a fiercely pagan ruler who arranged the murder of Ludmila at Tetín in 921 and persecuted Christians, though modern historians treat these accounts with caution. When Wenceslaus came of age he exiled her, then recalled her; after his murder in 935, she is said to have fled into exile.

Legacy

Drahomíra appears as a villain in Czech national mythology and art, notably in the myths surrounding St Wenceslaus, and as a character in opera and theatre. According to legend, she was swallowed by the earth near Prague Castle for cursing, a spot long pointed out to visitors.
