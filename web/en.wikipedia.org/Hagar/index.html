Hagar

Hagar (Hebrew: הָגָר, Hāgār; Arabic: هاجر, Hājar) is a biblical figure in the Book of Genesis. An Egyptian slave in the household of Abram (Abraham) and his wife Sarai (Sarah), she became Abraham's second wife or concubine and the mother of his first son, Ishmael.

Genesis narrative

According to Genesis 16, the childless Sarai gave Hagar to Abraham so that she might bear a child on her behalf. Once pregnant, Hagar treated her mistress with contempt, and Sarah dealt harshly with her in return; Hagar fled into the wilderness, where an angel found her by a spring, promised that her son Ishmael would father a great multitude, and instructed her to return. Hagar named God El Roi, "the God who sees me" — the only character in the Bible to give God a name.

In Genesis 21, after Sarah bore Isaac, she demanded that Abraham send Hagar and Ishmael away. In the desert of Beersheba, when their water was gone, Hagar placed the child under a bush and wept, but God heard the boy's voice and showed her a well. Ishmael grew up in the wilderness of Paran and became an archer and, in tradition, the ancestor of the Arab peoples and a patriarch in Islam.

In Islam

In Islamic tradition, Hājar is honoured as the wife of Ibrāhīm (Abraham) and mother of Ismāʿīl (Ishmael). Her search for water between the hills of Ṣafā and Marwah in Mecca is commemorated in the saʿy rite of the Hajj pilgrimage, and the well of Zamzam is associated with her story. In the New Testament, Paul allegorises Hagar in Galatians 4 as representing the covenant of slavery, contrasted with Sarah as the free woman.
