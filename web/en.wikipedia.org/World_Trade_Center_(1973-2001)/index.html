The original World Trade Center was a complex of seven buildings in the Financial District of Lower Manhattan, New York City. Its centerpiece, the Twin Towers — the North Tower (1 World Trade Center) and South Tower (2 World Trade Center) designed by architect Minoru Yamasaki — were the tallest buildings in the world when completed in the early 1970s. The complex was destroyed in the September 11 attacks of 2001.

Construction and design

Proposed by David Rockefeller to reinvigorate Lower Manhattan, the project was built by the Port Authority on the site of "Radio Row." Engineer Leslie Robertson's innovative framed-tube design used closely spaced perimeter columns of steel, allowing column-free office floors. The North Tower topped out in 1970 at 1,368 feet (417 m) with 110 floors; with its antenna it reached 1,728 feet. The complex included the Marriott hotel (3 WTC), three low-rise plaza buildings, and the U.S. Custom House (6 WTC), all above a vast underground concourse and the Austin J. Tobin Plaza.

Tenants and daily life

Some 430 companies from 28 countries rented space in the complex; about 50,000 people worked there and another 140,000 visited daily. The North Tower housed the Windows on the World restaurant, and the South Tower featured the Top of the World observation deck. In 1993 the complex survived a truck bombing in its underground garage that killed six people.

Destruction

On the morning of September 11, 2001, al-Qaeda terrorists hijacked American Airlines Flight 11 and United Airlines Flight 175 and crashed them into the North and South Towers respectively. Both towers collapsed within two hours, and 7 World Trade Center collapsed that afternoon from fire and debris damage; the remaining buildings were later demolished. Nearly 3,000 people were killed in the attacks overall. The site — "Ground Zero" — was subsequently redeveloped into the new World Trade Center, the National September 11 Memorial & Museum, and One World Trade Center.
