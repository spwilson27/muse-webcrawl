Related groups: Afro-Caribbeans, Black Canadians

African Americans, also known as Black Americans or Afro-Americans, are an ethnic group consisting of Americans with partial or total ancestry from any of the Black racial groups of Africa. The term typically denotes descendants of enslaved Africans who were brought from their African homelands by the transatlantic slave trade and forced into chattel slavery in the United States from the 17th century until slavery's abolition in 1865.

History

The first Africans arrived in English North America in 1619 at Jamestown, Virginia. Slavery became central to the colonial and early national economy, particularly in the South. Following the American Civil War, the Thirteenth Amendment (1865) abolished slavery, and the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments granted citizenship and voting rights to Black men, though these were undermined by Jim Crow laws, disenfranchisement and racial segregation. The Great Migration of the 20th century moved millions to northern and western cities. The civil rights movement of the 1950s–60s won landmark legislation including the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965. In 2008 Barack Obama became the first African American president of the United States.

Demographics and culture

African Americans are the second-largest racial or ethnic group in the United States. Black culture has made major contributions to American music, literature, cuisine, language and religion, with institutions such as the Black church playing a central role in community life.
