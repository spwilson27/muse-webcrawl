Pan-Americanism

Pan-Americanism is a movement that seeks to create, encourage, and organise relationships, associations, and cooperation among the states of the Americas, through diplomatic, political, economic, and social means. It holds that the nations of North, Central, and South America share common interests and should act in solidarity.

Origins

The idea grew from the Spanish American wars of independence; Simón Bolívar convened the Congress of Panama in 1826 to promote unity among the new republics. In the late 19th century the United States promoted inter-American conferences, beginning with the First International Conference of American States in Washington in 1889–90, often in tension with Latin American suspicions of US hegemony.

Institutions

The Pan-American Union, founded in 1910, became the secretariat of the Organization of American States (OAS), established in 1948 with its headquarters in Washington, D.C. Related bodies include the Inter-American Development Bank and the Pan American Health Organization.

Legacy

Pan-American ideals are embodied in the Pan American Games, the Pan-American Highway, and hemispheric trade initiatives, though regional integration has more often proceeded through sub-regional blocs such as Mercosur and the Pacific Alliance.
