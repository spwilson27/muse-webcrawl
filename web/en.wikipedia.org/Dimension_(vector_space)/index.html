Dimension (vector space)

In mathematics, the dimension of a vector space V is the cardinality (i.e., the number of vectors) of a basis of V over its base field. It is sometimes called the Hamel dimension (after Georg Hamel) or the algebraic dimension to distinguish it from other types of dimension.

For every vector space there exists a basis, and all bases of a vector space have equal cardinality; as a result, the dimension of a vector space is uniquely defined. If the field is F, the dimension of V over F is denoted dimF(V) or [V : F].

Examples

The vector space R3 has the standard basis of three unit vectors, so it has dimension 3. The real numbers R have dimension 1 over themselves, but dimension 2 over the rationals when viewed as C (the complex numbers have dimension 2 over R). The set of all polynomials has countably infinite dimension. A space is called finite-dimensional if it has a finite basis, and infinite-dimensional otherwise.

Properties

If W is a linear subspace of V, then dim(W) ≤ dim(V). Two finite-dimensional vector spaces over the same field are isomorphic if and only if they have the same dimension. The dimension theorem for vector spaces states that all bases have the same cardinality; its proof for infinite bases relies on the axiom of choice, which is also needed to show that every vector space has a basis at all.

The dimension of a vector space generalises in the rank–nullity theorem: for a linear map, the dimension of the domain equals the rank of the map plus the dimension of its kernel.
