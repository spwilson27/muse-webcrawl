Total angular momentum quantum number

Vector model of total angular momentum J = L + S

In quantum mechanics, the total angular momentum quantum number characterises the total angular momentum of a given particle, which combines its orbital angular momentum and its intrinsic angular momentum (i.e. its spin). It is conventionally denoted by the letter j.

If s is the particle's spin angular momentum quantum number and ℓ its orbital angular momentum quantum number, then the total angular momentum quantum number j can take values in the range:

|ℓ − s| ≤ j ≤ ℓ + s

where j increases in steps of one between the two bounds. The magnitude of the total angular momentum is given by √[j(j + 1)] ħ, and its projection along a chosen axis is mjħ, where mj ranges from −j to +j in integer steps.

Coupling of angular momenta

The allowed values arise from the quantum-mechanical rules for the addition of two angular momenta. The coefficients used to expand coupled states in terms of uncoupled states are the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. For an electron (s = 1/2) in an orbital with quantum number ℓ, the two possible values are j = ℓ + 1/2 and j = ℓ − 1/2 (the latter only when ℓ > 0).

Spectroscopic notation

The total angular momentum quantum number appears as the subscript in term symbols of the form 2S+1LJ, used to label atomic energy levels. For example, the ground state of sodium is written 2S1/2, indicating L = 0, S = 1/2, and J = 1/2. The splitting of levels with different J for the same L and S is the fine structure produced by spin–orbit coupling.
