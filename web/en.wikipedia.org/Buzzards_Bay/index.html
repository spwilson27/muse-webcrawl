Buzzards Bay

Buzzards Bay is a bay of the Atlantic Ocean adjacent to the U.S. state of Massachusetts. It is roughly 28 miles (45 kilometres) long by 8 miles (13 kilometres) wide. It is a popular destination for fishing, boating, and tourism. Since 1914, Buzzards Bay has been connected to Cape Cod Bay by the Cape Cod Canal. In 1988, under the Clean Water Act, the Environmental Protection Agency and the Commonwealth of Massachusetts designated Buzzards Bay as an Estuary of National Significance, part of the National Estuary Program.

Geography

The bay is bounded on the east by Cape Cod, on the northwest by the mainland towns of Bristol and Plymouth counties, on the southwest by the Elizabeth Islands, and on the south by open water to Rhode Island Sound and Vineyard Sound. Its principal communities include New Bedford, Fairhaven, Mattapoisett, Marion, Wareham, Bourne and Falmouth.

History

The bay was named by colonist Bartholomew Gosnold in 1602 for the large birds — probably ospreys or turkey vultures — he saw near its shores. In 1991 the bay suffered damage from Hurricane Bob, and in 2003 an oil spill from a barge damaged fisheries and shoreline.
