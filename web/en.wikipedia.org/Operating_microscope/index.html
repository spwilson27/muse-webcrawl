Operating microscope

An operating microscope or surgical microscope is an optical microscope specifically designed to be used in a surgical setting, typically to perform microsurgery. Design features of an operating microscope are: magnification typically in the range from 4x to 40x, components that are easy to sterilize or disinfect in order to ensure cross-infection control, and built-in illumination directed along the line of sight.

Applications

Operating microscopes are essential in neurosurgery, ophthalmology, otolaryngology, reconstructive and plastic surgery, dentistry (especially endodontics), and vascular surgery. They allow surgeons to visualize fine structures such as small blood vessels and nerves, enabling anastomosis and delicate dissection that would be impossible with the naked eye. The microscope is often mounted on a balanced, counterweighted stand or ceiling mount so it can be positioned precisely and moved with minimal effort.

History

The first use of a microscope in surgery is credited to the otologist Carl-Olof Nylén in 1921, and the first binocular operating microscope was developed in 1922. In the 1950s and 1960s firms such as Carl Zeiss produced purpose-built surgical microscopes, and the technique of microvascular surgery was pioneered by surgeons including Harry Buncke and, in neurosurgery, M. Gazi Yaşargil, who helped found modern microneurosurgery.
