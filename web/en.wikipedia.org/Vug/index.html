Vug

A vug is a small cavity in a rock, often lined with crystals of a different mineral composition from the surrounding matrix. Vugs are formed when minerals are dissolved out of a rock or when gas bubbles or other voids leave spaces that are later partially filled by precipitating minerals.

Formation and features

Vugs range from a few millimetres to several centimetres across. Because crystals growing into an open void can develop freely, vugs frequently contain well-formed crystals of quartz, calcite, fluorite or other minerals, making them prized by mineral collectors. A large vug lined with crystals is sometimes called a geode when it can be separated from its host rock.

Related terms

Similar cavities include vesicles in volcanic rocks and the crystal-lined pockets known as miarolitic cavities in granitic pegmatites. The adjective form is "vuggy", and vuggy porosity is an important property in some petroleum and groundwater reservoirs.
