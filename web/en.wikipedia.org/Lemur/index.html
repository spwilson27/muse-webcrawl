Lemur

Lemurs are wet-nosed primates of the superfamily Lemuroidea, endemic to the island of Madagascar. They range from the tiny mouse lemurs to the indri, and share traits such as a toothcomb and reliance on smell.

KingdomAnimalia
OrderPrimates
SuperfamilyLemuroidea
RangeMadagascar

Evolution

Lemurs are thought to have rafted from Africa to Madagascar 50–80 million years ago and diversified in isolation. See Madagascar and primate.

Behaviour and ecology

Many species are nocturnal; others are diurnal or cathemeral. Diets include fruit, leaves and insects. Female dominance is common, unusual among primates.

Conservation

Most lemur species are threatened by habitat loss from deforestation and hunting; lemurs are among the most endangered mammal groups.
