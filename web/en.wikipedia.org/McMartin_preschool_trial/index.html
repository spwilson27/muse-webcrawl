McMartin preschool trial

The McMartin preschool trial was a day care sexual abuse case in the 1980s, prosecuted by the Los Angeles County District Attorney's office. Members of the McMartin family, who operated a preschool in Manhattan Beach, California, were charged with numerous acts of sexual abuse of children in their care. Accusations, first made in 1983, included claims of secret tunnels, satanic rituals, and bizarre acts for which no physical evidence was found.

Investigations and trial

The case began with an accusation by a parent later diagnosed with acute paranoid schizophrenia. Hundreds of children were interviewed by the Children's Institute International using highly suggestive techniques, producing elaborate allegations. Seven defendants were initially charged; charges against five were dropped in 1986 for lack of evidence. The trial of Peggy McMartin Buckey and her son Ray Buckey (1987–1990) became the longest and most expensive criminal trial in American history at the time, costing about $15 million.

Outcome and legacy

In January 1990 the jury acquitted the Buckeys on 52 counts and deadlocked on others; a retrial ended in a second deadlock and all remaining charges were dismissed. No convictions were obtained. The case is now regarded as a central episode of the "Satanic panic" and day-care sex-abuse hysteria of the 1980s, and it prompted major reforms in how young children are interviewed as witnesses, as well as research into children's suggestibility.
