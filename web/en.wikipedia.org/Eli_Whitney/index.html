Eli Whitney

Eli Whitney (December 8, 1765 – January 8, 1825) was an American inventor best known for inventing the cotton gin in 1793, a machine that rapidly separated cotton fibres from their seeds.

Cotton gin

The gin made short-staple cotton profitable to process, transforming the economy of the American South. Paradoxically, by making cotton cultivation far more lucrative, it greatly increased demand for enslaved labour and entrenched slavery. Whitney spent years in patent disputes and earned little from the invention.

Interchangeable parts

Whitney later won a federal contract to manufacture muskets and promoted the idea of interchangeable parts and machine-made uniformity, a cornerstone of the "American system of manufacturing", though historians note his factory's actual interchangeability was limited and partly staged for demonstrations.

Life

Born in Westborough, Massachusetts, and educated at Yale, Whitney founded a firearms factory in New Haven, Connecticut, whose site is now a museum.
