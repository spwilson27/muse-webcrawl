The Early Jurassic is the first epoch of the Jurassic Period, spanning from about 201.4 to 174.7 million years ago. It began immediately after the Triassic–Jurassic extinction event, one of the major mass extinctions of the Phanerozoic, and is divided into four stages: the Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian and Toarcian.

Climate and geography

Throughout the Early Jurassic the supercontinent Pangaea continued to break apart, with the Central Atlantic opening between North America and Africa. The climate was warm and generally humid, without polar ice caps, and sea levels rose, flooding continental margins with shallow epicontinental seas. The epoch ended with the Toarcian Oceanic Anoxic Event, a global episode of ocean oxygen depletion linked to massive volcanism of the Karoo–Ferrar large igneous province.

Life

Ecosystems recovered from the end-Triassic extinction. In the seas, ammonites diversified explosively and became key index fossils; ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs were abundant marine reptiles; and belemnites appeared. On land, dinosaurs became the dominant large animals: sauropodomorphs grew to large sizes, early true sauropods emerged, and theropods such as Dilophosaurus hunted in the forests. Famous fossil sites include the Lias of Lyme Regis in England, made famous by Mary Anning, and the Posidonia Shale of Holzmaden in Germany.
