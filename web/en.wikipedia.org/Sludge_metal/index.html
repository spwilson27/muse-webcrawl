Sludge metal

Sludge metal (also known as sludge doom or simply sludge) is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music that melds elements of doom metal and hardcore punk. It is typically abrasive and dissonant, featuring shouted or screamed vocals, heavily distorted instruments, and sharply contrasting tempos. The Melvins are generally credited with founding the style in the mid-1980s, and it was developed further by bands in New Orleans such as Eyehategod, Crowbar and Acid Bath, whose work is sometimes called "sludgecore."

Characteristics

Sludge metal songs are usually slow to mid-tempo, built on downtuned, distorted guitar riffs played with thick, "muddy" tones and abundant feedback. Drumming alternates between slow doom passages and faster hardcore-inflected bursts. Lyrics often address misery, addiction, poverty, social decay and misanthropy, reflecting the environments — particularly the American South — from which many of the genre's bands emerged. Vocals are typically harsh, shouted or screamed, though some bands incorporate sung or spoken passages.

History

The genre grew from the Melvins' slow, heavy records of the mid-1980s, which fused Black Sabbath-style metal with hardcore tempos and stops. In the late 1980s and 1990s, a scene centered in Louisiana combined the sound with blues and Southern rock influences; Eyehategod's albums Take as Needed for Pain (1993) and Crowbar's Obedience Thru Suffering (1991) became touchstones. Later, bands such as Neurosis and Isis blended sludge with atmospheric and progressive elements, giving rise to the related "post-metal" or atmospheric sludge style, while groups like Mastodon and Baroness carried sludge influences toward progressive metal.

Related styles

Sludge metal overlaps with stoner metal, doom metal, Southern metal and post-metal, and is closely related to crust punk and grindcore in its harsher "sludgecore" form. See also: Melvins, Eyehategod.
