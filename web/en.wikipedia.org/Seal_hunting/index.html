Seal hunting

Seal hunting, or sealing, is the personal or commercial hunting of seals. It has been practised for thousands of years by Indigenous peoples of the Arctic, for whom seals provide food, oil, skins and other materials essential to subsistence life. Commercial sealing developed in the North Atlantic from the eighteenth century, centred on harp and hooded seals off Newfoundland and in the Barents Sea, for fur, leather and oil.

Controversy and regulation

The hunting of young harp seal pups, with their white coats, drew intense international protest from the 1960s onward. Canada banned the commercial hunting of whitecoat pups in 1987, and the European Union banned imports of most seal products in 2009, with exemptions for Indigenous hunts. Sealing remains economically important to some coastal and Inuit communities, and the debate over animal welfare, conservation and Indigenous rights continues.
