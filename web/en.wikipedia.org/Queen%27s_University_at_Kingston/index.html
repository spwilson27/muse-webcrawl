Queen's University at Kingston, commonly known as Queen's University, is a public research university in Kingston, Ontario, Canada. Founded in 1841 by royal charter issued by Queen Victoria, it predates the founding of Canada by 26 years and is one of the country's oldest degree-granting institutions.

History

Established by the Presbyterian Church of Scotland, Queen's held its first classes in 1842 with a handful of students and faculty. It became a secular institution in 1912. The university grew through the 20th century into a mid-sized residential university known for a strong undergraduate experience, school spirit, and traditions such as its Homecoming celebrations, the Golden Gaels teams, and the Tricolour Guard.

Academics

Queen's comprises faculties of Arts and Science, Engineering and Applied Science, Law, Medicine (School of Medicine), Business (Smith School of Business), Education, and Health Sciences, along with the Bader College at Herstmonceux Castle in England. It is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research universities and is noted for high graduation rates and research strengths in areas such as physics, including work associated with the SNOLAB neutrino observatory.

Campus

The main campus, near downtown Kingston and Lake Ontario, is known for its limestone buildings, including Grant Hall, Theological Hall, and the Agnes Etherington Art Centre.
