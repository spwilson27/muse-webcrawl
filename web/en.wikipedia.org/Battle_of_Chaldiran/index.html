Battle of Chaldiran

The Battle of Chaldiran was fought on 23 August 1514 near Chaldiran in northwestern Iran between the Ottoman Empire under Sultan Selim I and the Safavid Empire under Shah Ismail I. The Ottoman victory, won largely through gunpowder weapons, fixed the frontier between the two empires and began centuries of Ottoman–Persian conflict.

Background

Ismail I, founder of the Safavid dynasty, had proclaimed Twelver Shi'a Islam the state religion and won a devoted following among the Qizilbash Turkmen of Anatolia, alarming the Sunni Ottomans. Selim I, after securing his throne, marched east in 1514 with a large army — perhaps 60,000–100,000 men — including janissaries armed with muskets and field artillery.

The battle

Selim deployed his artillery and janissaries behind a barricade of chained wagons in the Ottoman manner. The Qizilbash cavalry, superb horsemen but lacking firearms, attacked the Ottoman wings and achieved initial success on one flank, yet their charges against the centre were shredded by massed musket and cannon fire. By day's end the Safavid army was routed; Ismail barely escaped, and his camp and harem were captured.

Consequences

The Ottomans briefly occupied Tabriz and annexed Eastern Anatolia and Upper Mesopotamia, shifting the strategic balance decisively. The defeat shattered Ismail's aura of invincibility and pushed the Safavids to adopt gunpowder weapons. The Ottoman–Safavid frontier stabilised along the Zagros, roughly the modern Iran–Turkey border.
