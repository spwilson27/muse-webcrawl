Venutius was a 1st-century AD king or prince of the Brigantes, the largest tribal group in northern Britain, and is best known as the husband — and later the enemy — of Queen Cartimandua. His resistance to Rome made him, in the words of the historian Tacitus, the most celebrated of the British kings in warfare of his day.

Conflict with Cartimandua

Cartimandua, a Roman ally, divorced Venutius and married his armour-bearer Vellocatus. Venutius seized the Brigantian throne around AD 69, during the Roman civil war known as the Year of the Four Emperors, exploiting the weakness of Roman garrisons. Roman forces rescued Cartimandua but could not initially defeat Venutius.

Defeat

The governor Quintus Petillius Cerialis campaigned against the Brigantes in AD 71–74, defeating Venutius and bringing the Brigantian territory firmly under Roman control. Venutius's fate after his defeat is unrecorded, and his stronghold is often identified with the great hillfort at Stanwick in North Yorkshire.
