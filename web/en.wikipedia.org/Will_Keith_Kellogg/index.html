Will Keith Kellogg

Will Keith Kellogg (April 7, 1860 – October 6, 1951), generally referred to as W. K. Kellogg, was an American industrialist in food manufacturing who founded the Kellogg Company, which produces a wide variety of popular breakfast cereals. He was also a noted philanthropist and breeder of Arabian horses.

Early life and the corn flake

Kellogg was born in Battle Creek, Michigan. He worked at the Battle Creek Sanitarium, run by his elder brother John Harvey Kellogg, where the brothers experimented with wheat- and corn-based foods for patients. In 1894, by accident, a batch of cooked wheat was left to stand and, when rolled, produced thin flakes that toasted crisp — the origin of the breakfast flake. Will Keith developed the corn flake and, after a bitter split with his brother over adding sugar to the product, founded the Battle Creek Toasted Corn Flake Company in 1906, later the Kellogg Company.

Later life and philanthropy

Kellogg's marketing innovations — including his signature as a trademark and early advertising campaigns — made Corn Flakes a national staple. In 1930 he established the W. K. Kellogg Foundation, one of the world's largest philanthropic foundations. He also founded the Kellogg Arabian Horse Ranch in Pomona, California, and donated land that became part of California State Polytechnic University, Pomona. He died in Battle Creek in 1951 at age 91.
