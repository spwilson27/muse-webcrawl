Sound pressure

Symbolp
SI unitPascal (Pa)
Reference (air)20 µPa (threshold of hearing)
Level scaleDecibel (dB SPL)

Sound pressure or acoustic pressure is the local pressure deviation from the ambient atmospheric pressure caused by a sound wave. In air, sound pressure can be measured with a microphone; in water, with a hydrophone. Its SI unit is the pascal (Pa).

Definition

Sound pressure is the difference between instantaneous total pressure and static pressure at a point in a medium traversed by a sound wave. The effective sound pressure is the root mean square (RMS) of the instantaneous pressure over a given interval. For a plane wave, pressure and particle velocity are related through the acoustic impedance of the medium.

Sound pressure level

Because the audible range spans roughly a million-to-one in pressure, levels are usually expressed logarithmically as sound pressure level (SPL) in decibels, referenced to 20 micropascals in air — approximately the threshold of human hearing at 1 kHz — or 1 micropascal in water. A doubling of sound pressure corresponds to an increase of about 6 dB. The threshold of pain lies around 120–140 dB SPL.

Examples

Typical levels include a whisper at about 30 dB, normal conversation at 60 dB, a busy road at 85 dB, and a jet engine at close range above 130 dB. Distance attenuates sound pressure following an inverse relationship in the free field.
