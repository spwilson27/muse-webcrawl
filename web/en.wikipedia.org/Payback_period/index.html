Payback period

The payback period is the length of time required for an investment to generate cash flows sufficient to recover its initial cost. It is one of the simplest capital-budgeting criteria: projects with shorter payback periods are usually preferred because their costs are recovered sooner and are considered less risky.

Calculation

If annual cash inflows are uniform, payback period = initial investment ÷ annual cash inflow. With uneven flows, cumulative cash flow is tracked until it turns positive. A variant, the discounted payback period, uses cash flows discounted at the cost of capital, so it accounts for the time value of money, though it still ignores cash flows after payback.

Criticism

The simple payback method ignores the time value of money and all returns beyond the cut-off, biasing decisions toward short-lived projects. It is therefore usually used alongside discounted-cash-flow measures such as net present value (NPV) and internal rate of return (IRR), rather than alone.
