Trade Facilitation Agreement

The Trade Facilitation Agreement (TFA) is a multilateral treaty of the World Trade Organization (WTO) that aims to expedite the movement, release, and clearance of goods across borders, including goods in transit. It was concluded at the WTO's Bali Ministerial Conference in December 2013 as the centrepiece of the "Bali Package" — the first multilateral agreement reached by the WTO since its founding — and entered into force on 22 February 2017 after ratification by two-thirds of the WTO membership.

Provisions

The agreement requires members to publish trade procedures and fees, provide advance rulings, allow pre-arrival processing and electronic payment of duties, use risk management and post-clearance audit, and speed release of perishable goods. It encourages single-window systems, cooperation among border agencies, and simplified procedures for authorized traders. A notable innovation is its treatment of developing and least-developed countries: each such member designates provisions as Category A (immediate implementation), B (after a transition period), or C (after transition and with technical assistance), making obligations self-scheduled according to capacity.

Expected impact

WTO economists estimated that full implementation could reduce global trade costs by an average of about 14.3%, with the largest gains for developing economies, and could raise world exports substantially by cutting delays at borders — reductions in clearance times of a day or more on average. A WTO Trade Facilitation Committee oversees implementation, and a dedicated facility coordinates donor assistance for countries needing support.
