Engineering tolerance

Engineering tolerance is the permissible limit or limits of variation in:

  1. a physical dimension;
  2. a measured value or physical property of a material, manufactured object, system, or service;
  3. other measured values (such as temperature, humidity, etc.);
  4. in engineering and safety, a physical distance or space (tolerance), as in a truck (lorry), train or boat under a bridge as well as a train in a tunnel (see structure gauge and loading gauge);
  5. in mechanical engineering, the space between a bolt and a nut or a hole, etc.

Dimensions, properties, or conditions may have some variation without significantly affecting functioning of systems, machines, structures, etc. A variation beyond the tolerance (for example, a temperature that is too hot or too cold) is said to be noncompliant, rejected, or exceeding the tolerance.

Specifying tolerances

On engineering drawings, tolerances are commonly given as bilateral limits (for example 10.00 ± 0.05 mm), unilateral limits, or as limit dimensions stating the maximum and minimum acceptable sizes directly. An international system of standard tolerance grades (IT grades) is defined in ISO 286, relating the size of a part to a graduated scale of precision.

Fits

When two parts must assemble — classically a shaft in a hole — the combination of their tolerances defines a fit: a clearance fit always leaves a gap, an interference fit always requires force or thermal expansion to assemble, and a transition fit may fall either way. Standard systems such as the ISO limits and fits describe these combinations with letter-and-number codes (e.g. H7/g6).

Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing

Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T), standardised in ASME Y14.5 and ISO 1101, controls not just size but form, orientation, location and run-out of features, using datums and tolerance zones. It allows larger, functionally correct tolerances than purely coordinate dimensioning.

Tolerance stack-up

In an assembly, individual part tolerances accumulate. Tolerance analysis or stack-up calculation — worst-case or statistical (root-sum-square) — verifies that the assembled product still functions when parts sit anywhere within their tolerance ranges.
