Aktionsart

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Aktionsart (German for "type of action") is a term in linguistics for the inherent temporal structure of a verb or verb phrase — also called lexical aspect or situation type. Unlike grammatical aspect (e.g. perfective vs imperfective), which is a property of how a clause presents an event, Aktionsart classifies events by their intrinsic duration, dynamism and endpoints: whether they are states, activities, accomplishments or achievements.

Vendler's classification

The philosopher Zeno Vendler proposed four classes of verbs in his influential 1957 paper "Verbs and Times":

  • States — static, no internal change (know, love, be tall)
  • Activities — dynamic, with no inherent endpoint (run, swim, push a cart)
  • Accomplishments — dynamic, proceeding toward a natural endpoint (build a house, run a mile)
  • Achievements — punctual, near-instantaneous changes (arrive, win the race, notice)

Later scholars such as David Dowty and Bernard Comrie refined the system using features like dynamicity, duration and telicity, and added classes such as semelfactives (single short events, e.g. cough).

Aktionsart in Slavic linguistics

The term originated in the study of Slavic languages (first used by Sigurd Agrell in 1908), where rich systems of verbal prefixes derive new verbs with specific modes of action — inchoative (beginning), delimitative (for a while), iterative, completive and others. These derivational "modes of action" differ from both lexical aspect in Vendler's sense and grammatical aspect proper.
