Flucytosine

Flucytosine, also known as 5-fluorocytosine (5-FC), is a synthetic fluorinated pyrimidine used as an antifungal medication. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is used primarily in combination with amphotericin B for serious fungal infections such as cryptococcal meningitis and severe Candida infections.

Mechanism of action

Fungal cells take up flucytosine via the cytosine permease enzyme and convert it, through cytosine deaminase, into 5-fluorouracil. This is further processed into compounds that inhibit fungal RNA and DNA synthesis. Mammalian cells lack cytosine deaminase, giving the drug selective toxicity, although gut flora can convert some of the drug to fluorouracil, contributing to side effects.

Clinical use

Flucytosine is never used alone for invasive disease because fungi rapidly develop resistance; combined with amphotericin B it shortens treatment and improves outcomes in cryptococcal meningitis, particularly in people with HIV/AIDS. Blood levels are monitored because the drug has a narrow therapeutic window.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, diarrhea, and rash. More serious toxicity includes bone marrow suppression (leukopenia and thrombocytopenia) and liver enzyme elevation, risks increased by renal impairment since the drug is excreted unchanged by the kidneys.
