Ex nihilo nihil fit

Ex nihilo nihil fit
LanguageLatin
Literal meaning"Nothing comes from nothing"
SourcesParmenides; Lucretius, De rerum natura

Ex nihilo nihil fit is a Latin phrase meaning "nothing comes from nothing". It expresses the ancient philosophical principle that nothing can arise without a cause or pre-existing material, a foundational assumption of Greek natural philosophy.

Origins

The idea derives from the thought of Parmenides, who argued that what exists cannot come into being from what does not exist. The Roman poet Lucretius gave the principle classic expression in his Epicurean poem De rerum natura, arguing that if things could arise from nothing, anything could be born from anything without seed or cause.

Influence

The principle shaped later philosophy and science, anticipating conservation principles such as the conservation of mass and energy. It stands in tension with the theological doctrine of creation ex nihilo, which holds that God created the universe out of nothing — a point debated by medieval theologians, many of whom held that the maxim applies to natural causes but not to divine omnipotence.
