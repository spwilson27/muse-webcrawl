Ducal Prussia

Ducal Prussia or the Duchy of Prussia (German: Herzogtum Preußen; Polish: Prusy Książęce) was a duchy in the eastern part of Prussia from 1525 to 1701. It was the first Protestant state to be established when Albert of Hohenzollern, Grand Master of the Teutonic Order, secularised the Prussian branch of the order's Monastic State, becoming Duke of Prussia and converting to Lutheranism.

Establishment

By the Treaty of Kraków (1525), Albert dissolved the Teutonic Order's rule in Prussia and received the territory as a hereditary fief of the Kingdom of Poland, performing the Prussian Homage to King Sigismund I the Old in Kraków. The duchy's capital was Königsberg (modern Kaliningrad). The Albertina University of Königsberg, founded in 1544, became a centre of Lutheran learning.

Brandenburg union and sovereignty

When Albert's son Albert Frederick died in 1618 without male heirs, the duchy passed to his son-in-law John Sigismund, Elector of Brandenburg, forming Brandenburg-Prussia. The Duchy remained a Polish fief until the Treaties of Wehlau and Bromberg (1657), confirmed at Oliva (1660), granted the Hohenzollern electors full sovereignty. In 1701 Elector Frederick III crowned himself "King in Prussia" at Königsberg, transforming the duchy into the heartland of the Kingdom of Prussia; as a unit it continued as the province of East Prussia.
