Medical school

A medical school is a tertiary educational institution, professional school, or part of such an institution, that teaches medicine, and awards a professional degree for physicians. Such medical degrees include the Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS, MBChB), the Doctor of Medicine (MD), and the Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine (DO). Medical schools can also carry out medical research and operate teaching hospitals.

Curriculum

Curricula vary by country. Typically they combine preclinical study of anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pharmacology and pathology with clinical training in teaching hospitals, where students take histories, examine patients, and learn diagnosis and treatment under supervision. In the United States, medical school is a four-year graduate programme followed by residency; in many Commonwealth countries the degree is a five- or six-year undergraduate course entered directly from secondary school.

Admissions

Admission is highly competitive, usually requiring strong results in science subjects and, in some countries, admissions tests such as the MCAT, UCAT or GAMSAT, together with interviews assessing communication and ethics.

History

Formal medical faculties emerged in medieval universities such as Salerno, Bologna and Montpellier; the modern research-oriented model was reshaped by the 1910 Flexner Report in North America.
