Basel (German: Basel [ˈbaːzl̩]; French: Bâle) is a city in northwestern Switzerland on the river Rhine, at the point where the Swiss, French, and German borders meet. It is Switzerland's third-most-populous city, with about 178,000 inhabitants, and is a major centre for the chemical and pharmaceutical industries, home to the headquarters of Novartis and Roche.

History

The site was a Celtic and then Roman settlement (Basilia; nearby Augusta Raurica). Basel became a bishop's seat in the early Middle Ages, and the University of Basel, founded in 1460, is Switzerland's oldest university — associated with Erasmus of Rotterdam, the mathematician Bernoulli family, and Leonhard Euler. The city joined the Swiss Confederation in 1501. The 1356 Basel earthquake was one of the most destructive in central European history.

The Council of Basel (1431–1449) was a major church council that split over conciliarism. In 1833 the canton divided into the half-cantons of Basel-Stadt and Basel-Landschaft. The Peace of Basel (1795) ended hostilities between revolutionary France and Prussia and Spain.

Economy and culture

Basel is a hub of the Swiss chemical and pharmaceutical sector, banking (the Bank for International Settlements is headquartered here), and trade fairs, including Art Basel, the world's leading modern art fair, and Baselworld (watch and jewellery). The city has around 40 museums, the highest density in Switzerland, including the Kunstmuseum Basel and the Fondation Beyeler in neighbouring Riehen.

Basel's carnival, the Basler Fasnacht, begins at 4 a.m. on the Monday after Ash Wednesday with the Morgestraich and is inscribed on UNESCO's list of intangible cultural heritage.

Transport

Basel is a major rail hub linking France, Germany, and Switzerland, with stations Basel SBB and Basel Badischer Bahnhof. EuroAirport Basel Mulhouse Freiburg, located in France, serves the trinational region. The Rhine port is Switzerland's only access to maritime shipping.
