Album

An album is a collection of audio recordings issued as a single item on CD, record, audio tape, or another medium. Albums of recorded sound were developed in the early 20th century as individual 78-rpm records collected in a bound volume resembling a photograph album; the format evolved through the LP record, cassette, and compact disc to today's digital downloads and streaming releases.

History

The word comes from the Latin album, a white tablet on which notices were posted. Record companies in the 1910s–1940s marketed sets of 78-rpm discs in album binders. Columbia Records introduced the long-playing (LP) record in 1948, holding about 22 minutes per side and transforming the album into a canvas for extended musical statements. The 1960s saw the rise of the album as an artistic unit in popular music, exemplified by works such as the Beatles' Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967).

Formats and types

Studio albums of new material are the most common type, alongside live albums, compilations, remix albums, and tribute albums. A typical album runs from about 30 to 60 minutes and contains eight to fifteen tracks. Extended plays (EPs) are shorter, and singles shorter still. Album artwork and packaging became a significant art form in the LP and CD eras.
