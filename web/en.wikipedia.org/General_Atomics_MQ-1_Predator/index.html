The General Atomics MQ-1 Predator is an American remotely piloted aircraft (RPA) built by General Atomics Aeronautical Systems and operated primarily by the United States Air Force and Central Intelligence Agency. Introduced in 1995 for aerial reconnaissance, it pioneered the modern era of armed unmanned aircraft when it was equipped with AGM-114 Hellfire missiles for strike missions.

Design and operation

The Predator is 8.2 metres long with a 14.8-metre wingspan, powered by a Rotax piston engine driving a pusher propeller. It can loiter for more than 14 hours at medium altitude carrying electro-optical/infrared sensors, a synthetic aperture radar, and (in armed versions) two Hellfire missiles. A ground crew pilots it via satellite link from ground control stations, often half a world away from the theatre.

Service

Predators flew surveillance over Bosnia in the 1990s and saw extensive combat service in Afghanistan, Iraq, Yemen, Libya, and Somalia, becoming an emblematic — and controversial — weapon of targeted killing campaigns. The US Air Force retired the MQ-1 in 2018, replacing it with the larger MQ-9 Reaper; exported and derivative versions continue in service elsewhere.
