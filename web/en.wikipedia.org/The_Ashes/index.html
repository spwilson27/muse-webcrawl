The Ashes is a men's international Test cricket series played biennially between England and Australia. The term originated in a satirical obituary published in a British newspaper, The Sporting Times, immediately after Australia's 1882 victory at The Oval, its first Test win on English soil, which stated that English cricket had died and "the body will be cremated and the ashes taken to Australia." The mythical ashes immediately became associated with the 1882–83 series when England captain Ivo Bligh vowed to "regain those ashes."

The urn

The Ashes urn, a small terracotta vessel about 10.5 cm (4 inches) tall, is said to contain the ashes of a wooden bail that was burned during the 1882–83 tour; Bligh was presented with it in Melbourne. The urn is kept permanently at the MCC Museum at Lord's in London regardless of the series result, and the winning captain is presented with a replica (since 1998–99, a Waterford crystal trophy in the urn's shape) for the purposes of celebration.

History

Series are held roughly every two years, alternating between the English summer and the Australian summer, and usually consist of five Test matches. Memorable series include the 1932–33 "Bodyline" tour, Don Bradman's dominant Australian sides, the 1948 "Invincibles," Botham's Ashes of 1981, and England's dramatic 2005 victory, regarded as one of the greatest series ever played. Overall, Australia has won more series and held the urn for long stretches, including 1989–2005, while England's successes have often been dramatic turning points in its cricket culture.

Women's Ashes

The Women's Ashes, contested by the England and Australia women's teams since 1934–35, adopted a points-based multi-format structure in 2013 combining Tests, one-day internationals, and Twenty20 matches.
