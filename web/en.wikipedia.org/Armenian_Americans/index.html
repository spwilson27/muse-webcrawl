Armenian Americans (Armenian: ամերիկահայեր, amerikahayer) are citizens or residents of the United States who have total or partial Armenian ancestry. They form the second-largest community of the Armenian diaspora after Armenians in Russia. The largest concentration is in the Los Angeles metropolitan area — including Glendale, sometimes nicknamed "Little Armenia" together with the Hollywood district of that name — with historic communities in Fresno, Boston and Watertown, New York, and Detroit.

History

The first recorded Armenian in America arrived in Virginia in 1618, but substantial immigration began in the late nineteenth century, driven by Ottoman persecution — the Hamidian massacres of the 1890s and above all the Armenian genocide of 1915, whose survivors founded much of the modern community. Later waves arrived from the Middle East after mid-century upheavals, from Soviet and post-Soviet Armenia, and following the 1988 Spitak earthquake and the Nagorno-Karabakh conflicts.

Culture and politics

Armenian churches, schools, press, and organizations such as the Armenian General Benevolent Union sustain community life. Armenian Americans have been prominent in business, entertainment, and politics, and community organizations have long lobbied for official United States recognition of the Armenian genocide, achieved in congressional resolutions of 2019 and a presidential statement in 2021.
