CGIAR (formerly the Consultative Group on International Agricultural Research) is a global partnership that unites international organisations engaged in research about food security. Its research aims to reduce rural poverty, increase food security, improve human health and nutrition, and sustainable management of natural resources. It is carried out by 15 research centres in close collaboration with hundreds of partners, including national and regional research institutes, civil society organisations, academia, development organisations and the private sector.

History

CGIAR grew out of the Green Revolution institutions founded by the Rockefeller and Ford Foundations — notably the International Rice Research Institute (IRRI, 1960) in the Philippines and CIMMYT (maize and wheat) in Mexico, whose dwarf wheat and rice varieties, associated with Norman Borlaug, transformed yields across Asia and Latin America. The group was formalised in 1971 under the co-sponsorship of the World Bank, FAO and UNDP.

Research

Centres maintain the world's largest public seed collections — more than 700,000 accessions in genebanks — and work on crops (rice, wheat, maize, cassava, banana), livestock, fisheries, agroforestry, climate adaptation and nutrition. Major achievements include flood-tolerant SUB1 rice, vitamin A "Golden Rice" development, disease-resistant cassava, and biological control of cassava mealybug in Africa. Since 2020 the centres have been integrating under the "One CGIAR" reform.
