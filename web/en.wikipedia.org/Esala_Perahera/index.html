Esala Perahera

Decorated elephants at the Kandy Esala Perahera

Esala Perahera (Sinhala: ඇසළ පෙරහැර) is a festival held annually in July and August in Kandy, Sri Lanka, to pay homage to the Sacred Tooth Relic of the Buddha, which is housed at the Sri Dalada Maligawa (Temple of the Tooth). It is considered one of the oldest and grandest of all Buddhist festivals in Sri Lanka, featuring dancers, jugglers, musicians, fire-breathers, and lavishly decorated elephants.

History

The Esala Perahera in Kandy is thought to have originated during the 3rd century BCE as a ritual enacted to request the gods for rainfall. The modern perahera dates to the reign of King Kirthi Sri Rajasinghe (1747–1781), during whose rule the Sacred Tooth Relic, previously the object of a private worship, was integrated into the procession at the request of the monk Upali Thera. The king decreed that the Tooth Relic be carried in procession so the public could worship it.

The procession

The Kandy Perahera begins with the Kap Situveema (planting of the consecrated jackfruit tree), followed by five nights of peraheras held at the four devales dedicated to the guardian gods Natha, Vishnu, Kataragama, and Pattini. The Maligawa Perahera then joins the procession. The main procession continues for five nights as the Kumbal Perahera and five further nights as the Randoli Perahera, growing more elaborate each night. The lead elephant is the Maligawa Tusker, which carries a golden casket (karanduwa) believed to contain the Sacred Tooth Relic; in fact, the relic itself remains in the temple, and the casket carried is a substitute.

Diya Kepeema

The festival concludes with the Diya Kepeema, the water-cutting ceremony, held at the Getambe ford on the Mahaweli River, followed by a daytime procession. The ritual symbolises the drawing of water for the coming year and the seeking of blessings for the island.
