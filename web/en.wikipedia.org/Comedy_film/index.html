Comedy film

A comedy film is a category of film which emphasizes humour. These films are designed to make the audience laugh through amusement. Films in this style traditionally have a happy ending (black comedy being an exception). Comedy is one of the oldest genres in film and it is derived from classical comedy in theatre. Some of the earliest silent films were comedies, as slapstick comedy often relies on visual depictions, without requiring sound.

History

Comedy films first appeared during the silent era, roughly 1895 to 1927. Visual slapstick, developed for vaudeville stages, was a signature of performers such as Charlie Chaplin, Buster Keaton, and Harold Lloyd. The Marx Brothers and later screwball comedies of the 1930s brought verbal wit and rapid-fire dialogue to the screen. In the sound era the genre diversified into romantic comedy, parody, satire, and black comedy.

Subgenres

Subgenres include slapstick, screwball comedy, romantic comedy, parody and spoof, black comedy, gross-out comedy, action comedy, and dramedy. Hybrid forms such as the horror comedy combine comedic elements with other genres.
