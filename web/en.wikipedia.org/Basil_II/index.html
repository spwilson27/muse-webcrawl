Basil II Porphyrogenitus (c. 958 – 15 December 1025), nicknamed the Bulgar-Slayer, was the senior Byzantine emperor for almost fifty years, from 976 until his death in 1025. He and his brother Constantine VIII were crowned as co-emperors before their father Romanos II died in 963, but power was exercised by the generals Nikephoros II Phokas and John I Tzimiskes until Basil came of age.

At the start of his reign, Basil faced two major revolts by the military aristocracy of Anatolia, led by Bardas Skleros and Bardas Phokas, which he suppressed by 989, aided by 6,000 Rus' warriors sent by Vladimir the Great of Kiev — the nucleus of the famous Varangian Guard. In return Basil gave his sister Anna in marriage to Vladimir, an event tied to the Christianization of the Kievan Rus'.

War with Bulgaria

Basil's most celebrated campaigns were against the Bulgarian Empire of Tsar Samuel. After an early defeat at the Gates of Trajan in 986, Basil returned to the offensive methodically, campaigning almost every year. The decisive victory came at the Battle of Kleidion in 1014, after which, according to later sources, Basil blinded the captured Bulgarian army — 99 of every 100 men fully, the hundredth left with one eye to guide them home — earning him his epithet. Samuel died shortly after, and by 1018 Bulgaria was fully incorporated into the empire.

Other campaigns and governance

Basil also campaigned successfully in Georgia and Armenia, annexed territories in the Caucasus, reasserted Byzantine control in southern Italy, and intervened in Sicily. Domestically, he restrained the great landed families of Anatolia through legislation, accumulated a large treasury surplus, and left the empire at its greatest territorial extent since the Muslim conquests of the seventh century. He never married and left no children; on his death the throne passed to his brother Constantine VIII.
