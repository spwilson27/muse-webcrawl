Brunt Ice Shelf

LocationCoats Land, Antarctica
SeaWeddell Sea
Known forHalley Research Station

The Brunt Ice Shelf borders the Antarctic coast of Coats Land between the Dawson-Lambton Glacier and the Stancomb-Wills Glacier Tongue, on the eastern shore of the Weddell Sea. It is named after David Brunt, a British meteorologist who served as Physical Secretary of the Royal Society. The shelf is best known as the site of the British Antarctic Survey's Halley Research Station.

Halley Research Station

The first Halley station was established in 1956 for the International Geophysical Year. Successive stations have been built on the moving shelf, which flows seaward at several hundred metres per year and periodically calves icebergs. Halley VI, opened in 2013, is built on hydraulic legs and skis so it can be relocated; in 2016–17 it was moved 23 km inland because of a growing crack in the shelf.

Cracks and calving

The shelf is crossed by major rifts, including the Halloween Crack, which appeared in October 2016, and Chasm 1, which began widening after decades of dormancy. In February 2021 a large iceberg designated A-74, about 1,270 km², calved from the shelf north of the Halloween Crack; a still larger berg, A-81, calved in January 2023. These events are part of the shelf's natural cycle and are closely monitored by glaciologists using satellite imagery and GPS.

Research

Measurements at Halley led to the discovery of the Antarctic ozone hole, announced in 1985 by British Antarctic Survey scientists using the station's long-running Dobson spectrophotometer record.
