Tiraspol

Tiraspol is the capital and largest city of Transnistria, a breakaway state internationally recognised as part of Moldova, and Moldova's second-largest city by population. It lies on the eastern bank of the Dniester River and has a population of roughly 130,000.

History

Tiraspol was founded in 1792 by the Russian general Alexander Suvorov as a fortress town guarding the new frontier of the Russian Empire after the Russo-Turkish War. Between 1924 and 1940 it was part of the Moldavian ASSR within the Ukrainian SSR, and from 1929 to 1940 its capital. After the Second World War it belonged to the Moldavian SSR. In 1990 the city became the capital of the self-proclaimed Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic (Transnistria), whose independence after the 1992 War of Transnistria is recognised by no UN member state.

Today

Tiraspol retains a strongly Soviet character, with monuments including a statue of Lenin outside the parliament building and a T-34 tank war memorial. It is the home of Sheriff FC, Moldova's most successful football club, and of the Sheriff conglomerate that dominates much of Transnistria's economy. The city hosts the de facto administration, a Russian military presence, and the seat of the OSCE mission's local office.
