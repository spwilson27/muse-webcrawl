The International Mathematical Union (IMU) is an international non-governmental and non-profit scientific organization with the purpose of promoting international cooperation in mathematics. It is a member of the International Science Council (ISC). The IMU is best known as the organiser of the International Congress of Mathematicians (ICM), held every four years, and as the body that awards the Fields Medal, the IMU Abacus Medal (formerly the Nevanlinna Prize), the Gauss Prize and the Chern Medal.

History

The IMU was established in 1920, but it dissolved in 1932 amid disputes over the exclusion of mathematicians from the former Central Powers after the First World War. It was founded anew in 1951 at a convention in New York, and the first General Assembly of the renewed union met in Rome in 1952. Since 2011 the IMU's permanent secretariat has been hosted at the Weierstrass Institute in Berlin, the first fixed headquarters in the union's history.

Activities

Through its commissions and committees—including the International Commission on Mathematical Instruction (ICMI), the Commission for Developing Countries (CDC), and the Committee on Electronic Information and Communication—the IMU supports mathematical research and education, assists developing countries in building mathematical capacity, and publishes the World Directory of Mathematicians. Member countries adhere through national adhering organisations, usually academies of science or mathematical societies.
