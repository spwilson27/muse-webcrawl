Giuseppe Peano ( /piˈɑːnoʊ/ ; Italian: [dʒuˈzɛppe peˈaːno] ; 27 August 1858 – 20 April 1932) was an Italian mathematician and glottologist who was a founder of mathematical logic and set theory, and whose notation and axioms became the standard basis for the natural numbers. The author of over 200 books and papers, he was a founder of mathematical logic and set theory, to which he contributed much notation. The standard axiomatization of the natural numbers is named the Peano axioms in his honor. As part of this effort, he made key contributions to the modern rigorous and systematic treatment of the method of mathematical induction. He spent most of his career teaching mathematics at the University of Turin. He also created an international auxiliary language, Latino sine flexione ("Latin without inflections"), a simplified version of Classical Latin.

Biography

Peano was born and raised on a farm at Spinetta near Cuneo, Piedmont. He entered the University of Turin in 1876, graduated with honours in 1880, and joined its faculty. His 1888 book on the geometrical calculus of Grassmann and his 1889 pamphlet Arithmetices principia, nova methodo exposita — written, remarkably, in Latin — presented the first rigorous axiomatisation of arithmetic. In 1890 he stunned the mathematical world by constructing a continuous curve passing through every point of a square — the first space-filling curve. From the 1890s he directed the Formulario mathematico project, an attempt to write all of mathematics in a symbolic language of his devising.

Work

Peano's axioms define the natural numbers with five axioms: 0 is a natural number; every natural number has a successor; 0 is not the successor of any natural number; distinct numbers have distinct successors; and the induction axiom. His logical symbols — such as ∈ for set membership and ⊃ for implication — were adopted (sometimes adapted) by Russell and Whitehead's Principia Mathematica and became standard notation. He also gave the first correct statement and proof of what is now called the Peano existence theorem for ordinary differential equations, supplied counterexamples that clarified the hypotheses of analysis theorems (including the theory of maxima and minima), and independently developed vector-space ideas in the 1888 Grassmann book.

Latino sine flexione

From 1903 Peano devoted growing energy to his simplified Latin as an international language, presiding over the Academia pro Interlingua. His mathematical teaching reputation suffered as his lectures increasingly promoted the auxiliary language, but the project attracted a following and illustrates his lifelong mania for clarity and simplification.

Legacy

Peano is regarded, with Frege, as a founder of modern logic. The Peano axioms remain the standard foundation for arithmetic in logic textbooks, Peano curves opened the study of fractal space-filling curves, and the lunar crater Peano and an asteroid bear his name.
