Joseph Grimaldi

Joseph Grimaldi
Born: 18 December 1778, London
Died: 31 May 1837, London
Occupation: Clown, actor, dancer

Joseph Grimaldi (18 December 1778 – 31 May 1837) was an English actor, comedian and dancer, who became the most popular English entertainer of the Regency era. In the early 19th century, he expanded the role of Clown in the harlequinade that formed part of British pantomimes.

Career

Born into a theatrical family of Italian descent, Grimaldi appeared on stage from early childhood at Drury Lane and Sadler's Wells. His whiteface make-up, extravagant costumes and physical comedy defined the modern clown archetype; clowns were nicknamed "Joey" after him.

Later life

Years of acrobatic performance ruined his health; he retired in 1823 and gave a farewell benefit in 1828. His memoirs were edited by Charles Dickens in 1838.

Legacy

Grimaldi is commemorated by an annual memorial service for clowns at Holy Trinity Church, Dalston, and by Grimaldi Park in Islington.
