Simon Blackburn (born 12 July 1944) is an English academic philosopher known for his work in metaethics, where he developed the position of quasi-realism, and in the philosophy of language. More recently, he has gained a large general audience for his popularisation of philosophy, notably his introductory book Think: A Compelling Introduction to Philosophy (1999).

Career

Blackburn studied at Clifton College and Trinity College, Cambridge. He taught for many years at Pembroke College, Oxford, before moving to the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, and later returned to Cambridge as Bertrand Russell Professor of Philosophy. He is a fellow of the British Academy and of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, and a foreign honorary member of the Royal Society of Canada.

Philosophical work

In metaethics, Blackburn's quasi-realism attempts to explain how moral discourse can earn the right to the trappings of realism — truth, facts, knowledge — while starting from an expressivist account of moral utterances as projections of attitudes and commitments. His other works include Spreading the Word (1984), Essays in Quasi-Realism (1993), Ruling Passions (1998), Truth: A Guide (2005), and the reference work The Oxford Dictionary of Philosophy, which he edits.
