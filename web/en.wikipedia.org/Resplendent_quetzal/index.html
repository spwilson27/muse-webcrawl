Resplendent quetzal

The resplendent quetzal (Pharomachrus mocinno) is a bird in the trogon family found in montane cloud forests from southern Mexico to western Panama. It is renowned for the male's metallic green and red plumage and exceptionally long upper-tail coverts.

Taxonomy
Order: Trogoniformes
Family: Trogonidae
Genus: Pharomachrus
Species: P. mocinno
Status: Near Threatened

Description

Adults measure roughly 36–40 centimetres in body length. Males have iridescent green upperparts, a red belly, a helmet-like crest, and flowing tail coverts that can extend around 65 centimetres. Females are duller, with grey-brown underparts and shorter ornamental feathers. The apparent colour changes with viewing angle because it is produced partly by feather structure.

Habitat and behaviour

Resplendent quetzals inhabit humid evergreen mountain forests, generally between 900 and 3,200 metres elevation. They feed heavily on fruits, especially wild avocados and other Lauraceae, swallowing many whole and later regurgitating the seeds. They also take insects, lizards, and small frogs, especially when feeding young. Pairs nest in cavities excavated in decaying trees, and both parents incubate the pale blue eggs.

Cultural significance

The bird was sacred in Maya and Aztec cultures and associated with the feathered-serpent deity Quetzalcoatl. Its feathers were traded as luxury goods; birds were often released after plucking because killing a quetzal was prohibited in some traditions. The resplendent quetzal is Guatemala's national bird and appears on its flag, coat of arms, and currency, which is itself called the quetzal.

Conservation

Cloud-forest clearing and fragmentation threaten nesting and feeding trees. Altitudinal movements in search of fruit make connected forest corridors important. The species is listed as Near Threatened, and captive breeding has proved difficult because adults and chicks are sensitive to diet and conditions.
