Crystal Palace, London

The Crystal Palace
Architect: Joseph Paxton
Opened: 1851 (Hyde Park)
Destroyed: 1936 (fire)

The Crystal Palace was a cast-iron and plate-glass structure built in Hyde Park, London, to house the Great Exhibition of 1851. Designed by Joseph Paxton, it was over 550 metres long, used prefabricated standardised components, and was erected in mere months — a landmark of industrialised building.

Re-erection and later life

After the exhibition the building was bought, dismantled and rebuilt in enlarged form on Sydenham Hill in south London, reopening in 1854 as a museum, concert hall and leisure attraction surrounded by parkland with gardens, fountains and life-size dinosaur models. The surrounding district took the name Crystal Palace. The venue hosted concerts, exhibitions and the FA Cup Final for many years, and Crystal Palace Football Club originated among its workers.

Destruction

On the night of 30 November 1936 the palace was destroyed by fire, its glow visible across London. The site is now Crystal Palace Park; two sphinxes and the terraced gardens survive.
