Fashoda Incident

The Fashoda Incident was the climax of imperial territorial disputes between Britain and France in East Africa in 1898. A French expedition under Major Jean-Baptiste Marchand reached Fashoda (now Kodok, South Sudan) on the White Nile in July 1898, seeking to extend French control across Africa.

In September, a British-Egyptian force under General Kitchener, fresh from victory at Omdurman, arrived. The two forces confronted each other politely but firmly. After tense diplomacy in Europe, France ordered Marchand to withdraw in November 1898, and the 1899 Anglo-French Convention settled spheres of influence. The crisis paved the way for the Entente Cordiale of 1904.
