Glossary of ancient Roman religion

The vocabulary of ancient Roman religion was highly technical. Roman ritual was a matter of precise words and actions, and its terminology was preserved by priestly colleges and antiquarian writers such as Varro. This glossary collects central terms of Roman cult practice.

Terms

augur
A priest who interpreted the will of the gods by observing signs, especially the flight of birds. The taking of the auspices (auspicia) was required before major public undertakings.
auspicia
Signs from the gods observed by magistrates or augurs; also the right (ius auspiciorum) of a magistrate to take them.
curia
One of the thirty archaic divisions of the Roman people, each with its own cults and festivals; also the senate house.
devotio
A ritual by which a Roman commander offered himself and the enemy army to the gods of the underworld in exchange for victory.
dies fasti / nefasti
Days in the calendar on which legal business was permitted (fasti) or forbidden (nefasti) for religious reasons.
evocatio
The ritual "calling out" of the tutelary deity of a besieged enemy city, promising it a temple in Rome.
flamen
A priest assigned to a particular deity; the three major flamines served Jupiter (flamen Dialis), Mars and Quirinus.
haruspex
A diviner, originally Etruscan, who inspected the entrails (exta) of sacrificed animals, especially the liver.
lares
Household and ancestral guardian deities honoured at a household shrine (lararium) and at crossroads.
lustrum
A purificatory sacrifice (suovetaurilia) performed after the census, held every five years — hence the term for a five-year period.
numen
The divine power or will of a god; in imperial times, the divine spirit of the emperor.
penates
The gods of the store-cupboard, guardians of the household's food supply, worshipped with the lares.
pietas
Dutiful respect toward the gods, one's parents, and the state; a cardinal Roman virtue.
pontifex
A member of the college of pontiffs, which supervised Roman religious law; its head was the pontifex maximus.
religio
The scrupulous observance of traditional ritual obligations toward the gods; the root of the modern word "religion".
sacrum
That which belongs to the gods; a sacred thing, place or rite, as opposed to the profane.
suovetaurilia
A sacrifice of a pig (sus), sheep (ovis) and bull (taurus) used in major lustrations.
templum
A ritually inaugurated space, on the ground or in the sky, within which omens were observed — not originally a building.
vestales
The Vestal Virgins, priestesses of Vesta who tended the perpetual fire in her temple in the Roman Forum.
votum
A vow: a promise to a god of an offering or temple conditional on the god granting a request.
