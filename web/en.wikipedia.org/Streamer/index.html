Streamer

A streamer or livestreamer is a type of social media influencer who broadcasts themselves online through a live stream to an audience. Streamers emerged in the mid-2010s, live-streaming various activities that could be anything from video games, lifestyle, political commentary, and "in real life" (IRL) experiences. Many streamers on various platforms have grown into full-time careers, attracting millions of viewers and building large online communities.

History

Live streaming of gameplay grew out of platforms such as Justin.tv and its gaming spin-off Twitch, launched in 2011, alongside Ustream and later YouTube Live. The ease of streaming software such as OBS Studio, along with the rise of esports, allowed streamers such as Ninja (Tyler Blevins) to reach mainstream prominence; Ninja's 2018 Fortnite stream with Drake set viewership records. Platforms including Twitch, YouTube, Facebook Gaming, Kick, and TikTok Live now host millions of streamers.

Content and culture

Popular content includes video game playthroughs and speedruns, esports, "Just Chatting" conversation, ASMR, gambling streams, virtual streamers (VTubers) who appear via animated avatars, and IRL streams filmed in public places. Streamers typically interact with their audience in real time via text chat, and communities develop their own slang, emotes, and in-jokes. Streamers monetize through platform partner and affiliate programs, subscriptions, viewer donations and "bits", sponsorships, and merchandise.

Impact

Streamers are influential in marketing games and products, and the largest command audiences comparable to traditional media celebrities. The field has also raised controversies over gambling promotion, harassment, copyright strikes, and platform moderation.
