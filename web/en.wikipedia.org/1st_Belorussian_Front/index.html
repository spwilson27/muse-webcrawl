The 1st Belorussian Front (Russian: 1-й Белорусский фронт) was a major formation of the Red Army during World War II, being equivalent to a Western army group. It was the largest and most powerful of the Soviet fronts that took part in the final offensives against Nazi Germany.

History

The front was created in October 1943 as the Belorussian Front and renamed the 1st Belorussian Front in February 1944. Under Marshal Konstantin Rokossovsky it played a central role in Operation Bagration, the destruction of German Army Group Centre in the summer of 1944, and in the Lublin–Brest and Warsaw–Poznań offensives.

Battle of Berlin

In November 1944 command passed to Marshal Georgy Zhukov. In April 1945 the front launched the main Soviet assault on Berlin, breaking through the German defences on the Seelow Heights and encircling the capital. Its troops raised the Soviet flag over the Reichstag and accepted the surrender of the Berlin garrison on 2 May 1945.

Composition

At the time of the Berlin operation the front included several combined-arms and tank armies, among them the 3rd Shock Army, 5th Shock Army, 8th Guards Army, 1st Guards Tank Army and 2nd Guards Tank Army, supported by the 16th Air Army.
