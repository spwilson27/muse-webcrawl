Mnemonic

A mnemonic device (from Greek mnēmē, "memory"), or memory device, is any learning technique that aids information retention or retrieval in the human memory, often by associating hard-to-remember material with something easier to remember.

Types

Common mnemonic techniques include:

  • Acronyms and acrostics — e.g., "ROYGBIV" for the colours of the rainbow, or "Every Good Boy Does Fine" for the lines of the treble clef staff.
  • Method of loci — the ancient "memory palace" technique of placing items to be remembered along a familiar route or within a familiar building.
  • Rhyme and song — setting information to rhythm or melody, as in alphabet songs.
  • Chunking — grouping long strings, such as telephone numbers, into smaller units.
  • Visualization and association — forming vivid mental images linking items together.
  • Spaced repetition — reviewing material at increasing intervals.

History

Mnemonic techniques were highly developed in classical antiquity, when orators such as Cicero and Quintilian described the method of loci, attributed by legend to the poet Simonides of Ceos. Memory arts (ars memorativa) remained a central part of rhetoric and education through the Middle Ages and Renaissance, with figures such as Giordano Bruno developing elaborate memory systems.

Applications

Mnemonics are used in education, medicine (for example, lists of symptoms or cranial nerves), music, and everyday tasks. In computing, "mnemonic" also refers to short codes, such as assembly-language instruction names, and to mnemonic seed phrases used to recover cryptocurrency wallets.
