Sisingamangaraja XII (18 February 1845 – 17 June 1907), born Patuan Bosar Sinambela, was the last priest-king (raja parmalim) of the Batak peoples of northern Sumatra and a leader of resistance against Dutch colonial rule. He is honoured in Indonesia as a National Hero.

Resistance

Inheriting the Sisingamangaraja title in 1876, he confronted the Dutch expansion into the Batak lands that followed the Aceh War. In 1878 he led an attack on Dutch positions at Bakal Batu, opening a guerrilla war that lasted nearly three decades. Allied at times with Acehnese fighters, he sustained resistance from strongholds around Lake Toba and the highlands despite repeated Dutch expeditions, the burning of his capital at Bakkara, and campaigns by colonial troops including forces under Gotfried van Daalen.

Death and legacy

Pursued into the forests of Dairi, Sisingamangaraja XII was killed on 17 June 1907 by a patrol of the Korps Marechaussee commanded by Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje's protégé Hans Christoffel; his sons and his daughter Lopian also died in the final period of the war. His death ended organised Batak resistance. He was reburied with honours in 1953 at Soposurung, Balige, and in 1961 President Sukarno declared him a National Hero of Indonesia. His name is borne by streets, a university in Medan (formerly), and the airport serving Lake Toba.
