Radioactive waste

Radioactive waste is a type of hazardous waste that contains radioactive material. It is a byproduct of many activities, including nuclear medicine, nuclear research, nuclear power generation, nuclear decommissioning, rare-earth mining, and nuclear weapons reprocessing.

Classification

Waste is commonly classified as low-level waste (such as contaminated clothing and tools), intermediate-level waste (resins, reactor components), and high-level waste (spent nuclear fuel and reprocessing liquids), which remains highly radioactive and thermally hot for thousands of years.

Management and disposal

Strategies include temporary surface storage in pools and dry casks, vitrification of liquid waste in glass, and deep geological repositories such as Finland's Onkalo facility and the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant in New Mexico for transuranic waste. Political and technical disputes over sites such as Yucca Mountain illustrate the long timescales involved.

Environmental and health concerns

Improper disposal can contaminate groundwater and soil. Major accidents and legacy sites, including Hanford in the United States and Mayak in Russia, require decades-long cleanup. International oversight is provided in part by the IAEA.
