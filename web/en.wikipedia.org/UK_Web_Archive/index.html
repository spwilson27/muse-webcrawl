UK Web Archive

The UK Web Archive preserves the UK's published web output.

The UK Web Archive is a web archive operated by the British Library in partnership with the other UK legal deposit libraries — the National Library of Scotland, the National Library of Wales, the Bodleian Libraries, Cambridge University Library and the library of Trinity College Dublin. It collects and preserves websites published in the United Kingdom as part of the nation's published output, making them available as a research resource recording British life and culture online.

Legal deposit

The archive began selectively collecting sites by permission in 2005. The Legal Deposit Libraries (Non-Print Works) Regulations 2013, under the Legal Deposit Libraries Act 2003, extended legal deposit to electronic publications and empowered the libraries to harvest the UK web domain at scale without seeking individual permissions. Since 2013 the archive has run an annual crawl of the .uk domain and associated UK-published sites, capturing billions of files. Because of copyright restrictions, most of the legal-deposit collection can be consulted only on library premises, while an openly accessible portion built from permissive or licensed material is available online.

Collections

Alongside the domain crawl, curators build special collections on themes and events — elections, the Olympics, the COVID-19 pandemic, blogs and community sites — often incorporating nominations from the public and researchers. The archive works with the International Internet Preservation Consortium and uses standard formats such as WARC for long-term preservation.
