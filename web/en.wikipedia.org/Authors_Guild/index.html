The Authors Guild is the United States' oldest and largest professional organisation for published writers. Founded in 1912 as the Authors' League of America, it advocates for authors' rights in copyright, fair contracts and free expression.

Activities

The Guild provides model contracts, legal advice, seminars and advocacy on issues such as copyright protection, e-book royalty rates and library lending. It has been a party or amicus in major copyright litigation, notably Authors Guild v. Google, concerning the Google Books scanning project, in which courts ultimately held Google's use to be fair use.

History

The organisation began as the Authors' League of America in 1912, with the Authors Guild and the Dramatists Guild as its principal branches. The Guild has represented prominent American writers throughout its history and continues to comment on issues including generative AI and publishing consolidation.
