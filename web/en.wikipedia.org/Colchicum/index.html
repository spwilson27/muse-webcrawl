Colchicum

Kingdom: Plantae

Order: Liliales

Family: Colchicaceae

Genus: Colchicum L.

Colchicum is a genus of flowering plants in the family Colchicaceae, containing roughly 160 species of perennial herbs that grow from corms. They are native to Europe, North Africa, western and central Asia, and parts of Africa. Species are commonly known as autumn crocus, meadow saffron, or naked ladies — the last from the flowers appearing in autumn without leaves, which emerge in spring.

Description

The flowers, which resemble true crocuses but have six stamens (crocuses have three), are goblet-shaped and usually pink, lilac, purple, or white, sometimes chequered. The best-known species is Colchicum autumnale, widespread in damp meadows across Europe. The plants are grown as ornamentals for their late-season flowers, and numerous cultivars are available.

Toxicity and medicine

All parts of the plant contain the alkaloid colchicine, a potent toxin that disrupts cell division; ingestion has caused fatal poisonings, sometimes through confusion with wild garlic or saffron. In purified form colchicine is a long-established medicine for gout and is used for familial Mediterranean fever and pericarditis. In plant breeding, colchicine is used to induce polyploidy.
