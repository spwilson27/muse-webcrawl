Battle of Benevento

The Battle of Benevento was fought on 26 February 1266 near Benevento in southern Italy between the forces of Charles of Anjou and those of Manfred of Sicily. Charles's decisive victory and Manfred's death ended Hohenstaufen rule in the Kingdom of Sicily and established Angevin power in southern Italy, reshaping the politics of the medieval Mediterranean.

Background

Manfred, natural son of Emperor Frederick II, had ruled Sicily since 1258 despite papal opposition. Pope Urban IV and then Clement IV offered the crown to Charles of Anjou, brother of Louis IX of France. Crowned in Rome in January 1266, Charles marched south; Manfred advanced to block him near Benevento, though some of his German mercenaries were still crossing the mountains.

Battle

Manfred's Saracen archers and German heavy cavalry initially pressed the French, but Charles committed his reserves in tight formation and his men attacked the horses of the German knights. When parts of Manfred's Italian baronage deserted, the Sicilian position collapsed. Manfred, refusing flight, charged into the melee and was killed; his body was found days later under a cairn of stones.

Aftermath

Manfred's widow Helena was captured and his children imprisoned, ending the Hohenstaufen line in Sicily (the last claimant, Conradin, was executed after Tagliacozzo in 1268). Dante places Manfred in Purgatory in the Divine Comedy, recalling the battle and his unshriven death. Angevin rule endured until the Sicilian Vespers of 1282 split the kingdom.
