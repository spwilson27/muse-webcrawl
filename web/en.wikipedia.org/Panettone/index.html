Panettone

Panettone is a Milanese type of sweet bread and fruitcake, originally from Milan, Italy, usually prepared and enjoyed for Christmas and New Year in Western, Southern, and Southeastern Europe, as well as in South America, Eritrea, Australia and North America.

Description

Panettone has a cupola shape made from a tall paper mould and a soft, airy, brioche-like crumb rich in butter and egg yolk, studded with candied orange and citron peel and raisins. The dough is proofed over several days with a naturally fermented starter (lievito madre), giving the bread its flavour and its long shelf life. After baking, loaves are traditionally hung upside down to prevent the delicate dome from collapsing as it cools.

History and legends

Several legends surround its origin: one tells of the nobleman Ughetto degli Atellani, who wooed a baker's daughter named Toni by creating a rich bread ("pan de Toni" — "Toni's bread"); another concerns a kitchen scullion named Toni who improvised a dessert for a Milanese ducal Christmas banquet. Whatever its origin, panettone became industrialised in the early 20th century by firms such as Motta and Alemagna, and it spread worldwide with Italian emigration, becoming a Christmas staple especially in Peru and Brazil.
