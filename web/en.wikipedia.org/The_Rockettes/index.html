The Rockettes

Founded: 1925

Home venue: Radio City Music Hall, New York City

Known for: Precision dance, eye-high kicks

The Rockettes are an American precision dance company, founded in 1925 in St. Louis, Missouri. They have performed at Radio City Music Hall in New York City since 1932 and are best known for starring in the Radio City Christmas Spectacular and for their signature eye-high kick line, in which the dancers appear to move as one.

History

The troupe was created by Russell Markert, inspired by the British Tiller Girls and the Ziegfeld Follies. Originally called the Missouri Rockets, they were renamed the Roxyettes and then the Rockettes when they moved to Radio City Music Hall in 1932. Markert directed the company until 1971.

Performances

The Rockettes star in the annual Christmas Spectacular, first staged in 1933, which includes the famous "Parade of the Wooden Soldiers" and the "Living Nativity". They have also performed at the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, Super Bowl halftime shows, and presidential inaugurations. Auditions are famously demanding: dancers must be between 5 feet 6 inches and 5 feet 10½ inches tall and proficient in tap, jazz, and ballet.
