Airbus A321neo

The Airbus A321neo is a single-aisle airliner developed and produced by Airbus. It is the longest and heaviest member of the A320neo family and the successor to the A321ceo. "Neo" stands for "new engine option": the aircraft offers a choice of CFM International LEAP-1A or Pratt & Whitney PW1100G geared turbofan engines, together with sharklet wingtip devices, giving roughly 20% lower fuel burn per seat than its predecessor.

Development and variants

The A321neo first flew in February 2016 and entered service with Virgin America in 2017. Airbus developed longer-range variants: the A321LR (long range), certified in 2018 with extra fuel tanks enabling transatlantic routes, and the A321XLR (extra long range), which entered service in 2024 with a range of up to about 4,700 nautical miles — the longest of any single-aisle airliner. A flexible "Airbus Cabin Flex" door arrangement allows high-density layouts of up to 244 seats; typical two-class seating is around 180–200.

Specifications

The A321neo is 44.51 m long with a wingspan of 35.8 m. Maximum takeoff weight for the XLR is 101 tonnes. It has become one of Airbus's best-selling models, accumulating thousands of orders and accounting for a growing majority of A320neo family deliveries as airlines favour its capacity and range.
