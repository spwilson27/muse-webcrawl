Samuel Eilenberg

Born: 30 September 1913, Warsaw, Congress Poland

Died: 30 January 1998, New York City, USA

Institutions: University of Michigan, Indiana University, Columbia University

Known for: Category theory, Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces, Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms, Eilenberg swindle

Awards: Wolf Prize (1986), Leroy P. Steele Prize (1987)

Samuel Eilenberg (September 30, 1913 – January 30, 1998) was a Polish-American mathematician who co-founded category theory with Saunders Mac Lane. His work in algebraic topology reshaped the subject, and in later life he made foundational contributions to automata theory and formal language theory. He spent most of his career at Columbia University in New York.

Career

Eilenberg received his Ph.D. from the University of Warsaw in 1936, studying with Kazimierz Kuratowski and Karol Borsuk in the celebrated Polish school of topology. He left Poland for the United States in 1939, narrowly ahead of the German invasion. After posts at the University of Michigan and Indiana University, he joined Columbia in 1947 and remained there until his retirement, chairing the department and training generations of algebraists.

Mathematics

With Norman Steenrod, Eilenberg axiomatised homology theory (the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms, published in their Foundations of Algebraic Topology, 1952). Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces, which realise a single homotopy group, became basic tools of homotopy theory. In their 1945 paper “General Theory of Natural Equivalences”, Eilenberg and Mac Lane introduced the concepts of category, functor and natural transformation — created, by their own later account, so that “natural” could be defined — which have since become a unifying language across mathematics and computer science. The witty Eilenberg swindle shows how infinite sums of isomorphic objects can “prove” paradoxical equalities, a trick used in K-theory. Unusually for a pure mathematician of his generation, Eilenberg was a member of the Bourbaki group — its only non-French founding-era member from outside the French system. With Calvin Elgot and others he later wrote the multi-volume Automata, Languages, and Machines.

Art collection

Eilenberg was also a celebrated collector and dealer-scholar of South and Southeast Asian art, especially Indonesian sculpture and textiles; much of his collection was donated to the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York.
