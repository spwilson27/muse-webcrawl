Fumaric acid

Fumaric acid is the chemical compound with formula HO2CCH=CHCO2H. It is the trans isomer of butenedioic acid, while maleic acid is the cis isomer. A white crystalline solid, fumaric acid occurs widely in nature — it is named after the plant genus Fumaria — and is a key intermediate in the citric acid cycle, where it is formed from succinate and converted to malate.

Uses

Fumaric acid is used as a food additive (E number E297), an acidulant in beverages, baking powders and confectionery, because of its strong sour taste and low hygroscopicity. Industrially it is used in the manufacture of unsaturated polyester resins, alkyd resins, printing inks and paper sizing. Its esters, notably dimethyl fumarate, have been used pharmaceutically, including in the treatment of multiple sclerosis and psoriasis.

Production

It is produced industrially by isomerisation of maleic acid, itself made by catalytic oxidation of benzene or butane, and can also be produced by fermentation of glucose with Rhizopus fungi.
