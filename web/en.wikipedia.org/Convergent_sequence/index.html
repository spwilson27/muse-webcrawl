Convergent sequence

In mathematics, a convergent sequence is a sequence whose terms approach a specific value, called its limit, as the index increases. Sequences that are not convergent are said to be divergent. The notion of convergence is made precise by the epsilon definition: a sequence of real numbers converges to a limit L if, for every positive ε, there is a natural number N such that all terms beyond the Nth lie within ε of L.

Examples

The sequence 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, … converges to 0. The constant sequence c, c, c, … converges to c. The sequence 1, −1, 1, −1, … diverges because it oscillates without settling on a single value.

Properties

Every convergent sequence of real numbers is bounded, and in the real numbers every Cauchy sequence converges — a property known as completeness. Limits respect the operations of addition, multiplication, and (where the limit is non-zero) division, which makes it possible to compute the limits of complicated sequences from simpler ones. Convergent sequences are foundational to calculus, analysis, and the definition of continuity. See also limit of a sequence and Cauchy sequence.
