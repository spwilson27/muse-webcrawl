Sahih al-Bukhari

AuthorMuhammad al-Bukhari (810–870)
LanguageArabic
SubjectHadith
GenreHadith collection

Sahih al-Bukhari (Arabic: صحيح البخاري‎) is a collection of hadith — reports of the sayings, actions and tacit approvals of the Islamic prophet Muhammad — compiled by the Persian scholar Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari (810–870). Regarded by Sunni Muslims as the most authentic (sahih) of all hadith collections, it is one of the six canonical collections known as the Kutub al-Sittah.

Compilation

Al-Bukhari spent some sixteen years travelling through the Islamic world — to centres of learning in Bukhara, Nishapur, Baghdad, Basra, Kufa, Mecca, Medina, Syria and Egypt — collecting hadith from more than a thousand teachers. Tradition holds that he examined some 600,000 reports and selected only those meeting his exceptionally stringent criteria for the reliability of each chain of transmission (isnad) and the integrity of every narrator in it.

Contents

The collection contains roughly 7,500 hadith counting repetitions, or around 2,600 distinct reports without them. It is organised into books (around 97 in standard editions) arranged by subject — beginning with the Book of Revelation and proceeding through faith, knowledge, ritual ablution, prayer, almsgiving, fasting, pilgrimage, trade, and on to medicine, dress, manners and the interpretation of dreams — so that the work functions as a comprehensive guide to belief and practice as well as a historical record. Its full title is commonly given as al-Jami' al-Musnad as-Sahih al-Mukhtasar min Umur Rasul Allah wa Sunanihi wa Ayyamihi.

Significance

Sahih al-Bukhari, together with the companion collection Sahih Muslim, forms the twin foundation of Sunni hadith literature; the two are jointly known as the Sahihayn ("the two authentic collections"). The work has attracted a vast commentary tradition, most famously Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani's Fath al-Bari, and remains central to Islamic scholarship and devotional life. It has been translated into English in full, most widely in the rendering by Muhammad Muhsin Khan.
