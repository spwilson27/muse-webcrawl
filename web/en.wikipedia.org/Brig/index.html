Brig

A brig under sail, showing square sails on both masts

A brig is a two-masted sailing vessel with square sails on both masts (the fore and main). Brigs were fast and manoeuvrable, and were used both as naval warships and as merchant vessels throughout the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. They are not to be confused with brigantines, which carry square sails on the foremast only, nor with the modern naval use of "brig" for a shipboard detention cell.

Rigging

A brig carries a fore mast and a main mast, both square-rigged, with a fore-and-aft sail (a spanker or gaff sail) behind the main mast, staysails between the masts, and jibs before the foremast. The rig required a relatively large crew for its size because of the many square sails, but it gave the vessel speed and the ability to sail close to the wind compared with a full-rigged ship.

History

The brig developed in the seventeenth century from earlier two-masted types and became a mainstay of European and American navies as a sloop-of-war: brigs carried ten to eighteen guns and served as escorts, dispatch vessels, and commerce raiders. Famous examples include the USS Niagara of the War of 1812. Merchant brigs traded worldwide, notably in the coal trade from Newcastle. As steam propulsion spread in the later nineteenth century, the brig declined, though barquentines and brigantines persisted in coastal trades.

Modern brigs

A few brigs survive or have been built as sail-training ships, including the British TS Royalist and the brig Niagara, a reconstruction based at Erie, Pennsylvania.
