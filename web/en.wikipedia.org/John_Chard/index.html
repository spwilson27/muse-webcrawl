John Chard

[Image placeholder: portrait of John Chard]

John Rouse Merriott Chard VC (21 December 1847 – 1 November 1897) was a British Army officer who received the Victoria Cross, the highest military decoration for valour "in the face of the enemy" that can be awarded to members of the British armed forces. He is best known for commanding the small British garrison that defended the mission station at Rorke's Drift in Natal during the Anglo-Zulu War on 22–23 January 1879.

Chard, a lieutenant in the Royal Engineers, happened to be at Rorke's Drift supervising repair work on the ponts over the Buffalo River when news arrived of the British disaster at Isandlwana. With Lieutenant Gonville Bromhead of the 24th Regiment, he organised the defence of the station: some 150 men held improvised barricades of mealie bags and biscuit boxes against repeated attacks by an estimated 3,000–4,000 Zulu warriors through the night, until the attackers withdrew.

Aftermath

Eleven Victoria Crosses were awarded for the defence of Rorke's Drift, the most ever given for a single action by one regiment. Chard was promoted and fêted on his return to Britain, presented to Queen Victoria. He continued his career in the Royal Engineers, serving in Malta, Cyprus, and at home, rising to the rank of colonel. He died of cancer of the tongue at Hatch Beauchamp, Somerset, in 1897.

Chard was portrayed by Stanley Baker in the 1964 film Zulu.
