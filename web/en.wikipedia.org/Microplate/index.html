Microplate

A microplate is a small fragment of lithosphere that moves independently of the larger tectonic plates surrounding it. Microplates are typically bounded by faults, spreading ridges, or subduction zones on all sides, and they commonly form where large plates break apart during continental rifting or where oceanic crust fragments near complex plate boundaries.

Microplate
TypeTectonic fragment
CompositionOceanic or continental lithosphere
BoundariesFaults, ridges, subduction zones
ExamplesEaster Plate, Juan Fernández Plate, Victoria Plate

Formation

Microplates form in several geological settings. During the early stages of continental breakup, rifting can isolate small blocks of continental crust that later rotate and translate as the surrounding ocean basin opens. In oceanic settings, changes in spreading direction can fracture a plate along pre-existing weaknesses, spawning new small plates. Near triple junctions, the geometry of three diverging boundaries often creates a enclosed fragment that is captured between larger plates.

Notable examples

Easter Plate

The Easter Plate, located west of Easter Island in the southeastern Pacific Ocean, is one of the largest and best-studied microplates. It is bounded by the East Pacific Rise and the Chile Rise, and it rotates rapidly in geological terms, at several degrees per million years.

Juan Fernández Plate

North of the Easter Plate lies the Juan Fernández Plate, another Pacific microplate situated near the Juan Fernández Islands offshore Chile. Together with the Easter Plate and the neighbouring Nazca and Antarctic plates, it forms a classic microplate system used to test models of rigid-plate rotation.

African microplates

Within the East African Rift, geodetic measurements indicate that the African Plate is splitting into the Nubian and Somali plates, with smaller fragments such as the Victoria Plate and Rovuma Plate rotating between them like ball bearings between larger blocks.
