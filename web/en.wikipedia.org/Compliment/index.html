Compliment

A compliment is an expression of praise, congratulation or encouragement. Compliments may be sincere or insincere; the latter are often used as social lubrication or flattery.

Social function

Sociolinguists such as Janet Holmes have studied compliments as speech acts that express solidarity and maintain social relationships; their subjects and expected responses vary strongly by culture. In some cultures, compliments are readily accepted with thanks; in others, modesty norms favour deflection or denial. Compliments about possessions may, in some settings, imply a request for the item.

A "backhanded compliment" (or left-handed compliment) is an insult disguised as praise — for example, praising someone in a way that implies low expectations. The word compliment is frequently confused with its homophone complement, meaning something that completes or goes well with something else.
