Polygenic score

A polygenic score (PGS), also called a polygenic risk score (PRS) in medical contexts, is a number that summarizes the estimated effect of many genetic variants on an individual's phenotype or disease risk. It is calculated as a weighted sum of trait-associated alleles, with weights derived from genome-wide association studies (GWAS).

Construction

Scores are computed by summing the number of risk alleles an individual carries, each multiplied by an effect size estimated in a GWAS. Methods range from simple pruning-and-thresholding of significant SNPs to Bayesian approaches such as LDpred and PRS-CS that model linkage disequilibrium across the whole genome.

Applications and limitations

Polygenic scores have been developed for traits such as height, educational attainment, schizophrenia, coronary artery disease, and type 2 diabetes. Their predictive accuracy is limited by heritability and GWAS sample sizes, and they transfer poorly across ancestries because most discovery studies have used European-ancestry samples — a major equity concern. Scores are used in research, risk stratification, and controversially in embryo selection.
