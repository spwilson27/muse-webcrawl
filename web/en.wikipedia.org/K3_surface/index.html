K3 surface

In mathematics, a K3 surface is a complex or algebraic two-dimensional manifold that is simply connected and admits a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic 2-form — equivalently, a smooth compact complex surface with trivial canonical bundle and irregularity zero. K3 surfaces are central objects in algebraic geometry, complex geometry and string theory.

Name and examples

The name, coined by André Weil, honours the three mathematicians Kummer, Kähler and Kodaira, and alludes to K2, the second-highest mountain. Key examples include smooth quartic surfaces in projective 3-space, Kummer surfaces (constructed from abelian surfaces), and complete intersections of a quadric and a cubic.

Properties

Every complex K3 surface is Kähler and is a Calabi–Yau manifold of complex dimension two, hence admits Ricci-flat metrics. All complex K3 surfaces are diffeomorphic to one another. Their second cohomology lattice is an even unimodular lattice of rank 22, and the period map gives a precise classification via the Torelli theorem. Algebraic K3 surfaces are polarised by an ample line bundle and form a 19-dimensional moduli space.

Applications

K3 surfaces arise in the study of elliptic fibrations, lattice theory, mirror symmetry and string compactifications, and they are related to exceptional structures such as the Mathieu group M24 through "Mathieu moonshine".
