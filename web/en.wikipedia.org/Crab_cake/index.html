Maryland crab cakes

A crab cake is a variety of fishcake popular in the United States, composed of crab meat and various other ingredients, such as breadcrumbs, mayonnaise, mustard, eggs, and seasonings. It is then sautéed, baked, grilled, deep-fried, or broiled. Crab cakes are traditionally associated with the Chesapeake Bay area, particularly the state of Maryland.

Styles

Two classic styles are common in the Chesapeake region. Boardwalk cakes are breaded and deep-fried, often heavily filled. Restaurant (or "gourmet") cakes use little filler, are made with jumbo lump meat of the blue crab (Callinectes sapidus), and are typically broiled. Maryland crab cakes are classically seasoned with Old Bay seasoning and served with a remoulade or tartar sauce, on saltine crackers or in a sandwich.

The term "crab cake" dates to the 1930s, appearing in Crosby Gaige's New York World's Fair Cook Book (1939) as "Baltimore Crab Cakes". Similar patties made with other shellfish or fish appear in many cuisines worldwide.
