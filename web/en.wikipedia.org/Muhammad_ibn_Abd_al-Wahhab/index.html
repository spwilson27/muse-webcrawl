Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab

Born1703, 'Uyayna, Najd, Arabia
Died1792 (aged 88–89), Diriyah
Known forFounder of the Wahhabi movement; author of Kitab al-Tawhid

Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab (1703–1792) was an Arabian Islamic scholar, theologian and religious leader from Najd who founded the revivalist movement later known as Wahhabism. He taught an uncompromising monotheism (tawhid) and condemned practices such as saint veneration, tomb pilgrimage and Sufi rituals as idolatrous innovations (bid'a), calling for Islam to be purified by return to the Quran and the practice of the first generations (salaf).

Life and alliance with the Saudis

Born in 'Uyayna into a family of Hanbali scholars, he studied in Medina and travelled in Basra and elsewhere before preaching his doctrine. Expelled from his hometown, in 1744 he formed a pact with Muhammad ibn Saud, ruler of Diriyah: Ibn Saud would protect and spread the movement politically, and Ibn Abd al-Wahhab would guide it religiously. This alliance underpinned the first Saudi state (Emirate of Diriyah) and endures today between the Saudi royal family and his descendants, the Al ash-Sheikh.

Works and influence

His principal work is Kitab al-Tawhid ("The Book of Monotheism"). His teachings, spread with Saudi power in the 19th and 20th centuries, profoundly shaped Islam in Arabia and, through funding of mosques and schools abroad, Salafi movements worldwide. Admirers see him as a reformer reviving authentic Islam; critics link his doctrine's strictness to intolerance and, in the modern era, debate its relationship to jihadist movements — a connection his followers and the Saudi establishment reject.
