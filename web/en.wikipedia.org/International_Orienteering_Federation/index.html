International Orienteering Federation

The International Orienteering Federation (IOF) is the international governing body of the sport of orienteering. Founded on 21 May 1961 in Copenhagen, Denmark, the IOF recognises and governs four official orienteering disciplines: foot orienteering, mountain bike orienteering, ski orienteering, and trail orienteering. Its headquarters are in Karlstad, Sweden. The federation has around 70 member federations and is recognised by the International Olympic Committee, though orienteering is not part of the Olympic programme.

Competitions

The IOF organises the World Orienteering Championships, held annually since 2003 (previously biennially from 1966), the Orienteering World Cup series, and world championships in the other disciplines, including the World Ski Orienteering Championships and the World Mountain Bike Orienteering Championships. Orienteering has featured in the World Games since 2001. The IOF also sets the rules of competition, the specifications for orienteering maps, and control description symbols used worldwide.

History

Orienteering originated in Scandinavia in the late nineteenth century as a form of military training, and became an organised competitive sport in Sweden and Norway in the early twentieth century. The ten founding members of the IOF in 1961 were Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, Denmark, Finland, Hungary, Norway, Sweden, Switzerland, West Germany and East Germany. Membership grew steadily as the sport spread through Europe, Oceania, Asia and the Americas. The federation has long campaigned for Olympic inclusion, and in the 2020s has promoted sprint and urban formats of the sport to broaden its audience.
