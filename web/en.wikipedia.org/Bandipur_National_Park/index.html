Bandipur National Park is a national park and tiger reserve in the Chamarajanagar district of Karnataka, southern India. Established in 1974 as one of the first reserves under Project Tiger, it protects dry deciduous forests at the foothills of the Western Ghats and forms part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, together with the adjoining Nagarhole, Mudumalai, and Wayanad sanctuaries — one of the largest protected forest tracts in India and home to the world's largest population of wild Asian elephants in a single landscape.

History

The forests were a private game reserve of the Maharaja of Mysore from 1931 (as the Venugopala Wildlife Park) before becoming a national park in 1974. The park is traversed by National Highway 766 (formerly NH-212) toward Wayanad and Ooty; night traffic through the park has been banned since 2009 to reduce wildlife road deaths, a frequently litigated conservation measure.

Flora and fauna

Bandipur's vegetation is dominated by teak, rosewood, sandalwood, and giant clumping bamboo. Wildlife includes Bengal tigers, Indian elephants, leopards, gaur, sloth bears, dholes, sambar, chital, four-horned antelope, and more than 200 bird species, including the crested hawk-eagle and Indian roller. The Kabini River bounds the park to the northwest, and the Moyar River to the south. In 2018–19, drought and fires in the park drew national attention, and the reserve supports one of India's highest tiger densities.
