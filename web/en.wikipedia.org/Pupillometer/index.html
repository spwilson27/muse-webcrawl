Pupillometer

A pupillometer is an instrument used to measure the diameter of the pupil of the eye, and changes in that diameter in response to light or other stimuli, a measurement known as pupillometry. Modern pupillometers are typically digital devices that use infrared illumination and a camera to record the size of the pupil without the measurement light itself provoking a pupillary light reflex.

History

Early pupillometers were simple graduated scales or cards held beside the eye, allowing a clinician to estimate pupil size in millimetres. In the mid-twentieth century, photographic and later infrared television techniques enabled continuous recording of pupil diameter, making pupillometry practical for research in ophthalmology, neurology, and psychology. Automated infrared pupillometers became standard in clinical and research settings from the 1980s onward.

Clinical use

Pupil size and reactivity are important indicators of neurological function. Pupillometers are used to quantify the pupillary light reflex in patients with head injury, in pharmacological studies of drugs acting on the autonomic nervous system, and in research on pain, fatigue, and cognitive load, where small changes in pupil diameter correlate with mental effort. Quantitative pupillometry has largely replaced subjective flashlight assessment in neurocritical care units, because it detects subtle asymmetries and trends that the unaided eye may miss.

Types

Handheld pupillometers are portable devices placed over one eye at a time, delivering a standardized light stimulus and reporting pupil size, constriction velocity, and latency. Binocular research pupillometers and video-based eye trackers record both eyes continuously at high sampling rates and are used in experimental psychology and human factors research. Some pupillometers are integrated into perimeter and aberrometer instruments used in refractive surgery planning.
