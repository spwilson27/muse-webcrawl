Hamdi Ulukaya

Hamdi Ulukaya
Born: 26 October 1972, İliç, Turkey
Occupation: Founder and CEO of Chobani

Hamdi Ulukaya (born 1972) is a Turkish-American businessman and philanthropist, best known as the founder and chief executive of Chobani, the Greek-style yogurt brand that became the best-selling yogurt in the United States.

Early life

Ulukaya was born into a Kurdish dairy-farming family in İliç, in eastern Turkey. He emigrated to the United States in 1994 to study English and business, settling in upstate New York, where he began importing his family's feta cheese and later founded the Euphrates brand.

Chobani

In 2005 Ulukaya bought a disused Kraft yogurt plant in South Edmeston, New York, with a Small Business Administration loan. Working with Turkish yogurt master Mustafa Kaya, he launched Chobani in 2007. The strained, high-protein yogurt grew rapidly, and the company opened enormous plants in Idaho and Australia. Ulukaya retained full ownership for many years and became a billionaire.

Philanthropy and activism

Ulukaya signed the Giving Pledge in 2015, committing the majority of his wealth to charity. He founded the Tent Partnership for Refugees to help companies hire and support refugees, and Chobani became known for employing refugees in its plants. In 2016 he gave employees shares worth up to 10% of the company. He received the Global Citizen and Oslo Business for Peace awards, and Chobani acquired the coffee company La Colombe in 2023.
