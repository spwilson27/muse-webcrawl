Proscenium arch

The proscenium arch frames the stage like a picture frame.

A proscenium arch is the frame or arch separating the stage from the auditorium in a theatre, through which the audience views the performance. A theatre whose stage is framed this way is called a proscenium theatre, the dominant form for Western theatres from the seventeenth century onward.

History

The permanent proscenium arch was introduced in Italian Renaissance theatre design, notably at the Teatro Farnese in Parma (1618), which is often credited with the first permanent example. The arch created a "picture frame" for illusionistic scenery, hid the machinery of scene changes, and supported the front curtain.

Effect on performance

The proscenium establishes a "fourth wall" between actors and audience, favouring presentational realism and elaborate scenic effects, with wing and fly space concealed behind it. Twentieth-century theatre movements reacted against this separation, developing thrust stages, theatre in the round, and black-box spaces that remove the frame.
