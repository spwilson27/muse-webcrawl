United States special operations forces are the elite military units of the United States Armed Forces specially organised, trained and equipped to conduct and support special operations. All active and reserve special operations forces are assigned to the United States Special Operations Command (USSOCOM), a unified combatant command headquartered at MacDill Air Force Base near Tampa, Florida.

Overview

Special operations forces include some of the most recognisable units in the American military: the Army's Green Berets, 75th Ranger Regiment and Delta Force; the Navy SEALs and their support units; the Air Force's special tactics squadrons, pararescuemen and combat controllers; and the Marine Corps Forces Special Operations Command (MARSOC) Raiders. These units are trained for missions including direct action, special reconnaissance, unconventional warfare, foreign internal defence, counter-terrorism, hostage rescue and military information support operations.

History

American special operations trace their heritage to colonial-era ranger units such as Rogers' Rangers, and to Second World War formations including the Office of Strategic Services, the Marine Raiders and the First Special Service Force. The modern joint structure emerged after the failed 1980 hostage rescue attempt in Iran, Operation Eagle Claw, which exposed gaps in joint special operations capability. The Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986 and the Nunn–Cohen Amendment led to the creation of USSOCOM in 1987, giving the community its own budget authority and a four-star commander.

Component commands

Each service maintains a component command under USSOCOM: Army Special Operations Command (USASOC) at Fort Liberty, North Carolina; Naval Special Warfare Command (NAVSPECWARCOM) at Coronado, California; Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC) at Hurlburt Field, Florida; and Marine Forces Special Operations Command (MARSOC) at Camp Lejeune, North Carolina. The Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC) controls the military's tier-one special mission units, including Delta Force and SEAL Team Six.

Selection and training

Entry into special operations is highly selective. Candidates undergo assessment and selection courses with high attrition, followed by qualification courses lasting months or years covering small-unit tactics, marksmanship, languages, survival, parachuting, diving and advanced communications. Many operators complete the demanding programs of the John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School or the Naval Special Warfare Center.

Operations

Since 2001, United States special operations forces have been in near-continuous combat, conducting counter-terrorism campaigns across the Middle East, Africa and South Asia. Their best-known mission was Operation Neptune Spear in May 2011, in which SEALs killed al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden in Abbottabad, Pakistan. Critics have noted the strain of this operational tempo on personnel and questioned the expanding global footprint of the units.
