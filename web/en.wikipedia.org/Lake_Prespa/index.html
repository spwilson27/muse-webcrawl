Lake Prespa

Location: North Macedonia, Albania, Greece
Type: tectonic lake
Surface area: 273 km²
Max. depth: 54 m
Surface elevation: 853 m
Islands: Golem Grad, Mal Grad

Lake Prespa is a high lake in the Balkan Peninsula, shared by North Macedonia, Albania and Greece. It is the highest tectonic lake in the Balkans, lying at an elevation of about 853 metres.

The system consists of the larger Great Prespa Lake (259 km² in the usual reckoning of the shared system) and the smaller Lake Small Prespa, separated by an isthmus in Greek territory. Great Prespa drains underground through karst channels beneath the Galičica mountains into Lake Ohrid, which lies about 150 metres lower.

Wildlife

The Prespa lakes are internationally important wetlands, listed under the Ramsar Convention. They host one of the world's largest breeding colonies of Dalmatian pelicans, along with pygmy cormorants and many endemic fish and invertebrate species. Parts of the lake are protected within national parks in all three countries.

History

The island of Golem Grad in the Macedonian part preserves Roman and medieval remains. In the tenth century the Tsar Samuel of Bulgaria built a fortress near the lake; the village of Asamati and the church of St. George at Kurbinovo (1191), famous for its frescoes, are regional landmarks. The Prespa Agreement of 2018, resolving the Macedonia naming dispute, was signed on the lake's Greek shore at Psarades.
