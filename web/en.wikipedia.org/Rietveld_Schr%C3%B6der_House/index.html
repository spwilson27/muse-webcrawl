Location: Utrecht, Netherlands
Architect: Gerrit Rietveld
Built: 1924
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2000)

Rietveld Schröder House

The Rietveld Schröder House in Utrecht, Netherlands, is a landmark of modern architecture designed in 1924 by Gerrit Thomas Rietveld for Truus Schröder-Schräder and her children. It is regarded as the only building built fully according to the principles of the De Stijl movement.

Design

The house appears as an abstract composition of white and grey planes, floating balconies, and accents in the primary colours red, blue, and yellow, with windows and lines that seem to dissolve the boundary between inside and outside. Its upper floor is a flexible open space: sliding and revolving panels can divide it into bedrooms and living areas or open it into a single room, an arrangement Schröder wanted so the family could live unconventionally. Built-in furniture, including Rietveld's designs, is integral to the scheme.

Legacy

Schröder lived in the house until her death in 1985. It is now a museum administered by the Centraal Museum and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000 as an icon of the Modern Movement in architecture.
