Chaff (countermeasure)

Chaff, originally called Window by the British Royal Air Force and Düppel by the German Luftwaffe, is a radar countermeasure consisting of many thin strips of metal-coated material dispensed by aircraft or ships to create false radar returns and confuse enemy radar and radar-guided missiles.

History

Window was first used operationally during the Hamburg raids of July 1943 (Operation Gomorrah), when clouds of aluminium strips cut to half the wavelength of German radar blinded air-defence systems and sharply reduced bomber losses. Both sides had developed the technique independently but withheld it for fear of retaliation in kind.

Modern use

Modern chaff consists of aluminium-coated glass fibres cut to lengths tuned to expected radar wavelengths and ejected from dispensers as cartridges. It is standard equipment on warships and combat aircraft for seducing radar-guided missiles away from their targets, and is also used as a training aid. Related decoys include flares, which counter infrared-guided weapons.
