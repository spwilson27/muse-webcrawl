The Decisive Moment (French: Images à la sauvette) is a 1952 book of photographs by Henri Cartier-Bresson, one of the most influential photography books of the 20th century.

Concept

The English title comes from a quotation of Cardinal de Retz: "There is nothing in this world that does not have a decisive moment." Cartier-Bresson defined the idea as the simultaneous recognition, in a fraction of a second, of the significance of an event and of a precise organisation of forms that give the event its proper expression. His essay in the book became a manifesto for candid street photography: photographs are taken, not made, and the photographer should never intervene in the scene.

Contents and design

The lavish large-format volume presents 126 photographs from Cartier-Bresson's travels in Europe, India, China, and the United States, with a cover designed by Henri Matisse. It established Cartier-Bresson's reputation and codified the aesthetics of photojournalism associated with the Magnum Photos agency, which he had co-founded in 1947.
