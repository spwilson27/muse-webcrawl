Engineering

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Engineering is the practice of using natural science, mathematics, and the engineering design process to solve technical problems, increase efficiency and productivity, and improve systems. Modern engineering comprises many subfields which include designing and improving infrastructure, machinery, vehicles, electronics, materials, and energy systems.

The term engineering is derived from the Latin ingenium, meaning "cleverness", and ingenerate, meaning "to contrive, devise". The earliest engineers built fortifications, irrigation works, aqueducts and siege engines; the profession emerged as a distinct, science-based discipline during the Industrial Revolution.

Main branches

The major traditional branches are civil engineering (structures, transport, waterworks), mechanical engineering (machines and thermal systems), electrical engineering (electricity, electronics and electromagnetism), and chemical engineering (industrial chemical processes). Around these have grown many others: aerospace, biomedical, computer, environmental, industrial, materials, mining, nuclear, petroleum, software and structural engineering, among others. Interdisciplinary fields such as mechatronics and systems engineering combine several branches.

Practice and method

Engineers apply the engineering design process: defining a problem and constraints, modelling and analysis (increasingly with computer simulation), prototyping, testing and iteration, within limits set by cost, safety, regulation and sustainability. In many jurisdictions the title of professional or chartered engineer is licensed, and engineers are bound by codes of ethics that place public safety first. Famous failures — bridge collapses, spacecraft disasters — are studied as case histories to improve practice.

History

Landmarks range from the pyramids and Roman aqueducts through the steam engine, railways, electrification, the aeroplane, the integrated circuit and the internet. The first engineering schools, such as France's École Polytechnique (1794), formalised the mathematical training of the profession.
