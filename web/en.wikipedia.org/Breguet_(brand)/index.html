Breguet is a Swiss luxury watch, clock and jewellery manufacturer founded by Abraham-Louis Breguet in Paris in 1775. The company is currently a member of The Swatch Group. Breguet is one of the oldest surviving watchmaking brands and a pioneer of numerous watchmaking technologies, most notably the tourbillon, invented by Abraham-Louis Breguet in 1801, as well as the world's first wristwatch, commissioned in 1810 and produced in 1812 for Caroline Bonaparte, Queen of Naples.

The brand is noted for its distinctive design codes: guilloché dials engine-turned by hand, blued steel "Breguet hands" with eccentric moon tips, coin-edge case bands, and individually numbered timepieces. Breguet's records were meticulously archived, and the company continues to maintain registers of every watch sold since the late eighteenth century.

History

Abraham-Louis Breguet (1747–1823), born in Neuchâtel, established his workshop on the Quai de l'Horloge in Paris in 1775. He became watchmaker to the French court, and his clients included Queen Marie Antoinette — for whom the legendary No. 160 "Marie-Antoinette" pocket watch, incorporating every known complication of the era, was commissioned — as well as Napoleon Bonaparte, Tsar Alexander I of Russia, and the Duke of Wellington.

After the founder's death the firm passed through his descendants and several owners, including the British watchmaking family of Edward Brown. In 1970 the brand was acquired by Chaumet, and in 1987 it passed to Investcorp. The Swatch Group acquired Breguet in 1999 under Nicolas G. Hayek, who led a revival of the manufacture, moving production to the Vallée de Joux in Switzerland.

Inventions and notable timepieces

  • The tourbillon (patented 1801)
  • The first wristwatch (1812, for Caroline Murat, Queen of Naples)
  • The Breguet overcoil balance spring
  • The pare-chute shock-protection system
  • The "Marie-Antoinette" No. 160 grande complication pocket watch, completed in 1827, stolen from a Jerusalem museum in 1983 and recovered in 2007
  • Perpetual calendars, minute repeaters, and the first self-winding watch (the "perpétuelle", c. 1780)
