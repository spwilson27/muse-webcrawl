Guyana

Co-operative Republic of Guyana
Capital: Georgetown
Official language: English
Population: ~800,000
Currency: Guyanese dollar (GYD)

Guyana, officially the Co-operative Republic of Guyana, is a country on the northern mainland of South America. It is bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the north, Brazil to the south and southwest, Venezuela to the west, and Suriname to the east. With an area of about 215,000 square kilometres (83,000 sq mi), Guyana is the third-smallest sovereign state by area in mainland South America, after Uruguay and Suriname.

History

The region was inhabited by Indigenous peoples, including the Arawak, Carib, and Warao, before European colonisation. The Dutch established the colonies of Essequibo, Demerara, and Berbice in the 17th and 18th centuries; Britain took control in the late 18th century, and the territories were united as British Guiana in 1831. Plantation agriculture relied first on enslaved Africans and, after emancipation in the 1830s, on indentured labourers, principally from India, whose descendants form a large share of the population today. Guyana gained independence from the United Kingdom on 26 May 1966 and became a republic on 23 February 1970, under Prime Minister Forbes Burnham. His successor Desi Bouterse-era politics aside, the country transitioned to multi-party elections; Cheddi Jagan became president in 1992.

Geography

Guyana's terrain includes a flat coastal plain where most of the population lives, a belt of sand and clay, the rugged Pakaraima Mountains and the Guiana Shield highlands in the interior, and savannah in the south. Dense rainforest covers about 80% of the country. Kaieteur Falls, on the Potaro River, is among the world's tallest single-drop waterfalls. Guyana, "land of many waters", has numerous rivers, including the Essequibo, Demerara, and Berbice.

Economy

The economy has traditionally depended on agriculture (sugar and rice), gold, bauxite, timber, and diamonds. The discovery and development of major offshore oil fields by ExxonMobil and partners, beginning production in 2019, transformed Guyana into one of the world's fastest-growing economies and a significant petroleum producer.

Demographics and culture

Guyana is ethnically diverse: Indo-Guyanese form the largest group, followed by Afro-Guyanese, mixed, and Indigenous (Amerindian) peoples. It is the only mainland South American country where English is the official language, and Guyanese Creole is widely spoken. Guyana is a member of the Caribbean Community (CARICOM), whose secretariat is headquartered in Georgetown.
