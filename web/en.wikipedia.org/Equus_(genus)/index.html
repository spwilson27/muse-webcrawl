Equus (genus)

Equus
Family: Equidae
Genus: Equus Linnaeus, 1758
Includes: horses, asses, zebras

Equus is a genus of mammals in the family Equidae, which includes horses, asses and zebras. Within the Equidae, Equus is the only recognised extant genus, comprising seven living species. Like the family as a whole, the genus has a long fossil record extending back to the Eocene.

Living species

The living species are the domestic and wild horse (Equus ferus, including Przewalski's horse), the African wild ass (E. africanus, ancestor of the donkey), the onager (E. hemionus), the kiang (E. kiang), and three zebra species: plains zebra (E. quagga), mountain zebra (E. zebra) and Grévy's zebra (E. grevyi). Hybrids such as mules and zorses are generally sterile.

Characteristics

Members of Equus are large, slender-bodied grazers with long heads and necks, thin legs ending in a single hoofed toe (the third digit), and tails ending in a tuft or fully haired. They are hindgut fermenters adapted to coarse grassland forage, and most live in social groups — harems or herds.

Evolution

The genus arose in North America around the end of the Pliocene and spread to Eurasia and Africa over the Bering land bridge and other routes. Wild horses became extinct in the Americas at the end of the Pleistocene and were reintroduced by Europeans in the 16th century.
