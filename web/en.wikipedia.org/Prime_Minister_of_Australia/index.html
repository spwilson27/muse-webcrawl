Prime Minister of Australia

The Prime Minister of Australia is the head of government of the Commonwealth of Australia. The prime minister leads the Cabinet and is the leader of the political party or coalition holding a majority in the House of Representatives. The office is not mentioned in the Constitution of Australia; it exists by convention of the Westminster system.

The prime minister is appointed by the governor-general, who formally exercises executive power on the advice of the prime minister and ministers. By convention, the governor-general commissions the person best able to command the confidence of the House of Representatives.

History

The first prime minister was Edmund Barton, who led the government at Federation in 1901 and oversaw the first federal election. Notable long-serving prime ministers include Robert Menzies, founder of the Liberal Party, who served over 18 years in total; John Howard (1996–2007); and Bob Hawke (1983–1991). Julia Gillard became Australia's first female prime minister in 2010.

Between 2010 and 2022, Australia saw a rapid turnover of prime ministers, with five changes of leader through party-room ballots as well as elections. Anthony Albanese became the 31st prime minister after the 2022 federal election, leading the Australian Labor Party to victory.

Role and residence

The prime minister chairs cabinet meetings, sets the government's legislative agenda, represents Australia internationally, and advises the governor-general on matters including the appointment of judges and the dissolution of parliament. The official residences are The Lodge in Canberra and Kirribilli House in Sydney.
