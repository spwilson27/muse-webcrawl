Carbonate minerals are minerals containing the carbonate ion, CO32−, combined with metal cations such as calcium, magnesium, and iron. They form a major class in mineral classification systems and are the principal constituents of sedimentary rocks such as limestone and dolomite rock.

Properties and identification

Carbonates are generally soft (hardness about 3), have perfect rhombohedral cleavage in the calcite group, and characteristically effervesce ("fizz") when treated with dilute hydrochloric acid, releasing carbon dioxide. Calcite and its polymorph aragonite are both calcium carbonate; dolomite is a calcium-magnesium carbonate.

Formation and importance

Most carbonates form in marine environments from the accumulation of shells and skeletal material, or precipitate chemically from water. They are important as building materials, cement feedstock, ore minerals (malachite, azurite), and as the largest long-term reservoir of carbon in the Earth's crust.
