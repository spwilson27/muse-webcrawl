Friends Committee on National Legislation

The Friends Committee on National Legislation (FCNL) is a national, nonpartisan Quaker organization that lobbies the United States Congress and the administration to advance peace, justice, and environmental stewardship. Founded in 1943 by members of the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers), it is headquartered in Washington, D.C., across from the Hart Senate Office Building, and describes itself as the oldest faith-based lobbying organization in Washington.

History and work

FCNL was established during the Second World War, when Quakers in the United States sought a permanent presence in the capital to advocate on issues such as conscientious objection, relief for war victims, and the creation of the United Nations. Its policy positions are set through a discernment process among Quaker meetings and churches across the country, expressed in a periodically revised "World We Seek" policy statement.

Programs

The organization works on issues including peacebuilding and conflict prevention, nuclear disarmament, gun violence prevention, immigration, Native American rights, economic justice, and climate change. Its associated foundation operates the Quaker Welcome Center and the William Penn House, and FCNL runs an advocacy corps and young-adult programs training grassroots lobbyists.
