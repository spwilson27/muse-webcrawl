Exarchate of Africa

Existedc. 585/591 – 698
CapitalCarthage
Parent empireByzantine Empire

The Exarchate of Africa (Latin: Exarchatus Africae) was an administrative division of the Byzantine Empire encompassing its North African territories, governed from Carthage by an exarch who combined supreme civil and military authority. It was created by the emperor Maurice around 585–591, following the model of the Exarchate of Ravenna in Italy, after Justinian's generals had reconquered North Africa from the Vandals in 533–534.

History

The exarchate comprised roughly modern Tunisia, northeastern Algeria, and northwestern Libya, together with Byzantine possessions that at times included Sardinia, Corsica, the Balearics, and a strip of southern Spain. The exarchs contended continually with Berber (Moorish) kingdoms in the interior. The exarch Heraclius the Elder launched the revolt in 608 that brought his son Heraclius to the imperial throne in 610.

Fall

From the 640s the exarchate faced the Arab Muslim conquests: raids reached Africa in 642, and despite the exarch Gregory's defeat and death at Sbeitla in 647, Carthage held out for decades more. Kairouan was founded as an Arab base in 670. Carthage fell briefly in 697, was briefly recovered by a Byzantine expedition, and was finally captured and destroyed in 698, ending Byzantine Africa.
