Coronation of the British monarch

The coronation of the British monarch is a ceremony in which the monarch of the United Kingdom is formally invested with regalia and crowned at Westminster Abbey. It usually takes place several months after the death of the previous monarch, following a period of mourning. The service is performed by the Archbishop of Canterbury and incorporates an Anglican Eucharist.

Ceremony

The essential elements of the service have remained largely unchanged for over a thousand years. They include the recognition and oath, the anointing with holy oil, the investiture with the regalia — including the spurs, sword, armills, orb, ring and sceptres — the crowning with St Edward's Crown, the enthronement, and the homage. The monarch sits in King Edward's Chair, beneath which was formerly set the Stone of Scone.

History

Coronations have been held at Westminster Abbey since 1066. The most recent was the coronation of Charles III and Camilla on 6 May 2023, the first coronation of a British monarch in the 21st century, following that of Elizabeth II on 2 June 1953. The form of the service derives substantially from the Liber Regalis of 1377.

Participants

Besides the monarch and consort, participants include the great officers of state, bishops, peers who carry the regalia, and representatives of the Commonwealth realms and foreign states. Guests number in the thousands, though the 2023 service was considerably smaller than that of 1953.
