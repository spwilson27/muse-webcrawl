Governor of Virginia

The Governor of the Commonwealth of Virginia is the head of government of the US state of Virginia and the commander-in-chief of the state's national guard. The governor serves a four-year term and is uniquely barred by the Virginia Constitution from serving consecutive terms, making Virginia the only US state with such a restriction—though former governors may run again after an intervening term.

History

The office traces its origins to the colonial governors of the Colony of Virginia, beginning in 1607 at Jamestown. Under the first Virginia Constitution of 1776, the governor was elected annually by the General Assembly; Patrick Henry was the first governor of the independent commonwealth, and Thomas Jefferson the second. The Constitution of 1851 provided for popular election. Eight US presidents were born in Virginia, and several served as governor, including Jefferson, James Monroe, and John Tyler.

Powers and duties

The governor signs or vetoes legislation (including a line-item veto on budget bills), grants pardons and clemency, appoints officials and judges to vacancies, and commands the Virginia National Guard when not federalized. The governor is elected in odd-numbered years—the November following a US presidential election—alongside the separately elected lieutenant governor and attorney general.

Residence

The official residence is the Executive Mansion on Capitol Square in Richmond, designed by Alexander Parris and occupied since 1813, making it the oldest governor's residence in the United States still used for its original purpose.
