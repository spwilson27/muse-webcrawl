Herod Agrippa

Herod Agrippa (c. 11 BC – AD 44), also known as Agrippa I, was a king of Judea from the Herodian dynasty, grandson of Herod the Great and brother of Herod Antipas. Raised in Rome, he cultivated friendships with the imperial family that eventually won him a kingdom reunifying much of his grandfather's realm.

Agrippa I
Reign: AD 41–44 (Judea)
Dynasty: Herodian
Died: AD 44, Caesarea

Rise to power

After years of debt and exile, Agrippa's fortunes revived under Caligula, who granted him the territories of Philip the Tetrarch in AD 37. Under Claudius he received Judea and Samaria, becoming king of a realm nearly as extensive as Herod the Great's. He courted Jewish favor, observed religious law in Jerusalem, and fortified the capital.

In the New Testament and Josephus

The Acts of the Apostles portrays Agrippa executing James the son of Zebedee and imprisoning Peter, and describes his sudden death at Caesarea after accepting divine honors — an account broadly paralleled by Josephus, who attributes his fatal illness to an episode at public games. His son, Agrippa II, was the last Herodian ruler. See also Judea.
