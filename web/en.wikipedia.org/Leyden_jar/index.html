Leyden jar

A Leyden jar (or Leiden jar) is an early device for storing static electric charge, and effectively the first capacitor. It consists of a glass jar lined inside and out with metal foil, with a conducting rod passing through an insulating stopper to the inner foil.

Invention

The device was invented independently by Ewald Georg von Kleist in 1745 and by Pieter van Musschenbroek in the Dutch city of Leiden in 1745–46, from which it takes its name. Early experimenters were startled by the powerful shocks it could deliver, and banks of connected jars ("batteries") powered much eighteenth-century electrical research.

Working

When charged by an electrostatic generator, equal and opposite charges accumulate on the two foils, separated by the glass dielectric. Discharging the jar through a conductor produces a brief spark. The Leyden jar remained a standard laboratory instrument into the late 19th century.
