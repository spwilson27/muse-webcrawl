Cisalpine Gaul

Cisalpine Gaul (Latin: Gallia Cisalpina, "Gaul on this side of the Alps") was the part of Italy inhabited by Celts (Gauls) during the 4th and 3rd centuries BC, corresponding roughly to the modern Po Valley in northern Italy. Its name contrasted with Transalpine Gaul, beyond the Alps in modern France.

History

Gallic tribes such as the Insubres, Boii, and Senones settled the region from about 400 BC; the Senones under Brennus sacked Rome around 390 BC. Rome conquered the region during the 3rd and 2nd centuries BC, founding colonies such as Placentia and Cremona and building the Via Flaminia and Via Aemilia. Cisalpine Gaul was administered as a Roman province from about 81 BC. Julius Caesar governed it together with Transalpine Gaul and used it as the base for his conquest of Gaul; he granted Roman citizenship to its inhabitants in 49 BC. In 42 BC the region was formally incorporated into Italy by Octavian.

People

The province produced notable Romans including the poets Catullus of Verona and Virgil of Mantua.
