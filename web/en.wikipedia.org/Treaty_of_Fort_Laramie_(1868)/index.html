The Treaty of Fort Laramie (also called the Sioux Treaty of 1868) is an agreement between the United States and bands of the Lakota Sioux, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho, signed at Fort Laramie in present-day Wyoming on 29 April and 6 May 1868. It ended Red Cloud's War, established the Great Sioux Reservation — including the sacred Black Hills — and guaranteed the Lakota and Arapaho exclusive use of that land, with allotments, annuities and services promised in return for peace.

Background

Red Cloud's War (1866–1868) was fought over the Bozeman Trail, which cut through Lakota hunting lands toward Montana goldfields. Sioux and Cheyenne forces besieged Forts Phil Kearny and C. F. Smith, inflicting the Fetterman disaster of 1866, and the US government sought peace with changing priorities as railroad construction advanced. The resulting treaty conceded nearly everything Red Cloud demanded, including the abandonment and burning of the Bozeman Trail forts.

Provisions

The Great Sioux Reservation covered all of present-day South Dakota west of the Missouri River, including the Black Hills. Article 12 required the consent of three-quarters of adult male Indians for any future land cession. The treaty promised annuities, clothing, farming instruction, schools and agencies, and guaranteed hunting rights "so long as the buffalo may range thereon in such numbers as to justify the chase".

Violation and legacy

After George Armstrong Custer's 1874 expedition confirmed gold in the Black Hills, miners flooded in; the US seized the hills by agreement in 1876 and the war of 1876–1877 followed, including the Battle of the Little Bighorn. In United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians (1980), the Supreme Court held the taking illegal and awarded compensation with interest, then exceeding $100 million. The Sioux nations have refused the money — now well over a billion dollars with interest — maintaining that the Black Hills were never for sale and that they want the land back.
