Motivic cohomology

Motivic cohomology is a cohomology theory in algebraic geometry whose existence was conjectured by Alexander Grothendieck in the 1960s as part of his theory of motives. It is designed as a universal cohomology theory for algebraic varieties: its groups are expected to specialize to, or receive maps from, other theories such as étale cohomology, de Rham cohomology, and algebraic K-theory.

Development

Key steps toward the theory included Spencer Bloch's work on higher Chow groups, which provided a candidate definition of motivic cohomology for smooth varieties in terms of cycles on products with algebraic simplices. Vladimir Voevodsky then constructed a full theory in the 1990s, embedding motivic cohomology in a triangulated category of motives and defining motivic homotopy theory with Morel. Voevodsky used the theory to prove the Milnor conjecture relating Milnor K-theory to Galois cohomology, for which he received the Fields Medal in 2002; motivic methods also underlay the proof of the Bloch–Kato conjecture.

Properties

Motivic cohomology groups Hp,q are bigraded, with the second index called the weight. Special cases recover familiar invariants: H1,1 relates to units of the coordinate ring, H2,1 to the Picard group, and diagonal groups to Chow groups of cycles. The theory remains an active area of research connecting algebraic geometry, number theory, and homotopy theory.
