Joseki

In the game of Go, jōseki (定石 in Japanese; jeongseok in Korean; dìngshì in Chinese) are studied, standardized sequences of moves in the corner of the board, generally regarded as giving both players an locally optimal, balanced result. Joseki form a vast body of professional knowledge accumulated over centuries of play.

Characteristics

Joseki usually begin with a corner enclosure or an approach move (kakari) against a corner stone. Thousands of joseki and variations exist, ranging from simple six-move sequences to complex fights of thirty moves or more involving ladders and ko threats. A sequence is joseki only in context: the "best" continuation depends on the surrounding stones, the ladder situation, and the whole-board strategy, so choosing between joseki is as important as knowing them.

Study

Professional players memorize large joseki dictionaries, but strong amateurs are advised to learn the reasoning — the shape, tesuji, and trade-offs — behind each sequence rather than rote memorization, since a move played without understanding invites punishment by an opponent who deviates. With the advent of superhuman Go AI such as AlphaGo, many classic joseki were revised or fell out of professional fashion after 2016.
