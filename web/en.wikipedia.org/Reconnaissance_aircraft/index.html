Reconnaissance aircraft

A reconnaissance aircraft is a military aircraft designed or adapted primarily to perform aerial reconnaissance — gathering imagery, signals, and other intelligence about enemy forces and territory. Reconnaissance is among the oldest military uses of aviation, dating to observation balloons and the aircraft of the First World War.

Roles

Reconnaissance aircraft collect photographic and radar imagery (IMINT), electronic emissions (ELINT), and signals intelligence (SIGINT). Some are purpose-built, such as the high-altitude Lockheed U-2 and the supersonic SR-71 Blackbird, while others are modified fighters or patrol aircraft fitted with camera and sensor pods.

Modern developments

Since the late 20th century, unmanned aerial vehicles such as the RQ-4 Global Hawk have taken over many long-endurance reconnaissance missions, while satellites handle strategic surveillance. Tactical reconnaissance continues to be performed by manned aircraft and smaller drones.
