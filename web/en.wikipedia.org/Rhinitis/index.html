Rhinitis

Rhinitis, also known as coryza, is irritation and inflammation of the mucous membrane inside the nose. Common symptoms are a stuffy nose, runny nose, sneezing, and post-nasal drip. Rhinitis is commonly caused by viral infections (the common cold) or allergies, but may also arise from non-allergic, non-infectious causes.

Types

Infectious rhinitis

The common cold is the most frequent form of infectious rhinitis, usually caused by rhinoviruses. It is self-limiting, typically resolving within a week to ten days.

Allergic rhinitis

Allergic rhinitis is caused by an IgE-mediated response to airborne allergens such as pollen (seasonal rhinitis, or "hay fever"), house dust mites, animal dander and moulds (perennial rhinitis). It affects a substantial fraction of the population in industrialised countries and is closely associated with asthma and atopic dermatitis.

Non-allergic rhinitis

Non-allergic rhinitis includes vasomotor rhinitis (triggered by irritants, weather changes or strong odours), rhinitis medicamentosa (rebound congestion from overuse of topical decongestants), hormonal rhinitis (including in pregnancy), gustatory rhinitis, occupational rhinitis and atrophic rhinitis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on history and examination; allergy testing (skin prick or serum specific IgE) can identify triggers in allergic rhinitis. Treatment depends on type: allergen avoidance, intranasal corticosteroids (the most effective single therapy for allergic rhinitis), oral or intranasal antihistamines, saline irrigation, short courses of decongestants for infectious rhinitis, and allergen immunotherapy for selected patients with allergic disease.
