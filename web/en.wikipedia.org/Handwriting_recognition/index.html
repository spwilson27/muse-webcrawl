Handwriting recognition

Handwriting recognition (HWR), also known as handwriting text recognition, is the ability of a computer to receive and interpret intelligible handwritten input from sources such as paper documents, photographs, touchscreens and stylus devices, converting it into machine-encoded text.

Off-line and on-line recognition

Off-line recognition works from static images of writing, similar to optical character recognition but harder because of the variability of handwriting. On-line recognition, used on tablets and PDAs since devices such as the Apple Newton and Palm Pilot, captures the sequence and dynamics of strokes, which greatly aids accuracy.

Techniques

Early systems used hand-crafted features and template matching or hidden Markov models. Modern systems use neural networks — convolutional and recurrent networks and transformers — trained on large corpora, achieving near-human accuracy on constrained tasks such as postal address reading and bank cheque processing.

Applications

Applications include digitising archives and forms, note-taking apps, signature verification, mail sorting and accessibility tools.
