Peterborough Abbey, now Peterborough Cathedral, was a Benedictine abbey in Peterborough, England. Founded in the Anglo-Saxon period and refounded after the Viking invasions, it grew into one of the wealthiest and most important monasteries in medieval England. Its great church, built mainly between 1118 and the thirteenth century, survives almost intact as the cathedral of the Diocese of Peterborough.

History

Anglo-Saxon foundation

A monastery was first established at Medeshamstede, as Peterborough was then known, around 655 by the Mercian nobleman Peada. The house was destroyed by the Danes in 870 and refounded as a Benedictine abbey around 966 under King Edgar, becoming one of the centres of the tenth-century monastic revival led by Dunstan and Æthelwold.

Norman rebuilding

After a fire in 1116 destroyed the Saxon church, Abbot John de Sais began the present great church in 1118. Built in the Romanesque style, it was consecrated in 1238. The abbey was closely involved in the events of the Norman Conquest: the rebel Hereward the Wake famously plundered it in 1070 in protest at the appointment of a Norman abbot.

Later Middle Ages

The abbey accumulated extensive estates and relics, including — it was claimed — an arm of St Oswald. The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle was continued at Peterborough until 1154, producing the important "Peterborough Chronicle". Two queens were buried in the church: Catherine of Aragon in 1536 and Mary, Queen of Scots in 1587 (her body was later removed to Westminster Abbey).

Dissolution

The abbey was surrendered to the Crown in 1539 during the Dissolution of the Monasteries. Unusually, its church was preserved when Henry VIII made it the cathedral of a new diocese in 1541, reputedly out of respect for Catherine of Aragon's burial there.

Architecture

The cathedral is celebrated for its Early English Gothic west front with three great arches, its painted wooden nave ceiling (unique in England), and the retrochoir known as the New Building. The Hedda Stone, an Anglo-Saxon carved stone, survives from the earlier monastery.
