Kaunas (Lithuanian: [ˈkɐʊnɐs]) is the second-largest city in Lithuania after Vilnius and the historical centre of Lithuanian economic, academic and cultural life. Located at the confluence of the Neris and Nemunas rivers, it served as the temporary capital of Lithuania between the two world wars, while Vilnius was under Polish control.

History

Kaunas Castle was first mentioned in 1361 in connection with the wars against the Teutonic Order; the town received Magdeburg rights in 1408 and became an important trading post of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania. It suffered heavily in the wars of the 17th and 18th centuries and in 1812 from Napoleon's retreat. As the interwar capital (1919–1940), Kaunas acquired a remarkable stock of modernist architecture, now inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List (2023). During the Second World War most of its large Jewish population was murdered in the Kaunas Ghetto and at the Ninth Fort.

Today

Kaunas is a major university city (Kaunas University of Technology, Vytautas Magnus University, the Lithuanian University of Health Sciences) and the home of the Žalgiris basketball club, a central institution of Lithuanian sporting identity. The city was a European Capital of Culture in 2022.
