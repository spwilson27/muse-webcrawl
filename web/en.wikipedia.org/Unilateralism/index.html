Unilateralism

Unilateral action is taken by one state without the agreement of others.

Unilateralism is any doctrine or agenda that supports one-sided action. Such action may be in disregard for other parties, or as an expression of a commitment toward a direction which other parties may find disagreeable. In brief, unilateralism is a tendency to conduct foreign policy independently, without seeking the cooperation or approval of allies or international institutions. It is the opposite of multilateralism.

In international relations

The term is most often applied to the foreign policy of the United States, where debates between unilateralist and multilateralist approaches recur. Examples frequently cited include the U.S. withdrawal from international agreements, the 2003 invasion of Iraq conducted without explicit UN Security Council authorization, and unilateral economic sanctions. Defenders argue that a great power must retain freedom of action; critics argue that unilateral action undermines international law and alliances.

Unilateral declarations

A unilateral declaration of independence, such as those issued by Rhodesia in 1965 and Kosovo in 2008, is a formal statement by a group or territory asserting statehood without the consent of the parent state.
