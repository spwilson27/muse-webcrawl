King consort

A king consort is the husband of a reigning queen (queen regnant) who is granted the title of king without sharing her sovereign powers. The status is rare because in most monarchies the title "king" is reserved for the sovereign, and the husband of a queen regnant is more commonly styled prince or prince consort.

Examples

Historical examples include Ferdinand II of Portugal, who became king consort (and later co-monarch in practice) on marrying Maria II; Francis, Duke of Cádiz, husband of Isabella II of Spain; and Antoine of Bourbon, king of Navarre through his marriage to Jeanne III. In Spain, husbands of queens regnant have traditionally held the title of king consort. By contrast, in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands, husbands of queens regnant have been styled prince consort—such as Prince Albert, husband of Queen Victoria, and Prince Philip, husband of Elizabeth II.
