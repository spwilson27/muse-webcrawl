Expert witness

An expert witness, particularly in common law countries such as the United Kingdom, Australia, and the United States, is a person whose opinion by virtue of education, training, certification, skills, or experience is accepted by a court as an expert.

FieldLaw, litigation
RoleOpinion testimony on matters beyond common knowledge

Role

Unlike fact witnesses, who testify only about what they directly perceived, expert witnesses are permitted to offer opinions within their field of expertise to assist the trier of fact. Expert testimony commonly concerns forensic science, medicine, engineering, finance, and accident reconstruction.

Standards

In the United States, federal courts apply the standard from Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals (1993), under which judges act as gatekeepers assessing the reliability and relevance of proffered expert testimony; some states still follow the older Frye "general acceptance" standard. In England and Wales, an expert's overriding duty is to the court rather than to the party who instructed them.

Criticism

Critics note risks of partisan bias, "battles of experts," and the admission of unreliable forensic techniques, which have prompted reforms in several jurisdictions.
