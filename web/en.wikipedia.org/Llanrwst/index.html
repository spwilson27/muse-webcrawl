Llanrwst

Llanrwst is a market town and community in Conwy County Borough, Wales. It lies on the east bank of the River Conwy, on the edge of Snowdonia (Eryri), near the towns of Betws-y-Coed and Conwy. The town grew around the wool trade and was historically in the county of Denbighshire. Its name derives from the Welsh llan (church) of Saint Grwst.

History

The site of Llanrwst is associated with Saint Grwst, a 6th- or 7th-century Welsh saint to whom the parish church is dedicated. The town developed as a trading centre for the surrounding agricultural area and for the lead mines of the Gwydir Forest. In the 13th century, Llywelyn the Great granted land nearby, and Gwydir Castle, built by the Wynn family in the 16th century, stands just across the river at Llanrwst's edge.

Pont Fawr, the town's three-arched stone bridge over the Conwy, is traditionally attributed to the architect Inigo Jones and dates from 1636; its steep single-carriageway crossing remains a local landmark. Beside it stands Tu Hwnt i'r Bont, a 15th-century cottage that later became a courthouse and is now a popular tea room, famous for its Virginia creeper covering.

The town's motto, "Cymru, Lloegr a Llanrwst" ("Wales, England and Llanrwst"), recalls the period when Llanrwst was a free borough independent of any diocese. In 1610 Sir John Wynn of Gwydir founded almshouses in the town, which survive today.

Geography and culture

Llanrwst is prone to flooding from the River Conwy, and significant flood defence works have been carried out in recent decades. The town hosted the National Eisteddfod in 1951 and 1989. It is served by Llanrwst railway station on the Conwy Valley line and by the A470 trunk road. Gwydir Forest Park surrounds the town, offering walking and cycling routes.
