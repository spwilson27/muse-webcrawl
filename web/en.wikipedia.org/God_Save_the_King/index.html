God Save the King

"God Save the King" (alternatively "God Save the Queen" when the monarch is a woman) is the national anthem of the United Kingdom and the royal anthem of the Commonwealth realms and their territories. The author of the tune is unknown and it may originate in plainchant, but an attribution to the composer John Bull has sometimes been made. It is one of two national anthems in New Zealand and the royal anthem of several other countries.

The anthem has never been formally adopted by statute in the United Kingdom; it is the national anthem by custom and usage. The words and title adapt to the gender of the current monarch: with the accession of Charles III in 2022, the anthem reverted from "Queen" to "King" for the first time since 1952.

History

The melody was first performed publicly in London in 1745, during the Jacobite rising, when it was sung in theatres after performances in support of King George II. The phrase "God save the King" is much older, appearing in biblical translations and in naval usage as a watchword. The tune became so widely used that many other nations adopted it: it served as the melody for the German imperial anthem "Heil dir im Siegerkranz", the Swiss anthem until 1961, and the American patriotic song "My Country, 'Tis of Thee". Liechtenstein's anthem, "Oben am jungen Rhein", still uses the same melody.

Lyrics

The standard first verse is:

God save our gracious King,
Long live our noble King,
God save the King!
Send him victorious,
Happy and glorious,
Long to reign over us:
God save the King!

Additional verses exist, though rarely more than the first (and occasionally the sixth) are sung. A verse referring to crushing "rebellious Scots" is sometimes attributed to the song but is not part of the standard version.

Usage

The anthem is played at state occasions, royal events, and international sporting fixtures involving England (which has no separate official anthem at most events) and the United Kingdom. In Commonwealth realms such as Canada and Australia it functions as the royal anthem, played in the presence of the monarch or viceregal representatives.
