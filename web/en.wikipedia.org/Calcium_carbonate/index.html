Calcium carbonate is a chemical compound with the formula CaCO3. It is a common substance found in rocks as the minerals calcite and aragonite, most notably in chalk and limestone, and is the main component of the shells of marine organisms, snails and eggs. Calcium carbonate is the active ingredient in agricultural lime and is created when calcium ions in hard water react with carbonate ions to form limescale.

Occurrence

About four percent of the Earth's crust consists of calcium carbonate. It forms vast sedimentary deposits of limestone, chalk and dolomite (the latter with magnesium), and metamorphic marble. Marine organisms build shells and skeletons of calcite or aragonite; over geological time these accumulate as sediments that lithify into rock. Travertine and stalactites and stalagmites in caves are also calcium carbonate, deposited from saturated groundwater.

Properties and polymorphs

Calcite is the stable polymorph at ambient conditions; aragonite is metastable and common in shells and coral; vaterite is rarer. Calcium carbonate is practically insoluble in pure water but dissolves in water containing carbon dioxide to form calcium bicarbonate, a reaction at the heart of karst landscape formation and temporary water hardness. It reacts with acids to release carbon dioxide, the basis of the familiar fizz test used by geologists.

Uses

Calcium carbonate is used in enormous quantities as a building material (limestone and marble), as the raw material for cement and quicklime, in iron and steel smelting as a flux, as a filler in paper, plastics, paints and adhesives, as an antacid and calcium supplement in medicine and food, and as agricultural lime to neutralise acidic soils. Calcination to calcium oxide releases carbon dioxide, making cement production a significant source of industrial CO2 emissions.

Environmental role

The compound is central to the global carbon cycle: dissolution and precipitation of carbonate buffers ocean chemistry, and ocean acidification threatens the ability of calcifying organisms such as corals and shellfish to build their skeletons.
