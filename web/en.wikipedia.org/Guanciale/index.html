Guanciale

Guanciale (Italian: [ɡwanˈtʃaːle]) is an Italian cured meat product prepared from pork jowl or cheek. Its name derives from guancia, the Italian word for 'cheek'. It is a speciality of central Italy, particularly Umbria and Lazio.

Production

Pork cheek is rubbed with salt and spices — typically black or red pepper, thyme or fennel, and sometimes garlic and wine — and then cured and aged for at least three weeks, losing about a third of its weight. Unlike pancetta (made from pork belly), guanciale is not smoked, and its fat is harder and more richly flavoured. The texture is firmer than pancetta but the meat is streaked with lean muscle, and the flavour is stronger and more distinctive.

Culinary uses

Guanciale is the traditional and defining ingredient of several classic Roman and central Italian pasta dishes: pasta alla carbonara, bucatini all'amatriciana, and pasta alla gricia. Rendered gently, its fat forms the base of these sauces, and the crisped meat provides their characteristic savour. Substituting pancetta or bacon is common outside Italy but is regarded by purists as a different dish.
