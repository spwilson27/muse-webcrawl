Mountains Beyond Mountains

Mountains Beyond Mountains: The Quest of Dr. Paul Farmer, A Man Who Would Cure the World is a 2003 non-fiction book by American author Tracy Kidder. It is a biographical account of Paul Farmer, the physician and anthropologist who co-founded Partners In Health and devoted his life to bringing modern medicine to some of the world's poorest communities, beginning in the village of Cange in Haiti's Central Plateau.

Summary

Kidder follows Farmer from his unconventional childhood through Harvard Medical School to his work in Haiti, Peru and Russia, where Partners In Health pioneered community-based treatment of multidrug-resistant tuberculosis in settings where experts had argued such treatment was impossible. The title comes from a Haitian proverb — "beyond mountains there are mountains" — expressing the endless succession of problems to be solved, and Farmer's refusal to accept limits on the care owed to the poor. The book portrays his model of accompaniment, in which community health workers support patients through treatment, and his preferential option for the poor, drawn from liberation theology.

Reception

The book was widely praised and became an influential text in global health, assigned widely in universities and credited with drawing a generation of students into medicine and public health. It won notice as both biography and an argument about equity in healthcare delivery.
