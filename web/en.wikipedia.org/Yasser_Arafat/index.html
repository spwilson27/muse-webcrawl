Yasser Arafat (Arabic: ياسر عرفات; born Mohammed Abdel Rahman Abdel Raouf al-Qudwa al-Husseini, 4 or 24 August 1929 – 11 November 2004), popularly known by his kunya Abu Ammar, was a Palestinian political leader. He was chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) from 1969 to 2004 and president of the Palestinian National Authority (PNA) from 1994 to 2004. Ideologically an Arab nationalist, he was a founding member of the Fatah political party, which he led from 1959 until 2004.

Early life and Fatah

Arafat was born in Cairo to Palestinian parents and studied civil engineering at Cairo University, where he headed the Palestinian students' union. He fought in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War and later worked as an engineer in Kuwait, where in the late 1950s he co-founded Fatah, a movement dedicated to the liberation of Palestine through armed struggle by Palestinians themselves. Fatah's first operation against Israel took place in January 1965. After the Arab defeat in the 1967 Six-Day War and Fatah's stand at the Battle of Karameh in 1968, Arafat's prestige soared; in 1969 he became chairman of the PLO.

PLO leadership

From bases in Jordan, then (after the Black September fighting of 1970–71) in Lebanon, Arafat led a PLO that combined guerrilla warfare and terrorism with diplomacy and state-building. In 1974 he addressed the UN General Assembly — the first representative of a non-governmental organisation to do so — declaring he had come bearing "an olive branch and a freedom fighter's gun." The PLO was expelled from Lebanon by Israel's 1982 invasion and relocated to Tunis, where Arafat survived an Israeli air strike in 1985. In 1988, in Algiers, the Palestinian National Council proclaimed the State of Palestine, and Arafat renounced terrorism and accepted UN Resolutions 242 and 338 before the UN in Geneva.

Peace process and the Palestinian Authority

Secret negotiations produced the Oslo Accords: in September 1993 Arafat and Israeli prime minister Yitzhak Rabin signed the Declaration of Principles on the White House lawn, and in 1994 they shared the Nobel Peace Prize with Shimon Peres. Arafat returned from exile to Gaza in July 1994 and in 1996 was elected president of the Palestinian Authority in the West Bank and Gaza. The peace process faltered at the Camp David summit of 2000, and the Second Intifada erupted that autumn. Israel confined Arafat to his Ramallah compound, the Mukataa, from 2001 until he was flown to Paris for treatment in October 2004. He died on 11 November 2004 at a military hospital in Clamart; the official cause was a stroke complicated by a bleeding disorder, though the circumstances of his illness have remained the subject of investigation and controversy. He is buried at the Mukataa in Ramallah, and remains the pre-eminent symbol of Palestinian nationalism.
