March 23 Movement

The March 23 Movement (French: Mouvement du 23 mars), commonly known as M23, is a Congolese rebel military group operating in the North Kivu province of the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. It takes its name from the peace agreement of 23 March 2009 between the government and the earlier CNDP rebellion, which its founders claimed Kinshasa had failed to honour.

History

M23 was formed in April 2012 by former CNDP soldiers who mutinied from the Congolese army. In November 2012 it briefly captured Goma, the provincial capital, before withdrawing under international pressure; it was militarily defeated in late 2013 by the Congolese army and the UN Force Intervention Brigade, and its fighters were cantoned in Uganda and Rwanda.

Resurgence

The group resumed major operations in 2021–2022, seizing territory in North Kivu including the border town of Bunagana, and in January 2025 it again captured Goma. UN experts and several governments have reported Rwandan support for M23, which Rwanda denies. The conflict has displaced hundreds of thousands of civilians.
