KPMG is a multinational professional services network and one of the "Big Four" accounting organisations, alongside Deloitte, EY and PwC. Member firms provide audit, tax and advisory services in more than 140 countries and territories. The initials stand for the founding partners of its predecessor firms: Klynveld, Peat, Marwick and Goerdeler.

History

The oldest predecessor, William Barclay Peat & Co., was founded in London in 1870. KPMG in its present form was created by the 1987 merger of Peat Marwick International and Klynveld Main Goerdeler — the first mega-merger of large accounting firms. The network is organised as a Swiss cooperative (KPMG International) to which legally independent national member firms belong.

Services and controversies

KPMG audits many of the world's largest companies and advises on deals, risk, technology and tax. Like its Big Four peers it has faced regulatory sanctions, including over the audits of Carillion in the UK, its role in the South African state-capture scandal involving the Gupta family, and a $50 million settlement with the U.S. SEC in 2019 over the misuse of regulator inspection data. The firm also pays penalties relating to exam cheating by staff in several countries.
