ATSC standards

TypeDigital terrestrial television broadcasting standards

ATSC standards are a set of standards developed by the Advanced Television Systems Committee for digital television transmission over terrestrial, cable and satellite networks. They are used principally in North America — the United States, Canada and Mexico — and in South Korea, replacing the analogue NTSC system.

ATSC 1.0

The original ATSC standard (document A/53) was approved in 1995 and adopted by the U.S. Federal Communications Commission in 1996. It uses 8VSB modulation for terrestrial broadcasting, carrying about 19.4 Mbit/s in a 6 MHz channel. Video was originally encoded with MPEG-2 (later standards also permit H.264) in formats up to 1080i high definition, with Dolby AC-3 digital audio supporting 5.1-channel surround sound. The United States completed its transition from analogue to digital broadcasting on 12 June 2009.

ATSC-M/H

ATSC-M/H was a mobile/handheld extension of the standard, designed to deliver services to portable receivers in moving vehicles. It saw limited commercial deployment.

ATSC 3.0

ATSC 3.0, marketed as NextGen TV, is a major overhaul that is not backward compatible with ATSC 1.0. It uses OFDM modulation and an IP-based transport, supports 4K Ultra HD video with HEVC encoding, high dynamic range, immersive audio, advanced emergency alerting and interactive services. South Korea adopted ATSC 3.0 for its UHD broadcasts beginning in 2017, and U.S. stations began voluntary rollouts in 2020.
