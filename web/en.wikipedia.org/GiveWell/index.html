GiveWell

GiveWell is an American non-profit charity assessment and grantmaking organisation founded in 2007 by Holden Karnofsky and Elie Hassenfeld. It conducts in-depth research to find outstanding giving opportunities — primarily in global health and poverty alleviation — and publishes its analyses openly online. GiveWell is closely associated with the effective altruism movement.

History

Karnofsky and Hassenfeld, then working in finance, founded the organisation after finding that existing charity information did not answer questions about the actual impact of donations. In 2011 they co-founded a sister grantmaking organisation, Good Ventures, with Dustin Moskovitz and Cari Tuna; Good Ventures later became the foundation Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy), which works closely with GiveWell's research. GiveWell directs several hundred million dollars per year in donations to its recommended programmes.

Recommendations

GiveWell's top-rated charities have consistently been organisations working on malaria prevention and treatment (such as the Against Malaria Foundation and the Malaria Consortium's seasonal malaria chemoprevention programme), vitamin A supplementation (Helen Keller International), and unconditional cash transfers (GiveDirectly). Its cost-effectiveness models estimate, for example, that top malaria programmes can save a life for roughly US$5,000–5,500.

Approach and criticism

GiveWell emphasises transparency, publishing full research reports, cost-effectiveness models, and a record of its own mistakes. Critics have questioned the limits of quantitative cost-effectiveness estimates, the focus on a narrow set of interventions, and moral assumptions embedded in the models comparing different kinds of outcomes.
