Enterprise resource planning

Enterprise resource planning (ERP) is integrated software used to manage core business processes across an organization. ERP systems connect functions such as accounting, procurement, inventory, manufacturing, sales, human resources, and customer orders through shared data.

Development

ERP grew out of material requirements planning and manufacturing resource planning systems of the late twentieth century. As databases and networks improved, vendors broadened the same integrated model to service firms, governments, hospitals, and universities.

Features and deployment

Modern ERP suites are modular: organizations adopt components for finance, supply chain, payroll, projects, or analytics while maintaining common records. Deployment may be on premises or cloud-based. Implementation often requires process redesign, data migration, training, and careful change management.

Benefits and risks

Successful ERP can reduce duplicate entry, improve reporting, and make inventory and cash flows visible in real time. Failed projects, however, are notorious for cost overruns and disruption when software is forced onto incompatible workflows. See also customer relationship management and supply chain management.
