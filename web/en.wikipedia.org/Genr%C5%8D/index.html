The genrō (元老, "elder statesmen") were an unofficial group of senior Japanese statesmen, mostly veterans of the Meiji Restoration, who acted as informal advisers to the Emperor of Japan in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Although the institution had no basis in the Meiji Constitution, the genrō exercised great influence, most importantly by recommending the appointment of prime ministers.

Members

The core group included Itō Hirobumi, Yamagata Aritomo, Matsukata Masayoshi, Inoue Kaoru, Ōyama Iwao, Saigō Tsugumichi, Katsura Tarō, and Saionji Kinmochi. Most came from the former Satsuma and Chōshū domains, which had dominated the Restoration government.

Influence and decline

During the Meiji and Taishō periods, governments effectively required genrō approval. As the elder members died, the function of selecting prime ministers passed in the 1930s to conferences of senior statesmen (jūshin). With the death of Saionji, the last surviving genrō, in 1940, the institution disappeared.
