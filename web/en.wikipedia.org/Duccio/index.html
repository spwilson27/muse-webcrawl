Duccio

Duccio di Buoninsegna (c. 1255–1260 – c. 1318–1319) was an Italian painter active in Siena during the late 13th and early 14th centuries. He is regarded as the founder of the Sienese school of painting and, with Giotto, one of the fathers of Western painting, moving Byzantine conventions toward a more naturalistic and emotionally expressive art.

Born: c. 1255–1260, Siena
Died: c. 1318–1319, Siena
Known for: Panel painting
Masterpiece: The Maestà (1308–1311)

Life and works

Little is documented about Duccio's life beyond his commissions. The Rucellai Madonna (1285), commissioned for a Florentine confraternity and long attributed to Cimabue, is now generally given to Duccio. His supreme work is the Maestà, a huge double-sided altarpiece for Siena Cathedral, contracted in 1308 and installed with great civic ceremony in 1311. Its front showed the Virgin and Child enthroned among saints and angels; its reverse carried dozens of scenes from the life of Christ and the Virgin, notable for their narrative clarity, graceful line and tender human interaction. The altarpiece was later dismantled, and panels are now scattered among museums, though the majority remain in Siena's Museo dell'Opera del Duomo.

Style and influence

Duccio retained the gold ground and refined elegance of Byzantine painting while softening its formalism: draperies flow more naturally, faces model emotion, and architectural settings begin to suggest real space. His workshop shaped a generation of Sienese painters, including Simone Martini and the Lorenzetti brothers.
