Eyalet

Eyalet (Ottoman Turkish: eyalet, from Arabic iyāla, "province"), also known as a beylerbeylik, was a primary administrative division of the Ottoman Empire. From 1453 to the beginning of the nineteenth century, Ottoman local government was loosely structured; the empire was at first divided into provinces called eyalets, each governed by a pasha of three tails (horsetails), the highest rank below the sultan.

History

The earliest eyalets were Rumelia and Anatolia, governed by beylerbeys ("lord of lords"), the empire's highest-ranking provincial governors. As the empire expanded, new conquests were organized into eyalets — Syria, Egypt, Hungary (Budin), Baghdad, and others — until there were more than thirty by the early 17th century. Eyalets were subdivided into sanjaks, governed by sanjak-beys.

Government

The beylerbey held military and civil authority in his province, presided over its divan (council), and led its timariot cavalry in war. Some eyalets, known as "salyane" provinces such as Egypt and Baghdad, remitted a fixed annual tribute to Istanbul and were administered with greater local autonomy; others, "hass" provinces, were organized under the timar land-grant system.

Replacement

As part of the Tanzimat reforms, the eyalet system was replaced beginning in 1864 by the vilayet system under the Vilayet Law, which introduced smaller, more uniform provinces modelled partly on French departments.
