New 7 Wonders of the World

Machu Picchu, among the seven winners announced in 2007.

The New 7 Wonders of the World was a campaign started in 2001 to choose a new list of seven wonders from a selection of 200 existing monuments, organized by the Swiss-based New7Wonders Foundation. Winners were selected by popular vote via the internet and telephone, with the foundation reporting more than 100 million votes cast. The results were announced on 7 July 2007 (07/07/07) in Lisbon, Portugal.

The seven winners

The chosen wonders were: the Great Wall of China; Petra in Jordan; Christ the Redeemer in Brazil; Machu Picchu in Peru; Chichen Itza in Mexico; the Colosseum in Italy; and the Taj Mahal in India. The Great Pyramid of Giza, the only surviving wonder of the ancient list and a finalist, was granted honorary status.

Reception

UNESCO, which maintains the official World Heritage List, issued a statement distancing itself from the campaign, noting that the vote reflected only the opinions of those with internet or telephone access and had no scientific or educational basis. Egypt's antiquities officials criticized the inclusion of the Giza pyramids in a popular vote. The foundation subsequently ran the New 7 Wonders of Nature campaign.
