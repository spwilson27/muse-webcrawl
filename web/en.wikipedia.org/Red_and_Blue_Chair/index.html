The Red and Blue Chair (Dutch: Rood-blauwe stoel) is a chair designed in 1917 by the Dutch furniture designer and architect Gerrit Thomas Rietveld. It is one of the most famous works of the De Stijl movement and an icon of modernist design, reducing a chair to an arrangement of straight wooden planes and bars in primary colours.

Rietveld's chair, built from standard lumber in primary colours.

Design

Rietveld designed the chair in 1917 as an exercise in constructing furniture from standard, machine-cut timber components joined simply, so that it could be mass-produced inexpensively. The original version was unpainted or finished in grey, black, and white; around 1923 Rietveld adopted the now-famous scheme of primary colours — red backrest, blue seat, black frame members with yellow ends — bringing the chair into line with the Neoplasticist palette of Piet Mondrian and De Stijl.

Legacy

The chair was an early product of Rietveld's association with De Stijl, founded by Theo van Doesburg. It embodies the movement's ideals of abstraction, reduction to elementary forms and colours, and spatial openness: the components appear to float in space rather than forming a solid mass. Examples are held by museums including the Museum of Modern Art in New York and the Stedelijk Museum in Amsterdam, and licensed reproductions have been produced by Cassina since 1971.
