Tāne

Tāne (also called Tāne-mahuta, Tāne-nui-a-Rangi, and several other names) is the god of forests and of birds in Māori mythology, and also by extension the god of other features of the forests such as timber. He is the son of Ranginui (the sky father) and Papatūānuku (the earth mother), who used to lie in a tight embrace where their many children lived in the darkness between them.

Separation of the parents

Weary of the darkness and cramped conditions, Tāne and his brothers debated how to separate their parents. After attempts by others failed, Tāne lay on his back and pushed with his legs against the sky, forcing Rangi and Papa apart and bringing light into the world. In some traditions it is instead the brother Tāwhirimātea who opposed the separation; Tāwhiri later attacked his brothers in revenge.

Creation of humankind

Tāne sought a wife among the beings of the earth and is credited with forming the first woman, Hine-ahu-one ("earth-formed maiden"), from the red soil of Papatūānuku. Their daughter was Hine-tītama, who later fled to the underworld and became Hinenuitepō, goddess of death.

Names and epithets

Tāne bears many names reflecting his roles: Tāne-mahuta (god of the forest), Tāne-nui-a-Rangi (who brought knowledge from the heavens), Tāne-te-kapiti, and Tāne-mataahi. He is credited with ascending to the highest heaven, Te Toi-o-ngā-rangi, to obtain the three baskets of knowledge (ngā kete o te wānanga) for humankind.
