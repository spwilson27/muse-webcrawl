John Cooper Works

Industry: Automotive
Founded: 2000 (as tuning company); sub-brand of Mini from 2007
Founder: Michael Cooper
Named after: John Cooper
Parent: Mini (BMW Group)

John Cooper Works (JCW) is the high-performance sub-brand of the British car marque Mini, owned by BMW. It is named after racing car maker John Cooper, whose Cooper Car Company prepared the classic Mini Cooper that won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1964, 1965 and 1967.

History

The John Cooper Works company was founded in 2000 by Michael Cooper, son of John Cooper, initially selling tuning kits for the new BMW-era Mini. The kits increased power of the Cooper S through a modified cylinder head, supercharger pulley and exhaust. In 2007 BMW acquired the rights to the name and made John Cooper Works the performance flagship of the Mini range.

Models

JCW variants of the Mini Hatch, Clubman, Countryman and Convertible feature more powerful turbocharged engines, sports suspension, larger brakes and distinctive styling. The limited-run Mini John Cooper Works GP (2013 and 2020) are the fastest production Minis, with the 2020 GP producing 306 hp. In the electric era, JCW versions of the electric Cooper and Aceman apply the badge to battery-electric models.

Motorsport

The brand continues Cooper's racing heritage through Mini's motorsport programmes, including rallying derivatives and one-make racing activities.
