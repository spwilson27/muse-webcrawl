Michael Joseph Sandel (born March 5, 1953) is an American political philosopher and the Anne T. and Robert M. Bass Professor of Government at Harvard University. His undergraduate course Justice is one of the most famous in Harvard's history and was the first Harvard course made freely available online and on television, reaching tens of millions of viewers worldwide.

Work

Sandel's first book, Liberalism and the Limits of Justice (1982), offered an influential critique of John Rawls's A Theory of Justice, arguing that liberalism's "unencumbered self" neglects the constitutive role of community and shared ends — a position associated with communitarianism. His later works include Democracy's Discontent (1996), The Case Against Perfection (2007), on the ethics of genetic enhancement, and What Money Can't Buy: The Moral Limits of Markets (2012), arguing that market values have crowded out civic and moral norms.

In The Tyranny of Merit (2020), Sandel argued that meritocratic ideals have corroded the dignity of work, fuelled populist resentment, and convinced winners that their success is deserved while humiliating those left behind; he proposed renewing the contribution economy and the dignity of labour.

Teaching

The Justice course uses Socratic dialogue on cases from price gouging and organ sales to affirmative action and same-sex marriage to explore utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill), libertarianism (Nozick), Kant, Rawls and Aristotle. Sandel has delivered the BBC Reith Lectures and hosted the BBC Radio 4 series The Public Philosopher.
