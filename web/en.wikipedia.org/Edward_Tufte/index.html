Edward Rolf Tufte (born 14 March 1942), sometimes known as "ET", is an American statistician and professor emeritus of political science, statistics and computer science at Yale University. He is noted for his books on data visualisation and information design, beginning with The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983), self-published to preserve its design quality and now regarded as a classic.

Ideas

Tufte advocates maximising the "data-ink ratio" — the proportion of a graphic's ink devoted to the data itself — and eliminating decorative "chartjunk". He popularised the sparkline, a small, dense, word-sized graphic, and insists on high data density, small multiples and honest scales. His essay "The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint" (2003) criticised slideware for fragmenting reasoning; he later served on the panel reviewing the Columbia Space Shuttle disaster, whose reports cited the weaknesses of slide-based engineering communication. He has frequently praised Charles Joseph Minard's 1869 figurative map of Napoleon's Russian campaign as perhaps the best statistical graphic ever drawn.

Other work

Tufte teaches a one-day course on information design taken by hundreds of thousands of students, and creates large outdoor sculptures in Connecticut and Virginia. In 2010 he was appointed by President Barack Obama to the Recovery Independent Advisory Panel overseeing stimulus spending transparency.
