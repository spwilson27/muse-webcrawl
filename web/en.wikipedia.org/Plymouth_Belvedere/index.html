Plymouth Belvedere

Overview
Manufacturer: Plymouth (Chrysler)
Production: 1954–1970
Class: Full-size (1954–1964); Mid-size (1965–1970)
Layout: FR layout

The Plymouth Belvedere is a series of American automobile models made by Plymouth from 1954 until 1970. Introduced as the top trim level of the Plymouth range, the Belvedere name was first used for a hardtop coupe in the Cranbrook line in 1951. Over its life the car ranged over full-size and later intermediate (mid-size) platforms.

History

The 1955–1956 models adopted Virgil Exner's styling, and the dramatic 1957 cars with tailfins became icons of the Forward Look era; a 1957 Belvedere was famously buried in Tulsa, Oklahoma, as a time capsule and exhumed in 2007. In the 1960s the Belvedere became Plymouth's mid-size line, and its high-performance derivatives included the Belvedere GTX, the Satellite, and the basis for the legendary Road Runner.

Motorsport

Belvederes powered by the 426 Hemi engine were highly successful in NASCAR competition in the mid-1960s, driven most famously by Richard Petty, whose 1967 Belvedere won 27 races in a single season.

Miss Belvedere

The buried 1957 Tulsa car, nicknamed "Miss Belvedere", was unearthed on 15 June 2007. Half a century underground had severely corroded the car, which was eventually conserved and displayed in a museum in Illinois.
