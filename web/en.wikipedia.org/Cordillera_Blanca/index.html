Cordillera Blanca

The Cordillera Blanca (Spanish for "white cordillera") is a mountain range in Peru that is part of the larger Andes range and extends for 200 kilometres (124 mi) between 8°08' and 9°58'S and 77°00' and 77°52'W, in the Ancash region. It contains several peaks exceeding 6,000 metres (19,690 ft), and is the most extensively glaciated mountain range in the tropics. The highest mountain in Peru, Huascarán (6,768 m / 22,205 ft), is located there.

Geography

The range runs roughly parallel to the Peruvian coast, separated from the Pacific by the Cordillera Negra, a lower, snow-free range across the Callejón de Huaylas valley through which the Santa River flows. The Cordillera Blanca contains hundreds of glaciers and glacial lagoons, many turquoise in colour, such as Llanganuco and Parón. Much of the range is protected within Huascarán National Park, established in 1975 and recognised by UNESCO as a Biosphere Reserve and World Heritage Site.

Climbing and tourism

The range is one of South America's premier mountaineering destinations, with celebrated peaks such as Alpamayo — often described as one of the most beautiful mountains in the world — Artesonraju, Pisco, and Chopicalqui. The city of Huaraz is the main gateway for trekkers and climbers. Glacial retreat attributed to climate warming has shrunk the range's ice cover significantly since the 1970s, threatening water supplies and creating hazardous glacial lakes prone to outburst floods, one of which devastated Huaraz in 1941.
