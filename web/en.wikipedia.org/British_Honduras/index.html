British Honduras

British Honduras was a Crown colony of the United Kingdom on the eastern coast of Central America, south of Mexico and east and north of Guatemala. It was the successor to a settlement founded by English and Scottish logwood cutters, the Baymen, in the 17th and 18th centuries in territory claimed by Spain. The colony existed from 1862, when it was formally constituted, until it renamed itself Belize in 1973 and achieved full independence on 21 September 1981.

History

British settlers, many of them former buccaneers, began cutting logwood — used to make textile dye — along the Belize River in the mid-1600s, later expanding into mahogany extraction worked by enslaved Africans. Spain conceded British cutting rights in treaties of 1763 and 1786 while retaining nominal sovereignty. The Baymen's defeat of a Spanish expedition at the Battle of St. George's Caye in 1798 ended Spanish attempts to expel them. The Anglo-Guatemalan Treaty of 1859 recognised British sovereignty over the territory, though Guatemala repudiated the treaty and maintained a territorial claim into the independence era and beyond.

Colonial society and independence

Administered as a dependency of Jamaica until 1884, the colony's economy rested on forestry, then chicle, citrus, and sugar. Hurricane Hattie devastated Belize City in 1961, prompting the building of a new capital, Belmopan, inland. Self-government came in 1964 under premier George Cadle Price. The colony was renamed Belize on 1 June 1973, and, despite Guatemala's unresolved claim and the continued presence of a British garrison after independence, became sovereign in 1981 — the last British colony on the American mainland to do so.
