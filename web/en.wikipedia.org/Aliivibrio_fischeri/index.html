DomainBacteria
PhylumPseudomonadota
FamilyVibrionaceae
GenusAliivibrio
TraitsGram-negative, rod-shaped, bioluminescent

Aliivibrio fischeri (formerly Vibrio fischeri) is a Gram-negative, rod-shaped bacterium found globally in marine environments. It is a key research organism for the study of bacterial bioluminescence, quorum sensing, and symbiosis with marine animals.

Symbiosis

The bacterium forms a famous mutualism with the Hawaiian bobtail squid (Euprymna scolopes), colonising the squid's light organ. The squid uses the bacteria's light for counter-illumination camouflage, while the bacteria receive nutrients and shelter. Each morning the squid expels most of the bacteria, which regrow during the day.

Quorum sensing and luminescence

A. fischeri produces light via the lux operon only when population density is high. The autoinducer molecule N-(3-oxohexanoyl)-homoserine lactone, produced by LuxI and sensed by LuxR, was one of the first quorum-sensing systems characterised, work pioneered by J. Woodland Hastings and Kenneth Nealson.

Genome

The complete genome was published in 2005; the species was reclassified from Vibrio to the new genus Aliivibrio in 2007 on the basis of phylogenetic analysis.
