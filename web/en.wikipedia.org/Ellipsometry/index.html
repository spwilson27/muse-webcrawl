Ellipsometry

Ellipsometry is an optical technique for investigating the dielectric properties (complex refractive index or dielectric function) of thin films. It measures the change in polarisation upon reflection or transmission and compares it to a model. Because the technique is highly sensitive to the thickness and optical constants of layers, it can be used to characterise composition, roughness, thickness (with sub-nanometre precision), crystalline nature, doping concentration, and electrical conductivity.

Basic principles

Ellipsometry measures the complex reflectance ratio of a system, parameterised by the amplitude component Ψ and the phase difference Δ between p- and s-polarised light. The measured values are related to the sample structure through optical modelling, often using the Fresnel equations and effective-medium approximations for mixed layers.

Applications

The technique is widely used in semiconductor manufacturing for in-line monitoring of film growth, as well as in materials science, surface chemistry, and biological sensing, since it is non-destructive and works in air or liquid environments.
