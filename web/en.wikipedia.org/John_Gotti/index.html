John Joseph Gotti Jr. (October 27, 1940 – June 10, 2002) was an American mafioso and boss of the Gambino crime family in New York City. He ordered and helped to orchestrate the murder of Gambino boss Paul Castellano in December 1985 and took over the family shortly thereafter, leading what was widely described as America's most powerful crime syndicate.

Rise to power

Raised in poverty in the Bronx and Queens, Gotti joined a gang as a teenager and became a protégé of Gambino underboss Aniello Dellacroce. After a manslaughter conviction in the 1970s and a controversial narcotics case involving his crew, Gotti plotted Castellano's assassination outside Sparks Steak House in Manhattan on December 16, 1985, and seized control of the family.

"The Teflon Don"

Gotti's flamboyant style, expensive suits and press-friendly manner earned him the nicknames "the Dapper Don" and, after acquittals in three trials during the 1980s (later alleged to involve jury tampering and witness intimidation), "the Teflon Don".

Conviction and death

In 1992 Gotti was convicted of five murders, including Castellano's, racketeering, loan-sharking and other crimes, after his underboss Salvatore "Sammy the Bull" Gravano testified against him. He was sentenced to life without parole and died of throat cancer in federal prison in Springfield, Missouri, in 2002.
