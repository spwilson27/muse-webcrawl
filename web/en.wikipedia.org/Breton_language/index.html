Breton language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Bilingual road sign in Brittany

Breton (Breton: brezhoneg) is a Southwestern Brittonic Celtic language spoken in Brittany in western France. It is the only Celtic language still spoken on the European mainland, having been brought from Great Britain by migrating Britons during the early Middle Ages. It is closely related to Cornish and more distantly to Welsh.

Status

Breton is classified by UNESCO as "severely endangered". At the start of the 20th century it was spoken by over a million people, mostly in western Brittany (Lower Brittany); today estimates of fluent speakers are around 200,000, most of them elderly, though immersion schools (the Diwan network, founded in 1977) and bilingual classes teach several thousand children. French government policy long discouraged the language — schoolchildren were historically punished for speaking it — and France has not ratified the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages, though a 2008 constitutional amendment recognised regional languages as part of France's heritage.

Dialects and orthography

Traditional dialect areas are named after the old bishoprics: Léon, Trégor, Cornouaille and Vannes (Gwened), the last being the most divergent. Competing 20th-century orthographies were largely reconciled in the "peurunvan" (fully unified) spelling used by most publishers and schools. Breton has distinctive features including initial consonant mutations, a vigesimal counting system, and vocabulary heavily influenced by French.
