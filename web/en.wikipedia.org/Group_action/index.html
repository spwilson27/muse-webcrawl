Group action

In mathematics, a group action of a group G on a set X is a group homomorphism from G into the group of bijections of X (its symmetric group). Equivalently, it is a rule that assigns to each element g of G a permutation of X, such that the identity element acts trivially and the action respects multiplication: applying the action of h and then of g is the same as applying the action of gh. Group actions provide a unified language for symmetry and underpin results such as the orbit–stabilizer theorem and Burnside's lemma.

Definitions

A (left) action of G on X is a function G × X → X, written g·x, satisfying e·x = x for all x, and g·(h·x) = (gh)·x. The orbit of a point x is the set of all points g·x as g ranges over G; the orbits partition X. The stabilizer of x is the subgroup of elements fixing x. An action is transitive if there is a single orbit, and faithful if no non-identity element fixes every point.

Examples

Every group acts on itself by left multiplication (giving Cayley's theorem), and by conjugation, whose orbits are the conjugacy classes. Symmetry groups act on geometric objects: the dihedral group of a polygon acts on its vertices, and the rotation group of a cube acts on its faces, edges and vertices. Galois groups act on the roots of polynomials, and permutation groups are themselves groups acting on finite sets.

Applications

Group actions are used to count combinatorial objects up to symmetry (via Burnside's lemma and the Pólya enumeration theorem), to prove Sylow's theorems in finite group theory, and throughout geometry and physics wherever symmetry groups act on spaces of states or configurations.
