Nativity of Jesus

The nativity of Jesus, or the birth of Jesus, is described in the biblical gospels of Matthew and Luke. The two accounts agree that Jesus was born in Bethlehem, in Judaea, that his mother, Mary, was engaged to a man named Joseph, who was descended from King David and was not his biological father, and that his birth was caused by divine intervention. Many scholars view the two gospel accounts as contradictory and their authors as drawing on different traditions.

The accounts of Matthew and Luke

In Matthew, wise men (Magi) follow a star to Bethlehem to bring gifts to Jesus, born the "king of the Jews". King Herod orders the massacre of all the boys in Bethlehem under the age of two, but the family flees to Egypt and later settles in Nazareth. In Luke, Joseph and Mary travel from Nazareth to Bethlehem for the census of Quirinius; Jesus is born in a stable and visited by shepherds told of his birth by angels. Luke also presents Jesus in the Temple in Jerusalem, while Matthew has no such episode.

Date and place of birth

Neither gospel gives a date for the birth. The Gospel of Matthew places it during the reign of Herod the Great, who died in 4 BC, while Luke associates it with the census of Quirinius in AD 6 — a discrepancy of about ten years. The feast of Christmas on 25 December was established in the fourth century. The Church of the Nativity in Bethlehem marks the traditional birthplace.

In art and tradition

The nativity scene (crèche or presepio), popularized by Saint Francis of Assisi in 1223, depicts the birth with the Holy Family, shepherds, angels, and the Magi. The nativity has been depicted by countless artists, from Byzantine icons to Renaissance masters such as Botticelli and Giotto, and remains central to Christmas celebrations worldwide.
