Duke of Hamilton is a title in the Peerage of Scotland, created in 1643 for James Hamilton, 2nd Marquess of Hamilton. It is the senior Scottish dukedom, and the holder is the premier peer of Scotland. Since 1711 the dukes have also held the Dukedom of Brandon in the Peerage of Great Britain.

History

The Hamilton family claimed descent from Walter fitz Gilbert, granted the lands of Cadzow in the 14th century. The 1st Duke was executed in 1649 for his support of Charles I; his brother William, 2nd Duke, died of wounds after the Battle of Worcester in 1651. The title passed through the female line to Anne Hamilton, 3rd Duchess, whose husband William Douglas was created Duke of Hamilton for life; their descendants took the name Douglas-Hamilton. Hamilton Palace, demolished in the 1920s, was the family's palatial Lanarkshire seat.

Roles

The Duke of Hamilton is the hereditary keeper of the Palace of Holyroodhouse and, traditionally, carries the Crown of Scotland before the monarch at state occasions in Scotland. The family seat is now Lennoxlove House in East Lothian.
