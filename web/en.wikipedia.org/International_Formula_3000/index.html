The FIA International Formula 3000 Championship was a motor racing series created by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) in 1985 as the final stepping stone for drivers seeking to enter Formula One, replacing Formula Two. It took its name from the 3,000 cc (3.0-litre) maximum engine capacity for the naturally aspirated engines used in the series.

History

Formula 3000 began with a mixture of year-old Formula One cars and purpose-built chassis from March, Lola and Ralt, powered largely by the Cosworth DFV/DFY V8. The series grew in prestige during the late 1980s and 1990s, with champions including Christian Fittipaldi, Olivier Panis, Jean Alesi's contemporary rivals, Juan Pablo Montoya (1998) and Nick Heidfeld (1999). From 1996 the championship became a one-make series using Lola chassis and Zytek engines to control costs.

Decline and replacement

Rising costs and shrinking grids in the early 2000s reduced the championship's appeal as a Formula One feeder. After the 2004 season the FIA series was discontinued and replaced in 2005 by the GP2 Series, organised by Bernie Ecclestone and Flavio Briatore, which later became the FIA Formula 2 Championship. The final International F3000 champion was Vitantonio Liuzzi (2004); Björn Wirdheim had won in 2003. National and regional F3000 series, such as Euro Formula 3000, continued for several more years using the older cars.

Notable champions

Champions who went on to Formula One success include Jean Alesi (1989), Damon Hill's contemporary Erik Comas (1990), Luca di Montezemolo-era drivers such as Olivier Panis (1993), Juan Pablo Montoya (1998) and Sébastien Bourdais (2002), who later won multiple Champ Car titles.
