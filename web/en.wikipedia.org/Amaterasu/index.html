Amaterasu

Amaterasu Ōmikami (天照大御神, "Great Divinity Illuminating Heaven"), often simply Amaterasu, is the goddess of the sun in Shinto religion and Japanese mythology, and one of the most important kami. According to the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, she was born from the left eye of the creator god Izanagi as he purified himself after returning from the underworld, and was made ruler of Takamagahara, the High Plain of Heaven. The Imperial House of Japan traditionally claims descent from her.

Mythology

Amaterasu's most famous myth concerns her conflict with her brother, the storm god Susanoo. Enraged by his destructive rampages — which culminated in his throwing a flayed horse into her weaving hall — Amaterasu hid herself in the Heavenly Rock Cave (Ama-no-Iwato), plunging the world into darkness. The other gods lured her out by staging a raucous dance by the goddess Ame-no-Uzume outside the cave and placing a mirror so that Amaterasu, curious and glimpsing her own radiance, emerged and light was restored.

Descent and the imperial regalia

Amaterasu sent her grandson Ninigi to rule the earthly realm of Japan, giving him the Three Imperial Regalia — the mirror Yata no Kagami, the sword Kusanagi no Tsurugi, and the jewel Yasakani no Magatama. The mirror, representing the goddess herself, is enshrined at the Ise Grand Shrine in Mie Prefecture, her principal shrine, which is ritually rebuilt every twenty years in the Shikinen Sengū ceremony.

Worship

As the divine ancestress of the emperor and embodiment of the sun, Amaterasu occupies the summit of the Shinto pantheon. Her worship was central to State Shinto before 1945 and remains central to shrine Shinto today.
