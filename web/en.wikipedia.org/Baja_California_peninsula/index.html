Baja California peninsula

The Baja California peninsula is a long, narrow peninsula in northwestern Mexico, separated from the Mexican mainland by the Gulf of California (Sea of Cortés). Extending about 1,247 kilometres from the United States border near Tijuana and San Diego southward to Cabo San Lucas, it is one of the longest peninsulas in the world and comprises the Mexican states of Baja California and Baja California Sur.

Geography

A mountainous spine, including the Sierra de Juárez, Sierra San Pedro Mártir and the Sierra de la Giganta, runs the length of the peninsula. Much of the terrain is arid desert, part of the Sonoran and Baja California deserts, though the southern cape region receives more rain. The peninsula's isolation has produced many endemic plants and animals, and the islands and protected areas of the Gulf of California are a UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for marine life including whales, sea lions and whale sharks.

History

European explorers in the 1530s initially believed California to be an island, an error repeated on maps for two centuries. Jesuit missionaries founded a chain of missions from 1697 onward, followed later by Franciscan and Dominican missions. The peninsula is also famous for its prehistoric rock art, including the great murals of the Sierra de San Francisco. In modern times the region is known for tourism, sport fishing, and off-road racing such as the Baja 1000.
