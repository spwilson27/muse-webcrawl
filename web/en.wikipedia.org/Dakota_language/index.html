Dakota language

Dakota
Native to: United States, Canada
Ethnicity: Dakota people
Family: Siouan
Dialects: Eastern Dakota (Santee), Western Dakota (Yankton-Yanktonai)

Dakota (Dakhótiyapi) is a Siouan language spoken by the Dakota people of the Sioux tribes, closely related to and mutually intelligible with Lakota. It is spoken in Minnesota, Nebraska, and the Dakotas, and in Canada.

Dialects

Eastern Dakota (Santee) and Western Dakota (Yankton and Yanktonai) differ notably in the realization of certain consonants, contrasting with Lakota's l-sounds in a pattern often summarized as d/l/n variation.

Status

Dakota is endangered, with fluent first-language speakers numbering in the thousands and declining; immersion schools and university programs support revitalization efforts.
