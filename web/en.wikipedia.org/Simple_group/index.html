Simple group

In mathematics, a simple group is a nontrivial group whose only normal subgroups are the trivial group and the group itself. Simple groups are the building blocks of all finite groups, in the sense of the Jordan–Hölder theorem: every finite group can be decomposed into simple "composition factors", and the list of those factors is unique up to order.

Examples and classification

The cyclic groups of prime order are the simplest examples, and they are the only abelian finite simple groups. Non-abelian finite simple groups are far richer. The alternating group A5, of order 60, is the smallest non-abelian simple group; the non-solvability of A5 underlies the proof that general quintic equations cannot be solved by radicals.

The classification of finite simple groups, completed in the late 20th century and regarded as one of the largest achievements in mathematics, states that every finite simple group belongs to one of four families: cyclic groups of prime order; alternating groups of degree at least 5; groups of Lie type; or one of 26 exceptional sporadic groups, the largest of which is the Monster group, with roughly 8 × 1053 elements.
