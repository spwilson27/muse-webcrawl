Lake Burley Griffin

Lake Burley Griffin is an artificial lake in the centre of Canberra, the capital of Australia. It was completed in 1963 by damming the Molonglo River with Scrivener Dam, realising the central feature of Walter Burley Griffin's prize-winning 1911 design for the city, after whom the lake is named.

The lake covers about 664 hectares, is about 11 km long, and is ringed by many of Canberra's national institutions — Parliament House, the National Library, National Gallery, High Court and National Museum — as well as parks and cycleways. Its landmarks include the Captain James Cook Memorial jet, Commonwealth and Kings Avenue bridges, and Aspen Island's National Carillon. Recreational uses include rowing, sailing, dragon boating and paddle craft; swimming is limited by water quality.
