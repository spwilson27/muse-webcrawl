Brian Nelson (loyalist)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Brian Nelson

Brian Nelson (1947 – 11 April 2003) was a Northern Irish loyalist paramilitary and British Army intelligence agent. He became the chief intelligence officer of the Ulster Defence Association (UDA), the largest loyalist paramilitary organisation, while secretly working for the British Army's Force Research Unit (FRU). His dual role became one of the most controversial episodes of the Troubles, raising enduring questions about collusion between the security forces and loyalist paramilitaries.

Background

Nelson, a former soldier from the Shankill Road area of Belfast, joined the UDA and in the early 1980s was imprisoned for his part in the torture and intimidation of a partially sighted man. After his release he was recruited by the Force Research Unit and by 1987 had risen to become the UDA's senior intelligence officer, responsible for maintaining its targeting files on republicans and other victims.

Collusion allegations

Critics alleged that, with his handlers' knowledge, Nelson helped the UDA select targets, including solicitor Pat Finucane, murdered in 1989, and that warnings were not passed on that might have prevented killings. The Stevens Inquiries into collusion investigated the handling of Nelson and other agents. In 1992 Nelson pleaded guilty to conspiracy to murder charges and was sentenced to ten years' imprisonment, in a plea arrangement that kept much of the FRU's involvement out of open court. He died of a heart attack in 2003, shortly before the publication of a Stevens report overview confirming widespread collusion.
