Intercooler
TypeHeat exchanger
Used inTurbocharged and supercharged engines
FunctionCool intake air after compression
TypesAir-to-air, air-to-liquid

An intercooler is a heat exchanger used to cool a gas after compression. In automotive and aviation engines it most commonly cools the intake air after it has been compressed by a turbocharger or supercharger and before it enters the engine. Cooling the charge increases its density, allowing more air — and thus more fuel — to be burned, and reduces the risk of engine knock.

Principle

Compressing air raises its temperature substantially. Hot, less dense air reduces engine efficiency and power and increases nitrogen oxide emissions and detonation risk. Passing the charge through an intercooler removes much of this heat. In engines with multiple compression stages, an intercooler may sit between stages; a cooler after the final stage is sometimes called an aftercooler, though the terms are often used interchangeably.

Types

Air-to-air intercoolers transfer heat directly to outside air and are usually mounted at the front of the vehicle or on top of the engine beneath a bonnet scoop. Air-to-liquid intercoolers use a coolant circuit and a separate radiator; they are more compact and respond quickly, but add complexity. Placement, size, and pressure drop all affect performance.

Applications

Intercoolers are standard on most modern turbocharged road car engines, diesel trucks, performance cars, and many aircraft piston engines, as well as in industrial gas compressors.
