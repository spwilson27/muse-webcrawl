Putrajaya, officially the Federal Territory of Putrajaya, is Malaysia's administrative capital and a planned city located about 25 kilometres south of Kuala Lumpur. It houses the seat of the federal government, including the Prime Minister's office and the federal courts, while Kuala Lumpur remains the national capital and seat of the King and Parliament.

History

The site, formerly oil palm plantation land known as Prang Besar, was selected in the 1990s under Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad to relieve congestion in Kuala Lumpur. Construction began in 1995, and government ministries relocated progressively from 1999. Putrajaya was declared a Federal Territory on 1 February 2001, becoming Malaysia's third such territory after Kuala Lumpur and Labuan. The city is named after Malaysia's first prime minister, Tunku Abdul Rahman Putra.

Design and landmarks

Putrajaya was built as a "garden city" and intelligent city, with about 38 percent of its area devoted to green spaces and an artificial lake of 650 hectares crossed by distinctive bridges such as the Seri Wawasan Bridge. Landmarks include Perdana Putra (the Prime Minister's office complex), the pink-domed Putra Mosque, the iron Tuanku Mizan Zainal Abidin Mosque, the Putrajaya Convention Centre, and the Palace of Justice.

Government

The city is administered by the Putrajaya Corporation (Perbadanan Putrajaya). As part of the Multimedia Super Corridor, its neighbor Cyberjaya hosts technology firms. The population is predominantly composed of civil servants and remains small relative to its planned capacity of several hundred thousand.
