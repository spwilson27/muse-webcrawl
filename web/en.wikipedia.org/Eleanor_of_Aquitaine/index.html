Eleanor of Aquitaine (c. 1124 – 1 April 1204) was Queen of France from 1137 to 1152 as the wife of King Louis VII, Queen of England from 1154 to 1189 as the wife of King Henry II, and Duchess of Aquitaine in her own right from 1137 until her death. She was one of the most powerful and influential figures of the Middle Ages.

Duchess and Queen of France

Eleanor inherited the vast Duchy of Aquitaine — one of the largest and richest provinces of France — on the death of her father, William X, in 1137. She married the young Louis VII the same year. She accompanied him on the disastrous Second Crusade (1147–1149); the marriage, strained and producing only daughters, was annulled in 1152 on grounds of consanguinity.

Queen of England

Eight weeks after the annulment Eleanor married Henry Plantagenet, Duke of Normandy, who became Henry II of England in 1154. Their marriage created the Angevin Empire stretching from Scotland to the Pyrenees. She bore eight children, including Richard I ("the Lionheart") and John. Eleanor is often associated with the "court of love" at Poitiers and patronage of troubadour poetry, though the extent of this is debated.

Rebellion, imprisonment, and later years

In 1173 Eleanor supported her sons' revolt against Henry II; she was captured and imprisoned for most of the remaining years of his reign. Released on his death in 1189, she became a formidable political actor for Richard I — including raising his ransom during his captivity — and for John. She died at Poitiers in 1204 and was buried at Fontevraud Abbey beside Henry II and Richard I.
