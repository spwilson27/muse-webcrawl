Studio One (record label)

Studio One is one of Jamaica's most renowned record labels and recording studios. It has been described as the Motown of Jamaica. The label was founded by Clement "Coxsone" Dodd in 1954 and is based in Kingston, Jamaica, recording ska, rocksteady, reggae, dub and dancehall. Dodd had previously issued music on a series of earlier imprints, before building his studio at 13 Brentford Road in 1963.

History

Studio One's house bands — including the Skatalites, the Soul Brothers, the Soul Vendors, and Sound Dimension — created the backing tracks (riddims) that underpin Jamaican music, and the studio launched or shaped the careers of artists such as Bob Marley and the Wailers, Burning Spear, Dennis Brown, Alton Ellis, the Heptones, Marcia Griffiths, and Freddie McGregor.

In a fiercely competitive industry built around sound systems, Dodd's productions dominated Jamaican popular music through the 1960s and early 1970s. Many Studio One riddims have been versioned thousands of times and remain foundational to reggae and dancehall.

Legacy

Dodd relocated to New York in the late 1970s, opening a Studio One record shop in Brooklyn, and continued reissuing the label's catalogue until his death in 2004. The label's recordings have been extensively reissued by labels such as Heartbeat Records and Soul Jazz Records, and its catalogue is regarded as a cornerstone of Jamaican music history.
