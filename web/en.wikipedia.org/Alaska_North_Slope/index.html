Alaska North Slope

The Alaska North Slope is the region of the U.S. state of Alaska located on the northern slope of the Brooks Range along the coast of two marginal seas of the Arctic Ocean, the Chukchi Sea being on the western side of Point Barrow, and the Beaufort Sea on the eastern.

Alaska North Slope
Region of Alaska, United States
Area: about 230,000 km²
Largest community: Utqiaġvik (Barrow)

Geography and resources

The North Slope is known for its vast petroleum reserves. The Prudhoe Bay Oil Field, discovered in 1968, was the largest oil field in North America at the time of its discovery. Oil is transported via the Trans-Alaska Pipeline System from Prudhoe Bay to Valdez, 800 miles (1,290 km) to the south. The region also contains the National Petroleum Reserve–Alaska (NPR-A) and borders the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge (ANWR) to the east.

Communities

The North Slope Borough, covering most of the region, is home to predominantly Iñupiat Alaska Native communities including Utqiaġvik (formerly Barrow), Kaktovik, Nuiqsut, Wainwright, Point Hope, Anaktuvuk Pass, Atqasuk, and Kaktovik. There are no roads connecting the region to the rest of Alaska; access is primarily by air.

Climate

The North Slope has an Arctic climate with long, cold winters and short, cool summers. The coastal plain is underlain by continuous permafrost and dotted with thousands of thaw lakes.
