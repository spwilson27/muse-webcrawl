Intolerable Acts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Intolerable Acts, known in Britain as the Coercive Acts, were a series of laws passed by the British Parliament in 1774 in response to the Boston Tea Party. They were intended to punish the colony of Massachusetts Bay, secure British authority in the Thirteen Colonies, and make an example of Boston. Instead, they united the colonies in opposition and were a major factor leading to the outbreak of the American Revolutionary War in 1775.

The acts

  • Boston Port Act (31 March 1774): closed the port of Boston until the East India Company was compensated for the destroyed tea and order was restored.
  • Massachusetts Government Act (20 May 1774): altered the Massachusetts charter, making the council an appointed rather than elected body and restricting town meetings.
  • Administration of Justice Act: allowed officials accused of capital crimes in enforcing the laws to be tried in another colony or in Britain, leading colonists to call it the "Murder Act".
  • Quartering Act (2 June 1774): permitted the quartering of British troops in unoccupied buildings in the colonies.
  • Quebec Act (passed alongside them and grouped with them by colonists): extended Quebec's boundaries into the Ohio country and granted toleration to Catholics, alarming Protestant colonists and land speculators.

Colonial response

The acts were denounced as violations of the colonists' rights as Englishmen — taxation without representation, denial of self-government, and obstruction of justice. Rather than isolating Massachusetts, they prompted the colonies to convene the First Continental Congress in Philadelphia in September 1774, which coordinated a boycott of British goods (the Continental Association) and petitioned the Crown for redress. General Thomas Gage, appointed military governor of Massachusetts, attempted to enforce the acts; the escalation culminated in the Battles of Lexington and Concord in April 1775.
