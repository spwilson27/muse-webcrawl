Blame

Blame is the act of censuring, holding responsible, or making negative statements about an individual or group that their actions or inaction are socially or morally irresponsible, the opposite of praise. When someone is morally responsible for doing something wrong, their action is blameworthy; by contrast, when someone is morally responsible for doing something right, they may receive praise.

Psychology

Psychologists study blame as an attribution process: people assign causes to events and judge agents by their intentions, knowledge, and control over outcomes. Blame is subject to biases such as the fundamental attribution error (over-weighting character and under-weighting circumstance), the actor–observer asymmetry, and the just-world hypothesis. Defensive blame-shifting and scapegoating are common in interpersonal conflict and organizational failure analysis.

Philosophy and law

Moral philosophers debate the conditions of blameworthiness, including free will, moral responsibility, and the role of reactive attitudes such as resentment and indignation — a theme central to P. F. Strawson's essay "Freedom and Resentment". In law, blame is formalized through concepts of fault, negligence, and liability, which apportion responsibility for harms and determine remedies.

Culture

Patterns of blame vary across cultures and institutions; "blame culture" in workplaces is criticized for discouraging error reporting, leading safety researchers to advocate "just culture" approaches that distinguish human error from reckless conduct.
