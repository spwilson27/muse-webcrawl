The Baltic languages are a branch of the Indo-European language family spoken by the Baltic peoples, mainly around the southeastern Baltic Sea. The only surviving members are Lithuanian and Latvian.

Classification

The branch is divided into Western Baltic, represented by extinct Old Prussian, and Eastern Baltic, comprising Lithuanian, Latvian and the extinct Curonian, Semigallian and Selonian. Baltic is often grouped with the Slavic languages in a Balto-Slavic branch, though the exact relationship is debated.

Features

Baltic languages preserve many archaic features of Proto-Indo-European, including rich case inflection, pitch accent distinctions and conservative vocabulary, making them of central interest to comparative linguistics.

History

The earliest written Lithuanian and Latvian texts date to the 16th century. Old Prussian, attested from the 14th century, died out in the early 18th century. Today Lithuanian has about 3 million speakers and Latvian about 1.5 million.
