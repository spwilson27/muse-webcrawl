Frequency response

In signal processing and electronics, the frequency response of a system is the quantitative measure of the magnitude and phase of the output as a function of input frequency. It characterises how a linear time-invariant system responds to sinusoidal inputs of different frequencies and is obtained by evaluating the system's transfer function along the imaginary axis.

Measurement and representation

Frequency response is commonly shown as a Bode plot: two graphs of magnitude (usually in decibels) and phase against frequency on a logarithmic scale. It can be measured by sweeping a sine wave across the band of interest, by applying an impulse and computing the Fourier transform of the response, or by using broadband noise. In audio engineering, specifications such as "20 Hz – 20 kHz, ±0.5 dB" summarise how flat a device's response is across the audible range.

Applications

Frequency response analysis is central to the design of filters, amplifiers, loudspeakers, microphones, and control systems. The bandwidth of a system, the resonant peaks in its response, and the phase behaviour all determine performance and stability; in control theory, Nyquist and Bode plots derived from the frequency response are used to assess stability margins.
