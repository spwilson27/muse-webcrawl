The 2nd Ukrainian Front (Russian: 2-й Украинский фронт) was a front — a formation equivalent to an army group — of the Soviet Red Army during the Eastern Front of World War II. It was formed on 20 October 1943 by the renaming of the Steppe Front, and it fought through Ukraine, Moldova, Romania, Hungary, Austria and Czechoslovakia before its dissolution in June 1945.

History

The front was created during the Soviet advance across Ukraine following the Battle of Kursk. Under its first commander, Ivan Konev, it took part in the Battle of the Dnieper, the liberation of Kiev's approaches from the east, the Korsun–Cherkassy Offensive of early 1944, and the Uman–Botoșani Offensive, which carried Soviet forces to the Romanian border.

In May 1944 Rodion Malinovsky took command. In August 1944 the front, together with the 3rd Ukrainian Front, conducted the Jassy–Kishinev Offensive, which knocked Romania out of the Axis and opened the Balkans to the Red Army. The front then advanced into Hungary, fighting in the Battle of Debrecen and the long Siege of Budapest (December 1944 – February 1945).

In the final months of the war the front repelled the German Operation Spring Awakening around Lake Balaton, took part in the Vienna Offensive in April 1945, and finished the war advancing into Czechoslovakia, participating in the Prague Offensive of May 1945. The front was disbanded on 10 June 1945, its headquarters forming the basis of the Odessa Military District.

Composition

At various times the front included the 4th, 5th, 6th, 7th and 9th Guards Armies, the 27th, 40th, 46th, 52nd and 53rd Armies, the 5th and 6th Guards Tank Armies, the 5th Air Army, the 1st Guards Cavalry-Mechanised Group, and, during the 1945 operations, the Romanian 1st and 4th Armies fighting alongside the Soviets.
