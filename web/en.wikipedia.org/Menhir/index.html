Menhir

A menhir (from Breton: men, "stone" and hir, "long") is a large upright standing stone, man-made and dating from the European middle Bronze Age. They can be found individually as monoliths, or as part of a group of similar stones, alignments, and stone circles.

Distribution

Menhirs are widespread across Europe, Africa, and Asia, but are most numerous in Western Europe, in particular in Brittany, France, where about 1,200 examples survive, including the great alignments at Carnac. The largest known menhir still standing is the Grand Menhir Brisé at Locmariaquer, once about 20.6 metres tall and now fallen and broken into four pieces.

History and purpose

Almost nothing is known of the social organisation or religious beliefs of the peoples who erected the menhirs. Proposed functions include territorial markers, calendrical or astronomical devices, memorials, and ritual or burial associations; many are found near tombs and later folk traditions attached healing and fertility powers to them.

In popular culture

Menhirs are familiar to modern readers through the Asterix comics, in which Obelix works as a menhir delivery man.
