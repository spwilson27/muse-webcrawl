Ranger tab

The Ranger tab is a military award of the United States Army awarded to soldiers who complete the U.S. Army Ranger School at Fort Moore (formerly Fort Benning), Georgia. It is a black-and-gold embroidered arc-shaped tab worn on the upper left sleeve of the uniform.

History and award

The tab was authorised in 1950 following the Korean War-era expansion of Ranger training. Ranger School is a roughly 62-day combat leadership course conducted in three phases (Benning, Mountain, and Florida/swamp phases) and is open to soldiers and members of other services; graduating earns the tab regardless of whether the soldier serves in a Ranger unit. Members assigned to the 75th Ranger Regiment additionally wear the distinctive tan beret and regimental scroll.

Notable milestones

In 2015, the first women graduated from Ranger School and earned the tab.
