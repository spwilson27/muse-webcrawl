Casco Bay is an inlet of the Gulf of Maine on the coast of Maine, United States. The bay, roughly 19 km (12 mi) long, is bounded by Cape Elizabeth to the south and Phippsburg to the northeast and shelters hundreds of islands, traditionally called the Calendar Islands after a (fanciful) count of 365 — a modern survey counted 785 islands and islets at low tide. The city of Portland, Maine's largest city and principal port, sits on its western shore.

Portland Head Light guards the entrance to Casco Bay.

History

The bay takes its name from the Spanish Bahía de Cascos, "Bay of Helmets," reported by the explorer Estêvão Gomes in 1525. English settlement began in the 1620s–30s; the settlement at present-day Portland was twice destroyed in wars with the Wabanaki Confederacy before being rebuilt. During the Second World War, Casco Bay was a major U.S. Navy anchorage and base, its islands fortified with coastal artillery; the defence site on Peaks Island and elsewhere survive as ruins and parks.

Islands and communities

Inhabited islands served by the Casco Bay Lines ferry from Portland include Peaks Island, Great Diamond Island, Long Island, Chebeague Island, Cliff Island, and Bailey Island, the last connected to the mainland by the unique cribstone Bailey Island Bridge (1928). Great Chebeague is among the largest unbridged islands on the U.S. east coast. Fort Gorges, a granite Civil War-era fort, stands on Hog Island Ledge.

Ecology

The bay is an estuary of national significance, supporting eelgrass beds, mudflats, and commercial lobster and shellfish fisheries. The nonprofit Friends of Casco Bay maintains the post of "Baykeeper" to monitor water quality, addressing stormwater pollution, nitrogen loading, and ocean acidification.
