Hyderabad is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Telangana and the de jure capital of Andhra Pradesh. It occupies 650 km² on the Deccan Plateau along the banks of the Musi River, in the northern part of Southern India. With a population of about 6.8 million in the city and 10 million in the metropolitan area, Hyderabad is the fourth-most populous city and sixth-most populous urban agglomeration in India.

History

Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah established Hyderabad in 1591 to extend the capital beyond the fortified Golconda. The city grew around the Charminar, a mosque and monument built in 1591 at its centre. The Qutb Shahi dynasty ruled until 1687, when the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb conquered the kingdom. The Asaf Jahi dynasty — the Nizams of Hyderabad — governed from 1724, presiding over Hyderabad State, the largest princely state in British India. In 1948, after Indian independence, the Indian Army annexed the state in Operation Polo, and Hyderabad became capital of Andhra Pradesh in 1956. When Telangana was carved out in 2014, Hyderabad remained the shared capital, a status that ended in 2024.

Geography and climate

The city sits on pink and grey granite hills interspersed with artificial lakes, the most famous being Hussain Sagar, excavated in 1562. Hyderabad has a tropical wet and dry climate with hot summers, a monsoon season from June to September, and mild winters.

Economy

Historically a trading centre for pearls and diamonds — hence "City of Pearls" — modern Hyderabad is a hub for information technology, biotechnology and pharmaceuticals. The HITEC City district houses major technology firms, and the city is often called "Cyberabad". It is also a major centre for the Telugu film industry, and Ramoji Film City is among the largest film studio complexes in the world.

Culture

Hyderabad blends Telugu and Deccani Urdu cultures. Its cuisine is famous for Hyderabadi biryani and haleem. Landmarks include the Charminar, Golconda Fort, the Mecca Masjid, Salar Jung Museum and the palaces of the Nizams such as Chowmahalla and Falaknuma. The Old City, south of the Musi, preserves the city's historic bazaars, including Laad Bazaar, known for bangles.
