Tirreno–Adriatico, nicknamed the "Race of the Two Seas", is an annual elite road cycling stage race held across Italy, traditionally running from the Tyrrhenian coast in the west to the Adriatic coast in the east and finishing in San Benedetto del Tronto. First held in 1966, it forms part of the UCI World Tour and, together with Paris–Nice run in the same week, serves as the leading early-season preparation race for Milan–San Remo and the Grand Tours.

Race character

The race typically lasts seven stages and combines flat sprint finishes, rolling stages in the Apennine foothills, short steep "wall" climbs characteristic of the Marche region, and an individual time trial on the final day along the San Benedetto seafront. The general classification leader wears a blue jersey, and the overall winner receives a trident trophy in honour of Neptune, god of the sea.

History

The Belgian Roger De Vlaeminck dominated the 1970s edition with six consecutive victories from 1972 to 1977, a record that still stands. Other multiple winners include Francesco Moser, Tony Rominger, Rolf Sørensen, Michele Bartoli, Paolo Bettini, Fabian Cancellara, Vincenzo Nibali and Primož Roglič, while Tadej Pogačar won back-to-back editions in 2021 and 2022.
