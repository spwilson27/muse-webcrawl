Frigate

A frigate is a type of warship. In different eras the term has referred to a variety of vessels, but in modern usage a frigate is a medium-sized surface combatant used to protect other warships and merchant-marine convoys, typically as an anti-submarine warfare (ASW) combatant and escort.

Age of sail

In the 17th and 18th centuries, frigates were fast, three-masted ships carrying their main armament on a single gun deck, used for scouting, commerce raiding, and patrolling. They were smaller and faster than ships of the line but powerful enough to defeat most smaller vessels. Famous examples include HMS Shannon and the American USS Constitution, one of the United States Navy's original six frigates.

Modern frigates

The name was revived in the Second World War for ocean-going escort vessels smaller than destroyers. Post-war frigates grew in size and capability, acquiring helicopters, guided missiles, and sophisticated sonar. Modern general-purpose frigates such as the FREMM class can displace over 6,000 tonnes, blurring the traditional boundary with destroyers.

Roles

Contemporary frigates perform anti-submarine warfare, anti-air defence of convoys, patrol, and presence missions. Many navies use "frigate" for their principal surface combatants.
