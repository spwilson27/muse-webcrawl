Olusegun Obasanjo

Olusegun Obasanjo (born 5 March 1937) is a Nigerian statesman and military leader who twice ruled Nigeria: as military head of state from 1976 to 1979, and as elected president from 1999 to 2007. He is unusual among African military rulers for having voluntarily handed power to a civilian government, presiding over Nigeria's first transfer from military to democratic rule in 1979.

Military career

Born in Abeokuta, in present-day Ogun State, Obasanjo joined the Nigerian Army in 1958 and trained in Britain. During the Nigerian Civil War (1967–70) he commanded the 3 Marine Commando Division and received the surrender of the Biafran forces in January 1970. After the 1975 coup he became chief of staff and deputy to head of state Murtala Muhammed; when Muhammed was assassinated in a failed coup attempt in February 1976, Obasanjo succeeded him.

First period in office, 1976–1979

As head of state, Obasanjo continued Muhammed's programme of returning Nigeria to civilian rule. Elections were held in 1979, and Obasanjo handed power to the elected president, Shehu Shagari, retiring to his farm. He became an international elder statesman, co-founding the African Leadership Forum and serving on various global commissions.

Imprisonment and return

Under the regime of General Sani Abacha, Obasanjo was convicted in 1995 by a secret tribunal of involvement in an alleged coup plot and imprisoned; he was released after Abacha's death in 1998. The following year he was elected president on the platform of the People's Democratic Party, and re-elected in 2003.

Presidency, 1999–2007

His civilian presidency restored democratic institutions, obtained substantial Paris Club debt relief for Nigeria, pursued economic reforms and an anti-corruption campaign, and deployed Nigerian troops in West African peacekeeping. Critics charged that elections under his watch were flawed and that his attempt to alter the constitution to allow a third term — defeated in the Senate in 2006 — marred his democratic legacy. He left office in 2007 and has since served as an African Union and United Nations envoy in African conflict mediation.
