Monjayaki

Place of originJapan
RegionKantō (Tsukishima, Tokyo)
Main ingredientsWheat-flour batter, dashi, cabbage, toppings

Monjayaki (often shortened to monja) is a Japanese pan-fried batter dish from the Kantō region, especially the Tsukishima district of Tokyo. It resembles okonomiyaki but uses a much more liquid batter, producing a thinner, softer result eaten directly from the griddle with small spatulas.

Preparation

Chopped ingredients such as cabbage, seafood, mentaiko, cheese, or mochi are stir-fried on a teppan griddle and formed into a ring; the seasoned flour-and-dashi batter is poured into the centre, allowed to bubble, then mixed through and spread thin. Diners scrape up small portions as the edges crisp.

History

Monjayaki is thought to descend from mojiyaki, a children's snack of the Meiji era in which batter was used to draw characters on a griddle, sold at dagashiya sweet shops in Tokyo's shitamachi neighbourhoods.
