African diaspora

The African diaspora is the worldwide collection of communities descended from people of African origin, predominantly in the Americas, Europe, and the Middle East. It was largely formed by the movement—mostly involuntary—of Africans through the trans-Atlantic, trans-Saharan, and Indian Ocean slave trades, and by later voluntary migration.

History

Between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries, an estimated 12.5 million Africans were shipped across the Atlantic, creating large populations in Brazil, the Caribbean, and North America. Twentieth- and twenty-first-century migration has created growing communities in Europe and elsewhere, with significant populations in France, the United Kingdom, and the United States.

Culture and identity

Diaspora communities developed distinctive cultures blending African, European, and Indigenous elements, shaping global music, religion, cuisine, and literature. The concept is central to Pan-Africanism, and the African Union recognizes the diaspora as its "sixth region," encouraging engagement with the continent.
