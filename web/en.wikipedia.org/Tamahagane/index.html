Tamahagane

Tamahagane (玉鋼) is a type of steel made in the Japanese tradition. The word tama means "precious", and hagane means "steel". Tamahagane is used to make Japanese swords (nihonto), such as katana, and some other bladed implements, and is produced from iron sand in a traditional clay furnace called a tatara.

Production

Tamahagane is made from satetsu (iron sand), a form of iron ore found in riverbeds and mountains, typically containing only about 1–2% iron by weight in its raw form, concentrated by washing. The sand is smelted in a tatara, a clay bloomery furnace that is built, fired for roughly three days, and then destroyed to extract the bloom of steel, called kera. Charcoal serves both as fuel and as the carbon source that carburizes the iron.

During the smelt, temperatures reach about 1,500 °C. The resulting kera weighs roughly two tonnes, of which perhaps half is usable steel. The swordsmith breaks the bloom into pieces and sorts them by carbon content: high-carbon pieces (around 1.0–1.5% carbon) form the hard outer skin (kawagane) of a blade, while lower-carbon steel forms the tough core (shingane).

Qualities

Good tamahagane is valued for its purity: the iron sand contains very little phosphorus and sulfur, impurities that weaken steel. The steel is inhomogeneous, with carbon content varying piece to piece, and the smith exploits this variation by forging and folding the metal many times, welding the pieces together and working out slag inclusions.

Modern production

Today most tamahagane is produced by the Nittoho Tatara operated in Shimane Prefecture by the Society for Preservation of Japanese Art Swords (NBTHK), which runs the furnace a few times each winter and distributes the steel to licensed swordsmiths. Authentic tamahagane is scarce and expensive, and it remains the legally required material for traditionally made Japanese swords.
