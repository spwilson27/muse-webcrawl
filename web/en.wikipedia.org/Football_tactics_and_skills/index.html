Football tactics and skills

Formations are described by numbers, such as the classic 4–4–2.

Football tactics and skills in association football encompass the formations, strategies, and individual techniques teams and players use to win matches. Tactics concern how a team is organized — its formation, style of play, pressing, and set pieces — while skills are the technical abilities of individual players, including passing, dribbling, shooting, heading, and tackling.

Formations

A formation describes how players are arranged, conventionally expressed as the numbers of defenders, midfielders, and forwards, excluding the goalkeeper. Historical formations include the attacking 2–3–5 "pyramid" of the early game, the WM formation developed in the 1920s, the 4–2–4 of Brazil's 1958 World Cup winners, and the 4–4–2 that became standard in much of Europe. Common modern formations include 4–3–3, 4–2–3–1, 3–5–2, and 3–4–3. Increasingly, teams use different shapes in possession and out of possession, and formations are treated as starting points rather than fixed structures.

Styles of play

Distinct tactical philosophies have shaped the game. Catenaccio, associated with Inter Milan in the 1960s, emphasized a sweeper and counter-attacking. Dutch "Total Football" of the 1970s demanded that players interchange positions fluidly. Possession-based tiki-taka, exemplified by Barcelona and Spain around 2008–2012, relied on short passing and positional play. Gegenpressing, popularized by German coaches such as Jürgen Klopp, seeks to win the ball back immediately after losing it. Other approaches include direct long-ball play, low-block defending with counter-attacks, and man-oriented pressing.

Skills

Core individual skills include:

  • Passing and first touch — moving the ball accurately and controlling it under pressure;
  • Dribbling — running with the ball past opponents, using feints and changes of pace;
  • Shooting — striking for goal with power, placement, volleys, and chips;
  • Heading — used for passing, clearing, and scoring, especially from crosses and set pieces;
  • Tackling and interception — regaining possession within the Laws of the Game;
  • Goalkeeping skills — handling, shot-stopping, positioning, and distribution.

Set pieces and game management

Set pieces — corners, free kicks, throw-ins, and penalties — account for a substantial share of goals and are rehearsed intensively, with specialist coaches now common. Tactics also cover substitutions, tempo control, time management, and adapting to match states, weather, and pitch conditions. Data analytics and video analysis have become central to tactical preparation in the professional game.
