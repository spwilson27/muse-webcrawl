Standard missile

A Standard Missile 3 (SM-3) interceptor is launched from a US Navy cruiser.

The Standard Missile (also written Standard missile) is a family of American shipborne surface-to-air and ballistic missile defense weapons produced by Raytheon and its predecessors for the United States Navy. The family includes the medium-range SM-1 and SM-2, the extended-range SM-2ER and SM-6, and the exo-atmospheric ballistic missile interceptor SM-3.

History

The Standard Missile programme began in the 1960s to replace the earlier Terrier, Tartar and Talos missiles with a common design. The SM-1 entered service in the 1970s on Tartar- and Terrier-equipped ships, while the SM-2, developed alongside the Aegis Combat System, added command/inertial midcourse guidance with terminal semi-active radar homing, greatly increasing engagement rates against saturation attacks.

Variants

SM-1 was the first generation, in medium-range (MR) and extended-range (ER) forms. SM-2 became the primary fleet air-defence weapon of Aegis cruisers and destroyers and was exported widely. The SM-3, developed with the Missile Defense Agency, uses a lightweight exo-atmospheric projectile (LEAP) kinetic warhead to intercept ballistic missiles outside the atmosphere and has destroyed a satellite in a 2008 demonstration. The SM-6 combines an active radar seeker derived from the AMRAAM with the SM-2 airframe, providing extended-range air defence and anti-ship strike capability.

Operators

The Standard family serves with the United States Navy and the navies of Japan, South Korea, Australia, Spain, the Netherlands, Germany, Canada and others.
