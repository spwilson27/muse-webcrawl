Backronym

A backronym is a type of acronym that is formed by treating an existing word as if it were an acronym and inventing an expansion for it, or by deliberately constructing a phrase whose initials spell a desired word. Unlike ordinary acronyms, where the phrase comes first, in a backronym the word comes first and the phrase is fitted to it.

Backronym
Type: Word formation
Term attested: 1983
Example: APGAR from Virginia Apgar's surname

Examples

The Apgar score used to assess newborn babies is named after its creator, anaesthesiologist Virginia Apgar; the mnemonic expansion "Appearance, Pulse, Grimace, Activity, Respiration" was devised later to fit her name. The USA PATRIOT Act of 2001 carries an official long title — "Uniting and Strengthening America by Providing Appropriate Tools Required to Intercept and Obstruct Terrorism" — engineered to produce the word PATRIOT. Mothers Against Drunk Driving is commonly said to have inspired similar engineered names in legislation and organisations.

Backronyms are also used humorously or as mnemonics, and folk backronyms frequently attach false expansions to words that were never acronyms at all, such as the claim that "posh" derives from "port out, starboard home".
