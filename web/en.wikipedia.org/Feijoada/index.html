Feijoada is a stew of beans with beef and pork, considered the national dish of Brazil. The name derives from feijão, the Portuguese word for bean. In its classic Brazilian form, the dish is made with black beans simmered for hours with a variety of salted and smoked pork and beef cuts, and it is traditionally served on Wednesdays and Saturdays in restaurants across the country.

History

A popular but contested legend holds that feijoada was created by enslaved Africans in Brazil, who were given the less desirable cuts of pork — ears, feet, tails — by plantation owners and combined them with black beans. Culinary historians have challenged this account, noting that similar bean-and-meat stews are common in Portuguese and broader European peasant cooking, and that dishes such as the French cassoulet and the Portuguese feijoada à transmontana share the same logic of slow-cooking beans with preserved meats. Whatever its precise origins, feijoada became a fixture of Brazilian communal eating in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, associated with weekend gatherings, samba schools, and family celebrations.

Preparation

The beans are typically soaked overnight, then simmered with salted pork products that have themselves been soaked to remove excess salt: ribs, ears, feet, bacon, sausage (linguiça), and dried beef (carne seca). The stew cooks slowly until the beans are tender and the broth thick and dark. It is seasoned with bay leaves, garlic, and onion.

Accompaniments

Feijoada is served with white rice, sautéed collard greens (couve) finely shredded, orange slices — said to aid digestion and cut the richness — toasted manioc flour (farofa), and a hot pepper sauce. A caipirinha, Brazil's cachaça-and-lime cocktail, is the traditional drink alongside it.

Variations

In Portugal, feijoada is made with white or red beans and includes vegetables such as tomatoes and carrots. In Brazil, regional variations exist, including lighter weekday versions made with fewer meats. Vegetarian adaptations substitute smoked tofu, mushrooms, and root vegetables for the pork.
