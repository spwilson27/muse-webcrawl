Brassicaceae (also called Cruciferae) is a medium-sized and economically important family of flowering plants commonly known as the mustards, the crucifers, or the cabbage family. Most are herbaceous plants; the family includes many familiar vegetables and oilseed crops.

Characteristics

Members typically have flowers with four petals arranged in a cross (hence "crucifer"), four sepals, and six stamens—four long and two short (tetradynamous). Fruits are often elongated pods called siliques or shorter silicles. Many species produce glucosinolates, sulphur-containing compounds responsible for the pungent flavours of mustard and horseradish and implicated in pest defence.

Important genera and crops

Brassica alone supplies cabbage, kale, broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, turnip, and canola (oilseed rape). Other crops include radish (Raphanus), mustard (Sinapis, Brassica juncea), watercress, rocket (arugula), and wasabi. The model organism Arabidopsis thaliana (thale cress) is the most studied plant in genetics and molecular biology.

Distribution

The family is distributed worldwide except Antarctica, with greatest diversity in the Mediterranean region, southwest and central Asia, and western North America.
