Alopecia

Definition: Hair loss from the scalp or body
Types: Androgenetic, areata, telogen effluvium, scarring
Specialty: Dermatology

Alopecia is the medical term for hair loss, whether from the scalp or from other parts of the body. It is a symptom rather than a single disease and arises from many causes, including genetics, autoimmune disease, hormonal change, infection, medication, and physical damage to the hair or follicles. The most common form is androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss), which affects a majority of men and a substantial fraction of women by late middle age.

Types and causes

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune condition causing patchy hair loss, occasionally progressing to total scalp loss (alopecia totalis) or whole-body loss (alopecia universalis). Telogen effluvium is a temporary shedding triggered by illness, childbirth, or stress. Scarring (cicatricial) alopecias, such as lichen planopilaris, destroy the follicle permanently. Other causes include tinea capitis, traction from tight hairstyles, trichotillomania, and chemotherapy.

Treatment

Treatments depend on the type: minoxidil and finasteride are standard for pattern hair loss; corticosteroids and JAK inhibitors are used for alopecia areata; hair transplantation redistributes follicles surgically. Hair loss can have significant psychological effects, and support groups exist in many countries.
