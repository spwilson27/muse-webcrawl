Minbar

LocationMosque, to the right of the mihrab
FunctionSeat/stair for the imam delivering the khutbah

A minbar (Arabic: منبر) is a pulpit in a mosque where the imam (leader of prayers) stands to deliver sermons (khutbah), especially the Friday sermon. In its simplest form the minbar is a short flight of steps with a seat and often a canopy, positioned to the right of the mihrab as one faces the qibla wall.

History

The Prophet Muhammad is said to have delivered sermons leaning against a palm trunk, and later from a simple seat of tamarisk wood with two steps; the form grew taller under the Umayyad caliphs, acquiring a door, canopy and sometimes a curtain. Over the centuries minbars became major works of Islamic art — in carved wood, marble or stone, decorated with geometric inlay, calligraphy and muqarnas. The minbar of the Kutubiyya Mosque in Marrakesh, made in Córdoba in the 12th century, and Saladin's minbar in the al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem (destroyed by arson in 1969 and later reconstructed) are among the most celebrated.

Symbolism

Beyond its practical role of elevating the speaker so the congregation can see and hear him, the minbar historically symbolised religious and political authority: delivering the khutbah from it, with the ruler's name mentioned, was a public sign of legitimate sovereignty.
