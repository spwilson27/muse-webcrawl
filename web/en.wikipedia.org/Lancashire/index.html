Lancashire is a ceremonial county in North West England, bordered by Cumbria, North Yorkshire, West Yorkshire, Greater Manchester, and Merseyside, with a coastline on the Irish Sea. The ceremonial county has a population of about 1.5 million; the historic County Palatine of Lancaster was larger, once encompassing much of present-day Greater Manchester and Merseyside.

History

The county took shape in the twelfth century around the castle at Lancaster. During the Industrial Revolution, Lancashire became the world centre of cotton textiles: mill towns such as Burnley, Blackburn, and Preston spun and wove cotton imported through Liverpool, powered by steam and coal from the Lancashire Coalfield. The rivalry with Yorkshire, expressed in the Wars of the Roses, endures symbolically in the red rose emblem.

Geography and culture

The landscape runs from the flat Fylde coast and the resort of Blackpool to the uplands of the Forest of Bowland and Pendle Hill. Lancashire is noted for its dialect and accent, its hotpot and cheese, and for exports such as the sitcom-era music hall tradition and numerous brass bands.
