Phanerozoic

Span538.8 million years ago – present
ErasPaleozoic, Mesozoic, Cenozoic
Preceded byProterozoic Eon
Name meaning"Visible life" (Greek phanerós + zōḗ)

The Phanerozoic is the current and most recent of the four geologic eons in the Earth's geologic time scale, covering the last 538.8 million years. Its name, meaning "visible life", reflects the abundant fossil record of animals, plants, and other complex organisms that characterises it. It began with the Cambrian explosion, the rapid diversification of animal life, and continues to the present day.

Subdivisions

The Phanerozoic is divided into three eras. The Paleozoic ("old life", 538.8–251.9 Ma) saw the colonisation of land by plants and animals, the evolution of fish, amphibians, and reptiles, and ended with the Permian–Triassic mass extinction, the largest in Earth history. The Mesozoic ("middle life", 251.9–66 Ma) was the age of reptiles, dominated by dinosaurs, and ended with the asteroid impact that caused the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction. The Cenozoic ("new life", 66 Ma–present) is the age of mammals and birds, encompassing the Quaternary ice ages and the evolution of humans.

Life

Although complex life originated earlier, in the Ediacaran, the Phanerozoic is when hard-shelled, readily fossilised organisms became abundant, making its fossil record far richer than that of the Precambrian. All five of the "Big Five" mass extinctions occurred during this eon.

Climate and geography

The eon spans the assembly and breakup of the supercontinent Pangaea, repeated cycles of greenhouse and icehouse climates, the opening of the Atlantic Ocean, and the rise of the Himalayas and other modern mountain ranges.
