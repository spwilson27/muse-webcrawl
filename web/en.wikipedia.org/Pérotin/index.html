Pérotin

Notation from a manuscript of Pérotin's organum

Pérotin (fl. c. 1200), also called Perotinus and Magister Perotinus, was a European composer, believed to be French, who flourished around the end of the 12th and the beginning of the 13th century. He was the most famous member of the Notre Dame school of polyphony and one of the very few composers of his day to be known by name.

Life and works

Virtually nothing is known of Pérotin's life. Our knowledge of him comes mainly from the writings of an anonymous English student at Notre Dame, now known as Anonymous IV, who described him as the best composer of discant ("optimus discantor") and credited him with revising the Magnus liber organi ("Great Book of Organum") of his predecessor Léonin. Pérotin is credited with pioneering organum in three and four voices — the organa quadrupla Viderunt omnes and Sederunt, performed at Notre Dame Cathedral around 1198–1199, were the first known four-part compositions in European music.

Style and significance

Pérotin's music is characterised by the rhythmic modes, by long melismatic passages over sustained chant notes in the tenor, and by passages of discant in which all voices move in measured rhythm. His works mark a decisive step toward the motet and toward the systematic notation of rhythm, and they remained famous throughout the 13th century.
