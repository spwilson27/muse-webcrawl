Guadiana

The Guadiana is a river of Spain and Portugal, one of the longest on the Iberian Peninsula at about 744 kilometres (462 miles). Rising in the Campo de Montiel region of La Mancha in east-central Spain, it flows generally westward through Extremadura, then turns south to form part of the Spanish–Portuguese border before emptying into the Gulf of Cádiz in the Atlantic Ocean between Vila Real de Santo António (Portugal) and Ayamonte (Spain).

Course

The river's headwaters are a complex of springs and lagoons near the Lagunas de Ruidera. In its upper course the Guadiana famously disappears underground in the karstic Mancha plain and re-emerges at the Ojos del Guadiana near Daimiel, a phenomenon associated with the Tablas de Daimiel wetlands. It flows past Mérida and Badajoz, enters Portugal near the Alqueva reservoir — one of western Europe's largest artificial lakes — and reaches the sea below Mértola and the border towns of its estuary.

Use and ecology

Numerous dams regulate the river for irrigation and hydroelectric power, most extensively in Spain's Plan Badajoz irrigation scheme. Heavy groundwater abstraction in La Mancha has at times reduced flows and dried the Tablas de Daimiel, prompting long-running disputes over water management. The basin supports rice and fruit farming in its lower reaches and contains protected habitats for waterbirds.

Name

The name derives from the Arabic Wadi Ana, combining wadi (river valley) with Anas, the river's Roman name.
