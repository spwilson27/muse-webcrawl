Royal Institution

The Royal Institution of Great Britain (often abbreviated as Ri) is an organisation for scientific education and research, based in London and founded in 1799 by the leading British scientists of the age, including Henry Cavendish and its first president, George Finch, 9th Earl of Winchilsea. It was founded to introduce new technologies and teach science to the general public, and it has been based at 21 Albemarle Street in Mayfair since its foundation.

History and research

The Institution's laboratories became famous in the nineteenth century. Humphry Davy, appointed lecturer in 1801, isolated several elements including potassium and sodium and invented the miners' safety lamp bearing his name. His protégé Michael Faraday, initially Davy's assistant, became the Institution's most celebrated scientist, discovering electromagnetic induction and the laws of electrolysis there. In total, fifteen scientists associated with the Royal Institution won Nobel Prizes, and elements including sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, strontium, barium, boron, and argon were discovered or isolated in its laboratories. John Tyndall, James Dewar, and William and Lawrence Bragg also worked there.

Lectures and public engagement

The Royal Institution is famed for its public lectures, modelled on Davy's popular demonstrations. Faraday founded the Friday Evening Discourses in 1825 and, in 1826, the Christmas Lectures for young people, which continue today and are broadcast on British television — lecturers have included many of the most prominent scientists of their day. The Institution runs schools programmes, a young science centre (the L'Oréal Young Scientist Centre), and a popular science video channel.

The Faraday Museum and recent history

The building houses the Faraday Museum, which preserves Faraday's laboratory much as it was in the 1850s. The Institution faced a financial crisis in the 2010s after an expensive refurbishment, and considered selling its historic home before a fundraising campaign and restructuring secured its future.
