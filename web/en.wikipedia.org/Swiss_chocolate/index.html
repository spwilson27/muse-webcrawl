Swiss chocolate

Image placeholder: Swiss chocolate bars.

Swiss chocolate is chocolate produced in Switzerland. Switzerland is particularly renowned for its milk chocolate: in 1875 Daniel Peter invented milk chocolate using condensed milk, and in 1879 Rodolphe Lindt invented the conching process, which gave chocolate its smooth, melting texture.

History

Chocolate factories were established in Switzerland in the 19th century by pioneers such as François-Louis Cailler (1819), Philippe Suchard (1826), and later Henri Nestlé, Theodor Tobler — creator of the triangular Toblerone in 1908 — and the Lindt & Sprüngli company. Swiss innovators combined high-quality cocoa with abundant Alpine milk and precision manufacturing, and Swiss chocolate became a byword for quality worldwide.

Industry today

Major Swiss chocolate manufacturers include Lindt & Sprüngli, Nestlé, Barry Callebaut (the world's largest chocolate producer, though mainly business-to-business), Chocolats Halba and Villars. The Swiss eat more chocolate per person than almost any other nation — around 8–9 kg per capita annually — and most production is exported. Swiss law requires chocolate labelled "Swiss" to be manufactured in Switzerland. See also Swiss cheese.
