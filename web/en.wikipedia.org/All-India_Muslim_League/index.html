The All-India Muslim League was a political party established in Dhaka in 1906 to safeguard the political rights of Muslims in British India. Under the leadership of Muhammad Ali Jinnah it came to demand the creation of a separate Muslim-majority state, and it was the driving force behind the Partition of India and the creation of Pakistan in 1947.

Early years

The League was founded by Muslim nobles, landowners and professionals, with the Aga Khan as honorary president, partly in response to fears that the Hindu-majority Indian National Congress could not represent Muslim interests. The Morley–Minto Reforms of 1909 granted Muslims separate electorates, a League objective. Jinnah, initially an ambassador of Hindu–Muslim unity, helped broker the Lucknow Pact of 1916, in which Congress and the League presented joint demands — but relations deteriorated after the collapse of the Khilafat movement and Congress rule in the provinces after 1937.

The Pakistan movement

In March 1940 the League's Lahore Resolution demanded independent states in the Muslim-majority regions of the north-west and east. Sweeping the Muslim seats in the 1945–46 elections, the League pressed its claim through negotiations such as the failed Cabinet Mission Plan and "Direct Action Day" (August 1946), which sparked widespread communal violence. The result was the Partition of British India in August 1947 and Jinnah as Pakistan's first Governor-General.

Aftermath

Independent India's Muslim League shrank to a small regional party (the Indian Union Muslim League, mainly in Kerala), while in Pakistan the League fragmented into competing factions, though its name has been revived by later parties, notably the Pakistan Muslim League.
