Preta

Preta (Sanskrit: प्रेत, "departed one"; Tibetan: ཡི་དྭགས་, yi dags; Chinese: 餓鬼, èguǐ, "hungry ghost") is the Sanskrit name for a type of supernatural being described in Hinduism, Buddhism, Taoism, and Chinese folk religion as undergoing suffering greater than that of humans, particularly an extreme degree of hunger and thirst. Pretas are believed to have been greedy, corrupt, compulsive, deceitful, jealous, or greedy people in a previous life. As a result of their karma, they are afflicted with an insatiable hunger for a particular substance or object.

Pretas are typically depicted with huge stomachs, tiny mouths, and thin necks.

Description

Pretas are usually depicted as beings with sunken, mummified skin, narrow limbs, distended bellies, and long, thin necks — a form reflecting their condition of maximum hunger with minimum capacity for satisfaction. Through the influence of Chinese folk beliefs, Japanese Buddhist tradition, and other sources, pretas have come to be popularly known in English as "hungry ghosts." Traditional sources describe several kinds of preta, each suffering a different affliction: some cannot find food or drink, some find that food turns to flame or excrement in their mouths, and others are tormented by demons who force them to eat corpses or their own children.

In Buddhism

In Buddhist cosmology, the preta realm (or "hungry ghost realm") is one of the six realms of rebirth. Beings are reborn there as a result of excessive greed, stinginess, and attachment in a previous life. The realm is a vast desert-like wasteland where pretas wander in search of food and drink. In East Asian Buddhism, pretas are the object of rituals such as the Japanese segaki ("feeding the hungry ghosts") ceremony and the Chinese Ghost Festival, during which food offerings are made to ease their suffering. Japanese tradition divides pretas into classes such as "no food" pretas, "food-finding" pretas, and "much food" pretas.

In Hinduism

In Hindu tradition, preta refers more broadly to the spirit of a dead person during the period between death and the performance of the funeral rites (śrāddha) that transform the deceased into an honoured ancestor (pitṛ). If the rites are not properly performed, the dead may linger as pretas. Hindu scriptures such as the Garuda Purana describe the journey of the preta through the underworld.
