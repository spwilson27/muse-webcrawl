Choir

A choir is a musical ensemble of singers. Choral music, in turn, is music written specifically for such an ensemble. Choirs may perform music from the classical repertoire through to popular songs, a cappella or accompanied by instruments, and range from small chamber groups to massed choirs of hundreds.

Structure

Most choirs are led by a conductor or choirmaster who directs rehearsals and performances with hand and arm gestures. Singers are typically divided by voice type into soprano, alto, tenor, and bass sections (SATB), though many other arrangements exist, including treble, male-voice, and female-voice choirs.

History

Group singing is ancient, but the choir as an institution developed within the Christian church, where plainchant and later polyphony were sung by clerks and choristers in cathedrals and monasteries. The Renaissance and Baroque eras produced elaborate choral works by composers such as Palestrina, Bach, and Handel, whose oratorios remain staples of the repertoire.

Types of choir

  • Church and cathedral choirs
  • Chamber choirs
  • Community and choral societies
  • School and youth choirs
  • Gospel choirs
  • Barbershop and a cappella groups
