Haft-Sin

Haft-Sin (Persian: هفت‌سین, "seven sins") is an arrangement of seven symbolic items whose names start with the letter "س" (sin), the 15th letter in the Persian alphabet; haft (هفت) is Persian for "seven". It is traditionally displayed at Nowruz, the Iranian New Year, celebrated on the day of the vernal equinox, marking the beginning of spring in the Northern Hemisphere.

The seven items

The primary items are:

  • Sabzeh (سبزه) – wheat, barley, mung bean or lentil sprouts grown in a dish — symbolizing rebirth and nature.
  • Samanu (سمنو) – sweet pudding made from germinated wheat — symbolizing affluence and fertility.
  • Senjed (سنجد) – oleaster or dried wild olive fruit — symbolizing love.
  • Sir (سیر) – garlic — symbolizing medicine and health.
  • Sib (سیب) – apple — symbolizing beauty.
  • Somāq (سماق) – sumac — symbolizing the colour of sunrise.
  • Serkeh (سرکه) – vinegar — symbolizing age and patience.

Other items

Other items commonly placed on the table include a mirror (symbolizing the sky and honesty), candles (light and happiness), painted eggs (fertility), a goldfish in a bowl (life), hyacinth flowers, coins (prosperity), and a book of wisdom, often the Quran, the Avesta, the Shahnameh, or the Divan of Hafez. Families gather around the table at the exact moment of the equinox to exchange gifts and good wishes.
