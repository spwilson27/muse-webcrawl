Yeomen of the Guard

Founded1485, by Henry VII
RoleRoyal bodyguard (ceremonial)
Nickname"Beefeaters"

The King's Body Guard of the Yeomen of the Guard is a ceremonial bodyguard of the British monarch, created by Henry VII in 1485 after the Battle of Bosworth Field. It is the oldest military corps in the United Kingdom still in existence.

Duties

Today the Guard's duties are purely ceremonial: attending the State Opening of Parliament, royal garden parties, and the Maundy service, and carrying out the traditional search of the cellars of the Houses of Parliament before the State Opening — a custom dating from the Gunpowder Plot of 1605. Members are retired senior non-commissioned officers of the armed forces.

Uniform

The Yeomen wear a distinctive Tudor-style uniform of red, gold, and dark blue. They are often confused with the Yeomen Warders of the Tower of London, a separate corps with a similar uniform and the same "Beefeater" nickname.
