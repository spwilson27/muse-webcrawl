Second Chōshū expedition

The Second Chōshū expedition (Japanese: 第二次長州征伐), also called the Summer War, was a punitive military campaign led by the Tokugawa shogunate against the rebellious Chōshū Domain in western Japan in 1866. Unlike the bloodless First Chōshū expedition of 1864, it ended in a humiliating defeat for the shogunate, exposing its military weakness and hastening the collapse of Tokugawa rule.

Background

Chōshū, a centre of the sonnō jōi ("revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians") movement, had been punished in 1864. Thereafter its reformist leadership, including Takasugi Shinsaku, modernised the domain's forces with Western rifles and mixed samurai–commoner militia units such as the Kiheitai.

The war

Shogunate forces attacked Chōshū on several fronts in the summer of 1866 but were repeatedly outfought by Chōshū's modernised troops. The sudden death of the shōgun Tokugawa Iemochi in August 1866 gave the shogunate a pretext to arrange a ceasefire, and the campaign was abandoned.

Consequences

The defeat shattered the shogunate's prestige. Chōshū and Satsuma, now secretly allied, moved openly against the Tokugawa, and within two years the Boshin War brought about the Meiji Restoration.
