Baldwin II of Jerusalem

Baldwin II (c. 1075 – 21 August 1131), also known as Baldwin of Bourcq, was Count of Edessa from 1100 to 1118 and King of Jerusalem from 1118 until his death. A cousin of Godfrey of Bouillon and Baldwin I, he joined the First Crusade and rose to prominence in the Crusader states of the Levant.

Count of Edessa

When Baldwin I left Edessa to become King of Jerusalem in 1100, Baldwin of Bourcq succeeded him as count. He was captured by the Seljuk Turks at the Battle of Harran in 1104 and held prisoner for several years, first at Khosrova and then at Mosul, before being ransomed in 1108. He then had to recover his county from his regent, Tancred of Antioch.

King of Jerusalem

On Baldwin I's death in 1118, Baldwin was elected king. His reign was dominated by near-constant warfare with the Muslim powers surrounding the kingdom. He was again captured, in 1123, and held by the Artuqids for over a year. In 1125 he won a major victory at the Battle of Azaz. Baldwin arranged the marriage of his eldest daughter Melisende to Fulk V of Anjou, and on his death in 1131 was succeeded by Melisende and Fulk.
