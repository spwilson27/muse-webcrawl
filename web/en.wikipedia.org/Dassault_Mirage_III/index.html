The Dassault Mirage III (French pronunciation: [miʁaʒ]) is a family of single-seat, single-engine, fighter aircraft developed and manufactured by French aircraft company Dassault Aviation. It was the first Western European combat aircraft to exceed Mach 2 in horizontal flight, a feat it achieved on 24 October 1958. The Mirage III's tailless delta-wing configuration became iconic and formed the basis of an entire family of Dassault fighters.

Development

The Mirage originated in a 1952 French Air Force requirement for a lightweight, all-weather interceptor. Dassault's MD.550 Mirage I and the larger Mirage III prototype led to the definitive pre-production Mirage IIIA and then the production Mirage IIIC interceptor, which entered service in 1961. The most numerous variant, the Mirage IIIE, was a multirole strike/interceptor version with a longer fuselage, improved avionics and an optional rocket engine pack; reconnaissance (IIIR) and two-seat trainer (IIIB) variants followed.

Operational history

The Mirage III saw extensive combat with the Israeli Air Force during the Six-Day War (1967) and the Yom Kippur War (1973), where it achieved a distinguished record in air-to-air combat. It was produced under licence in Australia (as the Mirage IIIO) and Switzerland, and inspired the Israeli IAI Nesher and Kfir derivatives based on plans obtained through espionage. More than 1,400 Mirage IIIs were built and the type served with some twenty air forces, including those of France, Israel, Australia, South Africa, Pakistan, Spain, Argentina and Brazil; upgraded examples remained in service, notably with Pakistan, into the 21st century.
