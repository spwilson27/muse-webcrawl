Satyajit Ray (2 May 1921 – 23 April 1992) was an Indian film director, screenwriter, author, illustrator, graphic designer and film critic. Regarded as one of the greatest filmmakers of all time, he directed 36 films and brought Indian cinema to world attention with the Apu Trilogy (1955–1959). He received an Honorary Academy Award shortly before his death, and India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna, in 1992.

Early life

Ray was born in Calcutta into a distinguished Bengali family: his grandfather Upendrakishore Ray Chowdhury was a writer and publisher, and his father Sukumar Ray a celebrated nonsense poet. Ray studied economics at Presidency College and fine arts at Visva-Bharati, Santiniketan, then worked as a commercial artist and illustrator. Inspired by Vittorio De Sica's Bicycle Thieves during a 1950 trip to London, he resolved to film Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay's novel Pather Panchali.

Career

Pather Panchali (1955), made on a shoestring with a largely amateur cast and crew, won the Best Human Document award at Cannes and launched the Parallel Cinema movement. The trilogy continued with Aparajito (1956, Golden Lion at Venice) and Apur Sansar (1959). Later masterpieces include Jalsaghar (1958), Devi (1960), Charulata (1964), the Calcutta trilogy of the 1970s, and his final films Ganashatru (1989), Shakha Proshakha (1990) and Agantuk (1991). Ray also wrote beloved children's literature — the detective Feluda and scientist Professor Shonku series — and revived the magazine Sandesh.

Style

Ray's humanist realism, economy of means, scriptwriting from detailed storyboards, and control over nearly every aspect of production (he composed music for most later films and designed his own posters and credit sequences) made him the model of the complete auteur.
