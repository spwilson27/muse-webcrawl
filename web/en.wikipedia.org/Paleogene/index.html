The Paleogene Period (also spelled Palaeogene) is the first of three periods of the Cenozoic Era in the geologic time scale. It spans the time from the end of the Cretaceous Period 66 million years ago to the beginning of the Neogene Period 23.04 million years ago. It is divided into the Paleocene, Eocene, and Oligocene epochs. The older term "Tertiary" covered the Paleogene plus the Neogene.

Life

The Paleogene begins immediately after the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event, which eliminated the non-avian dinosaurs and much other life. Mammals, previously mostly small, diversified explosively into the vacated niches, evolving the ancestors of most modern orders; birds, flowering plants, and insects likewise radiated. In the seas, foraminifera, molluscs, and eventually the first kelp forests and modern-style reef communities developed.

Climate and geography

The interval began warm, culminating in the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum and the Early Eocene Climatic Optimum, when tropical forests reached high latitudes and crocodilians lived within the Arctic Circle. Thereafter the climate cooled and dried, ending with a sharp cooling at the Eocene–Oligocene transition (~34 mya), when the Antarctic ice sheet formed. Continents drifted toward their modern positions: India collided with Asia, Australia separated from Antarctica, and the Atlantic widened.
