Chemoprophylaxis

Chemoprophylaxis is the administration of a medication for the purpose of preventing disease or infection. Antibiotics, antimalarials, and antivirals are commonly used as chemoprophylactic agents. It contrasts with treatment given after disease has developed and with immunoprophylaxis by vaccination, although the strategies are often combined in practice.

Uses

Examples include antimalarial prophylaxis for travellers to endemic regions, antibiotic prophylaxis before surgery or dental procedures in at-risk patients, post-exposure prophylaxis with antiretrovirals after potential HIV exposure, pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) for HIV prevention, and preventive therapy for tuberculosis in close contacts of infectious cases.

Considerations

Effective chemoprophylaxis requires weighing the risk of infection against drug adverse effects, cost, and adherence demands. Widespread prophylactic antibiotic use can promote antimicrobial resistance, so guidelines generally reserve it for situations where the risk of serious infection is demonstrably high.

Related concepts

Chemoprophylaxis in pregnancy and childhood — for example, intermittent preventive treatment of malaria — is a major public-health tool in endemic settings. In veterinary medicine, prophylactic medication is used to prevent disease in herds and flocks, a practice increasingly scrutinized because of resistance concerns.
