The Bronx


The Bronx is the northernmost borough of New York City.

The Bronx is a borough of New York City, coterminous with Bronx County, in the U.S. state of New York. It is south of Westchester County, north and east of the island and borough of Manhattan across the Harlem River, and north of the borough of Queens across the East River. The Bronx is the northernmost of the city's five boroughs and the only one located primarily on the U.S. mainland. With a population of roughly 1.4 million, it is the fourth-most populous of the five boroughs.

Etymology

The name derives from the Bronx River, which was named for Jonas Bronck, a Scandinavian-born settler who established the area's first recorded European settlement in 1639. The definite article in "the Bronx" is generally attributed to the practice of referring to the Bronck family and then the river by name.

History

Before European colonization, the area was inhabited by the Siwanoy, a band of the Wappinger people. The Dutch and English settled farms in the area during the 17th and 18th centuries. The territory was annexed to New York City in stages in 1874 and 1895 and became a borough in 1898 and a separate county in 1914. The arrival of subway lines in the early 20th century spurred rapid development. The borough experienced severe decline in the 1970s, a period associated with arson and disinvestment, followed by significant revitalization from the 1990s onward.

Geography and landmarks

The Bronx is home to Yankee Stadium, home of the New York Yankees; the Bronx Zoo, one of the largest metropolitan zoos in the world; the New York Botanical Garden; the Grand Concourse boulevard; and neighborhoods such as Riverdale, Fordham, Belmont (the "Little Italy" of the Bronx), and the South Bronx, the birthplace of hip-hop music and culture in the 1970s.

Demographics

The Bronx is one of the most ethnically diverse areas in the United States. It has a large Hispanic and Latino population, particularly Puerto Rican and Dominican communities, alongside historically significant African American, Irish, Italian, Jewish, and Albanian communities. It is the poorest of New York City's boroughs by median household income.
