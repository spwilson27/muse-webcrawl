Dream pop

Dream pop
Stylistic originsAlternative rock, psychedelia, ambient, ethereal wave
Cultural origins1980s, United Kingdom

Related genresShoegaze, slowcore, chillwave

Dream pop is a subgenre of alternative rock that emphasizes atmosphere, texture, and mood over conventional song structure. Emerging in the 1980s — the term was coined by Alex Ayuli of A.R. Kane — it is characterized by breathy, often indistinct vocals, lush reverb and echo, shimmering guitar effects, and production that submerges melody in a hazy, dreamlike soundscape.

History

Precursors include the Velvet Underground, Phil Spector's Wall of Sound, and the Beach Boys; the genre's definitive early sound was created by Scotland's Cocteau Twins, whose guitarist Robin Guthrie layered treated guitars beneath Elizabeth Fraser's soprano vocals. On the 4AD label, dream pop overlapped with the "ethereal wave" roster. In the late 1980s and early 1990s, bands such as My Bloody Valentine pushed the style toward the denser, louder sound that became shoegaze, while quieter acts (Galaxie 500, Mazzy Star) folded it into slowcore. A 2000s–2010s revival led by Beach House, and later artists such as Cigarettes After Sex, brought dream pop renewed prominence.
