Deccan Plateau

The Deccan Plateau is a large plateau in southern India, extending over most of the southern part of the country between the Western Ghats and the Eastern Ghats mountain ranges. It covers an area of roughly 500,000 square kilometres across the states of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.

Geology

Much of the plateau consists of basaltic lava flows of the Deccan Traps, erupted around 66 million years ago near the end of the Cretaceous period, in one of the largest volcanic events in Earth's history. The eruptions have been linked in some studies to the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event. The plateau tilts gently eastward, so most of its major rivers — the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri — flow east into the Bay of Bengal.

Climate and agriculture

The plateau lies in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats, giving it a semi-arid climate in the northwest and a tropical climate elsewhere. Its fertile black cotton soils (regur), derived from weathered basalt, support crops such as cotton, sorghum, pulses and oilseeds.

History

The Deccan was home to major powers including the Satavahana dynasty, the Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas, the Vijayanagara Empire and the Deccan Sultanates, and was a major theatre of the Anglo-Mysore and Anglo-Maratha wars in the 18th century.
