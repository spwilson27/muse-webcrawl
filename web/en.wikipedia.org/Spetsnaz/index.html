Spetsnaz

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Spetsnaz (Russian: спецназ, from voyska spetsialnogo naznacheniya, "special-purpose forces") is a Russian term for special forces units of Russia and other post-Soviet states. Historically, the term referred to the special military units controlled by the military intelligence service GRU (Spetsnaz GRU), but in modern usage it applies broadly to special operations and special-purpose units across Russian agencies, including the FSB's Alpha Group and Vympel, and units of the National Guard (Rosgvardiya) and other ministries.

History

The Soviet Union formed dedicated special-purpose reconnaissance units after the Second World War, placing Spetsnaz brigades under the GRU to conduct reconnaissance, sabotage, and targeting of enemy nuclear weapons deep behind NATO lines in the event of war. Spetsnaz personnel saw extensive service in Afghanistan and in Chechnya. Naval Spetsnaz units included combat frogmen trained for sabotage against ships and coastal installations.

Organization and selection

Modern Russian special forces are divided among several services: the Special Operations Forces Command (KSSO), established in 2013 for operations abroad; GRU Spetsnaz brigades of the armed forces; FSB special-purpose centers for counterterrorism; and police special units such as OMON and SOBR. Selection and training emphasize endurance, marksmanship, airborne insertion, hand-to-hand combat, and survival.

Notable operations

Spetsnaz units have been associated with operations including the storming of the Amin palace in Kabul in 1979 (Operation Storm-333), hostage rescues during the Beslan school siege and the Moscow theater hostage crisis (both marked by heavy hostage casualties), and more recent deployments reported in Syria and Ukraine.

In popular culture

The term entered Western awareness during the Cold War and remains common in fiction and video games, often applied loosely to any Russian special forces soldier.
