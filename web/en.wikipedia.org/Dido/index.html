Dido

Dido (also known as Elissa) was, according to ancient Greco-Roman sources, the founder and first queen of the Phoenician city-state of Carthage, located in modern-day Tunisia. Her story is best known from Virgil's epic poem the Aeneid.

Legend

Dido was a princess of Tyre. After her brother Pygmalion murdered her husband Sychaeus, she fled with her followers and wealth across the Mediterranean to North Africa. There, legend says, she obtained as much land as could be covered by an oxhide; by cutting the hide into thin strips she enclosed the hill of Byrsa, founding Carthage around 814 BC.

In the Aeneid

In Virgil's Aeneid, the Trojan hero Aeneas is shipwrecked at Carthage and falls in love with Dido. When Aeneas departs to fulfil his destiny of founding Rome, Dido, heartbroken, curses him and his descendants — mythically explaining the later Punic Wars — and kills herself upon a funeral pyre.

Cultural legacy

Dido's story has inspired numerous works of art, music, and literature, including Purcell's opera Dido and Aeneas (c. 1688), famous for the aria "Dido's Lament".
