University of Ez-Zitouna
Location: Tunis, Tunisia
Type: Public university
Historical origins: c. 737 CE mosque teaching

The University of Ez-Zitouna (Arabic: جامعة الزيتونة, Jāmi'at az-Zaytūnah) is a public university in Tunis, Tunisia. Its roots lie in the teaching circles of the Great Mosque of Zitouna (Ez-Zitouna Mosque), founded in the eighth century, making it among the oldest teaching institutions in the Islamic world and often described as the first Islamic university.

History

Scholars taught Qur'anic studies, law (fiqh), grammar, and other disciplines at the Zitouna Mosque for over a millennium. Noted alumni include the historian and sociologist Ibn Khaldun and the poet Aboul-Qacem Echebbi. In the modern era the institution was reorganized under the Tunisian state: a university was formally established in 1956, later suppressed under President Habib Bourguiba's secular reforms, and re-established in 1987 under Zine El Abidine Ben Ali.

Structure

Today the university comprises institutes focused on Islamic studies, Islamic civilization, and theology, teaching programs in sharia, usul al-din, and related fields.
