Książ Castle


Książ Castle in Wałbrzych, Poland — the largest castle in Silesia.

Książ Castle (Polish: Zamek Książ, pronounced [kɕɔ̃ʂ]; German: Schloss Fürstenstein) is a castle in Wałbrzych in Lower Silesian Voivodeship, Poland. It is the largest castle in the Silesia region, standing on a rocky promontory above the Pełcznica valley within the Książ Landscape Park.

History

The original fortress was built in the late 13th century, traditionally dated 1288–1292, by Duke Bolko I of Świdnica. Over the centuries it was repeatedly rebuilt in Gothic, Renaissance and Baroque styles. From 1509 until 1941 it was the seat of the Hochberg family (later von Pless), who transformed it into a grand aristocratic residence; its most famous resident was Daisy, Princess of Pless.

During the Second World War the castle was seized by the Nazi regime and incorporated into the Project Riese complex: two levels of tunnels (15 and 50 metres deep) were excavated beneath it for purposes that remain partly mysterious, and the interiors were stripped. After the war the castle deteriorated before restoration began in the 1970s.

Today

The castle is now a major tourist attraction, housing a museum, restaurants and event spaces, with terraced gardens and an annual Festival of Flowers. The underground tunnels are open to visitors.
