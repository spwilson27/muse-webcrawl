Bigos

Bigos (Polish pronunciation: [ˈbʲiɡɔs]), often translated into English as hunter's stew, is a Polish dish of chopped meat of various kinds stewed with sauerkraut, shredded fresh cabbage and spices. It is served hot and can be enriched with vegetables, spices, and wine. Originally from Poland, the dish became common in the territories of the former Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and is widely considered a Polish national dish.

Preparation

The typical ingredients include white cabbage, sauerkraut, various cuts of meat and sausages (often whole kielbasa), often whole or plum tomatoes, honey, and mushrooms. The meats may include pork, ham, bacon, beef, veal, and, as it is a "hunter's" stew, venison or other game. It is seasoned with peppercorns, allspice, juniper berries, bay leaves, dried prunes, and red wine.

Bigos is traditionally cooked in a cauldron over an open fire or in a large pot on a stove, simmered for many hours — often a day or two, with reheating said to improve its flavour. It is customarily served at Christmas and Easter festivities, at weddings, and during winter hunts, and is commonly accompanied by rye bread and vodka.

Cultural references

A famous literary description of bigos-making appears in Adam Mickiewicz's epic poem Pan Tadeusz (1834), celebrating the dish prepared in the Lithuanian forests after a hunt.
