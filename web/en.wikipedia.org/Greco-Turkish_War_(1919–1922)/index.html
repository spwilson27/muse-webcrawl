Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922)

Greek forces landing at Smyrna (İzmir) on 15 May 1919, the opening of the war.

The Greco-Turkish War of 1919–1922, known in Greece as the Asia Minor Campaign and in Turkey as the western front of the Turkish War of Independence, was fought between Greece and the Turkish nationalist forces of the Grand National Assembly government led by Mustafa Kemal (Atatürk). Greece sought to realise the Megali Idea by annexing territory in western Anatolia and eastern Thrace promised under the post-war settlement, while the Turkish National Movement fought to overturn the Treaty of Sèvres.

Course of the war

Greek troops landed at Smyrna on 15 May 1919 under Allied authorisation. Greek forces advanced deep into Anatolia in 1920–1921 but were checked at the battles of İnönü and decisively halted at the Battle of the Sakarya in the summer of 1921. On 26 August 1922 the Turkish army launched the Great Offensive, routing the Greek army at Dumlupınar and recapturing İzmir on 9 September 1922. The burning of Smyrna followed, and hundreds of thousands of Greek and Armenian civilians fled or were killed.

Consequences

The war ended with the Armistice of Mudanya and the Treaty of Lausanne (1923), which replaced the Treaty of Sèvres and recognised the Republic of Turkey. Greece and Turkey agreed to a compulsory population exchange affecting about 1.5 million Orthodox Christians and some 500,000 Muslims. In Greece the defeat is remembered as the "Asia Minor Catastrophe"; it led to the fall of the monarchy, the Trial of the Six, and decades of strained Greco-Turkish relations.
