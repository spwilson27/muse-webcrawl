Caesar's Civil War

Date10 January 49 BC – 17 March 45 BC
LocationRoman world: Italy, Hispania, Greece, Egypt, Africa
ResultVictory for Julius Caesar
BelligerentsCaesarians vs. the Senate's Pompeian forces

Caesar's Civil War (49–45 BC) was a Roman civil war between Julius Caesar and the forces of the Roman Senate, led by Pompey the Great. It began when Caesar crossed the Rubicon river on 10 January 49 BC, defying the Senate's order to disband his army, and ended with his victory at the Battle of Munda in 45 BC, leaving him master of the Roman world and dictator for life.

Course of the war

Caesar advanced rapidly through Italy as Pompey and the Senate evacuated to Greece. After securing Spain at Ilerda, Caesar crossed the Adriatic, survived a setback at Dyrrhachium, and decisively defeated Pompey at Pharsalus in Thessaly in August 48 BC. Pompey fled to Egypt, where he was murdered on arrival. Caesar intervened in the Egyptian civil war on behalf of Cleopatra, then defeated remaining senatorial armies at Thapsus in Africa (46 BC) and Munda in Spain (45 BC).

Consequences

The war destroyed the political settlement of the Roman Republic. Caesar's clemency toward defeated enemies won him support but did not prevent his assassination in March 44 BC, which triggered further civil wars ending in Augustus's principate. Caesar's own account, the Commentarii de Bello Civili, is a principal source for the conflict.
