Retinoid

Retinoids are a class of chemical compounds related to vitamin A (retinol) that regulate cell growth and differentiation, particularly in epithelial tissue, by binding to nuclear retinoic acid receptors (RARs) and retinoid X receptors (RXRs). They are used in medicine and cosmetics.

Generations and uses

First-generation retinoids include tretinoin (all-trans retinoic acid) and isotretinoin, widely used for acne; second-generation compounds include etretinate and acitretin, used for psoriasis; third-generation agents such as adapalene and bexarotene were designed for receptor selectivity. Topical retinoids are also used to treat photoaging. Bexarotene and alitretinoin have oncological applications.

Safety

Retinoids are potent teratogens: systemic use in pregnancy can cause severe birth defects, so isotretinoin is dispensed under strict pregnancy-prevention programmes. Common side effects include skin irritation, dryness, and photosensitivity.
