Shiing-Shen Chern (26 October 1911 – 3 December 2004) was a Chinese-American mathematician widely regarded as one of the greatest geometers of the twentieth century and often called the father of modern differential geometry. His work fused geometry with topology and laid foundations later used extensively in theoretical physics.

Life

Chern graduated from Nankai University in 1930 and studied under Wilhelm Blaschke in Hamburg and Élie Cartan in Paris before returning to China to teach at Tsinghua University. During the Second Sino-Japanese War he worked at the Academia Sinica and spent 1943 at Princeton, where his celebrated proof of the generalized Gauss–Bonnet theorem appeared. After the war he founded a mathematics institute in China, then emigrated to the United States, teaching at the University of Chicago and from 1960 at UC Berkeley. He co-founded the Mathematical Sciences Research Institute (MSRI) in Berkeley in 1982 and, returning often to China, founded the Nankai Institute of Mathematics in 1985.

Work

Chern's proof of the Gauss–Bonnet theorem for arbitrary Riemannian manifolds introduced methods of intrinsic differential geometry that transformed the field. His characteristic classes — Chern classes — became basic tools of algebraic geometry and topology, and Chern–Simons forms, developed with James Harris Simons, play a central role in gauge theory, knot theory, and string theory.

Honours

Chern received the US National Medal of Science, the Wolf Prize in Mathematics, and the inaugural Shaw Prize in Mathematical Sciences in 2004. The asteroid 29552 Chern is named for him, as is the Chern Medal awarded by the International Mathematical Union from 2010. See also differential geometry and Chern class.
