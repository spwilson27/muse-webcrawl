Alien is a 1979 science fiction horror film directed by Ridley Scott and written by Dan O'Bannon. It follows the crew of the commercial spacecraft Nostromo, who, after investigating an unknown distress signal, are stalked and killed one by one by a deadly extraterrestrial creature. The film's tagline — "In space no one can hear you scream" — became famous.

Plot

Answering a transmission from a desolate moon, LV-426, the crew finds a derelict alien ship; an organism attaches itself to executive officer Kane and gestates inside him, erupting from his chest at a meal in one of cinema's most notorious scenes. The creature grows into a towering predator. After the android science officer Ash is exposed as an agent of the Company, which considers the crew expendable, warrant officer Ellen Ripley (Sigourney Weaver) becomes the sole survivor, destroying the Nostromo and ejecting the creature from her escape shuttle before entering stasis with the ship's cat, Jones.

Production

The alien and the derelict ship were designed by Swiss artist H. R. Giger, whose biomechanical art gave the film its distinctive look; the production design blended industrial realism with the slogan "truckers in space". Alien won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects. The creature was played in a suit by Bolaji Badejo.

Legacy

Alien launched a major franchise — including James Cameron's action sequel Aliens (1986) — and established Weaver's Ripley as an iconic action heroine. It is credited with revitalising science fiction cinema, blending it with horror, and was selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry in 2002.
