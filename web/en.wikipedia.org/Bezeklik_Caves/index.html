Bezeklik Caves

The Bezeklik Caves (Chinese: 柏孜克里克千佛洞) are a complex of Buddhist cave grottoes dating from the 5th to 14th centuries between the cities of Turpan and Shanshan in the Taklamakan Desert of Xinjiang, China. They are located high on the cliffs of the Mutou Valley beneath the Flaming Mountains, near the ancient Silk Road city of Gaochang.

History

Of the original 83 caves, 57 survive. Forty preserve murals covering some 1,200 square metres, depicting Buddhas, bodhisattvas, and donor figures including Uyghur princes and princesses of the Qocho kingdom. The name "Bezeklik" derives from a Uyghur word meaning "place where there are paintings". The site flourished under the Uyghur kingdom of Qocho, whose rulers initially patronized Manichaeism before converting to Buddhism.

Exploration and removal of murals

In the early 20th century, expeditions led by Albert von Le Coq and Albert Grünwedel removed large sections of the murals to Berlin, where many were destroyed during Second World War bombing raids, while others remain in the Museum of Asian Art. Aurel Stein and Japanese expeditions also removed material. The murals that survive in situ, though damaged by later iconoclasm, remain an outstanding example of Central Asian Buddhist art along the Silk Road.
