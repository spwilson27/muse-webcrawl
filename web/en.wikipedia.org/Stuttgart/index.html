Stuttgart

Stuttgart (Swabian: Schduagert) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Baden-Württemberg. It is located on the Neckar river in a fertile valley known locally as the Stuttgarter Kessel (Stuttgart Cauldron) and lies an hour from the Swabian Jura and the Black Forest. Its area is 207 km² and its population is around 635,000, making it the sixth largest city in Germany; its metropolitan region is home to roughly 2.8 million people.

Stuttgart is known as the cradle of the automobile: both Mercedes-Benz and Porsche have their headquarters and museums in the city. It is also a major centre of engineering, publishing and wine production, and hosts the University of Stuttgart and a large public library, the Stadtbibliothek.

History

The name derives from Stuotgarten, a stud farm founded around 950 AD by Duke Liudolf of Swabia. Stuttgart became a town in the 13th century, the capital of the County (later Duchy and Kingdom) of Württemberg, and grew as a residence city with its Old Castle (Altes Schloss) and New Palace (Neues Schloss). Industrialisation in the 19th century — marked by Gottlieb Daimler's workshop in Cannstatt, where the automobile was pioneered in the 1880s — transformed it into one of Germany's leading manufacturing cities. The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War and became capital of the newly formed state of Baden-Württemberg in 1952.

Economy and culture

High-technology industry, automotive engineering and mechanical engineering dominate the economy. Cultural landmarks include the Staatsgalerie, the State Theatre (Staatstheater), the Mercedes-Benz Museum, the Porsche Museum, and the Wilhelma zoo and botanical garden. The city's football club is VfB Stuttgart. The long-running Stuttgart 21 railway project, rebuilding the main station as an underground through-station, has been a major political controversy since the 2010s.
