Hubble Deep Field

The Hubble Deep Field (HDF) is an image of a small region in the constellation Ursa Major, constructed from a series of observations by the Hubble Space Telescope. It covers an area about 2.6 arcminutes on a side, about one 24-millionth of the whole sky, which is roughly equivalent to the area of a tennis ball viewed from 100 metres away. The image was assembled from 342 separate exposures taken with the Space Telescope's Wide Field and Planetary Camera 2 over ten consecutive days between 18 and 28 December 1995.

Contents of the image

The field appears almost entirely filled with galaxies of every shape, colour and distance — about 3,000 in all — with only a handful of foreground stars in our own galaxy. Many of the faintest galaxies are seen as they were more than 10 billion years ago, providing a "core sample" of the history of galaxy formation and evolution.

Scientific impact

The HDF demonstrated that the early universe contained large numbers of small, irregular galaxies that later assembled into the grand spirals and ellipticals of today. The data were released publicly in January 1996 and generated hundreds of papers. Later, deeper surveys — the Hubble Deep Field South (1998), the Hubble Ultra-Deep Field (2004) and the eXtreme Deep Field (2012) — extended the approach.
