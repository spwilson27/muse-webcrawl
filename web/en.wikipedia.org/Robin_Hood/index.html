Robin Hood

Robin Hood is a legendary heroic outlaw from English folklore, traditionally depicted as a highly skilled archer and swordsman who robbed from the rich to give to the poor. With his band of Merry Men in Sherwood Forest, Nottinghamshire, he is usually shown opposing the Sheriff of Nottingham.

The legend

The earliest references to Robin Hood appear in 14th-century ballads, where he is a yeoman rather than the dispossessed nobleman of later versions. Core companions include Little John, Friar Tuck, and Maid Marian (a later addition). Stories such as the archery contest in disguise, the rescue of condemned men, and Robin's death at Kirklees Priory became standard. Whether any historical figure lies behind the legend remains debated.

In culture

Robin Hood has been retold in countless books, plays, and films, from Howard Pyle's The Merry Adventures of Robin Hood (1883) to the 1938 film with Errol Flynn and Disney's 1973 animated version. The phrase "Robin Hood" has entered economics and policy language for redistribution.
