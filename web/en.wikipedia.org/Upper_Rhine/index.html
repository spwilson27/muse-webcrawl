Upper Rhine

The Upper Rhine is the section of the Rhine river between Lake Constance and Basel downstream to Bingen, flowing through or bordering Switzerland, France and Germany. It forms much of the Franco-German border in the Upper Rhine Plain.

Geography

The river flows north through a rift valley between the Vosges and the Black Forest, passing Strasbourg, Karlsruhe and Mannheim where the Neckar joins.

History

In the 19th century Johann Gottfried Tulla straightened and channelled the river, reducing flooding but changing ecosystems. The Grand Canal d'Alsace later diverted flow for hydroelectric power.

Economy

The Upper Rhine is a major transport corridor, industrial region and cross-border cooperation area centred on Strasbourg.
