DeLorean DMC-12

The DeLorean DMC-12 is a sports car manufactured by John DeLorean's DeLorean Motor Company in Northern Ireland from 1981 to 1983. It is famous for its brushed stainless steel body panels, gull-wing doors and fibreglass underbody, and became an enduring cultural icon as the time machine in the Back to the Future film trilogy.

Development and design

The car was designed by Giorgetto Giugiaro of Italdesign, with engineering input from Colin Chapman's Lotus. It was powered by a rear-mounted 2.85-litre PRV V6 engine, developed jointly by Peugeot, Renault and Volvo, producing about 130 horsepower in US specification — performance widely considered disappointing for the car's looks and price. The "12" in the name referred to the originally intended $12,000 price, though the car actually sold for roughly double that.

Production and collapse

Production began in January 1981 at a purpose-built factory in Dunmurry, near Belfast, funded largely by the British government to create jobs in Northern Ireland. Approximately 9,000 cars were built before the company collapsed into receivership in 1982 amid poor sales, cost overruns and a recession; John DeLorean was arrested that year in an FBI sting on drug-trafficking charges of which he was later acquitted, but the company could not be saved.

Legacy

The DMC-12's starring role in Back to the Future (1985) transformed a commercial failure into one of the most recognisable cars ever made. A large fraction of the original production run survives, supported by an active owners' community and a Texas-based company that acquired the remaining parts inventory.
