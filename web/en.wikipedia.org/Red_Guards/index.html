Red Guards

The Red Guards were a mass student paramilitary movement mobilised by Mao Zedong in 1966 and 1967 during the Cultural Revolution in China. Composed largely of school and university students, they were encouraged to attack the "Four Olds" — old ideas, culture, customs and habits — and to denounce perceived counter-revolutionaries.

History

The movement began at Tsinghua University Middle School in May 1966 and spread nationwide after Mao endorsed it and received Red Guard delegates at mass rallies in Tiananmen Square. Red Guard factions soon turned on teachers, officials and each other, contributing to widespread violence and disorder. In 1968 Mao sent the People's Liberation Army to restore order and launched the "Down to the Countryside" movement, dispersing millions of Red Guards to rural areas.

Legacy

The Red Guards remain a symbol of the Cultural Revolution's turmoil, in which cultural heritage was destroyed and many people were persecuted.
