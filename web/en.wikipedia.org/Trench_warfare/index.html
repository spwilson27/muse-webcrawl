Trench warfare

[Image: Soldiers in a front-line trench on the Western Front, 1916]

Trench warfare is a type of land warfare using occupied lines of trenches, consisting largely of military trenches, in which troops are well-protected from the enemy's small arms fire and are substantially sheltered from artillery. It became archetypically associated with World War I, when the "Race to the Sea" rapidly expanded trench use on the Western Front, forming a continuous fortified system from the North Sea to Switzerland. The stalemate produced by these defensive systems lasted from September 1914 until the Allied spring offensives of 1918.

Trench systems

A typical system comprised three parallel lines — firing, support, and reserve trenches — connected by zigzag communication trenches, with dugouts, barbed-wire entanglements in front, and a contested "no man's land" between opposing lines. Fire trenches were dug in zigzag or stepped patterns so that an enemy entering one segment could not fire along its whole length. Soldiers rotated between the front line, support, reserve, and rest, enduring shelling, snipers, gas, trench foot, mud, rats, and disease.

Development and precedents

Field fortifications long predated 1914: trenches featured in the Crimean War, the American Civil War (notably the Siege of Petersburg, 1864–65), and the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–05. What changed was the firepower of quick-firing artillery, machine guns, and accurate rifles, which gave defenders an overwhelming advantage and forced armies underground once flanks could no longer be turned.

Breaking the deadlock

Commanders sought solutions through massed artillery, poison gas, infiltration ("stormtrooper") tactics, creeping barrages, and finally tanks and combined-arms operations; the German spring offensive and the Allied Hundred Days Offensive of 1918 restored mobility. Trench systems also appeared after 1918, notably in the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), the Eritrean–Ethiopian War, and along the front lines of the war in Ukraine, where trenches and drone warfare have combined in modernised form.
