Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties

Signed23 May 1969, Vienna, Austria
Effective27 January 1980
Parties118 states

The Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (VCLT) is an international agreement that regulates treaties between states. Known as the "treaty on treaties", it establishes comprehensive rules, procedures and guidelines for how treaties are defined, drafted, amended, interpreted and generally operated. It was adopted in 1969, opened for signature the same year, and entered into force in 1980.

Content

The Convention includes the principle pacta sunt servanda (Article 26: agreements must be kept), rules of interpretation (Articles 31–32), the prohibition on invoking internal law to justify failure to perform a treaty, grounds for invalidity, termination and suspension, and the concept of jus cogens — peremptory norms from which no derogation is permitted. Many of its provisions are regarded as codifying customary international law and are applied even by states not party to the Convention.
