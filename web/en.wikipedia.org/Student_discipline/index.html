Student discipline

Student discipline refers to the rules and strategies applied in schools to manage student behaviour and encourage adherence to the school's code of conduct. Disciplinary measures range widely, from verbal warnings and detention to suspension, expulsion, and — historically in many countries, and still in some — corporal punishment. Approaches to discipline are a recurring subject of debate in education policy, concerning their effects on learning, equity and students' rights.

Forms

Common measures include warnings, loss of privileges, detention (keeping a student after school hours), in-school and out-of-school suspension, and expulsion. Schools typically publish a code of conduct and apply consequences in a graded fashion. In the United States, the Supreme Court case Goss v. Lopez (1975) established that students facing suspension have a right to notice and a hearing under the Due Process Clause. "Zero tolerance" policies, which mandate predetermined punishments for specified offences, spread in the 1990s and have since been criticised for inflexibility and for disproportionate effects on minority and disabled students (the "school-to-prison pipeline" debate).

Alternative approaches

Alternatives include restorative justice practices, in which offenders, victims and the school community work together to repair harm; positive behavioural interventions and supports (PBIS), which emphasise teaching and rewarding expected behaviour; and school-wide social and emotional learning programmes. Research on exclusionary discipline has prompted many districts to limit suspensions, especially for young children and minor infractions.
