State park

State parks protect natural areas and provide public recreation.

A state park is a protected area managed by a U.S. state government, established to preserve natural, scenic, or historic features and to provide outdoor recreation such as hiking, camping, fishing, and boating. State parks are generally smaller and more numerous than national parks, and collectively cover millions of acres across all fifty states.

History

One of the earliest state parks was established at Niagara Falls, New York, in 1885, to protect the falls from commercial exploitation. Yosemite was first protected as a California state grant in 1864 before becoming a national park. The state park movement grew rapidly in the early 20th century, with extensive development by the Civilian Conservation Corps during the Great Depression, which built trails, lodges, and campgrounds still in use today.

Management and use

Each state operates its own park system with its own agency and funding; California, New York, Ohio, and Washington have among the largest systems. American state parks receive hundreds of millions of visits annually — more than the national parks — and often charge modest entrance or camping fees. Related designations include state recreation areas, state forests, and state historic sites.
