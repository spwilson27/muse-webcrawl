Ben Nevis (Scottish Gaelic: Beinn Nibheis) is the highest mountain in Scotland and the United Kingdom, standing 1,345 metres (4,413 ft) above sea level at the western end of the Grampian Mountains, near the town of Fort William in Lochaber. Its name is often interpreted as "mountain of heaven" or "venomous mountain".

Geology and summit

Ben Nevis is the collapsed dome of an ancient volcano, formed around 400 million years ago. The summit plateau is strewn with granite rubble and ringed by cliffs; the mountain's north face, with cliffs up to 600 m high, is one of the great rock and ice climbing venues in Britain. Ruins on the summit include a meteorological observatory that operated from 1883 to 1904, where the "smoking" daily observations were recorded before the station closed.

Climbing

An estimated 125,000 people climb Ben Nevis each year. Most ascend by the Mountain Track (the "Tourist Path") from Glen Nevis, a long but straightforward walk of about 17 km return. Conditions can nonetheless be severe: the summit is in cloud for much of the year, and navigation errors near the summit cliffs have caused many accidents. The mountain is also the highest of the three peaks in the National Three Peaks Challenge.

Environment

The mountain and surrounding estate are cared for by the John Muir Trust and partners. Ben Nevis is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest for its geology and its Arctic-alpine flora and fauna, which include snow buntings and rare mosses found almost nowhere else in Britain.
