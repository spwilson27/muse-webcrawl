Ibex

An ibex (plural: ibexes, ibices or ibex) is any of several species of wild goat of the genus Capra that live in the mountainous regions of Europe, north-central Asia and northern Africa. Ibex are distinguished by the large, ridged, backward-curving horns borne especially prominently by the males.

Species

Recognised ibex species include the Alpine ibex (Capra ibex), the Nubian ibex (C. nubiana), the Siberian ibex (C. sibirica), the Iberian ibex or Spanish ibex (C. pyrenaica), and the walia ibex (C. walie) of Ethiopia; some authorities also treat wild goat populations such as the bezoar ibex within the group.

Behaviour and habitat

Ibex are superb climbers, inhabiting steep, rugged terrain at elevations up to about 4,600 metres (15,000 ft), where they are relatively safe from predators. They are herbivores, grazing on grasses, herbs, shrubs and mosses. Males and females live apart for much of the year, coming together during the winter rut, when males clash horns for dominance.

Conservation

The Alpine ibex was hunted almost to extinction in the 19th century, surviving only in Italy's Gran Paradiso area; protection and reintroduction have since restored populations across the Alps. Other species, such as the walia ibex, remain rare, and one subspecies, the Pyrenean ibex, became extinct in 2000 — famously becoming the first taxon ever brought back (briefly) by cloning in 2009, when a cloned kid survived for several minutes.
