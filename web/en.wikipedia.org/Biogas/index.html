Biogas

UsesHeating, cooking, electricity generation, vehicle fuel (upgraded)

Biogas is a mixture of gases, primarily methane and carbon dioxide, produced by the anaerobic digestion of organic material by microorganisms. Raw materials include animal manure, sewage, municipal solid waste, food waste and energy crops. Biogas is a renewable energy source and also forms naturally in swamps, landfills and the digestive tracts of ruminants, where it is known as landfill gas or marsh gas in those contexts.

Production

In a biogas plant, organic material is fed into an airtight digester where successive groups of bacteria and archaea break it down through hydrolysis, acidogenesis, acetogenesis and methanogenesis. The residual material, digestate, is rich in nutrients and can be used as fertiliser. Biogas production is promoted worldwide: millions of small household digesters provide cooking gas in China and India, while larger farm and industrial plants are common in Europe, particularly Germany.

Uses

Biogas can be burned for heat and cooking, used in gas engines or turbines to generate electricity, or combined heat and power. When upgraded by removing carbon dioxide and contaminants it becomes biomethane, which can be injected into natural-gas grids or used as vehicle fuel. Capturing methane that would otherwise escape from manure and landfills also reduces greenhouse-gas emissions.

History

The flammability of gas from decomposing matter was noted in the 17th century, and Alessandro Volta studied marsh gas in the 1770s. The first digestion plant was built at a leper colony in Bombay in 1859, and biogas was used for street lighting in Exeter, England, in the 1890s.
