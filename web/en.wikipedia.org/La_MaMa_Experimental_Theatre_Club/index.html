La MaMa Experimental Theatre Club

La MaMa, a cornerstone of Off-Off-Broadway since 1961.

The La MaMa Experimental Theatre Club is an Off-Off-Broadway theatre founded in 1961 in New York City's East Village by Ellen Stewart. Dedicated to the support of experimental work and artists of all nations and cultures, La MaMa is one of the founding institutions of the Off-Off-Broadway movement and has presented thousands of productions, from early plays by Sam Shepard, Lanford Wilson, and Harvey Fierstein to international companies from every continent.

History

Stewart, a fashion designer with no theatrical training, began presenting plays in a tiny basement café space on East 9th Street, naming it after herself — playwrights called her "Mama". Harassed by fire and building inspectors, the company moved repeatedly before settling in 1969 at 74A East 4th Street, purchased with philanthropic support, and later adding the Annex (now the Ellen Stewart Theatre) and other spaces. La MaMa championed the Cafe Cino generation of playwrights, hosted playwright Tom Eyen, director Tom O'Horgan (who staged Hair workshops), and Andrei Serban's celebrated productions, and built a unique global network: Stewart founded the Great Jones Repertory Company and welcomed troupes from Poland, Korea, Japan, Nigeria, and elsewhere, making La MaMa a crossroads of world theatre.

Legacy

Under Stewart until her death in 2011, and thereafter under artistic director Mia Yoo, La MaMa has received numerous Obie Awards, a regional Tony Award (1978 shared with other Off-Off-Broadway founders), and a MacArthur "Genius" grant for Stewart. Its archives document over half a century of avant-garde performance.
