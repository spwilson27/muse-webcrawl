Existed552 – 603 CE
CapitalÖtüken (Orkhon Valley, Mongolia)

ExtentManchuria to the Black Sea steppe
Succeeded byEastern and Western Turkic Khaganates

The First Turkic Khaganate, also known as the Göktürk Khaganate, was a vast nomadic empire established by the Ashina clan of the Göktürks under Bumin Qaghan in 552 CE, after the overthrow of the Rouran Khaganate. At its height it was the first transcontinental steppe empire, stretching from Manchuria across Mongolia and Central Asia to the Pontic steppe, and it was the first state to use the name "Turk" as a political identity.

Expansion

Bumin's brother Istämi expanded westward, destroying the Hephthalites in alliance with Sasanian Persia (c. 557–561) and opening direct diplomatic contact with the Byzantine Empire — the embassy of Zemarchus in 568 sought a Silk Road alliance against Persia and the Avars. The khaganate controlled key stretches of the Silk Road and grew wealthy taxing and protecting its trade.

Division

Succession disputes after the death of Taspar Qaghan (581) split the empire into rival eastern and western khaganates, a division formalised by 603. The Eastern Khaganate eventually fell to Tang China in 630, while a Second Turkic Khaganate revived Turkic power in 682.

Legacy

The khaganate spread Turkic languages and culture across the Eurasian steppe. Its successor states left the earliest substantial texts in a Turkic language, the 8th-century Orkhon inscriptions, written in runic Old Turkic script.
