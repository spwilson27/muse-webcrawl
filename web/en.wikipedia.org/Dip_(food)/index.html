Dip (food)

A dip or dipping sauce is a common condiment for many types of food. Dips are used to add flavour or texture to a food, such as pita bread, dumplings, crackers, chopped raw vegetables, fruits, seafood, cubed pieces of meat and cheese, potato chips, tortilla chips, and falafel.

Characteristics

Unlike other sauces, instead of applying the sauce to the food, the food is typically placed or dipped into the sauce. Dips are commonly eaten with finger foods and snacks during parties and picnics.

Examples

Well-known dips include guacamole, hummus, salsa, tzatziki, sour cream and onion dip, spinach and artichoke dip, queso, aioli, and taramasalata. Thick dips based on cheese, beans, or yoghurt are also served as sandwich spreads or accompaniments to grilled meats.

Food safety

Because dips are often shared from a common bowl, etiquette and hygiene advice discourages "double-dipping" — dipping a piece of food again after taking a bite.
