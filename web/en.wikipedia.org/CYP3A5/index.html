CYP3A5

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cytochrome P450 3A5 is a protein in humans that is encoded by the CYP3A5 gene, located in a cluster of cytochrome P450 genes on chromosome 7q21.1. Like other CYP3A enzymes, CYP3A5 is a monooxygenase involved in the metabolism of drugs and the synthesis of cholesterol, steroids and other lipids.

Expression and variability

Unlike its close relative CYP3A4, which is expressed in nearly all adults, CYP3A5 is expressed polymorphically: only a minority of people produce substantial amounts of the protein. The common CYP3A5*3 allele creates a cryptic splice site that results in a truncated, non-functional protein, so most individuals of European ancestry are "non-expressers", whereas functional alleles are much more frequent in populations of African ancestry — a pattern thought to reflect adaptation related to salt and water retention in hot climates.

Clinical significance

Because CYP3A5 contributes to the clearance of many of the same drugs as CYP3A4, a person's CYP3A5 genotype can influence drug dosing. The best-established example is the immunosuppressant tacrolimus: CYP3A5 expressers metabolise it faster and require higher doses to reach therapeutic blood levels after organ transplantation, and pharmacogenetic guidelines recommend genotype-guided starting doses.

Research

CYP3A5 is also studied in relation to hypertension, kidney disease and the metabolism of endogenous steroids, and its expression in extrahepatic tissues such as lung, kidney and prostate has attracted interest in cancer research.
