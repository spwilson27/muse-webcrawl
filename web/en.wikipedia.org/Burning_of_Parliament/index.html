Burning of Parliament

The Burning of Parliament was a major fire that destroyed the old Palace of Westminster in London on 16 October 1834. The blaze began when wooden tally sticks — obsolete accounting records being burned in furnaces beneath the House of Lords — overheated and set fire to the building's panelling and flues.

The fire

The fire spread rapidly through the warren of timber-framed buildings. Westminster Hall was saved by the efforts of firefighters and a change in wind direction, but the House of Commons, House of Lords, and most of the palace complex were gutted. Huge crowds watched from across the Thames, and the scene was famously painted by J. M. W. Turner and John Constable.

Rebuilding

A competition for a new palace in the Gothic or Elizabethan style was won by architect Charles Barry, working with Augustus Pugin. The resulting building — the present Houses of Parliament, including the Elizabeth Tower housing Big Ben — was constructed over the following decades and stands as a landmark of Gothic Revival architecture.
