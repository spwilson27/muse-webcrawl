Zoom lens

A zoom lens is a type of camera lens system that offers a range of focal lengths within a single lens, in contrast to a prime lens, which has a fixed focal length. The focal length can be varied continuously, allowing the photographer to change the angle of view — and thus the apparent size of the subject — without moving position or changing lenses.

Design

Zoom lenses contain multiple lens elements in groups that move relative to one another. In a true zoom, focus is maintained throughout the range; in a varifocal lens, focus shifts as focal length changes. Common photographic ranges include 24–70 mm and 70–200 mm. The ratio of longest to shortest focal length describes the zoom range.

Advantages and trade-offs

Zooms offer convenience and framing flexibility, which made them dominant in consumer photography, cinema and television. Historically they traded off maximum aperture, sharpness and size against prime lenses, though modern designs have narrowed the gap. Superzoom lenses cover very wide ranges at greater optical compromise.

History

Early variable-focal-length lenses appeared in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; practical photographic zooms, such as the Voigtländer Zoomar of 1959, spread after the Second World War, aided by computer-assisted optical design.
