Education in Quebec

Education in Quebec is governed by the Ministry of Education (Ministère de l'Éducation) of the Government of Quebec. The system is distinctive within Canada for its structure, which includes elementary school, secondary school, and the CEGEP system — a uniquely Quebecois intermediate level between secondary school and university.

Structure

Primary education comprises six years of elementary school, followed by five years of secondary school (Secondary I to V). After secondary school, students wishing to attend university must first complete a two-year pre-university program at a CEGEP (Collège d'enseignement général et professionnel), or a three-year technical program leading directly to a career. University bachelor's degrees in Quebec typically take three years for Quebec CEGEP graduates, because the first year of general studies is completed at CEGEP.

History

Education in Quebec was historically organized along confessional (Catholic and Protestant) lines. The Quiet Revolution of the 1960s led to the creation of a Ministry of Education in 1964 and sweeping reforms under the Parent Commission, which modernized and democratized the system. A 1997 constitutional amendment allowed school boards to be reorganized along linguistic (French and English) rather than religious lines.

Language of instruction

Under the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101, 1977), most students must attend French-language schools, with English-language public schooling generally reserved for children whose parents were educated in English in Canada. Post-secondary education is offered in both languages; major institutions include Université Laval, Université de Montréal, McGill University and Concordia University.
