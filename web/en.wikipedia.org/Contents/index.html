Wikipedia: Contents

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Wikipedia: Contents is the central navigation portal of the English Wikipedia. Because Wikipedia contains millions of articles, readers can use this page — together with the lists, outlines, indices, and glossaries it links to — to browse the encyclopedia by subject rather than by searching for a specific title. The contents system is organised around a set of top-level topic areas, each of which leads to a more detailed outline of knowledge and to curated lists of articles.

Top-level topics

Culture and the arts

Architecture, art, books, dance, film, music, theatre, television, and popular culture.

Geography and places

Continents, countries, regions, cities, oceans, mountains, and other places of interest.

Health and fitness

Medicine, anatomy, diseases, nutrition, exercise, and public health.

History and events

Ancient, classical, medieval, and modern history, wars, and current events.

Mathematics and logic

Algebra, geometry, calculus, probability, statistics, and mathematical logic.

Natural and physical sciences

Astronomy, biology, chemistry, Earth sciences, physics, and ecology.

People and self

Biographies, genealogy, psychology, and the self.

Philosophy and thinking

Ethics, aesthetics, epistemology, metaphysics, and schools of thought.

Religion and belief systems

World religions, mythology, spirituality, and religious texts.

Society and social sciences

Economics, education, law, linguistics, politics, and sociology.

Sports and recreation

Team and individual sports, games, hobbies, and outdoor recreation.

Technology and applied sciences

Computing, electronics, engineering, transport, and communications.

Navigation aids

The contents system includes several complementary browsing tools:

  • Lists — alphabetical and topical lists of articles, from lists of countries to lists of films and authors.
  • Outlines — structured outlines that present a subject's topics in a hierarchical tree.
  • Indices — alphabetical indexes to sets of articles, useful when the exact title is unknown.
  • Glossaries — lists of terms with concise definitions, organised by field.
  • Categories — Wikipedia's category tree, rooted at the main topic classifications.
  • Portals — curated entry pages devoted to broad subjects, maintained by editors.

Reference

The reference section links to encyclopedic tools such as timelines, chronologies, books (collections of articles), academic disciplines, and the Dewey Decimal and Library of Congress classification schemes as applied to Wikipedia content. Featured content — articles, pictures, and lists recognised for their quality — can be reached from the contents page, as can the daily featured article and picture of the day shown on the Main Page.
