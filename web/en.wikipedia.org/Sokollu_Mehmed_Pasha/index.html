Sokollu Mehmed Pasha

Grand Vizier: 1565–1579

Sultans served: Suleiman the Magnificent, Selim II, Murad III

Born: c. 1505, Sokolovići, Bosnia

Died: 11 October 1579, Constantinople (assassinated)

Sokollu Mehmed Pasha was an Ottoman statesman of Serbian origin who served as Grand Vizier for nearly 15 years under three sultans, effectively running the empire during the reign of Selim II.

Early life

Born into an Orthodox Christian family in Bosnia, he was taken through the devşirme levy, converted to Islam and educated at the palace school in Constantinople, rising through naval and provincial commands.

Grand vizierate

Appointed in 1565, Sokollu directed conquests including Cyprus (1570–71), rebuilt the fleet after Lepanto (1571), managed relations with Persia, the Habsburgs and France, and supported the restoration of the Serbian Patriarchate of Peć in 1557, headed by his relative Makarije.

Legacy

He endowed mosques, bridges and caravanserais across the empire — most famously the Mehmed Paša Sokolović Bridge in Višegrad, now a UNESCO site. Despite centuries separating them, his rise from the Balkan lands once ruled by Stefan Nemanja illustrates Ottoman recruitment. He was assassinated by a dervish in 1579.
