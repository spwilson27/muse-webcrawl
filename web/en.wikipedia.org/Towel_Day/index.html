Towel Day is celebrated every year on 25 May as a tribute to the author Douglas Adams by his fans. On this day, fans openly carry a towel with them, as described in Adams's The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy, to demonstrate their appreciation for the books and the author.

Origin

Adams died on 11 May 2001. Two weeks later, on 25 May 2001, the first Towel Day was held after a proposal posted on a web forum invited fans to carry towels in his honour. The date has been observed annually ever since, growing into an international geek-culture celebration marked by readings, exhibitions, and charity events.

The towel in the Guide

In The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy, the Guide declares that a towel is "just about the most massively useful thing an interstellar hitchhiker can have": it can be used for warmth, shelter, signalling, hand-to-hand combat, and protection, and above all it marks its owner as someone who "has it together" — a "hoopy frood who really knows where his towel is."

Related observances

25 May is also Geek Pride Day, an initiative promoting geek culture that coincides with the anniversary of the 1977 release of Star Wars and Terry Pratchett's Glorious 25 May from the Discworld novels, giving the date a triple significance in fandom.
