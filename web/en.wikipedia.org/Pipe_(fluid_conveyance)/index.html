Pipe (fluid conveyance)

A pipe is a tubular section or hollow cylinder, usually but not necessarily of circular cross-section, used mainly to convey substances that can flow — liquids and gases — or slurries and fine solid particles. Pipes are fundamental components of plumbing, heating, water supply, sewage, and industrial process systems, and of long-distance pipelines for oil and gas.

Materials

Pipes are made from many materials depending on the fluid, pressure, temperature and environment. Metals include carbon steel, stainless steel, cast iron, copper and brass; non-metals include PVC, CPVC, polyethylene and other plastics, concrete, vitrified clay, glass and fiberglass-reinforced composites. Historically, pipes were made of lead (the origin of the word "plumbing"), wood and clay.

Sizes and standards

Pipe sizes are specified by nominal diameter and wall thickness. In North America the Nominal Pipe Size (NPS) system pairs a nominal diameter with a schedule number denoting wall thickness; metric systems use DN (diamètre nominal). Because the outside diameter is fixed for a given nominal size, increasing wall thickness reduces the inside diameter. Pressure ratings, joint types (threaded, welded, soldered, flanged or glued) and manufacturing methods (seamless versus welded) are governed by standards from bodies such as ASTM, ASME and ISO.

Uses

Beyond conveyance, pipes serve structural and mechanical roles: as scaffolding, handrails, machine components and even wind instruments. In buildings, networks of pipes deliver potable water and gas and remove waste; in industry they interconnect vessels, pumps, heat exchangers and reactors.
