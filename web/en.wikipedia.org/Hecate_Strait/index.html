Hecate Strait is a wide body of water on the Pacific coast of British Columbia, Canada, separating the archipelago of Haida Gwaii from the mainland. It extends from Dixon Entrance in the north to Queen Charlotte Sound in the south.

Geography and character

The strait is relatively shallow compared with the deep fjords of the mainland coast, a legacy of the continental shelf that was exposed as dry land during the last glacial period. It is notorious among mariners for violent storms and rough seas: shallow water and strong winds can combine to produce steep, dangerous waves, and it has been called one of the most dangerous stretches of water on the BC coast.

History and ecology

The strait was named by Captain George Henry Richards around 1861–62 after his survey ship HMS Hecate. Its waters support major fisheries for salmon, herring, halibut and crab, and its shores lie within the traditional territories of the Haida, Tsimshian and Heiltsuk peoples. Ferries of BC Ferries cross between Prince Rupert and Skidegate at its northern end. Proposals for oil tanker routes through the strait and Dixon Entrance have been a longstanding focus of environmental and Indigenous opposition.
