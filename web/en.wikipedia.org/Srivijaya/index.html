Srivijaya

Srivijaya
CapitalPalembang (believed)
Periodc. 650–1377 CE
ReligionMahayana Buddhism, Hinduism
LanguageOld Malay, Sanskrit

Srivijaya was a Buddhist thalassocratic empire based on the island of Sumatra, Indonesia, which influenced much of Maritime Southeast Asia from the 7th to the 13th century. Centred near modern Palembang, it controlled the Strait of Malacca and Sunda Strait trade between China and India.

History

The earliest evidence is the 682 Kedukan Bukit inscription and the account of Chinese monk Yijing, who studied Sanskrit and Buddhism there en route to India. Srivijaya's power rested on Malay sea peoples, port tolls, and diplomacy with Tang and Song China. Its influence reached Java, the Malay Peninsula, and Borneo coasts.

Decline

A devastating Chola raid in 1025 under Rajendra I, rising Javanese power, shifting trade to other ports, and silting of Palembang's rivers eroded the empire. By the late 13th century it had fragmented, succeeded in the straits region by later Malay kingdoms and ultimately the Malacca Sultanate. Srivijaya remained a major centre of Mahayana learning, associated with teachers such as Atisha's guru Dharmakirti.

Legacy

Srivijaya spread Old Malay as a regional lingua franca along the maritime Silk Road routes and left inscriptions in the earliest written Malay.
