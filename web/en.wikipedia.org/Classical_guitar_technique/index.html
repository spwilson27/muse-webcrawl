Classical guitar technique

Classical guitar technique is the set of physical methods used to play the classical guitar. The player sits, often with the left foot on a footstool or the guitar supported by a rest, holding the instrument at an angle so that both hands can move freely. The left hand stops the strings on the fingerboard while the right hand plucks with the fingernails and fingertips.

Right hand

The fingers are designated p (thumb), i (index), m (middle) and a (ring), from the Spanish pulgar, índice, medio, anular. Two basic strokes are used: tirando (free stroke), in which the finger plucks and clears the adjacent string, and apoyando (rest stroke), in which the finger comes to rest on the next string, producing a fuller tone often used for melodic lines. Techniques include arpeggios, tremolo (rapid repetition imitating sustain, as in Tárrega's Recuerdos de la Alhambra), rasgueado (strumming borrowed from flamenco), harmonics, and golpe (percussive tapping).

Left hand

The left hand presses strings just behind the frets with the fingertips, keeping the thumb behind the neck. Shifting, barré (barring several strings with the index finger), slurs (hammer-ons and pull-offs), vibrato along the string, and careful finger substitution for legato are core elements. Nail shape and maintenance are considered essential to tone production.

Pedagogy

Standard methods include those of Carcassi, Sor and Giuliani, and modern schools emphasise relaxed, efficient motion to avoid injury while achieving dynamic and tonal control.
