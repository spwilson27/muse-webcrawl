Hwange National Park

Summary

Location: Matabeleland North, Zimbabwe

Area: About 14,651 km² (5,656 sq mi)

Established: 1928 (as Wankie Game Reserve)

Notable wildlife: Elephants, lions, African wild dogs, sable antelope

Hwange National Park is the largest natural reserve in Zimbabwe, covering roughly 14,651 square kilometres in the northwest of the country, near the Kalahari Desert and the border with Botswana. Founded in 1928 as the Wankie Game Reserve by game ranger Ted Davison, it is named after a local Nhanzwa chief.

Landscape and wildlife

The park's landscape ranges from Kalahari sandveld and teak forest in the south to grasslands and mopane woodland in the north. It supports one of Africa's largest elephant populations and significant numbers of lions, leopards, cheetahs, African wild dogs, buffalo, giraffes, sable and roan antelope, and more than 400 bird species. Many natural pans dry up in the long dry season, and water is supplemented by pumped waterholes, around which game concentrates spectacularly between May and October.

Notable events

Hwange gained worldwide attention in 2015 when Cecil, a well-known collared lion studied by Oxford University researchers, was killed by an American trophy hunter just outside the park, sparking international debate about trophy hunting. In 2013, poachers poisoned waterholes with cyanide, killing more than 80 elephants. The park forms part of the Kavango–Zambezi Transfrontier Conservation Area, the world's largest transfrontier conservation region.
