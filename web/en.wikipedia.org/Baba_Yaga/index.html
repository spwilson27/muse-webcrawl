Baba Yaga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Baba Yaga is a supernatural being from Slavic folklore who appears as a ferocious, deformed old woman — a witch-like figure who may help or hinder those who encounter her. She is famous for flying in a mortar, wielding a pestle, and dwelling deep in the forest in a hut standing on chicken legs, surrounded by a fence of human bones topped with skulls.

Characteristics

Baba Yaga is ambiguous rather than purely evil: in many tales she tests heroes and heroines and rewards the courteous and clever with magical aid, while threatening to eat the rude or foolish. She is often associated with three riders — white, red and black, representing day, sunrise/sun, and night — and commands hosts of spirits. Heroines such as Vasilisa the Beautiful survive her tasks with the help of a magical doll and polite manners.

Interpretations

Scholars have debated whether Baba Yaga descends from a pre-Christian goddess of death or nature, a shamanic initiator, or a literary composite. Her name likely combines baba ("old woman, grandmother") with an obscure second element variously linked to words for horror, illness or snakes. Vladimir Propp's analysis of folktale structure treated her as the classic "donor" figure who furnishes the hero with a magical agent.

In popular culture

Baba Yaga appears in Mussorgsky's Pictures at an Exhibition ("The Hut on Fowl's Legs"), in ballets, operas, films, comics, video games, and the John Wick films, where the nickname is given to the protagonist. She remains the best-known witch figure of Slavic tradition.
