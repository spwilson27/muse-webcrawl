Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder

Alcohol exposure during pregnancy can cause lifelong effects

Fetal alcohol spectrum disorders (FASDs) are a group of conditions that can occur in a person whose mother drank alcohol during pregnancy. Symptoms can include an abnormal appearance, short height, low body weight, small head size, poor coordination, behavioural problems, learning difficulties, and problems with hearing and sight.

Diagnosis and forms

Fetal alcohol syndrome (FAS) is the most severe form, marked by characteristic facial features — a smooth philtrum, thin upper lip and small eye openings — growth restriction, and damage to the central nervous system. Other forms include partial fetal alcohol syndrome and alcohol-related neurodevelopmental disorder. Diagnosis is based on observed symptoms and confirmed prenatal alcohol exposure, where known.

Prevention and prevalence

FASDs are entirely preventable if a woman abstains from alcohol during pregnancy, and health authorities recommend avoiding alcohol when pregnant or planning a pregnancy, as no safe level of exposure has been established. Studies in the United States have estimated that FASDs may affect between roughly 1% and 5% of school-aged children, though many cases go undiagnosed.
