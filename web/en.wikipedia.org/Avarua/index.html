Avarua

Avarua, on the north coast of Rarotonga, is the capital of the Cook Islands.

Avarua (meaning "Two Harbours" in Cook Islands Māori) is a town and district on the north coast of Rarotonga, the largest island of the Cook Islands, a self-governing island country in free association with New Zealand. Avarua is the country's capital and its main commercial and administrative centre, with a population of roughly 5,000 in the district.

Geography and layout

The town stretches along the main road that rings Rarotonga between the island's two harbours, Avarua Harbour and the smaller Avatiu Harbour to the west, which serves as the country's principal port. Behind the town rises the island's forested volcanic interior, dominated by Te Manga. An older inland road, the Ara Tapu, and the back road Ara Metua — traditionally attributed to the chief Toi around the 11th century — pass through the district.

History

Missionaries of the London Missionary Society arrived in the 1820s, and Avarua became a centre of their activity in the Cook Islands; the Cook Islands Christian Church in the town, built from coral stone, dates to the nineteenth century. Avarua developed as the seat of government under the British protectorate (from 1888) and New Zealand administration (from 1901). The town was damaged by cyclones, notably in 2005 when a series of storms struck the island group.

Landmarks and institutions

Avarua contains the Parliament of the Cook Islands, government ministries, the National Library and the National Museum. The Punanga Nui market, held on Saturday mornings, is a focal point for produce, crafts and food. Near the town stands the wreck of the SS Maitai, wrecked on the reef in 1916, whose boiler remains visible in the harbour. Rarotonga International Airport lies a few kilometres west of the town.
