Roy Meadow

Sir Samuel Roy Meadow
Born: 1933, Wigan, Lancashire, England
Occupation: Paediatrician
Known for: Work on child abuse; controversial expert testimony in cot-death trials

Sir Samuel Roy Meadow (born 1933) is a British paediatrician, formerly professor of paediatrics at the University of Leeds. He became influential for his work on fabricated or induced illness — notably a 1977 paper that popularised the term "Munchausen syndrome by proxy" — and on the safeguarding of children. He later became a prominent expert witness in criminal trials of parents accused of murdering their babies, several of which resulted in convictions that were quashed on appeal, leading to a national controversy over expert evidence.

Expert testimony controversy

Meadow's maxim — "one sudden infant death is a tragedy, two is suspicious and three is murder, until proved otherwise", sometimes called Meadow's law — was quoted in prosecutions of mothers including Sally Clark (convicted 1999, freed on appeal in 2003), Trupti Patel (acquitted 2003), and Angela Cannings (convicted 2002, freed on appeal in 2003). In the Clark case Meadow testified that the chance of two natural cot deaths in one family was about 1 in 73 million, a figure attacked by the Royal Statistical Society as a misuse of statistics — the "prosecutor's fallacy". Following the acquittals, the Attorney General reviewed hundreds of similar convictions.

Professional proceedings

In 2005 the General Medical Council found Meadow guilty of serious professional misconduct over his evidence in the Clark trial and struck him off the medical register. He appealed, and in 2006 the High Court ruled in his favour on key points, holding that expert witnesses should generally be immune from such proceedings for their testimony; he was restored to the register, though the court criticised aspects of his evidence. He retired from practice.

Legacy

Meadow received a knighthood in 1998 for services to paediatrics. His career is regularly cited in debates on the role and limits of expert witnesses, the statistics of sudden infant death syndrome, and the diagnosis of child abuse.
