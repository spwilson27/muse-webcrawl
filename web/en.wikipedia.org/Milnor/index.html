Milnor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Milnor may refer to the surname of several people, most prominently the American mathematician John Willard Milnor (born 1931), one of the most influential topologists of the twentieth century. This page surveys the principal uses of the name.

John Milnor

John Milnor won the Fields Medal in 1962, the Wolf Prize in 1989, and the Abel Prize in 2011, a combination achieved by few mathematicians. While still an undergraduate at Princeton he proved the Fáry–Milnor theorem on the total curvature of knots. In 1956 he discovered exotic spheres — smooth manifolds homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to the ordinary seven-sphere — a result that launched the field of differential topology. His name attaches to Milnor numbers in singularity theory, Milnor's conjecture in algebraic K-theory (proved by Voevodsky), Milnor–Thurston kneading theory in dynamical systems, and standard texts including Morse Theory and Characteristic Classes (with Stasheff).

Other uses

  • James Milnor (1773–1844), American lawyer, Episcopal priest, and U.S. Representative from Pennsylvania.
  • Milnor, North Dakota, a city in Sargent County, named for a railroad engineer.
  • Pellerin Milnor Corporation, an American manufacturer of industrial laundry machinery.
  • Milnor fibre and Milnor map, concepts in the topology of hypersurface singularities.
