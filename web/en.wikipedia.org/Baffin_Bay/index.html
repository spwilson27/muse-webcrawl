Baffin Bay

Baffin Bay (Inuktitut: Saknirutiak Imanga; Greenlandic: Avannaata Imaa; French: Baie de Baffin) is a marginal sea of the North Atlantic Ocean between Baffin Island in Canada and the west coast of Greenland. It is connected to the Atlantic via the Davis Strait and to the Arctic Ocean through the Nares Strait and Smith Sound. The bay is about 1,450 km (900 mi) long and covers roughly 689,000 km² (266,000 sq mi).

History

The bay is named after the English navigator William Baffin, who explored it in 1616 while searching for the Northwest Passage; Baffin's reports of the bay were doubted for two centuries until confirmed by later expeditions. The area was long inhabited by Inuit peoples, and in the 19th century it became an important whaling ground for British and American fleets pursuing bowhead whales.

Geography and wildlife

Baffin Bay is covered by sea ice for much of the year. Its North Water Polynya, a recurring area of open water in the north of the bay, is one of the most biologically productive areas in the Arctic, supporting large populations of seabirds, seals, beluga whales, narwhals, and polar bears. Major islands include Baffin, Bylot, Devon, and Ellesmere on the Canadian side.
