Kitchener is a city in the Canadian province of Ontario, about 100 km west of Toronto. With a population of roughly a quarter of a million, it forms, with the neighbouring cities of Waterloo and Cambridge, a major urban and manufacturing centre and one of Canada’s leading technology clusters, anchored by the University of Waterloo and a dense start-up ecosystem.

History

The area was settled from 1800 by Mennonite farmers from Pennsylvania, and the town grew up as Berlin, reflecting the many German immigrants who followed; by the late nineteenth century it was a prosperous manufacturing centre known for buttons, rubber, furniture and felt. During the First World War, anti-German sentiment led to a controversial 1916 referendum and plebiscite that renamed the city Kitchener, after the British field marshal Lord Kitchener, killed that year.

Culture and economy

Kitchener hosts one of the largest Oktoberfest celebrations outside Germany, a legacy of its German heritage, and venues such as the Centre in the Square concert hall and the Kitchener Memorial Auditorium, home of the Kitchener Rangers junior hockey team. The downtown has been reshaped by tech offices, including a major Google engineering site, loft conversions in former factories, and an ION light-rail line shared with Waterloo.
