Smoked salmon

Smoked salmon is a preparation of salmon, typically a fillet that has been cured and hot or cold smoked. Due to its moderately high price, smoked salmon is often considered a delicacy. Although the term lox is sometimes applied to smoked salmon, they are different products: lox is cured in brine but not smoked.

Cold-smoked salmon, thinly sliced

Production

Smoking salmon has a long history in the cultures of northern Europe and among Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest. The fish is first cured in salt or brine, sometimes with sugar and spices. In cold smoking, the salmon is exposed to smoke at temperatures below about 30 °C (86 °F), which flavors the fish without cooking it, giving a silky texture. In hot smoking, temperatures of 50–80 °C cook the fish, producing a flaky texture similar to baked salmon.

Types and serving

Scottish, Irish, Norwegian, and Nova Scotia styles differ in cure, wood smoke, and slicing. Smoked salmon is commonly served on bagels with cream cheese, in scrambled eggs, on blinis with crème fraîche, or as part of a seafood platter. "Salmon candy" is a sweet, heavily cured hot-smoked preparation from the Pacific Northwest.

Nutrition and safety

Smoked salmon is rich in omega-3 fatty acids and protein but relatively high in sodium. Because cold-smoked fish is not cooked, food safety authorities advise care regarding Listeria contamination, particularly for pregnant people.
