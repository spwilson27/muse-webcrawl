NFAT5


Labelled image: NFAT5 (TonEBP) protein domains

Nuclear factor of activated T-cells 5, also known as NFAT5 or TonEBP (tonicity-responsive enhancer binding protein), is a protein that in humans is encoded by the NFAT5 gene. It is a member of the Rel family of transcription factors, along with NF-κB and the other NFAT proteins, but unlike its relatives it is activated by osmotic stress rather than by calcium-calcineurin signaling.

GeneNFAT5, chromosome 16q22
FamilyRel / NFAT transcription factors
Also known asTonEBP, OREBP

Function

NFAT5 is the central regulator of the cellular response to hypertonicity. When cells — most importantly those of the renal medulla, which are routinely exposed to very high salt and urea concentrations — face a hyperosmotic environment, NFAT5 accumulates in the nucleus and activates genes for osmolyte transporters and synthetic enzymes, including the sodium/myo-inositol cotransporter (SMIT), the betaine transporter (BGT1), and aldose reductase, which produces sorbitol. The accumulated organic osmolytes restore cell volume without perturbing protein function.

Other roles

Beyond osmoregulation, NFAT5 participates in immune function: it is required for optimal T-cell development and macrophage responses, is induced in lymphocytes in hypertonic lymphoid tissue, and has been implicated in inflammatory and autoimmune disease, embryonic development, and the survival of cells in the intervertebral disc and eye lens.
