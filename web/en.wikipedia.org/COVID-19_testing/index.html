COVID-19 testing

COVID-19 testing involves analysing samples to assess the current or past presence of SARS-CoV-2, the virus causing COVID-19. Testing was a central tool of pandemic control, used for diagnosis, contact tracing, screening and surveillance.

Types of tests

Molecular tests, principally reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) performed on nasopharyngeal or saliva samples, detect viral RNA and were the reference standard for accuracy. Antigen tests detect viral proteins, returning results in minutes; they are less sensitive but widely used as rapid self-tests. Antibody (serology) tests detect prior infection or vaccination response from blood samples.

Role in the pandemic

Testing capacity was a major early bottleneck in many countries in 2020. Large-scale testing, combined with isolation and contact tracing, helped some jurisdictions suppress transmission. Later, wastewater surveillance supplemented clinical testing as a population-level indicator.

Accuracy

Test performance depends on timing relative to infection, sample quality and the test's sensitivity and specificity; false negatives are most likely very early or late in infection.
