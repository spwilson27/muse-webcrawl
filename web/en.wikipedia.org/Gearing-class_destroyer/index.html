Gearing-class destroyer

The Gearing class was a class of 98 destroyers built for the United States Navy during and shortly after World War II. The class was a longer-hulled improvement on the preceding Allen M. Sumner class, with the extra length devoted mainly to additional fuel capacity for greater range in the Pacific.

Design

Gearing-class ships displaced about 2,616 tons, were 390 feet 6 inches long, and carried six 5-inch/38 guns in three twin mounts, along with anti-aircraft guns and torpedo tubes. Their geared steam turbines produced 60,000 shaft horsepower for a speed of about 36.8 knots.

Service

Many ships of the class served in the Korean and Vietnam Wars. Under the FRAM (Fleet Rehabilitation and Modernization) program of the 1960s, numerous ships were extensively modernized for anti-submarine warfare. Several were transferred to allied navies, and the last US examples were decommissioned in the early 1980s.
