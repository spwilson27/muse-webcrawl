Joscelin II of Edessa

Joscelin II (died 1159) was the fourth and last ruling count of Edessa, the first Crusader state founded in the Levant. He was the son of Joscelin I of Edessa and Beatrice of Armenia. Inheriting the county in 1131, he ruled a state increasingly exposed after the other Crusader principalities grew estranged from Edessa.

Fall of Edessa

Joscelin resided mostly at Turbessel, west of the Euphrates, leaving the city of Edessa weakly defended. In 1144, Zengi, atabeg of Mosul and Aleppo, besieged and captured Edessa — the first major Crusader loss in the Holy Land, which prompted the calling of the Second Crusade. Joscelin briefly recovered the city in 1146 after Zengi's death but was forced out again by Zengi's son Nur ad-Din, with great slaughter of the inhabitants.

Capture and death

In 1150 Joscelin was captured by Turkoman raiders while travelling to Antioch; he was blinded and imprisoned in Aleppo, where he died in 1159. His wife sold the remaining fortresses of the county to the Byzantine emperor Manuel I Komnenos, which were soon lost to the Muslims. His son Joscelin III held the title of count only nominally.
