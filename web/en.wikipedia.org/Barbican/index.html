Barbican

A barbican is a fortified outpost or gateway, such as an outer defensive structure before a castle or city gate. Barbicans formed a strongpoint outside the main walls — a "neck" through which attackers could be channelled into a killing ground overlooked by defenders.

Design

Typical barbicans consist of a walled passage or courtyard in front of a gate, sometimes with towers, a drawbridge and portcullis, forcing attackers to make turns under fire. Examples survive at the Tower of London, Kraków (the great circular Barbican of 1498–99), and many medieval towns.

Etymology

The word likely derives through Old French from Arabic or Persian terms for a fortified gatehouse.

Later uses

The name is preserved in place names, most famously London's Barbican Estate and Barbican Centre, built on a bombsite near where a Roman and medieval barbican-like outwork once stood.
