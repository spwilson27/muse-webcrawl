Positive-definite matrix

The quadratic form of a positive-definite matrix is a paraboloid opening upwards.

In mathematics, a symmetric matrix M with real entries is positive-definite if the real number xTMx is positive for every nonzero real column vector x. More generally, a Hermitian matrix is positive-definite if the real number z*Mz is positive for every nonzero complex vector z. Positive-definite matrices generalise the notion of a positive real number to matrices.

Characterisations

For an n × n Hermitian matrix M, the following conditions are equivalent to positive-definiteness:

  • All eigenvalues of M are positive.
  • The associated quadratic form takes only positive values for nonzero inputs.
  • All leading principal minors are positive (Sylvester's criterion).
  • There exists a unique lower triangular matrix L with positive diagonal entries such that M = LL* (the Cholesky decomposition).
  • M can be written as B*B for some invertible matrix B.

Applications

Positive-definite matrices arise in optimisation, where the Hessian of a function at a strict local minimum is positive-definite; in statistics, where covariance matrices are positive semi-definite; and in physics, where they describe stable equilibria. A matrix that is positive-definite defines an inner product on the underlying vector space, and hence a notion of length and angle.

Related classes include positive semi-definite matrices (allowing the value zero), negative-definite matrices, and indefinite matrices.
