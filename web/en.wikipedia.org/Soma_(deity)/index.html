Soma (deity)

In Hinduism, Soma (Sanskrit: सोम, Soma) is a Vedic deity associated with the moon, plants, and the sacred ritual drink of the same name. Soma is personified as a god and is one of the principal deities of the Rigveda, where the entire ninth book (mandala) of hymns is devoted to him.

In the Vedic religion, soma was a ritual drink of importance among the early Indo-Iranians, and the later Vedic and Greater Persian cultures. The juice was extracted from a plant whose identity is debated — candidates include ephedra, cannabis, and the fly agaric mushroom — and offered to the gods and consumed by priests for its invigorating and visionary effects. Soma is frequently praised as the "king of plants" and as a bringer of health, wealth, and immortality (amrita).

As lunar deity

In later Hindu tradition, Soma became identified with the moon god Chandra. According to myth, Soma was the son of the sage Atri and husband of the 27 daughters of Daksha, who represent the lunar mansions (nakshatras). His favoritism toward Rohini led Daksha to curse him to wane, explaining the moon's phases. In the Puranas, Soma also figures in the story of the churning of the ocean of milk, from which the nectar of immortality emerged.
