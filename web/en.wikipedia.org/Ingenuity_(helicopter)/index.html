Ingenuity (helicopter)

TypeRobotic coaxial-rotor helicopter (technology demonstrator)
OperatorNASA / Jet Propulsion Laboratory
Launched30 July 2020 (with the Perseverance rover)
First flight19 April 2021 — first powered, controlled flight on another planet
Total flights72 (mission ended January 2024)
Mass1.8 kg (0.68 kg weight on Mars)

Ingenuity, nicknamed Ginny, was a small robotic helicopter that operated on Mars as part of NASA's Mars 2020 mission, alongside the Perseverance rover. On 19 April 2021 it completed the first powered, controlled flight by an aircraft on another planet, rising about 3 metres above the floor of Jezero crater, hovering, and landing safely — a feat achieved in an atmosphere only about 1% as dense as Earth's.

Design

Because the Martian atmosphere is extremely thin, Ingenuity's two counter-rotating coaxial rotors, 1.2 metres in diameter, spun at up to roughly 2,400 revolutions per minute — many times faster than a terrestrial helicopter. The 1.8 kg craft carried a small box-like fuselage housing computers (a Qualcomm Snapdragon processor, more powerful than the rover's), heaters to survive frigid Martian nights, cameras, and sensors, powered by solar-charged lithium-ion batteries. It carried a small piece of fabric from the Wright brothers' 1903 Flyer as a tribute.

Operations

Planned as a technology demonstration of up to five flights in 30 days, Ingenuity far exceeded expectations. After its demonstration phase it transitioned to an operations role, scouting terrain ahead of Perseverance. Over nearly three years it made 72 flights, covering about 17 kilometres and logging just over two hours of flight time. The mission ended after flight 72 on 18 January 2024, when rotor damage sustained during landing prevented further flight; NASA announced the end of the mission on 25 January 2024.

Legacy

Ingenuity proved aerial exploration of other worlds practical, influenced the design of future Mars helicopters — including the larger sample-recovery helicopters planned for Mars sample return concepts and the Dragonfly rotorcraft bound for Titan — and became one of the most celebrated engineering achievements of the Mars 2020 mission.
