Myofibril

Skeletal muscle fibre showing myofibrils and their sarcomeres

A myofibril (also known as a muscle fibril or sarcostyle) is a basic rod-like organelle of a muscle cell. Skeletal muscles are composed of long, tubular cells known as muscle fibers, and these cells contain many chains of myofibrils. Each myofibril has a diameter of 1–2 micrometres. They are created during embryonic development in a process known as myogenesis.

Myofibrils are composed of long proteins including actin, myosin, and titin, and other proteins that hold them together. These proteins are organized into thick, thin, and elastic myofilaments, which repeat along the length of the myofibril in sections or units of contraction called sarcomeres. Muscles contract by sliding the thick myosin, and thin actin myofilaments along each other.

Structure

Each myofibril is surrounded by a sarcoplasmic reticulum and separated from neighbouring myofibrils by sarcoplasm containing mitochondria and nuclei. Under the electron microscope, myofibrils show a banded pattern of alternating light and dark bands, giving skeletal and cardiac muscle their striated appearance.

Sarcomere structure

The sarcomere is the repeating unit between two Z-lines. Its bands are named:

  • I band — light band containing only thin (actin) filaments
  • A band — dark band containing the full length of thick (myosin) filaments
  • H zone — the central region of the A band containing only thick filaments
  • M line — the centre of the sarcomere
  • Z disc — the boundary of the sarcomere, to which actin filaments are anchored

Function

When a muscle cell is excited by a motor neuron, calcium ions are released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum into the sarcoplasm. Calcium binds to troponin, moving tropomyosin away from the myosin-binding sites on actin. Myosin heads then form cross-bridges with actin and, powered by ATP hydrolysis, pull the actin filaments toward the centre of the sarcomere — the sliding filament theory of muscle contraction. Because all sarcomeres in a myofibril shorten simultaneously, the whole muscle fibre contracts.
