Flag Day

A flag day is a day set aside to celebrate or honour a national flag or other flag. Many countries designate flag days, often the anniversary of the adoption of the flag or of another significant national event. The best-known example is Flag Day in the United States, observed on June 14, which commemorates the adoption of the flag of the United States on June 14, 1777, by resolution of the Second Continental Congress.

United States

The idea of an annual flag day was promoted in the late nineteenth century, notably by schoolteacher Bernard J. Cigrand, who held observances from 1885 and campaigned for national recognition. President Woodrow Wilson issued a proclamation establishing June 14 as Flag Day in 1916, and in August 1949 an Act of Congress (Public Law 234, 81st Congress) designated June 14 of each year as National Flag Day, signed by President Harry S. Truman. Flag Day is not a federal public holiday — government offices remain open — except in Pennsylvania, which made it a state holiday in 1937, the first state to do so. Observances include flag-raising ceremonies, parades (a notable one is held in Troy, New York), and the proper display and retirement of worn flags, often by veterans' organisations and Scout troops. The week of June 14 is designated National Flag Week.

Other countries

Many nations celebrate their own flag days: Denmark marks the legendary fall of the Dannebrog on June 15; Finland's Flag Day falls on Midsummer Saturday; Sweden's National Day and Flag Day is June 6; Mexico celebrates Día de la Bandera on February 24; the Philippines on May 28; Russia on August 22; Ukraine on August 23; India observes Armed Forces Flag Day on December 7; and Australia celebrates National Flag Day on September 3, marking the day in 1901 when its flag was first flown. Argentina's Flag Day, June 20, commemorating the death of flag creator Manuel Belgrano, is a public holiday there.
