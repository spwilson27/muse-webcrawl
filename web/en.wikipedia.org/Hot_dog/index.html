A hot dog is a dish consisting of a grilled, steamed or boiled sausage served in the slit of a partially sliced bun. The sausage used is a wiener (Vienna sausage) or a frankfurter (Frankfurter Würstchen). Hot dogs are a staple of American street food and ballpark fare, and are traditionally dressed with condiments such as mustard, ketchup, onions, mayonnaise, relish, sauerkraut or chili.

History

Sausages in the frankfurter style originated in Frankfurt, Germany, while similar sausages were made in Vienna, Austria. German immigrants brought the sausages to the United States in the nineteenth century, where vendors in New York — notably at Coney Island, where Charles Feltman began selling them in 1867 and Nathan's Famous was founded in 1916 — popularized serving them in rolls. The name "hot dog" appears in print by the 1890s; the popular tale that it was coined by cartoonist Tad Dorgan is now considered a legend.

Regional styles

Distinct regional preparations include the Chicago-style hot dog, "dragged through the garden" with tomato, pickle, sport peppers, celery salt and a poppy-seed bun (and never ketchup, by local custom); the chili dog; the corn dog, battered and deep-fried on a stick; and the Sonoran hot dog wrapped in bacon. Nathan's Hot Dog Eating Contest, held each Fourth of July at Coney Island, has become a fixture of competitive eating.
