Salford Quays

Salford Quays is an area of Salford, Greater Manchester, England, built on the former docks of the Manchester Ship Canal at the eastern end of the canal near its terminus. Once among the busiest inland docks in Britain, the area declined with containerisation and the fall of the Manchester docks, which closed in 1982. A long regeneration programme transformed the derelict docklands into a waterfront district of offices, apartments, and cultural landmarks, home to The Lowry arts centre, the Imperial War Museum North (across the canal in Trafford), and MediaCityUK, the media hub occupied by the BBC and ITV.

History of the docks

The Salford Docks were built as part of the Manchester Ship Canal, opened in 1894 to allow ocean-going ships to reach Manchester directly, bypassing Liverpool. Docks 6 to 9 and their extensive quays, warehouses, and railway sidings made Salford one of the largest inland ports in the world, employing thousands. Trade declined after the Second World War as ships grew larger and cargo handling containerised; the docks closed to commercial traffic in 1982, leaving polluted water and derelict land.

Regeneration

Salford City Council acquired the docks in 1984 and, with the Salford/Trafford Urban Development Corporation from 1987, embarked on one of Britain's flagship urban regeneration schemes. A major water-treatment programme cleaned the dock basins, and new offices, housing, hotels, and the Lowry Outlet Mall followed. The Lowry, designed by Michael Wilford and named for the artist L. S. Lowry, opened in 2000; Daniel Libeskind's Imperial War Museum North opened in 2002 on the Trafford bank, linked by a footbridge. From 2007 the Peel Group developed MediaCityUK, and in 2011 the BBC moved major departments — including BBC Sport, BBC Breakfast, and Radio 5 Live — to the Quays, joined by ITV's Coronation Street studios. The area is served by Manchester Metrolink trams.

Today

Salford Quays now hosts thousands of jobs in media, digital, and financial services, along with waterside housing, leisure boating, open-water swimming, and events such as triathlons. It is frequently cited as a successful example of post-industrial docklands regeneration, though debates continue about affordability and the balance between corporate development and public space.
