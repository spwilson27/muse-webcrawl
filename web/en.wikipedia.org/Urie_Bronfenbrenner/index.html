Urie Bronfenbrenner

Urie Bronfenbrenner (1917–2005).

Urie Bronfenbrenner (29 April 1917 – 25 September 2005) was a Russian-born American psychologist best known for developing ecological systems theory, a framework for understanding human development in terms of the nested social environments in which it occurs. He spent most of his career at Cornell University.

Ecological systems theory

In The Ecology of Human Development (1979), Bronfenbrenner argued that a developing person is embedded in a series of interacting systems: the microsystem of immediate settings such as family and school; the mesosystem of connections between them; the exosystem of settings that affect the child indirectly; the macrosystem of cultural values and laws; and the chronosystem of change over time. His later bioecological model emphasised "proximal processes" — sustained, increasingly complex interactions — as the primary engines of development.

Policy work

Bronfenbrenner was a co-founder of the United States Head Start programme in 1965, which provides early childhood education and health services to low-income families, and he testified frequently before Congress on child and family policy. He received numerous honours, and the ecological systems framework remains one of the most widely taught models in developmental psychology.
