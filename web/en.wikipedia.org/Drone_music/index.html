Drone music

Drone music, drone-based music, or simply drone, is a minimalist genre that emphasises the use of sustained sounds, notes, or tone clusters — called drones. It is typically characterised by lengthy compositions featuring relatively slight harmonic variations throughout each piece compared to other musics.

Origins

The genre draws on ancient precedents: the drones of Indian classical music, Tibetan Buddhist chant, Byzantine ison singing and European bagpipe traditions. In the 1960s, New York composer La Monte Young and the Theatre of Eternal Music (with John Cale, Tony Conrad, Marian Zazeela and others) developed long-duration works built from precisely tuned sustained intervals, founding a modern drone practice. Éliane Radigue created slowly evolving electronic pieces on the ARP 2500 synthesiser, and Phill Niblock made dense microtonal works for amplified instruments.

Development

Drone techniques entered rock through the Velvet Underground, krautrock, and the ambient music of Brian Eno. In the 1990s and 2000s, drone metal — pioneered by Earth and Sunn O))) — pushed amplifier volume and low frequencies to extremes, while artists such as Stars of the Lid and William Basinski explored quiet, layered ambient drones. Drone remains a cross-cutting technique in experimental, folk, metal and electronic music, often experienced as immersive, meditative sound environments.
