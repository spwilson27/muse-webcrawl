Load cell

A load cell is a transducer used to create an electrical signal whose magnitude is directly proportional to a force being measured. Load cells are used in weighing systems of all sizes, from bathroom and kitchen scales to industrial hopper, tank and vehicle weighing, as well as in force measurement and materials testing.

Types

The most common type is the strain gauge load cell. Strain gauges are bonded to an elastic metal element; when a load is applied, the element deforms slightly, changing the electrical resistance of the gauges. The gauges are usually arranged in a Wheatstone bridge circuit, producing a small output voltage (typically specified in millivolts per volt of excitation) proportional to the applied load. Common geometries include S-beam, shear beam, cantilever (bending beam), canister (column) and pancake designs.

Other types include hydraulic load cells, which measure force via the pressure of a contained fluid; pneumatic load cells, which use air pressure balanced against a diaphragm; and piezoelectric load cells, which generate a charge under dynamic loading and are suited to measuring varying or impact forces rather than static weight.

Applications and accuracy

Load cells are rated by capacity, from grams to hundreds of tonnes, and by accuracy class; cells used in legal-for-trade weighing must meet standards such as OIML R60. Factors affecting performance include creep under sustained load, hysteresis, temperature effects on the span and zero point, and off-centre (eccentric) loading. Signal conditioning amplifies the bridge output for display or for connection to digital indicators and control systems.
