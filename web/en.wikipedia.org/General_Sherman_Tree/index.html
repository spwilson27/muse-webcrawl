General Sherman Tree

The General Sherman Tree is a giant sequoia (Sequoiadendron giganteum) located in the Giant Forest of Sequoia National Park in Tulare County, California. By volume, it is the largest known living single-stem tree on Earth. It is estimated to be around 2,200 to 2,700 years old.

Dimensions

The tree stands 83.8 metres (275 feet) tall, with a diameter of 7.7 metres (25 feet) at breast height and a maximum basal diameter of about 11 metres (36 feet). Its trunk volume is estimated at 1,487 cubic metres (52,500 cubic feet), and its total mass has been estimated at roughly 1,900 tonnes including roots and branches. Its first large branch emerges about 40 metres above the base and is itself nearly 2 metres in diameter.

History

The tree was named in 1879 by naturalist James Wolverton, who had served under William Tecumseh Sherman in the American Civil War. In 1931, after comparisons with the nearby General Grant Tree, it was designated the largest tree in the world by volume. For a period beginning in 1892 it was known as the Karl Marx Tree by the utopian Kaweah Colony, which claimed the surrounding land.

In January 2006 the largest branch of the tree broke and fell, an event typical of aging sequoias; it did not affect the tree's standing as largest by stem volume.
