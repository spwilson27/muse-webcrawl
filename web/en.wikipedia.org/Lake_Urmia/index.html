Lake Urmia

Lake Urmia (Persian: دریاچه ارومیه, Daryâče-ye Orumiye; Azerbaijani: Urmu gölü) is an endorheic salt lake in northwestern Iran, between the provinces of East Azerbaijan and West Azerbaijan. At its greatest extent it was the largest lake in the Middle East and one of the largest hypersaline lakes in the world.

Geography and ecology

The lake is fed by rivers including the Zarrineh and Simineh and has no outlet; its salinity historically ranged around 217–235 g/L, comparable to the Dead Sea. It contained about 102 islands and supported large breeding colonies of flamingos and pelicans, as well as brine shrimp (Artemia urmiana). The lake and its wetlands are a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve and a Ramsar site. A causeway carrying the Urmia Lake Bridge, opened in 2008, crosses the lake between Urmia and Tabriz.

Shrinkage and restoration

From the mid-1990s, dam construction on inflowing rivers, groundwater pumping for agriculture, and prolonged drought caused the lake to lose the great majority of its surface area — by some measures around 90 percent at its lowest point in the mid-2010s — exposing salt flats whose wind-blown dust threatened surrounding farmland and public health. Iran launched a major restoration programme, diverting water and curbing agricultural withdrawals; wetter years in 2019–2020 raised levels substantially, though subsequent drought again reduced them, and the lake's long-term future remains uncertain.
