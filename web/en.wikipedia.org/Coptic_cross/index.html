Coptic cross

The Coptic cross is a family of cross designs used by Egyptian (Coptic) Christians. Forms have varied over the centuries, from simple equal-armed crosses derived from the ancient Egyptian ankh to elaborate interlaced designs in metalwork and tattooing.

Forms

Early Coptic crosses resembled the Greek cross, often set within a circle representing eternity and the sun. Later designs developed intricate interlace and openwork, with arms terminating in smaller crosses or divided into three points symbolising the Holy Trinity; in some designs the twelve apostles are represented by twelve points or spikes around the cross.

Modern form

The form most commonly seen today in the Coptic Orthodox Church is a cross of interwoven lines forming a lattice, with each of the four arms ending in three points — twelve points in total for the twelve apostles. Coptic Christians in Egypt traditionally tattoo a small cross on the inner wrist as a mark of faith.
