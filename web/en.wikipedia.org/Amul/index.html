Amul is an Indian dairy cooperative society and brand, based at Anand in Gujarat. Formed in 1946, it is managed by the Gujarat Co-operative Milk Marketing Federation Ltd. (GCMMF), which is jointly owned by about 3.6 million milk producers in Gujarat. Amul spurred India's White Revolution, which made the country the world's largest producer of milk and milk products, and the Amul model of village dairy cooperatives has been replicated nationwide through Operation Flood.

History

Amul originated as the Kaira District Co-operative Milk Producers' Union, organised in 1946 by farmers resisting exploitation by middlemen. Under the leadership of Tribhuvandas Patel and its general manager Verghese Kurien, the cooperative built its own processing plant and pioneered the production of milk powder and butter from buffalo milk. The name "Amul" is derived from the Sanskrit word amulya ("priceless") and also served as an acronym of Anand Milk Union Limited. Its slogan, "The Taste of India", and the Amul girl mascot — created in 1966 and famous for topical advertising hoardings — are cultural icons.

Products and reach

Amul markets milk, butter, ghee, cheese, curd, ice cream, chocolates, and many other dairy products, and is India's largest food brand. The federation's turnover runs to tens of thousands of crores of rupees, exporting to dozens of countries.
