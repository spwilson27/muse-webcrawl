Chanhassen is a city in Carver and Hennepin counties in the U.S. state of Minnesota, a southwestern suburb of Minneapolis located about 18 miles (29 km) southwest of downtown. The population was about 26,000 at the 2020 census. Its name derives from a Dakota word meaning "sugar maple tree" (chan, tree, and hassen, sweet juice).

History

The area was settled in the 1850s and the township was organized in 1858. Chanhassen remained a farming community until suburban growth from the Twin Cities reached it in the late 20th century, when it developed into an affluent residential suburb with numerous lakes, including Lake Minnewashta, Lake Ann and Lotus Lake. The city was incorporated in 1969.

Points of interest

Chanhassen is best known as the home of Paisley Park, the recording studio complex and residence built by the musician Prince in 1985; after his death in 2016 it opened as a museum. Other attractions include the Minnesota Landscape Arboretum, a 1,200-acre public garden operated by the University of Minnesota at the city's eastern edge, and the Chanhassen Dinner Theatres, one of the largest dinner theatre complexes in the United States. The city has frequently ranked highly in national "best places to live" surveys for its schools, parks and incomes.

Economy and education

Employers include the headquarters of Life Time, Inc. (fitness clubs) and other corporate and light-industrial operations along the Highway 5 corridor. Most of the city is served by Eastern Carver County School District 112, with a small portion in the Minnetonka school district.
