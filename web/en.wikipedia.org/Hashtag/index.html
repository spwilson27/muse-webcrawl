Hashtag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hashtag is a metadata tag type used on social networks and microblogging services, written by prefixing a word or unspaced phrase with the hash symbol (#), as in #wikipedia. Hashtags make it possible to group posts by topic and to find content about a subject across a platform; clicking a hashtag typically displays other messages that use it. The convention began on Twitter in 2007 and has since spread to most social media services, as well as into speech, advertising and political campaigning.

History

The hash symbol had long been used in information technology to mark special meaning — for example in IRC (Internet Relay Chat) channels from the late 1980s, where channels were named with a leading #. On 23 August 2007 the American blogger and open-source advocate Chris Messina proposed using the hash sign on Twitter to group related tweets, asking: "how do you feel about using # (pound) for groups. As in #barcamp [msg]?" The idea was initially rejected by Twitter as "too nerdy", but it gained traction during the October 2007 San Diego forest fires, when Messina and others used #sandiegofire to aggregate updates. Twitter officially embraced hashtags in July 2009, hyperlinking them to search results; trending topics soon followed.

Spread and usage

Facebook introduced clickable hashtags in 2013, and Instagram, Tumblr, Pinterest, LinkedIn and TikTok all support them. Hashtags are used for breaking news, live events, marketing campaigns and social movements; prominent examples include #MeToo, #BlackLivesMatter, #IceBucketChallenge and #ArabSpring. Research and platform policies differ on how many hashtags aid discovery, and excessive use ("hashjacking" or tag spam) is discouraged.

Format and etiquette

A hashtag cannot contain spaces or punctuation other than letters, digits and underscores; multi-word tags are joined, often in camel case (e.g. #BlackLivesMatter), which also improves accessibility for screen readers. A hashtag made only of numbers may not hyperlink on some platforms. Using another organisation's campaign tag to insert unrelated content is considered poor etiquette and may breach platform rules.

In popular culture

The American Dialect Society named "hashtag" its Word of the Year for 2012, and the word entered the Oxford English Dictionary in 2014. In 2012, a French couple's attempt to name a child "Hashtag" was widely reported, and late-night television popularised the spoken use of "hashtag" as a humorous aside.
