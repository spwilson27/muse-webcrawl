Contract law

Contract law governs legally enforceable agreements.

Contract law is the body of law that relates to making and enforcing agreements. A contract is an agreement that a party can turn to a court to enforce, and contract law is the area of law of obligations concerned with contracts — determining which agreements are binding, what terms they contain, what counts as performance or breach, and what remedies are available.

Formation

In common law systems, a contract generally requires an offer, acceptance, consideration (something of value exchanged), an intention to create legal relations, and certainty of terms; parties must have legal capacity, and the agreement must not be for an illegal purpose. Civil law systems dispense with consideration but may require cause. Contracts may be vitiated by misrepresentation, mistake, duress or undue influence. Famous cases include Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co [1893], on unilateral offers to the world at large.

Breach and remedies

Failure to perform a contractual term is a breach, entitling the innocent party to damages — normally measured to put them in the position as if the contract had been performed (Hadley v Baxendale remoteness rules) — or, in some cases, specific performance or an injunction. Legislation such as the Uniform Commercial Code (US) and the UN Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (1980) governs many commercial transactions.
