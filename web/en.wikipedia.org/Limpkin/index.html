The limpkin (Aramus guarauna), also called the carrao, courlan, and crying bird, is a large wading bird related to cranes and rails. It is the only extant species in the family Aramidae. It is found in warm wetlands of the Americas, from Florida through the Caribbean and Mexico to northern Argentina.

Description

The limpkin is 56–71 cm long with brown plumage heavily streaked with white on the head, neck and upper body. Its long, slightly downcurved bill is specially adapted — the tip has a slight rightward bend and internal structure — for extracting apple snails (Pomacea) from their shells. Its name comes from its seemingly limping gait, and its eerie wailing call is a classic sound of Florida swamps, often heard at night.

Feeding and habitat

Limpkins feed almost exclusively on apple snails, hunting by sight and touch in shallow marsh vegetation; they also take mussels and other molluscs. Because of this specialisation their distribution tracks that of their prey, and in Florida the species declined with wetland drainage before recovering, aided in part by the spread of an invasive apple snail. They nest on floating vegetation or in shrubs above water, laying about six eggs.
