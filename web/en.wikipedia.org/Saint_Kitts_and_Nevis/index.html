Saint Kitts and Nevis

Saint Kitts and Nevis, officially the Federation of Saint Christopher and Nevis, is an island country in the West Indies. Located in the Leeward Islands chain of the Lesser Antilles, it is the smallest sovereign state in the Western Hemisphere, in both area and population, as well as the world's smallest sovereign federation.

History

The islands were inhabited by Kalinago people before Christopher Columbus sighted them in 1493. Saint Kitts became the site of the first British (1623) and French colonies in the Caribbean and was nicknamed the "Mother Colony of the West Indies". Sugar plantations worked by enslaved Africans drove the colonial economy; slavery was abolished in 1834. After periods in the Leeward Islands Federation and the West Indies Federation, the islands became an associated state in 1967 and gained full independence in 1983.

Geography

Saint Kitts, the larger island, is dominated by the dormant volcano Mount Liamuiga (1,156 m). Nevis, about 3 km to the southeast across the Narrows, is centred on Nevis Peak. The climate is tropical, tempered by trade winds, with a hurricane season from June to November.

Economy and politics

Sugar production dominated until the state sugar company closed in 2005; tourism, offshore financial services and a long-running citizenship-by-investment programme are now the main earners. The federation is a Commonwealth realm with the British monarch as head of state, represented by a Governor-General; Nevis has its own island assembly and a constitutional right to secede, exercised in a 1998 referendum that failed to reach the required two-thirds majority.
