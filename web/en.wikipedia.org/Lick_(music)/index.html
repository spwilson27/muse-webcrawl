Lick (music)

In popular music genres such as rock, blues, and jazz, a lick is a stock musical phrase or short melodic pattern that a musician uses as a building block in solos and improvisation. Licks are often associated with particular instruments, especially the electric guitar.

Characteristics

A lick differs from a riff in that a riff is usually a repeated, structural part of a composition, whereas a lick is typically a brief, often virtuosic phrase inserted into an improvised passage. Guitar licks commonly use techniques such as string bending, hammer-ons, pull-offs, and vibrato, and are frequently drawn from pentatonic and blues scales.

Famous examples

Many licks have become iconic, such as the opening licks of Chuck Berry's "Johnny B. Goode" and the phrases traded by blues guitarists like B. B. King, whose economical single-note lines were widely imitated. A modern meme example is "The Lick", a specific seven-note phrase that recurs throughout jazz recordings and videos.

Learning and use

Musicians learn licks by transcribing recordings, then adapt them to their own style. Building a vocabulary of licks is a standard part of jazz and blues pedagogy, though teachers caution that stringing licks together is not the same as genuine improvisation.
