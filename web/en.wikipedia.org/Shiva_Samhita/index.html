Shiva Samhita

Language: Sanskrit
Subject: Hatha yoga
Form: Dialogue between Shiva and Parvati
Chapters: Five

The Shiva Samhita is a Sanskrit text on hatha yoga, presented as a dialogue in which the god Shiva teaches his consort Parvati. It is considered one of the three classical surviving treatises on hatha yoga, alongside the Hatha Yoga Pradipika and the Gheranda Samhita, and is generally dated to the seventeenth or eighteenth century.

Contents

The text's five chapters cover metaphysics and the subtle body of nadis and chakras, the awakening of kundalini, mudras (seals) that preserve vital energy, meditation techniques including contemplation of inner sound and light, and the four grades of yoga and yogis. Unlike some hatha texts, it emphasises that a householder, not only a renunciant, can practise yoga successfully.

Influence

The Shiva Samhita was among the early hatha yoga texts translated into English in the nineteenth century, introducing its teachings on mudra, kundalini and meditation to Western readers and influencing the development of modern yoga scholarship.
