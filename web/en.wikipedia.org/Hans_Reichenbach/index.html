Hans Reichenbach

Hans Reichenbach
26 September 1891 – 9 April 1953
German-American philosopher of science

Hans Reichenbach (1891–1953) was a German philosopher of science and a leading figure of logical empiricism. He led the Berlin Circle (the Berlin Society for Empirical Philosophy), the counterpart to the Vienna Circle.

Life and career

Born in Hamburg, Reichenbach studied engineering, mathematics, and philosophy, attending Einstein's lectures in Berlin. His 1920 dissertation and early books defended a relativistic, empiricist interpretation of space and time. Appointed professor of philosophy of physics at the University of Berlin in 1926 with Einstein's support, he was dismissed by the Nazi regime in 1933 and emigrated to Istanbul, then in 1938 to the University of California, Los Angeles, where he taught until his death.

Philosophy

Reichenbach developed a probabilistic theory of induction, arguing that although induction cannot be justified deductively, the "straight rule" of induction is the best available strategy for prediction. He distinguished the context of discovery from the context of justification, a distinction that became standard in philosophy of science. His books include The Philosophy of Space and Time (1928), Elements of Symbolic Logic (1947), and the popular The Rise of Scientific Philosophy (1951). He proposed a three-valued logic for quantum mechanics and an influential account of time's direction grounded in entropy and causal order.
