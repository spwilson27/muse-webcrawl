Minar-e-Pakistan

LocationIqbal Park, Lahore, Pakistan
Height62 m (203 ft)
Built1960–1968
ArchitectNasreddin Murat-Khan
CommemoratesLahore Resolution (1940)

Minar-e-Pakistan (Urdu: مینارِ پاکستان, "Tower of Pakistan") is a national monument and public tower in Lahore, Pakistan, located in Iqbal Park (formerly Minto Park). It stands on the site where, on 23 March 1940, the All-India Muslim League passed the Lahore Resolution — the demand that led to the creation of Pakistan in 1947.

The tower was designed by architect Nasreddin Murat-Khan, a Russian-born Pakistani, and built between 1960 and 1968. Rising 62 metres above a star-and-crescent-shaped base, its unfolding flower-petals design symbolizes the blossoming of the Pakistan movement. The base inscriptions include the text of the Lahore Resolution in Urdu, Bengali, and English, the National Anthem, and quotations from Muhammad Ali Jinnah and Allama Iqbal carved in calligraphy.

Significance

The Minar is one of Pakistan's most recognizable landmarks and a frequent venue for political rallies and national celebrations, particularly on Pakistan Day (23 March). Its surrounding park, the Greater Iqbal Park, was extensively renovated in the 2010s and lies adjacent to the Badshahi Mosque and Lahore Fort.
