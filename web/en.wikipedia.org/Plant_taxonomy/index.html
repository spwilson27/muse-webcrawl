Plant taxonomy

Plant taxonomy is the science of identifying, describing, naming, and classifying plants. It is one of the principal branches of botany and systematics, concerned with organising the roughly 380,000 known species of plants into a hierarchical system that reflects their evolutionary relationships.

Nomenclature

Plant names are governed by the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICN), which sets the rules for valid publication of scientific names. Plants are named using binomial nomenclature — a genus name plus a specific epithet, such as Quercus robur for the pedunculate oak — a system standardised by Carl Linnaeus in the 18th century.

Classification systems

Historical systems, including those of Bentham and Hooker, Engler, Cronquist, and Takhtajan, grouped plants mainly by morphology. Modern classification is dominated by the molecular-phylogenetic work of the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group, whose APG IV system (2016) arranges flowering plants into orders and families based largely on DNA sequence evidence.

Ranks

The principal ranks in plant classification are kingdom (Plantae), division or phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species, with intermediate ranks as needed. Families are among the most widely used ranks in practice, such as the rose family Rosaceae and the grass family Poaceae.
