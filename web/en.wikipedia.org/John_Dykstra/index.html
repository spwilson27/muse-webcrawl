John Dykstra

[Image placeholder: portrait of John Dykstra]

John Charles Dykstra, ASC (born June 3, 1947) is an American special effects artist, pioneer in the development of the use of computers in filmmaking, and recipient of three Academy Awards. He is best known as the original special effects supervisor on Star Wars (1977), for which he led the development team that created Industrial Light & Magic.

Dykstra studied industrial design and worked with Douglas Trumbull on Silent Running before George Lucas hired him in 1975 to build a visual-effects facility for Star Wars. There he developed the Dykstraflex, the first computer-controlled motion-control camera system, which allowed precisely repeatable camera moves around miniatures and made the film's dynamic space battles possible. Star Wars won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects.

Later career

After a falling-out with Lucas, Dykstra left ILM and founded Apogee, Inc., producing effects for Battlestar Galactica and Star Trek: The Motion Picture. He later won further Academy Awards for Spider-Man 2 (2004) and was nominated for other superhero films; his credits include Batman Forever, Batman & Robin, Stuart Little, Spider-Man, X-Men: First Class, Godzilla (2014), and Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. In 2006 he received the Academy's Gordon E. Sawyer Award for technological contributions to the industry.
