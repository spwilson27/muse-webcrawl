Soufrière, Saint Lucia

Soufrière is a town and district on the southwest coast of Saint Lucia. It is named for the sulphur springs of the nearby volcano, and it sits in the shadow of the Pitons – two great volcanic plugs that are the island's emblem. Soufrière was Saint Lucia's first capital, founded by the French in 1746.

The Pitons

Gros Piton and Petit Piton are the eroded remnants of ancient volcanoes: magma that solidified in the vents remained as hard plugs after the surrounding cones weathered away. They rise directly from the Caribbean Sea to over 700 m and are protected as a World Heritage Site; Soufrière Bay lies between them and the town.

Economy

Tourism centred on the Pitons, the Sulphur Springs and nearby estates anchors the modern economy, alongside fishing and small-scale agriculture.
