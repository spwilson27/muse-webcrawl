Volcanic plug
Also calledVolcanic neck
Formed bySolidified magma in a volcanic vent
ExamplesThe Pitons (Saint Lucia), Devils Tower (Wyoming)

A volcanic plug, also called a volcanic neck, is a volcanic object created when magma hardens within a vent on an active volcano. When present, a plug can cause an extreme build-up of high gas pressure if rising volatile-charged magma is trapped beneath it, sometimes leading to explosive eruptions.

Formation

After a volcano becomes extinct, erosion may strip away the softer cone of ash and lava flows, leaving the resistant column of solidified magma standing as a dramatic spire or tower of rock.

Examples

Famous volcanic plugs include Devils Tower in Wyoming, the Pitons of Saint Lucia, Edinburgh Castle Rock in Scotland, and the Rocher Saint-Michel d'Aiguilhe in France, crowned by a chapel.
