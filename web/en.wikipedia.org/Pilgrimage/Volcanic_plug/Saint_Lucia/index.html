Saint Lucia

Saint Lucia is an island country in the eastern Caribbean Sea, part of the Windward Islands of the Lesser Antilles. A volcanic island, it is famed for the Pitons, two towering volcanic plugs on its southwestern coast near Soufrière. Saint Lucia gained independence from the United Kingdom in 1979 and remains a Commonwealth realm.

Geography

The island is mountainous and of volcanic origin, with the Qualibou caldera near Soufrière, hot sulphur springs, and rainforest in the interior. Gros Piton (798 m) and Petit Piton (743 m) are eroded volcanic plugs rising sheer from the sea; the Pitons Management Area is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Colonised by France and Britain in turn – it changed hands fourteen times – Saint Lucia's plantation economy was built on sugar and enslaved labour. It has produced two Nobel laureates, economist Arthur Lewis and poet Derek Walcott.
