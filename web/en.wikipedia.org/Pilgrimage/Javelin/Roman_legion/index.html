Roman legion

The Roman legion was the core heavy-infantry formation of the Roman army. In battle its legionaries relied on missile javelins – the pila – to disrupt the enemy line before fighting hand-to-hand with swords and shields. Under the Empire some thirty legions, each of about 5,200 citizens, garrisoned the frontiers.

Equipment

The legionary wore mail or segmented plate armour (lorica segmentata), an iron helmet, and carried the scutum, two pila and the gladius. Each eight-man tent group shared a mule for the heavy gear; soldiers nonetheless marched carrying packs, stakes and tools, earning the nickname "Marius's mules".

History

From the manipular legions of the Republic through the professional army of Augustus to the smaller, more numerous legions of late antiquity, the legion adapted over a thousand years while remaining the backbone of Roman military power.
