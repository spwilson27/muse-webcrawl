TypeLight spear designed for throwing

A javelin is a light spear designed primarily to be thrown. Historically it was wielded as a ranged weapon by soldiers and hunters, and in the modern era it survives in the athletics discipline of the javelin throw. Javelins were commonly carried by lightly armed skirmishers, such as the Greek peltasts and Roman velites.

The pilum

The most famous military javelin is the Roman pilum, a heavy throwing spear about 2 m (6.6 ft) long with a long iron shank. Legionaries hurled their pila just before closing with the enemy; the soft iron shank was designed to bend on impact, weighing down and disabling an enemy's shield so it had to be discarded.

Sport

The modern athletic javelin is thrown from a runway for distance. Men's javelins weigh 800 g and women's 600 g; the event has been part of the Olympic Games since 1908 for men and 1932 for women.
