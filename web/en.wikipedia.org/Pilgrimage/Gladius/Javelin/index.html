Javelin

A javelin is a light spear designed primarily to be thrown, historically as a ranged weapon. Javelins were a mainstay of ancient warfare, carried by skirmishers and by the heavy infantry of the Roman legion, whose soldiers threw their heavy javelins (the pilum) before closing with the gladius, the short sword.

In Greek and Roman warfare

Greek peltasts were specialist javelin skirmishers who harassed hoplite phalanxes from a distance. Roman legionaries each carried two pila, heavy javelins with long iron shanks designed to punch through shields and bend on impact, weighing the shield down. The volley was delivered at close range just before the charge with the gladius.

Modern sport

The javelin throw survives as a field event in athletics, contested at the Olympic Games since 1908 for men and 1932 for women. The modern implement is a metal-tipped spear of about 2.6–2.7 m for men and 2.2–2.3 m for women, thrown overarm after a run-up.
