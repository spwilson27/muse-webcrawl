Roman legion

The Roman legion (Latin: legio) was the largest permanent unit of the Roman army. In the Empire a legion numbered roughly 5,200 heavy infantry plus supporting troops, recruited from Roman citizens and organised into ten cohorts. The legionary's characteristic kit combined the gladius short sword, the curved rectangular scutum shield, and heavy throwing javelins called pila.

Activec. 753 BC – AD 1453 (in various forms)
CountryRoman Kingdom, Republic and Empire
TypeHeavy infantry
Sizec. 5,200 men (Imperial)

Organisation

An Imperial legion comprised ten cohorts; the first cohort was double-strength. The basic subunit was the century of 80 men commanded by a centurion. Senior officers included the legate (legatus legionis), tribunes and the camp prefect. Auxiliaries – non-citizen troops providing cavalry, archers and light infantry – were attached alongside.

Tactics

Legionaries typically opened battle by hurling their pila to disrupt the enemy and encumber shields, then charged with the gladius behind a wall of scuta. Discipline, entrenchment (each legion built a fortified camp nightly) and engineering were as much hallmarks of the legion as combat.
