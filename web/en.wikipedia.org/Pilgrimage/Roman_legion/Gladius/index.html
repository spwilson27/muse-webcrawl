Gladius

The gladius was the short sword of the Roman legion, carried on the right hip and drawn for the close fighting that followed the legion's javelin volley. Its name became the generic Latin word for a sword, and survives in words such as "gladiator".

TypeShort sword
OriginHispania (Celtiberian design)
UsersRoman legionaries

Design

The gladius had a double-edged blade of pattern-welded or carburised steel, a pronounced point, and a hilt of wood, bone or ivory with a spherical pommel. It was worn in a scabbard (vagina) suspended from a belt or baldric. Its moderate length let the legionary thrust around and over the rim of his scutum in the crush of battle.

Legacy

Romans called the sword ensis or gladius; gladiators took their name from it. The type gave way in late antiquity to the longer spatha, ancestor of the medieval arming sword.
