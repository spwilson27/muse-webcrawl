The Roman legion (Latin: legio) was the largest military unit of the Roman army. A legion of the Imperial era numbered roughly 5,000 heavy infantrymen (legionaries), organised into ten cohorts, supported by attached specialists and a small cavalry contingent.

Organisation

The basic tactical sub-unit was the century of about 80 men, commanded by a centurion. Six centuries formed a cohort, and ten cohorts the legion; the first cohort was double-strength and guarded the legion's eagle standard, the aquila. A professional army emerged after the reforms traditionally attributed to Gaius Marius, which opened service to landless citizens and standardised equipment issued by the state.

Tactics

Legionaries advanced behind the curved rectangular scutum, discharged their heavy pila at close range, then fought with the short gladius. Discipline, engineering skill and the fortified marching camp were as much hallmarks of the legion as its battlefield performance.
