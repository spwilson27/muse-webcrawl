Javelin

A javelin is a spear intended to be thrown. In the Roman legion the standard javelin was the pilum, a heavy weapon with a wooden shaft and a long, slender iron shank ending in a small pyramidal head; legionaries threw two pila at the enemy line immediately before engaging with the gladius.

Roman namePilum
Lengthc. 2 m (pilum)
RoleThrown weapon; shield encumbrance

The pilum

The pilum's iron shank was designed to penetrate a shield and bend, dragging the shield down or forcing its owner to discard it. Lighter javelins (veruta, lanceae) were used by light troops and auxiliaries. In the late Empire the pilum was largely replaced by weapons such as the spiculum and the lead-weighted dart (plumbata).

Other traditions

Javelins were universal in ancient warfare: Greek peltasts, Celtic and Germanic spearmen, and many African and Asian armies used throwing spears, and the weapon persists in the Olympic javelin throw.
