Dissolution of the Monasteries

The Dissolution of the Monasteries was the process between 1536 and 1541 by which Henry VIII disbanded Catholic monastic communities in England, Wales and Ireland, appropriating their income and assets. It was one of the most sweeping administrative acts of the English Reformation and was administered by Thomas Cromwell and the Court of Augmentations.

LocationEngland, Wales, Ireland

Course

Following the break with Rome, the Valor Ecclesiasticus (1535) surveyed Church wealth. An act of 1536 suppressed houses with incomes under £200 a year; a further act in 1539 provided for the greater houses, and abbots were pressured into surrender. Monks, nuns and friars received pensions; lands and plate passed to the Crown, which sold much of the land to the gentry.

Opposition and consequences

The dissolution was the principal grievance behind the Pilgrimage of Grace (1536), the rising led by Robert Aske, whose demands included the restoration of the abbeys. The transfer of monastic wealth transformed the English landed class, ruined great buildings such as Fountains and Rievaulx, and dismantled the monastic provision of education, hospitality and poor relief.
