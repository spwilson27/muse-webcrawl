Robert Aske

Robert Aske (1500 – 12 July 1537) was a Yorkshire lawyer and gentleman who led the Pilgrimage of Grace (1536), the rising provoked by Henry VIII's religious policies. Aske consistently maintained that the pilgrims were loyal to Henry VIII himself and opposed only the king's "evil counsellors" and heretical bishops.

Born1500, Aughton, Yorkshire
Died12 July 1537, York
AllegiancePilgrimage of Grace

Relationship with the king

Aske's petitions were addressed to Henry in terms of obedience, asking the king to restore the monasteries, defend the Church, and remove Thomas Cromwell and reforming bishops. After the dispersal at Doncaster, Aske was invited to court at Christmas 1536, where Henry received him graciously and asked him to write an account of the movement – a document later used against him.

Downfall

Once fresh disturbances gave Henry a pretext, Aske was arrested, convicted of treason in London, and executed at York, hanged in chains on 12 July 1537. His fate illustrated Henry's policy of conciliating the rebels until it was safe to destroy their leaders.
