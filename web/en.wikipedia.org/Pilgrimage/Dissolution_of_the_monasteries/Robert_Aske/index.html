Robert Aske

Robert Aske (1500 – 12 July 1537) was an English lawyer and landowner who became the leader of the Pilgrimage of Grace, the great northern rising of 1536 against the religious and political policies of King Henry VIII, in particular the Dissolution of the Monasteries.

The rising

Aske was a member of Gray's Inn and a leading figure among the Yorkshire gentry. When rebellion broke out in Lincolnshire and then Yorkshire in October 1536, the rebels chose Aske as their captain. Under his leadership the movement, styling itself a "pilgrimage" rather than a rebellion, swore an oath to defend the Church, restore the dissolved abbeys, and petition the king to dismiss his low-born counsellors, above all Thomas Cromwell.

Aske's host grew to perhaps 30,000–40,000 men, the largest Tudor rebellion. York surrendered to him, and he held Pontefract Castle. Rather than storm Doncaster against the Duke of Norfolk's smaller royal army, Aske negotiated; the pilgrims dispersed in December 1536 on promises of a free pardon, a parliament at York, and redress of grievances.

Arrest and death

When scattered new risings broke out in early 1537, Henry VIII seized the pretext to arrest the pilgrimage's leaders. Aske was brought to London, tried for high treason, and returned to York, where he was hanged in chains from Clifford's Tower on 12 July 1537.
