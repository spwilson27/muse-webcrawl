Soufrière, Saint Lucia

Soufrière is a town on the west coast of Saint Lucia, in the district of the same name. Founded by the French in 1746, it was the island's original capital. It lies beside the Qualibou caldera and beneath the twin Pitons, the famous volcanic plugs that dominate Saint Lucia's southwestern coast.

Geography and sights

The town stands on Soufrière Bay below steep volcanic hills. Nearby are the Sulphur Springs, advertised as the world's only "drive-in volcano", the Diamond Falls Botanical Gardens, and mineral baths commissioned by Louis XVI. Offshore, the Pitons – Gros Piton and Petit Piton – rise abruptly from the sea; both are ancient volcanic plugs, remnants of eroded volcanoes.

History

Soufrière served as Saint Lucia's capital under French rule before the capital moved to Castries. The estates around the town produced sugar, cocoa and limes, and the area figured in the island's brigand conflicts of the Revolutionary era.
