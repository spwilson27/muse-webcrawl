Saint Lucia is an island country in the West Indies of the Caribbean Sea, part of the Lesser Antilles. Its capital is Castries. The island was inhabited by Arawak and later Carib peoples before European colonisation, and it changed hands between France and Britain fourteen times, earning the nickname "Helen of the West Indies".

Geography

Saint Lucia is volcanic and mountainous, with lush rainforest in the interior. Its most famous landmarks are the twin peaks of the Pitons — Gros Piton and Petit Piton — near the town of Soufrière, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The island is also home to the world's only "drive-in volcano" at the Sulphur Springs.

History and culture

The British took definitive control in 1814, and Saint Lucia gained independence as a Commonwealth realm in 1979. Alongside English, a French-based Creole (Kwéyòl) is widely spoken. The island has produced two Nobel laureates: economist Arthur Lewis and poet Derek Walcott.
