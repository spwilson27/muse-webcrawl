Volcanic plug

A volcanic plug, also called a volcanic neck, is a landform created when magma hardens within the vent of an active volcano and the surrounding cone is then eroded away, leaving the resistant plug standing as a steep tower or spire. In Saint Lucia, the twin Pitons near Soufrière are celebrated examples.

TypeVolcanic landform
Formed bySolidified magma in a vent; erosion of cone
ExamplesThe Pitons (Saint Lucia); Devils Tower (Wyoming)

Formation

When viscous lava solidifies inside a volcano's conduit, it seals the vent. Subsequent erosion strips away the softer ash and lava of the cone, exposing the harder plug. Plugs may show columnar jointing and are often accompanied by radiating dykes.

Notable plugs

Devils Tower in Wyoming, Shiprock in New Mexico, Edinburgh Castle Rock in Scotland and Saint Lucia's Gros and Petit Piton are all volcanic plugs; the Pitons are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
