Pitons

LocationSoufrière District, Saint Lucia
ElevationGros Piton 798.25 m; Petit Piton 743 m
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (2004)

The Pitons are two towering volcanic plugs—Gros Piton and Petit Piton—that rise dramatically from the Caribbean Sea on the southwestern coast of Saint Lucia, near the towns of Soufrière and Choiseul. Linked by the Piton Mitan ridge, they are the island's most famous landmark and appear on its flag, its currency and the label of its local beer.

Geology and ecology

The Pitons are remnants of an eroded volcanic complex and form part of the Soufrière volcanic centre. The surrounding Pitons Management Area, covering some 2,909 hectares of land and sea, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004. The area contains tropical moist forest, coral reefs and geothermal features, and supports a rich diversity of plant and animal life, including many endemic species.

Climbing

Gros Piton (798.25 m) can be climbed with a licensed local guide in a strenuous hike of several hours, with views over the island and across to Martinique and St Vincent. Petit Piton (743 m), though slightly lower, is steeper and more technical, and climbing it is more restricted. The Pitons are also a popular anchorage and diving area, and the town of Soufrière, Saint Lucia's first capital, lies in their shadow.
