Dissolution of the Monasteries

The Dissolution of the Monasteries (1536–1541) was Henry VIII's suppression of England's religious houses. The policy, driven through Parliament by Thomas Cromwell, dissolved over 800 monasteries, nunneries and friaries and transferred their estates to the Crown. Robert Aske and the Pilgrimage of Grace demanded its reversal.

Date1536 – 1541
LegislationActs of 1536 and 1539
AgencyCourt of Augmentations

Background

The monasteries held perhaps a fifth of England's landed wealth. Cromwell's visitations of 1535 collected allegations of corruption, and the Valor Ecclesiasticus measured monastic incomes, enabling the staged suppression that followed – smaller houses first, then the great abbeys, whose abbots in several cases were executed for resisting.

Aftermath

Monastic lands were sold or granted to courtiers and gentry, binding the landed classes to the Reformation settlement. Former religious were pensioned; buildings were stripped for lead and stone, leaving the ruins that still mark the English landscape.
