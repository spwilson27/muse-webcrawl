Henry VIII

Henry VIII (28 June 1491 – 28 January 1547) was King of England from 1509 until his death. He is famous for his six marriages, his break with the Roman Catholic Church, and the English Reformation, of which he made himself Supreme Head of the Church of England. Robert Aske's Pilgrimage of Grace was the greatest domestic challenge to his rule.

Reformation and rule

Seeking annulment of his marriage to Catherine of Aragon, Henry repudiated papal authority; the Acts of Supremacy (1534) declared him head of the English Church. His chief minister Thomas Cromwell then carried through the Dissolution of the Monasteries, provoking the Pilgrimage of Grace in 1536, which Henry first conciliated and then crushed.

Later reign

Henry's reign saw the union with Wales, wars with France and Scotland, the expansion of the navy, and increasingly authoritarian rule marked by the executions of Thomas More, Anne Boleyn and Cromwell himself. He was succeeded by his son Edward VI.
