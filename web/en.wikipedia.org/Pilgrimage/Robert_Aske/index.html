Robert Aske (c. 1500 – 12 July 1537) was an English lawyer who became the leader of the popular rising in York in 1536 known as the Pilgrimage of Grace, a protest against King Henry VIII's break with the Roman Catholic Church, the dissolution of the lesser monasteries and the policies of the king's chief minister, Thomas Cromwell.

The rising

Aske, a barrister of Gray's Inn from a Yorkshire gentry family, was initially seized by the rebels and sworn to their cause, but soon emerged as their "captain". Under his leadership some 30,000–40,000 men occupied York and marched to Doncaster, facing royal forces under the Duke of Norfolk. Aske insisted the rising was a loyal "pilgrimage", petitioning the king rather than making war on him.

Betrayal and death

Norfolk offered a pardon and a promise that Parliament would consider the pilgrims' grievances, and Aske ordered the host to disperse. After a renewed rising by others in 1537, Henry used the breach as a pretext to arrest the leaders. Aske was convicted of high treason and hanged in chains from the walls of York Castle on 12 July 1537.
