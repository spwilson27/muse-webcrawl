Soufrière is a town on the west coast of Saint Lucia in the eastern Caribbean. Founded by the French in 1746, it was the island's first capital before the seat of government moved to Castries. Its name derives from the French for "sulphur", referring to the nearby volcanic springs.

Landmarks

The town sits in the shadow of the Pitons, the twin volcanic plugs of Gros Piton and Petit Piton that dominate the coastline. Nearby attractions include the Sulphur Springs — billed as the world's only drive-in volcano — the Diamond Botanical Gardens and waterfall, and historic sugar estates such as Fond Doux.

History

Soufrière was a prosperous plantation town during the French colonial period and was affected by the revolutionary Brigands' War of the 1790s. Today its economy is centred on tourism, fishing and agriculture.
