Red herring prospectus

A red herring prospectus is a preliminary prospectus filed by a company with a securities regulator, such as the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, in connection with a public offering of securities. It contains most of the information about the company and the offering but omits key details such as the offering price and the number of shares.

Name

The term comes from the bold red warning printed on the cover stating that the document is not final, that the information is subject to completion or amendment, and that the securities may not be sold until the registration statement is effective.

Purpose

The red herring is circulated to gauge investor interest during the waiting period before the registration becomes effective, supporting the "road show" marketing of the issue. Once pricing is set, a final prospectus replaces it.
