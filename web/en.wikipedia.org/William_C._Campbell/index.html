William C. Campbell

William Cecil Campbell (born 28 June 1930) is an Irish parasitologist and biologist known for his work on the discovery of avermectin and its derivative ivermectin, drugs that have dramatically reduced the incidence of river blindness and lymphatic filariasis. He shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Satoshi Ōmura and Tu Youyou.

Career

Born in Ramelton, County Donegal, Ireland, Campbell studied zoology at Trinity College Dublin and earned his doctorate at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. In 1957 he joined the Merck Institute for Therapeutic Research in New Jersey, where he spent his career. Campbell proposed testing bacterial cultures supplied by Ōmura's Kitasato Institute; one, Streptomyces avermitilis, produced a compound extraordinarily potent against parasitic worms. Developed as ivermectin, the drug was introduced for animal health in 1981 and, from 1987, donated by Merck free of charge for river blindness — "as much as needed, for as long as needed" — protecting hundreds of millions of people.

An accomplished artist and poet as well as a scientist, Campbell became a fellow of the National Academy of Sciences and a United States citizen in 1964.
