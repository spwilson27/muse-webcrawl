Wreck of the Titanic

The wreck of Titanic lies at a depth of about 3,800 metres (12,500 ft) in the North Atlantic Ocean, some 600 kilometres (370 mi) south-southeast of Newfoundland, Canada. The ocean liner, which sank on 15 April 1912 after striking an iceberg on her maiden voyage, broke in two during the sinking; the bow and stern sections lie about 600 metres apart on the seabed, surrounded by a debris field.

Discovery

The wreck was located on 1 September 1985 by a joint French-American expedition led by Jean-Louis Michel of IFREMER and Robert Ballard of the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, using the towed camera sled Argo. Ballard visited the site in 1986 aboard the submersible Alvin, landing on the deck and photographing the ship extensively.

Condition

The wreck is deteriorating rapidly owing to corrosion, strong currents, and iron-eating bacteria — one species found consuming the wreck was named Halomonas titanicae. The ship's superstructure has progressively collapsed, and scientists estimate the wreck will eventually disintegrate entirely.

Exploration and salvage

Numerous expeditions have surveyed the site, and thousands of artefacts have been recovered by RMS Titanic Inc., the salvor-in-possession. In 2012 the wreck came under UNESCO protection as an underwater cultural heritage site. Tourist submersible visits ended after the Titan submersible imploded en route to the wreck in June 2023, killing all five aboard.
