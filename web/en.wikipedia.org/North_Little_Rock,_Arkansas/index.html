North Little Rock, Arkansas

North Little Rock is a city in Pulaski County, Arkansas, United States, across the Arkansas River from Little Rock in the central part of the state. As of the 2020 census, the city's population was 64,591, making it the seventh-most populous city in Arkansas. It is part of the Little Rock–North Little Rock–Conway Metropolitan Statistical Area.

History

The community on the north bank of the Arkansas River was originally known as Argenta, named for silver mines once claimed to exist nearby ("argentum" is Latin for silver). It functioned for decades as an unincorporated ward of Little Rock. In 1901 Little Rock annexed the area as its Eighth Ward, prompting local residents to incorporate the separate town of Argenta in 1903. A 1917 court decision and referendum restored the territory as the independent city of North Little Rock.

Geography

The city lies on the north side of the Arkansas River opposite downtown Little Rock, connected by several bridges. Its terrain includes the river floodplain and wooded hills rising to the north. Major waterways include the Arkansas River and Dark Hollow and Five Mile Creek.

Attractions and economy

Landmarks include the Simmons Bank Arena (formerly Verizon Arena), Dickey-Stephens Park (home of the Arkansas Travelers minor league baseball team), Burns Park — one of the largest municipal parks in the United States — and the Old Mill at T. R. Pugh Memorial Park, a replica gristmill famous from the opening scenes of the film Gone with the Wind. The Arkansas National Guard's Camp Joseph T. Robinson occupies a large area in the north of the city. The downtown Argenta Arts District has been redeveloped with galleries, restaurants, and the Argenta Community Theater.
