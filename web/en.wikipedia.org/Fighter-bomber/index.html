Fighter-bomber

A fighter-bomber is a fighter aircraft that has been modified, or used primarily, as a light bomber or attack aircraft. It differs from a dedicated bomber or attack aircraft primarily in its origins as a fighter adapted to the ground-attack role, retaining the speed and agility to defend itself in air combat once its bombs have been dropped.

History

The concept arose during the Second World War, when single-seat fighters such as the Hawker Typhoon, Focke-Wulf Fw 190, Republic P-47 Thunderbolt and Vought F4U Corsair were armed with bombs and rockets for close air support and interdiction. In German service these were called Jagdbomber ("Jabo"). In the Korean War, ageing piston-engined fighter-bombers still bore much of the ground-attack burden, and in Vietnam the F-105 Thunderchief — a supersonic fighter-bomber — flew the bulk of strike missions against North Vietnam.

Modern role

Advances in avionics and precision-guided munitions have made virtually all modern fighters capable strike aircraft, so the distinct fighter-bomber category has largely merged into the multirole fighter concept, exemplified by aircraft such as the F-16 Fighting Falcon and F/A-18 Hornet. Dedicated strike variants, like the F-15E Strike Eagle, continue the fighter-bomber lineage.
