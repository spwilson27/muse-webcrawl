Nightshade

Nightshade most commonly refers to plants of the genus Solanum and more broadly to the family Solanaceae, which includes both deadly species and major food crops. The name is especially associated with deadly nightshade (Atropa belladonna), a highly toxic plant of Europe and Asia.

The family

Solanaceae includes potatoes, tomatoes, aubergines (eggplants), peppers and tobacco, as well as poisonous plants such as belladonna, henbane, mandrake and datura. Many contain alkaloids — atropine, nicotine, capsaicin, solanine — that have served as medicines, poisons, spices and recreational drugs. Bittersweet nightshade (Solanum dulcamara) and black nightshade (Solanum nigrum) are widespread wild species.

Cultural associations

Nightshades figure prominently in folklore and witchcraft traditions, and toxic species were historically used as poisons and in early anaesthesia. The debated edibility of black nightshade berries illustrates the family's fine line between food and poison.
