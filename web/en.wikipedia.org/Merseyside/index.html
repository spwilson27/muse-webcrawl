Merseyside

Merseyside is a ceremonial and metropolitan county in North West England, named after the River Mersey. It was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, from parts of the historic counties of Lancashire and Cheshire on either side of the Mersey estuary. It has a population of about 1.4 million.

Geography and boroughs

The county comprises five metropolitan boroughs: Liverpool, Sefton, Knowsley, St Helens and Wirral. Liverpool, on the east bank of the Mersey, is the dominant city; the Wirral Peninsula lies west of the river, reached by the Mersey Tunnels and the Merseyrail line.

Governance

The Merseyside County Council was abolished in 1986, and most functions passed to the borough councils, with joint boards for fire, police and transport. Since 2014 the Liverpool City Region Combined Authority, headed by an elected metro mayor (first Steve Rotheram, from 2017), has coordinated economic development and transport across Merseyside and Halton.

Culture

The area is famous for Merseybeat music (notably the Beatles), for the football clubs Liverpool FC and Everton FC, and for its maritime heritage as one of Britain's great ports.
