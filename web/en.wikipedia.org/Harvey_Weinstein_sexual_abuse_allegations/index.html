Harvey Weinstein sexual abuse allegations

The Harvey Weinstein sexual abuse allegations are a series of accusations of sexual harassment, sexual assault and rape against American film producer Harvey Weinstein, co-founder of Miramax and The Weinstein Company. Investigations by The New York Times and The New Yorker in October 2017 reported that dozens of women had accused Weinstein of misconduct spanning more than three decades. The revelations helped ignite the worldwide Me Too movement and a broader reckoning over sexual abuse by powerful men, sometimes called the "Weinstein effect".

Background

Weinstein and his brother Bob founded Miramax in 1979 and The Weinstein Company in 2005, producing or distributing acclaimed films including Pulp Fiction, Shakespeare in Love and The King's Speech. Rumours about his treatment of women circulated in Hollywood for years, and earlier reporting attempts were not published. In 2015 the New York Police Department recorded model Ambra Gutierrez confronting Weinstein, who appeared to admit groping her, but no charges followed at the time.

The 2017 investigations

On 5 October 2017, Jodi Kantor and Megan Twohey of The New York Times reported that Weinstein had paid settlements to at least eight women, including actresses Rose McGowan and Ashley Judd. Days later Ronan Farrow's investigation in The New Yorker detailed allegations of rape by three women and a pattern of advances, exposed settlements and intimidation. Weinstein was dismissed by The Weinstein Company's board and expelled from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences.

Accusers and scope

More than eighty women eventually made public allegations, among them Gwyneth Paltrow, Angelina Jolie, Salma Hayek, Lupita Nyong'o, Asia Argento, Annabella Sciorra and Mira Sorvino. Many described a pattern known as the "casting couch": meetings in hotel rooms, requests for massages, and career threats against those who refused or complained. Weinstein denied all allegations of non-consensual sex.

Criminal trials

In 2018 Weinstein was arrested in New York and charged with rape and other offences. His February 2020 trial ended in conviction on two counts; he was sentenced to 23 years in prison. In April 2024 the New York Court of Appeals overturned the conviction, ruling that testimony from women whose allegations were not part of the charges had prejudiced the trial, and ordered a retrial. In a separate 2022 Los Angeles trial he was convicted of rape and sexual assault and sentenced to 16 years, to run consecutively.

Impact

The case catalysed the Me Too movement's global spread, prompted companies to revisit harassment policies, and led to legislation limiting non-disclosure agreements in some jurisdictions. The Weinstein Company filed for bankruptcy in 2018. Kantor, Twohey and Farrow shared the 2018 Pulitzer Prize for Public Service for their reporting.
