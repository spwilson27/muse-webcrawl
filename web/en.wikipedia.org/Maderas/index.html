Maderas (Spanish: Volcán Maderas) is a volcano in Nicaragua, forming the south-eastern half of the island of Ometepe in Lake Nicaragua. At 1,394 metres it is the smaller and older of the island's two volcanoes, the other being the almost perfectly conical and still active Concepción (1,610 m). Unlike Concepción, Maderas has not erupted in historical times and is considered dormant or extinct.

Geology and landscape

Maderas is a heavily eroded stratovolcano whose crater contains a small crater lake, Laguna de Maderas. Its slopes are mantled in cloud forest and coffee plantations, in contrast to the bare ash slopes of its neighbour. The volcano and its forests form part of the Ometepe Biosphere Reserve designated by UNESCO in 2010. A single isthmus, the Istmo de Istián, joins Maderas to Concepción, giving Ometepe its distinctive hourglass shape — often described as the world's largest volcanic island in a freshwater lake.

Nature and tourism

The cloud forest on Maderas supports howler and white-faced capuchin monkeys, numerous bird species and the waterfalls of San Ramón on its southern flank. Hiking to the summit is a popular but demanding activity, typically requiring a local guide, and passes petroglyphs carved by the island's pre-Columbian inhabitants, the Chorotega and Nicarao peoples. Coffee fincas on the lower slopes, such as Finca Magdalena, are run as cooperatives dating from land reforms of the 1980s.
