Henry the Fowler

Henry I

Reign: 919 – 2 July 936

Predecessor: Conrad I

Successor: Otto I

Born: c. 876, Memleben, East Francia

Died: 2 July 936, Memleben

House: Ottonian

Henry receives the royal insignia, according to legend while fowling

Henry I, known as Henry the Fowler (German: Heinrich der Vogler or Heinrich der Finkler; c. 876 – 2 July 936), was Duke of Saxony from 912 and King of East Francia (Germany) from 919 until his death. The first non-Frankish king of East Francia and founder of the Ottonian dynasty, he is generally considered the founder of the medieval German state.

Accession

According to tradition, Henry was catching birds (fowling) at Seehausen when messengers brought him the royal insignia after his election at Fritzlar in 919, succeeding Conrad I. Unlike his predecessors he declined anointing, presenting himself as first among equals among the dukes of Saxony, Franconia, Swabia and Bavaria, whose allegiance he secured by agreement rather than subjugation.

Reign

Henry strengthened the realm against external enemies. He bought a nine-year truce from the Magyars in 924, using the interval to build fortified burghs (the Burgenordnung), train a mounted army, and reorganise military service. When the truce lapsed he defeated the Magyars decisively at the Battle of Riade (933). He also campaigned against the Slavs east of the Elbe, storming Brandenburg in 928/929, and compelled Bohemia's submission, laying foundations his son Otto I would build on. He secured the succession for Otto before dying at Memleben in 936; he is buried at Quedlinburg Abbey.
