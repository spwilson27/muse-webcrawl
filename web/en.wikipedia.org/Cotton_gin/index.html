Cotton gin

A cotton gin, short for cotton engine, is a machine that quickly and easily separates cotton fibers from their seeds, enabling much greater productivity than manual separation.

History

Roller gins were used in India for centuries. In 1793 Eli Whitney patented a saw gin in the United States that used wire teeth and brushes to pull lint through a grid. The invention made short-staple cotton profitable, greatly expanding cotton production in the American South and, historically, increasing demand for enslaved labour. Later gins include the McCarthy gin for long-staple cotton.

Modern gins

Modern cotton ginning plants clean, dry, separate, and bale lint using series of saws, air jets and presses.
