Street performance

Street performance or busking is the act of performing in public places for gratuities. In many countries, the rewards are generally in the form of money but other gratuities such as food, drink or gifts may be given. Street performance is practiced all over the world and dates back to antiquity. People engaging in this practice are called street performers or buskers. Though busking originated with wandering minstrels and other itinerant entertainers, not all performers who perform for gratuities are buskers, as formal events, festivals and catered entertainment also have gratuities.

Forms

Common forms of street performance include music (solo musicians, bands, one-man bands), juggling and circus skills, magic (especially close-up and card tricks), living statues, street theatre, portrait and caricature drawing, puppetry, dance and comedy. Performances may be "circle shows", which gather a crowd for a set routine ending with a request for donations (often called "passing the hat"), or "walk-by" acts in which the performer entertains passers-by continuously without formally stopping them.

Legality and culture

The legal status of busking varies widely: many cities require permits or designate pitches, and some regulate amplified sound or specific locations such as subway systems. Famous busking locations include Covent Garden in London, the Paris Métro, New York City subway stations and Faneuil Hall in Boston. Some performers, including Ed Sheeran, began their careers busking, while festivals such as the Edinburgh Festival Fringe have elevated street performance into a major art form. During the COVID-19 pandemic many street performers moved shows online.
