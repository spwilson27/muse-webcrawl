Stanisław II August, born Stanisław Antoni Poniatowski (17 January 1732 – 12 February 1798), was the last King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania, reigning from 1764 until his abdication in 1795 after the Third Partition of Poland ended the Commonwealth's existence.

Rise

A member of the powerful Czartoryski "Familia", Poniatowski served as a diplomat in St Petersburg, where he became a lover of the future Empress Catherine II. With Russian support he was elected king in 1764, initially intending a programme of reform to strengthen the dysfunctional Commonwealth.

Reforms and culture

His reign saw the founding of the Corps of Cadets, the Commission of National Education (1773) — Europe's first education ministry — and a brilliant cultural flowering in Warsaw, including the king's "Thursday Dinners" for artists and thinkers. The Four-Year Sejm adopted the Constitution of 3 May 1791, Europe's first modern codified national constitution, abolishing the liberum veto and hereditary-success reforms.

Partitions and abdication

Reform provoked Russian intervention: the Targowica Confederation and the war of 1792 led to the Second Partition (1793). The Kościuszko Uprising of 1794 failed, and the Third Partition (1795) erased Poland from the map. Stanisław abdicated on 25 November 1795 and lived out his life in Russia, dying in St Petersburg in 1798, burdened with debts and historical controversy over his dependence on Catherine.
