Charles XII, sometimes Carl XII (Swedish: Karl XII) or Carolus Rex (17 June 1682 – 30 November 1718), was King of Sweden from 1697 to 1718. He belonged to the House of Palatinate-Zweibrücken, a branch of the House of Wittelsbach. During his reign he led Sweden through the Great Northern War, spending most of it campaigning abroad, and his death in battle ended Sweden's era as a great power.


Charles XII, the warrior king whose reign ended the Swedish Empire.

Early reign and the Great Northern War

Charles succeeded his father Charles XI at fourteen and was declared of age in 1697. In 1700 Denmark–Norway, Saxony-Poland, and Russia attacked Sweden in the Great Northern War, judging the young king inexperienced. Charles responded with a brilliant series of campaigns: he knocked Denmark out of the war by the Peace of Travendal (1700), destroyed a much larger Russian army at Narva (1700), and spent six years defeating Augustus II of Poland-Saxony, imposing the Treaty of Altranstädt (1706).

Invasion of Russia and Poltava

In 1708 Charles invaded Russia, aiming at Moscow. Scorched-earth tactics, supply failure, and the terrible winter of 1708–09 crippled his army; his supply column was destroyed at Lesnaya. On 8 July 1709 the Swedes were decisively beaten by Peter the Great at the Battle of Poltava, the turning point of the war. Charles fled south into Ottoman exile at Bender, where he spent years trying to provoke an Ottoman war on Russia.

Return and death

Charles returned to Swedish territory in 1714 after a famous fifteen-day ride across Europe. He rebuilt his forces and invaded Norway twice. During the siege of Fredriksten on 30 November 1718 he was killed by a projectile while inspecting the trenches — whether from enemy fire or assassination has been debated ever since.

Legacy

Charles never married and left no children. His absolutist rule and death without an heir led Sweden's parliament to reassert power, inaugurating the Age of Liberty. Admired by contemporaries such as Voltaire, who wrote his biography, Charles remains a controversial figure: a military genius of extraordinary personal courage whose refusal to compromise destroyed the Swedish Empire his ancestors had built.
