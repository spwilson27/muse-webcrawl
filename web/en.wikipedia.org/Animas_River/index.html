Animas River

The Animas River (Spanish: Río de las Ánimas, "River of Souls") is a 126-mile (203 km) tributary of the San Juan River in the southwestern United States. It rises in the San Juan Mountains of Colorado and flows south through Durango and into New Mexico, joining the San Juan at Farmington.

Course

The river begins near the ghost town of Animas Forks in San Juan County, Colorado, fed by its North and West Forks amid peaks over 13,000 feet. It descends through the Animas Canyon, paralleled by the Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad, passes through Durango, crosses into New Mexico near Cedar Hill, and meanders across the Colorado Plateau to its confluence with the San Juan River at Farmington.

History

The full Spanish name, El Río de las Ánimas Perdidas en Purgatorio — "the River of the Souls Lost in Purgatory" — is traditionally said to commemorate members of a Spanish expedition lost on the river. Mining in the upper basin from the 1870s left a legacy of acid mine drainage from hundreds of abandoned mines around Silverton.

Gold King Mine spill

On 5 August 2015, contractors working for the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency accidentally breached the Gold King Mine near Silverton, releasing about three million gallons of acidic, metal-laden wastewater that turned the river bright orange-yellow. The plume travelled through Colorado into New Mexico and the Navajo Nation, prompting emergency declarations, temporary drinking-water precautions, and years of litigation and cleanup. In 2016 the upper basin was designated a Superfund site (Bonita Peak Mining District).

Recreation

The Animas is a popular whitewater river, with rafting and kayaking through Durango's whitewater park, and a gold-medal trout fishery on its lower stretches. The Animas River Trail follows the river through Durango.
