Rodeo Drive is a street in Beverly Hills, California, whose three-block shopping district is one of the most famous luxury retail destinations in the world, lined with flagship boutiques of global fashion houses.

[Image placeholder: Palm-lined Rodeo Drive with luxury boutiques]

History

The street's name comes from the Rancho Rodeo de las Aguas, the Mexican land grant that covered the area. Rodeo Drive was a residential street — and briefly a bridle path — until the 20th century. Giorgio Beverly Hills opened in 1961, and through the 1970s, as brands such as Gucci, Hermès and Cartier arrived, the street transformed into an international symbol of luxury. In 1976 Fred Hayman's Giorgio building, and in 1990 the Two Rodeo Drive complex — a cobbled, European-style shopping arcade — cemented its image.

The street today

The Golden Triangle, bounded by Rodeo Drive, Canon Drive and Brighton Way, contains flagship stores for Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Prada, Tiffany & Co., Harry Winston and many others, alongside the Beverly Wilshire Hotel at the Wilshire Boulevard end. The annual Rodeo Drive Concours d'Elegance displays classic cars each Father's Day. The street's cinematic shorthand for wealth — memorably in the film Pretty Woman (1990) — attracts millions of tourists, though most visitors window-shop rather than buy.
