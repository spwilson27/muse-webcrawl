Climate change adaptation

Climate change adaptation is the process of adjusting to current or expected effects of climate change. Alongside mitigation — reducing greenhouse gas emissions — adaptation is one of the two main policy responses to global warming. Its goal is to reduce the vulnerability of people, ecosystems and infrastructure to impacts such as sea level rise, heatwaves, droughts, floods and shifting rainfall.

Types of adaptation

Adaptation measures include building flood defences and sea walls, developing drought-resistant crops, improving water storage and irrigation, designing heat action plans for cities, restoring mangroves and wetlands as natural buffers, and relocating communities from areas of unavoidable risk. Adaptations can be incremental, adjusting existing practices, or transformational, changing fundamental attributes of a system. "Ecosystem-based adaptation" uses biodiversity and natural processes rather than hard engineering.

Governance and finance

Under the Paris Agreement, countries committed to a global goal on adaptation and prepare national adaptation plans. Adaptation finance — through funds such as the Green Climate Fund and the Adaptation Fund — remains far below estimated needs, especially in developing countries that are least responsible for emissions but most exposed to harm. The IPCC warns of "hard limits" to adaptation: beyond certain levels of warming, some systems, such as low-lying island nations and tropical coral reefs, cannot adapt at any cost.

Maladaptation

Poorly designed responses can backfire — for example, air-conditioning that raises emissions, or irrigation that depletes aquifers — a phenomenon called maladaptation.
