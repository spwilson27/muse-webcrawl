Territoire de Belfort

The Territoire de Belfort (French pronunciation: [tɛʁitwaʁ də bɛlfɔʁ]) is a department in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region of northeastern France. It is the fifth-smallest French department by area and is named after its prefecture, Belfort. Its INSEE and postcode number is 90.

History

The department has an unusual origin. Before 1871 the area around Belfort formed part of the department of Haut-Rhin in Alsace. Under the Treaty of Frankfurt ending the Franco-Prussian War, most of Alsace was annexed by the German Empire, but the arrondissement of Belfort — which had resisted a Prussian siege under Colonel Denfert-Rochereau — remained French. Because it did not correspond to any former department, it was administered as a "territory" and only became a full department in 1922, receiving the number 90. The Lion of Belfort, a monumental red sandstone sculpture by Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi, commemorates the siege of 1870–71.

Geography

The department lies in the Belfort Gap, the low corridor between the Vosges mountains to the north and the Jura to the south — a historic invasion route between the Rhine and the Rhône. It borders the departments of Haute-Saône, Doubs and Haut-Rhin, and the Swiss canton of Jura. The Savoureuse river flows through Belfort city. The Eurockéennes music festival is held each summer on the Malsaucy peninsula.
