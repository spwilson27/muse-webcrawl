Arctic fox

Scientific name: Vulpes lagopus
Family: Canidae
Order: Carnivora
Range: Arctic regions of the Northern Hemisphere
Status: Least Concern (IUCN), some populations endangered

The Arctic fox (Vulpes lagopus), also known as the white fox, polar fox, or snow fox, is a small fox native to the Arctic regions of the Northern Hemisphere and common throughout the Arctic tundra biome. It is well adapted to living in cold environments and is best known for its thick, warm fur, which changes colour with the seasons for camouflage.

Description

Arctic foxes measure about 46–68 cm in body length with a tail of roughly 30 cm, and weigh 3–5 kg on average. They have short, rounded ears, a short muzzle, and fur-covered soles — adaptations that reduce heat loss. Two colour morphs exist: the white morph, white in winter and brownish-grey in summer, and the rarer blue morph, which remains dark year-round and is most common on coasts and islands.

Behaviour and diet

The Arctic fox is an opportunistic feeder. Lemmings form the staple of its diet where available, and fox numbers rise and fall with lemming cycles; it also eats voles, birds and their eggs, fish, carrion, and seal pups, and follows polar bears to scavenge their kills. Food is cached in the ground for later use. Arctic foxes are generally solitary outside the breeding season, when pairs raise litters that can exceed ten pups, among the largest of any canid relative to body size.

Distribution and status

The species has a circumpolar distribution across North America, Greenland, Iceland, Svalbard, and northern Eurasia. Globally it is classified as Least Concern, but isolated populations in Fennoscandia declined drastically owing to fur hunting and competition with the larger red fox, and have been the focus of captive breeding and reintroduction efforts.

Adaptations

The Arctic fox has the warmest pelt of any Arctic mammal and can maintain its body temperature at ambient temperatures as low as −50 °C by virtue of dense fur, countercurrent heat exchange in the legs, and a compact body shape. Its hearing is acute enough to locate prey moving beneath snow, whereupon it dives headfirst to catch it.
