The baritone horn, sometimes known as the baritone, is a low-pitched brass instrument in the saxhorn family. It is pitched in B♭, one octave below the B♭ trumpet, and reads music in treble clef (transposing) or bass clef. The baritone is a staple of brass bands and concert bands, where it fills the tenor register of the ensemble.

Description

The baritone has a predominantly cylindrical bore, like the trombone, which gives it a brighter, more direct sound than the conical-bored euphonium, with which it shares range and much repertoire. It is sometimes called the "American baritone" in its bell-forward marching form. Most instruments have three piston valves; unlike the euphonium, a compensating fourth valve is uncommon.

Confusion with the euphonium

The terms "baritone" and "euphonium" are frequently used interchangeably, especially in the United States, where many school instruments labelled baritone are in fact small-bore euphoniums. In British brass-band usage the distinction is strict: the baritone is a separate, lighter-toned instrument with its own parts, distinct from both the tenor horn above it and the euphonium below.

Repertoire

Baritones double or share parts with trombones and euphoniums in concert bands, and hold an independent section in brass bands, where solo works and featured passages are common in contest repertoire.
