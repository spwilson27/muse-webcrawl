Snow Leopard Award

The Snow Leopard Award (Russian: Снежный барс) is a prestigious mountaineering award given to climbers who have successfully ascended all five peaks of 7,000 metres or higher in the mountains of the former Soviet Union. The award takes its name from the snow leopard, a big cat native to the high mountain ranges of Central Asia.

The five peaks

The five peaks that must be climbed to earn the award are:

  • Ismoil Somoni Peak (7,495 m), formerly Communism Peak, in the Pamir Mountains of Tajikistan — the highest of the five.
  • Jengish Chokusu (7,439 m), formerly Peak Pobeda (Victory Peak), in the Tian Shan on the border of Kyrgyzstan and China.
  • Ibn Sina Peak (7,134 m), formerly Lenin Peak, on the border of Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan.
  • Peak Korzhenevskaya (7,105 m) in the Pamirs of Tajikistan.
  • Khan Tengri (7,010 m) in the Tian Shan on the borders of Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and China.

History

The award was established in the Soviet Union in 1967. Climbers who completed all five summits were given the title "Conqueror of the Highest Peaks of the USSR," though the honorary name "Snow Leopard" became the popular designation for those who held it. After the dissolution of the Soviet Union, the award continued to be administered by mountaineering federations of the successor states, and it remains one of the most respected achievements in high-altitude mountaineering.

Notable recipients

The Polish climber Jerzy Kukuczka, one of the greatest Himalayan mountaineers of all time, was among the early recipients. The Kazakh climber Denis Urubko also completed the five peaks before going on to climb all fourteen eight-thousanders. As of the early 21st century, more than six hundred climbers had earned the award.
