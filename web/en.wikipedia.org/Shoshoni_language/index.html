Shoshoni language

Native to: Western United States (Wyoming, Idaho, Nevada, Utah)
Family: Uto-Aztecan, Numic
Speakers: A few thousand
Status: Endangered

Shoshoni, also written Shoshone, is a Uto-Aztecan language of the Numic branch spoken by the Shoshone people in the Great Basin and western Plains regions of the United States, in parts of Wyoming, Idaho, Nevada and Utah. It is closely related to Comanche, from which it diverged a few centuries ago.

Characteristics

Shoshoni is an agglutinative language with complex verb morphology. Its phonology features a vowel system with long vowels and a series of stops that alternate in predictable ways (consonant gradation) depending on their morphological environment — a classic feature studied by linguists of Numic languages.

Status and revitalisation

The number of fluent speakers has declined to a few thousand, mostly older adults, and the language is considered endangered. Tribal programmes, school classes, dictionaries and digital resources developed with linguists aim to teach Shoshoni to new generations of speakers.
