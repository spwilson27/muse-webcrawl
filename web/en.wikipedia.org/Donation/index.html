Donation

A donation is a gift for charity, humanitarian aid, or to benefit a cause. A donation may take various forms, including money, alms, services, or goods such as clothing, toys, food, or vehicles. A donation may satisfy medical needs such as blood or organs for transplant.

Charitable donations of goods or services are also called gifts in kind.

Law

In most legal systems, a donation is a voluntary transfer of property from one person (the donor) to another (the donee) without consideration. For a donation to be legally effective, the donor must intend to give, the donee must accept, and the property must be delivered. Donations made in contemplation of death (donatio mortis causa) may be revocable if the donor survives.

In many countries, donations to registered charities qualify the donor for tax deductions or credits, a policy intended to encourage private giving to public-benefit organizations. Rules generally require the recipient organization to hold charitable status and the donor to keep records of the gift.

Types

  • Monetary donations — cash, cheques, or electronic transfers.
  • Gifts in kind — goods or services given instead of money.
  • Blood donation — voluntary provision of blood for transfusion.
  • Organ donation — provision of organs for transplant, during life or after death.
  • Anonymous donations — gifts made without revealing the donor's identity.

Effectiveness

The effectiveness of charitable donations has become a subject of research, notably by the effective altruism movement, which evaluates charities by the measurable benefit produced per unit of money given. Charity evaluators such as GiveWell attempt to identify organizations that deliver unusually large benefits.
