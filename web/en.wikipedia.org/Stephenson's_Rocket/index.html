Rocket, winner of the 1829 Rainhill Trials.

Rocket was an early steam locomotive of 0-2-2 wheel arrangement, designed by George Stephenson, his son Robert Stephenson and Henry Booth for the Rainhill Trials held by the Liverpool and Manchester Railway in October 1829. Rocket won the trials, demonstrating a sustained speed and reliability far beyond its rivals, and its basic design became the template for steam locomotives for the next 150 years.

Design

Rocket's innovations included a multi-tubular boiler with 25 copper fire tubes, which greatly increased heating surface and steam generation; a blastpipe that used exhaust steam to draw the fire; and cylinders set at an angle, directly driving the large front wheels — later locomotives used near-horizontal cylinders. Its firebox was separately constructed and jacketed with water. During the trials Rocket averaged about 12 mph (19 km/h) hauling a load and reached around 30 mph (48 km/h) in lighter runs.

Service and preservation

Rocket worked on the Liverpool and Manchester Railway from its opening in 1830 — it hauled the train involved in the fatal accident to MP William Huskisson at the opening ceremony. After later service in Cumberland its design was heavily modified, and it was presented to the Patent Museum (now the Science Museum) in London in 1862, where it survives in altered form. A working replica built in 1979 is displayed at the National Railway Museum, York.
