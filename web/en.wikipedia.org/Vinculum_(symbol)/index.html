Vinculum (symbol)

A vinculum (from Latin vinculum, "fetter" or "tie") is a horizontal line used in mathematical notation for a specific purpose. It may be placed over an expression to indicate that the expression is to be considered grouped together. Today, however, the practice of grouping expressions with a vinculum has largely been replaced by the use of parentheses, although the vinculum survives in several specific roles.

Uses

Surviving uses include: the bar over a repeating decimal period (e.g. 0.123̅ indicating 0.123123123…); the radical symbol's horizontal bar over the radicand in a square root; the notation for a line segment AB in geometry; the conjugate of a complex number; and, in Roman numerals, a bar multiplying a numeral's value by 1,000. In older arithmetic texts the vinculum grouped terms much as brackets do now, and in division the "bus stop" bracket remains common in British primary education.

History

The vinculum was introduced by Frans van Schooten in the 17th century in his edition of Descartes's La Géométrie, though grouping lines had earlier precedents. Cajori's A History of Mathematical Notations documents its widespread use before parentheses became standard.
