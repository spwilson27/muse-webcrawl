Bijapur

Bijapur (Vijayapura)
State: Karnataka, India
Officially renamed: Vijayapura (2014)
Known for: Gol Gumbaz

Bijapur, officially known as Vijayapura, is a city in the Indian state of Karnataka. It was the capital of the Bijapur Sultanate under the Adil Shahi dynasty from 1490 to 1686 and is renowned for its monuments of Indo-Islamic architecture.

History

The city was founded in the 10th–11th centuries by the Kalyani Chalukyas as Vijayapura, "city of victory". It passed through the hands of the Yadavas, the Khalji and Tughlaq Delhi Sultanate, and the Bahmani Sultanate before Yusuf Adil Shah founded the independent Bijapur Sultanate in 1490. The sultanate flourished as a centre of Deccani culture until Aurangzeb annexed it to the Mughal Empire in 1686. Later ruled by the Marathas, the British, and Hyderabad, the city joined Mysore State (now Karnataka) in 1956.

Monuments

The Gol Gumbaz, the mausoleum of Mohammed Adil Shah completed in 1656, has one of the largest domes built before the modern era and a famous whispering gallery. Other monuments include the Ibrahim Rauza tomb complex, the Jama Masjid, the Malik-e-Maidan cannon, and the citadel walls and gateways.

Geography and economy

Bijapur lies on the Deccan plateau in a semi-arid region; agriculture in the district includes grapes, pomegranates, and cereals, aided by irrigation from the Krishna and Bhima rivers. The city is a district headquarters and a stop on the Solapur–Hubli railway.
