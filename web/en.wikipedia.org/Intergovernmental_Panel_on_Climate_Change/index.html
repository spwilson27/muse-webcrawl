The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) is an intergovernmental body of the United Nations. Its job is to advance scientific knowledge about climate change caused by human activities. The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) and the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) established the IPCC in 1988. The United Nations endorsed the creation of the IPCC later that year. It has 195 member states.

Role and assessments

The IPCC does not conduct original research or monitor climate data itself. Instead, thousands of volunteer scientists and other experts review the published scientific literature to draw conclusions about the state of knowledge on climate change. Its periodic Assessment Reports — published in 1990, 1995, 2001, 2007, 2013–2014 (Fifth Assessment Report), and 2021–2023 (Sixth Assessment Report) — are considered the most authoritative scientific assessments of climate change and inform international policy, including negotiations under the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change.

Work is organised into three working groups: Working Group I (the physical science basis), Working Group II (impacts, adaptation and vulnerability), and Working Group III (mitigation), plus a Task Force on National Greenhouse Gas Inventories. Key findings of the Sixth Assessment Report include that it is unequivocal that human influence has warmed the atmosphere, ocean, and land; that global surface temperature increased by about 1.1 °C between 1850–1900 and 2010–2019; and that limiting warming to 1.5 °C requires deep, rapid emission reductions.

Special reports

In addition to assessment reports, the IPCC produces special reports, including the 2018 Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5 °C, and 2019 reports on climate change and land, and on the ocean and cryosphere.

Nobel Peace Prize

In 2007 the IPCC shared the Nobel Peace Prize with former US Vice President Al Gore "for their efforts to build up and disseminate greater knowledge about man-made climate change, and to lay the foundations for the measures that are needed to counteract such change."

Governance

The Panel elects a Bureau including its Chair; Hoesung Lee of South Korea chaired the IPCC from 2015 to 2023, succeeded by Jim Skea of the United Kingdom in July 2023. Summaries for Policymakers are approved line by line by member governments, while the underlying reports remain the responsibility of the scientist authors.
