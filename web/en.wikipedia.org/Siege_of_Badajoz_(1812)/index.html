Siege of Badajoz (1812)

Date16 March – 6 April 1812
LocationBadajoz, Spain
ResultAnglo-Portuguese victory
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom, Portugal vs. French Empire
CommandersArthur Wellesley, Earl of Wellington vs. Armand Philippon

The Siege of Badajoz (16 March – 6 April 1812), also called the Third Siege of Badajoz, was an Anglo-Portuguese Army under the Earl of Wellington (later Duke of Wellington) besieging Badajoz, Spain, and forcing the surrender of the French garrison. The siege was one of the bloodiest in the Napoleonic Wars and was considered a costly success by the British, with some 4,800 Allied troops killed or wounded in just a few short hours of intense fighting during the storming of the breaches as the siege drew to an end.

Background

Having captured Ciudad Rodrigo earlier in 1812, Wellington moved to take Badajoz, the second of the great frontier fortresses guarding the invasion routes between Spain and Portugal. The French garrison of some 5,000 men under General Armand Philippon strengthened the defences, while Wellington's engineers dug siege parallels under heavy fire and spring rains flooded the trenches.

The storm

On the night of 6 April 1812, columns of the Light Division and the 4th Division assaulted the breaches in the walls at the Trinidad and Santa Maria bastions, while diversionary attacks were made against the castle and the San Vicente bastion. The fighting in the breaches was exceptionally savage; thousands fell before ladders were raised against the castle by troops under Thomas Picton, whose success turned the defence. Philippon surrendered the following morning.

Aftermath

After the storm, British and Portuguese troops sacked the city for up to three days, in one of the most notorious episodes of indiscipline of the Peninsular War. Wellington was nevertheless free thereafter to advance into Spain, leading to the victory at Salamanca later that year.
