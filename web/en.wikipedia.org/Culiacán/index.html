Culiacán

Culiacán, officially Culiacán Rosales, is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Sinaloa and the seat of the municipality of the same name. Located in northwestern Mexico at the confluence of the Tamazula and Humaya rivers, which form the Culiacán River, it is a major agricultural centre, sometimes called Mexico's largest garden for its production of tomatoes and other vegetables for export.

History

The city was founded on 29 September 1531 by the conquistador Nuño Beltrán de Guzmán as the Villa de San Miguel. It later served as a staging point for expeditions to the north, including that of Francisco Vásquez de Coronado. The name honours General Antonio Rosales, a 19th-century governor.

Modern issues

Culiacán is strongly associated with the Sinaloa Cartel, one of Mexico's most powerful drug-trafficking organizations, and has periodically experienced major violence, including the October 2019 clashes known as the Culiacanazo and further unrest in 2023 following the arrest of cartel leader Ovidio Guzmán.
