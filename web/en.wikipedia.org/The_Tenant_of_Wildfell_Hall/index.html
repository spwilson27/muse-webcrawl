The Tenant of Wildfell Hall

The Tenant of Wildfell Hall is the second and final novel by English author Anne Brontë, published in June 1848 under the pseudonym Acton Bell. It tells, in an epistolary frame, the story of Helen Graham, a mysterious widow who flees her drunken, abusive husband to live independently with her young son — a plot regarded as one of the most radical in Victorian literature.

Plot

The farmer Gilbert Markham narrates the arrival of the widowed Mrs Graham at the decaying Wildfell Hall and the gossip her reserve provokes. Her diary, which forms the novel's core, reveals she is Helen Huntingdon, who endured years of her husband Arthur's alcoholism and cruelty before escaping to protect her son — an act that defied both law and custom, since a wife had no legal right to leave or to keep her child or earnings.

Reception and themes

The novel was an immediate success but shocked reviewers with its unflinching depiction of alcoholism and its heroine's defiance. After Anne's death in 1849, her sister Charlotte prevented republication, and the book was long overshadowed. Modern critics regard it as an early feminist classic, notable for its realism, moral seriousness, and structural daring.
