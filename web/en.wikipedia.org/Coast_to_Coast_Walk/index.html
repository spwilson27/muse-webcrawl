Coast to Coast Walk

Length: approx. 192 miles (309 km)
Start: St Bees, Cumbria
Finish: Robin Hood's Bay, North Yorkshire
Devised by: Alfred Wainwright (1973)

The Coast to Coast Walk is a long-distance footpath in northern England that crosses the country from St Bees on the Irish Sea to Robin Hood's Bay on the North Sea. Unofficial and not originally a designated National Trail, the route was devised by the fell-walker and guidebook author Alfred Wainwright, who published it in his 1973 book A Coast to Coast Walk. It is commonly walked in 12 to 16 days and passes through three national parks: the Lake District, the Yorkshire Dales, and the North York Moors. In 2022 the route was announced for adoption as a National Trail, with works to complete the designation following later in the decade.

Route

By convention, walkers dip their boots in the Irish Sea at St Bees and climb St Bees Head before turning inland through the western Lakeland valleys of Ennerdale and Borrowdale, over the high passes around Helvellyn or via Grasmere, and on to Patterdale and Shap. The route then crosses the limestone country of the Westmorland Plateau to Kirkby Stephen, climbs over the watershed at Nine Standards Rigg, and descends Swaledale through Keld and Reeth to Richmond. Crossing the Vale of Mowbray, the path enters the North York Moors at Ingleby Cross, traverses the Cleveland Hills, and crosses the moors via the Wain Stones, Urra Moor, and Blakey Ridge before descending through Glaisdale and Egton Bridge to the coast at Robin Hood's Bay, where walkers dip their boots in the North Sea and traditionally throw a pebble carried from the start.

Wainwright's intentions

Wainwright intended the book as a "do-it-yourself" framework rather than a fixed path, encouraging walkers to devise their own variations between the two coasts; he proposed targets, such as staying off tarmac roads as much as possible, and the famous exhortation that "one should always have a definite objective, in a walk, as in life — it is so much more satisfying to reach a target by personal effort than to wander aimlessly." A tradition holds that the walk be completed by picking up a pebble at St Bees and depositing it at Robin Hood's Bay.

Popularity

The walk is among the most popular long-distance routes in Britain and has been ranked among the world's best walks by outdoor writers; Julia Bradbury's television series Wainwright Walks: Coast to Coast (2009) renewed its popularity. A network of baggage-transfer companies, shuttle services, and walker-oriented accommodation supports the route's villages each season.
