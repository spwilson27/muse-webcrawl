The Palestinian National Authority (PA or PNA; Arabic: السلطة الوطنية الفلسطينية) is the Fatah-controlled interim self-government body created in 1994 following the Oslo Accords to administer parts of the West Bank and, until 2007, the Gaza Strip. It is distinct from the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO), the internationally recognised representative of the Palestinian people that negotiated the accords and created the PA.

History

The PA was established under the 1994 Gaza–Jericho Agreement and expanded by the 1995 Oslo II Accord, which divided the West Bank into Area A (full Palestinian civil and security control), Area B (Palestinian civil control, joint security) and Area C (full Israeli control). Yasser Arafat became its first president in 1994 and won the 1996 general election. Following the death of Arafat in 2004, Mahmoud Abbas was elected president in 2005.

2006–2007 split

In the 2006 legislative elections, Hamas won a majority in the Palestinian Legislative Council, leading to a power struggle with Fatah. In June 2007, after the Battle of Gaza, Hamas seized control of the Gaza Strip, and Abbas dismissed the Hamas-led government and appointed an emergency government in the West Bank. Since then the PA has governed only the West Bank enclaves, while Hamas administers Gaza — a political division that repeated reconciliation attempts have failed to end.

Statehood bid

In November 2012 the UN General Assembly upgraded Palestine to non-member observer state status. Thereafter the PA began using the name "State of Palestine" on official documents and stamps, though its effective authority remains limited by the occupation and the Oslo framework. The PA depends heavily on foreign aid and on clearance revenues collected by Israel on its behalf.
