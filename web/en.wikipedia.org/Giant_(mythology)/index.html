Giant (mythology)

In folklore and mythology, a giant is a humanoid being of enormous size and strength, often portrayed as monstrous, primitive or opposed to gods and humans. Giants appear in the mythologies of cultures worldwide, from the Titans and Gigantes of Greek myth and the jötnar of Norse tradition to the Nephilim of the Hebrew Bible and the giants of British and Celtic folklore.

[Image: Gustave Doré's engraving of the giant Gargantua]

In world mythologies

Greek mythology features the Gigantes, offspring of Gaia who fought the Olympian gods in the Gigantomachy, and earlier generations of Titans and the one-eyed Cyclopes. Norse mythology is populated by the jötnar (singular jötunn) of Jötunheimr, beings of great size and power who are frequent adversaries — and occasional in-laws — of the gods, destined to fight them at Ragnarök. In the Hebrew Bible, the Nephilim and figures such as Og of Bashan, and most famously Goliath of Gath, champion of the Philistines slain by David, represent giant races and individuals.

Folklore and literature

Giants are central to European fairy tales — the ogre of "Jack and the Beanstalk", the giants of Cornish and Welsh legend, and the creations of Rabelais (Gargantua and Pantagruel) and Swift (the Brobdingnagians of Gulliver's Travels). Interpretations range from euhemeristic memories of extinct peoples or megafauna to explanations of fossil bones attributed to giants in pre-modern times.
