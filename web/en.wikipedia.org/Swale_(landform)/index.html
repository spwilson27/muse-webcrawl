Swale (landform)

A swale is a low-lying or shallow channel or depression in the land, natural or constructed, that manages water runoff, filters pollutants and increases rainwater infiltration. The word is of Scandinavian origin and is also used for a moist, low tract of land, particularly in chalk downland landscapes.

Natural swales

In geomorphology, a swale is a shallow trough between ridges or rises, such as the low ground between beach ridges on a prograding coastline, or the gentle depressions between dunes. Chalk downland swales collect finer soil and moisture, supporting lusher vegetation than the surrounding slopes.

Constructed swales

In sustainable drainage design, grassed swales are shallow, vegetated channels built to convey surface water while slowing its flow, trapping sediment and encouraging infiltration into the soil. A bioswale adds engineered soil media and dense planting to maximise pollutant removal, and swales are a standard component of low-impact development and sustainable water management.

Permaculture use

In permaculture, swales are dug on contour — level ditches with a downhill berm — to harvest rainwater, halt erosion and recharge groundwater, often planted with trees on the berm. Unlike drainage channels, contour swales are designed to hold water in the landscape rather than shed it.
