Rhythm

Rhythm (from Greek ῥυθμός, rhythmos, "any regular recurring motion, symmetry") generally means a "movement marked by the regulated succession of strong and weak elements, or of opposite or different conditions". This general meaning of regular recurrence or pattern in time can apply to a wide variety of cyclical natural phenomena having a periodicity or frequency of anything from microseconds to several seconds (as with the biorhythms) to several minutes or hours, or even many years.

Rhythm in music

In music, rhythm is the placement of sounds in time. In its most general sense, rhythm (Greek rhythmos, derived from ῥεῖν, rhein, "to flow") is an ordered, recurrent alternation of contrasting elements. The term is closely associated with the related concepts of metre, pulse, and beat. The study of rhythm, metre, and tempo is part of music theory.

Rhythm is one of the fundamental dimensions of music, alongside melody and harmony. All musicians, whether they articulate it explicitly or not, deal with rhythm; in many musical traditions, especially in West African and Latin American music, rhythm is the dominant organising element. Techniques such as syncopation (accents on weak beats), polyrhythm (simultaneous contrasting metres), and additive rhythm (patterns built from unequal groups of beats) are used in many genres.

Terminology

  • Pulse – a series of uniformly spaced beats that underlie the rhythm.
  • Beat – the basic unit of time, the pulse of the mensuration.
  • Metre – the grouping of beats into recurring measures, indicated by a time signature.
  • Tempo – the speed at which the beats pass.
  • Syncopation – a disturbance or interruption of the regular flow of rhythm, placing emphasis where it would not normally occur.
  • Groove – the sense of propulsive rhythmic "feel" in popular music.

Rhythm in other fields

In poetry, rhythm is the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables; in dance, it is the timing of movements to music. In the natural sciences, biological rhythms include circadian rhythms governing sleep and activity. In visual art and architecture, rhythm refers to the regular repetition of elements such as lines, shapes or columns. The perception of rhythm involves the auditory system and motor areas of the brain; humans spontaneously synchronise movement to a beat, a phenomenon called entrainment.
