Athanasius the Athonite

Athanasius the Athonite (c. 920 – c. 1003), also known as Athanasius of Trebizond, was a Byzantine monk who founded the monastic community on Mount Athos in Greece. With the patronage of his friend, the emperor Nikephoros II Phokas, he established the Great Lavra in 963 — the first and highest-ranking of the twenty monasteries of Athos — and is regarded as the founder of organised Athonite monasticism.

Life

Born Abraham in Trebizond around 920 and educated in Constantinople, he became a monk under Michael Maleinos on Mount Kyminas in Bithynia, taking the name Athanasius. Seeking greater solitude, he withdrew to the then-deserted peninsula of Mount Athos in the 950s, where hermits had lived scattered for generations.

Foundation of the Great Lavra

His former acquaintance Nikephoros Phokas, on becoming emperor in 963, funded a great coenobitic monastery: the Great Lavra, dedicated to the Dormition of the Theotokos. Athanasius drafted its rule and oversaw construction of its church, refectory, cells and aqueduct. His project met resistance both from the Athonite hermits, who feared institutionalisation, and from Emperor John I Tzimiskes, though they were eventually reconciled and the foundation confirmed by imperial chrysobull.

The Typikon

Athanasius's Typikon (c. 970s) regulated the community's prayer, labour, diet and property, blending the traditions of Stoudios and the Palestinian lauras. A general Typikon for the whole Mountain, endorsed under Tzimiskes, made the Lavra's abbot the leading figure of Athos.

Death and legacy

Athanasius died around 1003, reportedly in an accident while inspecting construction at the Lavra. He is venerated as a saint in the Orthodox Church, with his feast day on 5 July. The monastic republic he founded endures on Mount Athos to this day.
