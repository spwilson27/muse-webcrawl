Fermat's Last Theorem

In number theory, Fermat's Last Theorem states that no three positive integers a, b, and c satisfy the equation an + bn = cn for any integer value of n greater than 2.

FieldNumber theory
Conjectured byPierre de Fermat, c. 1637
Proved byAndrew Wiles (1994; published 1995)

History

Pierre de Fermat wrote the claim in the margin of his copy of Diophantus's Arithmetica around 1637, adding that he had a "truly marvelous proof" the margin was too narrow to hold. For 358 years the statement resisted proof, becoming one of the most famous problems in mathematics, though special cases were settled (for example n = 4 by Fermat himself, and n = 3, 5, and 7 by later mathematicians).

Proof

In 1994 Andrew Wiles, building on the Taniyama–Shimura–Weil conjecture and work by Gerhard Frey, Jean-Pierre Serre, and Ken Ribet, announced a proof; after an error was found, Wiles and Richard Taylor completed it, and the proof was published in 1995. The result implies the modularity of semistable elliptic curves.
