Fortnite is an online video game and game platform developed by Epic Games, released in 2017. It comprises several game modes built around the same engine and art style, most famously Fortnite Battle Royale, a free-to-play mode in which up to 100 players fight to be the last one standing on a shrinking island map. At its peak, Fortnite registered hundreds of millions of players and became a major cultural phenomenon, extending into virtual concerts, film tie-ins, and live in-game events.

Game modes

Save the World, the original mode, is a cooperative tower-defence and survival game in which players build fortifications against zombie-like husks. Battle Royale adds Fortnite's signature building mechanic: players harvest wood, stone, and metal to construct walls, ramps, and forts mid-combat ("Zero Build" variants remove this). Creative lets players design their own islands and games, and later additions such as LEGO Fortnite, Rocket Racing, and Fortnite Festival expanded the platform, following the user-generated-experience model of Unreal Editor for Fortnite (UEFN).

Business model and seasons

Battle Royale is free to play and monetised through cosmetic items — outfits ("skins"), emotes, and gliders — sold in an item shop and through a seasonal Battle Pass, using the in-game currency V-Bucks. Content is organised into chapters and seasons that rework the map and storyline, punctuated by live events such as the 2019 black hole that briefly took the game offline and concerts by artists including Travis Scott and Ariana Grande.

Reception and impact

Fortnite's popularity with younger audiences made it a fixture of debates about screen time and monetisation, while its dances became ubiquitous in popular culture and sport. Its cross-play support across platforms was an industry landmark. In 2020, Epic's dispute with Apple over in-app payments led to Fortnite's removal from the App Store and a major antitrust lawsuit, reshaping debate about platform fees; the game later returned to mobile storefronts in various forms.

Esports

Epic runs competitive circuits culminating in events such as the Fortnite World Cup (2019), whose solo champion won US$3 million, and the ongoing FNCS (Fortnite Champion Series).
