The President of the European Council is the person who chairs the European Council, the institution of the European Union comprising the heads of state or government of the member states. The office was made permanent by the Treaty of Lisbon, which entered into force on 1 December 2009, replacing the previous system in which the chair rotated with the Council presidency every six months.

Role

Under Article 15 of the Treaty on European Union, the President chairs the European Council and drives its work forward, ensures its preparation and continuity in cooperation with the President of the European Commission, endeavours to facilitate cohesion and consensus, and presents a report to the European Parliament after each meeting. At his or her level, the President also ensures the external representation of the Union on issues concerning its common foreign and security policy, without prejudice to the powers of the High Representative.

Appointment

The President is elected by the European Council by qualified majority for a term of two and a half years, renewable once. The office holder may not hold any national office simultaneously.

Office holders

The first permanent President was Herman Van Rompuy of Belgium (2009–2014), followed by Donald Tusk of Poland (2014–2019), Charles Michel of Belgium (2019–2024), and António Costa of Portugal, who took office on 1 December 2024.
