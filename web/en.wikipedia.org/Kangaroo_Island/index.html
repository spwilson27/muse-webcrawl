Kangaroo Island

Kangaroo Island is Australia's third-largest island, after Tasmania and Melville Island, lying in the state of South Australia about 112 km southwest of Adelaide, off the Fleurieu Peninsula. It is 145 km long and between 0.9 and 57 km wide, covering about 4,405 km². The largest town is Kingscote; the population is around 4,700.

History

Aboriginal people lived on the island for thousands of years before it was cut off by rising seas after the last ice age; archaeological evidence suggests occupation ended several thousand years ago, and the island was uninhabited when Matthew Flinders charted it in 1802 and named it for the kangaroos his crew killed for food. Sealers and whalers used the island in the early 1800s, and in 1836 Kingscote became South Australia's first formal European settlement.

Wildlife and environment

Separated from the mainland, the island escaped foxes and rabbits, making it a refuge for wildlife: koalas (introduced in the 1920s), the Kangaroo Island kangaroo, echidnas, platypuses, Australian sea lions at Seal Bay, and the endangered Kangaroo Island dunnart. Over a third of the island is protected in national parks, including Flinders Chase with its Remarkable Rocks and Admirals Arch. In the Black Summer bushfires of 2019–20, roughly half the island burned, killing many koalas and devastating habitat; recovery efforts followed.

Economy

Tourism, agriculture, sheep and cattle farming, fishing and aquaculture drive the economy. The island is also noted for Ligurian bee honey — Kangaroo Island is a declared sanctuary for pure Ligurian bees — and for eucalyptus oil and fine wines.
