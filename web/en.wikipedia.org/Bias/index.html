Bias

Bias is a disproportionate weight in favour of or against an idea, person or thing, usually in a way considered unfair. Biases can be innate or learned, conscious or unconscious, and they arise in many domains: psychology studies cognitive biases in judgement, statistics studies systematic error in sampling and estimation, and public debate concerns bias in media, research and decision-making.

Cognitive biases

Psychologists have catalogued many systematic patterns of deviation from rationality, including confirmation bias (favouring information that confirms prior beliefs), anchoring, availability bias and in-group bias. The research programme of Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman from the 1970s onward established cognitive bias as a central topic in behavioural economics and judgement research.

Statistical bias

In statistics, bias is a systematic error: a biased estimator consistently over- or under-estimates a parameter, while selection bias, sampling bias and reporting bias distort the data themselves. Related concepts include publication bias in research literature and algorithmic bias in machine learning systems, where unrepresentative training data produce systematically skewed outputs.

Social and cultural contexts

Bias also describes prejudice for or against groups, manifesting as implicit bias, media bias, or institutional discrimination. Mitigation strategies include blind review, pre-registration of studies, diverse data collection and structured decision-making.
