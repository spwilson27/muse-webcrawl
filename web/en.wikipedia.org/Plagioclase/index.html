Plagioclase

Plagioclase
Category: Feldspar group, tectosilicate
Formula: NaAlSi3O8 – CaAl2Si2O8
Hardness: 6 – 6.5 (Mohs)
Colour: White, gray, bluish

Plagioclase is a series of tectosilicate (framework silicate) minerals within the feldspar family. Rather than referring to a particular mineral with a specific chemical composition, plagioclase is a continuous solid solution series, more properly known as the plagioclase feldspar series. This was first shown by the German mineralogist Johann Friedrich Christian Hessel in 1826. The series ranges from albite to anorthite endmembers (with intermediate compositions), where sodium atoms can be replaced by calcium.

Members of the series

The composition of a plagioclase feldspar is typically indicated by its mole fraction of anorthite (%An). Named members are: albite (0–10 %An), oligoclase (10–30), andesine (30–50), labradorite (50–70), bytownite (70–90), and anorthite (90–100). Labradorite is notable for the iridescent play of colour called labradorescence, and the variety sold as sunstone or moonstone also belongs to this family.

Properties and occurrence

Plagioclase crystallizes in the triclinic crystal system — its name derives from the Greek for "oblique fracture", referring to its two cleavage directions meeting at nearly right angles. Its Mohs hardness is 6 to 6.5 and its specific gravity is about 2.6 to 2.8.

Plagioclase is a major constituent mineral in the Earth's crust and in igneous rocks; its proportion of albite- versus anorthite-rich composition is a primary criterion in rock classification under the QAPF diagram. It is also abundant in lunar highland rocks and in Martian basalts detected by orbiting spacecraft and rovers.
