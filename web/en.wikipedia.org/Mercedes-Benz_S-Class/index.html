Mercedes-Benz S-Class

The Mercedes-Benz S-Class, formerly known as Sonderklasse (German for "special class", abbreviated as "S-Klasse"), is a series of full-sized luxury sedans, limousines and armored sedans produced by the German automaker Mercedes-Benz. The S-Class is the designation for top-of-the-line Mercedes-Benz models and was officially introduced in 1972 with the W116, succeeding the previous W108/W109 models.

Innovations

The S-Class has debuted many of the company's latest innovations, including drivetrain technologies, interior features, and safety systems. The W116 introduced anti-lock brakes (1978); the W126 added airbags and seat-belt pretensioners; the W140 brought electronic stability control; the W220 introduced adaptive cruise control (Distronic) and Pre-Safe. It has ranked as the world's best-selling luxury sedan.

Generations

Generations include the W116 (1972–1980), W126 (1979–1991), W140 (1991–1998), W220 (1998–2005), W221 (2005–2013), W222 (2013–2020) and W223 (from 2020). Models are sold in standard and long-wheelbase versions, and the line has included coupés (later spun off as the CL-Class and then an S-Class coupé) and ultra-luxury Mercedes-Maybach variants.

Significance

In automotive terms, "S-Class" is often used as a generic benchmark for the large luxury sedan segment; rivals include the BMW 7 Series and Audi A8.
