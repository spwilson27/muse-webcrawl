The Fram Museum (Norwegian: Frammuseet) is a museum in Oslo, Norway, centred on the polar exploration ship Fram. It stands on the Bygdøy peninsula, near the Norwegian Maritime Museum and the Kon-Tiki Museum.

History

The museum was inaugurated on 20 May 1936. Its centrepiece is the wooden ship Fram ("Forward"), built in 1892 to Colin Archer's design and used in expeditions to the Arctic and Antarctic by Fridtjof Nansen (1893–96 drift across the Arctic Ocean), Otto Sverdrup (1898–1902), and Roald Amundsen (1910–12, the expedition that reached the South Pole in December 1911). Visitors can board the ship and view its preserved interiors.

Gjøa building

In 2013 the museum opened a second building housing the sloop Gjøa, the first vessel to transit the Northwest Passage (1903–06 under Amundsen). Exhibits cover the history of polar exploration, Arctic wildlife, and the explorers' equipment and diaries.
