Oryol Oblast (Russian: Орловская область, Orlovskaya oblast) is a federal subject of Russia (an oblast) in the Central Federal District, southwest of Moscow. Its administrative centre is the city of Oryol, located on the Oka River about 360 km from the capital.

Geography

The oblast lies on the Central Russian Upland, in the fertile black-earth (chernozem) belt. The Oka River rises in the south of the region and flows north through Oryol city. The climate is moderately continental, and much of the land is steppe and forest-steppe given over to agriculture.

Economy

Agriculture — wheat, sugar beet, potatoes and livestock — is the backbone of the economy, together with food processing, machine building and metallurgy in Oryol and Livny. The region is also known for its literary heritage: it is the birthplace of the novelist Ivan Turgenev, whose estate at Spasskoye-Lutovinovo is now a museum, and of the writer Leonid Andreyev.

History

The territory was heavily fought over in the Second World War; Oryol was occupied by Nazi Germany from 1941 until its liberation in August 1943 during Operation Kutuzov, after the Battle of Kursk. The oblast in its present borders dates from 1954.
