The Amor asteroids are a group of near-Earth asteroids named after the archetype object 1221 Amor. They approach the orbit of Earth from beyond it: their perihelion distances are slightly greater than Earth's aphelion (1.017 AU) but less than 1.3 AU, and their semi-major axes are greater than 1.0 AU. Unlike the Aten and Apollo asteroids, Amors do not cross Earth's orbit, although some cross the orbit of Mars.

Characteristics

Amor asteroids range from small fragments a few metres across to large bodies such as 1036 Ganymed, about 32 km in diameter, the largest known Amor. Because their orbits lie mostly outside Earth's, they can be observed for long periods and are considered good targets for rendezvous missions.

Notable members

  • 1221 Amor — the prototype, discovered by Eugène Delporte in 1932.
  • 433 Eros — the first near-Earth asteroid discovered (1898); visited by the NEAR Shoemaker spacecraft, which orbited and landed on it in 2001.
  • 1036 Ganymed — the largest known Amor asteroid.
  • 3908 Nyx and 1221 Amor are also Mars-crossing.

Planetary defence

Although Amors cannot currently hit Earth, gravitational perturbations — especially from Mars and Jupiter — can evolve their orbits over thousands of years, so they are monitored as part of planetary defence surveys alongside the Earth-crossing groups.
