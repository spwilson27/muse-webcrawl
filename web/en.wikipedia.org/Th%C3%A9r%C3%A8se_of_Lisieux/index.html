Thérèse of Lisieux

Thérèse of Lisieux (1873–1897), born Marie Françoise-Thérèse Martin and known as Saint Thérèse of the Child Jesus and the Holy Face, was a French Discalced Carmelite nun revered in the Catholic Church as a saint and Doctor of the Church. She is popularly known as "the Little Flower of Jesus".

Born2 January 1873, Alençon, France
Died30 September 1897, Lisieux, France
Canonized1925, by Pope Pius XI
Feast1 October

Life

Thérèse entered the Carmelite convent at Lisieux at the unusually young age of fifteen, after appealing directly to Pope Leo XIII. She lived an obscure life of prayer and small sacrifices, dying of tuberculosis at twenty-four. Her parents, Louis and Zélie Martin, were themselves canonized in 2015.

The "Little Way" and writings

Her autobiography, Story of a Soul, published after her death, describes her "little way" of spiritual childhood — attaining holiness through small acts done with great love rather than heroic deeds. The book became one of the most widely read spiritual works of the twentieth century, and Pope John Paul II named her a Doctor of the Church in 1997, one of only a few women so honoured.
