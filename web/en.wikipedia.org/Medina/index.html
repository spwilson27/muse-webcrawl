Medina, officially Al-Madinah al-Munawwarah (Arabic: المدينة المنورة, "The Enlightened City"), is the capital of Medina Province in western Saudi Arabia. It is the second-holiest city in Islam, after Mecca. Medina was the destination of Muhammad's Hijrah (migration) from Mecca in 622 CE, and the capital of the first Muslim polity.

History

Known in pre-Islamic times as Yathrib, the oasis city was inhabited by Arab tribes (Aws and Khazraj) and Jewish tribes. In 622 the inhabitants invited Muhammad to mediate their disputes; his migration — the Hijrah — marks the beginning of the Islamic calendar. In Medina he established a community under the Constitution of Medina, built the Prophet's Mosque, and fought the battles of Badr, Uhud and the Trench against Meccan opponents. Muhammad died in Medina in 632 and is buried beneath the Green Dome of the Prophet's Mosque.

Medina was the capital of the Rashidun Caliphate until 661, after which political power shifted to Damascus and later Baghdad, though the city retained its religious prestige. It later came under Mamluk, Ottoman and finally Saudi rule; the Ottomans built the Hejaz Railway linking it to Damascus.

Religious significance

Millions of pilgrims visit Medina each year, usually in connection with the Hajj or Umrah, to pray in the Prophet's Mosque — one of the largest mosques in the world — and to visit sites such as the Quba Mosque, the first mosque built in Islam, and Mount Uhud. The central area around the mosque is a sanctuary (haram) closed to non-Muslims.

Modern city

Modern Medina is a major pilgrimage and education centre, home to the Islamic University of Madinah, and is connected by the Haramain high-speed railway to Mecca and Jeddah.
