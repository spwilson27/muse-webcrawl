Cat on a Hot Tin Roof

Cat on a Hot Tin Roof is a 1955 play by American playwright Tennessee Williams. It premiered on Broadway in March 1955, directed by Elia Kazan, and won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama. Set on a Mississippi Delta plantation during the 65th birthday celebration of the family patriarch, "Big Daddy" Pollitt, the play explores mendacity, desire, mortality and family conflict.

Plot

Brick Pollitt, an embittered former football star drinking since the death of his friend Skipper, refuses intimacy with his wife Maggie — "the Cat" — who is determined to secure their share of Big Daddy's inheritance. Big Daddy, dying of cancer though told he is well, confronts Brick in a long central scene about his alcoholism and the family's lies. The eldest son Gooper and his wife scheme for the estate, while the play closes on an uneasy truce between Brick and Maggie.

Adaptations

The 1958 film adaptation, directed by Richard Brooks, starred Elizabeth Taylor as Maggie and Paul Newman as Brick, with Burl Ives reprising Big Daddy; it was a major commercial and critical success, though censorship softened the play's treatment of sexuality. The play remains frequently revived on Broadway and in London.
