Continental Association

The Continental Association, often known simply as the Association, was an agreement adopted by the First Continental Congress on 20 October 1774. It created a coordinated boycott of British goods across the Thirteen Colonies in response to the Intolerable Acts, and is considered a crucial step from colonial protest toward the American Revolution.

Terms

The Association bound the colonies to cease importing British goods from 1 December 1774 (non-importation), to stop exporting to Britain, Ireland and the British West Indies from September 1775 (non-exportation), and to refuse to consume British imports, including tea (non-consumption). Signatories pledged to discourage extravagant entertainments and to promote domestic manufactures and frugality.

Enforcement

Local committees of observation and inspection were elected in towns and counties throughout the colonies to monitor compliance, publish the names of violators, and ostracise them as enemies of American liberty. These committees became an embryo of revolutionary government, exercising real authority in place of royal officials.

Significance

Nearly universally adopted, the Association united the colonies in a common economic strategy, weakened support for compromise, and laid the organisational groundwork for the Second Continental Congress and, ultimately, independence in 1776.
