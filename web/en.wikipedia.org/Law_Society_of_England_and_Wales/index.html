Law Society of England and Wales

The Law Society of England and Wales (officially The Law Society) is the professional association that represents and governs solicitors for the jurisdiction of England and Wales. It provides services and support to practicing and training solicitors, as well as serving as a sounding board for law reform. Since the Legal Services Act 2007, the Society's regulatory functions are exercised through the independent Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA).

History

The Society grew out of the Society of Gentlemen Practisers, founded in 1739 to protect the interests of attorneys and solicitors. A new professional body was formed in 1825 and received a royal charter in 1831 as the Society of Gentlemen Practisers in the Courts of Law and Equity; it became "The Law Society" under a supplemental charter of 1903. The Society acquired its headquarters at 113 Chancery Lane in the 1830s. Over the following two centuries it came to oversee admission to the roll of solicitors, professional discipline, and education, until reforms in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries progressively separated those powers.

Functions

Today the Law Society acts as the representative voice of solicitors: it campaigns on access to justice and the rule of law, lobbies Parliament on proposed legislation, issues practice notes and guidance, accredits specialists through quality schemes (such as the Conveyancing Quality Scheme), and supports members through services including its library and helplines. Its governing Council is elected from constituencies of members and specialist sections.

Regulation

Following the Clementi review of legal services, the Legal Services Act 2007 required separation of representative and regulatory functions. Since 2007 regulation — setting standards for admission, conduct and discipline of solicitors and firms — has been carried out by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, overseen by the Legal Services Board. Complaints about service are handled by the Legal Ombudsman.

Presidents

The Society elects a president annually; the office has been held by many prominent solicitors. Its past presidents include figures who later served in Parliament and government.
