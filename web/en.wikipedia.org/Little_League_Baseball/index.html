Little League Baseball and Softball (officially, Little League Baseball, Incorporated) is a non-profit organization that organizes youth baseball and softball leagues around the world. Founded in 1939 in Williamsport, Pennsylvania, it has grown into the world's largest organized youth sports program, with roughly two million participants in more than 80 countries.

History

Carl Stotz, playing catch with his nephews in Williamsport, conceived a baseball league scaled for children, with smaller fields and adjusted rules. In 1939 he organized the first three teams with local sponsorship; the first game was played on 6 June 1939. The first Little League World Series was held in 1947, and by the 1950s leagues had spread across the United States and internationally. Girls were formally admitted in 1974 following legal challenges. A softball program and divisions for older players (Intermediate, Junior, and Senior League) were added over time.

Structure and rules

The Major Division (ages 9–12) plays on a field with 60-foot basepaths and a 46-foot pitching distance. Games are six innings, with strict pitch-count rules for safety. Local leagues are chartered by Little League International and grouped into districts, sections, states, and regions for tournament play each summer.

Little League World Series

The Little League World Series, held each August in South Williamsport at Howard J. Lamade Stadium and Volunteer Stadium, matches United States regional champions against international champions. It is televised nationally and has featured future major leaguers. Notable moments include Taiwan's era of dominance in the 1970s, the 2001 Danny Almonte age scandal, and Mo'ne Davis's celebrated performance in 2014.
