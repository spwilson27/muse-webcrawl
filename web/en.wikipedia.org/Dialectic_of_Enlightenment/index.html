Dialectic of Enlightenment

Dialectic of Enlightenment (German: Dialektik der Aufklärung) is a work of philosophy and social criticism written by Max Horkheimer and Theodor W. Adorno of the Frankfurt School. Written during their exile in the United States in 1944 and published in 1947, it is one of the founding texts of critical theory.

Argument

Horkheimer and Adorno argue that the Enlightenment, which promised to liberate humanity from myth and superstition through reason, has itself reverted into a new mythology: instrumental reason, concerned only with calculation, efficiency, and domination of nature, ends up dominating human beings as well. The rational mastery of nature thus turns back on society, producing the administered world, fascism, and mass culture. Myth, they claim, is already enlightenment, and enlightenment reverts to mythology.

Key chapters

The book includes an excursus reading Odysseus as the prototype of the bourgeois individual, an analysis of the Marquis de Sade, the famous chapter on "the culture industry" — which criticises film, radio, and popular entertainment as standardised products that pacify consumers — and a study of anti-Semitism.

Influence

The work profoundly influenced postwar philosophy, sociology, cultural studies, and media criticism, and its concept of the culture industry remains widely discussed, though critics have charged the book with pessimism and overgeneralisation.
