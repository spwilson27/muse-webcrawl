Metalexicography

Metalexicography is the theoretical and methodological study of dictionaries and other reference works. It is the branch of lexicography concerned not with compiling dictionaries itself, but with analysing how they are made and used, what structure they should have, and how they function in society.

Main areas

Metalexicographic research covers the history of dictionaries; dictionary criticism and evaluation; the typology of reference works (monolingual, bilingual, learners', terminological and encyclopedic dictionaries); dictionary structure — the macrostructure of the word list and the microstructure of the individual entry; the study of dictionary use and user skills; and the social and commercial aspects of dictionary publishing. In recent decades electronic and online dictionaries have become a central research topic, raising new questions about search, data linking and corpus-based compilation.

Scholars and institutions

The field developed as an academic discipline in the second half of the twentieth century, with scholars such as Ladislav Zgusta, Reinhard Hartmann and Herbert Ernst Wiegand shaping its theory. It is supported by associations including EURALEX (the European Association for Lexicography) and journals such as the International Journal of Lexicography and Lexikos.
