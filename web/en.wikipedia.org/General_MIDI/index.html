General MIDI standardises the instrument map used by MIDI files and sound modules.

General MIDI (GM) is a standardised specification for electronic musical instruments that respond to MIDI messages, introduced in 1991 by the MIDI Manufacturers Association. Its central feature is a fixed map of 128 program numbers grouped into 16 families of instruments — pianos, chromatic percussion, organs, guitars, and so on — so that a MIDI file composed on one device will play back with approximately the correct instrumentation on any other GM device.

Requirements

A General MIDI instrument must support 24 simultaneous voices (or 16 melodic plus 8 percussion voices), respond to velocity, support all 16 MIDI channels, and reserve channel 10 for percussion using a standardised drum kit map in which each note number corresponds to a specific drum or percussion sound. GM also defines standard controller behaviour, pitch bend range, and a Master Volume message.

Extensions and successors

Manufacturers created supersets such as Roland's GS and Yamaha's XG, which added instrument banks and effects while remaining GM-compatible. The MIDI Manufacturers Association later published General MIDI Level 2 (GM2), which expanded the specification with more controllers, sounds, and modulations. General MIDI remains widely used in music education software, games, karaoke, and mobile devices.
