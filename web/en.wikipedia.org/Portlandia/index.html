Portlandia

Portlandia is a sculpture by Raymond Kaskey, installed above the entrance of the Portland Building in downtown Portland, Oregon, in October 1985. Depicting a female figure in classical drapery kneeling and reaching down, it is the second-largest copper repoussé statue in the United States, after the Statue of Liberty.

Description and history

The statue measures about 36 feet (11 m) in height and weighs more than six tons. If standing upright, the figure would be roughly 50 feet tall. Kaskey sculpted the piece in Maryland using the repoussé technique — hammering sheets of copper over a form — and it was shipped to Portland in sections by rail and barge, then assembled and floated down the Willamette River to public attention before being hoisted into place on a third-floor terrace above Southwest 5th Avenue.

The design derives from the figure of Commerce on Portland's city seal. Portlandia crouches as if welcoming visitors to the Portland Building, a prominent postmodern structure designed by Michael Graves (see postmodernism in architecture). Because the statue sits back on a third-floor podium rather than at street level, it is relatively inconspicuous, and proposals to relocate it to a more prominent site have periodically been raised but not carried out.

In popular culture

The statue has become one of Portland's civic symbols. The poet Ronald Talney wrote "A Poem for Portlandia" for its dedication. Copyright in the sculpture rests with the artist, meaning commercial reproductions require permission; the figure appears in municipal branding only by arrangement. The name "Portlandia" was also adopted, with permission, by the IFC sketch comedy television series Portlandia (2011–2018), starring Fred Armisen and Carrie Brownstein, which satirized the city's culture.
