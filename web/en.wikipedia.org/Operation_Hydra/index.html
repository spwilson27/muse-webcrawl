Operation Hydra

Operation Hydra
Date: Night of 17/18 August 1943
Location: Peenemünde, Germany
Attacker: RAF Bomber Command (596 aircraft)
Target: V-weapons research establishment

Operation Hydra was a Royal Air Force bombing raid on the German rocket research establishment at Peenemünde on the Baltic coast on the night of 17/18 August 1943. It was the opening operation of the Allied Crossbow campaign against Germany's V-weapons programme.

Background

British intelligence, piecing together reports from the Oslo Report, agent sources and photographic reconnaissance, had concluded that Germany was developing long-range rockets (the V-2) and pilotless aircraft (the V-1) at Peenemünde, where Wernher von Braun's team worked. The War Cabinet authorised a maximum-effort night attack aimed not only at the factories and experimental works but at killing the scientists and engineers in their living quarters.

The raid

Some 596 bombers — Lancasters, Halifaxes and Stirlings led by a master bomber, Group Captain John Searby — attacked in three waves against the housing estate, the factory buildings and the experimental works. A diversion raid on Berlin by Mosquitoes drew off German night fighters, but once the deception was realised, fighters inflicted heavy losses on the final wave; 40 bombers and 245 aircrew were lost. Around 700–800 people were killed on the ground, many of them forced labourers in the Trassenheide camp hit by stray bombs, along with some 180 German personnel including the propulsion specialist Walter Thiel.

Effects

The raid wrecked much of the housing and some facilities and is generally credited with delaying the V-2 programme by several weeks to two months. Production of the V-2 was subsequently moved underground to the Mittelwerk near Nordhausen, and testing shifted to Poland. The V-1 and V-2 campaigns against London began in June and September 1944 respectively.
