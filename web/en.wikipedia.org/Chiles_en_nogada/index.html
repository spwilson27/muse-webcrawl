Chiles en nogada

Chiles en nogada
Place of originPuebla, Mexico
SeasonLate summer (July–September)
Main ingredientsPoblano chiles, picadillo, walnut sauce
GarnishPomegranate seeds, parsley

Chiles en nogada is a Mexican dish of poblano chiles stuffed with picadillo — a mixture of shredded meat, fruits and spices — topped with a walnut-based cream sauce (nogada), pomegranate seeds and parsley. The green, white and red presentation represents the colours of the Mexican flag, and the dish is traditionally served in late summer when walnuts and pomegranates are in season.

History

Tradition holds that Augustinian nuns at the Santa Mónica convent in Puebla created the dish in 1821 to honour Agustín de Iturbide and the Army of the Three Guarantees after the signing of the Treaty of Córdoba, combining the green, white and red of the new national flag. Puebla celebrates the dish with festivals each season, and local walnuts (nuez de Castilla) are considered essential to the authentic nogada.

Preparation

The picadillo blends pork (or beef and pork) with tomato, onion, garlic and seasonal fruits such as apple, pear, peach and plantain, plus almonds, raisins and spices. The roasted, peeled poblanos are stuffed, sometimes battered and fried (capeado) or served plain, then covered with the cold walnut sauce and scattered with pomegranate seeds and parsley. Variations and debates — battered or not, meat sweet or savoury — are a matter of strong local pride.
