SETI Institute

The SETI Institute is a not-for-profit research organization incorporated in 1984 whose mission is to explore, understand, and explain the origin and nature of life in the universe, and to use that knowledge to inspire and guide present and future generations. Its headquarters are in Mountain View, California.

Research

The Institute employs more than 100 scientists conducting research in astronomy, planetary science, and astrobiology. Its best-known work is the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI): scanning the radio sky for artificial signals using instruments such as the Allen Telescope Array in Northern California, which the Institute operates jointly with SRI International.

Beyond SETI, Institute scientists study exoplanets, Mars analog environments, extremophile organisms, and the formation of planetary systems, and contribute to NASA missions. The Institute's Center for Education runs programs including Airborne Astronomy Ambassadors, which placed teachers aboard the SOFIA flying observatory.

History and people

The SETI Institute grew out of NASA's SETI program and took its present form after United States federal funding for the agency's dedicated SETI project ended in 1993. Frank Drake, author of the Drake equation, served as president of its board of trustees, and Jill Tarter — long the public face of SETI research — directed its Center for SETI Research for many years. Other prominent figures associated with the Institute include planetary scientist Pascal Lee and astronomer Seth Shostak.
