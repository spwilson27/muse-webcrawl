IPv6 transition mechanism

An IPv6 transition mechanism is a technology that facilitates the transitioning of the Internet from the IPv4 infrastructure in current use to the addressing and routing system of Internet Protocol version 6 (IPv6). As IPv4 and IPv6 networks are not directly interoperable, transition technologies are designed to permit hosts on either network to communicate with hosts on the other.

Approaches

Transition mechanisms fall into three broad categories, as defined by the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF):

  • Dual stack — nodes run both IPv4 and IPv6 protocol stacks simultaneously and choose between them per connection. This is the preferred long-term approach but requires both networks to be available everywhere.
  • Tunneling — IPv6 packets are encapsulated inside IPv4 packets (or vice versa) to traverse a network of the other protocol. Examples include configured tunnels (6in4), 6to4, 6rd, Teredo and ISATAP.
  • Translation — a gateway translates between IPv4 and IPv6 packets. Examples include NAT64 with DNS64, which lets IPv6-only hosts reach IPv4 servers, and DS-Lite and 464XLAT, widely deployed by mobile operators.

Standardisation and deployment

The IETF originally developed the "Next Generation Transition" (NGTRANS) toolset, and later chartered the Softwires and v6ops working groups to refine transition tools as deployment realities became clear. Many early mechanisms — including 6to4, whose anycast relay operation was deprecated in 2015 — have fallen out of favour in favour of native dual-stack service and operator-managed translation such as 464XLAT on mobile networks and DS-Lite on fixed broadband.

Internet service providers use transition mechanisms to offer IPv6 service over IPv4-only access infrastructure, or, increasingly, to offer residual IPv4 connectivity ("IPv4 as a service") over IPv6-only networks, using technologies such as MAP-T and MAP-E.
