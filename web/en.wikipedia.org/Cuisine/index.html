A cuisine is a style of cooking characterised by distinctive ingredients, techniques and dishes, and usually associated with a specific culture or geographic region. Regional food preparation techniques, customs and ingredients combine to form dishes unique to a region. Cuisines are shaped by climate and available produce, trade, religious rules and social custom — rice-based cuisines of monsoon Asia, wheat and olive oil around the Mediterranean (see Cruciferous vegetables for characteristic ingredients), maize and beans in Mesoamerica.

Development

Cuisines evolve through exchange: the Columbian exchange transformed cooking worldwide by moving tomatoes, potatoes, chillies and maize between hemispheres. Court cooking codified elite traditions — France's haute cuisine was systematised by Carême and Escoffier — while street food and peasant dishes preserved regional identities. In the 20th century, migration and media globalised tastes, producing fusion cuisines and international restaurant cultures, alongside movements to protect traditional foods, such as Slow Food and UNESCO's recognition of gastronomic heritage (including the French gastronomic meal, washoku and Mexican cuisine).

Regional cuisines

Regional variations often follow historical boundaries rather than modern states — as in the cuisine of Nice, which blends French and Italian influences, or the cuisine of the Mid-Atlantic states of the US, shaped by Dutch, German, English and African-American traditions. National cuisines themselves are frequently modern constructions assembled from regional repertoires.
