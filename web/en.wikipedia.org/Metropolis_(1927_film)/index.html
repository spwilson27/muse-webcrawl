Metropolis is a 1927 German expressionist science-fiction drama film directed by Fritz Lang and written by Thea von Harbou. Set in the futuristic city of Metropolis in the year 2000, it depicts a dystopian society divided between a wealthy ruling class living in towering skyscrapers and impoverished workers who toil in vast underground machine halls to power the city.

Plot

Freder, the son of the city's master Joh Fredersen, falls in love with Maria, a saintly figure among the workers who preaches the coming of a mediator between the "hands" and the "brain". Fredersen has the inventor Rotwang create a robot in Maria's likeness to sow discord among the workers, but the plan leads the city toward catastrophe before Freder mediates a reconciliation between his father and the workers' leader Grot.

Production and legacy

Produced by UFA, Metropolis was the most expensive film of its era, costing over five million Reichsmarks. Its pioneering special effects, designed by Eugen Schüfftan, miniatures and huge Art Deco sets, made it a landmark of science-fiction cinema. Long circulated in shortened versions, the film was substantially restored after a near-complete print was discovered in Buenos Aires in 2008.
