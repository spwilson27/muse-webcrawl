Fortezza

Fortezza (German: Franzensfeste) is a comune (municipality) in South Tyrol in northern Italy, located about 60 kilometres northeast of Bolzano, in the Eisack Valley where the Brenner railway and motorway pass toward Austria. The village, with around 1,000 inhabitants, takes its name from the great Habsburg fortress — the Franzensfeste — built above it in the 19th century.

The fortress

The Franzensfeste, named for Emperor Francis I of Austria, was constructed between 1833 and 1838 to guard the strategic Wipp Valley route over the Brenner Pass. Built of granite quarried nearby, it was one of the largest fortifications in the Alps and reputedly one of the most expensive of its era; because artillery ranges quickly outgrew it, the fortress never saw battle. The Italian army used it as a depot until 2003. During the Second World War, gold reserves of the Bank of Italy were stored in a tunnel within the fortress. The complex has since been restored and opened to the public as a museum and exhibition venue, and is associated with the South Tyrolean provincial museums.

Geography and transport

The municipality includes the villages of Fortezza/Franzensfeste, Mezzaselva/Mittewald, Le Cave/Grasstein, and Pradisotto/Prat. The railway station at Fortezza is a junction on the Brenner line, where the Puster Valley railway branches east toward Innichen and Lienz. The area will be transformed by the Brenner Base Tunnel, whose southern portal lies just north of the village; Fortezza is planned as a major interchange for the new high-speed line.
