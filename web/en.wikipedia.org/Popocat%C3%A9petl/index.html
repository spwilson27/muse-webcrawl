Popocatépetl is an active stratovolcano in central Mexico, lying on the border of the states of Mexico, Puebla, and Morelos. At 5,426 metres it is the second-highest peak in Mexico after Citlaltépetl (Pico de Orizaba). Its name comes from the Nahuatl words popōca ("it smokes") and tepētl ("hill"): "Smoking Hill".

Activity

Popocatépetl reawakened in 1994 after decades of quiet and has been in near-continuous eruption since, with frequent ash emissions and occasional larger explosions. Ashfalls regularly affect Puebla and sometimes Mexico City, about 70 km to the northwest. A permanent exclusion zone surrounds the crater, and the volcano is monitored by CENAPRED.

Legend

In Aztec mythology, Popocatépetl was a warrior whose beloved, the princess Iztaccíhuatl, died of grief believing him killed in battle; the neighbouring dormant volcano Iztaccíhuatl bears her name and the outline of a sleeping woman.
