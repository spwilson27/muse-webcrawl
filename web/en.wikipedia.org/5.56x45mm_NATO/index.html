The 5.56×45mm NATO (official NATO nomenclature 5.56 NATO) is a rimless bottlenecked intermediate cartridge family developed in the late 1970s from the .223 Remington by FN Herstal in Belgium, and adopted as the second NATO-standard rifle cartridge under STANAG 4172 in 1980.

History

The cartridge evolved from the .223 Remington developed for the ArmaLite AR-15, which became the M16 rifle. Dissatisfied with the original M193 loading, NATO trials in the late 1970s selected the Belgian SS109 projectile — a heavier 62-grain bullet with a steel penetrator tip — which the United States adopted as the M855. Rifles were re-barreled with a faster 1:7 rifling twist to stabilize it.

Performance and use

The 5.56×45mm offers a flat trajectory and light recoil, allowing controllable automatic fire and greater ammunition carriage than full-power rifle rounds. It is chambered in the M16, M4 carbine, FN SCAR, HK416 and numerous other service rifles used by dozens of countries worldwide. Its civilian near-equivalent is the .223 Remington, though the two are not identical in chamber pressure and throat dimensions.
