The University of Oslo (Norwegian: Universitetet i Oslo, abbreviated UiO) is a public research university in Oslo, Norway. Founded in 1811 as the Royal Frederick University, it is Norway's oldest university and was the country's only university until 1946. It is consistently ranked the top university in Norway and among the leading universities of Northern Europe.

History

The university was founded in 1811, when Norway was still in union with Denmark, modelled on the University of Copenhagen and Humboldt University of Berlin, and named after King Frederick VI. It was renamed the University of Oslo in 1939. From 1947 until 1989 the Nobel Peace Prize ceremony was held in the university's Aula (ceremonial hall), decorated with murals by Edvard Munch; the ceremony is now held at Oslo City Hall.

Organisation

UiO has eight faculties: Theology, Law, Medicine, Humanities, Mathematics and Natural Sciences, Dentistry, Social Sciences, and Education. Most faculties are located at the Blindern campus northwest of the city centre; the Faculty of Law remains downtown. The university also runs museums including the Natural History Museum and the Museum of Cultural History, which houses the Viking ships from Oseberg and Gokstad (being rehoused in the new Museum of the Viking Age).

Nobel laureates

Five Nobel laureates have been affiliated with the university, including Fridtjof Nansen, Ragnar Frisch (the first laureate in economics, 1969), Odd Hassel, Ivar Giaever, and May-Britt and Edvard Moser (2014, Medicine).
