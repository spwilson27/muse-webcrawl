Bicycle tire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A bicycle tire is a pneumatic (usually) tyre fitted to the wheel of a bicycle. Modern bicycle tyres consist of a rubber tread and casing with a bead that seats on the rim; most contain an inner tube or are run tubeless with sealant.

Sizing

Tyre size is given by diameter and width, in ISO (ETRTO) form such as 37-622 or in traditional designations such as 700×35C or 26×2.0 in. Common wheel sizes include 622 mm (700C) for road bikes and 584 mm (650B) and 559 mm (26 in) for mountain and utility bikes.

Construction and pressure

Casings are woven fabric — nylon, or finer cotton or silk in racing tyres — measured in threads per inch (TPI). Running pressures range from under 2 bar for wide off-road tyres to 7 bar or more for narrow road tyres. Tread patterns vary from slick road tyres to heavily knobbed mountain-bike tyres; puncture-protection belts are common on commuting tyres.
