Skipjack tuna

The skipjack tuna (Katsuwonus pelamis) is a medium-sized perciform fish in the tuna family, Scombridae. It is a cosmopolitan pelagic fish found in tropical and warm-temperate waters worldwide and is the most heavily fished tuna species, used widely for canning.

Skipjack tuna

Scientific name: Katsuwonus pelamis
Family: Scombridae
Max length: ~108 cm
Conservation: Least Concern (IUCN)

Description

The skipjack has a streamlined, torpedo-shaped body, dark blue above and silvery below with dark horizontal stripes on the belly. Unlike most tunas it lacks scales except along the lateral line and corselet. It grows quickly, matures within a year or two and feeds on small fish, crustaceans and squid.

Fishery

Skipjack accounts for roughly half of the world's tuna catch, taken mainly by purse-seine vessels, often around fish-aggregating devices. In Japan it is prized fresh as katsuo and dried and fermented into katsuobushi, the shavings used in dashi stock.
