Smooth muscle

Smooth muscle is one of three types of muscle tissue in the human body, along with cardiac muscle and skeletal muscle. It is an involuntary, non-striated muscle found within the walls of hollow organs and structures such as the stomach, intestines, bladder, uterus, blood vessels, and the respiratory and urinary tracts. Its spindle-shaped cells each contain a single central nucleus and lack the regular sarcomere banding seen in striated muscle.

Function

Smooth muscle contraction moves contents through organs — for example peristalsis in the gut — regulates the diameter of blood vessels and airways, controls the flow of urine and bile, and adjusts the pupil and the lens of the eye. Contractions are generally slow, sustained and energy-efficient, and can be maintained for long periods without fatigue.

Control and mechanism

Smooth muscle is controlled by the autonomic nervous system, hormones and local chemical signals. Contraction is triggered by calcium ions, which bind calmodulin and activate myosin light-chain kinase, allowing myosin to interact with actin filaments arranged in a criss-cross network anchored to dense bodies. Some smooth muscle is organized in multi-unit fashion, with cells contracting independently, while visceral (single-unit) smooth muscle cells are electrically coupled by gap junctions and contract as a coordinated sheet.
