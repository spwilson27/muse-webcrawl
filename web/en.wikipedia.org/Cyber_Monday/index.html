Cyber Monday

Cyber Monday is a marketing term for e-commerce transactions on the Monday after Thanksgiving in the United States. It was created by retailers to encourage people to shop online. The term was coined by Ellen Davis of the National Retail Federation and Scott Silverman, and made its debut on 28 November 2005 in a Shop.org press release entitled "'Cyber Monday' Quickly Becoming One of the Biggest Online Shopping Days of the Year".

Origin

Retailers noticed a surge in online sales on the Monday after Thanksgiving, attributed to people returning to work — where they had faster internet connections and could shop privately — and continuing their holiday shopping. Although home broadband later erased that advantage, the day remained a major promotional event.

Sales

Cyber Monday has grown into one of the largest online shopping days of the year, with US sales exceeding ten billion dollars annually in the early 2020s according to Adobe Analytics. It complements Black Friday, which traditionally focuses on in-store sales, and is followed by Giving Tuesday. Many retailers now extend promotions across the whole "Cyber Week".

International spread

The concept has been adopted in many other countries, including Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Japan, and Australia, sometimes on the same date and sometimes aligned with local holidays.
