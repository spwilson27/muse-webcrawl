Sihwa Lake Tidal Power Station

Sihwa Lake Tidal Power Station is the world's largest tidal power installation, with a total generation capacity of 254 MW. It is located on Lake Sihwa, a man-made lake on the west coast of South Korea, near the city of Ansan in Gyeonggi Province. The station surpassed France's Rance Tidal Power Station (240 MW) when it entered operation in 2011.

History

Lake Sihwa was created in 1994 by a 12.7 km seawall built to enclose a tidal bay for land reclamation and freshwater storage. Pollution from industrial and urban runoff made the lake notorious as a failed reclamation project, and the government abandoned the freshwater plan. The tidal power plant, constructed between 2003 and 2011 by Daewoo Engineering & Construction, was partly intended to improve water quality: its turbines exchange some 160 million tonnes of seawater daily, flushing the lake.

Design

The plant uses ten bulb turbines of 25.4 MW each operating on the flood tide (generating as the tide flows into the lake), exploiting a tidal range of about 5.6 m (with a maximum near 9 m). Construction included eight sluice gates and an observatory and tidal park for visitors. Annual generation is about 552 GWh.
