Latter Day Saint movement

The Latter Day Saint movement (also called Mormonism) is a group of restorationist Christian denominations tracing their origin to Joseph Smith in the 1820s–1830s in upstate New York. Adherents accept the Book of Mormon, published in 1830, as scripture alongside the Bible, and believe Smith restored a lost primitive church.

History

Smith reported visions, including the First Vision and visits from the angel Moroni, and organised the Church of Christ in 1830. After Smith's killing in 1844, the movement split: the largest body, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, followed Brigham Young to Utah; others, including the Community of Christ, remained in the Midwest.

Beliefs

Shared themes include continuing revelation through prophets, additional scripture (Doctrine and Covenants, Pearl of Great Price in the LDS tradition), temples, and missionary work. The LDS Church reports over 17 million members worldwide.
