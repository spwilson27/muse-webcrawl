Spithead

Spithead is an area of the Solent and a roadstead off Gilkicker Point in Hampshire, England. It is protected from all winds except those from the south-east. It receives its name from the Spit, a sandbank stretching south from the Hampshire shore for 5 km (3.1 mi). Spithead is 22.5 km (14 mi) long by about 6.5 km (4 mi) in average width. A fleet review is held there every few years, and it served as the home for the payment and supplies of the Royal Navy ships lying in the roadstead.

History

Spithead was used by the British Royal Navy as an anchorage for centuries. During the Napoleonic Wars it was a major assembly point for convoys. In 1797 it was the site of the Spithead mutiny, in which sailors of the Royal Navy mutinied over pay and conditions; the demands were largely met. Queen Victoria held the first fleet review at Spithead in 1856 to celebrate the end of the Crimean War. The anchorage lies between Portsmouth and the Isle of Wight, near the entrance to Portsmouth Harbour.

Fleet reviews

Reviews of the fleet at Spithead have marked coronations, jubilees and other national occasions, with lines of warships anchored for inspection by the monarch arriving aboard the royal yacht.
