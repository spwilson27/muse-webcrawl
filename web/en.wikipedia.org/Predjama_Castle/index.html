Predjama Castle (Slovene: Predjamski grad) is a Renaissance castle built within the mouth of a cave near the village of Predjama, about 11 kilometres from Postojna in southwestern Slovenia. It is recognised as the largest cave castle in the world, set in the middle of a 123-metre-high cliff.

History

A fortress on the site was first mentioned in 1274. The castle is famous for its 15th-century lord, the knight Erazem Lueger (Erasmus of Lueg), a robber baron who defied imperial forces. According to legend, Erazem withstood a year-long siege thanks to a secret passage through the cave system behind the castle that allowed supplies to be brought in; he was eventually killed, the story says, in his privy after a servant betrayed his position with a signal.

The cave system

Beneath and behind the castle lies an extensive karst cave network, including a passage used since the Stone Age. Part of the cave system is open to visitors during the summer months.

Today

The present castle, built in 1570, houses a museum with period furnishings, weapons and displays on its history. It hosts an annual medieval festival and tournament in honour of Erazem, and is one of Slovenia's most visited attractions, often paired with the nearby Postojna Cave.
