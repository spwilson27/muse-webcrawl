Pueblo refers both to the distinctive multi-storey adobe and stone settlements of the Indigenous Pueblo peoples of the American Southwest and, by extension, to those peoples themselves. The Spanish word pueblo means "town" or "people"; Spanish explorers applied it to the settled farming communities they encountered in present-day New Mexico and Arizona, in contrast to nomadic groups. Their ancestors, sometimes called the Ancestral Puebloans (formerly "Anasazi"), built great cliff dwellings and great houses such as those at Chaco Canyon and Mesa Verde.

History

Pueblo culture emerged from earlier Mogollon, Hohokam and Ancestral Pueblo traditions in the Four Corners region, based on irrigated and dry farming of maize, beans and squash. Spanish colonisation began with the Coronado expedition (1540) and the settlement of New Mexico under Juan de Oñate (1598), bringing missions and forced labour. In 1680 the Pueblos united under Popé of Ohkay Owingeh in the Pueblo Revolt, expelling the Spanish for twelve years — the most successful Indigenous revolt in North American history. Spain reconquered the region from 1692, after which a more accommodative coexistence developed. The Pueblos came under Mexican rule in 1821 and United States rule in 1848.

Culture and architecture

Traditional pueblos are terraced complexes of adobe or stone rooms, historically entered by ladders through roof hatches, arranged around plazas and circular ceremonial rooms called kivas. Pueblo society is organised by clans and ceremonial societies; dances and feast days, many timed to an agricultural and ritual calendar, remain central to community life. Pueblo peoples speak languages from several unrelated families — Tanoan (Tewa, Tiwa, Towa), Keresan, Zuni and Hopi (Uto-Aztecan). Their pottery (notably that of Maria Martinez of San Ildefonso), jewellery and weaving are celebrated arts.

Modern pueblos

Today there are 19 federally recognised Pueblo tribes in New Mexico, plus the Hopi Tribe in Arizona and Ysleta del Sur Pueblo in Texas, each a sovereign nation with its own government, many maintaining their languages and ceremonies while operating modern enterprises.
