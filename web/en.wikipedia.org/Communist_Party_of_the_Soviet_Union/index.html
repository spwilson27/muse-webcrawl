The Communist Party of the Soviet Union (CPSU) was the founding and ruling political party of the Soviet Union. It originated as the Bolshevik faction of the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party under Vladimir Lenin, seized power in the October Revolution of 1917, and monopolised political power in the USSR until 1990–91.

History

After the revolution and civil war, the party—renamed the Russian Communist Party (Bolsheviks), then the All-Union Communist Party (Bolsheviks) in 1925, and finally the CPSU in 1952—became fused with the state apparatus. Under Joseph Stalin it directed forced collectivisation and industrialisation, and carried out purges that killed or imprisoned millions. Successive leaders Nikita Khrushchev, Leonid Brezhnev, and Mikhail Gorbachev led the party through de-Stalinisation, the "era of stagnation", and finally the reforms of glasnost and perestroika, which loosened the party's grip. The party's leading role was removed from the constitution in 1990; after the failed August 1991 coup, its activities were suspended and then banned, and the Soviet Union dissolved that December.

Organisation

The party was organised on the principle of democratic centralism. Its supreme body was nominally the Party Congress, held every five years in later decades, which elected the Central Committee. Real power lay with the Politburo and the Secretariat, headed by the General Secretary. The party controlled appointments throughout government, industry, and culture through the nomenklatura system, and was paralleled by republican-level parties in the union republics (the Russian republic being governed directly by the all-union party until 1990).
