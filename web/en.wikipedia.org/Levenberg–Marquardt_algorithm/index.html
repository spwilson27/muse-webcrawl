Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm

The Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm (LMA or LM), also known as the damped least-squares method, is used to solve non-linear least squares problems, most commonly in least-squares curve fitting. It interpolates between the Gauss–Newton algorithm and the method of gradient descent, combining the stability of the latter with the speed of the former.

Method

At each iteration the algorithm solves a damped version of the normal equations, (JTJ + λI)δ = JTr, where J is the Jacobian of the residuals and λ is a damping parameter adjusted dynamically: when λ is small the step resembles Gauss–Newton; when a step fails to reduce the error, λ is increased, rotating the step toward gradient descent. The method was first published by Kenneth Levenberg in 1944, rediscovered by Donald Marquardt in 1963, and further developed by others; Marquardt's version scales the damping by the diagonal of JTJ, improving performance when parameters differ greatly in magnitude.

Applications

LMA is a standard solver in scientific software — it underlies MINPACK's widely ported lmder and lmdif routines and implementations in SciPy, Ceres Solver and many curve-fitting packages — and is used in fields from spectroscopy and photogrammetry to training small neural networks.
