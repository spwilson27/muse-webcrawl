Advance healthcare directive

An advance healthcare directive, also known as a living will or advance decision, is a legal document in which a person specifies in advance what medical actions should be taken if they are no longer able to make decisions for themselves because of illness or incapacity.

Forms

Directives may state preferences about life-sustaining treatment, resuscitation and organ donation, and may appoint a healthcare proxy or attorney to decide on the person's behalf. Rules on form and validity vary by jurisdiction; in the United States they are governed by state law, while in England and Wales advance decisions to refuse treatment are recognised by the Mental Capacity Act 2005.

Purpose

Directives aim to preserve patient autonomy and guide families and clinicians in end-of-life care.
