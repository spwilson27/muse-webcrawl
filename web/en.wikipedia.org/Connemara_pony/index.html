The Connemara pony is a pony breed originating in Connemara, County Galway, in western Ireland. It is known for its athleticism, versatility, good temperament and hardiness, and is one of the most popular sport pony breeds in the world.

History

The breed developed over centuries in the harsh Connemara landscape, with local stock influenced by horses arriving through trade and, by tradition, from wrecked Spanish ships. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, Thoroughbred, Arab, Hackney and Welsh blood was introduced to improve the native ponies. The Connemara Pony Breeders' Society was founded in 1923 to preserve and improve the breed, and studbook inspection has maintained its type.

Characteristics

Connemaras have a refined head with a straight or slightly dished profile, a compact deep body, strong legs and good bone. Most are grey, though bay, brown, dun and black also occur. They are sure-footed and agile, suiting them to jumping, eventing, dressage and endurance, and they are widely crossed with Thoroughbreds to produce competition horses.

Distribution

From Ireland the breed has spread worldwide, with breed societies in many European countries, North America, Australia and New Zealand. The annual Clifden Show, held in County Galway each August, is the breed's principal showcase.
