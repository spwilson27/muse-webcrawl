Overfitting

In statistics and machine learning, overfitting occurs when a model learns the training data too closely — capturing noise and random fluctuations as if they were real structure — so that it performs well on the training set but poorly on new, unseen data. The opposite problem, a model too simple to capture the underlying pattern, is called underfitting.

Causes

Overfitting is more likely when a model is very complex relative to the amount of training data, when the data are noisy, or when training continues for too long. It is formalized in the bias–variance tradeoff: flexible models have low bias but high variance, making them sensitive to the particular sample used for training.

Prevention

Standard remedies include holding out validation and test sets, cross-validation, regularization (such as L1/Lasso and L2/Ridge penalties), pruning decision trees, dropout in neural networks, early stopping, and gathering more training data. In modern deep learning, very large models sometimes generalize surprisingly well despite fitting training data perfectly, a phenomenon studied as "double descent".
