The Basilica of the Sacred Heart of Paris (French: Basilique du Sacré-Cœur) is a Roman Catholic minor basilica crowning the hill of Montmartre, the highest point in Paris. Built of white travertine stone, it is one of the city's most recognisable landmarks.

History

The basilica was vowed after France's defeat in the Franco-Prussian War and the turmoil of the Paris Commune, and authorised by the National Assembly in 1873 as an act of national expiation. Designed by Paul Abadie in a Romano-Byzantine style, construction ran from 1875 to 1914; consecration was delayed by the First World War until 1919. Inside, one of the world's largest mosaics of Christ in Majesty covers the apse, and perpetual adoration of the Blessed Sacrament has continued day and night since 1885.
