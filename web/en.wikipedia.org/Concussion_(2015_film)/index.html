Concussion is a 2015 American biographical sports drama film written and directed by Peter Landesman, based on the 2009 GQ article "Game Brain" by Jeanne Marie Laskas. The film stars Will Smith as Dr. Bennet Omalu, the Nigerian-American forensic pathologist who first discovered and published findings on chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) in American football players, and his struggle against the National Football League's efforts to suppress his research.

Plot

Working in the Allegheny County coroner's office in Pittsburgh, Omalu performs the autopsy of former Pittsburgh Steelers centre Mike Webster, who died after years of mental decline. Finding no structural explanation, Omalu examines Webster's brain tissue and identifies neurofibrillary tangles resembling Alzheimer's disease, which he names chronic traumatic encephalopathy. Publishing his findings with colleagues, he faces fierce resistance from the NFL, which demands retractions and discredits him, even as more former players die by suicide and are found to have CTE. Supported by former Steelers physician Julian Bailes and his wife Prema, Omalu persists; years later the league is forced to confront the issue in congressional hearings, and Omalu declines an offer to become Chief Medical Examiner of the District of Columbia, choosing to remain with his family.

Cast

Will Smith stars as Bennet Omalu, with Alec Baldwin as Julian Bailes, Gugu Mbatha-Raw as Prema Mutiso, Albert Brooks as Cyril Wecht, and Arliss Howard as Pittsburgh mayor Joseph Doyle. Luke Wilson appears briefly as NFL commissioner Roger Goodell.

Reception

The film received generally mixed-to-positive reviews, with widespread praise for Smith's performance, for which he was nominated for the Golden Globe Award for Best Actor in a Drama. It grossed about $48.6 million worldwide against a reported $35 million budget, and it renewed public debate about head injuries and player safety in American football.
