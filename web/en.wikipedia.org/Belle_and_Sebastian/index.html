Belle and Sebastian

Belle and Sebastian are a Scottish indie pop band formed in Glasgow in 1996. Led by songwriter Stuart Murdoch, the band developed from a college music-business project into one of the most beloved acts of the indie era, known for literate, wistful songs arranged with strings, horns, and gentle 1960s-inflected pop craftsmanship. Their second album, If You're Feeling Sinister (1996), is frequently listed among the greatest albums of the 1990s. The band takes its name from Belle et Sébastien, the 1965 children's novel by Cécile Aubry about a boy and his dog.

History

Murdoch, recovering from chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), wrote songs while on a government training scheme at Stow College; the band's first album, Tigermilk, was recorded as a class project in 1996 in a limited pressing and reissued in 1999. If You're Feeling Sinister followed the same year and built a devoted following despite the band's early refusal of interviews and publicity photos. A run of EPs, The Boy with the Arab Strap (1998) — which won the BRIT Award for Best Newcomer in 1999 in a famous upset — and Fold Your Hands Child, You Walk Like a Peasant (2000) consolidated their reputation. Later records such as Dear Catastrophe Waitress (2003), The Life Pursuit (2006), Write About Love (2010), Girls in Peacetime Want to Dance (2015), and A Bit of Previous (2022) broadened their palette toward soul, disco, and synth-pop. Murdoch also directed the film God Help the Girl (2014).

Style and legacy

Belle and Sebastian's hushed early aesthetic — associated with "twee" — belied sharp, funny, melancholic writing about school, faith, illness, and outsiders. They are frequently cited alongside The Smiths and Nick Drake, and their DIY beginnings helped define independent music's post-1990s template.
