Cheque

A cheque (or check in American English) is a document that orders a bank to pay a specific amount of money from a person's account to the person in whose name the cheque has been issued. The person writing the cheque is known as the drawer, the bank on which it is drawn is the drawee, and the person to whom it is payable is the payee.

Characteristics

A cheque is a negotiable instrument instructing a financial institution to pay a specific sum from a demand deposit account. Standard printed cheques carry the amount in words and figures, the date, the payee's name, the drawer's signature, and machine-readable MICR encoding of the bank routing and account numbers. Variants include crossed cheques, bearer and order cheques, post-dated cheques, traveller's cheques, and bank drafts or cashier's cheques guaranteed by the issuing bank.

History

Cheque-like instruments trace to ancient Roman praescriptiones and to the sakk used in the medieval Islamic world. Modern cheques developed in 17th-century England and the Netherlands and became the dominant non-cash payment method in many countries during the 20th century.

Decline and clearing

Usage has fallen sharply in many countries in favour of debit cards and electronic transfers; some countries, such as the Netherlands and Sweden, have largely abandoned cheques, while they remain common in the United States and India. Clearing systems now commonly use cheque truncation — imaging the cheque and settling electronically — rather than physically transporting paper.
