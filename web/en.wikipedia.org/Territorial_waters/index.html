Territorial waters

Territorial waters, or the territorial sea, is the belt of coastal water extending at most 12 nautical miles (22.2 km) from the baseline of a coastal state. Within this zone the state exercises sovereignty, subject to the right of innocent passage for foreign vessels. The territorial sea is defined in the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), concluded in 1982.

Baselines

The breadth of the territorial sea is measured from baselines, which normally follow the low-water line along the coast. Where the coastline is deeply indented or fringed with islands, straight baselines may be drawn between appropriate points. Bays, river mouths and permanent harbour works have special rules, and waters landward of the baseline are internal waters, over which the state has full sovereignty.

Rights and duties

Within its territorial sea a coastal state may regulate navigation, fishing, customs, immigration and pollution, and may temporarily suspend innocent passage in specified areas for security reasons. Foreign ships, including warships according to most interpretations, enjoy the right of innocent passage: continuous and expeditious transit that is not prejudicial to the peace, good order or security of the coastal state. Submarines must navigate on the surface and show their flag.

History

The traditional breadth of three nautical miles derived from the "cannon-shot rule" associated with the Dutch jurist Cornelius van Bynkershoek: a state's control extended as far as its shore batteries could reach. Widely varying claims during the 20th century — some states asserted 200 nautical miles — were standardised by UNCLOS at 12 nautical miles, with a separate 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zone beyond it.

Disputes

Territorial sea claims remain contentious in areas such as the South China Sea, the Arctic and the Aegean, where baselines, island status and historic rights are contested. Delimitation between states with opposite or adjacent coasts is generally made by agreement or by reference to the median (equidistance) line.
