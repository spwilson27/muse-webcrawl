Stramenopiles

The stramenopiles, also called heterokonts, are a major lineage of eukaryotes comprising more than 100,000 known species, most of them algae, ranging from unicellular diatoms to giant multicellular kelps. The group is defined by the presence, at some stage of the life cycle, of cells with two unequal flagella, the anterior one bearing stiff hair-like structures called mastigonemes — "stramenopile" means "straw hair", referring to these hairs.

Characteristics

Most stramenopiles are photosynthetic, with chloroplasts surrounded by four membranes, acquired through secondary endosymbiosis of a red alga; they typically contain the pigment fucoxanthin, giving many a golden-brown colour. Others, like the oomycetes (water moulds), have lost photosynthesis and live as saprotrophs or parasites.

Important groups

Diatoms are major contributors to oceanic primary production and the global carbon cycle. Brown algae include kelps forming vast underwater forests. Oomycetes include Phytophthora infestans, cause of the potato late blight behind the Great Irish Famine. Stramenopiles are part of the SAR supergroup with alveolates and rhizarians.
