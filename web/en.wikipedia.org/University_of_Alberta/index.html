The University of Alberta (U of A) is a public research university in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada. Founded in 1908 by Alexander Cameron Rutherford, the province's first premier, and Henry Marshall Tory, its first president, it is one of Canada's top research universities and a member of the U15 group.

Campuses

The university operates five campuses. North Campus in Edmonton, along the river valley, is the original site; South Campus hosts agricultural research and the Foote Field sports complex; Campus Saint-Jean provides French-language programs; Augustana Campus is in Camrose; and an enterprise campus occupies downtown Edmonton. The university also maintains research facilities such as the Muttart Conservatory partnerships and the Edmonton Research Park.

Academics

More than 40,000 students study in some 400 programs across 18 faculties, including medicine and dentistry, engineering, the Alberta School of Business, and the Faculty of Native Studies — the first of its kind in Canada. The university is a world leader in artificial intelligence research through the Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute (Amii), and in energy, virology, and diabetes research; alumnus and affiliated researchers include Nobel laureate Richard Taylor in earlier eras and teams behind the Edmonton Protocol for islet transplantation.

Athletics

Men's teams are the Golden Bears and women's teams the Pandas. The Golden Bears hockey and volleyball programs and the Pandas hockey program are among the most decorated in Canadian university sport.
