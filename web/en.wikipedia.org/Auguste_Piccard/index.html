Auguste Antoine Piccard (28 January 1884 – 24 March 1962) was a Swiss physicist, inventor and explorer known for his record-breaking balloon flights into the stratosphere and for inventing the bathyscaphe, the first vessel to carry humans into the deep ocean. His twin brother Jean Félix Piccard was also a pioneering balloonist.

Stratosphere flights

To study cosmic rays, Piccard designed a pressurised aluminium gondola hung beneath a large hydrogen balloon. In May 1931 he and Paul Kipfer reached 15,781 m (51,775 ft), becoming the first humans to enter the stratosphere; a second flight in 1932 reached about 16,200 m. The flights proved that people could survive at altitudes where the sky appeared black.

The bathyscaphe

Applying the balloon principle in reverse, Piccard built the bathyscaphe — a deep-sea vessel with a heavy pressure sphere and a float filled with petrol for buoyancy. His Trieste, built with his son Jacques, descended in 1953 to over 3,000 m; sold to the US Navy, it carried Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh in 1960 to the bottom of the Mariana Trench, nearly 11,000 m down. Auguste Piccard's explorations up and down made him a popular scientific celebrity and an inspiration for the character Professor Calculus in Hergé's Tintin.
