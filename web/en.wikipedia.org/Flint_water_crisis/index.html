Flint water crisis

LocationFlint, Michigan, United States
Began25 April 2014
CauseLead contamination after source switch without corrosion control

The Flint water crisis was a public health crisis in Flint, Michigan, United States, that began in 2014 when the city switched its drinking water source from Lake Huron water treated by Detroit to the Flint River, as a cost-saving measure under state-appointed emergency management.

Contamination

Officials failed to apply corrosion inhibitors to the more corrosive river water, causing lead to leach from ageing pipes into thousands of homes. Residents reported discoloured water, rashes, and hair loss, but their complaints were dismissed for months. Independent testing by Virginia Tech researchers and elevated blood lead levels documented by paediatrician Mona Hanna-Attisha exposed the contamination in 2015.

Aftermath

Flint switched back to its previous supply in October 2015, and state and federal emergency declarations followed. Thousands of lead service lines were replaced, criminal charges were brought against several officials (most later dropped), and a 26 million settlement for residents was approved in 2021. The crisis became a national symbol of environmental injustice and government failure.
