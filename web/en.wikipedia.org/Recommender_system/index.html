Recommender system

Streaming and retail platforms use recommender systems to personalise the items each user sees.

A recommender system (also recommending system, recommendation system, or recommendation engine) is a subclass of information filtering system that provides suggestions for items most pertinent to a particular user. Recommender systems are used in a variety of areas, with commonly recognised examples taking the form of playlist generators for video and music services, product recommenders for online stores, and content recommenders for social media platforms and open web content recommenders.

Approaches

Collaborative filtering builds on the past behaviour of users — ratings, purchases, clicks — to find users with similar tastes or items often liked together, and predicts that a user will like what similar users liked. The Netflix Prize (2006–2009) popularised matrix factorization methods for this task. Content-based filtering instead compares features of items with a profile of the user's past preferences. Hybrid systems combine both approaches and add demographic, contextual or knowledge-based signals. Modern production systems typically use two-stage architectures: a candidate-generation stage retrieves hundreds of items from a catalogue of millions, and a ranking stage orders them with a machine-learning model, increasingly a deep neural network.

Challenges

Well-known problems include the cold start problem for new users and items with no history, data sparsity, popularity bias that amplifies already-famous items, and feedback loops in which recommendations shape the very preferences they measure. Evaluation divides between offline metrics on logged data, such as precision and ranking quality, and online A/B testing on engagement and business outcomes.

Societal effects

Because recommenders mediate much of what people watch, read and buy online, they have been criticized for creating filter bubbles, promoting outrage or addictive use, and giving platforms opaque power over culture and commerce; regulators in the European Union's Digital Services Act now require large platforms to disclose how their recommenders work and to offer at least one option not based on profiling.
