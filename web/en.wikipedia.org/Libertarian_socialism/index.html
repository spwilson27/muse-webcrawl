Libertarian socialism
Political philosophy combining liberty and social equality; advocates a society without hierarchy and private ownership of the means of production.

Libertarian socialism is an anti-authoritarian and anti-capitalist political current that stresses self-governance, freedom of association and direct democracy within a socialist framework. It includes anarchist, Marxist and other traditions that reject both capitalism and centralised state socialism, advocating instead federations of voluntary associations, workers' councils and communes.

History

Roots lie in Enlightenment thought, early socialist movements and the libertarian wing of the First International, where Mikhail Bakunin's collectivist anarchism opposed Karl Marx's centralism. Anarcho-syndicalism became influential in Spain, culminating in the collectivisations during the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939). Later currents include council communism, autonomism, Murray Bookchin's social ecology and Noam Chomsky's writings.

Principles

Core ideas include opposition to domination and hierarchy, workers' self-management of production, decentralisation, free association and mutual aid. Libertarian socialists differ on the role of markets, money and transitional institutions, but share a commitment to abolishing wage labour and private control of productive resources.
