Einsatzgruppen

The Einsatzgruppen (German for "deployment groups" or "task forces") were paramilitary death squads of the SS in Nazi Germany, responsible for mass murder, primarily by shooting, during the Second World War. They operated in German-occupied territories in Eastern Europe and played a central role in the first phase of the Holocaust, sometimes called the "Holocaust by bullets."

Organisation

The Einsatzgruppen were formed under the direction of Reinhard Heydrich, chief of the Reich Security Main Office (RSHA), in coordination with the Wehrmacht. For Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of the Soviet Union in June 1941, four Einsatzgruppen (A, B, C and D), each subdivided into Einsatzkommandos and Sonderkommandos and numbering roughly 3,000 men in total, followed the advancing armies. They were assisted by local auxiliary police and collaborators.

Killings

Initially ordered to murder Jewish men of military age, Communist officials and other designated enemies, the killing expanded within weeks to include Jewish women, children and the elderly, as well as Roma and others. Notorious massacres included Babi Yar near Kyiv, where 33,771 Jews were shot on 29–30 September 1941, and the Rumbula massacre near Riga. By the spring of 1943, the Einsatzgruppen and associated units are estimated to have murdered more than one million people, the great majority of them Jews.

Aftermath

After the war, 24 leaders of the Einsatzgruppen were prosecuted in the Einsatzgruppen trial at Nuremberg (1947–48), the largest of the subsequent Nuremberg proceedings; 14 death sentences were handed down, of which four were carried out. The detailed situation reports the units sent to Berlin — the Ereignismeldungen — remain key documentary evidence of the Holocaust.
