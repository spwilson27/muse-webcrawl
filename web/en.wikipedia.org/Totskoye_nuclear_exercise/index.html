Totskoye nuclear exercise


The explosion at Totskoye, 14 September 1954

The Totskoye nuclear exercise was a military training exercise conducted by the Soviet Union on 14 September 1954 at the Totskoye range in Orenburg Oblast, in the Southern Urals. It involved the detonation of a live RDS-4 atomic bomb of about 40 kilotons, dropped from a Tu-4 bomber, followed by manoeuvres of some 45,000 troops through the area near the explosion.

The exercise, codenamed Snezhok ("Snowball"), was commanded by Marshal Georgy Zhukov. Its purpose was to explore the possibility of offensive operations through a zone in which nuclear weapons had been used — testing whether troops, armour and aircraft could advance through a nuclear breach in an enemy's defences. Soldiers were issued protective equipment and, in some cases, manoeuvred within a few kilometres of ground zero shortly after the blast; civilian villages nearby were evacuated and their inhabitants later compensated.

The exercise remained classified until the early 1990s. After the collapse of the Soviet Union, veterans and local residents reported health effects attributed to radiation exposure, and the exercise became controversial, with participants later granted forms of recognition and benefits. It is frequently compared to the American Desert Rock exercises, in which U.S. troops also manoeuvred near live nuclear detonations.
