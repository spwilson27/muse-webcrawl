Hartree–Fock method

In computational physics and quantum chemistry, the Hartree–Fock (HF) method is a method of approximation for the determination of the wave function and the energy of a quantum many-body system in a stationary state.

Overview

The Hartree–Fock method often assumes that the exact N-body wave function of the system can be approximated by a single Slater determinant of N spin-orbitals. By invoking the variational method, one can derive a set of N coupled equations (the Hartree–Fock equations) for the N spin orbitals, whose solution gives the Hartree–Fock wave function and energy. The method was developed from the earlier Hartree method by Vladimir Fock and John C. Slater around 1930.

Hartree–Fock treats electron–electron interactions in an average (mean-field) way: each electron moves in the average field created by all the others. It neglects electron correlation beyond the exchange interaction required by antisymmetry, and therefore serves as the starting point for post-Hartree–Fock methods such as Møller–Plesset perturbation theory, configuration interaction and coupled cluster theory.
