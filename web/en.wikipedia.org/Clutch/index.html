Clutch

A clutch is a mechanical device that engages and disengages the transmission of power between two rotating shafts, typically between a driving shaft connected to a power source and a driven shaft connected to an output. Clutches allow one shaft to be started or stopped without stopping the other.

Types

Friction clutches, such as the single-plate and multi-plate designs used in motor vehicles, transmit torque through friction surfaces pressed together by springs. Other types include dog clutches, centrifugal clutches, cone clutches, electromagnetic clutches, and one-way (freewheel) clutches.

Automotive use

In a vehicle with a manual transmission, the driver operates the clutch with a foot pedal. Pressing the pedal separates the pressure plate from the flywheel, interrupting power from the engine to the gearbox so gears can be changed; releasing it smoothly re-engages drive.

Applications

Clutches are found in automobiles, motorcycles, chainsaws, machine tools, and many other machines.
