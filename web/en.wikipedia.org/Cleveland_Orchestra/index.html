The Cleveland Orchestra, based in Cleveland, Ohio, is one of the five American orchestras informally referred to as the "Big Five." Founded in 1918, it is renowned for its precision, clarity of ensemble, and distinctive string sound. Its winter home is Severance Hall; in summer it performs at Blossom Music Center in nearby Cuyahoga Falls and annually at the Lucerne Festival in Switzerland.

History

The orchestra was founded by the pianist and impresario Adella Prentiss Hughes, with Nikolai Sokoloff as its first music director (1918–1933). Under Artur Rodziński (1933–1943) it gained national prominence, and Erich Leinsdorf continued its development. The orchestra's legendary era began with George Szell, music director from 1946 to 1970, who rebuilt the ensemble into one of the world's finest, famous for flawless ensemble playing, extensive recordings, and the opening of Severance Hall (1931) as its refined home. Lorin Maazel (1972–1982) broadened the repertoire with more 20th-century and avant-garde works. Christoph von Dohnányi (1984–2002) strengthened the orchestra's European profile, and Franz Welser-Möst, appointed in 2002, has led it through extensive tours, residencies in Vienna, Lucerne and Miami, and acclaimed opera productions.

Sound and repertoire

The Cleveland sound is characterised by transparent textures, razor-sharp attacks, and a homogeneous string tone cultivated through decades of consistent personnel and disciplined rehearsal. The orchestra is noted for interpretations of the Austro-German symphonic repertoire — Beethoven, Brahms, Mahler and Strauss — alongside contemporary music. Its extensive discography, particularly the Szell-era recordings for Epic/Columbia, remains a reference.

Community and education

The orchestra operates the Cleveland Orchestra Youth Orchestra, children's choruses, extensive school programmes, and the Severance Hall open-house events that draw the wider Northeast Ohio community. Its musicians are active in chamber music through the Kent/Blossom Music summer institute.
