Henan (Chinese: 河南; pinyin: Hénán) is a province in Central China. Its name means "south of the (Yellow) River", as most of the province lies south of the Yellow River. Henan is often called Zhongyuan or the Central Plain, and is widely considered the cradle of Chinese civilisation: the capitals of more than twenty dynasties stood within its borders over some 3,000 years, and four of the Eight Great Ancient Capitals of China — Luoyang, Kaifeng, Zhengzhou, and Anyang — are located in Henan.

History

The Neolithic Yangshao and Longshan cultures flourished in the Yellow River valley of Henan, and the earliest Chinese dynasties were centred here: the Xia dynasty is traditionally associated with western Henan, and the Shang dynasty built its last capital at Yin, near modern Anyang, where oracle bones bearing the earliest Chinese writing have been excavated. Luoyang served as capital to the Eastern Zhou, Eastern Han, and several later dynasties, while Kaifeng was the capital of the Northern Song. Henan later suffered severely from Yellow River floods, famine, and war, including the catastrophic 1938 Yellow River flood.

Geography

The eastern and central parts of Henan belong to the flat North China Plain, while mountains — the Taihang, Funiu, and Dabie ranges among them — rise in the west and south. The Yellow River crosses the north of the province, and the Huai River rises in its south. Henan borders Hebei, Shanxi, Shaanxi, Hubei, Anhui, Jiangsu, and Shandong.

Economy

Henan is one of China's largest agricultural producers, leading in wheat output, and has substantial food-processing, textiles, aluminium, coal, and electronics industries. Zhengzhou is a national transport hub and home to one of the world's largest smartphone assembly plants. Despite rapid growth, the province's large population keeps its GDP per capita below the national average.

Culture

Henan is the birthplace of Shaolin kung fu at the Shaolin Monastery on Mount Song, near Dengfeng, and of tai chi traditions. Its cultural sites include the Longmen Grottoes near Luoyang, the White Horse Temple — traditionally China's first Buddhist temple — and the ruins of Yin at Anyang, several of which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
