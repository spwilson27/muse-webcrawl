Dianthus barbatus

FamilyCaryophyllaceae
GenusDianthus
Common nameSweet William
Native rangeSouthern Europe to western Asia

Dianthus barbatus, commonly known as sweet William, is a species of flowering plant in the carnation family Caryophyllaceae. It is a short-lived herbaceous perennial or biennial grown widely as an ornamental garden plant.

Description

Plants grow 30–75 cm tall with dense, flat-topped clusters of small flowers, each about 2–3 cm across with serrated petals in shades of pink, red, purple, and white, often with contrasting eyes. The flowers are edible and are said to attract bees, birds, and butterflies.

Cultivation

Sweet William is a traditional cottage-garden plant, self-seeding readily. Numerous cultivars have been selected for flower color and pattern, including the dark 'Nigricans' group. The species epithet barbatus refers to the fringed ("bearded") bracts beneath the flowers.
