Cervidae

Cervidae is a family of hoofed ruminant mammals commonly known as deer. Species include the red deer, elk (wapiti), moose, reindeer (caribou), fallow deer, roe deer, and muntjacs. They are native to all continents except Australia and Antarctica, though many have been widely introduced.

Characteristics

Males of most species grow antlers — bony structures shed and regrown annually — while females generally lack them, except in reindeer. Deer are herbivores with a four-chambered stomach adapted to fermenting plant material. Sizes range from the small pudú to the moose, the largest member.

Evolution and ecology

Deer evolved in Eurasia during the Miocene and spread to the Americas. They are important prey for large predators and can strongly shape vegetation where abundant; in many regions they are managed as game animals.
