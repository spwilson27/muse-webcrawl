Spectroscopic binary

FieldAstronomy
DetectionDoppler shifts in spectral lines
RelatedVisual binary, eclipsing binary, radial velocity

A spectroscopic binary is a binary star system in which the two components are too close together, or too distant from Earth, to be resolved as separate stars visually, but whose binary nature is revealed by periodic Doppler shifts in their spectral lines as they orbit their common centre of mass.

Types

In double-lined systems (SB2), lines from both stars are visible and shift in opposite directions; in single-lined systems (SB1), only one star's spectrum is seen, and the companion's mass can be estimated only through the mass function. The first spectroscopic binary, Mizar, was identified by Edward Pickering in 1889.

Significance

Spectroscopic binaries provide measurements of stellar masses, among the most fundamental data in astrophysics, and the radial-velocity technique used to study them is the same method that discovered the first exoplanets around sun-like stars.
