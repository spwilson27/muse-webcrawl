Ferdinand III of Castile

Ferdinand III (c. 1199/1201 – 30 May 1252), called the Saint (el Santo), was King of Castile from 1217 and King of León from 1230, ruling the permanently united crowns as Ferdinand III from 1231. He was one of the most successful kings of the Reconquista, conquering most of the remaining Muslim territories of al-Andalus, including Córdoba, Jaén, and Seville. He was canonised by Pope Clement X in 1671.

Accession and union of the crowns

Ferdinand was the son of Alfonso IX of León and Berengaria of Castile. On the death of his uncle Henry I of Castile in 1217, his mother inherited the Castilian throne and promptly abdicated in his favour. When Alfonso IX died in 1230, Ferdinand succeeded to León despite his father's attempt to leave the kingdom to his daughters; the resulting settlement, brokered by Berengaria, united Castile and León permanently under one monarch.

Conquests in al-Andalus

Exploiting the disintegration of Almohad power, Ferdinand campaigned almost annually in the Guadalquivir valley. He took Córdoba in 1236, Jaén in 1246, and — after a long siege aided by a fleet under Ramón de Bonifaz — Seville in 1248, the richest prize of his reign. By his death, the only remaining Muslim state in Iberia was the Emirate of Granada, which survived as a Castilian vassal paying tribute.

Rule and legacy

Ferdinand reorganised his conquered territories, distributing lands, granting charters (fueros), and converting mosques into churches, most famously the Great Mosque of Córdoba. He founded the cathedral of Burgos's later construction campaigns and supported the University of Salamanca. He died in Seville in 1252 and was buried in its cathedral; he was succeeded by his son Alfonso X, called the Wise.
