Heart transplantation

Heart transplantation is a surgical procedure in which a failing, diseased heart is replaced with a healthier heart from a deceased organ donor. It is a treatment of last resort for end-stage heart failure when medical therapy and devices can no longer sustain life. The first human heart transplant was performed by Christiaan Barnard in Cape Town in December 1967. Today roughly 5,500–6,000 heart transplants are performed worldwide each year, with median survival exceeding 12 years.

History

Norman Shumway at Stanford pioneered the surgical technique in animals in the 1960s. Barnard's patient Louis Washkansky survived 18 days; his second patient, Philip Blaiberg, lived 19 months. Early enthusiasm faded amid rejection problems, but the introduction of ciclosporin in the early 1980s transformed outcomes and made transplantation routine. Pioneers included Shumway, Richard Lower and Bruce Reitz, who performed the first successful heart–lung transplant in 1981.

Procedure and donor allocation

Donor hearts come from brain-dead donors matched by blood group, size and antibody compatibility, and are allocated by urgency status through national registries such as UNOS in the United States. In the standard bicaval technique the recipient's heart is removed and the donor organ is sewn to the venae cavae, aorta, pulmonary artery and left atrium, usually with the donor heart ischaemic for under four hours; newer perfusion systems can extend preservation and enable donation after circulatory death.

Aftercare and complications

Recipients require lifelong immunosuppression — typically tacrolimus, mycophenolate and corticosteroids — leaving them vulnerable to infection and some cancers. Routine endomyocardial biopsies (increasingly supplemented by gene-expression and cell-free DNA tests) monitor for acute rejection. The major long-term threat is cardiac allograft vasculopathy, a diffuse narrowing of the transplanted heart's arteries. Patients who cannot receive a donor heart may be bridged with a ventricular assist device or a total artificial heart; xenotransplantation with genetically modified pig hearts was first attempted in humans in 2022.
