Anne, Queen of Great Britain

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anne (6 February 1665 – 1 August 1714) was Queen of England, Scotland and Ireland from 1702, and from 1 May 1707, following the Acts of Union, Queen of Great Britain and Ireland until her death. She was the last monarch of the House of Stuart.

Life and reign

Anne was the younger daughter of James II and VII and his first wife, Anne Hyde. Raised as a Protestant, she married Prince George of Denmark in 1683. She supported the Glorious Revolution of 1688 against her father and succeeded her brother-in-law William III in 1702. Her reign was dominated by the War of the Spanish Succession, in which John Churchill, Duke of Marlborough, won famous victories at Blenheim, Ramillies and Oudenarde, and by rivalry between Whig and Tory parties, in which Anne's close friendship with Sarah Churchill, Duchess of Marlborough, was politically significant.

Personal sorrow and succession

Anne endured at least seventeen pregnancies, but none of her children survived to adulthood; the death of her son Prince William, Duke of Gloucester, in 1700 prompted the Act of Settlement 1701, which settled the succession on the Protestant House of Hanover. Plagued by ill health, including gout, Anne died in 1714 and was succeeded by George I.
