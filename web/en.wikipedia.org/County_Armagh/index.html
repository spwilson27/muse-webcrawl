County Armagh (from Irish Ard Mhacha, "Macha's height") is one of the six counties of Northern Ireland and one of the traditional thirty-two counties of Ireland. It lies in the province of Ulster, south of Lough Neagh, and is nicknamed the "Orchard County" for its many apple orchards.

Geography

The county ranges from rich drumlin farmland in the north to the rugged Ring of Gullion and Slieve Gullion — at 573 metres its highest point — in the south. The city of Armagh, its county town, is the ecclesiastical capital of Ireland, seat of both Catholic and Church of Ireland archbishops, with two cathedrals dedicated to Saint Patrick.

History

Armagh's importance reaches back to Emain Macha (Navan Fort), the ancient royal capital of Ulster just west of the modern city. In the twentieth century, South Armagh became known for intense activity during the Troubles, while the county town and its Georgian heritage remained a centre of religion, astronomy (the Armagh Observatory and Planetarium), and education.

Economy and culture

The county is famous for Bramley apples — Armagh Bramleys hold EU protected status — and for towns such as Lurgan, Portadown, and Craigavon, the planned new town developed from the 1960s. Gaelic football is strong, and Armagh's county team won the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship in 2002 and again in 2024.
