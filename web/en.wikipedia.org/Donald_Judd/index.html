Donald Judd
Born: 3 June 1928, Excelsior Springs, Missouri
Died: 12 February 1994, New York City
Movement: Minimalism

Donald Judd

Donald Clarence Judd (1928–1994) was an American artist associated with Minimalism, known for his geometric, three-dimensional works made from industrial materials such as aluminum, steel, and Plexiglas.

Work

Judd began as a painter and critic before turning to "specific objects" — works that were neither painting nor sculpture, intended to exist as physical objects in real space rather than as illusionistic images. His best-known series are the "stacks," vertical progressions of identical rectangular boxes mounted on a wall, and "progressions," sequences of forms whose dimensions follow mathematical intervals.

Marfa

In the 1970s Judd moved to Marfa, Texas, where he bought former army buildings and installed permanent large-scale works. The Chinati Foundation, which he founded, continues to preserve these installations. His writings, including the essay "Specific Objects" (1965), remain central to the theory of minimal art.
