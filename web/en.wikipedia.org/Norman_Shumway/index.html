Norman Shumway

Norman Edward Shumway
Born: February 9, 1923, Kalamazoo, Michigan, U.S.
Died: February 10, 2006, Palo Alto, California, U.S.
Alma mater: Vanderbilt University
Known for: First successful human-to-human heart transplant in the United States

Norman Edward Shumway (February 9, 1923 – February 10, 2006) was an American cardiac surgeon at Stanford University who is widely regarded as the father of heart transplantation. In January 1968 he performed the first successful human-to-human heart transplant in the United States, weeks after Christiaan Barnard's pioneering operation in South Africa.

Early life and education

Shumway was born in Kalamazoo, Michigan, and raised in Michigan and Texas. He studied at Baylor University and the University of Michigan before being drafted into the United States Army, which sent him to medical school at Vanderbilt University, where he earned his M.D. in 1949. He completed surgical training at the University of Minnesota, working under Owen Wangensteen, and earned a Ph.D. in surgery in 1956.

Transplant research

At Stanford, Shumway and his colleague Richard Lower developed the surgical technique of orthotopic heart transplantation in laboratory animals, demonstrating that a transplanted heart could sustain life. Their work established the operative approach and the postoperative management used worldwide. On January 6, 1968, Shumway transplanted a donor heart into 54-year-old Mike Kasperak at Stanford Hospital; the patient survived 15 days, and the program steadily improved outcomes thereafter.

Later career

Shumway remained at Stanford for the rest of his career, training a generation of transplant surgeons and refining immunosuppressive therapy, including the introduction of cyclosporine, which greatly improved survival. He received numerous honors, including the Lister Medal in 1994. He died of lung cancer in Palo Alto in 2006, one day after his 83rd birthday.
