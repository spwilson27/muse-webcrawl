Montreal Cognitive Assessment

AbbreviationMoCA
DeveloperZiad Nasreddine
Published2005
Score range0–30
Administrationabout 10 minutes

The Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) is a widely used one-page screening test for cognitive impairment and early dementia. It was created in 1996 in Montreal by neurologist Ziad Nasreddine and published in validated form in 2005, after studies showed it was more sensitive than the older Mini-Mental State Examination at detecting mild cognitive impairment.

The MoCA samples several cognitive domains on a single page.

Contents

The test assesses visuospatial and executive function (trail making, cube copy, clock drawing), naming, memory (delayed recall of five words), attention (digit span, letter tapping, serial subtraction), language (sentence repetition, fluency), abstraction and orientation. The maximum score is 30, and a score of 26 or above is generally considered normal; a point may be added for individuals with 12 or fewer years of education. Versions exist for people with low literacy and for the blind.

Use and reception

The MoCA has been translated into dozens of languages and adopted worldwide in clinical practice and research on Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, stroke and other conditions. It drew wide public attention in 2018 and again in later years when US president Donald Trump reported scoring perfectly on it. Since 2019, certification and training are required to administer and interpret the test professionally.
