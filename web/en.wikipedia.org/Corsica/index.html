Corsica

Corsica (French: Corse; Corsican: Corsica) is an island in the Mediterranean Sea and one of the 18 regions of France. It is the fourth-largest island in the Mediterranean, located southeast of the French mainland and west of Italy. Its capital is Ajaccio, the birthplace of Napoleon Bonaparte. The island has a population of roughly 350,000.

Geography

Corsica is famously mountainous — a "mountain in the sea" — with Monte Cinto rising to 2,706 metres. The GR20 long-distance trail crosses the island's spine. The coastline stretches over 1,000 km and includes the dramatic calanques of Piana and the Scandola nature reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

History

The island was ruled for centuries by the Republic of Genoa. In the 1750s Pasquale Paoli led an independent Corsican republic, one of the first modern democratic states, before France acquired the island by the Treaty of Versailles in 1768 and annexed it in 1769 — the year of Napoleon's birth in Ajaccio. A nationalist movement, sometimes violent, has sought greater autonomy or independence in modern times.

Culture and language

Corsican, a Romance language closely related to Tuscan Italian, is recognised as a regional language. The island is known for polyphonic singing, the vendetta tradition in its history, maquis scrubland, and products such as chestnuts, charcuterie and Brocciu cheese.

Administration

Since 2018, Corsica has had a single territorial collectivity with an elected Corsican Assembly, giving it a special status among French regions.
