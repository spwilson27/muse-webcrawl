Eurystheus

In Greek mythology, Eurystheus (Greek: Εὐρυσθεύς) was king of Tiryns and Mycenae in the Argolid, and the taskmaster of the hero Heracles. Born prematurely through the scheming of Hera, he inherited the kingship of the Perseid line that Zeus had intended for Heracles.

The Labours of Heracles

After Heracles, in a fit of madness sent by Hera, killed his own children, the Delphic oracle commanded him to serve Eurystheus for twelve years and perform the labours imposed on him. Eurystheus, advised by Hera and frightened of the hero, hid in a great bronze storage jar (pithos) and issued his orders through a herald, Copreus. He set the famous Twelve Labours — beginning with slaying the Nemean lion and the Lernaean Hydra — and repeatedly disqualified completed tasks (refusing to count the Hydra because Heracles had help, and the Augean stables because he took payment), adding extra labours to keep the total at twelve.

Later life and death

After Heracles' death, Eurystheus persecuted the hero's children, the Heracleidae, who took refuge in Athens. In the ensuing war Eurystheus was defeated and killed — in Euripides' play Heracleidae he is captured and executed by Alcmene, Heracles' mother, who gouges out his eyes. His sons perished with him, ending his line.
