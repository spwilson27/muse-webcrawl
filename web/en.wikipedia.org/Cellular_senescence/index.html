Cellular senescence
Process: Irreversible cell-cycle arrest
Discovered: Leonard Hayflick, 1960s
Markers: p16, p21, SA-β-gal

Cellular senescence is a stable state of cell-cycle arrest in which cells remain metabolically active but no longer divide. It is a response to DNA damage, telomere shortening, oncogene activation and other stresses, and acts as a tumour-suppressor mechanism while also contributing to ageing.

Mechanisms

Senescence is driven mainly by the p53–p21 and p16–Rb pathways. Senescent cells enlarge, alter gene expression, and secrete inflammatory factors known as the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), which affects neighbouring tissue.

Roles in health and disease

Transient senescence aids wound healing and embryonic development. Chronic accumulation is linked to osteoarthritis, fibrosis, atherosclerosis and frailty. Senolytic drugs that selectively clear senescent cells are under investigation.
