Neville Chamberlain

Arthur Neville Chamberlain (18 March 1869 – 9 November 1940) was a British politician of the Conservative Party who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from May 1937 to May 1940 and Leader of the Conservative Party from May 1937 to October 1940. He is best known for his foreign policy of appeasement, and in particular for his signing of the Munich Agreement on 30 September 1938, which ceded the German-speaking Sudetenland region of Czechoslovakia to Nazi Germany.

Early career

The son of Joseph Chamberlain and half-brother of Austen Chamberlain, Neville Chamberlain entered Parliament in 1918 as MP for Birmingham Ladywood, later moving to Birmingham Edgbaston. He served as Minister of Health, where he drove housing and local-government reforms, and twice as Chancellor of the Exchequer, most notably through the Depression years 1931–1937.

Prime Minister

Succeeding Stanley Baldwin in 1937, Chamberlain pursued negotiations with Adolf Hitler to avoid war. After the Munich Agreement he famously declared that he believed "it is peace for our time." When Germany occupied the rest of Czechoslovakia in March 1939, his government guaranteed Poland; following the German invasion of Poland, Britain declared war on 3 September 1939.

Fall and death

After the failure of the Norwegian campaign, Chamberlain lost the confidence of the House of Commons in May 1940 and resigned, being succeeded by Winston Churchill. He remained in the War Cabinet as Lord President of the Council until ill health forced his resignation in October 1940; he died of cancer the following month.

Legacy

Chamberlain's reputation was long dominated by the verdict of "appeaser," notably in the 1940 polemic Guilty Men. Later historians have offered more sympathetic readings, noting the weakness of Britain's military position, public opinion, and the time rearmament gained.
