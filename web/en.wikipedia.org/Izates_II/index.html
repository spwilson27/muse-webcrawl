Izates II

Adiabene lay in northern Mesopotamia
Reign: c. 34 – c. 54 AD
Kingdom: Adiabene
Dynasty: Royal house of Adiabene
Religion: Converted to Judaism

Izates II (c. 1 – c. 54 AD) was king of Adiabene, a vassal kingdom of the Parthian Empire in northern Mesopotamia (modern northern Iraq). He is famous in Jewish history for his conversion to Judaism, an episode recorded in detail by the historian Josephus in the Antiquities of the Jews.

Conversion

Izates was the son of Queen Helena of Adiabene and King Monobaz I. While staying at Charax Spasinu as a young man, he was instructed in Judaism by a Jewish merchant named Ananias, and his mother was likewise drawn to Jewish practice. On succeeding to the throne (c. 34 AD), Izates decided to convert fully, including circumcision, despite court fears — reported by Josephus — that his subjects would resent a ruler following foreign customs. His reign, in the event, was long and prosperous, and Josephus presents the conversion as met with divine protection.

Reign

Izates navigated the dangerous politics between Parthia and Rome, granted asylum to the deposed Parthian king Artabanus II and helped restore him, and was rewarded with territory. He sent several of his sons to Jerusalem to be educated in Jewish language and law, and his family became major benefactors of Jerusalem: Queen Helena built palaces there, sent aid during the famine under Claudius, and was buried with Izates in the royal tombs north of the city (the so-called Tombs of the Kings). Members of the Adiabene royal family later fought against Rome in the First Jewish–Roman War.
