Shibuya Station (渋谷駅, Shibuya-eki) is a major railway station complex in Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan, serving as one of the busiest transport hubs in the world and the gateway to the Shibuya district. Operated jointly by JR East, Tokyu, Keio, and Tokyo Metro, it connects nine lines. Outside its Hachikō exit lies the famous Shibuya Crossing — often called the busiest pedestrian scramble in the world — and the statue of the loyal dog Hachikō, one of Tokyo's best-known meeting points.

History

The station opened in 1885 on the Nippon Railway (later the Yamanote Line). Private railways arrived over the following decades — the Tamagawa (later Tokyu) line in 1927, the Teito (Keio Inokashira) line in 1933, and the first subway, today's Ginza Line, in 1938. In 1934 the bronze statue of Hachikō was erected in honour of the Akita dog who had waited daily at the station for his deceased master for nearly ten years. The station area grew into a centre of youth culture and fashion after the 1970s department-store developments by the Tokyu and Seibu groups.

Layout and lines

JR East's platforms serve the Yamanote, Saikyō, and Shōnan-Shinjuku lines beside the west side of the complex. Tokyu's Den-en-toshi and Tōyoko lines share deep-level platforms with the Tokyo Metro Hanzōmon and Fukutoshin lines, allowing through trains across the city. The Keio Inokashira Line terminates in the Mark City building to the west, while the semi-circular Ginza Line platforms were rebuilt atop the Shibuya Scramble Square building, reopening in 2020 with distinctive arched, M-shaped roof design.

Surrounding area

Shibuya Crossing, directly outside the station, can see thousands of pedestrians cross in a single green cycle and is a global symbol of Tokyo. Nearby are the 109 department store, Center Gai shopping street, Shibuya Sky observation deck, and numerous live-music venues and offices, including a growing cluster of technology companies that has earned the area the nickname "Bit Valley".

Redevelopment

Since 2013 the station district has been undergoing one of Japan's largest urban redevelopments, led by Tokyu: the Shibuya Hikarie tower opened in 2012, Shibuya Stream in 2018, and the 230-metre Shibuya Scramble Square east tower in 2019, with further towers and a reorganisation of the JR platforms — including the relocation of the Saikyō Line platforms alongside the Yamanote platforms in 2020 — under a plan extending toward 2027 and beyond.
