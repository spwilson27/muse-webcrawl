Daniel Barenboim

Daniel Barenboim (born 15 November 1942) is an Argentine-born classical pianist and conductor, holding citizenship in Argentina, Israel, Palestine, and Spain. He is known for his work with the West–Eastern Divan Orchestra, the Berlin Staatskapelle, and the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, and for his advocacy of music as a bridge between Israelis and Palestinians.

Early life and career

Barenboim was born in Buenos Aires to Russian Jewish parents, both piano teachers. A child prodigy, he gave his first concert at seven and studied with Edwin Fischer, Wilhelm Furtwängler, and Nadia Boulanger. His family moved to Israel in 1952. He built an international career as a concert pianist, recording the complete Beethoven piano sonatas while still in his twenties, and increasingly turned to conducting.

Conducting career

Barenboim served as music director of the Orchestre de Paris (1975–1989) and the Chicago Symphony Orchestra (1991–2006), and as general music director of the Berlin State Opera and music director of the Staatskapelle Berlin from 1992 until 2023, when he stepped down for health reasons. He also worked closely with La Scala, Milan. He is renowned for interpretations of Mozart, Beethoven, Brahms, Bruckner, and Wagner — his performances of Wagner in Israel in 2001 provoked public controversy.

West–Eastern Divan Orchestra

In 1999 Barenboim co-founded the West–Eastern Divan Orchestra with the Palestinian scholar Edward Said, bringing together young Arab and Israeli musicians. He became the first person to hold both Israeli citizenship and an honorary Palestinian passport. He co-authored the book Parallels and Paradoxes with Said and has written extensively on music, politics, and reconciliation. He founded the Barenboim–Said Akademie in Berlin, which opened in 2016.

Honours

Barenboim has won multiple Grammy Awards, received honorary knighthood in Britain (KBE, 2011), the German Order of Merit, the French Légion d'honneur, and numerous other honours for his music and peace work.
