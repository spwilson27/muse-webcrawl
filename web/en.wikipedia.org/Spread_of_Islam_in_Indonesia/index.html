Spread of Islam in Indonesia

The spread of Islam in Indonesia took place from roughly the 13th century onward, carried primarily by Muslim traders from Arabia, Gujarat, Persia and China who visited the archipelago's ports. Indonesia is today the world's most populous Muslim-majority country. Unlike the Middle East, Islam in Indonesia spread largely through commerce and preaching rather than conquest, and it layered over older Hindu, Buddhist and indigenous traditions.

Early evidence

Muslim traders were present in Southeast Asian ports from the early centuries of Islam, but the first clear evidence of local conversion comes from northern Sumatra in the late 13th century, where the tombstone of Sultan Malik al Saleh of Samudera Pasai is dated to 1297. Marco Polo reported a Muslim town at Perlak in 1292.

The Wali Sanga

Javanese tradition credits the Islamisation of Java to the Wali Sanga, the "Nine Saints", who are said to have preached along the north coast of Java in the 15th and 16th centuries, founding mosques and pesantren schools and adapting their teaching to Javanese culture, including the use of wayang puppet theatre and gamelan music.

Sultanates and later spread

By the 16th century, Muslim sultanates such as Demak, Banten, Aceh and Mataram dominated much of the archipelago. Islam continued to spread into Sulawesi, Kalimantan and the eastern islands in the following centuries, though Hindu Bali and some interior and eastern regions retained their older religions.
