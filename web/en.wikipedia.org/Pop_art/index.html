Pop art

PeriodMid-1950s to 1970s
CentresLondon, New York
Key artistsAndy Warhol, Roy Lichtenstein, Richard Hamilton, Jasper Johns, Robert Rauschenberg, Claes Oldenburg
SourcesAdvertising, comic books, mass-produced consumer goods, celebrity culture

Pop art is an art movement that emerged in the United Kingdom and the United States during the mid- to late 1950s. The movement presented a challenge to traditions of fine art by including imagery from popular and mass culture, such as advertising, comic books, and mundane mass-produced objects. Its characteristic imagery is often removed from its known context, isolated, or combined with unrelated material.

Origins

In Britain, the Independent Group at the Institute of Contemporary Arts in London discussed consumer culture through the early 1950s; Richard Hamilton's 1956 collage Just what is it that makes today's homes so different, so appealing? is often cited as an early pop work. The critic Lawrence Alloway used the term "pop art" for art engaging with mass culture. In the United States, the work of Jasper Johns and Robert Rauschenberg bridged Abstract Expressionism and pop, before the movement erupted publicly in the early 1960s.

American pop

Andy Warhol became the movement's most famous figure with his silkscreened depictions of Campbell's soup cans, Coca-Cola bottles, and celebrities such as Marilyn Monroe, produced in his studio, the Factory. Roy Lichtenstein enlarged comic-strip panels complete with Ben-Day printing dots; Claes Oldenburg sculpted oversized soft versions of everyday objects; James Rosenquist adapted billboard painting. Pop's cool, ironic embrace of commercial imagery contrasted sharply with the emotional intensity of Abstract Expressionism.

Themes and legacy

Pop art questioned the boundaries between "high" art and commercial culture, authorship and originality, and fine art and mass reproduction. It influenced later movements and remains strongly present in contemporary art, design, and advertising; major pop works now command record auction prices.
