Aphra Behn

Bornc. 1640, Canterbury (?), England
Died16 April 1689, London
OccupationPlaywright, poet, novelist, spy
Notable workOroonoko, The Rover

Aphra Behn (c. 1640 – 16 April 1689) was an English playwright, poet, and prose writer of the Restoration era. She was one of the first English women to earn a living by writing, producing plays, poetry, and fiction, and is regarded as a pioneering figure in the early English novel.

Life

Little is known with certainty of Behn's early life. She may have travelled to Surinam as a young woman — an experience that informed her novel Oroonoko — and in 1666 she worked as a spy in Antwerp for Charles II, using the code name Astrea. Underpaid by the Crown, she was imprisoned briefly for debt.

Works

Her first play, The Forc'd Marriage, was staged in 1670. She wrote some nineteen plays, including the popular comedy The Rover (1677), and became a central figure of Restoration theatre alongside Dryden. Her prose works include the epistolary Love-Letters Between a Nobleman and His Sister (1684–87) and Oroonoko (1688), the story of an enslaved African prince, an early and influential work of English fiction with anti-slavery themes.

Legacy

Behn was buried in Westminster Abbey. Virginia Woolf famously wrote in A Room of One's Own that Behn earned women "the right to speak their minds". Modern scholarship has restored her reputation after centuries of neglect.
