Butter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (recreated from memory)

Butter is a dairy product made from the fat and protein components of churned cream. It is a semi-solid emulsion consisting of approximately 80% butterfat, with water and milk solids making up the remainder. Butter is used as a spread, a cooking fat, and a key ingredient in baking and sauces. It is most commonly made from cow's milk, but can also be produced from the milk of sheep, goats, buffalo, and yaks.

Production

Cream is separated from milk and churned until the fat globules coalesce and separate from the buttermilk. The butter is then washed, sometimes salted, and worked to a uniform consistency. Clarified butter (such as Indian ghee) is made by heating butter to separate and remove the milk solids, giving a higher smoke point and longer shelf life. Cultured butter is produced from fermented cream and has a tangier flavor.

History

Butter making dates back thousands of years among herding peoples. It was valued in ancient India, Egypt, and the Mediterranean world, and later became central to northern European cuisines where cool climates favored butter over olive oil. Industrial churns and cream separators in the 19th century transformed production, and butter became the benchmark against which margarine — invented in 1869 — competed.

Culinary use

Butter is spread on bread, melted over vegetables, and used for sautéing and frying, though its low smoke point limits high-heat cooking. In baking it provides flavor, tenderness, and the lamination in puff pastry and croissants. Classic French sauces such as beurre blanc and hollandaise are butter-based emulsions.
