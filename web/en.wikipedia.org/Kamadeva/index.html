Kamadeva

Kamadeva (Sanskrit: Kāmadeva, "god of desire"), also called Madana, Manmatha and simply Kama, is the Hindu god of erotic love, desire and attraction — a counterpart in some respects to the Greek Eros and Roman Cupid. He is depicted as a handsome youth riding a parrot, armed with a bow made of sugarcane and a string of bees, and arrows tipped with five kinds of fragrant flowers, with which he kindles desire in gods and mortals alike.

Mythology

The best-known myth tells how the gods, needing Shiva to father a son who could defeat the demon Taraka, sent Kamadeva to disturb Shiva's meditation and make him notice the goddess Parvati. Shiva opened his third eye and reduced Kama to ashes; the god was later restored to life — in some versions remaining bodiless, hence his epithet Ananga, "the bodiless one" — after the pleading of his wife Rati. Kālidāsa's poem Kumārasambhava retells this story. In the Vaishnava tradition, Kamadeva is reborn as Pradyumna, the son of Krishna and Rukmini.

Worship and festivals

Kamadeva is associated with the spring festival of Holi in some regions, where his burning by Shiva is ritually re-enacted in parts of South India (Kaman Pandigai). Temples dedicated to him are rare, though he appears in the iconography of many shrines as an attendant deity of love and fertility.
