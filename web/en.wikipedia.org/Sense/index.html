Sense

A sense is a biological system used by an organism to sense its environment and gather information about the world, a process known as perception. Sensory systems consist of sensory neurons, neural pathways, and parts of the brain involved in processing the information.

Traditionally, five senses are recognised in humans: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. However, modern neuroscience recognises many additional senses, including balance, proprioception, nociception (pain), and thermoception (temperature).

Definition

A sense is generally defined as a faculty by which the body perceives an external stimulus. Sensory receptors respond to particular types of energy — such as light, sound pressure, chemicals, or mechanical deformation — and transduce them into neural signals.

Human senses

Beyond the classical five, humans possess an interoceptive awareness of internal states such as hunger and thirst, a vestibular sense of acceleration and orientation, and proprioception, the sense of the relative position of body parts. Estimates of the number of distinct human senses range from nine to more than twenty depending on definitions used.

Senses in other animals

Many animals possess senses absent in humans, including electroreception in sharks and some fish, magnetoreception in migratory birds, echolocation in bats and dolphins, and infrared sensing in pit vipers.
