Baire category theorem

The Baire category theorem (BCT) is an important result in general topology and functional analysis. The theorem has two forms, each of which gives sufficient conditions for a topological space to be a Baire space: namely, every complete metric space and every locally compact Hausdorff space is a Baire space.

Statement

In its standard form: a complete metric space is not the union of countably many nowhere dense sets; equivalently, the countable intersection of open dense sets is dense. A set that is a countable union of nowhere dense sets is called meagre (of the first category); spaces that are not meagre in themselves satisfy the conclusion of the theorem.

History and applications

The theorem is named for René-Louis Baire, who proved a version in his 1899 doctoral thesis for the real line; the general metric result is associated also with work by William F. Osgood. Its consequences in functional analysis include the uniform boundedness principle, the open mapping theorem, and the closed graph theorem, each often derived using Baire category arguments.
