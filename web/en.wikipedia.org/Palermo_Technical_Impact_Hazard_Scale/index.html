Palermo Technical Impact Hazard Scale

TypeLogarithmic risk scale
PurposeComparing near-Earth object impact hazards
Introduced2002 (IAU meeting, Palermo)

The Palermo Technical Impact Hazard Scale is a logarithmic scale used by astronomers to rate the potential hazard of a near-Earth object (NEO) impact. It combines two types of data — probability of impact and the estimated kinetic energy of an impact — into a single "hazard" value, expressed relative to the background hazard from objects of the same energy over the same time span.

Definition

A rating of 0 means the hazard is equivalent to the background hazard; a rating of +2 means the hazard is 100 times greater than a random background event of the same size. Ratings below −2 reflect events for which there are no likely consequences, while ratings between −2 and 0 indicate situations that merit careful monitoring. The scale was adopted following a 2002 IAU meeting in Palermo, Sicily, and is used by NASA's Center for Near-Earth Object Studies and ESA's NEO Coordination Centre in their Sentry and risk lists.

Comparison with the Torino scale

The Palermo scale is primarily a tool for specialists, whereas the simpler Torino scale (integer values 0–10) is designed for public communication. The asteroid 99942 Apophis briefly held a cumulative Palermo rating around −1 in 2004–2005, among the highest ever assigned.
