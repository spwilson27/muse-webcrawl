Confederate States Army

The Confederate States Army, also called the Confederate Army or the Southern Army, was the military land force of the Confederate States of America (commonly referred to as the Confederacy) during the American Civil War (1861–1865), fighting against the United States Army (the Union Army). On 28 February 1861, the Provisional Confederate Congress established a provisional volunteer army and gave control over military operations and authority for mustering state forces and volunteers to the newly chosen Confederate president, Jefferson Davis.

Organisation

The Confederate Army was structured into field armies — including the Army of Northern Virginia under Robert E. Lee and the Army of Tennessee — subdivided into corps, divisions, brigades and regiments, the regiment (about 1,000 men at full strength) being the basic administrative unit. The Confederacy relied first on volunteers and from April 1862 on the first general conscription in American history.

History

Confederate forces fired the first shots of the war at Fort Sumter in April 1861. The army won notable victories at First Bull Run, Fredericksburg and Chancellorsville, but suffered decisive defeats at Gettysburg and Vicksburg in July 1863. Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia at Appomattox Court House on 9 April 1865, and the remaining Confederate armies surrendered over the following weeks, the last in June 1865.

Supply and manpower

Outnumbered and industrially weaker, the Confederacy struggled with shortages of food, clothing and medicine, runaway inflation, and desertion, especially late in the war. Historians estimate that between 250,000 and 360,000 Confederate soldiers died from combat and disease.
