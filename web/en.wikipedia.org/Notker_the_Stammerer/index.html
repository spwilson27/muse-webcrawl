Notker the Stammerer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Notker Balbulus (c. 840 – 6 April 912), also known as Notker the Stammerer, Notker of Saint Gall, or Notker the Poet, was a Benedictine monk of the Abbey of Saint Gall in present-day Switzerland. A scholar, teacher, and musician, he is best remembered as the author of the Liber hymnorum ("Book of Hymns"), whose sequences transformed the liturgical music of the medieval church, and of the Gesta Karoli Magni, a collection of anecdotes about Charlemagne.

Life

Notker was born near Jonschwil in Thurgau, into a family of local standing. His byname Balbulus ("the stammerer") refers to a speech impediment, sometimes attributed to the early loss of his teeth. Educated at the abbey school of Saint Gall under Iso and the Irish monk Marcellus (Moengal), he spent his whole life at the abbey as a monk, teacher, and librarian. His pupils and friends included the community of scholars that made Saint Gall a leading centre of learning in the East Frankish kingdom. He was later beatified in the Roman Catholic Church.

Works

Sequences

According to Notker's own account, a monk fleeing the Norman destruction of Jumièges brought an antiphonary to Saint Gall in which the long melismas of the Alleluia had been fitted with syllabic texts (sequences) to aid memorisation. Notker adapted the technique into original Latin poems of great beauty. His Liber hymnorum, compiled around the 880s, contains about forty sequences including the Easter sequence Laudes Salvatori voce modulemur supplici and the Pentecost sequence Sancti spiritus assit nobis gratia. The sequence Media vita in morte sumus ("In the midst of life we are in death") was long, though probably mistakenly, attributed to him.

Gesta Karoli Magni

Written for the emperor Charles the Fat, who visited Saint Gall in 883, the Gesta Karoli Magni ("Deeds of Charlemagne") is not a systematic biography but a lively series of anecdotes illustrating Charlemagne's piety, justice, and care for learning. Though unreliable in detail, it shaped the medieval legend of Charlemagne and remains an engaging window into Carolingian monastic culture. Notker also wrote a martyrology, treatises on music and rhetoric, letters, and hymns.
