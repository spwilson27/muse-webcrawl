Hyenas (from Ancient Greek hýaina) are feliform carnivoran mammals belonging to the family Hyaenidae. With only four extant species (in four genera), it is the fifth-smallest family in the order Carnivora and one of the smallest in the class Mammalia. Despite their low diversity, hyenas are unique and vital components of most African ecosystems.

Although phylogenetically closer to felines and viverrids, hyenas are behaviourally and morphologically convergent with canids in several respects: both hyenas and canids are non-arboreal, cursorial hunters that catch prey with their teeth rather than claws. Hyenas are famed for their bone-crushing premolars and exceptionally powerful jaws.

Species

  • Spotted hyena (Crocuta crocuta) — the largest and most social species, living in matriarchal clans of up to 80 individuals; an accomplished hunter as well as a scavenger.
  • Striped hyena (Hyaena hyaena) — found from North Africa to India, mostly solitary and largely a scavenger.
  • Brown hyena (Parahyaena brunnea) — confined to southern African deserts and coasts.
  • Aardwolf (Proteles cristatus) — a small insectivorous species feeding mainly on termites.

Evolution

Hyenas originated in the Miocene, about 22 million years ago, as small civet-like tree-dwellers in Eurasia. The family diversified into dog-like hyenas and bone-crushing hyenas; the giant Pachycrocuta was among the largest land predators of its time. Only the bone-crushing lineage and the aardwolf survived the climate changes of the late Pliocene and Pleistocene.

Behaviour

Spotted hyenas live in clans with a strict matrilineal hierarchy; females dominate males, and cubs inherit their mother's rank. They communicate with a rich repertoire of calls, including the famous "laugh" — a giggling sound made by subordinate animals under stress. Contrary to the popular image of cowardly scavengers, spotted hyenas kill most of what they eat and frequently lose kills to lions rather than the reverse.

Relationship with humans

Hyenas appear in African and Middle Eastern folklore as creatures associated with witchcraft. They have been persecuted as livestock predators, and the striped hyena is listed as Near Threatened. In the Ethiopian city of Harar, "hyena men" traditionally feed wild hyenas by hand.
