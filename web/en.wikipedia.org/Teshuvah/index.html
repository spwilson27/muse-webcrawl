Teshuvah

[Placeholder image: Teshuvah.png]

Teshuvah (Hebrew: תשובה, literally 'return') is the Jewish practice of repentance — returning to God and to righteous conduct after transgression. It is a central theme of the High Holy Days, particularly Yom Kippur.

Process

Classical sources, notably Maimonides' Mishneh Torah, describe teshuvah as involving recognition of the sin, remorse, confession (vidui), restitution to the wronged party where applicable, and resolve not to repeat the act. Complete teshuvah, Maimonides writes, is demonstrated when one faces the same opportunity to sin and refrains.
