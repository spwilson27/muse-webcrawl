The Palestinian territories are the two regions of the former British Mandate for Palestine that have been occupied by Israel since the Six-Day War of 1967: the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and the Gaza Strip. Together they form the territory claimed by the State of Palestine, which was proclaimed in 1988 and recognised by a majority of United Nations member states; in 2012 the UN General Assembly accorded Palestine non-member observer state status.

History

Under the UN Partition Plan of 1947, the West Bank and Gaza Strip were to form part of an Arab state; instead, after the 1948 Arab–Israeli War the West Bank was annexed by Jordan and Gaza administered by Egypt. Israel captured both in 1967 and established settlements in them. The Oslo Accords of the 1990s created the Palestinian Authority and divided the West Bank into Areas A, B and C, with full Israeli civil and security control retained over Area C (about 60% of the West Bank). Israel withdrew its settlements and permanent ground presence from Gaza in 2005, while retaining control of its airspace, territorial waters and most crossings; since 2007 Gaza has been governed by Hamas.

Geography and demography

The West Bank (5,655 km²) is a landlocked highland region west of the Jordan River, containing the cities of Ramallah, Nablus, Hebron, Bethlehem and Jericho. The Gaza Strip (365 km²) is a narrow coastal enclave on the Mediterranean, among the most densely populated places in the world, centred on Gaza City. Arabic is the official language and Islam the predominant religion, with a small Christian minority.

Status

The international community, including the International Court of Justice, regards the territories as occupied, a position Israel disputes in part. The territories' final status remains a core issue of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, alongside Jerusalem, refugees, borders and security.
