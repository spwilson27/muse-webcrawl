M1 mortar

The M1 81 mm mortar

Type: infantry mortar
Calibre: 81 mm
Origin: United States
Weight: ~61.5 kg total
Max range: ~3,290 m (HE M43A1)

The M1 mortar is an American 81 millimetre calibre infantry mortar. It was based on the French Brandt mortar, whose licence the United States purchased in the 1930s, and served as the standard medium mortar of the US Army and Marine Corps during the Second World War and the Korean War.

Description

The weapon consists of three components for transport: a smoothbore barrel, a bipod with elevating and traversing mechanisms, and a heavy rectangular baseplate. It is muzzle-loaded and drop-fired: a shell slid down the barrel strikes a fixed firing pin at the base, igniting the propellant. A crew of four to five served the mortar, which could sustain roughly 18 rounds per minute, with higher rates possible for short periods.

Service

The M1 fired high-explosive, smoke, and illuminating rounds and provided battalion-level indirect fire support. It was widely exported and copied; a shortened, lighter airborne version was designated the M29's predecessor line, and the M1 was eventually replaced in US service by the M29 and later the M252 mortar. Variants and copies remained in use around the world for decades after the Second World War.
