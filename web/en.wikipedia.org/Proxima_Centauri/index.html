Proxima Centauri is a small, low-mass star located 4.2465 light-years (1.3020 pc) away from the Sun in the southern constellation of Centaurus. Its Latin name means the "nearest [star] of Centaurus". It was discovered in 1915 by Robert Innes and is the nearest-known star to the Sun. It is part of the Alpha Centauri triple star system.

Characteristics

Proxima Centauri is a red dwarf with about 12% of the Sun's mass and a diameter about 14% of the Sun's. Because of its low luminosity it is far too faint to be seen with the naked eye (apparent magnitude 11.1). It is a flare star, subject to sudden increases in brightness caused by magnetic activity, and rotates slowly, with a period of about 83 days.

Planetary system

In 2016 the European Southern Observatory announced the discovery of Proxima Centauri b, an Earth-sized exoplanet orbiting within the star's habitable zone at a distance of about 0.05 AU, with an orbital period of roughly 11.2 days. A second, smaller planet, Proxima Centauri d, orbiting closer in, was confirmed in 2022; the proposed outer planet Proxima Centauri c remains disputed. Because of its proximity, Proxima b is a target for interstellar probe concepts such as Breakthrough Starshot.
