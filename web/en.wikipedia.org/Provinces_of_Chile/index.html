Provinces of Chile

The provinces of Chile are the second-level administrative divisions of the country. Chile is divided into 16 regions, which are subdivided into 56 provinces, each headed by a presidential delegate appointed by the President of Chile. The provinces are in turn divided into communes.

History

The provincial system dates to the colonial period, but the modern structure was established by the regionalisation reforms of the 1970s under the military government, which created the current regions and reorganised the provinces. A 2017 constitutional reform provided for elected regional governors, who took office in 2021, while provincial presidential delegates retained responsibility for public order and oversight on behalf of the central government.

List

Provinces include Arica and Parinacota in the far north; central provinces such as Santiago, Valparaíso, and Cordillera; and southern provinces including Chiloé, Palena, and Antártica Chilena, the last of which administers Chile's Antarctic claim areas for municipal purposes.
