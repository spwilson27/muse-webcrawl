Uranium-235

Uranium-235 (U-235) is an isotope of uranium that makes up about 0.72% of natural uranium. It is fissile: its nucleus can split when struck by a thermal neutron, releasing energy and more neutrons, sustaining a nuclear chain reaction.

Properties

U-235 has a half-life of about 704 million years and decays by alpha emission. Unlike the more abundant U-238, it fissions readily with slow neutrons.

Uses

Enriched uranium containing higher proportions of U-235 fuels most nuclear power reactors; highly enriched uranium was used in the Little Boy bomb dropped on Hiroshima.

Enrichment

Separating U-235 from U-238, historically by gaseous diffusion and now mainly by gas centrifuges, is a central nuclear proliferation concern.
