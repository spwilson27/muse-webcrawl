Tyrant flycatcher

Tyrant flycatchers
Family: Tyrannidae
Order: Passeriformes
Distribution: North and South America
Species: over 400

The tyrant flycatchers (family Tyrannidae) are a family of passerine birds found across North and South America. With more than 400 species in over 100 genera, they form the largest family of birds in the world.

Description

Most tyrant flycatchers are plainly plumaged in browns, greys, yellows and whites, and many are notoriously difficult to identify except by voice. They range from tiny tody-tyrants about 6.5 cm long to the great shrike-tyrant at about 29 cm. As their name suggests, many sally from a perch to catch insects in flight, returning to the same perch, though the family includes ground-dwelling and fruit-eating species too.

Taxonomy and distribution

The family is entirely New World, most diverse in the tropics of South America. Familiar northern representatives include the phoebes, kingbirds, pewees and the colorful vermilion flycatcher. They belong to the suboscine radiation, differing from Old World songbirds in syringeal anatomy and song development.
