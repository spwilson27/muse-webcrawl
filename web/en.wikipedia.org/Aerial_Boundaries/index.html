Aerial Boundaries

Aerial Boundaries is an album by American fingerstyle guitarist Michael Hedges, released in 1984 on Windham Hill Records. Recorded while Hedges was still a student at the Peabody Conservatory, it is widely regarded as a landmark of modern acoustic guitar, showcasing his two-handed tapping, harmonics, percussive techniques, and use of altered tunings.

Music

The title track is a tour de force of tapped harmonics and slap harmonics, while pieces such as "Rickover's Dream", "Hot Type", and "Spare Change" combine lyrical melody with techniques drawn from Hedges's studies in electronic music and composition. The album closes with a cover of Neil Young's "After the Gold Rush" performed on harp guitar. Hedges described his style as "heavy mental" and helped define the Windham Hill sound.

Reception

Aerial Boundaries has appeared on numerous lists of the greatest acoustic guitar albums, and its techniques influenced generations of fingerstyle players including Andy McKee and Kaki King. Hedges died in a car accident in 1997, aged 43.
