History of education

The history of education traces the development of teaching and learning from informal transmission of skills in prehistory to formal schooling systems. Writing, invented around 3500 BC, enabled the first schools; mass public education is a product of the 19th and 20th centuries.

Ancient education

Sumerian and Egyptian scribal schools trained administrators. In India, gurukula and later Buddhist monastic universities such as Nalanda taught grammar, medicine, and philosophy. Confucian education in China centered on the classics and, from the Sui dynasty, civil service examinations. Greek education varied by city: Sparta's agoge emphasized military training, while Athens combined letters, music, and gymnastics; Plato's Academy and Aristotle's Lyceum pioneered higher learning. Rome adopted Greek models, and Quintilian systematized rhetoric teaching.

Medieval and early modern

Monastic and cathedral schools preserved learning in early medieval Europe; universities emerged at Bologna, Paris, and Oxford in the 11th–13th centuries. Islamic madrasas spread across the Middle East and beyond. Renaissance humanism reshaped curricula, and the printing press cheapened books. Comenius advocated universal education in the 17th century; Prussia pioneered compulsory schooling in the 18th.

Mass schooling

The 19th century saw state systems of free, compulsory primary education spread through Europe and North America; Horace Mann championed common schools in the United States, and kindergarten (Froebel) and Montessori methods transformed early education. Secondary and higher education expanded massively after 1945, alongside decolonization-driven school building in Africa and Asia. The late 20th and 21st centuries brought comprehensive schooling debates, standardized testing, and digital and online learning.
