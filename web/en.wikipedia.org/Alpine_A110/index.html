The Alpine A110 is a sports car produced by the French manufacturer Alpine. The original A110, introduced in 1963, was a lightweight fibreglass-bodied berlinette powered by rear-mounted Renault engines, and it became famous in international rallying. The name was revived in 2017 for a mid-engined sports coupé built in Dieppe.

The original A110 (1963–1977)

Developed under Alpine founder Jean Rédélé, the A110 used a steel backbone chassis, fibreglass bodywork and Renault running gear, with engines growing from 956 cc to 1.6 litres and beyond. Weighing little more than 700 kg, it was exceptionally agile. In rallying it won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1971 and 1973 and carried Alpine to the inaugural World Rally Championship for manufacturers in 1973, driven by stars such as Jean-Claude Andruet and Bernard Darniche.

The modern A110 (2017–present)

Unveiled at the 2017 Geneva Motor Show, the new A110 is an aluminium-bodied, mid-engined coupé with a turbocharged 1.8-litre engine (252 hp in standard form, more in the A110 S and R variants) driving the rear wheels through a seven-speed dual-clutch gearbox. Its low weight — around 1,100 kg — and emphasis on agility have earned widespread praise, and it has spawned the A110 R and track-focused Ultime editions.
