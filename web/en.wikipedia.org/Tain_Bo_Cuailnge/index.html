Táin Bó Cúailnge ("The Cattle Raid of Cooley") is an epic from Irish mythology and the central tale of the Ulster Cycle. It tells of the war waged by Queen Medb of Connacht to seize the great bull Donn Cúailnge from Ulster, defended by the boy hero Cú Chulainn.

Story

Medb, determined that her wealth equal her husband Ailill's, invades Ulster while its warriors are incapacitated by a curse. The seventeen-year-old Cú Chulainn single-handedly harasses her army, fighting a series of single combats at fords, including a tragic duel with his foster-brother Ferdiad. The bull is captured but later fights Ailill's bull Finnbennach, kills him, and dies of his wounds.

Texts

The tale survives in three recensions, in manuscripts such as the 12th-century Lebor na hUidre and the Book of Leinster. It is often called Ireland's national epic and has been translated by Thomas Kinsella and Ciaran Carson, among others.
