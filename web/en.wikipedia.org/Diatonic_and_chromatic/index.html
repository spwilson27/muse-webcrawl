Diatonic and chromatic

Diatonic and chromatic are terms in music theory used to characterize scales, and are also applied to musical instruments, intervals, chords, notes, and harmonic systems. The term diatonic most commonly describes music built from the pitches of a seven-note scale — such as the major and natural minor scales — while chromatic refers to the use of notes outside that scale, or to the twelve-note chromatic scale containing all semitones of the octave.

History

The terms descend from the ancient Greek genera of tetrachords: diatonic, chromatic, and enharmonic. The diatonic genus used two whole tones and a semitone; the chromatic genus used two semitones and a minor third. In modern usage the distinction developed through medieval and Renaissance theory into today's meanings.

Usage

A melody or chord progression confined to one key's scale is described as diatonic; harmony that borrows notes from outside the key — such as secondary dominants or Neapolitan chords — is chromatic. Instruments designed to play a single scale, such as the diatonic harmonica, are contrasted with chromatic instruments that can play all twelve notes.
