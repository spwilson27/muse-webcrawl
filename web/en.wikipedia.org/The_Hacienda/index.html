The Hacienda

Full nameFAC 51 The Haçienda
LocationWhitworth Street West, Manchester, England
Opened21 May 1982
Closed28 June 1997

The Haçienda (officially FAC 51 The Haçienda, with a cedilla under the "c") was a nightclub in Manchester, England, which became famous during the Madchester years of the 1980s and early 1990s. It was opened by Factory Records and the band New Order, managed in association with Rob Gretton, and its stark post-industrial interior — a converted yacht showroom and warehouse — was designed by Ben Kelly with graphic input from Peter Saville. Its Factory catalogue number, FAC 51, was assigned as though the club were a record release.

History

Opening in May 1982 as a members' club mixing live gigs with dance music, the Haçienda initially lost money heavily (subsidised by New Order's record sales) and hosted early British performances by artists including Madonna, whose first UK television appearance was filmed there in 1984. From the mid-1980s, resident and visiting DJs such as Mike Pickering, Graeme Park, Jon Dasilva and Laurent Garnier pioneered house music in Britain; combined from 1988 with the arrival of ecstasy, the club became the epicentre of acid house and the Second Summer of Love, and midwife to the Madchester scene of the Happy Mondays and the Stone Roses.

Decline and closure

Success brought chronic problems: gang violence and drug dealing, police pressure, licensing battles, and ruinous finances. The club closed briefly in 1991 after a shooting, and finally in June 1997; the building was demolished in 2002 and replaced by apartments retaining the Haçienda name. Its legacy — the fusion of guitar bands, DJ culture, and industrial design — has been chronicled in books, exhibitions, and the 2002 film 24 Hour Party People.
