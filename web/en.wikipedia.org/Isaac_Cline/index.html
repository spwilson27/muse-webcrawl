Isaac Monroe Cline (13 October 1861 – 3 August 1955) was an American meteorologist with the United States Weather Bureau, best known as the chief forecaster in Galveston, Texas, during the devastating Galveston hurricane of 1900, the deadliest natural disaster in United States history.

Early life and career

Cline grew up in Tennessee and taught school before joining the US Army Signal Corps, which then ran the national weather service, in 1882. After training at Fort Myer, Virginia, he held posts in Arkansas and Texas, earning a medical degree and studying the effects of weather on health. He became head of the Galveston weather office in 1889.

The 1900 Galveston hurricane

Cline had publicly argued that Galveston's geography made a catastrophic hurricane surge unlikely. When a tropical storm crossed Cuba in early September 1900, Weather Bureau officials in Washington expected it to recurve up the Atlantic coast. On the morning of 8 September 1900, Cline observed abnormal swells and a falling barometer and, acting on his own initiative, raised storm warnings; he later wrote that he rode along the beach urging residents to seek higher ground. The hurricane's storm surge inundated the island that night, killing at least 6,000 people — estimates range much higher. Cline's pregnant wife Cora died in the storm; Cline survived clinging to wreckage with his daughters and brother Joseph, also a Weather Bureau meteorologist.

Later life

Cline subsequently directed the Weather Bureau office in New Orleans, where his flood forecasting on the Mississippi River was widely praised. He published an autobiography, Storms, Floods and Sunshine (1945), and is a central figure in Erik Larson's bestselling history Isaac's Storm (1999).
