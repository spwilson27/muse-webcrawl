Michael DeBakey

Michael Ellis DeBakey (September 7, 1908 – July 11, 2008) was an American general and cardiovascular surgeon, scientist, and medical educator who was internationally known for his work in the treatment of heart patients and for his role in developing modern cardiovascular surgery.

Career

DeBakey was chancellor emeritus of Baylor College of Medicine in Houston, Texas, director of the Houston Methodist DeBakey Heart & Vascular Center, and senior attending surgeon at Houston Methodist Hospital. As a young medical student he invented the roller pump, a component crucial to the heart–lung machine. During the Second World War he helped develop the Mobile Army Surgical Hospital (MASH) concept.

Achievements

DeBakey performed some of the earliest successful coronary artery bypass operations, pioneered the use of Dacron grafts to repair blood vessels, and in 1966 led a team that successfully implanted an artificial heart component. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1969 and the Congressional Gold Medal in 2008.
