Stugna-P

The Stugna-P, also known as the Skif in its export version, is a Ukrainian anti-tank guided missile (ATGM) system developed by the Luch Design Bureau in Kyiv. It entered service with the Ukrainian Armed Forces in the early 2010s and has been widely used in combat, including during the Russo-Ukrainian War.

Stugna-P
Type: Anti-tank guided missile
Origin: Ukraine
Designer: Luch Design Bureau
Guidance: Laser beam-riding

Design

The system consists of a tripod-mounted launcher, a guidance unit with day and thermal imaging sights, and missiles carried in transport-launch containers. The operator can control the launcher remotely from up to about 50 metres away, improving crew survivability. Its tandem-charge warheads are designed to defeat explosive reactive armour.

Variants

Versions fire 130 mm or 152 mm missiles with ranges of roughly 4–5.5 km. The export Skif was developed jointly with Belarusian industry and has been sold to several countries.

Combat use

Stugna-P launchers gained prominence in 2022, when Ukrainian forces used them extensively against Russian armoured vehicles; videos of engagements recorded through the system's sight were widely shared.
