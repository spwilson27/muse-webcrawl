Healthcare in Sweden

The healthcare system of Sweden is a publicly funded, decentralised universal healthcare system, organised mainly by the country's 21 regions (county councils), which are responsible for financing and providing most medical care. It is consistently ranked among the world's better-performing systems, with high life expectancy and low infant mortality.

Organisation and funding

Healthcare is funded primarily through regional taxes, supplemented by national government grants. Care is provided by public providers along with private providers operating under public contracts. Patients pay modest capped fees for visits and hospital stays; an annual ceiling ("high-cost protection") limits total out-of-pocket costs, and care is free for children and young people in most regions. Dental care is subsidised separately and is free for those under 24.

Access and outcomes

Primary care is delivered through local health centres, with national rules aiming to guarantee a medical assessment within three days and treatment within 90 days (the "care guarantee"). Sweden spends around 11% of GDP on health. Recurring public debate centres on waiting times, regional variation, and the growing role of private providers within the publicly funded system.
