Mutagenesis

Mutagenesis is a process by which the genetic information of an organism is changed, producing a mutation. It may occur spontaneously in nature, or as a result of exposure to mutagens — physical or chemical agents such as ionizing radiation, ultraviolet light, or alkylating chemicals. Mutagenesis is also a laboratory technique used to introduce mutations into DNA for research or biotechnology.

Mechanisms

Mutagenic events include base substitutions, insertions, deletions and chromosomal rearrangements. They arise from errors in DNA replication, faulty repair of DNA damage, or direct chemical modification of bases. Organisms possess repair systems — including base excision repair, nucleotide excision repair and mismatch repair — that correct most lesions; mutations persist when damage escapes repair or is mis-repaired.

Mutagenesis in research

Scientists use random mutagenesis (by chemicals such as EMS, or by radiation) to generate libraries of mutants for genetic screens, and site-directed mutagenesis to change chosen nucleotides, enabling the study of protein function and the engineering of enzymes. Directed evolution couples iterative mutagenesis with selection to create proteins with new properties, work recognized by the 2018 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
