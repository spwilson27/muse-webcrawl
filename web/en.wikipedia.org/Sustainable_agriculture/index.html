Sustainable agriculture is farming in sustainable ways based on an understanding of ecosystem services, the study of relationships between organisms and their environment. It aims to meet society's present food and textile needs without compromising the ability of current or future generations to meet their needs, balancing environmental health, economic profitability, and social and economic equity. Sustainable agriculture includes environmentally friendly methods of farming that allow the production of crops or livestock without irreversible damage to the ecosystem.

Practices

Key practices include rotating crops and planting cover crops to build soil organic matter and break pest cycles; reducing or eliminating tillage to limit erosion and soil carbon loss; integrating livestock and crops so manure recycles nutrients; managing pests through integrated pest management (IPM) rather than routine pesticide use; agroforestry and hedgerows that support pollinators and biodiversity; and precise water management such as drip irrigation. Nutrient management — matching fertilizer inputs to crop needs — reduces runoff that causes eutrophication of waterways.

Dimensions and debate

Definitions vary: the US Department of Agriculture's 1990 legal definition emphasizes satisfying human food needs, enhancing environmental quality, using resources efficiently, and sustaining farm economic viability and quality of life. Debates persist over yields: meta-analyses find organic yields average roughly 20–25 percent lower than conventional, though the gap shrinks with good practices and diversification, prompting arguments for "sustainable intensification". Social dimensions include farmworker welfare, land tenure, seed sovereignty, and resilience to climate change. Certification schemes (such as organic labels) and policy instruments (subsidies, carbon markets, research funding) shape adoption.
