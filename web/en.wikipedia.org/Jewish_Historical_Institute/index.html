Jewish Historical Institute

The Jewish Historical Institute (Polish: Żydowski Instytut Historyczny, ŻIH) is a research institute in Warsaw, Poland, founded in 1947. It is one of the most important institutions for the study of Polish Jewish history and the Holocaust, and the custodian of the Oyneg Shabes (Ringelblum) Archive.

History

The institute grew out of the Central Jewish Historical Commission, established in Lublin in 1944 to document Nazi crimes and collect survivor testimony. In 1947 it moved to Warsaw and became the Jewish Historical Institute, housed in the former Main Judaic Library building on Tłomackie Street, adjacent to the site of the Great Synagogue destroyed by the Germans in 1943.

Collections

The institute holds the underground archive of the Warsaw Ghetto assembled by historian Emanuel Ringelblum and the Oyneg Shabes group, buried in milk cans and metal boxes and recovered after the war. The archive, inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register, contains diaries, documents, photographs, and ephemera documenting life and death in the ghetto. The institute also maintains extensive archives, a library, and collections of art and religious objects.

Activities

The institute conducts scholarly research, publishes journals and books, organizes exhibitions and educational programs, and supports genealogical research into Polish Jewish families.
