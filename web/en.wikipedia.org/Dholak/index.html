A dholak being played with the hands.

The dholak is a two-headed hand drum widely used in the folk and devotional music of South Asia, including India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, and the South Asian diaspora. Its barrel-shaped wooden shell is smaller than that of the related dhol, and it is played with the hands while held in the lap or hung from the neck.

Construction and playing

The drum is hollowed from a single block of wood, traditionally sheesham, and fitted with animal-skin heads laced with rope and tightened by rings or wooden blocks. The treble head is plain, while the bass head carries an inner coating of a paste made from iron filings, tar, and other materials—analogous to the syahi of the tabla—which lowers and enriches its tone. The treble side is struck with the fingers of one hand and the bass side with the palm and fingers, sometimes aided by a thin stick.

Musical roles

The dholak provides the rhythmic foundation for qawwali, bhajans and kirtans, ghazals, regional folk genres such as lavani and bhangra, wedding songs, and much classic and modern Indian film music. Skilled players maintain cycles such as the eight-beat kaherwa and dadra talas while improvising fills and flourishes.

Related instruments

The dholak belongs to a family of South Asian double-headed drums that includes the larger Punjabi dhol, the pakhawaj and mridangam of classical traditions, and the madal of Nepal.
