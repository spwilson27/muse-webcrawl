Downspout

A metal downspout carrying rainwater from a gutter to the ground

A downspout, also called a downpipe, rain leader, drain pipe, downspout pipe or (in Scotland and northern England) a rone, is a pipe for carrying rainwater from a rain gutter on a building down to ground level or to a drainage system. Downspouts are usually vertical and carry water collected from a roof, protecting the building's foundations, walls and surroundings from water damage and erosion.

Design and materials

Downspouts are commonly made of galvanised steel, aluminium, copper, zinc or uPVC plastic, and are usually rectangular or round in cross-section. A curved section called a gooseneck or elbow connects the gutter outlet to the vertical pipe, which is fixed to the wall with straps or brackets. At the bottom, a shoe directs water away from the wall into a gully, storm drain, soakaway or rain barrel. Leaf guards and strainers at the top prevent blockages from debris.

Function

By channelling roof runoff safely away, downspouts prevent soil erosion at the base of walls, basement flooding, damp penetration and frost damage to masonry. Modern sustainable drainage practice often disconnects downspouts from sewers and redirects water to rain gardens or water butts to reduce stormwater runoff.

Decorative use

In some architecture, rain chains are used in place of enclosed downspouts, especially in Japanese design, guiding water visibly down a chain of cups or links. Ornate hopper heads (leader heads) at the top of downspouts are a feature of historic buildings.
