Cariboo Gold Rush

Dates1860–1863 (peak)
LocationCariboo region, British Columbia, Canada
Key townsBarkerville, Quesnel, Williams Lake

The Cariboo Gold Rush was a gold rush in the Colony of British Columbia in the 1860s, centered on the remote Cariboo region east of Quesnel. Following the earlier Fraser Canyon Gold Rush of 1858, prospectors pushed into the Cariboo Mountains, where rich placer deposits were found on Williams Creek in 1861.

Barkerville

The town of Barkerville sprang up around Billy Barker's strike and became the largest town north of San Francisco west of Chicago for a time, with a cosmopolitan population of miners, merchants, and Chinese immigrants who formed a large share of the workforce. Barkerville is now a preserved heritage town.

Cariboo Road

To supply the goldfields, Governor James Douglas ordered construction of the Cariboo Wagon Road from Yale through the Fraser Canyon to Barkerville, an engineering feat completed in stages through 1865, along with the famous camel train experiment that briefly used Bactrian camels as pack animals.

Legacy

Gold from the Cariboo helped finance the colonies of Vancouver Island and British Columbia and hastened their union and entry into Canadian Confederation in 1871. Placer mining continued in the region for decades.
