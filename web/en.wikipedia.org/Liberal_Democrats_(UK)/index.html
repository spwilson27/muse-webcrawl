The Liberal Democrats, commonly known as the Lib Dems, are a liberal political party in the United Kingdom. The party was formed in 1988 by the merger of the Liberal Party and the Social Democratic Party (SDP), which had contested the 1983 and 1987 general elections together as the SDP–Liberal Alliance.

History

The party's first leader was Paddy Ashdown, under whom it consolidated and grew steadily. Under Charles Kennedy it won 62 seats at the 2005 general election, its best result in the modern era. After the 2010 election produced a hung parliament, the Liberal Democrats under Nick Clegg entered a coalition government with the Conservative Party — the UK's first peacetime coalition since the 1930s — with Clegg as Deputy Prime Minister. The party was heavily punished at the 2015 election, falling from 57 seats to 8, partly over its reversal on university tuition fees. It subsequently campaigned strongly against Brexit and, under Ed Davey, recovered at the 2024 general election to win 72 seats, its highest ever number.

Ideology and policies

The Liberal Democrats draw on both classical and social liberal traditions: they support civil liberties, constitutional reform (including proportional representation and House of Lords reform), a mixed economy with strong public services, environmental protection, and internationalism, including support for the European Union. The party constitution commits it to building "a fair, free and open society" balancing liberty, equality and community.

Organisation

The party is a federation of the English, Scottish and Welsh Liberal Democrats, with policy made by members at federal conferences. Its youth and student wing and affiliated organisations campaign alongside the main party. Notable former leaders include David Steel (of the predecessor Liberals), Jo Swinson and Tim Farron.
