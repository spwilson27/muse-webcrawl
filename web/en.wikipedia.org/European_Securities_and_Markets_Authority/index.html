The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) is an agency of the European Union located in Paris. It was established on 1 January 2011, replacing the Committee of European Securities Regulators, as part of the European System of Financial Supervision created after the financial crisis.

Mission and powers

ESMA's objectives are to enhance investor protection and promote stable and orderly financial markets. It contributes to a single rulebook for EU financial markets by drafting regulatory and implementing technical standards under legislation such as MiFID II/MiFIR, the Market Abuse Regulation, the Prospectus Regulation, and EMIR. Unusually among EU agencies, ESMA directly supervises certain entities: credit rating agencies and trade repositories registered in the EU, and certain third-country firms. It can also temporarily restrict or ban financial products or practices that threaten market stability or investor protection, as it did with binary options and contracts for difference offered to retail clients.

Organisation

ESMA is governed by a Board of Supervisors made up of the heads of national securities regulators, chaired by a full-time Chair, with day-to-day management led by an Executive Director. It works with the other European Supervisory Authorities (EBA and EIOPA) and the European Systemic Risk Board.
