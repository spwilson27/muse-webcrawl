Burdigala was a Roman town in Gaul, corresponding to modern Bordeaux in south-western France. Founded as an oppidum of the Bituriges Vivisci, a Celtic tribe, it came under Roman control in the 1st century BC and became the capital of the province of Gallia Aquitania. Its position on the Garonne River, near the Gironde estuary, made it a flourishing port exporting wine, metals, and other goods across the Roman world.

Roman prosperity

Burdigala prospered in the early Empire, receiving grand public buildings including temples, an amphitheatre (the Palais Gallien) seating perhaps 20,000, and monumental pillars such as the Piliers de Tutelle. Bordeaux wine was already celebrated by Roman authors, and Ausonius, the 4th-century poet and tutor to the emperor Gratian, was born there and praised the city's vines and learned life. Burdigala was ravaged by the barbarian invasions of the 3rd century, after which it was enclosed in a smaller circuit of walls.

Later history

In late antiquity Burdigala was an early centre of Christianity in Gaul, with a bishopric attested from the 4th century. The city passed to the Visigoths and then to the Franks, eventually developing into medieval Bordeaux under the Duchy of Aquitaine.
