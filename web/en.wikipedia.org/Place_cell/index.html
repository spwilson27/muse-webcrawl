Place cell

A spatial firing-rate map of a single place cell; the cell fires only when the animal is in one location (its place field)

A place cell is a type of pyramidal neuron in the hippocampus that becomes active when an animal enters a particular place in its environment, which is known as the place field. Place cells are thought to function collectively as a cognitive representation of a specific location in space, known as a cognitive map. They were first reported and given their name by John O'Keefe and Jonathan Dostrovsky in 1971, based on recordings from the hippocampus of freely moving rats.

Properties

Each place cell has one or a few place fields in a given environment and is largely silent outside them. When an animal enters a new environment, place fields form quickly, often within minutes, and the ensemble of active cells changes ("remaps") between environments. Place fields are influenced by visual landmarks, boundaries such as the structures of a controlled arena, and by the animal's own movement signals. Place-cell firing is also modulated by head direction, speed and task demands, and sequences of place cells replay during sleep in patterns thought to support memory consolidation.

Relation to other spatial cells

Place cells form one component of the brain's positioning system. Head direction cells signal the direction the head faces; grid cells in the entorhinal cortex fire in a regular hexagonal pattern across the environment; and border cells respond near environmental boundaries. Together these cell types are believed to underpin navigation and spatial memory. O'Keefe shared the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with May-Britt Moser and Edvard Moser, who discovered grid cells, "for their discoveries of cells that constitute a positioning system in the brain".

Human place cells

Single-unit recordings from epilepsy patients with implanted electrodes have identified place-responsive neurons in the human hippocampus and entorhinal cortex, and functional imaging studies show place-related signals during virtual navigation, supporting a conserved role in human spatial memory and episodic memory.
