Post-grunge

Post-grunge is a derivative of grunge that emerged in the mid-1990s, inspired by grunge's sound and aesthetics but with a more commercially acceptable and mainstream-friendly tone. Where grunge was raw and abrasive, post-grunge smoothed the dynamics, emphasised conventional song structures and choruses, and targeted album-oriented rock radio.

History

After the death of Kurt Cobain in 1994 and the waning of the original Seattle scene, bands such as Bush, Live, Collective Soul, and Stone Temple Pilots (sometimes classed with grunge itself) carried the sound to wider audiences. The late 1990s and early 2000s brought the style's commercial peak with Creed, Nickelback, Staind, Puddle of Mudd, 3 Doors Down, and later acts like Daughtry and Shinedown. Foo Fighters, founded by former Nirvana drummer Dave Grohl, straddled post-grunge and alternative rock to lasting success.

Characteristics and reception

Typical features include distorted electric guitars, quiet-verse/loud-chorus dynamics, mid-tempo tempos, earnest baritone vocals, and themes of personal struggle. Critics have often dismissed post-grunge as derivative and formulaic — Nickelback in particular became a byword for bland commercial rock — though the genre's leading acts sold tens of millions of albums and dominated rock radio for over a decade.
