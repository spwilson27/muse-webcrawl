Mikhail Lomonosov

Mikhail Vasilyevich Lomonosov
Born: 19 November 1711, Denisovka, Russian Empire
Died: 15 April 1765 (aged 53), Saint Petersburg
Fields: Chemistry, physics, astronomy, geology, poetry, grammar

Mikhail Vasilyevich Lomonosov (19 November 1711 – 15 April 1765) was a Russian polymath, scientist and writer who made lasting contributions to literature, education, chemistry and physics. He founded Moscow State University in 1755 — the institution now bears his name — and is regarded as a founding figure of Russian science.

Early life

Born in the village of Denisovka near Kholmogory in the far north of Russia, the son of a prosperous fisherman and shipowner, Lomonosov learned to read from church books and devoured the few secular texts available. In 1730, aged 19, he walked to Moscow with a fish caravan, falsely claiming noble-free status to enrol at the Slavic Greek Latin Academy. He studied in Kiev and then at the St. Petersburg Academy, and in 1736 was sent to Germany, studying mining at Freiberg and philosophy and chemistry at Marburg under Christian Wolff.

Scientific work

Lomonosov championed the corpuscular (atomic) theory of matter, anticipated the conservation of mass in chemical reactions in a 1748 letter to Euler and experiments of 1756, formulated a kinetic theory of heat rejecting the caloric theory, and explained the formation of icebergs and the origin of fossil fuels and earthquakes. In astronomy he observed the 1761 transit of Venus and correctly inferred that Venus has an atmosphere — the "Lomonosov arc". He also built Russia's first chemical laboratory, revived mosaic art, and wrote a reforming Russian grammar (1755) and the ode tradition of Russian poetry.

Legacy

Pushkin called him "himself our first university". Moscow State University, the lunar crater Lomonosov, a Martian crater, and numerous institutions bear his name.
