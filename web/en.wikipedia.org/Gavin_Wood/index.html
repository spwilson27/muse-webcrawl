Gavin James Wood (born 1980) is an English computer scientist, co-founder of Ethereum and founder of Polkadot and the Web3 Foundation. He coined the term "Web3" in 2014 and wrote the Ethereum Yellow Paper, the formal technical specification of the Ethereum protocol.

Early life and education

Wood was born in Lancaster, England. He attended Lancaster Royal Grammar School and studied at the University of York, where he completed a Master of Engineering (MEng) in Computer Systems and Software Engineering in 2002 and a PhD, titled "Content-based visualisation to aid common navigation of musical audio and visual content in a domotic environment", in 2005.

Career

Before working on Ethereum, Wood was a research scientist at Microsoft. In late 2013 and early 2014 he joined Vitalik Buterin and other co-founders to build Ethereum. Wood proposed and implemented the first functional Ethereum testnet, coded the first client (in C++), and served as the Ethereum Foundation's first chief technology officer. He authored the Ethereum Yellow Paper, which defined the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) in mathematical terms, and was an early proponent of the Solidity smart contract language.

In 2016 Wood left Ethereum to found Parity Technologies and the Web3 Foundation. He designed Polkadot, a heterogeneous multi-chain network intended to let specialised blockchains interoperate and share security, and its "canary" network Kusama. He also led development of the Substrate framework for building blockchains.

Web3

Wood coined the term "Web3" (later "Web 3.0") in a 2014 blog post, "ÐApps: What Web 3.0 Looks Like", describing a decentralised, trustless, serverless internet in which users control their own data, identity and applications.
