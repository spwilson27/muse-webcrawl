La Liga

Founded: 1929
Country: Spain
Teams: 20
Most titles: Real Madrid (36)
Current structure: Primera División, top tier of Spanish football

The Campeonato Nacional de Liga de Primera División, commonly known as La Liga, is the top men's professional football division of the Spanish football league system. Administered by the Liga Nacional de Fútbol Profesional, it is contested by 20 teams, with the three lowest-placed sides relegated to the Segunda División.

History

La Liga was founded in 1929, with Barcelona winning the inaugural title. The championship was suspended during the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939). From the 1950s onward the league was largely dominated by Real Madrid and Barcelona, whose rivalry — El Clásico — is one of the most-watched club fixtures in world sport. Atlético Madrid, Athletic Bilbao, and Valencia have also won titles, with Deportivo La Coruña (2000) the most recent first-time champion.

Records and standing

Real Madrid hold the record for most championships, followed by Barcelona. Lionel Messi is the league's all-time top scorer with 474 goals for Barcelona, and also holds records for assists and appearances by a foreign player. La Liga clubs have won the most UEFA Champions League and UEFA Europa League titles collectively, and the league regularly ranks at or near the top of UEFA's coefficients, celebrated for its technical, possession-based style.

Organisation

Since 1984 the league has been run by the LFP (Liga de Fútbol Profesional), independent of the Spanish federation (RFEF) which organises the Copa del Rey. Matches are played from August to May, each team meeting every other twice in a double round-robin.
