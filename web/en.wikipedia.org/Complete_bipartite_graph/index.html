Complete bipartite graph

The complete bipartite graph K3,3

In the mathematical field of graph theory, a complete bipartite graph or biclique is a special kind of bipartite graph where every vertex of the first set is connected to every vertex of the second set. A complete bipartite graph with partitions of size m and n is denoted Km,n and has m + n vertices and mn edges.

Examples

For any k, K1,k is called a star graph. The graph K3,3, known as the utility graph, is one of the two minimal non-planar graphs in Kuratowski's theorem (the other being K5); it arises in the classic "three utilities problem" of connecting three houses to three utilities without crossing lines.

Properties

  • A complete bipartite graph Km,n has a maximum matching that saturates the smaller partition.
  • It has vertex connectivity and edge connectivity equal to min(m, n).
  • Km,n is planar only when min(m, n) ≤ 2.
  • The eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix are √(mn), −√(mn), and 0 with multiplicity m + n − 2.

Counting subgraphs

The number of bicliques in an arbitrary graph is a subject of extremal graph theory, connected with the Zarankiewicz problem, which asks for the maximum number of edges in a bipartite graph that does not contain Ks,t as a subgraph.
