Wari Empire

The Wari Empire (Spanish: Imperio Huari) was a Middle Horizon Andean political formation that flourished roughly between 500 and 1000 AD, preceding the Inca Empire by several centuries. Centered on the city of Wari (Huari) in the south-central highlands of Peru, near modern Ayacucho, it was the first polity to unify much of the Andean region under a single state, creating administrative and artistic patterns the Inca later adapted.

Expansion and administration

Wari expansion was achieved through a combination of military conquest and alliance. The state built planned provincial capitals such as Pikillacta in the south and Viracochapampa in the north, laid out in rigid orthogonal grids of high-walled compounds. Standardized state pottery — polychrome ceramics depicting the Staff God of the southern tradition shared with Tiwanaku — and administrative architecture spread across an empire stretching some 1,300 km along the Andes and coast, from the Cajamarca region to the Sicuani area.

Culture and decline

Wari elites were buried with elaborate tunics woven of camelid fiber, fine ceramics, and offerings; the royal tomb at Castillo de Huarmey revealed rich burials of elite women. The empire pioneered terrace agriculture, road building, and the use of quipu-like recording devices. Around 1000 AD the Wari state collapsed, likely amid prolonged drought and internal conflict; its capital was partly burned and abandoned, and regional cultures re-emerged in the Late Intermediate Period.
