Musical tuning

In music, tuning is the practice of adjusting the pitch of one or many tones from musical instruments to establish typical intervals between these tones. Tuning is usually based on a fixed reference, such as A = 440 Hz, and a tuning system — a set of rules for choosing the pitches of a scale. Because the pure ratios of just intonation cannot all be satisfied simultaneously in every key, tuning systems are compromises, and the search for better compromises, called temperament, has shaped Western music for centuries.

Tuning systems

Pythagorean tuning builds scales from pure perfect fifths (3:2), producing bright thirds that are noticeably sharp. Just intonation tunes intervals to simple whole-number ratios (the major third as 5:4), sounding pure in one key but making modulation between keys awkward. Renaissance and Baroque musicians used meantone temperament, which flattened fifths to sweeten thirds in common keys at the cost of a harsh "wolf" interval in remote ones. Various well temperaments distributed the compromise unevenly so all keys were usable, each with its own character — the system celebrated (though not literally specified) in Bach's The Well-Tempered Clavier.

Equal temperament

The modern standard is twelve-tone equal temperament, which divides the octave into twelve equal semitones of 100 cents each. Every interval except the octave is slightly impure — fifths are narrowed by about two cents, major thirds sharpened by fourteen — but all keys sound equally (imperfectly) in tune, enabling unrestricted modulation and standardisation across instruments. Other divisions of the octave, such as 19, 31, or 53 equal steps, are explored in microtonal music and approximate just intervals more closely.

Pitch standards

The reference pitch itself has varied historically: Baroque pitch was often near A = 415 Hz, while some nineteenth-century orchestras crept above A = 450 Hz. A = 440 Hz was adopted as an international standard in the twentieth century, though many orchestras now tune to 442 or 443 Hz for brilliance. Singers and string players, unconstrained by fixed keys, often adjust pitches expressively toward just intonation.
