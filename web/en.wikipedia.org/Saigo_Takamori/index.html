Saigo Takamori

Saigō Takamori (西郷 隆盛, 23 January 1828 – 24 September 1877) was a Japanese samurai and nobleman, one of the most influential figures of the Meiji Restoration. A leader of the Satsuma Domain, he commanded imperial forces in the Boshin War that overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate.

Career

Saigō played a key role in the Satchō Alliance between Satsuma and Chōshū and in the surrender of Edo Castle without bloodshed. In the new Meiji government he became a councillor and commander of the Imperial Guard. He resigned in 1873 after his proposal for a mission to Korea (Seikanron) was rejected, and returned to Kagoshima, where he founded private schools for samurai.

Satsuma Rebellion and death

In 1877 dissatisfied Satsuma samurai rose in the Satsuma Rebellion, and Saigō reluctantly became their leader. Government forces defeated the rebels at the Battle of Shiroyama on 24 September 1877, where Saigō was mortally wounded. Though declared a rebel, he was posthumously pardoned in 1889 and later enshrined in popular memory as "the last true samurai".
