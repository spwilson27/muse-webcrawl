Utrecht University

The Academiegebouw (University Hall) on Domplein, Utrecht.

Utrecht University (Dutch: Universiteit Utrecht, abbreviated UU) is a public research university in Utrecht, Netherlands. Established on 26 March 1636, it is one of the oldest universities in the Netherlands and one of the largest in Europe, with more than 30,000 students and seven faculties covering humanities, social and behavioural sciences, law, economics and governance, geosciences, science, medicine, and veterinary medicine.

History

The university grew from an "illustrious school" founded in 1634; the philosopher René Descartes lived in Utrecht during this period and observed its early disputes. Utrecht developed into a major centre of Reformed theology and natural science. Its Veterinary Medicine faculty is the only one of its kind in the Netherlands. Alumni and faculty include numerous Nobel laureates, among them Christiaan Eijkman and the physicist Gerard 't Hooft.

Organisation

Much of the university is concentrated at the Utrecht Science Park (De Uithof) east of the city, home to striking modern architecture by firms including OMA, while humanities and law remain in the historic centre. The university's motto is Sol Iustitiae Illustra Nos — "Sun of Righteousness, shine upon us."
