Wakefield Cathedral


Full name: Cathedral Church of All Saints
Location: Wakefield, West Yorkshire, England
Denomination: Church of England
Cathedral since: 1888
Spire: 247 ft (75 m) — the tallest church spire in Yorkshire

Wakefield Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of All Saints, is a Church of England cathedral in Wakefield, West Yorkshire. Standing on a hill in the city centre on the site of a Saxon church, the present building is mainly of the thirteenth to fifteenth centuries, built in the English Gothic style, and is a Grade I listed building.

History

A church on the site is recorded from around 1100, with Norman work surviving in the nave pillars. The building was greatly enlarged in the 1300s and 1400s; its crocketed spire, at 247 feet the highest in Yorkshire, dates from the fifteenth century and was restored in the nineteenth. When the Diocese of Wakefield was created in 1888, the parish church of All Saints became its cathedral. George Gilbert Scott had overseen a major restoration in the 1850s–60s, and a large eastern extension, designed by John Loughborough Pearson and his son Frank, was completed in stages through the twentieth century, giving the cathedral a spacious chancel and retrochoir.

Following Church of England diocesan reorganisation, Wakefield became in 2014 one of three cathedrals — with Bradford and Ripon — of the new Diocese of Leeds, each with its own dean and chapter under a single bishop.

Features

The cathedral is notable for its fine late-medieval rood screen, fragments of fifteenth-century stained glass, a celebrated collection of carved roof bosses and misericords, and a font cover among its woodwork. Its organ and choir maintain a daily pattern of worship in the Anglican choral tradition.
