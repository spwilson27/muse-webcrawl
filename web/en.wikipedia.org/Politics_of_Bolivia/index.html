Politics of Bolivia

CountryPlurinational State of Bolivia
SystemUnitary presidential republic
LegislaturePlurinational Legislative Assembly
CapitalSucre (constitutional); La Paz (seat of government)

The politics of Bolivia take place in a framework of a presidential representative democratic republic, whereby the President of Bolivia is both head of state and head of government, and of a pluriform multi-party system. Executive power is exercised by the government, legislative power is vested in both the government and the two chambers of the Plurinational Legislative Assembly, and the judiciary is formally independent.

Constitution of 2009

The constitution adopted in 2009, under President Evo Morales, renamed the country the Plurinational State of Bolivia, recognising the rights and autonomies of the country's indigenous peoples, expanding social and economic rights, and strengthening state control over natural resources such as natural gas. It permits the president and vice president to be re-elected once consecutively, a provision at the centre of later controversy when Morales sought further terms.

Recent history

Morales and his Movement for Socialism (MAS) governed from 2006 until 2019, a period of economic growth and political stability unusual in Bolivian history. After the disputed 2019 election and mass protests, Morales resigned and an interim government under Jeanine Áñez took power; MAS returned to office with the election of Luis Arce in 2020. Bolivian politics remains marked by regional tensions between the western highlands and the eastern departments, and by the role of social movements and coca-grower unions.
