Local loop

In telephony, the local loop (also referred to as the local tail, subscriber line, or in the aggregate as the last mile) is the physical link or circuit that connects from the demarcation point of the customer premises to the edge of the common carrier or telecommunications service provider's network.

Traditional local loops

Historically, the local loop consisted of a twisted pair of copper wires running from a telephone in the subscriber's premises to the local telephone exchange. The same pair carried both voice signals and the DC power and ringing current supplied by the exchange.

Modern implementations

Local loops today may be implemented with optical fibre, coaxial cable, or wireless links in addition to copper pairs. Technologies such as DSL deliver broadband data over existing copper loops, while fibre-to-the-premises replaces the copper loop entirely with optical fibre.

Local loop unbundling

Many regulators require incumbent operators to lease their local loops to competing providers at regulated rates, a practice known as local loop unbundling, in order to promote competition in telephone and broadband services.
