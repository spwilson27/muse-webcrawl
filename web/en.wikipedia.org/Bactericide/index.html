Bactericide

A bactericide or bacteriocide, sometimes abbreviated Bcidal, is a substance that kills bacteria. Bactericides include disinfectants, antiseptics, and those antibiotics whose action is lethal to the target organism rather than merely growth-inhibiting. The complementary term is bacteriostatic, describing agents that stop bacterial growth and rely on the immune system to clear the infection.

Bactericide
Type: Antimicrobial agent
Effect: Kills bacteria
Contrast: Bacteriostatic (inhibits growth)
Examples: Penicillins, bleach, hydrogen peroxide

Classes

Among antibiotics, beta-lactams, aminoglycosides, and fluoroquinolones are typically bactericidal, damaging the cell wall, membrane, or DNA so that the cell dies; whether an antibiotic is cidal or static can depend on the drug, dose, and organism. Chemical disinfectants such as sodium hypochlorite (bleach), alcohols, and hydrogen peroxide are strongly bactericidal and are used on surfaces and, in antiseptic form, on skin.

Uses

Bactericidal treatment is preferred for infections in immunocompromised patients and at sites poorly reached by immune defences, such as infective endocarditis and meningitis. In agriculture, bactericides protect crops against bacterial diseases of plants.
