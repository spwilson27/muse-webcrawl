Kuratowski's theorem

Kuratowski's theorem, proved by Kazimierz Kuratowski in 1930, is a characterisation of planar graphs in graph theory. It states that a finite graph is planar (can be drawn in the plane without edge crossings) if and only if it does not contain a subgraph that is a subdivision of K5 (the complete graph on five vertices) or of K3,3 (the complete bipartite graph on six vertices, three of each side).

Statement and significance

A subdivision replaces edges by paths, so the theorem forbids "expanded" copies of the two basic nonplanar graphs. Wagner's theorem gives the equivalent formulation using graph minors. Kuratowski's theorem is fundamental to graph theory and is generalised by the Robertson–Seymour theorem on forbidden minors.
