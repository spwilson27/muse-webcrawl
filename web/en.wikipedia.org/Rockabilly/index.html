Rockabilly

Rockabilly is one of the earliest styles of rock and roll music. It dates back to the early to mid-1950s in the United States, particularly the Southern United States. As a genre, it blends the sound of Western musical styles such as country and hillbilly boogie with rhythm and blues, leading to what is considered "classic" rock and roll. The term "rockabilly" itself is a portmanteau of "rock" (from rock and roll) and "hillbilly", the latter a reference to the country music that contributed strongly to the style.

Characteristics

Classic rockabilly instrumentation usually consists of electric guitar, acoustic guitar, upright bass played with a slapping technique, and drums — sometimes minimal or absent in the earliest recordings. Vocals are often exuberant, with hiccups, growls, and echoes; heavy use of tape echo and slapback reverb, famously at Sun Studio in Memphis, is characteristic of the sound.

History

The style emerged from the musical mix of the post-war South. Elvis Presley's 1954 Sun recordings, such as "That's All Right", are widely regarded as pivotal. Other leading artists included Carl Perkins ("Blue Suede Shoes"), Jerry Lee Lewis, Johnny Cash, Gene Vincent, Eddie Cochran, Buddy Holly, Wanda Jackson, and Janis Martin. Memphis-based Sun Records, run by Sam Phillips, was the genre's epicentre, but labels across the South and beyond recorded rockabilly acts.

Rockabilly's chart dominance faded by the early 1960s as styles changed, but it remained influential: the Beatles and other British Invasion bands drew on it heavily. A revival in the late 1970s and 1980s, led by groups such as the Stray Cats, brought the style and its associated fashion — pompadours, rolled jeans, and vintage Americana — back to prominence, and a dedicated subculture persists worldwide, especially in Europe and Japan.

Influence

Rockabilly is considered a foundational genre for later rock music, punk (through psychobilly fusions), and Americana. Its records are prized by collectors, and hundreds of obscure 1950s singles by one-off artists have been reissued.
