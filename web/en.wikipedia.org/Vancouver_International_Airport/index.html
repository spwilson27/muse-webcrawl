Vancouver International Airport (IATA: YVR) is an international airport located on Sea Island in Richmond, British Columbia, about 12 km from downtown Vancouver. It is the second-busiest airport in Canada by passenger traffic and a major transpacific hub, serving as Air Canada's gateway to Asia and the Pacific.

History and facilities

The airport opened in 1931 and expanded repeatedly after the Second World War, when the city of Vancouver and later the federal government developed it as the region's principal airport. Since 1992 it has been operated by the not-for-profit Vancouver Airport Authority. The main terminal complex has domestic, international and transborder (US preclearance) areas, complemented by a south terminal serving regional carriers and floatplane connections nearby. The airport is noted for its extensive collection of Pacific Northwest Indigenous art, its aquarium, and repeated top rankings in passenger surveys of North American airports.

Operations

YVR has two main parallel runways and a crosswind runway. Airlines connect it to destinations across Canada, the United States, Asia, Europe, Oceania and Mexico; it handles roughly 25 million passengers a year in recent years and significant air cargo, including seafood exports. The Canada Line of the SkyTrain rapid transit system links the airport to downtown Vancouver in about 25 minutes, having opened in 2009 ahead of the 2010 Winter Olympics.
