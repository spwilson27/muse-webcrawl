Armistice of 11 November 1918

The Armistice of 11 November 1918 was the armistice signed at Le Francport near Compiègne that ended fighting on land, at sea, and in the air in the First World War between the Entente and their last remaining opponent, Germany. It took effect at 11:00 a.m. Paris time on 11 November 1918 — "the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month" — and marked a victory for the Entente and a defeat for Germany, although not formally a surrender.

Negotiations and signing

The German delegation, led by Matthias Erzberger, travelled by train to the Forest of Compiègne, where Marshal Ferdinand Foch, the Allied Supreme Commander, presented terms in his private railway carriage. The terms were non-negotiable in practice; after three days of discussion, the armistice was signed in the early morning of 11 November. The carriage, later known as the Compiègne Wagon, was used again in 1940 when France signed an armistice with Nazi Germany at the same site.

Terms

The agreement required Germany to evacuate all occupied territory, withdraw behind the Rhine, surrender the High Seas Fleet for internment, hand over large quantities of weapons, railway locomotives, and trucks, and renounce the treaties of Brest-Litovsk and Bucharest. The Allied naval blockade of Germany continued until the signing of the Treaty of Versailles in 1919.

Legacy

The date is commemorated in many countries: as Armistice Day in France and Belgium, Remembrance Day in the Commonwealth, and Veterans Day in the United States. The armistice was technically renewed several times until the Treaty of Versailles took effect in January 1920.
