Haworth is a village in the City of Bradford metropolitan borough, West Yorkshire, England, situated on the edge of the Pennine moors. It is world famous as the home of the Brontë family — the novelists Charlotte, Emily and Anne Brontë — and much of its economy is based on literary tourism.

The Brontës

The family moved to Haworth Parsonage in 1820, where Patrick Brontë was perpetual curate. There his daughters wrote Jane Eyre, Wuthering Heights and The Tenant of Wildfell Hall. The parsonage is now the Brontë Parsonage Museum, run by the Brontë Society, and the moorland landscape above the village inspired the setting of Wuthering Heights; Top Withens, a ruined farmhouse, is traditionally linked to the Earnshaw house.

The village

Haworth's steep cobbled Main Street is lined with stone cottages, tea rooms and shops. The village is served by the heritage Keighley and Worth Valley Railway, whose steam trains and station featured in the 1970 film The Railway Children.
