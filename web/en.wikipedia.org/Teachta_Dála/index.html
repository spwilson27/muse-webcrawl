Teachta Dála

TDs sit in Dáil Éireann at Leinster House, Dublin.

A Teachta Dála (plural Teachtaí Dála), abbreviated TD, is a member of Dáil Éireann, the lower house of the Oireachtas, the parliament of Ireland. The title is often translated as "Deputy to the Dáil" or simply deputy. TDs are directly elected by universal suffrage under the single transferable vote system of proportional representation.

Election

Dáil Éireann consists of 158 TDs (increased to 174 at the 2024 general election following a constituency review), each elected from a multi-seat constituency of three, four, or five seats. Elections must be held at least every five years. To stand for election, a candidate must be an Irish citizen of at least 21 years of age. The Ceann Comhairle (chairperson) is a TD elected by the Dáil and is automatically returned at the following general election.

Role

TDs legislate, scrutinize the government, and represent constituents; Irish deputies are noted for a strong constituency-service orientation. The Dáil elects the Taoiseach (prime minister), who is then formally appointed by the President of Ireland. Prominent historical TDs include Éamon de Valera and Michael Collins, who sat in the First Dáil of 1919, which proclaimed itself the parliament of the Irish Republic.

History

The term was first used for the Sinn Féin MPs elected in the 1918 general election who refused to take their seats at Westminster and instead assembled in Dublin as the First Dáil on 21 January 1919. The title was carried into the Constitution of the Irish Free State and retained under the 1937 Constitution of Ireland.
