Shopska salad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Shopska salad with sirene cheese]

Shopska salad (Bulgarian: шопска салата) is a cold salad popular throughout the Balkans and southeastern Europe. It is made from diced tomatoes, cucumbers, onions or scallions, and raw or roasted peppers, dressed with sunflower oil (or olive oil) and topped with a generous grating of sirene, a brined white cheese similar to feta.

The salad takes its name from the Shopi, the inhabitants of the Shopluk region around Sofia. Although often described as an ancient peasant dish, food historians note that it was promoted nationally in the 1950s and 1960s by Bulgaria's state tourism agency Balkantourist, whose chefs standardised the recipe and presented it — with its white, green, and red colours echoing the Bulgarian flag — as the country's signature salad.

Variations

Closely related salads appear across the region: Serbian, Macedonian, and Bosnian versions are essentially the same, while the Greek horiatiki and the Turkish çoban salatası ("shepherd's salad") share the chopped-vegetable base. Shopska salad is traditionally served as a starter, often accompanied by rakia.
