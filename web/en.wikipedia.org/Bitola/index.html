Bitola

Bitola (Macedonian: Битола) is a city in the southwestern part of North Macedonia. It is located in the southern part of the Pelagonia plain, surrounded by the Baba, Nidže, and Kajmakčalan mountain ranges, 14 kilometres north of the Medžitlija-Níki border crossing with Greece. The city has a population of roughly 70,000 and is an administrative, cultural, industrial, and educational centre, known historically under the name Manastır during Ottoman rule.

History

Bitola stands near the ancient city of Heraclea Lyncestis, founded by Philip II of Macedon in the 4th century BC, whose mosaics and theatre survive at the southern edge of the modern city. Under Ottoman rule (1382–1912) the town, called Manastır, became a major military, administrative and commercial centre — the "city of the consuls", hosting consulates of numerous European states. It was the site of the Ilinden Uprising's regional activity in 1903, a major battle in the First Balkan War in 1912, and heavy fighting on the Macedonian front in the First World War.

Culture

Bitola hosts the Manaki Brothers Film Festival, the oldest festival dedicated to cinematography, named for the pioneering Balkan filmmakers Yanaki and Milton Manaki. Landmarks include Širok Sokak street, the Clock Tower, the Yeni and Ishak Çelebi mosques, and the old bazaar. St. Clement of Ohrid University, founded in 1979, is based in the city. Kemal Atatürk attended the military academy in Monastir, where a memorial room is maintained.
