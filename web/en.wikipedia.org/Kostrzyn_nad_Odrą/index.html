Kostrzyn nad Odrą (German: Küstrin) is a town in western Poland, in Lubusz Voivodeship, lying on the Oder River at its confluence with the Warta, directly on the border with Germany opposite the German town of Küstriner Vorland. It is best known for its ruined Prussian fortress old town — one of the most striking war ruins in Central Europe — and as the former host of the Pol'and'Rock Festival (formerly Woodstock Festival Poland).

History

A castle and trading settlement existed here by the early Middle Ages. From the 16th century Küstrin was developed into a major Prussian fortress protecting Berlin's eastern approaches; the young Frederick the Great was imprisoned in its fortress in 1730 after his failed escape attempt and forced to watch the execution of his friend Hans Hermann von Katte. In early 1945 the old town and fortress were almost completely destroyed in the fighting between the Wehrmacht and the advancing Red Army during the Vistula–Oder Offensive, and the fortress held out until late March 1945. After the war the Oder–Neisse line placed the town in Poland; its German population was expelled and replaced by Polish settlers. The ruined old town was never rebuilt and remains a protected archaeological zone, while a new town centre developed to the east.

Modern town

Today Kostrzyn is an industrial and border-crossing town with paper and machinery industries, linked by rail and road bridges to Berlin. The Kostrzyn Fortress Museum interprets the site's history. Nearby lies Ujście Warty National Park, a major wetland bird reserve at the Warta mouth. The town hosted the giant free Pol'and'Rock music festival from 2004 to 2019 before it moved to a new site.
