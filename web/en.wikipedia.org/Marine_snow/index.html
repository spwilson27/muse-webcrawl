Marine snow

Composition: Dead plankton, faecal matter, mucus, dust, aggregates
Role: Transports carbon and nutrients to the deep ocean
Part of: Biological pump

Marine snow is a continuous shower of mostly organic detritus falling from the upper layers of the water column to the deep ocean, resembling falling snow when seen by divers and submersibles.

Formation and descent

Particles originate as dead or dying phytoplankton, zooplankton faecal pellets, moults, mucus feeding structures and inorganic dust, aggregating into larger flocs as they sink over weeks. Microbes colonise the aggregates, and animals consume much of the material en route.

Significance

Marine snow is the principal food source for many deep-sea organisms in the aphotic zone and a key part of the biological pump that sequesters carbon in deep water and sediments for centuries or longer.
