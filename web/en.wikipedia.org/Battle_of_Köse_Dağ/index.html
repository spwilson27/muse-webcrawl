Battle of Köse Dağ

The Battle of Köse Dağ was fought on 26 June 1243 between the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum, under Sultan Kaykhusraw II, and an invading army of the Mongol Empire commanded by the general Baiju. Fought at Köse Dağ, a mountain pass near modern Zara in Sivas Province, Turkey, the battle ended in a decisive Mongol victory that made the Sultanate of Rum a Mongol tributary and opened Anatolia to Mongol domination.

Course and consequences

Against the advice of experienced commanders, the Seljuk army abandoned a strong defensive position and advanced; its vanguard was routed, panic spread, and Kaykhusraw fled the field, after which the Mongols overran the camp. The Mongols then captured Sivas and Kayseri. By the ensuing peace, the sultanate agreed to a heavy annual tribute in gold, silk and livestock. Mongol overlordship (continued under the Ilkhanate) hollowed out Seljuk power: after 1243 Anatolia fragmented into Turkmen beyliks, among them the small frontier principality of Osman — the seed of the future Ottoman Empire.
