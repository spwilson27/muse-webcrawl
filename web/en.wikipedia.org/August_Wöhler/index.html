August Wöhler

Born: 22 June 1819, Soltau, Kingdom of Hanover

Died: 21 March 1914 (aged 94), Hanover, Germany

Nationality: German

Known for: Wöhler curves; fatigue testing of railway axles

August Wöhler (22 June 1819 – 21 March 1914) was a German engineer and railway administrator, best known for his systematic investigations of metal fatigue. His work laid the foundations for the modern understanding of how components fail under repeated cyclic loading.

Career

Wöhler studied engineering in Hanover and entered railway service, eventually becoming a senior official responsible for rolling stock. Prompted by a series of railway axle failures, in the 1850s and 1860s he designed testing machines that subjected axles and specimens to repeated bending and torsion, and conducted the first comprehensive programme of fatigue experiments.

Wöhler curve

His results, presented in 1870, plotted stress amplitude against the number of cycles to failure — the S–N or Wöhler curve — showing that steel could withstand indefinitely a stress range below a limiting value, the fatigue limit. His conclusions established the principles that failure depends on stress range rather than maximum stress, and that design against fatigue requires testing under repeated loads. Wöhler also contributed to standardised materials testing and to German engineering standards.
