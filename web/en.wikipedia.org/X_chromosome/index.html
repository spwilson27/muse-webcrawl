X chromosome

The X chromosome is one of the two sex chromosomes in many animals, including mammals. Females typically have two X chromosomes (XX), males one X and one Y (XY). In humans the X chromosome spans about 154 million base pairs and contains roughly 800–900 protein-coding genes.

Human X chromosome
Length: ~154 million bp
Genes: ~800+
Type: Sex chromosome

X-inactivation

In female mammals one X in each cell is randomly inactivated early in development (lyonisation), forming a Barr body, equalising dosage with males.

Disorders

X-linked disorders include haemophilia, red-green colour blindness, Duchenne muscular dystrophy and fragile X syndrome. See sex linkage.

Evolution

The X and Y evolved from an ancestral pair of autosomes; the X retains most original genes while the Y degenerated.
