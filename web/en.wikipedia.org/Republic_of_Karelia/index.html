Republic of Karelia

Kizhi Island's wooden churches, a UNESCO World Heritage site in Karelia.

The Republic of Karelia (Russian: Республика Карелия; Karelian and Finnish: Karjalan tasavalta) is a federal republic of Russia in the north-west of the European part of the country. Its capital is Petrozavodsk, on the shore of Lake Onega. The republic borders Finland to the west and contains parts of the drainage of Lakes Ladoga and Onega, two of Europe's largest lakes, in a landscape of taiga forest, granite outcrops, and tens of thousands of lakes and rivers.

History

Karelia was contested for centuries between Sweden and Russia, passing definitively to Russia in the eighteenth century. After the Russian Revolution it was organised as the Karelian Labour Commune and then as an autonomous republic within Soviet Russia; in 1940, following the Winter War with Finland, it was briefly elevated into the Karelo-Finnish SSR, a full union republic, before being demoted back to an autonomous republic in 1956. Territory ceded by Finland in 1940 and 1944 forms the republic's western districts. The indigenous Karelians, a Finnic people closely related to the Finns, are today a small minority in the republic, most of whose roughly 530,000 residents are ethnic Russians.

Economy and culture

Forestry, wood processing, mining, and fishing anchor the economy, and tourism draws visitors to the open-air museum of wooden architecture on Kizhi Island and to the White Sea–Baltic Canal. The Karelian language and the epic poetry tradition associated with the region — much of the Finnish national epic Kalevala was collected here — remain important to regional identity.
