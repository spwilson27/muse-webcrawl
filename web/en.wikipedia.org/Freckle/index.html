Freckle

Freckles (medical name: ephelides) are small, flat, light-brown spots on the skin, typically appearing on sun-exposed areas such as the face, shoulders, and arms. They result from localised concentrations of the pigment melanin produced in response to ultraviolet radiation, in skin whose melanocytes distribute pigment unevenly.

Causes and characteristics

Freckling is strongly hereditary and is most common in people with fair skin and red or blond hair. Variants of the MC1R gene, which also influence red hair and sun sensitivity, are associated with a tendency to freckle. Freckles darken and become more numerous with sun exposure in summer and typically fade in winter; they usually appear in childhood and may become less prominent with age. Unlike lentigines (such as age spots), simple freckles do not involve an increased number of pigment-producing cells, only increased pigment production in existing cells.

Health significance

Freckles themselves are harmless and require no treatment. However, a tendency to freckle marks skin that is more susceptible to sunburn and skin cancer, so sun protection is especially advisable. Cosmetically, freckles have been variously concealed or fashionable at different times; in some periods they were artificially imitated with makeup.
