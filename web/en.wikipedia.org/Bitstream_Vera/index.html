Bitstream Vera

Bitstream Vera is a digital typeface family released in 2003 by Bitstream Inc. under a permissive free licence, developed principally by Jim Lyles. It was created for the GNOME Foundation and quickly adopted across free-software systems as a default font family. Vera comprises three styles: a humanist sans-serif, a serif, and a monospaced typeface (Vera Mono), each in regular, bold, oblique and bold oblique variants.

Specimen of Bitstream Vera Sans.

Design

Vera Sans is a humanist sans-serif with generous letter spacing and open apertures designed for legibility at small sizes on screen; it drew on earlier Bitstream designs such as Prima. The licence permits redistribution and modification subject to restrictions on using the names "Vera" and "Bitstream" for derivatives.

Derivatives

Because of its liberal licence, Vera became the basis for several widely used font families. The DejaVu fonts expanded Vera's character coverage enormously, adding thousands of glyphs for international scripts and symbols, and became the default on many Linux distributions. The Bera fonts adapt Vera for TeX, and numerous other projects have used its outlines as a starting point.
