National parks of Canada

The national parks of Canada are protected natural spaces administered by Parks Canada under the Canada National Parks Act. The system comprises 37 national parks and 11 national park reserves, representing the country's major terrestrial natural regions, together with national marine conservation areas. Together they cover more than 340,000 km² — over 3% of Canada's land area.

History

The system began in 1885 with the reservation of hot springs at Banff in the Rocky Mountains; Banff became Canada's first national park in 1887 (as Rocky Mountains Park). In 1911 Canada created the world's first national parks service, the Dominion Parks Branch under James B. Harkin. The National Parks Act of 1930 dedicated the parks "to the people of Canada for their benefit, education and enjoyment" and to be left "unimpaired for the enjoyment of future generations"; ecological integrity became the legislated management priority in 2000 amendments.

Notable parks

Well-known parks include Banff and Jasper (Alberta), Yoho and Kootenay (British Columbia), Wood Buffalo (the largest, spanning Alberta and the Northwest Territories), Gros Morne (Newfoundland), Fundy (New Brunswick), Cape Breton Highlands (Nova Scotia), Point Pelee (Ontario) and the Arctic parks of Quttinirpaaq and Auyuittuq. National park reserves, such as Pacific Rim and Gwaii Haanas, are managed as parks pending settlement of Indigenous land claims.
