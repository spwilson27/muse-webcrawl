Blue ocean strategy

Blue ocean strategy is a business strategy concept set out in the 2005 book of the same name by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, professors at INSEAD. It argues that companies can succeed not by battling competitors in existing market space — a "red ocean" of bloody rivalry — but by creating new, uncontested market space, a "blue ocean", that makes the competition irrelevant.

Concepts

The central idea is value innovation: simultaneously pursuing differentiation and low cost, rather than treating them as a trade-off. Tools proposed in the book include the strategy canvas, which maps the factors an industry competes on, and the "four actions framework" — eliminate, reduce, raise, create — for reshaping that curve. Case studies commonly cited include Cirque du Soleil, which combined elements of circus and theatre while removing costly animal acts, and the Nintendo Wii, which expanded the games market beyond traditional players.

Reception

The book became an international bestseller and its vocabulary entered common management usage. Critics have argued that the framework is better at explaining successes in hindsight than predicting them, and that "uncontested" spaces quickly attract imitators, turning blue oceans red.
