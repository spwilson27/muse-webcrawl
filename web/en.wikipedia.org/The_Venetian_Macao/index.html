The Venetian Macao (Chinese: 澳門威尼斯人) is a luxury hotel and casino resort in Macau, owned by the American Las Vegas Sands company. It is a sister property to The Venetian Las Vegas and is modeled on the Italian city of Venice, with replicas of its landmarks, canals, painted skies, and singing gondoliers. At its opening in 2007 it was the largest casino in the world and among the largest buildings in the world by floor area.

Facilities

The resort stands on the Cotai Strip, the reclaimed area joining Macau's Taipa and Coloane islands. Its main tower is 39 stories tall, and the complex covers about 10.5 million square feet (980,000 m²), making it one of the largest single structures in Asia. The casino floor offers thousands of gaming tables and slot machines. Retail space at the Grand Canal Shoppes contains hundreds of shops arranged along indoor canals crossed by bridges and served by gondolas.

Cotai Arena

The 15,000-seat Cotai Arena (formerly the Venetian Arena) hosts concerts, basketball, boxing, mixed martial arts, and televised events, and has been a major venue for championship boxing in Asia.

History

Construction cost an estimated US$2.4 billion. The property opened on 28 August 2007 and drew tens of thousands of visitors in its first hours, becoming the flagship of Sands' Cotai development and a symbol of Macau's rise to surpass Las Vegas as the world's largest gambling market.
