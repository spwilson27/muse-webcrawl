Janmashtami

Janmashtami
Also called: Krishna Janmashtami, Gokulashtami, Saatam Aatham
Observed by: Hindus
Type: Hindu festival
Significance: Birth of Krishna
Observances: Fasting, prayer, Dahi Handi, night vigils
Date: Bhadrapada, Krishna Paksha, Ashtami (Hindu lunisolar calendar)

Krishna Janmashtami (Sanskrit: कृष्णजन्माष्टमी), also known simply as Janmashtami or Gokulashtami, is an annual Hindu festival that celebrates the birth of Krishna, the eighth avatar of the god Vishnu. It is observed according to the Hindu lunisolar calendar, on the eighth day (Ashtami) of the Krishna Paksha (dark fortnight) in Bhadrapada, which overlaps with August or September of the Gregorian calendar.

It is a particularly important festival in the Vaishnavism tradition of Hinduism. Dance-drama enactments of the life of Krishna according to the Bhagavata Purana (such as Rasa Lila or Krishna Lila), devotional singing through the midnight when Krishna was believed to have been born, fasting (upavasa), a night vigil (ratri jagaran), and a festival (mahotsava) on the following day are a part of the celebrations.

Significance

According to Hindu tradition, Krishna was born in Mathura, in the prison cell of the Yadava king Kamsa, to Devaki and Vasudeva. Kamsa, Devaki's brother, had imprisoned the couple after a prophecy declared that their eighth child would kill him. After Krishna's birth, Vasudeva carried the infant across the Yamuna River to Gokul, where he was raised by the cowherd couple Nanda and Yashoda.

Celebrations

Devotees fast until midnight, the traditional hour of Krishna's birth, when the deity's image is bathed, dressed in new clothes, and placed in a cradle. Temples dedicated to Krishna, such as those in Mathura, Vrindavan, Dwarka, and Udupi, hold special celebrations. In Maharashtra, the festival is marked by Dahi Handi, in which teams of young men form human pyramids to break a clay pot filled with curd hung high above the street, re-enacting Krishna's childhood fondness for butter. In Manipur and Assam, Raas Leela dance performances narrate episodes of Krishna's life. The day after Janmashtami is celebrated as Nandotsav in some regions.

Regional names

  • Gokulashtami – Maharashtra and Goa
  • Saatam Aatham – Gujarat
  • Ashtami Rohini – Kerala
  • Sri Krishna Jayanti – Tamil Nadu and Karnataka
  • Dahi Handi – the pot-breaking ceremony in Maharashtra
