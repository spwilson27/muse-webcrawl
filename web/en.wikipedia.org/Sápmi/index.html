Sápmi is the cultural region traditionally inhabited by the Sámi people. It stretches across the northern parts of Norway, Sweden, and Finland, together with the Kola Peninsula of Russia — a region formerly known in English as Lapland. Sápmi has no official political borders; its extent is generally defined by the distribution of Sámi settlement and culture.

Geography

The region extends from central Norway and Sweden in the south to the Barents Sea in the north, encompassing fjords, mountains, tundra, taiga forest, and thousands of lakes. Important Sámi centers include Kautokeino and Karasjok in Norway, Kiruna and Jokkmokk in Sweden, and Inari in Finland.

History

The Sámi have inhabited the region for thousands of years, traditionally living by reindeer herding, fishing, hunting, and gathering. From the Middle Ages onward the surrounding nation states taxed and missionized the Sámi, and in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries assimilation policies — such as Norway's "Norwegianization" — suppressed Sámi languages and culture. Since the late twentieth century, Sámi parliaments have been established in Norway (1989), Finland (1996), and Sweden (1993), and Sámi language and cultural rights have gained legal recognition.

Culture

Sámi culture is expressed in the joik vocal tradition, the colorful gákti clothing, and duodji handicrafts. Reindeer herding remains an important livelihood and cultural symbol for a minority of Sámi. The Sámi flag, adopted in 1986, and Sámi National Day on 6 February commemorate the first Sámi congress, held in Trondheim in 1917.
