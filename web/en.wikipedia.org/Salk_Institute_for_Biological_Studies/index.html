Salk Institute for Biological Studies

The Salk Institute for Biological Studies is an independent, non-profit scientific research institute located in La Jolla, California, United States. It was founded in 1960 by Jonas Salk, the developer of the polio vaccine, together with the philanthropist Nathan S. Collin and support from the National Foundation for Infantile Paralysis, later known as the March of Dimes.

History

Salk envisioned an institute where biologists, chemists, physicists and scholars from other fields could work together in a collaborative environment. Construction of the campus began in 1962 on land donated by the City of San Diego. The buildings were designed by architect Louis Kahn and completed in 1965. The complex, built of concrete, teak, lead, glass and steel, consists of two six-story laboratory blocks separated by a travertine courtyard bisected by a narrow channel of water aligned with the setting sun on the equinoxes. It is widely regarded as a masterpiece of modern architecture and was designated a National Historic Landmark.

Research

Research at the Salk Institute is organized around areas including neuroscience, genetics, molecular and cell biology, plant biology, and computational biology. Faculty have included several Nobel Prize laureates, among them Francis Crick, who joined the institute in 1976, and Renato Dulbecco. The institute consistently ranks among the leading research organizations in the life sciences by publication impact.
