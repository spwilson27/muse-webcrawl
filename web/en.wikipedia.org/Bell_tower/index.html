Bell tower

A bell tower is a tower that contains one or more bells, or that is designed to hold bells even if it has none. Such a tower usually stands as part of a church, and it contains church bells, though there are also many secular bell towers, often part of a municipal building, an observation tower, or a university campus. A bell tower attached to a church is sometimes called a campanile, especially in Italy, where the word derives from campana, the Italian word for bell.

Purpose

Church bells are rung to summon worshippers to prayer, to mark the hours and the Angelus, to announce weddings, funerals, and feasts, and historically to give alarms. The height of the tower helps the sound carry across a parish or town. Secular towers, such as clock towers, often combine bells with a public clock for timekeeping.

History

Freestanding campanili in Italy became emblematic during the medieval period; examples include St Mark's Campanile in Venice and Giotto's Campanile in Florence. The Leaning Tower of Pisa is the campanile of Pisa Cathedral. In northern Europe, belfries of civic buildings — such as those of Bruges and Ghent in Flanders, now UNESCO World Heritage sites — symbolized municipal wealth and independence. Chinese and Japanese traditions also produced bell towers (such as the Xi'an Bell Tower) housing large bells used to mark time and occasions of state.

Types

Bell towers may be detached (campanile), integrated into a church's west front or crossing, or take the form of a small rooftop structure called a bell-cot or bell-gable (espadaña in Spanish architecture). A tower containing a carillon — a set of at least 23 tuned bells played from a keyboard — is also a bell tower in function.
