Aesthetics


Works of art are central objects of aesthetic study

Aesthetics (also spelled esthetics) is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature of beauty, art and taste, and with the creation and appreciation of beauty. It studies aesthetic concepts, aesthetic experience and aesthetic judgement. The term derives from the Greek aisthētikós, "perceptive, sensitive", and was given its modern philosophical sense by Alexander Baumgarten in the 18th century.

History

Ancient Greek philosophers including Plato and Aristotle discussed beauty and art extensively; Aristotle's Poetics analysed tragedy and mimesis (imitation). In the 18th century, aesthetics emerged as a distinct discipline: Baumgarten defined it as the science of sensory knowledge, David Hume examined the standard of taste, and Immanuel Kant's Critique of Judgment (1790) argued that judgements of beauty are subjective yet claim universal validity. Later thinkers such as Hegel, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche and Tolstoy developed theories of art's essence and purpose.

Topics

Key topics in aesthetics include the definition of art, the ontology of artworks, aesthetic properties such as beauty, sublimity and elegance, the interpretation of art, the role of emotion in aesthetic experience, and applied fields such as environmental aesthetics and everyday aesthetics.
