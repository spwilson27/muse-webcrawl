Electro (music)

Electro
Stylistic originsFunk, hip hop, electronic music, boogie
OriginEarly 1980s, New York City

Electro (or electro-funk) is a genre of electronic dance music and early hip hop that emerged in New York City in the early 1980s. It is characterised by drum machine rhythms — especially the Roland TR-808 — robotic vocoder vocals, and synthesiser lines influenced by funk and by the German group Kraftwerk.

History

The genre-defining record was Afrika Bambaataa and the Soulsonic Force's "Planet Rock" (1982), which interpolated Kraftwerk's "Trans-Europe Express" and "Numbers". Artists such as Mantronix, Egyptian Lover, Cybotron (with Juan Atkins) and Herbie Hancock's "Rockit" developed the style, which fed into Miami bass, freestyle, techno and modern electro revivals.

Characteristics

Electro tracks typically run around 120–135 beats per minute, built on syncopated 808 kick, snare and hi-hat patterns, with minimal rapping or vocoded chanting and heavy use of analogue synthesisers and samples.

Legacy

After declining in the late 1980s, electro was revived in the 1990s by artists such as Anthony Rother and Dave Clarke, and its drum patterns remain foundational to dance music.
