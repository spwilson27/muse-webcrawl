Serials crisis

The serials crisis is the long-running problem faced by academic libraries that the cost of scholarly journals ("serials") has risen far faster than library budgets and inflation, forcing cancellations and reducing access to research. From the mid-1980s, journal subscription prices commonly rose by high single-digit percentages annually, driven by publisher consolidation, "big deal" bundled licensing and the prestige economy of academic publishing.

Responses

Responses include consortial negotiation, cancellation projects, and the open access movement: institutional repositories, preprint servers such as arXiv, funder mandates, and transformative agreements. The crisis is a central motivation for open-access advocacy and for initiatives challenging commercial publishers' margins.
