T-tail

A T-tail is an empennage configuration in which the horizontal stabiliser is mounted to the top of the vertical stabiliser, forming a "T" shape when viewed from the front or rear. The arrangement keeps the horizontal stabiliser out of the disturbed airflow behind the wing and fuselage, and is common on rear-engined airliners and military transport aircraft.

Advantages

Placing the tailplane at the top of the fin keeps it clear of the wing wake and engine exhaust, giving smoother airflow and more effective control, and allows a smaller vertical fin because the tailplane acts as an endplate. On aircraft with fuselage-mounted engines, such as the Boeing 727 and DC-9 families, the T-tail keeps the stabiliser clear of the engine efflux. The configuration also permits shorter landing gear on some designs and gives ground clearance for rear loading ramps on military transports such as the C-130 successors and the C-17.

Disadvantages

The T-tail is prone to a dangerous condition known as the deep stall, in which at high angles of attack the wing wake blankets the tailplane, rendering the elevators ineffective and making recovery difficult; this contributed to the loss of the BAC One-Eleven prototype in 1963. The fin must also be stronger and heavier to support the tailplane and withstand its bending loads, and maintenance access is more difficult.
