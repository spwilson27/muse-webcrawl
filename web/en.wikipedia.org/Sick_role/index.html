Sick role

Sick role is a term used in medical sociology regarding sickness and the rights and obligations of patients and society concerning sickness. The concept was introduced by the American sociologist Talcott Parsons in his 1951 book The Social System.

Parsons argued that being sick is a form of deviance, because it disrupts the normal functioning of an individual within society, but that it is a socially sanctioned form of deviance with its own set of rights and obligations.

Rights and obligations

According to Parsons, the sick person has two rights:

  • The sick person is exempt from normal social roles.
  • The sick person is not responsible for their condition.

And two obligations:

  • The sick person should try to get well.
  • The sick person should seek technically competent help and cooperate with the medical professional.

Criticism

The model has been criticised for fitting acute illness better than chronic conditions, for assuming a single physician–patient relationship, and for reflecting the values of mid-twentieth-century American society. Later sociologists have modified the model to account for patient activism, chronic disease, and variations across cultures and illnesses such as mental illness.
