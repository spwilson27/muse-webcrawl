Blade Runner is a 1982 science fiction film directed by Ridley Scott from a screenplay by Hampton Fancher and David Peoples. Starring Harrison Ford, Rutger Hauer, Sean Young, and Edward James Olmos, it is an adaptation of Philip K. Dick's 1968 novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? The film is set in a dystopian future Los Angeles of 2019, in which synthetic humans known as replicants are bio-engineered by the powerful Tyrell Corporation to work in space colonies. When a fugitive group of advanced replicants led by Roy Batty (Hauer) escapes back to Earth, burnt-out cop Rick Deckard (Ford) reluctantly agrees to hunt them down.

Plot

In 2019 Los Angeles, former police officer Rick Deckard is arrested and brought to his ex-supervisor, who informs him that four replicants of the Nexus-6 model are on Earth illegally. Deckard, a "blade runner" trained to hunt and "retire" replicants, is forced back into service. At the Tyrell Corporation he meets Rachael, an experimental replicant who believes herself human. As Deckard tracks down the replicants — Zhora, Leon, Pris, and their leader Roy Batty — he begins to question the morality of his mission and his own nature. The film culminates in Batty's famous "tears in rain" monologue as his four-year lifespan expires.

Production

Filmed largely on the Warner Bros. backlot in Burbank, the production was troubled by a difficult shoot, budget overruns, and conflict between Scott and the crew. The visual design, by Syd Mead and Douglas Trumbull's effects team, created an influential "retro-fitted" future aesthetic of perpetual rain, neon, and crowded streetscapes.

Versions

Several versions exist: the 1982 U.S. theatrical cut (with a voice-over narration and "happy ending"), the International Cut, the 1992 Director's Cut (which removed the narration and added the unicorn dream sequence), and the 2007 Final Cut, supervised by Scott, widely considered definitive.

Legacy

Initially a box-office disappointment that divided critics, Blade Runner became a cult classic and is now regarded as one of the greatest science fiction films ever made and a leading example of neo-noir and cyberpunk. It was selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry in 1993. A sequel, Blade Runner 2049, directed by Denis Villeneuve, was released in 2017.
