The Korean independence movement was a series of political, diplomatic and military efforts by Korean nationalists to liberate Korea from Japanese colonial rule, which lasted from the annexation of 1910 until Japan's defeat in the Second World War in 1945. The movement spanned peaceful mass demonstrations, armed resistance in Manchuria and China, and diplomatic lobbying in the United States and Europe.

Background

Japan forced Korea into protectorate status with the Eulsa Treaty of 1905 and formally annexed the peninsula in 1910. Korean resistance began immediately, with guerrilla "righteous armies" (uibyeong) fighting Japanese forces, and figures such as Ahn Jung-geun, who assassinated former Resident-General Itō Hirobumi in Harbin in 1909.

March 1st Movement (1919)

Inspired by Woodrow Wilson's principle of self-determination and the death of the former emperor Gojong, Korean leaders issued a Declaration of Independence on 1 March 1919. Peaceful demonstrations spread nationwide; Japanese authorities suppressed them violently, killing thousands. Although the movement failed to win immediate independence, it galvanized Korean nationalism and led directly to the establishment of the Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea in Shanghai in April 1919.

Provisional Government and armed struggle

The Provisional Government, led at various times by Syngman Rhee and Kim Gu, coordinated resistance from exile. In Manchuria, Korean independence armies won notable engagements such as the Battle of Qingshanli (1920). In the 1930s, the Korean Patriotic Organization carried out attacks including Yun Bong-gil's bombing in Shanghai's Hongkou Park in 1932. During the Second World War the Provisional Government formed the Korean Liberation Army (1940) and declared war on Japan and Germany in 1941.

Aftermath

Japan's surrender in August 1945 ended 35 years of colonial rule. Korea was divided at the 38th parallel into Soviet and American occupation zones, leading to the establishment of separate states in 1948. The Republic of Korea's constitution honors the independence movement, and 1 March and 15 August (Liberation Day) are national holidays.
