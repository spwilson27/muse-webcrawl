Neil deGrasse Tyson (born October 5, 1958) is an American astrophysicist, author, and science communicator. Since 1996, he has been the Frederick P. Rose Director of the Hayden Planetarium at the Rose Center for Earth and Space in New York City.

Career

Tyson studied at Harvard University, the University of Texas at Austin, and Columbia University, where he earned his PhD in astrophysics in 1991. His research interests include stellar formation, exploding stars, dwarf galaxies, and the structure of the Milky Way.

Science communication

He hosted the television series Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey (2014), a follow-up to Carl Sagan's original, and the podcast StarTalk. His books include Astrophysics for People in a Hurry (2017). Tyson was prominent in the scientific discussion that led to Pluto's reclassification as a dwarf planet in 2006.
