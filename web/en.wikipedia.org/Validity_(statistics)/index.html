Validity (statistics)

Validity is the main extent to which a concept, conclusion, measurement, or test is well-founded and likely corresponds accurately to the real world. In statistics and psychometrics, validity concerns whether a tool measures what it claims to measure.

Types

Common types include content validity (whether a measure represents all facets of a construct), criterion validity (whether results correlate with an external criterion, including concurrent and predictive forms), and construct validity (whether the measure actually captures the theoretical construct). Face validity is the informal judgement that a measure looks appropriate. In experimental design one also distinguishes internal validity (whether conclusions about causality are warranted) and external validity (whether results generalise).

Relation to reliability

Validity is related to but distinct from reliability, the consistency of a measurement. A measurement can be reliable without being valid — consistently measuring the wrong thing — but validity generally requires reliability. Modern testing standards treat validity as a unitary concept supported by multiple sources of evidence.
