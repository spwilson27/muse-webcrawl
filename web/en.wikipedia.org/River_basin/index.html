Drainage basin

A drainage basin (also called a river basin, watershed, or catchment) is an area of land where all flowing surface water converges to a single point, such as a river mouth, or flows into another body of water, such as a lake or ocean. The basin includes all of the surface water from rain runoff, snowmelt, hail, sleet and nearby streams that run downslope towards the shared outlet, as well as the groundwater underneath the earth's surface.

Boundaries and structure

Basins are separated from one another by drainage divides — ridges of high ground such as the Continental Divide of the Americas. Each basin forms a branching hierarchy: smaller tributary sub-basins join together into larger ones. The shape, slope, soils, vegetation and size of a basin determine its discharge and flood behaviour.

Largest basins

The Amazon basin is the world's largest, draining about 7 million km² of South America, followed by the Congo and Mississippi basins. Endorheic basins, such as those of the Caspian Sea and Central Asia, drain inland with no outlet to the ocean.

Management

Because activities throughout a basin affect water quantity and quality downstream, drainage basins are widely used as the unit for water-resource governance — reflected in river basin authorities and in the European Union's Water Framework Directive, which organises management by river basin districts.
