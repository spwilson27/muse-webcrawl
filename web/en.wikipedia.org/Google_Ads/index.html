Google Ads (formerly Google AdWords) is an online advertising platform developed by Google, where advertisers bid to display brief advertisements, service offerings, product listings, and videos to web users. It can place ads in the results of search engines like Google Search, on non-search websites, in mobile apps, and in videos.

History

Google launched AdWords in 2000, initially with a cost-per-impression model before moving to pay-per-click auctions. In July 2018 the service was renamed Google Ads as part of a broader rebranding of Google's advertising products.

How it works

Advertisers bid on keywords; when a user searches, an auction determines which ads appear and in what order, based on bid amounts and a quality score reflecting relevance. Advertisers generally pay per click (CPC), though cost-per-mille and cost-per-acquisition models are also available. Advertising on Google properties and partner sites generates the majority of Alphabet's revenue.

Criticism

The platform has faced criticism over trademark bidding, ad fraud, malware in ads, and its role in surveillance-based advertising.
