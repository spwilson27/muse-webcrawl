The Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) is an art museum located in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, on 53rd Street between Fifth and Sixth Avenues. Founded in 1929, it played a major role in developing and collecting modern art and is often identified as one of the largest and most influential museums of modern art in the world. MoMA's collection spans painting and sculpture, drawings and prints, photography, architecture and design, film, and media and performance.

Founding and early years

The museum was founded by three progressive collectors and patrons — Lillie P. Bliss, Mary Quinn Sullivan and Abby Aldrich Rockefeller — who saw the need for an institution devoted to the art of our time. Chartered in 1929, it opened days after the Wall Street Crash in rented quarters in the Heckscher Building, with Alfred H. Barr Jr. as its first director. Barr conceived a multi-departmental museum modelled partly on European precedents, and his landmark exhibitions — including Cubism and Abstract Art (1936) and Fantastic Art, Dada, Surrealism (1936) — helped define the canon of modernism for American audiences.

Architecture department and the International Style

MoMA established the first curatorial department of architecture and design in 1932. Its inaugural exhibition, Modern Architecture: International Exhibition, curated by Henry-Russell Hitchcock and the young Philip Johnson, introduced European modernism to America and named the International Style. The department's collection includes models, drawings and design objects from a paperclip to a helicopter, and Johnson served as its founding curator and a long-serving museum trustee.

Building

In 1939 the museum moved to its current site in a building by Philip S. Goodwin and Edward Durell Stone, an early example of International Style museum design, with an outdoor sculpture garden designed by Johnson in 1953. Major expansions followed: a Cesar Pelli-designed tower (1984), a renovation and expansion by Yoshio Taniguchi (2004), and a further expansion by Diller Scofidio + Renfro with Gensler (2019) that added more than 40,000 square feet of gallery space and rehung the collection in a more chronological, cross-disciplinary arrangement.

Collection and exhibitions

The collection of roughly 200,000 works includes such icons as Van Gogh's The Starry Night, Picasso's Les Demoiselles d'Avignon and Broadway Boogie Woogie by Mondrian, Monet's Water Lilies triptych, Matisse's The Dance, works by Cézanne, Dalí, Pollock, Warhol and Kahlo, and landmark holdings in photography, film and industrial design. Since its 2019 reopening, MoMA has sought to broaden the modernist canon, integrating work by women and artists from outside Europe and North America throughout its galleries.

MoMA PS1 and governance

Since 2000 the museum has been affiliated with MoMA PS1 in Long Island City, Queens, a centre for experimental contemporary art founded in 1971 as the Institute for Art and Urban Resources. The museum is governed by a board of trustees and has been led by directors including Barr, René d'Harnoncourt and Glenn D. Lowry (director from 1995).
