Lancia Stratos

The Lancia Stratos HF, widely known simply as the Lancia Stratos, is a rear mid-engined sports car and rally car made by Italian car manufacturer Lancia. The HF stands for High Fidelity. The car won the World Rally Championship in 1974, 1975 and 1976, and is regarded as one of the most successful purpose-built rally cars.

Lancia Stratos HF
Manufacturer: Lancia
Production: 1973–1978 (492 built)
Designer: Marcello Gandini, Bertone
Engine: 2.4 L Ferrari Dino V6
Layout: rear mid-engine, rear-wheel drive

History

The design grew from Bertone's striking Stratos Zero concept of 1970. Marcello Gandini designed the short, wedge-shaped production car, with a steel monocoque centre section, fibreglass bodywork and the 2,418 cc Dino V6 from Ferrari, producing 190 hp in road trim and up to about 280–320 hp (and more with turbocharging) in competition versions.

Rally success

Driven by Sandro Munari and others, the Stratos dominated mid-1970s rallying, also winning events such as the Targa Florio and the Giro d'Italia automobilistico. Later turbocharged Group 5 versions raced on circuits.

Legacy

Only 492 road-legal examples were built to satisfy homologation. The car remains an icon; a modern reinterpretation, the New Stratos, was produced in a very small series by Manifattura Automobili Torino from 2018.
