Bornc. 1499, Sahagún, Spain
Died23 October 1590, Mexico City, New Spain
OccupationFranciscan friar, ethnographer

Bernardino de Sahagún (c. 1499 – 1590) was a Spanish Franciscan friar and missionary in colonial Mexico. He is regarded as a pioneer of modern ethnography for his vast bilingual encyclopedia of Aztec (Nahua) life, the Historia general de las cosas de Nueva España, known as the Florentine Codex.

Work in New Spain

Sahagún arrived in Mexico in 1529 and taught at the Colegio de Santa Cruz de Tlatelolco, where he trained Nahua scholars who became his collaborators. Over decades he collected information from indigenous elders using systematic questionnaires, recording in Nahuatl and Spanish the religion, calendar, natural history, economy, and history of the Aztecs. The work was compiled in the Florentine Codex, a lavishly illustrated twelve-book manuscript now held in the Laurentian Library in Florence.

Legacy

Although his superiors grew suspicious of his project and parts of his work were confiscated, Sahagún's writings are today the single most important source for the study of pre-conquest and early colonial Nahua culture.
