Pizza is a dish of Italian origin consisting of a flat, usually round base of leavened wheat dough topped with tomatoes, cheese, and often various other ingredients — such as anchovies, mushrooms, onions, olives, vegetables, and meats — baked at a high temperature, traditionally in a wood-fired oven. A small pizza is sometimes called a pizzetta, and a person who makes pizza is known as a pizzaiolo.

History

Flatbreads with toppings were eaten across the ancient Mediterranean — Greeks baked bread with oils and herbs, and Roman authors describe similar dishes — but modern pizza was born in Naples in the 18th and 19th centuries as inexpensive food for the working poor (lazzaroni). The addition of the tomato, a New World fruit long distrusted in Europe, was decisive. Naples' Antica Pizzeria Port'Alba, founded in 1738 as a stand for travellers, is regarded as the world's first pizzeria. According to tradition, in 1889 pizzaiolo Raffaele Esposito created the Pizza Margherita — tomato, mozzarella, and basil — for Queen Margherita of Savoy, giving the dish patriotic prestige. Italian emigrants carried pizza to the United States, where Lombardi's opened in New York in 1905; after the Second World War it exploded in popularity worldwide, spawning regional styles and global chains such as Domino's and Pizza Hut.

Styles

Neapolitan pizza (pizza napoletana) is protected in the EU as a Traditional Specialty Guaranteed product: a soft, elastic dough, San Marzano tomatoes, and mozzarella di bufala or fior di latte, blistered in a wood-fired oven at about 485 °C for 60–90 seconds. Its two classic forms are the marinara (tomato, garlic, oregano, olive oil) and the Margherita. American styles include thin-crust New York-style pizza, sold by the slice; Chicago deep-dish, baked in a pan with chunky tomato sauce over thick cheese; Detroit-style; and New Haven apizza. Many other countries have developed localised versions, from Japanese seafood toppings to Brazilian dessert pizzas.

Production and economy

Pizza dough is made from flour, water, salt, and yeast, sometimes with oil or sugar, kneaded and proofed before being stretched by hand or roller. Baking temperatures and times vary by style; Neapolitan pizzas cook in about a minute in a wood oven, while deep-dish may bake for 40 minutes. Pizza is one of the world's most popular foods: the United States alone accounts for roughly 13% of all pizzerias globally and consumes billions of pizzas annually, and the worldwide pizza market is worth well over US$150 billion. In 2017 UNESCO inscribed "the art of the Neapolitan pizzaiuolo" on its list of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
