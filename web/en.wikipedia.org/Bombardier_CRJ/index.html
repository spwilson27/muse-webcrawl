Bombardier CRJ

The Bombardier CRJ (Canadair Regional Jet) is a family of regional jets introduced in 1991 by Bombardier Aerospace. Derived from the Canadair Challenger 600 business jet, the CRJ pioneered the 50-seat regional jet market and spawned a series of stretched variants. Production ended in 2020, and in 2019 Bombardier sold the program to Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, which supports the fleet through its MHI RJ Aviation Group.

Variants

The original CRJ100 and CRJ200 seat about 50 passengers and are powered by two rear-mounted General Electric CF34 turbofans. The stretched CRJ700 series (70 seats) introduced a redesigned wing and led to the CRJ900 (up to 90 seats) and the CRJ1000 (up to 104 seats), which compete with Embraer's E-Jet family. The "NextGen" models brought a revised interior with larger windows and bins.

Operations

Nearly 2,000 CRJs were delivered, mainly to North American and European regional airlines operating as feeders for major carriers under brands such as Delta Connection, American Eagle, and United Express — their use shaped by U.S. pilot "scope clause" limits on regional flying. The family's economics suited thin routes between small cities and hubs, though the 50-seat models fell from favor as fuel prices rose.

Accidents

Notable accidents involving the family include Comair Flight 5191 (CRJ100, Lexington, 2006), which attempted takeoff on the wrong runway, and the January 2025 mid-air collision near Washington, D.C. involving a CRJ700 operating as American Eagle Flight 5342. Investigations of several incidents have highlighted training, icing, and operational safety issues common to regional airline operations.
