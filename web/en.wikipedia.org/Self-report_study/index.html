Self-report study

A self-report study is a type of survey, questionnaire or poll in which respondents read questions and select responses by themselves, without an interviewer. Self-report measures are widely used in psychology, medicine and the social sciences to assess attitudes, behaviours, symptoms and personality.

Uses

Common instruments include personality inventories such as the Big Five questionnaires, symptom checklists used in clinical screening, and national health and crime surveys. Because they are cheap and easy to administer, self-reports allow researchers to collect large samples and to study internal states that cannot be observed directly.

Limitations

Self-reports are vulnerable to biases, including social desirability bias, recall inaccuracy, careless responding and differences in how respondents interpret items. Researchers mitigate these problems with validated scales, attention checks, anonymity assurances and by triangulating self-reports with behavioural or physiological measures.
