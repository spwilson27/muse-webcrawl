Jewish Agency for Israel

Founded1929 (predecessor founded 1921)
HeadquartersJerusalem, Israel
PurposeAliyah, Jewish–Israel engagement, community support

The Jewish Agency for Israel (Hebrew: הסוכנות היהודית לארץ ישראל) is the largest Jewish nonprofit organisation in the world. It was founded in 1929 as the operative branch of the World Zionist Organization and served as the de facto government of the Jewish community in Mandatory Palestine; after Israeli independence in 1948 its role was defined by Israeli law as promoting Jewish immigration (aliyah) and immigrant absorption.

History

Under the League of Nations Mandate, the Jewish Agency was the recognised representative body of world Jewry in Palestine, directing immigration, settlement, and institution-building; its chairman David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948 and became its first prime minister. The agency played central roles in the mass immigrations of the 1950s, Operation Moses and Operation Solomon bringing Ethiopian Jews, and the absorption of about a million immigrants from the former Soviet Union after 1989.

Activities today

The Jewish Agency operates globally through emissaries (shlichim) and runs programs including immigration and absorption services, Israel experience programs such as Masa, youth villages for at-risk youth, support for small businesses and communities in the Negev and Galilee, and emergency assistance for Jewish communities worldwide.
