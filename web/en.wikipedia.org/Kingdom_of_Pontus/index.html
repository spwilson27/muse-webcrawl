Kingdom of Pontus

The Kingdom of Pontus was a Hellenistic kingdom centred in the historical region of Pontus, on the southern shore of the Black Sea in north-eastern Anatolia (modern Turkey). Founded by Mithridates I in 281 BC, it was ruled by the Mithridatic dynasty — of Persian origin, claiming descent from the Achaemenid satraps — until Rome annexed it in 63 BC.

Rise

From its capitals at Amaseia and later Sinope, Pontus combined Persian and Greek culture: its kings patronised Greek cities and temples while maintaining Iranian cults. The kingdom grew wealthy from Black Sea trade in grain, timber, fish, and slaves, and controlled rich mines of iron and silver in the Pontic mountains.

Mithridates VI and the wars with Rome

Pontus reached its zenith under Mithridates VI Eupator (120–63 BC), who conquered the Bosporan Kingdom, Colchis, and much of Asia Minor and Greece, and fought three Mithridatic Wars against the Roman Republic. In 88 BC he ordered the massacre of some 80,000 Romans and Italians in Asia (the Asiatic Vespers). Defeated by Lucullus and then Pompey, Mithridates fled to the Crimea and died in 63 BC, by tradition ordering a guard to kill him after a failed suicide by poison — to which he had famously habituated himself with small doses.

Aftermath

Pompey reorganised western Pontus into the Roman province of Bithynia et Pontus; eastern districts passed to client kings, including Polemon I and Pythodorida, until Tiberius annexed the remainder in 64 AD. Pontic Greeks retained a distinct identity into the Byzantine and Ottoman eras, and the Pontic Greek dialect survives today.
