1960 Winter Olympics

The 1960 Winter Olympics, officially the VIII Olympic Winter Games, were held from 18 to 28 February 1960 at Squaw Valley, California, United States (the resort now known as Palisades Tahoe). Thirty nations and 665 athletes competed in 27 events across four sports.

Organisation

Squaw Valley was a barely developed ski area when it won the bid in 1955, and its organiser Alexander Cushing built the resort essentially from scratch with state and federal support. Walt Disney chaired the pageantry committee responsible for the ceremonies, which included elaborate opening festivities and a choir of thousands. For the first time, computers were used to tabulate results, provided by IBM.

Events

The programme included ice hockey, figure skating, speed skating, alpine skiing, cross-country skiing, Nordic combined, ski jumping and biathlon — the latter making its Olympic debut in its modern form. Because organisers considered a bobsleigh run too costly for the small field, bobsleigh was omitted, the only time it has been absent from a Winter Games. The United States ice hockey team, coached by Jack Riley, won an unexpected gold medal by defeating Canada and the Soviet Union, a triumph later echoed by the 1980 "Miracle on Ice." American Carol Heiss won the ladies' figure skating title, and the Soviet Union topped the medal table.

Legacy

The Games transformed Squaw Valley into a major resort and televised the Winter Olympics to a broad American audience for the first time; CBS paid for the broadcast rights. The Olympic flame tower and Blyth Arena sites remain part of the resort's Olympic Village district.
