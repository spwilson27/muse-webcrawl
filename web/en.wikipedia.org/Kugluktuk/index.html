Kugluktuk (Inuinnaqtun for "place of moving water"), formerly Coppermine, is a hamlet in the Kitikmeot Region of Nunavut, Canada, at the mouth of the Coppermine River on Coronation Gulf in the Arctic Ocean. It is the westernmost community in Nunavut and the traditional home of the Copper Inuit.

History

The area was long inhabited by Copper Inuit, named for their use of native copper from the Coppermine River region, explored for Europeans by Samuel Hearne in 1771. A Hudson's Bay Company trading post was established in the early 20th century, and the community grew around it. In 1996 the name was officially changed from Coppermine to Kugluktuk.

Geography and life

Kugluktuk lies north of the Arctic Circle, with continuous daylight in early summer and polar night in winter. There are no roads to the community; it is reached by air (Kugluktuk Airport) and by summer sealift. Subsistence hunting and fishing — Arctic char, caribou, muskox and seal — remain central to local life alongside wage employment. Nearby Kugluk/Bloody Falls Territorial Park protects the rapids and a historic site on the Coppermine River.
