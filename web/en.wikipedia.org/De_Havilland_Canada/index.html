De Havilland Aircraft of Canada Limited is a Canadian aircraft manufacturer known for rugged short takeoff and landing (STOL) bush planes and regional airliners. Founded in 1928 as a subsidiary of the British de Havilland company, it is based in Toronto, Ontario.

History

The company built trainers and licence-produced aircraft during the Second World War. Postwar it became famous for its own designs: the DHC-1 Chipmunk, DHC-2 Beaver, DHC-3 Otter, and the twin-engine DHC-6 Twin Otter, which became icons of bush flying. The four-engine Dash 7 and the Dash 8 turboprop airliner followed.

Ownership changes

The Canadian government privatized the company in 1986, selling it to Boeing; Bombardier acquired it in 1992. In 2019, Longview Aviation Capital bought the Dash 8 program and the De Havilland Canada name, reviving the brand, while Viking Air continued producing the Twin Otter.

Products

Current and recent programs include the DHC-515 Firefighter water bomber and continued support and production of the Twin Otter and Dash 8 families.
