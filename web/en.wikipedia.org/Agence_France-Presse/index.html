Agence France-Presse (AFP) is a French international news agency headquartered in Paris. It is the world's oldest news agency, founded in 1835 as Agence Havas, and one of the three largest global agencies alongside Reuters and the Associated Press.

History

Charles-Louis Havas founded the agency in Paris in 1835, translating foreign newspapers and relaying news by carrier pigeon and telegraph. After the liberation of Paris in 1944, the agency was refounded as Agence France-Presse. A 1957 statute guarantees its independence: AFP has no shareholders and is governed by a board representing clients and the French state.

Operations

AFP employs journalists in bureaus around the world and produces news in French, English, Spanish, Portuguese, German, and Arabic, supplying thousands of media and institutional clients with text, photographs, video, and infographics.
