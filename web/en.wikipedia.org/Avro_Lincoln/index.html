The Avro Type 694 Lincoln is a British four-engined heavy bomber, which first flew on 9 June 1944. Developed from the Avro Lancaster, the Lincoln was initially known as the Lancaster IV and V. It arrived too late to see combat in the Second World War but became the RAF's standard heavy bomber of the early Cold War.

Design and development

The Lincoln had a larger wing and fuselage, more powerful Rolls-Royce Merlin 85 engines, and greater range and ceiling than the Lancaster, designed for operations in the Pacific against Japan. It could carry up to 14,000 lb of bombs.

Operational history

RAF Lincolns saw action during the Malayan Emergency, bombing communist insurgent positions, and with the Royal Australian Air Force—where they were built by the Government Aircraft Factory—served into the 1960s. Argentine Air Force Lincolns were the last in service, retiring in 1967. The Lincoln was also adapted into the Avro Shackleton maritime patrol aircraft, which served for decades.
