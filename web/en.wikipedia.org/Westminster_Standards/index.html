Westminster Standards

The Westminster Standards are the doctrinal documents produced by the Westminster Assembly of Divines (1643–1653), convened by the English Parliament during the Civil War to reform the Church of England. They became the foundational confessional documents of Presbyterian churches worldwide.

Documents

The Standards comprise the Westminster Confession of Faith (1646), the Larger Catechism and the Shorter Catechism (1647–48) — the latter famous for its opening question, "What is the chief end of man? Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him for ever" — together with the Directory for Public Worship, the Form of Presbyterial Church Government, and the Directory for Family Worship.

Adoption and influence

The Church of Scotland adopted the Confession in 1647, and it became the subordinate standard of Presbyterian churches in Scotland, Ireland, America, and the wider Reformed world. The Confession sets out a Calvinist system of doctrine covering Scripture, God and the Trinity, predestination, covenant theology, the sacraments, and church government. Some American Presbyterian bodies later revised its chapters on the civil magistrate and religious liberty.
