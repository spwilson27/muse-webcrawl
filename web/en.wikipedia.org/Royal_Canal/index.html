The Royal Canal near Mullingar

The Royal Canal (Irish: An Chanáil Ríoga) is a canal in Ireland built for freight and passenger transport between Dublin and the River Shannon. It runs 146 kilometres from Spencer Dock in Dublin, through Maynooth, Mullingar and Longford, joining the Shannon at Cloondara in County Longford.

Construction

The canal was begun in 1790 by the Royal Canal Company, a rival concern to the Grand Canal company, whose southern route to the Shannon was already under way. Building costs overran badly — the company is famously said to have been swindled by a director's brother who diverted the line to serve his own town — and the canal was not completed to the Shannon until 1817, at a total cost of about £1.4 million, much of it borne by the government after the original company collapsed.

Working life

Passenger "fly boats", drawn by horses at a trot and later replaced by steam services, made the canal briefly fashionable, but the arrival of the Midland Great Western Railway alongside the canal in the 1840s and 1850s destroyed its passenger trade. Freight in grain, coal and turf continued into the twentieth century, declining after CIE took control in 1945. Commercial traffic ceased in 1955 and the canal was officially closed to navigation in 1961; a motorway-era proposal would have filled in part of the Dublin section as a road.

Restoration

The Royal Canal Amenity Group, founded in 1974, campaigned for restoration, and under the Office of Public Works and later Waterways Ireland the canal was progressively repaired. The main line was fully reopened to navigation in 2010. The towpath now forms the Royal Canal Greenway, a 130-kilometre walking and cycling route opened in 2021, linking Dublin to the Shannon and joining the wider Dublin–Galway greenway corridor.
