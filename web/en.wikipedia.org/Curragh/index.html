Curragh

The Curragh (Irish: An Currach, "place of the running horse") is a flat open plain of almost 5,000 acres (2,000 hectares) of common land in County Kildare, Ireland. This area is well known for Irish horse breeding and training, and for the Curragh Camp, historically one of the principal military camps of the British Army in Ireland and now the Defence Forces Training Centre of the Irish Defence Forces.

Geology and history

The plain was formed from glacial outwash sand and gravel deposited as the ice retreated after the last Ice Age, creating a unique free-draining grassland habitat. The Curragh is rich in archaeology, with ringforts, barrows, and enclosures, and features in early Irish literature — it is associated with the goddess Brigid and with the Fianna of legend, whose races were said to be held there.

Horse racing

The Curragh Racecourse hosts all five of Ireland's Classic flat races, including the Irish Derby, and the surrounding plains are ringed by training yards and stud farms, cementing the Curragh as the centre of the Irish thoroughbred industry. The first recorded race on the plain took place in 1727, though racing traditions are far older.

The Curragh Camp and notable events

A permanent military camp was established by the British Army in 1855, at the height of the Crimean War. In March 1914 the camp was the scene of the "Curragh incident" (often called the Curragh mutiny), when British cavalry officers indicated they would resign rather than move against Ulster loyalists resisting Home Rule — a crisis that shook the British government. During the Second World War (the Emergency in Ireland), internment camps at the Curragh held Allied and Axis airmen who came down on Irish soil, as well as IRA internees. Today the camp is the main training centre of the Irish Defence Forces.
