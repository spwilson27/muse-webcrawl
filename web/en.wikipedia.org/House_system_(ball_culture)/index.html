House system (ball culture)

The house system is the kinship structure at the heart of ball culture, the underground LGBTQ+ subculture that emerged among Black and Latino queer communities in New York City. A house is a chosen family: a social group led by a "mother" and sometimes a "father", which takes in members — its "children" — who adopt the house's name as a surname and compete together at balls.

History

The system is usually traced to Crystal LaBeija, who, protesting the racism of the mainstream drag pageant circuit, co-founded the House of LaBeija in the early 1970s, often dated to 1972. Houses offered housing, mentorship and protection to young queer people, many of them homeless or estranged from their families. Through the 1980s houses proliferated in Harlem and beyond, and ball categories centred on dance — most famously voguing — brought the culture wider attention through the documentary Paris Is Burning (1990) and Madonna's song "Vogue".

Structure and function

House mothers and fathers mentor children in ballroom competition, but also in employment, education and health. Children compete as a team, pooling trophies and reputation; houses can be at "war" on the ballroom floor while remaining networks of care off it. Later prominent houses include the House of Ninja, founded by Willi Ninja, the House of Xtravaganza, and the House of Ebony. The television series Pose (2018–2021) depicted the system for a mainstream audience.
