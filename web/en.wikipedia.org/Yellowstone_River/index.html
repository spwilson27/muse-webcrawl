Yellowstone River

The Yellowstone River is a tributary of the Missouri River, approximately 692 miles (1,114 km) long, in the western United States. It rises in Wyoming in the Absaroka Range, flows through Yellowstone National Park and Montana, and joins the Missouri in North Dakota near the Montana border. It is the longest undammed river in the contiguous United States.

Course

The river begins at the confluence of its North and South forks southeast of Yellowstone Lake, flows into the lake, and exits at Fishing Bridge before dropping over the Upper and Lower Falls into the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone — the Lower Falls, at 308 feet, is nearly twice the height of Niagara. It exits the park at Gardiner, Montana, turns east through Paradise Valley and past Livingston, Billings, Miles City, and Glendive, and passes near Makoshika and Theodore Roosevelt's Elkhorn Ranch country before meeting the Missouri below Fort Union.

History and ecology

The river's name translates the Hidatsa Mi tse a-da-zi, "Yellow Rock River," rendered Roche Jaune by French trappers. William Clark explored its lower course on the return of the Lewis and Clark Expedition in 1806. Fur trade posts such as Fort Union (1828) stood near its mouth.

The Yellowstone is a premier trout fishery (native Yellowstone cutthroat trout) and habitat for paddlefish, endangered pallid sturgeon, and the isolated Yellowstone grizzly and elk herds. It is also known for environmental incidents, including the 2011 ExxonMobil pipeline spill near Laurel and the 2015 Glendive oil spill, and for the 2016 parasite-driven fish kill and river closure.
