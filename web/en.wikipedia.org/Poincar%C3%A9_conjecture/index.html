Poincaré conjecture

The Poincaré conjecture is a theorem in geometric topology stating that every simply connected, closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere. Posed by Henri Poincaré in 1904, it was one of the most famous open problems in mathematics for nearly a century and one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems.

Solution

Grigori Perelman proved the conjecture in papers posted in 2002 and 2003, completing Richard Hamilton's program using Ricci flow with surgery. Perelman's work actually established the more general geometrization conjecture of William Thurston, of which the Poincaré conjecture is a special case.

Consequences

The result means that a three-dimensional space in which every loop can be shrunk to a point, and which has no boundary and finite extent, must be topologically a sphere. Perelman declined both the Fields Medal (2006) and the Millennium Prize of one million dollars (2010).
