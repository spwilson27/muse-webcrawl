The 400 metres, or 400-metre dash, is a sprinting event in track and field competitions. It has been featured in the athletics programme at the Summer Olympic Games since 1896 for men and since 1964 for women. On a standard outdoor running track, it is one lap around the track; runners start from staggered positions and race in lanes for the entire distance.

Race characteristics

The 400 metres is the longest event considered a sprint: it demands a combination of raw speed and speed endurance, as athletes must sustain near-maximal effort while managing the build-up of lactic acid. Runners typically accelerate hard from the blocks, run the back straight at controlled maximum speed, and fight fatigue through the final straight. Indoors, the distance is run over two laps of a 200 m track.

Records and notable athletes

The men's world record is 43.03 seconds, set by South Africa's Wayde van Niekerk in the final of the 2016 Rio Olympics, breaking Michael Johnson's long-standing mark of 43.18. Johnson remains the only man to win the 200 m and 400 m at the same Olympics (Atlanta 1996). The women's world record, 47.60 seconds by Marita Koch of East Germany in 1985, is one of the longest-standing records in athletics.
