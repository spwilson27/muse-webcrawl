Calgary is the largest city in the Canadian province of Alberta and the largest metro area within the three Prairie provinces. It stands at the confluence of the Bow River and the Elbow River in the foothills of the Rocky Mountains, about 80 km east of the mountain front ranges.

History

The area was home to Indigenous peoples, including the Blackfoot Confederacy, Tsuut'ina and Stoney Nakoda, for thousands of years. In 1875 the North-West Mounted Police built Fort Calgary at the rivers' confluence; the name was suggested by Colonel James Macleod after Calgary on the Isle of Mull in Scotland. The Canadian Pacific Railway arrived in 1883, and Calgary grew first as a ranching centre and then, after the 1947 Leduc oil discovery and the energy booms of the 1970s, as the headquarters city of Canada's petroleum industry.

Economy and culture

Calgary hosts the head offices of most of Canada's major energy companies and holds the country's second-largest concentration of corporate headquarters. Each July the city stages the Calgary Stampede, a ten-day rodeo and exhibition billed as "The Greatest Outdoor Show on Earth", which draws more than a million visitors. Calgary hosted the 1988 Winter Olympics, the first Winter Games held in Canada, and its legacy venues include the Olympic Oval and WinSport's Canada Olympic Park.

Geography and climate

The city has a humid continental climate with long, dry winters moderated by warm chinook winds that can raise temperatures by 20 °C or more within hours, and short, mild summers. Severe flooding of the Bow and Elbow rivers in June 2013 caused billions of dollars of damage and the largest evacuation in the city's history.
