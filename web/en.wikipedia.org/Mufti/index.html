Mufti

A mufti (Arabic: مفتي) is an Islamic jurist qualified to issue a nonbinding opinion (fatwā) on a point of Islamic law (Sharia). A fatwa issued by a mufti provides an informed opinion for judges and lay Muslims; formal legal rulings in court are made by a qadi.

A mufti is qualified to issue fatwas

Role and qualifications

Traditionally, a mufti had to be learned in the Qur'an, hadith, the legal school (madhhab) to which he belonged, and methods of legal reasoning (usul al-fiqh). In many Muslim-majority states, the highest official is a Grand Mufti, heading a state fatwa council (such as Egypt's Dar al-Ifta). A person who seeks a fatwa is called a mustafti.

"Mufti" clothing

Separately, in British military slang dating from the 19th century, "mufti" denotes plain civilian clothes worn by someone normally in uniform, possibly because off-duty officers' dress resembled the robes of a mufti.
