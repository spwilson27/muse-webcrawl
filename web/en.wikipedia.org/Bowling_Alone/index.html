Bowling Alone

Bowling Alone: The Collapse and Revival of American Community is a 2000 book by political scientist Robert D. Putnam. It argues that the United States has undergone an unprecedented collapse in civic, social, and political engagement — what Putnam calls social capital — since the 1960s.

The title comes from the observation that while total bowling increased in America, league bowling declined: people were bowling alone rather than in organized groups. Putnam marshalled data showing declining membership in churches, unions, PTAs, fraternal organizations, and informal socializing alike.

He attributed the decline mainly to generational change, television and electronic entertainment, suburbanization, and time and money pressures. The book became one of the most discussed works of American social science of its era and revived debate over community and civic life.
