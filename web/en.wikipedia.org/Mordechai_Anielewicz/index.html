Mordechai Anielewicz

Mordechai Anielewicz (1919 – 8 May 1943) was a Polish-Jewish resistance fighter and the commander of the Jewish Combat Organization (Żydowska Organizacja Bojowa, ŻOB) during the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising in German-occupied Poland during World War II.

Early life

Anielewicz was born in Wyszków, Poland, and grew up in Warsaw. He joined the Zionist youth movement Hashomer Hatzair and became an organiser of Jewish self-defence as conditions in the Warsaw Ghetto deteriorated under Nazi occupation.

Warsaw Ghetto Uprising

When mass deportations to Treblinka began in the summer of 1942, Anielewicz helped form the ŻOB. In April 1943, when German forces moved to liquidate the ghetto, the ŻOB launched an armed uprising. Anielewicz commanded the fighters from the command bunker at 18 Miła Street.

Death and legacy

On 8 May 1943, surrounded by German forces, Anielewicz and many of his comrades died in the bunker at Miła 18. He is remembered as a symbol of Jewish resistance during the Holocaust. Kibbutz Yad Mordechai in Israel is named in his honour.
