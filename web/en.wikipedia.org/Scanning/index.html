Scanning


Scanning an image with a flatbed scanner

Scanning is the process of systematically examining or measuring something, point by point or line by line, in order to collect information about it. The term is used in many fields, including imaging, computing, medicine, physics and telecommunications.

Image scanning

An image scanner is a device that optically scans images, printed text, handwriting or objects and converts them into digital form. Flatbed scanners, sheet-fed scanners, handheld scanners and drum scanners are common types. Scanned images are commonly saved in formats such as JPEG, PNG, TIFF or PDF, and optical character recognition (OCR) software can convert scanned text into editable characters.

Medical scanning

In medicine, scanning refers to diagnostic imaging techniques such as computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), ultrasound and positron emission tomography (PET), which build up pictures of internal structures slice by slice.

Other uses

In physics, a scanning probe microscope measures surfaces by moving a fine probe across them. In broadcasting, earlier television systems used mechanical and electronic scanning to assemble pictures line by line. In computing, scanning can also mean checking files for viruses or reading data from barcodes and other machine-readable codes.
