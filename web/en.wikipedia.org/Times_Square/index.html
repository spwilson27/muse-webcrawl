Times Square

Times Square is a major commercial neighbourhood and tourist destination in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, formed by the junction of Broadway, Seventh Avenue and 42nd Street. Nicknamed "the Crossroads of the World", it is famous for its bright illuminated billboards and for hosting the annual New Year's Eve ball drop.

History

Originally called Longacre Square, the area was renamed in April 1904 after The New York Times moved its headquarters to the newly built Times Building (now One Times Square). The first New Year's Eve celebration there was held that year, and the ball drop began in 1907. The surrounding blocks became the heart of the Broadway theatre district. After a period of decline from the 1960s to the 1980s, when the square was associated with crime and peep shows, a major redevelopment in the 1990s transformed it into a sanitised, family-oriented tourist zone.

Today

Times Square is one of the world's most visited tourist attractions, drawing on the order of 50 million visitors a year, with hundreds of thousands of pedestrians passing through daily. Zoning rules require buildings to display a minimum amount of illuminated signage, preserving the square's luminous character. Parts of Broadway through the square were pedestrianised in 2009.
