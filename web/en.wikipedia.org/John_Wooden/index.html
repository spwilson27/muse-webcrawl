John Wooden

John Robert Wooden (October 14, 1910 – June 4, 2010) was an American basketball coach and player. Nicknamed the "Wizard of Westwood", he won ten NCAA national championships in a 12-year period as head coach for the UCLA Bruins, including a record seven in a row. No other team has won more than four in a row in Division I college men's or women's basketball.

Playing career

Wooden played basketball at Purdue University, where he was a three-time consensus All-American guard and won a national championship in 1932. He was the first person inducted into the Basketball Hall of Fame both as a player (1960) and as a coach (1973).

Coaching career

After coaching at Indiana State, Wooden became UCLA's head coach in 1948. His Bruins won their first title in 1964 and dominated the sport through 1975, at one point winning 88 consecutive games, still a men's Division I record. He coached great players such as Lew Alcindor (later Kareem Abdul-Jabbar) and Bill Walton. Wooden retired in 1975 immediately after winning his tenth championship.

Philosophy

Wooden was renowned for his teaching and his "Pyramid of Success", a set of principles — including industriousness, friendship, loyalty, cooperation and enthusiasm — that he began developing in the 1930s and published in 1948. He was known for simple, quotable maxims such as "Be quick, but don't hurry" and "Failing to prepare is preparing to fail".

Honours

Wooden received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2003. He died of natural causes in Los Angeles in 2010, aged 99.
