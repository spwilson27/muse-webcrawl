Société des Bains de Mer


The Monte Carlo Casino, operated by the SBM

The Société des Bains de Mer et du Cercle des Étrangers à Monaco (SBM; English: Society of Sea Baths and of the Circle of Foreigners in Monaco) is a publicly traded company registered in the Principality of Monaco. It manages the Monte Carlo Casino and other gambling, hospitality, and leisure properties in the principality. The Monegasque state is the majority shareholder. SBM is the largest employer in Monaco.

History

The company was founded in 1863 under a concession granted by Prince Charles III to François Blanc, a French casino operator who had built successful gambling resorts at Bad Homburg. Charles III sought a revenue source for the principality, which had lost the towns of Menton and Roquebrune and most of its tax base. Blanc's company built the casino, grand hotels including the Hôtel de Paris (opened 1864), and the sea-bathing establishment from which the company takes its name, and developed the surrounding district, named Monte Carlo in the prince's honour. The success of the casino famously allowed Monaco to abolish direct taxation for residents.

Properties

SBM operates the Monte Carlo Casino, the Casino Café de Paris, the Sun Casino and Monte-Carlo Bay Casino; luxury hotels including the Hôtel de Paris Monte-Carlo, Hôtel Hermitage Monte-Carlo, Monte-Carlo Beach, and Monte-Carlo Bay Hotel & Resort; the Thermes Marins Monte-Carlo spa; restaurants, nightclubs, and the Salle des Étoiles sporting club venue. It also owns a large share of Monaco's high-end real estate and beach clubs.

Ownership

The company is listed on Euronext Paris. The Principality of Monaco holds a majority stake (about 59%), and in 2015 the casino and hospitality group Galaxy Entertainment Group and LVMH each acquired minority stakes of around 5%. The SBM's fortunes remain closely tied to Monaco's image as a luxury tourism and gaming destination.
