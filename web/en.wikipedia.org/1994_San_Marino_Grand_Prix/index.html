The 1994 San Marino Grand Prix was a Formula One motor race held at Imola on 1 May 1994. It is remembered as one of the darkest weekends in the history of the sport: Austrian driver Roland Ratzenberger was killed in qualifying on 30 April, and three-time world champion Ayrton Senna died in a crash during the race the following day.

The weekend

The meeting was marred by serious incidents from the start. During Friday practice, Rubens Barrichello survived a high-speed accident at the Variante Bassa. On Saturday, Ratzenberger's Simtek suffered a front wing failure at the Villeneuve curva; he died of a basilar skull fracture — the first fatality at a Formula One race meeting since 1982. Senna, deeply shaken, nonetheless took pole position.

The race

The race itself began with a start-line collision between JJ Lehto and Pedro Lamy that injured spectators and a police officer, bringing out the safety car. Two laps after the restart, on lap 7, Senna's Williams left the road at the Tamburello corner at around 233 km/h and struck an unprotected concrete wall. Senna sustained fatal head injuries and died at hospital in Bologna. The race was stopped and later restarted; Michael Schumacher won, ahead of Nicola Larini and Mika Häkkinen. A further pit-lane accident, when Michele Alboreto's Minardi shed a wheel, injured four mechanics.

Aftermath

The deaths of Ratzenberger and Senna ended a twelve-year period without a fatality in Formula One and triggered sweeping safety reforms: the reformation of the Grand Prix Drivers' Association, redesigned circuits with large run-off areas and chicanes at Tamburello and Villeneuve, reduced engine power, raised cockpit sides, improved crash testing, and eventually devices such as HANS and the halo. Senna received a state funeral in Brazil, where three days of national mourning were declared. Investigations in Italy continued for years; the technical cause of Senna's crash was attributed by investigators to a modified steering column failure, a finding contested by the Williams team.
