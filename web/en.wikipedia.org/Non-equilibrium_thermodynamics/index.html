Non-equilibrium thermodynamics

Non-equilibrium thermodynamics is the branch of thermodynamics that deals with systems not in thermodynamic equilibrium—most real systems—by describing flows of matter and energy, entropy production, and the approach to (or departure from) equilibrium.

Concepts

Classical irreversible thermodynamics, developed by Lars Onsager, Ilya Prigogine, and others, relates thermodynamic fluxes (heat flow, diffusion, chemical reaction rates) to forces (temperature and chemical-potential gradients) through phenomenological coefficients, subject to Onsager's reciprocal relations. Entropy production is the central quantity measuring irreversibility. Near equilibrium, relations are linear; far from equilibrium, nonlinear behaviour can produce ordered dissipative structures, such as convection cells and oscillating reactions, a field for which Prigogine received the 1977 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Applications

The theory underpins transport phenomena in engineering, atmospheric and climate science, biophysics, and theories of the origin of life that emphasize energy dissipation and self-organization.
