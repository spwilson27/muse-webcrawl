Counterculture

Counterculture
DefinitionA culture whose values and norms oppose those of mainstream society
Classic exampleCounterculture of the 1960s
Related termsSubculture, avant-garde, bohemianism

A counterculture is a culture whose values, norms, and behaviours run substantially counter to those of mainstream society. Countercultures typically emerge among marginalised or youthful groups and often seek to define themselves in deliberate opposition to dominant institutions.

The 1960s counterculture

The term is most strongly associated with the counterculture of the 1960s in the United States and other Western countries, encompassing the hippie movement, opposition to the Vietnam War, civil rights and feminist activism, communal living, psychedelic music and art, and experimentation with drugs and alternative spirituality. Centres included San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury and London's underground scene.

Earlier and later movements

Earlier examples include Romanticism, bohemianism, the Beat Generation of the 1950s (an immediate precursor), and various religious nonconformist groups. Later countercultures include punk in the 1970s, rave culture, and aspects of hacker and open-source culture.

Relationship with the mainstream

Countercultural styles and ideas are frequently absorbed and commercialised by the mainstream — a process critics call co-optation — while new countercultures form in reaction. Scholars debate whether this cycle blunts countercultures' transformative potential or spreads their innovations widely.
