Indonesian National Revolution

Date17 August 1945 – 27 December 1949
LocationDutch East Indies (Indonesia)
ResultDutch recognition of Indonesian sovereignty
Key figuresSukarno, Mohammad Hatta, Sudirman; Netherlands

The Indonesian National Revolution (Indonesian: Revolusi Nasional Indonesia) was an armed conflict and diplomatic struggle between the emerging Republic of Indonesia and the Netherlands following the end of the Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies. It began with the Indonesian declaration of independence by Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta on 17 August 1945 and ended with the Netherlands' formal transfer of sovereignty on 27 December 1949.

Republican forces during the revolution, 1945–1949.

Course of the conflict

British forces initially accepted the Japanese surrender and attempted to restore order, clashing with republican forces most famously at the Battle of Surabaya in November 1945. The Dutch returned in strength, negotiating the Linggadjati Agreement (1946) but twice launching large offensives — euphemistically called "police actions" in 1947 and 1948 — that captured much territory including Yogyakarta and Sukarno himself. Guerrilla resistance under General Sudirman continued, and a republican counteroffensive at Yogyakarta in March 1949 demonstrated the Republic's survival.

Outcome

International pressure, particularly from the United States and the United Nations, compelled the Dutch to negotiate at the Round Table Conference in The Hague, leading to the transfer of sovereignty to the United States of Indonesia in December 1949. Indonesia abrogated the federal structure in 1950, becoming a unitary republic.
