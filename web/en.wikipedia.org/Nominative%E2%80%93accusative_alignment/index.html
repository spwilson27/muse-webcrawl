Nominative–accusative alignment

Nominative–accusative alignment is a type of morphosyntactic alignment in which the single argument of an intransitive clause (S) is treated the same as the agent argument of a transitive clause (A), while the patient argument (P) is treated differently. The shared case for S and A is the nominative, and the case for P is the accusative.

Distribution

This alignment is found in most European languages, including Latin, German, Russian, and (in pronoun systems) English, as well as in many languages worldwide. In English it survives clearly only in pronouns: he (S/A) contrasts with him (P).

Comparison with other alignments

Nominative–accusative alignment contrasts with ergative–absolutive alignment, which groups S with P, and with tripartite and split systems. Some languages, called split-ergative, use nominative–accusative alignment in some contexts and ergative alignment in others, conditioned by tense, aspect, or the type of noun phrase.
