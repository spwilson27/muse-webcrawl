Persian architecture

Persian architecture or Iranian architecture is the architecture of Iran and parts of the rest of West Asia, the Caucasus and Central Asia. Its history dates back to at least 5,000 BC with characteristic examples distributed over a vast area from Turkey and Iraq to Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, and from the Caucasus to Zanzibar.

Historical styles

Pre-Islamic architecture includes Elamite ziggurats, the Achaemenid palaces of Persepolis and Pasargadae with their columned halls (apadana), and Sassanid vaulted halls and domes, exemplified by the Taq Kasra iwan at Ctesiphon. Islamic-era architecture developed the iwan, the dome on squinches, muqarnas, and elaborate tilework.

Key elements

Recurring features include the four-iwan courtyard plan, pointed arches, girih geometric tile patterns, calligraphic ornament, windcatchers (badgir) for cooling, and Persian gardens (charbagh) organised around water channels.

Notable examples

Celebrated works include the Shah Mosque and Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque in Isfahan, the mosques and madrasas of Samarkand and Bukhara under Timurid patronage, and the Golestan Palace in Tehran.
