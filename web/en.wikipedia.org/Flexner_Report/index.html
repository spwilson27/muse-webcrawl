Flexner Report

AuthorAbraham Flexner
Published1910
PublisherCarnegie Foundation
SubjectMedical education in the US and Canada

The Flexner Report is a book-length landmark study of medical education in the United States and Canada, written by Abraham Flexner and published in 1910 under the aegis of the Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching.

Findings

Flexner personally visited all 155 medical schools then operating and found many to be small, profit-driven proprietary schools with inadequate laboratories, no university affiliation, and minimal admission standards. He recommended closing most of them, requiring a university-based, science-driven curriculum modelled on Johns Hopkins, and raising entrance requirements to include prior college education.

Impact and criticism

The report catalysed sweeping reform: dozens of schools closed or merged, and American medical education was standardised around laboratory science and clinical teaching. Critics note that the closures fell heavily on schools training Black physicians and women — five of the seven Black medical schools closed — narrowing access to the profession for decades.
