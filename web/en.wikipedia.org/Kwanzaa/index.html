Kwanzaa

Kwanzaa is an annual celebration of African-American culture held from 26 December to 1 January. It was created in 1966 by activist Maulana Karenga, based on African harvest festival traditions from various parts of West and Southeast Africa, and was first celebrated in the United States in the aftermath of the Watts riots.

Seven principles

Each of the seven days is dedicated to one of the Nguzo Saba (seven principles): Umoja (unity), Kujichagulia (self-determination), Ujima (collective work and responsibility), Ujamaa (cooperative economics), Nia (purpose), Kuumba (creativity), and Imani (faith). A candle is lit each day on the kinara, a seven-branched candle holder, alongside symbols such as the mkeka mat, mazao (crops), and the kikombe cha umoja (unity cup). The name derives from the Swahili phrase matunda ya kwanza, "first fruits".
