81st Infantry Division (United States)

81st Infantry Division
Active1917–1919; 1942–1946
Nickname"Wildcat Division"
EngagementsWWI: Meuse-Argonne. WWII: Palau (Angaur, Peleliu), Leyte
InsigniaWildcat patch — first U.S. Army shoulder sleeve insignia

The 81st Infantry Division ("Wildcat") was an infantry division of the United States Army that served in both World Wars. Organized in 1917 at Camp Jackson, South Carolina, the division is credited with creating the first shoulder sleeve insignia in the U.S. Army: soldiers from the Wildcat Creek area wore a felt wildcat patch, a practice General John J. Pershing approved and which spread throughout the Army.

World War I

The division deployed to France in 1918 and fought in the Meuse-Argonne offensive in the final weeks of the war, suffering over 1,000 casualties before the Armistice. It was demobilized in 1919.

World War II

Reactivated in 1942 at Camp Rucker, Alabama, the 81st fought in the Pacific. It captured Angaur in the Palau Islands in September 1944 and then relieved the 1st Marine Division on Peleliu, clearing the island's stubborn defenses. The division later served on Leyte in the Philippines and performed occupation duty in Japan before inactivation in 1946.
