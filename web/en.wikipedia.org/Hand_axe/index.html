Hand axe

A hand axe is a prehistoric stone tool with two faces, characteristic of the Acheulean industry.

A hand axe (or biface) is a prehistoric stone tool with two faces that is the longest-used tool in human history. It is a characteristic tool of the Lower and Middle Palaeolithic periods, made by hominins including Homo erectus and Homo heidelbergensis, and later by Neanderthals and early modern humans.

Form and manufacture

Hand axes are typically teardrop-, oval-, or triangular-shaped pieces of stone — usually flint, chert, basalt, or quartzite — that have been worked on both faces (bifacially) with hammerstones and softer percussors of antler, bone, or wood to produce a symmetrical tool with a sharp edge around most of its perimeter. They range from a few centimetres to over 20 cm in length. The shape is often highly symmetrical, a feature some researchers have interpreted as evidence of an aesthetic preference or of a mental template held by the maker.

Distribution and the Acheulean

Hand axes are the signature tool of the Acheulean industry, named after Saint-Acheul in France and first appearing in Africa about 1.76 million years ago, for example at Kokiselei in Kenya and Konso in Ethiopia. The Acheulean spread with early humans across Africa, western and southern Asia, and Europe, persisting for well over a million years with remarkably little change — one of the most enduring technologies ever produced. East and Southeast Asia long appeared to lack hand axes east of the "Movius Line", though finds in China (such as the Bose basin) and Korea have weakened that division.

Function

Use-wear analysis and experiments indicate that hand axes were multipurpose tools, effective for butchering large animals, chopping wood, digging, and cutting plants and hides. Their exact role remains debated: proposals have ranged from portable cores for striking flakes, to throwing weapons, to social or sexual display objects. Exceptional caches and unused, oversized, or exceptionally beautiful examples suggest functions beyond the purely utilitarian in at least some cases.
