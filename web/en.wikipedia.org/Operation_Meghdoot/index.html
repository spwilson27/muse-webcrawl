Operation Meghdoot was the code name for the Indian Armed Forces operation launched on 13 April 1984 to seize control of the Siachen Glacier in the disputed Kashmir region. Named after the Sanskrit epic poem Meghadūta ("cloud messenger"), the operation pre-empted a planned Pakistani move onto the glacier by a matter of days and began the Siachen conflict, one of the longest-running and highest-altitude military confrontations in history.

Background

The 1949 Karachi Agreement and the 1972 Shimla Agreement had left the boundary beyond point NJ9842 undefined, "thence north to the glaciers". Through the 1970s and early 1980s Pakistan permitted or organized mountaineering expeditions in the area, which India interpreted as attempts to stake a claim. Indian intelligence learned in 1983 that Pakistan planned to occupy the glacier's key passes in the spring of 1984.

The operation

On 13 April 1984, troops of the Indian Army's Kumaon Regiment and Ladakh Scouts, lifted by Indian Air Force helicopters to altitudes above 5,000 metres, occupied the Bilafond La and Sia La passes on the Saltoro Ridge before Pakistani forces could arrive. Pakistan's counter-efforts over the following days failed to dislodge them. India thus gained control of the entire 76-kilometre glacier and its tributaries, while Pakistan held the Gyong La pass area to the west.

Aftermath

Both countries maintained permanent posts on the ridge for decades, suffering far more casualties from altitude, cold and avalanches than from combat. A ceasefire along the Actual Ground Position Line took effect in November 2003. Operation Meghdoot remains a celebrated episode in Indian military history.
