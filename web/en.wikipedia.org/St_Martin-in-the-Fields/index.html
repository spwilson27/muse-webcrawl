St Martin-in-the-Fields

St Martin-in-the-Fields is an Anglican church at the north-east corner of Trafalgar Square in the City of Westminster, London. Dedicated to Saint Martin of Tours, it was built in 1722–1726 to a design by James Gibbs in the Neoclassical style, with a prominent spire and a temple-like portico that became highly influential on church architecture, particularly in the United States.

History

A church has stood on or near the site since the medieval period, when it stood amid fields between Westminster and the City of London. The present building replaced an earlier church during the rebuilding programme of the early 18th century. The church has long royal connections as the parish church of Buckingham Palace and has been associated with social welfare work, including the BBC's first religious broadcast and its Christmas Appeal for the homeless.

Music and community

The church is renowned as a concert venue, home to the Academy of St Martin in the Fields, founded by Sir Neville Marriner in 1958. Its crypt houses a café, and the church runs extensive services for homeless people, including The Connection at St Martin's day centre.
