Lernaean Hydra

The Lernaean Hydra (Greek: Λερναία Ὕδρα) is a serpentine water monster in Greek mythology, offspring of Typhon and Echidna, that dwelt in the lake of Lerna in the Argolid. It possessed many heads — the number varies between five and 100 in ancient sources — and for every head severed, two more grew in its place. Its breath and blood were poisonous.

The second labour of Heracles

Killing the Hydra was the second of the Twelve Labours imposed on Heracles. Finding that decapitation only multiplied the monster's heads, Heracles had his nephew and charioteer Iolaus cauterise each neck stump with a burning torch as he cut, preventing regrowth. The one immortal head he buried beneath a heavy rock. He then dipped his arrows in the Hydra's poisonous gall — a weapon that would later cause the deaths of the centaur Nessus, Heracles himself, and others. Because Heracles had received Iolaus's help, Eurystheus refused to count the labour. During the fight Hera sent a crab, Karkinos, to bite Heracles' foot; he crushed it, and Hera placed it in the sky as the constellation Cancer.

Legacy

"Hydra" has become a byword for a problem that multiplies when attacked, and the creature's name survives in the freshwater genus Hydra, whose regenerative abilities recall the myth.
