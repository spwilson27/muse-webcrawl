Lakota (Lakȟótiyapi), also referred to as Teton Sioux, is a Siouan language spoken by the Lakota people of the Sioux tribes. Together with Dakota and Nakota it forms the Dakotan group within the Western Siouan family, and its speakers are concentrated in South Dakota, North Dakota, Nebraska, Minnesota, and Montana.

Characteristics

Lakota is written with several competing orthographies, the most widely used being the Standard Lakota Orthography promoted by the Lakota Language Consortium, which marks nasalised and aspirated consonants with diacritics. The language has a system of enclitic particles that mark evidentiality and speaker gender in certain contexts, and verbs carry rich prefixing morphology.

Status and revitalisation

Fluent first-language speakers number only in the low thousands and are mostly elderly, making Lakota endangered. Immersion schools such as those on the Pine Ridge and Standing Rock reservations, university programmes, and the Lakota Language Consortium's dictionaries and curricula lead revitalisation efforts.
