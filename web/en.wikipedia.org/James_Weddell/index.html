James Weddell

James Weddell (24 August 1787 – 9 September 1834) was a British sailor, navigator and seal hunter who explored the Antarctic and in 1823 reached the southernmost point attained at that time. The Weddell Sea in Antarctica is named after him.

Life and voyages

Born in Ostend to a Scottish father and Flemish mother, Weddell went to sea young, served in the Royal Navy during the Napoleonic Wars, and afterwards entered the merchant service. In 1822–24 he commanded the brig Jane on a sealing voyage to the South Orkney Islands and beyond.

Farthest south

In February 1823, in an exceptionally ice-free season, Weddell sailed to 74°15′ S — about three degrees (over 200 nautical miles) farther south than James Cook's record — in the sea he called the "Sea of King George IV," renamed the Weddell Sea in 1900. He found no land and open water, and, mindful that the season was advancing, turned north. His 1825 book, A Voyage Towards the South Pole, recorded the expedition and remains a classic of Antarctic literature.

The Weddell seal also bears his name. Weddell died in London in 1834, relatively poor, having argued unsuccessfully for further government-backed Antarctic exploration.
