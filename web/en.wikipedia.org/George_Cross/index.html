The George Cross (GC) is the highest award bestowed by the British government for non-operational gallantry or gallantry not in the presence of an enemy. It is equal in status to the Victoria Cross, the highest military award for gallantry, but is awarded for acts of civilian bravery and to military personnel for actions not in the face of the enemy.

Creation

The George Cross was instituted on 24 September 1940 by King George VI, at the height of the Blitz, to recognise the courage of civilians and service personnel during air raids. Recipients of the Empire Gallantry Medal exchanged their awards for the George Cross, as did holders of the Albert Medal and Edward Medal in later years.

Description

The medal is a plain silver cross pattée bearing the Royal Cypher of George VI and an image of Saint George slaying the dragon, suspended from a dark blue ribbon. The inscription reads "For Gallantry". Recipients are entitled to the postnominal letters GC.

Collective awards

The George Cross has been awarded collectively three times: to the island of Malta in 1942 for its people's endurance under siege, to the Royal Ulster Constabulary in 1999, and to the National Health Service in 2021.
