Golden Bear

The Golden Bear (German: Goldener Bär) is the highest prize awarded for the best film at the Berlin International Film Festival (the Berlinale). The bear is also the symbol of the city of Berlin. The prize was introduced in 1951, at the first festival, when the winning films were chosen by a West German panel.

[ Image placeholder: A Golden Bear statuette ]

The statuette depicts a bear standing on its hind legs, based on the heraldic animal of Berlin, and was designed by the sculptor Renée Sintenis. Between 1952 and 1955 the winners were determined by audience voting; since 1956, when the festival was officially recognised by FIAPF, the Golden Bear has been awarded by an international jury.

The award

The Golden Bear is awarded to the best feature film in the festival's Competition section. A second major prize, the Silver Bear Grand Jury Prize, ranks below it, along with Silver Bears for best director, best leading performance, and other categories. A separate Golden Bear is also given for the best short film, and an Honorary Golden Bear recognises lifetime achievement.

Notable winners

Winning films have included 12 Angry Men (1957), Wild Strawberries (1958), Alphaville (1965), Rain Man (1989), In the Name of the Father (1994), Sense and Sensibility (1996), Spirited Away (2002, shared), Head-On (2004), Grbavica (2006), A Separation (2011), Fire at Sea (2016), and On the Adamant (2023). The director Ang Lee is the only filmmaker to have won the Golden Bear twice, for The Wedding Banquet (1993) and Sense and Sensibility.
