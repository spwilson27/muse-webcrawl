Serie A

Serie A is the top tier of the Italian football league system. It has operated as a round-robin tournament since the 1929–30 season and is contested by 20 clubs. The winner receives the Scudetto (a shield-shaped badge worn the following season) and the Coppa Campioni d'Italia trophy. Serie A is regarded as one of the best football leagues in the world and is known for its historic emphasis on tactical and defensive play.

History

Italian football championships were first played in 1898, with regional group stages and playoffs. The modern single-table Serie A began in 1929. Juventus is the most successful club, with 36 official league titles, followed by Inter Milan (20) and AC Milan (19). The league's golden era in the late 1980s and 1990s attracted many of the world's best players, including Diego Maradona, Marco van Basten, and Ronaldo. The 2006 Calciopoli scandal, involving match-influence by leading clubs, led to Juventus's relegation and stripped titles.

Format

The season runs from August to May. Each club plays the others twice (home and away) for a total of 38 matches. The top four qualify for the UEFA Champions League, and the bottom three are relegated to Serie B. Clubs earn a star on their badge for every ten titles won.

Champions

A total of 16 clubs have won the title. Beyond the dominant trio, Roma, Napoli, Lazio, Fiorentina, Torino, and Cagliari are among the champions. Napoli's 2023 and 2025 titles were the club's first since the Maradona era.
