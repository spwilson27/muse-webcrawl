Coppermine River

Length: ~840 km
Mouth: Coronation Gulf, Arctic Ocean
Location: Northwest Territories and Nunavut, Canada

The Coppermine River is a river in the North Slave and Kitikmeot regions of the Northwest Territories and Nunavut, Canada. It flows roughly 840 kilometres north from Lac de Gras to Coronation Gulf on the Arctic Ocean, emptying near the community of Kugluktuk (formerly Coppermine).

History

The river is named for the copper deposits worked by Indigenous peoples of the region, the Copper Inuit. Samuel Hearne reached the river overland in 1771 in search of the copper mines, guided by the Chipewyan leader Matonabbee; near the river he witnessed the Bloody Falls massacre of Inuit by his Dene companions. John Franklin's disastrous Coppermine expedition of 1819–1822 descended the river and surveyed the Arctic coast eastward.

Geography

The river passes through tundra and taiga transition zones and includes rapids and the Bloody Falls, now within Kugluk/Bloody Falls Territorial Park. It is a Canadian Heritage River.
