Metanalysis

Metanalysis
FieldHistorical linguistics
Also calledRebracketing, false splitting
Term coined byOtto Jespersen (1894)

Metanalysis, also known as rebracketing or false splitting, is a change in the division between sounds or morphemes of a word, reinterpreting where one element ends and another begins. The term was introduced by the Danish linguist Otto Jespersen in 1894. It commonly occurs at the boundary between a word and a preceding article or possessive, where sounds are reassigned to the neighbouring word.

Examples

English preserves many metanalyses: an apron was formerly a napron; an adder was a nadder; a nickname was an ekename; and an umpire was a noumpere. In the opposite direction, a newt derives from an ewt, and pea was back-formed from the mass noun pease. French shows the same process: the l' of lierre ("ivy", from ierre) and of the name Lille's etymology involve article boundaries being reanalysed.

Relation to other changes

Metanalysis is closely related to back-formation and folk etymology, and is a natural consequence of how listeners segment continuous speech, especially in language acquisition, where children may assign morpheme boundaries differently from adults.
