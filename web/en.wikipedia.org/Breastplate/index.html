Breastplate

A breastplate or breastwork is a device worn over the torso to protect it from injury — historically the front component of a suit of plate armour, covering the chest and usually paired with a matching backplate to form a cuirass. Breastplates have been made of leather, bronze, iron and steel, and in modern times of ballistic materials such as Kevlar and ceramic composites; the term also covers religious garments such as the biblical priestly breastplate and protective equipment in sports and medicine.

Ancient and classical breastplates

The ancient Greek thorax included the "muscle cuirass", a bronze breastplate and backplate sculpted with idealised musculature, worn by officers and depicted across classical art; linen composite armour (the linothorax) offered a lighter alternative. Roman soldiers adopted mail and later the segmented lorica segmentata, while officers retained muscled bronze cuirasses visible on imperial statues. In the ancient Near East, the Hebrew Bible describes the hoshen, the jewel-set breastplate worn by the High Priest of Israel, mounted with twelve gemstones representing the twelve tribes.

Medieval and early modern development

As full plate armour developed in the 14th and 15th centuries, the breastplate became its most substantial piece: a shaped steel shell, often globose to deflect blows, fluted in German Gothic style or smooth in the Milanese fashion, and later given a central ridge or "tapul" point. Proof marks — dents from test shots — testify to the 16th-century demand for plates "of proof" against firearms. Breastplates remained in service with heavy cavalry (cuirassiers) into the 19th century; French, German and Austrian cuirassiers wore steel breastplates in 1914, and polished cuirasses still appear in ceremonial units such as the British Household Cavalry and the French Republican Guard.

Modern usage

Twentieth-century flak jackets and today's plate carriers revive the concept with hard ballistic plates of ceramic, steel or polyethylene worn over the chest and back. Medicine and sport use specialised breast protectors — catchers' chest protectors in baseball, commotio cordis guards in lacrosse and hockey, and fencing plastrons — while "breastplate" also names the plastron of turtles and parts of arthropod anatomy by analogy with armour.
