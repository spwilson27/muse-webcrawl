Nadi (yoga)

The three principal nadis and the chakras in the yogic subtle body

Nadi (Sanskrit: नाड़ी, nāḍī, "tube, pipe, nerve, blood vessel, pulse") is a term for the channels through which, in traditional Indian medicine and spiritual knowledge, the energies such as prana of the physical body, the subtle body and the causal body flow. Within this philosophical framework, the nadis are said to connect at special points of intensity, the chakras.

The three principal nadis

Of the many nadis — texts give figures of 72,000, 200,000 or 350,000 — the three most important are:

  • Sushumna — the central channel, running from the base of the spine (muladhara chakra) to the crown of the head (sahasrara), through which kundalini energy is said to rise
  • Ida — associated with the left nostril, the moon, and a cooling, feminine quality; it terminates in the left side and spirals around the sushumna
  • Pingala — associated with the right nostril, the sun, and a heating, masculine quality, mirroring the ida on the right

Function and practice

In hatha yoga, practices such as asana, shatkarma (cleansing techniques) and especially pranayama — notably nadi shodhana, alternate-nostril breathing — are intended to purify and balance the nadis so that prana flows freely and the sushumna becomes active. When ida and pingala are balanced, the breath is said to flow in the sushumna, awakening kundalini.

In traditional medicine

In Ayurveda, nadi also refers to the vessels and to pulse diagnosis (nadi pariksha), in which the physician reads the pulse at the wrist to assess the balance of the doshas. The concept parallels channels in other Asian systems, such as the meridians of traditional Chinese medicine, though their historical relationship is debated.
