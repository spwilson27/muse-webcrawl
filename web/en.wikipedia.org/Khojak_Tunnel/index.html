The Khojak Tunnel, also known as the Sheila Bagh Tunnel, is a 3.885-kilometre-long (2.414 mi) railway tunnel in the Khojak Pass in Balochistan province, Pakistan. It is situated on the Rohri–Chaman Railway Line and passes through the Toba Kakar mountain range, linking the town of Shelabagh with Chaman near the Afghan border.

History

The tunnel was constructed by the British as part of the strategic railway extension toward the Afghan frontier during the late 19th century, when fears of Russian expansion into Central Asia drove the Great Game. Construction began in 1888 and the tunnel opened in 1891. At the time of its completion it was one of the longest tunnels in the world and an engineering landmark of British India.

The boring was carried out from both ends of the mountain simultaneously, and the two headings met with remarkable precision. The tunnel allowed the railway from Sibi and Quetta to reach Chaman, giving British forces a direct rail connection to the border opposite Kandahar.

Features

The tunnel is a single-bore, double-track railway tunnel. A distinctive feature of its history is that the alignment through the pass was planned so that the tunnel's interior receives direct sunlight at certain times, and local legend holds that an escaping prisoner could be seen silhouetted along its straight bore. The Khojak Tunnel appears on older Pakistani 5-rupee banknotes, issued by the State Bank of Pakistan.
