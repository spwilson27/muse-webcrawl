Bantry House

Bantry House is a historic stately home overlooking Bantry Bay in County Cork, Ireland. Built in the early 18th century (c. 1710) and enlarged in the Victorian era, it was the seat of the White family, Earls of Bantry, and remains a family home open to visitors.

History

Originally called Blackrock, the house was renamed Seafield and later Bantry House. Richard White, who helped organise resistance to the French fleet in 1796, was created Earl of Bantry. The second Earl collected art and furniture on grand tours of Europe.

Gardens

The formal gardens feature Italianate terraces, a parterre, and the "Hundred Steps" climbing the hillside behind the house, offering celebrated views over the bay. A stable yard houses an exhibition on the 1796 French Armada.

Today

The house hosts the West Cork Chamber Music Festival and operates as a guesthouse and wedding venue, still owned by descendants of the White family.
