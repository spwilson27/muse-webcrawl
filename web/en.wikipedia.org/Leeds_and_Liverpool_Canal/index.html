Leeds and Liverpool Canal

The Leeds and Liverpool Canal is a canal in Northern England, linking the cities of Leeds and Liverpool over a distance of 127 miles (204 km), crossing the Pennines. It is the longest canal in England built by a single company, and the longest canal in Northern England. The main line has 91 locks; its most famous features include the Bingley Five Rise Locks, the Burnley Embankment, and the Foulridge Tunnel.

Length: 127 mi (204 km)
Locks: 91
Status: Open
Construction began: 1770
Date completed: 1816

History

An Act of Parliament authorising the canal was obtained in 1770, with James Brindley consulted on the route and John Longbotham appointed chief engineer. The first sections, from Bingley to Skipton and around Liverpool, opened in the 1770s, but shortage of capital meant the through route across the Pennines was not completed until 1816. The canal carried coal, limestone, textiles and general cargo throughout the nineteenth century; commercial traffic declined in the twentieth century, and the canal is now used mainly for leisure cruising. A link to Liverpool's southern docks, the Liverpool Canal Link, opened in 2009.
