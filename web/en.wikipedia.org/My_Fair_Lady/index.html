My Fair Lady

My Fair Lady is a musical with book and lyrics by Alan Jay Lerner and music by Frederick Loewe, adapted from George Bernard Shaw's 1913 play Pygmalion and the 1938 film of the same name. It is one of the most celebrated and enduring works of American musical theatre.

Plot

In Edwardian London, phonetics professor Henry Higgins wagers with Colonel Pickering that he can pass off Cockney flower-seller Eliza Doolittle as a duchess by teaching her to speak "properly". Over months of gruelling lessons, Eliza triumphs at the Embassy Ball, but her success forces a reckoning: she has outgrown being treated as an experiment, and demands to be treated as a person. Famous songs include "Wouldn't It Be Loverly", "With a Little Bit of Luck", "I Could Have Danced All Night", "On the Street Where You Live", "Get Me to the Church on Time", and "The Rain in Spain".

Productions

The original Broadway production opened on 15 March 1956 starring Rex Harrison and Julie Andrews, ran for 2,717 performances — then a record — and won the Tony Award for Best Musical. The London production, also with Harrison and Andrews, was equally successful. The 1964 film, directed by George Cukor and starring Harrison and Audrey Hepburn (whose singing was largely dubbed by Marni Nixon), won eight Academy Awards including Best Picture.

Legacy

My Fair Lady has been repeatedly revived on Broadway and in the West End and translated worldwide; its score is regarded as among the finest in the musical repertoire.
