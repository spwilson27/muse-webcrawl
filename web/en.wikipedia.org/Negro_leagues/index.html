Negro leagues

Negro leagues
Sport: Baseball
Founded: 1920 (first formal league, the Negro National League)
Folded: c. 1960
Country: United States (also Cuba connections)

The Negro leagues were United States professional baseball leagues comprising primarily African-American players, formed during the era when Black players were excluded from Major League Baseball by a color line enforced from the late 19th century until 1947. At their height the leagues fielded celebrated teams and stars and produced some of the finest players in baseball history.

History

Black professional teams existed from the 1880s, but organized league play began in 1920 when Andrew "Rube" Foster founded the Negro National League. The Eastern Colored League followed in 1923, and champions met in the Negro World Series (1924–27). The Depression collapsed the early leagues; a second Negro National League (1933) and the Negro American League (1937) carried Black baseball through its golden age, highlighted by the East–West All-Star Game. Legendary players included Satchel Paige, Josh Gibson, Oscar Charleston, Buck Leonard, and Cool Papa Bell, and teams such as the Homestead Grays, Kansas City Monarchs, and Pittsburgh Crawfords.

Integration and legacy

Jackie Robinson's debut with the Brooklyn Dodgers in 1947 broke the color line but also drew fans and talent away from the Negro leagues, which declined through the 1950s. In 2020 Major League Baseball officially recognized the seven major Negro leagues (1920–1948) as major leagues, and in 2024 incorporated Negro leagues statistics into its official records, reshaping all-time leaderboards. The Negro Leagues Baseball Museum in Kansas City preserves this history.
