César Milstein

César Milstein (8 October 1927 – 24 March 2002) was an Argentine-British biochemist in the field of antibody research. Milstein shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1984 with Niels Kaj Jerne and Georges J. F. Köhler for developing the hybridoma technique for the production of monoclonal antibodies.

Early life and education

Milstein was born in Bahía Blanca, Argentina, to a Jewish Ukrainian immigrant family. He studied at the University of Buenos Aires, graduating in chemistry and completing a doctorate in 1957 on enzyme kinetics under Andrés Stoppani. A fellowship took him to Cambridge, where he earned a second PhD in 1960 in the laboratory of Fred Sanger. After a brief return to Argentina — cut short by the political turmoil of the early 1960s — he settled permanently in Cambridge in 1963 at the invitation of Sanger, joining the Medical Research Council's Laboratory of Molecular Biology (LMB).

Career and research

At the LMB, Milstein initially worked on enzymes, then turned to the structure and genetics of antibodies, collaborating with Shirley Tilghman and others on antibody diversity. In 1975, with postdoctoral researcher Georges Köhler, he published the landmark paper describing how fusing antibody-producing B lymphocytes with immortal myeloma cells produced hybridomas — immortal cell lines each secreting a single, defined antibody. The technique made monoclonal antibodies available in unlimited quantities and transformed diagnostics and therapeutics.

The MRC controversially did not patent the discovery, a decision later cited as a costly missed opportunity for British biotechnology, though Milstein himself was content that the method spread freely. Milstein became head of the LMB's Protein and Nucleic Acid Chemistry division in 1983 and its deputy director. His later work studied somatic hypermutation and antibody affinity maturation.

Honours

Milstein was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1975, received the Royal Medal in 1982 and the Copley Medal in 1989, and was made a Companion of Honour in 1995. He died of a heart condition in Cambridge on 24 March 2002.
