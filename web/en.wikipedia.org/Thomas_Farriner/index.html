Thomas Farriner

Thomas Farriner (c. 1615 – 20 December 1670) was an English baker in 17th-century London. His bakery in Pudding Lane was the starting point of the Great Fire of London on 2 September 1666.

The fire

Farriner, baker to King Charles II, maintained that he had extinguished his ovens before bed, but sparks or embers ignited nearby fuel shortly after midnight. His maidservant perished — the fire's first victim — while Farriner and his family escaped through an upstairs window. He later signed the bill blaming the fire on a French Catholic conspiracy, a claim recanted by Robert Hubert before his execution and generally regarded as a miscarriage of justice fuelled by anti-Catholic panic.

Later life

Farriner survived the fire and continued as a baker and member of the Bakers' Company, dying in 1670. Pudding Lane is marked today near the Monument.
