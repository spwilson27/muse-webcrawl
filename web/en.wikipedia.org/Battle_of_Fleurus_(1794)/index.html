Battle of Fleurus (1794)

The Battle of Fleurus, fought on 26 June 1794, was the most significant battle of the Flanders Campaign in the Low Countries during the French Revolutionary Wars. The French army under Jean-Baptiste Jourdan, assisted by Louis Antoine de Saint-Just, defeated the Coalition army commanded by Prince Josias of Coburg near Fleurus in present-day Belgium, between the rivers Sambre and Meuse.

Background

In the spring of 1794 the French Republic, mobilised by the levée en masse, advanced into the Austrian Netherlands. Jourdan's Army of the Moselle joined the forces besieging Charleroi, crossing the Sambre repeatedly despite Coalition counterattacks. Charleroi surrendered on 25 June, just as Coburg marched to its relief.

The battle

Coburg attacked Jourdan's entrenched positions in five columns around a wide arc. Fighting lasted the whole day; the French left under Jean-Baptiste Kléber and the centre held, while on the right François Joseph Lefebvre repelled repeated assaults. The French famously used the reconnaissance balloon l'Entreprenant — the first recorded use of an aircraft in warfare — to observe Coalition movements. With his attacks stalled and Charleroi already lost, Coburg withdrew in good order during the night.

Aftermath

Fleurus broke Coalition resistance in Belgium. The French occupied Brussels and Antwerp, annexed the Austrian Netherlands, and advanced to the Rhine; Austria's defeat opened the way for the French conquest of the Dutch Republic in the winter of 1794–95. The battle is remembered as a turning point of the Revolutionary Wars and a landmark in military aviation history.
