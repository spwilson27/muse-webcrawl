De Hoge Veluwe National Park

De Hoge Veluwe National Park (Dutch: Nationaal Park De Hoge Veluwe) is a national park in the province of Gelderland in the Netherlands, covering about 55 square kilometres of heathland, woodland and drifting sand dunes. Unlike most Dutch national parks, it is privately owned by a foundation and charges visitors an entrance fee. The park is also home to the Kröller-Müller Museum, which holds one of the world's largest collections of paintings by Vincent van Gogh.

History

The estate was assembled between 1909 and 1923 by the wealthy couple Anton and Helene Kröller-Müller, who used it as a hunting reserve and built the St. Hubertus hunting lodge, designed by architect Hendrik Petrus Berlage. When the family's finances failed during the Great Depression, the estate was transferred in 1935 to a foundation, and the Dutch state took over the art collection, opening the park to the public.

Landscape and wildlife

The park sits on the Veluwe, a push moraine landscape formed during the Ice Ages, and includes the Deelen drifting sands, one of the last actively shifting sand landscapes in the Netherlands. Management maintains a mosaic of heath, grassland and pine and oak forest. Large mammals include red deer, wild boar, roe deer and mouflon, introduced for hunting in the early twentieth century. The park is famous for its free white bicycles, available at its entrances and visitor centres for touring its cycle paths.
