Pentaprism

A pentaprism is a five-sided reflecting prism used to deviate a beam of light by a constant 90 degrees, even if the entry beam is not at right angles to the prism. The beam reflects twice inside the prism, so the image is neither inverted nor reversed left-to-right, unlike a simple mirror.

Properties

Two of the prism's faces are silvered to act as mirrors; two others are ground and polished entry and exit faces, and the fifth face is unused optically. Because the deviation depends only on the geometry, it remains exactly 90 degrees regardless of small changes in alignment, which makes pentaprisms valuable in surveying and optical metrology for defining right angles.

In cameras

A modified form, the roof pentaprism, is the standard viewfinder prism in single-lens reflex cameras: it carries the image from the focusing screen to the photographer's eye, adding a roof (two mutually perpendicular faces) to one reflecting surface so the image appears the correct way round. Cheaper cameras substitute a lighter pentamirror of hollow mirrors.
