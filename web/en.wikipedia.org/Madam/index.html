Madam

Madam, abbreviated as Ma'am, is a polite form of address for women, originally a contraction of the French madame ("my lady"). It is the female equivalent of sir and is used in formal speech and correspondence, for example in the salutation "Dear Madam" and in expressions such as "Madam Chairperson" or "Madam President". In the British Parliament and other legislatures, "Madam Speaker" and "Madam Deputy Speaker" are standard forms.

Usage

In everyday service encounters in many English-speaking regions, ma'am is used as a respectful way to address a woman whose name is unknown. In some parts of the southern United States it is a routine courtesy for women of any age, while elsewhere it may be perceived as implying age. In the armed forces and police services, female superior officers are commonly addressed as "ma'am".

Other meanings

The word also has a distinct sense: a madam is a woman who runs a brothel. This usage, dating from the early modern period, derives from the brothel-keeper's role as the mistress of the house, addressed politely by clients and staff.
