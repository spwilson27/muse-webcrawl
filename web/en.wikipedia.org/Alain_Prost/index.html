Alain Prost

Alain Marie Pascal Prost (born 24 February 1955) is a French retired racing driver and Formula One team owner. Nicknamed "The Professor" for his intellectual, calculating approach, he won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship four times (1985, 1986, 1989 and 1993) and held the record for most Grand Prix victories (51) until surpassed by Michael Schumacher in 2001. His fierce rivalry with Ayrton Senna is regarded as one of the greatest in sport.

Early career

Prost, born near Saint-Étienne, discovered karting at age 14 and won multiple French and European karting titles before progressing through Formula Renault and French and European Formula Three, winning both championships in 1979.

Formula One career

Prost debuted with McLaren in 1980 and moved to Renault in 1981, winning his first Grand Prix in France that year. Returning to McLaren in 1984, he won titles in 1985 and 1986 alongside Niki Lauda. His partnership with Ayrton Senna at McLaren in 1988–89 produced intense conflict, including title-deciding collisions at Suzuka in 1989 and 1990 after Prost moved to Ferrari. Prost won his fourth title with Williams in 1993 and retired at season's end.

Later life

Prost ran his own team, Prost Grand Prix, from 1997 to 2001, and later co-founded the Renault e.dams Formula E team, winning three teams' titles. He also served as an adviser to the Renault/Alpine Formula One team until 2022.
