SRC is a human gene encoding the non-receptor tyrosine kinase c-Src. It is the cellular homologue of v-src, the transforming gene of the Rous sarcoma virus — the first oncogene ever identified, work recognised by the 1989 Nobel Prize to J. Michael Bishop and Harold Varmus. c-Src is the prototype of the Src family of kinases, which also includes Fyn, Yes and Lyn.

Structure and regulation

c-Src (536 amino acids in humans) contains SH3 and SH2 protein-interaction domains, a catalytic kinase (SH1) domain, and a C-terminal regulatory tyrosine (Tyr530 in humans). When that tyrosine is phosphorylated by Csk, intramolecular binding folds the enzyme into an inactive conformation; dephosphorylation and binding of SH2/SH3 ligands to partners opens and activates it.

Function

Src kinases relay signals from growth-factor receptors, integrins and other receptors to control cell proliferation, survival, adhesion, migration and cytoskeletal organisation.

Role in cancer

Unlike its viral counterpart, cellular Src is not usually mutated in human tumours, but its activity and expression are elevated in many cancers (including colon, breast and pancreatic cancers), promoting invasion and metastasis. Src signalling is a drug target: the dual Src/Abl inhibitor dasatinib is used in chronic myeloid leukaemia, and other Src inhibitors have been tested in solid tumours.
