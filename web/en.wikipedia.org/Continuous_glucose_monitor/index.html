Continuous glucose monitor

A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) is a medical device for monitoring blood glucose concentrations on a near-continuous basis, used by people with diabetes mellitus. A small sensor inserted under the skin measures glucose in the interstitial fluid every one to five minutes; a transmitter sends the readings to a receiver, smartphone app or insulin pump. Most sensors are worn for 10 to 15 days before replacement.

Function

Unlike fingerstick meters, which give single point-in-time readings, CGMs show glucose trends and rates of change, and can alert users to actual or predicted hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia. Because interstitial glucose lags behind blood glucose by several minutes, readings can differ slightly from fingerstick values, particularly when glucose is changing rapidly. Modern systems from manufacturers such as Dexcom, Abbott (FreeStyle Libre) and Medtronic are factory-calibrated and approved for insulin dosing decisions without confirmatory fingersticks.

Use in diabetes care

Clinical trials have shown that CGM use lowers HbA1c and reduces time in hypoglycemia in people with type 1 diabetes and in insulin-treated type 2 diabetes. Integrated with insulin pumps, CGMs enable hybrid closed-loop ("artificial pancreas") systems that automatically adjust basal insulin. Reported summary measures include time in range (typically 3.9–10.0 mmol/L, or 70–180 mg/dL).
