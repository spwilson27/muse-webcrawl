Study skills

Study skills, academic skill, or study strategies are approaches applied to learning. Study skills are an array of skills which tackle the process of organizing and taking in new information, retaining information, or dealing with assessments. They include mnemonics, which aid the retention of lists of information; effective reading; concentration techniques; and efficient note taking.

While often left up to the student and their support network, study skills are increasingly taught in secondary education and at the university level. A number of books and websites are available, from works on specific techniques such as Tony Buzan's books on mind-mapping to general guides to successfully studying, such as those by Stella Cottrell and Understanding Comics by Scott McCloud.

Types

More broadly, any skill which boosts a person's ability to study, retain and recall information which assists in and passing exams can be termed a study skill, and this could include time management and motivational techniques. Some examples include:

  • Summarization: summarizing information so it can be easily understood and remembered.
  • Retrieval practice: practicing actively retrieving information, such as through quizzing or flashcards.
  • Spaced repetition: spacing out learning over time to improve long-term retention.
  • Interleaving practice: mixing different problems or topics within a study session.
  • Elaborative interrogation: asking oneself "why" questions about facts and concepts.
  • Self-explanation: explaining steps and principles to oneself while working.

Evidence

Research in cognitive psychology has found that some commonly used strategies, such as rereading and highlighting, have relatively low utility, whereas practice testing and distributed practice (spaced repetition) have been rated as high-utility techniques. A widely cited 2013 review by Dunlosky and colleagues assessed ten learning techniques and recommended practice testing and distributed practice as the most effective.
