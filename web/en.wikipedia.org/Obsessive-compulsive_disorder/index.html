Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) is a mental disorder in which a person experiences intrusive, unwanted thoughts, images or urges (obsessions) that cause marked anxiety or distress, and engages in repetitive behaviours or mental acts (compulsions) in an attempt to suppress the thoughts or prevent a feared outcome. Typical obsessions concern contamination, harm, symmetry or forbidden thoughts; typical compulsions include excessive washing, checking, counting and ordering. The behaviours are recognised by most sufferers as excessive but are difficult to resist, and can consume hours each day.

Symptoms

Obsessions are persistent and recurrent impulses or images experienced as intrusive and inappropriate — for example, fears of contamination by germs, doubts about having locked a door or turned off a stove, aggressive or sexual intrusive thoughts, and a need for symmetry or exactness. Compulsions are repetitive behaviours (hand washing, ordering, checking) or mental acts (praying, counting, repeating words silently) performed in response to an obsession or according to rigid rules, aimed at reducing distress rather than producing pleasure.

Causes

The causes of OCD are not fully understood. Twin and family studies indicate a substantial genetic component, with heritability estimates around 40–50%. Neuroimaging research implicates dysfunction in cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuits, particularly involving the orbitofrontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex and caudate nucleus, and abnormalities in serotonin, dopamine and glutamate signalling. Onset is occasionally abrupt in children following streptococcal infection (the proposed PANDAS syndrome), a hypothesis that remains debated.

Diagnosis

Under the DSM-5, OCD is classified in its own category of obsessive-compulsive and related disorders (which also includes body dysmorphic disorder, hoarding disorder and trichotillomania), having been moved out of the anxiety disorders in 2013. Diagnosis requires obsessions and/or compulsions that are time-consuming (for example, more than one hour per day) or cause clinically significant distress or impairment. The Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) is widely used to rate severity.

Treatment

First-line treatment is a form of cognitive behavioural therapy called exposure and response prevention (ERP), in which patients are gradually exposed to feared stimuli while refraining from compulsive rituals, combined where needed with selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) such as sertraline or fluvoxamine, often at higher doses than for depression. The tricyclic clomipramine is also effective. For severe, treatment-resistant cases, augmentation with antipsychotics, deep brain stimulation or ablative neurosurgery may be considered.

Epidemiology

OCD affects roughly 1–2% of the population in any given year and about 2.3% over a lifetime, with similar rates across cultures. Onset is typically in late childhood, adolescence or early adulthood; males tend to have earlier onset. The disorder frequently co-occurs with tic disorders, anxiety disorders, depression and eating disorders.
