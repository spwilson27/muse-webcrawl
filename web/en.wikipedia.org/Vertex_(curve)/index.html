Vertex (curve)

In the geometry of plane curves, a vertex is a point of local extremum of the curvature: a point where the curvature attains a local maximum or a local minimum. Intuitively, vertices are points where the curve is most tightly bent (relative to its neighbourhood) or most nearly straight. The concept is fundamental to the four-vertex theorem.

Examples

On an ellipse, the curvature is maximal at the two ends of the major axis and minimal at the two ends of the minor axis, giving exactly four vertices. A circle has constant curvature, so every point is a vertex. On a parabola there is a single vertex, at the point where the curve meets its axis of symmetry — this is also the "vertex" of the parabola familiar from algebra. For a hyperbola, each branch has one vertex.

The four-vertex theorem

The four-vertex theorem states that every simple closed convex plane curve has at least four vertices — at least two maxima and two minima of curvature. It was first proved for convex curves by Syamadas Mukhopadhyaya in 1909; later proofs extended the result to broader classes of curves. The theorem has a converse: any continuous real function on a circle with at least two local maxima and two local minima is the curvature function of some closed plane curve.

Relation to other notions

Vertices of a curve are related to cusps of its evolute: the evolute (the locus of centres of curvature) has a cusp at each point corresponding to a vertex of the original curve. In the study of curves in computer-aided design, vertices and other curvature extrema are used to segment and describe shapes.
