Dolores Ibárruri

Born9 December 1895, Gallarta, Biscay, Spain
Died12 November 1989, Madrid, Spain
Known forRepublican orator in the Spanish Civil War; General Secretary of the PCE
NicknameLa Pasionaria

Dolores Ibárruri Gómez (1895–1989), known as La Pasionaria ("the Passionflower"), was a Spanish Republican politician and communist leader, famed as the most powerful orator of the Spanish Civil War. Her slogans "¡No pasarán!" ("They shall not pass!") and "It is better to die on your feet than live on your knees" became emblems of anti-fascist resistance worldwide.

Early life

Born into a Basque mining family in Gallarta, Ibárruri married a miner and socialist activist and adopted her pen name for an article published at Easter 1918. She joined the newly founded Communist Party of Spain (PCE), rose through its ranks despite repeated imprisonment, and by the 1930s sat on its Central Committee, editing the party newspaper Mundo Obrero. She was elected to the Cortes for Asturias in 1936.

Civil War and exile

During the Civil War (1936–39) her radio broadcasts and front-line speeches rallied Republican morale, and she helped organise the defence of Madrid. After the Republic's defeat she went into exile in the Soviet Union, becoming General Secretary of the PCE in 1942. Her son Rubén was killed at Stalingrad. She remained the party's symbolic leader through the Franco dictatorship.

Return and later life

Following Franco's death, Ibárruri returned to Spain in 1977 and was again elected deputy for Asturias at the age of 81, serving in the constituent Cortes that drafted the democratic constitution. She remained honorary president of the PCE until her death in Madrid in 1989.
