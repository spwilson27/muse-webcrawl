Sklar's theorem

Sklar's theorem, proved by Abe Sklar in 1959, is the central result of copula theory in probability and statistics. It states that any multivariate joint distribution can be expressed in terms of its univariate marginal distributions and a copula that captures the dependence structure between them.

Sklar's theorem

Proved by: Abe Sklar
Year: 1959
Field: Probability theory, statistics

Statement

If H is a joint cumulative distribution function of random variables X1, …, Xd with marginal distribution functions F1, …, Fd, then there exists a copula C such that H(x1, …, xd) = C(F1(x1), …, Fd(xd)). If the marginals are continuous, the copula is unique.

Significance

The theorem separates marginal behaviour from dependence, enabling statisticians to model complicated dependencies independently of the margins. Copula methods are widely used in finance, hydrology and insurance, though their misuse in modelling correlated credit risk was criticised after the 2008 financial crisis.
