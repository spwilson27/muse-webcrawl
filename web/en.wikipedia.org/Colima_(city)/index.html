Colima (city)

Colima is a city in west-central Mexico and the capital and largest city of the state of Colima. The city had a population of roughly 150,000 at the 2020 census, with its conurbation, including Villa de Álvarez, exceeding 300,000. It sits in the Colima Valley, southeast of the Colima volcano.

History

The settlement was founded by Spanish conquistadors in 1523, making it one of the oldest Spanish-founded cities in Mexico; the original site was later moved to its present location in 1527. The name is thought to derive from a Nahuatl phrase meaning "place of the old gods" or referring to the volcano. The city was briefly a territorial capital before becoming capital of the state of Colima when the state was created in 1857.

Geography and culture

Colima has a tropical savanna climate with a marked rainy season in summer. Landmarks include Colima Cathedral (Basílica Menor), the Government Palace with murals depicting the history of the region, and the Regional Museum of History. The city is known for its traditional tuba drink made from coconut palm sap, and for its pre-Hispanic shaft tomb traditions, famous for pottery "Colima dogs". The twin volcanoes Nevado de Colima and the active Volcán de Colima dominate the landscape to the north.
