The Santa Monica Pier is a large pier at the foot of Colorado Avenue in Santa Monica, California, extending into the Pacific Ocean. It opened on 9 September 1909 and is in fact two adjoining piers: the Municipal Pier and the shorter Pleasure Pier built by amusement entrepreneur Charles I. D. Looff in 1916. The pier is a designated Santa Monica Historic Landmark and one of the most visited attractions in Southern California.

Attractions

The pier hosts Pacific Park, a family amusement park opened in 1996, whose solar-powered Pacific Wheel Ferris wheel is a prominent landmark. The Santa Monica Looff Hippodrome, a National Historic Landmark built in 1916, houses a carousel whose horses appeared in the film The Sting. Other attractions include an aquarium, an arcade, a trapeze school, restaurants, and fishing facilities. The pier marks the western terminus of U.S. Route 66, commemorated by an "End of the Trail" sign, although the highway's official endpoint was nearby in Santa Monica.

History

The Municipal Pier was built to carry sewer water out to sea, a function abandoned early in its life. Storms in 1983 destroyed a significant portion of the pier's western end, which was subsequently rebuilt. The pier has appeared in countless films and television programmes, including Forrest Gump — the Bubba Gump Shrimp Company restaurant on the pier is named for the film.
