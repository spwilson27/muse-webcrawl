Brave New World

Brave New World is a dystopian novel by English author Aldous Huxley, published in 1932. Largely set in a futuristic World State whose citizens are engineered through an artificial reproductive system into an intelligence-based social hierarchy, the novel anticipates huge scientific advancements in reproduction, sleep-learning, psychological manipulation, and classical conditioning that combine to make a dystopian society.

Plot

In the World State of AD 2540, children are decanted in hatcheries and conditioned into castes from Alpha to Epsilon. Stability is maintained through the pleasure drug soma, consumerism, and entertainment. Bernard Marx, an Alpha who feels alienated, visits a Savage Reservation in New Mexico and brings back John, a man raised on Shakespeare and the reservation's traditional ways. John's clash with the World State's controller, Mustapha Mond, forms the novel's philosophical climax.

Themes

The novel explores the loss of individuality, the dangers of hedonism and state control through pleasure, and the tension between happiness and truth — themes Huxley revisited in his 1958 essay collection Brave New World Revisited.

Reception

The Modern Library ranked it fifth on its list of the 100 best English-language novels of the 20th century. It is frequently compared with George Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four.
