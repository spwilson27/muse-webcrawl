Middle Eastern theatre of World War I

The Middle Eastern theatre of World War I saw fighting between the Ottoman Empire (supported by Germany) and the Allies — principally the British Empire, the Russian Empire (until 1917), France, and Arab irregular forces — from 1914 to 1918. It encompassed the Caucasus, Mesopotamia, the Sinai and Palestine, Gallipoli, Persia and the Arabian Peninsula.

Background

The Ottoman Empire entered the war on the side of the Central Powers in late October 1914, attacking Russia in the Caucasus. Britain moved quickly to protect its oil interests and the route to India, landing forces in Mesopotamia, while the Ottomans attacked the Suez Canal in early 1915.

Major campaigns

Gallipoli (1915–16): an Allied attempt to force the Dardanelles and capture Constantinople ended in defeat and evacuation. Mesopotamia: British-Indian forces took Basra, suffered disaster at the Siege of Kut (1916), then captured Baghdad in 1917. Caucasus: Ottoman and Russian armies fought amid the Armenian genocide; after Russia's collapse, Ottoman forces advanced in 1918. Sinai and Palestine: after defending the Suez Canal, General Allenby's Egyptian Expeditionary Force captured Jerusalem (1917) and destroyed the Ottoman armies at Megiddo (1918). Arab Revolt (1916–18): Sharif Hussein's forces, advised by T. E. Lawrence, harassed the Hejaz railway and took Aqaba and Damascus.

Outcome

The Armistice of Mudros (30 October 1918) ended Ottoman participation. The postwar settlement — the Sykes–Picot Agreement, League of Nations mandates and the Treaty of Sèvres (later revised at Lausanne) — dismantled the Ottoman Empire and created the modern state system of the Middle East.
