Gulf of Ob

The Gulf of Ob is one of the world's largest estuaries.

The Gulf of Ob (Russian: Obskaya guba) is a bay of the Kara Sea in the Arctic Ocean, in northern Russia at the mouth of the Ob River. It is one of the world's largest estuaries, roughly 1,000 km long and up to 80 km wide, running in a generally north–south direction between the Yamal Peninsula to the west and the Gyda Peninsula and Taz Estuary to the east.

Geography and environment

The gulf is very shallow, generally 10–12 metres deep, and is frozen for most of the year. The Ob discharges an enormous volume of fresh water and sediment, making the gulf's waters brackish and biologically productive; it is an important habitat for fish such as nelma and muksun, and for migratory birds. The surrounding tundra coasts lie within the Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug and Krasnoyarsk Krai.

Energy development

The region around the gulf holds vast natural gas reserves. The port of Sabetta on the Yamal shore serves the Yamal LNG project, with ice-class LNG carriers transiting the gulf to the Northern Sea Route.
