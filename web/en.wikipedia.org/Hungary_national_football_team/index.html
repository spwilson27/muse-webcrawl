The Hungary national football team represents Hungary in men's international football and is controlled by the Hungarian Football Federation. The team plays its home matches at the Puskás Aréna in Budapest, named after the legendary forward Ferenc Puskás.

History

Hungary played its first official international match in 1902 against Austria. The team was a dominant force in world football in the first half of the 20th century, reaching the World Cup final in 1938, where it lost to Italy.

The 1950s side, known as the Aranycsapat (Golden Team) or "Magical Magyars", is regarded as one of the greatest teams in football history. Coached by Gusztáv Sebes and starring Ferenc Puskás, Sándor Kocsis, Zoltán Czibor, József Bozsik, and Nándor Hidegkuti, Hungary won the gold medal at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics and famously defeated England 6–3 at Wembley in 1953 and 7–1 in Budapest in 1954. The team entered the 1954 World Cup as overwhelming favourites but lost the final 3–2 to West Germany in the "Miracle of Bern", despite having beaten the same opponents 8–3 in the group stage.

Hungary also won Olympic football gold medals in 1964 and 1968, and achieved strong results at the European Championship, finishing third in 1964 and fourth in 1972. After 1972 the team's fortunes declined, and it failed to qualify for a major tournament between the 1986 World Cup and Euro 2016, a 30-year absence ended by qualification for the European Championship in France, where Hungary won its group and reached the round of 16. Hungary also qualified for Euro 2020 and Euro 2024.

Records

The most-capped Hungarian player is Balázs Dzsudzsák, and the top goalscorer is Ferenc Puskás, who scored 84 goals in 85 appearances for Hungary. Puskás's name is carried by FIFA's Puskás Award for the most aesthetically significant goal of the year.

Stadium

The Puskás Aréna, opened in 2019 on the site of the former Ferenc Puskás Stadium, seats around 67,000 spectators and hosted matches at Euro 2020.
