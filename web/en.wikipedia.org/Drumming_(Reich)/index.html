Drumming (Reich)

Drumming is a minimalist composition by the American composer Steve Reich, written in 1970–71 for a percussion ensemble with voices and piccolo. Lasting roughly an hour, it is one of Reich's best-known works and a landmark of musical minimalism.

Composer: Steve Reich
Composed: 1970–71
Premiere: December 1971, New York
Duration: c. 55–85 minutes

Background

Reich wrote the piece after a short trip to Ghana in 1970, where he studied Ewe drumming with a master drummer in Accra. Although he cautioned that the work is not an imitation of African music, its layered rhythmic cycles reflect that encounter. Drumming develops the phasing technique of Reich's earlier works into a larger architecture built on a single recurring rhythmic pattern.

Structure

The work is in four connected parts defined by instrumentation: tuned bongos; marimbas with female voices; glockenspiels with whistling and piccolo; and finally all the instruments combined. Within each part, patterns are built up one note at a time, players phase against one another, and rhythmic relationships generate resultant melodies that are sometimes doubled by the voices or piccolo.
