Nisa, Turkmenistan

Nisa (also called Parthaunisa) was an ancient settlement of the Parthian Empire, located near the modern village of Bagyr, about 18 km west of Ashgabat, Turkmenistan, at the foot of the Kopet Dag mountains. Founded in the 3rd century BCE and known to its founders as Mithradatkert ("fortress of Mithradates"), it served as a royal necropolis and ceremonial stronghold of the early Arsacid kings. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2007.

History and excavations

Nisa flourished as one of the first capitals of the Parthians before their court shifted westward. It was severely damaged by an earthquake in the 1st century BCE and eventually abandoned. Excavations led by Soviet archaeologists, notably Mikhail Masson from the 1930s, uncovered monumental halls, a treasury, and a "Red Building" and "Tower House", along with thousands of inscribed potsherds (ostraca) recording deliveries of wine and supplies, and a celebrated hoard of ivory rhytons (drinking horns) carved with Hellenistic mythological scenes.

Significance

The finds at Nisa illustrate the fusion of Persian, Central Asian, and Hellenistic Greek art and architecture characteristic of early Parthian culture, and make the site one of the key witnesses to the history of the Arsacid Empire.
