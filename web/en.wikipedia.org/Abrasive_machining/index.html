Abrasive machining

Abrasive machining is a machining process where material is removed from a workpiece using a multitude of small abrasive particles. Common examples include grinding, honing, and lapping. Abrasive machining works by forcing the abrasive particles, or grains, into the surface of the workpiece so that each particle cuts away a small bit of material.

Abrasive machining is usually used when the workpiece is too hard for conventional machining with defined cutting edges, or when an extremely fine surface finish or very tight tolerances are required. Because each abrasive grain removes only a tiny chip, abrasive processes can achieve dimensional accuracies on the order of a few micrometres.

Processes

Abrasive machining processes can be divided into two categories based on how the abrasive grains are applied. In bonded abrasive processes, such as grinding and honing, the grains are held together in a shaped tool such as a grinding wheel. In loose abrasive processes, such as lapping and abrasive flow machining, the grains are suspended in a fluid or used freely between the workpiece and a lap.

  • Grinding – a rotating bonded-abrasive wheel removes material from the workpiece surface.
  • Honing – bonded abrasive stones refine the bore of cylinders and similar internal surfaces.
  • Lapping – loose abrasive between a workpiece and a lap produces very flat, fine finishes.
  • Superfinishing – a bonded stone is applied with short strokes under light pressure.
  • Abrasive jet machining and ultrasonic machining – use high-velocity or vibrated abrasive particles.

Abrasives

The abrasive grains themselves are made from materials significantly harder than the workpiece; see abrasive for common natural and synthetic materials such as aluminium oxide, silicon carbide, cubic boron nitride and diamond.
