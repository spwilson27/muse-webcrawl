Angora wool

Angora hair or Angora fibre is the downy coat produced by the Angora rabbit, one of the oldest types of domestic rabbit, bred for its long, soft hair. It is not to be confused with mohair, the fibre of the Angora goat, nor with cashmere from Cashmere goats. Angora fibres are hollow, typically 11–13 micrometres in diameter, exceptionally warm and light, and mostly white, though the rabbits occur in many colours. China produces the overwhelming majority of the world's Angora wool — around 90% — with smaller output from countries including France, Hungary, Chile and India.

Properties and uses

The hollow core of Angora fibres traps air, giving garments outstanding thermal insulation for their weight, along with a characteristic silky "halo" and softness prized in knitwear, baby clothes, underwear for rheumatism sufferers and luxury blends. Because the fibres are smooth and short they shed and felt readily, so commercial yarns usually blend Angora (often 30–50%) with wool, silk or synthetics for strength. Four rabbit breeds dominate production: English, French, German and Giant Angora, the German type bred for continuous high yield.

Harvesting and animal welfare

Fibre is harvested about every three months as the coat naturally moults, by gentle plucking of loose hair, combing, or shearing. Controversy erupted in 2013 when undercover footage showed rabbits in China being violently plucked while restrained; many major retailers suspended Angora sales, and the industry responded with welfare standards — shearing and combing rather than live-plucking, and certification schemes such as Caregora — although monitoring of supply chains remains difficult.

History

Angora rabbits, originating in Turkey and popularised at European courts, were kept by French weavers from the 18th century and became a significant cottage industry in France; they were also raised in Britain and the United States during the world wars when wool was short, and in Germany for military uniforms. Large-scale Chinese production expanded from the late 20th century and now dominates the market.
