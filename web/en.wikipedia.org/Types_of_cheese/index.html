Types of cheese

Cheeses vary by milk source, texture, ageing and rind
Number of varietiesOver 1,800 (estimates vary)
Classification basesTexture, moisture, milk, rind, method

There are many types of cheese, with estimates of distinct varieties ranging from several hundred to over 1,800 worldwide. Cheeses can be grouped or classified according to criteria such as length of fermentation, texture, methods of production, fat content, animal milk, and country or region of origin. The most common classification is by moisture content and firmness.

Classification by texture

  • Fresh cheeses — unripened and soft, e.g. mozzarella, ricotta, cottage cheese, cream cheese, feta.
  • Soft-ripened cheeses — ripened from the outside with mould rinds, e.g. Brie and Camembert.
  • Semi-soft cheeses — e.g. Gouda (young), Havarti, Monterey Jack, Munster.
  • Semi-hard cheeses — e.g. Cheddar, Edam, Gruyère, Emmental, Manchego.
  • Hard cheeses — long-aged and low in moisture, e.g. Parmigiano-Reggiano, Pecorino Romano, aged Gouda.
  • Blue cheeses — veined with Penicillium roqueforti or P. glaucum, e.g. Roquefort, Stilton, Gorgonzola.
  • Processed cheeses — made from natural cheese with emulsifiers, e.g. American cheese.

Classification by milk

Most cheese is made from cow's milk, but important varieties come from goat's milk (e.g. chèvre), sheep's milk (e.g. Roquefort, Manchego, Pecorino), buffalo milk (e.g. mozzarella di bufala), and occasionally the milk of yaks, reindeer, or camels.
