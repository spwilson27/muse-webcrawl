Ouray is a small city in the San Juan Mountains of southwestern Colorado and the county seat of Ouray County. Set in a steep box canyon at about 7,800 feet, it is nicknamed the "Switzerland of America" for its alpine scenery. The town is named for Chief Ouray of the Ute people.

History

Ouray began in 1875 as a mining camp serving gold and silver mines in the surrounding mountains, including the Camp Bird Mine. Prospectors laid out the town in 1876, and much of its Victorian-era main street survives as a registered historic district.

Tourism

Today the economy depends on tourism: natural hot springs, the Ouray Ice Park — a famous artificial ice-climbing venue in the Uncompahgre Gorge — and jeep roads over high passes such as Imogene and Engineer. The Million Dollar Highway (U.S. 550) climbs south from Ouray to Silverton over Red Mountain Pass.
