Aortic aneurysm

An aneurysm is an abnormal widening of the aorta

An aortic aneurysm is an enlargement (dilatation) of the aorta to greater than 1.5 times its normal size. Aneurysms are classified by location: those in the abdomen are abdominal aortic aneurysms (AAA), and those in the chest are thoracic aortic aneurysms (TAA). They usually cause no symptoms unless they rupture, an event that is frequently fatal.

Causes and risk factors

Most aneurysms result from weakening of the aortic wall through atherosclerosis, chronic hypertension, and degeneration of the media layer. Risk factors include smoking (the strongest modifiable factor), male sex, older age, and family history. Connective tissue disorders such as Marfan syndrome and Ehlers–Danlos syndrome, bicuspid aortic valve, infections, and trauma can also cause aneurysms, particularly in the thoracic aorta.

Diagnosis and screening

Aneurysms are commonly detected incidentally on imaging or by ultrasound screening. Ultrasound screening is recommended in many countries for men aged 65 and over who have ever smoked. CT angiography is used to plan repair.

Treatment

Small aneurysms are monitored by surveillance imaging with control of blood pressure and smoking cessation. Larger or rapidly expanding aneurysms are repaired by open surgery with a graft or by endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR/TEVAR), in which a stent graft is placed through the femoral arteries. Rupture risk rises steeply with diameter; abdominal aneurysms are generally repaired at about 5.5 cm in men.
