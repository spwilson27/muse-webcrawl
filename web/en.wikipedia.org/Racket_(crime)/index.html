Racket (crime)

A racket is a criminal enterprise or a fraudulent service, scheme, or offence, usually involving the threat or use of violence, extortion, or deception. The classic example is the protection racket, in which criminals demand payment from businesses in exchange for "protection", typically from the very harm the racketeers themselves threaten to inflict. The term is especially associated with organised crime groups such as the American Mafia.

Types of rackets

Common rackets include numbers gambling, loan sharking, labour racketeering (corrupt control of trade unions), cargo theft, extortion of construction and waste-hauling industries, and protection rackets directed at shops and restaurants. What distinguishes a racket is its recurring, organised character: it operates as a continuing illegal business rather than a one-off crime.

Legal response

In the United States, the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act (RICO), enacted in 1970, allows prosecutors to charge the leaders of a criminal enterprise for the pattern of crimes committed by the organisation, with severe penalties and asset forfeiture. RICO has also been adapted, controversially, to some civil and non-mafia cases. Similar anti-mafia statutes exist in Italy, where Article 416-bis of the penal code targets mafia-type associations.
