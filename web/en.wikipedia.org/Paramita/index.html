Pāramitā (Sanskrit: पारमिता; Pali: pāramī) is a Buddhist term often translated into English as "perfection". Etymologically, it derives from parama ("supreme") or from para ("the further shore") and ita ("gone"), giving the sense of "that which has reached the other shore". The pāramitās are virtues cultivated by a bodhisattva on the path to Buddhahood. In some Mahayana sources, they are called the "transcendent actions" because, when practised with the wisdom realising emptiness (śūnyatā), they carry the practitioner beyond the world of suffering.

The six perfections

The most widely known list, found in the Prajñāpāramitā literature and the Daśabhūmika Sūtra, comprises six perfections:

  • Dāna pāramitā — generosity, giving of oneself and one's resources.
  • Śīla pāramitā — virtue, morality, discipline and proper conduct.
  • Kṣānti pāramitā — patience, tolerance, forbearance and acceptance.
  • Vīrya pāramitā — energy, diligence, vigour and effort.
  • Dhyāna pāramitā — one-pointed concentration and meditation.
  • Prajñā pāramitā — wisdom and insight into the true nature of reality.

The ten perfections

Some Mahayana texts expand the list to ten by adding four further perfections: upāya (skillful means), praṇidhāna (vow or resolution), bala (spiritual power) and jñāna (knowledge). In Theravada Buddhism, the Buddhavaṃsa and the Jātaka tales list ten pāramīs: generosity, morality, renunciation (nekkhamma), wisdom, energy, patience, truthfulness (sacca), determination (adhiṭṭhāna), loving-kindness (mettā) and equanimity (upekkhā). The Jātakas recount how the Buddha, in past lives as a bodhisattva, perfected each of these virtues.

Practice

The perfections are not practised in isolation: generosity unsupported by wisdom may be misdirected, while wisdom without compassion is said to be incomplete. Commentators such as Śāntideva, in the Bodhicaryāvatāra, devote a chapter to each of the six perfections. The perfection of wisdom is regarded as primary, since it is the insight into emptiness that transforms ordinary virtues into truly transcendent ones.
