Bloomsbury is a district in the West End of London, part of the London Borough of Camden. It is considered a fashionable residential area, noted for its numerous garden squares, including Russell Square, Bloomsbury Square, and Bedford Square, and for its many cultural, educational and healthcare institutions.

The area was developed by the Russell family (the Dukes of Bedford) in the 17th and 18th centuries into a fashionable residential district. The name Bloomsbury derives from the medieval manor of Blemondisberi, recorded in the Domesday era as land belonging to a Norman landowner.

Institutions

Bloomsbury is home to the British Museum, the first national public museum in the world, founded in 1753. It is also the historic centre of the University of London, containing University College London, Birkbeck, SOAS, and other colleges, as well as Great Ormond Street Hospital and numerous publishers.

Bloomsbury Group

In the early 20th century the district gave its name to the Bloomsbury Group, an influential circle of writers, intellectuals and artists including Virginia Woolf, John Maynard Keynes, E. M. Forster and Lytton Strachey, who lived and met in the area.
