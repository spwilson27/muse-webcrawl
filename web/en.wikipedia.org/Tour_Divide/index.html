Tour Divide

TypeUltra-distance bikepacking race
RouteBanff, Alberta, Canada to Antelope Wells, New Mexico, USA
DistanceAbout 2,745 miles (4,418 km)
First held2008
StartSecond Friday of June (Grand Depart)

The Tour Divide is an annual mountain biking race travelling the length of the Great Divide Mountain Bike Route. This ultra-distance, self-supported endurance event follows the Continental Divide of the Americas from Banff, Alberta, Canada to Antelope Wells, New Mexico, on the border with Mexico. At roughly 2,745 miles (4,418 km) with about 200,000 feet (60,000 m) of climbing, it is among the longest off-pavement cycling races in the world.

Rules and ethos

The Tour Divide is deliberately minimalist: there is no entry fee, no prizes, and no outside support. Riders must carry or purchase everything they need along the way, may not accept pre-arranged assistance, and typically track their progress by GPS. The race ethos — summed up in the instruction to "do nothing to benefit from traffic or pre-arranged support" — emphasizes self-reliance and equal footing.

Records

Mike Hall set a landmark record of 13 days, 22 hours and 51 minutes in 2016. Lael Wilcox set the women's record with a time of 15 days, 10 hours and 59 minutes in 2015. Finish times for most riders range from about three weeks to two months, and only a fraction of starters finish.

Culture

The race begins with a "Grand Depart" from Banff on the second Friday of June. It has been documented in the film Ride the Divide (2010) and has inspired a global bikepacking racing scene.
