Ambassador Bridge

The Ambassador Bridge is a suspension bridge that connects Detroit, Michigan, United States, with Windsor, Ontario, Canada. Opened in 1929, it is the busiest international border crossing in North America in terms of trade volume, carrying more than 25% of all merchandise trade between the United States and Canada.

History

After the First World War, proposals for a road bridge across the Detroit River gained momentum as automobile traffic outgrew ferry capacity. The bridge was built by the Detroit International Bridge Company, with financier Joseph Bower and the McClintic-Marshall company as contractor. Construction began in 1927 and the bridge opened on 15 November 1929. At the time, its main span of 564 metres (1,850 feet) made it the longest suspension bridge in the world, a record it held until 1931.

Ownership and controversy

The bridge is privately owned by the Detroit International Bridge Company, controlled for decades by the family of Manuel "Matty" Moroun. Its private ownership of a critical trade artery, and the company's long legal and political battles against the planned publicly owned Gordie Howe International Bridge downstream, have made it a subject of sustained controversy in both countries. In February 2022 the bridge was blockaded for several days by protesters opposing Canadian COVID-19 vaccine mandates, disrupting cross-border trade.
