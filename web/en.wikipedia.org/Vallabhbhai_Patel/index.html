Vallabhbhai Patel

Born31 October 1875, Nadiad, British India
Died15 December 1950, Bombay
OfficesFirst Deputy Prime Minister of India (1947–1950); Minister of Home Affairs
Known as"Sardar" (chief); "Iron Man of India"

Vallabhbhai Jhaverbhai Patel (31 October 1875 – 15 December 1950), commonly known as Sardar Patel, was an Indian statesman and barrister who served as the first Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister of independent India. A leading figure in the Indian National Congress and a close associate of Mahatma Gandhi, he is revered for his role in integrating the princely states into the Indian Union, earning him the epithet "Iron Man of India".

Early life and freedom struggle

Patel was born in Nadiad, Gujarat, into a landowning family, qualified as a barrister in England, and built a successful legal practice in Ahmedabad. Drawn into Gandhi's movement, he gave up his practice to lead campaigns including the Kheda Satyagraha of 1918 and the Bardoli Satyagraha of 1928, whose success earned him the title Sardar ("chief"). He was repeatedly imprisoned by the British, including during the Salt March period and the Quit India movement.

Integration of the princely states

At independence in 1947, more than 560 princely states were free in principle to join India or Pakistan or remain independent. As Home Minister, working with civil servant V. P. Menon, Patel used negotiation, persuasion and, where necessary, firmness to persuade nearly all of them to accede to India, shaping the territorial unity of the new republic. The police action that integrated Hyderabad in 1948 was undertaken under his direction. He also reorganised the civil services, championing the Indian Administrative Service as the "steel frame" of the country.

Death and legacy

Patel died in Bombay on 15 December 1950. His birthday, 31 October, is celebrated in India as Rashtriya Ekta Diwas (National Unity Day). In 2018 the Statue of Unity — at 182 metres the world's tallest statue — depicting Patel was inaugurated near the Sardar Sarovar Dam in Gujarat. He was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour, in 1991.
