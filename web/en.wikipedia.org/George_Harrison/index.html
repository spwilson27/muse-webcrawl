George Harrison (1943–2001) was an English musician and singer-songwriter who achieved international fame as the lead guitarist of the Beatles. Often called "the quiet Beatle," Harrison embraced Indian music and Hindu spirituality, broadened the scope of popular music, and wrote some of the band's most admired songs, including "Here Comes the Sun" and "Something."

The Beatles

Harrison joined the Quarrymen as a teenager at the invitation of his school friend Paul McCartney. In the Beatles his guitar playing defined much of the group's sound, and from the mid-1960s his songwriting matured rapidly: "Taxman," "Within You Without You," and the Abbey Road tracks "Something" and "Here Comes the Sun" — the latter among the Beatles' most streamed songs. His study of the sitar under Ravi Shankar introduced Indian instrumentation to Western pop.

Solo career

His triple album All Things Must Pass (1970), featuring "My Sweet Lord" and "What Is Life," was a major critical and commercial success. In 1971 he organised the Concert for Bangladesh, a pioneering benefit concert. Later highlights include the album Cloud Nine (1987) and his membership of the supergroup the Traveling Wilburys with Bob Dylan, Tom Petty, Jeff Lynne, and Roy Orbison. Through his company HandMade Films he also produced films including Monty Python's Life of Brian.

Death and legacy

Harrison died of lung cancer in 2001. He was posthumously inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a solo artist in 2004, having already been inducted with the Beatles.
