Robert Emerson Lucas Jr. (15 September 1937 – 15 May 2023) was an American economist at the University of Chicago. Widely regarded as the central figure in the development of the new classical approach to macroeconomics, he received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1995 "for having developed and applied the hypothesis of rational expectations, and thereby having transformed macroeconomic analysis and deepened our understanding of economic policy."

Contributions

Lucas's 1972 paper on expectations and the neutrality of money built the first equilibrium model in which only unanticipated monetary shocks affect output, reconciling the Phillips curve with optimizing behaviour. His 1976 "Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique"—the Lucas critique—argued that the parameters of econometric models change when policy changes, because they reflect agents' expectations; it fundamentally reshaped how economists evaluate policy rules.

Later, Lucas helped found modern growth theory with his 1988 paper "On the Mechanics of Economic Development", which modelled human-capital accumulation and learning-by-doing as engines of sustained growth. He also contributed the Lucas islands model of imperfect information, work on asset pricing (the Lucas tree model), and with Edward Prescott, early theory of recursive competitive equilibria in dynamic stochastic economies.
