Rio Grande

The Rio Grande (in Mexico known as the Río Bravo del Norte) is one of the principal rivers of the southwestern United States and northern Mexico. It begins in south-central Colorado, flows through New Mexico, and then forms the border between the U.S. state of Texas and the Mexican states of Chihuahua, Coahuila, Nuevo León and Tamaulipas, before emptying into the Gulf of Mexico.

Course

From its headwaters in the San Juan Mountains, the river flows south through the San Luis Valley and the Rio Grande rift in New Mexico, passing Albuquerque and Las Cruces before reaching El Paso. Below El Paso it turns southeast and for roughly 2,000 km marks the international boundary, joined by tributaries including the Rio Conchos, Pecos River and Devils River. Major dams include Elephant Butte and Amistad.

History and border

The river became the boundary between the United States and Mexico under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), which ended the Mexican–American War. Because the channel shifts, the two countries have repeatedly disputed and re-demarcated the line, creating the International Boundary and Water Commission. The river is also a major crossing point in modern debates over immigration.

Water use and ecology

Heavy irrigation and urban demand in an arid basin mean the river sometimes fails to reach the sea, as happened in 2001. Agreements such as the Rio Grande Compact (1938) and the 1944 water treaty allocate its flow between the U.S. states and Mexico. The basin supports distinctive desert and riparian ecosystems, including Big Bend National Park.
