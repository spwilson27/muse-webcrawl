Best linear unbiased prediction

Best linear unbiased prediction (BLUP) is a method in statistics, used within linear mixed models, for estimating random effects. BLUP was introduced by Charles Roy Henderson in the context of animal breeding, where it became the standard method for estimating the genetic merit (breeding values) of livestock.

Overview

In a mixed model, observations depend on both fixed effects and unobserved random effects. Whereas fixed effects are estimated by best linear unbiased estimation (BLUE), random effects cannot be estimated in the same way because they are random variables; instead they are predicted. BLUP gives the predictor that is linear in the observations, unbiased in the sense that its expected value equals the expected value of the quantity predicted, and has minimum prediction error variance among all such predictors.

Applications

BLUP is fundamental to modern animal and plant breeding, where it underpins estimated breeding values (EBVs) and genomic selection. The same mathematics underlies kriging in geostatistics, and BLUP-type predictors appear in small-area estimation in survey statistics. In practice, variance components are usually estimated first (for example by REML) and then plugged in, giving empirical BLUP (EBLUP).
