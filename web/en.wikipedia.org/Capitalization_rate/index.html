Capitalization rate

The capitalization rate, or cap rate, is a real estate valuation measure used to compare different real estate investments. It is calculated as the ratio of the net operating income (NOI) produced by an asset to its current market value or purchase price:

cap rate = annual net operating income / current market value

For example, a property generating $100,000 of net operating income and valued at $1,250,000 has a cap rate of 8%. Cap rates are used to estimate an investor's potential return; a higher cap rate generally implies higher risk and higher expected return. The measure is most useful for income-producing properties and is related to, but distinct from, measures such as cash-on-cash return.

Uses

Investors use cap rates to value a property from its income (value = NOI / cap rate), to compare markets, and to track trends in commercial real estate pricing over time.
