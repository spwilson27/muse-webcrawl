New Comedy

New Comedy is the final phase of ancient Greek comedy, following Old Comedy and Middle Comedy, flourishing roughly from the late 4th century BC into the Hellenistic period. Whereas Old Comedy satirised contemporary Athenian politics and personalities, New Comedy focused on fictional everyday citizens and domestic situations.

Characteristics

Plots typically revolved around young lovers obstructed by disapproving parents, clever slaves, mistaken identities and long-lost children recognised at the end. The chorus dwindled to a musical interlude between five acts.

Playwrights

Its leading figure was Menander, of whose works the Dyskolos survives nearly complete; other poets included Philemon and Diphilus. Through Roman adaptations by Plautus and Terence, New Comedy strongly influenced later European comedy of manners and situation comedy.
