Early human migrations

Early human migrations are the earliest movements of hominins and Homo sapiens out of Africa and across the world, beginning more than a million years ago. Migration occurred in waves as hominins and later humans adapted to new environments, made sea crossings, and populated every continent except Antarctica.

First dispersals

Homo erectus dispersed out of Africa beginning around 1.8 million years ago, reaching the Caucasus (Dmanisi, Georgia), Java, and China (the "Out of Africa I" migration). Later dispersals of archaic Homo—including Homo heidelbergensis—populated Europe and Asia, giving rise to Neanderthals in the west and Denisovans in the east.

Anatomically modern humans

Homo sapiens evolved in Africa around 300,000 years ago (evidence from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco). The principal modern-human dispersal ("Out of Africa II") began roughly 70,000–60,000 years ago; genetic evidence indicates all living non-Africans descend mainly from a single founding population that crossed from East Africa into Arabia, possibly via the Sinai or across the Bab-el-Mandeb strait during low sea levels.

The peopling of Asia

Modern humans reached southern Asia quickly, following a coastal "southern route" to Southeast Asia and beyond. Genetic studies show that Aboriginal Australians descend from this early wave, reaching Australia by at least 65,000 years ago. Modern humans reached China and Siberia, interbreeding with Denisovans and Neanderthals along the way; contemporary non-Africans carry roughly 1–2% Neanderthal DNA, and Melanesians up to ~5% Denisovan DNA.

Europe and the Neanderthals

Modern humans entered Europe by about 45,000 years ago (e.g. the Bacho Kiro cave in Bulgaria), overlapping with Neanderthals for several millennia before the latter's extinction around 40,000 years ago. Upper Palaeolithic cultures—the Aurignacian, Gravettian, and Magdalenian—produced cave art such as Lascaux and Chauvet.

Oceania and the Americas

Reaching Australia required open-ocean crossings, evidence of the earliest seafaring. The Americas were peopled later: humans crossed Beringia (the Bering land bridge) from Siberia, with secure evidence by about 16,000 years ago and rapid southward spread thereafter, including along a Pacific coastal route. Polynesia, the last major region settled, was colonised by Austronesian voyagers reaching Hawaiʻi, Easter Island, and New Zealand within the last two thousand years.
