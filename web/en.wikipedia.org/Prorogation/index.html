Prorogation

Prorogation in the Westminster system of government is the action of proroguing Parliament — formally ending a parliamentary session without dissolving Parliament, so that the legislature reconvenes later as a new session. It is distinct from adjournment (a break within a session) and from dissolution (which ends a Parliament and triggers a general election).

Procedure

In the United Kingdom, Parliament is prorogued by the monarch on the advice of the prime minister, typically for a short period before a new session opens with the State Opening of Parliament and the King's or Queen's Speech. Business that has not completed its passage generally falls, though some bills may be carried over. In Canada, the governor general prorogues Parliament on the advice of the prime minister; several prorogations have been controversial, including those of 2008 and 2020.

2019 United Kingdom prorogation

In 2019 Prime Minister Boris Johnson advised Queen Elizabeth II to prorogue Parliament for five weeks during the Brexit debates. In R (Miller) v The Prime Minister and Cherry v Advocate General for Scotland, the UK Supreme Court ruled unanimously that the advice was unlawful and the prorogation void, because it frustrated Parliament's constitutional functions without reasonable justification.
