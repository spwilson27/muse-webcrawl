Laurence Olivier Awards

The Laurence Olivier Awards, known simply as the Olivier Awards, are presented annually by the Society of London Theatre to recognise excellence in professional theatre in London. Established in 1976 as the Society of West End Theatre Awards, they were renamed in 1984 in honour of actor Laurence Olivier and are the most prestigious theatre awards in the United Kingdom, broadly the British equivalent of Broadway's Tony Awards.

Categories

Awards cover plays, musicals, opera and dance, including Best New Play, Best New Musical, Best Actor and Actress, directing, design and choreography, plus a Special Award for lifetime contribution. Productions eligible are those staged in theatres represented by the Society, mostly in the West End.

Ceremony

Recent ceremonies have been held at the Royal Albert Hall and broadcast on television and radio, with performances from nominated musicals. Statuettes depict Olivier as Henry V.
