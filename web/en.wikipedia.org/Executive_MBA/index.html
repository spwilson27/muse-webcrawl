Executive MBA

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An Executive Master of Business Administration (EMBA) is a graduate business degree designed for experienced working professionals, typically mid-career managers and executives, who study part-time while continuing in their jobs. The curriculum covers the same core subjects as a conventional Master of Business Administration — accounting, finance, marketing, organizational behaviour, operations, and strategy — but assumes substantial prior work experience and emphasizes leadership, corporate governance, and strategic decision-making.

History

The first EMBA program was offered by the University of Chicago's Booth School of Business in 1943, aimed at executives whose employers wanted them to gain management training without leaving the workforce. The format spread widely in the 1970s and 1980s, and today most major business schools offer an EMBA, often in modular, weekend, or blended online-and-residency formats.

Format and admissions

EMBA students commonly have ten or more years of professional experience, and many are sponsored in whole or part by their employers. Classes are commonly scheduled on alternate weekends or in intensive residential modules spread over 18 to 24 months, allowing cohorts to move through the program together. Some schools run multi-campus or cross-border "global" EMBAs jointly with partner institutions in other countries. Admissions place greater weight on professional achievement and managerial experience than on standardized test scores, and some programs waive the GMAT requirement for senior applicants.
