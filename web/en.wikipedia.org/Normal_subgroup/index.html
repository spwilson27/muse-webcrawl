Normal subgroup

In abstract algebra, a normal subgroup (also known as an invariant subgroup or self-conjugate subgroup) is a subgroup that is invariant under conjugation by members of the group of which it is a part. Formally, a subgroup N of a group G is normal if gNg⁻¹ = N for every g in G; the relation is written N ◃ G.

Definitions and equivalent conditions

A subgroup N of G is normal if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions hold: the left and right cosets coincide (gN = Ng for all g); N is a union of conjugacy classes of G; there exists a group homomorphism defined on G whose kernel is N. In particular, every subgroup of an abelian group is normal, and a subgroup of index 2 is always normal.

Quotient groups

The cosets of a normal subgroup can be given a well-defined group operation, forming the quotient group G/N. This construction underlies the first isomorphism theorem and is essential to the study of simple groups — groups whose only normal subgroups are the trivial group and the group itself.

Examples

The alternating group An is a normal subgroup of the symmetric group Sn, of index 2. The trivial subgroup and the whole group are always normal. In the quaternion group, every subgroup is normal, yet the group is non-abelian — an example of a Dedekind group.
