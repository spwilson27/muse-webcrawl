Perm, Russia

Country: Russia
Federal subject: Perm Krai (administrative centre)
Founded: 1723
Population: approx. 1 million
River: Kama

Perm (Russian: Пермь) is a city in eastern European Russia and the administrative centre of Perm Krai, located on the banks of the Kama River near the Ural Mountains. With around one million inhabitants, it is one of Russia's largest cities and a major industrial and cultural centre. The city gave its name to the Permian geological period, identified by the geologist Roderick Murchison from rock strata in the region in 1841.

History

Perm began in 1723 as the settlement around the Yegoshikha copper-smelting works founded under Peter the Great; it was granted city status and its present name in 1781 by Catherine the Great. It developed as a gateway to Siberia and a transshipment point on the Kama. From 1940 to 1957 it was called Molotov, after the Soviet foreign minister. During the Second World War the city was a major centre of artillery and armaments production, and nearby was Perm-36, one of the last Gulag labour camps, closed in 1988 and now a memorial museum.

Culture

Perm is noted for its opera and ballet theatre (home of the Diaghilev Festival, honouring Sergei Diaghilev, who grew up in the city), its state art gallery with a notable collection of wooden religious sculpture ("Perm gods"), and a prominent contemporary art scene led by the PERMM museum. The city was also the setting of Boris Pasternak's novel Doctor Zhivago (as "Yuriatin").

Economy and transport

Industry centres on oil refining, machinery, aviation engines and chemicals. Perm stands on the Trans-Siberian Railway and is served by Perm International Airport (Bolshoye Savino).
