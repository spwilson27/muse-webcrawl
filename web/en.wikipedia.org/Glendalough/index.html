Glendalough


The round tower at Glendalough.

Glendalough (Irish: Gleann Dá Loch, "valley of two lakes") is a glacial valley in County Wicklow, Ireland, renowned for an early medieval monastic settlement founded in the 6th century by St Kevin.

History

St Kevin, a hermit priest, founded the monastery in the valley beside the Upper Lake. After his death in 618 the settlement grew into one of Ireland's most important monastic schools, attracting pilgrims and scholars despite repeated raids by Vikings and later by English forces. The site continued as a place of pilgrimage after the monastery declined in the 13th century, and the feast of St Kevin on 3 June still draws visitors.

Monastic city

The surviving ruins include a distinctive 30-metre round tower, the cathedral, St Kevin's Church (known as "St Kevin's Kitchen" for its chimney-like bell turret), the Priest's House, and numerous carved crosses and grave slabs. A great gateway arch is a rare survival of an Irish monastic entrance.

The valley

The valley lies within the Wicklow Mountains National Park. The Lower and Upper Lakes are flanked by steep wooded slopes; a popular walking network includes the Spinc trail overlooking the Upper Lake and the Miners' Road along its shore.
