The western lowland gorilla (Gorilla gorilla gorilla) is one of two subspecies of the western gorilla (Gorilla gorilla) that lives in montane, primary and secondary forest and lowland swampland in central Africa in Angola (Cabinda Province), Cameroon, Central African Republic, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon. It is the most widespread of all gorilla subspecies and is the subspecies most commonly kept in zoos worldwide.

Description

The western lowland gorilla is the smallest subspecies of gorilla but still has exceptional size and strength. They stand upright at 1.55–1.75 m (5 ft 1 in – 5 ft 9 in), weigh 70–180 kg (150–400 lb), and have a brown-grey coat with a reddish-brown crest on the head. Adult males develop greyish or silver hair on their backs, giving rise to the term "silverback". Their skulls are broader and more pronounced than those of the mountain gorilla, and their brow ridges are more pronounced.

Behaviour and ecology

Western lowland gorillas live in family groups of one dominant silverback male, several females and their offspring, typically numbering between two and twenty individuals. They are largely herbivorous, feeding mainly on fruit, leaves, shoots and occasionally insects such as ants and termites. Groups occupy home ranges of up to 30 square kilometres and travel roughly one kilometre per day while foraging.

Females give birth to a single infant after a gestation of about 8.5 months, typically once every four years. Infants cling to their mothers for the first few months of life and begin walking at around nine months.

Threats and conservation

The subspecies is listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Populations have declined by more than 60% over 20–25 years, principally owing to commercial hunting for bushmeat, habitat loss from logging and agriculture, and disease, notably Ebola virus outbreaks that killed large numbers in the Republic of the Congo and Gabon in the early 2000s.

In captivity

Almost all gorillas kept in zoos outside Africa are western lowland gorillas. The first gorilla born in captivity, Colo, was born at the Columbus Zoo in Ohio in 1956 and lived to the age of 60. Famous zoo individuals have included Guy at London Zoo and Willie B. at Zoo Atlanta.
