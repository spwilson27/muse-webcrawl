Arithmetic mean

In mathematics and statistics, the arithmetic mean (pronounced "air-ith-MET-ik") is the sum of a collection of numbers divided by the count of numbers in the collection. The collection is often a set of results of an experiment or an observational study.

The arithmetic mean is frequently used in everyday language simply as an average. For example, per capita income is the arithmetic average income of a nation's population. While the arithmetic mean is often used to report central tendencies, it is not a robust statistic: it is greatly influenced by outliers (values much larger or smaller than most others).

Definition

Given a data set x1, x2, …, xn, the arithmetic mean (often denoted x̄) is defined as:

x̄ = (1/n) Σ xi = (x1 + x2 + … + xn) / n.

Properties

  • The mean of a sample is the value about which the sum of squared deviations is minimal.
  • The deviations of the values from the mean sum to zero.
  • The mean is sensitive to every value in the data set: changing any single value changes the mean.

Contrasts with median and mode

The arithmetic mean differs from the median (the middle value in an ordered set) and the mode (the most frequent value). For skewed distributions, the median is often preferred as a measure of central tendency because it is less affected by extreme values.

Generalizations

Related concepts include the geometric mean, harmonic mean, weighted arithmetic mean, and truncated mean. The arithmetic mean is also the expected value of a discrete random variable with equally likely outcomes.
