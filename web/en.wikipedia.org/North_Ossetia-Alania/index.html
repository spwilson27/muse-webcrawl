North Ossetia–Alania

The Republic of North Ossetia–Alania is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus of Eastern Europe, located in the Northern Caucasus. Its population according to the 2021 Census was 687,357. The republic's capital city is the city of Vladikavkaz, located on the foothills of the Caucasus Mountains.

Geography

The republic borders Kabardino-Balkaria and Stavropol Krai to the west and north, Chechnya and Ingushetia to the east, and Georgia (including the disputed territory of South Ossetia) to the south. The northern part lies on the Stavropol Upland plain, while the south is mountainous.

History

The Ossetians descend from the Alans, a Sarmatian tribe. The territory came under Russian control in the late 18th and 19th centuries. The North Ossetian Autonomous Oblast was created in 1924 and became an autonomous republic within the Russian SFSR in 1936. In 2004 the republic was the site of the Beslan school siege.

Demographics

Ossetians form the majority of the population, with Russians the largest minority. The main languages are Ossetian and Russian; most Ossetians are Orthodox Christian, with a Muslim minority.
