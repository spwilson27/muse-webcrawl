Applecross

Applecross (Scottish Gaelic: A' Chomraich, "the sanctuary") is a peninsula and small village in Wester Ross, in the Highland council area of north-west Scotland. It faces the Inner Sound and the islands of Raasay and Skye, and is known for its remote, mountainous scenery.

Access

For most of its history Applecross was accessible mainly by sea or by the Bealach na Bà ("pass of the cattle"), a steep mountain road with hairpin bends often described as the steepest ascent in Britain, rising to 626 metres. A longer coastal road around the peninsula was completed in the 1970s. Both routes are popular with touring motorists and cyclists and form part of the North Coast 500 route.

History

The area has early Christian associations: St Maelrubha founded a monastery here in 673 CE, and the parish church site remains a place of pilgrimage. The village itself consists largely of a single street along the shore, with a small population supported by tourism, crofting and fishing.
