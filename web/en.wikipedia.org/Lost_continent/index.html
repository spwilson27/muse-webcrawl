Lost continent

A lost continent is a landmass that was believed to exist during prehistory and to have since sunk beneath the sea or otherwise vanished. Proposed lost continents range from legendary places, such as Atlantis, Mu, and Lemuria, to scientifically identified submerged landmasses like Zealandia, the Kerguelen Plateau, and Doggerland.

Legendary continents

Plato's dialogues Timaeus and Critias describe Atlantis, a powerful island civilization destroyed in a cataclysm. In the nineteenth century, scientists proposed Lemuria — a sunken land bridge in the Indian Ocean — to explain the distribution of lemurs; it was later adopted by occult writers such as Helena Blavatsky. Augustus Le Plongeon and James Churchward popularized Mu as a lost Pacific homeland of humanity. Such ideas flourished before plate tectonics explained species distributions by continental drift.

Real submerged lands

Geology has confirmed genuine submerged continental fragments. Zealandia, around New Zealand, is a largely underwater continent of thinned continental crust. The Mascarene Plateau and Kerguelen Plateau in the Indian Ocean contain continental fragments, and Mauritia was identified beneath Mauritius. Doggerland, the plain connecting Britain to mainland Europe, flooded as sea levels rose after the last Ice Age.
