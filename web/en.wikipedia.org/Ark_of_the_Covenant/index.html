The Ark of the Covenant is the legendary gold-covered wooden chest described in the Hebrew Bible as containing the two stone tablets of the Ten Commandments. According to the biblical account in Exodus, it was built following God's instructions to Moses on Mount Sinai, carried by the Israelites during their wilderness wanderings, and eventually installed in the Holy of Holies of Solomon's Temple in Jerusalem.

Description

Exodus 25 describes an acacia-wood chest about 2.5 cubits long, 1.5 wide and 1.5 high (roughly 114 × 68 × 68 cm), overlaid inside and out with pure gold, with four rings through which gilded poles passed for carrying. Its lid, the "mercy seat" (kapporet), bore two golden cherubim facing each other with outstretched wings; God was said to speak to Moses from between the cherubim.

Biblical narrative

The Ark plays a central role in accounts of the crossing of the Jordan, the fall of Jericho, and wars with the Philistines, who captured it and suffered plagues before returning it (1 Samuel 4–6). Uzzah was struck dead for touching it during its transport. David brought it to Jerusalem, and Solomon placed it in the Temple. After the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem in 586 BC, the Ark disappears from the biblical record, and its fate is unknown; the Second Temple's Holy of Holies was empty.

Traditions and theories

Numerous traditions claim its survival: the Ethiopian Orthodox Church holds that the Ark rests in the Chapel of the Tablet at Aksum; other theories place it in a hidden Temple chamber (as in 2 Maccabees, where Jeremiah hides it on Mount Nebo), Egypt, or Ireland. No claim has been verified archaeologically.

In popular culture

The Ark is the object of the quest in the 1981 film Raiders of the Lost Ark, one of many portrayals in literature, film and games.
