Wildlife Conservation Society
Abbreviation: WCS
Founded: 1895 (as the New York Zoological Society)
Headquarters: Bronx Zoo, New York City
Type: Nonprofit conservation organisation

The Wildlife Conservation Society (WCS) is a non-governmental organisation headquartered at the Bronx Zoo in New York City that aims to conserve the world's largest wild places in some 14 priority regions. Founded in 1895 as the New York Zoological Society (NYZS), the organisation manages four New York City wildlife parks in addition to the Bronx Zoo: Central Park Zoo, New York Aquarium, Prospect Park Zoo, and Queens Zoo.

History

The society was founded in 1895 and opened the New York Zoological Park (the Bronx Zoo) in 1899 and took over the New York Aquarium in 1902. Its first director, William Temple Hornaday, was a prominent early conservationist who campaigned for the protection of the American bison; the society's bison herd at the Bronx Zoo supplied animals for reintroduction herds in the American West. In 1993 the organisation adopted its current name to reflect its growing global field work.

Work

WCS works in nearly 60 countries, supporting the protection of some 400 million acres of wild places. Its field programmes combine scientific research, protected-area management, and work with governments and local communities to conserve species including tigers, elephants, gorillas, and sharks. The society also operates the world's largest urban zoo network and conducts conservation education.
