Protestantism is a branch of Christianity that emphasizes justification of sinners through faith alone, the teaching that salvation comes by unmerited divine grace, the necessity for human sins to be revealed by the Holy Spirit, the priesthood of all believers, and the Bible as the sole infallible source of authority for Christian faith and practice. It originated with the Protestant Reformation of the 16th century; the word derives from the "protestation" of the Lutheran princes at the Diet of Speyer in 1529.

Protestants are generally estimated to number between 800 million and one billion adherents worldwide, making Protestantism the second-largest form of Christianity after Catholicism when treated as a single grouping, though it is divided into thousands of denominations with no central authority.

Beliefs

Core doctrines shared across most Protestant traditions include the Five solae of the Reformation era: sola scriptura, sola fide, sola gratia, solus Christus, and soli Deo gloria. Most Protestants recognize two sacraments or ordinances, baptism and the Lord's Supper (Eucharist), though their understanding ranges from sacramental (Lutheran, Anglican) to purely memorial (many Baptist and Anabaptist traditions). Protestants emphasize direct access to God without priestly mediation, vernacular worship and Bible reading, and generally reject the doctrines of purgatory, papal supremacy, and the intercession of saints as practiced in Catholicism.

Major traditions

  • Lutheranism – following Martin Luther; strong in Germany and Scandinavia.
  • Reformed (Calvinist) – following Zwingli and Calvin; includes Presbyterian and Congregational churches.
  • Anglicanism – the Church of England and the worldwide Anglican Communion.
  • Anabaptism – Mennonites, Amish, and Hutterites, stressing believer's baptism and nonviolence.
  • Baptists – congregational churches practicing believer's baptism by immersion.
  • Methodism – arising from John Wesley's 18th-century revival.
  • Pentecostalism – the fastest-growing movement since the early 20th century, emphasizing the gifts of the Holy Spirit.
  • Adventism – including Seventh-day Adventists, emphasizing the imminent Second Coming.

History

Protestantism emerged from the Reformation and spread through Northern and Western Europe and, via colonization and missions, to the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Oceania. Revivals such as the First and Second Great Awakenings shaped Anglo-American Protestantism, and the 20th century saw the rise of evangelicalism, the ecumenical movement, and explosive growth of Pentecostal and independent churches in the Global South. The World Council of Churches (founded 1948) counts many Protestant and Orthodox churches as members.
