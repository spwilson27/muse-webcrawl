Battle of Narva (1944)

The Narva front in 1944, where German and Estonian units held the river line for six months.

Date2 February – 10 August 1944
LocationNarva, Estonia
ResultGerman defensive success; eventual Soviet breakthrough elsewhere
BelligerentsNazi Germany vs. Soviet Union

The Battle of Narva was a military campaign fought between February and August 1944 on the Eastern Front of World War II, between the German Army Detachment "Narwa" and the Soviet Leningrad Front for the strategically important Narva Isthmus in north-eastern Estonia.

Background

After breaking the Siege of Leningrad in January 1944, Soviet forces pursued the German 18th Army back to the Panther Line along the Narva River. Stalin wanted Estonia seized quickly to secure the Baltic flank and threaten Finland and East Prussia. The German defence under Johannes Frießner rested on the river line and the Tannenberg Line in the Sinimäed Hills west of Narva.

The fighting

From February onward, the Leningrad Front under Leonid Govorov launched repeated assaults across the river, establishing and losing bridgeheads south and north of the city. The defenders — including III SS Panzer Corps with the SS divisions Nordland and Nederland, and thousands of Estonian volunteers motivated by hopes of restoring independence — repelled attack after attack. In late July a massive Soviet offensive finally forced the Germans from the Narva river line back to the Tannenberg Line, where the front stabilised amid ferocious fighting for the Sinimäed Hills, remembered in Estonia as the "Hills of Hell".

Outcome

The battle delayed the Soviet advance into the Baltic states by more than six months and cost several hundred thousand casualties on both sides combined. The defence collapsed only in September 1944, when Germany evacuated Estonia after Finland left the war, and Soviet forces entered Tallinn on 22 September. Narva itself was almost completely destroyed in the fighting and subsequent bombing.
