PlayStation VR, known by its codename Project Morpheus during development, is a virtual reality headset developed by Sony Interactive Entertainment and manufactured by Sony. It was released on 13 October 2016 for the PlayStation 4 console and is also compatible with the PlayStation 5 via an adaptor. By December 2019 the system had sold more than five million units, making it one of the best-selling virtual reality headsets of its generation.

Hardware

The headset features a 5.7-inch OLED display with a resolution of 1920 × 1080 (960 × 1080 per eye), a refresh rate of up to 120 Hz, and a field of view of approximately 100 degrees. Nine blue LEDs on the headset are tracked by the PlayStation Camera to determine the wearer's head position. A separate processor unit connects the headset to the console and provides "social screen" output so bystanders can watch on a television. Games can use the DualShock 4 controller, PlayStation Move motion controllers, or the PlayStation VR Aim Controller. A revised model (CUH-ZVR2) in 2017 added integrated stereo headphones, thinner cables, and HDR pass-through.

Software

The library includes Astro Bot Rescue Mission, Resident Evil 7: Biohazard, Beat Saber, Blood & Truth, Moss, and VR modes for games such as Gran Turismo Sport and No Man's Sky. The headset also supports a cinematic mode for watching standard games and video on a virtual screen.

Reception and successor

Reviewers praised the headset's comfort, price relative to PC-based rivals such as the Oculus Rift and HTC Vive, and Sony's game library, while criticising the camera-based tracking and lower display resolution. A successor, PlayStation VR2, was released for PlayStation 5 in February 2023 with inside-out tracking, eye tracking, and 4K HDR displays; PS VR2 is not natively backward compatible with PS VR games.
