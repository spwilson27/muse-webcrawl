1864 United States presidential election

1864 presidential election
Date: 8 November 1864
Winner: Abraham Lincoln / Andrew Johnson (National Union)
Electoral vote: Lincoln 212, McClellan 21
Popular vote: Lincoln 55.0%, McClellan 45.0%
Opponent: George B. McClellan (Democratic)

The 1864 United States presidential election was the 20th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, 8 November 1864, in the midst of the American Civil War. Incumbent President Abraham Lincoln, running as the candidate of the National Union Party, defeated Democratic nominee George B. McClellan, his former top general.

Campaign

Lincoln feared defeat for much of 1864 as the war dragged on with heavy casualties. The Democratic platform called for a negotiated peace, which McClellan personally repudiated. Union victories at Mobile Bay, Atlanta and in the Shenandoah Valley in the autumn transformed the political outlook. Soldiers voted overwhelmingly for Lincoln.

Results and significance

Lincoln won 212 electoral votes to McClellan's 21 and 55 percent of the popular vote, carrying every participating state except Kentucky, Delaware and New Jersey. The election, held only in the Union states, was a striking demonstration of democratic continuity during civil war; Lincoln's victory ensured the war would be prosecuted to complete Union victory and the abolition of slavery.
