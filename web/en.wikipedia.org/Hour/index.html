Hour

An hour (symbol: h; also abbreviated hr) is a unit of time, conventionally reckoned as 1/24 of a day and scientifically reckoned between 3,599 and 3,601 seconds, depending on the speed of the Earth's rotation. There are 60 minutes in an hour, and 24 hours in a day.

The hour is a unit of time equal to 60 minutes.

The hour was initially established in the ancient Near East as a variable measure of 1/12 of the night or daytime. Such seasonal hours, also known as temporal hours, varied by season and latitude. Equal hours, or equinoctial hours, were defined as 1/24 of the apparent solar day, the time from one noon to the next, or one midnight to the next.

History

The ancient Egyptians were among the first to divide the day into smaller parts, using sundials and water clocks. The division of the day into twelve hours of day and twelve hours of night can be traced to Mesopotamia, where the sexagesimal system of counting with base 60 influenced the subsequent division of hours into 60 minutes and minutes into 60 seconds.

The mechanical clock, first appearing in medieval Europe, helped standardise the hour as a fixed length of time independent of the season. Until then, hours in summer could be considerably longer than hours in winter at northern latitudes.

Counting hours

Modern timekeeping divides the day into 24 hours. The 12-hour clock divides the day into two periods, ante meridiem (a.m.) and post meridiem (p.m.), each of twelve hours, while the 24-hour clock runs from midnight (0:00) to the following midnight (24:00). The rush hour, happy hour, and the canonical hours of prayer are cultural uses of the term.
