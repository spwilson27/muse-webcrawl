Lias Group

The Lias Group is a lithostratigraphic unit (a sequence of rock strata) found in a large area of western Europe, including the British Isles, the North Sea and northern Germany. It consists of marine mudstones, shales, limestones and siltstones deposited during the Early Jurassic, roughly 201 to 174 million years ago.

Distribution and subdivisions

In England the Lias crops out from Yorkshire through the Midlands to the Dorset coast at Lyme Regis and Charmouth, where its fossil-rich cliffs are famous. Traditional subdivisions into Lower, Middle and Upper Lias correspond broadly to formations including the Blue Lias, Charmouth Mudstone, Dyrham, Marlstone Rock, Whitby Mudstone and Bridport Sands.

Fossils

The Lias is renowned for marine fossils — ammonites, belemnites, ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs — many first collected by Mary Anning and others on the Dorset coast. The name "Lias" derives from a quarrymen's pronunciation of "layers".
