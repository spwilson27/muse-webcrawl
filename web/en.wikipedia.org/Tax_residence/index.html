Tax residence

Tax residence determines which jurisdiction may tax a person's worldwide income.

Tax residence is the concept that determines, for tax purposes, the jurisdiction to which a person or company is connected, and hence which country (or countries) may tax their income. Most jurisdictions tax their residents on worldwide income, while taxing non-residents only on income sourced within the jurisdiction.

Individuals

Countries use a variety of tests to determine individual tax residence. Common criteria include physical presence for a threshold number of days (often 183 days in a tax year), having a permanent home in the country, or having one's centre of vital interests there. The United States taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of residence, one of very few countries to do so. Some individuals may be resident in two jurisdictions at once, or in none (so-called "tax nomads").

Companies

Corporate tax residence is typically determined by the place of incorporation, the place of central management and control, or both. The location of a company's tax residence affects its exposure to controlled foreign corporation rules and its access to double taxation treaties.

Double taxation and tie-breakers

Where two countries both claim a person as resident, double taxation agreements apply tie-breaker rules — based on permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode, and nationality — to assign residence to a single jurisdiction for treaty purposes. Residence is commonly evidenced by a tax residence certificate.
