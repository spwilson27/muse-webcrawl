Vince Lombardi Trophy

The Vince Lombardi Trophy is the trophy awarded each year to the winning team of the National Football League's championship game, the Super Bowl. The trophy is named in honor of NFL coach Vince Lombardi, who led the Green Bay Packers to victories in the first two Super Bowls.

Unlike most major sports trophies, a brand-new Lombardi Trophy is produced each year, and the winning team keeps it permanently. It is made by Tiffany & Co. from sterling silver, stands 22 inches (56 cm) tall, weighs about 7 pounds (3.2 kg), and depicts a regulation-size football in kicking position on a tapered base. The design was sketched by Tiffany vice president Oscar Riedener in 1966, reportedly on a cocktail napkin during a meeting with NFL commissioner Pete Rozelle.

History

The trophy was first awarded at Super Bowl I in 1967, when it was known as the "World Professional Football Championship Trophy." It was renamed in 1970 following Lombardi's death. After each Super Bowl, the trophy is engraved with the names of the competing teams and the score, and it is presented to the winning team's owner in a post-game ceremony.
