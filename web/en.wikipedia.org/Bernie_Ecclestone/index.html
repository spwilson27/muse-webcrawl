Bernard Charles Ecclestone (born 28 October 1930) is a British business magnate. He is the former chief executive of the Formula One Group, which manages Formula One motor racing and controls the commercial rights to the sport. As such, he was commonly described in journalism as the "F1 supremo".

Early life

Ecclestone was born in Bungay, Suffolk, the son of a fisherman. He grew up in Bexleyheath, Kent, and left school at 16 to work at a local gasworks. After the Second World War, he began trading in spare parts for motorcycles and later formed a motorcycle dealership, eventually becoming one of the largest dealers in Britain.

Racing career

Ecclestone began racing cars in 1949 in the 500cc Formula 3 series. A series of accidents led him to retire from driving in 1951. He later managed drivers including Stuart Lewis-Evans and Jochen Rindt, and in 1972 he bought the Brabham team, which he ran for 15 years, winning two drivers' championships with Nelson Piquet in 1981 and 1983.

Formula One commercial control

As a member of the Formula One Constructors Association (FOCA), Ecclestone became central to the commercial organisation of the sport during the FISA–FOCA war of the early 1980s. He negotiated the television and commercial rights that transformed Formula One into a global business worth billions. In January 2017, following the takeover of the Formula One Group by Liberty Media, Ecclestone was replaced as chief executive by Chase Carey and moved to the role of chairman emeritus.

Controversies and legal matters

Ecclestone's career has included numerous controversies, including a 1997 political donation scandal in Britain, remarks in interviews that drew widespread criticism, and a 2014 bribery trial in Germany that ended with a settlement payment of US$100 million without a conviction. In 2023 he pleaded guilty in London to fraud by false representation in connection with undisclosed overseas assets, receiving a suspended sentence and agreeing to pay more than £650 million in tax, interest and penalties.

Personal life

Ecclestone has been married three times and has four children. His daughter Tamara and Petra Ecclestone are well-known socialites. He has collected art and classic cars, and for many years was among the wealthiest people in Britain, though his fortune declined after substantial divorce and tax settlements.
