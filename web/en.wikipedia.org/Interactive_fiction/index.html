Interactive fiction

Interactive fiction (IF) is software simulating environments in which players use text commands to control characters and influence the environment. Works in this form can be understood as literary narratives or as video games; the archetypal example is the text adventure, in which the player types commands such as "take key" and the program responds with prose.

History

The genre began with Colossal Cave Adventure (1976) and was commercialised by Infocom, whose titles such as Zork (1980) and The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (1984) defined parser-based IF. After the commercial market faded, a hobbyist community developed authoring systems including Inform, TADS and Twine.

Forms

Beyond parser games, interactive fiction includes choice-based works and hypertext fiction in which readers select links. The annual Interactive Fiction Competition, founded in 1995, remains a central showcase for the form.
