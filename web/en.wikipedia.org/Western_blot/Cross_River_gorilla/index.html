Cross River gorilla

Cross River gorilla
Scientific name: Gorilla gorilla diehli
Subspecies of: Western gorilla
Range: Nigeria–Cameroon border region (Cross River headwaters)
Population: approx. 200–300 mature individuals
IUCN status: Critically Endangered

The Cross River gorilla (Gorilla gorilla diehli) is a subspecies of the western gorilla found in the forested hills and mountains of the Cross River region on the border between Nigeria and Cameroon. It is the rarest of the gorillas and among the most endangered primates in the world.

Description and habitat

Cross River gorillas differ from the more numerous western lowland gorilla in skull and tooth proportions and in aspects of their ecology, living at higher elevations in rugged, fragmented forest. They feed largely on fruit when available, supplementing with leaves, stems and bark, and build new nests each night on the ground or in trees.

Conservation

With perhaps 200–300 mature individuals scattered among a dozen or so subpopulations, the subspecies is listed as Critically Endangered. Threats include hunting for bushmeat and habitat loss to logging, farming and roads. Conservation efforts focus on protected areas such as Cross River National Park in Nigeria and Takamanda National Park in Cameroon, community engagement, and transboundary protection corridors.
