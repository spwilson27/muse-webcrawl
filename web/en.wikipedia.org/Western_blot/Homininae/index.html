Homininae, also known as "African hominids" or "African apes", is a subfamily of Hominidae. It includes two tribes, with their extant as well as extinct species: the Hominini (humans and chimpanzees, plus extinct human ancestors such as australopithecines) and the Gorillini (gorillas). The subfamily groups all hominids that are more closely related to humans than to orangutans; molecular evidence indicates that the gorilla lineage split off first, followed by the divergence of the chimpanzee and human lineages about 6–7 million years ago.

Taxonomy

Until the late 20th century, classification placed humans alone in the family Hominidae and the great apes in Pongidae. Genetic studies showed this was untenable: chimpanzees are more closely related to humans than to gorillas. Modern schemes place orangutans in the subfamily Ponginae and the African apes plus humans in Homininae. Some fossil genera also assigned to the subfamily include Chororapithecus, Nakalipithecus and Ouranopithecus (Gorillini), and Sahelanthropus, Orrorin, Ardipithecus, Australopithecus, Paranthropus and Kenyanthropus (Hominini).

Evolution

The last common ancestor of Homininae lived roughly 8–12 million years ago in Africa (or possibly Eurasia, given fossils such as Ouranopithecus from Greece). Within Hominini, the human branch (subtribe Hominina) became bipedal, while the chimpanzee branch (Panina) remained primarily arboreal and quadrupedal on the ground. The fossil record of the gorilla and chimpanzee lines is sparse because forest environments preserve bones poorly.
