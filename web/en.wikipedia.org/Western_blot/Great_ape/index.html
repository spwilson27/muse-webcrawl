The great apes, or hominids, are the members of the primate family Hominidae, comprising eight living species in four genera: the orangutans of Borneo and Sumatra, the gorillas of central Africa, the chimpanzees and bonobos of Africa, and humans. The term "great ape" distinguishes these large, tailless apes from the "lesser apes", the gibbons of the family Hylobatidae. All non-human great apes are endangered or critically endangered, threatened chiefly by habitat loss, hunting and disease.

Characteristics

Great apes are the largest primates. They have no external tail, an appendix, a distinctive Y-5 pattern of cusps on the lower molars, and relatively large brains with a well-developed cerebral cortex. Their shoulder joints allow the arms to rotate freely, suiting them to suspension and brachiation in trees, although gorillas and humans are predominantly ground-dwelling. Gestation lasts about eight to nine months, and infants depend on their mothers for many years — orangutan offspring may nurse for up to eight years.

Intelligence and behaviour

Great apes display advanced cognition: they use and make tools (chimpanzees fish for termites with sticks and crack nuts with stones; orangutans use leaves as gloves and umbrellas), learn sign languages and symbol systems in captivity, show evidence of self-recognition in mirrors, and maintain complex, long-term social relationships. Chimpanzee communities wage coordinated raids on neighbours, while bonobos are notable for peaceful, matriarchal societies.

Evolution and taxonomy

Molecular evidence indicates that the gibbons split from the great ape line about 18–20 million years ago; orangutans diverged about 14 million years ago, gorillas about 8–9 million years ago, and the human and chimpanzee lineages separated roughly 6–7 million years ago. Humans and chimpanzees share about 98–99% of their DNA. Taxonomy has been repeatedly revised to reflect these relationships: the family Hominidae, once restricted to humans, now includes all the great apes.

Conservation and legal status

All great ape species except humans are listed on Appendix I of CITES, prohibiting international commercial trade. Many countries have banned or restricted research on great apes; Spain's parliament in 2008 endorsed extending legal rights to great apes, and the Great Ape Project advocates a United Nations declaration of their rights to life, liberty and freedom from torture.
