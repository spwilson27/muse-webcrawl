Eastern gorilla

Eastern gorilla
Scientific name: Gorilla beringei
Subspecies: Mountain gorilla (G. b. beringei), eastern lowland gorilla (G. b. graueri)
Range: Eastern DR Congo, Uganda, Rwanda
IUCN status: Critically Endangered
Named after: Captain Robert von Beringe (1902)

The eastern gorilla (Gorilla beringei) is a critically endangered species of gorilla and the largest living primate, endemic to the mountainous and lowland forests of east-central Africa in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda and Rwanda. It comprises two subspecies: the mountain gorilla of the Virunga Mountains and Bwindi, and the eastern lowland (Grauer's) gorilla.

Description

Eastern gorillas are stocky, heavily built apes with long arms, broad hands and, in mountain populations, thick black fur adapted to cold high-altitude nights. Adult males, called silverbacks, develop a saddle of silvery hair and lead cohesive family groups. The species is primarily herbivorous, feeding on leaves, stems, bark and fruit, and knuckle-walks when moving on the ground.

Conservation

Habitat destruction, poaching, disease and decades of regional conflict have driven steep declines, especially in Grauer's gorilla, whose numbers fell by more than three quarters in a generation. Mountain gorilla numbers, by contrast, have slowly recovered to over a thousand individuals through intensive protection and regulated tourism, though the species as a whole remains Critically Endangered.
