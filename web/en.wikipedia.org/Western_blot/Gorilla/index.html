Gorilla

A mature male gorilla, or silverback, named for the saddle of grey hair on his back.

KingdomAnimalia
ClassMammalia
OrderPrimates
FamilyHominidae
GenusGorilla
SpeciesG. beringei (eastern); G. gorilla (western)
Conservation statusEndangered / Critically Endangered (IUCN)

Gorillas are herbivorous, predominantly ground-dwelling great apes that inhabit the tropical forests of equatorial Africa, divided into two species — the eastern gorilla and the western gorilla — and four or five subspecies. They are the largest living primates and the third-closest living relatives of humans after chimpanzees and bonobos, sharing about 98% of their DNA sequence with humans.

Description

Adult males ("silverbacks") weigh 135–220 kg, stand up to about 1.8 m tall, and develop a saddle of silver-grey hair at maturity; females weigh roughly half as much. Gorillas move quadrupedally by knuckle-walking, though they can walk bipedally for short distances. Each individual has a unique nose print.

Behaviour and diet

Gorillas live in stable family groups ("troops") led by a dominant silverback, who makes decisions, mediates conflicts and protects the group. They build nests of vegetation each night. Their diet consists mainly of stems, bamboo shoots, leaves and fruit, supplemented by insects; they rarely need to drink, obtaining moisture from vegetation. Despite their formidable appearance, gorillas are generally gentle and display complex communication including vocalisations, chest beating and gestures.

Conservation

Both species are threatened by habitat destruction, poaching, civil unrest and diseases such as Ebola. The mountain gorilla, studied for decades by Dian Fossey in the Virunga Mountains, has become a flagship of conservation; its numbers have recovered to over 1,000, though it remains Endangered.
