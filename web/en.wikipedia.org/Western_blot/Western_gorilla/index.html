Western gorilla

Western gorilla
Scientific name: Gorilla beringei group — Gorilla gorilla
Family: Hominidae
Range: West-central Africa
Conservation status: Critically Endangered (IUCN)

The western gorilla (Gorilla gorilla) is a great ape found in the forests of west-central Africa, in Angola, Cameroon, the Central African Republic, the Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and Nigeria. It is the most numerous of the two gorilla species and the one most commonly seen in zoos, yet it is classified as Critically Endangered.

Subspecies

Two subspecies are recognised: the western lowland gorilla (G. g. gorilla), by far the more numerous, and the Cross River gorilla (G. g. diehli), restricted to a small area on the Nigeria–Cameroon border and numbering only a few hundred individuals. Western gorillas are slightly smaller and lighter in colour than the eastern gorilla, with brown-grey fur and a reddish-brown crest in adult males.

Behaviour and diet

Western gorillas live in stable family groups led by a dominant silverback male, typically comprising five to ten individuals. They are mainly herbivorous, feeding on fruit, leaves, stems, pith and shoots, supplemented by insects. Groups range over large home ranges, and nests are built each night on the ground or in trees.

Threats

The species has suffered steep declines from hunting for bushmeat, habitat loss through logging, and outbreaks of the Ebola virus, which killed large numbers of gorillas in the early 2000s. Population estimates suggest numbers fell by more than 60 per cent over 20–25 years, and recovery is slow because of the species' low reproductive rate.
