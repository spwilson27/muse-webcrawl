Broadside (printing)

TypePrinted sheet
Other namesBroadsheet, poster
First produced16th century

A broadside is a large sheet of paper printed on one side only. Historically, broadsides were used as posters, announcing events or proclamations, giving political views, or advertising. They were among the most common forms of printed material between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries, particularly in Britain, Ireland, and North America, and are often associated with ballads, proclamations, and advertisements.

History

Broadsides emerged shortly after the invention of movable type printing in Europe. Because they required only a single sheet and one pass through the press, they were cheap to produce and could be distributed quickly. Governments used them for official proclamations, while printers sold them for a penny or less in the streets. The famous "broadside ballads" combined song lyrics with woodcut illustrations and formed a staple of popular culture.

Uses

Broadsides served many purposes: announcing executions and trials, advertising goods and entertainments, publishing election addresses, and spreading religious or political propaganda. During the American Revolution, broadsides such as Thomas Paine's works and the Dunlap broadside of the Declaration of Independence played a key role in public communication. The Dunlap broadside, printed on the night of 4 July 1776, was the first printed version of the Declaration.

Modern use

Today the term survives in poetry publishing, where a "broadside" is a single-sheet printing of a poem, often produced as a limited edition artwork. Libraries and archives preserve historical broadsides as important primary sources for social and political history.
