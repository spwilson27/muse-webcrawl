Mirza Asadullah Baig Khan (1797–1869), known by his pen name Ghalib and earlier Asad, was an Indian poet of Urdu and Persian ghazals during the final years of the Mughal Empire. He is widely regarded as the most celebrated and influential poet of the Urdu language.

Life

Born in Agra to a family of Turkic military ancestry, Ghalib moved to Delhi as a youth and married into an aristocratic family. He lived on a modest Mughal court pension, struggled financially for much of his life, and witnessed the turmoil of the Indian Rebellion of 1857 and the destruction of old Delhi, which he chronicled in his Persian account Dastanbuy.

Poetry

Ghalib's Urdu Divan is compact but famed for its philosophical depth, wit, ambiguity and metaphysical themes — love, wine, loss, and the mysteries of existence — expressed with unmatched linguistic sophistication. His Persian poetry, which he valued more highly, was larger in volume, but it is the Urdu ghazals and his lively, informal Urdu letters that secured his fame across South Asia. His haveli in Ballimaran, Delhi, is now a museum.
