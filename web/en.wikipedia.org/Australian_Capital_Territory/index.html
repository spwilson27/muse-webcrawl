The Australian Capital Territory (ACT) is a landlocked federal territory of Australia containing the national capital, Canberra, and some surrounding townships. Located entirely within the state of New South Wales, it is Australia's smallest self-governing internal territory by both area and population.

History

The territory was transferred from New South Wales to the Commonwealth on 1 January 1911 under the Seat of Government Acceptance Act 1909, two years before the capital city was formally established in 1913. The site was chosen as a compromise between the rival cities of Sydney and Melbourne. Jervis Bay Territory was later surrendered so the capital would have access to the sea.

Geography

The ACT covers 2,358 square kilometres in the southeast of Australia. About half of the territory is covered by Namadgi National Park. The highest point is Bimberi Peak at 1,912 metres. The Murrumbidgee River flows through the territory, and Lake Burley Griffin, an artificial lake, lies at the centre of Canberra.

Government

The ACT has been self-governing since 1988, with a Legislative Assembly of 25 members elected from five multi-member electorates. Unlike the states, however, the Commonwealth retains the power to override territory legislation. The ACT is represented in the federal Parliament by two members of the House of Representatives and two senators.

Demographics

Almost all of the ACT's population lives in Canberra. The territory has the highest median income, the highest level of educational attainment, and one of the youngest populations of any Australian state or territory, reflecting the concentration of public servants, students and academics.
