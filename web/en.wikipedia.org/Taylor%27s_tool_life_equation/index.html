Taylor's tool life equation

Taylor's tool life equation is a relationship in machining that predicts how long a cutting tool will last as a function of cutting speed. It was developed by Frederick Winslow Taylor from cutting experiments beginning in the 1880s, published in his 1907 work On the Art of Cutting Metals.

Equation

The basic form is V·Tn = C, where V is the cutting speed, T is the tool life in minutes, n is an exponent depending on the tool and workpiece materials (roughly 0.1 for high-speed steel and 0.2–0.5 for carbides), and C is a constant equal to the cutting speed that gives a tool life of one minute. Extended forms include feed rate and depth of cut. The equation remains a standard tool for selecting economical machining parameters.
