The Ásatrúarfélagið (Icelandic: [ˈauːsaˌtruːarˌfjeːlaɣɪð], "the Ásatrú Fellowship") is an Icelandic religious organisation dedicated to the revival of Norse paganism (Ásatrú, "faith of the Æsir"). Founded in 1972 by the farmer and poet Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson, it became one of the first modern pagan organisations in the world to receive official state recognition, granted by the Icelandic government in 1973.

History

The organisation was founded on the first day of summer in 1972 — a traditional Icelandic holiday — at a meeting in Reykjavík. Its founder, Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson, a sheep farmer from Borgarnes who was also a renowned chanter of traditional rímur poetry, served as its first allsherjargoði (high chieftain / chief goði) until his death in 1993. The fellowship sought legal recognition so that its officiants could perform legally binding marriages and naming ceremonies, and recognition was granted on 3 May 1973 after a much-publicised debate in the Althing.

For its first decades the fellowship remained small, with membership in the low hundreds. From the 1990s onward it grew steadily, and by the 2010s it was among the fastest-growing religious organisations in Iceland, passing one percent of the national population. Growth was partly attributed to Icelanders leaving the state Lutheran church.

Leadership

The head of the organisation bears the title allsherjargoði. Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson was succeeded by Jörmundur Ingi Hansen (1994–2002), Jónína Kristín Berg (2002–2003), and Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson, a musician and film composer, who has held the office since 2003.

Beliefs and practice

The fellowship describes Ásatrú as a pantheistic, nature-oriented faith centred on the Norse gods, land spirits (landvættir) and reverence for ancestors. It has no fixed dogma; members are free to interpret the mythology individually. Central rituals include seasonal blót feasts such as Þorrablót and Jól, as well as weddings (brúðkaup), naming ceremonies and funerals. The organisation has publicly emphasised tolerance and has rejected racism, distancing itself from racialist interpretations of Norse paganism.

The hof

In 2015 the society began construction of a temple (hof) in the Öskjuhlíð area of Reykjavík, on land granted by the city. The building, designed by architect Magnús Jensson, is partly sunk into a hillside with a dome aligned to the sun's path, and was intended as the first purpose-built pagan temple in Iceland in roughly a thousand years.
