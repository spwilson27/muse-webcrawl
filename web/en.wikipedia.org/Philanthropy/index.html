Philanthropy


Philanthropy ranges from individual almsgiving to large endowed foundations.

Philanthropy is a form of altruism that consists of private initiatives for the public good, focusing on quality of life. The word derives from the Greek philanthrōpía, "love of humanity." It is often distinguished from charity: charity typically relieves the immediate symptoms of social problems, whereas philanthropy in the modern sense aims to address their root causes — though in common usage the terms overlap heavily.

History

Organised giving has ancient roots in religious almsgiving — Christian tithes and poor relief, Islamic zakat and the waqf endowment, and analogous institutions elsewhere. Modern, large-scale philanthropy emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with industrial fortunes: Andrew Carnegie argued in "The Gospel of Wealth" (1889) that the rich were obliged to administer surplus wealth for the public benefit, and he and John D. Rockefeller founded enduring foundations — the Carnegie Corporation (1911) and the Rockefeller Foundation (1913) — that professionalised grant-making in education, public health, and science.

Forms and scale

Philanthropy includes donations of money, property, and time (volunteering), both by individuals and through private foundations, community foundations, corporate giving programmes, and donor-advised funds. In the United States, annual charitable giving has recently exceeded $500 billion, with individuals providing the majority and religion, education, and health the largest recipient categories. Global health philanthropy by the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation has made it a significant actor alongside governments and the WHO.

Debates

Philanthropy is debated on several grounds: that tax deductions for donations subsidise the preferences of the wealthy; that large foundations exercise public influence without democratic accountability; and conversely, that private giving funds experimentation and pluralism that governments cannot. The effective altruism movement urges donors to maximise measurable impact per dollar, favouring evidence-based interventions, and initiatives such as The Giving Pledge (2010) have sought public commitments by billionaires to give away most of their wealth.
