Resin identification code

The ASTM International Resin Identification Coding System, often abbreviated as the RIC, is a set of symbols appearing on plastic products that identify the plastic resin out of which the product is made. It was developed in 1988 by the Society of the Plastics Industry (SPI) in the United States; since 2008 it has been administered by ASTM International.

Purpose and confusion with recycling

The RIC was designed to help waste sorters identify resin types, not to indicate that an item is recyclable. Because the numbers appear inside a "chasing arrows" triangle resembling the recycling symbol, consumers widely misread them as a recyclability claim. In practice, only codes 1 and 2 are accepted in most curbside programmes; others are rarely recycled. In 2013 ASTM changed the surrounding graphic from chasing arrows to a solid triangle to reduce the confusion, and several U.S. states have since legislated against misleading use of the symbol; California's SB 343 (2021) restricts the chasing-arrows symbol to materials that are actually recycled in practice.

Similar systems

China uses a more granular system of over 140 codes. The European Union uses its own resin labelling conventions under packaging waste rules.
