Port Adelaide Football Club

The Port Adelaide Football Club, nicknamed the Power in the Australian Football League (AFL) and the Magpies in the South Australian National Football League (SANFL), is a professional Australian rules football club based in Alberton, South Australia. Founded in 1870, it is the oldest professional football club in South Australia and one of the oldest in the world, playing its home games at Alberton Oval and, for AFL matches, Adelaide Oval.

History

Port Adelaide was a powerhouse of the SANFL, winning a record 36 premierships, including six in a row from 1954 to 1959 under coach Fos Williams. The club's black-and-white "prison bar" guernsey became one of the most famous in Australian sport. After a controversial failed bid to join the AFL in 1990 — which prompted the formation of the Adelaide Crows — Port Adelaide was granted the second South Australian AFL licence and entered the national competition in 1997 under the Power name, with a teal, black, and white strip.

AFL era

Coached by Mark Williams, Port Adelaide finished minor premiers in 2002, 2003, and 2004, and in 2004 defeated the Brisbane Lions in the Grand Final to win its first AFL premiership. The club endured a record 119-point Grand Final loss to Geelong in 2007, financial difficulties in the following years, and a revival under coach Ken Hinkley from 2013. Port Adelaide also fields a women's team in the AFL Women's competition, which it joined in 2022. Its fierce rivalry with the Adelaide Crows, contested in the "Showdown," is among the AFL's most intense.
