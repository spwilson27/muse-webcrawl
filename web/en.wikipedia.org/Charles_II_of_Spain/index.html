Charles II (Spanish: Carlos II; 1661–1700), known as the Bewitched (el Hechizado), was King of Spain from 1665 to 1700 and the last ruler of the Spanish Habsburgs. He inherited the throne at the age of three and reigned over a vast empire including Spain, the Spanish Netherlands, much of Italy, and the overseas colonies.

Life and health

Charles was the product of generations of inbreeding within the Habsburg dynasty and suffered lifelong physical and developmental disabilities, including the pronounced Habsburg jaw; he was unable to chew properly, spoke only from about the age of four, and was widely considered incapable of fathering children. His mother, Mariana of Austria, served as regent during his minority, and factions at court competed for influence throughout the reign.

Succession crisis

Charles's childlessness made the Spanish succession the central question of European diplomacy for decades. On his deathbed he willed the entire inheritance to Philip of Anjou, grandson of Louis XIV of France. Charles's death in November 1700 without an heir triggered the War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714), which ended with Philip recognised as Philip V of Spain but Spain's European possessions largely partitioned.
