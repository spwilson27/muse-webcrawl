The dissolution of the Ottoman Empire (1908–1922) was the period in which the government of the Ottoman Empire, long in decline, was overthrown and the empire's territories partitioned among the victorious Allies and new national states. It began with the Young Turk Revolution of 1908 and ended with the emergence of the Republic of Turkey and the abolition of the Ottoman sultanate in 1922.

Background

Through the nineteenth century the empire — the "sick man of Europe" — lost territory in the Balkans and North Africa while reform movements (the Tanzimat and the constitutional eras) struggled to modernise the state. Defeat in the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 stripped the Ottomans of nearly all their remaining European possessions and brought the Committee of Union and Progress to power.

First World War and partition

The empire entered the First World War on the side of the Central Powers in 1914. Despite victories at Gallipoli and Kut, Ottoman forces were defeated in the Arab Revolt and the Sinai and Palestine campaigns. The Armistice of Mudros (30 October 1918) ended hostilities, and the Treaty of Sèvres (1920) envisaged the partition of Anatolia itself. Secret wartime agreements, notably the Sykes–Picot Agreement, divided the Arab provinces into British and French mandates.

Turkish War of Independence and aftermath

The Turkish national movement under Mustafa Kemal Atatürk rejected Sèvres, defeated Greek, Armenian, and French forces, and secured recognition of the new state's borders in the Treaty of Lausanne (1923). The Grand National Assembly abolished the sultanate on 1 November 1922, ending more than six centuries of Ottoman rule; the Republic of Turkey was proclaimed on 29 October 1923, and the caliphate was abolished the following year.
