Sydney Opera House

The Sydney Opera House is a multi-venue performing arts centre at Sydney Harbour in Sydney, New South Wales, Australia. Its distinctive roof of white, sail-like shells makes it one of the most famous and recognisable buildings of the 20th century and a symbol of Australia.

Design and construction

Danish architect Jørn Utzon won the 1957 international competition with a visionary design of soaring shells. Engineering the shells defeated conventional methods until Utzon's "spherical solution" derived every shell from the geometry of a single sphere, allowing precast ribs of identical curvature. Cost overruns and political conflict drove Utzon to resign in 1966; an Australian team led by Peter Hall completed the interiors. The building cost A$102 million against an original estimate of A$7 million, funded largely by a state lottery. Queen Elizabeth II opened it in 1973.

Venues and performances

The complex houses the Concert Hall, the Joan Sutherland Theatre (opera), drama and chamber venues, and hosts Opera Australia, the Sydney Symphony Orchestra and many festivals. Its sails are covered with over one million glossy white and matte cream tiles made in Sweden.

Recognition

Utzon received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2003. In 2007 the Opera House became one of the youngest UNESCO World Heritage Sites, and one of few cultural sites listed during its architect's lifetime.
