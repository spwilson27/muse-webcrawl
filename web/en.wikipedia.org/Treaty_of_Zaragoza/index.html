Treaty of Zaragoza

Signed22 April 1529
LocationZaragoza, Spain
PartiesJohn III of Portugal; Charles I of Spain

The Treaty of Zaragoza, also called the Capitulation of Zaragoza, was a peace treaty between Castile and Portugal signed on 22 April 1529 by King John III of Portugal and the Spanish Emperor Charles V in the Aragonese city of Zaragoza. The treaty defined the areas of Castilian and Portuguese influence in Asia, resolving the so-called "Moluccas issue" of which hemisphere the valuable spice islands belonged to.

Background

The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) had divided the non-European world between Spain and Portugal along a meridian in the Atlantic, but it did not define the line's continuation on the other side of the globe. After Magellan's expedition reached the Moluccas sailing west for Spain, both crowns claimed the islands. A joint commission at Badajoz–Elvas in 1524 failed to fix the location of the meridian astronomically.

Terms

Spain sold its claimed rights to the Moluccas to Portugal for 350,000 gold ducats. The treaty set a line of demarcation 297.5 leagues (about 17 degrees) east of the Moluccas, near the 144th meridian east; territories west of the line were Portuguese and to the east Spanish. Spain retained a right to recover the islands by repaying the sum if it later proved the islands lay in its hemisphere.

Consequences

The settlement allowed Portugal to consolidate its position in the East Indies while Spain concentrated on the Pacific and the Americas; Spain later colonised the Philippines, which lay west of the line, on the argument that they were not covered by the Moluccas arrangement.
