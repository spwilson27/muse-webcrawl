Affine geometry

Affine geometry is the study of geometric properties that remain unchanged under affine transformations — combinations of linear transformations and translations. It retains the notions of parallelism and ratios of lengths along a line, but not angles or absolute distances.

History

The subject grew out of Felix Klein's Erlangen program (1872), which classified geometries by their transformation groups; affine geometry sits between Euclidean geometry and the more general projective geometry.

Concepts

Central objects include affine spaces, in which points and vectors are distinct, and affine transformations, which map lines to lines and preserve parallelism and midpoints. Affine geometry underlies affine coordinates and much of computer graphics.
