Tulip

KingdomPlantae
FamilyLiliaceae
GenusTulipa

Tulips (Tulipa) are spring-blooming perennial herbaceous bulbiferous geophytes in the lily family. Native to a band from Southern Europe to Central Asia, they were widely cultivated in the Ottoman Empire and introduced to Europe in the 16th century, where they became the subject of the famous Dutch "tulip mania" of the 1630s.

Description

Tulip plants typically grow 10–70 cm tall from bulbs and produce cup- or star-shaped flowers with six similar tepals in almost every colour, often with contrasting markings. Leaves are strap-shaped and waxy.

Cultivation

The Netherlands is the world's largest producer of tulip bulbs and cut flowers. Thousands of cultivars are grown, grouped by flowering time and form, including Single Early, Triumph, Darwin Hybrid, and Parrot tulips.

History

During tulip mania (1634–1637), prices for rare "broken" bulbs—striped by a mosaic virus—reached extraordinary levels before collapsing, an episode often cited as an early speculative bubble.
