Apollo and Daphne (Bernini)

Apollo and Daphne is a life-sized marble sculpture by the Italian artist Gian Lorenzo Bernini, created between 1622 and 1625. Housed in the Galleria Borghese in Rome, it depicts the climax of the myth of Apollo and Daphne from Ovid's Metamorphoses: the moment the nymph Daphne, fleeing the god Apollo, is transformed into a laurel tree.

The myth

Struck by Cupid's golden arrow, Apollo pursued the nymph Daphne, who had been struck by a leaden arrow causing her to flee love. As Apollo caught her, Daphne prayed to her father, the river god Peneus, and was changed into a laurel. Bernini captures the instant of transformation: bark encases her torso, her fingers sprout leaves, and her toes take root even as Apollo's hand touches her.

Commission and reception

The work was commissioned by Cardinal Scipione Borghese. Although Bernini was only in his early twenties, the sculpture established his reputation as the leading sculptor of Baroque Rome. A moralising couplet by Maffeo Barberini (later Pope Urban VIII) was inscribed on the base, framing the pagan subject as a warning that those who chase fleeting pleasures grasp only leaves and bitter berries.
