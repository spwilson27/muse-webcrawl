Primal cut

A primal cut is a large piece of meat initially separated from the carcass of an animal during butchering. Different countries and cuisines have different primal cuts. During butchering, primal cuts are further divided into smaller subprimals and retail cuts such as steaks, roasts, and chops.

Beef primals

In the United States, beef carcasses are divided into eight primals: chuck, rib, loin (including short loin and sirloin), round, brisket, plate, flank, and shank. The British system uses different divisions and names, such as topside and silverside from the hindquarter.

Other meats

Pork primals in the U.S. include the shoulder (Boston butt), picnic shoulder, loin, ham, belly, and spare ribs. Lamb is typically divided into shoulder, rack, loin, and leg. Primal cuts are often traded wholesale, vacuum-sealed as "boxed beef" in modern meatpacking.
