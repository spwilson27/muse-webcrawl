Estrogens are a class of steroid hormones that are the primary female sex hormones, responsible for the development and regulation of the female reproductive system and secondary sex characteristics. There are four major endogenous estrogens: estrone (E1), estradiol (E2), estriol (E3) and estetrol (E4). Estradiol is the most potent and the predominant estrogen during the reproductive years; estrone predominates after menopause and estriol during pregnancy.

Biosynthesis

Estrogens are synthesised from cholesterol via androgen precursors. In the ovaries, the enzyme aromatase converts testosterone and androstenedione into estradiol and estrone respectively, mainly in granulosa cells under the influence of follicle-stimulating hormone. Aromatase in adipose tissue, skin, bone and the brain provides an important source of estrogen after menopause and in males, in whom small amounts are produced by the testes.

Function

Estrogens promote the development of the uterus, fallopian tubes, vagina and breasts at puberty, regulate the menstrual cycle and endometrial growth, and maintain bone density by restraining osteoclast activity. They also influence fat distribution, skin, the cardiovascular system, and cognition and mood. In males, estrogen is essential for bone maturation, libido and spermatogenesis.

Medical uses

Synthetic and natural estrogens are used in combined oral contraceptives, in menopausal hormone therapy to relieve vasomotor symptoms, and in feminising hormone therapy. Selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs) such as tamoxifen and raloxifene exploit tissue-specific agonist and antagonist actions. Excess estrogen exposure is associated with increased risk of endometrial and breast cancers, while deficiency causes bone loss and infertility.
