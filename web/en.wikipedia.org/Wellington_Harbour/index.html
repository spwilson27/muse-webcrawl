Wellington Harbour

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Wellington Harbour (Māori: Te Whanganui-a-Tara) is a large natural harbour at the southern tip of New Zealand's North Island, on Cook Strait. The city of Wellington, the country's capital, is situated on its western shoreline, with the cities of Lower Hutt and Upper Hutt to the north-east at the head of the Hutt Valley. The harbour has an area of about 85 square kilometres and a single entrance, between Pencarrow Head and Palmer Head, through which ferries cross to the South Island.

Geography and geology

The harbour is a drowned river valley, flooded by rising seas after the last ice age, and lies along active faults including the Wellington Fault; earthquakes in 1460 and 1855 uplifted parts of the shoreline, the latter raising land that now carries the city's central business district and airport. Its islands include Matiu / Somes Island, used historically as a quarantine station and now a scientific reserve.

History

Māori tradition names the harbour for Tara, son of the explorer Whatonga. European settlement began in 1840 when the New Zealand Company established Wellington at Petone and then Thorndon. The harbour port, now CentrePort, handles container, bulk and cruise traffic, and inter-island ferries connect Wellington with Picton. In 1968 the ferry Wahine sank at the harbour entrance during a storm with the loss of 53 lives.

Recreation and wildlife

The harbour is used for sailing, kayaking, rowing and swimming, and is home to little penguins, fur seals and visiting orcas and dolphins.
