Dunhuang

The Mogao Caves, Dunhuang

Dunhuang (Chinese: 敦煌) is a county-level city in northwestern Gansu Province, China. Situated at a crossroads of the ancient Silk Road, it was one of the great trading and garrison towns between China and Central Asia, and is famed worldwide for the nearby Mogao Caves.

History

Dunhuang, whose name means "Blazing Beacon", was established as a Han dynasty commandery in 111 BCE, guarding the western end of the Hexi Corridor where the Silk Road splits into northern and southern routes around the Taklamakan Desert. It flourished as a centre of trade, Buddhism and art for over a thousand years, passing through periods of Chinese, Tibetan, Tangut and Mongol rule.

Mogao Caves

The Mogao Caves (the "Caves of the Thousand Buddhas"), carved into a cliff southeast of the city from the 4th to the 14th centuries, contain some of the finest Buddhist art in the world: hundreds of painted and sculpted grottoes. In 1900 the monk Wang Yuanlu discovered the sealed Library Cave containing tens of thousands of manuscripts — including the Diamond Sutra of 868 CE, the world's oldest dated printed book — many of which were acquired by Aurel Stein and other explorers and are now held in London, Beijing and elsewhere.

Geography and sights

The city is an oasis on the edge of the Gobi Desert. Nearby attractions include the Singing Sand Dunes (Mingsha Shan) and Crescent Lake (Yueyaquan), the Yumen Pass and Yangguan passes of the Han frontier, and the Yardang landforms to the west.
