Nobel Prize in Physics

The Nobel Prize in Physics is an annual award given by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences for outstanding contributions to physics. It is one of the five Nobel Prizes established by Alfred Nobel's will in 1895 and first awarded in 1901.

Nobel Prize in Physics

First awarded: 1901, to Wilhelm Röntgen
Presented by: Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences

History

The first prize went to Wilhelm Röntgen for discovering X-rays — the radiation behind X-ray crystallography and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy. Other laureates include Marie and Pierre Curie, Albert Einstein (for the photoelectric effect), and the Braggs for crystal structure analysis. Prizes are presented in Stockholm on 10 December, the anniversary of Nobel's death.

Selection

Nominations are invited from qualified nominators; the Nobel Committee for Physics screens candidates and the Academy votes. A maximum of three laureates may share a prize.
