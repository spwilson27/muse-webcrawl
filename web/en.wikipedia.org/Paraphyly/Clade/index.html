Clade

A clade is a group of organisms that includes a common ancestor and all of its descendants. Clades are the fundamental units of cladistics, the approach to biological classification based on evolutionary relationships. A group consisting of a common ancestor and all its descendants is called monophyletic.

Definition and usage

The term was coined by Julian Huxley in 1957, from the Greek klados, "branch". In a phylogenetic tree, a clade corresponds to a branch that can be cut from the tree at a single point, separating it together with everything that grows from it. Clades are nested hierarchically: a large clade contains many smaller ones.

Naming

Traditional Linnaean ranks may or may not correspond to clades. Systems of phylogenetic nomenclature, such as the PhyloCode, define taxon names explicitly in terms of clades, using node-based, stem-based, or apomorphy-based definitions.

Clades and grades

Clades contrast with evolutionary "grades" — groups united by a similar level of organization, such as "reptiles" excluding birds — which are paraphyletic and not clades.
