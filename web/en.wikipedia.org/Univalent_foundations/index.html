Univalent foundations

Univalent foundations are an approach to the foundations of mathematics in which mathematical structures are built from homotopy types. The program is based on Martin-Löf type theory and was initiated by Vladimir Voevodsky, who introduced the univalence axiom and developed the idea that types in type theory can be interpreted as spaces (homotopy types) and propositions as types in a homotopy-theoretic sense.

The univalence axiom states, informally, that equivalent types are identical — that identity is equivalent to equivalence. This principle, combined with higher inductive types, forms the core of homotopy type theory (HoTT), a branch of mathematics that combines ideas from homotopy theory, higher category theory, and mathematical logic.

Development

Voevodsky began developing the framework in the mid-2000s, motivated in part by a desire for computer-verified foundations after an error was found in one of his published proofs. A special year on univalent foundations was held at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton in 2012–2013, producing the collaboratively written book Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics (2013).

Goals

Univalent foundations aim to provide a foundation for mathematics that is well suited to formalization in proof assistants such as Coq and Lean, in which isomorphic structures can be treated as literally equal, allowing theorems to be transported along equivalences automatically.
