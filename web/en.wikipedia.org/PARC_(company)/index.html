PARC (Palo Alto Research Center), formerly Xerox PARC, is a research and development company in Palo Alto, California. Founded in 1970 as a division of Xerox, it is famous for inventing or incubating many elements of modern computing, including the laser printer, the graphical user interface (GUI), the computer mouse as refined there, the Alto personal computer, Ethernet networking, and object-oriented programming concepts embodied in the Smalltalk language.

Xerox established the centre, at the suggestion of its chief scientist Jack Goldman, to secure a foothold in the future of computing. Located far from Xerox headquarters in Rochester, New York, PARC recruited leading researchers — including many from Douglas Engelbart's Augmentation Research Center and the ARPA community — and gave them unusual freedom. Its work in the 1970s defined the modern personal computer, but Xerox was famously slow to commercialise most of it; instead, companies such as Apple and Microsoft, whose staff visited PARC, brought GUI computing to mass markets, while 3Com (Ethernet) and Adobe (founded by PARC alumni) grew directly out of its technology.

Later years

In 2002 PARC was incorporated as a wholly owned Xerox subsidiary, Palo Alto Research Center Incorporated, and broadened its work to outside clients in fields such as clean technology, printed electronics, sensing and artificial intelligence. In 2023 Xerox donated PARC to SRI International, combining it with another storied Silicon Valley research institution.

Key inventions and contributions

  • Xerox Alto (1973) — among the first personal computers, with a bitmapped display, mouse and GUI
  • Ethernet (1973–74), developed by Robert Metcalfe and David Boggs
  • Laser printing, pioneered by Gary Starkweather
  • Smalltalk and object-oriented programming research led by Alan Kay's Learning Research Group
  • Bravo and Gypsy, early WYSIWYG text editors; Interpress page description language
  • Ubiquitous computing research in the late 1980s under Mark Weiser
