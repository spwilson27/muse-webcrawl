Steve Ditko

Ditko's surreal other-dimensional artwork on Doctor Strange defined the character's visual identity.

Stephen J. Ditko (November 2, 1927 – c. June 27, 2018) was an American comics artist best known as the co-creator, with Stan Lee, of the Marvel superheroes Spider-Man and Doctor Strange. His distinctive, surreal style and his uncompromising personal philosophy — he was a committed follower of Ayn Rand's Objectivism — made him one of the most influential and enigmatic figures in the medium.

Career

Ditko studied under Batman artist Jerry Robinson at the Cartoonists and Illustrators School in New York and worked through the 1950s for Charlton Comics and others. At Marvel in the early 1960s he plotted and drew the first 38 issues of The Amazing Spider-Man, designing the hero's costume, web-shooters and much of his supporting cast and rogues' gallery, and created Doctor Strange in Strange Tales. He left Marvel in 1966 after a breakdown in relations with Lee.

Later work

At Charlton he created the Blue Beetle and the Question, and at DC Comics he created the Creeper, Hawk and Dove, and Shade, the Changing Man. From the late 1960s he increasingly produced independent, explicitly Objectivist works such as Mr. A, alongside mainstream assignments, working steadily into his eighties while refusing interviews and publicity.

Legacy

Ditko was inducted into the Jack Kirby Hall of Fame in 1990 and the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame in 1994. The question of creative credit and compensation for Spider-Man, one of the world's most valuable fictional characters, kept his name in public debate long after his withdrawal from public life; he was found dead in his New York apartment on June 29, 2018, aged 90.
