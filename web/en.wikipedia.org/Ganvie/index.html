Ganvie is a lake village in Benin, West Africa, lying in Lake Nokoué, north of Cotonou. Built almost entirely on stilts above the water, it is often called the "Venice of Africa" and is commonly described as the largest lake village on the continent, with a population estimated at 20,000 to 30,000 people, principally of the Tofinu ethnic group.

History

Ganvie was founded in the sixteenth or seventeenth century by the Tofinu people. According to tradition, the Tofinu fled to the lake to escape Fon warriors from the kingdom of Dahomey, who were raiding villages for captives to sell into the slave trade. Fon religious prohibitions forbade their warriors from attacking people on water, making the shallow lake a natural refuge. The name Ganvie is often translated as "we are saved" or "the community that found peace."

Life on the lake

All transport in Ganvie is by wooden canoe (pirogue). Houses, shops, schools, churches, a mosque and a hotel stand on stilts driven into the lake bed; a small area of artificial land supports a market and cemetery. Fishing is the economic mainstay: fishermen build underwater enclosures of branches (acadja) that attract and trap fish. Tourism has also become an important source of income, with visitors arriving by motorboat from the jetty at Abomey-Calavi.

Heritage status

Ganvie was added to the UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List in 1996 in the cultural category, in recognition of its unique lacustrine architecture and living traditions.
