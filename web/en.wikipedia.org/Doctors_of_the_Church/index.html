Doctors of the Church

The Doctors of the Church are those saints whom the Catholic Church has formally recognised with the title Doctor of the Church for their eminent learning and sanctity, whose teaching is commended to the whole Church. This article lists them in the order of their proclamation.

The first Doctors

Pope Boniface VIII in 1298 recognised the four great Latin Doctors of the early Church:

Subsequent proclamations

Later Doctors include Thomas Aquinas and Bonaventure (1567–68), Anselm, Bernard of Clairvaux, Albertus Magnus, Anthony of Padua, John of the Cross, Robert Bellarmine, and Alphonsus Liguori. The four women Doctors are Catherine of Siena and Teresa of Ávila (1970), Thérèse of Lisieux (1997), and Hildegard of Bingen (2012). Recent proclamations include Gregory of Narek (2015), Irenaeus of Lyons (2022), and John Henry Newman (2025), bringing the total to 38.

Usage in other traditions

The Eastern Orthodox Church does not use the title formally but honours the "Three Holy Hierarchs" — Basil the Great, Gregory of Nazianzus, and John Chrysostom. Some Anglican and Lutheran calendars commemorate "Teachers of the Faith" in a comparable way.
