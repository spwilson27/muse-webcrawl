Twelve Labours

HeroHeracles (Hercules)
Imposed byEurystheus, king of Mycenae
SourceGreek mythology; Apollodorus, Bibliotheca

The Twelve Labours are a series of tasks performed by the Greek hero Heracles (Hercules in Roman tradition) as penance after, in a fit of madness sent by Hera, he killed his wife Megara and their children. The Delphic oracle ordered him to serve King Eurystheus of Mycenae for twelve years and perform the labours the king commanded.

The labours

The canonical twelve are: (1) slay the Nemean lion; (2) slay the Lernaean Hydra; (3) capture the Ceryneian Hind; (4) capture the Erymanthian Boar; (5) clean the Augean stables in a day; (6) slay the Stymphalian birds; (7) capture the Cretan Bull; (8) steal the Mares of Diomedes; (9) obtain the girdle of Hippolyta, queen of the Amazons; (10) obtain the cattle of the monster Geryon; (11) steal the apples of the Hesperides; and (12) capture and bring back Cerberus, the hound of Hades.

Notes on the count

Eurystheus originally set ten labours but disallowed two — the Hydra, because Heracles' nephew Iolaus helped, and the Augean stables, because Heracles accepted payment — and imposed two more, giving the famous twelve.

Legacy

The labours were a favourite subject of Greek and Roman art, including the metopes of the Temple of Zeus at Olympia, and remain among the best-known cycles in Western mythology.
