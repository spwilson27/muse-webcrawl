Balanced line

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Signal typeDifferential
Common mediaTwisted pair, star quad
ApplicationsProfessional audio, telephony, Ethernet

A balanced line is a transmission line consisting of two conductors of the same type, each with equal impedances along its length and equal impedances to ground and other circuits. The signal is carried as the difference in voltage between the two conductors (differential signaling).

Noise rejection

Interference induced along the cable appears equally on both conductors as common-mode noise. A differential receiver at the far end subtracts the two signals, cancelling the common-mode component while preserving the desired differential signal. This is why balanced connections, typically with XLR connectors, are standard in professional audio.

Balanced audio

In audio, balanced lines allow long cable runs in electrically noisy environments such as concert venues and studios. Phantom power for condenser microphones is delivered as a common-mode voltage over the same pair.
