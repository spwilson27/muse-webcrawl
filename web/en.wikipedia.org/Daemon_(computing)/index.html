Daemon (computing)

In multitasking computer operating systems, a daemon (pronounced "demon" or "day-mon") is a computer program that runs as a background process, rather than being under the direct control of an interactive user. Traditionally, the process names of daemons end with the letter d, such as sshd, httpd, and syslogd.

Terminology

The term was coined by programmers at MIT's Project MAC in the 1960s, borrowing from Maxwell's demon in physics and from Greek mythology, where a daemon is a background being that works invisibly. Daemons are typically started at boot time and perform system-level tasks: handling network requests, scheduling jobs, monitoring hardware, or logging events. On Unix-like systems, a daemon is usually detached from a controlling terminal; the process of becoming a daemon is sometimes called "daemonizing" and involves forking and calls such as setsid.

Equivalents on other systems

On Microsoft Windows, the equivalent programs are called Windows services. On classic Mac OS and modern Apple systems, similar roles are filled by system extensions and launchd-managed agents and daemons. Examples of well-known daemons include web servers, mail servers, the cron scheduler, and the Secure Shell server.
