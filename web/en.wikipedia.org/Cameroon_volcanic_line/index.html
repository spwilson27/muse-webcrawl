The Cameroon volcanic line is a 1,600 km (1,000 mi) long chain of volcanoes and volcanic highlands extending from the Atlantic Ocean into the interior of Cameroon in West-Central Africa. It includes the islands of the Gulf of Guinea — Pagalu, São Tomé, Príncipe and Bioko — and continues on the mainland through Mount Cameroon, the Cameroon Highlands and the Adamawa Plateau region.

Geology

Unlike many volcanic chains, the Cameroon line shows no simple age progression from one end to the other, and its origin remains debated: hypotheses include a mantle hotspot, reactivated ancient fault zones along an old suture, and edge-driven convection. Volcanism along the line has been active for tens of millions of years and continues today.

Notable features

Mount Cameroon is one of Africa's largest and most active volcanoes. The line is also known for two deadly limnic eruptions: at Lake Monoun in 1984 and Lake Nyos in 1986, where sudden releases of carbon dioxide asphyxiated nearly 1,800 people at Nyos — leading to degassing programmes at both crater lakes.
