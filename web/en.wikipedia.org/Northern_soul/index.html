Northern soul
Stylistic originsAmerican soul, Motown, mod culture
Cultural originsLate 1960s, northern England
Key venuesTwisted Wheel (Manchester), Wigan Casino, Blackpool Mecca

Northern soul is a music and dance movement that emerged in northern England in the late 1960s from the British mod scene. It centred on obscure American soul recordings — particularly uptempo Motown-style singles that had been commercial failures in the United States — played by DJs at all-night club sessions.

History

The term was popularised around 1970 by journalist Dave Godin. Venues such as Manchester's Twisted Wheel, the Golden Torch in Stoke-on-Trent and, most famously, the Wigan Casino (1973–1981) drew thousands of dancers for all-nighters characterised by athletic, acrobatic dancing and a devoted record-collecting culture.

Music and culture

Rarity defined the scene: DJs competed to discover unknown American singles, and some records became so coveted that their labels were covered to hide their identity. Fashion drew on mod roots — wide trousers, bowling shirts and leather-soled shoes suited to the talcum-dusted dancefloors.

Legacy

Northern soul influenced later British club culture and rare-groove scenes, and a loyal following keeps the tradition alive through weekenders and all-nighters in the UK and abroad.
