Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament

Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament, also called Benediction with the Blessed Sacrament or the Rite of Eucharistic Exposition and Benediction, is a devotional ceremony in the Roman Catholic Church (and in some Anglo-Catholic and Lutheran traditions) in which a priest or deacon blesses the congregation with the Eucharist, displayed in a monstrance. The ceremony ordinarily follows a period of Eucharistic adoration.

Rite

Before the benediction, the consecrated host is placed in a monstrance and set upon the altar for adoration, often accompanied by hymns such as O salutaris hostia and incense. Near the end of the service the hymn Tantum ergo is sung while the celebrant, wearing a humeral veil, takes up the monstrance and makes the sign of the cross over the people in silence. The Divine Praises are then traditionally recited, and the host is returned to the tabernacle after the concluding hymn Adoremus in aeternum.

History

The devotion grew out of the medieval practices of Eucharistic exposition and processions associated with the feast of Corpus Christi, instituted in 1264. By the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries Benediction had become a popular evening service, distinct from Mass, throughout Catholic Europe and later the world.
