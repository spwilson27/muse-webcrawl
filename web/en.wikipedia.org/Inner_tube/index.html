Inner tube

An inner tube is an inflatable torus-shaped ring, usually made of rubber, that fits inside a pneumatic tyre and holds the pressurised air that gives the tyre its shape and cushioning. Air is admitted through a valve stem — commonly a Schrader valve on motor vehicles and many bicycles, or a narrower Presta valve on performance bicycles. The tube is slightly elastic, expanding to fill the cavity between the tyre casing and the wheel rim.

Use

Inner tubes remain standard in bicycle tyres, most motorcycle tyres with spoked wheels, many agricultural and aircraft tyres, and older or heavy-duty vehicle tyres. In most modern passenger cars they have been replaced by tubeless tyres, in which the tyre bead seals directly against the rim and a rubberised inner liner retains the air. Tubeless bicycle systems also exist, using sealant and airtight rims, but tubes are still widely used for their simplicity and ease of roadside repair.

Materials and manufacture

Tubes are made from butyl rubber, which retains air far better than natural rubber, or from natural latex, common in lightweight racing bicycle tubes because of its lower rolling resistance and greater suppleness, at the cost of needing more frequent inflation. Tubes are sized to match the tyre diameter and width range; an undersized tube stretches thin and punctures more easily, while an oversized one can fold and chafe inside the tyre.

Punctures and repair

Tubes fail by puncture from sharp objects, by "pinch flats" (snake bites) when the tyre is compressed against the rim, piercing the tube in two places, and by valve damage or abrasion. Small punctures can be repaired with a patch kit: the hole is located, the surface roughened, vulcanising fluid applied, and a patch pressed on. Spare tubes are commonly carried by cyclists for quick replacement. Liquid sealants injected through the valve can close small holes automatically.

Recreational use

Large inner tubes, traditionally from truck tyres, are popular as flotation devices for river tubing and snow tubing, and the term has extended to purpose-made inflatable rings for water parks and pools.
