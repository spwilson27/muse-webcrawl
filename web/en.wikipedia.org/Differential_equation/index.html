Differential equation

In mathematics, a differential equation is an equation that relates one or more unknown functions and their derivatives. In applications, the functions generally represent physical quantities, the derivatives represent their rates of change, and the equation defines a relationship between the two.

Types

Differential equations are classified by order (the highest derivative present), by whether they involve one independent variable (ordinary differential equations, ODEs) or several (partial differential equations, PDEs), and by linearity. Famous examples include Newton's second law, the wave equation, Maxwell's equations, the heat equation, and the Schrödinger equation.

Solutions

Solving a differential equation means finding a function satisfying it, often subject to initial or boundary conditions. Many equations have no closed-form solution and must be studied qualitatively or solved numerically using methods such as Euler's method and Runge–Kutta methods. The theory of existence and uniqueness, exemplified by the Picard–Lindelöf theorem, guarantees solutions under suitable conditions.

Applications

Differential equations are the principal language of modelling in physics, engineering, biology (population dynamics, epidemics), economics, and many other fields.
