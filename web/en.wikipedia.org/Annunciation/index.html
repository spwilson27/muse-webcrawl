Annunciation

SourceLuke 1:26–38

The Annunciation (from Latin annuntiatio) is, according to the Gospel of Luke, the announcement by the archangel Gabriel to Mary that she would conceive and bear a son through a virgin birth and become the mother of Jesus Christ, the Messiah and Son of God, marking his Incarnation. Gabriel's message, and Mary's consent — her fiat ("let it be done") — are central to Christian theology and to Marian devotion.

Biblical account

In Luke 1:26–38, Gabriel greets Mary with the words "Hail, full of grace" (in the Vulgate, Ave gratia plena) — the origin of the Hail Mary prayer. Troubled by the greeting, Mary is reassured and told that the Holy Spirit will overshadow her. She answers: "Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word." In the Gospel of Matthew, an analogous announcement is made to Joseph in a dream.

Feast

The Feast of the Annunciation is celebrated on 25 March, nine months before Christmas, in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican and many other churches. It is one of the principal Marian feasts; in the Orthodox tradition it ranks among the Twelve Great Feasts.

In art

The Annunciation is one of the most frequently depicted subjects in Western art, treated by artists including Fra Angelico, Leonardo da Vinci, Sandro Botticelli and Jan van Eyck. Typical iconography shows Gabriel bearing a lily (symbol of Mary's purity), a dove representing the Holy Spirit, and Mary reading or at prayer, sometimes in an enclosed garden (hortus conclusus).
