Partners In Health (PIH) is an international non-profit public health organisation founded in 1987 by physicians and activists including Paul Farmer, Jim Yong Kim and Ophelia Dahl. Based in Boston, Massachusetts, PIH works to provide high-quality health care to poor and marginalised communities in developing countries, and to accompany public health systems in strengthening their capacity.

History

PIH grew out of work begun in Cange, a village in Haiti's Central Plateau, where the organisation's sister project Zanmi Lasante ("Partners In Health" in Haitian Creole) built a clinic that expanded into a full hospital serving a catchment population of well over a million people. From Haiti the model spread to other countries, including Rwanda, Peru, Russia, Lesotho, Malawi, Mexico, Kazakhstan, Liberia and Sierra Leone.

Model of care

The organisation is known for its insistence that diseases such as HIV/AIDS and multidrug-resistant tuberculosis can be treated effectively in poor settings, contrary to the prevailing view of the 1990s. A central element of its approach is the training and employment of community health workers — known as accompagnateurs — who visit patients at home, observe therapy, and help address the social and economic barriers, such as hunger and lack of transport, that undermine treatment. PIH describes its mission in terms of a "preferential option for the poor".

Notable programmes

PIH supported treatment programmes for multidrug-resistant tuberculosis in Peru and in Tomsk, Russia; worked with the Rwandan Ministry of Health to build health infrastructure in rural districts, including Butaro Hospital; and responded to the 2010 Haiti earthquake, building the Hôpital Universitaire de Mirebalais teaching hospital. During the West African Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016, PIH worked in Liberia and Sierra Leone, and it later supported national COVID-19 contact-tracing efforts, including the community tracing programme in Massachusetts.
