Hainaut

Hainaut is a province of Wallonia in western Belgium, named after the river Haine. It is the most populous province of Wallonia, with about 1.35 million inhabitants, and its capital is Mons (Bergen). Other major cities include Charleroi, the province's largest city, La Louvière, and Tournai.

Geography and economy

The province borders France to the south and the Belgian provinces of West Flanders, East Flanders, Flemish Brabant, Walloon Brabant, and Namur. Its industrial heritage rests on coal mining and steelmaking in the Borinage and around Charleroi—the historic sillon industriel—which declined sharply in the later twentieth century, leaving the province to rebuild around services, logistics, and technology.

History

The modern province derives largely from the medieval County of Hainaut, though its borders were redrawn after the Belgian Revolution; the French department of Hainaut across the border preserves the name of the part ceded to France.
