Associated Equipment Company


An AEC Routemaster, the company's most famous product.

The Associated Equipment Company (AEC) was a British manufacturer of buses, coaches and commercial vehicles, principally lorries, based in Southall, London. Founded in 1912 as a subsidiary of the London General Omnibus Company and its successor the Underground Group, AEC supplied most of London's buses for much of the twentieth century.

History

AEC grew out of the need of London's bus operators to build their own vehicles. Its early B-type buses served widely during the First World War. In the interwar years AEC produced the Q-type and other models, and during the Second World War it built the Matador artillery tractor and other military vehicles.

The company's best-known designs were the Regent double-decker, the Regal single-decker, and above all the Routemaster, developed in the 1950s for London Transport and regarded as an icon of London. AEC's Regent III RT and Routemaster dominated London streets for decades.

In 1948 AEC acquired rival manufacturers and became the core of the Associated Commercial Vehicles (ACV) group. ACV merged with Leyland Motors in 1962, and AEC's designs were progressively absorbed into the Leyland range. The AEC name disappeared from new vehicles in the late 1970s, and the Southall factory closed in 1979.

Products

  • B-type bus
  • Regent, Regal and Reliance bus and coach chassis
  • Routemaster double-decker
  • Matador and Militant lorries
  • Swift and Merlin single-deckers
