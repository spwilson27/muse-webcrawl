Cosmos (Humboldt book)

Cosmos
AuthorAlexander von Humboldt
Original titleKosmos: Entwurf einer physischen Weltbeschreibung

LanguageGerman
SubjectPhysical description of the universe; natural science

Cosmos: A Sketch of a Physical Description of the Universe (German: Kosmos: Entwurf einer physischen Weltbeschreibung) is an influential treatise on science and nature written by the German naturalist and explorer Alexander von Humboldt. Published in five volumes between 1845 and 1862, it sought to unify the branches of scientific knowledge and to show the interconnectedness of the natural world, combining empirical observation with aesthetic appreciation of nature.

Contents

Humboldt based the work on the popular "Kosmos lectures" he had delivered in Berlin in 1827–28. The first volume surveys the whole of nature, from nebulae and the solar system to Earth's geography and the distribution of life. The second treats the history of humanity's contemplation of nature — landscape painting, poetry, and the gradual discovery of natural laws. Subsequent volumes deal in detail with astronomy, the Earth sciences, and the geography of plants and animals.

Reception and influence

Cosmos was an international bestseller, translated into many languages, and made Humboldt arguably the most famous scientist of his age. It inspired scientists and writers alike — Charles Darwin took Humboldt's earlier Personal Narrative on the Beagle and admired Cosmos, and the book influenced figures from Henry David Thoreau to John Muir. Modern historians of science, including Andrea Wulf in her biography The Invention of Nature (2015), credit Humboldt's holistic vision in Cosmos with anticipating ecology and environmentalism, including an early recognition that human action could alter the climate.
