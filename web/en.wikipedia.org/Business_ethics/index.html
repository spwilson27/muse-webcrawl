Business ethics (also known as corporate ethics) is a form of applied ethics or professional ethics that examines ethical principles and moral or ethical problems that can arise in a business environment. It applies to all aspects of business conduct, from corporate governance and insider trading to bribery, discrimination, corporate social responsibility, fiduciary responsibilities, and the duties firms owe to employees, consumers, shareholders, and the wider community.

History and approaches

Ethical reflection on commerce is ancient — from Aristotle on trade to religious prohibitions on usury — but business ethics emerged as a distinct academic field in the 1970s, alongside the growth of business schools, consumer and environmental movements, and corporate scandals. Key debates contrast Milton Friedman's shareholder doctrine — that the social responsibility of business is to increase its profits within the rules of the game — with stakeholder theory, associated with R. Edward Freeman, which holds that firms should create value for all stakeholders, not only shareholders.

Issues

Major issues include fair competition and marketing (truth in advertising, product safety), financial ethics (fraud, insider trading, creative accounting, as in the Enron scandal), human resources (workplace discrimination, privacy, whistleblower protection), production and supply chains (sweatshop labour, conflict minerals), and environmental responsibility in the face of climate change. Practical implementation takes the form of codes of ethics, ethics officers and compliance programmes, social auditing, and frameworks such as ESG (environmental, social, and governance) criteria.
