The litre (British English spelling) or liter (American English) is a metric unit of volume. It is equal to 1 cubic decimetre (dm³), 1,000 cubic centimetres (cm³) or 0.001 cubic metres (m³). Although not an SI unit, the litre is accepted for use with the SI by the CGPM.

Definition

From 1901 to 1964 the litre was defined as the volume of 1 kilogram of pure water at its maximum density, which made it slightly larger than a cubic decimetre (about 1.000028 dm³). In 1964 the definition was restored to exactly 1 dm³.

Symbol

The symbol for the litre is lowercase l or capital L. The capital letter was adopted by the CGPM in 1979 as an alternative to avoid confusion between lowercase "l" and the numeral "1".

Subdivisions and multiples

Common prefixed forms include the millilitre (mL, one thousandth of a litre), centilitre (cL) and hectolitre (hL, used in brewing and agriculture). One litre of water has a mass of almost exactly one kilogram, a legacy of the original design of the metric system.
