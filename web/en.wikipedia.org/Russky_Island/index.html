Russky Island

LocationPeter the Great Gulf, Sea of Japan
Area97.6 km²
Highest pointMount Russkikh, 291 m
AdministrationVladivostok, Primorsky Krai, Russia

Russky Island (Russian: остров Русский, "Russian Island") is an island in Peter the Great Gulf in the Sea of Japan, off the coast of Vladivostok in the Russian Far East. Separated from the Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula by the Eastern Bosphorus strait, it covers about 97.6 square kilometres and is part of the city of Vladivostok.

The Russky Bridge, one of the world's longest cable-stayed bridges, links Vladivostok to the island.

History

The island was named in the mid-19th century, when the area became part of the Russian Empire under the Treaties of Aigun and Peking. It was developed as part of the defences of Vladivostok fortress, with coastal artillery batteries and military installations; during the Soviet era it was a closed military zone with restricted access, hosting training grounds and a notorious naval disciplinary unit.

Development

For the 2012 APEC summit held on the island, the Russian government built the Russky Bridge — a cable-stayed bridge with the world's longest main span of its type at 1,104 metres — and the new campus of the Far Eastern Federal University, built on the site of the summit facilities at Ajax Bay. Planned development has aimed to turn the island into an educational, scientific, and tourism hub, while much of its coastline remains forested military and recreational land with popular beaches.
