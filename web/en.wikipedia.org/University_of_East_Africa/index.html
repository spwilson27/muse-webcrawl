The University of East Africa was a federal university serving Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda from 1963 to 1970. It was inaugurated on 29 June 1963 — the year of Kenyan independence and shortly after the independence of Tanganyika (1961) and Uganda (1962) — bringing together three university colleges that had previously awarded degrees of the University of London: Makerere University College in Kampala, the Royal College Nairobi, and the University College, Dar es Salaam.

History

Higher education in British East Africa had developed through the external degree system of the University of London. As the three territories approached independence, planners proposed a single federal university to maintain common standards and serve the East African Community. The University of East Africa awarded its first degrees in 1963, with Julius Nyerere, president of Tanganyika, as its first chancellor.

Dissolution

National pressures within the three partner states led each to seek its own national university. On 1 July 1970 the University of East Africa was dissolved and its colleges became independent universities: Makerere University in Uganda, the University of Nairobi in Kenya, and the University of Dar es Salaam in Tanzania. All three remain leading universities in the region.
