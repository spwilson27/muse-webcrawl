Ātman (Hinduism)

Ātman is a Sanskrit word that refers to the (real) self of the individual, transcending the phenomenal body and mind. It is a central concept in Hindu philosophy, particularly in the Upanishads and Vedanta.

Meaning

In the Upanishads, Ātman is the true self beyond identification with the body, senses and ego. The famous dictum tat tvam asi ("you are that") in the Chandogya Upanishad expresses the identity of Ātman with Brahman, the ultimate reality, in Advaita Vedanta. Other schools, such as Dvaita, hold the individual self to be eternally distinct from God.

Liberation

Realising the nature of Ātman is held to bring moksha, liberation from the cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra). Practices of meditation, self-inquiry and knowledge (jñāna) are directed toward this realisation.
