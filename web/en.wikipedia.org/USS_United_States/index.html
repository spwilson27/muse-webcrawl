USS United States was a wooden-hulled, three-masted heavy frigate of the United States Navy and the first of the six original frigates authorised by the Naval Act of 1794. She was the first warship ordered and built for the new US Navy and the first American warship launched. Her name was specified by President George Washington.

Designed by Joshua Humphreys, the frigates were longer, broader and more heavily armed than European frigates of the day, combining the firepower of a ship of the line's broadside with the speed to escape one. United States was built in Philadelphia under the supervision of John Barry, who became her first captain.

Service history

United States first saw service in the Quasi-War with France (1798–1800), cruising the Caribbean to protect American merchantmen from French privateers. She later served in the Mediterranean against the Barbary states.

Her most famous action came in the War of 1812. Under Commodore Stephen Decatur, on 25 October 1812 she engaged the British frigate HMS Macedonian in the mid-Atlantic. Using her heavier long guns to great effect, United States battered Macedonian into surrender; the British ship was taken into the US Navy as a prize, the only British frigate captured at sea and brought into port in that war.

Blockaded at New London for much of the remainder of the war, United States returned to peacetime service in the Mediterranean, the Pacific and the African coast. By the 1840s she was worn and spent long periods in ordinary.

End

Laid up at Norfolk Navy Yard when Virginia seceded in 1861, she was seized by the Confederacy and briefly commissioned as CSS United States, used as a harbour defence battery. When Confederate forces abandoned Norfolk in May 1862 they sank her in the Elizabeth River to obstruct the channel. Union forces later raised the hulk, and she was broken up in 1865, ending the career of the Navy's first capital ship.
