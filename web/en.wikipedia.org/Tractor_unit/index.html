Tractor unit


A tractor unit without a trailer attached

A tractor unit (also known as a truck tractor, semi-tractor, prime mover in Australia, or simply a tractor) is a heavy-duty towing engine that provides motive power for hauling a towed or trailer load. Tractor units are designed to pull semi-trailers, which carry most of their weight on their own rear axles and transfer the remainder onto the tractor.

The tractor connects to the trailer at a pivoting coupling called a fifth-wheel coupling, mounted over the tractor's rear axles; the trailer's kingpin locks into the fifth wheel. This articulation lets the combination turn corners while the tractor bears a substantial share of the trailer's weight. Electrical and air-brake lines ("gladhands") connect the two units.

Tractor units are typically classified by their number of axles and cab configuration. In North America the conventional (bonneted) cab predominates, while in Europe, where overall-length limits are strict, cab-over-engine designs are standard. Day cabs are used for local work, and sleeper cabs with bunks for long-haul routes. Together with a semi-trailer, the tractor forms a semi-trailer truck (an "articulated lorry" in British English).
