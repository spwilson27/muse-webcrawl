Putamen

The putamen is a round structure located at the base of the forebrain (telencephalon). It is the outermost portion of the basal ganglia and, together with the caudate nucleus, forms the dorsal striatum. The putamen and the caudate are joined at their anterior ends and share a similar cellular composition; functionally the putamen is most strongly associated with the regulation of movement and with procedural learning, including motor skill and habit formation.

Anatomy

The putamen lies lateral to the globus pallidus, from which it is separated by the lateral medullary lamina, and medial to the external capsule and the insular cortex. Together with the globus pallidus it forms the lentiform (lenticular) nucleus. Its principal input arrives from the cerebral cortex and the intralaminar thalamic nuclei; dopaminergic input from the substantia nigra pars compacta modulates its output, which passes to the globus pallidus and substantia nigra pars reticulata via the direct and indirect pathways.

Function

The putamen is a core component of the motor loop of the basal ganglia, receiving somatotopically organized input from motor and somatosensory cortex. It is involved in the preparation and execution of movements, motor learning, and the performance of learned sequences. The posterior putamen in particular is engaged during habitual, automatic behaviour, in contrast to the caudate, which is more involved in goal-directed action.

Clinical significance

Degeneration of dopaminergic input to the putamen is central to Parkinson's disease, in which putaminal dopamine loss correlates with motor symptoms. Abnormalities of the putamen have also been reported in Huntington's disease, dystonia, Tourette syndrome, and schizophrenia, and the putamen is a target for deep brain stimulation and for experimental cell and gene therapies in movement disorders.
