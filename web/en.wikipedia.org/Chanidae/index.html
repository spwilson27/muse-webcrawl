Chanidae is a family of ray-finned fishes in the order Gonorynchiformes. Its only living representative is the milkfish (Chanos chanos), a streamlined, silvery schooling fish of the tropical Indo-Pacific that is of major importance to aquaculture in Southeast Asia, particularly the Philippines, Indonesia, and Taiwan. The family has a rich fossil record, however, with numerous extinct genera known from marine and freshwater deposits going back to the Early Cretaceous.

Fossil record

Fossil chanids include genera such as Dastilbe, Parachanos, Tharrhias, and Gordichthys, found in famous deposits including the Santana Formation of Brazil and the Tlayúa Formation of Mexico. These Cretaceous forms show that the family was once far more diverse and widespread, occurring in the Americas as well as the Old World, whereas today it survives only in the single species Chanos chanos.

Milkfish

The milkfish can grow to about 1.8 m in length, feeds on algae and small invertebrates, and tolerates wide ranges of salinity, moving between the sea, estuaries, and freshwater lakes. It has been farmed in brackish-water ponds for centuries — among the oldest aquaculture species in the world. Captive adults do not readily breed, so farms traditionally rely on wild-caught fry collected along coasts, though hatchery production has been developed.
