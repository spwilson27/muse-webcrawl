Via Podiensis

The Via Podiensis (Latin for the route from Le Puy) is one of the four great pilgrimage routes through France to Santiago de Compostela in Spain. It begins in Le Puy-en-Velay in the Auvergne and runs southwest for roughly 740 km to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port at the foot of the Pyrenees, where it joins the Camino Francés over the mountains.

History

The route is associated with Bishop Godescalc of Le Puy, who made the pilgrimage to Compostela in 951 — among the first recorded pilgrims from beyond the Pyrenees — and with the 12th-century Pilgrim's Guide in the Codex Calixtinus, which names the four French routes. The Via Podiensis passes through Conques, Figeac, Cahors and Moissac, whose Romanesque abbeys, carved tympana and cloisters served the medieval pilgrim traffic. Sections of the route, with associated monuments such as the abbey church of Sainte-Foy in Conques and the Pont Valentré in Cahors, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1998 as part of the Routes of Santiago de Compostela in France.

The route today

The modern long-distance footpath GR 65 largely follows the historic way from Le Puy. It is the most popular starting route in France, walked in about four to five weeks through the volcanic Velay, the Aubrac plateau, the Lot valley and Gascony. Pilgrims typically carry a crédencial stamped at churches and refuges along the way.
