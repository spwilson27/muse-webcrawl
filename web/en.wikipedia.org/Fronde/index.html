Fronde

The Fronde (French for "sling") was a series of civil wars and rebellions in the Kingdom of France between 1648 and 1653, during the minority of Louis XIV. The uprisings began as resistance by the Parlement of Paris and the great nobility to the fiscal policies and centralizing ambitions of Cardinal Mazarin and the regent Anne of Austria.

Course

The first phase, the Fronde of the Parlements (1648–1649), began when the Parlement resisted new taxes imposed to fund the war against Spain. Royal troops blockaded Paris, but the crown was forced to concede the Peace of Rueil. The second phase, the Fronde of the Princes (1650–1653), was a struggle among aristocratic factions led by the Prince de Condé, whose ambitions turned the movement into open civil war. In July 1652 fighting reached Paris itself, with the Grande Mademoiselle famously ordering the Bastille's cannon to fire on royal troops to admit Condé's army.

Outcome

The revolts collapsed through disunity, and Mazarin, twice driven into temporary exile, returned in triumph. The Fronde's failure discredited resistance to royal authority; Louis XIV, deeply marked by having twice fled Paris as a boy, went on to build the absolute monarchy centred on Versailles, determined that the high nobility would never again challenge the crown.
