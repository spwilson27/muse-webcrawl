August Horch (12 October 1868 – 3 February 1951) was a German engineer and automobile pioneer, and the founder of the motor vehicle manufacturers Horch and Audi.

Early life and career

Horch trained as a blacksmith and studied engineering at the Technikum Mittweida. He worked at Carl Benz's company in Mannheim from 1896 to 1899, gaining experience in early automobile construction, before founding A. Horch & Cie in Cologne in 1899. The company moved to Zwickau in Saxony in 1904 and produced respected luxury cars and successful racing machines.

Founding Audi

After a dispute with his board, Horch left his own company in 1909. Barred from using his surname for a new firm, he translated "Horch" (the imperative of horchen, "listen!") into Latin: Audi. He founded Audi Automobilwerke in Zwickau in 1910. In 1932, Audi, Horch, DKW, and Wanderer merged to form Auto Union, symbolized by four interlocking rings — the emblem Audi still bears.

Later life

Horch served on the Auto Union board and was made an honorary citizen of Zwickau. He died in 1951 in Münchberg, Bavaria.
