Refugee camp

A refugee camp is a temporary settlement built to accommodate people who have been forced to flee their homes because of war, persecution, or violence. Camps are usually administered by governments, the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), or humanitarian organizations, and provide shelter, food, water, sanitation, and medical care.

Characteristics

Although intended as temporary, many camps persist for years or decades; residents of camps such as Dadaab and Kakuma in Kenya, or the Palestinian camps established after 1948, may live in them for generations. Conditions vary widely, and overcrowding, limited livelihoods, and restricted freedom of movement are common concerns.

Alternatives

Most refugees worldwide live outside camps, in cities and host communities. Humanitarian policy increasingly favors alternatives to camps, including cash assistance and integration into local services, while camps remain a common response to sudden mass displacement.
