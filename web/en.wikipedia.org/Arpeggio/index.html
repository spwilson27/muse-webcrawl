Arpeggio

An arpeggio is a type of broken chord in which the notes of a chord are played or sung in sequence, either ascending or descending, rather than simultaneously. The word comes from the Italian arpeggiare, meaning "to play on a harp".

Technique

Arpeggios are fundamental exercises for many instruments. Pianists and guitarists practise them across multiple octaves in all keys, and they form the basis of much figuration in classical and popular music. On string instruments, arpeggiation can be combined with string crossing and position shifts.

In composition

Composers from Bach and Beethoven to modern film and game composers use arpeggios to outline harmony while creating melodic motion. In electronic music, an arpeggiator is a device or software function that automatically cycles through the notes of a held chord in a rhythmic pattern.
