Michael Strogoff: The Courier of the Czar (French: Michel Strogoff) is an adventure novel by Jules Verne, published in 1876. Unlike Verne's science-fiction works, it is a historical adventure set in Imperial Russia, and is often considered one of his best novels.

Plot

Michael Strogoff, a courier of Tsar Alexander II, is sent from Moscow to Irkutsk — 5,200 versts (about 5,500 km) across Siberia — carrying a letter warning the Tsar's brother, the Grand Duke, of the treason of Ivan Ogareff, who has allied with Tatar hordes under Feofar Khan invading Siberia. Travelling incognito as a merchant, Strogoff is aided by the young Livonian Nadia and by two war correspondents, the Frenchman Alcide Jolivet and the Englishman Harry Blount. Captured by the Tatars, Strogoff is apparently blinded with a heated sword — in one of literature's famous twists, his tears, shed as he gazed at his mother, had protected his eyes — and, pretending blindness, reaches Irkutsk in time to expose Ogareff, whom he kills in a duel.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was a great success in Verne's lifetime and was adapted into a stage play (1880, co-written with Adolphe d'Ennery) that remained a Parisian spectacle for decades, including a staged horse race and the flooding of the stage. Numerous film versions followed, and the hero's journey is sometimes cited as a quintessential adventure quest.
