Manure

Manure is organic matter that is used as organic fertilizer in agriculture. Most manure consists of animal feces; other sources include compost and green manure. Manures contribute to the fertility of soil by adding organic matter and nutrients, such as nitrogen, that are utilised by bacteria, fungi and other organisms in the soil. Higher organisms then feed on the fungi and bacteria in a chain of life that comprises the soil food web.

Types

Animal manure from livestock — cattle, horses, sheep, pigs and poultry — is the most common type, varying widely in nutrient content; poultry litter is especially rich in nitrogen and phosphorus. Manure is often mixed with bedding such as straw to form farmyard manure. "Green manure" consists of cover crops grown specifically to be ploughed into the soil, while compost is decomposed organic matter of mixed origin. Human excreta ("night soil") has a long history of use in East Asian agriculture, subject to treatment to manage disease risk.

Use and management

Farmers apply manure fresh, composted or as slurry stored in pits and lagoons and spread with tankers or muck spreaders. Composting reduces volume, odour, weed seeds and pathogens while stabilising nutrients. Because manure is bulky and its nutrient content variable, precise application is difficult; over-application causes nitrate leaching and phosphorus runoff that drive eutrophication of waterways, and manure storage produces methane and nitrous oxide, both potent greenhouse gases. Anaerobic digesters can convert manure into biogas while retaining most fertiliser value in the digestate.

History

Manuring has been practiced since the earliest agriculture; Roman writers such as Cato and Columella described the use of animal dung and green manures, and the manure system tied crop and livestock farming together across pre-industrial Europe. The 19th-century trade in Peruvian guano and Chilean nitrates foreshadowed industrial fertilisers, which displaced manure on many farms in the 20th century, though manure remains an essential nutrient source worldwide.
