Escape from Alcatraz

Alcatraz Island, from which Morris and the Anglin brothers attempted to escape in 1962

Escape from Alcatraz may refer to the June 1962 escape attempt from Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary, in which inmates Frank Morris and brothers John and Clarence Anglin vanished from the island prison in San Francisco Bay, or to the 1979 film of the same name based on the event.

The 1962 escape

On the night of 11 June 1962, Frank Morris and brothers John and Clarence Anglin carried out an escape from Alcatraz that had been planned with a fourth inmate, Allen West, who failed to leave his cell in time. Over months, the men had widened the ventilation ducts in their cells using spoons and a makeshift drill made from a vacuum cleaner motor, concealing the holes with painted cardboard and the noise with music played during the music hour. They placed papier-mâché dummy heads with real hair in their beds to fool the night guards.

From a utility corridor the group climbed to the roof, descended to the shore, and launched a raft made from more than 50 stolen raincoats, inflated with a concertina. They were never seen again. The FBI concluded that the men drowned in the cold, swift waters of the bay, though their bodies were never recovered, and the case remains officially open. A paddle and personal effects were later found on nearby Angel Island, and a Norwegian freighter reported seeing a body in a prison-like shirt floating north of the Golden Gate Bridge.

1979 film

Escape from Alcatraz (1979) is an American prison thriller film directed by Don Siegel and starring Clint Eastwood as Frank Morris. It dramatises the 1962 escape and was the fifth and final collaboration between Siegel and Eastwood. The film was shot on location at Alcatraz and is regarded as one of the most acclaimed prison films.

Legacy

The escape remains the most famous of the 14 documented escape attempts from Alcatraz. The U.S. Marshals Service continues to investigate, and age-progressed images of the three men have been released. Alcatraz closed as a prison in 1963, less than a year after the escape.
