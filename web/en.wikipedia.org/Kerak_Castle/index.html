Kerak Castle

Kerak Castle (Arabic: قلعة الكرك) is a large medieval castle located in al-Karak, Jordan. It is one of the largest castles in the Levant, built by the Crusaders in the 12th century on a hilltop plateau overlooking the King's Highway trade route and the Dead Sea region.

History

Construction began in the 1140s under Pagan the Butler, Lord of Oultrejordain. The castle became the centre of the Crusader lordship of Transjordan, controlling Bedouin herders and the lucrative caravan routes between Damascus and Egypt and Arabia. Its most notorious lord was Raynald of Châtillon, whose attacks on Muslim caravans and pilgrims helped provoke Saladin to war.

Saladin besieged Kerak in 1183 and 1184 — famously interrupting the wedding of Humphrey IV of Toron and Isabella of Jerusalem — and after the Crusader defeat at Hattin in 1187 the castle finally surrendered in 1188. It later passed to the Ayyubids and Mamluks, who added substantial fortifications, and remained in use under the Ottomans before being partially slighted in the 19th century.

Description

The castle is roughly 220 metres long and 125 metres wide at its north end, with massive Crusader masonry, vaulted halls, a chapel, and a deep rock-cut moat. The upper and lower courts are separated by steep slopes and walls. Kerak is today one of Jordan's most visited historic sites and the centrepiece of the modern city of al-Karak.
