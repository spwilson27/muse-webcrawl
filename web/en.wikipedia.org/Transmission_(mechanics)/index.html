A transmission (also called a gearbox) is a mechanical device that uses a gear set — two or more gears working together — to change the speed or torque of a rotating power source, usually an engine or motor, before delivering power to the driven wheels or machinery.

Purpose

Internal combustion engines produce useful power only within a limited speed range, so vehicles need multiple gear ratios: low gears multiply torque for starting and climbing, while high gears allow economical cruising. The transmission also provides a neutral position and usually a reverse gear.

Types

Manual transmissions require the driver to select gears and operate a clutch. Automatics use planetary gearsets and a torque converter with hydraulic or electronic control. Continuously variable transmissions (CVTs) vary the ratio seamlessly with belts, chains, or toroidal drives. Dual-clutch transmissions preselect gears on two shafts for very fast shifts. Electric vehicles typically use a single fixed reduction gear because electric motors deliver torque over a wide speed range.

Other applications

Gearboxes appear in wind turbines, industrial machinery, bicycles (hub gears and derailleurs), and machine tools — any system needing speed or torque conversion.
