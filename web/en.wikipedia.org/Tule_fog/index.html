Tule fog

Tule fog is a thick ground fog that settles in California's San Joaquin Valley and Sacramento Valley during the late fall and winter. Named after the tule reeds of the region's wetlands, it is a form of radiation fog that forms on clear, calm nights when moist ground air cools to its dew point.

Formation

After rains soak the soil, long winter nights and light winds allow the air near the ground to cool rapidly. The fog can persist for days when a high-pressure system traps it beneath an inversion layer, and visibility can drop to a few feet or less.

Hazards

Tule fog is notorious for causing multi-vehicle pileups on Central Valley highways such as Interstate 5 and State Route 99. Notable crashes include an 108-vehicle pileup near Fresno in November 2007.

Trends

Studies have found that the frequency of tule fog in the Central Valley has declined in recent decades, a trend linked to warming winters and changes in air pollution that provides condensation nuclei.
