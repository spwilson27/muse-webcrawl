Michael Kenji Shinoda (born 11 February 1977) is an American musician best known as co-founder, co-vocalist, rapper, rhythm guitarist, and primary producer of the rock band Linkin Park. Shinoda's rapped verses, paired with Chester Bennington's singing, defined the band's nu metal and alternative rock sound on albums such as Hybrid Theory (2000) and Meteora (2003).

Career

Shinoda formed the band Xero with Brad Delson and Rob Bourdon in 1996, which became Linkin Park. Beyond performing, he handled much of the band's production and visual identity, drawing on his degree in graphic design from the Art Center College of Design. In 2005 he launched the hip-hop side project Fort Minor, whose single "Where'd You Go" reached the Billboard top five.

Solo work

Following Bennington's death in 2017, Shinoda released the solo album Post Traumatic (2018), an exploration of grief begun with the EP of the same name. He has since released the Dropped Frames trilogy (2020), produced music for other artists, and embraced livestreaming and digital art projects. In 2024, he re-formed Linkin Park with new co-vocalist Emily Armstrong for the album From Zero.

Art and design

Shinoda has exhibited paintings and digital art internationally and designed album artwork and merchandise throughout his career, merging his visual art practice with his music.
