Mariana Islands
Location: Western North Pacific Ocean
Major islands: Guam, Saipan, Tinian, Rota
Population: about 180,000 (Guam and CNMI)
Languages: English, Chamorro, Carolinian

The Mariana Islands (also the Marianas) are a crescent-shaped archipelago of fifteen volcanic islands in the western North Pacific, forming the northern part of the Micronesia subregion of Oceania. Politically they are divided between the United States territory of Guam in the south and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands (CNMI) — principally Saipan, Tinian and Rota — in the north.

Geography

The islands are the exposed tops of undersea mountains at the edge of the Mariana Plate, immediately west of the Mariana Trench, the deepest oceanic trench on Earth. The southern islands are older, limestone-capped and well vegetated; the northern islands are younger and still volcanically active.

History

The Marianas were settled by the ancestors of the Chamorro people around 1500 BC, among the earliest of the Pacific island voyages. Ferdinand Magellan reached Guam in 1521, and Spain claimed the islands, naming them for Queen Mariana of Austria; Spanish colonisation from 1668 devastated the indigenous population through war and disease. After the Spanish–American War of 1898, Guam was ceded to the United States and the northern islands sold to Germany, then mandated to Japan. Fierce battles were fought on Saipan, Tinian and Guam in 1944; Tinian was the departure point of the atomic bomb missions of August 1945. The CNMI entered political union with the United States in the 1970s–80s.
