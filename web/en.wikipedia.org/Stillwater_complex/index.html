The Stillwater complex is a large layered mafic intrusion exposed along the northern front of the Beartooth Mountains in south-central Montana, United States. Formed about 2.7 billion years ago during the Archaean, it is one of the world's classic examples of a layered igneous body and hosts the J-M Reef, the highest-grade deposit of platinum-group elements (PGE) known in the world.

Geology

The complex is a lopolithic intrusion roughly 48 km long and up to 8.8 km thick, divided into a Basal series, an Ultramafic series (peridotites and bronzitites with chromitite seams) and a Banded series of norites, gabbros and anorthosites. Its layering records the slow crystallisation and settling of minerals in a vast magma chamber, and in structure and mineralisation it is closely compared with South Africa's Bushveld Complex.

Mining

Chromite was mined from the complex during the Second World War and the 1950s. Since 1986 the Stillwater Mine (and later the East Boulder Mine) has exploited the J-M Reef — a layer typically only 1–3 metres thick containing palladium and platinum — making it the only primary PGE mining operation in the United States. The mines are operated by Sibanye-Stillwater near the towns of Nye and Big Timber.
