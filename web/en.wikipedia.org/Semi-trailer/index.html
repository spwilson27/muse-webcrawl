Semi-trailer


A semi-trailer coupled to a tractor unit.

A semi-trailer is a trailer without a front axle. The term "semi" reflects that a large proportion of its weight is supported by a tractor unit, a detachable front axle assembly known as a dolly, or the tail of another trailer. Semi-trailers are coupled to the tractor by a type of hitch called a fifth wheel, in which a kingpin on the trailer engages a horseshoe-shaped coupling device on the tractor.

Characteristics

Road tractors and semi-trailers are a dominant part of freight transport worldwide. In North America the common box-type trailer is 53 feet (16.2 m) long, while European trailers are typically about 13.6 m (44 ft 8 in) within an overall vehicle length limit of 16.5 m. When uncoupled, a semi-trailer is supported at the front by retractable landing gear legs.

Types

Semi-trailers are built in many specialized forms, including:

  • Box or dry van trailers — fully enclosed boxes for general freight;
  • Refrigerated trailers ("reefers") — insulated and cooled for perishables;
  • Flatbed trailers — open decks for construction materials and machinery;
  • Tanker trailers — for liquids, gases and dry bulk;
  • Lowboy trailers — with a low deck for tall or heavy equipment;
  • Curtain-sided trailers — rigid front and rear with flexible side curtains, common in Europe;
  • Car carriers, livestock trailers, dump trailers and logging trailers for specialized cargoes.

Coupling

The fifth-wheel coupling allows articulation between tractor and trailer and transfers much of the trailer's front weight to the tractor's rear axles. Air brake lines and electrical connections link the two units. A converter dolly can be used to turn a semi-trailer into a full trailer, or to build road trains with multiple trailers.
