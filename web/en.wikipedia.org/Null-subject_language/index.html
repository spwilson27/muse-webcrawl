Null-subject language

FieldLinguistic typology, syntax
Also calledPro-drop language
ExamplesSpanish, Italian, Arabic, Japanese, Mandarin

A null-subject language, also known as a pro-drop language, is a language in which certain classes of pronouns may be omitted when their referent is pragmatically or grammatically inferable. In Spanish, for example, hablo ("I speak") is a complete sentence because the first-person singular subject is encoded in the verb's inflection, so the pronoun yo is normally dropped.

Characterisation

In generative grammar, the phenomenon is analysed in terms of the null subject parameter (or pro-drop parameter): languages set the parameter positively license a phonetically empty pronoun (pro) in subject position. Classic null-subject languages such as Spanish, Italian, Greek, and Arabic have rich verbal agreement morphology, traditionally taken to "recover" the content of the dropped pronoun. However, languages like Japanese, Korean, and Mandarin Chinese allow extensive pronoun omission despite lacking subject–verb agreement, a fact that has driven refinements distinguishing agreement-based pro-drop from discourse-based (topic-drop) omission.

Partial and consistent null-subject languages

Some languages, such as Finnish, Hebrew, and Brazilian Portuguese, allow null subjects only under restricted conditions and are described as partial null-subject languages. Others, such as French and English, generally require overt subjects — English permits omission only in limited registers, such as diaries ("Went to the shops today") or imperatives.

Expletive subjects

Null-subject languages also typically lack the dummy expletive subjects found in English sentences like "It is raining"; Spanish llueve simply means "rains/is raining" with no subject at all.
