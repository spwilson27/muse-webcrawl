The Gotthard Pass or St. Gotthard Pass (Italian: Passo del San Gottardo) is a mountain pass in the Alps traversing the Saint-Gotthard Massif between Airolo in Ticino and Göschenen in Uri, Switzerland. At 2,106 metres, it connects northern and southern Switzerland and forms part of one of the most important transalpine routes between northern Europe and Italy.

History

Though known to the Romans, the pass only became a major trade route in the 13th century after the construction of bridges through the Schöllenen Gorge, including the famous Devil's Bridge. Mule trains carried goods over the pass for centuries, and a carriage road was built in the 1830s. The hospice at the summit, associated with Saint Gotthard of Hildesheim, sheltered travellers for centuries.

Tunnels

The pass has been progressively bypassed by tunnels: the Gotthard Railway Tunnel opened in 1882, the Gotthard Road Tunnel in 1980, and the Gotthard Base Tunnel — at 57 km the world's longest railway tunnel — opened in 2016. The old cobbled pass road (the Tremola) remains a scenic summer route.
