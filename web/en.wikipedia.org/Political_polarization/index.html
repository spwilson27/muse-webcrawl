Political polarization

Political polarization is the divergence of political attitudes toward ideological extremes, where public opinion and parties sort into opposing camps with little common ground. Researchers distinguish ideological polarization of positions from affective polarization — dislike and distrust of opposing partisans.

In the United States polarization has increased since the late 20th century in Congress and among voters, driven by partisan sorting, media fragmentation and primary elections. Consequences debated include gridlock, democratic erosion and social conflict.
