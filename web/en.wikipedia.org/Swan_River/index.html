Swan River

The Swan River at Perth Water, with the city skyline behind.

The Swan River (Nyungar: Derbarl Yerrigan) is a major river in the south-west of Western Australia. It flows through the city of Perth and enters the Indian Ocean at Fremantle. Its main tributaries include the Avon River, the Canning River, and the Helena River. The river is named for the black swans that inhabit it.

Course

The Swan begins as the Avon River, rising near Yealering in the Darling Range wheatbelt, and flows north and then west; below its confluence with Wooroloo Brook it becomes known as the Swan River. It passes through the Swan Valley wine region, widens into the broad Perth Water between Perth and South Perth, narrows at the Narrows Bridge crossing, and opens into Melville Water before passing through Fremantle harbour to the sea.

History

The river is of central spiritual significance to the Nyungar people, associated with the Wagyl (rainbow serpent) of Dreaming tradition. Dutch navigator Willem de Vlamingh named it in 1697 after observing the black swans. The Swan River Colony, founded in 1829, was the first free settlement in Australia, with Perth established on the river's banks.

Ecology and use

The estuary supports dolphins, black swans, pelicans, and recreational boating, rowing, and sailing. The river suffers periodic algal blooms and nutrient pollution from its agricultural catchment; the Swan River Trust (now part of state parks and rivers agencies) manages its health. Iconic features include Kings Park on Mount Eliza overlooking the river, Heirisson Island, and the Swan Bells (Bell Tower) at Barrack Square.
