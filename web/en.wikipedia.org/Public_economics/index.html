Public economics

Public economics (or economics of the public sector) is the study of government policy through the lens of economic efficiency and equity. It analyzes how taxation, public expenditure, regulation, and the design of fiscal institutions affect the allocation of resources and the distribution of income. The field provides the theoretical basis for cost–benefit analysis and for evaluating programs from pensions and health insurance to education and environmental policy.

Core topics

Classical topics include the theory of public goods, which are non-rival and non-excludable and therefore under-provided by markets; externalities and corrective (Pigouvian) taxes; and the incidence and excess burden of taxation. Richard Musgrave's influential framework distinguished three branches of the public budget: allocation, distribution, and stabilization. Modern public economics, following James Mirrlees's work on optimal income taxation, emphasizes incentive constraints and asymmetric information, and has become increasingly empirical, studying how taxpayers and beneficiaries actually respond to policy — for example through bunching at tax kinks, natural experiments, and randomized evaluations of welfare programs.

Political economy and applications

Because policy is chosen by political processes, public economics overlaps with public choice theory, which models voters, politicians, and bureaucracies as self-interested actors. Major applied areas include social insurance design, tax reform, fiscal federalism, public provision of health care and education, and the economics of climate policy instruments such as carbon taxes and cap-and-trade systems.
