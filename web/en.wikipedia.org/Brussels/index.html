Brussels (French: Bruxelles; Dutch: Brussel) is the capital of Belgium and the administrative centre of the European Union. The Brussels-Capital Region is one of Belgium's three regions and comprises 19 municipalities, including the City of Brussels itself. Although historically Dutch-speaking, Brussels is today predominantly French-speaking and officially bilingual.

History

Brussels grew from a small settlement on the river Senne, traditionally dated to around the 10th century. It prospered as a trading post between Bruges, Ghent and Cologne, and became a centre of tapestry and lace production. It served as a capital of the Burgundian Netherlands and later of the Spanish and Austrian Netherlands. After Belgian independence in 1830, Brussels became the national capital and expanded rapidly during the 19th century under King Leopold II.

Institutions

Brussels hosts the principal institutions of the European Union, including the European Commission in the Berlaymont building, the Council of the European Union, and a seat of the European Parliament. NATO's headquarters are also located in the city, making Brussels one of the world's leading centres of international diplomacy.

Landmarks and culture

The Grand-Place, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is the city's central square, surrounded by guildhalls and the Town Hall. Other landmarks include the Manneken Pis statue, the Royal Palace, the Atomium built for Expo 58, and the Art Nouveau houses designed by Victor Horta. Brussels is famous for chocolate, waffles, beer and comic strips, and is home to the Magritte Museum and the Belgian Comic Strip Center.
