Jugurtha

Jugurtha or Jugurthen (c. 160 – 104 BC) was a king of Numidia. When the Numidian king Micipsa died in 118 BC, the kingdom was shared between Jugurtha, an illegitimate son whom Micipsa had adopted, and Micipsa's two sons, Hiempsal and Adherbal. Jugurtha had Hiempsal murdered and defeated and killed Adherbal, provoking war with Rome.

Jugurthine War

The Jugurthine War (112–105 BC) exposed corruption in the Roman Senate, which Jugurtha was said to have bribed extensively — on leaving Rome he allegedly called it "a city for sale, and doomed to perish if it finds a buyer." Roman commanders Quintus Caecilius Metellus and then his legate Gaius Marius gradually wore down Jugurtha's resistance in a difficult guerrilla war.

Capture and death

Jugurtha was betrayed by his father-in-law Bocchus of Mauretania, whose alliance Marius's quaestor Sulla negotiated, and was handed over to the Romans in 105 BC. He was led in Marius's triumph and died in the Tullianum prison in Rome in 104 BC. The war is the subject of Sallust's monograph Bellum Jugurthinum, a classic of Latin historiography.
