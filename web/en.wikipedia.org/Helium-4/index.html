Helium-4


Illustration: Helium-4

Helium-4 (4He) is the most abundant isotope of helium, accounting for about 99.99986% of helium on Earth. Its nucleus, identical to an alpha particle, contains two protons and two neutrons and is exceptionally stable.

Properties

Helium-4 is produced on Earth mainly by alpha decay of heavy elements in the crust. It liquefies at 4.2 K at atmospheric pressure. Below the lambda point of 2.17 K, liquid helium-4 becomes a superfluid (helium II) with zero viscosity and extremely high thermal conductivity.

Superfluidity

The superfluid transition is a manifestation of Bose–Einstein condensation, since helium-4 atoms are bosons. The pioneering liquefaction was achieved by Heike Kamerlingh Onnes in 1908, and superfluidity was discovered in 1937 by Pyotr Kapitsa and, independently, John F. Allen and Don Misener.
