Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast (ADS-B) is a surveillance technology in which an aircraft periodically broadcasts its identity, position, altitude and velocity, determined from satellite navigation (GNSS), for use by air traffic control and other aircraft.

How it works

The system is "automatic" because no external interrogation is needed, "dependent" because it relies on the aircraft's own navigation systems, and "broadcast" because data are transmitted openly. ADS-B Out transmits the aircraft's data; ADS-B In allows suitably equipped aircraft to receive traffic information. Transmissions use either the 1090 MHz Mode S Extended Squitter link or, in the United States for aircraft below 18,000 ft, the 978 MHz Universal Access Transceiver.

Adoption

ADS-B equipage became mandatory in many airspaces in the 2010s and 2020s, including the United States (from 1 January 2020) and Europe. Space-based ADS-B receivers on satellite constellations extend coverage over oceans and remote regions, improving tracking and search-and-rescue awareness.
