Measles virus

The measles virus (Measles morbillivirus) is a single-stranded, negative-sense RNA virus of the family Paramyxoviridae that causes measles, a highly contagious disease of humans with no known animal reservoir.

Structure and transmission

The enveloped virus carries haemagglutinin (H) and fusion (F) surface proteins and a genome of about 16,000 nucleotides encoding eight proteins. It spreads through respiratory droplets and airborne particles and is among the most contagious known pathogens, with a basic reproduction number often estimated between 12 and 18.

Disease

Infection causes fever, cough, Koplik spots and a characteristic rash, and can lead to pneumonia, encephalitis and the late complication SSPE. Immune amnesia following infection erases prior immune memory. The live attenuated MMR vaccine, introduced in the 1960s–70s, is highly effective.
