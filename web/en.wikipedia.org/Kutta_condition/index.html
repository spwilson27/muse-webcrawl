Kutta condition

The Kutta condition is a principle in steady-flow fluid dynamics, especially aerodynamics, named after the German mathematician Martin Kutta. It states that for flow around a body with a sharp trailing edge, the circulation is such that the flow leaves the trailing edge smoothly, with finite velocity there — the fluid from above and below the body meets smoothly at the edge and flows away together.

Role in lift theory

In potential-flow theory, an infinite family of circulations is mathematically possible around an airfoil; the Kutta condition selects the physically correct one, without which the velocity at a sharp trailing edge would be infinite. Combined with the Kutta–Joukowski theorem, it yields the standard prediction of lift. In unsteady flow, the condition is explained by the shedding of a starting vortex (Kelvin's circulation theorem).
