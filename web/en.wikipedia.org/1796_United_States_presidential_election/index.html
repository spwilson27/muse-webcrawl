1796 United States presidential election

The 1796 United States presidential election was the third quadrennial presidential election, held from Friday, November 4, to Wednesday, December 7, 1796. It was the first contested American presidential election and the first in which political parties played a dominant role. Incumbent Vice President John Adams, of the Federalist Party, defeated Secretary of State Thomas Jefferson, of the Democratic-Republican Party.

Background

George Washington, who had been unanimously elected in 1788 and 1792, announced his retirement in his Farewell Address of September 1796. For the first time the presidency was openly contested. The Federalists rallied behind Vice President John Adams, while the Democratic-Republicans supported Thomas Jefferson. Under the original rules of the Constitution, each elector cast two undifferentiated votes; the leading candidate became president and the runner-up vice president.

Campaign and results

The election was dominated by sectional loyalties and by the young republic's foreign-policy divide between pro-British Federalists and pro-French Democratic-Republicans. Adams won most of New England and the mid-Atlantic electoral votes, while Jefferson carried most of the South. Adams received 71 electoral votes and Jefferson 68. Federalist Thomas Pinckney and Democratic-Republican Aaron Burr trailed with 59 and 30 votes respectively.

Consequences

Because the runner-up became vice president, the United States was left with a president and vice president from opposing parties — the only such occurrence in its history under the original constitutional system. Their strained administration, and the tied election of 1800, helped prompt the Twelfth Amendment, ratified in 1804, which required separate ballots for president and vice president.
