Castillo San Felipe del Morro

Castillo San Felipe del Morro, commonly called El Morro, is a sixteenth-century Spanish citadel in San Juan, Puerto Rico. Lying on the northwestern point of the islet of Old San Juan, it guarded the entrance to San Juan Bay and protected the city from seaborne attack. It is part of the San Juan National Historic Site and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

History

Construction of the fortress began in 1539, when a simple tower and battery — La Fortaleza's complement on the headland ("el morro") — were authorised by Charles V of Spain. Over the following two centuries the fort was repeatedly enlarged into a massive six-level bastioned fortress designed to dominate the bay entrance with crossfire, working together with the smaller fort El Cañuelo across the bay.

El Morro repelled attacks by Sir Francis Drake in 1595 and the Earl of Cumberland in 1598; a Dutch force under Boudewijn Hendricksz burned San Juan in 1625 but could not take the castle. In 1898, during the Spanish–American War, United States warships bombarded the fort. Puerto Rico passed to the United States following the war, and El Morro became part of Fort Brooke; it served in both World Wars before being transferred to the National Park Service in 1961.

Today

The fortress is now a museum. Its ramparts, sentry boxes (garitas), lighthouse and the broad green esplanade in front of it are iconic images of Puerto Rico; the lawn is popular for kite flying, and the garita has become a national symbol appearing on Puerto Rican license plates and the quarter coin.
