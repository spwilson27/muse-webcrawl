Jaisalmer

Jaisalmer is a city in the Indian state of Rajasthan, located in the heart of the Thar Desert. Known as the "Golden City" for its yellow sandstone architecture, it was founded in 1156 CE by the Bhati Rajput ruler Rawal Jaisal, who built its great fort.

History

Jaisalmer grew wealthy as a staging point on trade routes linking India with Persia, Arabia, and Central Asia, its merchants building ornate carved havelis such as Patwon Ki Haveli and Salim Singh Ki Haveli. The opening of sea trade through Bombay and the partition of India in 1947 diminished its commercial importance.

Tourism

Jaisalmer Fort, a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan, is one of the world's few living forts, with a substantial population residing within its walls. Attractions include Jain temples inside the fort, Gadisar Lake, and camel safaris to the Sam sand dunes.
