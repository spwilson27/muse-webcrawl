Llanos

The Llanos ("plains") are a vast tropical grassland region east of the Andes in northwestern South America, spanning eastern Colombia and western Venezuela across roughly 450,000 square kilometres, drained by the Orinoco River and its tributaries such as the Meta and Apure.

Landscape and climate

The Llanos are strongly seasonal: in the rainy season much of the low plain floods, while the dry season leaves it parched. Gallery forests line the rivers, and slightly raised ground supports palms and scattered trees. Wildlife includes capybaras, giant anteaters, Orinoco crocodiles, anacondas, pumas, scarlet ibis and huge numbers of waterbirds.

People and culture

The region is cattle country, worked by llaneros, the plains cowboys whose horsemanship, harp-driven joropo music and work songs (recognised by UNESCO) define a shared Colombian–Venezuelan culture. Llaneros famously fought in the independence wars under José Antonio Páez. Oil production in both countries and expanding agriculture have transformed parts of the plains in recent decades.
