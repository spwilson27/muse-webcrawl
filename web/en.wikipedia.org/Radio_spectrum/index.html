Radio spectrum

The radio spectrum is divided into bands from extremely low frequency to extremely high frequency.

The radio spectrum is the part of the electromagnetic spectrum with frequencies from 3 Hz to 3,000 GHz (3 THz). Electromagnetic waves in this range, called radio waves, are used in radio communication, broadcasting, radar, navigation and many other technologies. Elongated wavelengths range from about 100,000 kilometres at the lowest frequencies to about 0.1 millimetre at the highest.

Bands

The spectrum is conventionally divided into bands by decade of frequency: ELF (3–30 Hz), SLF, ULF, VLF, LF (30–300 kHz, longwave), MF (300 kHz–3 MHz, medium wave, including AM broadcasting), HF (3–30 MHz, shortwave), VHF (30–300 MHz, including FM broadcasting), UHF (300 MHz–3 GHz, television and mobile phones), SHF (3–30 GHz, radar, satellite links, Wi-Fi) and EHF (30–300 GHz).

Regulation

Because radio waves cross borders and interfere with one another, spectrum use is coordinated internationally by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) and licensed by national regulators such as the FCC in the United States and Ofcom in the United Kingdom. Allocations divide the spectrum among services, and valuable bands are sometimes auctioned. Some bands are designated for unlicensed use (the ISM bands used by Wi-Fi and Bluetooth).
