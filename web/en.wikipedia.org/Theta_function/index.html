Theta function

In mathematics, theta functions are special functions of several complex variables, first studied by Carl Gustav Jacobi. The Jacobi theta function ϑ(z; τ) is a Fourier series summed over all integers, periodic in z with a quasi-periodicity in the lattice generated by 1 and τ.

Properties

Theta functions satisfy remarkable transformation laws under the modular group, making them central to the theory of elliptic functions — every elliptic function can be expressed through them — and to modular forms. Jacobi's triple product identity expresses ϑ as an infinite product and yields combinatorial results such as Euler's pentagonal number theorem.

Applications

The Riemann theta function generalises the construction to higher-genus Riemann surfaces. Theta functions appear in the heat equation, number theory, string theory, and in explicit solutions of integrable systems.
