Pringles

Pringles is an American brand of stackable potato-based crisps. Originally sold by Procter & Gamble (P&G) in 1968 and marketed as "Pringle's Newfangled Potato Chips", the brand was sold in 2012 to Kellogg's, whose snack business later became Kellanova. Pringles are sold in more than 140 countries and were the fourth most popular snack brand worldwide as of 2012.

History

Procter & Gamble tasked Fredric Baur with developing a uniformly shaped crisp that would not break in the bag; Baur devised the saddle-shaped (hyperbolic paraboloid) chip made from a dough of dehydrated potato, rice flour, and wheat starch, fried in moulds, and packaged in a cylindrical can — a design for which Baur is famous (he was later buried in a Pringles can). Gene Wolfe developed the machine that cooks them. Alexander Liepa is credited with improving the flavour. Pringles were test-marketed in 1968 and launched nationally in the US in 1971.

The "potato chip" question

Because Pringles contain only about 42% potato, P&G argued in the UK in 2008–09 that they were not potato crisps subject to VAT, but a savoury snack; the Court of Appeal ultimately ruled they are made from potato flour "in a sense" and are subject to tax. In the US, the FDA required the product to be called "chips" made from dried potatoes.

Flavours and marketing

Standard flavours include Original, Sour Cream & Onion, Cheddar Cheese, Barbecue, Salt & Vinegar, and Pizza, with many regional and limited-edition varieties. The brand's slogan "Once you pop, you can't stop" (and variants) and its moustached mascot, refreshed in 2020, are widely recognised.
