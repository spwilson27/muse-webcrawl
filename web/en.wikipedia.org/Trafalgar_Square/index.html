Trafalgar Square is a public square in the City of Westminster, Central London, laid out in the early 19th century around the area formerly known as Charing Cross. It takes its name from the Battle of Trafalgar, the British naval victory in the Napoleonic Wars over France and Spain that took place on 21 October 1805 off the coast of Cape Trafalgar.

The square has been a significant landmark since the 13th century, when the area housed the King's Mews. Today it is dominated by Nelson's Column, a 52-metre (169 ft) monument guarded by four bronze lions, and is bordered on its north side by the National Gallery. The square is a major tourist attraction and a traditional site for political demonstrations, celebrations, and public gatherings.

History

The site was the location of the royal stables, the King's Mews, from the reign of Edward I. In the 1820s, the architect John Nash redesigned the area, but the square's present layout is largely the work of Sir Charles Barry, completed in 1845. The square was designed to commemorate Admiral Horatio Nelson, who died at the Battle of Trafalgar, and Nelson's Column was erected in 1843.

During the 20th century the square became strongly associated with political protest, including the Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament marches and the 1990 poll tax riots. In 2003, the north side of the square was pedestrianised as part of the World Squares for All project.

Monuments and statues

Nelson's Column

Nelson's Column, completed in 1843, is a granite Corinthian column topped by a statue of Admiral Lord Nelson. At its base sit four bronze lions, designed by Sir Edwin Landseer and cast from cannon captured from the French fleet.

The plinths

Three of the square's four plinths hold statues of generals and an admiral: Major General Sir Henry Havelock, General Sir Charles Napier, and an equestrian statue of King George IV. The fourth plinth, originally intended for a statue of William IV, stood empty for over 150 years and since 1999 has hosted a rotating programme of commissioned contemporary artworks.

Fountains

The square's two fountains were designed by Sir Edwin Lutyens in 1939, replacing earlier fountains by Barry. They commemorate Admirals Jellicoe and Beatty of the First World War.

Events

Trafalgar Square hosts annual celebrations including a Christmas tree gifted each year by Norway, New Year's Eve gatherings, and the national Remembrance commemorations nearby. It is also famous for its pigeons, though feeding them was banned in the early 2000s.
