Theory of computation

The theory of computation is the branch of computer science and mathematics that studies what problems can be solved by computation, how efficiently, and with what abstract models of machines.

Branches

Automata theory studies machines such as finite automata, pushdown automata, and Turing machines, and the classes of languages they recognise. Computability theory asks which functions are computable at all; the halting problem is provably undecidable. Complexity theory classifies problems by resource use, most famously the classes P and NP and the open question whether P = NP.

Foundations

The field was founded in the 1930s by Alan Turing, Alonzo Church, Kurt Gödel, and others. The Church–Turing thesis holds that any effectively calculable function can be computed by a Turing machine.
