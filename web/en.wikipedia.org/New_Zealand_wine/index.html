New Zealand wine

New Zealand wine is produced in several distinct regions, mostly on the country's two main islands, and is internationally renowned above all for Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc. Although New Zealand produces well under one percent of the world's wine, its cool maritime climate yields intensely aromatic wines that command premium prices.

History

Vines were planted as early as 1819 by Samuel Marsden, and the Croatian gum-digger families of West Auckland founded enduring wineries in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The modern industry took off from the 1970s, when Montana and others planted Sauvignon Blanc in Marlborough; Cloudy Bay's 1985 vintage brought global acclaim.

Regions and grapes

Marlborough, at the top of the South Island, accounts for the majority of plantings, dominated by Sauvignon Blanc. Other significant regions include Hawke's Bay (Chardonnay, Bordeaux blends), Central Otago (famed for Pinot Noir), Gisborne, Wairarapa (Martinborough Pinot Noir), Nelson, Canterbury and Auckland. Pinot Noir is the leading red variety, followed by Merlot and Syrah.

Industry

Exports are a major earner, with the United States, United Kingdom and Australia the main markets. Nearly all producers belong to Sustainable Winegrowing New Zealand, an industry sustainability programme.
