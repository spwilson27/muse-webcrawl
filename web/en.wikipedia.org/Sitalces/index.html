Sitalces

Sitalces King of the Odrysian kingdom of Thrace
Reign: c. 431 – 424 BC
Predecessor: Sparatocos
Successor: Seuthes I
Died: 424 BC

Sitalces (Ancient Greek: Σιτάλκης) was king of the Odrysian kingdom of Thrace from about 431 until his death in 424 BC. The son of Teres I, the founder of the Odrysian state, Sitalces expanded the kingdom into the largest empire in the northern Balkans, and his reign is described in detail by the historian Thucydides in the History of the Peloponnesian War.

Reign

Sitalces inherited a kingdom built up by his father Teres in the eastern Balkans and extended Odrysian power over a territory stretching from the Danube to the Aegean and Propontis. Thucydides reports that the annual revenue of the kingdom, in tribute and presents, amounted to about 400 talents of silver, and that its military strength was formidable: Sitalces could reportedly field up to 150,000 men.

At the outbreak of the Peloponnesian War in 431 BC, Sitalces allied himself with Athens. His son Sadocus was granted Athenian citizenship. In 429 BC Sitalces launched a great invasion of Macedonia and Chalcidice with a huge army, in support of his brother-in-law Amyntas's claim to the Macedonian throne, but withdrew after about thirty days when provisions ran short and the expected Athenian naval support failed to arrive.

Death

In 424 BC Sitalces was killed in battle against the Triballi, a Thracian people of the north-west. He was succeeded by his nephew Seuthes I, son of Sparatocos, under whom the Odrysian kingdom reached its greatest extent before fragmenting in the following generations.
