Amortization (accounting)

In accounting, amortization is a method of lowering the cost value of an intangible asset over its useful life. It is analogous to depreciation, which applies to tangible assets. Amortization is also used for the gradual repayment of a loan in equal installments over time, in which case each payment covers interest and a portion of principal.

Intangible assets

Intangible assets with finite useful lives — such as patents, copyrights, and licenses — are amortized systematically, most commonly by the straight-line method, so that the expense recognized each period matches the consumption of the asset's benefits. Goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles are not amortized under US GAAP but are tested annually for impairment.

Loan amortization

An amortization schedule for a loan shows how each fixed payment is divided between interest and principal, with the interest share declining as the outstanding balance falls. Mortgages and auto loans are typical examples.

Tax treatment

Many jurisdictions allow amortization deductions for business intangibles, spreading the deduction over a fixed statutory period, such as 15 years under Section 197 of the US Internal Revenue Code.
