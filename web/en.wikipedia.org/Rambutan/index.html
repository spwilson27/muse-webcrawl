Rambutan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The rambutan (Nephelium lappaceum) is a medium-sized tropical tree in the family Sapindaceae. The name also refers to the edible fruit produced by this tree. The rambutan is native to Southeast Asia and is cultivated throughout the region, including Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines, as well as in parts of Central America and other tropical areas. It is closely related to several other edible tropical fruits including the lychee, the longan, the pulasan and the mamoncillo.

Description

The rambutan is an evergreen tree growing to a height of 12–20 metres (39–66 ft). The leaves are alternate, pinnately compound, with three to eleven leaflets. The flowers are small and apetalous, borne in branching panicles. Trees may be male, female (hermaphroditic flowers functioning as female), or hermaphroditic producing some male flowers; commercial orchards typically plant grafted hermaphrodite trees.

The fruit is a round to oval single-seeded drupe, 3–6 cm long, covered in fleshy pliable spines or "hairs" — from which it takes its name, derived from the Malay word rambut meaning "hair". The fruit's skin ranges from green through yellow to bright red or orange-red when ripe. Inside is a translucent, whitish, sweet and mildly acidic flesh surrounding a single glossy brown seed. The seed is poisonous raw but can be eaten cooked in some regions; the flesh is eaten fresh, canned in syrup, or made into jam.

Cultivation

Rambutan trees require a hot, humid tropical climate with well-distributed rainfall and are frost-sensitive. They are propagated commercially by grafting, air-layering and budding, and grafted trees may bear fruit after two to three years. Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia are major producers, and the fruit is exported fresh to markets in Asia, Europe and North America, although its short shelf life — the spines and skin brown quickly after harvest — limits long-distance trade in the fresh form.

Nutrition

Rambutan flesh is a source of vitamin C, copper, manganese and other micronutrients, and consists mostly of water and carbohydrate, with small amounts of fibre. Studies have also examined antioxidant compounds, including geraniin, found in the fruit's peel.
