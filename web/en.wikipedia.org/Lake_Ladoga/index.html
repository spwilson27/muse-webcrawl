Lake Ladoga (Russian: Ладожское озеро; Finnish: Laatokka) is a freshwater lake located in the Republic of Karelia and Leningrad Oblast in northwestern Russia, in the vicinity of Saint Petersburg. It is the largest lake entirely in Europe and the 14th largest freshwater lake by area in the world.

Geography

The lake has an average depth of about 51 metres and a maximum of 230 metres. Around 660 islands dot its waters, many along the northern skerry coast. The most famous are the Valaam archipelago, home to the 14th-century Valaam Monastery, Konevets, and Kizhi in nearby Lake Onega's cultural orbit. Major inflow rivers include the Svir (from Lake Onega), the Volkhov (from Lake Ilmen), and the Vuoksi. The Neva River carries Ladoga's waters to the Gulf of Finland at Saint Petersburg.

History

In the Middle Ages Ladoga was a central segment of the trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks, linking Scandinavia with Constantinople. The fortress town of Staraya Ladoga near the Volkhov mouth is sometimes called Russia's first capital. The lake's shores were contested between Sweden and Novgorod, and later Russia, for centuries.

During the Second World War, the ice road across frozen Lake Ladoga — the "Road of Life" — was the only supply route into besieged Leningrad during the winters of 1941–44, carrying food in and evacuating over a million civilians.

Ecology

The lake holds some 60 species of fish, including Ladoga smelt and the endangered Ladoga subspecies of the ringed seal, one of the few freshwater seals in the world.
