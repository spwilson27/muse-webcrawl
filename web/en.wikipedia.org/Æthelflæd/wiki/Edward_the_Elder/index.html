Edward the Elder (c. 874 – 17 July 924) was king of the Anglo-Saxons from 899 until his death. The eldest son of Alfred the Great, he inherited the war against the Danish Vikings and, campaigning in close alliance with his sister Æthelflæd, Lady of the Mercians, conquered the southern Danelaw and brought the Midlands under West Saxon rule.

Reign

Edward's succession was challenged by his cousin Æthelwold, whose revolt (899–902) ended with Æthelwold's death at the Battle of the Holme. From about 909 Edward and Æthelflæd pursued an offensive strategy, building burhs deep in Danish territory — at places including Hertford, Buckingham, Bedford, and Stafford — and defeating Danish armies in the field. After Æthelflæd's death in 918, Edward took direct control of Mercia and deposed her daughter Ælfwynn.

By 918 all the Danes south of the Humber had submitted, and in 920 (according to the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle) the kings of Scotland and Strathclyde and the Norse king of York acknowledged his overlordship at Bakewell — a claim modern historians treat with caution.

Family and death

Edward had some fourteen children by three marriages, including the kings Æthelstan, Edmund I, and Eadred. He died at Farndon in Cheshire in 924, shortly after suppressing a Mercian and Welsh revolt, and was buried in the New Minster at Winchester.
