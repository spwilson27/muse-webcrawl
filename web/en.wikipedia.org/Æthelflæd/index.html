Æthelflæd, eldest daughter of Alfred the Great.

Æthelflæd (c. 870 – 12 June 918), known as the Lady of the Mercians, was ruler of Mercia in the English Midlands from 911 until her death. The eldest child of Alfred the Great, king of Wessex, and his Mercian wife Ealhswith, she played a central part in the reconquest of the Danelaw from the Vikings.

Married to Æthelred, Lord of the Mercians, she governed jointly with her husband during his final illness and succeeded him in 911 — a rare instance of a woman ruling an English kingdom in her own right. In alliance with her brother Edward the Elder of Wessex, she built a chain of fortified burhs across the Midlands — including Tamworth, Stafford, Warwick, Runcorn and Chirbury — and led campaigns that recovered Derby and Leicester from Danish rule.

Death and legacy

She died at Tamworth in June 918, just before a planned joint offensive, and was buried at St Oswald's Priory, Gloucester, which she had founded. Her daughter Ælfwynn briefly succeeded her before Edward deposed her and brought Mercia under direct West Saxon rule, completing a key stage in the unification of England. Long overshadowed by her father and brother in the chronicles, Æthelflæd has been celebrated by modern historians as one of the most effective rulers of Anglo-Saxon England; statues stand at Tamworth and Gloucester.
