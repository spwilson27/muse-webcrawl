GameCube

The GameCube (GCN) is a home video game console developed and marketed by Nintendo. Released in Japan in September 2001 and in North America that November, it was Nintendo's entry in the sixth generation of consoles, competing with Sony's PlayStation 2, Microsoft's Xbox, and Sega's Dreamcast. It succeeded the Nintendo 64 and was succeeded by the Wii in 2006.

Hardware

The compact cube-shaped console used a PowerPC-based CPU (IBM's "Gekko") and an ATI graphics processor ("Flipper"). In a first for Nintendo, games were distributed on proprietary 8 cm miniDVD-based optical discs holding 1.5 GB — a choice that limited storage compared with rivals' full-size DVDs and was intended partly to deter piracy. The console supported up to four controllers, memory cards, progressive scan output on later cables, and peripherals such as the Game Boy Player, which played Game Boy cartridges on a television.

Games and reception

Its library exceeded 600 games, including acclaimed titles such as Super Smash Bros. Melee, The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker, Metroid Prime, Resident Evil 4, Pikmin, and Animal Crossing. Despite praise for its games and controller, the GameCube sold about 21.7 million units worldwide — fewer than its competitors — and support ended in 2007. It is now regarded fondly by enthusiasts as a distinctive, game-focused machine.
