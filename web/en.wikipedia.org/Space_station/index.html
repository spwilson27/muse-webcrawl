PurposeResearch, habitation, technology testing

A space station is a spacecraft designed to remain in orbit for extended periods and to support a human crew, serving as a home, laboratory and observation platform in space. Unlike other spacecraft, stations lack major propulsion and landing capability and are reached by separate crew and cargo vehicles.

History

The Soviet Union launched the first space station, Salyut 1, in 1971, followed by the Salyut series, the United States' Skylab (1973), and the much larger Mir (1986–2001), assembled in orbit from modules. The International Space Station (ISS), a collaboration of five space agencies, has been continuously occupied since November 2000. China's Tiangong station was completed in 2022.

Purpose and research

Stations provide a long-duration microgravity environment for research in biology, medicine, physics, materials science and astronomy that cannot be duplicated on Earth. They also test life-support, recycling and radiation-protection technologies needed for future missions to the Moon and Mars.

Operations

Crews typically serve expeditions of about six months. Visiting vehicles deliver supplies, fuel and experiments, and stations require periodic reboosts to counteract orbital decay from atmospheric drag.
