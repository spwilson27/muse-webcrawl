John Champlin Gardner Jr. (July 21, 1933 – September 14, 1982) was an American novelist, essayist, literary critic and university professor. He is best known for his novel Grendel (1971), a retelling of the Beowulf legend from the monster's point of view, and for his influential books on writing, The Art of Fiction and On Moral Fiction.

Life and career

Gardner grew up on a farm near Batavia, New York. The accidental death of his younger brother in a farm accident in 1945 haunted his writing throughout his life. He studied at DePauw University and the University of Iowa, where he earned a PhD in medieval literature. He taught at many universities, most notably Southern Illinois University and Binghamton University, and was a legendary, demanding teacher of young writers, including Raymond Carver.

Works

His novels include The Resurrection (1966), The Wreckage of Agathon (1970), The Sunlight Dialogues (1972), Nickel Mountain (1973), October Light (1976, winner of the National Book Critics Circle Award) and Mickelsson's Ghosts (1982). In On Moral Fiction (1978) he argued that fiction should be moral in purpose and attacked many of his contemporaries, provoking lasting controversy. He also wrote libretti, children's stories, biographies of Chaucer, and translations of medieval texts.

Death

Gardner died in a motorcycle accident near his home in Pennsylvania in September 1982, a few days before he was to be married.
