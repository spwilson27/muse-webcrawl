Sucker (botany)

In botany, a sucker is a plant shoot that arises from a bud at the base of a tree or shrub, or from its roots. Suckers growing from the base of a grafted plant may originate from the rootstock rather than the desired cultivar (scion), and are generally removed because they can outgrow and differ from the grafted variety. The capacity to sucker — producing stems from root buds some distance from the parent — allows many species to spread clonally and to regenerate after damage such as fire or felling.

Horticultural practice

Gardeners commonly prune suckers from grafted roses, fruit trees, and lilacs, cutting them away at their point of origin. In tomato cultivation, "suckers" are the side shoots that emerge from leaf axils; many growers pinch them out to direct the plant's energy into fruit production, a matter of ongoing debate among gardeners.

Ecological role

Suckering species such as aspen, black locust, and sumac form clonal colonies; the famous Pando aspen colony in Utah consists of thousands of stems joined by a single root system. Suckers from roots can also make some ornamentals, such as raspberry and tree-of-heaven, aggressive spreaders in gardens.
