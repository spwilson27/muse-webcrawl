Jhansi

Jhansi is a historic city in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh, in the Bundelkhand region on the banks of the Pahuj River. It lies about 415 kilometres (258 mi) south of New Delhi and is an important road and rail junction. Jhansi is best known as the seat of Rani Lakshmibai, the queen who became one of the most celebrated leaders of the Indian Rebellion of 1857.

History

Jhansi Fort was built in 1613 by the Bundela Rajput chief Bir Singh Deo of Orchha. The town later came under Maratha rule; its ruler Naru Shankar built the extended fort and the city below. In the 19th century the state of Jhansi was annexed by the British East India Company in 1853 under the Doctrine of Lapse after the death of Maharaja Gangadhar Rao, whose widow, Rani Lakshmibai, disputed the annexation. During the rebellion of 1857 the Rani defended the city and died fighting near Gwalior in June 1858.

Geography and transport

Jhansi stands on rocky ground typical of Bundelkhand and has a hot semi-arid climate with very hot summers. Jhansi Junction railway station is a major stop on the Delhi–Chennai and Delhi–Mumbai lines, and national highways link the city to Kanpur, Lucknow, and Gwalior.
