Medical drama

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A medical drama is a television programme or film in which events centre upon a hospital, an ambulance staff or a medical environment. In the United States, most medical dramas run for an hour and are set in a hospital. The genre typically combines case-of-the-week medical storylines with continuing arcs about the professional and personal lives of doctors, nurses and other staff.

History

Early examples on American television included Dr. Kildare and Ben Casey in the 1960s, followed by M*A*S*H, St. Elsewhere and later ER, which popularised fast-paced emergency-room storytelling in the 1990s. Twenty-first-century hits include House, Grey's Anatomy, Scrubs (which mixes comedy and drama) and The Good Doctor.

Common themes

Medical dramas commonly feature ethical dilemmas, rare diagnoses, resource allocation, workplace romance and the emotional toll of clinical work. Medical advisers are often employed to check the plausibility of procedures and terminology, though dramatic licence remains common.

Around the world

The format has been produced in many countries, including British series such as Casualty and Holby City, and popular dramas in Japan, South Korea and India.
