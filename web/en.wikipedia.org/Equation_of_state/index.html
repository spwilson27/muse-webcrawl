Equation of state

In physics and chemistry, an equation of state is a thermodynamic equation relating state variables which describe the state of matter under a given set of physical conditions, such as pressure, volume, temperature, or internal energy. Most modern equations of state are formulated in the Helmholtz free energy. Equations of state are useful in describing the properties of pure substances and mixtures in liquids, gases, and solid states, as well as the state of matter in the interior of stars.

Ideal gas law

The simplest known equation of state is the ideal gas law, PV = nRT, relating the pressure P, volume V, temperature T and amount of substance n of an ideal gas through the gas constant R. It is accurate for gases at low pressures and moderate temperatures, but becomes increasingly inaccurate at higher pressures and lower temperatures.

Real gas equations

The van der Waals equation (1873) introduced corrections for the finite volume of molecules and for intermolecular attraction, and was the first equation able to describe gas–liquid transitions and critical behaviour. Later cubic equations of state include the Redlich–Kwong equation (1949), the Soave modification (1972), and the Peng–Robinson equation (1976), all widely used in chemical engineering process simulation.

Other equations of state

Equations of state exist for solids (such as the Birch–Murnaghan equation), for water and steam (IAPWS formulations), for stellar interiors, and for the cosmological equation of state relating pressure and energy density in cosmology, where the dark energy component has a ratio close to w = −1.
