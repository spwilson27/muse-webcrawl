Rosaceae

Rosaceae, the rose family, is a family of flowering plants comprising around 90–100 genera and roughly 3,000–4,800 species of herbs, shrubs, and trees. It is one of the most economically important plant families, including many familiar fruit crops such as apples, pears, peaches, plums, cherries, strawberries, raspberries, and almonds, as well as ornamental plants such as roses.

Description

Members of Rosaceae typically have alternate, often stipulate leaves and flowers with five sepals and five petals (though the number varies), numerous stamens, and a characteristic floral cup called a hypanthium. Fruit types are remarkably diverse: pomes (apple, pear), drupes (peach, cherry), aggregate fruits (raspberry), achenes on a fleshy receptacle (strawberry), and follicles or capsules in other groups.

Taxonomy and subfamilies

Modern classifications based on molecular data recognise three subfamilies: Rosoideae (including Rosa, Fragaria and Rubus), Dryadoideae, and Amygdaloideae (including Malus, Prunus, Pyrus and Spiraea). The family belongs to the order Rosales within the rosid clade of flowering plants.

Distribution

The family is distributed worldwide, though it is most diverse in the temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. Rosaceae species occupy habitats from woodlands and grasslands to mountainsides.

Economic importance

Rosaceae includes many of the world's most important temperate fruit crops. The apple (Malus domestica) is among the most widely cultivated fruits on Earth. Stone fruits of the genus Prunus (peaches, cherries, plums, apricots, almonds), pears, quinces, loquats, strawberries, blackberries, and raspberries are all members. Roses (Rosa) are among the most popular ornamental garden plants and cut flowers, and rose hips and rose oil are used in food and perfumery. Timber species such as cherry are also valued.
