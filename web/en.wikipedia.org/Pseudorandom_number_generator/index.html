Pseudorandom number generator

A pseudorandom number generator (PRNG), also known as a deterministic random bit generator (DRBG), is an algorithm for generating a sequence of numbers whose properties approximate the properties of sequences of random numbers. The PRNG-generated sequence is not truly random, because it is completely determined by an initial value, called the PRNG's seed, which may include truly random components.

History

Deterministic generators date to John von Neumann's middle-square method of 1949. Lehmer's linear congruential generator (1951) became widely used, and the Mersenne Twister (1997) became the default generator in many programming languages because of its very long period of 219937 − 1 and good statistical properties.

Applications

PRNGs are central to computer simulations (such as Monte Carlo methods), procedural generation in games, statistical sampling, and randomised algorithms. For cryptography, ordinary PRNGs are inadequate; cryptographically secure pseudorandom number generators (CSPRNGs) are designed so that their output cannot be predicted without the seed, even after seeing previous outputs.

Testing

The quality of generators is assessed with statistical test suites such as Diehard, TestU01, and the NIST statistical test suite. Poor generators have caused real failures, including biased simulations and broken cryptographic systems where predictable seeds made keys recoverable.
