Retinal

Retinal (also known as retinaldehyde) is a polyene chromophore. Retinal, bound to proteins called opsins, is the chemical basis of visual phototransduction, the light-detection stage of visual perception (vision).

Structure of all-trans-retinal

Some microorganisms use retinal to convert light into metabolic energy. In fact, a recent study suggests that retinal was the dominant light absorber in early ocean ecosystems, before chlorophyll-based photosynthesis became widespread.

Retinal itself is considered a form of vitamin A when eaten by an animal. It can be converted to other forms of vitamin A, and the molecule that transports vitamin A in the skin is known as retinol-binding protein. There are many forms (isomers) of retinal which correspond to different states: the most important are 11-cis-retinal and all-trans-retinal. 11-cis-retinal is the chromophore of rhodopsin.

History

The German physiologist Franz Christian Boll observed in 1876 that the normally purple-red frog retina was bleached in bright light. In later work, Wilhelm Friedrich Kühne investigated the chemistry of this phenomenon, which he called "visual purple". George Wald received the 1967 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his work showing that vitamin A was crucial to vision and that the visual pigments contained retinal.

Visual cycle

Retinal allows certain microorganisms to convert light into metabolic energy. In animals, retinal is interconverted with other members of the retinoid family, and the visual cycle continuously regenerates 11-cis-retinal from all-trans-retinal after photoisomerisation. When a photon is absorbed by the 11-cis-retinal chromophore of rhodopsin, it isomerises within picoseconds to all-trans-retinal, triggering the conformational changes that initiate vision.

Retinal in other systems

All-trans-retinal is also a component of microbial rhodopsins such as bacteriorhodopsin and channelrhodopsin, which are used in optogenetics. Retinal is also oxidised to retinoic acid, a hormone-like signalling molecule that regulates gene expression and embryonic development.
