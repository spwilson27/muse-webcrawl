Proteomics

Proteomics is the large-scale study of proteomes — the entire set of proteins produced or modified by an organism, cell, or system, which varies with time, conditions, and cellular state. Coined as a counterpart to genomics, the field studies protein identity, abundance, modifications, interactions, and functions at a systems level.

Methods

The core technology of modern proteomics is mass spectrometry: proteins are typically digested into peptides (for example with trypsin), separated by liquid chromatography, and identified from their masses and fragmentation patterns ("bottom-up" or shotgun proteomics). Other approaches include two-dimensional gel electrophoresis, protein microarrays, and affinity-based platforms. Bioinformatics is essential for searching spectra against sequence databases and quantifying results.

Applications

Proteomics is used to discover disease biomarkers, map protein–protein interaction networks, characterize post-translational modifications such as phosphorylation, and compare healthy and diseased tissues to find drug targets.

Challenges

Unlike the genome, the proteome is dynamic and spans an enormous range of abundances; proteins cannot be amplified like DNA, making low-abundance species hard to detect.
