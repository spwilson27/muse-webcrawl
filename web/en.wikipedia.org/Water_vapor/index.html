Water vapor

Water vapor, water vapour or aqueous vapor is the gaseous phase of water. It is one state of water within the hydrosphere and can be produced by evaporation or boiling of liquid water, or by sublimation of ice. Water vapor is transparent and invisible; the visible clouds of "steam" are actually condensed liquid droplets.

Atmospheric role

Water vapor typically makes up a fraction of a percent up to about 4% of the atmosphere. It is the most important greenhouse gas, amplifying warming through a positive feedback, and its condensation releases latent heat that powers storms and drives the water cycle.

Measurement

Atmospheric water vapor content is expressed through humidity measures such as dew point, relative humidity, and specific humidity, and is observed by radiosondes, satellites and surface instruments.
