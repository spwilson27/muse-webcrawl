The Dadasaheb Phalke Award is India's highest award in the field of cinema, presented annually at the National Film Awards ceremony by the Directorate of Film Festivals. It honours recipients for their "outstanding contribution to the growth and development of Indian cinema" and is named after Dhundiraj Govind Phalke — Dadasaheb Phalke — regarded as the father of Indian cinema, who directed India's first full-length feature film, Raja Harishchandra (1913).

History and presentation

The award was instituted in 1969, on the occasion of Phalke's birth centenary. Actress Devika Rani was its first recipient. The award comprises a Swarna Kamal (Golden Lotus) medallion, a ceremonial shawl, and a cash prize (₹1,000,000 in recent years). It is conferred by the President of India as part of the National Film Awards.

Recipients

Distinguished recipients include Satyajit Ray (1984), Raj Kapoor (1987), Lata Mangeshkar (2001), Dev Anand (2002), Pran (2012), Gulzar (2013), Amitabh Bachchan (2018), Rajinikanth (2019), and Waheeda Rehman (2021 award). The recipients span actors, directors, composers, playback singers, and producers from across India's film industries.
