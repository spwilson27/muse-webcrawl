Heidelberg

Heidelberg is a city in the German state of Baden-Württemberg, situated on the river Neckar in southwestern Germany. With a population of about 160,000, it is famous for its partially ruined Renaissance castle, its baroque Old Town, and Heidelberg University, founded in 1386 and the oldest university in modern Germany. A centre of German Romanticism and a scientific hub, Heidelberg is one of Germany's most visited tourist destinations.

History

The area has been settled since prehistoric times — the jawbone of Homo heidelbergensis was found in nearby Mauer in 1907. The modern town grew beneath its castle in the 12th century and became the residence of the Counts Palatine of the Rhine. Heidelberg University was founded by Elector Rupert I in 1386. The city flourished as a Palatine capital but was devastated in the Thirty Years' War and again in 1689 and 1693 during the Nine Years' War, when French troops blew up the castle. Rebuilt in baroque style, the city became a symbol for the Romantics — Goethe, Hölderlin, Eichendorff and the painters of the Heidelberg School celebrated its setting. Largely spared bombing in the Second World War, it later hosted the headquarters of U.S. Army Europe until 2013.

Sights

Heidelberg Castle, a red-sandstone ruin above the Old Town, contains the Great Vat (Großes Fass, a wine barrel holding some 220,000 litres) and the German Pharmacy Museum. The Old Bridge (Karl Theodor Bridge, 1788) crosses the Neckar below. The Philosophenweg (Philosophers' Walk) on the opposite bank offers the classic view of castle and town, and the Königstuhl funicular climbs to the summit above. The Hauptstraße, one of Europe's longest pedestrian streets, runs the length of the Old Town past the Church of the Holy Spirit and the university's historic aula.

Science and economy

Heidelberg University and its medical school anchor a research cluster including the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL), the German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ), Max Planck institutes and the Heidelberg Institute for Theoretical Studies. Historic industries include printing machinery (Heidelberger Druckmaschinen) and publishing (Springer Nature). Tourism and conferences add to the service economy.
