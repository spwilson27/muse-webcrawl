Ayodhya dispute

[Image placeholder: the Ram Mandir in Ayodhya]

The Ayodhya dispute is a political, historical and socio-religious debate in India centred on a plot of land in the city of Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh. Hindus regard the site as the birthplace of the deity Rama (Ram Janmabhoomi), and a mosque, the Babri Masjid, built there in the sixteenth century, was claimed to have replaced a demolished temple.

Background

The Babri Masjid was commissioned in 1528–29 by Mir Baqi, a commander of the Mughal emperor Babur. Hindu belief holds that it stood on the site of a temple marking Rama's birthplace. Communal tensions over the site produced violence as early as the 1850s, and in 1949 idols of Rama were placed inside the mosque, which was then locked by court order.

Demolition and aftermath

The dispute became a mass political movement in the 1980s. On 6 December 1992 a large crowd of Hindu activists demolished the mosque, triggering nationwide riots in which about 2,000 people died. Title suits over the land continued for decades.

Supreme Court verdict

In 2010 the Allahabad High Court divided the disputed land three ways. On appeal, on 9 November 2019, a five-judge bench of the Supreme Court of India awarded the entire disputed site for the construction of a Hindu temple, while directing that five acres elsewhere in Ayodhya be allotted for a mosque. The Ram Mandir was consecrated on 22 January 2024.
