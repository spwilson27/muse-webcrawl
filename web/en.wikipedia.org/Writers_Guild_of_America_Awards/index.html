The Writers Guild of America Awards is an award ceremony honouring outstanding achievement in screen, television, radio and video-game writing. They have been presented annually since 1949 by the Writers Guild of America, East and the Writers Guild of America West, the two unions representing writers in the American entertainment industry.

Categories and eligibility

The principal film categories are Best Original Screenplay and Best Adapted Screenplay; television categories cover drama and comedy series, individual episodes, new series, animation, and long-form and children's programming, with additional awards for documentary, news and video-game writing. Eligibility is generally restricted to works produced under the Guild's collective bargaining agreements or those of affiliated international guilds, which sometimes excludes notable non-Guild films from competition.

Significance

Because the WGA's screenplay winners often correlate with the Academy Awards — particularly in the Original Screenplay category — the awards are closely watched during awards season, though eligibility differences mean the fields are not identical. Simultaneous ceremonies are held in Los Angeles and New York. Honorary awards include the Laurel Award for Screenwriting Achievement and the Paddy Chayefsky Laurel Award for television.
