Wavelength

Symbol: λ (lambda)
SI unit: metre
Relation: λ = v / f

In physics, the wavelength is the spatial period of a periodic wave — the distance over which the wave's shape repeats. It is usually measured as the distance between corresponding points on consecutive cycles, such as crest to crest or trough to trough, and is denoted by the Greek letter lambda (λ).

Relation to frequency

Wavelength is inversely related to frequency: λ = v / f, where v is the wave's speed and f its frequency. For electromagnetic waves in a vacuum, v is the speed of light, so visible light has wavelengths of about 380–750 nanometres, radio waves range from millimetres to kilometres, and gamma rays have wavelengths smaller than atoms.

Media and effects

When a wave enters a medium in which it travels more slowly, its wavelength decreases while its frequency stays constant; this underlies refraction and the concept of refractive index. Waves whose size is comparable to their wavelength undergo noticeable diffraction around obstacles. In quantum mechanics, matter also exhibits wavelength, given by the de Broglie relation λ = h / p.
