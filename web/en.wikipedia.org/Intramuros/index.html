Intramuros (Latin for "within the walls") is the 0.67-square-kilometre (0.26 sq mi) historic walled area within the city of Manila, the capital of the Philippines. It is administered by the Intramuros Administration, an agency of the Department of Tourism created in 1979 under Presidential Decree No. 1616.

The fortifications of Intramuros, called the Fortificaciones de Manila, were built beginning in the late 16th century by the Spanish colonial government to protect the city against foreign invasion. The present structure is the result of continuous development and rebuilding over several centuries, including after the extensive destruction of the Battle of Manila in 1945.

History

The site was originally a Muslim settlement led by Rajah Sulayman near the mouth of the Pasig River. Spanish forces under Miguel López de Legazpi arrived in 1571, and Manila was formally established on 24 June 1571. Defensive walls of wood and earth were progressively replaced with stone fortifications designed according to contemporary European military architecture, featuring bastions, ravelins, redoubts, and a moat.

The walled city was the seat of government and the political, educational, and religious center of the Spanish East Indies for over three centuries. Inside the walls stood the Manila Cathedral, San Agustin Church, government offices, schools, convents, and the homes of elite families. The British briefly occupied Manila in 1762–1764. During the American colonial period, the moat was filled in and converted into a golf course, and parts of the walls were breached for roads.

World War II and restoration

Intramuros was almost entirely destroyed during the Battle of Manila in February 1945, one of the most destructive urban battles of the Second World War, in which an estimated 100,000 Filipino civilians died. Of the structures within the walls, only San Agustin Church survived largely intact. Post-war restoration began in the 1950s and was systematized with the creation of the Intramuros Administration in 1979.

Landmarks

Major landmarks include Fort Santiago, a citadel at the mouth of the Pasig River and the site of José Rizal's imprisonment before his execution in 1896; the Manila Cathedral; San Agustin Church, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the oldest stone church in the Philippines; Casa Manila, a museum recreating a 19th-century colonial house; and Bahay Tsinoy, a museum of Chinese Filipino heritage.

Intramuros is one of the most popular tourist destinations in Manila, drawing visitors to its cobbled streets, horse-drawn kalesa carriages, and preserved colonial architecture.
