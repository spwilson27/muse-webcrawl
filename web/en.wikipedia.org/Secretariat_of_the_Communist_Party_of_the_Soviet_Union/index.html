Secretariat of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union


Central Committee headquarters, Staraya Square, Moscow

The Secretariat of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union (CPSU) was the executive arm of the Central Committee, responsible for the day-to-day administration of the party apparatus. It supervised the Central Committee departments, monitored the implementation of party decisions, and managed cadre selection — powers that made it one of the most influential organs of the Soviet system.

History

The Secretariat was established in 1919, following the Eighth Party Congress, as the party grew beyond the capacity of the Politburo and Orgburo to manage routine business. Originally a purely administrative body, it became a center of power under Joseph Stalin, who as General Secretary (from 1922) used control of appointments through the Secretariat to build his dominance over the party. After 1953 the office of First Secretary (later General Secretary again from 1966) remained the post of the Soviet leader.

Functions

The Secretariat directed the work of the Central Committee's departments, which mirrored the ministries and spheres of Soviet life — industry, agriculture, ideology, international affairs, and the military. Members of the Secretariat (secretaries of the Central Committee) typically each supervised a portfolio of departments; the Second Secretary, often the General Secretary's deputy, coordinated the body. The Secretariat administered the nomenklatura system of appointments to key posts throughout the state.

Relation to other organs

Formally subordinate to the Central Committee and overshadowed in prestige by the Politburo, which decided policy, the Secretariat implemented policy and controlled personnel — in practice often determining outcomes. The body was abolished along with the party's leading role in 1991 during the dissolution of the Soviet Union.
