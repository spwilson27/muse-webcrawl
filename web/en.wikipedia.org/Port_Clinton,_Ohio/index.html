Port Clinton, Ohio

Port Clinton is a city in and the county seat of Ottawa County, Ohio, United States. Located on the south shore of Lake Erie at the mouth of the Portage River, it had a population of about 6,000 at the 2020 census. Together with the surrounding Lake Erie Islands and shoreline communities, Port Clinton is part of a major resort region nicknamed "Vacationland", and styles itself the "Walleye Capital of the World".

History

Port Clinton was founded in 1828 and named in honour of DeWitt Clinton, the New York governor associated with the Erie Canal. It grew slowly at first — only 105 residents were recorded in 1846 — but developed as a fishing, boatbuilding, and shipping port, and later as a tourism centre. The city was incorporated in 1875. During the 19th century it hosted numerous commercial fishing fleets; the surrounding waters of western Lake Erie remain among the most productive freshwater fisheries in the world.

Tourism and events

Port Clinton is a gateway to the Lake Erie Islands, with ferry services to Put-in-Bay on South Bass Island departing from nearby Catawba Island. Its best-known event is the annual Walleye Drop on New Year's Eve, when a 20-foot, 600-pound fibreglass walleye named "Wylie" is lowered at midnight — a tradition begun in 1996 — along with the Walleye Festival held over Memorial Day weekend. Nearby attractions include Marblehead Lighthouse, the African Safari Wildlife Park, and Camp Perry, the military installation west of the city that hosts the National Rifle and Pistol Matches.

Geography

The city covers about 2.3 square miles on the Marblehead Peninsula shore, at the transition between Lake Erie's main basin and its western basin, near Sandusky Bay.
