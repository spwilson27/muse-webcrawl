Epcot

Epcot, stylized in all caps as EPCOT, is a theme park at the Walt Disney World Resort in Bay Lake, Florida. It opened on October 1, 1982, as EPCOT Center, and was the second of four theme parks built at the resort. The park's name derives from Walt Disney's unrealized concept of the "Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow" — a planned city that was intended to serve as a testbed for urban living innovations.

Spaceship Earth, the 180-foot geodesic sphere that is Epcot's icon

Layout

The park is organized around a central area (originally Future World, now divided into World Celebration, World Discovery, and World Nature) devoted to technology and innovation, and the World Showcase, a ring of pavilions representing eleven countries — including Mexico, Norway, China, Germany, Italy, the United States, Japan, Morocco, France, the United Kingdom, and Canada — arranged around the World Showcase Lagoon.

Attractions

Signature attractions include Spaceship Earth, housed inside the park's iconic geodesic sphere; Test Track; Soarin' Around the World; Mission: SPACE; and Guardians of the Galaxy: Cosmic Rewind, one of the world's largest enclosed roller coasters. The park hosts popular annual festivals, including the International Flower & Garden Festival and the International Food & Wine Festival.

History

After Walt Disney's death in 1966, his city concept was abandoned, but the name and spirit were carried into the theme park developed in the late 1970s. Epcot has undergone several major updates, including a multi-year transformation completed in the early 2020s.
