Low-cost carrier

Ryanair of Ireland is Europe's largest low-cost carrier by passengers.

A low-cost carrier (LCC) or low-cost airline, also called a no-frills, budget or discount carrier, is an airline that is operated with an emphasis on minimizing operating costs. It sacrifices certain traditional airline services, such as assigned seating, free meals and multiple cabin classes, to offer lower fares and higher flight frequencies.

Business model

Typical features of the model include a single aircraft type (usually the Boeing 737 or Airbus A320 family) to reduce training and maintenance costs; a single-class, high-density cabin; short-haul, point-to-point routes between secondary or less congested airports; fast turnarounds; direct online sales; and unbundled pricing, charging extra for checked baggage, seat selection, food and priority boarding. Ancillary revenue from such fees is a major part of the model.

History

The model was pioneered by Southwest Airlines in the United States from the 1970s. After deregulation, it spread to Europe with Ryanair and easyJet in the 1990s, then to Asia (AirAsia, IndiGo), Latin America, the Middle East and Africa. Notable low-cost carriers include Ryanair, easyJet, Wizz Air, Southwest, JetBlue, Spirit, AirAsia, IndiGo and Vueling.
