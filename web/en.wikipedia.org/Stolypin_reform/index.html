Stolypin reform

Pyotr Stolypin, architect of the reforms

The Stolypin reform was a series of changes in the agrarian sector of the Russian Empire, implemented by Prime Minister Pyotr Stolypin between 1906 and 1911. Aimed at transforming the traditional obshchina (peasant commune) system, the reforms allowed peasants to withdraw from the commune, consolidate their scattered strips into private farms, and obtain title to their land.

Background

The Revolution of 1905 and widespread peasant unrest exposed the instability of the communal land system. Stolypin sought to create a class of prosperous, independent peasant proprietors — his famous "wager on the strong" — who would anchor rural conservatism and raise agricultural productivity.

Measures

The decree of 9 November 1906 permitted heads of households to claim their communal allotments as private property. Peasant Land Bank loans financed purchases, state lands were sold to peasants, and resettlement to Siberia was encouraged. Consolidated farms took two forms: the khutor, an isolated farmstead on one's own land, and the otrub, a consolidated plot with the farmhouse remaining in the village.

Results and legacy

By 1916 roughly a fifth to a quarter of communal households had taken title, and agricultural output rose. The reform ended with Stolypin's assassination in 1911 and the upheavals of war and revolution, and remains debated by historians as a possible lost alternative to collectivization.
