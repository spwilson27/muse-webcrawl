Tankōbon

Tankōbon (Japanese: 単行本, "independent/standalone book") is the Japanese term for a book that is not part of an anthology or corpus. In modern Japanese publishing, the term is most commonly used for a volume that collects chapters of a single manga series that were originally serialized in a magazine, in contrast to the zasshi (magazines) in which the chapters first appear.

Manga volumes

Most manga are first published chapter by chapter in weekly or monthly anthology magazines. Successful series are then collected into tankōbon, typically containing four to twelve chapters and about 170–240 pages in the common B6 size (about 12.8 × 18.2 cm). Tankōbon editions often include bonus material, author comments and redrawn art. Because tankōbon remain in print and sell for many years, they account for the majority of manga revenue; a hit series may run to dozens or more than a hundred volumes. Translations of manga into English and other languages are almost always based on the tankōbon editions.

Other formats

Related collected-edition formats include the larger aizōban ("treasured edition") hardcovers, the deluxe kanzenban ("complete edition"), the smaller pocket-sized bunkoban, and the thick, cheap wideban reprints. The term tankōbon itself predates manga: in general publishing it simply denotes a standalone book as opposed to a volume of a collected-works series (zenshū).
