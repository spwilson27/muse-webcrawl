Race (human categorization)

Race
Categorization of humans into groups based on shared physical or social qualities
Generally viewed as a social construct by scholars

Race is a categorization of humans based on shared physical or social qualities into groups generally viewed as distinct within a given society. The term first came into use in the early modern period and was used in the sense of "nation, ethnic group" during the 16th to 19th centuries. Race acquired its contemporary social meaning in the fields of physical anthropology, where attempts were made to classify humans into discrete biological types.

Historical development

Eighteenth-century naturalists such as Carl Linnaeus and Johann Friedrich Blumenbach classified humans into a handful of varieties tied to geography and skin color. In the 19th and early 20th centuries these schemes were bound up with scientific racism, which claimed innate hierarchies of ability and worth — claims later used to justify colonialism, slavery and eugenics.

Modern scholarly view

Since the mid-20th century, anthropologists and biologists have concluded that human genetic variation is continuous, clinal and mostly found within rather than between populations, so discrete biological races do not exist in humans. The UNESCO statements on race (1950 onwards) reflected this consensus. Nevertheless race remains a powerful social and political category: census classifications, civil-rights law and lived experience continue to be organized around it. See Racial segregation in the United States.

Race in censuses

Countries differ widely: the United States census asks residents to self-identify with categories such as White, Black or African American, Asian and others, while some European states avoid official racial classification entirely.
