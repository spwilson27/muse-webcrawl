Victor Hasselblad AB is a Swedish manufacturer of medium format cameras, photographic equipment and image scanners based in Gothenburg, Sweden. The company originally became known for its classic analog medium-format camera system, the V-System, which was used for many famous photographs, including the Apollo program missions to the surface of the Moon.

History

The parent trading company, F. W. Hasselblad & Co., was founded in 1841 by Fritz Wiktor Hasselblad as an import-export business that later distributed Kodak products in Sweden. During the Second World War, Victor Hasselblad, a keen amateur photographer and ornithologist from the founding family, was commissioned to develop an aerial reconnaissance camera for the Swedish Air Force, the HK-7. After the war he set out to build "the best camera in the world" for civilians, launching the 1600F in 1948.

The V-System and the Moon landings

The modular 500C (1957) and successors, with interchangeable Carl Zeiss lenses, film magazines and viewfinders, became standard equipment for studio and portrait professionals for half a century. Modified Hasselblad cameras accompanied NASA astronauts from the Mercury and Gemini programs onward; camera bodies of the 500EL/70 and 553ELX were used on the Apollo missions, and most of the iconic photographs taken on the lunar surface between 1969 and 1972 were made with Hasselblads — a dozen camera bodies remain on the Moon, with only their film magazines brought back.

Digital era

Hasselblad merged its digital operations with Danish scanner-maker Imacon in 2004, producing the H-System digital medium-format line. In 2016 it launched the X1D, the first mirrorless medium-format camera. Chinese drone maker DJI acquired a minority stake in 2015 and a majority stake in 2017; Hasselblad cameras were integrated into DJI's Mavic drones, and the X2D 100C (2022) continued the X line with a 100-megapixel sensor.
