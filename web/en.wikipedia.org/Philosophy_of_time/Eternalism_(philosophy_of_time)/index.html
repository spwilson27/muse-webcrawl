Eternalism (philosophy of time)

Eternalism
Also calledBlock universe, block time
ThesisPast, present, and future events all equally exist
Associated withB-theory of time; four-dimensionalism
Opposed byPresentism; growing block theory

Eternalism is a philosophical approach to the ontological nature of time which takes all points in time to be equally real, as opposed to presentism, which holds that only the present exists. On the eternalist view, the universe is a four-dimensional "block" of spacetime in which past and future events exist tenselessly, just as distant places exist without being here. The apparent flow of time is a feature of how conscious beings are situated within the block, not of the block itself.

Relation to physics

Eternalism draws support from special relativity. Because simultaneity is relative to an observer's frame of reference, there seems to be no single privileged "now" slicing the universe; what is future for one observer is already past for another, suggesting all events are equally real. Arguments along these lines were developed by Hilary Putnam and by Howard Stein, and the "Rietdijk–Putnam argument" is often taken to show that relativity commits us to eternalism—though this inference is contested.

Debates

Critics object that eternalism denies genuine change and temporal becoming, reduces the openness of the future, and sits uneasily with free will and with our experience of passage. Eternalists reply with the B-theory: tensed facts reduce to tenseless relations between events and utterances, and the feeling of flow is explained psychologically. Related views include the growing block theory (the past and present exist, the future does not) and the moving spotlight theory, which adds an objective present moving over the eternal block.
