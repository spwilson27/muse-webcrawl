Four-dimensional space

Four-dimensional space is a mathematical and physical framework in which three spatial dimensions are joined by a fourth dimension, time. In the philosophy of time, the four-dimensional picture underlies the theory of four-dimensionalism, according to which objects are extended not only in space but also through time, possessing temporal parts just as they possess spatial parts.

Spacetime and the block universe

Following the work of Hermann Minkowski, who recast Einstein's special relativity in geometric terms, physicists came to treat space and time as a single four-dimensional manifold called Minkowski spacetime. In this picture, every event occupies a point in spacetime, and the history of an object is a tube-like region — its world line — traced through the four dimensions. Philosophers who emphasise this structure often defend eternalism, the view that past, present and future events all exist on an equal footing, forming a static "block universe".

Temporal parts

Four-dimensionalism contrasts with endurantism, on which objects are wholly present at each moment of their existence. The four-dimensionalist, such as David Lewis, instead holds that an object perdures: it persists by having different temporal parts at different times, much as a road has different spatial parts in different places. This conception is often motivated by the problem of temporary intrinsics — how one and the same object can be bent when sitting and straight when standing — which the four-dimensionalist dissolves by assigning the incompatible properties to different temporal parts.
