Spacetime

Spacetime
ConceptFour-dimensional continuum combining three dimensions of space and one of time
Introduced byHermann Minkowski (1908), building on Albert Einstein's special relativity (1905)
Dimensions3 spatial + 1 temporal
MetricMinkowski metric (flat); pseudo-Riemannian metric (curved, in general relativity)

In physics, spacetime is any mathematical model that fuses the three dimensions of space and the one dimension of time into a single four-dimensional continuum. Spacetime diagrams can be used to visualize relativistic effects such as why different observers perceive differently where and when events occur.

Until the turn of the 20th century, the assumption had been that the three-dimensional geometry of the universe (its spatial expression) was independent of one-dimensional time. Einstein's theory of special relativity (1905) postulated that the speed of light in vacuum is the same for all inertial observers, from which it follows that the spatial and temporal separations between events depend on the observer. In 1908, Hermann Minkowski reformulated special relativity geometrically, showing that time and space can be treated as components of a single four-dimensional manifold.

History

The concept grew out of the geometry of non-Euclidean manifolds developed in the 19th century. Minkowski's 1908 lecture "Raum und Zeit" ("Space and Time") declared that "henceforth space by itself, and time by itself, are doomed to fade away into mere shadows, and only a kind of union of the two will preserve an independent reality."

Spacetime in special relativity

In special relativity, spacetime is flat and described by the Minkowski metric. The spacetime interval between two events is invariant under Lorentz transformations, even though spatial distance and time duration separately are not. Events are classified by their separation as timelike, lightlike, or spacelike, and the light cone structure determines causal relationships.

Spacetime in general relativity

In general relativity (1915), spacetime is a curved, dynamical entity: matter and energy curve spacetime, and the curvature tells matter how to move. This is summarized by John Archibald Wheeler's dictum: "Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve." Gravitational waves, black holes, and the expansion of the universe are all described as features of spacetime geometry.
