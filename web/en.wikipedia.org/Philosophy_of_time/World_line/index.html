World line

A world line is the path that an object traces through four-dimensional spacetime as time passes. In Minkowski space, a point particle's world line is a curve whose tangent everywhere lies within the light cone; the length of the curve between two events measures the proper time experienced by the object, which is why travellers on different world lines can age at different rates, as in the twin paradox of special relativity.

World lines and persistence

In the philosophy of time, the world line is the geometric embodiment of four-dimensionalism (see four-dimensional space): the persisting object just is the whole world line, or the "spacetime worm" it bounds, and what we call the object at a moment is a temporal part — a cross-section of the worm. Defenders of three-dimensionalism object that this conflates an object with its history or career. The concept is also central to discussions of identity over time, of how world lines may branch or merge in thought experiments, and of closed timelike curves in general relativity, where a world line loops back on itself and time travel becomes a formal possibility.
