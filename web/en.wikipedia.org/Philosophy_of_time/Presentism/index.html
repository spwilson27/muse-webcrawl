Presentism

Presentism
ThesisOnly present entities and events exist
Associated withA-theory of time
Historical defendersAugustine of Hippo; Arthur Prior
Opposed byEternalism; growing block theory

Presentism is the view in the philosophy of time that only present entities exist: the past is gone and the future is not yet. Dinosaurs once existed but do not exist now; our descendants will exist but do not exist yet. Presentism matches the intuitive A-theoretic idea that temporal becoming is real and that the present is metaphysically privileged.

Historical background

Anticipations of presentism appear in Augustine's Confessions, which locates past and future in present memory and expectation, and in various medieval accounts of time. In the twentieth century Arthur Prior's tense logic gave presentists formal tools for taking tense as primitive, and the view has been defended in contemporary analytic metaphysics by philosophers including Ned Markosian and Trenton Merricks.

Objections

The truthmaker problem asks what makes past-tense statements true (for example, "Caesar crossed the Rubicon") if nothing past exists to make them so; presentists respond with presently existing traces, primitive tensed facts, or ersatz abstract times. The relativity objection argues that special relativity recognizes no unique global present, so presentism's privileged "now" cannot be frame-independent; some presentists accept a privileged foliation of spacetime, while others relativize existence or appeal to Lorentzian interpretations. Cross-temporal relations—such as admiring Socrates—pose further puzzles.

Alternatives

The growing block theory agrees that the future does not exist but grants reality to the past, while eternalism treats all times as equally real. The debate between these views, organized by the A-series/B-series distinction, remains a central topic in metaphysics.
