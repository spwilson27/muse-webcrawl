The HAL Prachand (Sanskrit: प्रचण्ड, "fierce") is an Indian multi-role light attack helicopter designed and manufactured by Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) under the Light Combat Helicopter (LCH) project. It is the world's lightest attack helicopter of its class and was developed specifically for high-altitude warfare, having demonstrated take-offs and landings at forward bases in the Siachen sector at altitudes above 4,000 metres.

Development

India's experience in the 1999 Kargil War highlighted the need for an attack helicopter able to operate at extreme altitude. Derived from the HAL Dhruv's dynamic components, the LCH first flew in March 2010. After extensive trials in deserts, mountains and at sea level, the type received operational clearance and was formally inducted into the Indian Air Force in October 2022 at Jodhpur, receiving the name Prachand.

Design and armament

The Prachand features a narrow tandem-seat fuselage with armour protection, stealth-shaping features, crashworthy landing gear and two HAL/Turbomeca Shakti engines. Armament includes a 20 mm turret gun, 70 mm rocket pods, air-to-air missiles (Mistral), and the Dhruvastra (Helina) anti-tank guided missile. A glass cockpit, helmet-mounted sight, electronic warfare suite and obstacle avoidance system are fitted.

Orders

An initial limited series of 15 aircraft (10 Air Force, 5 Army) was followed by approval for 156 additional helicopters for both services. The Indian Army stood up its first Prachand squadron in 2023.
