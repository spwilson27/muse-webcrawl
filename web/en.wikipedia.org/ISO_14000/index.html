ISO 14000


ISO 14000 helps organisations manage their environmental responsibilities.

The ISO 14000 family of standards by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) provides tools for organizations seeking to manage their environmental responsibilities. The most widely used standard in the family is ISO 14001, which sets out the criteria for an environmental management system (EMS) and can be certified to.

Overview

The ISO 14000 standards exist to help organizations (a) minimize how their operations negatively affect the environment (i.e., cause adverse changes to air, water, or land); (b) comply with applicable laws, regulations, and other environmentally oriented requirements; and (c) continually improve in the above. The family is built around the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle of continual improvement, similar in approach to the ISO 9000 quality management family.

Principal standards

  • ISO 14001 — Environmental management systems: requirements with guidance for use. First published 1996; major revisions in 2004 and 2015. The 2015 version adopted the Annex SL high-level structure shared with other management system standards.
  • ISO 14004 — general guidelines on implementation.
  • ISO 14006 — guidelines for incorporating ecodesign.
  • ISO 14015 — environmental assessment of sites and organizations; ISO 14020 series — environmental labels and declarations; ISO 14031 — environmental performance evaluation; ISO 14040/14044 — life-cycle assessment; ISO 14064 — greenhouse gas accounting and verification.

Certification

Organizations are certified against ISO 14001 by accredited third-party auditors; hundreds of thousands of certificates are in force worldwide, with the largest numbers in China, Japan, and Europe. Certification is voluntary and does not itself set environmental performance levels — it requires a system for managing compliance and improvement.
