Hieronymus Bosch

Hieronymus Bosch (born Jheronimus van Aken; c. 1450 – 9 August 1516) was a Dutch painter from Brabant. He is one of the most notable representatives of the Early Netherlandish painting school. His work, generally oil on oak wood, mainly contains fantastic illustrations of religious concepts and narratives. Within his lifetime his work was collected in the Netherlands, Austria, and Spain, and widely copied, especially his macabre and nightmarish depictions of hell.

Life

Bosch was born into a family of painters in 's-Hertogenbosch, the capital of the Duchy of Brabant in the Habsburg Netherlands, and spent most of his life there. His birth name, Jheronimus van Aken, indicates that his family came from Aachen. He joined the prestigious Brotherhood of Our Lady, a religious confraternity, and married Aleid van de Meervenne, a wealthy merchant's daughter, around 1480. Few contemporary documents survive, and none of his paintings is dated; signatures appear on only a handful of works.

Work

The Garden of Earthly Delights

His most famous work, The Garden of Earthly Delights (c. 1490–1510, Museo del Prado, Madrid), is a triptych showing the Garden of Eden in the left panel, a vast landscape crowded with nude figures, oversized fruit, and fantastical creatures in the center, and a dark, torment-filled hell at right. Interpretations range from a moral warning against lust to an alchemical or heretical allegory; the debate remains unresolved.

Style and technique

Bosch painted in a free, sketchy manner quite unlike the polished finish of his Netherlandish contemporaries, building forms directly in paint. His imagery — hybrid monsters, demons with household objects, sinners punished by their own vices — drew on proverbs, devotional literature, astrology, and folklore. Technical studies such as dendrochronology and infrared reflectography, coordinated by the Bosch Research and Conservation Project, have revised attributions: only about twenty paintings are now widely accepted as autograph.

Legacy

Bosch's work was avidly collected by Philip II of Spain, and many of his paintings are now in the Prado. Pieter Bruegel the Elder absorbed his influence, and in the twentieth century the Surrealists claimed him as a precursor. The 500th anniversary of his death in 2016 was marked by a major exhibition in 's-Hertogenbosch, the largest retrospective of his work ever held.
