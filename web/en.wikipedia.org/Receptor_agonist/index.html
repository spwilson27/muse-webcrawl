Receptor agonist

An agonist is a chemical that activates a receptor to produce a biological response. In contrast, an antagonist blocks the action of the agonist, while an inverse agonist causes an action opposite to that of the agonist.

Types

A full agonist produces the maximal response of which the tissue is capable, whereas a partial agonist produces a submaximal response even at full receptor occupancy. Agonists may be endogenous — produced by the body, such as neurotransmitters and hormones — or exogenous, such as drugs. Morphine, for example, is an agonist at the μ-opioid receptor, and salbutamol at β2 adrenergic receptors.

Mechanism

Agonists bind to a receptor's active site, stabilising a conformation that triggers intracellular signalling. Their activity is characterised by affinity (binding strength) and intrinsic efficacy (ability to elicit a response), summarised in dose–response curves by the EC50.
