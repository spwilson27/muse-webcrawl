Strained yogurt

Strained yogurt, commonly marketed as Greek yogurt, is yogurt that has been strained to remove most of its whey, resulting in a thicker consistency than regular unstrained yogurt while retaining its characteristic sour taste. It is a staple across the Middle East, Central Asia, and the eastern Mediterranean, where it appears in forms such as labneh (strained further into a soft cheese-like spread).

Production and nutrition

Traditional straining is done through cloth; industrial producers use centrifuges or add thickeners. Because whey (which contains lactose and water) is removed, strained yogurt is higher in protein and lower in sugar and sodium than ordinary yogurt, contributing to its popularity as a health food. In the 2000s and 2010s, brands such as Fage and Chobani drove rapid growth of the category in the United States.

Uses

Strained yogurt is eaten plain or with honey and nuts, and is the base for dips and sauces such as tzatziki, raita variants, and Turkish cacık. Labneh, rolled into balls and preserved in olive oil, is a traditional Levantine preserve.
