List of science fiction authors

This is a list of science fiction authors — notable writers of science fiction, a genre of speculative fiction that deals principally with the impact of actual or imagined science and technology on society and individuals. Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818) is often considered the first science fiction novel; the modern genre took shape in the pulp magazines of the 20th century, above all under editor John W. Campbell at Astounding Science Fiction.

Pioneers (before 1900)

  • Mary Shelley (1797–1851) — Frankenstein (1818); The Last Man
  • Jules Verne (1828–1905) — Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea, From the Earth to the Moon, Journey to the Centre of the Earth
  • H. G. Wells (1866–1946) — The Time Machine, The War of the Worlds, The Island of Doctor Moreau, The Invisible Man
  • Edward Bellamy (1850–1898) — Looking Backward

Early 20th century and the pulp era

  • Edgar Rice Burroughs (1875–1950) — the Barsoom (Mars) series beginning with A Princess of Mars
  • Hugo Gernsback (1884–1967) — founder of Amazing Stories; the Hugo Award is named for him
  • Karel Čapek (1890–1938) — R.U.R., which coined the word "robot"; War with the Newts
  • H. P. Lovecraft (1890–1937) — cosmic horror, the Cthulhu Mythos
  • Olaf Stapledon (1886–1950) — Last and First Men, Star Maker
  • E. E. "Doc" Smith (1890–1965) — the Lensman and Skylark space-opera series
  • Robert A. Heinlein (1907–1988) — Stranger in a Strange Land, Starship Troopers, The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress
  • Jack Williamson (1908–2006) — The Legion of Space
  • John W. Campbell (1910–1971) — "Who Goes There?"; editor who shaped the Golden Age
  • A. E. van Vogt (1912–2000) — Slan, The World of Null-A
  • Leigh Brackett (1915–1978) — planetary romance; screenwriter
  • C. L. Moore (1911–1987) — Jirel of Joiry; Northwest Smith

The Golden Age and after

  • Isaac Asimov (1920–1992) — the Foundation series, the Robot stories and the Three Laws of Robotics, I, Robot
  • Arthur C. Clarke (1917–2008) — 2001: A Space Odyssey, Rendezvous with Rama, Childhood's End
  • Ray Bradbury (1920–2012) — The Martian Chronicles, Fahrenheit 451
  • Theodore Sturgeon (1918–1985) — More Than Human
  • Alfred Bester (1913–1987) — The Demolished Man, The Stars My Destination
  • James Blish (1921–1975) — Cities in Flight, A Case of Conscience
  • Poul Anderson (1926–2001) — the Technic History; Tau Zero
  • Hal Clement (1922–2003) — Mission of Gravity
  • Frederik Pohl (1919–2013) — Gateway; The Space Merchants (with C. M. Kornbluth)
  • Clifford D. Simak (1904–1988) — City, Way Station
  • Stanisław Lem (1921–2006) — Solaris, The Cyberiad
  • Arkady and Boris Strugatsky — Roadside Picnic, Hard to Be a God
  • Philip K. Dick (1928–1982) — The Man in the High Castle, Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?, Ubik
  • Frank Herbert (1920–1986) — Dune and its sequels
  • Robert Silverberg (b. 1935) — Dying Inside, the Majipoor series

New Wave and 1970s

  • J. G. Ballard (1930–2009) — The Drowned World, Crash
  • Michael Moorcock (b. 1939) — editor of New Worlds; the Jerry Cornelius books
  • Brian Aldiss (1925–2017) — Hothouse, the Helliconia trilogy
  • Harlan Ellison (1934–2018) — editor of Dangerous Visions; "I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream"
  • Samuel R. Delany (b. 1942) — Babel-17, Dhalgren
  • Roger Zelazny (1937–1995) — Lord of Light, the Amber chronicles
  • Ursula K. Le Guin (1929–2018) — The Left Hand of Darkness, The Dispossessed, the Earthsea books
  • Joanna Russ (1937–2011) — The Female Man, How to Suppress Women's Writing
  • James Tiptree Jr. (Alice Sheldon) (1915–1987) — "The Women Men Don't See", "Houston, Houston, Do You Read?"
  • Kate Wilhelm (1928–2018) — Where Late the Sweet Birds Sang
  • Gene Wolfe (1931–2019) — The Book of the New Sun
  • Larry Niven (b. 1938) — Ringworld, Known Space
  • Joe Haldeman (b. 1943) — The Forever War
  • John Varley (b. 1947) — the Eight Worlds stories; Titan
  • C. J. Cherryh (b. 1942) — the Alliance–Union universe; Downbelow Station

1980s onward: cyberpunk and contemporary SF

  • William Gibson (b. 1948) — Neuromancer; coined "cyberspace"
  • Bruce Sterling (b. 1954) — Schismatrix; cyberpunk anthologist
  • Orson Scott Card (b. 1951) — Ender's Game
  • David Brin (b. 1950) — the Uplift saga; The Postman
  • Greg Bear (1951–2022) — Blood Music, Eon
  • Greg Egan (b. 1961) — Permutation City, Diaspora
  • Lois McMaster Bujold (b. 1949) — the Vorkosigan saga
  • Octavia E. Butler (1947–2006) — Kindred, the Xenogenesis/Lilith's Brood trilogy, Parable of the Sower
  • Connie Willis (b. 1945) — Doomsday Book, To Say Nothing of the Dog
  • Dan Simmons (b. 1948) — Hyperion
  • Vernor Vinge (1944–2024) — A Fire Upon the Deep; popularised the technological singularity
  • Iain M. Banks (1954–2013) — the Culture series
  • Neal Stephenson (b. 1959) — Snow Crash, The Diamond Age, Anathem
  • Kim Stanley Robinson (b. 1952) — the Mars trilogy; The Ministry for the Future
  • Peter Watts (b. 1958) — Blindsight
  • Ted Chiang (b. 1967) — "Story of Your Life", Exhalation
  • Cixin Liu (b. 1963) — The Three-Body Problem and the Remembrance of Earth's Past trilogy
  • N. K. Jemisin (b. 1972) — the Broken Earth trilogy (science fantasy)
  • Ann Leckie (b. 1966) — Ancillary Justice
  • Andy Weir (b. 1972) — The Martian, Project Hail Mary
  • Becky Chambers (b. 1985) — the Wayfarers series
  • Arkady Martine (b. 1985) — A Memory Called Empire
  • Martha Wells (b. 1964) — the Murderbot Diaries
  • Adrian Tchaikovsky (b. 1972) — Children of Time
  • Ken MacLeod (b. 1954) — the Fall Revolution series
  • Alastair Reynolds (b. 1966) — the Revelation Space series; House of Suns
  • Charles Stross (b. 1964) — Accelerando; the Laundry Files
  • Hannu Rajaniemi (b. 1978) — The Quantum Thief
  • Ian McDonald (b. 1960) — River of Gods, the Luna trilogy
  • Paolo Bacigalupi (b. 1972) — The Windup Girl
  • Ernest Cline (b. 1972) — Ready Player One
  • John Scalzi (b. 1969) — Old Man's War
  • Brandon Sanderson (b. 1975) — chiefly fantasy; SF includes the Skyward series
