Jyutping (Chinese: 粵拼; Jyutping: Jyut6ping3, "Yue [Cantonese] spelling") is a romanization system for Cantonese developed by the Linguistic Society of Hong Kong (LSHK) in 1993. It was designed to be an accurate, unambiguous, and computer-friendly phonetic transcription of Hong Kong Cantonese, and is now the most widely used Cantonese romanization in linguistics, education, and digital input methods.

Design

Jyutping represents each Cantonese syllable as an initial, a final, and a tone number. It recognizes 19 initials (b, p, m, f, d, t, n, l, g, k, ng, h, gw, kw, w, z, c, s, j) and 53 finals. Distinctive features include the letters z, c, and j for the affricates and fricative roughly like English "dz", "ts", and "y"; the vowel eo and oe for sounds absent from Mandarin; and the syllabic nasals m and ng.

Tones

Tones are written as digits appended to the syllable. Jyutping uses six tone numbers for the six contrastive tones of modern Hong Kong Cantonese: 1 (high level or high falling), 2 (high rising), 3 (mid level), 4 (low falling), 5 (low rising), and 6 (low level). The historical checked tones are subsumed under 1, 3, and 6 according to pitch.

Comparison with other systems

Jyutping differs from the older Yale romanization, which uses diacritics for tones and different spellings (j, ch, y) for sounds Jyutping writes z, c, j. Unlike government and place-name romanizations in Hong Kong, which are unsystematic survivals of nineteenth-century conventions, Jyutping is fully consistent and reversible, making it suitable for dictionaries, language teaching, and Cantonese input methods on computers and phones.

Usage

The system is used by the Chinese University of Hong Kong's Cantonese pronunciation databases, the popular dictionary words.hk, and Cantonese courses internationally. Input method editors based on Jyutping allow users to type Chinese characters by Cantonese pronunciation.
