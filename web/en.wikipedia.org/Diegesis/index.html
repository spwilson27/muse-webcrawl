Diegesis

Diegesis (Greek: διήγησις, "narration") is a style of fiction storytelling that presents an interior view of the world of the story, in which events are recounted by a narrator, as opposed to mimesis, in which events are directly shown. In film theory, the term denotes the total world of the story — everything that exists within the narrative.

Origins

The distinction comes from Plato and Aristotle: Plato contrasted diegesis (the poet speaking in his own voice) with mimesis (the poet speaking through characters). Aristotle treated both as modes of poetic imitation.

In film

In cinema, elements are described as diegetic if they belong to the world experienced by the characters — for example, music played by an orchestra visible on screen — and non-diegetic (or extra-diegetic) if they exist only for the audience, such as a film score or voice-over narration.
