Truman Doctrine


Truman addressing Congress, 12 March 1947

The Truman Doctrine was an American foreign policy announced by President Harry S. Truman in an address to Congress on 12 March 1947. Truman declared that the United States would support free peoples resisting attempted subjugation by armed minorities or outside pressures, and requested $400 million in military and economic aid for Greece and Turkey.

Context

Britain, financially exhausted after the Second World War, had informed Washington that it could no longer support the Greek government fighting communist insurgents or assist Turkey against Soviet pressure over the Dardanelles Straits. Truman and his advisers framed the aid as part of a global struggle between democracy and totalitarianism, marking a decisive break with pre-war isolationism and wartime cooperation with the Soviet Union.

Impact

The doctrine provided the ideological foundation for the Cold War policy of containment and justified later American interventions and alliances, including the Marshall Plan, the formation of NATO, and support for anti-communist governments during the Korean and Vietnam Wars. Aid helped the Greek government prevail in the Greek Civil War by 1949.
