A gigafactory produces battery cells and vehicles at enormous scale under one roof.

A gigafactory is a very large-scale industrial facility for manufacturing batteries — especially lithium-ion cells — and electric vehicles. The term was coined and popularized by Tesla, Inc., whose CEO Elon Musk used it from 2013 onward to describe factories whose annual battery output would be measured in gigawatt-hours ("giga"). The word has since entered general use for the wave of giant battery plants built worldwide by automakers and cell manufacturers.

Tesla gigafactories

The first facility, Gigafactory Nevada (originally Gigafactory 1), near Sparks, Nevada, was announced in 2014 in partnership with Panasonic and began producing battery cells in 2017; for a time it was among the largest buildings in the world by footprint. Subsequent Tesla plants include Gigafactory New York (solar products, in Buffalo), Gigafactory Shanghai (opened 2019, the company's first vehicle plant overseas), Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg in Germany (opened 2022), and Gigafactory Texas in Austin (opened 2022, alongside Tesla's headquarters). Additional sites, such as a planned plant in Mexico and lithium refining and cathode facilities in Texas, have been announced in connection with the strategy.

Rationale

The gigafactory concept rests on vertical integration and economies of scale: co-locating cell production, pack assembly and vehicle assembly reduces logistics costs and, Tesla argued, could cut battery costs substantially through scale, automation and process innovation. Securing cell supply at scale was essential to Tesla's plan to mass-produce lower-cost electric cars such as the Model 3.

Wider adoption

As electric vehicle sales grew in the 2020s, other companies adopted the term for their own plants: Volkswagen, Northvolt, CATL, LG Energy Solution, SK On and others announced multi-billion-dollar gigafactories in Europe, North America and Asia, often supported by industrial policy such as the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act of 2022. Commentators have noted that the word is now applied to battery plants generally, losing its original Tesla-specific meaning.
