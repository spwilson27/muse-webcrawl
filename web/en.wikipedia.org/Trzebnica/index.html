Trzebnica (German: Trebnitz) is a town in southwestern Poland, in Lower Silesian Voivodeship, approximately 20 kilometres north of Wrocław. It is the seat of Trzebnica County. The town is best known for its sanctuary of Saint Hedwig, one of Poland's major pilgrimage destinations.

History

Trzebnica was first mentioned in 1138. In the early thirteenth century, Duke Henry I the Bearded founded a Cistercian convent for nuns here—the first female monastic house in Silesia—at the request of his wife, Hedwig of Andechs. Hedwig, later canonised as Saint Hedwig, patron saint of Silesia, lived at the abbey after her husband's death and was buried in its church in 1243.

The town received town rights in the thirteenth century. Historically part of the Duchy of Silesia and later of Prussia and Germany under the name Trebnitz, it became part of Poland after the Second World War, when its German population was expelled and replaced by Poles.

Landmarks

The abbey church of Saint Bartholomew and Saint Hedwig, rebuilt in the Baroque style in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, contains the tomb of Saint Hedwig and is a major pilgrimage site. The town is also known for its sanatoria, which developed around its mild microclimate and mineral springs in the nineteenth century.
