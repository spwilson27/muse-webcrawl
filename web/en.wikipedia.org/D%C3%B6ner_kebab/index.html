Döner kebab

CourseMain course, fast food
Place of originOttoman Empire / Turkey; sandwich form popularised in Berlin, Germany

Main ingredientsMeat (lamb, beef, chicken), flatbread, salad, sauces

Döner kebab is a dish of Turkish origin made of meat cooked on a vertical rotisserie. Seasoned meat stacked in the shape of an inverted cone is turned slowly against a vertical grill; the cooked outer layer is shaved off in thin slices with a long knife as it cooks. The name comes from the Turkish döner ("rotating") and kebap ("roasted meat"). Related dishes include the Arab shawarma and the Greek gyros.

History

Roasting meat on a vertical spit was developed in the Ottoman Empire in the 19th century; the town of Bursa is associated with İskender kebab, döner served over pide bread with tomato sauce and butter, named after İskender Efendi. The sandwich form — döner meat served in flatbread with salad and sauce — was popularised in Berlin in the early 1970s by Turkish guest workers; Kadir Nurman is often credited with selling it at Zoo Station in 1972, though the claim is disputed. From Germany the döner sandwich spread across Europe and the world as one of the most popular fast foods.

Preparation and serving

The meat cone is built from stacked slices of marinated meat — traditionally lamb, now commonly beef, veal, or chicken, sometimes combined with ground meat. The shaved meat is served on a plate with rice and salad, wrapped in a thin flatbread as a dürüm, or in a toasted pita or flatbread pocket with lettuce, tomato, onion, cabbage, and sauces such as garlic yogurt, herb, or hot sauce. In Germany the döner industry employs tens of thousands of people and produces hundreds of tonnes of meat daily.
