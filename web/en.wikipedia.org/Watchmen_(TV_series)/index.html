Watchmen (TV series)

Watchmen is an American superhero drama limited series based on the 1986 DC Comics series of the same name by Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons. Created by Damon Lindelof for HBO, the series is set in an alternate contemporary reality 34 years after the events of the comic, in Tulsa, Oklahoma, and focuses on the aftermath of a white supremacist attack on the local police force. It ran for nine episodes from 20 October to 15 December 2019.

Premise

In an alternate 2019, masked vigilantes are outlawed, and Tulsa police wear masks to protect their identities after the “White Night” attacks by the white supremacist group the Seventh Kavalry, which wears Rorschach masks. Detective Angela Abar (Regina King), operating as the masked Sister Night, investigates the murder of the police chief, uncovering a conspiracy tied to race, reparations for the 1921 Tulsa race massacre, and the legacy of the original comic’s characters — including Adrian Veidt (Jeremy Irons), Laurie Blake (Jean Smart) and Doctor Manhattan.

Production

Lindelof described the series as a “remix” of the comic rather than a direct sequel or adaptation; Alan Moore had his name removed from the credits, while Gibbons contributed as a consulting producer. The score was composed by Trent Reznor and Atticus Ross. HBO confirmed in January 2020 that Lindelof had no interest in a second season, and the series was reclassified as a limited series.

Reception

The series was widely acclaimed for its performances, writing and handling of American racism and policing, and it drew new public attention to the Tulsa race massacre, which opens the premiere. At the 72nd Primetime Emmy Awards it received 26 nominations and won 11, including Outstanding Limited Series and acting awards for Regina King and Yahya Abdul-Mateen II.
