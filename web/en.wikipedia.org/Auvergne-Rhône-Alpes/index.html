Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes is a region of France created by the territorial reform of French regions in 2014; it resulted from the merger of Auvergne and Rhône-Alpes and came into existence on 1 January 2016. It covers an area of more than 69,711 km², making it the third largest region in metropolitan France, and is the second most populous, after Île-de-France.

Geography

The region stretches from the Massif Central in the west to the French Alps in the east, where it borders Switzerland and Italy. It includes Mont Blanc, the highest mountain in Western Europe, and the source and valley of the Rhône river. Major cities include Lyon (the prefecture), Saint-Étienne, Grenoble, Villeurbanne, Clermont-Ferrand, Annecy, and Chambéry.

Economy

Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes has one of the strongest regional economies in Europe, with strengths in industry, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, digital technology, and tourism, particularly winter sports in alpine resorts such as Chamonix, Courchevel, and Val d'Isère. Lyon is a major banking, gastronomic, and cultural centre.

Administration

The region comprises the departments of Ain, Allier, Ardèche, Cantal, Drôme, Haute-Loire, Haute-Savoie, Isère, Loire, Puy-de-Dôme, Rhône, and Savoie, together with the Metropolis of Lyon. The regional council sits in Lyon.
