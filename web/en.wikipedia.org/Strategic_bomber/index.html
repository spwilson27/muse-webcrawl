Strategic bomber

A strategic bomber is a medium- to long-range penetration bomber aircraft designed to drop large amounts of air-to-ground weaponry onto a distant target so as to degrade the enemy's capacity to wage war. Unlike tactical bombers, which attack battlefield targets, strategic bombers strike strategic targets such as factories, military installations, infrastructure, and cities deep in enemy territory.

History

The concept emerged in the First World War with aircraft such as the German Gotha bombers and Zeppelins that raided London. In the Second World War, fleets of B-17 Flying Fortresses, B-29 Superfortresses, and Avro Lancasters conducted massive strategic bombing campaigns. During the Cold War, strategic bombers — the B-52 Stratofortress, Tu-95, and later the B-1 Lancer, B-2 Spirit stealth bomber, and Tu-160 — became one leg of the nuclear triad, carrying nuclear bombs and later cruise missiles.

Role today

With precision-guided munitions, strategic bombers also perform long-range conventional strike and close air support missions, as in Afghanistan and Iraq. New types such as the U.S. B-21 Raider continue the role. See also strategic bombing, B-52 Stratofortress, and nuclear triad.
