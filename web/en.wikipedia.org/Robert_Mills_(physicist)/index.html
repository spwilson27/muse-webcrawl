Robert Mills (physicist)

Robert L. Mills (1927–1999)

Robert Laurence Mills (April 15, 1927 – October 27, 1999) was an American physicist, specializing in quantum field theory, the theory of alloys, and many-body theory. With Chen Ning Yang he developed in 1954 the theory of non-abelian gauge fields — now known as Yang–Mills theory — a cornerstone of the Standard Model of particle physics.

Life

Born in Englewood, New Jersey, Mills attended Columbia University and received his PhD from Columbia in 1955. After a year at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton he became professor of physics at Ohio State University in 1956, remaining there until his retirement in 1995.

Yang–Mills theory

While at Brookhaven National Laboratory, Mills and Yang proposed generalising the gauge invariance of electromagnetism to non-abelian symmetry groups. Initially the theory predicted massless force carriers and was shelved, but after the discovery of asymptotic freedom and electroweak unification it became the mathematical framework of both quantum chromodynamics and the electroweak theory. Yang and Mills never received the Nobel Prize, though the work underpinned several.
