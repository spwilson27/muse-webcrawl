The Giudicato of Gallura was one of the four giudicati — independent kingdoms into which Sardinia was divided in the Middle Ages. It occupied the northeastern part of the island, corresponding roughly to the modern historical region of Gallura, with its capital at Civita (modern Olbia). Each giudicato was ruled by a judge (judike) who combined royal and judicial authority.

History

As Byzantine authority in Sardinia faded in the 9th century, the island's four districts developed into hereditary giudicati. Gallura, the smallest and poorest of the four, was the closest to Italy and fell earliest under mainland influence, particularly that of the Republic of Pisa, whose merchant and noble families — including the Visconti of Pisa — intermarried with the local dynasty. Among its notable rulers was Elena of Gallura (c. 1190–1220), one of the first female rulers of the giudicati, who married Lamberto Visconti.

The last effective judge, Nino Visconti — remembered as a friend of Dante, who placed him favourably in the Purgatorio — died in 1296, after which Gallura passed under direct Pisan control before becoming part of the Aragonese Kingdom of Sardinia in the 14th century. The arms of Gallura, a rooster, survive in local heraldry.
