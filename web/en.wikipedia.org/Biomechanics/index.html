Biomechanics

Biomechanics is the study of the mechanical laws relating to the movement or structure of living organisms. It applies principles of mechanics — forces, moments, materials and fluid dynamics — to cells, tissues, organs and whole bodies in humans, animals and plants.

Branches

Subfields include musculoskeletal and orthopedic biomechanics, sports biomechanics, cardiovascular mechanics, cell mechanics, comparative biomechanics of locomotion (walking, flight, swimming), and plant biomechanics. Methods range from motion capture and force plates to finite-element modelling and in-vivo strain measurement.

Applications

Biomechanics informs prosthetics, implants, rehabilitation, ergonomics, injury prevention, footwear and athletic technique, and the design of robots inspired by animal movement. Clinical gait analysis helps plan surgery and therapy for movement disorders.

History

Antecedents run from Aristotle and Leonardo da Vinci through Borelli's De Motu Animalium (1680) to 19th-century chronophotography by Muybridge and Marey, and modern quantitative labs in the 20th century.
