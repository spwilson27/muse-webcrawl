Shaolin Kung Fu

Shaolin Kung Fu (Chinese: 少林功夫) refers to the traditional Chinese martial arts styles associated with the Shaolin Temple, a Buddhist monastery in Henan province, China. It is among the oldest institutionalised styles of Chinese martial arts (wushu) and one of the most famous, characterised by dynamic movements, animal-inspired forms, acrobatic techniques, and a close historical link with Chan (Zen) Buddhism.

History

The Shaolin Temple was founded in the late 5th century at the foot of Mount Song. Martial practice there developed over centuries; legend credits the Indian monk Bodhidharma with introducing exercises that became the basis of Shaolin fighting arts, though historians treat this as myth. Shaolin monks famously aided the future Tang emperor in the 7th century, and the temple's warrior monks were repeatedly called upon by imperial governments. During the Ming dynasty Shaolin staff fighting was especially renowned.

Styles and training

Shaolin training traditionally includes bare-hand forms (quanfa), weapons such as the staff (gun), spear, and broadsword, conditioning methods, and "internal" practices such as qigong. Forms imitate animals including the tiger, crane, snake, and mantis. Modern Shaolin schools also teach contemporary wushu competition routines and sanda (Chinese kickboxing).

Cultural influence

Shaolin kung fu has had an enormous influence on martial arts cinema, from the films of the Shaw Brothers studio to Jet Li's debut in Shaolin Temple (1982), and on martial arts worldwide, including Japanese karate traditions that trace part of their heritage to Shaolin.
