Exterior derivative

In differential geometry, the exterior derivative is an operator d that takes a differential form of degree k to a form of degree k + 1, generalising the gradient, curl and divergence of vector calculus into a single operation defined on any smooth manifold. It underlies the modern formulation of Stokes' theorem and the de Rham cohomology.

Properties

The exterior derivative is characterised by three properties: for a smooth function f, df is its differential, matching the gradient in coordinates; it satisfies a graded Leibniz rule, d(α ∧ β) = dα ∧ β + (−1)k α ∧ dβ for a k-form α; and it is nilpotent, d² = 0, the single fact that encodes both "the curl of a gradient vanishes" and "the divergence of a curl vanishes". A form α is called closed if dα = 0 and exact if α = dβ for some β; the quotient of closed by exact forms defines the de Rham cohomology of the manifold.

Stokes' theorem

Together with integration of forms, the exterior derivative gives the generalised Stokes theorem: the integral of dω over a manifold with boundary equals the integral of ω over its boundary. This one statement contains the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green's theorem, the classical Stokes theorem and the divergence theorem as special cases.

History

The formalism arose from Élie Cartan's work on exterior differential systems in the early 20th century, developing ideas of Grassmann and Poincaré, and was set out systematically in Cartan's Leçons sur les invariants intégraux (1922).
