Vanderbilt University (informally Vandy or VU) is a private research university in Nashville, Tennessee, United States. Founded in 1873, it was named in honor of shipping and railroad magnate Cornelius Vanderbilt, who provided the school its initial $1 million endowment. Vanderbilt is a member of the Association of American Universities and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity."

History

The university was chartered in 1873 with support from the Methodist Episcopal Church, South; it severed ties with the church in 1914 following legal disputes. In the 20th century Vanderbilt integrated its student body in the 1960s and expanded its graduate and professional schools, including medicine, law, divinity, engineering, and the Owen Graduate School of Management. Peabody College, a historic teachers college, merged with Vanderbilt in 1979.

Campus and academics

The 330-acre campus is a designated national arboretum, noted for its greenery in an urban setting. The university enrolls roughly 13,800 students across ten schools and colleges, with a low student-to-faculty ratio and highly selective undergraduate admissions. Vanderbilt University Medical Center is among the largest academic medical centers in the southeastern United States.

Athletics

The Commodores compete in the Southeastern Conference. Vanderbilt won NCAA national championships in baseball (2014, 2019) and women's bowling (2007, 2018, 2023).
