Codelco

Industry: Mining

Founded: 1976

Headquarters: Santiago, Chile

Owner: Government of Chile

Products: Copper, molybdenum

The Corporación Nacional del Cobre de Chile (Codelco, from Corporación del Cobre) is a Chilean state-owned mining company and one of the world's largest copper producers. It was created in 1976 to manage the copper deposits nationalised by Chile in 1971, including mines previously operated by American companies such as Anaconda and Kennecott.

Operations

Codelco operates several major divisions in northern and central Chile, including Chuquicamata and Radomiro Tomic in the Antofagasta Region, Ministro Hales, Gabriela Mistral, Salvador, Andina in the Valparaíso Region, and El Teniente in the O'Higgins Region — the world's largest underground copper mine. The company typically produces on the order of one and a half million tonnes of fine copper per year, along with significant quantities of molybdenum.

Significance

Copper has long been central to the Chilean economy, and Codelco's revenues — including a statutory share reserved for the armed forces under the Copper Law for much of its history — are a major source of state income. The company faces challenges from declining ore grades at its ageing mines and has undertaken large structural investment projects, such as the conversion of Chuquicamata to an underground mine, to extend their lives.
