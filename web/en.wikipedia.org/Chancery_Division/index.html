Chancery Division

CourtHigh Court of Justice
JurisdictionEngland and Wales
Established1875 (Judicature Acts)
HeadChancellor of the High Court
LocationRolls Building, London

The Chancery Division is one of three divisions of the High Court of Justice of England and Wales, alongside the King's Bench Division and the Family Division. It is headed by the Chancellor of the High Court. The division descends from the Court of Chancery, the historic court of equity, and today handles business and property disputes of considerable technical complexity.

Jurisdiction

The Chancery Division hears cases concerning business disputes, company law, insolvency, intellectual property (including patents, heard in the Patents Court and the Intellectual Property Enterprise Court), trusts, probate, land, mortgages, and tax appeals from the tax tribunals. Much of its company and insolvency work is organized within the Business and Property Courts, established in 2017, which also operate in major regional centres.

Judges

The division is served by full-time Chancery judges, together with specialist insolvency and companies judges and intellectual property judges. Chancery cases are generally tried by a judge sitting alone rather than by a jury.

History

The Court of Chancery developed from the medieval Lord Chancellor's office as a court of conscience, mitigating the rigidity of the common law through the principles of equity — trusts, injunctions, and specific performance arose here. The two systems coexisted, sometimes contentiously, until the Judicature Acts of 1873–1875 fused their administration into a single High Court, of which the Chancery Division became a part. The Division sat for many years in Lincoln's Inn and the Royal Courts of Justice before moving in 2011 to the Rolls Building on Fetter Lane, the largest specialist business court centre in the world.
