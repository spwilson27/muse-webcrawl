DNA exoneration

DNA exoneration is the clearing of a convicted person through post-conviction DNA testing that shows they did not commit the crime. Hundreds of people in the United States have been exonerated this way since the late 1980s.

History

Gary Dotson, cleared in 1989, is generally considered the first person exonerated by DNA testing in the United States. The Innocence Project, founded in 1992 by Barry Scheck and Peter Neufeld, has used DNA evidence to free many wrongfully convicted prisoners, including people on death row.

Causes of wrongful conviction

Exoneration cases have highlighted eyewitness misidentification, false confessions, flawed forensic science, and prosecutorial misconduct as leading causes of wrongful convictions.

Impact

DNA exonerations have driven reforms in evidence preservation, police lineup procedures, and compensation statutes for the wrongfully convicted.
