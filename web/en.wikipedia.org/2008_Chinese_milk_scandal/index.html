The 2008 Chinese milk scandal was a major food safety incident in China in which milk and infant formula were found to have been adulterated with melamine, a nitrogen-rich industrial chemical added to watered-down milk to make it appear to contain more protein.

Health effects

The contamination caused kidney stones and kidney failure in infants. Chinese authorities estimated that around 300,000 babies were affected, tens of thousands were hospitalised, and at least six infants died. The products of the Shijiazhuang-based Sanlu Group were at the centre of the scandal, though melamine was eventually detected in products from numerous other dairy companies.

Response and aftermath

The scandal broke publicly in September 2008, prompting mass recalls in China and import bans on Chinese dairy products in many countries. Sanlu collapsed into bankruptcy. Two men were executed for their roles in producing and selling the contaminated milk, Sanlu's chairwoman Tian Wenhua was sentenced to life imprisonment, and other officials and executives were jailed or dismissed. The case severely damaged confidence in Chinese food safety regulation and in the domestic dairy industry, driving lasting consumer preference for imported infant formula.
