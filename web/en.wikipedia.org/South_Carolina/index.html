South Carolina
Capital and largest city: Columbia
State nickname: The Palmetto State
Admitted to the Union: May 23, 1788 (8th)

South Carolina is a state in the southeastern United States, bordered by North Carolina to the north, Georgia to the southwest across the Savannah River, and the Atlantic Ocean to the southeast. It consists of the coastal plain (Lowcountry), the central Piedmont, and the Blue Ridge Mountains in the far northwest.

History

The English colony of Carolina was founded in 1670 at Charles Town (Charleston); it split into North and South Carolina in 1712. The colony grew wealthy on rice and indigo produced by enslaved Africans, who formed a majority of its population. South Carolina was the first state to secede from the Union, in December 1860, and the Civil War began with the Confederate bombardment of Fort Sumter in Charleston Harbor in April 1861.

Geography and economy

The Grand Strand resort coast around Myrtle Beach, the historic port of Charleston, and the state capital Columbia anchor the state's economy, which also includes automotive and aerospace manufacturing (BMW, Boeing), agriculture, and tourism. The state tree, the sabal palmetto, commemorates the palmetto-log fort that repelled a British attack in 1776.
