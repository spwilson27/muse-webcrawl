Elton Mayo

George Elton Mayo (26 December 1880 – 7 September 1949) was an Australian-born psychologist, industrial researcher and organisational theorist. Mayo is known as the founder of the Human Relations movement and for his research, including the Hawthorne Studies, conducted at the Western Electric Company's Hawthorne Works in Chicago in the late 1920s and early 1930s.

Hawthorne studies

The Hawthorne studies examined the effects of working conditions — such as lighting, rest breaks and working hours — on worker productivity. Mayo and his colleagues concluded that productivity rose not primarily because of physical changes, but because workers responded to the attention they received and to the social dynamics of their work groups. This observation gave rise to the term "Hawthorne effect". Mayo argued that workers' social needs and informal group norms were crucial determinants of workplace behaviour, challenging earlier purely mechanistic approaches to management.

Legacy

Mayo's work at Harvard Business School helped establish industrial sociology and the human relations school of management thought, emphasizing communication and group relationships in organizations.
