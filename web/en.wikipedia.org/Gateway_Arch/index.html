The Gateway Arch is a 630-foot-tall (192 m) monument in St. Louis, Missouri, United States. Clad in stainless steel and built in the form of a weighted catenary arch, it is the world's tallest arch and the tallest man-made monument in the Western Hemisphere. It was built as a monument to the westward expansion of the United States and is the centrepiece of Gateway Arch National Park (formerly the Jefferson National Expansion Memorial).

Design and construction

Finnish-American architect Eero Saarinen won the design competition in 1947–48 with his proposal for a great arch on the St. Louis riverfront, symbolising the "Gateway to the West" from which pioneers departed. Construction began in 1963; the two legs were built simultaneously as triangular steel sections stacked and welded, and the final keystone section was inserted on October 28, 1965. The arch's legs are 630 feet apart, and its cross-section is an equilateral triangle. Saarinen did not live to see it built, having died in 1961.

Visiting

A unique tram system, designed by Dick Bowser, carries visitors inside the hollow legs to an observation deck at the top, from which they can see up to 30 miles on a clear day. The museum beneath the arch, renovated and reopened in 2018, presents exhibits on westward expansion, the Lewis and Clark expedition, and St. Louis history. The surrounding park was redesignated a national park in 2018.
