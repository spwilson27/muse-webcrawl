Donation of Constantine

The Donation of Constantine (Latin: Donatio Constantini) is a forged Roman imperial decree by which the 4th-century emperor Constantine the Great supposedly transferred authority over Rome and the western part of the Roman Empire to the Pope. The document was probably composed in the 8th century, most likely between 750 and 850. It was long used by the medieval Church to support claims of temporal power and territorial rule in Italy, including the Papal States.

Content

The forged decree is written in the voice of Constantine. In it, the emperor professes the Christian faith, recounts his miraculous cure from leprosy and baptism by Pope Sylvester I, and in gratitude grants the pope primacy over the other patriarchates, the imperial insignia, and dominion over Rome, Italy, and the western provinces. Constantine declares that he will remove his seat to the East — to the new city of Constantinople — because it is not fitting for an earthly emperor to hold power where the head of the Christian religion resides.

Exposure as a forgery

The document was doubted by several medieval writers, but it was definitively exposed as a forgery by the Italian humanist Lorenzo Valla in 1440, in his treatise De falso credita et ementita Constantini donatione. Valla showed that the Latin of the document contained anachronisms — words and expressions that did not exist in Constantine's time, references to Constantinople before its founding, and grants of titles and institutions belonging to a later age. Nicholas of Cusa had raised similar objections independently in 1433.

Historical significance

Valla's work is regarded as a landmark of textual and historical criticism. The Donation had figured in disputes between popes and emperors for centuries and was cited in the Decretum Gratiani, the foundational collection of canon law. Although its exposure weakened the papacy's legal case, the Papal States endured until the unification of Italy in 1870, resting on other donations such as the Donation of Pepin (756).
