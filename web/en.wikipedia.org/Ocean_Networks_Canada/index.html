Ocean Networks Canada (ONC) is a Canadian ocean-observing organisation based at the University of Victoria that operates cabled ocean observatories supplying continuous power and data connections to scientific instruments on the seafloor. Its data are freely available in real time through its Oceans 3.0 data management system.

Observatories

ONC's flagship infrastructure began with two observatories off British Columbia: VENUS, in the coastal Strait of Georgia and Saanich Inlet (first installed in 2006), and NEPTUNE, an 800-kilometre loop of powered fibre-optic cable on the Juan de Fuca Plate offshore, with instrument nodes at depths of up to about 2,660 metres (completed in 2009). ONC has since expanded to operate community observatories in the Canadian Arctic (at Cambridge Bay, Nunavut) and on the Atlantic coast (Halifax, Nova Scotia), as well as shore-based radar and ferry-based instruments.

Research

The observatories stream data from hydrophones, seismometers, current meters, cameras, chemical sensors and other instruments. Research supported includes earthquake and tsunami monitoring on the Cascadia margin, studies of deep-sea ecosystems and hydrothermal vents, tracking of marine mammal sounds, ocean acidification and hypoxia monitoring, and observations of Arctic sea ice. Because the instruments are powered and connected continuously, events such as undersea landslides and whale vocalisations can be captured as they happen.

Data and outreach

ONC makes its data openly accessible to researchers, policymakers, educators and the public, and runs education programs connecting classrooms with live ocean data. It is funded principally by the Government of Canada through the Canada Foundation for Innovation and by the Province of British Columbia.
