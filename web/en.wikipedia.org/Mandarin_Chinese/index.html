Mandarin (官話, Guānhuà, "speech of the officials") is a group of Sinitic varieties spoken across most of northern and southwestern China. It is the largest branch of Chinese by number of speakers and the most widely spoken native language in the world. The standard language, Standard Chinese, is based on the Beijing dialect of Mandarin and is the official language of China and Taiwan and one of four official languages of Singapore.

Phonology

Mandarin varieties characteristically have four tones (Northern Mandarin) and a reduced set of syllable-final consonants. The standard language has about 400 distinct syllables (over 1,200 counting tone) and retroflex initials.

Subdivisions

Mandarin is divided into eight subgroups, including Northeastern, Beijing, Ji–Lu, Jiao–Liao, Central Plains, Lan–Yin, Lower Yangtze (Jiang–Huai) and Southwestern Mandarin—the latter alone having around 260 million speakers.

Grammar

Like other Chinese varieties, Mandarin is an analytic language with subject–verb–object order, topic prominence, measure words (classifiers) and little inflection.
