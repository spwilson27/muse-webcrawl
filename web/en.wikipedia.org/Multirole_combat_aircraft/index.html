Multirole combat aircraft

A multirole combat aircraft (MRCA) is a military aircraft designed to perform several different combat roles — such as air-to-air combat, air-to-ground attack, and reconnaissance — within a single mission or across missions, rather than being optimised for one specialised task. The multirole concept gives air forces greater flexibility and economy by allowing one aircraft type to replace several specialised ones.

History

The term rose to prominence with the 1970s European MRCA programme that produced the Panavia Tornado. Aircraft such as the F-4 Phantom II had already demonstrated versatility, but the maturation of digital avionics, fly-by-wire controls, multi-mode radar and precision-guided munitions in the 1980s and 1990s made true swing-role capability routine in types like the F-16 Fighting Falcon, F/A-18 Hornet, Dassault Rafale, Eurofighter Typhoon and Saab Gripen. Fifth-generation types such as the F-35 extend the concept with stealth and sensor fusion.

Design trade-offs

Multirole designs accept some compromise versus dedicated interceptors or attack aircraft in exchange for commonality in training, maintenance and procurement, and the ability to re-role aircraft as operational needs change during a campaign.
