Seitan

Seitan
OriginChina / Japan, Buddhist cuisine
Main ingredientWheat gluten
Proteinc. 25 g per 100 g

Seitan is a food made from gluten, the main protein of wheat, by washing wheat-flour dough to remove starch and leaving the elastic gluten mass, which is then cooked, often simmered in a seasoned broth. Its chewy, meat-like texture makes it a popular meat substitute in vegetarian and vegan cooking.

History

Wheat gluten has been eaten in China since at least the 6th century, notably by Buddhist monks seeking meat alternatives, and is known in Japan as fu. The word seitan was popularised in the 1960s by George Ohsawa, founder of macrobiotics.

Production and Nutrition

Commercial seitan is usually made from vital wheat gluten powder mixed with water and seasonings. It is high in protein and low in fat but low in the essential amino acid lysine, and is entirely unsuitable for people with coeliac disease or wheat allergy.

Culinary Use

Seitan can be grilled, fried, baked, or stewed, absorbing marinades readily; it is used for vegan roasts, cutlets, sausages, and mock meats modelled on chicken, beef, or duck.
