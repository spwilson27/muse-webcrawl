Counterfeit money is imitation currency produced without the legal sanction of the state or government, usually to be passed off as genuine. Counterfeiting is one of the oldest crimes: fake coins circulated in antiquity, and the offence has historically been punished with extreme severity, at times as treason.

History

Counterfeiting became a major problem with the spread of paper money. In the United States, so much Confederate and privately issued currency was forged during the Civil War era that the Secret Service was created in 1865 specifically to suppress counterfeiting — a mission it retains alongside protective duties. State-sponsored counterfeiting has also been used as economic warfare, as with Nazi Germany's Operation Bernhard, which forged British banknotes.

Prevention

Central banks combat counterfeiting with security features such as watermarks, security threads, colour-shifting inks, microprinting, raised printing, polymer substrates, and holograms. Modern banknote series are periodically redesigned to stay ahead of advances in printing and imaging technology.

Impact

Although counterfeit currency is a small fraction of money in circulation, victims who unknowingly accept it bear the loss, since fake notes are confiscated without compensation. High-quality "supernotes" purporting to be US $100 bills have been the subject of long-running investigations and diplomatic accusations.
