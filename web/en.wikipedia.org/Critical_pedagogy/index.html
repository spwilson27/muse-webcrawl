Critical pedagogy

Critical pedagogy is a philosophy of education and a social movement that holds that teaching should challenge learners to examine power structures and patterns of inequality within the status quo. It is founded on critical theory and is most closely associated with the Brazilian educator Paulo Freire, whose Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1968) contrasted liberating, dialogical education with the "banking model," in which students passively receive deposits of knowledge from the teacher.

Principles

Critical pedagogy aims to develop conscientização — critical consciousness — through which learners perceive social, political, and economic contradictions and take action against oppressive elements of reality. Teachers and students are understood as co-investigators in dialogue, and the curriculum is built around "generative themes" drawn from learners' own lives. Other influential figures include Henry Giroux, Peter McLaren, bell hooks, and Ira Shor.

Influence and criticism

The approach has shaped literacy campaigns, popular education movements, and teacher education worldwide, and informs fields such as critical literacy and anti-oppressive education. Critics have argued that critical pedagogy can blur the line between education and political advocacy, and some within the movement have criticised its reliance on academic jargon disconnected from classroom practice.
