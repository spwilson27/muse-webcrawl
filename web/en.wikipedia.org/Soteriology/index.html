Soteriology

Soteriology (from Greek sōtēria, "salvation", from sōtēr, "saviour") is the study of religious doctrines of salvation. It forms part of theology in many religions and studies how the ultimate problem of the human condition — sin, suffering, death, or the cycle of rebirth — is overcome.

In Christianity

Christian soteriology centres on salvation through Jesus Christ. Debate includes the order of salvation (ordo salutis), the role of faith and works, predestination, and theories of the atonement — among them the ransom theory, Christus Victor, satisfaction (Anselm), penal substitution, and moral influence theories. Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant traditions frame the relation of grace, sacraments, and justification differently.

In other religions

In Hindu traditions, soteriology concerns moksha — liberation from saṃsāra — attained through knowledge (jñāna), devotion (bhakti), or disciplined action (karma yoga). Buddhism seeks nirvana, release from suffering and rebirth, through the Noble Eightfold Path; Pure Land traditions emphasise reliance on the vow of Amitābha Buddha. Jainism aims at liberation of the jīva through nonviolence and asceticism. In Islam, salvation is by God's mercy, faith, and righteous deeds, with final judgment determining entry to paradise.
