Fuxing (train)

Fuxing (Chinese: 复兴号, "Rejuvenation") is a series of high-speed electric multiple unit trains developed and operated by China Railway, built by CRRC subsidiaries. Introduced in 2017, the Fuxing series represents China's indigenously designed "China Standardised EMU", intended to standardise a fleet previously built from several foreign-derived Hexie (CRH) designs.

Development

Development of a standardised Chinese high-speed train began in 2012, drawing lessons from the imported and jointly developed CRH series based on Japanese, German, French and Swedish technology. Prototypes (CRH-0503) were tested from 2015. On 26 June 2017, the CR400AF and CR400BF entered service on the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway, initially at up to 350 km/h — restoring the world's fastest commercial train operation. The name "Fuxing" ("rejuvenation") echoes the political slogan of the "great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation".

Variants

The family has expanded across speed classes: the CR400 series for 350 km/h operation (including the 17-car CR400AF-B and the intelligent CR400AF-Z / BF-Z variants used around the Beijing Winter Olympics); the CR300 series for 250 km/h; and the CR200J power-concentrated trains for 160 km/h services on conventional lines, which replaced many locomotive-hauled "green skin" trains. The CR450 project, tested from 2024, targets 400 km/h commercial service.

Features

Fuxing trains emphasise domestic intellectual property, standardised parts across builders, improved energy efficiency, onboard monitoring with thousands of sensors, and interiors with more legroom, power sockets and Wi-Fi. They operate across China's high-speed network, the largest in the world, and export derivatives have been supplied for the Jakarta–Bandung high-speed railway in Indonesia.
