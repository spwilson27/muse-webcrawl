Rædwald (Old English: Rædwald), also spelled Raedwald, was a king of East Anglia who reigned from about 599 until his death around 624. A member of the Wuffingas dynasty, he became the most powerful English ruler south of the Humber and is listed by Bede among the kings who held imperium over the southern Anglo-Saxon kingdoms — the so-called bretwaldas.

Reign

Rædwald was baptized in Kent but, according to Bede, maintained in his temple both a Christian altar and an altar for pagan sacrifices. He sheltered the exiled Edwin of Northumbria and, in 616, defeated and killed Æthelfrith of Northumbria at the Battle of the River Idle, installing Edwin on the Northumbrian throne — a victory that confirmed Rædwald's supremacy in England.

Sutton Hoo

The magnificent ship burial excavated at Sutton Hoo in 1939, with its helmet, shield, gold buckle, and Byzantine silver, is widely believed to commemorate Rædwald, though no inscription names the deceased. Its splendor transformed understanding of the wealth and connections of early seventh-century English kingship.
