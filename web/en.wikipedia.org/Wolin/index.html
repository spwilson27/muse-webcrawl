Wolin

Wolin (Polish: Wolin; German: Wollin) is an island in the Baltic Sea, just off the Polish coast in West Pomeranian Voivodeship. It lies at the mouth of the Oder (Odra) River, between the Szczecin Lagoon to the south and the Baltic Sea to the north, and is separated from the island of Usedom to the west by the Świna strait and from the Polish mainland to the east by the Dziwna strait.

Geography

The island covers an area of about 265 square kilometres, making it one of the largest Polish islands. Its landscape was shaped by the last Ice Age and includes moraine hills, cliffs along the northern coast, lakes and large areas of forest. The highest point rises to roughly 115 metres above sea level. Much of the northern part of the island is protected within Wolin National Park, known for its beech forests, sea cliffs and white-tailed eagles.

Towns and history

The principal town is Wolin, on the island's southern shore, which in the early Middle Ages was one of the great trading ports of the Baltic and is associated in legend with the semi-mythical stronghold of Jomsborg and the Jomsvikings. Other settlements include the seaside resort of Międzyzdroje on the north coast and Świnoujście, which extends onto the island's western tip. After centuries under Pomeranian, Swedish, Brandenburg-Prussian and German rule, the island became part of Poland after the Second World War in 1945.

Economy and transport

Tourism is central to the island's economy, with visitors drawn to its beaches, spa towns and national park. The island is connected to the mainland by bridges and to Usedom by ferry and bridge links at Świnoujście, and is crossed by the S3 expressway and a railway line toward Szczecin.
