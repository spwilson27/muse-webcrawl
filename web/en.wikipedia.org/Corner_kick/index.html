Corner kick

A corner kick is a method of restarting play in association football, awarded when the ball passes over the goal line, without a goal having been scored, and having last been touched by a player of the defending team. The kick is taken from the corner of the field of play nearest to the place where the ball crossed the goal line.

Procedure

Under Law 17 of the Laws of the Game, the ball is placed in the corner arc nearest to where it went out of play and is kicked by a player of the attacking team. Opponents must remain at least 9.15 m (10 yards) from the corner arc until the ball is in play. A goal may be scored directly from a corner kick — an "Olympic goal" — but only against the opposing team; if the ball goes directly into the kicker's own goal, a corner is awarded to the opponents instead. There is no offside offence from a corner kick, which makes corners valuable attacking opportunities, usually delivered into the penalty area for teammates to head at goal.

Tactics

Common deliveries include the inswinger (curving toward goal), the outswinger (curving away), and the short corner, in which the ball is played a short distance to a nearby teammate to change the angle of attack or retain possession. Defences organise with zonal marking, man-to-man marking, or a hybrid, and often leave one player forward to enable counter-attacks. Corners account for a significant share of goals in professional football, and teams devote considerable training time to set-piece routines.
