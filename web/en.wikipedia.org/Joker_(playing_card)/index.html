The Joker is a playing card found in most standard 52-card decks, usually distinguished from the suited cards by its depiction of a jester or clown. It has no fixed rank or suit and is used differently — or removed — depending on the game.

Origin

The Joker was introduced in the United States around the 1860s for the game of Euchre, where an extra trump card, the "Best Bower", was needed beyond the Jacks. American manufacturers such as Samuel Hart produced cards labelled "Joker" bearing a jester image, and the card spread worldwide. Its name may derive from Jucker, an Alsatian name for Euchre.

Uses

In many games the Joker is a wild card that can substitute for any other card — notably in Canasta, Poker variants and Rummy games. In others, such as the Chinese game Dou Dizhu or the Swiss Jass, jokers serve as the highest trumps. Some decks include a second, differently coloured joker (red and black) to allow such uses and to act as a spare.

In culture

The grinning jester of the card inspired the DC Comics villain the Joker, created in 1940, and remains a widespread symbol of wildness, trickery and unpredictability.
