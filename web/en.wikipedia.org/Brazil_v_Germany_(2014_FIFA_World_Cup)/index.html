Brazil v Germany was the first semi-final of the 2014 FIFA World Cup, played on 8 July 2014 at the Estádio Mineirão in Belo Horizonte. Germany defeated the host nation Brazil 7–1, in one of the most shocking results in World Cup history. The match became known in Brazil as the Mineirazo, echoing the Maracanazo of 1950, and it ended Brazil's 62-match unbeaten run in competitive home matches.


The Estádio Mineirão in Belo Horizonte.

Background

Brazil reached the semi-final without its captain Thiago Silva, suspended, and its star forward Neymar, who had suffered a fractured vertebra in the quarter-final against Colombia. Germany, managed by Joachim Löw, had a full-strength squad and had impressed throughout the tournament.

Match summary

Thomas Müller opened the scoring in the 11th minute. Miroslav Klose then scored in the 23rd minute, becoming the World Cup's all-time top scorer with 16 goals. In a devastating six-minute spell, Toni Kroos scored twice and Sami Khedira once, making it 5–0 before the half-hour mark. André Schürrle added two more goals in the second half, and Oscar scored a late consolation for Brazil in the 90th minute.

Records and aftermath

The result equalled Brazil's worst ever defeat and was its first home competitive loss since 1975. Germany went on to beat Argentina 1–0 in the final to win its fourth World Cup. Brazil lost the third-place match to the Netherlands and coach Luiz Felipe Scolari resigned shortly afterwards.
