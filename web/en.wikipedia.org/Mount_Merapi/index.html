Mount Merapi

Mount Merapi (Indonesian: Gunung Merapi, 'Fire Mountain') is an active stratovolcano in Central Java, Indonesia, on the border with Yogyakarta. It is Indonesia's most active volcano, erupting regularly since 1548.

Elevation2,910 m (approx., varies with eruptions)
LocationCentral Java / Yogyakarta, Indonesia
TypeStratovolcano
Major eruption2010

Activity

Merapi typically produces small eruptions every few years and larger ones every decade or so, with pyroclastic flows (locally wedhus gembel) posing the main hazard to villages on its fertile slopes, home to hundreds of thousands of people. See island arc volcanism.

2010 eruption

The October–November 2010 eruption, rated VEI 4, killed more than 350 people, displaced hundreds of thousands, and closed airports due to ash. Earlier deadly eruptions occurred in 1930 and 1994.

Cultural significance

Merapi holds spiritual importance in Javanese belief; the Yogyakarta sultanate appoints a gatekeeper (juru kunci) and makes annual labuhan offerings linking the volcano, the palace and the Queen of the South Sea.
