The Normandy Format (French: Format Normandie) is a diplomatic group of senior representatives of four countries — France, Germany, Russia, and Ukraine — established to resolve the war in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine and, later, the wider Russo-Ukrainian War. The group is also known as the Normandy contact group or the Normandy Four.

History

The format was created on 6 June 2014, when the leaders of the four countries met informally on the sidelines of the 70th anniversary commemorations of the D-Day landings in Normandy, France. The meeting at the Château de Bénouville brought together French President François Hollande, German Chancellor Angela Merkel, Russian President Vladimir Putin, and Ukrainian President Petro Poroshenko — the first direct encounter between Putin and Poroshenko since the annexation of Crimea and the outbreak of fighting in Donbas.

Subsequent meetings of leaders and foreign ministers were held in Milan (October 2014), Minsk (February 2015), Paris (October 2015), and Berlin (October 2016). The marathon Minsk summit of 11–12 February 2015, lasting some 16 hours, produced the Minsk II package of measures, endorsed by UN Security Council Resolution 2202.

Revival in 2019

After a three-year pause, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy pushed to revive the format. A leaders' summit was held in Paris on 9 December 2019 — the first Normandy meeting attended by Zelenskyy and the first face-to-face meeting between Zelenskyy and Putin. The parties reaffirmed their commitment to Minsk II, agreed a full ceasefire and an exchange of prisoners, but made no breakthrough on the political provisions of the agreements.

After 2022

The format effectively ceased to function following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, which rendered the Minsk framework defunct. Advisor-level contacts in early 2022 (Paris, January 2022; Berlin, February 2022) were the last meetings held under the format. France and Germany subsequently pursued other channels of support for Ukraine, while direct negotiations between Kyiv and Moscow were attempted in Belarus and Turkey in the spring of 2022.
