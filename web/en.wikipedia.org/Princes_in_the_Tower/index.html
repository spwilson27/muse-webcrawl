Princes in the Tower

PersonsEdward V (b. 1470); Richard of Shrewsbury, Duke of York (b. 1473)
DisappearedSummer 1483, Tower of London
ProtectorRichard, Duke of Gloucester (later Richard III)

The Princes in the Tower were Edward V of England and his younger brother Richard of Shrewsbury, Duke of York, the two sons of Edward IV. After their father's death in April 1483, the boys were lodged in the Tower of London by their uncle and Lord Protector, Richard, Duke of Gloucester. They were declared illegitimate by an act of Parliament (Titulus Regius), Richard took the throne as Richard III, and the princes disappeared from public view during the summer of 1483.

The mystery

The boys' fate is one of the most famous unsolved mysteries of English history. The traditional account, propagated under the Tudors and dramatised by Shakespeare, holds that they were murdered on Richard III's orders. Alternative candidates proposed by revisionist writers include Henry VII and the Duke of Buckingham, and some have argued that one or both survived, pointing to the later pretenders Lambert Simnel and Perkin Warbeck.

Evidence

In 1674, workmen at the Tower discovered a wooden chest containing the skeletons of two children, which were reburied in Westminster Abbey as the princes' remains. Examinations in 1933 were inconclusive, and later requests for DNA testing have not been granted. The discovery of Richard III's remains in Leicester in 2012 renewed public interest in the case.
