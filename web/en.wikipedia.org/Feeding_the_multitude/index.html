Feeding the multitude

Feeding the multitude is a term used to refer to two separate miracles of Jesus reported in the Gospels: the Feeding of the 5,000 and the Feeding of the 4,000. In both accounts, Jesus multiplies a small amount of food — loaves of bread and fish — so that it suffices to feed a large crowd, with baskets of leftovers gathered afterwards.

The Feeding of the 5,000 is the only miracle (apart from the Resurrection) recorded in all four canonical Gospels: Matthew 14, Mark 6, Luke 9, and John 6. The Feeding of the 4,000 appears only in Matthew 15 and Mark 8.

The Feeding of the 5,000

According to the accounts, Jesus withdrew by boat to a deserted place near Bethsaida, but crowds followed him on foot. Seeing the large crowd, he had compassion on them and healed their sick. As evening approached, the disciples suggested sending the crowds away to buy food, but Jesus replied, "They need not go away; you give them something to eat." The disciples reported having only five loaves and two fish, which Jesus blessed, broke, and distributed. All ate and were satisfied, and the disciples collected twelve baskets full of leftover fragments. The number of men fed was about five thousand, besides women and children.

The Feeding of the 4,000

In the second account, a crowd had followed Jesus for three days in a remote place and had nothing to eat. The disciples had seven loaves and a few small fish, which Jesus blessed and distributed in the same manner. After the crowd of about four thousand men (besides women and children) had eaten, seven baskets of leftovers were collected.

Interpretation

Commentators have long debated whether the two accounts describe distinct events or are variant traditions of a single episode. The Gospel of Mark itself presents them as two events, with Jesus later referring to both separately (Mark 8:17–21). The miracles are frequently read as echoing Old Testament precedents, such as Elisha feeding a hundred men with twenty loaves (2 Kings 4), and as anticipating the Eucharist; in John 6 the feeding is followed by the "Bread of Life" discourse.
