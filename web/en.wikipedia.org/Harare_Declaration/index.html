Harare Declaration

The Harare Declaration is a declaration of the Commonwealth of Nations adopted at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Harare, Zimbabwe, on 20 October 1991, setting out the Commonwealth's core political values at the end of the Cold War.

Content

The declaration reaffirmed commitment to democracy, democratic processes and institutions, the rule of law, independent judiciaries, human rights, equality for women, and good governance. It built on the Singapore Declaration of 1971.

Significance

The Harare principles, together with the later Millbrook Action Programme (1995), gave the Commonwealth a basis for suspending member states that violate democratic principles. See also Commonwealth of Nations.
