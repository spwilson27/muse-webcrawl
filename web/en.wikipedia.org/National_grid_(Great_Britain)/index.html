National Grid (Great Britain)

The National Grid is the high-voltage electricity transmission network serving Great Britain — England, Scotland and Wales. It carries bulk power from generators to regional distribution networks and large industrial users, operating principally at 400 kV and 275 kV, and is linked to neighbouring countries by subsea interconnectors.

History

The grid originated in the Electricity (Supply) Act 1926, which created the Central Electricity Board and a standardised national network at 132 kV operating at 50 Hz, completed in the 1930s. After nationalisation in 1948 the system was expanded as a "supergrid" at 275 kV and then 400 kV. Following privatisation in 1990–91, transmission in England and Wales passed to National Grid plc; in Scotland the networks are owned by ScottishPower and SSE. System operation — balancing supply and demand in real time — became independent of National Grid in 2024 with the creation of the publicly owned National Energy System Operator (NESO).

Operation

The grid must keep frequency at 50 Hz within tight limits, matching generation to demand second by second. Interconnectors link Britain to France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Norway, Denmark and Ireland (via the separate Northern Irish system), trading power across borders. The network is undergoing major reinforcement to connect offshore wind and other low-carbon generation as coal has been eliminated and gas declines.
