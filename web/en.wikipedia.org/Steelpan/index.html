Steelpan

The steelpan (also known as a steel drum) is a musical instrument originating in Trinidad and Tobago. It is made from a 55-gallon industrial oil drum whose playing surface is hammered into a concave bowl with distinct areas, or "notes", each tuned to a pitch. It is the national instrument of Trinidad and Tobago and the only acoustic musical instrument invented in the twentieth century.

History

Steelpan developed in the 1930s and 1940s in the impoverished neighbourhoods of Port of Spain, when bamboo tamboo bamboo bands were suppressed and players turned to metal containers, biscuit tins, and finally oil drums. Pioneers such as Winston "Spree" Simon, Ellie Mannette, and Tony Williams created the first tuned pans and the modern tenor pan. In 1951 the Trinidad All-Steel Pan Percussion Orchestra (TASPO) performed in Britain, introducing the instrument internationally.

Construction and tuning

Making a pan involves sinking the drum head, marking note areas, grooving, tempering, and fine-tuning the fundamental and overtones with hammers. Different voices—tenor, double seconds, guitar, cello, and bass pans—form the steel orchestra, or steelband.

Culture

Steelbands are central to Trinidad Carnival, competing in the Panorama competition with elaborate arrangements of calypso music. The instrument has spread worldwide and is used in education, jazz, and classical performance.
