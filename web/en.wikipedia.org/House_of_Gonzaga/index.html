The House of Gonzaga was an Italian princely family that ruled Mantua in Lombardy from 1328 to 1708, first as captains-general (capitani del popolo), then as marquesses, and finally as dukes. Renowned patrons of the arts, the Gonzaga made Mantua a major centre of Renaissance culture.

Rise and rule

Ludovico I Gonzaga seized power in Mantua in 1328 with the support of the Scaligeri of Verona. Gianfrancesco I obtained the title of Marquis of Mantua from Emperor Sigismund in 1433. Under Francesco II and his wife Isabella d'Este — one of the most celebrated patrons of the Renaissance — the court attracted artists such as Andrea Mantegna, Leonardo da Vinci, and Raphael. Federico II was raised to Duke of Mantua by Emperor Charles V in 1530 and acquired Montferrat by marriage.

Decline

The main line produced Vincenzo I, a lavish patron who employed Claudio Monteverdi, but succession disputes led to the War of the Mantuan Succession (1628–1631) and the sack of Mantua in 1630. The last duke, Ferdinando Carlo, sided with France during the War of the Spanish Succession and was deposed in 1708, when Mantua passed to the Habsburgs. Cadet branches, including the Gonzaga-Nevers line in France, continued beyond the main line's extinction.
