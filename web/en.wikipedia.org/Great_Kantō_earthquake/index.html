Great Kantō earthquake

Ruins in Tokyo following the earthquake and firestorm of 1 September 1923.

The Great Kantō earthquake (関東大地震, Kantō dai-jishin) struck the Kantō Plain on the main Japanese island of Honshū at 11:58:44 JST on Saturday, 1 September 1923. The earthquake, with a moment magnitude of about 7.9, devastated Tokyo, the port city of Yokohama, and the surrounding prefectures of Chiba, Kanagawa, and Shizuoka. It is among the deadliest earthquakes in Japanese history.

Damage and fires

Because the quake struck at lunchtime, when many people were cooking over open flames, fires broke out across the cities and merged into firestorms. The single greatest loss of life came at the Army Clothing Depot in Honjo, Tokyo, where about 38,000 people who had taken refuge were killed by a fire whirl. Broken water mains hampered firefighting for days. The quake also triggered a tsunami in Sagami Bay with waves up to about 10 metres in places, and widespread landslides.

Aftermath

In the chaos after the quake, false rumours spread that Koreans were poisoning wells and committing arson, leading to the Kantō Massacre, in which vigilantes, police, and soldiers killed an estimated 6,000 ethnic Koreans and others mistaken for Koreans. Martial law was declared, and the government of Prime Minister Yamamoto Gonnohyōe undertook a large-scale reconstruction of Tokyo, although ambitious redesign plans were scaled back. Since 1960, 1 September has been observed in Japan as Disaster Prevention Day.
