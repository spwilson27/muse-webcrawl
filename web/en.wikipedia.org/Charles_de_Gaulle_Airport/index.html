Paris Charles de Gaulle Airport (IATA: CDG, ICAO: LFPG), also known as Roissy Airport, is the largest international airport in France and one of the busiest airports in Europe. Located in Roissy-en-France, about 23 kilometres (14 miles) northeast of Paris, it is named after statesman Charles de Gaulle, leader of the Free French Forces and founder of the French Fifth Republic.

History

Construction of the airport, then known provisionally as Aéroport de Paris-Nord, began in 1966 to relieve congestion at Orly Airport and Le Bourget. The airport opened on 8 March 1974. Its futuristic circular Terminal 1, designed by architect Paul Andreu, features a central cylindrical building ringed by seven satellite terminals connected by moving walkways through tubes.

Terminal 2, originally built exclusively for Air France, has expanded into a complex of halls (2A through 2G). A long-distance railway station, Aéroport Charles de Gaulle 2 TGV, opened in 1994 on the LGV Interconnexion Est, linking the airport to the French high-speed rail network. The airport also connects to Paris via the RER B suburban line and the motorway A1.

Facilities

The airport occupies about 32 square kilometres across several communes in the departments of Seine-et-Marne, Seine-Saint-Denis and Val-d'Oise. It has four parallel runways and three main terminal complexes. Terminal 2E's Hall M, opened in 2012, is dedicated to long-haul flights. On 26 May 2004 part of Terminal 2E collapsed shortly after opening, killing four people; the rebuilt structure reopened in 2008.

Airlines and traffic

Charles de Gaulle is the principal hub of Air France and a base for the SkyTeam alliance. In 2019 it handled over 76 million passengers, ranking among the ten busiest airports in the world. It is operated by Groupe ADP under the brand Paris Aéroport.
