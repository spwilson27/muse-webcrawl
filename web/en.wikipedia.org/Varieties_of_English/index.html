Varieties of English

Varieties of English are the many regional and social forms of the English language spoken around the world. English is a pluricentric language, with major standard varieties including British English, American English, Canadian English, Australian English, New Zealand English, Indian English, Nigerian English and South African English, among many others.

Classification

Linguists often group varieties according to the "Three Circles" model proposed by Braj Kachru: the Inner Circle (countries where English is the primary native language), the Outer Circle (where English has an official or historical institutional role), and the Expanding Circle (where English is learned mainly as a foreign language).

Differences

Varieties differ in pronunciation (accent), vocabulary, spelling conventions — such as the British "-our" versus American "-or" in words like colour/color — grammar and idiom. Regional dialects within countries add further diversity, and creoles and pidgins based on English, such as Jamaican Patois and Tok Pisin, form an extended family of English-derived languages.
