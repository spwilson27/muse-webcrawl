HMS Dreadnought (1906)

Launched18 February 1906, Portsmouth
Displacement18,120 long tons
Armament10 × 12-inch guns
PropulsionParsons steam turbines, 21 knots
FateSold for scrap, 1921

HMS Dreadnought was a Royal Navy battleship whose design revolutionised naval power. Launched in 1906, she was the first battleship to combine an "all-big-gun" main armament — ten 12-inch guns in five twin turrets — with steam turbine propulsion, making every existing battleship obsolete overnight. Her name, meaning "fear nothing", became a generic term: subsequent all-big-gun battleships were called "dreadnoughts", and earlier ones "pre-dreadnoughts".

Design and construction

Championed by Admiral Sir John "Jacky" Fisher, First Sea Lord, the ship was designed by a Committee on Designs convened in 1905. Built at Portsmouth Dockyard in extraordinary haste — just over a year from laying down to launch — she demonstrated British industrial capacity and intensified the naval arms race with Imperial Germany, contributing to the tensions preceding the First World War.

Service

Dreadnought served as flagship of the Home Fleet from 1907 to 1912. During the First World War she missed the Battle of Jutland while refitting, but on 18 March 1915 she rammed and sank the German submarine U-29 — the only battleship ever to sink a submarine unaided. Rendered obsolete by post-war standards, she was sold for scrap in 1921.
