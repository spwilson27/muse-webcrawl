Federal Switchblade Act

Image placeholder: the Federal Switchblade Act of 1958.

The Federal Switchblade Act of 1958 (15 U.S.C. §§ 1241–1244) is a United States federal law that prohibits the manufacture, importation, distribution, transportation and sale of switchblade knives in interstate commerce, subject to certain exceptions.

Provisions

The Act bans the interstate shipment of automatic knives to ordinary individuals, with exceptions for sales to the armed forces, law enforcement, and persons with the use of only one arm. It also prohibits carrying switchblades on federal property and in territories under federal jurisdiction in some circumstances. Possession itself is primarily regulated by state law rather than by the Act.

Background and amendments

The law was enacted amid a 1950s moral panic over switchblades and youth crime, fuelled by magazine articles, congressional hearings and popular culture. In 2009 Congress amended the Act to clarify that it does not cover assisted-opening or "spring-assisted" knives, which open with manual pressure on the blade rather than a button in the handle, after customs officials had moved to classify such knives as switchblades. Knife-rights organisations continue to campaign for the repeal of state-level restrictions.
