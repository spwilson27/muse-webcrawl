Half-wave plate

A half-wave plate is a waveplate that shifts the polarization direction of linearly polarized light.

A half-wave plate is a type of retarder (a waveplate) that retards the phase of one polarization component relative to its orthogonal component by Γ = π (a half wavelength). It is used to rotate the plane of polarization of a linearly polarized beam.

Principle

A half-wave plate consists of a thin layer of birefringent material, such as quartz or mica, whose thickness is chosen so that the optical path difference between light polarized along the fast axis and light polarized along the slow axis is exactly half a wavelength (λ/2). When linearly polarized light enters the plate at an angle θ to the fast axis, the component along the fast axis advances relative to the component along the slow axis by half a wavelength, which mirrors the polarization direction about the fast axis. The result is that the emerging light remains linearly polarized, but its plane of polarization is rotated by an angle of 2θ relative to the incident direction.

Rotating the half-wave plate by θ therefore rotates the polarization plane by 2θ — a property exploited in optical instruments to adjust polarization direction continuously. If circularly polarized light passes through a half-wave plate, its handedness is reversed (left-circular becomes right-circular and vice versa), because the half-wave retardation flips the phase relationship between the two components.

Construction and variants

True zero-order half-wave plates are made from two thin plates with their axes crossed, or from a single thin sheet of mica or polymer; multiple-order plates use thicker crystals and are more sensitive to wavelength and temperature. Because retardance depends on wavelength, a half-wave plate is designed for a specific wavelength; achromatic designs combine materials such as quartz and magnesium fluoride to maintain half-wave retardance over a broad spectral band. Half-wave plates are standard components in polarimetry, laser systems, optical isolators, and quantum-optics experiments, where they are used to prepare and analyse polarization states of photons.
