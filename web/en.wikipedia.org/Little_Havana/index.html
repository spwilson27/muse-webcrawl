Little Havana

Little Havana (Spanish: Pequeña Habana) is a neighbourhood of Miami, Florida, United States, and the historic heart of the city's Cuban exile community. Centred on Southwest 8th Street — Calle Ocho — the district took its present form after 1959, when large numbers of Cubans fled the Cuban Revolution and settled west of downtown Miami.

Calle Ocho

Calle Ocho is lined with Cuban restaurants, cafés serving cafecitos through walk-up windows (ventanitas), cigar shops where rollers hand-make cigars, and record stores playing son and salsa. Landmarks include the Tower Theater (1931), one of Miami's oldest cinemas; Domino Park (Máximo Gómez Park), where older residents play dominoes; and the Walk of Fame honouring Latin artists. The annual Calle Ocho Festival, part of the Miami Carnival, is one of the largest Hispanic street festivals in the United States.

Demographics

While Cubans remain the neighbourhood's cultural anchor, Little Havana has become increasingly diverse, with substantial Nicaraguan, Honduran and other Central American communities arriving from the 1980s onward. The area is known for its dense, walkable street life, roosters roaming freely, and painted murals celebrating Cuban heritage.

History

In the 1930s the area, then called Riverside, was a largely Jewish and working-class neighbourhood. The Cuban influx of the 1960s transformed it into the capital of the Cuban diaspora and a centre of exile politics. In recent decades the neighbourhood has faced gentrification pressures from the adjacent downtown and Brickell districts.
