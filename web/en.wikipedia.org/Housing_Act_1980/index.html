Housing Act 1980

Housing Act 1980
Long title: An Act to give security of tenure, and the right to buy their homes, to tenants of local authorities and other bodies
Citation: 1980 c. 51
Territorial extent: England and Wales, Scotland (in part)
Royal assent: 8 August 1980

The Housing Act 1980 (c. 51) is an Act of Parliament of the United Kingdom, passed under the Conservative government of Margaret Thatcher. It is best known for introducing the Right to Buy, which gave council house tenants a statutory right to purchase their homes at a discount, and for establishing the "secure tenancy" for public-sector tenants.

Right to Buy

Part I of the Act gave tenants of local authorities and certain other public landlords who had been tenants for at least three years the right to buy the freehold of their house (or a long lease of their flat) at a discount of 33% of market value, rising with length of tenancy to a maximum of 50%. Secretary of State for the Environment Michael Heseltine described the policy as enabling families to take a "giant step" into home ownership. Over the following decade more than a million council homes were sold, sharply increasing owner-occupation and reducing the council housing stock, as receipts were largely not reinvested in replacement building.

Other provisions

The Act also introduced secure tenancies with statutory succession rights, a Tenants' Charter of rights for council tenants, and new arrangements for housing finance and subsidies. Later legislation — notably the Housing and Building Control Act 1984 and the Housing Act 1985, which consolidated much of the 1980 Act — extended and revised the scheme, while the Housing Act 1988 reshaped the private and housing association sectors.

Legacy

The Right to Buy became one of the signature policies of Thatcherism and a defining influence on British housing tenure, contributing both to wider home ownership and, critics argue, to later shortages of social rented housing. The policy was ended in Scotland in 2016 and in Wales in 2019, but continues in England in modified form.
