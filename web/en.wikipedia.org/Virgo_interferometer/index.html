Virgo interferometer

The Virgo interferometer is a large laser interferometer designed to detect gravitational waves predicted by general relativity. It is located in Santo Stefano a Macerata, near Pisa, Italy, and is operated by the European Gravitational Observatory (EGO).

History

Conceived by Alain Brillet and Thibault Damour, Virgo was approved in 1993 and completed in 2003. After major upgrades ("Advanced Virgo"), it joined the two LIGO detectors in August 2017 and within weeks made its first detection, GW170814, a merging binary black hole, and helped localize the neutron-star merger GW170817.
