Lassie

Lassie is a fictional female Rough Collie dog and is featured in a 1940 novel by Eric Knight titled Lassie Come-Home. Knight's portrayal of the character was based on a real dog, also named Lassie. In the novel, the collie undertakes a long journey to return to the boy she loves after being sold by his impoverished family. The character became one of the most famous animal stars in history, headlining a series of Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer films in the 1940s and 1950s, a long-running American television series (1954–1973), and numerous remakes and adaptations.

Origins

Eric Knight, a British-born American author, published the short story "Lassie Come-Home" in The Saturday Evening Post in 1938 and expanded it into a novel in 1940. Set in Depression-era Yorkshire, the book tells of the Carraclough family, forced by poverty to sell their beloved collie to the Duke of Rudling; Lassie escapes repeatedly and finally travels hundreds of miles back to the family's village in Scotland-to-Yorkshire fashion, arriving half-starved at the school gate where the boy Joe waits. The novel was a bestseller and is considered a classic of children's literature.

Films

MGM adapted the novel as Lassie Come Home (1943), starring Roddy McDowall and Elizabeth Taylor, with the male collie Pal in the title role. The film's success led to a series of sequels, including Son of Lassie (1945) and Courage of Lassie (1946). A remake, Lassie, appeared in 1994, and a further adaptation in 2005. The role has always been played by male collies, all descendants of the original Pal, trained by Rudd Weatherwax and his family.

Television

The Emmy Award-winning television series Lassie ran on CBS from 1954 to 1973 — 591 episodes — making it one of the longest-running scripted series in American television. Its best-known format featured the Miller (later Martin) family on a farm, with young Timmy (Jon Provost) as Lassie's companion; contrary to popular catchphrase, Timmy never fell down a well in the series. Later seasons made Lassie the companion of U.S. Forest Service rangers. Animated series (1973; 2015) and a Canadian revival (The New Lassie, 1997) followed.

Legacy

Lassie is one of only three animals to have been awarded a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame (alongside Rin Tin Tin and Strongheart). The character symbolizes loyalty and devotion in popular culture, and the name "Lassie" is often used generically for heroic dogs that fetch help for their owners.
