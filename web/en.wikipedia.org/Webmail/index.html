Webmail

TypeEmail client in a web browser
First servicesHotmail and RocketMail (1996)
Major providersGmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo Mail
Access protocolHTTP/HTTPS via browser

Webmail (or web-based email) is an online email service implemented as a web application accessible through a web browser. Instead of using a dedicated email client installed on one computer, users read, send and organise their messages on the provider's website, with mail stored on remote servers. Webmail made email accessible from any connected computer and became the dominant way consumers use email.

History

One of the first webmail services, Hotmail, was launched in July 1996 by Sabeer Bhatia and Jack Smith, offering free accounts usable from anywhere; Microsoft acquired it in 1997 for an estimated $400 million. RocketMail, later Yahoo Mail, also launched in 1996–97. Early services offered only a couple of megabytes of storage. Google's Gmail, launched on 1 April 2004 with 1 gigabyte of free storage, conversation threading and powerful search, reset expectations and pushed rivals to expand capacity and features.

Features and trade-offs

Modern webmail includes spam filtering, virus scanning, labels and folders, keyboard shortcuts, attachment previews, calendars and integration with other cloud services. Because messages reside on the provider's servers, users gain convenience and automatic backup but depend on an Internet connection and on the provider's policies, and free services have historically been funded by advertising, raising privacy questions. Many services also support standard IMAP access so users can combine webmail with local clients and mobile apps.
