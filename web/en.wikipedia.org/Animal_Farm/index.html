Animal Farm is an allegorical novella by the English author George Orwell, first published in England on 17 August 1945. The book tells the story of a group of farm animals who rebel against their human farmer, hoping to create a society where the animals can be equal, free, and happy. Ultimately, the rebellion is betrayed, and under the dictatorship of a pig named Napoleon, the farm ends up in a state as bad as it was before.

Plot summary

Inspired by the dying boar Old Major, the animals of Manor Farm expel the drunken farmer Mr. Jones and establish their own government under the principles of "Animalism", summarised in Seven Commandments, the most important being "All animals are equal." The pigs, as the cleverest animals, become the leaders; Snowball and Napoleon vie for power. Napoleon raises puppies in secret as enforcers, drives Snowball into exile, and gradually concentrates power. The commandments are twisted one by one, the hardworking horse Boxer is betrayed and sent to the knacker, and in the end the pigs walk upright, wear clothes, and are indistinguishable from men. The single remaining commandment reads: "All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others."

Themes and allegory

Orwell, a democratic socialist, wrote the book as a satire of the Russian Revolution and the Stalinist era of the Soviet Union. Old Major blends elements of Marx and Lenin; Napoleon represents Stalin; Snowball, Trotsky; the dogs, the secret police; Boxer, the loyal working class. Orwell described it as a "fairy story" and had difficulty finding a publisher because of its criticism of Britain's wartime ally. It became a major success after the war began to turn into the Cold War.

Legacy

Animal Farm remains widely read and taught; Time magazine selected it as one of the 100 best English-language novels from 1923 to 2005. Adaptations include the 1954 animated film and a 1999 live-action version.
