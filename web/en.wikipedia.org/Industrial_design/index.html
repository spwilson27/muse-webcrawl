Industrial design

Industrial design applies art and applied science to mass-produced products.

Industrial design is a professional practice of designing products, devices, objects, and services used by millions of people around the world every day. Industrial designers typically focus on the physical appearance, functionality and manufacturability of a product, though they are often involved much more deeply in the development cycle of a product's entire life.

History

The term "industrial design" is often attributed to Joseph Claude Sinel in 1927, although its origins lie in the mid-19th century growth of industrial production and the work of designers such as Christopher Dresser, sometimes regarded as the first independent industrial designer. The Deutscher Werkbund, founded in 1907, and the Bauhaus school in Weimar, founded in 1919, were central to establishing design as a discipline bridging craft and mass production. In the United States, figures such as Raymond Loewy, Henry Dreyfuss and Walter Dorwin Teague professionalised the field during the 1930s, styling everything from locomotives to refrigerators.

Process

Industrial design is a strategic problem-solving process that drives innovation, builds business success, and leads to a better quality of life through innovative products, systems, services, and experiences. The process commonly involves research into user needs, sketching and concept generation, computer-aided design modelling, prototyping, and refinement for manufacturing processes such as injection moulding or die casting.

Product design

Product design is often treated as a subset of industrial design, covering the creation of physical goods from consumer electronics to furniture. Designers must balance aesthetics, ergonomics, materials, cost and sustainability. Dieter Rams's ten principles for good design, developed at Braun, became a widely cited manifesto: good design is innovative, useful, aesthetic, understandable, unobtrusive, honest, long-lasting, thorough, environmentally friendly, and involves as little design as possible.
