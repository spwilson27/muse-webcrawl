Intrinsic motivation

Intrinsic motivation is the drive to do an activity for its inherent satisfaction rather than for an external reward. It contrasts with extrinsic motivation, driven by money, grades, praise or punishment.

Self-determination theory

Edward Deci and Richard Ryan's self-determination theory holds that intrinsic motivation flourishes when needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness are met. Research studied how expected tangible rewards can sometimes undermine intrinsic interest — the overjustification effect.

Applications

Education, workplace design, gamification and sports psychology seek conditions — choice, challenge matched to skill, and meaningful feedback — that support intrinsic motivation and flow.
