Xeon Phi

ManufacturerIntel
Launched2012 (Knights Corner)
ArchitectureManycore x86 (MIC)
Max cores76 (Knights Landing / Knights Mill)
Discontinued2018–2020

Xeon Phi was a series of manycore x86 processors designed and marketed by Intel for high-performance computing. Descended from Intel's Larrabee and Many Integrated Core (MIC) research, the chips packed dozens of relatively simple cores — each capable of wide vector operations — onto a single die, targeting supercomputers and workstations running highly parallel workloads.

Generations

The first product, Knights Corner (2012), offered up to 61 cores as a PCIe coprocessor card. Its successor, Knights Landing (2016), reached 72–76 cores with AVX-512 instructions and on-package high-bandwidth MCDRAM memory, and could run as a self-hosting bootable processor. Knights Mill (2017) optimized the design for deep learning, and a further generation, Knights Hill, was cancelled.

Use and discontinuation

Xeon Phi processors powered prominent supercomputers, including Tianhe-2 and Cori. Despite this success, the line struggled against GPU accelerators, and Intel discontinued the Xeon Phi family in 2018, with shipments ending by 2020, folding its vector technology into mainstream Xeon processors instead.
