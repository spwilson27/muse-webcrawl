Avant-garde

Marcel Duchamp's Fountain (1917), an icon of the avant-garde readymade.

The avant-garde (from French, "advance guard" or "vanguard") is a group of people who develop new and experimental ideas, particularly in the arts. The term originally described the foremost part of an army advancing into battle; from the 19th century it was applied metaphorically to artists and movements that "march ahead" of their time, challenging accepted aesthetic, social and political norms.

History of the concept

The metaphorical use of avant-garde appears in the circle of the utopian socialist Henri de Saint-Simon, whose followers cast artists as the vanguard of social transformation. By the late 19th century the term was associated with radical artistic movements — Symbolism, Fauvism, Cubism and Futurism — and in the 20th century with Dada, Surrealism, abstract art, experimental music and avant-garde cinema. The concept implies a deliberate opposition to mainstream or commercial culture, and often a programme, announced in manifestos, for remaking art and life together.

Theory

Theorists have debated whether the avant-garde attacks the institution of art itself or merely renews its forms. Peter Bürger's Theory of the Avant-Garde (1974) argued that the historical avant-gardes (Dada, early Surrealism, the Russian avant-garde) sought to abolish the separation between art and everyday life, and failed — their gestures being absorbed by the museum and the market. Clement Greenberg, by contrast, defined avant-garde culture as the pursuit of the absolute through medium-specific self-criticism, opposed to the mass-produced sentimentality he called kitsch.

Examples

Canonical avant-garde moments include Stravinsky's The Rite of Spring (1913), Duchamp's readymades, the Bauhaus, John Cage's experimental music, Abstract Expressionism, Fluxus, and the French New Wave in cinema. In later usage the label broadens to any cutting edge in art, fashion, literature or music, though purists reserve it for movements with an explicit radical programme.
