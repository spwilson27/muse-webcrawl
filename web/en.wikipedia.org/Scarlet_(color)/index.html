Scarlet (color)

Scarlet (hex #FF2400).

Scarlet is a bright red color, sometimes with a slightly orange tinge. In the spectrum of visible light, and on the traditional color wheel, it is one-quarter of the way between red and orange, slightly less orange than vermilion. Scarlet is a brilliant, strong red, often associated with courage, passion, religion, and in some contexts with sin and immorality.

Etymology

The word comes from the Middle English scarlet, via the Old French escarlate, from the Persian saqalat, a type of rich cloth. In medieval Europe, scarlet originally referred not to a color but to a high-quality woolen fabric; only later did it become attached to the bright red dye—often kermes—used to color the finest scarlets.

In culture

Scarlet is the color of the robes of cardinals of the Roman Catholic Church and of academic dress at many universities. British soldiers wore scarlet tunics from the seventeenth century until khaki was adopted around the turn of the twentieth century. In literature, Nathaniel Hawthorne's novel The Scarlet Letter (1850) made the color a symbol of shame and adultery in Puritan New England.

Variations

Related shades include vermilion, carmine, crimson, and flame. Scarlet pigments were historically produced from kermes insects and later from cochineal; modern scarlets are usually synthetic.
