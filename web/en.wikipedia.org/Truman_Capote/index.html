Truman Capote
Born: 30 September 1924, New Orleans, Louisiana
Died: 25 August 1984, Los Angeles, California
Notable works: Breakfast at Tiffany's (1958), In Cold Blood (1966)

Truman Garcia Capote (born Truman Streckfus Persons) was an American novelist, screenwriter, playwright and actor. Several of his short stories, novels and plays are recognised literary classics, and his works have been adapted into numerous films.

Career

Capote gained early acclaim with the novel Other Voices, Other Rooms (1948) and the novella Breakfast at Tiffany's (1958), whose heroine Holly Golightly became an American cultural icon. His "nonfiction novel" In Cold Blood (1966), researched with his friend Harper Lee, recounts the murders of the Clutter family in Kansas and is considered a pioneering work of New Journalism and true crime.

Later life

A flamboyant celebrity, Capote hosted the famous Black and White Ball in 1966. His unfinished novel Answered Prayers, whose published excerpts exposed the secrets of his high-society friends, alienated many of them, and he struggled with addiction in his final years, dying in 1984.
