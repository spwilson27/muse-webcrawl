Tonotopy

Tonotopy is the spatial arrangement of where sounds of different frequency are processed in the auditory system. Places tuned to high frequencies are laid out systematically apart from places tuned to low frequencies, preserving a map of pitch from the inner ear through to the auditory cortex.

In the cochlea

The best-known tonotopic map is in the cochlea: the stiff base of the basilar membrane responds best to high frequencies and the flexible apex to low frequencies, an arrangement described by Georg von Békésy's travelling-wave theory. Hair cells at each position therefore signal a characteristic frequency to the auditory nerve.

Central pathways

Tonotopic order is maintained at every relay of the auditory pathway — cochlear nucleus, inferior colliculus, medial geniculate body and primary auditory cortex, where neighbouring neurons respond to neighbouring frequencies. Cochlear implants exploit tonotopy by stimulating electrode positions matched to the frequency bands of speech.
