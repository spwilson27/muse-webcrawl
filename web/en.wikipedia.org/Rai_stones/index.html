Rai stones

A large rai stone on Yap
LocationYap Island, Micronesia
MaterialLimestone (quarried in Palau)
SizeUp to about 3.6 m (12 ft) in diameter
UseTraditional money / store of value

Rai stones (Yapese: raay) are large circular stone disks, usually with a hole in the centre, used as a form of money on the island of Yap in Micronesia. Carved from limestone quarried on Palau and other islands and transported hundreds of kilometres by canoe and raft, they range from a few centimetres to about 3.6 metres across.

Value and exchange

The value of a stone depended not only on size but on its history — the difficulty and danger of its transport, and notable past owners. Because moving large stones was impractical, ownership could change by oral agreement while the stone stayed in place; one famous stone lies on the seabed after sinking during transport yet continued to count as wealth. Rai stones were used in social transactions such as marriages, inheritances, political deals and compensation.

Later history

In the 19th century the Irish-American trader David Dean O'Keefe mass-produced stones using modern ships, causing inflation. German colonial authorities later restricted their use. Rai stones remain culturally significant symbols of wealth on Yap, appear on local licence plates, and are frequently cited as an early example of a public, consensus-based ledger of ownership.
