Varvara Stepanova

Born: 3 November 1894, Kovno, Russian Empire
Died: 20 May 1958, Moscow, USSR
Movement: Constructivism
Fields: Textile design, typography, stage design, photography
Spouse: Alexander Rodchenko

Varvara Fyodorovna Stepanova (Russian: Варва́ра Фёдоровна Степа́нова; 3 November 1894 – 20 May 1958) was a Russian and Soviet artist, designer and photographer. With her husband Alexander Rodchenko, she was a leading figure of the Constructivist avant-garde.

Career

Stepanova studied in Odessa and Moscow and by 1921 had abandoned easel painting for "production art": she designed textiles for the First State Textile Factory in Moscow, creating bold geometric printed fabrics, and designed functional work and sports clothing. She created stage and costume designs for Meyerhold's 1922 production of The Death of Tarelkin, contributed to the journals LEF and Novyi LEF, and taught textile design at VKhUTEMAS. In the 1930s she turned to book and magazine design and photomontage, working with Rodchenko on propaganda publications such as USSR in Construction.
