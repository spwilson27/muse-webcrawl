Professional ethics

Professional ethics encompass the personal and corporate standards of behaviour expected of professionals. Professions typically codify these expectations in codes of ethics enforced by professional bodies, covering duties to clients, the public, and the profession itself.

Components

Common components include honesty and integrity, competence and diligence, confidentiality, avoidance of conflicts of interest, and accountability. Some scholars identify honesty and integrity as the core of professional ethics because trust is fundamental to professional relationships. Codes may be aspirational, educational, or regulatory, and breaches can lead to discipline or loss of licence.

By profession

Examples include medical ethics (rooted in the Hippocratic tradition and principles of beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, and justice), legal ethics (duties of advocates and judges, attorney–client privilege), engineering ethics (holding paramount public safety), and journalistic ethics (accuracy, independence, minimising harm). Business ethics overlaps where professional standards apply to managers and accountants.
