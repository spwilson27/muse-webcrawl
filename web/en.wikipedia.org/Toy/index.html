Toy

A toy is an object used in play, generally designed for children but often enjoyed by adults as well. Toys are commonly made to represent familiar things — animals, people, vehicles, tools — in miniature or simplified form, and they play a central role in children's learning about the world and in the development of motor, cognitive, and social skills.

History

Toys are among the most ancient of artifacts: dolls, balls, rattles, toy carts, and spinning tops have been found in archaeological contexts from ancient Egypt, Greece, Rome, and the Indus Valley. Many toys originated as miniatures of adult implements. Industrialization in the nineteenth century made toys cheap and widely available, and the twentieth century added plastics, licensed characters, and electronic and video games, building a global toy industry.

Types

Toys are commonly grouped into categories: dolls and action figures; construction sets such as wooden blocks, LEGO, and Meccano; vehicles; puzzles and board games; educational and scientific toys; plush toys such as teddy bears; outdoor and sports toys; and electronic and digital toys. Collectible toys are also produced for adult hobbyists.

Developmental role

Play with toys supports children's physical coordination, language, problem solving, and imagination. Developmental psychologists and educators, following thinkers such as Friedrich Fröbel, have emphasized toys designed to encourage open-ended, creative play. Toys are also used in therapy and in work with animals — pet toys occupy a substantial market of their own.

Safety

Modern toys are regulated for hazards such as small parts that can be choked on, toxic paints and materials, sharp edges, and unsafe batteries. Standards include the European EN 71 and the American ASTM F963 requirements, and age grading guides purchasers to age-appropriate products.
