Neo-psychedelia

Neo-psychedelia
Stylistic origins: Psychedelic rock, post-punk, new wave
Cultural origins: Late 1970s; United Kingdom and United States
Related: Paisley Underground, shoegaze, dream pop, Madchester

Neo-psychedelia is a diverse genre of psychedelic music that draws inspiration from the sounds of 1960s psychedelia, either updating its approach or copying its style entirely. It emerged in the late 1970s as part of the post-punk and new wave scenes, and has flourished in successive waves ever since. Neo-psychedelic music typically combines atmospheric, swirling textures, extended jams, tape effects, and surreal lyrics with melodic songcraft, and varies from guitar-based indie rock to electronic dance music.

History

Early neo-psychedelic acts included the Teardrop Explodes, Echo & the Bunnymen, and the Soft Boys; Siouxsie and the Banshees and the Cure are also often cited. In the United States, the Paisley Underground scene of 1980s Los Angeles — bands such as the Dream Syndicate, the Bangles (early), Green on Red, and the Rain Parade — explicitly revived 1960s influences. The Church carried the style in Australia. British groups like the Stone Roses and the scene around Madchester and shoegaze (Spacemen 3, the Verve) carried it into the 1990s, while American acts including the Flaming Lips, Mercury Rev, and later MGMT, Tame Impala, and Animal Collective brought neo-psychedelia to festival-scale audiences in the 2000s and 2010s.

Characteristics

Common features include droning or jangling guitars, heavy use of effects (phaser, echo, reverse reverb), droning keyboards, and dreamy or detached vocals. Critics note the term is loose, often applied to any indie music with hazy, hallucinogenic qualities rather than to a single coherent movement.
