Group GT3

Group GT3, known technically as Cup Grand Touring Cars and commonly referred to as simply GT3, is a set of regulations maintained by the FIA for grand tourer racing cars designed for use in various auto racing series throughout the world. Introduced in 2005, GT3 allows for a wide variety of car types to be homologated with almost no limit on engine sizes and configurations or chassis construction and layout.

Concept

GT3 cars must be based on production road cars in series production. Performance is equalised between different models by the FIA and series organisers through Balance of Performance (BoP) adjustments — changes to weight, engine restrictors or boost, ride height, and aerodynamics — rather than by rigid technical specification. Cars are typically built by manufacturers and sold to customer teams, with prices in the region of half a million euros. ABS and traction control are permitted, and the cars produce roughly 500–600 horsepower depending on BoP.

Series and events

GT3 has become the most widely used GT formula in the world. Major championships using GT3 include the GT World Challenge series on several continents, the Intercontinental GT Challenge, IMSA's GTD classes, the DTM (from 2021), and numerous national series. Famous GT3 races include the Bathurst 12 Hour, the Spa 24 Hours, the Nürburgring 24 Hours (SP9 class), and the Dubai 24 Hour. Manufacturers that have built GT3 cars include Audi, BMW, Mercedes-AMG, Porsche, Ferrari, Lamborghini, McLaren, Aston Martin, Bentley, Nissan, Lexus, Honda/Acura, and Corvette.

Related categories

GT3 sits above GT4, a slower, cheaper entry-level class, and alongside the LM GTE/GTE categories formerly used at Le Mans; the FIA and ACO adopted GT3-based cars for the LMGT3 class of the World Endurance Championship from 2024.
