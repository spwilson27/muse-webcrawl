Interleukin 6 (IL-6) is an interleukin that acts as both a pro-inflammatory cytokine and an anti-inflammatory myokine. In humans, it is encoded by the IL6 gene. IL-6 is secreted by T cells and macrophages to stimulate immune response, for example during infection and after trauma, especially burns or other tissue damage leading to inflammation.

IL-6 is also considered a myokine, a cytokine produced by muscle cells that is elevated in response to muscular contractions. It is significantly elevated with exercise, and precedes the appearance of other cytokines in the circulation.

Function

IL-6 is an important mediator of fever and of the acute phase response. It can be secreted by macrophages in response to specific microbial molecules, referred to as pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs). IL-6 signals through the interleukin-6 receptor and the signal-transducing subunit gp130, activating the JAK-STAT signalling pathway, particularly STAT3.

Role in disease

Inhibition of IL-6 has roles in treating certain diseases. Many autoimmune diseases are treated by blocking IL-6, such as rheumatoid arthritis and the cytokine storm associated with severe infections. The monoclonal antibody tocilizumab, which blocks the IL-6 receptor, is used in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis and other conditions. Elevated IL-6 is also studied in association with chronic inflammation, obesity, and several cancers.
