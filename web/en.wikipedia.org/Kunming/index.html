Kunming is the capital and largest city of Yunnan province in southwestern China. A prefecture-level city, it is the province's political, economic, and transport hub, located on the northern shore of Lake Dian at an elevation of about 1,900 metres, which gives it a mild climate year-round and the nickname "Spring City".

History

The area was home to the ancient Dian Kingdom, absorbed into the Han dynasty in 109 BCE. Kunming later lay under Nanzhao and Dali rule before Mongol conquest. During the Second Sino-Japanese War it was the terminus of the Burma Road and a base for the Flying Tigers; it became an important industrial and military centre.

Economy and transport

Modern Kunming is a gateway to Southeast Asia, served by Kunming Changshui International Airport and railways to Vietnam and (via the China–Laos railway) to Vientiane. The city is known for flowers, tobacco, and tourism to sites such as the Stone Forest.
