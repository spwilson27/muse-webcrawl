Whitney Biennial

VenueWhitney Museum of American Art, New York City
FrequencyBiennial (roughly every two years)
First held1932 (as annual; biennial since 1973)

The Whitney Biennial is an exhibition of contemporary American art held at the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York City. It is generally considered one of the most important exhibitions in the American art world, known for showcasing emerging artists and for provoking debate about the state of contemporary art.

History

The museum began holding annual exhibitions of its collection's peers in 1932, alternating painting and sculpture. In 1973 the format was unified into a single biennial covering all media. Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney founded the museum to support living American artists, and the Biennial continues that mission.

Notable editions

The 1993 Biennial, curated by Thelma Golden and others, was famously devoted to identity politics and multiculturalism and sharply divided critics. The 2014 edition was the last held in the museum's Marcel Breuer building before its move downtown, and the 2017 edition was the first in the Renzo Piano-designed building in the Meatpacking District. The 2019 edition saw protests over a museum trustee's business ties, leading several artists to withdraw their work.

Reception

Critics routinely pronounce individual Biennials triumphs or failures, but the exhibition remains a career-making platform: inclusion is widely seen as a mark of arrival for American artists.
