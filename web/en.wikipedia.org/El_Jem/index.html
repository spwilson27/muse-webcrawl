El Jem

El Jem or El Djem is a town in Mahdia Governorate, Tunisia, famed for its remarkably preserved Roman amphitheatre, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

El Jem or El Djem (Tunisian Arabic: الجم) is a town in Mahdia Governorate, Tunisia. Its population was 21,234 in 2014. It is home to some of the most impressive Roman remains in Africa, particularly the world-famous Amphitheatre of El Jem.

Roman Thysdrus

The town was founded on the site of ancient Thysdrus, a Punic and then Roman city that prospered through the olive oil trade, becoming one of the richest towns in Roman Africa. A first, small amphitheatre was built in the 1st century AD, followed by a larger one around AD 238.

The amphitheatre

The Amphitheatre of El Jem is the largest Roman colosseum in North Africa and one of the largest in the world, capable of holding an estimated 35,000 spectators. Built entirely of stone blocks with three tiers of arcades, it was never fully completed, yet it survives in remarkable condition and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979. Local legend holds that it served as a fortress for the Berber leader Al-Kahina in the 7th century.

Today

A museum displays mosaics recovered from Roman villas in the town, and the amphitheatre hosts an annual international festival of symphonic music. The site famously appeared in films including Monty Python's Life of Brian.
