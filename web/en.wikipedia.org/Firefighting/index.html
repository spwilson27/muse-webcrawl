Firefighting is a profession aimed at controlling and extinguishing fires. A person who engages in firefighting is known as a firefighter or fireman. Firefighters typically undergo a high level of technical training spanning fire dynamics, rescue techniques, hazardous materials handling and emergency medical care. This technical training is in addition to general training in the prevention and mitigation of hazards.

Tactics

Firefighting tactics prioritise, in order, life safety, incident stabilisation and property conservation. At structure fires, crews perform size-up, establish a water supply, deploy hose lines for an interior or exterior attack, ventilate heat and smoke, and conduct primary and secondary searches for occupants. Water cools burning fuel below its ignition temperature; foam, dry chemical and carbon dioxide agents are used for liquid fuel and electrical fires where water would be ineffective or dangerous.

Wildland firefighting

Forest and wildland fires are fought with different tools and strategies: hand crews cut firelines to remove fuel, engines patrol fire edges, and aircraft drop water and retardant. Controlled backburning and the creation of defensible space are central to wildfire management.

Hazards

Firefighting is a dangerous occupation: risks include burns, smoke inhalation, structural collapse, heat stress and long-term exposure to carcinogenic combustion products. Modern turnout gear and self-contained breathing apparatus substantially reduce, but do not eliminate, these risks.

History

Organised firefighting began in ancient Rome with the Corps of Vigiles. Bucket brigades, hand pumps and insurance-company brigades preceded the municipal fire departments of the nineteenth century, from which modern professional services developed alongside steam and then motorised fire engines.
