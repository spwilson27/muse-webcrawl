Molding (process)


Image placeholder: plastic injection moulding machine

Molding (American English) or moulding (British and Commonwealth English) is the process of manufacturing by shaping liquid or pliable raw material using a rigid frame called a mould or matrix. The mould is a hollow container, often made of hardened steel, into which material — molten metal, plastic, glass, or rubber — is poured, injected, or pressed; the material takes the cavity's shape as it cools, solidifies, or cures, and is then ejected or extracted.

Principal processes

Injection moulding forces molten plastic into a closed mould under high pressure and is the dominant process for mass-produced plastic parts. Blow moulding inflates a heated plastic tube (parison) against a mould to make bottles and other hollow goods. Compression moulding presses material — commonly thermosetting plastics or rubber — between heated mould halves. Rotational moulding coats the inside of a slowly rotating heated mould to make large hollow items such as tanks. Casting pours liquid metal or resin into a mould under gravity or low pressure, a technique thousands of years old, as in bronze casting and sand casting.

Mould design

Moulds generally consist of two or more halves meeting at a parting line, with sprues, runners, and gates delivering material to the cavity, vents for escaping air, and ejector pins to release the finished part. Draft angles, uniform wall thickness, and cooling channels are designed to avoid defects such as warping, sink marks, and short shots. High-precision moulds ("tools") can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars and produce millions of parts.
