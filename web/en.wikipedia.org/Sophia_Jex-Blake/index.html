Sophia Louisa Jex-Blake (21 January 1840 – 7 January 1912) was an English physician, teacher, and feminist. She led the campaign to secure medical education for women in Britain and was one of the first female doctors in the United Kingdom.

Early life and education

Born in Hastings to a retired lawyer, Jex-Blake was educated at home and briefly attended Queen's College, London, where she later taught mathematics despite her father's objection to her taking a salary. A visit to the United States in 1865, where she observed women practising medicine, fixed her ambition.

The Edinburgh Seven

In 1869 Jex-Blake and six other women — the "Edinburgh Seven" — were admitted to study medicine at the University of Edinburgh, the first women undergraduates at a British university. Hostility culminated in the Surgeons' Hall riot of 1870, and although the women passed their examinations, the university ultimately refused to graduate them. Jex-Blake finally qualified in Bern, Switzerland, and took her MD in Dublin in 1877, the year the Medical Register was opened to women.

Later career

She founded the London School of Medicine for Women (with Elizabeth Garrett Anderson and others) and later the Edinburgh School of Medicine for Women, and established a dispensary and hospital for women and children in Edinburgh. She retired to Sussex, where she lived with her companion Dr Margaret Todd, who wrote her biography.

Legacy

In 2019 the University of Edinburgh awarded the Edinburgh Seven posthumous honorary degrees, collected on their behalf by current female medical students.
