Cinema of Hong Kong

Hong Kong's film industry became famous worldwide for its martial arts and action cinema.

The cinema of Hong Kong (Chinese: 香港電影) is one of the three major threads in the history of Chinese-language cinema, alongside the cinema of China and the cinema of Taiwan. As a former British colony, Hong Kong developed a highly commercial film industry freed from the censorship of the mainland, and for decades ranked among the largest motion picture exporters in the world.

Golden age

After the Second World War, refugee filmmakers from Shanghai rebuilt the industry, producing Cantonese and Mandarin films. The studio era was dominated by Shaw Brothers and Golden Harvest. In the 1970s, martial arts films starring Bruce Lee — The Big Boss (1971), Fist of Fury (1972) and Enter the Dragon (1973) — conquered international markets, followed by the kung fu comedy of Jackie Chan and Sammo Hung.

New wave and beyond

The Hong Kong New Wave of the late 1970s and 1980s brought directors such as Tsui Hark, Ann Hui and Wong Kar-wai, while John Woo's "heroic bloodshed" films, notably A Better Tomorrow (1986) and The Killer (1989), redefined action cinema globally. Infernal Affairs (2002) was later remade in Hollywood as The Departed. Output declined from the mid-1990s amid piracy, the Asian financial crisis and Hollywood competition, and production after the 1997 handover has increasingly taken the form of co-productions with mainland China.

Characteristics

Hong Kong cinema is noted for its kinetic action choreography, genre hybridity, and stars with global recognition, including Chow Yun-fat, Stephen Chow, Tony Leung Chiu-wai and Maggie Cheung.
