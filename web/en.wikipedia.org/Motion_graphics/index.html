Motion graphics

[Image: An example of animated graphic design]

Motion graphics are pieces of animation or digital footage which create the illusion of motion or rotation, and are usually combined with audio for use in multimedia projects. The term distinguishes video content that moves graphic elements - shapes, images and text - from character animation and live-action footage. Motion graphics are a mainstay of film and television title sequences, broadcast design, advertising, user interfaces and social media.

History

Early precedents include the abstract films of Viking Eggeling, Hans Richter and Oskar Fischinger in the 1920s and 1930s. The field took professional form in the 1950s, when designer Saul Bass revolutionized film titles with sequences for The Man with the Golden Arm (1955) and Hitchcock films, and John Whitney - who co-founded Motion Graphics Incorporated in 1960 and coined the term's modern usage - applied mechanical and later computer techniques to titles and commercials. Maurice Binder's James Bond sequences and Pablo Ferro's work extended the form, and the arrival of desktop software in the 1990s, especially Adobe After Effects, democratized production.

Techniques

Common techniques include keyframed 2D and 3D animation, kinetic typography, stop-motion, compositing, and procedurally driven design in tools such as Cinema 4D and Houdini. Motion design overlaps with but differs from traditional animation in focusing on abstract and typographic elements rather than characters and narrative.
