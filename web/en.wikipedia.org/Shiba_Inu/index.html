Shiba Inu

The Shiba Inu (Japanese: 柴犬) is a Japanese breed of hunting dog, the smallest of the six original and distinct spitz breeds native to Japan. A small, agile dog bred to hunt small game in mountainous terrain, it is one of Japan's designated natural monuments.

Characteristics

Shibas stand roughly 35–43 cm at the withers and have a dense double coat in red, black and tan, sesame, or cream, with characteristic white markings (urajiro). They have pricked triangular ears, dark eyes, and a tail curled over the back. The breed is known for its spirited, independent, cat-like temperament, cleanliness, and the dramatic "Shiba scream" when distressed or excited.

History and popularity

Nearly lost during the Second World War through bombing and distemper, the Shiba was rebuilt from three surviving regional lines (Shinshu, Mino, and San'in). It has since become Japan's most popular companion dog breed and achieved wide international fame, including as the face of the "Doge" internet meme.
