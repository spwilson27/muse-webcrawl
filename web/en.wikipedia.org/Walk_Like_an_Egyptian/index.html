Walk Like an Egyptian


Artist: The Bangles
Album: Different Light
Released: 1986 (single)
Songwriter: Liam Sternberg
Billboard Hot 100: No. 1 (1987)

"Walk Like an Egyptian" is a song by the American pop rock band the Bangles, released in 1986 as the third single from their second studio album, Different Light. It became the band's first number-one hit, topping the Billboard Hot 100 in 1987 and finishing as Billboard's top single of that year.

Background

Songwriter Liam Sternberg wrote the song after watching ferry passengers in the English Channel awkwardly balancing to keep their footing, which reminded him of the angular, two-dimensional poses of figures in ancient Egyptian tomb paintings. A demo of the song was offered to other artists, including Toni Basil, before the Bangles recorded it. Producer David Kahne assigned the verses to be sung in turn by three of the four band members — Vicki Peterson, Michael Steele and Susanna Hoffs — over a groove built around a drum machine and tambourine.

Reception and legacy

The accompanying music video, intercutting the band with footage of people attempting the song's namesake dance on the street, was a staple of MTV and was nominated at the 1987 MTV Video Music Awards. The novelty dance it inspired — arms bent at right angles in imitation of Egyptian art — became an enduring pop-culture reference. After the attacks of September 11, 2001 the song appeared on a widely reported (though often mischaracterised) list of titles circulated within the radio industry as lyrically questionable. The Bangles recorded a new version decades later for a television commercial, and the original remains one of the signature hits of the 1980s.
