Nero Claudius Caesar Augustus Germanicus (born Lucius Domitius Ahenobarbus; AD 37–68) was Roman emperor from AD 54 to 68 and the last emperor of the Julio-Claudian dynasty. He is associated with tyranny and extravagance, the Great Fire of Rome, and the first imperial persecution of Christians, although modern historians question many of the hostile ancient accounts.

Accession and early reign

Nero's mother Agrippina married the emperor Claudius, who adopted Nero and named him heir over his own son. After Claudius's death in AD 54 — which ancient sources attribute to Agrippina's poisoning — Nero became emperor at sixteen, initially guided by Agrippina, the philosopher Seneca and the prefect Burrus. The first five years (the quinquennium Neronis) were later praised by Trajan as well governed. In AD 59 Nero had his mother murdered, and his rule grew increasingly autocratic.

Fire, building and persecution

The Great Fire of Rome (AD 64) destroyed large parts of the city. Nero organised relief and rebuilt in fire-resistant stone, but also appropriated a vast central estate for his palace, the Domus Aurea ("Golden House"). Tacitus reports that Nero blamed the Christians for the fire and executed many of them cruelly — the first Roman state persecution of Christians. The popular image of Nero "fiddling while Rome burned" is apocryphal (the fiddle did not exist); ancient sources more plausibly accuse him of singing amid the flames.

Revolts and death

Nero's passion for public performance as a singer, lyre-player and charioteer scandalised the aristocracy. In AD 68 the revolts of Vindex in Gaul and Galba in Spain, compounded by unrest over grain and taxation, destroyed his support; declared a public enemy by the Senate, he fled Rome and took his own life on 9 June AD 68, reportedly lamenting "What an artist dies in me!" His death ended the dynasty and triggered the civil wars of the Year of the Four Emperors (AD 69).
