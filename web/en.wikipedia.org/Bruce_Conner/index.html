Bruce Conner (November 18, 1933 – July 7, 2008) was an American artist and filmmaker who pioneered found-footage film and assemblage sculpture. A central figure in the San Francisco Beat and Funk art scenes, he worked across film, sculpture, painting, collage, drawing, and photography, often refusing to limit himself to a single medium or style and repeatedly announcing his "retirement" from the art world.

Career

Conner moved from Kansas to San Francisco in 1957 and quickly gained notoriety for assemblage sculptures made from discarded materials — nylon stockings, broken furniture, dolls, and bicycle wheels — such as RATBASTARD (1958). He was a founder of the Rat Bastard Protective Association, an informal group of Bay Area artists including Joan Brown, Jay DeFeo, Wally Hedrick, and Michael McClure.

His 1958 film A MOVIE, assembled entirely from stock footage, newsreels, stag films, and B-movie fragments with a title card declaring "A Movie by Bruce Conner," is considered a landmark of montage and American avant-garde cinema, anticipating music videos and later found-footage art. REPORT (1963–1967) obsessively reworked news footage of the assassination of President John F. Kennedy. CROSSROADS (1976), made from declassified footage of the 1946 Bikini Atoll atomic tests, slows the mushroom cloud into a sublime, terrifying spectacle, with music by Patrick Gleeson and Terry Riley. Later films include VALSE TRISTE (1977) and AMERICA IS WAITING (1981), the latter made with music by David Byrne and Brian Eno.

Legacy

Conner also produced intricate inkblot and felt-tip drawings, collages under the pseudonym "Anonymous," and photographs of the 1970s San Francisco punk scene. A major retrospective, "Bruce Conner: It's All True," organized by SFMOMA and MoMA, opened in 2016. His work is held by major museums including the Museum of Modern Art and the Whitney Museum of American Art.
