Patrick Quinlan (1919–1997) was an Irish Army officer who commanded "A" Company, 35th Battalion, serving with the United Nations Operation in the Congo (ONUC), during the Siege of Jadotville in September 1961. His leadership during the five-day siege, in which his company of about 155 men held off a force of some 3,000–4,000 Katangese troops without losing a single soldier killed, is regarded as one of the most distinguished actions in Irish military history.

Early life and career

Quinlan was born in Cashel, County Tipperary, in 1919 and was commissioned into the Irish Army. By 1961 he held the rank of Commandant (equivalent to major) and was selected to command A Company of the 35th Battalion for service in the Congo during the Congo Crisis.

Siege of Jadotville

In September 1961, A Company was deployed to Jadotville (now Likasi) in the breakaway province of Katanga. On 13 September, Katangese forces supported by foreign mercenaries attacked the Irish position. Over five days Quinlan's lightly armed company — mostly young soldiers, many in their teens, with no combat experience — repelled repeated assaults, inflicting heavy casualties (estimated at up to 300 killed) while suffering only a handful of wounded and none killed. Quinlan chose defensive positions skilfully and maintained morale and discipline throughout.

On 17 September, cut off, out of water and ammunition, and with relief attempts having failed, Quinlan negotiated a ceasefire and surrendered to avoid the annihilation of his men. The company was held as prisoners of war for about a month before being released in a prisoner exchange.

Aftermath and recognition

On returning to Ireland, the Jadotville veterans received little official recognition for decades; the surrender was widely misunderstood. Quinlan retired as a Colonel. Following a long campaign led by veterans and historians, Ireland formally honoured the unit: in 2016 the men were awarded a Presidential Unit Citation, the first in the history of the Irish state, and a commemorative medal followed in 2017. Quinlan's actions were depicted in the 2016 film The Siege of Jadotville, in which he was played by Jamie Dornan.
