Innocence Project

The Innocence Project is an American nonprofit legal organization that is committed to exonerating individuals who have been wrongly convicted — through the use of DNA profiling — and to reforming the criminal justice system to prevent future injustices. The group cites various studies estimating that in the United States between 1% and 5% of all prisoners are innocent.

History

The Innocence Project was founded in 1992 by Barry Scheck and Peter Neufeld, who were then professors at the Benjamin N. Cardozo School of Law at Yeshiva University in New York City. It began as a legal clinic and became an independent nonprofit in 2004. By the 2020s, the Project and the wider Innocence Network had helped free hundreds of wrongfully convicted people; exonerees had collectively served thousands of years in prison.

Work

The Project litigates post-conviction DNA testing cases, assists exonerees after release, and advocates policy reforms such as improved eyewitness identification procedures, mandatory recording of interrogations, better preservation of biological evidence, and access to post-conviction DNA testing. Studies of DNA exonerations show that leading causes of wrongful conviction include eyewitness misidentification, misapplication of forensic science, false confessions, and informant perjury.
