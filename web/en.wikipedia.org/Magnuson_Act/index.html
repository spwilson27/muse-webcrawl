Magnuson Act

Full title: Chinese Exclusion Repeal Act of 1943
Enacted: 17 December 1943
Sponsor: Rep. Warren G. Magnuson (D-WA)
Effect: Repealed the Chinese Exclusion Acts; set an annual quota of 105 Chinese immigrants

The Magnuson Act, formally the Chinese Exclusion Repeal Act of 1943, was legislation enacted by the United States Congress that repealed the Chinese Exclusion Act of 1882 and the laws that had extended it for six decades. Named for its sponsor, Representative (later Senator) Warren G. Magnuson of Washington, it was signed into law by President Franklin D. Roosevelt on 17 December 1943.

Provisions

The Act made Chinese immigrants eligible for naturalisation for the first time since the 19th century and established a small annual immigration quota of 105 persons of Chinese ancestry, calculated in a way that applied worldwide rather than by nationality alone. Symbolically, it ended the statutory exclusion of a named ethnic group from American immigration law.

Context and legacy

Repeal was driven partly by wartime politics: China was a valued ally against Japan, and Japanese propaganda exploited American exclusion laws across Asia. Although the quota remained tiny and broader discriminatory quotas persisted until the Immigration and Nationality Acts of 1952 and 1965, the Magnuson Act is remembered as the first breach in the exclusion system and an important milestone in Asian American civil rights history.
