Brand management

Brand management is a function of marketing that uses techniques to increase the perceived value of a product line or brand over time. It involves planning, developing and overseeing a brand's identity, positioning and communications so as to build awareness, reputation and loyalty, and ultimately brand equity — the commercial value derived from consumers' perception of the brand name.

History

The discipline is usually traced to a 1931 memo by Procter & Gamble executive Neil McElroy proposing dedicated "brand men" for products such as Camay soap — the origin of the brand-manager role that spread through consumer-goods companies after the Second World War. In the late 20th century, scholars including David Aaker and Kevin Lane Keller formalised brand equity and customer-based brand management models.

Key activities

Brand management encompasses defining brand identity and values; positioning the brand against competitors; designing and guarding visual identity (name, logo, packaging, distinctive assets); planning advertising and sponsorship; managing brand architecture — the relationships among corporate, product and sub-brands; and deciding on extensions, licensing and co-branding. Digital media has added continuous listening, social-media management and rapid response to reputational crises.

Measurement

Managers track awareness, associations, perceived quality and loyalty, and use valuation methods (including the rankings published by Interbrand and Brand Finance) to estimate the financial value of brands, among the largest intangible assets of many companies.
