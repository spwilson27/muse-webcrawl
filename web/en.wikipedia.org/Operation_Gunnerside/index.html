Operation Gunnerside was a British- and Norwegian-led operation during the Second World War to sabotage the production of heavy water at the Vemork hydroelectric plant in Telemark, occupied Norway. Heavy water was a key component of Nazi Germany's nuclear weapons research, and the February 1943 attack is considered one of the most successful acts of sabotage of the war.

The Vemork plant at Rjukan, site of the heavy water sabotage.

Background

The Norsk Hydro plant at Vemork was the world's first industrial-scale producer of heavy water (deuterium oxide), a moderator that could enable a plutonium-producing reactor. A first commando attempt, Operation Freshman in November 1942, ended in disaster when both gliders crashed and the survivors were executed by the Gestapo. Gunnerside sent six additional Norwegian Special Operations Executive commandos, led by Joachim Rønneberg, to join the four-man advance party (Operation Grouse/Swallow) already surviving on the Hardangervidda plateau.

The attack

On the night of 27–28 February 1943, the team descended a steep ravine, crossed the frozen Måna river and climbed to the plant. Avoiding guards, they entered through a cable duct, placed explosive charges on the heavy-water electrolysis cells and escaped before detonation. The explosion destroyed the cells and some 500 kg of heavy water. All ten commandos escaped without a shot fired; most reached Sweden, while the advance party remained in Norway.

Aftermath

Production resumed within months, prompting Allied bombing in November 1943, after which Germany decided to move its heavy water stocks to Germany. Norwegian saboteurs sank the ferry SF Hydro carrying the stocks across Lake Tinn in February 1944, effectively ending the German heavy-water effort. The story has been dramatised in the film The Heroes of Telemark (1965) and the TV series The Heavy Water War.
