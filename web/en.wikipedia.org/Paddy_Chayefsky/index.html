Paddy Chayefsky

Paddy Chayefsky
Born: Sidney Aaron Chayefsky, January 29, 1923, Bronx, New York City, U.S.
Died: August 1, 1981 (aged 58), New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Screenwriter, playwright, novelist
Notable works: Marty, The Hospital, Network

Sidney Aaron "Paddy" Chayefsky (January 29, 1923 – August 1, 1981) was an American playwright, screenwriter and novelist. He is the only person to have won three solo Academy Awards for Best Screenplay — for Marty (1955), The Hospital (1971), and Network (1976).

Chayefsky was one of the most renowned dramatists of the Golden Age of Television. His intimate, realistic scripts provided a significant influence on the style of television drama in the 1950s. He wrote for the anthology series Philco Television Playhouse; his teleplay Marty (1953), the story of a lonely Bronx butcher, was adapted into the 1955 film that won the Academy Award for Best Picture and the Palme d'Or at Cannes.

Network and later work

His satirical film Network (1976), directed by Sidney Lumet, about a television news anchor who suffers a breakdown on air and is exploited for ratings, is considered one of the greatest screenplays ever written; its line "I'm as mad as hell, and I'm not going to take this anymore!" became a cultural catchphrase. Chayefsky also wrote the science-fiction novel Altered States (1978) and its 1980 film adaptation, from which he disassociated himself after creative disputes, using the pseudonym "Sidney Aaron".
