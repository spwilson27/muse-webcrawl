Siege of Malta (1565)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Siege of Malta occurred in 1565 when the Ottoman Empire attempted to conquer the island of Malta, then held by the Knights Hospitaller (the Order of St John). Often considered one of the most ferocious sieges of the sixteenth century, it ended in a famous victory for the Knights and their Maltese allies and checked Ottoman expansion in the western Mediterranean.

Forces

The Ottoman expedition, ordered by Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent, brought perhaps 35,000–40,000 troops under Mustafa Pasha and the admiral Piali Pasha, with the corsair Dragut among its commanders. The defenders numbered roughly 6,000–9,000, including about 600 Knights and men-at-arms under Grand Master Jean Parisot de Valette, Spanish and Italian soldiers, and Maltese militia.

The siege

The Ottomans landed on 18 May 1565 and first attacked the small Fort St Elmo, guarding the entrance to Grand Harbour. Expected to fall in days, St Elmo resisted for over a month of bombardment and assaults, costing the Ottomans thousands of men — Dragut was mortally wounded — before its last defenders were overwhelmed on 23 June. The focus then shifted to the towns of Birgu and Senglea across the harbour, subjected to massive assaults in July and August. A small relief force from Sicily (the piccolo soccorso) slipped in during the summer, and a larger relief (the gran soccorso) landed in September, prompting the Ottomans to re-embark after a final defeat on land.

Aftermath

Ottoman losses were enormous — perhaps 25,000–35,000 dead. The victory was celebrated across Christian Europe. The Knights rebuilt their defences and founded the new fortified capital, named Valletta after the Grand Master. Malta remained in the Order's hands until 1798.
