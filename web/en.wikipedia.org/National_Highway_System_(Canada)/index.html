National Highway System (Canada)

The National Highway System (NHS) in Canada is a federal designation for a network of about 38,000 kilometres of key interprovincial and international highways. Although highways in Canada are built and maintained by the provinces and territories, the NHS identifies the routes considered most important to the national economy and mobility.

Structure

The system, established in 1988 by the Council of Ministers Responsible for Transportation and Highway Safety, is divided into three classes: Core Routes (principal interprovincial and international corridors, including the Trans-Canada Highway), Feeder Routes connecting core routes to population and economic centres, and Northern and Remote Routes providing access to northern communities.

Funding

Designation does not transfer ownership to the federal government, but NHS routes are prioritised in federal infrastructure funding programmes. The system was reviewed and expanded in 2005 to include additional routes of national significance.
