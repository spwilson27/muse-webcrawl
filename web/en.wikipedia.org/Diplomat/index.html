Diplomat

A diplomat is a person appointed by a state or an intergovernmental institution such as the United Nations or the European Union to conduct diplomacy with one or more other states or international organisations.

Functions

Diplomats represent and protect the interests of their sending state, negotiate agreements, gather and report information on conditions in the host country, and promote friendly relations. Their status, privileges and immunities are governed largely by the 1961 Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations.

Ranks

Diplomatic ranks descend from ambassador and high commissioner, through envoy and minister, to chargés d'affaires and attachés. Ambassadors are normally accredited to heads of state and head an embassy.

Diplomatic service

Most states maintain a professional foreign service, recruiting and training career diplomats, though heads of government may also appoint political envoys. Diplomacy is among the oldest professions of statecraft, with roots in ancient emissaries and the resident embassies of Renaissance Italy.
