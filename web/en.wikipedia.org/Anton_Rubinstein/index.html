Anton Grigoryevich Rubinstein (28 November 1829 – 20 November 1894) was a Russian pianist, composer and conductor who became a pivotal figure in Russian culture when he founded the Saint Petersburg Conservatory in 1862. He was the elder brother of the pianist and composer Nikolai Rubinstein, founder of the Moscow Conservatory.

Early life

Rubinstein was born in the village of Vikhvatinets in Podolia, in what is now Moldova. His father was a pencil manufacturer and his mother a music teacher who gave him his first piano lessons. A child prodigy, he gave his first public performance in Moscow at the age of nine and soon toured Europe, performing in Paris, London, Vienna and Berlin, where he was heard and encouraged by Felix Mendelssohn and Giacomo Meyerbeer. In Paris he also came to the attention of Franz Liszt, who became an important influence.

Career as a pianist

As a virtuoso pianist Rubinstein ranked among the greatest of the nineteenth century, often compared with Liszt. His playing was noted for its power, depth of tone and emotional breadth. He toured extensively in Europe and, in 1872–73, undertook a celebrated tour of the United States with the violinist Henryk Wieniawski, giving more than 200 concerts. His monumental cycle of seven recitals tracing the history of piano literature became a landmark of concert programming.

Saint Petersburg Conservatory

Frustrated by the amateur status of musicians in Russia, Rubinstein founded the Russian Musical Society in 1859 and, with the patronage of Grand Duchess Elena Pavlovna, opened the Saint Petersburg Conservatory in 1862 — the first music conservatory in Russia. He served as its director and professor of piano. His pupils included Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, the conservatory's first composition graduate.

Compositions

Rubinstein was a prolific composer, writing twenty operas, six symphonies, five piano concertos, and a large quantity of chamber and piano music. His best-known works include the opera The Demon (1871, after Lermontov), the Fourth Piano Concerto, and the famous "Melody in F" for piano. Although his compositions were immensely popular during his lifetime, his reputation as a composer declined after his death, while his historical importance as a pianist and educator endured.

Later life and death

Rubinstein returned to direct the conservatory from 1887 to 1891, working to raise its standards. He died at Peterhof in 1894, a week before his sixty-fifth birthday. The Anton Rubinstein competition and the street named after him in Saint Petersburg preserve his memory, and the conservatory he founded remains one of Russia's leading musical institutions.
