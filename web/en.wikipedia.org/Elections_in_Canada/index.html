Elections in Canada are held at the federal, provincial and territorial, and municipal levels. Federal elections choose members of the House of Commons, who in turn determine the government of Canada. The country uses a first-past-the-post plurality system in single-member districts called ridings.

Federal elections

General elections are administered by Elections Canada, an independent non-partisan agency headed by the Chief Electoral Officer. Under the Canada Elections Act, a general election must be held on the third Monday of October in the fourth calendar year following the previous election, although the Governor General may dissolve Parliament earlier on the advice of the Prime Minister. Campaigns are formally launched when the writs of election are issued.

Voting

Canadian citizens aged 18 or older are eligible to vote. Voters may cast ballots in person on election day, at advance polls, by mail, or at Elections Canada offices. The secret ballot has been used federally since 1874.

Political parties

Major federal parties have included the Liberal Party, the Conservative Party, the New Democratic Party, the Bloc Québécois, and the Green Party. Party leaders who win a plurality of seats are typically invited to form government.
