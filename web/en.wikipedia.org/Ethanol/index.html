Ethanol

Ethanol (also called ethyl alcohol, grain alcohol, drinking alcohol, or simply alcohol) is an organic compound with the chemical formula CH3CH2OH. It is an alcohol, with its formula also written as C2H5OH, C2H6O or EtOH, where Et stands for ethyl. Ethanol is a volatile, flammable, colorless liquid with a characteristic wine-like odor and pungent taste. It is a psychoactive substance and is the principal active ingredient in alcoholic drinks.

Production

Ethanol is naturally produced by the fermentation of sugars by yeasts; this is the process used for alcoholic beverages and much fuel ethanol, made chiefly from corn in the United States and sugarcane in Brazil. Industrially, ethanol is also produced petrochemically by the acid-catalysed hydration of ethylene. Ethanol is a renewable fuel when made from biomass, and is widely blended with gasoline (e.g. E10, E85).

Uses

Beyond beverages, ethanol serves as a solvent, antiseptic and disinfectant (including hand sanitizers), a chemical feedstock, a fuel, and the working fluid of alcohol thermometers. In medicine it is used as an antidote for methanol and ethylene glycol poisoning.

Effects and chemistry

Ethanol acts as a central nervous system depressant. In the body it is metabolised first to acetaldehyde by alcohol dehydrogenase and then to acetic acid. As a two-carbon alcohol it mixes fully with water, burns with a pale blue flame, and forms an azeotrope with water at about 95.6% ethanol by mass, which is why distillation alone cannot yield absolute ethanol.
