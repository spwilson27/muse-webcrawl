Ernst Ising

Ernst Ising
Born: May 10, 1900, Cologne, Germany
Died: May 11, 1998, Peoria, Illinois, United States
Field: Physics
Known for: Ising model

Ernst Ising was a German-American physicist best remembered as the developer of the Ising model of ferromagnetism. The model, suggested by his teacher Wilhelm Lenz, was the subject of his 1924 doctoral thesis in Hamburg.

The Ising model

Ising studied a one-dimensional chain of magnetic moments ("spins") that could point up or down, interacting only with their nearest neighbours. He showed correctly that the one-dimensional model has no phase transition at any finite temperature, but incorrectly generalised this to higher dimensions and concluded the model could not describe ferromagnetism. Lars Onsager's exact solution of the two-dimensional model in 1944 demonstrated a phase transition, and the model became a cornerstone of statistical mechanics, with applications in physics, chemistry, biology, neuroscience and social science.

Life

Barred from teaching under Nazi racial laws because of his Jewish ancestry, Ising worked at a school for Jewish children and fled Germany in 1939. After wartime years in Luxembourg he emigrated to the United States in 1947, teaching at Minot State Teachers College and then for three decades at Bradley University in Peoria, Illinois.
