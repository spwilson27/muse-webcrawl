Mont Pelerin Society

Founded1947
LocationMont Pèlerin, Switzerland (first meeting)
Key figuresFriedrich Hayek, Milton Friedman, Karl Popper

The Mont Pelerin Society (MPS) is an international organisation of economists, philosophers, and intellectuals committed to classical liberal ideas: free markets, limited government, private property, and the rule of law. It was founded in April 1947 at a conference organised by Friedrich Hayek at Mont Pèlerin, above Lake Geneva in Switzerland.

History

Thirty-nine scholars attended the founding meeting, including Ludwig von Mises, Milton Friedman, George Stigler, and Karl Popper. Members later founded or staffed influential think tanks such as the Institute of Economic Affairs in London and the Heritage Foundation, and several members won the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics.

Influence

The society is often credited with laying the intellectual groundwork for the market-oriented policy turn of the late 1970s and 1980s associated with Margaret Thatcher and Ronald Reagan, though its meetings are private and its influence informal.
