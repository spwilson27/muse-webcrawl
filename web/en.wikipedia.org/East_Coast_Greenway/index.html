The East Coast Greenway is a developing 3,000-mile (4,800 km) system of off-road trails and low-traffic routes for cyclists and pedestrians, intended to link cities along the Atlantic coast of the United States from Calais, Maine, to Key West, Florida. It passes through 15 states and the District of Columbia and connects 450 communities.

Development

The project was launched in 1991 by a group of cyclists and trail advocates, who formed the East Coast Greenway Alliance in 1992 to coordinate the route. Rather than a single new trail, the greenway links existing and planned local trails — rail trails, canal towpaths and waterfront paths — into one signed spine route, supplemented by an inland complementary route through parts of the Carolinas and Georgia. Roughly a third or more of the route is now on completed off-road paths, with the remainder on interim on-road alignments.

Use

The greenway serves both long-distance touring cyclists riding the whole coast and local users making short trips. Segments through metropolitan areas such as Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Washington, D.C., and Miami are among the most heavily used trails in the United States.
