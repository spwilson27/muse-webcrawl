Haunting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A haunting is the supposed recurrence of certain paranormal phenomena associated with a particular location, often a building, and attributed to the presence of a ghost or other supernatural being. Reported phenomena include apparitions, unexplained sounds such as footsteps or knocking, moving or disappearing objects, sudden temperature changes, and feelings of an unseen presence.

History

Accounts of haunted places are ancient: the Roman playwright Plautus wrote a comedy about a haunted house, and the historian Pliny the Younger described a haunted house in Athens. Belief in hauntings is woven into the folklore of many cultures and religions. Interest surged during the nineteenth-century spiritualist movement, and investigators such as the Society for Psychical Research (founded 1882) began documenting famous cases, including the Borley Rectory in England, often called "the most haunted house in England".

Types

Parapsychologists have proposed informal categories: residual hauntings, in which phenomena repeat like a recording without apparent intelligence; intelligent hauntings, in which the entity seems to interact with witnesses; and poltergeist activity, characterised by noisy disturbances and moving objects, which some investigators associate with a living person rather than a place.

Scientific explanations

Sceptical researchers attribute haunting reports to natural causes and psychology: misperception in low light, sleep paralysis and hypnagogic hallucinations, suggestion and expectation, carbon monoxide poisoning, infrasound, and electromagnetic fields have all been proposed as explanations for some reported experiences. No haunting has been verified under controlled scientific conditions, and the existence of ghosts remains unproven.

In popular culture

Haunted houses are a staple of horror fiction and film, from The Haunting of Hill House by Shirley Jackson to countless movies, and "haunted house" attractions are a feature of Halloween in the United States.
