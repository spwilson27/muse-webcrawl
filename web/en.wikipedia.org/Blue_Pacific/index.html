Blue Pacific

The Blue Pacific is a geopolitical and cultural framing of the Pacific Islands region, promoted in particular by the Pacific Islands Forum, that presents the island nations of Oceania as a single oceanic continent of connected peoples rather than a scattering of small, isolated states. The phrase anchors the Forum's 2050 Strategy for the Blue Pacific Continent, adopted by Pacific leaders in 2022.

The concept

The Blue Pacific narrative emphasises shared stewardship of the world's largest ocean, collective identity, and the region's combined exclusive economic zones, which together cover millions of square kilometres of sea. It deliberately reframes the islands from “small island developing states” to “large ocean states”, strengthening the region's negotiating position on climate change, fisheries and seabed resources.

Political use

The framing has become central to Pacific diplomacy amid strategic competition between outside powers, including the United States, China and Australia. Pacific governments use the Blue Pacific concept to insist that external partners engage the region on terms set by Pacific peoples, through existing regional architecture centred on the Pacific Islands Forum.

Climate diplomacy

The Blue Pacific underpins regional declarations on climate action, including calls to keep global warming below 1.5 °C, to preserve maritime zones notwithstanding sea-level rise, and to recognise the ocean as central to Pacific cultures and livelihoods.
