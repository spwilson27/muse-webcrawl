Lake Asal and its surrounding salt flats

Lake Asal (French: Lac Assal; Arabic: بحيرة عسل) is a crater lake in the Danakil Depression of central Djibouti, in the Horn of Africa. Its surface lies 155 metres (509 ft) below sea level — the lowest point in Africa and the third-lowest land depression on Earth, after the Dead Sea and the Sea of Galilee.

Geography and salinity

The lake occupies a rift basin within the Afar Depression, where the African, Arabian and Somali plates are pulling apart. It has no outlet; its water balance is maintained by evaporation under some of the hottest average temperatures on Earth and by inflow from hot saline springs and, scientists believe, seepage of seawater from the nearby Gulf of Tadjoura. With a salinity of about 348 grams per litre — roughly ten times that of seawater and comparable to the Dead Sea — it is one of the saltiest bodies of water in the world.

Salt trade and significance

For centuries the Afar people have harvested the vast salt deposits on the lake's western shore, loading blocks of salt onto camel caravans for the Ethiopian highlands. Industrial and artisanal salt extraction continues today, and the salt flats are a significant source of income in Djibouti. The lake and its surrounding rift landscape are also a major tourist attraction and a site of geological research into continental rifting.
