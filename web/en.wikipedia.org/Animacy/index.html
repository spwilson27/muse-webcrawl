Animacy

The animacy hierarchy: human > animal > inanimate

Animacy is a grammatical and semantic feature of nouns reflecting how sentient or alive the referent of a noun is considered to be. In many languages, animacy influences morphology, case marking, agreement, and word order. The concept is commonly described through an animacy hierarchy, which ranks referents roughly as: first/second person pronouns > third person pronouns > human proper names > human common nouns > animals > inanimate objects.

Effects across languages

In Slavic languages such as Russian and Polish, animate masculine nouns take accusative forms identical to the genitive, while inanimate ones match the nominative. In Spanish, animate direct objects are marked with the "personal a". Many Algonquian languages, such as Ojibwe, divide all nouns into animate and inanimate genders — with some unexpected members, such as certain plants or objects treated as animate. Basque and Georgian also show animacy-sensitive case alignment (split ergativity).

Cognitive and typological aspects

Animacy is a key factor in differential object marking and in the typology of alignment systems. Cognitively, humans appear to detect animacy rapidly, and infants distinguish animate from inanimate motion early in development. In natural language processing and robotics, animacy classification is used to improve parsing and pronoun resolution.
