Braemar Gathering

The Braemar Gathering is a traditional Scottish Highland games event held annually on the first Saturday in September in Braemar, Aberdeenshire, Scotland. It is one of the most famous Highland games in the world, noted for its long-standing association with the British royal family, whose nearby residence is Balmoral Castle. Events include heavy events such as the caber toss and hammer throw, piping and dancing competitions, hill races, and track and field events.

History

The Braemar Royal Highland Society was founded in 1815, originally as a friendly society. Its members began holding an annual procession and games in the 1830s; the first games in something like the modern form were held in 1832. The games grew in prominence after Queen Victoria attended in 1848, having first visited the area that would become the royal family's Highland home. Victoria granted the society the title "Royal" in 1866, and members of the royal family have attended regularly ever since, traditionally watching from the royal pavilion. The reigning monarch is the society's patron, and serving soldiers of Scottish regiments have long taken part, notably in the tug o' war and the massed pipe bands.

Events

The programme combines traditional heavy events — tossing the caber, throwing the hammer, putting the stone and throwing the weight over the bar — with athletics, a hill race up Morrone, solo piping, Highland dancing and junior events. Massed pipe bands, often including the pipes and drums of Scottish regiments and local bands such as Ballater and District, perform throughout the day.

Royal association

The Gathering's royal links distinguish it from other Highland games. The royal family typically attends while in residence at Balmoral, a few miles away, and photographs of the monarch laughing at the tug o' war have become a fixture of late-summer royal coverage. In 2022, the games were the last public engagement attended by Queen Elizabeth II in Scotland only days before her death at Balmoral; a wreath-laying and tributes were incorporated into subsequent gatherings. The 2020 and 2021 gatherings were cancelled owing to the COVID-19 pandemic — the first cancellations since the Second World War era.
