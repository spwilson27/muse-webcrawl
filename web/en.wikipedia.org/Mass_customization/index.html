Mass customization

Mass customization is a business and manufacturing strategy that combines the low unit costs of mass production with the flexibility of individual customization, producing goods and services tailored to each customer at near mass-production efficiency. The term was popularized by Stan Davis in his 1987 book Future Perfect and developed by B. Joseph Pine II in Mass Customization (1993).

Approaches

Common techniques include modular product architectures (mixing standard components into many variants), postponement or delayed differentiation, flexible manufacturing and CNC equipment, and digital configurators that let customers design their own version online. Familiar examples are build-to-order computers, custom-printed books and apparel, configurable automobiles, and print-on-demand publishing.

Challenges

Mass customization demands accurate demand information, supply chains that can handle variety without excess inventory, and interfaces that guide customers without overwhelming them with choice. Advances in digital fabrication, including 3D printing, have expanded its range from marketing variations toward genuinely individualized products.
