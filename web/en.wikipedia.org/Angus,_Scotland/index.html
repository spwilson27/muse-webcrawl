Angus, Scotland

Angus (Scottish Gaelic: Aonghas) is one of the 32 local government council areas of Scotland, and a historic county and registration county on the North Sea coast of eastern Scotland. Bounded by Aberdeenshire, Perth and Kinross, and the City of Dundee, it has a population of around 116,000. The administrative centre is Forfar, and the largest towns include Arbroath, Montrose, Brechin, Carnoustie, and Kirriemuir.

Geography

Angus falls into three broad zones: the fertile lowland vale of Strathmore running through its centre; the Sidlaw Hills to the south; and, in the north and west, the Angus Glens — Glen Isla, Glen Prosen, Glen Clova, Glen Lethnot, and Glen Esk — cutting into the Grampian Mountains. The coast features the port towns of Arbroath and Montrose, the latter beside the tidal Montrose Basin nature reserve.

History

The area was a heartland of the Picts, whose carved symbol stones survive at places such as Aberlemno and Eassie. The county was formerly known as Forfarshire. Arbroath Abbey is famous as the place where the Declaration of Arbroath, asserting Scottish independence, was drawn up in 1320. Nearby Glamis Castle is the childhood home of Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother and the legendary setting associated with Shakespeare's Macbeth. The landscape author J. M. Barrie was born in Kirriemuir, "the little red town".

Economy and notable produce

Agriculture is prominent: soft fruit, potatoes, and the Aberdeen Angus cattle breed, developed in the region and now world-famous for beef. The Arbroath smokie — haddock smoked over hardwood — is a protected local delicacy. Golf tourism centres on Carnoustie Golf Links, a repeated venue of The Open Championship.
