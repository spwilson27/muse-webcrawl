Kainji Dam

Location: River Niger, Niger State, Nigeria

Construction: 1964–1968

Reservoir: Kainji Lake

Installed capacity: 760 MW (design 960 MW)

The Kainji Dam is a hydroelectric dam across the Niger River in Niger State, Nigeria. Construction began in 1964 and the dam was completed in 1968; its reservoir, Kainji Lake, stretches about 135 kilometres and is roughly 30 kilometres at its widest. The dam is about 10 kilometres long including its saddle sections across a side valley.

Power generation

The powerhouse was designed for twelve generating units with a total capacity of 960 MW, but only eight units were installed, giving an installed capacity of 760 MW. For many years Kainji was Nigeria's principal source of electricity, and part of its output is sold to neighbouring Niger. The smaller Jebba Dam lies downstream. Operation was long the responsibility of the state utility (successively NEPA and PHCN); after Nigeria's power-sector reforms the plant was concessioned to Mainstream Energy Solutions.

Impacts

Impoundment displaced around 55,000 people, who were resettled with support from the government. The lake created a significant fishery and enabled dry-season irrigation, but also flooded farmland and villages, and the surrounding area includes the Kainji Lake National Park, one of Nigeria's oldest national parks.
