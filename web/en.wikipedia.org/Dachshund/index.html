Dachshund

Dachshund
Origin: Germany
Nickname: Wiener dog, sausage dog, doxie
Coat varieties: Smooth, wire-haired, long-haired
Sizes: Standard and miniature

The dachshund is a short-legged, long-bodied, hound-type dog breed. The name is German for "badger dog" (Dachs "badger" and Hund "dog"), reflecting the breed's original purpose: to scent, chase, and flush out badgers and other burrow-dwelling animals. The breed's long, narrow build allows it to enter and fight inside underground tunnels.

Characteristics

Dachshunds come in two sizes — standard and miniature — and three coat types: smooth (short), long-haired, and wire-haired. Common colours include red, black and tan, chocolate, and dapple patterns. The breed is known for a lively, clever, and sometimes stubborn temperament, with a loud bark disproportionate to its size. Because of their elongated spine, dachshunds are prone to intervertebral disc disease, and care is often taken to limit jumping and stair climbing.

History

The breed was developed in Germany over several centuries, with roots in scent hounds crossed with terriers. By the 19th century, standardized breeding produced the modern dachshund, used for hunting badger, rabbit, and fox, and in packs even for larger game. The breed became internationally popular and has long been a symbol of Germany; a dachshund named Waldi was the official mascot of the 1972 Munich Olympic Games.
