Discourse on Colonialism

Discourse on Colonialism (French: Discours sur le colonialisme) is an essay by the Martinican poet, playwright and politician Aimé Césaire, first published in 1950. It is one of the founding texts of postcolonial literature and a foundational work of the Négritude movement.

Overview

In the essay, Césaire delivers a fierce indictment of European colonialism, arguing that colonialism dehumanizes both the colonized and the colonizer. He contends that the violence Europe practiced in its colonies eventually returned to Europe itself, a phenomenon he called the "boomerang effect," linking colonial brutality to the rise of fascism and the Holocaust.

Arguments

Césaire rejects the idea that colonization was a civilizing mission, arguing instead that it was driven by greed and that it produced "thingification" — the reduction of human beings to objects. He criticizes European humanism as hypocritical, since it proclaimed universal values while denying them to colonized peoples. He also attacks prominent European intellectuals and writers of his day for racist assumptions embedded in their work.

Legacy

The essay influenced later anticolonial thinkers, including Frantz Fanon, who had been Césaire's student in Martinique. It remains widely read in postcolonial studies, comparative literature and political theory courses.
