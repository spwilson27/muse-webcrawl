Salt Lake Tabernacle

LocationTemple Square, Salt Lake City, Utah
Built1864–1867
ArchitectHenry Grow (roof); Truman O. Angell (design)
Seatingc. 3,500 (after 2007 renovation)
OwnerThe Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

The Salt Lake Tabernacle, also known as the Mormon Tabernacle, is a large meeting hall on Temple Square in Salt Lake City, Utah, built between 1864 and 1867 to house meetings of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Its remarkable acoustics and its great pipe organ made it famous worldwide, and for over a century it was the home of the Mormon Tabernacle Choir (now the Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square).

Construction and acoustics

The building has a distinctive elongated dome shape, 250 feet long and 150 feet wide, with a roof built as a lattice-truss arch system of wooden beams pinned together with wooden pegs and rawhide — a bridge-building technique adapted by Henry Grow at the direction of Brigham Young. The roof rests on 44 sandstone piers with no interior columns, leaving the hall unobstructed. The acoustics are so sensitive that a pin dropped on the pulpit platform can be heard at the back of the hall, a demonstration still given to visitors.

The organ and choir

The Tabernacle organ, originally built by Joseph H. Ridges in 1867, has been rebuilt and enlarged several times; it now has 11,623 pipes and is one of the largest pipe organs in the world, with its golden façade pipes made from Utah pine. The building hosted the church's general conferences until 2000, when they moved to the larger Conference Center. Between 2005 and 2007 the Tabernacle underwent a major seismic upgrade and renovation. See also Temple Square and Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square.
