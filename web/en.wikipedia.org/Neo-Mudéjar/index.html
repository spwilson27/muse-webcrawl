Neo-Mudéjar

Neo-Mudéjar is an architectural movement in Spain, emerging in the late 19th century, that revived the Mudéjar style — the brick-built, Islamic-influenced architecture developed in medieval Iberia under Christian rule. It is considered a Spanish strand of Moorish Revival architecture.

Characteristics

Buildings in the style feature horseshoe and pointed arches, ornate brickwork laid in relief patterns, glazed ceramic tiles, and muqarnas-like cornices. Championed by architects such as Emilio Rodríguez Ayuso, who treated it as a national Spanish style, Neo-Mudéjar was applied to theatres, markets, factories, railway stations, and especially bullrings.

Examples

Notable examples in Madrid include the former bullring of Goya (on whose site the Palacio de Deportes stood), the Las Ventas bullring (1929–1931) — the movement's grandest surviving monument — and the Escuelas Aguirre. The style spread throughout Spain, as in the Gran Teatro Falla in Cádiz and the post office in Zaragoza.
