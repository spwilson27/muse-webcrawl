The British Nationality Act 1981 is the principal statute governing British nationality law. It replaced the single status of Citizen of the United Kingdom and Colonies with three categories: British citizenship, British Dependent Territories citizenship (later British Overseas Territories citizenship) and British Overseas citizenship.

Key changes

The Act ended the automatic acquisition of citizenship by birth in the UK alone, requiring a child born in Britain to have a parent who is a British citizen or settled in the UK. It also equalised the ability of mothers and fathers to pass citizenship to children born abroad, and abolished recognition of Commonwealth citizens as British subjects in most contexts.
