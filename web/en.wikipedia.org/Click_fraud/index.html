Click fraud

Click fraud is a type of internet fraud that occurs in pay-per-click (PPC) online advertising when a person, automated script, or computer program imitates a legitimate user clicking on an advertisement, for the purpose of generating a charge per click without having actual interest in the advertisement's target. Click fraud is the subject of some controversy and increasing litigation because advertising networks are the principal beneficiaries of the fraud.

Motives and methods

Fraudulent clicks arise from several motives. Website publishers who host advertisements may click them, or hire others to do so, to increase their own revenue. Competitors may click a rival's advertisements to exhaust its advertising budget, since each click is charged to the advertiser. At scale, fraud is automated through botnets — networks of compromised computers that generate clicks while mimicking human browsing patterns — and through click farms, groups of low-paid workers clicking manually.

Detection and prevention

Advertising networks such as Google Ads filter clicks they classify as "invalid activity", analysing IP addresses, timing patterns, user agents, and conversion behaviour, and credit advertisers for detected fraudulent clicks. Advertisers also monitor their own analytics for anomalous spikes. Despite these measures, estimates of the proportion of fraudulent clicks vary widely and the problem persists, particularly in mobile and programmatic advertising.

Legal issues

Click fraud has been prosecuted under computer fraud and wire fraud statutes in the United States, and large botnet operations have been dismantled by law enforcement. Advertisers have also brought class-action lawsuits against advertising networks over the adequacy of their fraud detection.
