The Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry (The Very Rich Hours of the Duke of Berry) is the most famous and possibly the best surviving example of French Gothic manuscript illumination, showing the late International Gothic phase of the style. It is a book of hours — a prayer book for lay devotion — commissioned by John I, Duke of Berry (1340–1416), the greatest bibliophile of his age.

Production

Work began around 1412 under the Limbourg brothers (Paul, Jean, and Herman), Dutch miniaturists of extraordinary talent, but was interrupted when all three brothers and the Duke died — the brothers of the plague — in 1416. The unfinished manuscript passed through several owners and was completed decades later, around the 1470s–1480s, possibly with contributions attributed to the workshop of Jean Colombe.

Content and legacy

The manuscript contains 206 leaves with 66 large and 65 small miniatures. It is celebrated above all for its calendar cycle: twelve full-page miniatures, one for each month, showing the Duke's palaces, castles, and estates alongside the seasonal labours of peasants — January's feast in the Duke's household, the ploughing of March, May's riding party at Vincennes. These scenes pioneered naturalistic landscape painting and aristocratic leisure imagery. The manuscript is held at the Musée Condé in Chantilly.
