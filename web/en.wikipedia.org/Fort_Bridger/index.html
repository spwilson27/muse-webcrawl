Fort Bridger

Fort Bridger was originally a 19th-century fur trading outpost established in 1843 on Blacks Fork of the Green River in what is now Uinta County, Wyoming, United States. It became a vital resupply point for wagon trains on the Oregon Trail, the California Trail, and the Mormon Trail, and later served as a United States Army post. The site is preserved today as Fort Bridger State Historic Site.

Trading post

Mountain man Jim Bridger and his partner Louis Vasquez built the post in 1843, consisting of a small log stockade with a blacksmith shop, to trade with Native Americans and supply the growing emigrant traffic heading west. Its location near the point where the trails divided toward Oregon, California, and the Salt Lake Valley made it one of the most important waypoints on the overland routes. In 1847 Brigham Young and the first Mormon pioneer company stopped at the fort. Bridger and Vasquez sold the post to the Mormons in 1855, but during the Utah War of 1857–58 the Mormon occupants burned it as United States troops advanced; the army then rebuilt and occupied the site.

Army post and later years

The army formally purchased the site in 1858 and garrisoned it through the Civil War era and beyond. The Pony Express and the transcontinental telegraph used the station, and the post protected emigrant routes and, later, construction of the Union Pacific Railroad. The army abandoned Fort Bridger in 1878, briefly reoccupied it in 1880, and finally closed it in 1890. The town of Fort Bridger, Wyoming, grew up nearby.

Historic site

Wyoming acquired the site in 1928, and it is now operated as a state historic site with restored and reconstructed buildings, a museum, and archaeological remains. Since 1972 the site has hosted the Fort Bridger Rendezvous each Labor Day weekend, a living-history gathering re-creating the Rocky Mountain fur trade rendezvous era.
