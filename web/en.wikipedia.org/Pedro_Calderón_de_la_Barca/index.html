Pedro Calderón de la Barca

Pedro Calderón de la Barca (17 January 1600 – 25 May 1681) was a Spanish dramatist, poet, and writer of the Spanish Golden Age. He is regarded, with Lope de Vega, as one of the two supreme playwrights of Spain's Siglo de Oro, and his work marks the culmination of the Baroque theatre.

Life

Born in Madrid, Calderón was educated by the Jesuits and at the University of Salamanca. He served as a soldier in Italy and Flanders, then became the leading court dramatist of Philip IV, writing elaborate spectacles for the Buen Retiro palace theatre. In 1651 he was ordained a priest, and later became chaplain of honour to the king, continuing to write sacramental plays (autos sacramentales) for the Corpus Christi festivals of Madrid.

Works

Calderón wrote some 120 comedias and about 80 autos sacramentales. His masterpiece is Life Is a Dream (La vida es sueño, 1635), a philosophical drama about the Polish prince Segismundo, imprisoned from birth, which meditates on free will, fate, and the illusory nature of worldly life. Other celebrated plays include The Mayor of Zalamea, The Physician of His Own Honour, The Constant Prince, and the auto The Great Theatre of the World. His honour dramas and intricate, metaphysical verse style defined the second phase of the Spanish Baroque stage.
