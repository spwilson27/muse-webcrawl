The Archbishop of Armagh is an archiepiscopal title that takes its name from the city of Armagh in Northern Ireland. Since the Reformation there have been parallel successions: one in the Roman Catholic Church and one in the Church of Ireland. Both archbishops style themselves Primate of All Ireland, reflecting Armagh's traditional status as the ecclesiastical capital of Ireland, associated with Saint Patrick.

History

According to tradition, Saint Patrick founded his main church at Armagh in 445. By the early Middle Ages the abbots and later bishops of Armagh claimed primacy over the Irish church, a position recognized at the Synod of Kells in 1152, when Armagh became an archdiocese.

The two successions

After the Irish Reformation, the Church of Ireland retained the medieval cathedrals and sees, while the Catholic Church maintained its own succession, often in exile or under penal restrictions. Today the Catholic archbishop resides in Armagh and heads the Archdiocese of Armagh; the Church of Ireland archbishop heads the Diocese of Armagh and is Primate of All Ireland within that church.
