Dispute resolution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dispute resolution or dispute settlement is the process of resolving disputes between parties. The term refers to a range of processes, from informal negotiation between the parties themselves to formal adjudication in courts of law. Legal systems use dispute resolution to settle conflicts over contracts, property, family matters, commerce and international relations.

Methods

The principal methods are commonly arranged along a spectrum of increasing formality and third-party control:

  • Negotiation — the parties communicate directly and attempt to reach a voluntary agreement without outside assistance.
  • Mediation — a neutral third party facilitates discussion and helps the parties craft their own settlement, without imposing an outcome.
  • Arbitration — a neutral arbitrator (or panel) hears the case and renders a decision, usually binding, outside the court system.
  • Litigation — adjudication by a judge (and possibly jury) in a public court, producing an enforceable judgment.

Mediation, arbitration and related processes are collectively known as alternative dispute resolution (ADR), which is often cheaper, faster and more private than litigation. Online dispute resolution applies similar techniques using digital platforms, notably for e-commerce complaints. In international law, dispute settlement mechanisms such as those of the World Trade Organization govern conflicts between states.
