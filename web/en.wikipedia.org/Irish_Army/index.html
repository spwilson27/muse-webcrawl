Irish Army

The Irish Army (Irish: an tArm) is the land component of the Defence Forces of Ireland, alongside the Naval Service and the Air Corps. As of recent establishment figures it comprises roughly 7,300 active personnel and a reserve force, the Reserve Defence Forces. The army's primary roles are the defence of the state against armed aggression, aiding the civil power, and participation in international peacekeeping.

History

The army traces its origins to the Irish Volunteers and the National Army formed during the Irish revolutionary period; it fought on the pro-Treaty side in the Irish Civil War (1922–23). During the Second World War — "the Emergency" — Ireland remained neutral but expanded its forces considerably. Irish troops have served with the United Nations since 1958, most famously in the Congo, Lebanon (UNIFIL), and numerous other missions.

Organisation

The army is organised into two infantry brigades, based at Dublin/Curragh and Cork, plus corps troops including artillery, cavalry (armoured reconnaissance), engineers, communications, and the special operations Army Ranger Wing. Headquarters is at McKee Barracks, Dublin, and the main training centre is the Curragh Camp, County Kildare.
