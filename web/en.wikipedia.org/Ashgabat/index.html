Ashgabat is the capital and largest city of Turkmenistan. It lies between the Karakum Desert and the Kopet Dag mountain range, near the border with Iran. The city is known for its abundance of white marble-clad buildings, which earned it a Guinness World Record for the highest density of white marble buildings in the world.

History

Ashgabat was founded in 1881 as a Russian military outpost near the ancient Parthian city of Nisa and the village of Askhabad. It served as capital of the Turkmen Soviet Socialist Republic. A catastrophic earthquake in October 1948 destroyed much of the city and killed a large share of its population. After Turkmenistan's independence in 1991, President Saparmurat Niyazov rebuilt the city with monumental architecture, including the Monument of Neutrality and the Turkmenbashi Ruhy Mosque.

Landmarks

Notable landmarks include the Alem Cultural and Entertainment Center, whose enclosed Ferris wheel is among the world's largest, the Independence Monument, and the National Museum. The city hosted the 2017 Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games in a vast Olympic complex.
