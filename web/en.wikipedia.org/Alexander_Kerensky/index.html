Alexander Fyodorovich Kerensky (4 May 1881 – 11 June 1970) was a Russian lawyer and revolutionary who led the Russian Provisional Government from July to November 1917, between the fall of the Tsar and the Bolshevik seizure of power in the October Revolution.

Rise in 1917

A prominent defence lawyer and Socialist Revolutionary deputy in the Fourth Duma, Kerensky became famous for investigating abuses such as the Lena goldfields massacre of 1912. After the February Revolution of 1917 he uniquely held office in both the Provisional Government — as minister of justice, then minister of war — and the Petrograd Soviet, making him the indispensable link between the two. As war minister he toured the front urging continued resistance, and in July he launched the Kerensky Offensive, Russia's last major attack of the First World War, which collapsed within days. Becoming minister-chairman, he attempted to steer between the revolutionary left, the military, and the right, surviving the Kornilov affair in August only by arming the workers of Petrograd.

Fall and exile

His government, weakened by war-weariness, land hunger, and economic collapse, was overthrown by the Bolsheviks in the October Revolution (7 November 1917). Kerensky fled Petrograd, failed to rally troops for a counter-attack, and escaped Russia in 1918. He spent the rest of his long life in exile — in Paris, then from 1940 in the United States, where he lectured and wrote extensively about 1917 — dying in New York in 1970, one of the last surviving leaders of revolutionary Russia.
