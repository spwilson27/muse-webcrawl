Dashi

Dashi (Japanese: 出汁, だし) is a family of stocks used in Japanese cuisine. It forms the base for miso soup, clear broth, noodle broth, and many simmering liquids. The most common form is made by heating water containing kombu (edible kelp) and kezurikatsuo (shavings of katsuobushi — preserved, fermented skipjack tuna) and straining the resulting liquid.

Types and ingredients

Kombu dashi is made from kelp alone and is suited to delicate dishes and vegetarian cooking; niboshi dashi uses dried sardines or anchovies; shiitake dashi uses dried shiitake mushrooms. The first stock extracted from the ingredients is ichiban dashi ("first stock"), prized for clear soups; a second extraction gives niban dashi, used for everyday cooking. Instant dashi granules, introduced commercially in the early 20th century, are now widely used.

Umami

Dashi is rich in umami: kombu supplies glutamates while katsuobushi supplies inosinates, and the two together multiply the savoury effect — a synergy identified by Kikunae Ikeda, who isolated glutamate from kombu dashi in 1908 and named the taste umami.
