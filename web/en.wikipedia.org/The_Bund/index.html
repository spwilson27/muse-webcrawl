The Bund (Chinese: 外滩, Wàitān) is a waterfront area and protected historical district in central Shanghai, running along the western bank of the Huangpu River opposite the skyscrapers of Lujiazui in Pudong. It is famous for its row of 52 historic buildings in Western architectural styles — often called a "museum of international architecture".

History

After Shanghai was opened as a treaty port by the Treaty of Nanking (1842), the riverfront embankment became the centre of foreign trade and finance in China. In the first decades of the twentieth century the muddy towpath was rebuilt with granite-faced banks and grand neoclassical, Beaux-Arts and Art Deco headquarters for banks, trading houses, hotels and consulates, including the domed HSBC Building (1923) and the Custom House (1927) with its clock tower, "Big Ching".

After 1949

Following the founding of the People's Republic, most foreign firms departed and many buildings became government offices. With reform and opening up from the 1990s, the Bund was restored as a financial and luxury district; the elevated waterfront promenade was rebuilt for Expo 2010, and the skyline dialogue with Pudong's towers — Oriental Pearl Tower, Jin Mao Tower and Shanghai Tower — made it one of China's most recognisable urban views.

Architecture

Styles range from Romanesque Revival and Gothic Revival to Neoclassical, Beaux-Arts and Art Deco, as in the Sassoon House (now the Peace Hotel, 1929). The buildings form a designated conservation area.
