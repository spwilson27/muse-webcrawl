Sprain

A sprain is a soft tissue injury of the ligaments within a joint, often caused by a sudden movement resulting in the joint being forced into an unnatural position — for example, the ankle being twisted. In severe cases the ligament may tear. Sprains are distinguished from strains, which involve muscles or tendons.

Sprain
SymptomsJoint pain, swelling, bruising, limited mobility
CausesTwisting or trauma forcing a joint beyond its normal range
TreatmentRest, ice, compression, elevation; physiotherapy; rarely surgery
SpecialtyOrthopedics, sports medicine

Classification

Sprains are graded by severity: grade I involves stretching and microscopic tearing of ligament fibres; grade II a partial tear with some joint laxity; and grade III a complete rupture, often with marked instability. The ankle is the most commonly sprained joint, followed by the knee and wrist.

Treatment

Initial management follows the RICE protocol — rest, ice, compression and elevation — together with analgesia. Early mobilisation and physiotherapy, including balance and proprioception training, speed recovery and reduce the risk of recurrent sprains and chronic instability. Severe ruptures, particularly of knee ligaments, may require surgical reconstruction.
