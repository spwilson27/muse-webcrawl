42nd Canadian Parliament

The 42nd Canadian Parliament was in session from 3 December 2015 to 11 September 2019, when it was dissolved ahead of the 2019 federal election. Its membership was determined by the 2015 federal election, held on 19 October 2015, in which the Liberal Party under Justin Trudeau won a majority government, ending nearly a decade of Conservative government under Stephen Harper.

Composition

In the House of Commons, the Liberals won 184 of 338 seats, the Conservatives 99, the New Democratic Party 44, the Bloc Québécois 10, and the Green Party 1. Justin Trudeau became the 23rd Prime Minister of Canada, and his first cabinet was noted for being gender-balanced, with an equal number of men and women. The Speaker of the House was Geoff Regan.

Major legislation and events

The Parliament legalised recreational cannabis with the Cannabis Act of 2018, making Canada the second country in the world to do so, and passed the Medical Assistance in Dying law (Bill C-14) in 2016. It approved the Paris climate agreement, introduced a federal carbon pricing framework, oversaw the renegotiation of NAFTA into the Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA), and conducted a long-running (ultimately unsuccessful) inquiry into electoral reform, abandoning the government's promise to replace the first-past-the-post system. The Parliament's final months were dominated by the SNC-Lavalin affair, an ethics controversy involving the Prime Minister's Office.

Parliament was dissolved on 11 September 2019; the ensuing election returned the Liberals with a minority government, opening the 43rd Canadian Parliament.
