TARDIS

The TARDIS (Time And Relative Dimension In Space) is a fictional spacecraft and time machine in the British science fiction television series Doctor Who and its spin-offs. Piloted by the Doctor, a Time Lord, the TARDIS is characterised by its interior being much larger than its exterior — a blue British police box — and by the distinctive groaning, wheezing sound of its materialisation.

Characteristics

The TARDIS is a product of the advanced technology of the Time Lords of Gallifrey. Its "chameleon circuit" is supposed to change its outward form to blend into its surroundings, but the Doctor's TARDIS became stuck in the shape of a 1960s London police box after its first televised adventure in 1963. Inside, the control room contains the central console with its time rotor; beyond it lie an effectively unlimited number of rooms, corridors and facilities.

History in the programme

The police box shape was chosen for budgetary reasons at the programme's creation in 1963, when the design was developed under producer Verity Lambert and designer Peter Brachacki. The TARDIS has remained a constant of the series across its 1963–1989 run, the 1996 television film, and the revived series from 2005, with the interior console room redesigned periodically.

Cultural impact

The word "TARDIS" has entered the Oxford English Dictionary and is a registered trademark of the BBC. "Tardis-like" is widely used in British English to describe something larger on the inside than the outside. Real police boxes styled after the TARDIS remain a familiar sight in some British cities.
