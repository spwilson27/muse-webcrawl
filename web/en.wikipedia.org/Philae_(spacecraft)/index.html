Philae is a robotic European Space Agency lander that accompanied the Rosetta spacecraft until it separated to land on comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko, ten years and eight months after departing Earth. On 12 November 2014, Philae touched down on the comet, becoming the first spacecraft to land on a comet nucleus.

Landing

Philae's anchoring harpoons failed to fire and it bounced twice, coming to rest in the shadow of a cliff at a site later named Abydos. Its batteries ran down after about two days of surface science, and contact was lost until a brief reawakening in June–July 2015 as the comet approached the Sun. The lander was finally located in images in September 2016, shortly before the Rosetta mission ended.

Instruments and results

Philae carried ten instruments designed to analyse the comet's surface composition, structure and magnetic and plasma environment. Its measurements detected organic compounds on the comet's surface and characterized the hard, icy material beneath a layer of dust.
