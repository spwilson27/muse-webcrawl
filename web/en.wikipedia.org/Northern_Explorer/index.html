Northern Explorer

The Northern Explorer is a long-distance passenger train service operated by the Great Journeys New Zealand division of KiwiRail between Auckland and Wellington on New Zealand's North Island Main Trunk railway. The service replaced the Overlander in June 2012 and is marketed primarily as a scenic tourist experience, featuring panoramic windows, an open-air viewing carriage, and GPS-triggered commentary in several languages.

Route

The train travels the length of the North Island Main Trunk between Auckland and Wellington, stopping at Papakura, Hamilton, Otorohanga, National Park, Ohakune, Palmerston North, and Paraparaumu. Highlights of the journey include the volcanic landscapes of Tongariro National Park, with views of Mounts Ruapehu, Ngauruhoe, and Tongariro; the engineering feat of the Raurimu Spiral, by which the line climbs the central plateau; and the viaducts of the Manawatū Gorge region and the Kapiti Coast.

Service

The Northern Explorer operates several times a week in each direction (southbound and northbound on alternating days), with seasonal variations. Trains consist of AK-class panoramic carriages built at the Hillside Engineering workshops in Dunedin, with large windows, skylight panels, and a café car. The service was suspended during the COVID-19 pandemic and resumed with a revised schedule in 2022.

History

Long-distance passenger service on the route dates to the completion of the Main Trunk in 1908. Famous predecessors include the Night Limited, the Silver Star sleeper train, and the Overlander daylight service (1991–2012). The Northern Explorer was created when KiwiRail reoriented its long-distance trains toward the tourist market under what became the Great Journeys New Zealand brand, alongside the TranzAlpine and Coastal Pacific in the South Island.
