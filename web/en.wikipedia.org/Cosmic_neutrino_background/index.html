Cosmic neutrino background

The cosmic neutrino background (CνB or CNB) is the universe's background particle radiation composed of neutrinos. Predicted by the Big Bang model, it decoupled about one second after the Big Bang, when the universe was cool enough for neutrinos to stop interacting.

Properties

Its present temperature is predicted to be about 1.95 K, slightly below the 2.725 K cosmic microwave background, with a density of roughly 336 neutrinos per cubic centimetre across all flavours.

Detection

Direct detection is extraordinarily difficult because the neutrinos' energies are so low; indirect evidence comes from Big Bang nucleosynthesis and microwave background measurements of the effective number of relativistic species. Experiments such as PTOLEMY aim to capture relic neutrinos on tritium.
