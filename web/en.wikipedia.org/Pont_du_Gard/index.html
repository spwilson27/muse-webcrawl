Pont du Gard

The Pont du Gard is an ancient Roman aqueduct bridge built in the first century AD to carry water over 50 km (31 mi) to the Roman colony of Nemausus (Nîmes). It crosses the river Gardon near the town of Vers-Pont-du-Gard in southern France.

Description

The bridge has three tiers of arches and stands 48.8 m (160 ft) high. The whole aqueduct descends in height by only 25 m (82 ft) over its entire length, while the bridge itself descends by a mere 2.5 cm (0.98 in) – a gradient of only 1 in 18,241 – testifying to the great precision that Roman engineers were able to achieve using simple technology.

History

The aqueduct was constructed around 40–60 AD, during the reigns of Claudius and Nero. In the Middle Ages, the bridge was used as a toll bridge; in the 18th century it was widened to carry a road. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.
