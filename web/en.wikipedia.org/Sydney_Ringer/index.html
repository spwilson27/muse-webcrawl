Sydney Ringer

Sydney Ringer
Born: 1835, Norwich, England
Died: 14 October 1910
Field: Physiology, pharmacology
Known for: Ringer's solution

Sydney Ringer (1835–1910) was a British clinician, physiologist, and pharmacologist at University College London, best known for developing Ringer's solution, a balanced salt solution still widely used in medicine and laboratory research.

Work

In experiments in the early 1880s on the isolated frog heart, Ringer discovered that a heart perfused with a solution containing the right proportions of sodium, potassium, and calcium salts would keep beating far longer than one perfused with distilled water or simple saline — famously after noticing that tap water used by a technician worked better than distilled water. His systematic studies established the essential roles of inorganic ions in cardiac contraction and laid foundations for the modern understanding of electrolytes.

Legacy

Ringer's solution, and its later modification Ringer's lactate (Hartmann's solution), became standard intravenous fluids and physiological media. Ringer was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and is regarded as a pioneer of experimental pharmacology in Britain.
