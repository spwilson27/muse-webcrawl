Greyhound (cocktail)

Greyhound
TypeCocktail
Base spiritVodka or gin
IngredientsVodka (or gin), grapefruit juice
ServedOn the rocks, in a highball or rocks glass
VariantSalty dog (salted rim)

The greyhound is a cocktail consisting of grapefruit juice mixed with vodka or gin and served over ice. If the rim of the glass is salted, the drink is instead called a salty dog. The drink's simplicity — two ingredients in roughly a 2:1 ratio of juice to spirit — made it a staple of mid-20th-century American bars.

History

A recipe for a grapefruit-and-gin drink appeared in Harry Craddock's The Savoy Cocktail Book (1930). The name "greyhound" is often traced to the 1940s and 1950s, when the drink was popularised in the United States and reputedly served at the Greyhound bus company's restaurant chain, Post House. Vodka increasingly replaced gin as the base spirit in the post-war decades.

Variations

The salty dog adds a salted rim. Contemporary variations may use fresh grapefruit juice, a grapefruit twist or rosemary garnish, or substitute mezcal or tequila for a smoky or agave character. The Italian greyhound adds Campari or Aperol.

See also

Related citrus highballs include the screwdriver (vodka and orange juice) and the sea breeze (vodka, grapefruit and cranberry juice).
