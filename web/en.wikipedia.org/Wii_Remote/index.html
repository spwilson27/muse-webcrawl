Wii Remote

The Wii Remote, often called the Wiimote, is the primary controller for Nintendo's Wii console, released in 2006. Its motion sensing and infrared pointer capabilities made physical gestures a central part of gameplay and helped broaden the audience for video games.

Technology

The controller contains accelerometers for detecting movement and tilt, an infrared camera that tracks lights in the Sensor Bar for pointing, buttons, a speaker and rumble motor. It connects by Bluetooth and accepts attachments such as the Nunchuk and Classic Controller. The later Wii MotionPlus accessory added gyroscopic sensing.

Impact

The Wii Remote's simple one-handed design influenced motion control across the industry and became a cultural symbol of the Wii era. It was also reused with the Wii U.
