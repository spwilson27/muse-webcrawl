Assyrian Neo-Aramaic, often called Suret by its speakers, is a Northeastern Neo-Aramaic language spoken by Assyrian Christians originally from the highlands of northern Mesopotamia — the region spanning the Nineveh Plains of Iraq, Hakkari in Turkey, and Urmia in Iran — and now by a large global diaspora.

Dialects and standard

The language exists in numerous village dialects, of which the Urmia dialect became the basis of a literary standard in the 19th century, when missionaries printed books and a Bible translation in it. A later "Iraqi koine" emerged around Baghdad. Dialects differ considerably in phonology and vocabulary, including loanwords from Kurdish, Turkish, Persian, Arabic, and English.

Status

Assyrian Neo-Aramaic is considered endangered in its historic homeland, where speaker communities were devastated by the Assyrian genocide of 1915 and subsequent persecutions and emigration. It remains in daily use in diaspora communities in the United States, Sweden, Australia, and elsewhere, and is one of the recognised minority languages in Iraq's Kurdistan Region, where it is used in schools and media.
