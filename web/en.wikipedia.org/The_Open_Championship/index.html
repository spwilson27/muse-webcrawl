The Open Championship

The Open Championship, often referred to as The Open or the British Open, is the oldest golf tournament in the world and one of the four men's major championships. Founded in 1860 at Prestwick, it is organized by The R&A and played annually on a links course in the United Kingdom.

Format and traditions

The championship is 72 holes of stroke play over four days, with a cut after 36 holes. The winner receives the Claret Jug (since 1873) and the title "Champion Golfer of the Year." Venues rotate among links courses including St Andrews (the Old Course), Royal St George's, Royal Birkdale, Muirfield, Carnoustie and Royal Portrush.

Records

Harry Vardon won a record six Opens; Old Tom Morris and others dominated the early years; Jack Nicklaus, Tiger Woods and Tom Watson are among the modern greats. See also The Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews.
