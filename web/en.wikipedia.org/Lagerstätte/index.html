Lagerstätte

Exceptionally preserved fossils characterise a Konservat-Lagerstätte

A Fossil-Lagerstätte (from German Lagerstätte, "place of storage", plural Lagerstätten) is a sedimentary deposit that exhibits extraordinary fossils with exceptional preservation — sometimes including preserved soft tissues. Such deposits are rare and are among the most important windows into ancient life, recording organisms and body parts that are otherwise almost never fossilised.

Types

Adolf Seilacher distinguished two kinds. Konservat-Lagerstätten (conservation deposits) preserve soft parts through conditions that halt decay — rapid burial, anoxic bottom waters, early mineralisation (as in phosphatisation or pyritisation) or entombment in amber. Famous examples include the Cambrian Burgess Shale of Canada and Chengjiang biota of China, the Jurassic Solnhofen Limestone of Germany (source of Archaeopteryx), the Eocene Messel oil shale, and the Green River Formation. Konzentrat-Lagerstätten (concentration deposits) instead contain extraordinary abundances of hard parts, such as bone beds, shell beds and mammal-trapping asphalt seeps like La Brea.

Formation

Exceptional preservation typically requires that carcasses escape scavengers, burrowers and oxidising conditions — through obrution (sudden burial by sediment flows), stagnation of bottom waters, hypersalinity, or microbial mats sealing the sediment surface. Because such accidents of burial are unevenly distributed in time and space, Lagerstätten introduce a strong bias into the known fossil record: much of what is known of soft-bodied phyla comes from a handful of such deposits.
