Wade–Giles

Wade–Giles is a romanization system for Mandarin Chinese developed from a system created by Thomas Francis Wade in the mid-19th century and completed by Herbert A. Giles in his Chinese–English Dictionary of 1892. It was the predominant system in the English-speaking world for most of the 20th century, before being largely supplanted by Hanyu Pinyin.

Features

The system distinguishes aspirated from unaspirated consonants with an apostrophe — for example p versus p', t versus t', k versus k', ch versus ch' — and uses umlauts and superscript numerals to mark tones in reference works. These diacritics were often dropped in popular usage, creating ambiguity.

Legacy

Familiar Wade–Giles spellings survive in English in names such as Peking (Beijing), Mao Tse-tung (Mao Zedong), Chiang Kai-shek, Tao Te Ching, kung fu, and tai chi. The system remains in use in Taiwan for some proper names and is still encountered throughout older sinological literature.
