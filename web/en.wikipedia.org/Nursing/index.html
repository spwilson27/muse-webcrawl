Nursing

Nursing is a health care profession that "integrates the art and science of caring and focuses on the protection, promotion, and optimization of health and human functioning; prevention of illness and injury; facilitation of healing; and alleviation of suffering through compassionate presence". Nurses practice in many specialties with varying levels of certification and responsibility.

History

Care of the sick was historically provided by families and religious orders. Modern professional nursing is associated with Florence Nightingale, whose work during the Crimean War (1853–56) and founding of a training school at St Thomas' Hospital in London in 1860 established nursing as a trained profession. Figures such as Clara Barton, Mary Seacole, and Dorothea Dix advanced nursing and hospital reform in the Americas and elsewhere.

Practice

Nurses assess patients, plan and deliver care, administer medicines, monitor conditions, educate patients and families, and coordinate with physicians and other professionals. Roles range from licensed practical nurses to registered nurses and advanced practice nurses such as nurse practitioners and nurse anaesthetists, who may diagnose and prescribe. Nursing education is offered through diploma, associate, bachelor's, and graduate programs, and licensure is regulated by national or regional boards.

Workforce

Nursing is the largest health care profession in many countries and remains predominantly female, though the proportion of men is growing. Many health systems face persistent nursing shortages.
