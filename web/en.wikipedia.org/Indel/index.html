Indel

An indel is a molecular biology term for an insertion or deletion of bases in the DNA of an organism. It is a class of mutation intermediate in size between single-nucleotide changes and large chromosomal rearrangements.

Effects

Indels in coding regions that are not multiples of three nucleotides cause frameshift mutations, altering every amino acid downstream and usually destroying protein function. Indels of three or a multiple of three bases add or remove whole amino acids.

Occurrence

Indels arise from replication slippage, especially in repetitive sequences, and from errors during DNA repair. They are a major source of genetic variation between individuals and species.

In analysis

In sequence alignment, indels appear as gaps; distinguishing a true insertion in one sequence from a deletion in another depends on comparison with an outgroup. Indels are widely used as markers in population genetics and forensics.
