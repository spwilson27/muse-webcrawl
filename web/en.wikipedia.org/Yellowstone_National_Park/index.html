Yellowstone National Park is a national park of the United States located in the northwest corner of Wyoming, extending into Montana and Idaho. It was established by the United States Congress and signed into law by President Ulysses S. Grant on 1 March 1872, making it the first national park in the United States and widely held to be the first in the world.

Geology and geothermal features

The park sits atop the Yellowstone Caldera, the largest supervolcano in North America, formed by a cataclysmic eruption about 640,000 years ago. Yellowstone contains about half of the world's known geothermal features, including more than 10,000 hot springs and geysers — among them the famous Old Faithful and the Grand Prismatic Spring — fuelled by the underlying magma chamber.

Wildlife and ecosystem

Yellowstone is the core of the Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem, one of the largest remaining nearly intact temperate-zone ecosystems on Earth. It is home to free-ranging herds of bison and elk, grizzly bears, and gray wolves, which were reintroduced in 1995 after an absence of about 70 years. In 1978 the park was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Native American peoples lived in and hunted in the region for at least 11,000 years. Reports from fur trappers such as John Colter (1807–08) were long dismissed as tall tales; organised exploration began with the Cook–Folsom–Peterson expedition of 1869 and the Washburn expedition of 1870, whose accounts and photographs helped persuade Congress to protect the area. Major events in the park's later history include the fires of 1988, which burned about 36 per cent of the park, and severe flooding in 2022.
