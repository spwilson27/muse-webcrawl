Co-Princes of Andorra

Co-Princes of Andorra
Office: Joint heads of state of Andorra
Holders: Bishop of Urgell; President of France
Established: Paréages of 1278 and 1288

The Co-Princes of Andorra are jointly the heads of state of the Principality of Andorra, a small landlocked country in the Pyrenees between France and Spain. The office is shared between the Bishop of Urgell in Catalonia, Spain, and the President of the French Republic — an arrangement that makes Andorra unique among the world's states.

Origins

The co-principality originated in the Paréages of 1278 and 1288, agreements that settled a dispute over sovereignty between the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix, whose rights passed through inheritance to the French crown and, ultimately, to the head of the French state.

Modern role

Under Andorra's 1993 constitution, the co-princes are largely ceremonial but retain certain constitutional functions: they sanction and promulgate laws, appoint members of some judicial bodies, accredit diplomats, and may call elections or referendums in defined circumstances. Each acts through a personal representative resident in Andorra.

Current holders

Because one co-prince is always the sitting French president and the other the sitting Bishop of Urgell, neither is chosen by Andorrans. The current episcopal co-prince is the Bishop of Urgell (Joan-Enric Vives i Sicília until his 2025 resignation, succeeded by Josep-Lluís Serrano Pentinat), and the French co-prince is the President of France, Emmanuel Macron.
