Alibaba Group Holding Limited is a Chinese multinational technology company specialising in e-commerce, retail, internet and technology. Founded in 1999 in Hangzhou by Jack Ma and a group of co-founders, it grew into one of the world's largest online commerce companies and the operator of China's dominant consumer marketplaces, Taobao and Tmall.

History

Alibaba began as Alibaba.com, a business-to-business marketplace connecting Chinese manufacturers with overseas buyers. In 2003 it launched Taobao, a free consumer marketplace that outcompeted eBay in China, followed by the payment service Alipay (later spun into Ant Group) and the premium retail platform Tmall (2008). Its Singles' Day shopping event each 11 November became the world's largest online sales day. In September 2014 Alibaba's initial public offering on the New York Stock Exchange raised about $25 billion, then the largest IPO in history.

Businesses

Beyond core commerce — including international platform AliExpress, Southeast Asian Lazada, and the Freshippo grocery chain — Alibaba is China's largest cloud computing provider through Alibaba Cloud, operates the Cainiao logistics network, and holds interests in media, entertainment and local services. In 2023, under new leadership, the group announced a major restructuring into six business units, though plans for separate listings of some units were later shelved.

Regulation

From late 2020 Alibaba faced intensified Chinese regulatory scrutiny: the suspension of Ant Group's IPO, an antitrust fine of 18.2 billion yuan in 2021 for monopolistic practices, and a broader technology-sector crackdown that reshaped the company's strategy and market value.
