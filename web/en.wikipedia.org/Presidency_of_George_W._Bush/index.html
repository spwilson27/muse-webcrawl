The presidency of George W. Bush began on January 20, 2001, when he was inaugurated as the 43rd president of the United States, and ended on January 20, 2009. Bush, a Republican from Texas, took office after defeating Democratic nominee Al Gore in the closely contested 2000 presidential election, which was decided by the Supreme Court case Bush v. Gore. He won re-election in 2004 over John Kerry.

September 11 and the War on Terror

The defining event of Bush's presidency was the September 11 attacks of 2001. In response, Bush launched the "War on Terror", invading Afghanistan to overthrow the Taliban and destroy al-Qaeda, and in 2003 invading Iraq on the stated grounds that Saddam Hussein's regime possessed weapons of mass destruction and had ties to terrorism. No stockpiles of such weapons were found, and the Iraq War became deeply controversial as it evolved into a prolonged occupation and insurgency.

Domestic policy

Domestically, Bush signed major tax cuts in 2001 and 2003, the No Child Left Behind Act education reform, the Medicare Prescription Drug, Improvement, and Modernization Act, and the USA PATRIOT Act expanding surveillance powers. His second term was marked by criticism of the federal response to Hurricane Katrina in 2005 and by the financial crisis of 2008, to which his administration responded with the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) bank bailout.

Legacy

Bush left office with low approval ratings amid the Iraq War and the Great Recession, though assessments of his presidency have moderated somewhat over time, and his President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR) is widely credited with saving millions of lives in Africa.
