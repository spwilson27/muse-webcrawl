The Fourth Plinth is the northwest plinth in Trafalgar Square in central London. It was originally intended to hold an equestrian statue of William IV, but remained empty from 1841 due to insufficient funds. For over 150 years the fate of the plinth was debated; since 1999 it has been used for a rolling programme of temporary contemporary artworks commissioned by the Mayor of London, making it one of the United Kingdom's most prominent platforms for public art.

History

Trafalgar Square's other three plinths received statues in the 19th century, but the fourth stayed bare. In the 1990s, proposals to fill it permanently were superseded by the idea of temporary commissions. Early works included Mark Wallinger's Ecce Homo (1999), Bill Woodrow's Regardless of History (2000), and Rachel Whiteread's Monument (2001).

The commissioning programme

Subsequent commissions have included Marc Quinn's Alison Lapper Pregnant (2005), Antony Gormley's One & Other (2009), in which 2,400 members of the public each occupied the plinth for an hour, Yinka Shonibare's Nelson's Ship in a Bottle (2010), Katharina Fritsch's Hahn/Cock (2013), Heather Phillipson's THE END (2020), and Samson Kambalu's Antelope (2022). The programme is administered by the Greater London Authority with advice from the Fourth Plinth Commissioning Group.
