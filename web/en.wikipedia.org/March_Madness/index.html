March Madness

An NCAA tournament game under the March Madness banner

March Madness is the popular name for the NCAA Division I men's and women's basketball tournaments, single-elimination tournaments played each spring in the United States to determine the national champions of college basketball. The men's tournament, first held in 1939, is one of the most-watched sporting events in the country.

Format

Each tournament fields 68 teams: conference champions receiving automatic bids and at-large selections chosen by a committee, whose seedings are announced on "Selection Sunday." Four play-in games (the First Four) reduce the field to 64, followed by rounds producing the Sweet Sixteen, Elite Eight, and Final Four. The structure gives small-conference teams ("Cinderellas") a path to famous upset victories over seeded powers.

History of the term

The phrase "March Madness" was first used for the Illinois state high school basketball tournament — sportscaster Brent Musburger popularized it in connection with the NCAA tournament during CBS broadcasts in the 1980s, and the NCAA eventually acquired full trademark rights after litigation and a licensing settlement with the Illinois High School Association.

Culture and economics

The tournament drives an enormous bracket-prediction culture: tens of millions of fans fill out brackets, and office pools and legal sports betting generate billions of dollars in wagers. Television rights are correspondingly valuable — the NCAA's deals with CBS and Turner (now Warner Bros. Discovery), extended in 2016, run through 2032 at roughly $1.1 billion per year. Famous moments include NC State's 1983 title run, Duke's Christian Laettner's 1992 buzzer-beater against Kentucky, and UMBC's 2018 upset of Virginia, the first 16-seed over 1-seed win in the men's tournament.
