Parks Canada

Parks Canada (French: Parcs Canada), legally the Parks Canada Agency, is the agency of the Government of Canada responsible for the country's national parks, national historic sites, and national marine conservation areas. Established in 1911 as the Dominion Parks Branch — the world's first national park service — it manages dozens of national parks and reserves, more than 170 national historic sites, and several national marine conservation areas, covering over 450,000 km² of land and water.

History

Canada's first national park grew around the Cave and Basin hot springs at Banff in 1885; Banff National Park was formally created in 1887, the world's third national park. In 1911 Commissioner James B. Harkin established the Dominion Parks Branch to administer the growing system professionally. The National Parks Act of 1930 dedicated the parks "to the people of Canada for their benefit, education and enjoyment" and required they be left "unimpaired for the enjoyment of future generations." The agency became a separate service agency of the federal government under the Parks Canada Agency Act of 1998.

System

The national parks system aims to represent each of Canada's 39 terrestrial natural regions, from Banff and Jasper in the Rockies to Torngat Mountains in Labrador and Ivvavik in the Yukon. National historic sites — administered directly or by others under Parks Canada's programme — commemorate persons, places and events of national significance, including L'Anse aux Meadows, Head-Smashed-In Buffalo Jump and the Rideau Canal. National marine conservation areas, including Saguenay–St. Lawrence and Lake Superior, protect marine ecosystems while allowing sustainable use.

Mandate and work

Parks Canada's guiding principles place ecological integrity first in park management. The agency conducts species-at-risk recovery, ecological monitoring, prescribed fire, visitor services and interpretation for tens of millions of visits per year, and works with Indigenous peoples as partners in establishing and managing many protected areas, including through Indigenous Guardian programmes. Its red chairs, discovery passes and extensive trail and campground networks are familiar features of Canadian outdoor life.
