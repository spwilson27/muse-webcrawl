Military police

Military police (MP) are law-enforcement and security personnel within a military organisation, responsible for policing service personnel, enforcing military law, guarding installations and prisoners, controlling traffic and routes, and conducting criminal investigations alongside specialist bodies. Their jurisdiction normally covers military personnel and property; in many countries gendarmerie-type forces also police civilians.

Roles

Core tasks include garrison law enforcement and discipline, battlefield circulation control (marking and patrolling supply routes), handling prisoners of war and detainees, force protection and base security, customs and border support, and close protection of senior officers. In counter-insurgency and stabilisation operations, military police frequently train and mentor local civilian police.

By country

The United States fields the Army Military Police Corps, the Navy Master-at-Arms, Air Force Security Forces and Marine Corps military police, with criminal investigation by CID, NCIS and OSI. The United Kingdom's Royal Military Police (the "Redcaps", from their distinctive cap covers) police the Army, complemented by the Royal Navy Police and RAF Police. France's Gendarmerie Nationale, Italy's Carabinieri and Spain's Guardia Civil are military-status forces with general civilian policing roles. Many countries mark military police with MP armbands, red or white accoutrements, or distinctive helmet markings so they are identifiable in combat zones, a status recognised under the Geneva Conventions when engaged in law-enforcement duties.

History

Provost marshals have maintained discipline in armies since the Middle Ages, but permanent corps are largely a 19th- and 20th-century development, expanding massively in the World Wars to manage millions of conscripts, traffic and prisoners.
