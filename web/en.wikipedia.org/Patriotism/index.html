Patriotism

Patriotism is the feeling of love, devotion, and sense of attachment to a homeland or the country, and alliance with other citizens who share the same sentiment to create a feeling of oneness among the people. It can be understood as a complex of feelings that entails a positive and supportive attitude toward one's country.

Patriotism is often distinguished from nationalism: while nationalism typically implies a belief in the superiority of one's nation or its elevation above others, patriotism is often defined as love of one's country without requiring hostility toward others, though the boundary between the two is contested. The word derives from the Greek patriṓtēs, "fellow countryman", via Latin patriota.

History

Ideas of devotion to one's homeland appear in ancient Greek and Roman thought, in which the patria commanded loyalty and sacrifice. Modern patriotism developed alongside the nation-state and mass politics in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, expressed through flags, anthems, monuments, and civic education.

Perspectives

Philosophers debate whether patriotism is a virtue, a morally neutral attachment, or a dangerous bias. "Constitutional patriotism", associated with Jürgen Habermas, proposes loyalty to democratic principles rather than ethnic identity. Critics such as Leo Tolstoy condemned patriotism as a root cause of war, while others defend moderate patriotism as a source of social solidarity and public spirit.
