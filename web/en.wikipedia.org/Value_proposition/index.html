Value proposition

A value proposition is, in marketing, the value a company promises to deliver to customers should they choose to buy its product or service. It is the primary reason a prospect should buy from one firm rather than another, and forms part of the overall marketing strategy.

Concept

The term was popularised in a 1988 McKinsey & Co. paper by Michael Lanning and Edward Michaels. A value proposition is typically a short statement that communicates the tangible benefits customers receive, how the offering solves their problems or improves their situation, and why it is distinct from competitors' offerings.

Development

Developing a value proposition involves identifying the target customer segment, the jobs or problems to be solved, the gains sought and pains avoided, and the features of the product or service that create those gains and relieve those pains. Tools such as the Value Proposition Canvas, developed by Alexander Osterwalder, map the fit between a customer profile and a value map.

Use

Value propositions are used in brand positioning, product development, sales communication and fundraising by start-ups, where a clear articulation of customer value is considered essential to gaining investment and early adopters.
