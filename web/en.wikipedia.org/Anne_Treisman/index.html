Anne Marie Treisman FRS (27 May 1935 – 9 February 2018) was an English psychologist who specialised in cognitive psychology and the study of attention and perception. She is best known for the feature integration theory of attention and for her attenuation model of selective attention, and is regarded as one of the most influential cognitive psychologists of her generation.

Career

Treisman studied at the University of Cambridge and completed her doctorate at the University of Oxford under Donald Broadbent. Her early work revised Broadbent's filter model of attention into an "attenuation" model, proposing that unattended messages are weakened rather than blocked entirely. From 1993 she was a professor at Princeton University in the United States, where she remained until her retirement.

Feature integration theory

In feature integration theory (developed with Garry Gelade and published in 1980), Treisman proposed that visual features such as colour, shape and orientation are registered automatically and in parallel, but that binding them into coherent objects requires focused attention. The theory explained visual-search results and "illusory conjunctions", in which features from different objects are wrongly combined when attention is diverted.

Honours

Treisman was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and a member of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences. In 2013 she received the U.S. National Medal of Science. She was married to the psychologist and Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman.
