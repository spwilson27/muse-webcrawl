Texas Instruments Incorporated (TI) is an American multinational semiconductor company headquartered in Dallas, Texas. One of the world's largest semiconductor manufacturers, it focuses on analog integrated circuits and embedded processors. TI is also widely known to the public for its graphing calculators, such as the TI-84 Plus series ubiquitous in schools.

History

TI descends from Geophysical Service Incorporated (GSI), founded in 1930 to provide seismic exploration services to the petroleum industry. The company was reorganized and renamed Texas Instruments in 1951 under leaders including Cecil Green and J. Erik Jonsson. In 1954 TI introduced the first commercial silicon transistor, and in 1958 its engineer Jack Kilby built the first working integrated circuit, for which he later shared the Nobel Prize in Physics.

During the 1970s TI became a major consumer electronics company, launching the first handheld calculator with the Cal-Tech prototype lineage and the popular TI-30 and Speak & Spell lines; it also built an early home computer, the TI-99/4A. After exiting most consumer markets and defense electronics (sold in 1997), TI concentrated on semiconductors, becoming the world's leading producer of analog chips and digital signal processors, long used in mobile phones.

Products

TI's catalog spans tens of thousands of analog products — amplifiers, power management chips, data converters, and interface devices — and embedded processors including the MSP430 and Sitara families. Its Digital Light Processing (DLP) technology, based on micromirror arrays, is used in projectors and cinema projection. The education technology division produces the TI-Nspire and TI-84 calculator lines.
