Longford (Irish: An Longfort, meaning "the fort") is the county town of County Longford in the midlands of Ireland. It stands on the River Camlin, a tributary of the Shannon, near the Royal Canal.

History

The town grew around a Dominican priory founded in the thirteenth century and a fortification of the O'Farrell clan, the local ruling family from whom the territory took the name Longphort Uí Fhearghail. Longford developed as a garrison and market town; its military barracks, later named Sean Connolly Barracks, dated from the early nineteenth century. St Mel's Cathedral, built from 1840 and regarded as a fine example of neoclassical architecture in Ireland, was badly damaged by fire on Christmas Day 2009 and reopened after restoration in 2014.

Transport and culture

Longford lies on the N4 Dublin–Sligo road and is served by a station on the Dublin–Sligo railway line. The Backstage Theatre and the town's annual literary connections, including associations with the writer Padraic Colum, form part of its cultural life.
