Chicago Stadium

Location1800 West Madison Street, Chicago, Illinois
OpenedMarch 28, 1929
ClosedSeptember 9, 1994
Demolished1995
Capacity17,317 (hockey); 18,676 (basketball)

Chicago Stadium was an indoor arena in Chicago, Illinois, that opened in 1929 and closed in 1994. Nicknamed the "Madhouse on Madison", it was home to the Chicago Blackhawks of the National Hockey League and the Chicago Bulls of the National Basketball Association.

History

The stadium was built by sports promoter Paddy Harmon at a cost of about $9.5 million. When it opened on March 28, 1929, with a boxing match between Tommy Loughran and Mickey Walker, it was the largest indoor arena in the world. It featured a 3,663-pipe Barton organ, played famously by Al Melgard, that became synonymous with the building's thunderous atmosphere.

Events

Beyond hockey and basketball, the Stadium hosted boxing, professional wrestling, political conventions — including the 1932, 1940, 1944 and 1952 Democratic and Republican national conventions — and concerts by artists such as Frank Sinatra and Elvis Presley.

Closure and legacy

The Blackhawks and Bulls moved across the street to the United Center in 1994. Chicago Stadium was demolished in 1995; its site is now a parking lot. The center-ice faceoff circle and other memorabilia were preserved, and the building remains fondly remembered for its deafening crowd noise.
