Ivan Asen II

Tsar of Bulgaria
Reign: 1218 – 1241
Predecessor: Boril
Successor: Kaliman I
Dynasty: Asen

Ivan Asen II (died 1241) was Tsar (emperor) of Bulgaria from 1218 to 1241. Under his rule the Second Bulgarian Empire reached its greatest territorial extent and political influence, and his reign is often regarded as its golden age.

Accession

Ivan Asen was a son of Ivan Asen I, one of the founders of the restored Bulgarian empire. After the usurper Boril seized the throne in 1207, the young prince fled first to the Cumans and then to Rus'. In 1218 he returned with Rus' and Cuman support, besieged Boril in the capital Tarnovo, and took the throne after Boril was captured and blinded.

Expansion and the Battle of Klokotnitsa

Through diplomacy and war Ivan Asen II steadily enlarged his state. His marriage alliances linked Bulgaria to Hungary and to the Empire of Nicaea. On 9 March 1230, at the Battle of Klokotnitsa, he decisively defeated Theodore Komnenos Doukas, ruler of Epirus, who had broken a treaty and invaded Bulgaria. Theodore was captured, and Ivan Asen annexed great territories in Thrace, Macedonia and Albania, so that Bulgaria once again extended to three seas — the Black Sea, the Aegean and the Adriatic. Famous for his clemency, he released the captured Epirote soldiers and treated the conquered lands leniently; an inscription in the Church of the Forty Martyrs in Tarnovo commemorates his victory.

Church and state

In 1235, in alliance with the Nicaean emperor John III Vatatzes, Ivan Asen secured recognition of an autocephalous Bulgarian Patriarchate at Lampsacus, restoring the patriarchal dignity lost after the Byzantine reconquest of Bulgaria two centuries earlier. His capital flourished as a centre of building, trade and literature; he granted trade privileges to Ragusa (Dubrovnik) in 1230 and struck abundant gold and copper coinage. Bulgaria navigated carefully between the Latin Empire of Constantinople, Nicaea, Hungary and the papacy, though the approaching Mongol storm shadowed the end of his reign. He died in 1241, shortly before the Mongol invasion of eastern Europe, and was succeeded by his young son Kaliman I.
