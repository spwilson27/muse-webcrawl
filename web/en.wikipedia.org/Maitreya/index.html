Maitreya (Sanskrit: मैत्रेय) is a bodhisattva regarded in Buddhist eschatology as the future Buddha of this world who will appear on Earth when the teachings of Gautama Buddha have been forgotten, achieve complete enlightenment, and teach the pure dharma. His name derives from the Sanskrit word maitrī, meaning loving-kindness or friendliness.

Sources

Maitreya is mentioned in the canonical literature of all major Buddhist schools, including the Pali Canon, where he appears in the Cakkavatti Sutta of the Dīgha Nikāya. He is currently said to reside in Tuṣita Heaven, awaiting the proper time to descend to Earth.

Iconography

In art, Maitreya is often depicted seated on a throne in a Western fashion, holding a stupa in his crown, or as the cheerful "Laughing Buddha" (Budai) in Chinese tradition. The massive Leshan Giant Buddha in Sichuan is a depiction of Maitreya.

Maitreya in other traditions

Maitreya has influenced millenarian movements in China and appears in adapted forms in other religions, including the Baháʼí Faith, Theosophy, and Hinduism, where he is sometimes associated with Kalki.
