Second Opium War

The Second Opium War (1856–1860), also known as the Arrow War, was fought by the United Kingdom and France against the Qing dynasty of China. It followed the First Opium War (1839–1842) and arose from demands to expand foreign trade, legalise the opium trade, and establish diplomatic representation in Beijing.

Course

The pretext was the boarding of the Chinese-registered lorcha Arrow by Qing officials in Canton in October 1856. Anglo-French forces captured Canton, then in 1858 took the Taku Forts and imposed the Treaties of Tientsin. When the Qing refused ratification and repelled a British attempt to force the passage to Beijing in 1859, a larger expedition in 1860 captured Beijing. As reprisal for the torture and killing of allied envoys, Lord Elgin ordered the looting and burning of the Old Summer Palace (Yuanmingyuan).

Outcome

The Convention of Peking (1860) ratified and extended the Tientsin treaties: more ports opened, opium effectively legalised, Kowloon ceded to Britain, indemnities imposed, missionaries granted rights, and Russia gained territory north of the Amur. The war deepened China's "century of humiliation" in later historical memory.
