Dead Poets Society

Dead Poets Society is a 1989 American coming-of-age drama film directed by Peter Weir and written by Tom Schulman. Set in 1959 at the fictional elite boarding school Welton Academy in Vermont, it stars Robin Williams as John Keating, an unconventional English teacher who inspires his students through poetry to question conformity and seize the day — carpe diem.

Plot

Keating, himself a Welton alumnus, encourages his class to rip out the textbook introduction, stand on their desks to gain new perspectives, and revive the Dead Poets Society, a secret club that meets in a cave to read poetry. Inspired, student Neil Perry pursues acting against the wishes of his domineering father; forbidden from performing and facing military school, Neil takes his own life. The school forces the boys to sign a statement blaming Keating, who is dismissed — but as he leaves, the class stands on their desks in tribute, reciting Walt Whitman's "O Captain! My Captain!".

Reception and legacy

The film was a commercial success, grossing over $235 million worldwide, and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture; Schulman won the Oscar for Best Original Screenplay. Williams's performance is among his most celebrated, and the film has been widely used — and debated — in discussions of teaching, conformity and adolescent suicide. It was later adapted into a novel and a stage play.
