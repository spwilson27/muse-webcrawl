Venous blood is deoxygenated blood travelling through the veins toward the heart after delivering oxygen to the tissues. It is darker red than oxygen-rich arterial blood because of the differing absorption properties of deoxyhaemoglobin. The one major exception to the "venous means deoxygenated" pattern is the pulmonary veins, which carry oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium.

Properties

Mixed venous blood normally has an oxygen saturation of about 75 percent and a slightly higher carbon dioxide content and lower pH than arterial blood. It contains metabolic waste products, hormones, and absorbed nutrients collected from the organs it drains, including the hepatic portal circulation from the gut.

Clinical use

Venous blood is the usual specimen for laboratory testing, drawn by venipuncture from veins such as the median cubital vein. Venous blood gas analysis is used to assess acid–base status and ventilation. Central venous catheters allow sampling of blood near the right atrium and measurement of central venous pressure.
