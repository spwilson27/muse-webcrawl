The Iron Bridge

The Iron Bridge is a cast iron arch bridge that crosses the River Severn in Shropshire, England. Opened in 1781, it was the first major bridge in the world to be made of cast iron, and was greatly celebrated after construction owing to its use of the new material.

Construction

The bridge spans the Ironbridge Gorge, in the heart of the region where Abraham Darby had pioneered coke smelting of iron. It was designed by Thomas Farnolls Pritchard and built by Abraham Darby III; its 30.6 m (100 ft) single arch was erected in 1779 without a single fatal accident, using carpentry-like joints — mortise and tenon and dovetails — in iron castings. The structure weighs about 384 tonnes.

Legacy

The bridge became a potent symbol of the Industrial Revolution and gave its name to the town of Ironbridge that grew around it. The gorge is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Closed to vehicles in 1934 and now a pedestrian bridge, it underwent a major conservation project by English Heritage in 2017–2018.
