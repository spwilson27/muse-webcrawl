Literary criticism

Literary criticism is the study, evaluation, and interpretation of literature. It ranges from ancient commentary on poetry to modern theoretical schools, and asks what literary works mean, how they achieve their effects, and how they should be valued. In contemporary usage, "literary theory" often denotes the philosophical frameworks informing criticism, while criticism proper applies them to particular texts.

History

Western criticism begins with Plato's suspicion of poetry in the Republic and Aristotle's Poetics, which analysed tragedy through concepts such as mimesis, catharsis, and the unities. Horace, Longinus (On the Sublime), and medieval allegorical commentary followed. Renaissance and neoclassical critics codified rules; Samuel Johnson and the Romantics emphasized judgement and expressive genius. In the 19th century criticism became a public profession (Matthew Arnold's "function of criticism"), and academic study institutionalized it.

Major schools of the 20th century

  • New Criticism (1930s–50s): close reading of the text itself; the "intentional" and "affective" fallacies (Wimsatt and Beardsley); Cleanth Brooks, John Crowe Ransom.
  • Russian formalism and structuralism: defamiliarization (Shklovsky); narratology; Lévi-Strauss, Barthes, Genette.
  • Psychoanalytic criticism: Freud and Lacan applied to authors, characters, and readers.
  • Marxist criticism: ideology, class, and the material conditions of production (Lukács, Raymond Williams, Terry Eagleton).
  • Post-structuralism and deconstruction: Derrida; indeterminacy of meaning; Paul de Man.
  • New Historicism and cultural materialism: texts embedded in history and power (Stephen Greenblatt).
  • Feminist, postcolonial, and queer criticism: canon revision, representation, and identity (Showalter, Said's Orientalism, Spivak, Butler).
  • Reader-response and reception theory: meaning made by readers (Iser, Fish).
  • Ecocriticism and postcritique: literature and environment; later challenges to "suspicious" reading.

Criticism today

Criticism continues in academic journals, review pages, and online venues; debates concern the balance between interpretation and evaluation, the status of theory after the "theory wars", and new methods such as digital humanities and distant reading (Franco Moretti).
