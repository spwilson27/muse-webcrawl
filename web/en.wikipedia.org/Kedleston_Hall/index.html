Kedleston Hall

Kedleston Hall is a neo-classical and Palladian country house in Kedleston, Derbyshire, England, about four miles north-west of Derby. It was the seat of the Curzon family, and is now owned by the National Trust.

Commissioned by Nathaniel Curzon, 1st Baron Scarsdale, and built 1759–1765, the house was designed by James Paine and Matthew Brettingham, with interiors famously completed by a young Robert Adam — Adam's first great country-house commission. Its Marble Hall, with alabaster columns and statues in niches, and the domed saloon, are high points of English Neoclassicism.

Collections

The house displays paintings, sculpture and furniture collected by the Curzons, plus an Indian museum assembled by George Nathaniel Curzon, Viceroy of India 1899–1905, whose peacock dress is a celebrated exhibit.
