Thermal cycler

A thermal cycler (also thermocycler or PCR machine) is a laboratory instrument that amplifies segments of DNA by the polymerase chain reaction (PCR). It holds reaction tubes in a metal block whose temperature is cycled rapidly and precisely.

Operation

A typical programme repeats 25–40 cycles: denaturation near 95 °C separates DNA strands, annealing near 50–65 °C lets primers bind, and extension near 72 °C lets the polymerase synthesise new strands. Modern instruments use Peltier elements, heated lids to prevent condensation, and gradient blocks to optimise annealing temperatures.

History

Early PCR required manual transfer of tubes between water baths; automated cyclers, introduced in the late 1980s, made the technique routine and underlie modern molecular biology, diagnostics, and forensics.
