Coyoacán

Coyoacán is a borough (demarcación territorial) in Mexico City. The former village is now the borough's historic centre, known for colonial streets, museums and cultural life.

History

The name comes from Nahuatl Coyohuacán, 'place of coyotes'. Hernán Cortés established headquarters here after the conquest of Tenochtitlan. In the 20th century it was home to Frida Kahlo (whose Casa Azul is now a museum), Diego Rivera, Leon Trotsky — who was assassinated here in 1940 — and writer Octavio Paz.

Landmarks

Jardín Centenario, Jardín Hidalgo, the Frida Kahlo Museum, Trotsky Museum, and the central campus of UNAM (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) in the south of the borough.
