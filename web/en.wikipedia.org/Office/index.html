Office

An office is a space where the employees of an organization perform administrative work in order to support and realize the various objects and goals of the organization. The word "office" may also denote a position within an organization with specific duties attached (see officer, office-holder, official). In legal writing, a company or organization has offices in any place where it has an official presence, even if that presence consists of a storage silo.

History

Dedicated office spaces emerged with large bureaucracies in ancient Rome and in medieval chanceries, and expanded with banking, insurance and railway companies in the nineteenth century. Landmark early office buildings include London's Admiralty and the purpose-built headquarters of insurers and railways. The skyscraper, the elevator, the typewriter, the telephone and later the computer progressively reshaped office design and work through the twentieth century.

Types and layout

Offices range from a single room to entire floors of office buildings and dedicated campuses. Layouts have evolved from rows of private rooms to the open-plan floor, cubicles, and today's activity-based and hybrid models combining desks, meeting rooms and remote work. Common facilities include workstations, conference rooms, reception areas, kitchens and server rooms.

Office work

Activities performed in offices include administration, accounting, management, design, sales and customer service. The nature of office work has been transformed by personal computing, email and videoconferencing, and since the 2020s by widespread hybrid and remote working, which has reduced demand for traditional office space in many cities.
