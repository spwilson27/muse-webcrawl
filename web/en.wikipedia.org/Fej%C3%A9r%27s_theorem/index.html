Fejér's theorem

In mathematics, Fejér's theorem, named after the Hungarian mathematician Lipót Fejér, states that if f is a continuous periodic function with period 2π, then the sequence of arithmetic means of the partial sums of its Fourier series converges uniformly to f.

Statement and significance

While the Fourier series of a continuous function need not converge pointwise everywhere, Fejér's theorem shows that the Cesàro means σn(f) — the averages of the first n partial sums — always converge uniformly for continuous f. The proof uses the positivity of the Fejér kernel, an advantage over the Dirichlet kernel. Corollaries include the uniqueness of Fourier coefficients for continuous functions and an easy route to the Weierstrass approximation theorem.
