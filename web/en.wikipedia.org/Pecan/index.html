Pecan

The pecan (Carya illinoinensis) is a species of hickory native to the southern United States and northern Mexico in the region of the Mississippi River. The tree is cultivated for its seed, a pecan nut, primarily in the United States and Mexico. The seed is an edible nut used as a snack and in various recipes, such as praline candy and pecan pie.

Pecan
Scientific name: Carya illinoinensis
Family: Juglandaceae
Native range: South-central North America

Cultivation

Pecan trees can live and bear nuts for more than 300 years. Commercial production relies on grafted cultivars selected for nut size, yield and disease resistance; Georgia, New Mexico and Texas are leading U.S. producers, and Mexico is the other major grower. The name "pecan" derives from an Algonquian word for nuts requiring a stone to crack.

Uses

Pecans are eaten raw or roasted and are a signature ingredient of Southern U.S. cooking. They are rich in monounsaturated fats, manganese and antioxidants. Pecan wood is also valued for furniture and flooring.

State symbols

The pecan is the state tree and state nut of Texas and features in the state symbols of several other states.
