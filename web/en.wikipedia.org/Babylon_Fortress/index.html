Babylon Fortress

Babylon Fortress (Arabic: حصن بابليون) is an ancient Roman fortress located in the area now known as Coptic Cairo, in Old Cairo, Egypt. Situated on the eastern bank of the Nile, it formed part of the defensive system controlling river traffic between Upper and Lower Egypt and stands today at the heart of Cairo's historic Christian quarter.

History

A fortification existed at the site from pharaonic and Persian times, guarding the junction between the Nile valley and the canal leading to the Red Sea. The surviving structure is largely Roman, built under the emperor Diocletian around 300 AD when the garrison of the Legio XIII Gemina was stationed there. The fortress's massive round towers and curtain walls, constructed of limestone blocks banded with red brick, are characteristic of late Roman military architecture.

The fortress today

Within and above the fortress walls stand several of Cairo's oldest churches, including the Church of the Holy Virgin Mary (the "Hanging Church", which sits atop the southern gatehouse) and the Church of St. Sergius and Bacchus, traditionally associated with the Holy Family's stay in Egypt. The Greek Orthodox Church of St. George and the Ben Ezra Synagogue are also nearby. Much of the fortress now lies below modern ground level because the Nile's course has shifted and debris has accumulated over the centuries.
