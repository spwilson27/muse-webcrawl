Windsor Castle is a royal residence at Windsor in the English county of Berkshire. It is strongly associated with the English and succeeding British royal family and embodies almost a millennium of architectural history. The original castle was built in the 11th century after the Norman invasion by William the Conqueror, and it has been used by the reigning monarch since Henry I. It is the largest inhabited castle in the world.

History

Successive monarchs enlarged the castle: Henry III built a luxurious palace, Edward III transformed it into a Gothic showpiece centred on St George's Hall, and Charles II modernised the interiors in Baroque style. George IV created the present skyline, including the heightened Round Tower. During the Second World War the castle sheltered the royal family, and in 1992 a major fire destroyed part of the state apartments, which were restored by 1997.

St George's Chapel

Within the castle precincts stands St George's Chapel, a masterpiece of late medieval Perpendicular Gothic architecture and the spiritual home of the Order of the Garter, founded by Edward III in 1348.
