Alesia

Alesia was the capital of the Mandubii, one of the Gallic tribes allied with the Aedui. The oppidum was situated on Mont Auxois, near the sources of the Seine in modern-day France, at the site identified with Alise-Sainte-Reine in the Côte-d'Or department.

Battle of Alesia

Alesia is famous as the site of the Battle of Alesia in 52 BC, the decisive confrontation of the Gallic Wars. The Gallic leader Vercingetorix retreated to the stronghold with his army, and Julius Caesar besieged it with elaborate double fortifications: a circumvallation facing inward and a contravallation facing outward against a Gallic relief force. After the relief army was repulsed, Vercingetorix surrendered, effectively ending organized Gallic resistance to Roman rule.

Archaeology

Excavations sponsored by Napoleon III in the 1860s uncovered extensive remains of Caesar's siege works, confirming the identification of the site. Further excavations continued into the 20th century. Because the ground was waterlogged, wooden structures and organic artefacts were exceptionally well preserved.

MuséoParc Alésia

A museum and interpretive centre, the MuséoParc Alésia, opened in 2012 near the site, presenting the battle, the Gallo-Roman town that later stood there, and reconstructed sections of the Roman siege lines. A monumental statue of Vercingetorix, commissioned by Napoleon III, overlooks the plain from Mont Auxois.
