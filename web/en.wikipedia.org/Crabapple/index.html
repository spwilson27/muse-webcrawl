Crabapple

Genus: Malus
Family: Rosaceae
Fruit size: Typically under 5 cm diameter

Crabapples are small-fruited species and varieties in the apple genus Malus. By horticultural convention, a malus fruit two inches (5 cm) or less in diameter is classed as a crabapple, while larger fruit are considered apples. Many crabapples are grown purely for ornament, prized for their profuse spring blossom, colourful small fruit, and compact habit.

Ornamental varieties

Several hundred named cultivars exist, derived from species such as the Japanese flowering crabapple (Malus floribunda), the Siberian crabapple (M. baccata), and the prairie crabapple (M. ioensis). Breeders have selected for flower colour ranging from white through pink to carmine, for red or purple foliage, for persistent fruit that feeds birds in winter, and for resistance to apple scab and fire blight.

Culinary use

Crabapple fruit is generally too sour and astringent to eat raw, being high in malic acid and pectin, but it makes excellent jelly — the high pectin content ensures a firm set — as well as spiced pickles, cider blends, and preserves. The European wild crabapple (Malus sylvestris) is one ancestor of the domesticated apple.

Role in orchards

Because crabapples bloom abundantly over a long period and produce compatible pollen, orchardists plant them within apple orchards as pollinizers for commercial varieties that are not self-fertile.
