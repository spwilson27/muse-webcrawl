Adivasi

Adivasi is an umbrella term for the tribal and Indigenous peoples of India, often corresponding to communities listed as Scheduled Tribes under the Constitution of India. The term derives from Hindi words meaning "original inhabitants".

Demographics

Scheduled Tribes numbered about 104 million people in the 2011 census, roughly 8.6 percent of India's population, with large populations in Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh.

Culture and languages

Adivasi groups speak languages from several families, including Austroasiatic, Dravidian, Indo-Aryan and Tibeto-Burman languages, and maintain distinct religious practices, festivals and traditions.

Rights and movements

Land alienation, displacement by development projects and forest rights have been central political issues; the Forest Rights Act of 2006 recognised individual and community forest rights. See also Scheduled Tribes and India.
