Allied occupation of Germany

The four occupation zones of Germany, 1945–1949.

The Allied occupation of Germany began after the defeat of Nazi Germany in the Second World War, when the victorious Allied powers — the United States, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom and France — divided the country into four occupation zones and administered it through the Allied Control Council. The occupation lasted in its original form until 1949, when the zones of the three Western powers were merged into the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) and the Soviet zone became the German Democratic Republic (East Germany).

Background

At the Yalta Conference in February 1945 and the Potsdam Conference in July–August 1945, the Allies agreed on the division of Germany, the demilitarisation and denazification of German society, reparations, and the westward shift of Poland's borders to the Oder–Neisse line. Berlin, although deep inside the Soviet zone, was itself divided into four sectors.

The occupation zones

The American zone covered Bavaria, Hesse and northern parts of Württemberg-Baden; the British zone comprised the northwest, including Hamburg and the Ruhr; the French zone lay in the southwest along the Rhine; and the Soviet zone covered the east, including Saxony, Brandenburg and Mecklenburg. Each zone was governed by a military governor.

From occupation to division

Growing tensions of the emerging Cold War led the Western powers to merge their zones economically (the Bizone in 1947, later the Trizone) and to introduce the Deutsche Mark in June 1948, prompting the Soviet blockade of West Berlin and the Allied airlift. In 1949 the Federal Republic of Germany was founded on 23 May and the German Democratic Republic on 7 October, formalising the division of Germany for the next four decades.

End of the occupation

The occupation statute formally ended for West Germany with the Paris Agreements of 1955, when the Federal Republic joined NATO, although Allied troops remained stationed in the country. Full sovereignty over a reunified Germany was restored by the Two Plus Four Treaty of 1990.
