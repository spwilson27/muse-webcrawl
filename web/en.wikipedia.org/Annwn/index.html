Annwn

Annwn (pronounced approximately "AH-noon"), also spelled Annwfn or Annwvyn in Middle Welsh, is the Otherworld in Welsh mythology. It is a realm of delights and eternal youth, free of disease and want, ruled variously by the kings Arawn and, in later tradition, Gwyn ap Nudd. Annwn features in the Four Branches of the Mabinogi and in the poem Preiddeu Annwfn ("The Spoils of Annwn") in the Book of Taliesin.

In the Mabinogi

In the First Branch, Pwyll Prince of Dyfed, the mortal prince Pwyll offends Arawn, king of Annwn, and agrees to exchange places and shapes with him for a year and a day in order to defeat Arawn's rival Hafgan. In the Third Branch, Annwn is associated with the magical otherworld from which the enchanter Llwyd ap Cil Coed's spell descends on Dyfed. The names of places and characters suggest a realm reached through mists and islands rather than a land of the dead.

Preiddeu Annwfn

The early Welsh poem Preiddeu Annwfn, preserved in the 14th-century Book of Taliesin, recounts a raid by Arthur and his warriors — by ship to a series of otherworldly fortresses — to seize a magical cauldron of rebirth guarded in Annwn; only seven men return. The poem is one of the earliest Arthurian texts in any language.

Nature of Annwn

Unlike a hell or a heaven, Annwn is portrayed chiefly as a parallel world of abundance and enchantment, perilous mainly through its rulers and its laws; a tradition holds that its hounds, the white-bodied, red-eared Cwn Annwn, hunt the skies on stormy nights. Later folklore sometimes merged Annwn with the Christian hell, but medieval Welsh literature generally treats it as a distinct otherworld.
