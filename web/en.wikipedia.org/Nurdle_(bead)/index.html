Nurdle (bead)

A nurdle, also known as a pre-production plastic pellet (or, erroneously, mermaid's tear), is a small plastic pellet about the size of a lentil, typically under 5 mm in diameter. Nurdles are the primary form in which plastic resin is manufactured and transported; they are melted down and moulded into final plastic products.

Environmental impact

Nurdles are a major source of marine debris and microplastic pollution. They are spilled during manufacturing, handling, and transport — including from ships, as in the 2021 X-Press Pearl disaster off Sri Lanka — and wash onto beaches worldwide. Because they resemble fish eggs and other food, they are eaten by seabirds, fish, and other wildlife, and they can absorb and transport toxic pollutants. Volunteer beach surveys count nurdles to monitor pollution, and campaigns and proposed regulations seek to classify nurdles as hazardous and tighten handling rules.

Industry handling

The plastics industry promotes voluntary programs such as Operation Clean Sweep to prevent pellet loss, though environmental groups argue binding regulation is needed.
