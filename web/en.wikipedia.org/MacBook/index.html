MacBook is a brand of Mac notebook computers developed and marketed by Apple since 2006. The original MacBook replaced the iBook and 12-inch PowerBook lines during the Mac transition to Intel processors, and became one of the world's best-selling laptop lines. The name has been applied to several distinct designs: the consumer MacBook (2006–2012), the ultrathin 12-inch "Retina MacBook" (2015–2019), and, as a family name, the current MacBook Air and MacBook Pro lines.

MacBook (2006–2012)

The first-generation MacBook was introduced in May 2006 in white and black polycarbonate casings reminiscent of the iBook G4, with Intel Core Duo processors, a 13.3-inch glossy display, an integrated iSight webcam, and the MagSafe power connector. A unibody aluminium design followed in October 2008, succeeded in 2009 by a unibody polycarbonate model. The standalone MacBook was discontinued in February 2012 as the MacBook Air took over the consumer segment.

MacBook (2015–2019)

At a special event in March 2015, Apple unveiled an all-new 12-inch MacBook with a Retina display, a single USB-C port, a fanless Intel Core M design, a Force Touch trackpad, and a butterfly-mechanism keyboard, in silver, space gray, and gold finishes. The butterfly keyboard proved controversial for reliability, and the model was discontinued in July 2019.

Apple silicon era

In November 2020 Apple began replacing Intel processors with its own M-series chips, starting with the MacBook Air and 13-inch MacBook Pro using the M1. Subsequent generations introduced the redesigned MacBook Air (M2, 2022; 15-inch, 2023) and MacBook Pro models with M1 Pro/Max, M2 Pro/Max, M3, and later chips. No plain "MacBook" model is sold in the current lineup, but the family name persists through the Air and Pro lines.
