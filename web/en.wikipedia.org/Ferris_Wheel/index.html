Ferris Wheel

A modern Ferris wheel

A Ferris wheel (also called a Giant Wheel or an observation wheel) is an amusement ride consisting of a rotating upright wheel with multiple passenger-carrying components (commonly referred to as passenger cars, cabins, or gondolas) attached to the rim in such a way that as the wheel turns, they are kept upright, usually by gravity.

The original Ferris Wheel

The original Ferris Wheel was designed and constructed by George Washington Gale Ferris Jr., an American engineer, as the landmark of the World's Columbian Exposition held in Chicago in 1893. Standing 80.4 metres (264 feet) tall with 36 cars, each capable of carrying 60 passengers, it was intended to rival the Eiffel Tower of the 1889 Paris exposition.

Observation wheels

Modern giant observation wheels, such as the London Eye, are often fully enclosed and rotate continuously at a slow speed, with passengers boarding while the wheel moves. The tallest Ferris wheel in the world is Ain Dubai in the United Arab Emirates, at 250 metres.
