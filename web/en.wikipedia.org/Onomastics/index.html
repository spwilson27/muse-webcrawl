Onomastics

Onomastics (or onomatology) is the study of the etymology, history, and use of proper names. The word derives from the Greek onomastikós, from ónoma, "name". Onomastics draws on linguistics, history, geography, anthropology, and genealogy, because names preserve information about the languages, migrations, religions, and social customs of the people who coined and used them.

Toponymy, the study of place names, is a major branch of onomastics.

Branches

The two principal branches are toponymy, the study of place names (toponyms), and anthroponymy, the study of personal names (anthroponyms), including given names and surnames. Other subdivisions include hydronymy (names of bodies of water) and the study of names of organizations, brands, and animals. Place names may describe landscape features, commemorate persons or events, or preserve words from extinct languages; personal names may originate as patronymics, occupational terms, nicknames, or place names.

Applications

Onomastic research helps historians reconstruct settlement patterns and language boundaries, assists genealogists tracing family histories, and informs the work of official naming authorities such as geographic names boards, which standardize spellings and resolve disputes. Literary onomastics examines the deliberate choice of character names in fiction.
