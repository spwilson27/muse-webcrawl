Peerage of England

The Peerage of England comprises all peerages created by the monarchs of England before the Acts of Union 1707, which united the Kingdoms of England and Scotland into Great Britain. After 1707, new creations for English titles were made in the Peerage of Great Britain.

Ranks and history

The peerage comprises dukes, marquesses, earls, viscounts, and barons (including baronesses in their own right). The first dukedom, Cornwall, was created in 1337 for the Black Prince; the marquessate followed in 1385, and the viscountcy in 1440. Barons were originally summoned to Parliament individually by writ, and baronies by writ descend to heirs general, which can lead to abeyance among co-heirs.

Holders of English peerages created before 1707 ranked below equivalent peers of Scotland in the original table of precedence, a grievance addressed by later rulings. All surviving English peerages are now part of the peerage of the United Kingdom for practical purposes, and their holders follow the order of precedence by rank and creation date.

Surviving titles

Notable surviving English dukedoms include Norfolk (created 1483, held by the Howard family), Somerset, and Richmond. The oldest surviving barony is generally considered de Ros (1264). Many early peerages are extinct, forfeit, or in abeyance.
