Liberation fonts
Developer: Ascender Corp / Red Hat
Typefaces: Liberation Sans, Serif, Mono
Licence: SIL Open Font License (v2+)

The Liberation fonts are a family of free and open-source typefaces developed by Ascender Corporation and commissioned by Red Hat. They are metric-compatible with Microsoft's most widely used fonts: Liberation Sans with Arial, Liberation Serif with Times New Roman, and Liberation Mono with Courier New. Metric compatibility allows documents formatted with the proprietary fonts to be displayed and printed identically when the Liberation fonts are substituted.

History

The first release appeared in 2007 under the GNU General Public License with a font exception. Version 2.00, released in 2012 and based on Google's Croscore fonts (Arimo, Tinos and Cousine), was relicensed under the SIL Open Font License. The fonts are default in many Linux distributions and in LibreOffice.

Families

Liberation Sans is a humanist sans-serif; Liberation Serif is a transitional serif; Liberation Mono is a monospaced design. Each is available in regular, bold, italic and bold italic styles. Liberation Sans Narrow, metric-compatible with Arial Narrow, was also produced.
