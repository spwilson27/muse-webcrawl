Karl Barth (German: [bart]; 10 May 1886 – 10 December 1968) was a Swiss Reformed theologian who is regarded as one of the most important Christian thinkers of the 20th century. He was a leader of the movement known as dialectical theology or neo-orthodoxy, and his vast multi-volume Church Dogmatics is widely considered one of the great works of systematic theology.

Life and work

Barth began his career as a pastor in Safenwil, Switzerland. His commentary on The Epistle to the Romans (1919, substantially revised 1922) caused a sensation by breaking with liberal theology and stressing the radical transcendence of God and the centrality of revelation in Jesus Christ. He later taught theology at Göttingen, Münster, and Bonn in Germany.

Barth was a principal author of the Barmen Declaration of 1934, in which the Confessing Church rejected the Nazi-aligned German Christians and affirmed that Christ alone is the church's Lord. Expelled from Germany in 1935 for refusing to swear an oath to Adolf Hitler, Barth returned to Basel, where he taught until his retirement.

Church Dogmatics

The Church Dogmatics, published from 1932 until Barth's death and left unfinished, comprises over six million words in thirteen part-volumes. Its theology is centred on the doctrine of the Word of God, the election of humanity in Jesus Christ, and the reconciliation accomplished in him.
