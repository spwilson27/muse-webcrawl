The Ahom kingdom (1228–1826) was a late medieval kingdom in the Brahmaputra Valley of present-day Assam, India. Founded by the Tai prince Sukaphaa, it endured for nearly 600 years and is remembered for successfully resisting Mughal expansion into Northeast India.

History

The Ahom state was organized around the paik system of corvée labor, in which adult males owed service to the state in rotation. Over centuries the originally Tai-speaking Ahom elite adopted Hinduism and the Assamese language, and royal chronicles called buranjis recorded the kingdom's history.

The kingdom repelled repeated Mughal invasions, most famously at the Battle of Saraighat (1671), where the Ahom general Lachit Borphukan defeated the Mughal forces on the Brahmaputra River. Internal conflict and Burmese invasions in the early 19th century weakened the state, and by the Treaty of Yandabo (1826) the territory passed to the British East India Company.
