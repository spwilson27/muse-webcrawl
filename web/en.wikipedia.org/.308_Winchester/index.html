The .308 Winchester is a smokeless powder rimless bottlenecked rifle cartridge widely used for hunting, target shooting, military and law enforcement applications. Introduced in 1952 by Winchester Repeating Arms, it is the commercial version of the military 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge, which was adopted by the United States and NATO allies two years later.

History

After World War II the US Army sought a shorter cartridge that could match the performance of the .30-06 Springfield in a more compact action. Experiments with the .300 Savage case led to the T65 series. Winchester released the commercial .308 in 1952, chambered in the Model 70 bolt-action and Model 88 lever-action rifles.

Performance and use

With bullet weights typically from 110 to 180 grains and muzzle velocities around 2,600–2,800 ft/s, the .308 is effective for medium to large game including deer, elk and black bear. It is renowned for inherent accuracy and remains one of the most popular short-action hunting cartridges worldwide. It is also widely used in precision rifle competition and by military designated marksmen in its 7.62 NATO form.
