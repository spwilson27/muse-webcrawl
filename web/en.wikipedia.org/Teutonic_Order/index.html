Teutonic Order

The Teutonic Order, officially the Order of Brothers of the German House of Saint Mary in Jerusalem (Latin: Ordo domus Sanctae Mariae Theutonicorum Hierosolymitanorum), is a Catholic religious military order founded c. 1190 in Acre, in the Levant, during the Third Crusade.

History

Under Grand Master Hermann von Salza, the Order grew in imperial favour. After a brief episode in Hungary, it was invited by Duke Konrad of Masovia in 1226 to crusade against the pagan Prussians, building a territorial state — the State of the Teutonic Order — in Prussia, later extending into Livonia through the Livonian Brothers of the Sword.

Wars with Poland-Lithuania

The Order seized Gdańsk Pomerania in 1308, provoking a century of conflict with Poland and Lithuania. Its defeat by Jogaila and Vytautas at Grunwald (1410) and the Treaty of Thorn began its decline. After the Thirteen Years' War, the Second Peace of Thorn (1466) split Prussia; in 1525 Grand Master Albert secularised the remainder as the Duchy of Prussia under Polish suzerainty.

Modern order

Suppressed and reorganised several times, the order survives today as a purely religious order of priests, sisters and associates based in Vienna.
