Vlad III, commonly known as Vlad the Impaler (Romanian: Vlad Țepeș) or Vlad Dracula (Romanian: Vlad Drăculea), was Voivode of Wallachia three times between 1448 and his death in 1476/77. He is often considered one of the most important rulers in Wallachian history and a national hero in Romania.

He was the second son of Vlad Dracul, who became the ruler of Wallachia in 1436. Vlad and his younger brother Radu were held as hostages in the Ottoman Empire in 1442 to secure their father's loyalty. During his second reign (1456–1462), his chief achievement was resisting Ottoman expansion: he refused tribute to Sultan Mehmed II and in 1462 carried out the famous Night Attack at Târgoviște. He is credited with impaling huge numbers of enemies and criminals — the practice from which his epithet derives — although contemporary sources mix fact with hostile propaganda.

Name and legend

"Dracula" is the Slavonic genitive of his father's name, Dracul ("the Dragon", from his membership in the Order of the Dragon); in modern Romanian it also connotes "son of the devil". Stories of his cruelty circulated widely in German pamphlets and Russian manuscripts in his lifetime and after his death. In the 19th century his name and castle associations were linked in the popular imagination with Bram Stoker's 1897 novel Dracula, though Stoker's knowledge of the historical Vlad appears to have been slight.

Death and reputation

After years of imprisonment in Hungary, Vlad regained the Wallachian throne in 1476 with Hungarian and Moldavian support but was killed in battle against the Ottomans within months. In Romania he is remembered chiefly as a defender of independence and a scourge of corrupt boyars and invaders, while elsewhere his name remains synonymous with cruelty.
