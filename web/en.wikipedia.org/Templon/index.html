Templon

A templon (from Greek τέμπλον, meaning "temple") is a feature of Byzantine, and later Orthodox, church architecture: a barrier separating the sanctuary (bema) from the nave, reserved for the clergy. In its early form the templon was a low barrier of carved parapet slabs between columns supporting an architrave; above the architrave, or later on the barrier itself, icons were displayed.

Development

The templon replaced the simple chancel rails of early Christian basilicas. From roughly the 11th century, icons were increasingly placed in the intercolumnar spaces, and in late Byzantine and post-Byzantine practice the barrier developed into the tall wooden or masonry iconostasis of Orthodox churches, with multiple tiers of icons and holy doors. In Greece the marble templon survived longer, while in Russia the screen grew into a wall of five tiers or more.

Function

The templon marked the boundary between the earthly congregation and the heavenly realm represented by the sanctuary, while its gates and openings allowed processions and the movement of clergy during the Divine Liturgy. Curtains hung between the columns could be drawn at the most solemn moments of the service.
