The Open Government Partnership (OGP) is a multilateral initiative that aims to secure concrete commitments from national and sub-national governments to promote open government, combat corruption, and encourage citizen participation in governance. It was formally launched on 20 September 2011 by eight founding governments: Brazil, Indonesia, Mexico, Norway, the Philippines, South Africa, the United Kingdom and the United States.

OGP members commit to action plans co-created with civil society.

Structure and process

Participating governments endorse an Open Government Declaration and work with local civil society organisations to develop National Action Plans, typically covering two years, containing specific, measurable commitments. An Independent Reporting Mechanism (IRM) evaluates the design and implementation of each plan. A Steering Committee, composed of government and civil society representatives in equal numbers, governs the partnership.

Eligibility

To join, countries must meet minimum eligibility criteria in four areas: fiscal transparency, access to information, public officials' asset disclosure, and citizen engagement. Progress is assessed using third-party data.

Growth and criticism

OGP expanded rapidly to more than 75 countries, and in 2016 launched a pilot programme for local governments. Critics have questioned the enforceability of commitments and the wide variation in ambition between members; a few countries have been made inactive or have withdrawn after failing to meet the partnership's standards.
