Ferris wheel

A Ferris wheel at an amusement park

A Ferris wheel is a nonbuilding structure consisting of a rotating upright wheel with passenger gondolas suspended from its rim. It is a staple of fairs, carnivals and amusement parks around the world, offering riders a slow, elevated view of the surroundings.

History

Early "pleasure wheels" appeared in the 17th century. The modern ride takes its name from George Ferris, whose 264-foot wheel for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago became a sensation and gave the generic name "Ferris wheel" to all such rides. Smaller portable wheels became fixtures of travelling fairs in the 20th century.

Types

Wheels range from small carnival models to enormous observation wheels with enclosed, climate-controlled capsules. Notable examples include the London Eye (135 m), the Singapore Flyer (165 m), the High Roller in Las Vegas (167.6 m), and Ain Dubai (250 m), the tallest ever built.
