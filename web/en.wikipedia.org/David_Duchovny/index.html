David William Duchovny (born August 7, 1960) is an American actor, writer, director, and musician. He is best known for playing FBI agent Fox Mulder on the television series The X-Files (1993–2002, 2016–2018) and writer Hank Moody on the television series Californication (2007–2014), both of which earned him Golden Globe Awards.

Early life and education

Duchovny was born in New York City, the son of a writer and publicist of Ukrainian Jewish descent and a Scottish-born teacher. He attended the Grace Church School and the Collegiate School in Manhattan, then graduated from Princeton University with a degree in English literature. He earned a master's degree in English literature from Yale University and began work toward a PhD, which he did not complete.

Career

Duchovny's breakthrough came with the role of Special Agent Fox Mulder in Chris Carter's The X-Files, in which he starred opposite Gillian Anderson as Dana Scully. The show became a cultural phenomenon and ran for nine seasons, followed by two feature films and a revival. Duchovny also appeared in films including Evolution (2001), Zoolander (2001), and Trust the Man (2005), which he wrote and directed.

From 2007 to 2014 he starred as the troubled novelist Hank Moody in Showtime's Californication. He later played detective Sam Hodiak in the NBC crime drama Aquarius (2015–2016).

Writing and music

Duchovny is the author of several novels, including Holy Cow (2015), Bucky F*cking Dent (2016), Miss Subways (2018), and Truly Like Lightning (2021). As a singer-songwriter he has released albums including Hell or High Water (2015) and Every Third Thought (2018).
