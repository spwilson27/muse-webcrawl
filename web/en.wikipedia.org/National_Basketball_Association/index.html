National Basketball Association

Founded: 6 June 1946 (as BAA), New York City
Teams: 30 (29 US, 1 Canada)
Commissioner: Adam Silver
Most championships: Boston Celtics

The National Basketball Association (NBA) is a professional basketball league in North America composed of 30 teams. It is the premier men's professional basketball league in the world and one of the major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada.

History

The league was founded in New York City in 1946 as the Basketball Association of America (BAA) and adopted the NBA name in 1949 after absorbing the rival National Basketball League. The Boston Celtics dynasty of Bill Russell won eleven championships in thirteen seasons (1957–69). The 1980s rivalry between Magic Johnson's Los Angeles Lakers and Larry Bird's Celtics, followed by Michael Jordan's Chicago Bulls winning six titles in the 1990s, globalised the sport. In 1976 the NBA absorbed four teams from the rival American Basketball Association.

Structure

The regular season runs from October to April, each team playing 82 games, followed by playoffs culminating in the NBA Finals between the Eastern and Western Conference champions. Players are selected through the annual draft, dominated by college and international prospects. The league operates under a salary cap with a luxury tax. Sister and affiliate leagues include the WNBA and the NBA G League.

Global reach

Stars such as Dirk Nowitzki, Giannis Antetokounmpo, Luka Dončić and Nikola Jokić reflect the league's internationalisation; NBA games are broadcast in over 200 countries, and basketball's global popularity is second only to association football.
