Scrapbooking

Scrapbooking is a method of preserving, presenting and arranging personal and family history in the form of a book, box or card. Typical memorabilia include photographs, printed media, artwork, tickets and written journaling. Scrapbooking is a widely practised craft and hobby, particularly in the United States, with a large industry of supplies, patterned papers, stickers, stamps and cutting tools.

History

The practice descends from the commonplace books of early modern England and the friendship albums of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. In the nineteenth century, printed ephemera and trade cards were pasted into albums. Mark Twain patented a self-pasting scrapbook in 1873. The modern hobby took off in the 1980s and 1990s with the availability of acid-free, archival materials designed to protect photographs from deterioration.

Styles and digital scrapbooking

Traditional scrapbook pages are commonly 12 by 12 inches (30 by 30 cm), decorated with layered papers, titles and embellishments, and protected in plastic page sleeves. Since the 2000s, digital scrapbooking has grown in popularity, using graphics software to arrange scanned photographs and digital elements that can be printed or shared online.
