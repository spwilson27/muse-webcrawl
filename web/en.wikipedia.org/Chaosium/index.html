Chaosium Inc. is an American publisher of tabletop role-playing games and board games founded in 1975 by Greg Stafford. It is one of the oldest surviving RPG companies and is best known for Call of Cthulhu (1981), the horror game based on the works of H. P. Lovecraft, and RuneQuest (1978), set in Stafford's fantasy world of Glorantha.

History

Chaosium began with the board game White Bear and Red Moon (1975), introducing Glorantha. RuneQuest, designed by Steve Perrin and others, introduced the percentile skill-based system that became the company's house engine, Basic Role-Playing (BRP). In 1981 Sandy Petersen's Call of Cthulhu added the influential Sanity mechanic and became one of the best-selling and most acclaimed role-playing games ever published, later winning numerous awards and entering the Academy of Adventure Gaming Arts & Design Hall of Fame. Other notable titles include Stormbringer (based on Michael Moorcock's Elric), King Arthur Pendragon (1985, by Stafford), Elfquest, and the board game Arkham Horror (1987, later redeveloped by Fantasy Flight Games).

Later years

The company went through financial difficulty and reorganization in the 1990s and 2000s; Stafford and Petersen returned to leadership in 2015 following a turbulent period, and Chaosium subsequently recovered with successful Kickstarter campaigns for the seventh edition of Call of Cthulhu and new editions of RuneQuest: Roleplaying in Glorantha and Pendragon. Chaosium also licenses its properties widely, including fiction lines and the Call of Cthulhu license held by numerous international publishers.
