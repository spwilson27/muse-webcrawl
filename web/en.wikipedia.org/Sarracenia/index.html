Sarracenia

A stand of trumpet pitchers, Sarracenia.

Sarracenia is a genus of carnivorous plants comprising eight to eleven species of North American pitcher plants, commonly called trumpet pitchers. They are native to wetlands of eastern and North America, from the Gulf Coast of the United States north to Canada. The genus belongs to the family Sarraceniaceae, together with Darlingtonia and Heliamphora.

Trapping mechanism

Each leaf is modified into a hollow, upright tube — the pitcher — topped by a hood (operculum) that keeps out excess rain. Insects are lured by nectar secreted around the rim and by colour patterns; downward-pointing hairs and slippery surfaces make escape difficult, and prey drowns in fluid at the base of the tube, where it is digested by enzymes and commensal organisms. Carnivory supplements the poor nitrogen supply of the waterlogged, acidic soils the plants inhabit.

Species and distribution

The best-known species is the yellow pitcherplant, Sarracenia flava, of the southeastern coastal plain. Sarracenia purpurea, the purple pitcherplant, has the widest range, extending into Canada, and holds rainwater rather than secreting much fluid. Sarracenia leucophylla is prized by growers for its white-veined pitchers. Many natural and horticultural hybrids exist.

Conservation

Most species are threatened by habitat loss as wetlands are drained for agriculture, forestry, and development, and by poaching for the plant trade. Several are protected under national law, and the genus is listed in CITES Appendix II (with S. rubra subspecies and S. oreophila in Appendix I).

Cultivation

Sarracenias are popular with carnivorous plant enthusiasts. They require full sun, permanently wet, nutrient-poor acidic soil, and water low in dissolved minerals; temperate species need a cold winter dormancy.
