Conservation area

A conservation area is a tract of land or a district that has been awarded protected status in order to conserve its special character. The term is used in several countries, most prominently in the United Kingdom, where it denotes an area of special architectural or historic interest, the character or appearance of which it is desirable to preserve or enhance.

United Kingdom

Conservation areas were introduced by the Civic Amenities Act 1967, and the concept is now governed principally by the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990. Local planning authorities designate the areas and have a duty to pay special attention to preserving or enhancing their character. Designation brings extra planning controls: demolition of most buildings requires consent, alterations are more tightly regulated, permitted development rights are reduced, and trees are protected. The first conservation areas were designated in 1967, including areas in York, Bath, Chester, and Chichester; there are now roughly 10,000 in England alone, ranging from historic town centres and Georgian squares to industrial villages, cemeteries, and stretches of canal.

Other uses

Elsewhere the term may refer to protected areas for nature conservation: for example, "conservation areas" in Tanzania and other African countries are wildlife management zones, and several Canadian and Australian jurisdictions use similar terms for protected natural land. In planning contexts more broadly, the conservation-area model of area-based heritage protection has been widely imitated.
