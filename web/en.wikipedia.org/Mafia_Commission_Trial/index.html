Mafia Commission Trial

The Mafia Commission Trial (formally United States v. Salerno, et al.) was a criminal trial in New York City in 1985–86 before the United States District Court for the Southern District of New York. The case charged the leaders of New York's "Five Families" under the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations (RICO) Act, alleging that they constituted and directed the Commission, the ruling body of the American Mafia.

Background

The prosecution, led by United States Attorney Rudolph Giuliani and prosecutors including Michael Chertoff, was made possible by years of FBI surveillance and wiretaps of Mafia social clubs and telephones. The indictment argued that the Commission itself was the criminal enterprise, responsible for coordinating murders, extortion of the concrete industry, labour racketeering, and loansharking.

Defendants and verdict

Eight defendants went to trial, including the bosses of the Gambino, Lucchese, Genovese, Colombo, and Bonanno families — among them Anthony "Fat Tony" Salerno, Anthony "Tony Ducks" Corallo, and Carmine "The Snake" Persico, who acted as his own lawyer. On 19 November 1986, the jury convicted all eight defendants on all counts. In January 1987, most were sentenced to 100 years in prison, the effective maximum sought by prosecutors.

Significance

The verdict was a landmark in the federal campaign against organized crime, demonstrating that RICO could be used against the Mafia's entire leadership structure rather than only individual offenders, and it crippled the Commission as a governing body.
