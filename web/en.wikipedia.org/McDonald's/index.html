McDonald's

McDonald's Corporation
Founded: 15 May 1940, San Bernardino, California (restaurant); 15 April 1955, Des Plaines, Illinois (corporation)
Founders: Richard and Maurice McDonald; Ray Kroc (corporation)
Headquarters: Chicago, Illinois
Locations: over 40,000 in more than 100 countries

McDonald's Corporation is an American multinational fast food restaurant chain and the world's largest restaurant chain by revenue. It serves tens of millions of customers daily in more than 100 countries, primarily selling hamburgers, cheeseburgers, french fries, chicken products, breakfast items, and soft drinks. The company's golden arches are among the most recognised logos in the world.

History

Brothers Richard and Maurice McDonald opened their first restaurant in San Bernardino in 1940 and in 1948 pioneered the "Speedee Service System," an assembly-line method of preparing a limited menu of hamburgers, fries, and shakes at low prices. Multimixer salesman Ray Kroc, impressed by the brothers' volume, became their franchising agent and opened his own restaurant in Des Plaines in 1955; in 1961 he bought out the brothers for $2.7 million and built McDonald's into a franchise empire, aided by real-estate executive Harry Sonneborn's model of owning the land beneath franchised restaurants.

Menu and operations

Signature products include the Big Mac (introduced 1968), Quarter Pounder, Chicken McNuggets (1983), Filet-O-Fish, Egg McMuffin, and the Happy Meal. Most restaurants are franchised; the corporation earns much of its income from franchise rents and royalties. Regional menus adapt to local tastes, from the McFalafel to the Teriyaki McBurger.

Criticism

McDonald's has faced criticism over nutrition and obesity, labour practices, environmental impact, and cultural globalisation; the term "McDonaldization" describes the spread of its efficiency-driven model. The company has responded with menu changes, sustainability pledges, and wage policies in various markets.
