EirGrid plc is the state-owned electric power transmission operator in Ireland. It is responsible for operating, developing and maintaining the national high-voltage electricity transmission grid in the Republic of Ireland, and, together with its Northern Ireland counterpart SONI (System Operator for Northern Ireland), it operates the all-island Single Electricity Market.

History

EirGrid was established under European Union directives requiring the separation of electricity generation and supply from transmission. It was incorporated in 2001 and on 1 July 2006 took over operation of the transmission system from ESB National Grid. In 2009 it acquired SONI, the transmission system operator for Northern Ireland, from Northern Ireland Electricity.

Interconnectors

EirGrid owns the East–West Interconnector, a 500 MW high-voltage direct current link between Ireland and Wales that entered service in 2012. It is also developing the Celtic Interconnector, a subsea link between Ireland and France planned to allow direct electricity trade with the continental European grid.

Role

As transmission system operator, EirGrid plans grid reinforcements needed to connect renewable generation — Ireland has high levels of wind power — and manages programmes such as "Delivering a Secure Sustainable Electricity System" (DS3) to accommodate very high instantaneous shares of non-synchronous generation.
