Arjunawiwāha ("The Marriage of Arjuna") is an Old Javanese kakawin poem composed by the court poet Mpu Kanwa around 1030, during the reign of King Airlangga of Kahuripan in East Java. It is the earliest surviving kakawin from the East Javanese period and adapts an episode from the Sanskrit Mahābhārata tradition (ultimately related to the Kirātārjunīya story).

Story

The demon king Niwatakawaca threatens the gods by demanding the hand of the heavenly nymph Suprabha. Unable to defeat him, since he can be killed only by a human, Indra sends nymphs to disturb the meditation of the Pandava hero Arjuna, who is performing austerities on Mount Indrakila. Arjuna resists the temptation, then meets the god Shiva disguised as a hunter (Kirata); after a quarrel and combat over a slain boar, Shiva reveals himself and grants Arjuna the irresistible Pasupata weapon. Arjuna then defeats Niwatakawaca — entering the demon's body through his open mouth to strike his vulnerable tongue — and is rewarded with seven days as king of heaven and marriage to the nymphs, before returning to earth.

Significance

The poem is widely read as an allegory of its patron: Arjuna's victory mirrors Airlangga's reunification of Java after the destruction of the Mataram kingdom. The Arjunawiwāha remained one of the most popular subjects in Javanese and Balinese art, appearing in temple reliefs, wayang performance and painting.
