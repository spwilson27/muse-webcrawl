Engelbert Kaempfer

Engelbert Kaempfer
Born: 16 September 1651, Lemgo, Germany
Died: 2 November 1716, Lemgo
Occupation: Naturalist, physician, explorer

Engelbert Kaempfer (16 September 1651 – 2 November 1716) was a German naturalist, physician, and explorer known for his tour of Russia, Persia, India, Southeast Asia, and Japan between 1683 and 1693. His books, published posthumously, were the main source of Western knowledge about Japan for more than a century, and he gave Europe some of the first systematic accounts of Japanese plants, including the ginkgo.

Travels

Kaempfer studied medicine and natural science and travelled via Poland and Sweden, joining a Swedish embassy to Persia in 1683. After two years at Isfahan he entered the service of the Dutch East India Company as a surgeon, reaching Batavia in 1689 and then Siam. In 1690 he was posted to the Dutch trading post at Dejima, Nagasaki, where he spent two years. From Dejima he twice made the court journey to Edo, observed the shogun Tokugawa Tsunayoshi, gathered information from Japanese informants, and collected specimens and artefacts despite strict controls on foreigners' movements.

Works

His Latin Amoenitatum Exoticarum (1712), published in his lifetime, described Persian, Indian, and Japanese natural history — including the first Western description of the ginkgo tree and of acupuncture. His manuscripts were bought by Sir Hans Sloane, and an English translation of his History of Japan appeared in 1727, remaining the standard reference on Japan until the nineteenth century.

Legacy

The plant genus Kaempferia (in the ginger family) is named for him. His careful, relatively sympathetic ethnography of Tokugawa Japan influenced later travellers and scholars such as Philipp Franz von Siebold.
