Columbia River

Columbia River
Length: 2,000 km (1,243 mi)
Source: Columbia Lake, British Columbia
Mouth: Pacific Ocean near Astoria, Oregon
Basin size: about 668,000 km²
Largest tributary: Snake River

The Columbia River is the largest river in the Pacific Northwest region of North America. It rises in the Rocky Mountains of British Columbia, Canada, flows northwest then south through the U.S. state of Washington, turns west to form most of the border between Washington and Oregon, and empties into the Pacific Ocean near Astoria.

Course and geography

The river drains an area of about 668,000 square kilometers spanning seven U.S. states and one Canadian province. Its largest tributary is the Snake River. On its way to the sea, the Columbia passes through the Columbia Plateau and cuts the Columbia River Gorge through the Cascade Range, a dramatic canyon noted for waterfalls and windsurfing.

History

Indigenous peoples have lived along the river for thousands of years, depending on its salmon runs. In 1792 Captain Robert Gray became the first non-Indigenous navigator known to enter the river, naming it after his ship, the Columbia Rediviva. The Lewis and Clark Expedition reached the Pacific by following the Columbia in 1805. The river later served as a key route for the fur trade and the Oregon Trail era.

Development

The Columbia and its tributaries host more than 400 dams, including Grand Coulee and Bonneville, which provide hydroelectric power, irrigation, navigation, and flood control, but have drastically reduced salmon populations. Treaties and ongoing litigation involving the United States, Canada, and Indigenous nations govern the river's management.
