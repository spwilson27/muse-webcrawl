Domus Aurea

Domus Aurea
LocationOppian Hill, Rome, Italy
BuiltAD 64–68
Commissioned byEmperor Nero
ArchitectsSeverus and Celer (per Tacitus)
Meaning"Golden House" (Latin)

The Domus Aurea (Latin for "Golden House") was a vast landscaped palace complex built by the Emperor Nero in the heart of ancient Rome after the Great Fire of AD 64 had cleared the area. At its center lay a gilded palace fronted by an artificial lake and surrounded by pavilions, gardens, pastures, and woods spread over the Palatine, Esquiline, Oppian, and Caelian hills — the complex included a colossal bronze statue of the emperor, the Colossus of Nero, some 30 metres high.

Architecture and art

The surviving Oppian pavilion is famous for its innovative concrete barrel vaults and its painted decoration by the artist Famulus (Fabullus): delicate "grotesque" ornaments — fanciful combinations of human, animal, and plant forms — that, when rediscovered in the Renaissance after the palace had been buried beneath the Baths of Trajan, directly inspired artists such as Raphael and gave the ornamental style its name (grottesche, from the cave-like buried rooms).

Aftermath

After Nero's death in AD 68, his successors sought to erase his memory and return the land to public use: Vespasian drained the lake to build the Flavian Amphitheatre — the Colosseum, named for the nearby Colossus — and Trajan's baths were built over the palace. Excavated since the 15th century, the surviving rooms remain fragile and are open to visitors on guided tours.
