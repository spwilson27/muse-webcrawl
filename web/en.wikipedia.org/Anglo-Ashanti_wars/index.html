Anglo-Ashanti wars

Date1824–1901
LocationGold Coast (modern Ghana)
BelligerentsBritish Empire vs. Ashanti Empire
ResultAshanti annexed to the Gold Coast colony (1902)

The Anglo-Ashanti wars were a series of five conflicts between the Ashanti Empire and the British Empire in the 19th century, fought over trade, territory and sovereignty on the Gold Coast of West Africa, in present-day Ghana.

The wars

The first war (1824–1831) began after the Ashanti clashed with British-backed coastal peoples; the British governor Sir Charles MacCarthy was killed at the Battle of Nsamankow in 1824. Further wars followed in 1863–64 and 1873–74, when Sir Garnet Wolseley's expedition burned the Ashanti capital Kumasi. The fourth war (1895–96) ended with the arrest and exile of Asantehene Prempeh I without a shot fired in the capital. The fifth conflict, the War of the Golden Stool (1900), was sparked by a British demand to sit upon the sacred Golden Stool and was led on the Ashanti side by the queen mother Yaa Asantewaa.

Outcome

Although the Ashanti were never decisively beaten in the field in 1900, their territory was annexed to the Gold Coast colony on 1 January 1902. The Ashanti monarchy was restored in 1935, and the region later became part of independent Ghana.
