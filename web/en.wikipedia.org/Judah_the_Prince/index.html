Judah the Prince (Hebrew: יהודה הנשיא, Yehudah ha-Nasi), also known simply as Rabbi or Rabbeinu HaKadosh ("our holy rabbi"), was a second-century rabbi and chief redactor and editor of the Mishnah. He lived in Galilee under Roman rule and served as nasi — patriarch — of the Jewish community and head of the Sanhedrin in the late second and early third centuries CE.

Life

Judah was the son of Simeon ben Gamaliel II and a descendant of Hillel the Elder. Tradition dates his birth to the day Rabbi Akiva died during the Bar Kokhba period. He studied under his father and other leading sages, and after succeeding to the patriarchate he moved the Sanhedrin to Beit She'arim and later to Sepphoris, where he remained for many years. Talmudic sources describe his wealth, his command of both Hebrew and Greek, and unusually close relations with a Roman ruler identified in tradition as "Antoninus", often taken to be the emperor Antoninus Pius or Caracalla.

The Mishnah

Judah's enduring achievement was the compilation of the Mishnah around 200 CE. Fearing that the oral tradition of Jewish law would be lost after the destruction of the Temple and the persecutions following the Bar Kokhba revolt, he collected, organized and edited the teachings of earlier sages into six orders covering agriculture, festivals, family law, civil and criminal law, holy things and ritual purity. The Mishnah became the foundation of rabbinic Judaism and the core text around which the Talmud later formed.

Legacy

Judah the Prince is remembered for combining political leadership with scholarship, and for insisting on Hebrew as the language of the Mishnah. He was buried at Beit She'arim, whose necropolis became a major Jewish burial site.
