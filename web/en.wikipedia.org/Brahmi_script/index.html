Brahmi script

Brahmi
Type: Abugida
Time period: c. 3rd century BCE – c. 5th century CE
Languages: Sanskrit, Prakrit, Tamil and others

The Brahmi script (Sanskrit: ब्राह्मी) is a writing system of ancient India that first appears as a fully developed system in the 3rd century BCE in the rock inscriptions of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka. Brahmi is an abugida which uses a system of diacritical marks to associate vowels with consonant symbols.

Brahmi is the common ancestor of most of the scripts used for writing South Asian and Southeast Asian languages, including Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia, Gujarati, Gurmukhi, Tibetan, and many others. Together with the Kharoshthi script, it was one of the two main scripts used in ancient India.

Origins

The origin of Brahmi is debated. Some scholars derive it from the Aramaic script, itself derived from Phoenician, while others argue for an indigenous development, possibly connected to the undeciphered Indus script. The script was deciphered in the 1830s by James Prinsep, who identified the language of the Ashokan edicts as a form of Prakrit.

Characteristics

Brahmi is written from left to right. Each consonant carries an inherent vowel, usually /a/, which can be modified by diacritics (matras) to indicate other vowels or suppressed with a virama. Consonant clusters are written with ligatures. The script eventually diversified regionally into the many Brahmic scripts used today.
