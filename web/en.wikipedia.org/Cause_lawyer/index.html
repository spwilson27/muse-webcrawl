Cause lawyer

A cause lawyer is a lawyer who uses litigation and other legal strategies to pursue social, political, or legal change beyond the interests of an individual client. The term is closely associated with the sociological study of lawyers who subordinate, in whole or in part, their professional practice to a moral or ideological mission.

Concept

The concept was developed in the sociology of the legal profession, notably in the work of Austin Sarat and Stuart Scheingold. Cause lawyers differ from conventional "hired gun" practitioners in that they select cases for their potential to advance a cause — such as civil rights, environmental protection, or, on the other side of the spectrum, conservative religious or free-market agendas — rather than primarily for fees or client service.

Practice settings

Cause lawyers work in public interest law firms, legal aid organizations, government agencies, nonprofit advocacy groups, and sometimes in private practice financed by foundations or movement organizations. Impact litigation — choosing plaintiffs and cases to create favorable precedent — is a hallmark strategy, exemplified by the NAACP Legal Defense Fund's campaign culminating in Brown v. Board of Education.

Criticism

Critics argue that cause lawyering can distort professional obligations, privilege the lawyer's agenda over the client's wishes, and shift policy-making to unelected lawyers and judges. Supporters respond that it gives voice to otherwise unrepresented interests.
