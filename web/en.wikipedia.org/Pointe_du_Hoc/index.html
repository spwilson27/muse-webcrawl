Pointe du Hoc is a promontory with a 110-foot (34 m) cliff overlooking the English Channel on the northwestern coast of Normandy in the Calvados department, France. During World War II it was the highest point between the American landing beaches of Utah and Omaha. The German army fortified the area with concrete casemates and gun pits, part of the Atlantic Wall, mounting six captured French 155 mm guns that could threaten both beaches.

The D-Day assault

On D-Day, 6 June 1944, the United States Army Ranger Assault Group, led by Lieutenant Colonel James Earl Rudder, attacked and captured Pointe du Hoc after scaling the cliffs with ropes, ladders and grappling hooks fired by rocket, under fire from the defenders above. The Rangers discovered that the casemates were empty of the main guns — the weapons had been removed and were later found, hidden in an orchard inland, and disabled with thermite grenades. Of the 225+ Rangers who landed, only about 90 remained able to fight after two days of holding the position against counterattacks until relieved on 8 June.

Memorial

France transferred the site to American control in 1960, and it is maintained by the American Battle Monuments Commission. The ground remains pockmarked with bomb craters and the ruined bunkers are preserved as they were left. A dagger-shaped granite monument commemorates the Rangers. On 6 June 1984, US President Ronald Reagan delivered one of his most famous speeches at Pointe du Hoc, honouring "the boys of Pointe du Hoc" on the 40th anniversary of D-Day.
