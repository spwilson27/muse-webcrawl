Lantingji Xu

The Lantingji Xu (traditional Chinese: 蘭亭集序; simplified Chinese: 兰亭集序; pinyin: Lántíngjí Xù; lit. "Preface to the Poems Collected from the Orchid Pavilion"), or Lanting Xu, is a Chinese calligraphy work generally considered to be written by the well-known calligrapher Wang Xizhi (303–361) of the Eastern Jin dynasty. Written in 353 CE, it is the preface to a collection of poems composed at a gathering of literati at the Orchid Pavilion near Shaoxing, Zhejiang, during the Spring Purification Festival. The work is widely regarded as the greatest masterpiece of Chinese running-script (xingshu) calligraphy.

The gathering

In the spring of 353, on the third day of the third lunar month, Wang Xizhi invited forty-one friends and family members — including Xie An and Sun Chuo — to the Orchid Pavilion (Lanting) for the Spring Purification Festival, a ritual of cleansing. The guests composed poems while seated beside a winding stream along which cups of wine were floated; a guest before whom a cup stopped had to drink if he could not produce a poem. Twenty-six of the guests succeeded in composing a total of thirty-seven poems, which were gathered into a collection, and Wang, somewhat in his cups, dashed off the preface in a celebrated burst of inspiration.

Content

The preface begins by celebrating the beauty of the setting, the pleasant company and the joys of life, but its tone shifts to a meditation on the transience of existence: the pleasures of today will become the memories — and sorrows — of tomorrow, and those who read the text in later ages will feel the same emotions. The text comprises 324 characters in 28 columns.

Calligraphy

The work is famed for the fluidity and variety of its brushwork, with twenty occurrences of the character 之 (zhī) each written differently. Wang reportedly tried to rewrite the preface the following day while sober but could not match the original — hence the saying that the piece was "written by the gods". Its characters show the grace and naturalness that made Wang Xizhi the "Sage of Calligraphy".

Transmission

The original is lost. According to a famous legend, the Tang emperor Taizong so coveted the work that, after obtaining it, he ordered it buried with him in his mausoleum, Zhao Mausoleum. What survive are traced copies — most famously the "Shenlong" version attributed to the Tang copyist Feng Chengsu, now in the Palace Museum, Beijing — and ink rubbings from stone engravings such as the Dingwu version.

Legacy

The Lantingji Xu has set the standard for running-script calligraphy for over sixteen centuries and remains compulsory study for calligraphers. The Orchid Pavilion gathering itself became a canonical motif in East Asian art and letters, imitated in later gatherings and paintings in China, Korea and Japan.
