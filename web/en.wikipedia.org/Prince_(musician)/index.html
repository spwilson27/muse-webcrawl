Prince Rogers Nelson (June 7, 1958 – April 21, 2016) was an American singer, songwriter, musician, and actor. Regarded as one of the most influential musicians of his generation, he was known for his flamboyant stage presence, wide vocal range, and virtuosity on multiple instruments, as well as for pioneering the Minneapolis sound. He sold more than 100 million records worldwide and won seven Grammy Awards and an Academy Award.

Career

Prince signed with Warner Bros. Records at 19 and released his debut album, For You, in 1978. His breakthrough came with 1999 (1982) and the film and album Purple Rain (1984), recorded with his band the Revolution, which spent 24 consecutive weeks atop the Billboard 200 and produced the hits "When Doves Cry" and "Let's Go Crazy". Subsequent acclaimed albums include Parade (1986), Sign o' the Times (1987), and Diamonds and Pearls (1991).

The symbol and later years

In 1993, amid a contractual dispute with Warner Bros., Prince changed his stage name to an unpronounceable symbol combining the male and female glyphs, and he was widely referred to as "the Artist Formerly Known as Prince". He resumed using the name Prince in 2000 after the contract expired. He continued recording prolifically, releasing albums including Musicology (2004), and was celebrated for his live performances, notably his Super Bowl XLI halftime show in 2007. He also wrote hit songs for other artists, including "Nothing Compares 2 U" for Sinéad O'Connor and "Manic Monday" for the Bangles.

Death and legacy

Prince died on April 21, 2016, at his Paisley Park estate in Minnesota from an accidental overdose of fentanyl. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2004, and his estate has since overseen reissues and releases from his extensive unreleased archive, known as "the Vault".
