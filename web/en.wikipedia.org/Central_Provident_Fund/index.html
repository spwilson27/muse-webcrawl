Central Provident Fund

Country: Singapore
Established: 1 July 1955
Administered by: CPF Board, Ministry of Manpower

The Central Provident Fund (CPF) is a mandatory social security savings scheme funded by contributions from employers and employees in Singapore. Established by the British colonial government in 1955, it has grown from a simple retirement savings plan into a comprehensive system covering retirement, housing, healthcare, and education.

Structure

Members' savings are held in three accounts: the Ordinary Account, usable for housing and education; the Special Account, for retirement; and the MediSave Account, for medical expenses. Contribution rates vary by age, with total contributions for younger workers reaching 37% of wages (20% from the employee and 17% from the employer, subject to a salary ceiling). At age 55, savings are transferred to a Retirement Account used to fund monthly payouts, including under the CPF LIFE annuity scheme.

Role in housing

The ability to use CPF savings to buy Housing and Development Board (HDB) flats has been central to Singapore's high rate of home ownership. The scheme is administered by the Central Provident Fund Board, a statutory board under the Ministry of Manpower.
