Emacs

Emacs is a family of text editors that are characterised by their extensibility. The most popular variants are GNU Emacs, created by Richard Stallman, and XEmacs. The original Emacs was developed at MIT in the 1970s by Stallman, Guy Steele and Dave Moon.

GNU Emacs, first released in 1985, is written largely in Emacs Lisp, a dialect of Lisp, and users customise and extend it by writing Lisp code. It is far more than a text editor: it includes a mail reader, news reader, debugger interface, calendar and games, and is often described, half-jokingly, as "a great operating system, lacking only a decent editor".

Emacs uses distinctive key bindings based on control and meta (alt) keys, such as C-x C-s to save and C-x C-c to quit. It has been a central tool in free software culture and in the long-running "editor war" with vi.
