Lemon

Scientific nameCitrus × limon
FamilyRutaceae
OriginHybrid of bitter orange and citron, South Asia

The lemon (Citrus × limon) is a species of small evergreen tree in the flowering plant family Rutaceae, and the name of its yellow, sour fruit. Genetic studies show the lemon is a hybrid between bitter orange (itself a pomelo–mandarin hybrid) and citron. Native to South Asia, primarily Northeast India, lemons spread through the Middle East to Europe, where they were cultivated in Italy by the fifteenth century, and reached the Americas with Columbus in 1493.

Description

The tree grows 3–6 metres tall with thorny branches, glossy elliptical leaves, and fragrant white-to-pink flowers. The fruit is a hesperidium with a thick, bright yellow rind rich in aromatic oils; the pale, segmented flesh is highly acidic, containing about 5–6 percent citric acid, which gives lemon juice a pH of around 2.2.

Uses

Lemons are used worldwide for juice, zest and slices in cooking, baking, drinks (such as lemonade), and preserves. Lemon juice is a standard remedy flavouring and a source of vitamin C — historically crucial in preventing scurvy, leading British sailors to be nicknamed "limeys" after the citrus juice ration. Lemon oil flavours foods and fragrances, and the juice is used in cleaning products. In 2022 world production was around 22 million tonnes, led by India, Mexico and China; leading cultivars include 'Eureka', 'Lisbon' and 'Meyer'.
