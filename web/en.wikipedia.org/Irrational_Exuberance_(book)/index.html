Irrational Exuberance

AuthorRobert J. Shiller
Published2000 (1st ed.); 2005 (2nd); 2015 (3rd)
PublisherPrinceton University Press
SubjectAsset price bubbles, behavioural economics
Key measureCAPE (cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio)

Irrational Exuberance is a book by Yale economist Robert J. Shiller examining how psychological and cultural factors drive speculative bubbles in asset markets. Its title adapts Federal Reserve chairman Alan Greenspan's famous December 1996 question about how to know when "irrational exuberance" had unduly escalated asset values.

Contents

Shiller argues that market prices — for stocks and later for houses — cannot be explained by fundamentals alone. He presents long-run data on stock prices, earnings and dividends, and popularizes the cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE), which at the 2000 market peak reached unprecedented levels. The book describes twelve "precipitating factors" of the 1990s boom, from the internet's arrival to the decline of inflation, and amplifying mechanisms such as feedback loops, media coverage and "new era" thinking.

Editions and timing

The first edition appeared in March 2000, the very month the dot-com bubble peaked, and is often cited as having called the market top. The second edition (2005) added an analysis of the developing housing bubble; the third edition (2015) revisited both stock and bond markets and warned again about elevated valuations. Shiller shared the 2013 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for work on asset prices.

Reception

The book became a bestseller and a landmark of behavioural finance, credited with showing that markets are not always efficient and that narrative and feedback drive booms and busts. Critics noted that valuation measures alone cannot time market turning points.
