Ice skating

Equipment: Ice skates
Disciplines: Figure, speed, short track, synchronised, tour skating
Governing body: International Skating Union

Ice skating is the self-propulsion and gliding of a person across an ice surface using metal-bladed ice skates. People skate for transport, recreation, exercise, and sport, on naturally frozen lakes and canals as well as artificial rinks.

History

Bone skates were used in Scandinavia and the Netherlands thousands of years ago for winter travel; iron blades appeared in the medieval Netherlands, where skating on canals became a popular pastime and sport. Edging and steel blades made manoeuvring possible, and clubs in Edinburgh and London codified skating in the 18th and 19th centuries. Jackson Haines introduced expressive, dance-like figure skating in the 1860s.

Disciplines

Figure skating, an Olympic sport since 1908, includes singles, pairs, ice dance, and synchronised skating, judged on jumps, spins, and artistry. Speed skating races over 400-metre ovals, and short track's pack-style racing, are Olympic staples; the Netherlands' Elfstedentocht tour tradition is famous. Skating underlies ice hockey and bandy as well.

Physics and safety

Skates glide because the blade melts a microscopically thin water film under pressure and friction, drastically reducing resistance. Risks include falls on hard ice and breaking through natural ice.
