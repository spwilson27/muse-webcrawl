Sigismund I the Old
Born: 1 January 1467, Kozienice, Poland
Died: 1 April 1548 (aged 81), Kraków, Poland
Reign: 8 December 1506 – 1 April 1548
Spouse: Barbara Zápolya; Bona Sforza
Dynasty: Jagiellonian
Successor: Sigismund II Augustus

Sigismund I the Old (Polish: Zygmunt I Stary; Lithuanian: Žygimantas II Senasis; 1 January 1467 – 1 April 1548) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1506 until his death in 1548. A member of the Jagiellonian dynasty, he was the son of King Casimir IV and Elizabeth of Austria, and succeeded his brother Alexander I.

Reign

Before his accession, Sigismund governed Silesian duchies (Głogów and Opava) as duke, where he gained a reputation as an able administrator. As king, he strengthened royal finances, reformed the coinage, and maintained a standing defence tax to support an army against Tatar raids along the south-eastern border.

Foreign policy

Sigismund fought wars with Muscovy (1507–1508, 1512–1522 and 1534–1537) and with the Teutonic Order (1519–1521). The latter conflict ended with the Treaty of Kraków (1525), under which Grand Master Albert of Prussia secularised the Teutonic state, converted to Lutheranism, and paid homage to Sigismund in Kraków, creating the Duchy of Prussia as a Polish fief. The king also dealt diplomatically with the Habsburgs at the First Congress of Vienna (1515) and confronted Ottoman pressure through wars involving his nephew John Zápolya in Hungary.

Renaissance court

His second marriage, to the Italian princess Bona Sforza in 1518, brought Italian Renaissance culture to Kraków. Sigismund rebuilt Wawel Castle in Renaissance style and commissioned the Sigismund Chapel, a masterpiece of Renaissance architecture, as his funerary chapel. The great Sigismund Bell, hung in Wawel Cathedral in 1521, still bears his name. He was succeeded by his son, Sigismund II Augustus, the last Jagiellonian king of Poland.
