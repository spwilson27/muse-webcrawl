Outsider art

Outsider art is art made by self-taught or supposedly naïve artists with little or no contact with the conventions of the art world. The English term was coined by art critic Roger Cardinal in 1972 as an equivalent of the French art brut ("raw art"), a term originated by artist Jean Dubuffet for art created outside the boundaries of official culture, particularly by psychiatric hospital patients, prisoners, and other marginalized creators.

History

Interest in the art of institutionalized patients grew in the early twentieth century; psychiatrist Hans Prinzhorn's collection at Heidelberg and his 1922 book Bildnerei der Geisteskranken influenced modern artists. Dubuffet assembled the Collection de l'Art Brut, now housed in Lausanne, and championed work he considered authentic and uncorrupted by cultural training.

Artists and reception

Noted outsider artists include Adolf Wölfli, Henry Darger, Martín Ramírez, Bill Traylor, and Nek Chand, creator of the Rock Garden in Chandigarh. Major venues include the Collection de l'Art Brut in Lausanne, the American Folk Art Museum in New York, and London's Museum of Everything. The field remains debated: critics question whether the label romanticizes mental illness or marginality, and whether artists cease to be "outsiders" once their work enters the market.
