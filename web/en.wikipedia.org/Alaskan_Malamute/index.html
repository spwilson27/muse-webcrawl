Alaskan Malamute

The Alaskan Malamute is a large breed of dog originally bred in Alaska for strength and endurance in hauling heavy freight as a sled dog. It is one of the oldest Arctic sled dog breeds, named after the Mahlemiut, an Inuit group of Alaska's Kotzebue Sound region. The Malamute is the official state dog of Alaska.

Characteristics

Malamutes are powerful, heavily boned dogs with a broad head, almond-shaped brown eyes and a thick double coat suited to severe cold. Males typically stand about 63.5 cm (25 in) at the shoulder and weigh around 39 kg (85 lb). Coat colours range from light grey through black, sable, and shades of red, usually with white markings and a distinctive cap-like marking on the face. Unlike the smaller, faster Siberian Husky, the Malamute was bred to pull heavy loads at a steady pace rather than race.

History

The breed was developed by the Mahlemiut people for hauling freight, hunting seals and distracting polar bears. Malamutes served in both World Wars and on Admiral Byrd's Antarctic expeditions. The breed was recognised by the American Kennel Club in 1935.

Temperament and care

Malamutes are loyal, playful and friendly with people, though they have a strong prey drive and considerable independence. They require substantial exercise, shed heavily, and are prone to "woo-woo" vocalisations rather than barking.
