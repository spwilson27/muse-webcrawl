Flag of Canada

The flag of Canada, commonly known as the Maple Leaf (French: l'Unifolié), is a red flag with a white square at its centre bearing a stylised red maple leaf with 11 points. Adopted in 1965 to replace the Union Jack and the Canadian Red Ensign, it was the first flag to be specified by statute as the national flag of Canada. Its design, by George F. G. Stanley and John Matheson, emerged from the Great Canadian Flag Debate of 1964 under Prime Minister Lester B. Pearson.

Design and symbolism

The flag's proportions are two by one. The white centre square is the width of the flag, flanked by red vertical bars. The maple leaf had been a symbol of Canada since the nineteenth century and appeared on the badges of Canadian soldiers in both world wars; red and white were proclaimed Canada's national colours by King George V in 1921. The leaf's 11 points have no specific significance — the design was chosen partly because it remained distinctive in wind-tunnel tests at small sizes.

Adoption

Canada's lack of a distinctive national flag became a political issue after the Second World War. In 1964 Prime Minister Lester Pearson committed to adopting a new flag before the Canadian Centennial in 1967. After months of heated parliamentary debate and thousands of public submissions, the Stanley–Matheson design was selected; it was proclaimed by Queen Elizabeth II on 28 January 1965 and first raised on Parliament Hill on 15 February 1965 — a date now celebrated as National Flag of Canada Day.
