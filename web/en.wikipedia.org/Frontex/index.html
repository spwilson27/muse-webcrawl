Frontex, legally the European Border and Coast Guard Agency, is an agency of the European Union headquartered in Warsaw, Poland. In coordination with the border and coast guards of member states, it exercises border control of the European Schengen Area, a task within the area of freedom, security and justice domain. Frontex was established in 2004 as the European Agency for the Management of Operational Cooperation at the External Borders and was primarily responsible for coordinating border control efforts.

In response to the 2015 European migrant crisis, the European Commission proposed on 15 December 2015 to extend Frontex's mandates and to transform it into a full-fledged European Border and Coast Guard Agency. On 18 December, the European Council roundly supported the proposal, and after a vote in the European Parliament, the European Border and Coast Guard was officially launched on 6 October 2016 at the Bulgarian external border with Turkey.

History

Frontex began operations in 2005 from its Warsaw headquarters. Its role expanded after the 2015 migrant crisis. The 2016 regulation created the European Border and Coast Guard, combining Frontex with national authorities. A further regulation in 2019 provided for a standing corps of 10,000 border guards to be built up by 2027, the first uniformed EU service, and gave the agency a budget of around €5.6 billion for the 2021–2027 period.

Tasks

The agency coordinates joint operations at the EU's external land, sea and air borders, carries out risk analyses, organises joint return operations of irregular migrants, trains national border guards, and supports search and rescue in the context of border surveillance. It operates the European Border Surveillance System (EUROSUR) and contributes to the Entry/Exit System.

Criticism

Frontex has faced criticism from human rights organisations and journalists over alleged involvement in, or failure to report, pushbacks of migrants in the Aegean Sea and elsewhere. Investigations by the EU anti-fraud office OLAF and the European Parliament led to the resignation of Executive Director Fabrice Leggeri in April 2022. The agency states that it is bound by fundamental rights obligations and maintains a Fundamental Rights Officer and monitors.
