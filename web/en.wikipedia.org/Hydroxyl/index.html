Hydroxyl

Hydroxyl group
Functional group –OH: an oxygen atom covalently bonded to a hydrogen atom. As a free radical (•OH) it is among the most reactive species in chemistry.

In chemistry, a hydroxyl group is a functional group with the chemical formula –OH and composed of one oxygen atom covalently bonded to one hydrogen atom. It is a defining feature of alcohols and phenols and appears in sugars, carboxylic acids and many other organic compounds. In the hydroxyl radical, •OH, the group exists as a neutral, extremely reactive free radical.

Hydroxyl groups in compounds

Compounds bearing hydroxyl groups are hydrophilic: the polar O–H bond hydrogen-bonds with water, increasing solubility. Sugars such as glucose carry several hydroxyl groups, and polysaccharides derived from them — including hyaluronic acid — owe much of their water-binding behaviour to these groups. Deprotonation of a hydroxyl group in water yields the hydroxide ion, OH−, the basis of alkalinity.

The hydroxyl radical

The hydroxyl radical is the most important oxidant in the troposphere, where it is sometimes called the "detergent of the atmosphere" because it removes methane and many pollutants. It forms when excited oxygen atoms react with water vapour. In biology, hydroxyl radicals arise from ionising radiation and from reactions of hydrogen peroxide with metal ions (the Fenton reaction); they damage DNA, proteins and lipids indiscriminately and contribute to oxidative stress.

Astronomy

Hydroxyl is also observed in interstellar space: the 18 cm OH lines were among the first molecular radio lines detected (1963) and power natural masers in star-forming regions, giving the radical a role in observational astrophysics.
