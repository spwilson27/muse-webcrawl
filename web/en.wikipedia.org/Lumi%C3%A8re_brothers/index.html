Lumière brothers

Auguste Lumière (1862–1954)
Louis Lumière (1864–1948)
Nationality: French
Known for: Cinématographe; first motion-picture screenings

The Lumière brothers, Auguste Marie Louis Nicolas Lumière and Louis Jean Lumière, were French manufacturers of photography equipment, best known for their Cinématographe motion picture system and the short films they produced between 1895 and 1905.

The Cinématographe

Patented in 1895, the Cinématographe was a combined camera, printer and projector that was lighter and more practical than Edison's Kinetograph. Their first public screening, on 28 December 1895 at the Salon Indien du Grand Café in Paris, is often regarded as the birth of cinema.

Films

Early Lumière films, each about 50 seconds long, included Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory (1895), L'Arroseur arrosé (1895) and The Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat (1896). Operators sent around the world shot more than a thousand films and popularised the new medium.

Later work

The brothers subsequently turned to colour photography, patenting the Autochrome Lumière process in 1903, which became the first commercially successful colour photographic process.
