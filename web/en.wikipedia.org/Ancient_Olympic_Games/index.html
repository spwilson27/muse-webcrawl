Ancient Olympic Games

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ancient Olympic Games (Greek: Ὀλυμπιακοὶ ἀγῶνες) were a series of athletic competitions among representatives of Greek city-states, held at the sanctuary of Olympia in honour of Zeus. Traditionally dated from 776 BC, when the first recorded victor, Koroibos of Elis, won the stadion footrace, they were held every four years — a cycle known as an Olympiad — for nearly twelve centuries, until the late 4th century AD.

History

For the first 13 festivals the only event was the stadion, a sprint of about 192 metres. Over time the programme expanded: the diaulos (double-length sprint), the dolichos (long-distance race), wrestling, boxing, the pankration, the pentathlon, and equestrian events including chariot racing. The games were one of the four Panhellenic Games, alongside those at Delphi, Nemea and Isthmia. During the festival a sacred truce (ekecheiria) allowed athletes and spectators to travel safely. Victors received a wreath of olive leaves cut from the sacred tree behind the Temple of Zeus, and enduring fame at home. The games declined under Roman rule and were suppressed in the era of the Christian emperor Theodosius I, traditionally in AD 393.

The site

The sanctuary (the Altis) contained the great Temple of Zeus, housing Phidias's chryselephantine statue of Zeus — one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World — as well as the Heraion, the Philippeion, treasuries of various cities, and the stadium, which held about 45,000 spectators. Athletes trained at the gymnasium and palaestra, and swore oaths before the statue of Zeus Horkios.

Rules and participants

Competitors had to be free-born Greek males; married women were barred from attending on pain of death, though the priestess of Demeter Chamyne was present, and unmarried girls may have watched. Athletes competed nude, anointed with oil. Separate games for women, the Heraean Games in honour of Hera, were also held at Olympia.

Legacy

The ancient games inspired Pierre de Coubertin's revival of the modern Olympic Games, first held at Athens in 1896. The four-year Olympiad became a standard unit of Greek chronology used by historians to date events.
