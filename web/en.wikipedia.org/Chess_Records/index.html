Chess Records

Chess Records was an American record company established in Chicago in 1950 by Polish immigrant brothers Leonard and Phil Chess. Specializing in blues, rhythm and blues, and early rock and roll, Chess became one of the most important independent labels of the twentieth century, recording artists such as Muddy Waters, Howlin' Wolf, Chuck Berry, Bo Diddley, Etta James, and Little Walter.

History

The Chess brothers, who had run nightclubs and the earlier Aristocrat Records label, founded Chess Records in 1950. Their studio at 2120 South Michigan Avenue became legendary; the Rolling Stones later named an instrumental track "2120 South Michigan Avenue" after recording there in 1964. Bassist and songwriter Willie Dixon served as a central talent scout, producer, and house songwriter for the label.

Artists and sound

Chess defined the electric Chicago blues sound. Muddy Waters' amplified band recordings of the early 1950s laid groundwork for rock and roll, while Chuck Berry fused blues and country into a new idiom beginning with "Maybellene" (1955). Etta James' "At Last" (1960) became an enduring standard. The label's subsidiaries included Checker, Argo (later Cadet), and Cadet Concept.

Decline and legacy

The Chess brothers sold the company to General Recorded Tape (GRT) in 1969; Leonard Chess died the same year. The label ceased operating in the mid-1970s. Its catalog is now controlled by Universal Music Group, and the 2120 South Michigan Avenue building houses Willie Dixon's Blues Heaven Foundation.
