The University of Calgary (U of C) is a public research university in Calgary, Alberta, Canada. It began in 1945 as the Calgary branch of the University of Alberta and was granted full autonomy in 1966. It is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research universities.

Academics

The university offers more than 250 programs through 14 faculties to roughly 35,000 students. Its strengths include energy and environmental research, the Cumming School of Medicine, the Schulich School of Engineering, the Haskayne School of Business, and the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, established in 2005. The university's "Eyes High" strategy aimed to place it among Canada's top five research universities. A satellite campus in Doha, Qatar, offers nursing programs.

Campus

The main campus covers about 200 hectares in northwest Calgary, adjacent to the Bow River. Notable facilities include the Taylor Family Digital Library, the Olympic Oval — a legacy of the 1988 Winter Olympics and one of the fastest speed-skating ovals in the world — and the Taylor Institute for Teaching and Learning.

Athletics

Varsity teams compete as the Dinos. The football Dinos have won multiple Vanier Cups, and the university's Olympic Oval has hosted world championship speed skating and produced many Olympians.
