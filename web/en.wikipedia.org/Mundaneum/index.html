Mundaneum

The Mundaneum was an institution created in 1910 in Brussels, Belgium, by lawyer Paul Otlet and Nobel Peace Prize laureate Henri La Fontaine. Its goal was to gather together all the world's knowledge and classify it according to the Universal Decimal Classification system developed by Otlet and La Fontaine. It has been called an early analogue precursor of the Internet and of modern search engines.

At its height, the Mundaneum's Universal Bibliographic Repertory held more than 12 million index cards cataloguing books and documents, together with vast collections of posters, photographs, postcards and newspapers. Users could submit queries by mail and receive answers copied from the card index — a manual search service decades before the web.

History

The institution occupied part of the Cinquantenaire Palace in Brussels until 1934, when the Belgian government withdrew support. During the Second World War the collection was damaged and partly displaced by a Nazi exhibition. After decades of storage, the surviving archives were moved to Mons, where a museum and archive centre opened in 1998 in a former department store. The Mundaneum archive was later added to UNESCO's Memory of the World programme.
