Peroxidase

Peroxidases are a large family of enzymes that catalyse oxidation–reduction reactions in which peroxides, typically hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), serve as the electron acceptor and are reduced to water. The general reaction is: donor + H2O2 → oxidised donor + 2 H2O. Most peroxidases contain a heme cofactor; others use cysteine or selenocysteine residues at the active site.

Occurrence and function

Peroxidases occur throughout nature — in plants, animals, fungi and bacteria. Important examples include glutathione peroxidase, which protects cells against oxidative damage; thyroid peroxidase, which iodinates tyrosine residues in thyroid hormone synthesis; myeloperoxidase, used by neutrophils to generate microbicidal hypochlorous acid; and lignin peroxidase, secreted by white-rot fungi to degrade lignin. In plants, peroxidases participate in cell-wall cross-linking, lignification, and defence against pathogens.

Applications

Horseradish peroxidase (HRP) is widely used in molecular biology as a reporter enzyme because it is small, stable and cheap: it is conjugated to antibodies in ELISA and Western blotting, where it catalyses a coloured, fluorescent or chemiluminescent product from substrates such as TMB, DAB or luminol. Peroxidases are also used industrially for decolourising dyes and treating phenolic waste.
