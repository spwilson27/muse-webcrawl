Libido


Libido is a person's overall sexual drive or desire for sexual activity. The term was popularised by Sigmund Freud within psychoanalytic theory.

Libido (from Latin libīdō, "desire") is a person's overall sexual drive or desire for sexual activity. In psychoanalytic theory, the term, originally used by Sigmund Freud, refers to the psychic energy of the life instincts.

Psychoanalytic theory

Freud conceived of the libido as the energy of the id, governed by the pleasure principle, and broadened the concept beyond sexuality to a general life drive (Eros), later contrasted with the death drive (Thanatos). Carl Jung used the term more generally for psychic energy as a whole.

Factors

Sexual desire is influenced by biological factors such as sex hormones, psychological states, relationship factors, age, illness and medication. Persistently low or high libido can be a symptom of underlying medical or psychological conditions.
