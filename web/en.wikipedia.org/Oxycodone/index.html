Oxycodone

Oxycodone is a semi-synthetic opioid analgesic synthesised from thebaine, an alkaloid of the opium poppy. First developed in Germany in 1916, it is used to treat moderate to severe pain and is sold under brand names including OxyContin and in combination products such as Percocet (with paracetamol).

Pharmacology

Oxycodone acts mainly as an agonist at the μ-opioid receptor. It is metabolised in the liver, partly by CYP2D6 to the active metabolite oxymorphone. Common adverse effects include constipation, nausea, sedation, respiratory depression and dependence.

Regulation

Because of its high abuse potential, oxycodone is a Schedule II controlled substance in the United States and similarly restricted in most countries. It remains on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines as an alternative to morphine.
