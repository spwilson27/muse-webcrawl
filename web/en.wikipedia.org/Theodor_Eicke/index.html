Theodor Eicke

Theodor Eicke (17 October 1892 – 26 February 1943) was a German SS functionary and Nazi war criminal, a key figure in the development of the Nazi concentration camp system. As commandant of Dachau from 1933 and Inspector of Concentration Camps from 1934, he organised the camps into the system later administered by the SS.

Career

Eicke, a First World War veteran, joined the Nazi Party and SS in 1930. At Dachau he imposed the brutal disciplinary regime — codified in his camp regulations — that became the model for all later camps, and trained the guards who staffed them. He personally participated in the murder of Ernst Röhm during the Night of the Long Knives in 1934.

Totenkopf division and death

During the Second World War Eicke commanded the SS Division Totenkopf, notorious for massacres of prisoners and civilians on both fronts. He was killed in February 1943 when his reconnaissance aircraft was shot down near Orel during the Third Battle of Kharkov.
