Twelve-bar blues

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The twelve-bar blues (or blues changes) is one of the most prominent chord progressions in popular music. The blues progression has a distinctive form in lyrics, phrase, chord structure, and duration. In its basic form, it is predominantly based on the I, IV, and V chords of a key.

Structure

A basic twelve-bar progression in C, using dominant seventh chords, runs:

C7C7C7C7
F7F7C7C7
G7F7C7G7

The verses typically follow an AAB lyric pattern: a line is sung over the first four bars, repeated (often with slight variation) over the next four, and answered by a concluding line over the final four. A "quick change" variant moves to the IV chord in bar 2, and jazz musicians add turnarounds and substitutions such as the ii–V in the last bars.

History and influence

The form crystallised in African-American blues of the early 20th century and became the backbone of boogie-woogie, rhythm and blues, rock and roll (from Chuck Berry to countless others), and much jazz and country music. Mastery of the blues progression and its associated scales is considered a fundamental skill across popular-music styles.
