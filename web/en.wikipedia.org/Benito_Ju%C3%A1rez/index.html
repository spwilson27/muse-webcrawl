Benito Pablo Juárez García (21 March 1806 – 18 July 1872) was a Mexican politician and lawyer who served as the 26th president of Mexico from 1858 until his death in 1872. Of Zapotec origin from Oaxaca, he is regarded as Mexico's greatest and most popular leader.

Early life and La Reforma

Orphaned as a child, Juárez moved to Oaxaca City, studied law, and rose in liberal politics, becoming governor of Oaxaca. As Minister of Justice in 1855 he authored the Juárez Law abolishing special courts for clergy and military. The liberal programme known as La Reforma continued with the Ley Lerdo and the Constitution of 1857, provoking the Reform War (1858–1861) between liberals and conservatives, which Juárez's government won.

French intervention

When France invaded Mexico in 1862 and installed Maximilian of Habsburg as emperor, Juárez led the republican resistance from northern Mexico, refusing to surrender sovereignty. After the French withdrew under diplomatic and military pressure, Maximilian was captured and executed in 1867, and Juárez restored the republic — declaring that respect for the rights of others means peace ("El respeto al derecho ajeno es la paz").

Later years and legacy

Re-elected in 1867 and 1871, Juárez pursued education reform, infrastructure, and the curtailment of Church and military privileges, though his final re-election sparked revolts including Porfirio Díaz's Plan de la Noria. He died in office in 1872. Juárez is honoured as a national hero; his birthday, 21 March, is a Mexican public holiday.
