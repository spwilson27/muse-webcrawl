Gonzalo Daniel Sánchez de Lozada Sánchez Bustamante (born 1 July 1930), often known as Goni, is a Bolivian businessman and politician who served as president of Bolivia twice: from 1993 to 1997 and from 2002 to 2003. A leading figure of the Revolutionary Nationalist Movement, he was the principal architect of Bolivia's free-market "shock therapy" economic reforms.

Reforms

As planning minister in the 1980s, Sánchez de Lozada designed Decree 21060 (1985), which curbed hyperinflation through austerity and liberalisation. In his first presidency he implemented the "Plan de Todos": capitalisation (partial privatisation) of state enterprises, pension reform, decentralisation through the Law of Popular Participation, and bilingual education reform.

Second term and resignation

His second term was overwhelmed by the Bolivian gas conflict: plans to export natural gas through Chile triggered mass protests in 2003, and military repression of demonstrators left dozens dead in "Black October." On 17 October 2003 he resigned and left for the United States. The Bolivian government has pursued his extradition; in 2018 a U.S. civil jury found him liable for damages for the deaths of protestors' family members, though litigation continued in subsequent years.
