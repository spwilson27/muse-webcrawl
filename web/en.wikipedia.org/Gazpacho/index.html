Gazpacho

Gazpacho (Spanish: [ɡaθˈpatʃo]) is a cold soup of raw, blended vegetables originating from the southern Spanish region of Andalusia. It is widely eaten in Spain and Portugal, particularly during the hot summer months, and is most commonly made with tomato as its base.

History

Gazpacho has ancient roots in the Iberian Peninsula, descending from Roman and Moorish preparations of bread, olive oil, water, garlic and vinegar. The tomato and peppers that define the modern version arrived from the Americas in the 16th century and were only fully incorporated by the 19th century, when gazpacho evolved from a peasant dish of field workers into a celebrated item of Spanish cuisine.

Preparation

The traditional Andalusian gazpacho blends ripe tomatoes, cucumber, green pepper, onion, garlic and soaked stale bread with extra-virgin olive oil, sherry vinegar and salt, then chills the mixture. It is served cold, often garnished with finely diced vegetables and croutons. Related dishes include the breadless salmorejo of Córdoba, thickened with more bread, and the white ajoblanco made with almonds and garlic.

Variations

Modern variations use watermelon, strawberries, beets, avocado or green grapes in place of tomato, and the name has been extended to many chilled blended soups worldwide.
