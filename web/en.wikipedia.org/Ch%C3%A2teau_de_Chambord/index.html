Château de Chambord

LocationChambord, Loir-et-Cher, France
Construction1519–1547
Commissioned byKing Francis I of France
StyleFrench Renaissance

The Château de Chambord is the largest château in France's Loire Valley, built as a hunting lodge for King Francis I. With its distinctive silhouette of turrets, chimneys and a famous double-helix staircase, it is a masterpiece of French Renaissance architecture.

Architecture

The château's keep has four towers and a central staircase built as two interlocking spirals — visitors ascending and descending never meet — traditionally attributed to the influence of Leonardo da Vinci, who spent his final years at nearby Clos Lucé. The roof terrace is crowded with ornate chimneys, dormers and the royal emblems of Francis I, the salamander and the letter F.

History

Construction began in 1519 but Francis spent little time there, using it mainly to impress foreign ambassadors. Later monarchs visited only occasionally; in the 18th century it housed the exiled Polish king Stanisław Leszczyński. The surrounding walled park, about the size of inner Paris, is the largest enclosed forest park in Europe. The château and its domain are UNESCO World Heritage sites as part of the Loire Valley listing.
