Murder hole

A murder hole or murder-hole is a hole in the ceiling of a gateway or passageway in a fortification through which the defenders could fire, throw or pour harmful substances or objects down on attackers. Harmful substances that could be used include boiling water, hot sand, quicklime, tar, or boiling oil; the latter was expensive and comparatively rare. Other missiles included stones, arrows and crossbow bolts.

History and use

Murder holes were common features of medieval castles, particularly in gatehouses, where attackers who had penetrated the outer defences could be trapped in a confined passage between two portcullises and subjected to attack from above. Similar devices appear in fortifications from many cultures and periods, including ancient Roman works and later colonial forts.

The murder hole is often confused with the machicolation, an opening between corbels on the outside of a wall, and with arrow slits, which are vertical openings in walls. Some historians dispute the traditional account that boiling oil was routinely poured through murder holes, noting its cost and scarcity; boiling water and heated sand were more plausible alternatives.
