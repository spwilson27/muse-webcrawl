The Olt (Romanian: Oltul; Latin: Aluta or Alutus) is a river in Romania, a major left tributary of the Danube and, at 615 kilometres, the longest river flowing exclusively within Romanian territory.

Course

The Olt rises in the Hășmaș Mountains of the Eastern Carpathians in Harghita County, flows south through the Ciuc and Făgăraș depressions, then cuts through the Southern Carpathians in the dramatic Turnu Roșu gorge — for centuries a principal pass between Transylvania and Wallachia. It continues across the Wallachian plain, passing Sfântu Gheorghe, Făgăraș, Sibiu (nearby), Râmnicu Vâlcea, and Slatina, before joining the Danube near Turnu Măgurele.

Use

The river is heavily developed for hydroelectric power: a cascade of more than twenty dams and power stations was built along its middle and lower course during the communist era. The Olt gives its name to the historical region of Oltenia, which it bounds to the east.
