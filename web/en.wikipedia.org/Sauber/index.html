Sauber Motorsport AG is a Swiss motorsport engineering company and Formula One team founded by Peter Sauber in 1970. Based in Hinwil in the canton of Zürich, the team competed in Formula One under its own name from 1993 to 2005 and again from 2011 to 2018, and in various guises — BMW Sauber (2006–2009), Alfa Romeo Racing (2019–2023) and Stake F1 Team Kick Sauber (2024–2025) — using the Sauber facilities and licence. Audi acquired the team to become its works Formula One entry from 2026.

History

Peter Sauber began building sports cars in the 1970s, and Sauber became famous in endurance racing as Mercedes-Benz's partner: the Sauber-Mercedes C9 won the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1989, and the team won the World Sportscar Championship in 1989 and 1990 with drivers including Michael Schumacher, Karl Wendlinger and Heinz-Harald Frentzen in its junior programme.

Sauber entered Formula One in 1993 with Ilmor-built engines badged as Mercedes, scoring points on its debut. The team's best result in its independent years was a string of podium finishes, including Heinz-Harald Frentzen's third places and a fourth place in the 2001 Constructors' Championship. BMW bought the team in 2006; as BMW Sauber it won its only Grand Prix — the 2008 Canadian Grand Prix, a 1–2 finish led by Robert Kubica — before BMW withdrew in 2009 and sold the team back to Peter Sauber.

In the 2010s the team raced with Ferrari engines and modest budgets, surviving a financial rescue in 2016. From 2019 it ran as Alfa Romeo Racing, and after two seasons branded as Stake F1 Team Kick Sauber the team was taken over entirely by Audi, whose works programme continues from Hinwil.
