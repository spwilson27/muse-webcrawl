Off the Wall is the fifth studio album by the American singer Michael Jackson, released on August 10, 1979, by Epic Records. It was Jackson's first album for Epic and the first produced by Quincy Jones, whom he met while working on the film The Wiz (1978).

Background

Following his work with the Jacksons and his film debut, Jackson sought a breakthrough solo record that would establish him as an adult artist. Songwriters included Jackson himself, Rod Temperton, Stevie Wonder, and Paul McCartney. The album blends disco, funk, pop, and R&B balladry.

Singles and success

The album produced four top-10 singles in the United States, including the number-one hits "Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough" and "Rock with You", as well as "Off the Wall" and "She's Out of My Life". With these, Jackson became the first solo artist to have four singles from the same album reach the top 10 of the Billboard Hot 100. "Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough" won Jackson his first Grammy Award, for Best Male R&B Vocal Performance.

Legacy

Off the Wall has sold over 20 million copies worldwide and is widely regarded as a landmark of the disco era and the springboard for Jackson's later superstardom with Thriller (1982). It was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2008 and is frequently ranked among the greatest albums of all time.
