Benrath line

TypeIsogloss (linguistics)
Named forBenrath, a district of Düsseldorf
Distinctionmaken (north) vs. machen (south)

The Benrath line (German: Benrather Linie) is the linguistic border dividing Low German dialects to the north from High German dialects to the south in Germany. It is the most important isogloss of the High German consonant shift in the west, separating dialects that say maken (Low German) from those that say machen (High German, with shifted /k/ → /x/).

Course

The line is named after Benrath, now a district of Düsseldorf on the Rhine, where it crosses the river. From the Dutch border area it runs eastward near Aachen and Düsseldorf, crosses the Rhine, and continues through the uplands roughly past Kassel and Magdeburg toward the Oder river. It is not a single sharp boundary but a bundle of related isoglosses, and it marks the northern limit of the second consonant shift's full effect on the stop k.

Related isoglosses

Together with the Uerdingen line (the ik/ich isogloss) farther south in places, the Benrath line forms the traditional boundary between Low German and Central German within the West Germanic dialect continuum. See also High German consonant shift and Low German.
