Bretwalda

Bretwalda (also brytenwalda, "wide ruler") is an Old English term used in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle to describe a series of Anglo-Saxon kings who achieved overlordship over some or all of the other kingdoms of early medieval Britain.

The list

The Chronicle's entry for 827 names eight bretwaldas, echoing a list of seven overlords in Bede's Ecclesiastical History: Ælle of Sussex, Ceawlin of Wessex, Æthelberht of Kent, Rædwald of East Anglia, Edwin of Northumbria, Oswald of Northumbria, and Oswiu of Northumbria, with Egbert of Wessex added as the eighth.

Interpretation

Historians debate whether "bretwalda" was a formal title used in the kings' lifetimes or a chronicler's retrospective label. Later scholars sometimes described holders as exercising a "hegemony" rather than direct rule; the term fell out of use as the kingdoms were unified into the Kingdom of England in the 10th century.
