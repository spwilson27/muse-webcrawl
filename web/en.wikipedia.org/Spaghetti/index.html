Spaghetti is a long, thin, solid, cylindrical pasta. A staple of Italian cuisine, it is made of milled durum wheat and water, extruded and dried, and is typically cooked in boiling salted water and served with sauce. The name is the diminutive plural of Italian spago, "thin string" or "twine".

History

Long pasta of the spaghetti type is documented in Sicily by the 12th century — the geographer al-Idrisi recorded a pasta-making industry at Trabia in 1154 — and production spread through Naples and the rest of Italy. Industrial extrusion in the 19th and 20th centuries made spaghetti a global food, carried worldwide by Italian emigration.

Dishes

Classic preparations include spaghetti aglio e olio, cacio e pepe, carbonara, puttanesca, alle vongole (with clams), and spaghetti with tomato sauce; outside Italy, spaghetti bolognese and spaghetti with meatballs (an Italian-American creation) are common.

In culture

The BBC's famous 1957 April Fools' Day hoax reported a bumper "spaghetti harvest" from trees in Switzerland. The "spaghetti western" nickname for Italian-made Western films, and the computing term "spaghetti code", both derive from the pasta's tangled appearance.
