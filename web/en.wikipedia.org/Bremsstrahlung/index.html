Bremsstrahlung

Bremsstrahlung
Meaning"Braking radiation" (German)
Produced byDecelerating charged particles
SpectrumContinuous
ApplicationsX-ray tubes, astrophysics

Bremsstrahlung (German for "braking radiation") is electromagnetic radiation produced by the deceleration of a charged particle, typically an electron, when it is deflected by the electric field of an atomic nucleus or another charged particle. The lost kinetic energy is converted into a photon, and because many different amounts of energy can be lost, the radiation forms a continuous spectrum.


An electron brakes in a nuclear field and emits X-radiation.

X-ray production

In an X-ray tube, electrons striking a metal anode (often tungsten) are slowed by the nuclei, producing the continuous bremsstrahlung background of the tube's spectrum, upon which sharp characteristic lines from inner-shell transitions are superimposed. The maximum photon energy is set by the accelerating voltage, the Duane–Hunt law.

In astrophysics

Hot ionised gases — such as the intracluster medium of galaxy clusters — emit thermal bremsstrahlung as free electrons scatter off ions without being captured ("free–free emission"). This X-ray emission lets astronomers measure the temperature and density of the gas. Bremsstrahlung also limits energy confinement in fusion plasmas, since electrons radiate energy away as they are deflected by ions.

Theory

Classically, the radiation follows from the Larmor formula for an accelerating charge. Quantum-mechanical treatments by Bethe and Heitler give the cross-section for photon emission, including screening and Coulomb corrections; the radiated power scales with the square of the charge of the deflecting nucleus, favouring heavy targets in X-ray tubes.
