Nuri Killigil, also known as Nuri Pasha (1889–1949), was an Ottoman and Turkish general, a founder of the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic's army, and later an arms manufacturer. He was the half-brother of Enver Pasha, the Ottoman Minister of War.

Military career

Killigil served in the Italo-Turkish War in Libya and in the First World War. In 1918 he commanded the Army of Islam, a force sent to the Caucasus, which captured Baku from Bolshevik and Armenian forces in September 1918 — an event commemorated in Azerbaijan, where Killigil is regarded as a national hero.

Later life

After the war he founded a weapons factory in Istanbul producing pistols and munitions. He died, along with others, in a factory explosion in 1949; the disaster is sometimes attributed to sabotage, though this has not been proven.
