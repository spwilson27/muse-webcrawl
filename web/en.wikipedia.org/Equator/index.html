The equator is a circle of latitude that divides a spheroid, such as Earth, into the Northern and Southern hemispheres. On Earth, it is an imaginary line located at 0 degrees latitude, about 40,075 kilometres (24,901 mi) in circumference, halfway between the North and South poles. The term can also be used for any other celestial body's equatorial plane.

Near the equator, sunlight strikes the surface nearly vertically throughout the year, so equatorial regions have warm climates with little seasonal variation in temperature, though many have distinct wet and dry seasons. The Sun is directly overhead at noon on the equinoxes (around 20 March and 23 September). Because Earth bulges slightly at the equator — the equatorial diameter exceeds the polar diameter by about 43 km — points on the equator are farthest from the planet's centre, and gravity there is marginally weaker. The equator also has the fastest rotational speed on the surface, about 1,670 km/h, which is why several spaceports, such as the Guiana Space Centre in Kourou, are sited near it.

The equator on Earth

The line crosses the land or territorial waters of fourteen sovereign states, including São Tomé and Príncipe, Gabon, the Republic of the Congo, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda, Kenya, Somalia, Indonesia, Ecuador, Colombia, and Brazil. The country of Ecuador is named after it (Spanish for "equator"), and a monument near Quito — the Mitad del Mundo ("Middle of the World") — marks the line calculated by the French Geodesic Mission of 1736, though modern GPS measurements place the true equator about 240 metres to the north.

Equatorial climates

Lowlands near the equator generally experience a tropical rainforest climate with high temperatures and rainfall year-round. However, altitude moderates the climate in places such as Quito and Nairobi, and ocean currents can create surprisingly cool, dry conditions, as in the Galápagos Islands. Despite its name and location on the equator, Mount Kenya and other equatorial highlands bear glaciers.
