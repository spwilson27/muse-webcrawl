Ernst Ludwig Kirchner

Ernst Ludwig Kirchner
Born: May 6, 1880, Aschaffenburg, Germany
Died: June 15, 1938, Frauenkirch-Wildboden, Switzerland
Movement: Expressionism, Die Brücke

Ernst Ludwig Kirchner was a German expressionist painter and printmaker and one of the founders of the artists group Die Brücke ("The Bridge"), a key group leading to the foundation of Expressionism in 20th-century art. He is regarded as one of the most important German artists of the early 20th century.

Die Brücke and Berlin

Kirchner co-founded Die Brücke in Dresden in 1905 with Fritz Bleyl, Erich Heckel and Karl Schmidt-Rottluff. The group pursued raw emotion, bold colour and woodcut printmaking. After moving to Berlin in 1911, Kirchner painted his celebrated street scenes — including Berlin Street Scene (1913) and Potsdamer Platz (1914) — depicting the nervous energy of the modern metropolis.

Later life

Service in the First World War led to a breakdown, and Kirchner settled near Davos, Switzerland, painting Alpine landscapes. In 1937 the Nazi regime declared his work "degenerate" and removed more than 600 of his works from German museums. Distraught and in poor health, he took his own life in 1938. The Kirchner Museum in Davos preserves a major collection of his art.
