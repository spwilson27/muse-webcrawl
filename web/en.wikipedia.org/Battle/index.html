Battle

A battle is an occurrence of combat in warfare between opposing military units of any number or size. A war usually consists of multiple battles. In general, a battle is a military engagement in which opposing forces fight for control of terrain, to destroy enemy forces, or to achieve some other military objective, and which is well defined in duration, area and force commitment.

Characteristics

Battles are fought at the tactical level, below campaigns and operations in the hierarchy of war. They are usually named after a nearby geographic feature — a town, river or hill — and sometimes after a date. The outcome of a battle may be decisive, shaping the course of a war, or indecisive, with both sides disengaging. Historically, pitched battles were agreed-upon, formal encounters; modern warfare more often involves fluid engagements of combined arms.

Types

Battles are classified in various ways: land battles and naval battles; pitched battles fought on prepared ground; encounter battles arising from chance contact; battles of attrition, annihilation or breakthrough; and sieges, sometimes counted separately. Famous examples include Hastings (1066), Agincourt (1415), Waterloo (1815), Gettysburg (1863), the Somme (1916) and Stalingrad (1942–43).

Factors of success

Numbers, terrain, logistics, morale, technology, leadership and intelligence all influence outcomes. Theorists from Sun Tzu to Clausewitz have emphasized concentration of force, surprise, and the "fog of war" — the uncertainty inherent in combat.
