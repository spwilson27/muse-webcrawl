West Pomeranian Voivodeship occupies the north-western corner of Poland along the Baltic Sea.

West Pomeranian Voivodeship (Polish: województwo zachodniopomorskie) is a voivodeship (province) in northwestern Poland, situated on the Baltic Sea coast. Its capital and largest city is Szczecin. The province was created on 1 January 1999 in the Polish local government reforms, chiefly from the former Szczecin and Koszalin voivodeships.

Geography

The voivodeship borders Pomeranian Voivodeship to the east, Greater Poland and Lubusz voivodeships to the south, and the German states of Brandenburg and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern to the west. Its landscape includes the Szczecin Lagoon, the islands of Wolin and Usedom (shared with Germany), long Baltic beaches, and numerous lakes, including the largest in the region, Lake Dąbie and Lake Miedwie. The Oder River reaches the sea here, and Wolin National Park protects coastal cliffs and beech forest.

Cities and economy

Other major towns include Koszalin, Stargard, Kołobrzeg, and Świnoujście. The economy combines the ports of Szczecin and Świnoujście, shipbuilding and maritime industries, agriculture, chemicals, and Baltic coastal tourism centred on resorts such as Międzyzdroje and Kołobrzeg.
