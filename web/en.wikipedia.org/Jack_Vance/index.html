Jack Vance

Jack Vance
Born: John Holbrook Vance, 28 August 1916, San Francisco, California
Died: 26 May 2013, Oakland, California
Occupation: Novelist
Genre: Science fiction, fantasy, mystery

John Holbrook "Jack" Vance (August 28, 1916 – May 26, 2013) was an American mystery, fantasy and science fiction author, best known for the Dying Earth series and the Lyonesse trilogy. His work is celebrated for its elaborate, ironic prose style, exotic settings, and picaresque rogues, and it influenced generations of writers as well as the role-playing game Dungeons & Dragons, whose magic system derives from his fiction.

Life

Vance grew up in California, studied at the University of California, Berkeley, and worked in a variety of jobs including merchant seaman, surveyor, carpenter and potter. He served in the United States Merchant Marine during the Second World War. He travelled extensively, often living abroad while writing, and later settled in Oakland, California. In his later years he lost his sight but continued to write with the aid of software.

Work

His first published story appeared in 1945. Under his own name he wrote science fiction and fantasy including The Dragon Masters (1962), The Last Castle (1966), the Demon Princes series, the Cadwal Chronicles and the Tschai (Planet of Adventure) books. As Ellery Queen and under the pseudonym John Holbrook he also wrote mystery novels, winning an Edgar Award for The Man in the Cage (1960).

Awards and legacy

Vance won Hugo Awards for The Dragon Masters and The Last Castle, a Nebula Award for the latter, and in 1997 was named a Grand Master by the Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers of America. The Vance Integral Edition, a collected deluxe edition of his works, was published in the early 2000s.
