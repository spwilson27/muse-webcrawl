Immigration to Europe has a long history, but increased substantially in the later 20th century. Post-war labour recruitment, decolonisation, family reunification, freedom of movement within the European Union, and asylum seeking have all contributed to large foreign-born populations in many European countries.

History

After the Second World War, Western European states recruited guest workers from Southern Europe, Turkey, and North Africa. Decolonisation brought migrants from former colonies to Britain, France, the Netherlands, and Portugal. The end of the Cold War and EU enlargement added large East–West flows.

Contemporary patterns

In 2015, over a million people sought asylum in Europe, principally fleeing wars in Syria, Iraq, and Afghanistan, leading to the so-called European migrant crisis and changes in EU asylum policy. Immigrants and their descendants form significant minorities in countries including Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Spain, and Sweden.
