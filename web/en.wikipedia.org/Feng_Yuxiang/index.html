Feng Yuxiang (simplified Chinese: 冯玉祥; traditional Chinese: 馮玉祥; pinyin: Féng Yùxiáng; 6 November 1882 – 1 September 1948) was a prominent Chinese warlord and military leader in the Republic of China during the early 20th century. He is best known as the leader of the Guominjun (National People's Army) and for his shifting political allegiances, which earned him the nickname the "Betrayer General."

Early life

Feng was born in Chaohu, Anhui province, to a poor family. His father was a stonemason who had joined the army, and Feng himself enlisted as a soldier at the age of 14. He rose through the ranks by ability and discipline, gradually building a reputation as a strict, ascetic commander who forbade his troops gambling, opium, and prostitution.

The Christian General

Feng converted to Christianity (Methodism) in 1914 and became famous for baptizing his troops en masse, sometimes reportedly using a fire hose. Western missionaries praised his moral rigor, and he became known in the foreign press as the "Christian General." His forces were renowned for their discipline, rigorous training, singing of hymns, and austere lifestyle.

Warlord career

In 1924 Feng staged the Beijing Coup, overthrowing President Cao Kun and briefly seizing the capital. He invited Sun Yat-sen north to negotiate national reunification, though Sun died soon after arriving. Feng's Guominjun later fought in the Northern Expedition era and aligned at times with the Kuomintang and the Soviet Union. In 1930 he joined Yan Xishan in the Central Plains War against Chiang Kai-shek, a conflict that ended in defeat and the dissolution of his independent power. He later held largely ceremonial posts while continuing to criticize the government.

Death

In 1948 Feng traveled to the United States and publicly broke with Chiang Kai-shek's government, announcing his support for a coalition government. He died on 1 September 1948 in a fire aboard the Soviet liner SS Pobeda in the Black Sea while returning to China. The circumstances of the fire were never fully clarified, and his death has been the subject of lasting speculation.

Legacy

Feng remains one of the most colorful figures of the warlord era: simultaneously admired for his discipline, egalitarian habits, and patriotism, and criticized for his repeated changes of allegiance during China's turbulent republican decades.
