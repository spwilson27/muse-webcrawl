Daman and Diu is a coastal region of western India comprising two separate districts, Daman and Diu, on the shores of the Arabian Sea. Both were Portuguese colonies for over four centuries before their annexation by India in 1961. From 1987 to 2020 they formed a single union territory; on 26 January 2020 the territory was merged with Dadra and Nagar Haveli to form the new union territory of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, of which Daman is the capital.

Geography

Daman lies at the mouth of the Daman Ganga River on the Gujarat coast, divided by the river into Nani Daman and Moti Daman. Diu is an island off the southern coast of Gujarat's Kathiawar peninsula, connected to the mainland by bridges; the two districts are separated by roughly 650 kilometres of Gujarat coastline.

History

Portuguese forces took Daman in 1559 and Diu in 1535, building fortresses such as the Moti Daman fort and Diu Fort that survive today. Together with Goa they formed Portuguese India (Estado da Índia). In December 1961, Indian forces annexed the territories in Operation Vijay, ending 450 years of Portuguese rule; Portugal recognised Indian sovereignty only after the Carnation Revolution, in 1974. When Goa became a state in 1987, Daman and Diu remained a union territory until the 2020 merger.

Economy and culture

Fishing, tourism (notably Diu's beaches and the former Portuguese churches and forts), small-scale industry, and salt production support the local economy. A large Indo-Portuguese heritage persists in architecture, cuisine, and Catholic communities, while Gujarati is the predominant language of daily life.
