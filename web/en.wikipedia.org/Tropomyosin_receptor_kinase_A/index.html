Tropomyosin receptor kinase A

The high-affinity nerve growth factor receptor

Tropomyosin receptor kinase A (TrkA), also known as the high-affinity nerve growth factor receptor, is a protein that in humans is encoded by the NTRK1 gene. It is the cell-surface receptor for nerve growth factor (NGF) and the founding member of the Trk family of receptor tyrosine kinases, which also includes TrkB (the receptor for BDNF) and TrkC (the receptor for NT-3).

Function

Binding of NGF causes TrkA dimerisation and autophosphorylation of tyrosine residues in its intracellular kinase domain. This activates signalling pathways — including Ras/MAPK, PI3K/Akt, and PLC-γ — that promote the survival, differentiation, and growth of sympathetic and sensory neurons. TrkA is essential for the development of nociceptive (pain-sensing) neurons and for thermoregulation and sweating.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in NTRK1 cause congenital insensitivity to pain with anhidrosis (CIPA, also called hereditary sensory and autonomic neuropathy type IV), a rare disorder in which affected individuals cannot feel pain or temperature and cannot sweat. Conversely, chromosomal fusions that join NTRK1 to other genes occur in a small fraction of many cancers — including lung, colorectal, thyroid cancers and infantile fibrosarcoma — and drive tumour growth; the drugs larotrectinib and entrectinib are approved TRK inhibitors that target such NTRK fusion-positive tumours regardless of tissue of origin. Because NGF–TrkA signalling mediates chronic pain, anti-NGF antibodies such as tanezumab have been developed as analgesics.
