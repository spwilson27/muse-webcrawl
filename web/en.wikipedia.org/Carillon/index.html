Carillon

A carillon is a pitched percussion instrument played with a keyboard and consisting of at least 23 bells. It is usually housed in the bell tower of a church or municipal building. The player, a carillonneur, sits in a cabin beneath the bells and strikes a keyboard of wooden levers (batons) and pedals connected by wires to the bells' clappers.

History

The carillon originated in the Low Countries in the 16th century, where tower bells were combined with the mechanical drum used to signal the hours. Flanders, the Netherlands and northern France retain the densest concentration of instruments; notable modern carillons include those at the Netherlands Carillon in Arlington, Virginia, and the Peace Tower in Ottawa.

Repertoire

Carillon music ranges from Baroque transcriptions to contemporary works. The bells of a carillon are cast in bronze and tuned in just temperament historically and equal temperament today; the largest bell may weigh several tonnes.
