Differentiated services

Differentiated services or DiffServ is a computer networking architecture that specifies a mechanism for classifying and managing network traffic and providing quality of service (QoS) on modern IP networks. Defined principally in RFC 2475 and RFC 2474, it works on the principle of traffic classification at network edges and per-hop behaviours within the core.

Mechanism

Packets are marked with a Differentiated Services Code Point (DSCP) in the DS field of the IP header — replacing the older IPv4 Type of Service field. Routers apply per-hop behaviors (PHBs) according to the code point: Expedited Forwarding for low-latency traffic, Assured Forwarding classes for preferential treatment, and best effort by default.

Comparison with IntServ

Unlike the Integrated Services model, which reserves resources per flow, DiffServ scales by treating aggregates of traffic, making it practical across large provider networks. It is widely used in enterprise and carrier networks to prioritise voice and video traffic.
