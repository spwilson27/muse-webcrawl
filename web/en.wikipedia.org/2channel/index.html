2channel

2channel (Japanese: 2ちゃんねる, ni channeru), often abbreviated to 2ch, was a Japanese anonymous textboard founded on 30 May 1999 by Hiroyuki Nishimura. At its peak it was the largest online forum in Japan and one of the most influential websites in Japanese internet culture, receiving millions of posts per day.

Overview

Unlike imageboards, 2channel was a textboard: users posted plain text messages in threads organised across hundreds of topical boards, covering everything from news and politics to cooking, sport, and video games. Posting was anonymous by default, with the name field typically left as "Nanashi-san" (Mr. Nameless). The site's culture prized wit, speed, and in-group knowledge, and it generated a large vocabulary of slang, ASCII art, and copypasta.

Cultural impact

2channel was the origin of numerous Japanese internet phenomena, including the Densha Otoko ("Train Man") story, which was adapted into a novel, film, and television drama, and the elaborately maintained ASCII art characters such as Monā. The site's "VIP" board culture also fed into later communities. Hiroyuki's management style and disputes over the site's ownership led to a split in 2014, after which the original community largely migrated to a successor site, 5channel (5ch.net), while 2channel itself continued under new management under the name 2ch.sc.

Controversies

The site's anonymity and light moderation made it a focus of controversy in Japan over defamation, doxxing, and criminal threats posted by users, several of which led to arrests. Japanese courts repeatedly ordered the disclosure of poster information in defamation cases.
