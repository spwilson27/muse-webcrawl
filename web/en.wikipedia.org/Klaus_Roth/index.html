Klaus Friedrich Roth (1925–2015) was a German-born British mathematician who won the Fields Medal in 1958 for his theorem on the Diophantine approximation of algebraic numbers — the first Briton to receive the medal. His work transformed analytic number theory, including fundamental results on arithmetic progressions and the large sieve.

Life and career

Roth was born in Breslau to Jewish parents and fled Nazi Germany with his family to Britain in 1933. He read mathematics at Peterhouse, Cambridge, did war work as a schoolteacher and briefly as an RAF trainee, and took his PhD at University College London under Theodor Estermann. He spent most of his career at UCL and Imperial College London, retiring to Oxford.

Mathematics

Roth’s theorem (1955) states that every irrational algebraic number has irrationality measure exactly 2 — that is, it cannot be approximated by rationals much better than Dirichlet’s theorem allows — settling a problem that had resisted Liouville, Thue and Siegel. In 1953 he had proved that any set of integers of positive density contains a three-term arithmetic progression, founding methods later central to additive combinatorics.
