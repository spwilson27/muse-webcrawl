Tenskwatawa

Tenskwatawa (c. 1775 – November 1836), originally named Lalawethika, known to Anglo-Americans as the Shawnee Prophet or simply The Prophet, was a Native American religious and political leader of the Shawnee tribe. He was the younger brother of the military leader Tecumseh, and together they founded a confederacy of Native nations resisting the expansion of the United States into the Old Northwest.

Early life and vision

Lalawethika grew up in Ohio during decades of warfare between Native nations and American settlers. By his thirties he had a reputation as a heavy drinker and failure. In 1805, after falling into a trance and being taken for dead, he awoke with a vision: the Master of Life had shown him a path for Native peoples to regain divine favour by renouncing alcohol, European goods and ways, and returning to traditional customs. Taking the name Tenskwatawa — "The Open Door" — he began preaching a message of moral renewal and pan-Indian unity, and his accurate prediction of a solar eclipse in 1806 bolstered his authority.

Prophetstown and Tippecanoe

In 1808 Tenskwatawa and Tecumseh founded Prophetstown at the junction of the Wabash and Tippecanoe rivers in Indiana, which drew thousands of followers from many tribes. While Tecumseh travelled to recruit allies, Indiana Territory governor William Henry Harrison marched on the town in November 1811. Against Tecumseh's instructions, Tenskwatawa ordered an attack; the resulting Battle of Tippecanoe ended with Prophetstown burned and the prophet's influence badly damaged.

Later life

Tenskwatawa accompanied Tecumseh during the War of 1812 but never regained his former standing after his brother's death in 1813. He lived in Canada on a British pension before returning to the United States, and in 1826 helped lead the Shawnee removal westward, dying in 1836 in what is now Kansas City, Kansas.
