Matlatzinca

Matlatzinca is a name used for several related ethnic groups in the Toluca Valley in the modern Mexican state of México (State of Mexico), as well as for their Oto-Pamean language. At the time of the Spanish conquest, the Valley of Toluca — the "Matlatzinco" in Nahuatl, meaning land of the net-makers (from matlatl, net) — was home to speakers of at least four languages: Matlatzinca, Otomi, Mazahua, and Nahuatl. Today only a small number of elderly speakers of Matlatzinca remain, mainly in the village of San Francisco Oxtotilpan in the municipality of Temascaltepec.

Language

The Matlatzinca language belongs to the Otopamean branch of the Oto-Manguean language family, closely related to Tlahuica (Ocuiltec). Two varieties are sometimes distinguished, Matlatzinca proper and the variety of Atzingo. The language is severely endangered, with fluent speakers numbering only in the low hundreds or fewer, nearly all elderly.

History

The Matlatzinca were conquered by the Aztec ruler Axayacatl in the 1470s, and their territory was incorporated into the Aztec Empire as a tributary province. Their principal city was Calixtlahuaca, whose ruins near modern Toluca include a circular temple dedicated to Ehecatl, the wind god. Under Spanish rule the population declined sharply through disease and resettlement.
