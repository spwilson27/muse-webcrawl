BNC connector

The BNC connector is a miniature quick connect/disconnect radio frequency connector used with coaxial cable. Its distinctive bayonet mount — two lugs on the female connector engaging slots in a rotating coupling nut on the male — allows fast, secure connection with a quarter turn.

Name and origin

The connector was developed in the late 1940s and patented by Bell Labs engineers Paul Neill and Carl Concelman. The name is commonly explained as "Bayonet Neill–Concelman", after the bayonet coupling and its two inventors, though backronyms such as "British Naval Connector" also circulate.

Characteristics and uses

BNC connectors are made in 50-ohm and 75-ohm versions and are usable to frequencies of several gigahertz, though performance falls off above about 4 GHz. They became standard fittings on test equipment such as oscilloscopes, on professional video equipment (including SDI links), amateur radio gear, and early computer networks — 10BASE2 Ethernet used BNC-terminated thin coaxial cable with BNC T-connectors.
