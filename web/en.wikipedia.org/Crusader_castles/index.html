Crusader castles
Region: Levant (Outremer)
Period: 12th–13th centuries
Builders: Crusader states, military orders

Crusader castles are fortifications built by European Crusaders and the military orders in the Levant during the 12th and 13th centuries to defend the Crusader states collectively known as Outremer.

Design

Many were large concentric castles with multiple rings of walls, massive towers, glacis slopes, and cisterns for sieges. Military orders — the Knights Hospitaller and Knights Templar — held the greatest fortresses, such as Krak des Chevaliers and Margat in Syria and Belvoir in the Galilee.

History

Castles anchored Crusader settlement, controlled roads and ports, and sheltered garrisons in a hostile landscape. As Muslim forces under leaders such as Saladin and Baybars reconquered the region, fortresses fell one by one; the last, at Acre, fell in 1291. Many survive as imposing ruins, and Krak des Chevaliers is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
