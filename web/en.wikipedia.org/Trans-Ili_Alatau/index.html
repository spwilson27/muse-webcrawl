The Trans-Ili Alatau (Kazakh: Іле Алатауы, Ile Alatauy; Kyrgyz: Иле Ала-Тоосу), also known as the Zailiyskiy Alatau, is a mountain range in the northern Tian Shan, on the border between Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan. Its name means "the mottled mountains beyond the Ili River." It is the northernmost ridge of the Tian Shan system, stretching about 350 km from west to east, and forms the dramatic backdrop to the city of Almaty, Kazakhstan's largest city, which lies at its foothills.

Geography

The range's highest summit is Talgar Peak (4,979 m), visible from Almaty on clear days. Other notable peaks include Mount Komsomol (4,376 m), popular with local climbers. The northern slopes are drained by rivers such as the Big and Little Almaty and the Talgar, fed by glaciers that are important water sources for the region; the range holds hundreds of glaciers, although, as elsewhere in Central Asia, many have retreated substantially over the past century. The Medeu skating rink and the Shymbulak ski resort are located in the Little Almaty gorge south of Almaty.

Nature protection

Much of the Kazakh side of the range is protected within the Ile-Alatau National Park, established in 1996 and covering roughly 200,000 hectares, and the Almaty State Nature Reserve, founded in 1931. The foothills are noted for wild apple forests (the wild ancestor of the domestic apple, Malus sieversii) and for wildlife including the snow leopard, brown bear, ibex and golden eagle. The southern slopes fall within Kyrgyzstan's Ala Archa National Park region and the Chuy Valley.
