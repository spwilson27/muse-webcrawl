Sphincter of Oddi

The sphincter of Oddi (also hepatopancreatic sphincter or Glisson's sphincter), abbreviated as SO, is a muscular valve that in some animals, including humans, controls the flow of bile and pancreatic juice out of the ducts entering the duodenum. The sphincter of Oddi is named after Ruggero Oddi, the physiologist who first described it in 1887.

Anatomy

The sphincter surrounds the ampulla of Vater, where the common bile duct and pancreatic duct join and enter the second part of the duodenum through the major duodenal papilla. It keeps the duct closed between meals, allowing bile to be diverted into the gallbladder, and relaxes under the influence of cholecystokinin after eating.

Clinical significance

Sphincter of Oddi dysfunction is a condition in which the sphincter fails to relax properly, obstructing bile and pancreatic flow and causing pain resembling biliary colic or pancreatitis. It may be diagnosed by manometry during ERCP and treated by sphincterotomy. Opioid medications can provoke spasm of the sphincter.
