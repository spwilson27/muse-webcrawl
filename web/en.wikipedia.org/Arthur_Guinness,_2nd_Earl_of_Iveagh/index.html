Arthur Ernest Guinness, 2nd Earl of Iveagh (29 March 1874 – 14 July 1967) was an Irish brewer, businessman and politician. The younger son of Edward Guinness, 1st Earl of Iveagh, he succeeded his father as chairman of the Guinness brewery in 1927 and led the company for four decades.

Early life and career

Guinness was educated at Eton College and Trinity College, Cambridge. He entered the family brewing business and served in the South African War with the Imperial Yeomanry. He was elected to the House of Commons as a Unionist member and later sat in the House of Lords after succeeding to the earldom on his father's death in 1927.

Guinness chairmanship

As chairman from 1927 to 1962, Guinness oversaw the expansion of the brewery's operations, including the opening of the Park Royal brewery in London in 1936, which allowed the company to serve the British market more efficiently. Under his leadership Guinness became one of the world's best-known brewing brands.

Philanthropy and later life

Like his father, he was a noted philanthropist, supporting housing and charitable causes in Dublin and London. He died in 1967 and was succeeded by his grandson Jonathan Guinness, Lord Moyne, in the family business leadership, while the earldom passed to his son Benjamin.
