Sikorsky Aircraft

Sikorsky Aircraft is an American aircraft manufacturer based in Stratford, Connecticut. It was established by the aviation pioneer Igor Sikorsky in 1923 and was among the first companies to manufacture helicopters for both civil and military use. Sikorsky was owned by United Technologies Corporation (later Raytheon Technologies) from 1929 until it was sold to Lockheed Martin in November 2015 for $9 billion.

History

Igor Sikorsky, formerly a leading aircraft designer in Imperial Russia—where he built the world's first four-engine airplane, the Ilya Muromets—emigrated to the United States after the Russian Revolution. His Sikorsky Aero Engineering Corporation began building amphibious aircraft and flying boats, including the S-42 used by Pan American World Airways to pioneer transoceanic routes. In 1939 Sikorsky flew the VS-300, the first practical single-main-rotor helicopter, and his R-4 became the first mass-produced helicopter.

Products

Sikorsky's best-known products include the UH-60 Black Hawk utility helicopter and its many derivatives, including the presidential VH-60 and the SH-60 Seahawk; the heavy-lift CH-53 family culminating in the CH-53K King Stallion; the SH-3 Sea King; and the commercial S-76 and S-92. It also supplies the presidential Marine One fleet and developed the X2 and S-97 Raider compound helicopter demonstrators, and with Boeing the SB-1 Defiant.
