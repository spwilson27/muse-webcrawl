Semisynthesis

Semisynthesis, or partial chemical synthesis, is a type of chemical synthesis that uses chemical compounds isolated from natural sources (such as microbial cell cultures or plant material) as the starting materials to produce novel compounds with distinct chemical and medicinal properties. Semisynthesis is a means of preparing many medicines more cheaply than by total synthesis, since the starting materials are harvested from nature rather than built from simple precursors.

Description

Semisynthesis is usually used when the precursor molecule is too structurally complex, too costly, or too inefficient to be produced by total synthesis. In many cases the natural precursor is a fermentation product or a metabolite that can be isolated in quantity, and a relatively small number of chemical steps then convert it into the desired pharmaceutical.

Examples in drug development

Examples of semisynthesis include the production of the antibiotic penicillin derivatives from 6-aminopenicillanic acid, the anticancer drug paclitaxel (Taxol) from 10-deacetylbaccatin III isolated from the needles of the European yew, and the antimalarial drug artemether from artemisinin extracted from the plant Artemisia annua. The schizophrenia drug apomorphine is also prepared by semisynthesis from morphine.
