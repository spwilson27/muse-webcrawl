Orchard

An orchard is an intentional plantation of trees or shrubs maintained for food production, most often fruit or nut trees. Orchards comprise fruit-producing trees grown for market on a commercial basis, and are distinguished from groves and forest gardens by their deliberate layout — usually in regular rows — that facilitates pruning, pollination, pest control, and harvest.


An apple orchard in blossom

Layout and management

Traditional orchards used widely spaced, standard-sized trees, often with grassland grazed beneath them; such orchards are now valued as wildlife habitat, notably in England and central Europe. Modern commercial orchards favor high-density plantings of dwarf or semi-dwarf trees on clonal rootstocks, trained on trellises, which bear earlier and suit mechanical harvesting. Orchard management includes irrigation, fertilization, thinning of fruit, pollination services from managed bees, and integrated pest management.

Types

Common orchard crops include apples, pears, plums, cherries and other stone fruit, citrus, olives, almonds, walnuts, and avocados. Orchards require a long-term investment: a newly planted orchard may take several years to reach full production but can remain productive for decades, and siting must account for chilling requirements, frost pockets, soil drainage, and water supply.
