Child euthanasia in Nazi Germany

Child euthanasia (German: Kinder-Euthanasie) was the organised killing of disabled children in Nazi Germany, beginning in 1939. It was the precursor and model for the adult "euthanasia" programme known as Aktion T4, and is estimated to have claimed at least 5,000 victims, with wider estimates substantially higher.

Background and implementation

In August 1939 the regime required midwives and doctors to report newborns with severe congenital conditions to the "Reich Committee for the Scientific Registering of Serious Hereditary and Congenital Illnesses". Children selected by medical referees were transferred to special children's departments (Kinderfachabteilungen) in clinics, where they were killed by starvation or by overdoses of medication such as Luminal.

Aftermath

The programme continued in decentralized form until the end of the war. Few perpetrators were prosecuted; some cases arising from the children's departments were examined in postwar trials, and the victims have since been commemorated at memorials including the former clinics at Hadamar and Am Spiegelgrund in Vienna.
