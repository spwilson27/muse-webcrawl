Primer (firearms)

A primer is the chemical and/or device responsible for initiating the combustion of propellant in a firearm cartridge, which in turn propels the projectile out of the gun barrel. In modern metallic cartridges, the primer is a small cup containing a shock-sensitive explosive mixture, seated in the base of the cartridge case. When struck by the firing pin, the priming compound detonates and vents flame through flash holes into the case, igniting the main propellant charge.

A centrefire primer seated in the head of a cartridge case.

Types

Centrefire primers come in two main systems: Boxer primers, containing their own anvil and common in American ammunition and among handloaders, and Berdan primers, whose anvil is part of the cartridge case, common in European military ammunition. Rimfire cartridges have no separate primer; the priming compound is spun into the hollow rim of the case and ignited by crushing the rim. Earlier ignition methods included the flintlock's priming pan and the percussion cap, a small copper cap containing fulminate placed on a nipple.

Chemistry

Early priming compounds were based on mercury fulminate, which embrittled brass cases. Twentieth-century non-corrosive primers used lead styphnate with barium nitrate and antimony sulfide; concerns over lead exposure have spurred development of lead-free ("green") primers using compounds such as diazodinitrophenol (DDNP).
