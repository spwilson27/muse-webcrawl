Mount Teide

Mount Teide (Pico del Teide) is a volcano on Tenerife in the Canary Islands, Spain. At 3,718 m (12,198 ft) it is the highest point in Spain and the highest peak of any Atlantic island; measured from its base on the ocean floor it rises over 7,500 m, making it one of the tallest volcanoes on Earth.

Geology and park

Teide stands within the Las Cañadas caldera and last erupted in 1909 (the Chinyero vent on its flank). It was sacred to the aboriginal Guanches, who believed it held up the sky and was the dwelling of the demon Guayota. Teide National Park, established in 1954, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most visited national parks in Europe; a cable car carries visitors near the summit, whose shadow at sunrise is the largest cast onto the sea anywhere in the world.
