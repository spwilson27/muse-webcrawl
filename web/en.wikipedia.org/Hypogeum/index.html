Hypogeum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hypogeum
EtymologyGreek hypo ("under") + gaia ("earth")
MeaningUnderground chamber, often a tomb or temple
Famous examplesĦal Saflieni Hypogeum (Malta); Hypogeum of the Volumni (Perugia); Egyptian hypogea of the Valley of the Kings

A hypogeum (plural hypogea) is an underground temple or tomb. The name derives from Greek words for "under the earth". Hypogea were often used as burial places, sometimes within necropoleis, and range from simple rock-cut chambers to elaborate multi-storey complexes with pillared halls and painted decoration.

Examples

The most famous hypogeum is the Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum in Paola, Malta — a Neolithic underground complex of halls and chambers cut into limestone around 4000–2500 BCE, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In Egypt, the royal tombs of the Valley of the Kings are hypogea cut into the Theban hills. Etruscan and Roman Italy produced numerous rock-cut tomb complexes, such as the Hypogeum of the Volumni near Perugia. Underground burial chambers also occur in Nabataean Petra, in Sardinian domus de janas, and in the catacombs of early Christian Rome.

Later uses

By extension, the term is sometimes applied to any underground structure, including early Christian catacombs and modern underground vaults. In architecture of the ancient Mediterranean, hypogea contrast with above-ground monuments such as mausolea and pyramid tombs.
