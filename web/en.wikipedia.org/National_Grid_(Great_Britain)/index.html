National Grid (Great Britain)

The National Grid is the high-voltage electric power transmission network in Great Britain, connecting power stations and major substations so that electricity generated anywhere in England, Scotland or Wales can be used to supply demand elsewhere. It is owned and operated by National Grid Electricity Transmission, part of National Grid plc.

History

The grid grew out of the Central Electricity Board, created by the Electricity (Supply) Act 1926 to build a national network of interconnecting transmission lines — the first sections operated at 132 kV. After nationalisation in 1948, the Central Electricity Generating Board developed the 275 kV and later 400 kV "supergrid". The system was privatised in 1990.

Operation

The network operates synchronously at 50 Hz and must keep supply and demand balanced second by second. Interconnectors link the grid to France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Norway, Denmark and Ireland, and offshore links connect to the separate Northern Ireland network. The system operator function passed to the publicly owned National Energy System Operator (NESO) in 2024.
