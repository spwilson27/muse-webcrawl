Atta (genus)

Atta
Family: Formicidae (ants)
Common name: Leafcutter ants
Species: about 15 described
Range: Central and South America, southern North America

Atta is a genus of New World ants of the subfamily Myrmicinae. It contains some of the best-known leafcutter ants, which cut fragments of leaves and carry them to underground nests—not to eat directly, but to feed the fungus gardens on which the colony depends.

Biology

Atta colonies are among the largest and most complex insect societies: a mature colony of Atta colombica or Atta cephalotes may contain several million workers organised into castes of very different sizes, from tiny gardeners to large soldiers (majors) with powerful mandibles. The ants cultivate the fungus Leucoagaricus gongylophorus on the harvested leaf material, prune and protect it with antibiotic-producing bacteria, and feed on the swollen hyphal tips it produces. A foundress queen, which may live for decades, carries a pellet of fungus in her mouth when founding a new colony.

Ecological impact

Leafcutters are major herbivores in Neotropical ecosystems and important soil engineers; they are also significant agricultural pests capable of defoliating crops and ornamental plants. Their conspicuous foraging trails and excavated nest mounds are prominent features of tropical landscapes.
