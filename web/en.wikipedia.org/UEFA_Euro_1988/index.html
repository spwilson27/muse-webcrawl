The 1988 UEFA European Football Championship, commonly referred to as UEFA Euro 1988, was the eighth UEFA European Championship, the quadrennial football championship of Europe organised by UEFA. It took place in West Germany from 10 to 25 June 1988.

Eight teams contested the final tournament. The Netherlands won their first major international title, defeating the Soviet Union 2–0 in the final at the Olympiastadion in Munich. The tournament is remembered especially for Marco van Basten's volley from an acute angle in the final, widely considered one of the greatest goals in the history of the competition.

Qualification

Thirty-two teams entered the qualifying competition, which was played in seven groups. The seven group winners — West Germany qualifying automatically as hosts — joined the hosts in the finals: Denmark, England, Italy, the Netherlands, the Republic of Ireland (making their debut at a major tournament), the Soviet Union and Spain.

Venues

Matches were played in eight stadiums: the Olympiastadion in Munich, the Parkstadion in Gelsenkirchen, the Volksparkstadion in Hamburg, the Waldstadion in Frankfurt, the Rheinstadion in Düsseldorf, the Niedersachsenstadion in Hanover, the Neckarstadion in Stuttgart and the Müngersdorfer Stadion in Cologne.

Group stage

In Group 1, West Germany and Italy advanced, ahead of Spain and Denmark. In Group 2, the Soviet Union topped the group and the Netherlands finished second; England lost all three of their matches, while the Republic of Ireland famously beat England 1–0 in Stuttgart in their opening game.

Knockout stage

In the semi-finals, the Netherlands defeated hosts West Germany 2–1 in Hamburg — their first competitive victory over the Germans since the Second World War, watched amid intense rivalry — and the Soviet Union beat Italy 2–0 in Stuttgart.

Final

The final was held on 25 June 1988 at the Olympiastadion, Munich. The Netherlands won 2–0 with goals from captain Ruud Gullit, who headed in the opener, and Marco van Basten, whose second-half volley from a tight angle over Soviet goalkeeper Rinat Dasayev sealed the victory. It remains the Netherlands' only major tournament triumph.

Goalscorers

Marco van Basten was the tournament's top scorer with five goals, including a hat-trick against England in the group stage.
