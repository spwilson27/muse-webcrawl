Mount Massive

Mount Massive is the second-highest summit of the Rocky Mountains of North America and the US state of Colorado. The prominent 14,428-foot (4,398 m) fourteener is located in the Sawatch Range, 10.6 miles (17 km) west-southwest of the city of Leadville in Lake County, Colorado. It lies within the Mount Massive Wilderness, part of San Isabel National Forest, and only about 15 feet lower than Mount Elbert, Colorado's highest peak — a difference long debated by supporters of each mountain.

Geography

True to its name, Mount Massive has a broad, elongated summit ridge with five summits above 14,000 feet along a three-mile crest, giving it the largest area above 14,000 feet of any peak in the contiguous United States. Its gentle profiles contrast with the sharper summits of nearby ranges.

Climbing

The standard East Slopes route gains about 4,500 feet over 13.5 miles round trip, making it a long but non-technical hike. The peak is a popular goal for climbers collecting Colorado's fourteeners.

Name dispute

Partisans of Mount Massive once attempted to build cairns to raise its measured height above Mount Elbert's; the piles were later dismantled by Elbert loyalists. Modern surveys confirm Elbert is higher by a small margin.
