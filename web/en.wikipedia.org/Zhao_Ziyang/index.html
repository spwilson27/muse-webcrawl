Zhao Ziyang (Chinese: 赵紫阳; 17 October 1919 – 17 January 2005) was a Chinese politician who served as Premier of the People's Republic of China from 1980 to 1987 and as General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party from 1987 to 1989. A leading reformer, he directed much of China's market-oriented economic reform in the 1980s.

Career

Zhao joined the Communist movement as a teenager and rose through the party in Guangdong and then Sichuan, where in the late 1970s his agricultural and industrial reforms revived a province devastated by famine — giving rise to the saying "to find grain, seek Ziyang". As Premier he oversaw the opening of special economic zones and championed price and enterprise reform. As General Secretary after 1987 he pushed further economic liberalisation and explored cautious political reforms.

1989 and house arrest

During the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests, Zhao opposed a military crackdown and famously appeared before the students in the square on 19 May 1989, urging them to end their hunger strike. He was purged by party hardliners, removed from all posts, and spent the remaining fifteen years of his life under house arrest in Beijing. He died in 2005; his secretly recorded memoirs were published posthumously in 2009 as Prisoner of the State.
