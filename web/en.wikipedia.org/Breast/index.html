Breast

The breast is one of two prominences on the upper ventral region of the torso of primates. In females, it contains the mammary glands, which secrete milk to feed infants. Both males and females develop breasts from the same embryonic tissues, but female breasts enlarge at puberty under the influence of estrogen.

Structure

The breast lies over the pectoralis major muscle and is composed of glandular tissue (lobes and lobules connected by lactiferous ducts to the nipple), adipose tissue, and the suspensory Cooper's ligaments. The pigmented areola surrounds the nipple and contains modified sebaceous glands.

Development

Breast development (thelarche) is usually the first sign of puberty in girls and is described by Tanner stages. During pregnancy, hormonal changes prepare the glands for lactation; after birth, prolactin stimulates milk production and oxytocin drives the let-down reflex.

Clinical significance

Breast conditions include mastitis, fibroadenoma, cysts, and breast cancer, the most common cancer in women worldwide. Screening methods include mammography, ultrasound, and MRI, while treatment may involve surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and endocrine therapy.
