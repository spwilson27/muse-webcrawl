Epic of Sundiata

The Epic of Sundiata, also called the Epic of Sundjata or Sundiata Keita, is a West African oral epic about Sundiata Keita, founder of the Mali Empire. Preserved and performed by Mandinka griots, it blends history, genealogy, praise poetry, magic, and moral instruction.

Story

The epic recounts Sundiata's birth to Naré Maghann Konaté and Sogolon Condé, his difficult childhood, exile, and return to defeat the sorcerer-king Soumaoro Kanté of Sosso. The victory at Krina, traditionally dated to the thirteenth century, opens the way for Mali's expansion.

Transmission

Because the epic is oral, versions differ by performer and region. A famous written version was narrated by Djeli Mamoudou Kouyaté and published by D. T. Niane as Sundiata: An Epic of Old Mali. Griots remain central custodians of the tradition.

Historical significance

The epic is a foundational narrative for Mandé identity and a key source—used cautiously alongside archaeology and Arabic chronicles—for the early Mali Empire. See also Sundiata Keita, Mali Empire, and griot.
