Factory Records

Factory Records was a Manchester-based British independent record label founded in 1978 by Tony Wilson and Alan Erasmus. Under the creative direction of designer Peter Saville, and with producer Martin Hannett, the label became central to the post-punk and Madchester scenes, releasing records by Joy Division, New Order, A Certain Ratio, The Durutti Column, Happy Mondays, and James. It is also famous for its nightclub, The Haçienda, and for giving catalogue numbers (FAC numbers) to nearly everything associated with the company, including posters, the club (FAC 51), and even a cat.

History

Factory grew out of Wilson and Erasmus's Factory club nights at the Russell Club in 1978. Its first release, A Factory Sample (FAC 2, 1979), was a double 7-inch EP featuring Joy Division, The Durutti Column, John Dowie, and Cabaret Voltaire. Joy Division's Unknown Pleasures (FACT 10, 1979), with Saville's iconic pulsar-waveform sleeve, established the label's aesthetic. After singer Ian Curtis's death in 1980, the surviving members formed New Order, whose 1983 single "Blue Monday" became the best-selling 12-inch single of all time; legend holds that its elaborate die-cut sleeve cost so much to produce that the label lost money on every copy sold.

The Haçienda and decline

The Haçienda opened in 1982 and, after a slow start, became the epicentre of acid house and rave culture in the late 1980s, though its popularity — and the gang problems it attracted — drained the label's finances rather than filling them. Factory's famously informal contracts ("The musicians own everything, the company owns nothing") and extravagant spending contributed to its collapse; the label went bankrupt in November 1992. London Records took over parts of the catalogue.

Legacy

Factory's story was dramatised in the 2002 film 24 Hour Party People. Saville's minimalist design, Hannett's spacious production, and Wilson's evangelism made Factory a model for the modern independent label, and its catalogue numbers — extending to FAC 461 and beyond — remain a celebrated piece of music lore.
