Mauritius, officially the Republic of Mauritius, is an island country in the Indian Ocean, about 2,000 kilometres off the south-eastern coast of Africa, east of Madagascar. The republic includes the main island of Mauritius, Rodrigues, Agaléga, and the Cargados Carajos shoals; Mauritius also claims the Chagos Archipelago (including Diego Garcia) and Tromelin Island. The country is known for its beaches, lagoons, and reefs, its multi-ethnic society, and for having been the home of the extinct dodo.

History

Uninhabited when first recorded by Arab and later Portuguese sailors, the island was colonised by the Dutch from 1638, who named it after Prince Maurice of Nassau and introduced sugar cane — and whose settlers' era saw the extinction of the flightless dodo by the late 17th century. France took control in 1715, renaming it Isle de France and developing it into a sugar-producing colony worked by enslaved Africans. Britain captured the island in 1810 during the Napoleonic Wars and retained it under the Treaty of Paris (1814), restoring the name Mauritius. After the abolition of slavery in 1835, large numbers of indentured labourers arrived from India, founding the Indo-Mauritian majority of today's population. Mauritius became independent in 1968 with Seewoosagur Ramgoolam as first prime minister, and a republic in 1992.

Geography and nature

The volcanic main island is ringed by coral reefs and features a central plateau rising to Piton de la Petite Rivière Noire (828 m). Its endemic wildlife, much of it endangered, includes the Mauritius fruit bat, the pink pigeon, and the echo parakeet, saved from near-extinction by celebrated conservation work. Two UNESCO World Heritage Sites — Aapravasi Ghat, the indentured-labour immigration depot, and Le Morne Cultural Landscape — mark its history.

Economy and society

Since independence Mauritius has transformed from a low-income sugar monoculture into an upper-middle-income economy based on tourism, textiles, financial services, information technology, and seafood processing. Its population is a blend of Indian, African (Creole), Chinese, and European ancestries, and its religious composition is predominantly Hindu with large Christian and Muslim minorities. English is the official language of parliament, while French and Mauritian Creole dominate everyday life.
