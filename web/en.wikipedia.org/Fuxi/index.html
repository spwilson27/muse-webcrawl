Fuxi

Fuxi (Chinese: 伹羲), also romanised as Fu Xi or Fu Hsi, is a culture hero in Chinese legend and mythology, credited along with his sister and wife Nüwa with creating humanity and inventing hunting, fishing, domestication of animals, and cooking, as well as the Cangjie system of writing Chinese characters around 2000 BC. Fuxi was also the first of the Three Sovereigns in Chinese mythology, traditionally dated to around 2800 BC.

According to legend, Fuxi observed the patterns of heaven and earth and devised the eight trigrams (bagua) of the I Ching, the basis of Chinese divination and cosmology. He is often depicted holding a carpenter's square, while Nüwa holds a compass, symbolising order in the world; in Han dynasty tomb art the pair appear with intertwined serpent bodies and human heads.

Mythology

Traditions place Fuxi's birth at Chengji (near modern Tianshui, Gansu) and his capital at Chen (modern Huaiyang, Henan), where a large temple complex and his reputed tomb remain a pilgrimage site. Among his attributed inventions are the fishing net, the trapping of wild animals, the institution of marriage, musical instruments such as the se zither, and the division of land and clan names.

The trigrams

The arrangement of trigrams attributed to Fuxi, the "Earlier Heaven" or Xiantian sequence, is distinguished from the "Later Heaven" arrangement attributed to King Wen of Zhou. The trigrams later expanded into the sixty-four hexagrams of the I Ching.
