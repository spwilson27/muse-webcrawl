Anne Sullivan Macy (born as Johanna Mansfield Sullivan; 14 April 1866 – 20 October 1936) was an American teacher best known for being the instructor and lifelong companion of Helen Keller, who was both deaf and blind. Drawing on her own experience of near-blindness and her education at the Perkins School for the Blind, Sullivan taught Keller language by spelling words into her hand, beginning a fifty-year partnership.

Early life

Sullivan was born to Irish immigrant parents in Massachusetts. A childhood eye disease, trachoma, left her nearly blind, and after her mother's death she spent years with her brother in the state almshouse at Tewksbury. She enrolled at Perkins School for the Blind in 1880, underwent surgery that partly restored her sight, and graduated as valedictorian in 1886.

Work with Helen Keller

In March 1887, aged twenty, Sullivan arrived at the Keller home in Tuscumbia, Alabama. Within weeks, at the famous water-pump scene, the seven-year-old Keller grasped that the manual-alphabet signs Sullivan had been spelling into her palm signified the objects around her — the breakthrough dramatised in the play and film The Miracle Worker. Sullivan remained Keller's teacher, interpreter and companion through Radcliffe College and their later lecture tours and advocacy work.

Later life

Sullivan married the writer and critic John Albert Macy in 1905; the marriage later ended in separation. Her eyesight failed progressively, and she died in New York in 1936. She and Keller are both buried at Washington National Cathedral.
