Missile

A missile is an airborne ranged weapon capable of self-propelled flight aided usually by a propulsion system such as a jet engine or rocket motor. Missiles are thus also called guided missiles or guided rockets (when in rocket form). Missiles are generally categorized by their intended mission and launch/engagement platform.

Components

Guided missiles have a number of system components:

  • A targeting or guidance system, which may use radar, infrared, laser, inertial navigation, GPS, or electro-optical sensors
  • A flight system, including fins, wings, thrust vectoring, or reaction jets for steering
  • An engine or motor, typically a rocket motor or air-breathing jet engine
  • A warhead, conventional or nuclear, together with a fuze

History

The first missiles to be used operationally were the German V-1 flying bomb and V-2 rocket of the Second World War. After the war, missile development accelerated during the Cold War, producing intercontinental ballistic missiles, anti-tank guided missiles, and sophisticated surface-to-air and air-to-air systems.

Classification

Missiles are commonly grouped by launch platform and target: surface-to-surface, surface-to-air, air-to-air, air-to-surface, anti-ship, and anti-tank. Ballistic missiles follow a ballistic trajectory for most of their flight, while cruise missiles fly an aerodynamic, powered course at relatively low altitude.
