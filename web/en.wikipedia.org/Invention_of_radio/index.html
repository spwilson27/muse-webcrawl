Invention of radio

The invention of radio communication was preceded by decades of theory and experiment. Maxwell's equations (1860s) predicted electromagnetic waves, which Heinrich Hertz demonstrated in 1887–88. Many inventors then contributed to wireless telegraphy in the 1890s, making priority a long-running controversy.

Principal claimants

Guglielmo Marconi commercialized wireless telegraphy, achieving the first transatlantic signal in 1901 and dominating early radio. Nikola Tesla demonstrated wireless signalling and held key patents; in 1943 the U.S. Supreme Court restored some of Tesla's priority over Marconi's tuning patent, though Marconi's practical achievements stand. Other contributors include Alexander Popov, Oliver Lodge, Jagadish Chandra Bose, and Reginald Fessenden, who made the first audio broadcast in 1906. Edwin Armstrong's FM and superheterodyne work completed the foundations of modern radio.
