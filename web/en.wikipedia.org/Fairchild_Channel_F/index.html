Fairchild Channel F

Manufacturer: Fairchild Camera and Instrument
Released: November 1976
CPU: Fairchild F8
Successor: Channel F System II

The Fairchild Channel F, short for "Channel Fun", is a home video game console released by Fairchild Camera and Instrument in November 1976. It was the first programmable ROM cartridge-based video game console, and the first console to use a microprocessor (the Fairchild F8). The cartridge system was designed by a team including Jerry Lawson, a pioneering African-American engineer.

Design

Unlike earlier dedicated consoles whose games were built into the hardware, the Channel F played interchangeable cartridges ("Videocarts"), of which about 26 were released. Its distinctive controllers combined a joystick with a rotating knob and a plunger button in a single handset. The console included two built-in games, Hockey and Tennis.

Legacy

The Channel F sold modestly and was quickly overshadowed by the Atari 2600 (1977), which adopted its cartridge concept with greater success. A redesigned Channel F System II followed in 1979, and Fairchild exited the games business soon after. The console is remembered as the originator of the cartridge-based home console market.
