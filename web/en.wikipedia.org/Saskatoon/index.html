Saskatoon is the largest city in the Canadian province of Saskatchewan. It straddles the South Saskatchewan River in the province's central region and is known as the "Bridge City" for its nine river crossings. The name derives from the Cree word misâskwatômina for the sweet purple saskatoon berries that grow along the riverbanks.

History

The area has been inhabited for thousands of years; the Wanuskewin Heritage Park preserves evidence of Northern Plains peoples over some 6,000 years. Saskatoon was founded in 1883 as a temperance settlement led by John Lake under the Toronto-based Temperance Colonization Society. Growth accelerated with the arrival of railways in the 1890s and 1900s and a wheat-boom influx of settlers; Saskatoon was incorporated as a city in 1906.

Geography and climate

Saskatoon lies in the aspen parkland biome at an elevation of about 480 metres. It has a humid continental climate with long, cold winters and short, warm summers; it is among Canada's sunniest cities. The river valley, managed as the Meewasin Valley, provides parks and trails through the urban core.

Economy and institutions

The economy is anchored in agriculture and agri-food research, potash and uranium mining (Nutrien and Cameco have major operations headquartered or based in the city), and technology. Saskatoon hosts the University of Saskatchewan, founded in 1907, the Canadian Light Source synchrotron, and Innovation Place research park. Cultural institutions include the Remai Modern art gallery (opened 2017) and the Western Development Museum.
