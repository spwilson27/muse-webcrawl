Concorde Agreement

The Concorde Agreement is a contract between the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA), the Formula One Group, and the competing Formula One teams which dictates the terms by which the teams compete in races, and how the television revenues and prize money are shared.

History

The first Concorde Agreement was signed in 1981, ending the FISA–FOCA war, a dispute between the governing body and the teams' association led by Bernie Ecclestone and Max Mosley. It took its name from the Place de la Concorde in Paris, where the FIA is headquartered. Successor agreements were signed in 1987, 1992, 1997, 1998, 2009 and 2013.

The 2021 agreement

All ten teams signed the eighth Concorde Agreement in August 2020, running from 2021 to 2025. It accompanied a package of reforms including a budget cap (cost cap) introduced in 2021, intended to level the playing field and make the sport more financially sustainable, and a revised, more equitable distribution of prize money, with a historic payment preserving part of Ferrari's traditional bonus.

Successor

A ninth agreement, covering 2026 to 2030, was signed by the teams and Formula One Group in March 2025, with the FIA subsequently completing the governance elements; it aligns with the new 2026 technical and power-unit regulations.

Effect

The agreement effectively commits teams to the championship for its term, giving commercial stability to the sport; its details are confidential, and disputes over its negotiation have repeatedly shaped Formula One politics.
