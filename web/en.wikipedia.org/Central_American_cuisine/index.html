Central American cuisine

Central American cuisine is the traditional cooking of the countries of Central America — Belize, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, and Panama — blending Indigenous Mesoamerican ingredients with Spanish, African, and Caribbean influences.

Staples

Maize (corn) is the foundational staple, used for tortillas, tamales, and drinks such as atole. Beans and rice are eaten daily, often together, as in Nicaraguan gallo pinto and Honduran baleadas. Plantains, yuca (cassava), squash, tomatoes, avocados, and chillies are common, and the region is a major producer and consumer of coffee.

Regional dishes

El Salvador is known for pupusas, thick corn tortillas stuffed with cheese, beans, or pork. Guatemala has dishes such as pepián, a rich meat stew, and kak'ik, a turkey soup. Honduras is known for baleadas and fried fish with plantains on its Caribbean coast. Panama's cuisine, shaped by its canal-era diversity, includes sancocho and carimañolas. Belize combines Mexican, Caribbean, and Garifuna influences, exemplified by hudut, a fish and plantain dish.
