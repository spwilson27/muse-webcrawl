Buckingham Palace is a royal residence and the administrative headquarters of the monarch of the United Kingdom in London. Located in the City of Westminster, the palace is often at the centre of state occasions and royal hospitality. It has been the official London residence of the United Kingdom's sovereigns since 1837 and is the principal workplace of the monarchy.

History

The site began as Buckingham House, a large townhouse built for the Duke of Buckingham in 1703. King George III acquired it in 1761 as a private residence for Queen Charlotte, and it became known as the Queen's House. In the 19th century George IV commissioned John Nash to expand it into a palace; William IV and then Queen Victoria continued the work, and Victoria became the first monarch to reside there in 1837. The famous east front and balcony were added under Victoria, with the façade refaced in Portland stone to Sir Aston Webb's design in 1913.

The palace today

The palace has 775 rooms, including 19 State rooms, 52 royal and guest bedrooms, 188 staff bedrooms, 92 offices, and 78 bathrooms. Its garden, at 39 acres, is the largest private garden in London. The State Rooms are opened to visitors each summer. The Changing of the Guard ceremony takes place in the forecourt, and the Victoria Memorial stands before the palace gates at the head of the Mall.

Symbolic role

The palace balcony is the traditional vantage point for royal appearances on occasions such as Trooping the Colour and royal weddings, and the palace has served as a focal point for national celebration and mourning, notably during the Second World War and at the deaths of Diana, Princess of Wales, and Queen Elizabeth II.
