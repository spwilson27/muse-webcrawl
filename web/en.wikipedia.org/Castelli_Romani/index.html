Castelli Romani

The Castelli Romani ("Roman Castles") are a group of comuni in the Metropolitan City of Rome, located at a short distance southeast of the Italian capital, in the Alban Hills. The area is a popular weekend destination for Romans and is known for its wine production, volcanic lakes, and historic villas.

Overview

The towns traditionally counted among the Castelli Romani include Albano Laziale, Ariccia, Castel Gandolfo, Colonna, Frascati, Genzano di Roma, Grottaferrata, Lanuvio, Lariano, Marino, Monte Compatri, Monte Porzio Catone, Nemi, Rocca di Papa, Rocca Priora, and Velletri. They grew up around the fortified residences of Roman baronial families during the Middle Ages.

Wine and cuisine

The volcanic soils of the Alban Hills produce notable white wines, including Frascati, Marino, and Colli Albani. The area is also known for porchetta, traditionally associated with Ariccia, and for the strawberries of Nemi, grown on the slopes above the small crater lake of the same name.

Geography

The Castelli occupy the collapsed volcanic complex of the Alban Hills (the Latian volcano), dominated by Monte Cavo and containing two crater lakes: Lake Albano and Lake Nemi. Much of the area is protected as the Parco Regionale dei Castelli Romani.
