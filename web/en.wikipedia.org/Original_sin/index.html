Original sin

Original sin is a Christian doctrine that holds that all humans are born into a state of sinfulness deriving from the Fall of Man — Adam and Eve's disobedience in eating from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil in the Garden of Eden. It explains humanity's propensity to sin and separation from God.

Development

The doctrine was shaped in the 4th and 5th centuries, above all by Augustine of Hippo, who argued against Pelagius that Adam's guilt is inherited by all his descendants and that unbaptised infants share in it. Earlier church fathers such as Irenaeus and Origen taught related ideas about the Fall's effects.

Denominational views

Roman Catholic teaching holds that original sin is transmitted by propagation, deprives humans of original holiness, and is remitted by baptism, though concupiscence remains. Eastern Orthodoxy speaks of ancestral sin, emphasising mortality and corruption more than inherited guilt. Many Protestant traditions affirm total depravity; Arminian traditions stress prevenient grace.

Criticism

Critics from Pelagius onward have questioned the justice of inherited guilt; Jewish and Islamic theology do not include the doctrine, though both recount Adam's expulsion from paradise.
