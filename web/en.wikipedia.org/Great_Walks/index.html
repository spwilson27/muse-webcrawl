Great Walks

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Walks are a network of premier multi-day tramping (hiking) tracks in New Zealand, maintained by the Department of Conservation (DOC) to a high standard, with well-formed tracks, serviced huts, and campsites. Because of their popularity, most operate a booking system during the Great Walks season (roughly late October to late April), with hut and campsite fees that are higher for international visitors.

The tracks

The network comprises ten walks: Lake Waikaremoana and Tongariro Northern Circuit in the North Island; the Abel Tasman Coast Track, Heaphy Track, Paparoa Track, Routeburn Track, Milford Track, Kepler Track, Rakiura Track (Stewart Island), and the Whanganui Journey — the latter a river voyage by canoe or kayak rather than a walk. The Milford Track, often called "the finest walk in the world," is limited by a strict quota and must be walked in one direction during the season. The Paparoa Track, opened in 2019 as a memorial to the 29 men killed in the 2010 Pike River Mine disaster, also admits mountain bikers. A further track, the Hump Ridge Track, joined the network in the mid-2020s.

Management

Great Walks huts typically offer bunk beds, heating, cooking facilities, and flush toilets, with wardens present in season; camping is permitted only at designated sites on most tracks. Fees fund track maintenance and conservation, and the walks are a major draw for New Zealand tourism while showcasing national parks and reserves protected under the Conservation Act.
