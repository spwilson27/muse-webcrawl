Extended play

An extended play (EP) is a musical recording that contains more tracks than a single but fewer than an album or LP record. Contemporary EPs generally contain three to five songs, and are considered less expensive and time-consuming for an artist to produce than an album.

History

EPs were released in various sizes in different eras. The first EPs were 7-inch vinyl records with more tracks than a normal single, typically four songs, and were introduced by RCA Victor in 1952.

Definitions

Official classification rules vary by chart and award body; the Recording Industry Association of America counts an EP as having three to five distinct tracks and a total running time under 30 minutes.
