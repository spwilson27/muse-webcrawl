The United States federal budget comprises the spending and revenues of the U.S. federal government. It is the world's largest government budget. Federal outlays have in recent years equalled roughly 22–24 percent of gross domestic product. The government primarily spends on health care programmes, Social Security, and national defence, and primarily collects revenue through individual and payroll taxes.

Overview

The budget records a deficit when spending exceeds revenue in a fiscal year, which the Treasury finances by issuing debt. The federal government ran surpluses only in a handful of years since 1970 — most recently from 1998 to 2001 — and has run large deficits since, notably during the Great Recession (2009) and the COVID-19 pandemic (2020, when the deficit reached about $3.1 trillion, nearly 15 percent of GDP).

Revenue

In fiscal year 2023, federal receipts were about $4.44 trillion. The largest sources are:

  • Individual income taxes — roughly half of all receipts
  • Payroll taxes (Social Security and Medicare) — about 35 percent
  • Corporate income taxes — about 11 percent
  • Excise taxes, customs duties, estate taxes, and other sources make up the remainder

Spending

Federal spending divides into mandatory spending — required by permanent law, including Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and net interest — which accounts for about 60 percent of outlays, and discretionary spending, set annually through appropriations acts and split roughly evenly between defence and non-defence categories. In FY2023, outlays were about $6.13 trillion, with Social Security the single largest programme (about $1.35 trillion), followed by health programmes and national defence (about $805 billion for the Department of Defense base and related activities).

Debt

Cumulative borrowing produces the federal debt. Gross federal debt exceeded $34 trillion in early 2024; debt held by the public was about $27 trillion, roughly equal to annual GDP. Net interest costs have risen with higher interest rates, reaching about $659 billion in FY2023. Total federal debt is subject to a statutory limit — the debt ceiling — which Congress has raised dozens of times.
