Low intensity conflict

Low intensity conflict (LIC) is a military conflict usually fought between a state and non-state or irregular forces, below the threshold of conventional war. It commonly involves insurgency, counterinsurgency, terrorism, guerrilla warfare and prolonged civil unrest, often with outside support to one or both sides.

Characteristics

LIC emphasises political as much as military objectives: control and legitimacy among the population are usually decisive. Operations may include foreign internal defence, peacekeeping, advisory missions and special operations. The term was used extensively by the United States during the Cold War and its aftermath, though later doctrine has tended to speak of irregular warfare instead.
