Satoshi Nakamoto

Known forInventing Bitcoin, implementing the first blockchain
Active2008–2011
IdentityUnknown (pseudonym)

Satoshi Nakamoto is the name used by the person or people who developed Bitcoin, authored the Bitcoin white paper, and created and deployed Bitcoin's original reference implementation. As part of the implementation, Nakamoto also devised the first blockchain database. Nakamoto was active in the development of Bitcoin until December 2010, gradually handing control to developer Gavin Andresen, and disappeared from public communication in 2011. The identity behind the pseudonym remains unknown.

Development of Bitcoin

On 31 October 2008, Nakamoto published the white paper Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System on a cryptography mailing list, describing a decentralised digital currency secured by proof-of-work. The Bitcoin network went live on 3 January 2009, when Nakamoto mined the genesis block; embedded in it was a newspaper headline from that day's The Times: "Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks". Nakamoto collaborated with early adopters such as Hal Finney — the recipient of the first Bitcoin transaction — and released successive versions of the software before withdrawing.

Identity and fortune

Nakamoto is estimated to control roughly one million bitcoins mined in the network's first year, held in wallets that have never spent them; at various market peaks this would make the pseudonymous inventor one of the wealthiest individuals in the world. Numerous people have been proposed or have claimed to be Nakamoto — including Dorian Nakamoto (named in a 2014 magazine article and who denied the claim), Nick Szabo, and Craig Wright, whose claims were rejected by the English High Court in 2024, which ruled that Wright is not the author of the white paper. As of the present, no identification has been proven, since the definitive test — signing a message with Nakamoto's earliest private keys — has never been publicly satisfied.
