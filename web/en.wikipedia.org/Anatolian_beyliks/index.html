Anatolian beyliks

The Anatolian beyliks (Turkish: Anadolu beylikleri) were small Turkish principalities (beyliks, ruled by beys) that arose in Anatolia in two waves. The first followed the Turkish victory over Byzantium at Manzikert (1071) and included the Danishmendids, Saltukids, Mengujekids, and Artuqids. The second and more famous wave emerged after the Mongol victory at Köse Dağ (1243) shattered the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum: Turkmen frontier (uç) lords in western Anatolia founded dozens of independent principalities during the late 13th and 14th centuries. From among them, the small northwestern beylik of the Ottomans expanded to conquer first its neighbours and then the Balkans and the Byzantine Empire, ultimately absorbing all the other beyliks.

The second-period beyliks

  • Karamanids (Karaman, south-central) — the Ottomans' most persistent Anatolian rivals; they claimed the Seljuk inheritance and made Turkish the language of their court (Karaman Mehmed Bey's 1277 decree). Finally annexed by Mehmed II in the 1470s.
  • Germiyanids (Kütahya) — wealthy inland beylik; much of its territory passed to the Ottomans by marriage and purchase around 1381–1429.
  • Aydınids (Birgi, then Ayasuluk/Selçuk) — maritime beylik famous for the naval raids of Umur Bey (d. 1348) in the Aegean.
  • Menteşe (Milas) — seafaring beylik in the southwest (ancient Caria).
  • Saruhan (Manisa), Karasi (Balıkesir — the first beylik absorbed by the Ottomans, c. 1345, providing their first fleet), Hamid (Isparta), Teke (Antalya), Candarids/Isfendiyarids (Kastamonu–Sinop), Eretna and later Kadi Burhan al-Din (Sivas), Dulkadir (Elbistan–Maraş, a buffer with Mamluk Egypt), Ramadanids (Adana), and the Ak Koyunlu and Kara Koyunlu Turkmen confederations in the east.

History

The beyliks were products of the frontier society of gazi warriors and dervish colonists, of Mongol Ilkhanid overlordship weakening after 1335, and of Byzantine decline. Their courts patronized Turkish-language literature, distinctive mosque and mausoleum architecture, and trade with Genoa and Venice. Timur's crushing victory over the Ottomans at Ankara (1402) briefly restored many beyliks Sultan Bayezid I had annexed, but during the Ottoman interregnum and the reigns of Mehmed I and Murad II most were reabsorbed; the Karamanids held out longest. By the early 16th century the last beyliks (Dulkadir, 1515; Ramadanids, 1608) had been incorporated into the Ottoman provincial system.
