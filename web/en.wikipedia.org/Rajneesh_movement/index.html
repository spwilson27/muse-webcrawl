Rajneesh movement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rajneesh movement is a religious movement inspired by the Indian mystic Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh (1931–1990), later known as Osho. Rajneesh's followers, called sannyasins, were recognizable in the 1970s and 1980s by their orange or red clothing and the mala necklace bearing his portrait. The movement was controversial worldwide for its liberal attitudes toward sexuality, its confrontations with local communities, and the crimes committed by its leadership in the United States.

History

Rajneesh began teaching in India in the 1960s and established an ashram in Pune in 1974, attracting many Western seekers with techniques such as Dynamic Meditation and a synthesis of Eastern spirituality, Western psychotherapy, and celebration of material life (he styled his ideal "Zorba the Buddha"). In 1981 the movement relocated to a ranch in Wasco County, Oregon, where it built the city of Rajneeshpuram. Conflicts with local residents and authorities escalated; in 1984, members of the commune's leadership carried out a bioterror attack, contaminating salad bars in The Dalles with Salmonella to influence a local election — the largest bioterror attack in US history — and were implicated in other crimes including attempted murder and widespread wiretapping.

Rajneesh was arrested in 1985, pleaded guilty to immigration violations, and was deported from the United States. He returned to Pune, adopted the name Osho in 1989, and died in 1990. The movement's Oregon commune was dismantled.

Aftermath and legacy

The former Rajneeshpuram site is now a Christian youth camp. The Pune centre continues as the Osho International Meditation Resort, and Osho's books and meditation techniques remain widely distributed, though his organization has distanced itself from the Oregon-era crimes. The events were revisited in the 2018 Netflix documentary series Wild Wild Country, which renewed public interest in the movement and in Rajneesh's secretary Ma Anand Sheela.

Beliefs and practices

Rajneesh taught that enlightenment comes through meditation, awareness, love, celebration, and transcendence of dualities, rejecting ascetic renunciation and organized religion's dogmas. Practices include active meditations, encounter groups, and discourses on a wide range of religious texts, which Rajneesh reinterpreted freely.
