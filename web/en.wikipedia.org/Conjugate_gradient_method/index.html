Conjugate gradient method

The conjugate gradient method is an algorithm for the numerical solution of systems of linear equations whose matrix is positive-definite. It is an iterative method, especially effective for sparse systems too large for direct methods such as Cholesky decomposition, and it also forms the basis of methods for unconstrained optimisation.

History

The method was developed independently by Magnus Hestenes and Eduard Stiefel, who published it jointly in 1952, building on related work by Cornelius Lanczos. It later gave rise to a family of Krylov subspace methods and to nonlinear conjugate gradient variants such as the Fletcher–Reeves and Polak–Ribière formulas.

The algorithm

To solve Ax = b with A symmetric and positive-definite, the method generates a sequence of search directions that are mutually A-conjugate, minimising the quadratic form associated with the system along each direction. In exact arithmetic it converges in at most n steps for an n-dimensional system; in practice, convergence speed depends on the eigenvalues of A, and preconditioning is often used to accelerate it.

Applications

Conjugate gradient methods are widely used in finite element analysis, computational fluid dynamics, image processing and machine learning, where the system matrix may be available only through matrix–vector products.
