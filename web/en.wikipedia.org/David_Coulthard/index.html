David Marshall Coulthard (born 27 March 1971) is a British retired racing driver from Scotland, later a broadcaster and commentator. Nicknamed "DC", he competed in Formula One from 1994 to 2008 for Williams, McLaren and Red Bull Racing, winning 13 Grands Prix and finishing runner-up in the 2001 World Championship to Michael Schumacher.

Early career

Coulthard began karting at eleven and progressed through Formula Ford, winning the first McLaren/Autosport Young Driver of the Year award in 1989. He won the Macau Grand Prix and the Masters of Formula Three in 1991 and finished third in International Formula 3000 in 1993, while serving as Williams' test driver.

Formula One career

After the death of Ayrton Senna at Imola in 1994, Coulthard was promoted to the Williams race seat. He won his first Grand Prix in Portugal in 1995. Moving to McLaren in 1996, he spent nine seasons there, winning twelve races and twice winning at Monaco (2000, 2002) and at home at Silverstone (1999, 2000). His best season was 2001, when he won four races and finished second in the championship. From 2005 he drove for the new Red Bull Racing team, scoring its first podium at Monaco in 2006, and retired at the end of 2008 with 535 career points — at the time the highest total for a British driver.

Later career

Coulthard raced in the DTM from 2010 to 2012 and became a leading Formula One pundit and commentator for the BBC and later Channel 4. He was appointed MBE in 2010, co-founded the production company Whisper, and served as president of the British Racing Drivers' Club. He was a prominent advocate of the all-female W Series and works as an ambassador for Red Bull Racing.
