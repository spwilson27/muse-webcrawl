The Balkans campaign of World War II began with the Italian invasion of Greece on 28 October 1940 and concluded with the German conquest of mainland Greece and Yugoslavia and the airborne capture of Crete in May–June 1941.

Greco-Italian War

Italy's invasion from Albania was repelled by Greek forces, which counterattacked into Italian-held Albania. The stalemate prompted German intervention, partly to secure the Balkans before the invasion of the Soviet Union.

German invasion

On 6 April 1941 Germany launched Operation Marita against Greece and simultaneously invaded Yugoslavia, whose government had signed and then repudiated the Tripartite Pact. Yugoslavia capitulated on 17 April; mainland Greece fell by the end of April, and the Allied garrison was evacuated. The Battle of Crete (20 May – 1 June 1941) completed the campaign at heavy cost to German paratroops.

Consequences

Greece and Yugoslavia were partitioned and occupied. Resistance movements, notably the Yugoslav Partisans, later tied down substantial Axis forces. Historians debate whether the campaign delayed Operation Barbarossa.
