Limasawa

Limasawa, officially the Municipality of Limasawa, is an island municipality in the province of Southern Leyte, Philippines. It is famous in Philippine history as the traditional site of the first Catholic Mass in the Philippines, celebrated on Easter Sunday, 31 March 1521, during Ferdinand Magellan's expedition.

The first Mass controversy

The account of chronicler Antonio Pigafetta places the expedition at an island he called Mazaua, where Magellan's fleet anchored and a Mass was held ashore. For centuries Limasawa was accepted as Mazaua, and the National Historical Commission of the Philippines has repeatedly affirmed Limasawa's claim; however, some historians have argued for Masao in Butuan, Mindanao. Official panels in 1998 and 2020 upheld Limasawa as the site of the first Easter Sunday Mass.

Geography and today

The island lies south of Leyte in the Bohol Sea, with a population of roughly 7,000. Its economy depends on fishing and agriculture, with growing heritage tourism centred on the first-Mass shrine and a hilltop cross commemorating the 1521 events.
