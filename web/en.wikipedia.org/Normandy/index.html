Normandy (French: Normandie) is a geographical and cultural region in northwestern France, roughly coextensive with the historical Duchy of Normandy. Since 2016 it has formed a single administrative region created by merging Upper and Lower Normandy. Its coastline faces the English Channel and includes the Cotentin Peninsula and the Channel Islands' nearby waters.

History

The region takes its name from the Norse ("Northmen") Vikings who settled there; in 911 their leader Rollo received the territory from King Charles the Simple by the Treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte, founding the Duchy of Normandy. A descendant, Duke William, conquered England in 1066. Normandy was a contested frontier between England and France for centuries. In the Second World War it was the site of the D-Day landings of 6 June 1944 and the ensuing Battle of Normandy, commemorated by numerous cemeteries and museums along the landing beaches.

Geography and culture

Normandy is known for its bocage farmland, cider and Calvados production, cheeses such as Camembert, and landmarks including Mont-Saint-Michel and the port of Le Havre. Impressionist painting began here, with Claude Monet's house at Giverny and the harbour scenes of Le Havre and Rouen.
