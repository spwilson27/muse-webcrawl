Obafemi Awolowo University (OAU) is a federal government-owned university in Ile-Ife, Osun State, Nigeria. It was founded in 1962 as the University of Ife by the regional government of Western Nigeria, led by premier Obafemi Awolowo; following Awolowo's death in 1987, the university was renamed in his honor.

History

The university was one of the institutions recommended by the Ashby Commission on higher education in Nigeria. It admitted its first students in 1962 and developed on a large, purpose-built campus designed with notable modernist buildings amid landscaped grounds. Control passed to the federal government in 1975.

Academics

OAU comprises numerous faculties, including Administration, Agriculture, Arts, Education, Environmental Design and Management, Law, Pharmacy, Social Sciences, Technology, and the College of Health Sciences, together with institutes such as the Institute of African Studies and the Natural History Museum. It is consistently ranked among Nigeria's leading universities and has produced prominent alumni in academia, law, politics, and the arts, including Nobel laureate-adjacent literary figures and Nigerian public officials.

Campus

The campus is frequently praised as one of the most beautiful in Africa, featuring modernist architecture by the Israeli architect Arieh Sharon and others, wide boulevards, and a central core of academic buildings. Its teaching hospital complex (OAUTHC) is a major referral center.
