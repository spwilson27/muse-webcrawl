National League (English football)

Founded: 1979 (as the Alliance Premier League)

Country: England (with some Welsh clubs)

Level: 5th tier (top division), with regional 6th-tier divisions

Divisions: National League; National League North; National League South

The National League is an association football league in England consisting of three divisions: the National League, National League North, and National League South. The top division, also called the National League, is the highest level of the National League System and the fifth tier of English football overall, sitting immediately below the English Football League (EFL). Together the three divisions contain 72 clubs.

History

The league was founded in 1979 as the Alliance Premier League, the first national single division for England's leading semi-professional clubs outside the Football League. It was later known as the Football Conference, and from 2015 it has been called the National League. Automatic promotion between the Conference and the Football League began in 1987, ending the old re-election system; since 2003 two clubs are promoted annually — the champions and the winners of promotion play-offs — to EFL League Two, with two relegated in return.

Structure

The National League (24 clubs) is a nationwide division in which most clubs are now fully professional. National League North and National League South (24 clubs each) form the sixth tier on a regional basis. Four clubs are relegated from the top division each season. The league also operates the FA Trophy eligibility for its member clubs and has been sponsored by companies including Gola, Vauxhall, Nationwide, Blue Square, Skrill and Vanarama.
