Alliance 90/The Greens (German: Bündnis 90/Die Grünen) is a green political party in Germany. It was formed in 1993 by the merger of the West German party The Greens, founded in 1980, and Alliance 90, a coalition of East German civil rights groups formed during the peaceful revolution of 1989–90. One of the world's most successful green parties, it has participated in federal governments in coalition with the Social Democrats (1998–2005) and again from 2021 in a coalition with the SPD and the Free Democrats.

History

The Greens emerged from the environmental, anti-nuclear, feminist, and peace movements of the 1970s. Founded in Karlsruhe in January 1980, the party entered the Bundestag in 1983 with 5.6 percent of the vote. Its early years were marked by a struggle between the pragmatic "Realo" wing, associated with Joschka Fischer, and the fundamentalist "Fundi" wing, associated with figures such as Petra Kelly and Rudolf Bahro.

After German reunification, the western Greens fell out of the Bundestag in 1990, while the eastern Alliance 90 entered it. The two merged in 1993. In 1998 the party joined its first federal government under Chancellor Gerhard Schröder, with Fischer as foreign minister and vice-chancellor; the coalition oversaw the phase-out of nuclear power and Germany's first combat deployment since 1945, in Kosovo.

Policies

The Greens prioritize climate protection and the transition to renewable energy, alongside social justice, gender equality, civil rights, and a pro-European foreign policy. Since 2011 the party has governed the state of Baden-Württemberg under Minister-President Winfried Kretschmann, its first head of a state government. In the 2021 federal election the party achieved its best national result with 14.8 percent and entered the "traffic light" coalition government.
