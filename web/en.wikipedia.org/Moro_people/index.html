The Moro people or Bangsamoro people are the Muslim ethnolinguistic groups of the southern Philippines, principally in Mindanao, the Sulu Archipelago and Palawan. The name derives from "Moor", the term Spanish colonisers applied to Muslim peoples. The Moros comprise some thirteen ethnolinguistic groups, including the Tausug, Maguindanaon, Maranao, Yakan, Sama and Iranun.

History

Islam arrived in the Sulu Archipelago and Mindanao through trade with Arab, Malay and Gujarati merchants from the 14th century onward. Powerful sultanates — notably Sulu and Maguindanao — emerged and resisted Spanish colonisation for over three centuries; the Spanish never fully subdued the Moro sultanates, and the region was only incorporated into the Philippine state under American colonial rule in the early 20th century.

Struggle for self-determination

Grievances over land, migration of Christian settlers, and political marginalisation fuelled an armed separatist movement from the late 1960s, led successively by the Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF) and the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF). Peace agreements in 1996 and 2014 led to the creation of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) in 2019, giving the Moro people a measure of self-government.

Culture

Moro culture blends indigenous Austronesian traditions with Malay and Islamic influences, expressed in brassware, woven textiles such as the malong, the kulintang gong-chime music, and seafaring traditions symbolised by the colourful vinta boats of the Sama and Tausug.
