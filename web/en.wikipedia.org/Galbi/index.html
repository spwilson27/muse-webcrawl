Galbi

OriginKorea
TypeGrilled marinated ribs
Main ingredientsBeef (or pork) short ribs, soy sauce, sugar, garlic, sesame oil
VariantsSo-galbi (beef), dwaeji-galbi (pork), LA galbi

Galbi (Korean: 갈비), also written kalbi, is a popular Korean dish of meat — most famously beef short ribs — marinated in a sweet-savoury sauce and grilled, usually at the table over charcoal. The name literally means "ribs", and the dish is a cornerstone of Korean barbecue.

Preparation

Beef short ribs are cut either along the bone in thick pieces butterflied into long strips, or across the bones in the flanken style known in the United States as "LA galbi", said to be named for Los Angeles, where Korean immigrants popularised the cut. The marinade typically combines soy sauce, sugar or honey, grated Asian pear (which sweetens and tenderises), garlic, sesame oil, black pepper, and often rice wine or ginger. After marinating for hours, the meat is grilled quickly and eaten wrapped in lettuce leaves with rice, garlic, ssamjang, and side dishes.

Variants

Pork ribs (dwaeji-galbi) may use a similar soy marinade or a spicy gochujang-based one. Galbi-jjim is a braised version simmered with vegetables and chestnuts, served on festive occasions. The Suwon region is particularly renowned for its beef galbi restaurants, and grilled galbi features prominently at Korean holidays such as Chuseok.

Cultural reach

Through the global spread of Korean barbecue restaurants, galbi has become one of the best-known Korean dishes abroad, and marinated galbi-style preparations appear widely in Korean-American cooking.
