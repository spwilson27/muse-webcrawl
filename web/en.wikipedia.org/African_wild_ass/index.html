African wild ass

The African wild ass (Equus africanus) is a wild member of the horse family, Equidae, and the ancestor of the domestic donkey. It survives in small, critically endangered populations in the deserts of Eritrea, Ethiopia and Somalia.

Description

It stands about 1.25 m at the shoulder, with a greyish coat, white underparts, a dark dorsal stripe and, in the Somali subspecies, striped legs reminiscent of a zebra. Adapted to arid country, it can endure long periods without water and tolerate substantial dehydration.

Conservation

Only a few hundred mature individuals are thought to remain in the wild. Threats include hunting, competition with livestock for water and forage, and interbreeding with domestic donkeys. It is listed as Critically Endangered by the IUCN.
