British Armed Forces

The British Armed Forces are the military services of the United Kingdom, responsible for the defence of the UK, its Overseas Territories, and the Crown Dependencies, and for promoting the UK's wider security interests. They consist of the Royal Navy, the British Army, and the Royal Air Force, together with supporting joint organisations. The monarch is the commander-in-chief, while executive authority rests with the prime minister and the secretary of state for defence through the Ministry of Defence.

Organisation

Professional headship of the armed forces belongs to the Chief of the Defence Staff. The Royal Navy, one of the world's foremost blue-water navies, operates the UK's continuous at-sea nuclear deterrent of Trident-armed Vanguard-class submarines, to be replaced by the Dreadnought class, and two Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers. The British Army fields armoured, infantry, aviation, and special forces units including the SAS. The Royal Air Force operates Typhoon and F-35B Lightning combat aircraft. Total regular personnel number roughly 140,000, supplemented by volunteer reserves.

History

The modern services trace their origins to the standing forces created after the English Civil War — the army to 1660, the Royal Navy earlier still, and the RAF, the world's first independent air force, to 1918. British forces were central to both world wars, the Korean War, the Falklands War of 1982, the Gulf War, and the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan. Since 1949 the UK has anchored its defence in NATO, and its defence spending remains among the largest in the world.
