Reentry vehicle

A conical re-entry vehicle, designed to survive atmospheric entry at hypersonic speed.

A reentry vehicle (RV) is the part of a ballistic missile, spacecraft or payload delivery system designed to survive the intense heating and deceleration of atmospheric entry and reach the Earth's surface. In the military context, the term usually denotes the housing that carries a nuclear or conventional warhead through re-entry in the terminal phase of a ballistic missile's flight; modern intercontinental ballistic missiles (ICBMs) may carry several re-entry vehicles on a single missile bus, known as MIRVs (multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles).

Design

Re-entry vehicles are typically blunt cones or cone-sphere shapes. The blunt body creates a detached shock wave that keeps most of the superheated plasma away from the surface, while an ablative heat shield — historically of carbon-phenolic or silica-phenolic composites — chars and erodes away, carrying heat with it. Shape and mass distribution also set the vehicle's ballistic coefficient, which determines how much it is slowed by the atmosphere and so affects accuracy and resistance to wind.

History

Early ballistic missiles carried their warheads in the missile body itself, but the severe heating of re-entry soon forced the development of separable vehicles. The blunt-body principle was established in the 1950s by H. Julian Allen and A. J. Eggers at NACA. Successive generations of RVs became smaller, more accurate and harder to intercept, and the same physics governs crewed capsules and sample-return vehicles, which are also, in essence, re-entry vehicles protected by ablative or reusable thermal protection systems.

Types

Beyond simple ballistic RVs, manoeuvring re-entry vehicles (MaRVs) can alter their trajectory in the terminal phase to evade defences or improve accuracy, and hypersonic glide vehicles are boosted to altitude and then glide to their targets within the atmosphere. Decoys and penetration aids may be deployed alongside genuine vehicles to complicate interception.
