Franklin Patrick Herbert Jr. (8 October 1920 – 11 February 1986) was an American science-fiction author, best known for the 1965 novel Dune and its five sequels. Dune is the best-selling science fiction novel of all time and one of the most influential works in the genre, exploring ecology, politics, religion, power and humanity's long-term future.

Early life and career

Born in Tacoma, Washington, Herbert worked as a journalist, photographer, cameraman and researcher. During the Second World War he served briefly in the US Navy as a photographer. His research into desert sand-dune control in Oregon, begun for a magazine article in the late 1950s, grew into the ecological and political epic of Arrakis, the desert planet Dune. After rejection by many publishers, Dune was issued by Chilton Books in 1965 and won the inaugural Nebula Award and shared the Hugo Award.

The Dune saga

Herbert followed Dune with Dune Messiah (1969), Children of Dune (1976), God Emperor of Dune (1981), Heretics of Dune (1984) and Chapterhouse: Dune (1985). He wrote many other novels, including The Dosadi Experiment and the Pandora sequence with Bill Ransom. He died of a pulmonary embolism in 1986 while recovering from surgery for pancreatic cancer. His son Brian Herbert, with Kevin J. Anderson, later expanded the Dune universe with numerous prequels and sequels.
