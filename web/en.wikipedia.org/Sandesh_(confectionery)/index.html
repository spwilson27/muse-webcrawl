Sandesh (confectionery)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

TypeSweet
Place of originBengal region, Indian subcontinent
Main ingredientsChhena (curdled milk), sugar or jaggery

Sandesh (Bengali: সন্দেশ, Shondesh) is a dessert, originating from the Bengal region of the Indian subcontinent, created with milk and sugar. Some recipes of sandesh call for the use of chhena or paneer, which is made by curdling milk and separating the whey from it, in place of milk itself. Some people in the region of Dhaka make a form of sandesh called pranahara (literally "heart-stealer"), which is softer and made with mawa and the essence of curd.

History

Sweets made from curdled milk developed in Bengal under the influence of Portuguese settlers in the 16th and 17th centuries, who introduced cheese-making techniques to the region. Sandesh in its refined modern form is associated with the sweet-making culture of Kolkata in the 19th century, when confectioners such as those of the famous Bhim Chandra Nag and Krishna Chandra Das shops created numerous named varieties.

Varieties

Plain sandesh is lightly cooked and delicately sweet. Many varieties are flavoured or filled: nolen gurer sandesh is made in winter with date-palm jaggery; others incorporate mango, saffron, pistachio, chocolate, or a liquid jaggery centre (jalbhara). Sandesh is commonly moulded into decorative shapes and is a standard offering at Bengali festivals, weddings, and religious observances, and a popular gift during Durga Puja and Pohela Boishakh.
