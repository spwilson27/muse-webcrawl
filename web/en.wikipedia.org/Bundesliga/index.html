The Bundesliga (German: [ˈbʊndəsˌliːɡa], "Federal League"), sometimes referred to as the Fußball-Bundesliga or 1. Bundesliga, is a professional association football league in Germany and the country's primary football competition. It comprises 18 teams and operates on a system of promotion and relegation with the 2. Bundesliga. Seasons run from August to May, with most games played on Saturdays and Sundays.

History

The Bundesliga was founded in 1963 in Dortmund by the German Football Association (DFB), unifying the regional Oberligen into a single national league. 1. FC Köln won the inaugural 1963–64 championship. Unlike other major European leagues, the Bundesliga and its clubs are governed by the "50+1 rule", under which the club's members must retain majority voting control, limiting outside investor influence.

Dominance of Bayern Munich

Bayern Munich have won the title a record 33 times, including an unprecedented run of 11 consecutive championships from 2012–13 to 2022–23, broken by Bayer Leverkusen in 2023–24, who completed the season unbeaten. Other multiple champions include Borussia Mönchengladbach, Borussia Dortmund and Werder Bremen.

Attendance

The Bundesliga regularly records among the highest average attendances in world football, drawn by affordable ticket prices, large standing terraces and strong local club identities. Borussia Dortmund's Westfalenstadion is Europe's largest club stadium by capacity.
