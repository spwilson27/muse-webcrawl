Bandoneon

The bandoneon is the signature instrument of tango music.

The bandoneon (or bandonion, Spanish: bandoneón) is a type of concertina particularly popular in Argentina and Uruguay. It is a typical instrument in most tango ensembles. As with other members of the concertina family, the bandoneon is held between both hands and played by pulling and pushing air through bellows while pressing buttons with the fingers.

History

The instrument was developed in Germany in the mid-nineteenth century, credited largely to Heinrich Band of Krefeld, from whom its name derives, as an affordable substitute for church organs in small congregations and for religious processions. German and other European emigrants carried it to the Río de la Plata region, where by the early twentieth century it had become the defining voice of the tango orchestra (orquesta típica).

Playing

Most bandoneons are bisonoric — each button produces a different note on opening and closing the bellows — making the instrument notoriously difficult to learn. A standard tango bandoneon has 71 buttons giving 142 reeds. The composer and bandleader Astor Piazzolla, who began playing as a child prodigy, raised the bandoneon to a concert instrument in his nuevo tango works.
