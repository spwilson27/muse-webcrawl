Carbon fiber

Carbon fiber (also spelled carbon fibre, and sometimes called graphite fiber) is a material consisting of fibers about 5 to 10 micrometers in diameter composed mostly of carbon atoms. The fibers have high tensile strength, low weight, high stiffness, high temperature tolerance, and low thermal expansion, making them popular in aerospace, civil engineering, military, motorsports, and high-end sporting goods.

Production

Most carbon fiber is made from polyacrylonitrile (PAN) precursor: the polymer is spun into filaments, stabilized by heating in air, carbonized in an inert atmosphere at over 1,000 °C, and surface-treated for bonding to resins. The fibers are usually woven into fabric or combined with a polymer matrix (often epoxy) to form carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP).

Applications

Major uses include airframes (the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 use CFRP extensively), wind turbine blades, racing car monocoques, bicycle frames, and strengthening wraps for concrete structures. Cost remains the main barrier to wider use in mass-market automobiles.
