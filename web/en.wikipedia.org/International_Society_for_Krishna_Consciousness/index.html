International Society for Krishna Consciousness

The International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON), otherwise known as the Hare Krishna movement, is a religious organization that follows the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, which is a monotheistic tradition within Hinduism. Its core beliefs are based on the Vedic scriptures, particularly the Bhagavad Gita and the Bhagavata Purana.

History

ISKCON was founded in New York City in 1966 by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, who had arrived from India the previous year. Drawing on the lineage of the 16th-century saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, the movement spread rapidly during the counterculture of the late 1960s and 1970s. After Prabhupada's death in 1977, governance passed to a Governing Body Commission.

Beliefs and practices

Devotees worship Krishna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Central practices include chanting the Hare Krishna mantra (Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare), congregational chanting (kirtan), book distribution, deity worship in temples, and adherence to four regulative principles: no meat-eating, no intoxication, no illicit sex, and no gambling.

Organization and reach

ISKCON reports hundreds of temples, centres, schools, and farm communities worldwide, along with vegetarian restaurants and the Food for Life relief programme. Its large temples in India, notably at Mayapur and Vrindavan, attract many pilgrims.
