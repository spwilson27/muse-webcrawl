Banff Springs Hotel

The Fairmont Banff Springs, commonly known as the Banff Springs Hotel, is a historic luxury hotel in Banff, Alberta, Canada, within Banff National Park. Styled like a Scottish baronial castle, it overlooks the Bow River valley below Sulphur Mountain.

History

The original wooden hotel opened in 1888, built by the Canadian Pacific Railway to promote tourism along its transcontinental line — part of William Cornelius Van Horne's vision: "If we can't export the scenery, we'll import the tourists." After a fire in 1926, it was rebuilt in concrete and stone in the Scottish Baronial style, reopening in 1928.

Features

The hotel has several hundred guest rooms, restaurants, a spa, and an adjacent championship golf course designed by Stanley Thompson. It is a National Historic Site of Canada.

In popular culture

The hotel is famed for ghost stories, including the tale of a bride who fell on its marble staircase, and has hosted royalty and celebrities for over a century.
