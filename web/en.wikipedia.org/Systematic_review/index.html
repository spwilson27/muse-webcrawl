Systematic review

A systematic review is a type of literature review that uses systematic methods to collect secondary data, critically appraise research studies, and synthesise findings qualitatively or quantitatively. Systematic reviews formulate research questions with a defined scope, identify and select relevant studies according to pre-specified eligibility criteria, extract data, assess the risk of bias in included studies, and analyse results—frequently using meta-analysis to pool effect estimates.

Systematic reviews of randomised controlled trials are regarded as among the highest forms of evidence in evidence-based medicine and underpin clinical practice guidelines and health technology assessments.

Methods

The standard workflow includes: (1) defining a focused question, often framed with the PICO scheme (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome); (2) writing and registering a protocol (for example in PROSPERO); (3) a comprehensive search of multiple bibliographic databases and other sources; (4) screening titles, abstracts, and full texts, typically in duplicate; (5) structured data extraction and risk-of-bias assessment with validated tools; and (6) synthesis, which may be narrative or statistical. Reporting follows the PRISMA 2020 statement, whose flow diagram documents the identification, screening, and inclusion of studies.

Organisations

The Cochrane collaboration (founded 1993, named for epidemiologist Archie Cochrane) produces and maintains a large library of systematic reviews of health interventions, notable for requiring regular updating. Campbell Collaboration reviews cover social policy and education. Hundreds of thousands of systematic reviews are now published, and methodological research addresses problems such as publication bias, selective outcome reporting, and redundancy among overlapping reviews.

Versus other reviews

Unlike a traditional narrative review, a systematic review aims to be reproducible and minimally biased: the question, search strategy, and analysis plan are fixed in advance. Rapid reviews streamline some steps for urgent decisions; scoping reviews map the breadth of a field without formal quality appraisal; umbrella reviews synthesise existing systematic reviews.
