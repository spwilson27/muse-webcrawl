Nicole Oresme

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Oresme presenting his translation of Aristotle to Charles V

Nicole Oresme (French: [nikɔl ɔʁɛm]; c. 1320 – 11 July 1382), also known as Nicolas Oresme, was a French philosopher of the later Middle Ages. He wrote influential works on economics, mathematics, physics and astronomy, was a counsellor and translator for King Charles V of France, and was later Bishop of Lisieux. He is considered one of the most original thinkers of the 14th century.

Contributions

In mathematics, Oresme developed graphical representations of varying quantities — an early forerunner of the graph and of coordinate geometry — and gave the first proof that the harmonic series diverges. In natural philosophy he discussed the possible rotation of the Earth, presenting arguments for and against it more than a century before Copernicus, and analysed motion and falling bodies in ways that anticipated later work.

His treatise De origine, natura, jure et mutationibus monetarum (c. 1360) is among the earliest works on monetary economics, condemning the debasement of coinage by rulers. Oresme translated Aristotle's Ethics and Politics into French for Charles V, coining many French words in the process, and wrote against astrology, distinguishing it from legitimate astronomy. He became dean of Rouen Cathedral and, in 1377, Bishop of Lisieux, where he died.
