Food dehydrator

A food dehydrator is an appliance that removes moisture from food in order to preserve it, using a heat source and air flow to dry fruits, vegetables, herbs and meats. Drying inhibits the growth of bacteria, yeasts and mould, dramatically extending shelf life without refrigeration. Common products include dried fruit, fruit leathers, herbs and jerky.

Design

Most domestic dehydrators consist of stackable trays above or beside a heating element and fan. Air is warmed — typically to between 35 °C and 70 °C depending on the food — and circulated across the trays so that moisture evaporates evenly. Solar dehydrators achieve the same effect using sunlight and natural convection and are used where electricity is unavailable. Drying times range from a few hours for herbs to a day or more for thick slices of fruit or meat.

Safety and technique

For meat jerky, food-safety authorities recommend heating meat to a safe internal temperature before or during drying, and produce should be washed and, for some fruits, pre-treated to prevent browning. Finished foods should be brittle or leathery with no pockets of moisture, and stored in airtight containers.

History

Drying is one of the oldest methods of food preservation, practised for millennia using sun and wind. The electric dehydrator brought the technique into the home kitchen in the twentieth century, and it remains popular with gardeners, hikers and raw-food enthusiasts.
