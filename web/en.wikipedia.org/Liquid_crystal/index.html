Liquid crystal

Liquid crystal is a state of matter whose properties are between those of conventional liquids and solid crystals: it may flow like a liquid while its molecules maintain orientational order.

Phases include nematic, smectic and cholesteric. Because their optical properties change with an applied electric field, liquid crystals are the basis of liquid-crystal displays (LCDs) used in watches, phones, monitors and televisions. Friedrich Reinitzer observed the phenomenon in cholesteryl benzoate in 1888.
