Cuauhtémoc (Nahuatl: Cuāuhtēmoc, "one who has descended like an eagle"; c. 1495–1525) was the last Aztec emperor (tlatoani) of Tenochtitlan. He succeeded his cousin Cuitláhuac, who died of smallpox during the Spanish siege, and led the Mexica resistance against Hernán Cortés until the fall of the city on 13 August 1521.

Siege of Tenochtitlan

Elevated to the throne around the age of 25, Cuauhtémoc organised the defence of the island capital, cutting the causeways and fighting from canoes and rooftops as Cortés's brigantines and indigenous allies closed in. After some eighty days of siege, starving and outnumbered, he was captured on Lake Texcoco while attempting to leave the city by canoe, and famously asked Cortés to kill him. According to legend, the Spaniards tortured him by burning his feet to reveal the location of Aztec treasure.

Death and legacy

Cuauhtémoc was kept as a hostage and taken on Cortés's expedition to Honduras, where he was hanged in 1525 on suspicion of plotting a revolt. In Mexico he is honoured as a national hero who resisted the conquest to the end; a monumental statue of him stands on Paseo de la Reforma in Mexico City, and his name is borne by a borough of the capital and by the Mexican Navy's training ship.
