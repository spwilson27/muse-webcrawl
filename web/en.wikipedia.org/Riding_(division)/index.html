Type: Historical administrative division
Etymology: Old Norse þriðjungr, "third part"
Examples: Yorkshire ridings; Lindsey; New York

Riding (division)

A riding is an administrative division of a county or other territory, historically a third part of a larger unit. The word derives from the Old Norse þriðjungr, meaning "a third part", reflecting Scandinavian settlement in parts of England.

England

The most famous examples are the three ridings of Yorkshire — the East Riding, West Riding, and North Riding — which functioned as separate administrative counties with their own quarter sessions and lieutenancies for centuries, until local-government reorganisation in 1974. The Parts of Lindsey in Lincolnshire were similarly divided into North, South, and West Ridings. The ridings retain strong cultural identities, and Yorkshire Day is celebrated on 1 August.

Elsewhere

In Canada, "riding" is the common term for an electoral district that elects a member to the House of Commons or a provincial legislature — a usage derived from the English divisions. Ridings also existed in parts of Ireland and in colonial New York, which was divided into ridings in the seventeenth century.
