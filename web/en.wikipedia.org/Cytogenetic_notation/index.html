Cytogenetic notation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cytogenetic notation is the system of symbols and abbreviations used to describe the composition of chromosomes and chromosomal abnormalities in a karyotype. The standard is maintained in the International System for Human Cytogenomic Nomenclature (ISCN), periodically revised since the 1960s.

Basic structure

A normal human karyotype is written as 46,XX (female) or 46,XY (male): the total chromosome number, followed by the sex chromosome complement. Abnormalities follow after further commas, e.g. 47,XY,+21 denotes a male with trisomy 21 (Down syndrome).

Common symbols

SymbolMeaning
pShort arm of a chromosome (petit)
qLong arm of a chromosome
delDeletion
dupDuplication
invInversion
tTranslocation, e.g. t(9;22) — the Philadelphia chromosome
iIsochromosome
rRing chromosome
mosMosaicism
+ / −Gain or loss of a chromosome or segment

Band notation

Chromosomal regions are addressed by arm, region, band, and sub-band, read outward from the centromere: for example, 7q31.2 is on chromosome 7, long arm, region 3, band 1, sub-band 2 — the locus of the CFTR gene. G-banding patterns make the bands visible under the microscope and give each chromosome a reproducible "barcode".
