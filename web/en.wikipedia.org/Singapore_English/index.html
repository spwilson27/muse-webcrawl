Singapore English

Singapore English (Singlish, SE, en-SG) is the set of varieties of the English language native to Singapore. English is one of the four official languages of Singapore, together with Malay, Mandarin Chinese and Tamil. There are two main forms of English spoken in Singapore: Standard Singapore English and Singapore Colloquial English, better known as Singlish.

English was introduced to Singapore in 1819 when the modern port was founded by Stamford Raffles. Because of the country's multilingual society, the local variety of English acquired vocabulary and grammatical features from Malay, Hokkien, Cantonese, Mandarin, Tamil and other languages spoken in the region.

Standard Singapore English

Standard Singapore English is the standard form used in formal contexts: government, education, the courts and the media. It is very similar to standard British English in grammar and spelling, reflecting Singapore's colonial history, although American English influence has been growing.

Singlish

Singlish is a creole-like colloquial variety with a distinctive vocabulary and syntax, including discourse particles such as lah, lor, meh and hor, drawn largely from Hokkien and Malay. It functions as a marker of local identity, but its use in schools and official settings is discouraged; the government's Speak Good English Movement, launched in 2000, promotes the standard variety.

Classification

Sociolinguists often describe Singapore English as a continuum, or lectal range, from acrolect (standard) through mesolect to basilect (Singlish), with speakers shifting along the range according to context, formality and audience.
