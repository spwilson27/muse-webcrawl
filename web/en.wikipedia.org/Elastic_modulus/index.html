DefinitionRatio of stress to strain in elastic deformation
SI unitPascal (Pa), usually GPa

An elastic modulus (also known as a modulus of elasticity) is a quantity that describes a material's resistance to being deformed elastically — that is, non-permanently — when a stress is applied to it. It is defined as the slope of the material's stress–strain curve in the elastic region: modulus = stress / strain. The higher the modulus, the stiffer the material.

Types of elastic modulus

Young's modulus (E) measures resistance to uniaxial stretching or compression and governs, for example, how much a beam deflects. The shear modulus (G) measures resistance to shear deformation, and the bulk modulus (K) measures resistance to uniform compression, i.e. the inverse of compressibility. For an isotropic material any two of these, together with Poisson's ratio, determine the third.

Values and measurement

Elastic moduli vary enormously: rubber has a Young's modulus of roughly 0.01–0.1 GPa, aluminium about 69 GPa, steel about 200 GPa, and diamond over 1,000 GPa. Moduli are measured in tensile tests, by resonant/ultrasonic methods, or by nanoindentation for small samples. They generally decrease with temperature.

Applications

Elastic moduli are fundamental inputs in structural engineering, mechanical design, geophysics (where seismic wave speeds reveal moduli of the Earth's interior) and materials selection. Unlike strength, stiffness is largely insensitive to microstructure and depends mainly on atomic bonding.
