Diary

A diary is a record, originally in handwritten format, with discrete entries arranged by date reporting on what has happened over the course of a day or other period. Diaries have served as personal journals, historical records, literary works, and tools of self-reflection for centuries.

History

The earliest known diaries come from Middle Eastern and East Asian cultures. The diary of Samuel Pepys, kept from 1660 to 1669, is one of the most famous in English, offering a detailed eyewitness account of the Restoration period, including the Great Plague and the Great Fire of London. In the twentieth century, The Diary of a Young Girl by Anne Frank became one of the most widely read books in the world.

Types

Diaries may be personal or institutional: travel diaries, dream journals, food diaries, war diaries, and appointment diaries all follow the dated-entry format. With the advent of the internet, online diaries and blogs became widespread, and diary-style applications are now common on smartphones.

Fictional diaries

The diary format has been used as a literary device in novels such as Dracula and Bridget Jones's Diary, presenting narrative through dated first-person entries.
