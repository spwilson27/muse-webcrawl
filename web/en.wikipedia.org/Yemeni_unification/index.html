Yemeni unification

Image placeholder: Flag of unified Yemen

Yemeni unification took place on 22 May 1990, when the Yemen Arab Republic (North Yemen) and the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen (South Yemen) merged to form the Republic of Yemen.

Background

After the Ottoman and British colonial eras, Yemen was divided into a conservative northern republic and a Marxist southern state, which fought border wars in 1972 and 1979. Economic pressures, the discovery of oil along their border, and the weakening of the Soviet Union pushed leaders Ali Abdullah Saleh and Ali Salem al-Beidh toward merger.

Union and aftermath

The unified state adopted a constitution and held parliamentary elections in 1993, but power-sharing disputes culminated in the 1994 civil war, in which northern forces prevailed and the union was preserved by force. Southern grievances continued to fuel the Southern Movement and later conflict.
