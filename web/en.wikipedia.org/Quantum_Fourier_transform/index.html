Quantum Fourier transform

In quantum computing, the quantum Fourier transform (abbreviated QFT) is a linear transformation on quantum bits, and is the quantum analogue of the discrete Fourier transform. The QFT is a key part of several quantum algorithms, most famously Shor's factoring algorithm and quantum phase estimation.

Definition and circuit

The QFT maps a basis state |j⟩ to an equal superposition of all basis states with phases given by roots of unity. For n qubits it can be implemented with a circuit of O(n²) gates — Hadamard gates and controlled phase rotations — exponentially fewer than the O(n2n) operations of the classical discrete Fourier transform on the same input size.

Applications

The inverse QFT is the final step of quantum phase estimation, which in turn powers Shor's algorithm for integer factorization and period-finding, and algorithms for solving linear systems.
