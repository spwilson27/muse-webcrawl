Birria

Birria is a traditional Mexican dish from the state of Jalisco: meat — historically goat, now often beef — marinated in an adobo of dried chiles, garlic, herbs and spices, then slow-cooked until tender in a rich broth (consomé). It is served at celebrations and as street food.

Origins

Birria arose in the colonial era, adapting Spanish-introduced goats to indigenous cooking methods including earth-oven roasting (barbacoa). Regional styles include birria tatemada (roasted, from Jalisco) and Tijuana-style beef birria served in tacos.

Preparation

Chiles such as guajillo, ancho and árbol are toasted and blended with vinegar and spices (cumin, cloves, bay, oregano). The meat marinates, then braises for hours. The skimmed, chile-stained fat is used to crisp tortillas for quesabirria tacos filled with meat and Oaxaca cheese, served with consomé for dipping.

Popularity

In the late 2010s and 2020s, quesabirria tacos spread widely in the United States via taquerias, food trucks and social media, becoming a prominent example of regional Mexican dishes achieving global popularity.
