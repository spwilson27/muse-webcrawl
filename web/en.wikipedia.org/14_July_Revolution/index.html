The 14 July Revolution, also known as the 1958 Iraqi military coup d'état, took place on 14 July 1958 in Iraq, resulting in the overthrow of the Hashemite Kingdom of Iraq. The Iraqi Republic was established following the coup, and King Faisal II, Crown Prince 'Abd al-Ilah, and Prime Minister Nuri al-Said were killed.

Background

Resentment had grown against the monarchy's close ties with Britain and its membership in the Baghdad Pact, particularly after the Suez Crisis of 1956. The Free Officers movement, modelled on the Egyptian officers who had seized power in 1952, planned the coup under Brigadier Abd al-Karim Qasim and Colonel Abdul Salam Arif.

The coup and aftermath

Troops moving through Baghdad seized the royal palace, the radio station, and key ministries in the early hours of 14 July. The royal family was killed at al-Rihab Palace. Qasim became prime minister of the new republic, which withdrew from the Baghdad Pact and pursued land reform and closer ties with the Soviet Union. The revolution ended Iraq's Hashemite era, which had begun in 1921 under Faisal I.
