Flightless bird

Flightless birds are birds that have lost the ability to fly through evolution. There are over 60 extant species, including well-known ratites (ostriches, emus, cassowaries, rheas, and kiwis) and penguins.

Evolution

Flightlessness evolves readily in environments without mammalian predators, particularly on islands: ground-feeding birds no longer needing flight for escape could redirect energy to body size and reproduction. Convergent evolution of flightlessness has occurred in many bird lineages, over 1,000 recorded times in prehistory.

Extant species

The largest flightless bird is the common ostrich (up to 2.7 m tall), the smallest the Inaccessible Island rail. The kākāpō is the only flightless parrot.

Flightlessness in penguins

Penguins lost flight while becoming specialized divers; their wings evolved into stiff flippers suited to "underwater flight."
