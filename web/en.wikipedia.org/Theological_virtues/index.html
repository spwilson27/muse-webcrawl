Theological virtues

The virtuesFaith, Hope, Charity (Love)
Source1 Corinthians 13:13
TraditionChristian theology

The theological virtues are the three virtues of faith, hope, and charity (or love) in Christian theology. They are called "theological" because they relate directly to God: they have God as their origin, their motive, and their object. The list derives principally from the Apostle Paul's First Letter to the Corinthians: "And now these three remain: faith, hope and love. But the greatest of these is love" (1 Corinthians 13:13).

Background

The theological virtues are traditionally contrasted with the four cardinal virtues — prudence, justice, fortitude, and temperance — which are attainable through human effort and reason. In Catholic theology, the theological virtues are considered "infused" virtues, given by God through grace rather than acquired by practice, and they dispose Christians to live in relationship with the Holy Trinity.

The three virtues

Faith is the virtue by which one believes in God and in what God has revealed. Hope is the virtue by which one confidently awaits eternal life and the means to attain it, relying on God's help. Charity is the virtue by which one loves God above all things and one's neighbour as oneself for the love of God; Paul and the subsequent tradition regard charity as the greatest of the virtues, the "form" of all the others.

In moral theology

Thomas Aquinas treated the theological virtues extensively in the Summa Theologiae, placing them at the summit of the moral life. The Catechism of the Catholic Church likewise presents them as the foundation and animating principle of the Christian's moral activity.
