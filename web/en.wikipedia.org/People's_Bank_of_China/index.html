People's Bank of China

The People's Bank of China (PBC or PBOC) is the central bank of the People's Republic of China, responsible for carrying out monetary policy and regulation of financial institutions in mainland China. It has the largest stock of financial assets of any central bank in the world.

Headquarters: Beijing, China
Established: 1 December 1948
Currency: Renminbi (CNY)
Governor: Pan Gongsheng (from 2023)

History

The bank was established on 1 December 1948, based on the consolidation of the Huabei Bank, the Beihai Bank and the Xibei Farmer Bank, with its headquarters initially in Shijiazhuang, Hebei, before moving to Beijing in 1949. Between 1949 and 1978 the PBC performed both central bank and commercial bank functions.

In September 1983 the State Council decided that the PBC would function exclusively as a central bank; its commercial lending functions were transferred to newly created state-owned banks, including the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China. Its central bank status was legally confirmed in 1995.

Functions

The PBC formulates and implements monetary policy, issues the renminbi, regulates interbank markets, manages China's foreign exchange reserves through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), and works to prevent systemic financial risk. Unlike many Western central banks, the PBC is not formally independent: its major policy decisions require approval of the State Council.

Interest rates and reserves

The PBC sets benchmark interest rates and uses the required reserve ratio (RRR) for commercial banks as a primary policy tool. It manages one of the world's largest pools of foreign exchange reserves, which peaked at around US$4 trillion in 2014.
