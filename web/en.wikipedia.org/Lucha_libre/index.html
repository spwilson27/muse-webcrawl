Lucha libre

Lucha libre (Spanish for "free fight") is the term for professional wrestling in Mexico. It is characterised by colourful masks, rapid sequences of holds and maneuvers, and high-flying aerial techniques. Wrestlers, known as luchadores, are divided into rudos (villains, who break the rules) and técnicos (heroes, who play by the rules and perform the more spectacular maneuvers).

History

Professional wrestling was introduced to Mexico in the early 20th century, but the distinctly Mexican style was established in 1933 when Salvador Lutteroth founded the Empresa Mexicana de Lucha Libre (EMLL), today known as Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre (CMLL), the world's oldest still-active wrestling promotion. Lutteroth is often called the father of lucha libre.

Masks

The mask (máscara) is sacred in lucha libre; a wrestler who loses his mask in a lucha de apuestas (betting match) must unmask in public and reveal his identity, and ordinarily may never wear that mask again. Famous masked wrestlers include El Santo and Blue Demon, cultural icons who starred in numerous films. Masks have roots in Aztec and Mayan imagery and in the masks of early 20th-century circus and wrestling performers.

Match types

Most lucha libre matches are contested by teams, commonly trios matches (luchas de tríos) of three against three, fought over the best of three falls. Betting matches (luchas de apuestas), in which the loser loses his mask or hair, are the most prestigious contests.

In Mexican culture

Lucha libre is a major element of Mexican popular culture. In 2018 it was declared an intangible cultural heritage of Mexico City. Its imagery appears widely in art, film and advertising.
