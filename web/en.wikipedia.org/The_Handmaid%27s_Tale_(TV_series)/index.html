The Handmaid's Tale (TV series)

The Handmaid's Tale is an American dystopian television series created by Bruce Miller, based on the 1985 novel of the same name by Canadian author Margaret Atwood. The series was ordered by the streaming service Hulu and premiered on April 26, 2017. It ran for six seasons, concluding in 2025.

Premise

After a Second American Civil War, a totalitarian, theocratic society subjects fertile women, called "Handmaids", to ritualised child-bearing slavery. The story follows June Osborne (Elisabeth Moss), renamed Offred, who is assigned to the home of Commander Fred Waterford and his wife Serena Joy. As June struggles to survive and to find the daughter taken from her, the series expands into a portrait of resistance against the regime of Gilead.

Reception and awards

The first season won eight Primetime Emmy Awards from thirteen nominations, including Outstanding Drama Series — the first series on a streaming service to win the award — and Outstanding Lead Actress for Moss; Ann Dowd won for supporting actress, and Alexis Bledel for guest actress. The red robes and white bonnets of the Handmaids became a widely used symbol in real-world protests concerning women's rights. Later seasons received more mixed reviews while remaining awards contenders; a sequel series based on Atwood's 2019 novel The Testaments was put into development.
