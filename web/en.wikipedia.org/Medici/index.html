House of Medici

The House of Medici (Italian: de' Medici) was an Italian banking family and political dynasty that first consolidated power in the Republic of Florence under Cosimo de' Medici during the first half of the 15th century. The family originated in the Mugello region of Tuscany and rose to wealth through the Medici Bank, then the largest financial institution in Europe. Their patronage enabled the Italian Renaissance, and the dynasty produced four popes and two queens of France, and ruled Florence and later Tuscany — as dukes and grand dukes — until the main line died out in 1737.

Rise of the bank

Giovanni di Bicci de' Medici founded the Medici Bank in Florence in 1397. Under his son Cosimo (1389–1464), the bank ran branches across Europe and became banker to the papacy. Cosimo used his wealth to dominate Florentine politics from 1434 while preserving republican forms, and to patronise artists and scholars including Brunelleschi and Donatello.

Lorenzo the Magnificent

Cosimo's grandson Lorenzo (1449–1492) presided over the high point of Medici Florence, surviving the Pazzi conspiracy of 1478 and patronising Michelangelo and Botticelli. After Lorenzo's death, his son Piero was expelled in 1494; the family regained Florence in 1512 with Spanish and papal support.

Popes, dukes and grand dukes

Giovanni, Lorenzo's second son, became Pope Leo X in 1513; his cousin Giulio became Clement VII. In 1532 Alessandro de' Medici was made hereditary Duke of Florence, and in 1537 Cosimo I established the cadet line that ruled as Dukes and, from 1569, Grand Dukes of Tuscany. Catherine de' Medici (1519–1589), daughter of Lorenzo II, became queen consort of France and a dominant figure as queen mother; Marie de' Medici later also became queen of France.

Decline and legacy

The grand duchy stagnated in the 17th and early 18th centuries. The last Medici grand duke, Gian Gastone, died childless in 1737, and Tuscany passed to Francis of Lorraine by the settlement known as the Family Pact arranged by Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici, who bequeathed the family's art collections to Florence, stipulating they never leave the city — the basis of the Uffizi's holdings.
