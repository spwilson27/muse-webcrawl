Shakuntala

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shakuntala (Sanskrit: शाकुन्तला, Śakuntalā) is a heroine of ancient Indian literature, the heroine of the story told in the Mahābhārata and dramatised most famously by Kālidāsa in his Sanskrit play Abhijñānaśākuntalam ("The Recognition of Shakuntala"), generally considered the masterpiece of Sanskrit drama. She is the daughter of the sage Vishvamitra and the apsara Menaka, mother of the emperor Bharata, from whom India takes its Sanskrit name, Bhārata.

The story

Abandoned at birth, Shakuntala was found by the sage Kanva among birds (śakunta) and raised in his forest hermitage. King Dushyanta, hunting in the forest, fell in love with her, and they married by the gandharva rite. Departing, he gave her a signet ring. Absentmindedly failing to attend to the irascible sage Durvasa, she was cursed to be forgotten by her beloved until he saw the ring again. The ring was lost in a river; when Shakuntala came to court, Dushyanta did not know her, and she withdrew in shame. A fisherman later recovered the ring from a fish, memory returned, and after years of separation the king found Shakuntala and their son Bharata at the hermitage of Maricha, and the family was reunited.

Reception

Kālidāsa's play, dating to roughly the 4th–5th century CE, became the first work of Indian literature to achieve fame in the West: William Jones's English translation of 1789 electrified European writers, and Goethe famously praised the play in verse. It has been retold in dance-drama, opera, and Indian cinema ever since.
