Arctic amplification

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Arctic amplification is the phenomenon whereby the Arctic is warming faster than the rest of the planet. Also termed polar amplification when discussed generally for both poles, the effect is robustly observed: recent studies estimate the Arctic has warmed nearly four times faster than the global average since 1979, with the Barents Sea region warming faster still. Arctic amplification was anticipated by early climate models and Svante Arrhenius's calculations in the 19th century, and it is a central feature of projected climate change.

Mechanisms

Several feedbacks and processes contribute:

  • Ice–albedo feedback — melting snow and sea ice expose darker ocean and land surfaces that absorb more solar radiation.
  • Lapse-rate feedback — in the stably stratified Arctic atmosphere, warming is concentrated near the surface rather than radiated from aloft, amplifying surface temperature rise.
  • Planck feedback differences — cold regions radiate less additional energy per degree of warming than warm regions.
  • Water vapour and cloud changes — increased atmospheric moisture and cloud cover trap outgoing longwave radiation, especially in the dark polar winter.
  • Heat transport — changes in atmospheric and oceanic transport carry additional heat poleward.

Relative contributions vary by season; amplification is strongest in autumn and winter, when heat stored in newly ice-free ocean is released to the atmosphere.

Observed changes and impacts

September Arctic sea ice extent has declined by roughly 13 per cent per decade since 1979, and multi-year ice has thinned dramatically. Greenland's ice sheet loses mass each year, permafrost is thawing (releasing carbon and damaging infrastructure), and boreal ecosystems are shifting. Amplified warming reduces the equator-to-pole temperature gradient; a debated hypothesis holds that this weakens and meanders the jet stream, influencing mid-latitude weather extremes such as heat waves and cold outbreaks, though the strength of this link remains contested in the literature.
