Gustav Roch

Gustav Adolf Roch
Born: 9 December 1839, Dresden, Germany
Died: 21 November 1866, Venice, Italy
Known for: Riemann–Roch theorem

Gustav Adolf Roch (9 December 1839 – 21 November 1866) was a German mathematician who made important contributions to the theory of Riemann surfaces. He studied at Leipzig, Göttingen and Berlin, where he worked in the circle of Bernhard Riemann.

Riemann–Roch theorem

Roch's name is attached to the Riemann–Roch theorem, a fundamental result relating the dimension of the space of meromorphic functions on a compact Riemann surface to the surface's genus and the degree of a divisor. Riemann had proved one part of the relation as an inequality; Roch's 1865 dissertation supplied the complementary term that turned it into an equality.

Death

Roch's career was cut short by tuberculosis; he died in Venice in November 1866, aged 26, while travelling in search of a better climate.
