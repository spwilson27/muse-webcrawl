Saturday-morning cartoon

A Saturday-morning cartoon is an animated television series or programming block traditionally aired on Saturday mornings in the United States, chiefly on the major broadcast networks, from the 1960s through the early 2000s. The blocks were aimed at children and were dominated by animation studios such as Hanna-Barbera, Filmation, and Warner Bros.

History

Networks discovered in the 1960s that Saturday mornings offered a dependable child audience attractive to advertisers of toys, cereal, and snacks. Series such as The Flintstones (in repeats), Jonny Quest, Scooby-Doo, Where Are You!, and later The Smurfs and He-Man defined the era. Each autumn the networks unveiled their new line-ups with prime-time preview specials.

Decline

The tradition faded from the 1990s onward: cable channels such as Nickelodeon and Cartoon Network offered cartoons all week, the Children's Television Act of 1990 and FCC rules required educational programming, and home video and streaming changed viewing habits. By the mid-2010s the major networks had replaced Saturday-morning animation blocks with live-action educational or news programming.
