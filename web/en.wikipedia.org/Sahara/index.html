The Sahara is the largest hot desert in the world and the third-largest desert overall, after Antarctica and the Arctic. Covering most of North Africa — an area comparable to China or the United States — it stretches from the Atlantic Ocean in the west to the Red Sea in the east, and from the Mediterranean Sea in the north to the Sahel belt in the south. Its name comes from the Arabic ṣaḥrāʾ, simply meaning "desert".

Geography

Contrary to popular imagery, sand seas (ergs) cover only a minority of the Sahara. Much of it consists of barren rock plateaus (hamadas), gravel plains (regs), dry valleys, salt flats and mountain ranges including the Ahaggar of Algeria and the Tibesti of Chad, whose Emi Koussi is the desert's highest peak. The Nile is the great exception to its dryness, sustaining Egypt through an otherwise waterless region.

Climate

The Sahara is among the harshest climates on Earth: some areas receive less than 25 mm of rain a year, and decades may pass without rain in the hyper-arid core. Summer daytime temperatures above 50 °C have been recorded, yet nights can approach freezing. The dry harmattan wind carries dust far across the Atlantic, fertilising the Amazon rainforest with minerals. Climate has not been constant: driven by orbital cycles, the Sahara was a green savanna with lakes and rock art of cattle herders as recently as 5,000–7,000 years ago, during the African Humid Period.

People and history

Despite its harshness, the Sahara has long been inhabited, by peoples including the Tuareg, Toubou, Berbers, Arabs and others. Trans-Saharan caravan routes carried gold, salt and slaves between West Africa and the Mediterranean for over a millennium; famous trading cities such as Timbuktu grew rich on the trade. Today the population is concentrated in oases and along the desert margins, while the interior holds significant oil, gas, phosphate and uranium resources.

Flora and fauna

Adapted wildlife includes the dromedary camel, addax, fennec fox, sand cat, dorcas gazelle and the Saharan cheetah; vegetation is sparse, dominated by drought-resistant grasses, acacias and oasis date palms. Desertification of the southern fringe, the Sahel, is a major environmental concern, addressed in part by the Great Green Wall initiative.
