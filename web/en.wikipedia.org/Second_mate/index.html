Second mate

The second mate is traditionally the ship's navigation officer.

A second mate (also second officer) is a licensed member of the deck department of a merchant ship, holding a Second Mate's Certificate of Competency. The second mate is the third in command of the vessel, after the master and the chief mate, and serves as the ship's navigation officer.

Duties

The second mate is responsible for the upkeep of charts and nautical publications, the planning of passages (voyage planning), and the maintenance of navigational equipment including the gyrocompass, radar and electronic chart systems (ECDIS). At sea the second mate traditionally keeps the 12-to-4 watch (the "graveyard watch" at night and the afternoon watch). In port, the second mate supervises cargo operations as directed by the chief mate and may be assigned medical duties on some vessels.

Licensing

Under the STCW Convention, a second mate's certificate (officer in charge of a navigational watch, operational level) requires approved sea service as a cadet or rating, completion of courses in navigation, cargo handling, meteorology and safety, and passing examinations in chartwork, celestial navigation and seamanship. With further sea time and examinations, a second mate may progress to chief mate and then master.

Watch system

In the traditional three-officer watch system, the chief mate keeps the 4-to-8, the second mate the 12-to-4, and the third mate the 8-to-12 watches, each watch lasting four hours on and eight hours off.
