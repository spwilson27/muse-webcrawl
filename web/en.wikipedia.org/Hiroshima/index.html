Hiroshima

Hiroshima (Japanese: 広島市, Hiroshima-shi) is the capital of Hiroshima Prefecture in Japan and the largest city in the Chūgoku region of western Honshu. Founded in 1589 as a castle town on the Ōta River delta, it became a major military and industrial centre during the Meiji era and the Empire of Japan.

The atomic bombing

On 6 August 1945, during the final stages of the Second World War, Hiroshima became the first city in history to be targeted by a nuclear weapon. The United States Army Air Forces B-29 bomber Enola Gay dropped the "Little Boy" uranium fission bomb, which detonated over the city centre. Tens of thousands were killed outright, and many more died later from burns and radiation sickness; estimates of the death toll by the end of 1945 range from about 70,000 to 140,000. The ruined Hiroshima Prefectural Industrial Promotion Hall, now the Hiroshima Peace Memorial (Genbaku Dome), was preserved and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The modern city

Rebuilt after the war, Hiroshima was proclaimed a City of Peace in 1949. The Peace Memorial Park and Peace Memorial Museum draw visitors from around the world. The modern city, with a population of about 1.2 million, is a centre for the automotive industry (Mazda is headquartered nearby), shipbuilding, and tourism; Itsukushima Shrine on nearby Miyajima is famed for its "floating" torii gate.
