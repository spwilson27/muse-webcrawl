Palazzo Pitti

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Palazzo Pitti (Italian pronunciation: [paˈlattso ˈpitti]) is a vast Renaissance palace in Florence, Italy, situated on the south bank of the Arno river, a short distance from the Ponte Vecchio. Today it houses several major museums of the Gallerie degli Uffizi group.

History

Construction began in 1458 for the banker Luca Pitti, a rival of the Medici, to a design associated with Filippo Brunelleschi's circle. The original building was only the central block of the present facade. In 1549 the palace, unfinished and encumbered by the Pitti family's declining fortunes, was bought by Eleonora di Toledo, wife of Cosimo I de' Medici, and became the chief residence of the Medici grand dukes of Tuscany.

Under the Medici and their successors the palace was greatly enlarged, acquiring its two lateral wings and its great courtyard by Bartolomeo Ammannati. Behind it were laid out the Boboli Gardens, the prototype of the Italian garden. After the extinction of the Medici line in 1737, the palace passed to the House of Lorraine, and later served as a residence of Napoleon and of the kings of Italy when Florence was briefly the national capital (1865–1871). Victor Emmanuel III donated it to the state in 1919.

Museums

The palace contains the Palatine Gallery, with works by Raphael, Titian, Rubens and others displayed in frescoed state rooms; the Gallery of Modern Art; the Treasury of the Grand Dukes (Museo degli Argenti); the Museum of Costume and Fashion; the Porcelain Museum; and the Carriages Museum. Together with the Boboli Gardens it forms one of Florence's principal museum complexes.
