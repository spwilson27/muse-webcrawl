Industrial disaster

An industrial disaster is a disaster caused by industrial companies, either by accident, negligence or incompetence. They are a form of industrial accident where great damage, injury or loss of life is caused. Other disasters can also be considered industrial disasters, if their causes are rooted in the products or processes of industry. Industrial disasters have often led to major reforms in occupational safety and environmental law.

Notable disasters

  • Bhopal disaster (1984) — a methyl isocyanate leak from a Union Carbide pesticide plant in Bhopal, India, killed thousands (estimates range from about 3,800 to over 10,000) and injured hundreds of thousands; it is regarded as the world's worst industrial disaster.
  • Chernobyl disaster (1986) — explosion and fire at a Soviet nuclear power plant released radioactive contamination across Europe.
  • Halifax Explosion (1917) — a munitions ship collision destroyed much of Halifax, killing about 2,000.
  • Texas City disaster (1947) — the explosion of ammonium nitrate aboard the SS Grandcamp killed 581 people.
  • Seveso disaster (1976) — dioxin release in Italy that led to the EU's Seveso Directives on major-accident hazards.
  • Deepwater Horizon (2010) — offshore drilling rig explosion causing the largest marine oil spill in US history.
  • Rana Plaza collapse (2013) — garment factory building collapse in Bangladesh killing 1,134 workers, prompting global factory-safety accords.

Causes

Investigations commonly find layered causes: design flaws, inadequate maintenance, cost-cutting, poor training, regulatory failure and organisational cultures that discount warnings. The study of such disasters informs process-safety engineering, the "Swiss cheese" model of accident causation, and high-reliability organisation theory.
