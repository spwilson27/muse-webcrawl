Hypothermia

Definition: Core temperature below 35 °C (95 °F)
Causes: Cold exposure, immersion
Field: Emergency medicine

Hypothermia is defined as a core body temperature below 35.0 °C (95.0 °F) in humans. It occurs when heat loss exceeds the body's ability to produce heat, most commonly from exposure to cold weather or immersion in cold water.

Classification and symptoms

Mild hypothermia (32–35 °C) brings shivering, confusion, and loss of fine motor control. In moderate hypothermia (28–32 °C) shivering stops, consciousness declines, and the heart becomes irritable. Below 28 °C, severe hypothermia, the victim appears lifeless, with fixed pupils and high risk of ventricular fibrillation. Paradoxical undressing and terminal burrowing behaviour are documented in fatal cases.

Physiology

The hypothalamus regulates temperature; initial responses are vasoconstriction and shivering thermogenesis. Alcohol, sedatives, hypothyroidism, sepsis, old age, and infancy impair these defences. Cold diuresis depletes circulating volume.

Treatment

Management emphasises gentle handling, insulation, and rewarming: passive external rewarming for mild cases, active external and internal rewarming (warmed intravenous fluids, heated humidified oxygen) for worse, and extracorporeal life support for cardiac arrest or instability. The aphorism that a victim "is not dead until warm and dead" reflects rare recoveries after prolonged arrest at very low temperatures.

Prevention and uses

Layered clothing, shelter, and avoiding immersion prevent most cases. Controlled hypothermia is induced medically in some cardiac surgery and was historically used after cardiac arrest (targeted temperature management).
