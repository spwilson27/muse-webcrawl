Lighthouse of Alexandria

The Lighthouse of Alexandria, sometimes called the Pharos of Alexandria, was a lighthouse built by the Ptolemaic Kingdom of Egypt on the island of Pharos at the entrance to the harbour of Alexandria. Completed around 280 BC under Ptolemy II Philadelphus, it stood over 100 metres (330 ft) tall — among the tallest human-made structures of the ancient world — and was counted among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. It served as the prototype for all later lighthouses, and the word pharos gave many languages their word for "lighthouse".

Construction

The tower was begun under Ptolemy I Soter and completed under his son, around 280 BC. Ancient tradition attributes the design to the architect Sostratus of Cnidus, whose name an inscription reportedly once bore beneath a dedication to Ptolemy. It stood on the eastern tip of Pharos Island, connected to the city by a causeway, the Heptastadion.

Description

Arabic descriptions and ancient coins and mosaics suggest three stacked tiers: a square base, an octagonal middle section, and a cylindrical top carrying a lantern and a statue — variously identified as Zeus or Poseidon. A fire burned at the top at night, possibly magnified by mirrors, and could be seen from far out at sea; later writers claimed the light was visible some 47 km away.

Destruction

The Pharos survived into the Islamic period and was repeatedly repaired, but a series of earthquakes progressively destroyed it: damage in 956 and 1303, and a final destructive quake in 1323 left it a ruin. Sultan Qaitbay built his citadel on the site in 1477, reusing some of its stone.

Rediscovery

In 1994 French underwater archaeologists working in Alexandria's Eastern Harbour located massive fallen blocks, sphinxes, and columns on the seabed attributed to the Pharos and the Ptolemaic palace quarter. The site is now protected as part of an underwater archaeological park.
