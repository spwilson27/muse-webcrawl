Lighthouse of Alexandria

The Pharos of Alexandria, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World

The Lighthouse of Alexandria, sometimes called the Pharos of Alexandria, was a lighthouse built by the Ptolemaic Kingdom of Ancient Egypt, during the reign of Ptolemy II Philadelphus (280–247 BC), which has been estimated to be at least 100 metres (330 ft) in overall height. One of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, for many centuries it was one of the tallest man-made structures in the world.

The lighthouse was built on the island of Pharos at the entrance to the harbor of Alexandria, the city founded by Alexander the Great in 331 BC, and was designed by the Greek architect Sostratus of Cnidus. It consisted of three tapering tiers—a square lower section, an octagonal middle, and a cylindrical top—surmounted by a statue, with a fire and a system of mirrors (possibly of polished bronze) projecting its light out to sea. Its name, Pharos, became the generic word for "lighthouse" in many languages, including French (phare) and Italian (faro).

Destruction and rediscovery

Badly damaged by earthquakes in 956 and 1303, the lighthouse collapsed almost entirely in the earthquake of 1323. In 1480 the Mamluk sultan Qaitbey built a citadel on its foundations, reusing its stone. The Pharos survived in memory through coins, mosaics, and travelers' descriptions. In 1994, French archaeologist Jean-Yves Empereur discovered enormous submerged blocks and statues of the lighthouse on the seabed of Alexandria's Eastern Harbor, confirming its location and scale.
