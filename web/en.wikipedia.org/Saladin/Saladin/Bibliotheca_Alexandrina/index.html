Bibliotheca Alexandrina

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina, inaugurated in 2002

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina (Latin, 'Library of Alexandria') is a major library and cultural center on the shore of the Mediterranean Sea in Alexandria, Egypt. It is a commemoration of the ancient Library of Alexandria, which was lost in antiquity, and an attempt to rekindle something of the brilliance that the original library represented. The idea of reviving the old library dates from 1974, when a committee set up by Alexandria University selected a site for its new library.

Construction work began in 1995 after some $65 million had been raised for the project. The project was allocated an area near the Eastern Harbor, close to where the ancient library is thought to have stood. The Bibliotheca Alexandrina was inaugurated on 16 October 2002 in the presence of dignitaries from around the world.

Architecture

The competition-winning design, by the Norwegian firm Snøhetta, is a striking cylinder of concrete and glass tilted toward the sea like a rising sun, some 33 metres high and 160 metres in diameter. Its exterior wall of Aswan granite is carved with characters from 120 different human scripts. Inside, the vast main reading room terraces over fourteen levels beneath a glazed roof, with space for up to eight million books; the library was built with substantial shelf space left empty to allow for future growth. The complex also houses museums, a planetarium, specialized libraries (including one for children and the Taha Hussein Library for the blind), art galleries, and a conference center.

Role

Beyond lending and research, the Bibliotheca Alexandrina hosts exhibitions, conferences, and concerts, holds a copy of the Internet Archive, and runs digital preservation projects, including the digitization of rare manuscripts. It serves as a symbol of Alexandria's ancient heritage as a center of learning and of Egypt's modern cultural ambitions.
