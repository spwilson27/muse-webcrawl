Battle of Hattin

The Battle of Hattin took place on 3–4 July 1187 near the village of Hattin in Galilee, between the Crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem and the forces of Saladin, founder of the Ayyubid dynasty. It was a catastrophic defeat for the Crusaders that led directly to the fall of Jerusalem and provoked the Third Crusade.

Background

The truce between Saladin and Jerusalem collapsed after Raynald of Châtillon attacked Muslim caravans and a pilgrim convoy. Saladin invaded Galilee and besieged Tiberias, whose citadel held the wife of Raymond III of Tripoli. King Guy of Lusignan, against Raymond's advice, marched the kingdom's entire field army — about 1,200 knights and 15,000–18,000 infantry — across waterless country to relieve it.

The battle

Harassed by horse archers and cut off from water, the Crusader army camped thirsty on the plateau between the twin hills known as the Horns of Hattin. Saladin's forces set fire to the dry brush, adding smoke to the army's torment. On 4 July the Crusaders attempted to break through to the springs of Hattin and the Sea of Galilee but were driven back; the infantry crowded onto the Horns while the cavalry charges failed. By evening the army disintegrated.

Aftermath

King Guy and Raynald of Châtillon were captured; Saladin personally executed Raynald for his oath-breaking, while Guy was later ransomed. The relic of the True Cross was taken, and the military orders suffered devastating losses — the captured Templars and Hospitallers were executed on Saladin's orders. With the kingdom's army destroyed, Saladin captured Acre, Beirut, Sidon and, after a short siege, Jerusalem itself on 2 October 1187.
