Bibliotheca Alexandrina

The Bibliotheca Alexandrina is a major library and cultural centre in Alexandria, Egypt, opened on 16 October 2002. Built near the site of the ancient Library of Alexandria, it commemorates the lost library of antiquity while functioning as a modern research library, museum complex, and centre for dialogue. It was constructed under the auspices of the Egyptian government with UNESCO support, following a design by the Norwegian architectural firm Snøhetta, winner of an international competition.

Concept and construction

The idea of reviving the great library of Alexandria gained force in the 1970s at the University of Alexandria and was adopted as a national project by Egypt, with UNESCO backing from 1986. An international architectural competition in 1988 drew over 1,300 entries; the winning design by Snøhetta envisioned a vast tilting disc rising from the harbour. The Egyptian government donated the land near the ancient library's presumed location on the Corniche, and fundraising targeted roughly US$200 million. The library was inaugurated in 2002 with heads of state and Nobel laureates attending.

Architecture

The main building is a cylinder 160 metres in diameter, tilted so that its roof slopes down toward the Mediterranean like a rising sun. The exterior wall of Aswan granite is carved with characters from some 120 scripts of the world. Inside, the main reading room cascades over seven terraced levels beneath a glazed roof, accommodating around 2,000 readers and said to be among the largest reading rooms in the world.

Collections and facilities

Beyond the general collection — strong in Arabic and Mediterranean studies — the complex includes specialised libraries for children, young people, the blind and visually impaired (the Taha Hussein Library), and rare books and manuscripts; four museums (Antiquities, Manuscripts, the Sadat Museum, and a History of Science museum); a planetarium science centre; art galleries; and research institutes. The library hosts an Egyptian mirror/archive of the Internet Archive, preserving hundreds of billions of web pages. It holds millions of volumes, with donations from France, the United States, and other countries forming its core collections.
