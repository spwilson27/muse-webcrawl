Jagannath

Jagannath (Sanskrit: जगन्नाथ, romanised: Jagannātha, literally "Lord of the Universe") is a deity worshipped in regional traditions of Hinduism in India and Bangladesh. Jagannath is considered a form of Vishnu — or of Krishna in the Vaishnava tradition — and is worshipped as part of a triad with his brother Balabhadra and his sister Subhadra. His principal temple is the Jagannath Temple at Puri, Odisha, one of the four Char Dham pilgrimage sites.

Iconography

Unlike most Hindu deities, Jagannath is represented not anthropomorphically but as a stylised wooden figure: a large, round-eyed, symmetrically featured head with a square, painted face, almost no neck, and stubby arms, carved from neem wood. The idols of the triad are renewed in a sacred ceremony called Nabakalebara, usually every 12 or 19 years, when the old images are ritually buried and replaced. The abstract form is thought by many scholars to preserve very ancient, possibly tribal, origins within Hindu worship.

Worship and festivals

Jagannath worship is notable for its inclusiveness and for the deity's close integration with the life of Odisha, where he is treated as the state's true sovereign — the Gajapati king of Puri serves as his first servant and sweeps his chariot with a golden broom. The temple's vast kitchen is said to be the largest in the world, and its consecrated food (mahaprasad) is shared by devotees of all castes.

The most famous festival is Ratha Yatra (also Ratha Jatra), the great chariot festival of Puri, in which the three deities are drawn through the streets on enormous wooden chariots before millions of pilgrims. The festival is celebrated worldwide by the Hare Krishna movement (ISKCON) and gave the English language the word "juggernaut", from the chariots' immense size.

Significance

Jagannath has been revered across Hindu traditions — by Vaishnavas as Krishna, by Shaivas, by followers of Adi Shankara (who founded one of the Char Dham monasteries at Puri), and even in Jain and early Buddhist contexts. Saint-poets such as Chaitanya Mahaprabhu deepened his association with Krishna bhakti, while in Odia culture Jagannath is a unifying symbol of regional identity.
