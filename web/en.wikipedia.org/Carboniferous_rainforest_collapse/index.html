Carboniferous rainforest collapse
Date~305 million years ago (Moscovian–Kasimovian boundary)
TypeExtinction / ecosystem collapse event
CauseClimate cooling and drying
AffectedTropical lycopsid rainforests of Euramerica; amphibian faunas

The Carboniferous rainforest collapse was a minor extinction event that occurred around 305 million years ago in the Carboniferous period. It altered the vast coal forests that covered the equatorial region of Euramerica, fragmenting them into isolated "islands" as the climate became cooler and drier.

Causes

The collapse is attributed to climate change driven by the intensification of the Late Palaeozoic Ice Age: falling temperatures and increased seasonality dried out the tropical wetlands on which the rainforests depended.

Effects

The dominant tree-like lycopsids, such as Lepidodendron, largely died out and were replaced by tree ferns in wetter refuges, while drier habitats were colonised by seed plants. Among animals, large amphibians dependent on wet environments declined, whereas early reptiles (amniotes), freed from dependence on water for reproduction, diversified — a shift some researchers see as paving the way for later terrestrial ecosystems.
