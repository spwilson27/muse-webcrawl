Paul Abadie (9 November 1812 – 3 August 1884) was a French architect, best known as the designer of the Basilica of the Sacré-Cœur in Montmartre, Paris. He was a prominent figure in the French movement for the restoration of medieval buildings in the 19th century.

Life and career

Abadie was born in Paris, the son of the architect Paul Abadie the Elder, and studied at the École des Beaux-Arts. He worked in the atelier of the restoration service under figures including Eugène Viollet-le-Duc, participating in the restoration of Notre-Dame de Paris. Appointed diocesan architect for the south-west, he restored numerous Romanesque churches in the Charente, Dordogne, and Gironde regions, most notably the cathedrals of Angoulême (Saint-Pierre) and Périgueux (Saint-Front), whose domed silhouette strongly influenced his later work. His restorations, like those of his contemporaries, often involved substantial rebuilding in the supposed original style, an approach later criticised by conservationists.

Sacré-Cœur

In 1873 Abadie won the competition for the basilica vowed by the National Assembly after the Franco-Prussian War and the Paris Commune, to be built on the hill of Montmartre. His Romano-Byzantine design — white travertine domes, an elongated central cupola, and a tall campanile — drew on Saint-Front de Périgueux. Construction began in 1875; Abadie died in 1884 and the work was completed by successors with modifications, the basilica being consecrated in 1919. The Sacré-Cœur became one of the most recognisable landmarks of Paris and a defining example of 19th-century French ecclesiastical architecture.
