Cold reading

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cold reading is a set of techniques used by mentalists, psychics, fortune-tellers, and mediums to imply that the reader knows much more about the person than they actually do. Without prior knowledge, a practiced cold reader can quickly obtain a great deal of information by analyzing the person's body language, age, clothing, fashion, hairstyle, gender, religion, education level, manner of speech, place of origin, and so on, during a line of questioning.

Techniques

Cold readers commonly use high-probability guesses, quickly picking up on signals of whether their guesses are in the right direction and emphasizing and reinforcing correct interpretations while restricting or discarding incorrect ones. Key methods include:

  • Barnum statements — vague, generally applicable personality descriptions ("You have a great need for other people to like and admire you") that the subject accepts as uniquely true of themselves, an effect also called the Barnum or Forer effect.
  • Fishing — phrasing statements as questions or impressions and watching reactions, then developing hits and quietly dropping misses.
  • Shotgunning — firing broad statements at a large audience ("I'm getting an older male figure, a father or grandfather...") and homing in on whoever responds.
  • Fine flattery and "push statements" — assertions constructed so that either outcome can be claimed as correct.

Explanations and skepticism

Skeptics and magicians, notably Ian Rowland in his book The Full Facts Book of Cold Reading, have documented these techniques to explain how readings work without paranormal powers. The subject's own cooperation — filling in details, remembering hits rather than misses (confirmation bias), and the subjective-validation effect — supplies most of the apparent accuracy. Cold reading is also used benignly by actors to improvise believable interaction, and in sales and interrogation contexts. It is distinguished from "hot reading", in which information is secretly gathered in advance.
