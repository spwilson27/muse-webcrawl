The Brooklyn-class cruisers were a class of nine light cruisers built for the United States Navy and commissioned during 1937 and 1938. Influenced by the Japanese Mogami-class, they carried a main armament of fifteen 6-inch (152 mm) guns in five triple turrets — an unusually heavy gun armament for a light cruiser of their displacement, near the 10,000-ton limit set by the naval treaties.

Design

The class combined the heavy gun battery with a new hull form that placed the hangar and aviation facilities aft. The last two ships, St. Louis and Helena, incorporated improved machinery arrangement and armament and are sometimes treated as a separate subclass. The design formed the basis for the later Cleveland-class cruisers.

Service

Ships of the class served extensively in the Second World War in both the Atlantic and Pacific. USS Boise fought at the Battle of Cape Esperance, Honolulu and Helena at Kula Gulf — where Helena was sunk in 1943, the only ship of the class lost in the war — and Savannah was badly damaged by a German guided bomb off Salerno. After the war, six surviving ships were transferred to South American navies: Brooklyn and Nashville to Chile, Boise and Phoenix to Argentina, and Philadelphia and St. Louis to Brazil. The ex-Phoenix, serving as ARA General Belgrano, was sunk by a British submarine during the Falklands War in 1982.
