Number line

In elementary mathematics, a number line is a picture of a graduated straight line that serves as visual representation of the real numbers. Every point of a number line is assumed to correspond to a real number, and every real number to a point.

Description

The integers are often shown as specially-marked points evenly spaced on the line, with zero in the middle, positive numbers to the right, and negative numbers to the left. The line extends indefinitely in both directions, often indicated by arrowheads. Fractions and irrational numbers such as π and √2 also have positions on the line.

Uses

Number lines are used in teaching to illustrate addition, subtraction, comparison, and absolute value. More advanced representations include the extended real number line and the complex plane, in which complex numbers correspond to points in two dimensions. The real number line is a central object in analysis and topology, exemplifying a complete ordered field.
