Platinum group

The six platinum group metals: ruthenium, rhodium, palladium, osmium, iridium and platinum

The platinum group metals (PGMs), also called the platinum group, platinides or platoid metals, are six noble, precious metallic elements clustered together in the periodic table: ruthenium (Ru), rhodium (Rh), palladium (Pd), osmium (Os), iridium (Ir) and platinum (Pt). They have similar physical and chemical properties and tend to occur together in the same mineral deposits. All are transition metals in groups 8, 9 and 10 of periods 5 and 6.

Properties

The platinum group metals are dense, have high melting points, and are notably resistant to corrosion, oxidation and chemical attack; osmium and iridium are among the densest elements known. They are excellent catalysts, a property that underlies their largest application, in catalytic converters for vehicle exhausts, as well as in chemical and petroleum refining. Electrical contacts, crucibles, spark plugs, jewellery and fuel-cell electrodes also use PGMs. Because of their scarcity and value they are classified as precious metals, alongside gold and silver.

Occurrence and production

PGMs are rare in Earth's crust and are mined mainly from layered igneous intrusions, above all South Africa's Bushveld Complex (which holds the great majority of world reserves of platinum), the Norilsk deposits in Russia (a major source of palladium as a by-product of nickel mining), and the Stillwater Complex in Montana. Famous platinum nuggets come from alluvial deposits in the Ural Mountains and Colombia. Prices are volatile, driven by automotive demand and supply concentration; recycling from scrapped catalytic converters is a significant secondary source.
