Penguin

Penguins (order Sphenisciformes, family Spheniscidae) are a group of aquatic flightless birds almost exclusively native to the Southern Hemisphere. Highly adapted for life in the ocean, penguins have countershaded dark and white plumage, flippers for swimming, and feed mainly on krill, fish and squid. About 18 species are recognised, ranging from the little penguin (about 33 cm tall) to the emperor penguin (up to about 1.2 m).

Biology

Penguins are superb swimmers, using their stiff flippers to "fly" underwater at speeds of 6–12 km/h or more. On land they waddle upright or toboggan on their bellies. Dense, waterproof feathers and a layer of blubber insulate them against cold; Antarctic species endure some of the harshest conditions on Earth, with male emperor penguins incubating eggs through the polar winter.

Distribution and species

Most species live in temperate to polar southern waters, but the Galápagos penguin lives near the equator. Notable species include the emperor, king, Adélie, chinstrap, gentoo, macaroni, African and little penguins. Many populations are declining under pressure from climate change, shifting prey and fisheries, and several species are classified as endangered or vulnerable.

Relationship with humans

Penguins are popular symbols of the Antarctic and appear widely in films, advertising and as mascots—including Tux, the Linux mascot. Historically they were hunted for oil, eggs and skins, but today the Antarctic Treaty system protects their habitat.
