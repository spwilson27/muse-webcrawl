Public goods game

The public goods game is a standard experiment in behavioural economics used to study cooperation and free-riding. Players are given an endowment and privately choose how much to contribute to a common pot. The experimenter multiplies the pot by a factor greater than one and divides it equally among all players regardless of their contribution, so each individual benefits from others' contributions while keeping their own endowment is privately optimal.

Results

Standard game theory predicts zero contributions, but experiments typically find contributions of 40–60% of the endowment in early rounds, declining with repetition toward low levels. Allowing players to punish free-riders at a cost to themselves sustains much higher cooperation, a finding associated with the work of Ernst Fehr and colleagues.

Interpretation

The game models real public goods such as public broadcasting, climate mitigation and team production. Variations explore communication, reputation, group size and cultural differences in cooperation and antisocial punishment.
