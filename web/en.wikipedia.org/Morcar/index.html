Morcar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Morcar (or Morkere) (died after 1087) was a Northumbrian nobleman who was briefly Earl of Northumbria in 1065–1066. The younger brother of Edwin, Earl of Mercia, and grandson of Leofric, Earl of Mercia, Morcar belonged to one of the most powerful families in late Anglo-Saxon England.

Life

Morcar was the younger son of Ælfgar, Earl of Mercia, and brother of Edwin, Earl of Mercia, and of Ealdgyth, who married Gruffydd ap Llywelyn, king of Wales, and later Harold Godwinson, king of England.

In October 1065, Northumbrian rebels deposed the unpopular earl Tostig Godwinson and chose Morcar as their earl. Morcar marched south with the rebels as far as Northampton; at Oxford the rebels' demands were negotiated by Harold Godwinson, and King Edward the Confessor confirmed Morcar's election and exiled Tostig.

1066 and after

In September 1066 Morcar and his brother Edwin met the Norwegian invasion of Harald Hardrada and Tostig at the Battle of Gate Fulford, where they were defeated. They afterwards played little part in resistance to William the Conqueror's invasion, submitting to him after the fall of London.

In 1068 the brothers joined the revolt of Edgar Ætheling and fled north, but the rising collapsed and they submitted again. In 1071, however, Morcar joined Hereward the Wake in the Isle of Ely; when Ely fell, Morcar was captured and imprisoned. He remained in custody, first under the Conqueror and then briefly freed by William Rufus in 1087, who nevertheless ordered his re-imprisonment. He died in prison, at an unknown date.
