4chan is an anonymous English-language imageboard website launched in 2003 by Christopher Poole, known online as "moot". Modelled on Japanese textboards and imageboards such as 2channel and Futaba Channel, it allows users to post images and comments anonymously without registration; threads are ephemeral and are deleted as new ones push them off the board.

History and culture

The site began as a place to discuss anime and manga but grew to dozens of boards covering topics from video games and technology to fitness and politics. Its random board, /b/, became known as a source of internet memes — including lolcats, Rickrolling and numerous image macros — and the hacker collective Anonymous emerged from 4chan's culture in the mid-2000s.

Influence and controversy

4chan has been credited with shaping much of early social-media meme culture, and its anonymous, lightly moderated format has also drawn criticism for offensive content, harassment campaigns and extremist material on some boards. Poole sold the site in 2015 to Hiroyuki Nishimura, the founder of 2channel. In April 2025 the site was taken offline following a hack and remained down for several weeks before returning.
