Dilmun

Dilmun was an ancient civilisation and trading centre in the Persian Gulf, mentioned in Mesopotamian texts from the late 4th millennium BCE onward. It is generally identified with the island of Bahrain, the adjacent coast of eastern Arabia, and at times Failaka Island and parts of the Kuwait coast.

Trade

Dilmun occupied a strategic position between Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley civilisation (Meluhha). Sumerian and Akkadian texts record imports of copper (from Magan, in Oman), timber, ivory, carnelian, and pearls passing through Dilmun, and it later exported dates and its own goods. Standardised weights following the Indus system have been found in Bahrain and Mesopotamia.

Mythology

In Sumerian myth, Dilmun was a pure, bright paradise land without sickness or death, blessed with fresh water by the god Enki; it features in the myth of Enki and Ninhursag and as the home of Utnapishtim, the flood hero of the Epic of Gilgamesh, who was granted immortality there.

Archaeology

Major sites include Qal'at al-Bahrain, a harbour city and later Portuguese fort built over successive Dilmun settlements; the Barbar Temple; and tens of thousands of burial mounds, among the largest prehistoric cemeteries in the world. Dilmun's power waned in the later 2nd millennium BCE and the region came under Babylonian, Assyrian, and Persian control.
