Cryptocurrency wallet

A cryptocurrency wallet is a device, program, or service that stores the public and private keys used to transact in cryptocurrency such as Bitcoin and Ethereum. Contrary to a common misunderstanding, a wallet does not literally store coins; the coins exist as records on a blockchain. The wallet stores the cryptographic keys that prove ownership and authorize transactions.

How wallets work

Each wallet controls one or more key pairs. The public key is used to derive addresses that can receive funds, while the private key signs transactions, proving the sender's authorization without revealing the key itself. Loss of the private key generally means permanent loss of access to the associated funds, since there is no central authority able to restore it.

Types

Hot and cold wallets

A hot wallet is connected to the internet, making it convenient for frequent transactions but more exposed to attack. A cold wallet keeps keys offline, for example on a dedicated hardware device or on paper, and is preferred for long-term storage of large amounts.

Custodial and non-custodial

Custodial wallets are operated by a third party, such as an exchange, which holds the keys on the user's behalf. Non-custodial (self-custody) wallets leave the user in sole control of the keys. The phrase "not your keys, not your coins" summarizes the risk that a custodian may fail, freeze accounts, or be hacked.

Seed phrases

Most modern wallets are hierarchical deterministic (HD) wallets following standards such as BIP-39. From a single seed, usually represented as a mnemonic phrase of 12 or 24 words, the wallet can derive a large tree of keys. Backing up the phrase is sufficient to restore the entire wallet.

Security

Wallet security threats include malware that swaps clipboard addresses, phishing sites imitating wallet software, and physical theft of backups. Hardware wallets mitigate many software attacks by signing transactions inside an isolated device and never exposing the private key to the host computer.
