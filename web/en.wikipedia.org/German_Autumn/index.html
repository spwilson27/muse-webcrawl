German Autumn

The German Autumn (German: Deutscher Herbst) was a series of events in late 1977 in West Germany, associated with the kidnapping and murder of industrialist Hanns Martin Schleyer, the hijacking of Lufthansa Flight 181, and the deaths of leading members of the far-left militant Red Army Faction (RAF) in prison. It was the most serious crisis faced by the Federal Republic to that date.

The crisis of autumn 1977 dominated West German public life.

Events

On 5 September 1977 the RAF kidnapped Hanns Martin Schleyer, president of the German employers' federation, killing his driver and three police escorts, and demanded the release of eleven imprisoned RAF members. Chancellor Helmut Schmidt's government refused to negotiate. On 13 October, Palestinian militants allied with the RAF hijacked Lufthansa Flight 181 ("Landshut") to force the same demands; West German GSG 9 commandos stormed the aircraft at Mogadishu on the night of 17–18 October and freed the hostages. Hours later, RAF leaders Andreas Baader, Gudrun Ensslin, and Jan-Carl Raspe were found dead in their cells at Stammheim Prison — officially suicides, though the circumstances remain debated. Schleyer's body was found in Mulhouse, France, on 19 October.

Aftermath

The crisis hardened West German anti-terrorism law and public debate about the limits of state power, and it marked the effective end of the RAF's first generation. The events were portrayed in the omnibus film Germany in Autumn (1978) and later in The Baader Meinhof Complex (2008).
