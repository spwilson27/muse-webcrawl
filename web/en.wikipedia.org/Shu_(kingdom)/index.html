Shu (kingdom)

Shu (Chinese: 蜀; pinyin: Shǔ) was an ancient kingdom in what is now Sichuan Province in China. It was based on the Chengdu Plain, in the western Sichuan basin, with some extensions on the upper Han River. Shu was conquered by Qin in 316 BC. In subsequent periods of history, the name "Shu" has been used to refer to Sichuan.

History

Archaeological finds at Sanxingdui and Jinsha, both near modern Chengdu, show that a distinct Bronze Age civilization flourished in the region. The Sanxingdui culture produced bronze masks, statues, and ritual objects quite unlike those of the contemporaneous Shang dynasty in the Yellow River valley.

By the Warring States period, Shu had developed irrigation agriculture, silk production, and salt wells. Its last independent king feuded with the neighbouring kingdom of Ba. In 316 BC, the state of Qin exploited a conflict between Shu and Ba, invaded both, and annexed them, giving Qin the agricultural resources that later helped it unify China.

Later uses of the name

During the Three Kingdoms period, Liu Bei founded the state of Shu Han (221–263) in the same region. The name Shu remains a literary name and abbreviation for Sichuan today.
