Satawal

Satawal is a small coral island and municipality in Yap State, Federated States of Micronesia, in the western Pacific Ocean. Only about 1.3 km long and less than a kilometre wide, with a land area of roughly 1.3 km², it is one of the Caroline Islands and home to around 500 people who speak Satawalese, a Chuukic language.

Traditional navigation

Satawal is internationally famous as the home of master navigators who preserved the art of wayfinding — long-distance ocean voyaging by outrigger canoe using only stars, swells, winds, birds, and other natural signs, without instruments. The most celebrated of these was Mau Piailug (1932–2010), who in 1976 navigated the Hawaiian voyaging canoe Hōkūleʻa from Hawaii to Tahiti, helping spark the Polynesian voyaging renaissance and the revival of traditional navigation across the Pacific.

Life on the island

Islanders subsist largely on taro, breadfruit, coconut, and reef and pelagic fish. Satawal is low-lying and vulnerable to typhoons and sea-level rise; it was severely damaged by Typhoon Maysak in 2015. The island has no airstrip and is reached by infrequent field-trip ships from Yap or Chuuk.
