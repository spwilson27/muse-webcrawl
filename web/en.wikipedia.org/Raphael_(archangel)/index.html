Raphael (archangel)

Raphael (Hebrew: רְפָאֵל, Rəfāʾēl, "God has healed") is an archangel of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. In the deuterocanonical Book of Tobit, Raphael travels with Tobias, son of Tobit, heals Tobit's blindness, and expels the demon Asmodeus — making him a patron of travellers, healing, and the blind.

In scripture and tradition

Raphael appears by name in the Book of Tobit and is also prominent in the Book of Enoch, where he is one of the seven archangels who stand before God and a guide to the underworld. Tradition often identifies him with the angel who stirred the waters at the Pool of Bethesda in the Gospel of John. In Islam he is known as Israfil, the angel who will blow the trumpet at the Day of Judgment, though some traditions distinguish the two.

Veneration

The Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodoxy, Oriental Orthodoxy, and the Anglican tradition venerate Raphael as a saint. His feast day, formerly 24 October, is now celebrated with Michael and Gabriel on 29 September in the Roman calendar. He is the patron saint of apothecaries, physicians, and travellers, and of the sick.
