Endorphins

Endorphins (contracted from "endogenous morphine") are endogenous opioid neuropeptides and peptide hormones produced by the central nervous system and the pituitary gland. They inhibit the transmission of pain signals and can produce a feeling of euphoria.

Types

The endorphin class consists of α-endorphin, β-endorphin, and γ-endorphin, cleaved from the precursor protein proopiomelanocortin (POMC). β-endorphin is considered the most potent, binding primarily to μ-opioid receptors.

Release and effects

Endorphins are released during exercise, pain, excitement, and other stress responses. The euphoric sensation known as a "runner's high" was long attributed to endorphins, though later research also implicates endocannabinoids. Their discovery in the 1970s followed the identification of opioid receptors in the brain.
