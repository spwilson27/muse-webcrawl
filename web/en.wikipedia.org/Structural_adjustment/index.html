Structural adjustment
Institutions: IMF, World Bank
Era: 1980s–2000s
Instruments: SAPs, conditionality

Structural adjustment refers to economic policies imposed by the International Monetary Fund and World Bank on developing countries as conditions for loans, particularly through Structural Adjustment Programs (SAPs) from the 1980s.

Typical measures

Programs typically required currency devaluation, trade liberalisation, privatisation of state enterprises, deregulation, fiscal austerity and cuts to subsidies and public spending, aiming to correct balance-of-payments deficits and promote market-led growth.

Criticism and legacy

Critics — including UNICEF and many development economists — argued SAPs worsened poverty, health and education outcomes in Africa, Latin America and Asia, while supporters credited them with macroeconomic stabilisation in some countries. In the late 1990s the institutions shifted toward Poverty Reduction Strategy Papers and debt relief under the HIPC initiative.
