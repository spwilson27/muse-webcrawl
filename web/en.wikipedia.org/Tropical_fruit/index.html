Tropical fruit

Mango, papaya, pineapple, and other tropical fruits

A tropical fruit is any fruit produced by a plant native to the tropics, typically requiring a warm, frost-free climate. Tropical fruits are usually sensitive to cold and are grown roughly within the band between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn, although many are now cultivated in subtropical regions as well. Well-known examples include the banana, mango, pineapple, papaya, avocado, coconut, guava, passion fruit, lychee, and durian.

Major tropical fruits

The banana and plantain form the largest tropical fruit trade by volume; a single cultivar, the Cavendish banana, dominates world exports and is threatened by the fungal disease Tropical Race 4. The mango, called the "king of fruits" in South Asia, is among the most widely eaten tropical fruits, with India the largest producer. Pineapples, first spread from South America and hybridised in Hawaii, are exported fresh and canned. Papaya, avocado, and passion fruit have grown rapidly in international trade in recent decades.

Some tropical fruits are famous for extremes of flavour and aroma: the durian of Southeast Asia is prized locally but banned from many hotels and public transport because of its powerful smell, while the mangosteen is famed for its delicate sweetness. Others, such as dragon fruit (pitaya), rambutan, and jackfruit — the largest tree-borne fruit — have spread from regional to global markets.

Cultivation and trade

Tropical fruits are generally perishable and are harvested before ripening for export, ripening en route or at destination; many are climacteric, ripening in response to ethylene. Cold-chain shipping and air freight have made once-exotic fruits staples of supermarkets worldwide, and producing countries include the Philippines, Thailand, Indonesia, Mexico, Brazil, India, and Costa Rica.
