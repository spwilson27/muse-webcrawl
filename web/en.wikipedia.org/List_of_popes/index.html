List of popes

[Image: St. Peter's Basilica in Vatican City]
The pope is the bishop of Rome and head of the Catholic Church.

The pope is the bishop of Rome and visible head of the worldwide Catholic Church. The Annuario Pontificio lists 266 popes in an unbroken line from Saint Peter to the present day. This article summarises the succession and lists selected popes of particular historical note.

Overview

Popes are elected by the College of Cardinals in a conclave held in the Sistine Chapel. The longest-reigning pope is traditionally Saint Peter (about 34–37 years), followed by Pius IX (1846–1878), whose reign saw the definition of papal infallibility at the First Vatican Council. The shortest reign was that of Urban VII, who died twelve days after his election in 1590. John Paul II (1978–2005) was the first non-Italian pope in over 450 years; Benedict XVI (2005–2013) was the first pope to resign since Gregory XII in 1415; Francis (2013–2025) was the first pope from the Americas and the first Jesuit pope.
