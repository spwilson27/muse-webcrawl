Mulberry


Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Rosales
Family: Moraceae
Genus: Morus L.

Mulberries are the fruit of trees in the genus Morus, a group of flowering plants in the family Moraceae comprising roughly 16 species of deciduous trees native to warm temperate and subtropical regions of Asia, Africa, and North America. The best-known species are the white mulberry (Morus alba), black mulberry (M. nigra), and red mulberry (M. rubra).

Description

Mulberry trees grow quickly when young and can reach 10–15 metres. The fruit is a multiple fruit, 2–3 cm long, resembling an elongated blackberry or raspberry. Fruit colour is not a reliable guide to species: white mulberry fruits are usually pale but may ripen pink or purple, while black mulberries are dark purple to black.

Uses

The leaves of the white mulberry are the sole food source of the silkworm (Bombyx mori), making the tree central to sericulture for thousands of years in China and beyond. The fruit is eaten fresh and dried, and is used in pies, jams, wines, and the Persian and Central Asian dried-mulberry traditions. Mulberry wood and bark have been used for paper and timber.

Cultivation and ecology

Mulberries are widely planted as shade and street trees, though fruiting female trees can stain pavements; fruitless male cultivars are common in landscaping. The red mulberry is threatened in parts of North America by hybridization with the introduced white mulberry. Mulberry pollen is a notable allergen in some cities.
