Stack Exchange

Stack Exchange is a network of question-and-answer (Q&A) websites on topics in diverse fields, each site covering a specific topic, where questions, answers, and users are subject to a reputation award process. The reputation system allows the sites to be self-moderating. As of 2024, the three most heavily visited sites in the network are Stack Overflow (which focuses on computer programming), Unix & Linux, and Mathematics.

All sites in the network are modeled after the initial site Stack Overflow, a Q&A site for computer programming questions created by Jeff Atwood and Joel Spolsky. Further Q&A sites in the network are established, defined, and eventually—if found relevant—created by registered users through a special site named Area 51.

History

In 2008, Jeff Atwood and Joel Spolsky created Stack Overflow, a question-and-answer website for computer programming questions. In 2009 they started additional sites based on the Stack Overflow model: Server Fault for questions related to system administration and Super User for questions from computer power users. In September 2010, the network adopted the name Stack Exchange, and the Stack Exchange 2.0 platform began allowing users to propose new sites through Area 51.

Site mechanics

Users earn reputation points and badges for asking and answering questions, editing posts, and other contributions. Reputation unlocks privileges, from commenting and voting up or down to closing questions and accessing moderator tools. Content on the sites is licensed under Creative Commons licenses, and periodic public data dumps of questions and answers have made the network a widely used resource for research and for training in natural language processing.

Acquisition

In June 2021, Stack Overflow was acquired by the technology investor Prosus for $1.8 billion. The Stack Exchange network sites continue to operate as community-moderated Q&A communities under the same model.
