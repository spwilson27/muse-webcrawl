Metepec is a city and municipality in the State of Mexico in central Mexico, part of the metropolitan area of Toluca, the state capital. The name comes from Nahuatl, meaning "on the hill of the magueys". The municipality had a population of roughly 243,000 in the 2020 census, and the town is known nationwide for its pottery and ceramics.

Crafts

Metepec's signature craft is the Árbol de la Vida ("Tree of Life"), an elaborate clay sculpture of a tree crowded with figures depicting biblical and folk scenes, originally created by potter Modesta Fernández Mata and developed by the Soteno family of potters. The town also produces suns and moons, candelabra, and other ceramics, and holds an annual pottery fair. Its historic centre retains colonial buildings, including the Franciscan ex-convent and church of San Juan Bautista.

Geography and economy

The city sits in the Toluca Valley at high elevation on the slopes of the Cerro de los Magueyes. Once largely agricultural, Metepec has become a largely suburban and commercial area hosting shopping centres, offices, and residential developments serving the Toluca metropolitan area, while a clay quarry and potteries sustain its craft tradition.
