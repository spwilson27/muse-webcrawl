Terroir

Terroir (from French terre, "land") is the set of environmental factors that affect the character of an agricultural product — especially wine — including the soil, topography, climate, and traditional farming practices of a specific place. The concept holds that a product carries the distinctive qualities, or "sense of place", imparted by its environment.

Elements

Terroir encompasses soil composition and drainage, slope and aspect (sun exposure), altitude, temperature ranges, rainfall, and the vineyard's microclimate, together with human factors such as grape varieties and viticultural traditions. It underpins the French appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) system, which regulates where and how wines such as Champagne, Burgundy and Bordeaux may be produced.

Beyond wine

The concept has been extended to coffee, tea, cheese, chocolate, maple syrup and other artisanal foods. Its scientific basis is debated: soil mineral content has a limited direct effect on flavour, but water availability, temperature and sunlight — all governed by the site — strongly shape plant chemistry and hence the final product.
