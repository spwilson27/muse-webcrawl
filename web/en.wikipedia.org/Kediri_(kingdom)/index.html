Kediri (also known as Panjalu) was a Hindu-Buddhist Javanese kingdom based in East Java from 1042 to 1222. Its capital was Dahanapura, in the vicinity of modern Kediri city, near the banks of the Brantas River. The kingdom arose from the partition of Airlangga's Kahuripan kingdom between his two sons.

History

In 1042 Airlangga divided his kingdom into Janggala and Panjalu (Kediri) and abdicated in favour of ascetic life. After a period of rivalry, Kediri absorbed Janggala (by about 1135 under Jayabaya), reuniting East Java. The reign of Jayabaya (1135–1157) is remembered as a golden age of Old Javanese literature; the court poet Mpu Panuluh composed the Bharatayuddha, and Jayabaya's prophecies (Jangka Jayabaya) remain famous in Javanese tradition.

Literary culture flourished further under later kings: the Smaradahana of Mpu Dharmaja and the Sumanasantaka of Mpu Monaguna date to this era, and it was in the late Kediri period that the early Panji tales began to circulate.

Fall

The last king, Kertajaya (c. 1194–1222), came into conflict with the Brahmin priestly class, who sought the help of Ken Arok, the ruler of Tumapel. In 1222 Ken Arok defeated Kertajaya at the Battle of Ganter, ending the Kediri kingdom and founding the new kingdom of Singhasari. Kediri later rose again under Majapahit as a vassal duchy, and in 1478 briefly regained independence under Girindrawardhana before falling to Demak.
