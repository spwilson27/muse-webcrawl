Management buyout

Typical structure of an MBO, in which incumbent managers acquire the business they run.

A management buyout (MBO) is a form of acquisition in which a company's existing managers buy a controlling share of the company from its current owners. Management buyouts are similar in principle to leveraged buyouts, in that a large part of the purchase price is financed with debt secured against the company's assets and cash flows, but the acquiring party is the incumbent management team rather than an outside investor.

Management buyout
BuyersIncumbent management team
FinancingDebt, seller financing, private equity backing
Related formsLeveraged buyout (LBO), management buy-in (MBI), BIMBO

Rationale

MBOs typically occur when a parent company wishes to divest a non-core division, when a founder or family owner retires without a successor, or when a business is taken private from public markets. Because the buyers know the business intimately, due diligence can be quicker and the transition smoother than in a sale to outsiders. Managers gain the prospect of substantial equity returns, while sellers may value the confidentiality and continuity an internal sale offers compared with a trade auction.

Financing

Managers rarely have enough personal capital to buy the business outright, so MBOs are typically structured with a mixture of management equity, bank or mezzanine debt, vendor loans or deferred consideration from the seller, and frequently a private equity sponsor that provides equity in exchange for a stake and board representation. The debt is serviced from the target's cash flows, and the buyout vehicle is usually a new holding company that acquires the target's shares or assets.

Advantages and risks

Advocates argue MBOs align ownership with management, preserve tacit knowledge, and can unlock performance improvements once managers share directly in the value they create. Critics point to potential conflicts of interest — managers possess insider information and may be tempted to suppress the price — and to the financial risk of high leverage. Variants include the management buy-in (MBI), where an external team buys in, and the combined buy-in management buyout (BIMBO).
