Marian devotions

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A statue of the Virgin Mary at a Marian shrine]

Marian devotions are external pious practices directed to the person of Mary, mother of Jesus, by members of certain Christian traditions, especially Roman Catholicism and, to lesser degrees, Eastern Orthodoxy, Oriental Orthodoxy, Anglicanism and Lutheranism. They include prayers, hymns, pilgrimages, feasts and acts of consecration offered through Mary's intercession.

Forms of devotion

The most widespread Marian devotion is the Rosary, a meditative prayer using beads; others include the Angelus, the Hail Mary, litanies, novenas, the scapular, and May crownings. Pilgrimage sites associated with reported Marian apparitions—such as Lourdes, Fátima, Guadalupe and Medjugorje—draw millions of visitors.

Theology

Catholic teaching distinguishes the worship due to God alone (latria) from the special veneration of Mary (hyperdulia) and of other saints (dulia). The Second Vatican Council's Lumen gentium emphasised that Marian devotion should be Christ-centred. Titles for Mary include Theotokos ("God-bearer"), Queen of Heaven, and numerous local advocations.

History

Marian devotion grew markedly after the Council of Ephesus (431) affirmed Mary as Theotokos. It flourished in the medieval period—producing the Ave Maria, Marian antiphons and Gothic cathedrals dedicated to Notre Dame—and was reinforced by Counter-Reformation piety and the dogmas of the Immaculate Conception (1854) and the Assumption (1950).
