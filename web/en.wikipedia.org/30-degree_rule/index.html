30-degree rule

The 30-degree rule is a basic film editing guideline that states that the camera should move at least 30 degrees between successive shots of the same subject. If consecutive shots are taken from angles differing by less than 30 degrees, the cut can appear as an unintended jump cut, making the subject seem to shift position abruptly.

Purpose

Changing the angle by at least 30 degrees ensures that the audience perceives the second shot as a genuinely new viewpoint rather than a mistake. It also usually involves changing the shot size—for example, from a medium shot to a close-up—so the two images differ enough to cut together smoothly.

Relation to other rules

The 30-degree rule is part of the classical continuity editing system, alongside the 180-degree rule (maintaining the axis of action), eyeline matches and shot/reverse-shot patterns. Some directors deliberately violate the rule, using jump cuts as a stylistic device, as in French New Wave cinema.

Application

The principle applies to filming dialogue scenes, interviews and any coverage of a single subject from multiple camera positions, and is taught in introductory courses in cinematography and editing.
