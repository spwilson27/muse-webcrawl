Malay Archipelago

The Malay Archipelago is the archipelago between mainland Southeast Asia and Australia. It is the largest island group in the world by area and includes the islands of Indonesia, the Philippines, East Malaysia on Borneo, Brunei, Singapore, East Timor and Papua New Guinea's western half.

Geography

The archipelago contains more than 25,000 islands, including the Greater Sunda Islands (Borneo, Sumatra, Java, Sulawesi), the Lesser Sunda Islands, the Maluku Islands and the Philippine archipelago. It straddles the equator and is crossed by the Wallace Line, a biogeographical boundary separating Asian and Australasian fauna.

Natural history

The region was made famous in science by Alfred Russel Wallace, whose travels here in the 1850s–60s produced his book The Malay Archipelago (1869) and contributed to the theory of evolution by natural selection.

People and history

The islands have been shaped by Austronesian settlement, Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms, Islam, and European colonial rule by the Portuguese, Spanish, Dutch and British. Maritime trade in spices long connected the archipelago to the wider world.
