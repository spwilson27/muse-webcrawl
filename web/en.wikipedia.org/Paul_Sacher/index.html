Paul Sacher (1906–1999)

Paul Sacher (28 April 1906 – 26 May 1999) was a Swiss conductor, patron of new music, and one of the most significant commissioners of 20th-century classical music. He founded the Basel Chamber Orchestra (Basler Kammerorchester) in 1926 and the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis in 1933, and commissioned works from many of the era's leading composers, including Bartók, Stravinsky, Honegger, Martinů, Britten, Tippett, Lutosławski, Henze, and Richard Strauss.

Life and work

Born in Basel, Sacher studied conducting and musicology and at only twenty founded the Basel Chamber Orchestra, devoted to both early music performed on period-appropriate principles and contemporary repertoire. His marriage in 1934 to Maja Hoffmann-Stehlin, an heiress of the Hoffmann-La Roche pharmaceutical fortune, gave him the wealth to sustain ambitious commissioning and performance projects for the rest of his life.

Among his most famous commissions were Bartók's Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta (1936) and the Divertimento (1939), Stravinsky's Concerto in D (1946), Honegger's Second and Fourth Symphonies, Martinů's chamber works, and Strauss's Metamorphosen (1945), premiered under Sacher's direction. The twelve-note theme S-A-C-H-E-R (E♭–A–C–B–E–D in German notation, "Sacher hexachord") was used by Boulez and others in works honouring him.

Legacy

In 1973 Sacher established the Paul Sacher Foundation in Basel, which acquired the estates and archives of Stravinsky, Webern, Bartók and many other composers, becoming one of the world's leading research collections for 20th-century music. He directed the Schola Cantorum Basiliensis and the Basel Academy of Music for decades. Sacher died in Basel in 1999, aged 93, widely regarded as the defining musical patron of his century.
