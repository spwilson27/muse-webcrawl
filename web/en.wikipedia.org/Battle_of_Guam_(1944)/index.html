Battle of Guam (1944)

The Battle of Guam (21 July – 10 August 1944) was the American recapture of the Japanese-held island of Guam, a U.S. territory in the Mariana Islands captured by Japan in December 1941. Part of Operation Forager in the Pacific War, the battle returned Guam to American control, and the island became a major base for the final campaigns against Japan.

Background

Guam had been an American possession since 1898 and was defended in 1944 by some 18,500 Japanese troops under Lieutenant General Takeshi Takashina. After weeks of naval and air bombardment — the heaviest yet delivered in the Pacific — the U.S. III Amphibious Corps, comprising the 3rd Marine Division, the 1st Provisional Marine Brigade and the Army's 77th Infantry Division, landed on 21 July 1944 on both sides of the Orote Peninsula.

The battle

The Marines fought through fortified beach defences and endured massed night counterattacks, including a major banzai assault on 25–26 July that killed Takashina. The 77th Infantry Division expanded the southern beachhead, and by early August American forces drove north, liberating the capital, Agana, and clearing the island's northern end. Organised resistance ended on 10 August, though Japanese holdouts — most famously Shōichi Yokoi, found in 1972 — remained hidden for years.

Losses and aftermath

American casualties were about 1,700 killed and 6,000 wounded; nearly the entire Japanese garrison died. Guam was transformed into a vast air and naval base, hosting the B-29s that bombed Japan in 1945. The liberation is still celebrated annually on 21 July.
