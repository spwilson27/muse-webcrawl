This article lists events of the year 1991 in the United States.

Events

January–March: A US-led coalition fought the Gulf War (Operation Desert Storm) to liberate Kuwait from Iraqi occupation; a ceasefire took effect at the end of February. In March, Los Angeles police officers were filmed beating motorist Rodney King, sparking national controversy.

April–August: The early-1990s recession officially ended in March, though unemployment continued to rise. In August, Tim Berners-Lee released the first website, hosted at CERN, introducing the World Wide Web to the public.

October–December: Clarence Thomas was confirmed to the Supreme Court in October after contentious hearings featuring testimony by Anita Hill. In November, basketball star Magic Johnson announced he was HIV-positive and retired. In December, the Soviet Union dissolved, formally ending the Cold War. Rapper Tupac Shakur's debut album and Nirvana's Nevermind (September) marked a turning point in popular music.

Deaths

Notable American deaths in 1991 included Dr. Seuss (Theodor Geisel), Miles Davis, and actor Michael Landon.
