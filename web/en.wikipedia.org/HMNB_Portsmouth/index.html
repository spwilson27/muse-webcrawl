His Majesty's Naval Base, Portsmouth (HMNB Portsmouth) is one of the Royal Navy's three operating bases, alongside HMNB Clyde and HMNB Devonport. Located on the eastern side of Portsmouth Harbour in Hampshire, it is the home port of the Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers and around two-thirds of the Royal Navy's surface fleet, including the Type 45 destroyers.

History

Portsmouth has been a naval port since the 12th century; King John established its first dock in the early 1200s, and Henry VII built the world's first dry dock there in 1495. The dockyard expanded enormously in the age of sail and steam, building HMS Victory and much of the fleet of the Napoleonic and Victorian eras. The base was heavily bombed in the Second World War and was the principal embarkation port for much of the D-Day armada in 1944.

Present role

Investment in the Princess Royal Jetty and dredging of the harbour entrance enabled Portsmouth to host the 65,000-tonne carriers HMS Queen Elizabeth and HMS Prince of Wales, whose arrival in 2017–2019 was the largest naval engineering programme in the base's history. Support and maintenance are provided in partnership with BAE Systems. The adjoining Portsmouth Historic Dockyard preserves HMS Victory, HMS Warrior and the Mary Rose Museum as visitor attractions.
