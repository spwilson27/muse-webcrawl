Japan Trench

The Japan Trench is an oceanic trench, part of the Pacific Ring of Fire, in the floor of the northern Pacific Ocean off the north-east coast of Japan. It extends from the Kuril Islands to the Bōsō Peninsula, a length of roughly 800 kilometres, and reaches a maximum depth of about 8,000 metres below sea level.

Location: Pacific Ocean, offshore north-east Honshu, Japan

Length: ~800 km

Maximum depth: ~8,000 m

Type: Subduction zone

Geology

The trench marks the convergent boundary where the Pacific Plate subducts westward beneath the Okhotsk Plate at a rate of several centimetres per year. This subduction drives the volcanism of northern Honshu and generates powerful megathrust earthquakes. The southern end of the trench meets the Sagami Trough near the Bōsō Triple Junction.

Earthquakes

The trench was the source of the magnitude 9.0–9.1 Tōhoku earthquake of 11 March 2011, the most powerful earthquake ever recorded in Japan, which produced a devastating tsunami and the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster. Earlier historic ruptures include the 869 Jōgan earthquake. In 2012 the deep-submergence vehicle Kaikō-class ROVs and the drilling vessel Chikyū sampled the fault zone to study the rupture.
