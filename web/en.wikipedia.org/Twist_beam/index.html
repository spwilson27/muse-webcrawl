Twist-beam rear suspension

A twist-beam rear suspension, also known as a torsion-beam axle, is a type of automobile suspension based on a large H- or C-shaped member. The front of the H is attached to the body by bushings, and the rear carries the two wheel stub-axles. The cross beam linking the trailing arms twists as the wheels move, providing roll stiffness and partially coupling the motion of the two wheels — a semi-independent design.

Characteristics

The twist-beam axle is inexpensive, compact and durable, and its flat layout intrudes little into luggage space, making it the standard rear suspension for front-wheel-drive superminis, hatchbacks and many compact cars, such as the Volkswagen Golf in its earlier and some later generations. Its limitations compared with fully independent multi-link suspension include less freedom to tune camber and toe behaviour, and a tendency for lateral forces to steer the axle. Engineers tune its behaviour through the position and profile of the cross beam and the stiffness of the pivot bushings, sometimes adding a separate anti-roll bar or a Watt's linkage.
