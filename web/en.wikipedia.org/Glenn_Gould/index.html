Glenn Herbert Gould (25 September 1932 – 4 October 1982) was a Canadian classical pianist, widely regarded as one of the greatest pianists of the 20th century. He was renowned for his interpretations of the keyboard music of Johann Sebastian Bach, his unorthodox technique, and his eccentricities, which included humming while he played and conducting himself with his free hand.

Career

Gould made his orchestral debut at age 14 and gained international fame with his 1955 debut recording of Bach's Goldberg Variations, one of the best-selling classical albums of all time. In 1964, at the height of his fame, he abruptly retired from concert performance to concentrate on studio recording, broadcasting, and writing — a decision he defended in his essay "The Prospects of Recording" (1966). He produced innovative radio documentaries for the CBC, including the Solitude Trilogy.

Style and legacy

Gould favoured extremely clear articulation, brisk tempos, and a detached, analytical approach, and he championed composers from Byrd and Gibbons to Schoenberg and Hindemith while disparaging much Romantic repertoire. He re-recorded the Goldberg Variations in 1981 in a strikingly slower interpretation; the album was released shortly before his death from a stroke at age 50. His recordings and writings continue to influence musicians and debates about performance.
