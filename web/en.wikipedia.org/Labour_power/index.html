Labour power

Labour power (German: Arbeitskraft; French: force de travail) is a concept central to Karl Marx's critique of political economy: the capacity to do work, as distinct from labour itself, the actual performance of work. Under capitalism, Marx argued, workers sell their labour power as a commodity to the owners of the means of production in exchange for a wage.

The value of labour power, like that of other commodities, is determined by the labour time socially necessary for its reproduction — the cost of the food, housing, clothing, training and other means of subsistence needed to keep the worker and their family able to work. Because the working day can produce more value than the value of labour power itself, the difference is surplus value, the source, in Marx's theory, of capitalist profit. The distinction between labour and labour power was Marx's answer to the puzzle of how profit could arise from exchange of equivalents.
