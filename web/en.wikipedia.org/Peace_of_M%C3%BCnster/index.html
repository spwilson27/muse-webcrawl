Peace of Münster

The Peace of Münster was a treaty signed on 30 January 1648 in Münster between the Dutch Republic and Spain. It ended the Eighty Years' War and formally recognized the independence of the Dutch Republic. The treaty formed part of the wider Peace of Westphalia, concluded later the same year.

Peace of Münster
Signed: 30 January 1648
Location: Münster, Westphalia
Parties: Dutch Republic; Spanish Empire

Background

The Dutch Revolt against Spanish rule had begun in 1568. Decades of warfare, interrupted by the Twelve Years' Truce (1609–1621), left both sides exhausted. Negotiations in Münster opened in 1646 as part of the great European peace congress.

Terms

Spain recognized the United Provinces as a free and sovereign state. The Dutch retained territories they held at the time, including parts of Brabant and Flanders, and their overseas conquests gained since the truce. The Scheldt estuary remained closed to shipping from Antwerp, a clause that favoured Amsterdam's trade.

Significance

The peace ended eighty years of conflict and secured international recognition of Dutch independence, confirming the Republic's emergence as a leading commercial and maritime power.
