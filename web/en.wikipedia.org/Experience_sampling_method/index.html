Experience sampling method

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The experience sampling method (ESM), also called ecological momentary assessment (EMA) or the daily diary method in related forms, is a research methodology in which participants report on their thoughts, feelings, activities, and surroundings at repeated moments during their everyday lives. Rather than asking people to recall experiences retrospectively, the method samples experience close to the moment it occurs, reducing memory bias.

Procedure

In a typical study, participants carry a signalling device — originally a pager or programmable watch, later a palmtop computer, and now usually a smartphone app — that prompts them at random or scheduled intervals, often several times a day for one or more weeks. At each signal they complete a short questionnaire about what they are doing, who they are with, and their current mood, concentration, and motivation. The method was developed and popularized by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi and colleagues in the 1970s and 1980s, notably in studies of adolescents and in research on the psychological state of "flow".

Applications

Experience sampling is used in psychology, psychiatry, and health research to study mood variability, stress, social interaction, symptoms of disorders such as depression and schizophrenia in daily life, and the contexts of behaviour such as eating, smoking, and pain. Strengths include ecological validity and the ability to examine within-person processes over time; limitations include participant burden, reactivity (the measurement itself changing behaviour), and the cost of repeated assessment.
