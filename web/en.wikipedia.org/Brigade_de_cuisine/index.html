Brigade de cuisine

A kitchen brigade working the line during service

The brigade de cuisine ("kitchen brigade") is a system of hierarchy in a professional kitchen, in which responsibilities are divided among specialised stations. It was codified by Auguste Escoffier in the late nineteenth century, adapting the military-style organisation he had encountered during the Franco-Prussian War to the grand hotel kitchens of London and Paris.

History

Before Escoffier, large kitchens were organised around the older parties system, with overlapping duties. Escoffier streamlined it in kitchens such as the Savoy Hotel and the Ritz, reducing waste and speeding service at a time when restaurant dining was becoming faster-paced. His Le Guide Culinaire (1903) documented the system.

Modern practice

Most modern restaurants adopt a simplified brigade: an executive chef, sous-chefs, line cooks assigned to stations (grill, sauté, pantry, pastry), and dishwashers. The terminology remains standard in culinary schools and fine-dining kitchens worldwide, and the white toque and neckerchief associated with the brigade remain symbols of the profession.
