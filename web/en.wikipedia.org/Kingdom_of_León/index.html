The Kingdom of León was an independent kingdom in the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula. It was founded in 910, when the Christian princes of Asturias moved their capital from Oviedo to the city of León, and it became one of the principal Christian realms of medieval Spain.

History

León grew out of the Kingdom of Asturias, the Christian state that survived the Muslim conquest of most of Iberia. García I became the first king of León in 910. His successors, including Ramiro II, who defeated the Caliphate of Córdoba at Simancas in 939, consolidated the kingdom and repopulated the Duero valley.

In 1230 Ferdinand III permanently united León with the Crown of Castile, though León retained its own laws, institutions and Cortes — its assembly of 1188 is often cited as one of the earliest parliamentary bodies in Europe. The kingdom survived as a constituent realm of the Spanish monarchy until the territorial reorganisation of 1833.

Culture

The Leonese language, a Romance variety descended from the Latin spoken in the region, was the language of royal documents for centuries. Romanesque art flourished in the kingdom, seen in the Royal Pantheon of San Isidoro in León.
