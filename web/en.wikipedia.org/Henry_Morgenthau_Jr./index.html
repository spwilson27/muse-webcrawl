Henry Morgenthau Jr.

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Henry Morgenthau Jr. (May 11, 1891 – February 6, 1967) was an American government official who served as the 52nd United States Secretary of the Treasury from 1934 to 1945, nearly the entire presidency of Franklin D. Roosevelt. A close friend and Dutchess County neighbour of Roosevelt, he presided over the financing of the New Deal and the Second World War, championed aid to the Allies and to Jewish refugees, and authored the controversial Morgenthau Plan for postwar Germany.

Early life and New Deal

Morgenthau was born in New York City, the son of Henry Morgenthau Sr., a real-estate magnate and former ambassador to the Ottoman Empire. He studied architecture and agriculture at Cornell without graduating and became a gentleman farmer. After managing Roosevelt's farm-relief agencies — the Federal Farm Board and the Farm Credit Administration — he became Secretary of the Treasury on William Woodin's resignation in January 1934. He raised funds for New Deal programmes through bond issues and famously marketed Series E "defense bonds" during the war; total federal debt rose from about $22 billion to about $260 billion during his tenure as war spending dwarfed everything before it.

International monetary policy

Morgenthau was the driving force behind the Bretton Woods Conference of July 1944, presided over it, and signed the agreements creating the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank, whose design (drafted largely by his aide Harry Dexter White) made the US dollar the anchor of the postwar monetary order.

The Holocaust and refugees

Learning in 1943–44 that the State Department had obstructed rescue of European Jews, Morgenthau's staff prepared the blistering "Report to the Secretary on the Acquiescence of This Government in the Murder of the Jews", which he personally pressed on Roosevelt. The result was the creation of the War Refugee Board in January 1944, which is credited with saving tens of thousands of lives.

The Morgenthau Plan and resignation

In September 1944 Roosevelt briefly approved Morgenthau's plan to deindustrialise and partition Germany into a largely agricultural state; when the plan leaked, provoking domestic criticism and Nazi propaganda, Roosevelt retreated from it. Morgenthau resigned in July 1945, shortly after Roosevelt's death, at President Truman's request. In retirement he was active in Jewish philanthropy, including support for Israel. His son Robert M. Morgenthau later served for decades as Manhattan District Attorney.
