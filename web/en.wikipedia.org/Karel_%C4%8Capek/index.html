Karel Čapek (1890–1938) was a Czech writer, playwright, and critic, one of the most influential Czech authors of the twentieth century. He is best known internationally for his science fiction and drama, including the play R.U.R. (1920), which introduced the word "robot" to the world, and the satirical novel War with the Newts (1936).

Life and work

Čapek studied philosophy in Prague, Berlin, and Paris, and worked as a journalist, including for Lidové noviny. With his brother Josef, who coined the word "robot" from the Czech robota (forced labor), he wrote plays and stories; his own works include The Absolute at Large, Krakatit, the noetic trilogy Hordubal, Meteor, and An Ordinary Life, and the beloved children's book Dashenka.

Politics and legacy

A friend of President Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, whose conversations he published, Čapek was a prominent defender of democracy against both fascism and communism. He died of pneumonia in December 1938, shortly after the Munich Agreement; the Gestapo later sought to arrest him, unaware he was dead. He was repeatedly nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature but never received it.
