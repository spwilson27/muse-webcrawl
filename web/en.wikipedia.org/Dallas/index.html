Dallas is a city in the U.S. state of Texas and the most populous city in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, the fourth-largest metropolitan area in the United States. Located in North Texas, it is the county seat of Dallas County, with portions extending into Collin, Denton, Kaufman, and Rockwall counties. With a population of roughly 1.3 million, Dallas is the third-most populous city in Texas and the ninth-most populous in the United States.

History

Dallas was founded in 1841 by John Neely Bryan, who established a trading post on the east bank of the Trinity River. It was incorporated as a city in 1856. The arrival of railroads in the 1870s transformed Dallas into a commercial hub for cotton, cattle, and later oil. In the 20th century the city became a center for banking, telecommunications, and technology.

On November 22, 1963, President John F. Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas as his motorcade passed through Dealey Plaza, an event commemorated today by the Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza.

Geography

Dallas lies in the Blackland Prairie region of North Texas along the Trinity River. The city has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers, mild winters, and occasional severe thunderstorms and tornadoes during spring.

Economy

The Dallas economy is diversified across defense, financial services, information technology, telecommunications, and transportation. The city is home to numerous corporate headquarters, including AT&T (headquartered in downtown Dallas), and the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas serves the Eleventh Federal Reserve District.

Culture and landmarks

Notable landmarks include Reunion Tower, the Dallas Arts District (the largest contiguous urban arts district in the United States), the Perot Museum of Nature and Science, and Fair Park, which hosts the annual State Fair of Texas. The Dallas Cowboys of the NFL, Dallas Mavericks of the NBA, and Dallas Stars of the NHL represent the region in professional sports.
