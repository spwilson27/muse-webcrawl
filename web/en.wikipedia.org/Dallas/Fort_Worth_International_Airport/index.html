Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (IATA: DFW) is the primary international airport serving the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex in the U.S. state of Texas. Situated roughly midway between the cities of Dallas and Fort Worth, it is one of the busiest airports in the world by passenger traffic and aircraft movements, and serves as the largest hub of American Airlines.

History

Construction began in the late 1960s after Dallas and Fort Worth agreed to build a shared regional airport. The airport opened for commercial service on January 13, 1974, with a dedication ceremony that included the landing of a Concorde — its first visit to the United States. It was renamed Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport in 1985.

Facilities

DFW covers about 17,207 acres and has five terminals (A, B, C, D, and E) with a semi-circular design, plus an under-construction sixth terminal (F). Terminals are connected landside by the DFW Skylink automated people mover, one of the largest airport train systems of its kind. Terminal D serves as the main international terminal.

Operations

American Airlines operates the overwhelming majority of flights at DFW, making the airport its largest hub. The airport offers nonstop service to numerous domestic destinations and dozens of international destinations across the Americas, Europe, Asia, and Oceania.
