The Germans of Romania (German: Rumäniendeutsche; Romanian: Germanii din România) are an ethnic group in Romania, historically one of the country's significant minorities. German-speaking communities have lived in present-day Romania for more than 800 years, most notably the Transylvanian Saxons, settled from the twelfth century, and the Banat Swabians, settled under the Habsburgs in the eighteenth century.

History

The Saxons were invited by the kings of Hungary to colonise and defend the southeastern frontier of Transylvania, and they founded towns such as Hermannstadt (Sibiu), Kronstadt (Brașov), and Schäßburg (Sighișoara), building the famous fortified churches of the region. The Swabians of the Banat and Satu Mare arrived later as part of Habsburg colonisation after the wars with the Ottoman Empire.

In the interwar period there were roughly 800,000 Germans in Greater Romania. The Second World War brought catastrophe: territorial losses, the deportation of some 70,000 Romanian Germans to forced labour in the Soviet Union in 1945, and expropriation. Under communism, West Germany paid Romania per-capita sums to permit German emigration, and after 1989 the great majority of the remaining community emigrated to Germany. A small but culturally active community remains, and Klaus Iohannis, an ethnic German from Sibiu, served as President of Romania from 2014 to 2024.
