Reboot (fiction)

In serial fiction, a reboot is a new start for an established fictional universe, franchise, or series that discards most or all previous continuity and begins the story again. The term is borrowed from computing, where rebooting restarts a system from scratch.

Types

A "hard reboot" wipes the continuity entirely — for example, Casino Royale (2006) restarting the James Bond films, or DC Comics' 1985 Crisis on Infinite Earths. A "soft reboot" retains some continuity while effectively relaunching the property, often serving simultaneously as a sequel for new and old audiences. Reboots differ from remakes, which retell a specific work, and from retcons, which revise continuity in place.

Rationale

Studios and publishers reboot franchises to attract new audiences, modernize dated elements, and free creators from accumulated continuity. Reboots are common in film, television, comics, and video games, and their reception among longtime fans is often mixed.
