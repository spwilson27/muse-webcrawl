Van der Waerden's theorem

Van der Waerden's theorem is a theorem in Ramsey theory stating that for any given positive integers r and k, there is some number N such that if the integers {1, 2, …, N} are coloured with r colours, there must be at least one monochromatic arithmetic progression of length k. In other words, sufficiently long finite colourings of the integers cannot avoid long monochromatic arithmetic progressions.

History

The theorem was conjectured by Baudet and proved in 1927 by the Dutch mathematician B. L. van der Waerden. It is a foundational result of Ramsey theory and a precursor to deeper results on arithmetic structure in sets of integers, including Szemerédi's theorem, which concerns arithmetic progressions in sets of positive density.

Van der Waerden numbers

The smallest such N is denoted W(r, k), the van der Waerden number. Only a few exact values are known: W(2,3) = 9, W(2,4) = 35, W(2,5) = 178, W(2,6) = 1,132. Bounds obtained from the original proof are extremely large; significant improvements came from the work of Timothy Gowers and others.
