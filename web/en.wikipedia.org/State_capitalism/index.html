State capitalism
Related conceptsDirigisme, developmental state, state-owned enterprise, sovereign wealth fund

State capitalism is an economic system in which the state undertakes commercial economic activity — through state-owned enterprises, sovereign wealth funds and directed credit — and markets are relied upon for production and allocation, but the state dominates or steers the economy for political and strategic ends. The term has been used with different meanings by Marxist theorists, liberal economists and political scientists.

History of the term

Anarchist and Marxist writers used "state capitalism" in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Wilhelm Liebknecht and later left critics of the Soviet Union, including Tony Cliff, applied it to societies where a bureaucracy exploited wage labour in place of private owners. Lenin described the New Economic Policy as state capitalist under a workers' state. In contemporary usage, popularised by analysts such as Ian Bremmer (The End of the Free Market, 2010), it describes systems in countries such as China, Russia, and Gulf states where governments use markets and national champions to maximise state power.

Features

Common features include large state-owned enterprises in energy, banking, telecoms and defence; industrial policy and subsidies; sovereign wealth funds such as Norway's Government Pension Fund Global, Singapore's Temasek, and funds in the Gulf; and control over capital flows. China's system following economic reform — mixing private enterprise with Party-state direction — is the most debated modern example, though Chinese officials describe it as a socialist market economy under Deng Xiaoping Theory and its successors.

Debates

Supporters argue state direction can drive rapid development, infrastructure and stability, citing East Asian developmental states. Critics argue it distorts competition, entrenches corruption and authoritarianism, and disadvantages foreign and private firms. Scholars distinguish state capitalism from state socialism, crony capitalism and mixed economies, and dispute whether it is a distinct model or a continuum of state intervention present in all capitalist economies.
