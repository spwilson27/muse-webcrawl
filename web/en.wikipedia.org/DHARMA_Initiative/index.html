The DHARMA Initiative is a fictional research project featured in the American television series Lost (2004–2010). The acronym stands for Department of Heuristics and Research on Material Applications. Founded in 1970 by University of Michigan researchers Gerald and Karen DeGroot and funded by the Hanso Foundation of Danish industrialist Alvar Hanso, the Initiative established a network of research stations on the show's mysterious island to study its unusual properties — electromagnetism, time, psychology, zoology, and meteorology among them.

Role in the series

The survivors of Oceanic Flight 815 discover the Initiative's abandoned stations, beginning with the Swan station (the "hatch"), whose occupants were required to enter the numbers 4, 8, 15, 16, 23, 42 into a computer every 108 minutes to contain an electromagnetic anomaly. Each station is identified by a name and an octagonal logo, and orientation films narrated by "Pierre Chang" explain the stations' purposes. In the show's mythology, the Initiative's island community was wiped out in 1992 in the "Purge", carried out by the island's original inhabitants (the "Others") under Benjamin Linus. The fifth season's time-travel storyline places several main characters among the Initiative at its 1977 peak, where their actions contribute to the "Incident" that necessitated the Swan's button protocol.

Cultural impact

The DHARMA Initiative became one of Lost's most recognisable elements; its logo, branded food labels, and jumpsuits are frequent references in popular culture, and the organisation featured prominently in the show's alternate reality games, including The Lost Experience.
