Veronica Lake (born Constance Frances Marie Ockelman; 1922–1973) was an American film, stage, and television actress. She became one of the most popular stars of the 1940s, famed for her peek-a-boo hairstyle, which covered one eye, and for her film noir pairings with Alan Ladd.

Career

Lake rose to fame in I Wanted Wings (1941) and Preston Sturges's Sullivan's Travels (1941). Her small stature paired well with the equally compact Alan Ladd, and together they made the classic noirs This Gun for Hire (1942), The Glass Key (1942), and The Blue Dahlia (1946). During the Second World War she changed her famous hairstyle at the government's request, after women working in factories imitated it and caught their hair in machinery.

Later life

Lake's career declined after her Paramount contract ended in the late 1940s, amid difficulties with alcohol and a reputation for being hard to work with. She later worked in television and theatre, published an autobiography in 1970, and died of hepatitis and kidney failure in 1973. A star bearing her name stands on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
