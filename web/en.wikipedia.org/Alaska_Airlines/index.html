Alaska Airlines

Alaska Airlines is a major American airline headquartered in SeaTac, Washington, near Seattle–Tacoma International Airport, its largest hub. Founded in Alaska in 1932 as McGee Airways, it is one of the oldest U.S. carriers still operating under a related name. Alaska and its sister regional carrier Horizon Air form the Alaska Air Group. The airline joined the Oneworld alliance in 2021 and, after acquiring Virgin America in 2016, agreed in 2024 to acquire Hawaiian Airlines.

History

Linious "Mac" McGee founded the airline in Anchorage in 1932 with a single three-seat Stinson aircraft. Through mergers it became Alaska Airlines in 1944 and expanded down the West Coast, pioneering routes to Russia's Far East in the Cold War's final years. The crash of Flight 261 off California in 2000, caused by a maintenance failure of the MD-80's jackscrew, killed 88 people and led to major reforms. Known for its customer-service awards and the smiling Alaska Native portrait on its tails (redesigned in 2016), the airline built a large West Coast network anchored on Seattle, Portland, San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Anchorage.

Fleet and services

Alaska operates an all-Boeing 737 mainline fleet, including the 737 MAX, while Horizon Air flies Embraer 175s. Its Mileage Plan programme became a full member benefit within Oneworld after 2021 membership.
