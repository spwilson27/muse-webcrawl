Dansgaard–Oeschger event

A Dansgaard–Oeschger event (often abbreviated D–O event) is a rapid climate fluctuation that occurred repeatedly during the last glacial period. About 25 such events have been identified in Greenland ice cores, roughly every 1,470 years on average. Each event is marked by an abrupt warming of Greenland temperatures — often 8–16 °C within a few decades — followed by a slower, gradual cooling back toward full glacial conditions, giving the record a characteristic sawtooth shape.

Discovery and causes

The events are named for ice-core researchers Willi Dansgaard and Hans Oeschger, whose work on the Greenland ice cores in the 1980s revealed them clearly. They are thought to involve rapid reorganisations of North Atlantic ocean circulation and sea ice, possibly coupled with changes in tropical rainfall and atmospheric circulation felt worldwide. In Antarctica, a more muted, opposite-phase signal appears, consistent with the "bipolar seesaw" of interhemispheric heat transport.

Relation to other events

The coldest phases between D–O warmings sometimes coincide with Heinrich events — episodes of massive iceberg discharge into the North Atlantic recorded in marine sediments. The mechanism behind the near-regular recurrence of D–O events remains an active research topic.
