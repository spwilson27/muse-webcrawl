Surrey

The North Downs near Box Hill, part of the Surrey Hills Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Surrey is a ceremonial county in South East England, bordered by Greater London to the north-east, Kent to the east, East and West Sussex to the south, Hampshire to the west, and Berkshire to the north-west. The county town is Guildford, although the county council (Surrey County Council) is based in Reigate. Surrey is the most wooded county in England and one of the most affluent; much of its northern area forms part of the London commuter belt.

Geography

The North Downs, a chalk ridge, runs across the county from west to east; south of it lies the Greensand Ridge and the Weald. Leith Hill, at 294 metres, is the highest point in south-eastern England. The Surrey Hills are designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. Rivers include the Thames, which forms much of the northern boundary, and its tributaries the Wey, Mole, and Tillingbourne.

History

Surrey's name derives from the Old English Sūþrīge, "southern district", reflecting its position south of the Thames relative to Middlesex. In the early medieval period it was a small Anglo-Saxon territory contested between Mercia, Wessex, and Kent. Kingston upon Thames, historically in Surrey, was a traditional coronation place of Saxon kings. The county's boundaries changed in 1889 and 1965, when areas closest to London were transferred to the expanding capital.

Settlements

Major towns include Guildford, Woking, Epsom, Ewell, Reigate, Redhill, Staines-upon-Thames, Weybridge, Farnham, and Camberley. Heathrow Airport lies just outside the historic county boundary; Gatwick Airport is on the Surrey–Sussex border.

Economy and transport

Surrey has one of the highest GDP per capita figures in the UK, with many corporate headquarters, technology firms, and research institutions (including the Surrey Research Park in Guildford). The M25, M3, and M23 motorways cross the county, and rail lines from London Waterloo and London Victoria serve its commuter towns.
