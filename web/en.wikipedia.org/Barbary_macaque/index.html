Barbary macaque

The Barbary macaque (Macaca sylvanus), also known as Barbary ape or magot, is a macaque species native to the Atlas Mountains of Algeria, Tunisia, and Morocco, together with a small introduced population in Gibraltar. It is one of the best-known Old World monkey species and the only macaque native to Africa.

Characteristics

Barbary macaques are tailless as adults, with yellowish-brown to grey fur and lighter undersides. Males weigh around 16 kg and females around 11 kg. Unusually among primates, males play an active role in caring for infants. Groups range from 10 to 100 individuals and are matriarchal.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered by the IUCN; wild populations, once widespread, have declined due to habitat loss and illegal capture for the pet trade. The Gibraltar population, long maintained by the British military, is a famous tourist attraction and the only wild monkey population in Europe.
