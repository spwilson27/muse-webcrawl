We Wish You a Merry Christmas

"We Wish You a Merry Christmas" is a traditional English Christmas carol, originating in the West Country of England. The song's lyrics wish listeners a merry Christmas and a happy New Year and famously demand "figgy pudding" from the hosts whom carolers visit.

History

The carol reflects the old custom of carol singing, in which bands of singers — often poor — performed at the doors of wealthy households in expectation of food, drink, or money; the demand "we won't go until we get some" captures that tradition. The tune was popularized in the 1930s by an arrangement of the Exeter composer Arthur Warrell, who published it in 1935 and performed it with the University of Bristol Madrigal Singers.

Lyrics and popularity

The refrain "We wish you a Merry Christmas and a Happy New Year" and verses asking for figgy pudding make it one of the most widely recognized carols in the English-speaking world, recorded by countless choirs and popular artists.
