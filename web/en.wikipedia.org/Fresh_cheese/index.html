Fresh cheese

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fresh cheese is a category of cheese that is not aged or ripened, and is intended to be consumed shortly after production. Unlike aged cheeses such as cheddar or Parmigiano-Reggiano, fresh cheeses are soft, mild in flavour, and often high in moisture, because they are made by curdling milk and draining the curds without a prolonged maturation period.

Production

Fresh cheeses are typically produced by acidifying milk, either through the action of lactic acid bacteria or by adding an acid such as lemon juice, vinegar, or citric acid, sometimes in combination with a small amount of rennet. The milk separates into curds and whey; the curds are drained, sometimes lightly salted, and shaped or packaged. Because no aging cultures develop complex flavours, fresh cheeses taste milky, mild, and slightly tangy.

Varieties

Well-known fresh cheeses include mozzarella, traditionally made from Italian buffalo milk and used on pizza; ricotta, made from whey; cottage cheese, a drained curd cheese popular in North America and Europe; queso fresco, a crumbly Mexican cheese; paneer, an acid-set cheese central to South Asian cooking; and mascarpone, a rich Italian cream cheese used in desserts such as tiramisu. French fromage blanc and German quark are spoonable fresh cheeses eaten with fruit or used in baking.

Storage and food safety

Because of their high moisture content and lack of protective aging, fresh cheeses are highly perishable. They must be refrigerated and are generally consumed within days of opening. Soft fresh cheeses have occasionally been implicated in listeriosis outbreaks when made from unpasteurised milk, which is why many jurisdictions require them to be produced from pasteurised milk.
