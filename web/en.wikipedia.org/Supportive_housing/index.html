Supportive housing

Supportive housing (also called permanent supportive housing, PSH) is an intervention that combines affordable, permanent housing with voluntary support services such as case management, mental health care, substance-use treatment and employment assistance. It is designed primarily for people experiencing chronic homelessness, often with disabilities or serious mental illness, and is the central housing model of the Housing First approach.

Models

Supportive housing is delivered in single-site buildings where all units are supportive, in mixed buildings, or as scattered-site apartments rented in the private market with mobile support teams. Tenants hold leases and pay a portion of their income in rent; services are offered but participation is not a condition of tenancy, distinguishing the model from treatment-first or transitional programmes.

Evidence

A large body of research, including randomised trials such as Canada's At Home/Chez Soi study and evaluations of the Pathways Housing First programme in New York, finds that supportive housing substantially improves housing stability for chronically homeless people. Studies also report reductions in the use of emergency rooms, hospitals, jails and shelters, which can offset a significant portion of programme costs, though effects on health and substance-use outcomes are more modest and debated.

Funding and policy

In the United States, supportive housing is funded mainly through the Department of Housing and Urban Development's Continuum of Care programme, often combined with Medicaid-funded services and tax-credit financed construction. Shortages of affordable units and local opposition ("NIMBYism") are persistent obstacles to expansion.
