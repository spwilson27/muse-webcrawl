The threshold limit value (TLV) is an occupational exposure limit published by the ACGIH for chemical substances and physical agents in the workplace. It represents the airborne concentration to which nearly all workers may be repeatedly exposed, day after day, without adverse health effects.

Definitions

Three categories of TLV are used. The TLV-TWA is the time-weighted average concentration for a conventional 8-hour working day and 40-hour week. The TLV-STEL is a short-term exposure limit, the average concentration over any 15-minute period, which should not be exceeded even if the TWA is satisfied. The TLV-C (ceiling) is a concentration that should not be exceeded at any instant. Values are expressed in parts per million (ppm) or milligrams per cubic metre (mg/m³).

Development and use

The ACGIH TLV Committee reviews scientific literature and updates the values annually; the first list appeared in 1946. Many U.S. OSHA permissible exposure limits (PELs) were adopted from the 1968 TLV list and have since diverged from current TLVs, which are generally stricter. TLVs also exist for physical agents including noise, heat stress, ultraviolet radiation and hand-arm vibration, and Biological Exposure Indices (BEIs) give corresponding levels of biomarkers in workers' bodies.

Criticism

Critics have questioned the influence of industry on the setting of some values and point out that TLVs are guidelines designed for occupational hygienists, not bright lines between safe and dangerous exposure; sensitive individuals may be affected below the TLV.
