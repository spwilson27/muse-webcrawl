The Battle of Hürtgen Forest (German: Schlacht im Hürtgenwald) was a series of battles fought from 19 September to 16 December 1944, between American and German forces on the Western Front during World War II, in the Hürtgen Forest, a forested area of about 50 square miles east of the Belgian–German border. It was the longest battle on German ground during World War II and the longest single battle the U.S. Army has ever fought.

Background

The U.S. objective was to pin down German forces in the area so they could not reinforce the front lines farther north in the Battle of Aachen, and to advance toward the Roer River dams. The dense forest, poor roads, mud, and tree-burst artillery fire negated American advantages in armour and air power.

The fighting

Successive American divisions — including the 9th, 28th, 4th and 8th Infantry Divisions — were ground down in attacks on villages such as Schmidt and Vossenack along the Kall Trail. German defenders under Field Marshal Walter Model held prepared positions of the Siegfried Line. The battle ended as attention shifted to the Ardennes offensive, and the forest was finally cleared in February 1945.

Casualties

American casualties were heavy, commonly estimated at around 33,000, while German casualties were roughly 28,000. The battle has often been criticised as an unnecessary attritional fight for ground of limited value.
