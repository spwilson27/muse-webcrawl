Tiananmen Square (天安门广场, Tiān'ānmén Guǎngchǎng) is a vast city square in central Beijing, China, named after the Tiananmen ("Gate of Heavenly Peace") at its northern end, which separates it from the Forbidden City. Measuring roughly 880 by 500 metres, it is among the largest public squares in the world and the symbolic centre of Chinese state power.

Features

The square was enlarged to its present form in 1959 for the tenth anniversary of the People's Republic of China. It is flanked by the Great Hall of the People to the west and the National Museum of China to the east; at its centre stands the Monument to the People's Heroes, and to the south the Memorial Hall housing the embalmed body of Mao Zedong. On 1 October 1949 Mao proclaimed the founding of the People's Republic from the Tiananmen rostrum.

Historical events

The square has been the stage for defining moments of modern Chinese history: the May Fourth Movement of 1919 began with protests here; the April 1976 Tiananmen Incident saw mourning for Zhou Enlai turn into protest; and in 1989 a seven-week pro-democracy protest movement centred on the square was ended by a military crackdown on the night of 3–4 June 1989, in which large numbers of protesters and bystanders were killed in western Beijing and around the square's approaches. The events remain censored within China.
