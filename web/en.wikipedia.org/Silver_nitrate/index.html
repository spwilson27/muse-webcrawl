Silver nitrate

Silver nitrate is an inorganic compound with chemical formula AgNO3. It is a versatile precursor to many other silver compounds, such as those used in photography. It is far less sensitive to light than the halides. It was once called lunar caustic because silver was called luna by ancient alchemists who associated silver with the moon.

Synthesis and properties

In solid silver nitrate, the silver ions are three-coordinated in a trigonal planar arrangement. Silver nitrate can be prepared by reacting silver, such as silver bullion or foil, with nitric acid, producing the salt with nitrogen oxide by-products. It is a colourless, odourless crystalline solid (melting point 212 °C) that is highly soluble in water. Silver nitrate readily stains skin and cloth black by reduction to metallic silver, and it is a strong oxidizer and mild antiseptic.

Applications

Silver nitrate is the least expensive salt of silver and the starting material for synthesizing silver iodide, silver bromide, and other photographic halides. In analytical chemistry it is used in Volhard and Mohr titrations to determine halide concentration. In medicine it is applied topically as an antiseptic and cauterizing agent (for example, in applicator sticks to remove warts and granulation tissue), and historically dilute solutions were dropped into newborns' eyes to prevent gonococcal ophthalmia neonatorum. It is also used in mirror making (Tollens' reagent), indelible inks, and hair dyes.

Safety

Silver nitrate is corrosive and toxic if ingested, and repeated exposure can cause argyria, a blue-gray discoloration of skin and mucous membranes.
