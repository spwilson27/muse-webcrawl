The 6th Airborne Division was an airborne infantry division of the British Army during the Second World War. It was formed in May 1943 under Major-General Richard "Windy" Gale and is best known for its role in the Normandy landings.

Normandy

In Operation Tonga, the airborne phase of the D-Day invasion on 6 June 1944, the division landed east of the River Orne to protect the left flank of the seaborne assault. Its troops captured the Caen canal and Orne river bridges — the famous "Pegasus Bridge" coup de main by the Oxfordshire and Buckinghamshire Light Infantry — destroyed the Merville Gun Battery, and held the ridge east of the Orne against German counter-attacks until relieved. The division remained in the line until August 1944, advancing to the Seine during the breakout.

Later operations

Rushed back to the continent in December 1944 during the Battle of the Bulge, the division fought in the Ardennes before taking part in Operation Varsity on 24 March 1945, the largest single-day airborne operation of the war, dropping east of the Rhine. It then advanced across northern Germany to the Baltic, reaching Wismar ahead of the Red Army. After the war the division served on internal security duties in Palestine before being disbanded in 1948.
