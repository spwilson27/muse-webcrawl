Lawsuit

A lawsuit is a civil legal proceeding in which a plaintiff brings a claim against a defendant before a court, seeking a remedy such as damages, an injunction or specific performance. Lawsuits are governed by rules of civil procedure and are distinguished from criminal prosecutions brought by the state.

Stages

A case typically begins with pleadings (complaint and answer), proceeds through discovery — depositions, interrogatories and document production — motions practice, and, if not settled or dismissed, trial before a judge or jury, followed by possible appeals. The vast majority of lawsuits settle before trial. Class actions aggregate many similar claims; litigation funding and contingency fees shape access in some jurisdictions.

Alternatives

Parties increasingly use arbitration and mediation as alternatives to litigation. See Law and society.
