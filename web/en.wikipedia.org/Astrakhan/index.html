Astrakhan


The white walls of the Astrakhan Kremlin.

Astrakhan is a city in southern Russia and the administrative centre of Astrakhan Oblast. It lies on the delta of the Volga River, close to where it discharges into the Caspian Sea, and has a population of roughly half a million.

History

The city was the capital of the Khanate of Astrakhan, a Tatar successor state of the Golden Horde, until Ivan the Terrible annexed it in 1556, giving Russia control of the entire Volga and an outlet to the Caspian. The present site dates from 1558; its white-stone kremlin was built in the late sixteenth century. Astrakhan was a centre of the Stenka Razin revolt in 1670–71 and later an important trading port linking Russia with Persia and Central Asia, dealing in silk, salt and fish.

Geography and economy

Astrakhan sits on eleven islands in the Volga delta, crossed by canals and bridges, with a continental, semi-arid climate of hot summers and cold winters. Historically famous for sturgeon and caviar, watermelon cultivation and salt, the modern economy includes shipbuilding, fishing and Caspian oil and gas services. The word "astrakhan" also denotes the curly pelt of karakul lambs traded through the city.
