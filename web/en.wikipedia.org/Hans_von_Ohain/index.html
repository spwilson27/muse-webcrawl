Hans Joachim Pabst von Ohain (14 December 1911 – 13 March 1998) was a German physicist and engineer, regarded as one of the two inventors of jet propulsion alongside the English engineer Frank Whittle, who developed the turbojet independently. Ohain's engine powered the world's first turbojet flight in 1939.

Work at Heinkel

After taking his doctorate in physics at the University of Göttingen in 1935, Ohain patented a centrifugal-flow turbojet design and in 1936 joined Ernst Heinkel's aircraft company, which was eager to reach record speeds. His Heinkel HeS 1 experimental engine ran on hydrogen in 1937; the refined HeS 3, burning liquid fuel, powered the Heinkel He 178 on its first flight on 27 August 1939 — the first flight of a turbojet aircraft, days before the outbreak of the Second World War.

Later career

Ohain's later wartime designs did not enter production, and the operational Messerschmitt Me 262 used Junkers engines instead. After the war he was brought to the United States under Operation Paperclip, working for the US Air Force at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base on propulsion and directed-energy research; he became a US citizen and retired in 1979. In 1991 he shared the Charles Stark Draper Prize with Whittle, the two former rivals having become friends.
