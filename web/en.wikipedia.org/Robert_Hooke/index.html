Robert Hooke

Robert Hooke FRS (18 July 1635 – 3 March 1703) was an English polymath and natural philosopher who played a central role in the Scientific Revolution. As curator of experiments to the Royal Society, he made contributions to microscopy, mechanics, gravitation, architecture, and instrument design.

Science

Hooke's Micrographia (1665), the first great work of microscopy, contained celebrated engravings of insects and objects seen through his microscope and introduced the word "cell" for the structures he saw in cork. He formulated Hooke's law of elasticity — the extension of a spring is proportional to the force applied. He also built telescopes and microscopes, proposed a wave theory of light, and suggested that gravity follows an inverse-square law, a claim that led to a famous priority dispute with Isaac Newton.

Architecture

As Surveyor to the City of London after the Great Fire of 1666, Hooke worked with Christopher Wren on rebuilding projects, including the Monument and several churches.
