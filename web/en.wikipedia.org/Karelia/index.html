Karelia
Region: Northern Europe
Divided between: Russia and Finland
Peoples: Karelians, Finns, Russians
Major lakes: Ladoga, Onega

Karelia is a historical region in Northern Europe, the land of the Karelian people, now divided between Russia (the Republic of Karelia and parts of Leningrad Oblast) and Finland (North and South Karelia). It lies between the White Sea, Lake Ladoga and the Gulf of Finland.

History

For centuries Karelia was contested between Sweden and Russia. After the Winter War (1939–40) and the Continuation War, Finland ceded large parts of Karelia, including the city of Vyborg, to the Soviet Union, and the Finnish population was evacuated westward.

Culture

Karelia is the heartland of the Finnish national epic, the Kalevala, compiled by Elias Lönnrot from oral poetry collected largely in Karelian villages. The Karelian language is closely related to Finnish. See also Kalevala.
