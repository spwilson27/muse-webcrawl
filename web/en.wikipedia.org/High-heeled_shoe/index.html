High-heeled shoe

A high-heeled shoe, commonly called a high heel, is a shoe with a raised heel, typically raising the wearer's heel substantially above the toes. High heels come in many styles, including pumps (court shoes), stilettos, platforms, wedges, and block heels, and are worn predominantly by women, though they have also been fashion items for men at various times.

History

Heeled footwear originated in Persia, where mounted soldiers in the 10th century wore heeled boots to keep their feet secure in stirrups. The style spread to Europe in the late 16th and early 17th centuries, where high heels became a mark of aristocratic status for men; Louis XIV of France famously wore red-heeled shoes and restricted their use by decree. By the mid-18th century heels had become associated mainly with women's fashion, and the French Revolution's egalitarian ideals pushed them out of fashion for a time, before their return in the mid-19th century.

Design

Heel height is measured from the ground to the bottom of the shoe's insole at the heel. Types include the stiletto (a thin heel at least 5 cm long), the kitten heel (short and slender), the block heel, the wedge, and the platform, which raises the toe as well and reduces the effective pitch. Christian Louboutin's red-lacquered soles and Salvatore Ferragamo's invention of the metal-reinforced stiletto in the 1950s are landmark designs.

Health effects

Prolonged use of high heels shifts body weight forward onto the forefoot and can contribute to foot deformities such as bunions and hammertoes, knee and back pain, shortened calf muscles, and an increased risk of falls and ankle sprains. These concerns have prompted debate over workplace dress codes that require women to wear heels.
