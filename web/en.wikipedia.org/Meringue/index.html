Meringue

Meringue is a type of dessert or candy traditionally made from whipped egg whites and sugar, and occasionally an acidic ingredient such as lemon, vinegar, or cream of tartar. A binding agent such as salt, flour, or gelatin may also be added. The key to a good meringue is the formation of stiff peaks, as the proteins in the egg whites form a stable foam.

Types

There are three main types of meringue. French meringue is made by whipping sugar into raw egg whites and is the least stable. Swiss meringue is made by heating egg whites and sugar over a bain-marie before whipping. Italian meringue is made by drizzling hot sugar syrup into whipped egg whites and is the most stable of the three.

Uses

Meringue is used in desserts such as pavlova, lemon meringue pie, baked Alaska, macarons, and Eton mess, and as a topping that is lightly browned before serving.
