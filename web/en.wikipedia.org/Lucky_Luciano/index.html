Lucky Luciano

Charles "Lucky" Luciano (born Salvatore Lucania; 1897–1962) was an Italian-born American gangster considered the father of modern organized crime in the United States. He reorganized the Italian-American Mafia into the Five Families and established the Commission as its governing body.

Early life and rise

Born in Lercara Friddi, Sicily, Luciano emigrated to New York as a child. He rose during Prohibition through bootlegging, allied with Meyer Lansky and Bugsy Siegel. In 1931 he ordered the murders of Joe Masseria and Salvatore Maranzano, ending the Castellammarese War.

Conviction and deportation

In 1936 prosecutor Thomas E. Dewey convicted Luciano on compulsory prostitution charges; he was sentenced to 30 to 50 years. During World War II he cooperated with naval intelligence. In 1946 his sentence was commuted on condition of deportation to Italy, where he lived until his death in Naples in 1962.

Legacy

Luciano is credited with turning the Mafia into a national syndicate run like a corporation, cooperating across ethnic lines.
