The College of William & Mary (abbreviated as W&M) is a public research university in Williamsburg, Virginia, United States. Founded in 1693 under a royal charter from King William III and Queen Mary II, it is the second-oldest institution of higher education in the United States, after Harvard University.

History

The college was founded at Middle Plantation, later Williamsburg, following advocacy by the Reverend James Blair, who served as its first president for fifty years. The Wren Building, the oldest academic building in continuous use in the United States, dates to the college's earliest decades. Alumni include U.S. presidents Thomas Jefferson, James Monroe, and John Tyler, as well as Supreme Court Chief Justice John Marshall.

Academics

William & Mary is known for its strong undergraduate liberal arts program alongside graduate and professional schools in law, business, education, and marine science. Its law school, founded in 1779, is the oldest in the United States. The college established the first intercollegiate honor code in the country and the Phi Beta Kappa honor society was founded there in 1776.

Campus

The historic campus adjoins Colonial Williamsburg and includes the Sunken Garden and Lake Matoaka. The university enrolls roughly 9,000 students and is classified among research universities with high research activity.
