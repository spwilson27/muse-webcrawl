Dry Tortugas National Park

Dry Tortugas National Park is an American national park located about 68 miles (109 km) west of Key West in the Gulf of Mexico, in Monroe County, Florida. The park preserves Fort Jefferson and the seven Dry Tortugas islands, plus extensive coral reefs and marine ecosystems. It covers about 64,700 acres (262 km²), most of it water, and is accessible only by boat or seaplane.

History

The islands were named by Juan Ponce de León in 1513: Las Tortugas for the many sea turtles, with "dry" added later to warn mariners of the absence of fresh water. The United States began building Fort Jefferson on Garden Key in 1846; the massive hexagonal brick fort — the largest masonry structure in the Americas — was intended to guard shipping lanes in the Gulf. It remained unfinished and served as a military prison during and after the Civil War; its most famous prisoner was Dr. Samuel Mudd, convicted in the Lincoln assassination conspiracy. The area became a national monument in 1935 and was redesignated a national park in 1992.

Nature

The park protects coral reefs, seagrass beds, shipwrecks, and nesting colonies of sooty terns and magnificent frigatebirds. Loggerhead and green sea turtles nest on its beaches, and the surrounding waters hold sharks, rays, and tropical fish popular with snorkelers and divers. A large portion of the park is designated a Research Natural Area where fishing is restricted to protect marine life.

Visiting

Most visitors arrive by the ferry from Key West or by seaplane, tour Fort Jefferson, snorkel, birdwatch, and camp on Garden Key. The park is among the least visited U.S. national parks, receiving well under 100,000 visitors in most years.
