Ecotourism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ecotourism (also called eco tourism and green tourism) is a form of tourism marketed as "responsible" travel to natural areas, conserving the environment, and improving the well-being of the local people. The stated purpose is generally to educate the traveller, provide funds for ecological conservation, directly benefit the economic development and political empowerment of local communities, and foster respect for different cultures and for human rights.

Wildlife viewing in a rainforest reserve

Since the 1980s, ecotourism has been considered an important endeavour by environmentalists, who see it as a way to make conservation economically viable — for example by funding protected areas through visitor fees and by giving local residents incentives to protect wildlife rather than exploit it. Costa Rica, Kenya, the Galápagos Islands, and Madagascar are frequently cited as leading ecotourism destinations; gorilla trekking in Rwanda and Uganda supports conservation of mountain gorillas through high-value permits.

Definition and principles

The International Ecotourism Society defines ecotourism as "responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment, sustains the well-being of the local people, and involves interpretation and education." Common principles include minimising physical and social impacts, building environmental and cultural awareness, providing direct financial benefits for conservation, and delivering financial benefits and empowerment to local people.

Criticism

Critics argue that the term is often co-opted as marketing ("greenwashing"), that tourism infrastructure can damage fragile environments, that revenue frequently leaks out to foreign operators rather than reaching local communities, and that the long-haul flights ecotourists take contribute to climate change. Certification schemes have been introduced to distinguish genuinely sustainable operations, with varying rigour.
