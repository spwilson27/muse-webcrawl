Szemerédi regularity lemma

In graph theory, the Szemerédi regularity lemma states that every large graph can be approximated by a structure consisting of a bounded number of parts such that the edges between most pairs of parts behave in a pseudo-random way. The lemma was proved by Endre Szemerédi, who introduced it in work connected with his proof of Szemerédi's theorem on arithmetic progressions in the 1970s.

FieldGraph theory, combinatorics
Proved byEndre Szemerédi
Year1975–1978
Key notionsε-regular pairs, energy increment

Statement

For every ε > 0 and every positive integer m there exist integers M and N such that every graph with at least N vertices can be partitioned into k parts, with m ≤ k ≤ M, where the parts differ in size by at most one, and all but an ε-fraction of the pairs of parts are ε-regular: for any two large subsets of a regular pair, the density of edges between them is close to the density between the parts themselves.

Proof idea

The standard proof uses an energy increment argument. One defines a mean-square density (energy) of a partition, shows that refining an irregular pair strictly increases the energy, and notes that the energy is bounded above by 1. Since each refinement step adds a definite amount, the process must stop after finitely many steps, yielding a regular partition. A consequence of this argument is that the bound M can be enormous — a tower of exponentials — and examples due to Gowers show that tower-type bounds are in fact necessary.

Applications

The regularity lemma is one of the most powerful tools in extremal and probabilistic combinatorics. It underlies the graph removal lemma, property testing results for dense graphs, and counting lemmas for small subgraphs. Variants include the hypergraph regularity lemma used by Gowers and by Nagle, Rödl, Schacht and Skokan in work on Szemerédi's theorem.
