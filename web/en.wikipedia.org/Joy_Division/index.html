Joy Division were an English rock band formed in Salford in 1976. The group consisted of vocalist Ian Curtis, guitarist/keyboardist Bernard Sumner, bassist Peter Hook and drummer Stephen Morris. One of the pioneering bands of the post-punk movement, they developed a sparse, atmospheric sound characterised by Hook's melodic high-register bass, Morris's machine-like drumming, and Curtis's deep baritone and bleak lyrics.

History

Sumner and Hook formed the band after attending the Sex Pistols' famous June 1976 Lesser Free Trade Hall concert in Manchester. Initially called Warsaw, they renamed themselves Joy Division in early 1978 (the name deriving from the sexual slavery wing of a Nazi concentration camp described in the novel House of Dolls) and signed to Tony Wilson's Factory Records. Produced by Martin Hannett, their debut album Unknown Pleasures (1979) was critically acclaimed, as was its Peter Saville-designed sleeve depicting the radio pulses of the first discovered pulsar. The single "Love Will Tear Us Apart" became their signature song.

Death of Ian Curtis and aftermath

Curtis struggled with epilepsy, depression and personal turmoil. On 18 May 1980, on the eve of the band's first American tour, he died by suicide, aged 23. The second album, Closer, was released posthumously in July 1980 to great acclaim. The surviving members, joined by Gillian Gilbert, regrouped as New Order, members having previously agreed that the band would not continue under the name if any member left. New Order went on to major success in electronic and dance music.

Legacy

Joy Division's influence extends across alternative rock, gothic rock, indie and electronic music; their story has been retold in the films 24 Hour Party People (2002) and Anton Corbijn's biopic Control (2007). Unknown Pleasures and Closer routinely appear on lists of the greatest albums of all time.
