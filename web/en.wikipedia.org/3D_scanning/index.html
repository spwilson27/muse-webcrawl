3D scanning

3D scanning is the process of analysing a real-world object or environment to collect data on its shape and appearance, and constructing a digital three-dimensional model from that data. The resulting models are used in reverse engineering, quality inspection, digital archiving of cultural heritage, medicine, entertainment and 3D printing.

Technologies

Scanners fall broadly into contact and non-contact types. Coordinate measuring machines probe surfaces physically. Non-contact active scanners emit light or radiation and measure its return: time-of-flight and phase-shift laser scanners (lidar) measure distance; structured-light scanners project patterns and infer depth from their deformation; and laser triangulation scanners use a camera offset from a laser line. Passive methods such as photogrammetry and stereo vision reconstruct geometry from ordinary photographs taken from multiple viewpoints.

Reconstruction

Raw scans produce a point cloud of millions of sampled points. Processing software registers multiple scans into a common coordinate system, removes noise and outliers, and converts the points into a polygonal mesh — typically a triangle mesh — which can be textured, repaired and exported in formats such as STL, OBJ or PLY.

Applications

Industrial metrology uses scanners to compare manufactured parts against CAD models. Museums scan artefacts for preservation and virtual exhibitions. In film and games, actors and props are scanned for digital doubles, and in dentistry intraoral scanners have largely replaced physical impressions.
