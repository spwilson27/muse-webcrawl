Inverted sugar syrup

Inverted sugar syrup, also called invert syrup or invert sugar, is a mixture of glucose and fructose produced by hydrolysing sucrose, either by heating with an acid such as citric acid or by the enzyme invertase. The name comes from the inversion of the direction of rotation of plane-polarized light by the mixture compared with the original sucrose solution.

Properties and uses

Invert syrup is sweeter than an equivalent sucrose solution because fructose is sweeter than sucrose, and its sugars resist crystallization and retain moisture. It is widely used by bakers and confectioners — in fondants, ganache, and sorbets — and in brewing and commercial golden syrups. Partially inverted syrups, such as golden syrup and treacle, are produced industrially by acid or enzymatic conversion.
