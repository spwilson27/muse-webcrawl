The Großer Tiergarten, Berlin

The Großer Tiergarten is Berlin's most popular inner-city park, located in the district of Tiergarten in the borough of Mitte. Covering some 210 hectares (520 acres), it is comparable in size to Hyde Park in London or Central Park in New York, and is one of the largest urban gardens in Germany.

The area originated in the 16th century as a hunting ground (German: Tiergarten, "animal garden") for the electors of Brandenburg. In the 18th century Frederick the Great had it remodelled into a pleasure park. In the 1830s and 1840s the landscape architect Peter Joseph Lenné redesigned it as an English-style landscape park.

The park is traversed by the Straße des 17. Juni, which runs from the Brandenburg Gate westward through the park to Charlottenburg. At a large roundabout in the centre stands the Berlin Victory Column (Siegessäule), erected to commemorate Prussian victories in the 19th century. The park also contains the Soviet War Memorial, Bellevue Palace (the residence of the President of Germany), numerous embassies, and the Haus der Kulturen der Welt.

Heavily damaged in the Second World War and stripped of trees for firewood in the post-war winters, the Tiergarten was replanted from 1949 onwards. Today it is a protected garden monument and a popular place for recreation, bordered to the south by the Landwehr Canal and Potsdamer Platz.
