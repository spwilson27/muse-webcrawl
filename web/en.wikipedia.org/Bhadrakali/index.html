Bhadrakali

Bhadrakali (Sanskrit: Bhadrakālī, literally "the auspicious Kali") is a Hindu goddess, a benevolent and protective form of the goddess Kali (Mahakali). She is especially worshipped in South India, above all in Kerala, where she is regarded as the presiding deity of many temples and as a guardian mother goddess who protects her devotees and destroys evil.

Mythology

According to the Markandeya Purana and regional traditions, Bhadrakali was created from the wrath of Shiva — in one well-known Kerala tradition, from Shiva's third eye or from his matted hair — to destroy the demon Darika, who had obtained a boon making him invulnerable to men. Accompanied by the vetala and by Shiva's attendants, Bhadrakali slew Darika and is said to have danced in triumph, pacified only when Shiva lay in her path, a motif she shares with Kali of the Chidambaram and Daksha myths.

Worship

Bhadrakali temples are found throughout Kerala and parts of Tamil Nadu and coastal Karnataka; notable shrines include the Kodungallur Bhagavathy Temple, linked to the legend of Kannaki, and the Attukal temple in Thiruvananthapuram, famous for the Attukal Pongala festival in which millions of women offer cooked rice to the goddess. Ritual arts such as Mudiyettu — inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage — and Theyyam enact her myths. As one of the Sapta Matrikas and a form of Adi Shakti, she is also propitiated for protection from epidemics and misfortune.
