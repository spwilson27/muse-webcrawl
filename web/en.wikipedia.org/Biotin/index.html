Biotin

Biotin (also known as vitamin B7 or formerly vitamin H) is one of the B vitamins. It is involved in a wide range of metabolic processes, both in humans and in other organisms, primarily related to the utilization of fats, carbohydrates, and amino acids. Biotin functions as a covalently bound coenzyme for carboxylase enzymes, which catalyse the transfer of carbon dioxide in fatty acid synthesis, gluconeogenesis and amino acid catabolism.

Sources and deficiency

Biotin is found in foods such as eggs, meat, nuts and seeds, and is also produced by gut bacteria. Deficiency is rare but can cause hair loss, dermatitis, conjunctivitis and neurological symptoms; it may occur with consumption of raw egg whites, whose protein avidin binds biotin strongly, or in genetic disorders such as biotinidase deficiency.

Uses

Biotin supplements are widely marketed for hair and nail health, although evidence of benefit in non-deficient people is weak. High-dose supplemental biotin can interfere with laboratory immunoassays, including thyroid and troponin tests. In biochemistry, the extremely strong biotin–streptavidin interaction is exploited for purification and labelling of molecules.
