Lake Winnebago

Lake Winnebago is a freshwater lake in east-central Wisconsin, United States. At about 137,700 acres (215 square miles), it is the largest lake entirely within the state. Cities on its shores include Oshkosh, Fond du Lac, Neenah and Menasha.

Geology and geography

The lake is a remnant of Glacial Lake Oshkosh, formed about 12,000 years ago when ice dammed drainage. It is shallow, averaging under 16 feet deep. The Fox River enters from Lake Poygan through Lake Butte des Morts and leaves at Neenah and Menasha toward Green Bay; locks and dams regulate the level.

Ecology and recreation

Lake Winnebago supports one of the largest self-sustaining lake sturgeon populations in North America, famous for its winter spearfishing season. Walleye, perch and white bass fisheries are also important. The lake is used for boating, sailing and ice fishing. Water-quality problems include algal blooms and phosphorus loading.

Communities

Fond du Lac lies at the southern tip, Oshkosh on the western shore, and the Fox Cities communities of Neenah, Menasha and Appleton (via the river) at the north.
