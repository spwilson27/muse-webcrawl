Wisconsin Legislature

Image placeholder: The Wisconsin State Capitol in Madison

The Wisconsin Legislature is the state legislature of the U.S. state of Wisconsin. It is a bicameral body composed of the upper house, the Wisconsin Senate, and the lower house, the Wisconsin State Assembly. Both houses meet at the Wisconsin State Capitol in Madison.

Composition

The Senate has 33 members, one elected from each of the state's 33 senate districts, serving four-year terms. The Assembly has 99 members, each representing a single-member district and serving two-year terms. Legislative sessions run in two-year biennia beginning in January of odd-numbered years.

History

The legislature was created when Wisconsin became a state in 1848, succeeding the territorial legislature established in 1836. In recent decades the drawing of legislative district maps — notably the 2011 redistricting — has been the subject of major litigation, including the U.S. Supreme Court case Gill v. Whitford.
