Megaloblastic anemia

Megaloblastic anemia is a type of macrocytic anemia. An anemia is a red blood cell defect that can lead to an undersupply of oxygen. Megaloblastic anemia results from inhibition of DNA synthesis during red blood cell production. When DNA synthesis is impaired, the cell cycle cannot progress from the G2 growth stage to the mitosis (M) stage, leading to continuing cell growth without division, which presents as macrocytosis.

Causes

The most common causes are deficiency of vitamin B12 (cobalamin) and folate. B12 deficiency may result from pernicious anemia (autoimmune destruction of gastric parietal cells and loss of intrinsic factor), gastrectomy, dietary insufficiency, malabsorption, or certain drugs; folate deficiency may arise from poor diet, alcoholism, pregnancy, malabsorption, or antifolate medicines such as methotrexate. Other causes include copper deficiency and certain inherited metabolic disorders.

Signs and diagnosis

Symptoms include fatigue, pallor, glossitis, and — in B12 deficiency — neurological features such as peripheral neuropathy and cognitive changes. Blood tests show a raised mean corpuscular volume, hypersegmented neutrophils, and megaloblastic precursors in the bone marrow; serum B12, folate, homocysteine, and methylmalonic acid help identify the cause.

Treatment

Treatment consists of replacing the deficient vitamin, by injection or high-dose oral B12, or oral folic acid, together with correcting the underlying cause. Folate must not be given alone in B12 deficiency, as it may improve the anemia while neurological damage progresses.
