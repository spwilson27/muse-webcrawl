Glasgow

Glasgow (Scots: Glesca; Scottish Gaelic: Glaschu) is the most populous city in Scotland, situated on the banks of the River Clyde in west central Scotland. With a population of roughly 635,000 in the city council area and around 1.8 million in the wider metropolitan region, it is Scotland's largest city and the third most populous in the United Kingdom.

History

Glasgow grew from a religious settlement associated with Saint Mungo (Kentigern) in the 6th century and became a bishopric and later archbishopric; the University of Glasgow was founded in 1451. After the Acts of Union 1707, Glasgow became a major Atlantic trading port — first in tobacco, then cotton and sugar — and in the 19th century it was celebrated as the "Second City of the Empire", a powerhouse of heavy engineering and shipbuilding on the Clyde, whose yards launched a large share of the world's tonnage.

Economy and culture

The decline of heavy industry in the 20th century brought hardship, but Glasgow reinvented itself through finance, education, tourism and culture; it was European City of Culture in 1990 and hosted the 2014 Commonwealth Games and the COP26 climate summit in 2021. The city is famous for its Victorian and Edwardian architecture, the work of Charles Rennie Mackintosh, its art collections, and its two great football clubs, Celtic and Rangers, whose rivalry is known as the Old Firm.
