Dong Qichang

Dong Qichang (Chinese: 董其昌; pinyin: Dǒng Qíchāng; Wade–Giles: Tung Ch'i-ch'ang, 1555–1636), courtesy name Xuanzai (玄宰), was a Chinese painter, scholar, calligrapher, and art theorist of the later period of the Ming dynasty. He is regarded as one of the most influential figures in the history of Chinese painting, both for his own landscape paintings and for his theory of the Northern and Southern schools of painting.

Life

Dong was born in Huating, in present-day Shanghai. A precocious student, he passed the provincial examinations at seventeen but initially placed low because of his poor calligraphy — a humiliation that drove him to master the art. He eventually passed the imperial examinations with the rank of jinshi in 1589 and rose to high office, becoming Minister of Rites in Nanjing. His official career was interrupted by periods of retirement, during which he painted, wrote, and amassed a famous collection of earlier masters' works.

Painting

Dong's landscape paintings are deliberately abstract and intellectual, built from calligraphic brushwork organised into bold, often dislocated masses of mountains. He studied the Song and Yuan masters, above all Huang Gongwang, Ni Zan, and Wang Meng, and transformed their manners into a personal, expressive style. His motto was that although in reading ten thousand books and travelling ten thousand miles, the artist's true aim was the expression of the brush-and-ink idea rather than naturalistic resemblance.

The Northern and Southern schools theory

Dong's most consequential contribution was his theory dividing Chinese painting into two lineages, by analogy with the Northern and Southern schools of Chan (Zen) Buddhism. The Northern school — professional, academic, and gradually accomplished — he traced from Li Sixun through the Song academy painters. The Southern school — sudden, spontaneous, and amateur — he traced from the Tang poet-painter Wang Wei through the Yuan literati to himself. Although historically tendentious, the theory elevated literati (wenren) painting to canonical supremacy and shaped taste in China for three centuries, guiding the work of the Four Wangs of the early Qing.

Calligraphy

As a calligrapher Dong modelled himself on Yan Zhenqing, Yu Shinan, and especially Mi Fu, producing a fluid, elegant running script that was widely imitated in his lifetime.
