The Rolling Stones are an English rock band formed in London in 1962. Active across seven decades, they are one of the most popular and enduring bands of the rock era, identified with the youthful rebellion of the 1960s and the counterculture.

Their core partnership is singer Mick Jagger and guitarist Keith Richards, with drummer Charlie Watts from 1963 until his death in 2021.

History

Founded by Brian Jones with Jagger, Richards, Ian Stewart, Bill Wyman and Watts, the band first played blues and R&B covers before the Jagger–Richards songwriting team produced hits like "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction" (1965). Albums Beggars Banquet (1968), Let It Bleed (1969), Sticky Fingers (1971) and Exile on Main St. (1972) are considered their classic peak.

Later career

The Stones became one of the world's highest-grossing live acts, with tours including Steel Wheels, Voodoo Lounge, A Bigger Bang and No Filter. Hackney Diamonds (2023) was their first album of original material in 18 years.

Legacy

They have sold over 200 million records, were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1989, and their tongue-and-lips logo is an icon of popular music.
