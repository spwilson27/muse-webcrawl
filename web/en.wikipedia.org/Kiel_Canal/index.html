The Kiel Canal (German: Nord-Ostsee-Kanal, "North Sea–Baltic Sea Canal"; until 1948 officially the Kaiser-Wilhelm-Kanal) is a 98-kilometre (61 mi) freshwater canal in the German state of Schleswig-Holstein. It links the North Sea at Brunsbüttel to the Baltic Sea at Kiel-Holtenau, saving ships an average of about 460 km (290 mi) compared with sailing around the Jutland Peninsula.

History

The first connection between the two seas was the Eider Canal, completed in 1784. Construction of the present canal began in 1887 under Kaiser Wilhelm I, driven by the German navy's need to move warships between the Baltic and the North Sea without passing through Danish waters. Kaiser Wilhelm II opened it on 21 June 1895. It was widened between 1907 and 1914, internationalised by the Treaty of Versailles, and remains one of the busiest artificial waterways in the world by number of vessels.
