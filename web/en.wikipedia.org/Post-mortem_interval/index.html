Post-mortem interval

The post-mortem interval (PMI) is the time that has elapsed since an individual's death. When the time of death is not known, forensic investigators estimate the PMI using evidence left by post-mortem changes in the body.

Stages and signs

Early post-mortem changes include pallor mortis, algor mortis (cooling), rigor mortis (stiffening) and livor mortis (lividity). Later stages involve decomposition: autolysis, putrefaction, and eventual skeletonisation.

Methods of estimation

Techniques include measuring body temperature (Henssge's nomogram), examining potassium levels in vitreous humour, forensic entomology (insect succession), and assessing the degree of decomposition. All methods carry margins of error that widen as the PMI grows.
