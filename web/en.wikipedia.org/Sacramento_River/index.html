Sacramento River

The Sacramento River is the principal river of Northern California in the United States and, at 400 miles (640 km) long, the state's longest river. Rising in the Klamath Mountains near Mount Shasta, it flows southwards through the Sacramento Valley before joining the San Joaquin River and flowing into Suisun Bay, San Pablo Bay and San Francisco Bay.

Geography

The river drains about 26,500 square miles in 19 California counties, mostly within the fertile Sacramento Valley bounded by the Sierra Nevada to the east and the Coast Ranges to the west. Major tributaries include the Pit, Feather and American rivers. The river passes the cities of Redding, Chico, Colusa, Sacramento and Rio Vista.

History and use

The river was named Río de los Sacramentos ("River of the Sacraments") by Spanish explorer Gabriel Moraga in 1808. It became a key transport route during the California Gold Rush. Today, the Sacramento is central to California's water system: Shasta Dam creates Shasta Lake, and the river supplies irrigation for the Sacramento Valley and water exported south through the Central Valley Project and State Water Project via the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta.

Ecology

The river supports runs of Chinook salmon and steelhead, though populations have been affected by dams, diversions and habitat change. Riparian habitat and flood bypasses such as the Yolo Bypass provide important flood control and wildlife areas.
