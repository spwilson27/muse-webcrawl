Fernando José Corbató (July 1, 1926 – July 12, 2019) was an American computer scientist, notable as a pioneer in the development of time-sharing operating systems. He spent his career at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where he led the team that built the Compatible Time-Sharing System (CTSS) and initiated the Multics project.

Early life and education

Corbató was born in Oakland, California, to parents from Villarreal, Spain. During the Second World War he served as a technician in the U.S. Navy. He earned his bachelor's degree from the California Institute of Technology in 1950 and his PhD in physics from MIT in 1956, joining the MIT Computation Center that year.

Time-sharing and Multics

Frustrated by the inefficiency of batch processing, Corbató led the development of CTSS, first demonstrated in 1961, which allowed many users to share a single computer interactively through terminals. CTSS introduced foundational practices including the use of computer passwords for individual accounts. His 1962 experience with CTSS led to Multics, the ambitious time-sharing system that influenced the design of Unix and of modern operating systems with its hierarchical file system, dynamic linking, and emphasis on security.

Honours

Corbató received the ACM Turing Award in 1990 "for his pioneering work in organizing the concepts and leading the development of the general-purpose, large-scale, time-sharing and resource-sharing computer systems." "Corbató's Law" states that the number of lines of code a programmer can write in a fixed period depends on the language but not the programmer — a remark on the limits of productivity.
