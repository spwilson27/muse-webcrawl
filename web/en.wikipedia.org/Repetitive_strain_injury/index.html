Repetitive strain injury

Repetitive strain injury
SpecialtyOccupational medicine, orthopaedics
SymptomsAching, pain, tingling, weakness in affected muscles or joints
CausesRepetitive movements, overexertion, poor posture, vibration
PreventionErgonomic equipment, rest breaks, technique changes

A repetitive strain injury (RSI) is an injury to part of the musculoskeletal or nervous system caused by repetitive use, vibrations, compression or sustained awkward positions. RSIs are a category of injury rather than a single diagnosis, encompassing specific conditions such as carpal tunnel syndrome, tendinitis, bursitis, cubital tunnel syndrome and trigger finger, as well as diffuse pain syndromes without a clear structural lesion.

Causes and risk factors

RSIs develop when repeated loading of muscles, tendons or nerves exceeds the tissue's capacity to recover. Contributing factors include high repetition, forceful exertion, static posture, cold, vibration and insufficient rest. Occupations at elevated risk include assembly-line work, meat processing, musicianship, and prolonged computer use, where the condition is sometimes called "mouse arm". Psychosocial factors such as job stress and low job control also influence symptoms and recovery.

Symptoms

Typical symptoms are aching or shooting pain, stiffness, throbbing, tingling or numbness, weakness and cramps in the affected limb, most often the hands, wrists, forearms, elbows, neck or shoulders. Symptoms often begin gradually, occurring first during the provoking activity and resolving with rest, but may become persistent if the activity continues unchanged.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on history and examination, identifying the activities that provoke symptoms and excluding other causes such as inflammatory arthritis or cervical radiculopathy. Treatment emphasises modifying or resting from the provoking activity, ergonomic adjustment of the workstation, splinting, physiotherapy and graded exercise; anti-inflammatory medication may help specific conditions. Surgery is reserved for defined conditions — for example carpal tunnel release — that fail conservative care.

History

Bernardino Ramazzini described occupational overuse disorders in his 1713 treatise on workers' diseases. The term RSI spread widely in the 1980s, when Australia experienced a much-debated epidemic of arm pain among office workers, prompting lasting controversy about the relative roles of tissue injury, workplace design and psychosocial factors.
