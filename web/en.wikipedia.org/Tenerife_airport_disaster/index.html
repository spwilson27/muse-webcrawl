Tenerife airport disaster

The two aircraft collided on the runway at Los Rodeos Airport

The Tenerife airport disaster occurred on 27 March 1977, when two Boeing 747 passenger jets collided on the runway at Los Rodeos Airport (now Tenerife North Airport) on the Spanish island of Tenerife. With 583 fatalities, it remains the deadliest accident in aviation history.

Background

Both aircraft had been diverted to Los Rodeos after a bomb explosion at Gran Canaria Airport closed that airport. The airport's single runway became congested as diverted flights waited; dense fog reduced visibility so that neither flight crew nor the control tower could see the full runway. KLM Flight 4805 and Pan Am Flight 1736 were instructed to backtaxi down the runway to position for departure.

Collision

The KLM captain, Jacob Veldhuyzen van Zanten, began the takeoff roll without having received explicit takeoff clearance, believing he had been cleared. The Pan Am aircraft, which should have exited the runway at an earlier taxiway, was still on the runway in the fog. The KLM 747 struck the Pan Am jet at speed; all 248 aboard the KLM flight died, as did 335 of the 396 aboard the Pan Am aircraft, leaving 61 survivors.

Investigation and legacy

Investigators cited the KLM crew's premature takeoff, ambiguous radio phraseology (the non-standard "We are now at takeoff"), simultaneous transmissions that blocked a warning, and the fog. The disaster led to worldwide reforms: standardized aviation English phraseology, emphasis on crew resource management, and the requirement that takeoff clearances be read back explicitly.
