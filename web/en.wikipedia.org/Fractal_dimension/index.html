Fractal dimension

FieldGeometry, chaos theory

In mathematics, a fractal dimension is a term invoked in the science of geometry to provide a rational statistical index of complexity detail in a pattern. It measures how the detail in a fractal pattern changes with the scale at which it is measured, and unlike ordinary topological dimensions it need not be an integer.

Definitions

The Hausdorff dimension is the most rigorous definition, based on covering a set with balls of decreasing radius. The box-counting (Minkowski) dimension is computed practically: if N(ε) boxes of side ε are needed to cover the set, the dimension is the limit of log N(ε) / log(1/ε) as ε → 0. For self-similar sets made of N copies scaled by factor r, the similarity dimension is log N / log(1/r).

Examples

The Koch snowflake has dimension log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.262. The Sierpiński triangle has dimension log 3 / log 2 ≈ 1.585. Natural objects have statistical fractal dimensions over limited ranges: the coastline of Britain is often measured around 1.25, clouds around 1.3–1.35 in volume projections.

Applications

Fractal dimensions are used to characterize coastlines, porous materials, turbulence, biological structures such as lungs and neurons, and in image analysis and medical diagnostics.
