Tinariwen


Tinariwen performing live

Tinariwen (Tamasheq: "deserts") is a group of Tuareg musicians from the Sahara region of northern Mali. Formed in 1979 in Tamanrasset, Algeria, by guitarist and singer Ibrahim Ag Alhabib, the band pioneered the style known as assouf or "desert blues", fusing traditional Tuareg melodies and rhythms with electric guitar and the influence of Western rock and blues.

The founding members were exiles and former rebels: many had received military training in Libya and took part in the Tuareg rebellion of the early 1990s in Mali and Niger. Their early recordings circulated on cassette among Tuareg communities, with songs expressing exile, longing and the political aspirations of the Kel Tamasheq people.

Tinariwen reached an international audience with the albums Amassakoul (2004) and Aman Iman (2007). Their 2011 album Tassili, recorded acoustically in the Algerian desert, won the Grammy Award for Best World Music Album. Subsequent records include Emmaar (2014), recorded in California, Elwan (2017) and Amatssou (2023).

The band has collaborated with artists such as Robert Plant, Carlos Santana and members of TV on the Radio, and has performed at festivals including Glastonbury and Coachella. Despite instability in Mali, which forced members into exile again after 2012, Tinariwen remains the most internationally prominent exponent of Tuareg music.
