River Ravensbourne

The River Ravensbourne is a tributary of the River Thames in south London, England. It flows into the tidal River Thames at Deptford, where its tidal reach is known as Deptford Creek. The Ravensbourne is 17.4 km (10.8 mi) in length and flows through the London boroughs of Bromley, Greenwich and Lewisham. In the period 1985–2009 several stretches of the river were restored from concrete channels into more natural courses in local parks.

Course

The river rises at Caesar's Well on Keston Common near Bromley and flows north through Bromley town centre, Beckenham, Catford and Lewisham, where it is joined by the River Quaggy. From Lewisham to Deptford it passes through Brookmill Park and Deptford, joining the Thames just west of Greenwich. The river gave its name to Ravensbourne University London and to the surrounding district of Deptford historically associated with watermills, including the mills recorded at Domesday.

History

Legend associates the river with Julius Caesar and with a raven omen. Watermills along its course powered flour mills and, in the 17th century, mills supplying the Royal Naval Dockyard at Deptford. Like other London rivers, much of the Ravensbourne was culverted or confined in concrete during the 20th century, but sections in Ladywell Fields and Beckenham Place Park have been re-naturalised to reduce flood risk and improve habitats.
