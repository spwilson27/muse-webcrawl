Bogeyman

GroupingLegendary creature
FolkloreWorldwide
Other namesBoogeyman, boogie man, boogie monster, boogieman

The bogeyman (also spelled boogeyman, boogie man, or boogieman) is a type of legendary creature used by adults to frighten children into good behaviour. Bogeymen have no specific appearance, and conceptions vary drastically by household and culture, but they are often described as masculine or androgynous monsters that punish children for misbehaviour.

Origins

The word bogeyman originates from the Middle English bogge or bugge, meaning a frightening spectre or hobgoblin; related terms include "bugbear". Comparable figures exist in many cultures: the sack man (el hombre del saco) in Spanish-speaking countries, Krampus in Alpine folklore, and Baba Yaga in Slavic tradition.

Characteristics

Unlike specific monsters, the bogeyman is deliberately vague: it may hide under beds or in closets, kidnap disobedient children in a sack, or merely watch. Its ambiguity lets each child's imagination supply the frightening details, and caregivers have invoked it for centuries as a disciplinary device.

In popular culture

The bogeyman is a recurring figure in horror fiction and film, appearing as the title character in Stephen King's short story "The Boogeyman" and in numerous movies. It has also become a metaphor in politics and journalism for an exaggerated or imaginary threat.
