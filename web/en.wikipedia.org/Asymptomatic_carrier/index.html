Asymptomatic carrier

An asymptomatic carrier is a person or other organism that has become infected with a pathogen, but displays no signs or symptoms of disease. Such carriers can still transmit the infection to others — sometimes for years — making them a central challenge in epidemiology and public health. If a carrier later develops symptoms, they were "presymptomatic" during the earlier phase; true asymptomatic carriers never develop symptoms.

Typhoid Mary

The most famous asymptomatic carrier was Mary Mallon (1869–1938), an Irish-American cook in New York who carried Salmonella Typhi, the bacterium causing typhoid fever. Traced by sanitary engineer George Soper, she was linked to dozens of infections and several deaths, refused to accept that she could spread a disease she had never suffered, and spent much of her later life in enforced quarantine on North Brother Island — remembered as "Typhoid Mary".

Diseases with carrier states

Chronic carriage occurs in typhoid fever (the gallbladder serving as a reservoir), hepatitis B and C, cholera, diphtheria, meningococcal disease, group A streptococcal infection, polio (in which about 90–95% of infections are asymptomatic) and MRSA colonisation, among others. Carriers may be incubatory, convalescent or healthy (never symptomatic).

COVID-19

During the COVID-19 pandemic, transmission by presymptomatic and asymptomatic people infected with SARS-CoV-2 proved a major driver of spread, complicating symptom-based screening and motivating mass testing, masking and ventilation measures.

Management

Control strategies include screening of blood donors and food handlers, contact tracing, vaccination, carrier treatment (for example eradicating typhoid carriage), and, historically, quarantine — raising enduring ethical questions about liberty versus public protection.
