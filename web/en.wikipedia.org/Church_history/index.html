Church history

Church history, or history of the church, is the study of the Christian Church from its origins in the first century AD to the present day. It is also known as ecclesiastical history. As an academic discipline it examines the institutional, intellectual, social, and spiritual development of Christianity, drawing on theology, archaeology, and the documentary record.

Scope and periods

Church historians commonly divide the subject into periods: the early church (to c. 325 and the Council of Nicaea), the imperial church of late antiquity, the medieval church in East and West following the Great Schism of 1054, the Reformation and confessional age of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, and the modern era of missions, ecumenism, and global Christianity.

Historiography

The first great church historian was Eusebius of Caesarea, whose Ecclesiastical History (early fourth century) chronicled the church from the apostles to his own time and set the genre's pattern. Socrates Scholasticus, Sozomen, and Theodoret continued his work in the East. In the modern era, critical methods reshaped the field; historians such as Adolf von Harnack in the nineteenth century and Jaroslav Pelikan in the twentieth applied rigorous source criticism to doctrinal development.

Themes

Recurring themes in church history include the relationship between church and state, the formation of doctrine and heresy, monasticism, missions and expansion beyond Europe, the role of women, and the interactions of Christianity with other religions and cultures.
