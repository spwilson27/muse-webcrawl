Phytanic acid

Phytanic acid (3,7,11,15-tetramethylhexadecanoic acid) is a branched-chain fatty acid derived from the phytol side chain of chlorophyll. Humans obtain it from the diet, principally from dairy products, ruminant fats and certain fish.

Metabolism

Because the methyl group at the 3-position blocks beta-oxidation, phytanic acid is first shortened by alpha-oxidation in peroxisomes, a pathway whose first step is catalysed by the enzyme phytanoyl-CoA hydroxylase (PhyH), producing pristanic acid, which can then undergo beta-oxidation.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of phytanoyl-CoA hydroxylase causes Refsum disease, an autosomal recessive disorder in which phytanic acid accumulates in tissues, leading to retinitis pigmentosa, peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar ataxia and other symptoms. Management centres on dietary restriction of phytanic acid and its precursor phytol.
