Marie Georges Picquart (6 September 1854 – 19 January 1914) was a French Army officer and the Minister of War, best known for his central role in the Dreyfus affair, in which his investigation uncovered that the real traitor was Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy and that Alfred Dreyfus was innocent.

The Dreyfus affair

In 1895 Picquart became head of the army's intelligence section, the Section de statistique. Reviewing the evidence, he discovered in 1896 the petit bleu telegram addressed to Esterhazy by a German military attaché, and recognised Esterhazy's handwriting as that of the bordereau for which Dreyfus had been convicted of treason in 1894. When Picquart pressed his findings on his superiors, he was ordered to keep silent, transferred to Tunisia, and later arrested and imprisoned for allegedly revealing secrets. His revelations, passed through the lawyer Louis Leblois to Senator Auguste Scheurer-Kestner, helped ignite the campaign that culminated in Émile Zola's J'accuse…! in 1898.

Aftermath

Following Dreyfus's exoneration and reinstatement in 1906, Picquart was promoted to general and appointed Minister of War in Georges Clemenceau's government, serving until 1909. He died in 1914 from injuries after falling from a horse. Picquart is the central character of Robert Harris's novel An Officer and a Spy (2013) and its film adaptation.
