Fibrosis

Fibrosis, also known as fibrotic scarring, is a pathological wound healing response in which connective tissue replaces normal parenchymal tissue to the extent that it goes unchecked, leading to a considerable extracellular matrix remodelling and formation of a permanent scar tissue.

SpecialtyPathology, rheumatology

Excess collagen deposition stiffens and disables the affected organ.

Mechanism

Repeated tissue injury triggers chronic inflammation. Activated fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, driven largely by TGF-β, deposit excess collagen and other matrix proteins.

Types

Fibrosis can affect many organs: idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and cystic fibrosis of the lung; cirrhosis of the liver; myelofibrosis of bone marrow; retroperitoneal fibrosis; and cardiac fibrosis.

Treatment

Antifibrotic drugs such as pirfenidone and nintedanib slow progression in pulmonary fibrosis; transplantation is the last resort for end-stage disease.
