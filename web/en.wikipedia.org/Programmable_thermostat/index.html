Programmable thermostat

A programmable thermostat adjusts temperature on a schedule.

A programmable thermostat is a thermostat designed to adjust the temperature of a building according to a series of programmed settings that take effect at different times of the day. By automatically lowering heating or raising cooling setpoints while occupants are asleep or away, it reduces energy consumption compared with a manual thermostat held at a single temperature.

Types

Common models follow 7-day, 5+2-day, or 5-1-1-day schedules, allowing separate programs for weekdays and weekends. Later "smart" thermostats add occupancy sensing, learning algorithms, and remote control via smartphones. Clock thermostats with mechanical timers predate digital models.

Energy savings debate

Although programmable thermostats can save energy when used correctly, studies have found that confusing interfaces lead many households to override or abandon the schedule, eroding the savings. In 2009 the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency suspended the Energy Star specification for programmable thermostats because field evidence did not show consistent savings; learning thermostats were later certified under a revised approach based on measured performance.
