Shortwave radio

Frequencies: 1.6–30 MHz (HF band)
Propagation: Ionospheric skywave
Uses: International broadcasting, amateur radio, aviation, maritime

Shortwave radio is radio transmission using shortwave frequencies in the high-frequency (HF) band, roughly 1.6 to 30 MHz. Shortwave signals can reflect off the ionosphere (skywave propagation), allowing communication over intercontinental distances with modest power — a property that made shortwave the first means of global wireless communication.

Uses

For much of the twentieth century, state broadcasters such as the BBC World Service, Voice of America and Radio Moscow used shortwave for international broadcasting. Shortwave remains used by amateur radio operators, long-distance aviation and maritime services, military communications, time signal stations and clandestine and religious broadcasters.

Listening

The hobby of shortwave listening (SWLing) involves tuning distant stations and collecting QSL cards confirming reception. Although satellite and internet distribution have reduced shortwave broadcasting since the 1990s, the band's independence from local infrastructure keeps it valuable in emergencies and remote regions.
