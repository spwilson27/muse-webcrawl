Dhikr

Dhikr (Arabic: ذِكْر, "remembrance") is a form of Islamic worship in which phrases or prayers are repeatedly recited in order to remember God. The Qur'an commands believers to remember God often, and dhikr is a central practice in Islam, especially within Sufism.

Practice

Dhikr commonly involves the repetition of the names of God (the 99 names of Allah), the phrase Subhan Allah ("Glory be to God"), Alhamdulillah ("Praise be to God"), Allahu Akbar ("God is greatest"), and the shahada. Repetitions are often counted on prayer beads known as a misbaha or tasbih, typically in sets of 33 or 99.

In Sufism

Within Sufi orders (tariqas), dhikr may be performed silently or aloud, individually or in gatherings, sometimes accompanied by rhythmic movement, controlled breathing, or music and whirling, as among the Mevlevi order. The goal is purification of the heart and closeness to God.
