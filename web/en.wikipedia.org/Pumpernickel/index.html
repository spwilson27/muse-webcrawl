Pumpernickel

Pumpernickel is a typically dense, slightly sweet rye bread traditionally made with sourdough starter and coarsely ground rye. It is strongly associated with the Westphalia region of Germany, where the earliest known bakery at Soest dates to 1450. Traditional German pumpernickel is steamed in covered pans for up to 24 hours, giving it a dark colour from the Maillard reaction rather than from colourings.

Varieties

North American pumpernickel is a lighter, yeasted wheat-and-rye loaf usually coloured with molasses, cocoa or coffee. German-style pumpernickel contains no colourants and is made from wholemeal rye, rye flour and sourdough, baked in steam.

Etymology

The name is often said to derive from early modern German words meaning, roughly, "hard and indigestible" — a humorous reference to the bread's density.
