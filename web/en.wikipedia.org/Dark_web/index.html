The dark web is the World Wide Web content that exists on darknets, which are overlay networks that use the public Internet but require specific software, configurations, or authorization to access. Typical dark webs include small, friend-to-friend peer-to-peer networks as well as large, popular networks such as Tor, I2P and Freenet, which are operated by public organizations and individuals.

The term is often conflated with the deep web, the parts of the web not indexed by search engines, although the dark web is a small portion of the deep web. The darknets which constitute the dark web include small, friend-to-friend networks, as well as large, popular networks such as Tor, Freenet, I2P, and Riffle operated by public organizations and individuals. Users of the dark web refer to the regular web as the clearnet due to its unencrypted nature.

Terminology

The dark web is often confused with the deep web, which comprises all unindexed portions of the web: databases, webmail, private pages and forums. The dark web is a subset of the deep web whose content is intentionally hidden and requires special software to reach. Content on the dark web is typically encrypted or routed through anonymizing networks.

Content

A 2015 study of Tor hidden services estimated that the largest category of content was related to drugs, followed by fraud, hacking tools, and counterfeit goods. Other studies have found that a substantial share of dark-web traffic relates to legitimate privacy-oriented uses: circumvention of censorship, whistleblowing platforms, and secure communications. The proportion of illicit to licit activity is disputed and difficult to measure.

Uses

Legitimate uses of dark-web technology include:

  • Protecting the privacy and anonymity of journalists, activists and dissidents
  • SecureDrop and similar whistleblowing submission systems
  • Circumventing internet censorship in restrictive regimes
  • Anonymous academic and medical research communications

Illicit uses include marketplaces for illegal drugs, stolen data, weapons and counterfeit documents, as well as forums coordinating cybercrime.

Law enforcement

Law enforcement agencies have run operations targeting dark-web marketplaces, including the takedowns of Silk Road (2013), AlphaBay and Hansa (2017). Techniques include undercover operations, infiltration of marketplaces, exploitation of operational-security mistakes, and traffic-analysis attacks against anonymizing networks. Because of the anonymity involved, investigations are typically long-running and technically complex.
