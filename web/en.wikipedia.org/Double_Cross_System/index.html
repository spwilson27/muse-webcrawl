Double Cross System


The Double Cross System turned German agents against their masters.

The Double Cross System, also known as the XX System, was a British counter-espionage and deception operation of the Second World War. Run by MI5, it captured German espionage agents sent into Britain and "turned" them, so that they broadcast false information to the German Abwehr under British control.

Operation

The system was overseen by the Twenty Committee, chaired by John Cecil Masterman, meeting under a pun on the Roman numeral XX ("double cross"). Turned agents such as Juan Pujol García (codename GARBO) and Dušan Popov (TRICYCLE) fed their handlers carefully composed mixtures of true but harmless information, chickenfeed, and strategic lies. By the war's middle years, virtually every German agent in Britain was under British control.

Deception campaigns

The networks were the backbone of Operation Fortitude, which misled Germany about the location of the 1944 D-Day landings, convincing the German command that the invasion would come at the Pas-de-Calais. In consequence, German forces remained positioned away from Normandy even after the landings began.
