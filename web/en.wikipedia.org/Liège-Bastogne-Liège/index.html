Liège-Bastogne-Liège, sometimes Liège–Bastogne–Liège, is a one-day professional cycling race held annually in the Ardennes region of Belgium each spring. First run in 1892, it is the oldest of the five Monuments of the European professional road cycling calendar and is known by the nickname La Doyenne ("The Old Lady").

Course

The race runs from Liège south to Bastogne and back, a distance of roughly 250 km through the hills of Wallonia. Its defining features are the short, steep climbs known as côtes, including the Côte de la Redoute, the Côte de Stockeu and the Côte de Saint-Nicolas near the finish in Ans and, in recent editions, Liège itself.

History and records

Eddy Merckx holds the record with five victories (1969, 1971, 1972, 1973, 1975). Other multiple winners include Moreno Argentin and Alejandro Valverde with four each. The 1980 edition, won by Bernard Hinault in driving snow with an 80-man attrition, is regarded as one of the harshest races in cycling history. A women's race has been held since 2017.
