Gallura

Gallura (Gallurese: Gaddura) is a geographical and cultural region in the north-east of Sardinia, Italy. It corresponds roughly to the province of Sassari's northeastern area and includes the Costa Smeralda, the Maddalena archipelago and the granite landscapes around Tempio Pausania, Arzachena and Olbia.

Geography

Gallura is characterized by rugged granite mountains, cork oak forests and a deeply indented coastline facing the Strait of Bonifacio, which separates Sardinia from Corsica. Mount Limbara, at 1,359 metres, is the highest point. The region's windswept granite formations, such as the bear-shaped rock at Palau and Capo Testa, are famous landmarks.

Culture and language

The local language, Gallurese, is a Romance variety closely related to Corsican rather than to Sardinian, reflecting medieval settlement from Corsica and Tuscany. Traditional rural settlement is based on scattered farmsteads called stazzi. In the Middle Ages much of Gallura formed the Giudicato of Gallura, one of the four independent Sardinian kingdoms, later ruled by Pisa.

Economy

Historically based on pastoralism and cork production, the economy was transformed from the 1960s by luxury tourism along the Costa Smeralda, developed by the Aga Khan. Olbia is the main port and transport hub, with ferry links to mainland Italy.
