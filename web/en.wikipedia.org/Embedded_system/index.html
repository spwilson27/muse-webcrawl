Embedded system

An embedded system is a specialised computer system — a combination of a computer processor, computer memory, and input/output peripheral devices — that has a dedicated function within a larger mechanical or electronic system. It is embedded as part of a complete device, often including electrical or electronic hardware and mechanical parts.

Characteristics

Embedded systems typically are designed to do one specific task, operate under real-time computing constraints, and be produced at low cost and power consumption. They range from tiny microcontroller-based devices, such as digital watches and microwave controllers, to complex installations like industrial robots, medical imaging systems, and the avionics of aircraft. Many run a real-time operating system or a minimal embedded Linux.

History

One of the first recognisably modern embedded systems was the Apollo Guidance Computer, developed in the 1960s. The proliferation of inexpensive microprocessors from the 1970s onward made embedded systems ubiquitous; today the great majority of all processors manufactured are used in embedded devices rather than general-purpose computers.
