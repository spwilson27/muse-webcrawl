The International Rice Research Institute (IRRI) is an international agricultural research and training organization with its headquarters in Los Baños, Laguna, in the Philippines, and offices in numerous rice-growing countries across Asia and Africa. IRRI is known for its work developing rice varieties that contributed to the Green Revolution of the 1960s and 1970s.

History

IRRI was established in 1960 with support from the Ford Foundation, the Rockefeller Foundation and the government of the Philippines. Its most famous early achievement was the semi-dwarf variety IR8, released in 1966 — nicknamed "miracle rice" — whose short, stiff straw resisted lodging under fertilizer application and roughly doubled yields in favourable conditions. IR8 and its successors, including the widely grown IR36 and IR64, spread across Asia and are credited with averting famine for hundreds of millions of people.

Research

The institute maintains the International Rice Genebank, holding well over 100,000 rice accessions. Its breeding programmes have produced flood-tolerant rice carrying the SUB1 gene, drought- and salt-tolerant lines, and "Golden Rice" — genetically engineered to produce beta-carotene as a source of vitamin A — which IRRI helped develop for deployment in countries with high rates of vitamin A deficiency. IRRI also works on agronomy, crop modelling, climate adaptation, mechanization and rice policy economics, and it is a member of the CGIAR consortium of international agricultural research centres.
