Integral kernel

In mathematics, an integral kernel is a function of two variables that defines an integral transform or integral operator by providing the weighting used in integrating over one of the variables. Given a kernel K(x, y), the associated operator maps a function f to the function whose value at x is the integral of K(x, y) f(y) over the domain of y.

Examples

Famous kernels include the Green's function used to solve differential equations, the Poisson kernel for the disc, the Dirichlet kernel in Fourier analysis, and the heat kernel, which gives the fundamental solution of the heat equation. Integral equations in which the unknown function appears under an integral with a given kernel are classified as Fredholm or Volterra equations depending on their limits of integration.

Theory

Kernels that are square-integrable define Hilbert–Schmidt operators, which are compact and admit a spectral decomposition resembling that of matrices, developed in the Fredholm theory of integral equations. Singular kernels, such as those of the Hilbert transform, require principal-value interpretations.
