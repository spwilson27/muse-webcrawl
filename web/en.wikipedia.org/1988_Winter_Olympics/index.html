The 1988 Winter Olympics, officially the XV Olympic Winter Games, were held from 13 to 28 February 1988 in Calgary, Alberta, Canada. They were the first Winter Olympics staged over sixteen days and among the first to be extended across three weekends, a format that became standard. The Games proved profitable and left Calgary with lasting sporting infrastructure overseen by CODA.

Highlights

Finnish ski jumper Matti Nykänen won all three ski jumping events. In speed skating, Yvonne van Gennip of the Netherlands won three gold medals, and Christa Luding-Rothenburger of East Germany won two — becoming the only athlete to win Winter and Summer Olympic medals in the same year, adding a cycling sprint medal in Seoul.

The Games are fondly remembered for two underdog stories: the Jamaican national bobsleigh team, making an improbable debut that later inspired the film Cool Runnings, and British ski jumper Eddie "the Eagle" Edwards, whose last-place finishes made him a folk hero. Their popularity prompted the IOC to tighten qualification standards afterward.

Weather and venues

Warm Chinook winds pushed temperatures above 15 °C at times, forcing postponements in alpine events and requiring extensive artificial snowmaking; the bobsleigh track at Canada Olympic Park was artificially refrigerated. The Soviet Union topped the medal table in its final Winter Games, and Canada, despite strong organisation, failed to win an official gold medal as host.
