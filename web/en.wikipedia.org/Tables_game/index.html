Tables game

Tables games are a family of board games for two players in which pieces are moved according to rolls of dice on a board divided into four quadrants of six points each. The best known tables game today is backgammon; others include the Persian game nard, Greek tavli, Turkish tavla, Russian nardy, tric-trac, and the medieval European games from which the family name derives — "tables" comes from the Latin tabulae, "boards".

Common features

Tables games are race games: each player moves their pieces (men, checkers, or stones) around the 24 points and tries to bear them off first. Dice control movement; landing on an opponent's single piece (blot) usually sends it to the bar to re-enter. Regional variants differ in the starting layout, the direction of play, hitting rules, and whether a doubling cube is used.

History

Dice race games go back to the Royal Game of Ur and Roman duodecim scripta and tabula. Codified descriptions appear in the 13th-century Spanish manuscript Libro de los juegos of Alfonso X, and in Persian tradition the game of nard was attributed to the Sasanian court. Tables games were hugely popular in medieval Europe, sometimes banned as gambling, and evolved into modern backgammon by the 17th–18th centuries.
