Torkham (Pashto/Urdu: تورخم) is a border crossing and town in the Khyber District of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, Pakistan, on the international border with Afghanistan. It lies at the western end of the historic Khyber Pass and connects the Pakistani city of Peshawar with Jalalabad and Kabul in Afghanistan. Torkham is the busiest port of entry between the two countries and a critical crossing point for trade, transit cargo and passenger movement.

Geography and function

The crossing sits on the Durand Line, the border drawn in 1893 between British India and Afghanistan, which Afghanistan has never formally recognised. On the Pakistani side, the highway west from Peshawar passes through Jamrud and Landi Kotal before reaching Torkham; on the Afghan side the road descends to Jalalabad. Facilities include immigration and customs posts, cargo terminals, and a pedestrian gate used by thousands of travellers, traders, patients and day labourers each day.

History and significance

Torkham became a major logistics artery during the Soviet–Afghan War (1979–1989) and again during the NATO-led mission in Afghanistan after 2001, when a large share of supplies for international forces and of Afghanistan's imports passed through the crossing from the port of Karachi. Its strategic sensitivity means it has frequently been closed during periods of tension between Islamabad and Kabul, including disputes over border fencing, cross-border militant activity and clashes between Pakistani and Afghan forces; a major closure in September 2023 followed an armed clash at the crossing. In 2016 Pakistan introduced stricter border management at Torkham, requiring valid travel documents from Afghan nationals, ending the long practice of largely open movement.

Because landlocked Afghanistan depends heavily on the route, even short closures at Torkham strand thousands of trucks and sharply affect prices of food and fuel on both sides of the border.
