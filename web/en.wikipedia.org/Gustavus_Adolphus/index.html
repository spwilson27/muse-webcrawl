Gustavus Adolphus (Gustav II Adolf, 1594–1632) was King of Sweden from 1611 until his death in battle in 1632. Regarded as one of the greatest military commanders in history, he transformed Sweden into a great power and decisively intervened in the Thirty Years' War, earning the titles "father of modern warfare" and "the Lion of the North".

Reforms

Working with Chancellor Axel Oxenstierna, Gustavus reorganised Sweden's government, army and navy. His military reforms combined mobile field artillery, linear infantry tactics integrating musketeers and pikemen, and disciplined cavalry charges — a combined-arms system that outclassed the ponderous tercio formations of his enemies.

Thirty Years' War

Sweden intervened in Germany in 1630 to defend Protestant interests and Baltic dominance. Gustavus won a crushing victory at Breitenfeld (1631), the first major Protestant triumph of the war, and again at Rain am Lech (1632), where the imperial commander Tilly was mortally wounded. On 16 November 1632, at the fog-shrouded Battle of Lützen, the Swedish army won the day but Gustavus was killed leading a cavalry charge. His death shifted the war's course, though Sweden's great-power status endured until the early 18th century.
