Mont Saint-Hilaire

Mont Saint-Hilaire is a mountain in southern Quebec, Canada, rising to about 414 metres (1,358 ft) above the St. Lawrence lowlands near the Richelieu River, roughly 30 kilometres east of Montreal. It is one of the Monteregian Hills, a chain of isolated igneous intrusions aligned across the St. Lawrence plain, and gives its name to the town of Mont-Saint-Hilaire at its foot.

Geology

The mountain is an erosional remnant of a Cretaceous igneous intrusion of alkaline rock — principally gabbro, syenite, and related rocks — that solidified deep underground about 125 million years ago and was exposed as the surrounding sedimentary rock eroded. Its complex mineralogy has made it a celebrated locality for mineral collectors; more than 400 mineral species have been recorded there, including many rare and type-locality specimens from quarries on its northeastern flank.

Nature reserve

Much of the mountain belongs to McGill University, which received it from the Gault family and maintains it as the Gault Nature Reserve. In 1978 Mont Saint-Hilaire became Canada's first UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. It preserves one of the last substantial remnants of the old-growth maple–hickory forest of the St. Lawrence valley, with some 600 plant species and significant populations of birds and salamanders. Roughly 25 kilometres of trails cross the reserve, and Lac Hertel sits in a glacial basin on the summit plateau.
