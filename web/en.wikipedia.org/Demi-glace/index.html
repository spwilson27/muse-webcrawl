Demi-glace

Demi-glace (French for "half glaze") is a rich brown sauce in French cuisine used by itself or as a base for other sauces. The traditional recipe combines equal parts of veal stock and espagnole sauce (one of the five mother sauces of classical French cooking), and reduces the mixture by half.

Preparation

The basic recipe for demi-glace is as follows: equal parts brown stock (traditionally veal) and sauce espagnole are combined and simmered until reduced by half, producing a glossy, intensely flavored sauce. Because the process is laborious, many modern chefs use a shortcut: reducing a good brown stock alone until it is thick enough to coat the back of a spoon, sometimes called a "cheater's demi-glace".

Use

Demi-glace is a foundational preparation in classical French kitchens. It underpins derivative sauces such as bordelaise (with red wine and bone marrow), sauce chasseur (with mushrooms and tomatoes), and sauce Robert (with mustard), and is served with roasted and grilled meats. The finished sauce can be portioned and frozen for later use.
