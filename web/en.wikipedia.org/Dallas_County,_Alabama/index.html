Dallas County, Alabama

Dallas County is a county located in the central part of the U.S. state of Alabama. It was created on February 9, 1818, and named in honor of Alexander James Dallas, U.S. Secretary of the Treasury under President James Madison. The county seat and largest city is Selma, situated on the Alabama River.

History

Dallas County lies in Alabama's Black Belt, a region of dark, fertile prairie soil that made it a centre of cotton plantation agriculture in the antebellum era, worked by enslaved African Americans whose descendants form the majority of the county's population. In the 20th century, the county became a focal point of the American civil rights movement: Selma was the starting point of the 1965 Selma to Montgomery marches, and the violent attack on marchers on the Edmund Pettus Bridge on "Bloody Sunday", March 7, 1965, helped spur passage of the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Geography and demographics

The county covers roughly 980 square miles, including portions of the Alabama River and its tributaries. Its population has declined from its mid-20th-century levels; recent censuses place it at around 38,000 residents. Other communities include Valley Grande, Orrville, and Marion Junction.
