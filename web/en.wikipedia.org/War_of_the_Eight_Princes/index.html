War of the Eight Princes
Date291 – 306 AD
LocationNorthern China
ResultDevastation of the Jin heartland; weakening of the Western Jin dynasty

The War of the Eight Princes, also known as the Rebellion of the Eight Princes or Rebellion of the Eight Kings, was a series of civil wars among princes of the Chinese Western Jin dynasty from 291 to 306 AD. It is traditionally considered one of the most devastating civil wars in Chinese history, shattering the Jin state's capacity to resist the Xiongnu-led uprisings that followed in the Disaster of Yongjia (311 AD).

Background

The Jin founder, Sima Yan (Emperor Wu), enfeoffed many members of the imperial Sima clan as princes with their own armies. When his developmentally disabled son Emperor Hui succeeded in 290, control of the feeble emperor became the object of struggle, beginning with the coup of Empress Jia Nanfeng in 291.

The princes

The eight princes principally involved were Sima Liang, Sima Wei, Sima Lun, Sima Jiong, Sima Ying, Sima Ai, Sima Yong, and Sima Yue. Through shifting coups and campaigns around Luoyang and Chang'an, they successively seized the regency, deposed and restored emperors, and fought pitched battles employing Xiongnu, Xianbei, and Qiang auxiliaries — drawing the non-Han peoples into northern China's power struggles.

Consequences

Sima Yue emerged as the final victor by 306, but the north was in ruins, the treasury emptied, and the dynasty's armies destroyed. Within five years Luoyang and Chang'an fell to the Han-Zhao state, ending the Western Jin and opening the era of the Sixteen Kingdoms.
