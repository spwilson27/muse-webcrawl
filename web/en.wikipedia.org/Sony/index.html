Sony Group Corporation, commonly known as Sony, is a Japanese multinational conglomerate headquartered in Tokyo. Founded in 1946 by Masaru Ibuka and Akio Morita as Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo, the company became a pioneer of consumer electronics, producing Japan's first tape recorder and the transistor radio, and later co-developing the compact disc and Blu-ray formats. Its name derives from the Latin sonus (sound) and the contemporary slang "sonny".

Business segments

Sony's major businesses include Game & Network Services (the PlayStation consoles and PlayStation Network), music (Sony Music Group, the world's second-largest record company), pictures (Sony Pictures Entertainment, including Columbia Pictures), entertainment technology and consumer electronics (Bravia televisions, Alpha cameras, Xperia phones), semiconductor image sensors — where Sony is the global market leader — and financial services in Japan.

History highlights

Iconic products include the Walkman (1979), which created the personal stereo market; the PlayStation (1994), which became the best-selling home console brand; and the Trinitron television line. Sony was involved in notable format contests: its Betamax lost to VHS in the 1980s, while Blu-ray, championed by Sony, defeated HD DVD in the 2000s. The company acquired CBS Records in 1988 and Columbia Pictures in 1989, becoming a major entertainment power, and in 2011 suffered a major PlayStation Network data breach and outage.
