Oceanids

Image placeholder: Oceanids in classical art
Oceanus and Tethys, parents of the Oceanids

In Greek mythology, the Oceanids (Ancient Greek: Ὠκεανίδες) are the nymph daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. According to Hesiod there were 3,000 of them — as many as their brothers, the Potamoi (river gods) — though only 41 names are given in the Theogony. Each Oceanid was associated with a particular spring, river, sea, lake, pond, flower, or cloud.

Role in myth

The Oceanids were important deities in their own right and frequently appear as companions and attendants of the gods. Notable Oceanids include:

  • Styx, the eldest, goddess of the underworld river by whose waters the gods swore their binding oaths.
  • Metis, goddess of wisdom and first wife of Zeus, mother of Athena.
  • Doris, wife of Nereus and mother of the Nereids.
  • Eurynome, mother of the Charites by Zeus.
  • Tyche, goddess of fortune.
  • Calypso, who detained Odysseus on Ogygia (in some traditions an Oceanid; in others an Atlasid).
  • Asia and Europa, eponyms of continents in some accounts.
  • Electra, mother of Iris and the Harpies.
  • Clymene, mother of Phaethon and of Prometheus's wife in some traditions.

The Oceanids in literature

A chorus of Oceanids visits the chained Prometheus in Aeschylus's Prometheus Bound, expressing sympathy for his suffering. The Oceanids personify the bountiful, boundary waters of the world in archaic Greek cosmology, where Oceanus encircled the earth.
