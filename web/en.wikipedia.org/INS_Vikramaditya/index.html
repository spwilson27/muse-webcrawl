INS Vikramaditya

INS Vikramaditya (Sanskrit for "brave as the sun") is a modified Kiev-class aircraft carrier and the flagship of the Indian Navy. She was built in the Soviet Union as Baku and served in the Soviet and Russian navies as Admiral Gorshkov before being sold to India. The ship was commissioned into the Indian Navy on 16 November 2013 at Severodvinsk, Russia, and formally inducted at her home port of Karwar, Karnataka, in June 2014.

Conversion

Under a deal first agreed in 2004, the ship was extensively rebuilt at the Sevmash shipyard. The missile armament on the foredeck was removed and replaced with a full-length flight deck fitted with a 14.3-degree ski jump and three arrestor wires, converting her from a hybrid cruiser-carrier for STOVL aircraft into a STOBAR carrier operating conventional fixed-wing fighters. The conversion included new boilers, over 2,300 kilometres of new cabling, a refurbished hull and new sensors and command systems.

Repeated cost increases and delays — including damage to boiler insulation during sea trials in 2012 — pushed the final price to roughly US$2.35 billion and delayed delivery by several years, making the programme a subject of prolonged public debate in India.

Characteristics

Vikramaditya is 284 metres long and displaces about 44,500 tonnes at full load. She has a crew of around 1,600 officers and sailors. Her air group is built around Mikoyan MiG-29K/KUB multirole fighters and Kamov Ka-31 airborne early warning and Ka-28 anti-submarine helicopters. For self-defence she carries Barak 1 and later Barak 8 surface-to-air missiles and AK-630 close-in weapon systems.

Service

Since her induction the carrier has served as the centrepiece of the Western Fleet's carrier battle group, based at INS Kadamba in Karwar. She has participated in major exercises including TROPEX and the multinational Malabar exercise, and has undergone periodic refits. In January 2020 the naval variant of the indigenous HAL Tejas light combat aircraft made its first arrested landing and ski-jump take-off from the ship's deck.
