Winnie-the-Pooh is a fictional anthropomorphic teddy bear created by English author A. A. Milne, first published in the book Winnie-the-Pooh (1926) and its sequel The House at Pooh Corner (1928). The stories, illustrated by E. H. Shepard, are set in the Hundred Acre Wood, modelled on Ashdown Forest in Sussex.

Characters and stories

Pooh, a bear of "very little brain" fond of honey, is joined by Piglet, Eeyore, Owl, Rabbit, Kanga and Roo, and Tigger, along with the boy Christopher Robin, based on Milne's son. The toys were real: the original stuffed animals are displayed at the New York Public Library. The name combines Winnie, a Canadian black bear at London Zoo, and Pooh, a swan.

Legacy

The books are classics of children's literature, translated into dozens of languages, including a bestselling Latin version, Winnie ille Pu. Disney acquired rights in 1961 and built Pooh into a major franchise. Milne's original book entered the US public domain in 2022.
