Oakland, California

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Downtown Oakland and Lake Merritt

Oakland is a city on the east side of San Francisco Bay in the US state of California. It is the county seat and most populous city of Alameda County, with a population of about 430,000 in the 2020 census, making it the eighth-most populous city in California and a principal city of the San Francisco Bay Area.

History

The area was inhabited for thousands of years by the Huchiun people, Ohlone speakers. Oakland was incorporated in 1852, named for the coast live oak trees that covered the region. It grew rapidly as a terminus of the transcontinental railroad (1869) and as the site of the Port of Oakland, now one of the busiest container ports on the US West Coast. The city was damaged by the 1906 earthquake's refugees boom, the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake, which collapsed the Cypress Street Viaduct, and the 1991 Oakland firestorm.

Geography and culture

Oakland combines flatlands along the bay with hills rising to the northeast. Landmarks include Lake Merritt, a tidal lagoon in the city centre; Jack London Square; and the Oakland Museum of California. The city is known for its historically large African American community, its role in the founding of the Black Panther Party in 1966, and a vibrant arts and food scene.

Economy and transport

Major employers include Kaiser Permanente and Clorox. Oakland International Airport and BART and Amtrak rail services connect the city regionally.
