The 5.56×45mm NATO (official NATO nomenclature 5.56 NATO) is a rimless, bottlenecked, intermediate cartridge developed in the late 1970s by FN Herstal in Belgium for the NATO standardization program. It is the standard service rifle cartridge for NATO forces and many other countries.

History

The cartridge derives from the .223 Remington, developed for the ArmaLite AR-15 rifle adopted by the United States as the M16 during the Vietnam War. The Belgian SS109 projectile, with a steel penetrator tip and a heavier 62-grain bullet, was selected in 1980 as the NATO standard, designated M855 in U.S. service.

Performance

The 5.56×45mm round offers low recoil, a flat trajectory, and light weight, allowing soldiers to carry more ammunition than with larger 7.62×51mm rounds. Its effectiveness depends heavily on velocity-dependent fragmentation of the projectile. It is chambered in weapons including the M16, M4 carbine, FN SCAR, HK416, and many others.

Criticism and development

Debates over stopping power, especially from short barrels, led to improved loads such as the Mk 262 and M855A1 enhanced performance round. The cartridge's near-twin, the .223 Remington, is dimensionally similar but loaded to lower pressures and is not fully interchangeable in all respects.
