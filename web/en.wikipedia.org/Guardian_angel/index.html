Guardian angel

A guardian angel is a type of angel that is assigned to protect and guide a particular person, group or nation. Belief in tutelary beings can be traced throughout all antiquity. The idea of angels that guard over people played a major role in ancient Judaism, and in Christianity theologians developed the concept further: in Roman Catholic theology, every person is believed to have a guardian angel who intercedes and watches over them from birth.

In Judaism

In Judaism there are references to angels appointed over individual nations and persons. The Book of Daniel describes angels assigned to nations, and rabbinic literature speaks of guardian angels who accompany and protect each person, with some sages teaching that every individual has at least one guardian angel who pleads their cause before God.

In Christianity

The concept rests on passages such as Matthew 18:10 ("their angels always see the face of my Father") and Psalm 91:11 ("He will command his angels concerning you"). The Church Fathers, including Jerome and Basil of Caesarea, taught that each soul has an assigned guardian. Thomas Aquinas held that guardian angels belong to the lowest choir of angels and that every human being has one from birth. The Memorial of the Holy Guardian Angels is celebrated on 2 October in the Roman Catholic calendar. The Angel of God prayer ("Angel of God, my guardian dear...") is a traditional Catholic devotion taught to children.

In Islam

In Islam, the Mu'aqqibat are angels who guard human beings, mentioned in the Quran (13:11): "For each one are successive [angels] before and behind him who protect him by the decree of Allah." The Kiraman Katibin record a person's good and bad deeds.

In other traditions

Similar ideas appear in Zoroastrianism, where the fravashi acts as a guardian spirit, and in Theosophy and New Age spirituality, where guardian angels are personal spiritual protectors regardless of religious affiliation.
