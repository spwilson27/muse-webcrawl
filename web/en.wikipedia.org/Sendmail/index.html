Sendmail

Sendmail
Developer: Sendmail Consortium / Proofpoint
Initial release: 1983
Type: Mail transfer agent
Licence: Sendmail License
Written in: C

Sendmail is a general-purpose internetwork email routing facility that supports many kinds of mail-transfer and delivery methods, including the Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP) used for email transport over the Internet. It is one of the oldest and historically most widely deployed mail transfer agents (MTAs).

History

Sendmail descends from the delivermail program written by Eric Allman at the University of California, Berkeley, and was distributed with BSD Unix from 1983. For decades it was the default MTA on most Unix systems and carried a large share of the Internet's email. Its monolithic design and enormously complex configuration file (sendmail.cf, usually generated from m4 macros) made it powerful but famously difficult to administer, and a series of security vulnerabilities — beginning with its exploitation by the Morris worm in 1988 — damaged its reputation.

Later development and alternatives

The open-source version is maintained under the Sendmail License; a commercial line passed through Sendmail, Inc. to Proofpoint. Many sites have migrated to alternatives such as Postfix, Exim and OpenSMTPD, but sendmail remains in use and is still shipped with several operating systems.
