The Siege of Antioch took place during the First Crusade in 1097 and 1098, when the Crusaders besieged the strategically vital city of Antioch on the Orontes River, held for the Seljuk Turks by the emir Yaghi-Siyan.

The siege

The Crusader army arrived before Antioch on 20 October 1097. The city's massive Byzantine-era fortifications, climbing Mount Silpius, could not be fully invested, and the siege dragged through winter amid severe famine and disease. Reinforcements and supplies arrived by sea through the port of Saint Symeon. On the night of 2–3 June 1098, the Crusader leader Bohemond of Taranto arranged for a tower commander, Firouz, to betray a section of the wall, and the city fell amid a massacre of its inhabitants.

The second siege

Days later the Crusaders found themselves besieged in turn by a large relief army under Kerbogha, atabeg of Mosul. Starving within the walls, the Crusaders' morale revived after the visionary Peter Bartholomew claimed to discover the Holy Lance in the cathedral of St Peter. On 28 June 1098 the Crusaders marched out and defeated Kerbogha's army in the Battle of Antioch — or Battle of the Iron Bridge road — securing their hold on the city.

Aftermath

A dispute over possession of Antioch between Bohemond and Raymond of Toulouse, and the Byzantine claim to the city, delayed the march on Jerusalem. Bohemond established the Principality of Antioch, which endured until 1268.
