Daifuku

Daifuku (大福), literally "great luck", is a Japanese confection (wagashi) consisting of a small round mochi — glutinous rice cake — stuffed with a sweet filling, most commonly anko, a sweetened red bean paste made from azuki beans. Daifuku are typically coated with a light dusting of potato starch or corn starch to keep them from sticking.

Varieties

Common varieties include ichigo daifuku, containing a whole strawberry inside with anko; yukimi daifuku, a brand of mochi ice cream; mame daifuku, with whole soybeans or azuki beans kneaded into the mochi; and yomogi daifuku, flavoured with Japanese mugwort. Fillings may also include matcha cream, custard, or fruit.

History

Daifuku evolved from the Edo-period sweet habutai mochi and the larger uzura mochi. The name changed from daifukumochi (大腹餅, "big belly rice cake") to 大福餅 ("great luck rice cake") as a play on words in the late 18th century, and the sweet became associated with good fortune.
