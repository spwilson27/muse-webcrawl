Gethsemane

Gethsemane (Greek: Γεθσημανή, from Aramaic Gat Šmānê, "oil press") is a garden at the foot of the Mount of Olives in Jerusalem. According to the four Gospels of the New Testament, Jesus Christ underwent the Agony in the Garden and was arrested there the night before his crucifixion, after the Last Supper.

New Testament accounts

The Gospels of Matthew, Mark, and Luke describe Jesus going with his disciples to Gethsemane to pray in anguish over his impending death — "My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me; yet not what I want but what you want" — while the disciples repeatedly fell asleep. Judas Iscariot then arrived with an armed crowd and identified Jesus with a kiss, leading to his arrest. John's Gospel refers to a garden across the Kidron Valley without naming it Gethsemane, though it likewise records the arrest there.

Location and churches

Tradition places the garden on the lower slope of the Mount of Olives, where a grove of ancient olive trees still stands; some of the trees have been dated to roughly a thousand years old, and tradition holds they descend from trees of Jesus's time. The site is marked by the Church of All Nations (Basilica of the Agony), built in 1919–1924 over earlier Byzantine and Crusader churches and housing the bedrock traditionally identified as the place of Jesus's prayer. Nearby stand the Church of the Tomb of the Virgin Mary and the Russian Orthodox Church of Mary Magdalene.

Significance

Gethsemane is one of the most important pilgrimage sites in Christianity, and the Agony in the Garden is a major subject in Christian art, hymnody, and devotion; Holy Hour devotions are traditionally associated with Jesus's hour of prayer in the garden.
