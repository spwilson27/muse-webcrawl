Harmonic

A harmonic is a sinusoidal wave whose frequency is a positive integer multiple of the frequency of a periodic signal, called the fundamental frequency. The fundamental itself is the first harmonic; a wave at twice the fundamental frequency is the second harmonic, and so on. Harmonics are a central concept in acoustics, music, electronics, and signal processing.

In music and acoustics

When a string or air column vibrates, it does so simultaneously at its fundamental frequency and at a series of harmonic overtones. The relative strengths of these harmonics determine the timbre that distinguishes instruments playing the same note. Musicians exploit natural harmonics by lightly touching a string at a nodal point, producing flute-like tones an octave, a twelfth, or higher above the open string.

In electrical systems

In power engineering, harmonics are unwanted multiples of the mains frequency (50 or 60 Hz) introduced by non-linear loads such as rectifiers and variable-speed drives; they cause heating and inefficiency and are regulated by standards. In radio and signal processing, harmonic distortion measures the spurious harmonics generated by a device.

Mathematics

Fourier analysis represents any periodic waveform as a sum of harmonics. Related mathematical uses include the harmonic series, harmonic mean, and harmonic functions, which satisfy Laplace's equation.
