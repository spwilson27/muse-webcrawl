Fushimi Inari-taisha

Fushimi Inari-taisha (Japanese: 伏見稲荷大社) is the head shrine of the Shinto kami Inari, located in Fushimi-ku, Kyoto, Japan. The shrine sits at the base of Mount Inari and includes trails up the mountain to many smaller shrines across roughly 4 kilometres.

History

The shrine's origins are traditionally dated to 711, when the deity Inari was enshrined on the summit of Mount Inari. Inari is the kami of rice, agriculture, fertility, industry, and worldly success. Fushimi Inari is the head of a network of some 30,000 Inari shrines across Japan.

Torii gates

The shrine is famous for its thousands of vermilion torii gates, donated by individuals and businesses seeking favours; the donor's name and date are inscribed on each gate. The densely packed gates on the lower mountain form the celebrated tunnels called Senbon torii ("thousands of torii gates"). A full circuit of the mountain trails takes two to three hours.

Foxes

Foxes (kitsune) are regarded as Inari's messengers, and fox statues abound at the shrine, often holding symbolic objects such as keys, scrolls, or sheaves of rice in their mouths. Fried tofu known as aburaage, said to be a favourite food of foxes, gives its name to inarizushi.

In popular culture

The shrine appears in the film Memoirs of a Geisha and is consistently ranked among Kyoto's most visited attractions and Japan's most popular shrines for hatsumōde (New Year visits).
