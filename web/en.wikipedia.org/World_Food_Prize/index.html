The World Food Prize is an international award recognising the achievements of individuals who have advanced human development by improving the quality, quantity or availability of food in the world. It was conceived by Nobel Peace Prize laureate Norman Borlaug, the father of the Green Revolution, and established in 1986.

History

Borlaug proposed the prize to recognise contributions to world food security comparable to those honoured by the Nobel Prizes. After early sponsorship by General Foods, the prize was taken over in 1990 by Iowa businessman and philanthropist John Ruan, who moved its home to Des Moines, Iowa, where it is administered by the World Food Prize Foundation. The award ceremony is held each October in the Iowa State Capitol, alongside the Borlaug Dialogue symposium.

Laureates

The first laureate, in 1987, was Indian agricultural scientist M. S. Swaminathan, a leader of India's Green Revolution. Subsequent winners have included plant breeders, soil scientists, nutritionists and policymakers — among them Catherine Bertini, former head of the World Food Programme, and Ethiopian-American plant breeder Gebisa Ejeta. The monetary award is currently $500,000.
