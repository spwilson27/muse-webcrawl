Axum

Axum (also spelled Aksum) is a town in the Tigray Region of northern Ethiopia. It was the capital of the ancient Kingdom of Aksum, one of the great powers of the ancient world, and remains a holy city of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church.

History

The Kingdom of Aksum flourished from roughly the 1st century CE to the 7th century, controlling trade routes between the Roman Empire and India through its Red Sea port of Adulis. Its kings were among the first to mint their own coins in sub-Saharan Africa and to adopt Christianity, under King Ezana in the 4th century. Aksumite forces under King Kaleb intervened in South Arabia in the 6th century.

Archaeology

Axum is famous for its fields of carved granite stelae — towering obelisk-like monuments, the largest of which stood over 30 metres tall — erected as royal grave markers. The ruins also include royal tombs, palaces, and the church complex of Mary of Zion. The archaeological site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1980.

Religious significance

According to Ethiopian tradition, the Ark of the Covenant is kept in the Chapel of the Tablet beside the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion, guarded by a monk appointed for life. Axum remains a major pilgrimage destination, particularly for the festival of Timkat.
