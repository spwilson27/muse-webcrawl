Battle of Mycale

Mount Mycale, on the coast of Ionia, where the Greeks destroyed the Persian fleet in 479 BC.

Date27 August 479 BC (traditional)
LocationMycale, Ionia (present-day Turkey)
ResultGreek victory
BelligerentsGreek city-states vs. Achaemenid Persian Empire

The Battle of Mycale was one of the two major battles that ended the second Persian invasion of Greece during the Greco-Persian Wars. According to tradition it was fought on the same day as the Greek victory at Plataea in 479 BC, on the slopes of Mount Mycale in Ionia, on the western coast of Anatolia.

Background

After the Persian defeat at Salamis in 480 BC, the remaining Persian fleet wintered in Asia and then took station at Samos, while a Persian land army guarded the Ionian coast at Mycale. The Greek fleet, some 110 ships under the Spartan king Leotychides and the Athenian Xanthippus, sailed to Delos and, encouraged by Samian envoys, resolved to challenge the Persians directly.

The battle

The Persian commanders, distrusting the loyalty of their Ionian Greek crews, beached their ships and built a stockade around them, guarded by the Persian army under Tigranes. The Greeks landed and advanced in two wings — Athenians and their allies along the shore, Spartans over the rough hills. The Athenian wing broke into the stockade first; the Persians and most of their allies fled, while the Medizing troops were cut down. The Greeks then burned the beached ships and the camp, destroying what remained of the Persian navy.

Aftermath

Mycale, together with Plataea, marked the turning point of the wars: the Greeks now carried the conflict to Asia, liberating the Ionian cities. Samos, Chios and Lesbos were admitted to the Greek alliance, the seed of the Athenian-led Delian League. The victory also led directly to the Siege of Sestopos — the first operation of the new alliance — in the following year.
