Mobility as a service

Mobility as a service (MaaS) is a concept of transport in which users plan, book, and pay for journeys across multiple modes — public transport, ride-hailing, car-sharing, bike-sharing, taxis, and car rental — through a single digital platform, often a smartphone app, instead of owning a personal vehicle. Payment may be pay-as-you-go or via subscription bundles combining monthly transit passes with allowances for other modes.

Concept and origins

The concept was popularised in Finland in the mid-2010s, notably through Sampo Hietanen and the Helsinki-based company MaaS Global, whose Whim app (launched in 2016) was an early commercial MaaS product offering bundled mobility packages. Transport for Helsinki's regional authority and other cities experimented with integrated ticketing and journey planning as steps toward MaaS.

Features

Core elements of MaaS include integrated journey planning, booking, and payment; a single user account; and data sharing between transport operators and the platform provider. Proponents argue MaaS can reduce private car ownership and emissions, while critics point to business-model difficulties, questions about equity and data governance, and the risk of drawing passengers away from public transport — illustrated by the financial difficulties of early operators, including MaaS Global's bankruptcy in 2024.
