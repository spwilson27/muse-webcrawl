Saptapadi

Saptapadi (Sanskrit: सप्तपदी, "seven steps") is the most important rite of a Hindu wedding ceremony. The bride and groom take seven steps together around the sacred fire (agni), reciting vows with each step, after which the marriage is considered complete and binding. The ceremony is conducted by a priest before the fire, which serves as witness to the union. In many regional traditions the couple's garments are tied together in a knot before they take the steps, symbolising their bond.

A couple performing the saptapadi around the sacred fire during a Hindu wedding.

The seven steps

Each of the seven steps is accompanied by a vow, though the exact wording varies by region and community. In a common rendering, the couple pray: with the first step, for nourishment and sustenance; with the second, for strength; with the third, for prosperity; with the fourth, for mutual happiness; with the fifth, for progeny; with the sixth, for longevity together; and with the seventh, for lifelong friendship and companionship.

Legal significance

Under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, in India, where the rites of a marriage include the saptapadi, the marriage becomes complete and binding when the seventh step is taken. The ceremony is one of the essential rites (along with kanyadana and panigrahana) whose performance is recognised for a valid Hindu marriage, unless the customs of a particular community provide otherwise.

Regional variations

In South Indian weddings, the groom leads the bride by her hand or little finger as the couple walk seven steps toward the northern direction. In other traditions the bride touches seven betel nuts or heaps of rice with her right toe as the steps proceed. Despite these variations, the seven steps remain the central and concluding act of most Hindu marriage ceremonies.
