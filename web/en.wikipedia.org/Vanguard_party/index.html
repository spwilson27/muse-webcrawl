Vanguard party

In Leninist theory, a vanguard party is a political party composed of the most class-conscious and politically advanced elements of the proletariat, which leads the working class in revolutionary struggle and, after the seizure of power, exercises leadership over the state. The concept is central to the organisational doctrine of Vladimir Lenin.

Origins

Lenin developed the idea in What Is to Be Done? (1902), arguing that the working class, left to itself, develops only "trade-union consciousness" and that socialist theory must be brought to it from without by a disciplined party of professional revolutionaries. Such a party would be organised on the principle of democratic centralism.

In practice

After the October Revolution of 1917, the Bolshevik (Communist) Party became the ruling vanguard party of the Soviet Union, and the model was adopted by communist parties worldwide and later by ruling parties in China, Cuba, Vietnam and other states. Vanguard parties typically claim to rule on behalf of the working class during a transitional period toward communism.

Criticism

Critics, including Rosa Luxemburg and later anarchist and council-communist writers, argued that vanguardism substitutes the party for the class, tends toward authoritarianism, and can lead to a permanent one-party dictatorship. Supporters reply that a disciplined leadership is necessary for revolutionary success.
