Henry Robinson Luce (3 April 1898 – 28 February 1967) was an American magazine publisher who founded Time, Fortune, Life and Sports Illustrated. Often called the most influential private citizen in twentieth-century America, he shaped the way generations of Americans viewed their country and the world.

Early life

Luce was born in Penglai, China, the son of Presbyterian missionaries, and spent his childhood there — an origin that gave him a lifelong interest in China. He was educated at the Hotchkiss School and Yale, where he was a member of Skull and Bones and chairman of the Yale Daily News. With his classmate Briton Hadden, he raised funds to launch a new weekly news magazine.

Time Inc.

Time published its first issue on 3 March 1923, written in the distinctive, compressed "Timese" style. After Hadden's early death in 1929, Luce became the dominant figure at Time Inc. He launched Fortune in 1930, devoted to business and beautifully produced despite the Depression; acquired and relaunched Life in 1936 as a picture magazine that became an American institution; and added Sports Illustrated in 1954. Luce insisted that his magazines reflect his own convictions — internationalist, anti-communist, and pro-business — and is credited (and criticised) for turning journalism toward personality and narrative.

The American Century

In a famous Life editorial of February 1941, "The American Century", Luce urged the United States to abandon isolationism, enter the Second World War, and embrace a destiny of global leadership. The phrase became a defining shorthand for American ambitions in the century. Married from 1935 to the playwright and congresswoman Clare Boothe Luce, he remained editor-in-chief of Time Inc. until 1964 and died in Phoenix in 1967.
