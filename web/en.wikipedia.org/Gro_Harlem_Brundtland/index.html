Gro Harlem Brundtland

Gro Harlem Brundtland (born 20 April 1939) is a Norwegian politician and physician who served as prime minister of Norway in 1981, from 1986 to 1989, and from 1990 to 1996, and as director-general of the World Health Organization from 1998 to 2003. She is widely known internationally for chairing the World Commission on Environment and Development, whose 1987 report Our Common Future (the "Brundtland Report") popularised the concept of sustainable development. She is often called "the mother of the nation" (landsmoderen) in Norway.

Early life and career

Born in Bærum into a political family — her father Gudmund Harlem was a Labour cabinet minister — Brundtland trained as a physician at the University of Oslo, graduating in 1963, and earned a Master of Public Health at Harvard in 1965. She worked in public health administration before becoming Minister of Environmental Affairs in 1974.

Prime minister

In February 1981 Brundtland became Norway's first female prime minister (and its youngest at the time), leading a Labour government until October 1981. She returned to office in 1986 after the Willoch government fell over a petrol-tax vote, leading minority Labour governments until 1989 and again from 1990 to 1996. Her governments pursued economic modernisation, expanded women's representation — her 1986 cabinet famously had 8 women among 18 ministers — and negotiated Norway's application to the European Union, which voters rejected in the 1994 referendum. She resigned in 1996, succeeded by Thorbjørn Jagland.

International work

From 1983 she chaired the UN World Commission on Environment and Development; its report defined sustainable development as "development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." As WHO director-general (1998–2003) she prioritised tobacco control — achieving the Framework Convention on Tobacco Control — malaria ("Roll Back Malaria"), and led the global response to the SARS outbreak in 2003. In 2007 she became a UN special envoy on climate change and a founding member of The Elders. In 2011 she was on Utøya island shortly before the terrorist attack there and escaped unharmed; the attacker had intended to target her.
