Mean Girls is a 2004 American teen comedy film directed by Mark Waters and written by Tina Fey. It stars Lindsay Lohan as Cady Heron, a teenager previously home-schooled in Africa who enters an American public high school and infiltrates the clique of popular girls known as "the Plastics", led by Regina George (Rachel McAdams).

Plot

Cady, raised by zoologist parents, struggles to navigate the social hierarchies of North Shore High School. Befriended by outcasts Janis and Damian, she agrees to spy on the Plastics but gradually becomes one of them, sabotaging Regina while competing for the affections of Regina's ex-boyfriend Aaron Samuels. The scheme unravels after a disastrous house party and the exposure of the "Burn Book", a scrapbook of gossip, leading to school-wide conflict and eventual reconciliation.

Reception and legacy

The film grossed about $130 million worldwide and received generally positive reviews for Fey's script and McAdams's performance. It has since become a major pop-culture touchstone, quoted extensively ("On Wednesdays we wear pink"; "October 3rd" is celebrated by fans as Mean Girls Day). Fey adapted it into a Broadway musical that premiered in 2018, and a film adaptation of the musical was released in 2024.
