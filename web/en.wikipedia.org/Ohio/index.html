Ohio

Ohio is a state in the Midwestern region of the United States. It borders Lake Erie to the north, Pennsylvania to the east, West Virginia and Kentucky to the south (across the Ohio River), Indiana to the west, and Michigan to the northwest. With a population of nearly 11.8 million, Ohio is the seventh-most populous state. Its capital and largest city is Columbus; other major cities include Cleveland, Cincinnati, Toledo, Akron, and Dayton.

History

Ohio takes its name from the Ohio River, whose name in turn derives from a Seneca word meaning "good river" or "great river". Indigenous peoples, including the builders of the great earthworks at Newark and Serpent Mound, inhabited the region for millennia. Ohio became the 17th state on March 1, 1803, carved from the Northwest Territory. The state played a major role in the Underground Railroad, industry, and national politics: eight U.S. presidents were born or lived in Ohio, earning it the nickname "Mother of Presidents".

Geography and economy

Much of Ohio is rolling plains tilled for agriculture, while the southeast reaches into the Appalachian foothills. The state is a transportation and manufacturing hub, with strengths in aviation (Dayton was home to the Wright brothers), steel, rubber, finance, and agriculture. Lake Erie ports and the Ohio River connect it to national freight networks.
