The River Stour is a river in Kent, England, which flows into the English Channel at Pegwell Bay. At 93 km (58 mi) it is sometimes called the Great Stour to distinguish it from its tributary the Little Stour and from other rivers of the same name in England. The Stour rises near Lenham, flows through Ashford — where it is joined by the East Stour — and cuts through the North Downs in a gap at Wye before meandering through Canterbury and the flat Wantsum Channel marshes to the sea near Sandwich.

History

In Roman and early medieval times the tidal lower Stour formed part of the Wantsum Channel, which separated the Isle of Thanet from mainland Kent and made the ports of Richborough and Sandwich havens for cross-Channel trade. Thomas Becket's body was carried along the river after his murder in Canterbury Cathedral. The river drove numerous watermills along its course — Canterbury's King's Mill and mills at Chartham and Chilham produced paper and corn — and was improved for navigation as far as Fordwich in medieval times.

Ecology and recreation

The Stour is a chalk-fed lowland river noted for its beds of water-crowfoot, brown trout, grayling and chub, as well as kingfishers and otters along its banks. The Stour Valley Walk follows its length, and the stretch through Canterbury, past the Marlowe Theatre and the Westgate Gardens, is popular with punting tours.
