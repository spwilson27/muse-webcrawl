Korean Wave

K-pop concerts are a leading edge of the Korean Wave.

The Korean Wave, or Hallyu (Korean: 한류), is a cultural phenomenon in which the global popularity of South Korean popular culture has dramatically risen since the 1990s. The term is most often used in reference to the global spread of South Korean pop culture, encompassing music, television dramas, film, food, cosmetics and language.

History

The wave first spread across China and Japan in the late 1990s, driven by television dramas such as What Is Love and later Winter Sonata. The rise of K-pop groups — including BTS, Blackpink, Girls' Generation and Psy, whose 2012 single "Gangnam Style" was the first video to reach one billion views on YouTube — carried the phenomenon worldwide in the 2010s. Korean films such as Parasite (2019), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture, and the Netflix series Squid Game (2021) further cemented its global reach.

Economic and cultural impact

The South Korean government has actively promoted Hallyu as a form of soft power and a major export industry. The wave drives tourism to South Korea, boosts exports of cosmetics, food and electronics, and has spurred a worldwide increase in learners of the Korean language.
