Spree killer

A spree killer is someone who commits two or more murders in a short period of time and in multiple locations. The United States Bureau of Justice Statistics defines a spree killing as "killings at two or more locations with almost no time break between murders."

Definitions

Criminology traditionally distinguishes three categories of multiple murder: serial murder (killings separated by cooling-off periods), spree murder (killings in multiple locations within a single extended event), and mass murder (multiple victims in one location and event). The FBI's 2005 symposium on serial murder recommended abandoning the term "spree killer" as unhelpful, since the boundaries with serial and mass murder blur in practice, but the term remains in common use.

Characteristics

Spree killings are typically impulsive or driven by a precipitating grievance, and they frequently end with the perpetrator's death — by suicide or by police — or capture within hours or days. Examples frequently cited in criminology textbooks include the 1958 Starkweather–Fugate killings in the American Midwest and the 1996 Cumbria shootings' later analogue cases; researchers note the rarity of such events relative to single homicides.
