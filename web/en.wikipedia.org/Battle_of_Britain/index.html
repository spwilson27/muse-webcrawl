The Battle of Britain was a major air campaign fought largely over southern England in the summer and autumn of 1940, in which the Royal Air Force and the Fleet Air Arm defended the United Kingdom against large-scale attacks by Nazi Germany's Luftwaffe. Germany sought air superiority as a prelude to Operation Sea Lion, a planned amphibious invasion. Its failure was the first major defeat of the Second World War for Hitler's forces and a turning point of the conflict.

Background

After the fall of France in June 1940, Britain stood alone against Germany in western Europe. Prime Minister Winston Churchill declared that "the Battle of France is over" and "the Battle of Britain is about to begin". The RAF's Fighter Command, under Air Chief Marshal Hugh Dowding, relied on an integrated air-defence system — the Dowding system — combining Chain Home radar stations, the Observer Corps, and centralised plotting rooms that directed Hurricane and Spitfire squadrons efficiently onto incoming raids.

Course of the battle

The campaign opened with attacks on Channel shipping (the Kanalkampf), escalated on 13 August (Adlertag, "Eagle Day") with assaults on airfields and radar stations, and reached its climax on 15 September — now Battle of Britain Day — when the Luftwaffe mounted two massive raids on London and suffered heavy losses. From 7 September Germany had switched to night bombing of cities (the Blitz), effectively conceding its failure to win daylight air superiority. Hitler postponed Sea Lion on 17 September and cancelled it indefinitely.

Outcome and significance

Fighter Command lost about 1,500 aircraft and over 500 aircrew killed; the Luftwaffe lost roughly 1,900 aircraft and some 2,600 aircrew. A significant share of RAF pilots came from the Commonwealth and occupied Europe — including Poles, Czechs, Canadians, and New Zealanders — whom Churchill honoured in his famous phrase: "Never in the field of human conflict was so much owed by so many to so few." Victory kept Britain in the war as a base for the later liberation of Europe.
