Planck length

Value≈ 1.616255 × 10−35 m
Definition√(ħG/c³)
SystemPlanck (natural) units

In physics, the Planck length, denoted ℓP, is a unit of length in the system of Planck units originally proposed by Max Planck. It is defined from three fundamental constants — the speed of light c, the reduced Planck constant ħ, and the gravitational constant G — as ℓP = √(ħG/c³) ≈ 1.616 × 10−35 metres, some 10−20 times the diameter of a proton.

Significance

The Planck length is the scale at which the classical ideas of gravity and space-time are expected to break down and quantum-gravitational effects to dominate. A photon energetic enough to resolve structures at this scale would concentrate so much energy in so small a region that it would form a microscopic black hole, defeating the measurement; for this reason the Planck length is often described as approximately the smallest meaningful length. Approaches to quantum gravity, including string theory and loop quantum gravity, typically treat it as the fundamental length scale of nature.

Physical relevance

No current experiment probes anywhere near the Planck scale — the smallest structures indirectly tested, at the Large Hadron Collider, are about 10−19 m. The Planck length nevertheless anchors the Planck units (time, mass, energy, temperature) used in cosmology and theoretical physics, and it enters discussions of the holographic principle and the entropy of black holes, whose surface area is measured in Planck-length squares.
