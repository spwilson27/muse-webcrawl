Strategic Forces Command

The Strategic Forces Command (SFC) is the operational command of India responsible for the management and administration of the country's tactical and strategic nuclear weapons stockpile. It was created on 4 January 2003 by the government of Atal Bihari Vajpayee. The SFC forms part of India's Nuclear Command Authority and is headed by a Commander-in-Chief of the rank of Air Marshal or equivalent.

Role

The SFC is charged with executing the directives of the Nuclear Command Authority through a well-established command and control chain. It manages all of India's land-based nuclear delivery systems — the Prithvi and Agni series ballistic missiles — and coordinates with the naval and air arms, which operate sea-based (nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines) and aircraft-delivered nuclear weapons respectively. The mandate covers readiness, targeting plans sanctioned by the political leadership, and the safety and security of the arsenal.

Nuclear doctrine

India's declared nuclear doctrine rests on a credible minimum deterrent and a policy of no first use: nuclear weapons will only be used in retaliation against a nuclear attack on Indian territory or on Indian forces anywhere, with retaliation intended to be massive and designed to inflict unacceptable damage. The political council of the Nuclear Command Authority, chaired by the Prime Minister, is the sole body that can authorize the use of nuclear weapons.

History

India's nuclear command arrangements were formalised after the 1998 Pokhran-II tests and the publication of the draft nuclear doctrine in 1999. The establishment of the SFC in 2003, together with the Chemical, Biological, Radiological and Nuclear (CBRN) defence arrangements, marked the institutional separation of custodial control (held by the services and the SFC) from political authorization.
