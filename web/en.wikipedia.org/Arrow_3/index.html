Arrow 3 (Hebrew: חץ 3, Hetz 3) is an exoatmospheric hypersonic anti-ballistic missile, jointly developed and produced by Israel Aerospace Industries and Boeing for Israel's missile defence organization, with funding and cooperation from the United States Missile Defense Agency. It forms the upper tier of Israel's multi-layered missile defence, above Arrow 2, and is designed to intercept ballistic missiles during the space-flight portion of their trajectory, at altitudes above 100 kilometres.

Design

Unlike its predecessor, Arrow 3 destroys its target by direct hit-to-kill collision using a gimballed electro-optical seeker and thrust-vectoring divert capability, rather than a proximity-fuzed warhead — a method intended to ensure the complete destruction of incoming warheads, including weapons of mass destruction, at a safe distance from defended areas. The system works with the EL/M-2080 "Super Green Pine" radar.

Development and service

Development began in the late 2000s; the first exoatmospheric intercept test took place in 2013, with further successful tests in Israel and Alaska (2019). Arrow 3 was declared operational in January 2017. In November 2023 it made its first operational interception, destroying a ballistic missile launched from Yemen over the Red Sea area, and it took part in Israel's defence against Iranian missile attacks in April and October 2024. In 2023 Germany selected Arrow 3 for its European Sky Shield Initiative, the system's first export sale.
