Protests of 1968

Date1968 (with unrest continuing into the early 1970s)
LocationsUnited States, France, West Germany, Czechoslovakia, Mexico, Poland, Italy, Brazil, Japan, and elsewhere
CausesVietnam War, authoritarianism, civil rights, student grievances

The protests of 1968 comprised a worldwide series of political and social upheavals that peaked in 1968. Largely carried out by students and young people, the movements opposed the Vietnam War, authoritarian governments, racial segregation, and restrictive social norms, though their causes and character differed sharply between countries.

Background

Post-war economic growth, mass higher education, and the spread of television created a large, politically aware youth generation. The American civil rights movement provided a model of protest, while the escalation of the Vietnam War, dramatised by the Tet Offensive of January 1968, radicalised opinion. The assassinations of Martin Luther King Jr. in April and Robert F. Kennedy in June deepened turmoil in the United States, where demonstrations culminated in violent clashes at the Democratic National Convention in Chicago.

Major episodes

  • France: In May 1968, student occupations and a general strike involving some ten million workers nearly brought down the government of Charles de Gaulle.
  • Czechoslovakia: The Prague Spring reforms of Alexander Dubček were crushed by a Warsaw Pact invasion in August 1968.
  • Mexico: Security forces killed student demonstrators in the Tlatelolco massacre of 2 October 1968, days before the Mexico City Olympics.
  • Poland: The March 1968 student protests were met with an antisemitic campaign and purges.
  • West Germany and Italy: Student movements challenged the post-war establishment and contributed to longer "hot autumns" of labour unrest.

Legacy

Although few of the movements achieved their immediate aims, 1968 had lasting cultural and political consequences, reshaping debates about authority, sexuality, race, and the environment, and it remains a reference point in the politics of France, Germany, and the United States.
