Indigenous peoples of the Americas
Total population: tens of millions
Regions: North, Central and South America and the Caribbean
Languages: hundreds, including Algonquian, Quechua, Maya, Nahuatl

The Indigenous peoples of the Americas are the peoples who inhabited the Americas before European colonisation beginning in 1492, and their descendants. They comprise diverse nations, tribes and cultures across two continents.

History

Ancestors migrated from Asia across Beringia during the last Ice Age, at least 15,000 years ago, and developed complex societies including the Olmec, Maya, Aztec, Inca, Mississippian and Pueblo peoples, as well as the Anishinaabe and many others in North America.

Colonisation and impact

European arrival brought disease, warfare, displacement and the fur trade, causing catastrophic population decline. Indigenous peoples resisted, adapted, and today maintain sovereignty movements, languages and cultural revival, notably in Canada, the United States, Mexico, Bolivia and elsewhere.

Culture

Cultures varied from Arctic hunters to Andean farmers cultivating maize, potatoes and wild rice in the Great Lakes region. Contemporary Indigenous life spans rural communities and large cities.
