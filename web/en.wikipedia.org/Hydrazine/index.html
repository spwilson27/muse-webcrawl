Hydrazine

Hydrazine
Chemical formula: N2H4
A colourless, fuming, oily liquid with an ammonia-like odour; a powerful reducing agent and rocket propellant.

Hydrazine is an inorganic compound with the chemical formula N2H4. It is a simple pnictogen hydride, a colourless flammable liquid with an ammonia-like odour. Hydrazine is highly toxic and dangerously unstable unless handled in solution; it is mainly used as a foaming agent, a precursor to pesticides and pharmaceuticals, and as a rocket propellant.

Production and structure

Hydrazine is produced industrially by the Olin Raschig process, in which sodium hypochlorite reacts with ammonia, and by peroxide-based processes using hydrogen peroxide (see hydrogen peroxide). Each nitrogen atom is pyramidal, and the molecule adopts a gauche conformation about the N–N single bond.

Uses

Hydrazine and its derivatives (monomethylhydrazine and unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine) are storable liquid rocket propellants, used in spacecraft thrusters and in missiles; hydrazine monopropellant thrusters decompose it over an iridium catalyst to produce thrust. In power plants, hydrazine serves as an oxygen scavenger in boiler water to prevent corrosion. It is also a building block for blowing agents, herbicides and drugs such as isoniazid.

Safety

Hydrazine is corrosive, a skin and respiratory irritant, and is classified as a probable human carcinogen; exposure limits are extremely low, and specialised handling procedures are required wherever it is used in spaceflight operations.
