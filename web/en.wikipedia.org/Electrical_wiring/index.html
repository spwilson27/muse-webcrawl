Electrical wiring

Electrical wiring
ComponentsCables, conduits, switches, outlets, distribution boards
StandardsNEC (US), BS 7671 (UK), IEC 60364

Electrical wiring is the system of conductors, devices and protective equipment installed in a building or structure to distribute electric power for lighting, appliances and machinery. Wiring practices are governed by safety codes that vary by country.

Regulation

In the United States, installations follow the National Electrical Code (NFPA 70); in the United Kingdom, BS 7671 (the IET Wiring Regulations); and internationally the IEC 60364 series provides a framework harmonised by many national codes. Codes specify conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, earthing (grounding), and protection against electric shock, including residual-current devices.

Methods and materials

Modern wiring uses copper or aluminium conductors insulated with thermoplastic materials. Non-metallic sheathed cable is common in North American homes, while conduit and trunking systems predominate in commercial and European installations. Older methods include knob-and-tube and lead-sheathed cable.

Circuit design

Buildings are wired in circuits radiating from a distribution board (panelboard) containing circuit breakers or fuses. Ring final circuits are characteristic of British practice, while radial circuits are used elsewhere.
