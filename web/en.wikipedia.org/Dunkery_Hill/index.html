Dunkery Hill

Dunkery Beacon, the summit of Dunkery Hill (placeholder image)

Dunkery Hill is the highest point in Somerset, England, lying within Exmoor National Park. Its summit, Dunkery Beacon, reaches 519 metres (1,703 ft) above sea level, making it also the highest point in southern England outside Dartmoor. On clear days there are panoramic views across Exmoor, the Bristol Channel to Wales, and as far as Dartmoor and the Mendips.

Geography and geology

The hill is formed of Devonian sandstones of the Hangman Grits. It is covered with heather moorland and is part of the Dunkery and Horner Woods National Nature Reserve area, managed largely by the National Trust as part of the Holnicote Estate.

History

Dunkery Beacon is crowned by a large cairn of stones, of Bronze Age origin and added to over the centuries; tradition holds that a chain of beacons across the hills relayed warning signals, and the beacon was lit for national celebrations. The hill appears in R. D. Blackmore's novel Lorna Doone (1869), set on Exmoor.

Access

The summit can be reached by paths from several car parks on the surrounding lanes, including Dunkery Gate. The hill is popular with walkers, and Exmoor ponies and red deer graze the slopes.
