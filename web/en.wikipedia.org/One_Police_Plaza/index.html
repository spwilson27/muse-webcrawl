One Police Plaza

One Police Plaza (often abbreviated 1PP) is the headquarters of the New York City Police Department (NYPD). The 14-story brick building stands on Park Row in the Civic Center neighbourhood of Manhattan, near New York City Hall and the Brooklyn Bridge. The phrase "One Police Plaza" is used metonymically for the NYPD's senior leadership, much as "the Pentagon" refers to the U.S. military command.

History

The building was completed in 1973, designed by the firm Gruzen and Partners, replacing the old headquarters at 240 Centre Street. The police commissioner, chief of department and other senior officials have their offices there, along with the department's press room — the famous "Shack" where accredited police reporters work — and the Real Time Crime Center.

The plaza and surroundings

The raised, landscaped plaza in front of the building, known as Police Plaza, was long a contested public space: it has been repeatedly closed or restricted, especially after the September 11 attacks, and the nearby Park Row was closed to general traffic for years, affecting access to Chinatown. The plaza contains Barnett Newman's sculpture Broken Obelisk. Louie capped marble arches and the Municipal Building frame the site, and Frank J. Ahearn's memorial fountain and other civic artworks stand nearby.

In popular culture

One Police Plaza appears frequently in police procedurals set in New York, including the Law & Order franchise, where characters routinely refer to decisions handed down from "1PP".
