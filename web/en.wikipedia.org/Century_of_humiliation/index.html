Century of humiliation

Periodc. 1839/1842 – 1945/1949
BeginsFirst Opium War / Treaty of Nanking
EndsEnd of WWII or founding of the PRC (1949)

The century of humiliation (Chinese: 百年国耻) is a term used in Chinese historiography and political discourse for the period from the First Opium War in the mid-19th century to the mid-20th century, during which China suffered defeats, unequal treaties and foreign incursions at the hands of Western powers and Japan.

Events

The period is commonly said to begin with the First Opium War (1839–1842) and the Treaty of Nanking, which ceded Hong Kong to Britain and opened treaty ports. It includes the Second Opium War, the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95), the Eight-Nation Alliance's occupation of Beijing during the Boxer Rebellion, the Twenty-One Demands, and the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945).

Legacy

The narrative of national humiliation became central to Chinese nationalism, taught in schools and invoked in the founding story of the People's Republic of China in 1949, when Mao Zedong declared that the Chinese people had "stood up". It continues to shape Chinese foreign-policy rhetoric and patriotic education.
