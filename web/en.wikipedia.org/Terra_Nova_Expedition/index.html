Terra Nova Expedition

The Terra Nova Expedition, officially the British Antarctic Expedition, was an expedition to Antarctica which took place between 1910 and 1913. Led by Captain Robert Falcon Scott, the expedition had various scientific and geographical objectives. Scott wished to continue the scientific work that he had begun when leading the Discovery Expedition from 1901 to 1904, and wanted to be the first to reach the geographic South Pole.

Background

The expedition was named after its supply ship, the Terra Nova, a former whaler purchased for the venture. It was privately financed, with contributions from the public supplemented by a government grant. Scott's party sailed from Cardiff on 15 June 1910 and established its base at Cape Evans on Ross Island in January 1911.

The polar journey

Scott's motor sledges, ponies and dogs supported depots across the Ross Ice Shelf in the summer of 1911–12. On 1 November 1911 the main southern journey began. Motor sledges failed and the Manchurian ponies died as planned at the foot of the Beardmore Glacier. The final polar party of five — Scott, Edward Wilson, Henry Bowers, Lawrence Oates and Edgar Evans — man-hauled up the glacier and across the polar plateau, reaching the South Pole on 17–18 January 1912, only to find that Roald Amundsen's Norwegian expedition had beaten them there by some five weeks, on 14 December 1911.

Death of the polar party

All five men died on the return journey. Evans died at the foot of the Beardmore on 17 February 1912. Oates, suffering severe frostbite, walked out of the tent into a blizzard on 17 March, saying "I am just going outside and may be some time." Scott, Wilson and Bowers died in their tent about 29 March 1912, only 11 miles from the One Ton Depot. A search party found the tent, diaries and geological specimens the following November. The expedition nonetheless completed a substantial scientific programme, including the winter journey to Cape Crozier to collect emperor penguin embryos, described by Apsley Cherry-Garrard in The Worst Journey in the World (1922).
