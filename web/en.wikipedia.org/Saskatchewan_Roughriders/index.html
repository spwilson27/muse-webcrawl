The Saskatchewan Roughriders are a professional Canadian football team based in Regina, Saskatchewan. Founded in 1910, the club plays in the West Division of the Canadian Football League (CFL). The Roughriders are one of Canadian sport's most beloved community institutions: for most of their history the club was community-owned, and despite representing the CFL's smallest market, their fan base — "Rider Nation" — is among the largest and most passionate in the league.

History

The club began as the Regina Rugby Club in 1910 and adopted the Roughriders name in the 1920s. Saskatchewan reached the Grey Cup final repeatedly in its early decades but lost every time, building a reputation for heartbreak. The breakthrough came in 1966, when, led by quarterback Ron Lancaster and fullback George Reed, the Roughriders defeated the Ottawa Rough Riders 29–14 to win the franchise's first Grey Cup.

The team's second championship, in 1989, came in one of the greatest Grey Cup games ever played: a 43–40 victory over Hamilton sealed by Dave Ridgway's last-minute field goal, "The Kick". A third title followed in 2007 under quarterback Kerry Joseph. In 2013 the Roughriders won their fourth Grey Cup at home, defeating Hamilton 45–23 at Taylor Field in the stadium's final championship season, with running back Kory Sheets named the game's most valuable player.

Stadium and identity

The team played for a century at Taylor Field (later Mosaic Stadium at Taylor Field) before moving to the new Mosaic Stadium in 2017. The club's green-and-white colours, watermelon-smashing fan rituals and province-wide following make game days in Regina a signature Canadian sporting spectacle. For decades the Roughriders coexisted with Ottawa's Rough Riders — the "Rough Riders versus Roughriders" matchup — until Ottawa's club folded in 1996.
