Cochrane (organisation)

Cochrane is a British international charitable organisation formed to organise medical research findings so as to facilitate evidence-based choices about health interventions involving health professionals, patients and policy makers. It was founded in 1993 as the Cochrane Collaboration, named after the Scottish physician and epidemiologist Archie Cochrane.

Work

Cochrane comprises roughly 30,000 volunteers in more than 130 countries who produce systematic reviews of health care interventions and diagnostic tests, published in the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, part of the Cochrane Library. Cochrane Reviews are widely regarded as a gold standard for evidence synthesis because of their rigorous methodology, including comprehensive literature searches, assessment of risk of bias, and meta-analysis.

History

The organisation grew out of Archie Cochrane's 1972 book Effectiveness and Efficiency, which criticised the lack of reliable evidence about health care interventions. The first Cochrane Centre opened in Oxford in 1992 under Iain Chalmers, and the international Collaboration was launched the following year.
