Income

Income is the consumption and saving opportunity gained by an entity within a specified timeframe, generally expressed in monetary terms. For households and individuals, income is the sum of wages, salaries, profits, interest payments, rents and other forms of earnings received in a given period. For businesses, income may refer to gross revenue or to net income — profit remaining after expenses, depreciation and taxes.

Economic definitions

In economic theory, the Haig–Simons definition treats income as the sum of consumption and the change in net worth over a period. In national accounts, income is one side of the production account: total output can be measured as the sum of incomes earned (wages, profits, rents and taxes on production), giving rise to measures such as gross national income.

Types

Income may be classified as earned (employment and business), unearned or passive (interest, dividends, rents, capital gains), and transfer income (pensions and social benefits). Disposable income is income remaining after direct taxes, while discretionary income further subtracts necessities such as housing and food.

Distribution

Income distribution within a society is a central topic in economics and politics, commonly summarised by measures such as the Gini coefficient and quintile shares, and related to debates on inequality, poverty and taxation.
