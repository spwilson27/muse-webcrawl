Poverty

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Poverty encompasses inadequate income, nutrition, housing, education, and health care.

Poverty is the state of having inadequate material resources for one's living requirements, or, in broader definitions, of lacking the means to participate fully in ordinary social life. Economists and policymakers commonly distinguish absolute poverty — living below a fixed minimum threshold such as the World Bank's international poverty line — from relative poverty, defined in relation to the median income of a society. Poverty is multidimensional: it involves not only low income but also hunger and malnutrition, limited access to education and health care, poor housing, social exclusion, and vulnerability to shocks.

Measurement

The World Bank's international poverty line, updated to $2.15 per day in 2017 purchasing-power-parity dollars, defines extreme poverty. Higher lines ($3.65 and $6.85 per day) are used for lower-middle- and upper-middle-income countries. National poverty lines vary widely: the United States, for example, uses thresholds originating in a 1960s food-budget calculation. Multidimensional measures, such as the global Multidimensional Poverty Index produced by UNDP and the Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative, count deprivations in health, education, and living standards simultaneously. Development economist Amartya Sen's capability approach influenced this broader view, arguing that poverty is best understood as deprivation of basic capabilities — the freedoms people have to live lives they value.

Trends and causes

Global extreme poverty fell dramatically from about 36% of the world's population in 1990 to under 10% by 2019, driven above all by growth in China and India; the COVID-19 pandemic reversed the trend for the first time in a generation, pushing tens of millions back into extreme poverty. The remaining extreme poor are increasingly concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa and in fragile and conflict-affected states. Causes and remedies are contested and range across economic growth, education, public health, social protection and cash transfers, microfinance, property rights, governance, and access to markets. Eradicating poverty in all its forms is the first of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.
