Steam reforming

Type: Chemical process
Feedstock: Natural gas (methane)
Products: Hydrogen, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide
Temperature: 700–1,100 °C

Steam reforming or steam methane reforming (SMR) is a method for producing hydrogen and synthesis gas from hydrocarbon fuels such as natural gas. It is currently the cheapest and most widely used method of industrial hydrogen production, supplying much of the hydrogen used in petroleum refining and in the manufacture of ammonia for fertiliser.

Process

In the primary reformer, methane reacts with steam at high temperature (700–1,100 °C) and moderate pressure over a nickel catalyst:

CH4 + H2O ⇌ CO + 3 H2

A subsequent water–gas shift reaction converts the carbon monoxide with further steam to produce additional hydrogen:

CO + H2O ⇌ CO2 + H2

The hydrogen is then separated, commonly by pressure swing adsorption. Hydrogen produced this way without carbon capture is often called "grey hydrogen"; with carbon capture and storage it is termed "blue hydrogen".

Applications

Steam reforming supplies hydrogen for the Haber process, hydrocracking and desulfurisation in refineries, and is a candidate source of hydrogen for fuel cells and a low-carbon fuel economy, though its carbon dioxide emissions motivate alternatives such as electrolysis.
