Action Fraud

TypeNational fraud and cybercrime reporting centre
CountryUnited Kingdom
Operated byCity of London Police
Launched2009 (national rollout by 2013)

Action Fraud is the United Kingdom's national reporting centre for fraud and financially motivated cybercrime. Members of the public and businesses report incidents online or by telephone; the reports are analysed by the National Fraud Intelligence Bureau (NFIB), run by the City of London Police, which matches reports against known suspects and disseminates intelligence packages to police forces for investigation.

Operation

Action Fraud itself does not investigate individual crimes; its role is to record and analyse reports on a national basis so that patterns, linked bank accounts, phone numbers and websites can be identified. Victims receive a crime reference number, and cases meeting evidential thresholds are passed to the appropriate local force or agency.

Criticism

The service has been criticised over the proportion of reports leading to investigation and prosecution, the quality of its call handling (an undercover investigation by The Times in 2019 exposed poor practices by outsourced call handlers), and long waits for the replacement of its reporting system. A replacement service, developed after reviews by HM Inspectorate of Constabulary and others, has been planned to improve victim experience and data quality.
