Automated dialogue replacement

[Image placeholder: an actor at a microphone during an ADR session]

Automated dialogue replacement (ADR), also known as additional dialogue recording or looping, is the process in film and television post-production of re-recording dialogue in a studio, after filming, to improve audio quality or to change the delivery of a line. The term "automated" refers to the systems that synchronise the playback of picture and recorded takes.

Reasons for ADR

Production sound may be unusable because of background noise (traffic, aircraft, wind, crowd scenes), technical faults, or unclear diction. Lines may also be altered for story reasons, to remove profanity for broadcast versions, or to add off-screen dialogue and reactions. Entire performances are sometimes re-voiced by another actor, and in many film industries — historically including Italian and Indian cinema — shooting without live sound and dubbing all dialogue afterwards was the standard practice.

Process

In an ADR session the actor watches the relevant scene, often looped repeatedly, listening to the original guide track through headphones. Streamers, beeps or a visual cue help the actor match the timing of the original lip movements. The recording engineer captures multiple takes, and dialogue editors later fine-tune synchronisation and blend the new recording with production sound and room tone.

Group ADR

"Walla" — background crowd murmur and incidental voices — is supplied by groups of actors known as loop groups. They improvise conversations and reactions matching the on-screen setting, recorded in the same post-production phase.
