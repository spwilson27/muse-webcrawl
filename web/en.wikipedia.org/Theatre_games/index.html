Theatre games

Theatre games are structured activities and exercises used in rehearsal, actor training and drama education to develop spontaneity, concentration, ensemble trust and improvisational skill. They are a foundational tool of improvisational theatre and of creative drama in schools.

Origins

The modern tradition of theatre games is credited principally to Viola Spolin, who developed them while working with children and immigrants in Chicago in the 1930s and 1940s, and codified them in her 1963 book Improvisation for the Theater. Spolin framed each exercise as a game with a point of concentration, encouraging players to solve problems through play rather than self-consciousness. Her son Paul Sills used the games to found The Second City, the influential Chicago improvisational troupe.

Use

Theatre games are used worldwide in actor training, corporate workshops, therapy and education. Augusto Boal adapted similar techniques in his Theatre of the Oppressed. See also Improvisational theatre and Theatre director.
