Heisman Trophy

The Heisman Memorial Trophy is awarded annually in the United States to the most outstanding player in college football. First presented in 1935 by the Downtown Athletic Club of New York City as the DAC Trophy, it was renamed in 1936 in honour of John W. Heisman, the club's athletic director and a pioneering football coach, after his death. The trophy itself is a bronze figure of a ball carrier side-stepping and stiff-arming a would-be tackler, modelled on New York University player Ed Smith.

Voting

The winner is chosen by a panel of sports journalists and broadcasters, past winners, and a fan vote, each submitting a three-player ballot weighted by points. The award is presented each December in New York City. Running backs and quarterbacks dominate the list of winners; Ohio State's Archie Griffin remains the only two-time recipient (1974 and 1975).

Notable history

The first winner was Jay Berwanger of the University of Chicago in 1935. In 2010, Reggie Bush of USC forfeited his 2005 trophy after NCAA findings that he had received improper benefits — the award remained vacated for years before being reinstated to Bush in 2024. Several winners, such as Roger Staubach and Tim Tebow, are as famous for their subsequent public careers as for their football. The trophy is widely regarded as the most prestigious individual award in American college sports.
