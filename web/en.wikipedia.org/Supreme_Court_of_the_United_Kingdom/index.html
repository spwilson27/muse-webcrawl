Supreme Court of the United Kingdom

The Supreme Court of the United Kingdom is the final court of appeal in the United Kingdom for all civil cases and for criminal cases from England, Wales, and Northern Ireland. Established by the Constitutional Reform Act 2005, it began work on 1 October 2009, taking over the judicial functions of the House of Lords (exercised by the Law Lords) and the devolution jurisdiction of the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council. It sits in the Middlesex Guildhall on Parliament Square, London.

Jurisdiction and composition

The court consists of twelve Justices, including a President and Deputy President, appointed by the Crown on the advice of the Prime Minister following selection by an independent commission. It hears appeals on arguable points of law of general public importance, decides devolution issues concerning the Scottish Parliament, Senedd, and Northern Ireland Assembly, and gives landmark rulings such as the 2017 and 2019 judgments on Article 50 and the prorogation of Parliament (R (Miller) cases).

For Scottish criminal cases, the High Court of Justiciary remains the final court, though the Supreme Court may hear Scottish criminal appeals involving devolution or human-rights compatibility issues.
