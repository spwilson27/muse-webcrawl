Earl of Wessex

Earl of Wessex is a title that has been created three times in British history: twice in the Anglo-Saxon period for powerful nobles of the kingdom of Wessex's successor state, England, and once in the Peerage of the United Kingdom in 1999 for Prince Edward, the youngest son of Elizabeth II.

Early creations

Godwin, appointed Earl of Wessex around 1020 by Cnut, became the most powerful earl in England under Edward the Confessor; his son Harold Godwinson, also Earl of Wessex, became the last Anglo-Saxon king of England in 1066 and was killed at the Battle of Hastings. The title did not survive the Norman Conquest.

The 1999 creation

On his marriage to Sophie Rhys-Jones on 19 June 1999, Prince Edward was created Earl of Wessex and Viscount Severn — an unusual choice for a prince (younger sons of monarchs were more often made dukes), reportedly reflecting his wish and a reported understanding that he would in time succeed to the dukedom of Edinburgh. The title's name recalled the fictional Earl of Wessex in the film Shakespeare in Love, as well as the historical Wessex. On 10 March 2019 he was additionally created Earl of Forfar. On 10 March 2023, his 59th birthday, Charles III conferred the dukedom of Edinburgh on him for life, whereupon he became Duke of Edinburgh while retaining the Wessex earldom as a subsidiary title. His son James, previously Viscount Severn, then became Earl of Wessex as a courtesy title.
