Samuel Taylor Coleridge (21 October 1772 – 25 July 1834) was an English poet, literary critic, philosopher and theologian who, with his friend William Wordsworth, was a founder of the Romantic Movement in England and a member of the Lake Poets.

Poetry

Coleridge is best known for his poems The Rime of the Ancient Mariner and Kubla Khan, published in the joint volume Lyrical Ballads (1798) and in 1816 respectively. Kubla Khan, presented as a fragment of an opium dream interrupted by a "person from Porlock", remains one of the most famous unfinished poems in English. His "conversation poems", including Frost at Midnight and Dejection: An Ode, are also highly regarded.

Criticism and philosophy

His prose work Biographia Literaria (1817) is a landmark of literary criticism, introducing the famous phrase "willing suspension of disbelief" and drawing German idealist philosophy — Kant, Schelling and the Schlegels — into English intellectual life. Coleridge coined or popularised many words and phrases in English, and his lectures on Shakespeare deeply influenced later criticism.

Life

The youngest son of a Devon vicar, Coleridge was educated at Christ's Hospital and Jesus College, Cambridge. In his youth he planned, with Robert Southey, a utopian community called Pantisocracy in America. Plagued by ill health, financial insecurity and a lifelong opium addiction, he spent his final years at Highgate under the care of the surgeon James Gillman, where he became a celebrated conversationalist known as the "Sage of Highgate".
