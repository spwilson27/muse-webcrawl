Golden Circle (Iceland)

LengthApprox. 300 km (190 mi) loop
Principal stopsÞingvellir National Park, Geysir, Gullfoss
Start / endReykjavík

The Golden Circle (Icelandic: Gullni hringurinn) is a popular tourist route in southern Iceland, covering about 300 kilometres (190 mi) and looping from the capital, Reykjavík, into the southern uplands and back. It is the most popular day-trip itinerary in Iceland and includes three of the country's best-known natural attractions.

Attractions

Þingvellir National Park is a site of historical, cultural, and geological significance, where the Althing, one of the world's oldest parliaments, was founded in 930 AD. The park lies in a rift valley marking the boundary between the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates.

The Geysir geothermal area in the Haukadalur valley contains hot springs and erupting geysers. Although the Great Geysir itself is now largely dormant, the neighbouring Strokkur erupts every 5 to 10 minutes to heights of around 15–20 metres.

Gullfoss ("Golden Falls") is a two-tiered waterfall on the Hvítá river, plunging a total of 32 metres into a rugged canyon. It is one of Iceland's most iconic and beloved natural features.

Other stops

Tours frequently add the volcanic crater lake Kerið, the Faxi waterfall, and the greenhouse village of Hveragerði. The route can be driven in a single day and is offered year-round by numerous tour operators.
