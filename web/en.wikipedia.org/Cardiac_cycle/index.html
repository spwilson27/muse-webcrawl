Cardiac cycle

The cardiac cycle is the sequence of mechanical and electrical events that repeats with every heartbeat: the contraction of the heart muscle (systole) and its relaxation and refilling (diastole). At a resting rate of about 70 beats per minute, one cycle lasts about 0.85 seconds.

Phases

The cycle is usually divided into atrial systole, ventricular systole (isovolumetric contraction and ejection), and diastole (isovolumetric relaxation and ventricular filling). The sinoatrial node initiates the electrical impulse, which spreads across the atria, is delayed at the atrioventricular node, and then travels via the bundle of His and Purkinje fibres to coordinate ventricular contraction from the apex upward.

Pressures, volumes, and sounds

Valve movements govern the direction of blood flow: the mitral and tricuspid valves close at the start of ventricular systole (producing the first heart sound, S1, "lub"), and the aortic and pulmonary valves close at the end of systole (the second heart sound, S2, "dub"). The relationship between ventricular pressure and volume through the cycle is summarised in the pressure–volume loop, and the Wiggers diagram aligns pressure, volume, ECG, and heart sounds in time.

Regulation

Cardiac output — the product of heart rate and stroke volume — is adjusted by the autonomic nervous system, circulating adrenaline, and the Frank–Starling mechanism, by which increased venous return stretches the myocardium and increases the force of contraction.
