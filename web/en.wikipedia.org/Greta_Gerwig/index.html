Greta Celeste Gerwig (born August 4, 1983) is an American actress, screenwriter, and film director. She first gained attention for her work in low-budget independent "mumblecore" films, including collaborations with Joe Swanberg, before appearing in mainstream films such as Greenberg (2010) and Frances Ha (2012), the latter of which she co-wrote.

Gerwig's solo directorial debut, Lady Bird (2017), earned five Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director. Her adaptation of Little Women (2019) was also nominated for Best Picture. In 2023 she directed Barbie, which grossed over $1.4 billion worldwide and became the highest-grossing film ever directed solely by a woman, as well as the highest-grossing film of 2023.

Early life

Gerwig was born and raised in Sacramento, California. She attended St. Francis High School and later Barnard College in New York, where she studied English and philosophy. She originally intended to become a playwright before turning to acting and filmmaking.

Career

Gerwig appeared in films by the Duplass brothers and co-wrote and co-directed Nights and Weekends (2008) with Swanberg. Her collaboration with Noah Baumbach produced Frances Ha and Mistress America (2015). As an actress she also appeared in Jackie (2016), 20th Century Women (2016) and voiced a character in Isle of Dogs (2018).

Directing

Lady Bird, set in Gerwig's hometown of Sacramento, was widely praised for its portrayal of a mother-daughter relationship. Little Women restructured Louisa May Alcott's novel with a non-linear timeline. Barbie, co-written with Baumbach, combined comedy with commentary on gender and consumerism. Gerwig has been attached to direct adaptations of C. S. Lewis's The Chronicles of Narnia for Netflix.

Personal life

Gerwig is in a relationship with filmmaker Noah Baumbach, with whom she has two sons. They married in 2023.
