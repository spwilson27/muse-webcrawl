Contract carrier

A contract carrier is a transportation company or operator that provides carriage of goods or passengers under individual contracts with particular customers, rather than offering service to the general public. The concept is defined in contrast to the common carrier, which holds itself out to serve all comers on standard terms.

Legal definition

In United States transportation law, the Motor Carrier Act of 1935 distinguished common carriers from contract carriers, which were subject to somewhat lighter regulation by the Interstate Commerce Commission. Contract carriers negotiated rates and routes bilaterally and were historically limited in the number of customers they could serve; the Motor Carrier Act of 1980 substantially deregulated both categories. Similar distinctions exist for air, rail and maritime carriage, and in the law of other common-law jurisdictions.

Characteristics

Because services are tailored to a shipper's needs — dedicated vehicles, schedules and equipment — contract carriage suits regular, predictable freight flows. The carrier may refuse customers outside its contracts and is generally not obliged to publish tariffs or serve the public indiscriminately, though safety, insurance and licensing rules still apply.

Examples

Dedicated contract carriage divisions of large logistics firms, chartered bus operators under contract to schools or companies, and airlines providing ACMI (aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance) leases to other carriers all operate in forms of contract carriage.
