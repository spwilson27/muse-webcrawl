Poisson algebra

FieldAlgebra, mathematical physics
Key structureCommutative algebra + Lie bracket
Named afterSiméon Denis Poisson

In mathematics, a Poisson algebra is an associative algebra together with a Lie bracket that also satisfies Leibniz's law, that is, the bracket is also a derivation with respect to the associative product.

Definition

A Poisson algebra is a vector space A over a field equipped with two bilinear products: an associative, commutative multiplication · and a Lie bracket {·, ·}, such that for all x, y, z in A, the product rule {x · y, z} = x · {y, z} + {x, z} · y holds. The bracket is called the Poisson bracket of the algebra.

Examples

The canonical example arises in Hamiltonian mechanics: the algebra of smooth functions on a symplectic manifold, with pointwise multiplication and the Poisson bracket defined by the symplectic form, forms a Poisson algebra. The observables of a classical mechanical system form a Poisson algebra, with the bracket governing time evolution through Hamilton's equations.

Relation to quantization

Poisson algebras are the classical limit of associative algebras of quantum observables: deformation quantization deforms the commutative product of a Poisson algebra into a noncommutative product whose commutator recovers the Poisson bracket to first order in Planck's constant.
