Inverclyde (Scots: Inerclyde, Scottish Gaelic: Inbhir Chluaidh) is one of 32 council areas used for local government in Scotland. Together with the East Renfrewshire and Renfrewshire council areas, Inverclyde forms part of the historic county of Renfrewshire, which currently exists as a registration county and lieutenancy area. Inverclyde is located in the west central Lowlands.

It borders the North Ayrshire, Renfrewshire and Argyll and Bute council areas, and is otherwise surrounded by the Firth of Clyde. The council area covers the northern part of the historic county, taking in the towns of Greenock, Port Glasgow, and Gourock, and the villages of Inverkip, Wemyss Bay, Kilmacolm, and Quarrier's Village.

History

The area developed rapidly during the Industrial Revolution, driven by shipbuilding on the lower Clyde, sugar refining, and trade through Greenock's port. Greenock was the birthplace of engineer James Watt. Shipbuilding declined sharply in the late 20th century, leading to sustained population decline, although Ferguson Marine at Port Glasgow remains a notable yard.

Inverclyde District was created in 1975 under the Local Government (Scotland) Act 1973, and the present unitary council dates from 1996. The council's headquarters are in Greenock, at the Municipal Buildings.

Governance and demographics

Inverclyde Council has 22 councillors elected from six multi-member wards. The area returns one Member of Parliament to Westminster for the Inverclyde and Renfrewshire West constituency and is part of the Greenock and Inverclyde constituency in the Scottish Parliament. Inverclyde is one of the smallest council areas by population in mainland Scotland and has faced challenges with deprivation and population loss, alongside regeneration projects along the Greenock and Port Glasgow waterfronts.
