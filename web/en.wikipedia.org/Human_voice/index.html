Human voice

The human voice consists of sound made by a human being using the vocal tract, including talking, singing, laughing, crying, screaming, shouting, humming, and yelling. The sound is produced in the larynx (voice box), where the vocal folds (vocal cords) vibrate as air from the lungs passes between them; the resulting sound is filtered and amplified by the throat, mouth, and nasal passages to produce speech and song.

Production

Voice production (phonation) involves three subsystems: the respiratory system supplies airflow, the larynx acts as a vibrator, and the supralaryngeal tract (pharynx, tongue, lips) articulates the sound into vowels and consonants. The fundamental frequency of an adult man's voice typically lies between 85 and 155 Hz, and an adult woman's between 165 and 255 Hz. Pitch, loudness, and timbre can be modified by adjusting vocal fold tension, length, and airflow.

Voice types and health

Singing voices are classified into types such as soprano, mezzo-soprano, alto, tenor, baritone, and bass. Voice changes occur at puberty, especially in males. Disorders of the voice include laryngitis, vocal fold nodules, spasmodic dysphonia, and vocal cord paresis; specialists called phoniatricians and speech-language pathologists diagnose and treat them.
