3GPP

The 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) is an umbrella term for a number of standards organizations which develop protocols for mobile telecommunications. Established in December 1998, it originally set out to produce globally applicable technical specifications for third-generation (3G) mobile systems based on evolved GSM core networks.

Scope

3GPP work covers radio access, the core transport network, and service capabilities, including codecs, security, and quality of service. Its specifications underpin UMTS, LTE and LTE-Advanced, and the 5G NR radio and 5G Core standards.

Organization

The project brings together seven regional standards development organizations (ARIB, ATIS, CCSA, ETSI, TSDSI, TTA, and TTC) as Organizational Partners, along with market representation partners and hundreds of member companies. Day-to-day work takes place in Technical Specification Groups and their Working Groups.

Releases

3GPP publishes its standards in staged "releases". Release 99 introduced UMTS; Release 8 introduced LTE; Release 15, completed in 2018, delivered the first 5G specifications. Later releases added features such as enhanced machine-type communication, vehicle-to-everything services, and satellite access.
