Steel is an alloy of iron and carbon, containing between about 0.02% and 2% carbon by weight, often with other alloying elements such as manganese, chromium, nickel and vanadium. Carbon strengthens iron enormously: steel can be many times stronger than pure iron while remaining workable, and its properties can be tuned by composition and heat treatment. It is the world's most important construction and manufacturing material and is highly recyclable.

Properties and grades

Steels are classified by carbon content — mild (low-carbon) steels are ductile and weldable, used in construction and car bodies; medium and high-carbon steels are harder and used for springs, rails and tools. Alloy steels add elements for specific properties: stainless steel contains at least about 10.5% chromium, forming a passive oxide layer that resists corrosion, and is ubiquitous in cutlery, architecture and medicine. Heat treatments — quenching, tempering, annealing — control the microstructure (ferrite, pearlite, martensite and others) and hence hardness and toughness.

Production

Most steel is made by reducing iron ore in a blast furnace to pig iron, then refining it in a basic oxygen furnace, where oxygen blown through the melt burns off excess carbon and impurities. A growing share comes from melting scrap in electric arc furnaces, which uses far less energy; direct-reduced iron offers a lower-carbon route and is key to "green steel" efforts using hydrogen. Steel production accounts for roughly 7–9% of global CO₂ emissions, making decarbonisation a major industrial challenge. Global crude steel output is about 1.9 billion tonnes a year, with China producing more than half.

History

Early steels were made by carburising wrought iron; Indian wootz steel, exported and forged into famed Damascus blades, was produced from about the 1st millennium BCE. Industrial steelmaking began with Henry Bessemer's converter (1856) and the Siemens–Martin open-hearth process, slashing costs and enabling steel ships, rails, bridges and skyscrapers — the material backbone of the industrial age.
