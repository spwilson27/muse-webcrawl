Dismal Swamp Canal

The Dismal Swamp Canal is a canal in the U.S. states of Virginia and North Carolina, running along the eastern edge of the Great Dismal Swamp. It connects Chesapeake Bay in Virginia with Albemarle Sound in North Carolina, and at roughly 22 miles (35 km) long it is among the oldest continually operating man-made canals in the United States.

History

The canal was proposed as early as the colonial era — George Washington visited the swamp and backed a venture to drain and develop it — and construction began in 1793 under the Dismal Swamp Canal Company. Dug largely by enslaved laborers working in harsh conditions, the canal opened to navigation in 1805 and was progressively deepened and widened during the 19th century. It carried timber, shingles and agricultural goods, and the town of Deep Creek, Virginia, grew up at its northern locks.

Modern use

Today the canal is maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers as part of the Atlantic Intracoastal Waterway, offering an alternative to the Albemarle and Chesapeake Canal route. It is used mainly by recreational boaters, and passes through the Great Dismal Swamp National Wildlife Refuge, known for its birdlife and for Lake Drummond, which supplies the canal's water via a feeder ditch. The canal is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
