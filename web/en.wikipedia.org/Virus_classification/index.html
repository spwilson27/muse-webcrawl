Virus classification

Virus classification is the process of naming viruses and placing them into a taxonomic system similar to the classification systems used for cellular organisms. Viruses are classified by the International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses (ICTV) on the basis of phenotypic characteristics including morphology, nucleic acid type, mode of replication, host organisms, and the type of disease they cause.

Baltimore classification

The Baltimore classification, devised by Nobel laureate David Baltimore, groups viruses into seven classes according to their genome type and the way messenger RNA is produced:

  • I: double-stranded DNA viruses
  • II: single-stranded DNA viruses
  • III: double-stranded RNA viruses
  • IV: positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses
  • V: negative-sense single-stranded RNA viruses
  • VI: single-stranded RNA viruses that replicate through a DNA intermediate (retroviruses)
  • VII: double-stranded DNA viruses that replicate through an RNA intermediate

ICTV taxonomy

The ICTV system uses a hierarchy of realm, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species, mirroring the ranks used for cellular life. In recent years, metagenomic sequencing has led to the discovery of vast numbers of new viruses and a rapid expansion of virus taxonomy, including the establishment of higher ranks such as realms (for example, Riboviria for most RNA viruses).
