The Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic, also referred to as the Azerbaijan SSR, was one of the constituent republics of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics from 1922 to 1991. It was created on 28 April 1920 when the Red Army invaded the short-lived Azerbaijan Democratic Republic, the first secular democratic Muslim-majority state.

History

From 1922 to 1936 Azerbaijan formed part of the Transcaucasian SFSR together with Armenia and Georgia; in 1936 it became a separate union republic. The republic's oil industry around Baku was strategically vital, supplying the majority of Soviet petroleum and much of the fuel for the Red Army during the Second World War. Under Soviet rule the republic underwent industrialisation, urbanisation and alphabet changes: the Azerbaijani language was written first in a Latin alphabet, then Cyrillic, and after independence again in Latin.

Late Soviet period

In the late 1980s the conflict over Nagorno-Karabakh, an autonomous oblast within the Azerbaijan SSR with a majority Armenian population, sparked mass movements in both republics and pogroms, including the Sumgait pogrom of 1988. In January 1990 Soviet troops entered Baku in the crackdown remembered as Black January, killing over a hundred civilians. Azerbaijan declared independence on 30 August 1991, confirmed by referendum and constitutional act in October, before the Soviet Union formally dissolved in December 1991.

Geography and economy

The republic occupied the southeastern Caucasus on the Caspian Sea, bordered by Russia, Georgia, Armenia, Turkey and Iran. Besides oil and natural gas, its economy included cotton, wine, tea and citrus production in the subtropical Lankaran lowlands.
