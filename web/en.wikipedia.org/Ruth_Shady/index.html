Ruth Shady

Ruth Martha Shady Solís (born December 29, 1946) is a Peruvian anthropologist and archaeologist. She is the founder and director of the Caral Archaeological Project and is best known for her research into the Caral–Supe civilization, the oldest known civilization in the Americas.

Career

Shady studied at the National University of San Marcos in Lima and worked at the National Museum of Archaeology, Anthropology and History of Peru. Beginning fieldwork in the Supe Valley in 1994, she directed excavations at Caral, a large monumental centre of pyramids, plazas, and residential buildings dating to roughly 3000–1800 BCE. Her team's work, published prominently in 2001, demonstrated that a complex, urban society had arisen in coastal Peru contemporaneous with the early civilizations of Egypt and Mesopotamia — long before ceramics or maize agriculture in the region.

Recognition and threats

Shady has received numerous national and international honours and has been listed among the BBC's 100 Women. Her efforts to protect Caral and other Supe Valley sites from land trafficking and encroachment have made her the target of threats, drawing international attention to the defence of Peru's archaeological heritage. Caral was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2009.
