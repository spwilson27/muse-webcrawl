Tír na nÓg

Oisín and Niamh ride to Tír na nÓg.

Tír na nÓg (Irish: "Land of the Young") is one of the best-known names for the Otherworld in Irish mythology, a supernatural realm of everlasting youth, beauty, health, abundance, and joy, inhabited by the Tuatha Dé Danann and other spirits. It was said to lie far to the west, across the sea, and could be reached by arduous voyage or by invitation from one of its residents.

Oisín in Tír na nÓg

In the most famous tale, the warrior-poet Oisín falls in love with Niamh, a woman of the Otherworld, and rides with her on a magical horse across the sea to Tír na nÓg. What seems to him three years there is in fact three hundred years in Ireland; when he returns and touches Irish soil, the centuries fall upon him and he becomes an old man. Versions of the tale appear in the Fenian Cycle and were retold by W. B. Yeats in The Wanderings of Oisin (1889).

Related realms

Tír na nÓg is one of several Irish otherworlds, alongside Mag Mell, Tír Tairngire, and Emain Ablach; similar lands of the undying appear across Celtic tradition, paralleling Avalon in Welsh and Arthurian legend.
