Boil

Other namesFuruncle
SpecialtyDermatology
CauseBacterial infection of a hair follicle, usually Staphylococcus aureus
TreatmentWarm compresses; incision and drainage; antibiotics in some cases

A boil, also called a furuncle, is a deep folliculitis — a deep infection of a hair follicle — most commonly caused by the bacterium Staphylococcus aureus. It results in a painful swollen area of skin caused by an accumulation of pus and dead tissue. A cluster of interconnected boils is called a carbuncle.

Signs and symptoms

Boils typically begin as red, tender lumps that gradually fill with pus, enlarge, and eventually rupture and drain. They can occur anywhere on the skin but are most common on the face, neck, armpits, buttocks, and thighs — areas subject to friction and sweating.

Causes and risk factors

Most boils arise when S. aureus bacteria, which commonly colonise the skin and nose, enter through a break in the skin or infect a hair follicle. Risk factors include poor hygiene, diabetes, weakened immunity, close contact with carriers, and skin conditions such as eczema.

Treatment

Many boils heal on their own after draining; warm compresses encourage this process. Large or persistent boils may need incision and drainage by a clinician, and antibiotics are used when infection spreads or the patient is immunocompromised. Squeezing a boil is discouraged, as it can push infection deeper.
