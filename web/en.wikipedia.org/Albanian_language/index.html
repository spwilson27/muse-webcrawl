Albanian language

Albanian (endonym: shqip [ʃcip] or gjuha shqipe [ˈɟuha ˈʃcipɛ]) is an Indo-European language and the only surviving representative of its own branch of that family. It is spoken by the Albanians in the Balkans and by the Albanian diaspora, with around 7.5 million speakers. It is the official language of Albania and Kosovo, and co-official in North Macedonia and Montenegro.

Dialects

Albanian comprises two major dialects: Gheg, spoken in the north, and Tosk, spoken in the south, divided roughly by the Shkumbin River. The modern standard language, adopted in 1972, is based mainly on Tosk. Older varieties survive among the Arbëreshë of Italy and the Arvanites of Greece.

History

The first surviving written Albanian text is the baptismal formula of 1462, and the first book, Gjon Buzuku's Meshari (1555). Albanian's origins are debated; it is often linked to ancient Illyrian or another Paleo-Balkan language. Its vocabulary preserves early borrowings from Latin, Greek, Slavic, Turkish, and Italian alongside its native Indo-European core.

Grammar

Albanian is an inflected language with three genders, cases that merge genitive and dative, and a postposed definite article attached to the end of the noun. It shares features with other Balkan languages as part of the Balkan sprachbund.
