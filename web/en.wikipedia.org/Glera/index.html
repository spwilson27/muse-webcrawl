Glera

Glera grapes ripening in northeastern Italy.

Glera is a white variety of grape of Italian origin, long grown in the Veneto and Friuli Venezia Giulia regions of northeastern Italy. It is best known as the principal grape variety used in the production of Prosecco, the sparkling wine of the region.

History and naming

The variety was formerly known simply as Prosecco. In 2009, when the Prosecco production area was reorganized — with Prosecco elevated to a protected DOCG for the hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene and a broader DOC created across the northeast — the grape's name was officially changed to Glera so that "Prosecco" would be protected as a geographical designation rather than a grape variety name. The new name was taken from the village of Prosecco near Trieste, where the grape is thought to have originated; "Glera" appears in old local records as a synonym.

Viticulture

Glera is a fairly vigorous, late-ripening variety with long, loose bunches of thin-skinned, greenish-golden berries. It is prized for its naturally high acidity and relatively neutral aromatic profile, qualities that make it suitable for the Charmat (tank) method of sparkling wine production, in which secondary fermentation in pressurized tanks preserves fresh fruit and floral aromas. The grape produces light wines scented with white flowers, pear, apple, and citrus.

Synonyms

Historical synonyms include Prosecco Tondo, Prosecco Lungo, Serprina, and Glera Lunga, though some of these are now regarded as distinct biotypes or related varieties.
