Terry Riley

Terry Riley (born June 24, 1935) is an American composer and performing musician best known as a pioneer of the minimalist school of composition in classical music. Influenced by jazz and Indian classical music, his music uses repetition, tape loops, and just intonation to create hypnotic, slowly evolving textures. His 1964 composition In C is often regarded as the work that launched the minimalist movement.

Career

Riley studied composition at San Francisco State University and the University of California, Berkeley. In Paris in the early 1960s he worked with tape loops, developing his "time-lag accumulator" technique. In C consists of 53 short melodic patterns that performers repeat at their own pace over a steady C pulse, producing shifting patterns. His album A Rainbow in Curved Air (1969), recorded with overdubbed keyboards and saxophones, brought his music to a wide audience and influenced rock musicians including Pete Townshend of the Who.

Indian music and later work

In 1970 Riley began studying Hindustani vocal music with Pandit Pran Nath, with whom he performed and collaborated for decades. His later works explore just intonation and improvisation, including The Well-Tuned Piano and numerous pieces for the Kronos Quartet, beginning a long association with Cadenza on the Night Plain (1984). Riley has also collaborated with his son Gyan Riley, a guitarist.
