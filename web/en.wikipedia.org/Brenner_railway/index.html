The Brenner railway (German: Brennerbahn; Italian: Ferrovia del Brennero) is a major mountain railway line running from Innsbruck in Austria through the Brenner Pass to Verona in Italy. Opened in 1867, it was the first transalpine railway to cross the main Alpine watershed without a summit tunnel, and it remains one of Europe's busiest freight corridors.


The line near the Brenner Pass.

History

The line was built by the Austrian Southern Railway under engineer Karl von Etzel and opened in stages in 1867, linking the earlier routes from Kufstein to Innsbruck and from Verona northward. Electrification followed in the early twentieth century; to this day trains change systems at the Brenner border station, where Italy's 3 kV DC meets Austria's 15 kV AC.

Route

Rising from Innsbruck (582 m), the line climbs the Wipp valley with gradients up to 25 per mille to the pass at 1,371 m, then descends the Eisack (Isarco) valley past Brixen/Bressanone and Bolzano/Bozen and on through the Adige valley to Verona. The Europabrücke and numerous viaducts and spiral sections feature on the ascent.

Brenner Base Tunnel

To relieve the steep, busy mountain line, the 55 km Brenner Base Tunnel is under construction between Innsbruck and Fortezza, with completion planned for the 2030s. Together with access routes it will form the core of the Scandinavian–Mediterranean TEN-T corridor.
