Aegis Ashore

The Aegis Ashore Missile Defense System (AAMDS) is a land-based adaptation of the United States Navy's Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System, developed by the Missile Defense Agency. It uses the AN/SPY-1 radar, command and control systems, and a vertical launching system firing SM-3 interceptor missiles from a fixed land site.

Aegis Ashore
Type: Land-based missile defense
Interceptors: SM-3
Sites: Romania, Poland

Deployments

The first operational site, at Deveselu, Romania, became operational in 2016 as part of the European Phased Adaptive Approach. A second site at Redzikowo, Poland, was completed and transferred to U.S. Navy control in the mid-2020s after construction delays.

Controversy

Russia has objected that the Mk 41 launchers used by Aegis Ashore could also fire Tomahawk cruise missiles, an allegation the United States denied. Japan planned to deploy two Aegis Ashore sites but cancelled the program in 2020 over cost and technical concerns, opting instead for additional Aegis-equipped destroyers.
