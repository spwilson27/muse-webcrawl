Defeat in elections

Defeat in elections refers to the loss suffered by a candidate or political party in an election, and to the conventions and consequences that follow — including the concession speech, the transfer of power, and the political aftermath for the losing side.

Concession

In democratic traditions, the losing candidate is expected to concede defeat, publicly acknowledging the result and congratulating the winner. The concession is a norm rather than a legal requirement in most systems, but it is regarded as important for legitimising the outcome and enabling a peaceful transfer of power. Famous concession moments are often remembered for their graciousness; refusal to concede, as in disputed elections, can precipitate constitutional crises or litigation over the count.

Consequences

Defeat may lead to leadership resignations, party realignments, or the retirement of the losing candidate, though many defeated politicians return to win later elections. In parliamentary systems, a government's defeat at a general election results in the prime minister resigning and the opposition leader being invited to form a government. Electoral defeat is also studied by political scientists through concepts such as retrospective voting, in which voters punish incumbents for poor performance.
