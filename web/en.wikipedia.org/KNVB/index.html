The KNVB (Koninklijke Nederlandse Voetbalbond, "Royal Dutch Football Association") is the governing body for football in the Netherlands. Founded in 1889, it organizes the Dutch national teams and the country's principal competitions, including the Eredivisie, the Eerste Divisie, and the KNVB Cup. With more than 1.2 million registered members, it is the largest sports federation in the Netherlands.

History

The association was founded on 8 December 1889 in The Hague, largely through the efforts of Pim Mulier, a pioneer of Dutch sport. The KNVB was a founding member of FIFA in 1904 and of UEFA in 1954. It received the royal designation "Koninklijke" in 1929. The Netherlands national team, administered by the KNVB, won the European Championship in 1988 and reached the FIFA World Cup final in 1974, 1978, and 2010.

Competitions

The KNVB runs the professional pyramid headed by the Eredivisie and Eerste Divisie, the amateur leagues below them, the KNVB Cup (first held in 1899, one of the oldest cup competitions in Europe), and the Johan Cruyff Shield. It also governs women's football — the Netherlands women's team won UEFA Women's Euro 2017 on home soil — futsal, and youth development from its national training centre in Zeist.

Organization

The KNVB is based at the KNVB Campus in Zeist, which opened in 2016 and serves as the training base for national teams and coach education. The association is known internationally for its coach-education system and the development philosophy associated with "Total Football", and it played a leading role in introducing professional football in the Netherlands in 1954.
