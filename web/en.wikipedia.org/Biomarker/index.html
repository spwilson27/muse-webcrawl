Biomarker

A biomarker (short for biological marker) is a measurable characteristic that indicates a biological state — normal or pathogenic processes, or responses to an exposure or intervention. Biomarkers include molecules in blood or tissue, physiological measures, imaging features and genetic variants.

Types and uses

Regulatory frameworks (such as the FDA–NIH BEST glossary) classify biomarkers as susceptibility/risk, diagnostic, monitoring, prognostic, predictive, pharmacodynamic/response and safety markers. Examples: blood glucose and HbA1c in diabetes, troponin in myocardial infarction, PSA in prostate disease, and HER2 status guiding cancer therapy.

Validation

A useful biomarker needs analytical validity (accurate, reliable measurement), clinical validity (association with the condition or outcome) and clinical utility (improving decisions and outcomes). Many candidate markers fail replication or add little beyond existing tests.

In other fields

In geology and astrobiology, biomarkers are molecular fossils or chemical signatures indicating past or present life. In ecology, biomarker species or compounds indicate environmental conditions and pollution.
