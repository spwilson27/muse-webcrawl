Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) is a human tyrosine kinase protein encoded by the JAK1 gene. It is a member of the Janus kinase family of non-receptor tyrosine kinases, which transduce signals from cytokine receptors through the JAK-STAT signalling pathway. JAK1 is essential for signalling by many type I and type II cytokines, including interferons and interleukins that use the common gamma chain and gp130 receptor subunits.

Function

When a cytokine binds its receptor, receptor-associated JAK1 molecules phosphorylate each other and the receptor's cytoplasmic tail, creating docking sites for STAT transcription factors. The STATs are phosphorylated, dimerise, and move to the nucleus to switch on target genes. JAK1 pairs with JAK3 at common gamma-chain receptors (used by IL-2, IL-4, IL-7, IL-9, IL-15 and IL-21) and with JAK2 or TYK2 at gp130-family and interferon receptors.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in JAK1 cause immunodeficiency with susceptibility to mycobacterial and viral infections, while gain-of-function mutations are associated with allergic inflammation, eosinophilia and, in some cases, malignancy. Because of its central role in inflammatory signalling, JAK1 is a major drug target: selective and non-selective JAK inhibitors such as upadacitinib, filgotinib, tofacitinib and baricitinib are used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, atopic dermatitis, ulcerative colitis and other immune-mediated diseases.
