Sergei Vasilyevich Rachmaninoff (1 April 1873 – 28 March 1943) was a Russian composer, virtuoso pianist, and conductor. One of the last great representatives of Romanticism in Russian classical music, he is celebrated for his deeply expressive melodies, virtuosic piano writing, and lush orchestration.

Life and career

A graduate of the Moscow Conservatory, Rachmaninoff won early fame with his Prelude in C-sharp minor (1892), but the disastrous 1897 premiere of his First Symphony plunged him into years of depression. Treatment with Dr. Nikolai Dahl helped restore his confidence, and the Piano Concerto No. 2 (1901), dedicated to Dahl, became one of the most beloved concertos in the repertoire. The Piano Concerto No. 3 (1909), premiered during his first American tour, is famed for its technical demands.

Exile

Rachmaninoff left Russia after the 1917 Revolution and settled eventually in the United States and Switzerland, sustaining a punishing career as a touring pianist that left little time to compose. His later works include the Rhapsody on a Theme of Paganini (1934), the Third Symphony (1936), and the Symphonic Dances (1940). His recordings preserve one of the great piano styles of the early 20th century.

Musical style

Rachmaninoff's enormous hand span enabled the wide chords characteristic of his piano music. His style combines long-breathed melody, rich harmony, and motifs drawn from Russian liturgical chant, such as the Dies irae that recurs throughout his output.
