Self-driving car

A self-driving car, also called an autonomous vehicle or driverless car, is a vehicle capable of sensing its environment and moving safely with little or no human input. It combines cameras, radar, lidar, ultrasonic sensors, GPS and inertial measurement with onboard computing to perceive the road, localise itself, predict other road users' behaviour, and plan and execute driving manoeuvres.

Levels of automation

The SAE J3016 standard defines six levels: Level 0 (no automation), Level 1 (driver assistance such as adaptive cruise control), Level 2 (partial automation, with the driver supervising at all times), Level 3 (conditional automation, in which the system drives but may request takeover), Level 4 (high automation within a defined operational design domain, with no driver needed there), and Level 5 (full automation everywhere). Commercial robotaxi services, such as those operated by Waymo, run at Level 4 in geofenced areas; consumer systems sold as "Autopilot" or "Full Self-Driving" remain Level 2 in regulators' classification.

History

Experiments date to the 1920s with radio-controlled cars, and serious research to the 1980s — notably Ernst Dickmanns' work in Germany and Carnegie Mellon's Navlab. The DARPA Grand Challenges of 2004 and 2005 and the 2007 Urban Challenge galvanised the field; Google's self-driving project, begun in 2009, became Waymo in 2016. In the following decade, startups and automakers invested heavily; some, such as Cruise, launched driverless services, though Cruise suspended operations after a serious 2023 incident, and industry timelines slipped repeatedly.

Issues

Proponents argue automation can cut the large death toll from human error, improve mobility for non-drivers and optimise traffic. Concerns include safety validation for rare edge cases, liability, cybersecurity, effects on employment, and deaths in early deployments, such as the 2018 fatal crash of an Uber test vehicle in Tempe, Arizona — the first recorded pedestrian death caused by a self-driving car. Regulation varies widely by country and state.
