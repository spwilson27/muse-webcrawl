Concert

A concert is a live music performance in front of an audience. The performance may be by a single musician, sometimes called a recital, or by a musical ensemble such as an orchestra, choir, or band. Concerts are held in a wide variety of settings, including dedicated concert halls, nightclubs, arenas, stadiums, parks, and private homes.

History

Although music had been performed publicly for centuries, formal concerts with paying audiences emerged in the late seventeenth century, notably in London, where John Banister organised some of the first public concerts in 1672. The modern concert tradition developed through the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries with the rise of public concert societies, subscription series, and purpose-built halls.

Formats

In classical music, a concert typically presents a programme of works, sometimes featuring a soloist in a concerto, with an intermission in longer programmes. In popular music, concerts range from small club sets to tours and festivals attended by tens of thousands; the largest concert tours are major commercial enterprises, and headline acts may be supported by opening performers.

Concert culture

Conventions of audience behaviour differ by genre: silence and applause between movements in classical concerts, singing and dancing at pop and rock shows. Advances in amplification, lighting, staging, and large-scale video have made the modern arena concert a multimedia spectacle.
