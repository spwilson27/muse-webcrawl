Tito–Stalin split

The Tito–Stalin split was a political conflict between the leaders of Yugoslavia and the Soviet Union, Josip Broz Tito and Joseph Stalin, which culminated in Yugoslavia's expulsion from the Cominform on 28 June 1948 and a period of strained relations known as the Informbiro period.

Causes

Tensions arose from Yugoslavia's independent foreign policy in the Balkans and Greece, disagreements over economic arrangements and Soviet advisers, and Stalin's determination to subordinate the Yugoslav party to Moscow. Yugoslav communists, who had liberated much of their country with their own Partisan forces, resisted Soviet control. In 1948 the Cominform, on Stalin's initiative, accused the Yugoslav leadership of nationalism and deviationism and expelled it.

Consequences

Yugoslavia survived an economic blockade and the threat of invasion by turning toward the West for aid while maintaining a socialist system at home, purging pro-Soviet "Cominformists". The split opened the way for Yugoslavia's distinctive model of workers' self-management and, later, a leading role in the Non-Aligned Movement. Relations with Moscow improved only after Stalin's death, with Khrushchev's visit to Belgrade in 1955.
