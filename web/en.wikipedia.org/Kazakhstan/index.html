Kazakhstan, officially the Republic of Kazakhstan, is a landlocked country in Central Asia, with a small portion in Eastern Europe. It is the world's ninth-largest country by land area and the largest landlocked country. It borders Russia, China, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan, and the Caspian Sea. The capital is Astana, while the largest city and former capital is Almaty.

History

The territory has been inhabited since the Stone Age and was historically home to nomadic Turkic and Mongol groups. The Kazakh Khanate emerged in the 15th century, and the Kazakhs were gradually incorporated into the Russian Empire during the 18th and 19th centuries. As the Kazakh SSR it suffered a devastating famine in the 1930s and received mass deportations of other peoples under Stalin. Kazakhstan was the last Soviet republic to declare independence, on 16 December 1991. Nursultan Nazarbayev led the country until his resignation in 2019, succeeded by Kassym-Jomart Tokayev.

Geography

The country spans steppe, semi-desert and desert, with mountains in the east and south-east — the Altai and Tien Shan ranges, including Khan Tengri (6,995 m). Major lakes include the Caspian Sea coast, Lake Balkhash, and the shrinking Aral Sea. The climate is continental with cold winters and hot summers.

Economy

Kazakhstan has Central Asia's largest economy, driven by oil (Tengiz and Kashagan fields), natural gas, uranium — of which it is the world's top producer — coal, and metals. It is also a major grain exporter. The Baikonur Cosmodrome, leased to Russia, remains a principal space launch site.

Demographics and culture

Ethnic Kazakhs form around 70 percent of the population, with a significant Russian minority and many other groups. The majority religion is Islam, followed by Christianity. The Kazakh language is written in Cyrillic, with an ongoing transition to a Latin alphabet.
