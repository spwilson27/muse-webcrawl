Ecclesiology

Ecclesiology is the theological study of the Christian Church — its nature, structure, mission and role in salvation. The word derives from the Greek ekklēsia, the assembly or congregation. Ecclesiology asks what the Church is, who belongs to it, how it should be governed, and how visible institutions relate to the spiritual body of Christ.

Marks and models

The Nicene Creed describes the Church as "one, holy, catholic and apostolic". Theologians such as Avery Dulles (in Models of the Church, 1974) have analysed the Church through complementary models: institution, mystical communion, sacrament, herald, servant and community of disciples.

Polity

Denominations differ in church government: episcopal polity (bishops, as in Catholic, Orthodox and Anglican churches), presbyterian polity (elders and assemblies) and congregational polity (autonomous local churches, as in Baptist traditions).

Ecumenism

Ecclesiology underlies the modern ecumenical movement; documents such as the World Council of Churches' Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry (1982) seek convergence on the doctrine of the Church among divided traditions.
