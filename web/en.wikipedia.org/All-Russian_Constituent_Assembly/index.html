The All-Russian Constituent Assembly was a constitutional body elected in November 1917 — in the first free national election in Russian history — to determine Russia's permanent form of government after the fall of the monarchy. It met for a single 13-hour session in Petrograd in January 1918 before being dispersed by the Bolsheviks, an event that became a symbol of the death of Russian parliamentary democracy.

The election

Elections, organised by the Provisional Government and held on 25 November 1917 (after the Bolsheviks had already seized power in the October Revolution), used universal suffrage — including women — across most of the former empire, with turnout around 44 million voters. The Socialist Revolutionary Party (SRs) won a plurality with about 40% of the vote, strongest among the peasantry; the Bolsheviks took roughly a quarter, dominating the cities, soldiers and sailors; the liberal Kadets and Mensheviks trailed far behind. The SR list had been drawn up before that party split, understating the pro-Bolshevik Left SRs.

The single session and dispersal

The Assembly opened on 18 January 1918 under SR leader Viktor Chernov. It refused to endorse the Bolshevik programme (the Declaration of the Rights of Working and Exploited People); the Bolshevik and then Left SR deputies walked out, and after an all-night session the guard commander — in the famous phrase, "the guard is tired" — closed the sitting. That day the Bolshevik government formally dissolved the Assembly; a peaceful demonstration in its support was fired upon. Anti-Bolshevik forces later gathered a rival government (Komuch) at Samara in the Assembly's name during the Civil War.

Legacy

The Assembly's dispersal remains contested ground: Bolsheviks argued the soviets were a higher form of democracy and the election outdated by the SR split; critics regard the episode as proof of the regime's contempt for democratic legitimacy.
