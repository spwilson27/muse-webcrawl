MV Wilhelm Gustloff

Wilhelm Gustloff
Launched: 5 May 1937
Operator: Strength Through Joy / Kriegsmarine
Fate: Sunk 30 January 1945

MV Wilhelm Gustloff was a German military transport ship which was sunk on 30 January 1945 by Soviet submarine S-13 in the Baltic Sea while evacuating civilians and military personnel from East Prussia during Operation Hannibal. An estimated 9,400 people died, making it the largest loss of life in a single ship sinking in history.

History

Named after Wilhelm Gustloff, the assassinated leader of the Nazi Party's Swiss branch, the ship was launched in 1937 as a cruise liner for the Strength Through Joy organisation. During the Second World War she served as a hospital ship, barracks ship, and finally as an evacuation transport.

Sinking

Grossly overloaded with refugees fleeing the Red Army, the ship was hit by three torpedoes and sank within the hour in freezing waters; only about 1,200 survivors were rescued.
