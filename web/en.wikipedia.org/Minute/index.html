Minute

A minute is a unit of time equal to 60 seconds, or 1/60 of an hour. It is accepted for use with the International System of Units (SI) although it is not itself an SI unit. The symbol for the minute as a unit of time is min; the prime symbol (′) is sometimes used informally, and is the standard symbol when the minute denotes a unit of angle.

History

The division of the hour into 60 minutes—and the minute into 60 seconds—derives from Babylonian sexagesimal (base-60) arithmetic, transmitted through Greek astronomy. The word comes from the Latin pars minuta prima, "first small part" (of the hour), while the second was the pars minuta secunda. Mechanical clocks displaying minutes became common in the late 16th century; before that, most public timekeeping was to the hour or quarter-hour.

Definitions and standards

In the modern time standard, a minute is almost always 60 seconds. Very rarely, a minute in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) has 61 seconds (or, in principle, 59) because of the insertion of a leap second to keep UTC in step with Earth's irregular rotation. The minute is also used as a unit of angle: a minute of arc is 1/60 of a degree, subdivided into 60 seconds of arc.
