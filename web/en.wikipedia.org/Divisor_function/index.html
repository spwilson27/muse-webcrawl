Divisor function

In mathematics, and specifically in number theory, a divisor function is an arithmetic function related to the divisors of an integer. When referred to as the divisor function, it counts the number of divisors of an integer n. The function σx(n) is defined as the sum of the xth powers of the positive divisors of n:

σx(n) = Σd|n dx.

Special cases

  • σ0(n), often written d(n), τ(n) or Ω̄(n), is the number of divisors of n.
  • σ1(n), written σ(n), is the sum of the divisors of n, called the sigma function.
  • The aliquot sum s(n) = σ(n) − n is the sum of proper divisors, used in classifying perfect, abundant and deficient numbers.

Properties

Divisor functions are multiplicative but not completely multiplicative. If n = Π pap, then σx(n) = Π (p(ap+1)x − 1) / (px − 1) for x ≠ 0. For example, σ(12) = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 6 + 12 = 28.

Growth and series

The average order of d(n) is log n. The sigma function figures in Ramanujan's work on highly composite numbers and in the study of colossally abundant numbers; the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to a bound on σ(n) proved conditionally by Guy Robin in 1984 (Robin's inequality). Divisor functions also appear in Dirichlet series: Σ σa(n) n−s = ζ(s) ζ(s − a).
