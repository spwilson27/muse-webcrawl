Edwina Mountbatten, Countess Mountbatten of Burma

Born: Edwina Cynthia Annette Ashley, 28 November 1901, London, England

Died: 21 February 1960 (aged 58), Kota Kinabalu, North Borneo

Spouse: Louis Mountbatten, 1st Earl Mountbatten of Burma (m. 1922)

Children: Patricia Knatchbull, 2nd Countess Mountbatten of Burma; Lady Pamela Hicks

Known for: Vicereine of India; humanitarian relief work

Edwina Cynthia Annette Mountbatten, Countess Mountbatten of Burma, CI, GBE, DCVO (née Ashley; 28 November 1901 – 21 February 1960), was an English heiress, socialite, and relief worker. She was the last Vicereine of India as the wife of Rear Admiral the Viscount Mountbatten of Burma, later 1st Earl Mountbatten of Burma, who served as the final Viceroy of India and first Governor-General of independent India.

Early life

Edwina Ashley was born in London in 1901, the elder daughter of Wilfrid Ashley, later 1st Baron Mount Temple, and Amalia Cassel. Through her maternal grandfather, the financier Sir Ernest Cassel, she inherited a considerable fortune, including the Broadlands estate in Hampshire. She was educated at home and at finishing schools, and became one of the leading figures of London society after the First World War.

Marriage

She married Lord Louis Mountbatten, a great-grandson of Queen Victoria, on 18 July 1922 at St Margaret's, Westminster, in one of the society weddings of the decade. The couple had two daughters. Their marriage was famously unconventional and frequently strained, but the two ultimately formed a close working partnership during and after the Second World War.

War and India

During the Second World War Edwina devoted herself to relief work with the St John Ambulance Brigade, travelling extensively in South East Asia after the war to inspect prisoner-of-war camps and press for better conditions for released prisoners. In 1947 Mountbatten was appointed Viceroy of India to oversee the transfer of power; as Vicereine during the Partition of India, Edwina worked tirelessly on refugee relief amid the massive communal violence and displacement that accompanied independence.

Later life and death

After India she continued her work with St John Ambulance, the Save the Children Fund, and numerous charitable organisations, earning widespread respect for her tireless humanitarian efforts in Asia. She died in her sleep on 21 February 1960 in Kota Kinabalu, North Borneo, while on a tour of inspection for St John Ambulance, and was buried at sea off Portsmouth in accordance with her wishes.
