Health disparities

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Health disparities are differences in health outcomes and their determinants between different population groups. In the United States, Healthy People defines a health disparity as a health difference closely linked with social, economic, and/or environmental disadvantage, adversely affecting groups who have systematically experienced greater obstacles to health based on race or ethnicity, religion, socioeconomic status, gender, age, disability, sexual orientation or gender identity, geographic location, or other characteristics historically linked to discrimination or exclusion.

Examples

Well-documented disparities include large gaps in life expectancy and infant mortality between racial and ethnic groups in the US (for example, higher maternal and infant mortality among Black Americans), elevated rates of diabetes and kidney disease among Indigenous and Hispanic populations, worse outcomes in rural areas with limited hospital access, and a "deaths of despair" pattern among less-educated Americans driven by opioids, alcohol, and suicide. Globally, life expectancy differs by decades between high-income and low-income countries, and child mortality is far higher in sub-Saharan Africa than in Europe.

Causes

Research attributes disparities primarily to social determinants of health: income and wealth, education, housing, environmental exposures, structural racism, access to and quality of medical care, health insurance coverage, and health behaviours shaped by those conditions. Disparities are related to but distinct from health inequalities (any measurable differences) and health inequities (differences judged avoidable and unjust).

Policy responses

Reducing disparities is a stated goal of the US Healthy People programme and the World Health Organization. Interventions include expanding insurance coverage, community health workers, targeted screening and prevention, place-based investment, and data collection disaggregated by race, ethnicity, and other factors.
