The Sverdrup Islands are an archipelago of the northern Queen Elizabeth Islands, in Nunavut, Canada. The islands, part of the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, lie in the Arctic Ocean west of Ellesmere Island and north of the Parry Channel. They are named after the Norwegian explorer Otto Sverdrup, who explored and mapped them during the Second Fram Expedition (1898–1902) and claimed them for Norway; the claim was settled in 1930 when Norway recognised Canadian sovereignty in exchange for Canadian recognition of Norwegian claims to Jan Mayen.

Geography

The principal islands are Axel Heiberg Island, Ellef Ringnes Island, Amund Ringnes Island, Meighen Island, King Christian Island, and Cornwall Island. The terrain is largely polar desert: folded sedimentary hills, salt diapirs (notably on Axel Heiberg, home to the Lost Hammer and other hypersaline springs), and permanent ice caps. Ellef Ringnes Island hosted the Isachsen weather station, staffed from 1948 to 1978, and the north magnetic pole was located among these islands through much of the 20th century.

Research

McGill University's Arctic Research Station on Axel Heiberg Island, established in 1960, supports studies of the island's fossil forests — mummified remains of lush Eocene forests preserved where the Arctic was once warm. The Sverdrup Islands' extreme cold, dryness, and Mars-like terrain also make them valuable analogues for planetary science.
