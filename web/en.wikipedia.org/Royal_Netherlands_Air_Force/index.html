Royal Netherlands Air Force

The Royal Netherlands Air Force (RNLAF; Dutch: Koninklijke Luchtmacht, KLu) is the aerial warfare branch of the Netherlands Armed Forces. Its ancestor, the army aviation section (Luchtvaartafdeling), was founded on 1 July 1913, making the Dutch military air arm one of the oldest in the world. It became an independent service in 1953 and today operates as a modern NATO air force.

History

Dutch military aviation began in 1913 with a small army air section equipped with Farman aircraft built by the Dutch firm Fokker. The Netherlands remained neutral in the First World War, but on 10 May 1940 the small Dutch air arm fought against the German invasion, inflicting losses before being overwhelmed within days. Dutch airmen who escaped formed No. 320 (Netherlands) Squadron RAF and other units under British command, while Dutch squadrons also served in the Netherlands East Indies against Japan.

Cold War

The air force became an independent branch of the armed forces on 27 March 1953 and received the "Royal" prefix. As a NATO member, the Netherlands operated types including the F-84, the F-104 Starfighter, the NF-5, and from 1979 the F-16 Fighting Falcon, sharing nuclear tasks within the alliance and manning Nike Hercules and Patriot air defences in Germany.

Modern operations

Since the 1990s the RNLAF has taken part in operations over the Balkans, Afghanistan, Libya, and Iraq and Syria against ISIL, and in Baltic Air Policing. The F-16 fleet is being replaced by the F-35A Lightning II, of which the Netherlands has ordered more than fifty, with the first operational aircraft based at Leeuwarden Air Base. The force also operates AH-64 Apache and CH-47 Chinook helicopters (under the joint Defence Helicopter Command), C-130 Hercules transports, and participates in the multinational MRTT tanker fleet based at Eindhoven.

Structure and bases

The Commander of the Air Force leads the service from headquarters in Breda. Principal bases include Leeuwarden, Volkel (fighters), Eindhoven (transport), Gilze-Rijen (helicopters), and Woensdrecht (maintenance and training). The RNLAF numbers roughly 6,400 personnel. Its roundel — orange, white, and blue — reflects the national colours and the House of Orange.
