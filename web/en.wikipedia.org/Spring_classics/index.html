Spring classics

SportRoad bicycle racing
RegionWestern Europe
PeriodMarch–April
Key racesMilan–San Remo, Tour of Flanders, Paris–Roubaix, Liège–Bastogne–Liège, Amstel Gold Race

The spring classics are a series of prestigious one-day road cycling races held each spring, mostly in Western Europe, between March and late April. They form the traditional opening of the European professional road racing season and include several of the sport's oldest and most revered events.

Overview

The spring classics season typically begins with the semi-classic races of the Belgian "opening weekend" in late February or early March, such as Omloop Het Nieuwsblad and Kuurne–Brussels–Kuurne, and builds toward the five Monuments of cycling. Races are characterised by unpredictable spring weather, narrow farm roads, cobbled sectors and short, steep climbs known as bergs or murs.

The Monuments

Four of the five Monuments fall within the spring classics period:

  • Milan–San Remo (Italy) — the longest classic, often called "La Primavera", decided on the Poggio climb near the finish.
  • Tour of Flanders (Belgium) — cobbled climbs including the Oude Kwaremont and Paterberg.
  • Paris–Roubaix (France) — the "Hell of the North", raced over brutal pavé sectors such as the Arenberg Forest.
  • Liège–Bastogne–Liège (Belgium) — the oldest classic, "La Doyenne", through the Ardennes hills.

Other major races

Other prominent spring classics include the Amstel Gold Race in the Dutch Limburg hills, La Flèche Wallonne with its Mur de Huy finish, Strade Bianche over the white gravel roads of Tuscany, Gent–Wevelgem, E3 Saxo Classic and Dwars door Vlaanderen. The Ardennes classics (Amstel, Flèche and Liège) form their own sub-series in mid-to-late April.

Characteristics

Unlike stage races, the classics reward explosive power, bike-handling on cobbles, tactical nous and resilience in cold, wet conditions. Riders who specialise in them are known as classics riders or, for the cobbled races, flandriens. Eddy Merckx, Roger De Vlaeminck, Tom Boonen, Fabian Cancellara, Mathieu van der Poel and Tadej Pogačar are among the most celebrated exponents.
