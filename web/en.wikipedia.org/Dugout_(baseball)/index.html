Dugout (baseball)

In baseball, the dugout is a team's bench and is located in foul territory between home plate and either first or third base. There are two dugouts, one for the home team and one for the visiting team. Generally, the dugout holds the team's players who are not on the field, along with the manager, coaches, and other authorized personnel.

Description

The dugout is typically slightly below field level, with a bench, bat racks, water coolers, and a telephone connecting to the bullpen. A protective fence or railing often fronts the dugout to shield players from foul balls. In many Major League Baseball parks, tunnels connect the dugout to the clubhouse.

Rules and customs

Players in the dugout must remain within it during play, with exceptions for on-deck batters. The choice of dugout (first-base or third-base side) varies by ballpark; teams traditionally keep a consistent side in their home stadium. The dugout is also a familiar setting for superstitions, celebrations, and the ceremonial showering of players with sports drinks after notable achievements.
