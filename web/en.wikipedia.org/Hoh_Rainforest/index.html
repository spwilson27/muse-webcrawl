Hoh Rainforest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bigleaf maples draped in clubmoss in the Hall of Mosses.

The Hoh Rainforest is one of the largest temperate rainforests in the United States, located on the Olympic Peninsula in western Washington state, within Olympic National Park. It is named for the Hoh River, along whose valley the forest grows; "Hoh" derives from the Quileute name for the river.

The region receives abundant precipitation — around 3.6 metres (12 feet) of rain per year — with frequent fog and mist that sustain the forest through summer dry periods. The dominant trees are Sitka spruce and western hemlock, some reaching over 90 metres in height and 500 years in age, along with Douglas-fir, western red cedar and bigleaf maple.

Ecology

The Hoh is famous for its lush epiphytes: mosses, ferns, lichens and clubmoss drape the trees, and fallen "nurse logs" support rows of young trees. The understory includes devil's club, huckleberry and sorrel. Wildlife includes Roosevelt elk, black-tailed deer, black bear, cougar, bobcat, river otter and the northern spotted owl; the Hoh River hosts salmon and steelhead runs. Olympic National Park was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981, and the area forms part of a biosphere reserve.

Visiting

The Hoh Rainforest Visitor Center, reached by the Upper Hoh Road from U.S. Highway 101, is the starting point for two short nature trails, the Hall of Mosses (0.8 mile) and the Spruce Nature Trail (1.2 miles), as well as the Hoh River Trail, which backpackers follow some 17 miles to Glacier Meadows below Mount Olympus. A large Sitka spruce near the area, over 80 metres tall, long served as a landmark for visitors.
