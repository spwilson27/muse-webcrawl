Nucleus basalis

The nucleus basalis, also known as the nucleus basalis of Meynert, is a group of neurons in the substantia innominata of the basal forebrain. It is the principal source of cholinergic innervation to the cerebral cortex and plays an important role in arousal, attention, learning and memory.

Anatomy

The nucleus lies below the globus pallidus and anterior commissure. Its large cholinergic neurons project widely across the neocortex, as well as to the amygdala and other limbic structures. It is named after the German psychiatrist Theodor Meynert.

Clinical significance

Degeneration of nucleus basalis neurons is a prominent feature of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias, and the resulting loss of cortical acetylcholine contributed to the cholinergic hypothesis of Alzheimer's disease and the use of cholinesterase inhibitors as treatment.
