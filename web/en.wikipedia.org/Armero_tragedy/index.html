Armero tragedy

The Armero tragedy (Spanish: Tragedia de Armero) occurred following the eruption of the Nevado del Ruiz stratovolcano in Tolima, Colombia, on 13 November 1985. The town of Armero was destroyed by lahars — volcanic mudflows — generated when the eruption melted part of the volcano's summit glacier. Around 25,000 people died in the disaster, including some 20,000 in Armero alone, making it one of the deadliest volcanic events of the 20th century.

Background

Nevado del Ruiz had shown signs of activity since late 1984, with increased seismicity, fumarolic activity and small phreatic eruptions. Hazard maps prepared by scientists in October 1985 correctly identified Armero as lying in the path of potential lahars along the Lagunilla River valley, but warnings were not effectively communicated or acted upon, and no evacuation was ordered.

The eruption and lahars

The volcano erupted at about 9:08 p.m. on 13 November 1985, producing pyroclastic flows that melted snow and ice on the summit. The meltwater mixed with ash and debris to form lahars that rushed down the river valleys at high speed. The lahar reached Armero shortly after 11:30 p.m., burying most of the town under metres of mud and debris. Further lahars struck the town of Chinchiná in the neighbouring department of Caldas, killing around 1,800 people.

Aftermath

Relief efforts were hampered by the scale of the destruction, poor weather and the difficulty of reaching the site. The disaster became internationally known in part through the case of Omayra Sánchez, a thirteen-year-old girl trapped in the debris for three days whose final hours were broadcast on television; she died before rescuers could free her. The Colombian government was widely criticised for failing to evacuate the town despite the warnings, and the tragedy led to reforms in Colombia's disaster preparedness and volcanic monitoring systems. The site of Armero was declared a memorial, and survivors were resettled in nearby towns.
