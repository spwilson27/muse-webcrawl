Frank Árpád Darabont (born 28 January 1959) is an American film director, screenwriter and producer. He is best known for his film adaptations of Stephen King stories, including The Shawshank Redemption (1994), The Green Mile (1999) and The Mist (2007), and for developing the television series The Walking Dead.

Early life and career

Darabont was born in a refugee camp in Montbéliard, France, to Hungarian parents who had fled Hungary after the 1956 revolution; the family settled in the United States when he was an infant. He began as a screenwriter on horror films such as A Nightmare on Elm Street 3: Dream Warriors (1987), The Blob (1988) and The Fly II (1989), and wrote for the television series The Young Indiana Jones Chronicles.

Breakthrough

His directorial debut, The Shawshank Redemption, was nominated for seven Academy Awards including Best Picture and Best Adapted Screenplay; though a modest performer on release, it became one of the most beloved films of all time through home video and television. The Green Mile earned four further Oscar nominations. Darabont later adapted and launched AMC's The Walking Dead (2010), serving as showrunner for its first season, and created the noir series Mob City (2013).
