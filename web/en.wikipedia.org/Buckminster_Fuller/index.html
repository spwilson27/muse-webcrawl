Richard Buckminster Fuller (July 12, 1895 – July 1, 1983) was an American architect, systems theorist, designer and inventor. He published more than 30 books and popularised terms such as "Spaceship Earth" and "ephemeralization", while developing numerous inventions, chiefly architectural designs, most famously the geodesic dome.

Life and work

Expelled from Harvard twice, Fuller worked in industry and served in the US Navy before a personal crisis in 1927 — bankrupt and grieving the death of his daughter — led him to dedicate his life to finding "what a single penniless human could do for humanity". His Dymaxion house, car and world map of the 1930s and 1940s explored doing more with less. At Black Mountain College in 1948–49 he developed the geodesic dome, patenting it in 1954; its strength-to-weight ratio made it widely used, from radar stations to the US pavilion at Expo 67 in Montreal.

Ideas

Fuller called himself a "comprehensive anticipatory design scientist". His books, including Operating Manual for Spaceship Earth (1969) and Synergetics (1975), argued that technology and resource efficiency could provide for all humanity. His World Game proposed a global simulation of resource allocation as an alternative to war.

Legacy

The carbon molecules known as fullerenes were named for their resemblance to his domes. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1983, shortly before his death.
