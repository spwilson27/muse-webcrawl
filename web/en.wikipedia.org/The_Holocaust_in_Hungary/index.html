The Holocaust in Hungary

Period1941–1945 (mass deportations 1944)
LocationKingdom of Hungary
VictimsApproximately 560,000 Hungarian Jews murdered

The Holocaust in Hungary was the persecution, deportation, and murder of Jews in the Kingdom of Hungary during the Second World War, culminating in 1944 after Nazi Germany occupied the country. Of a Jewish population of roughly 825,000 (including annexed territories), approximately 560,000 were murdered.

Background

From 1938 onward, the regime of Regent Miklós Horthy enacted antisemitic "Jewish laws" restricting employment, property, and citizenship. Hungarian authorities also deported some 20,000 "foreign" Jews in 1941, most of whom were massacred at Kamianets-Podilskyi, and Jewish men were conscripted into forced labour service in which tens of thousands died. Nevertheless, most Hungarian Jews survived until 1944, and Budapest was sometimes seen as a relative refuge.

1944 deportations

After Germany occupied Hungary on 19 March 1944, Adolf Eichmann's Sondereinsatzkommando, working closely with the Hungarian interior ministry and gendarmerie, organised the ghettoisation and deportation of provincial Jewry. Between mid-May and early July 1944, some 437,000 Jews were deported, most to Auschwitz-Birkenau, where the majority were murdered on arrival. Horthy halted the deportations in July, but after the Arrow Cross seized power in October 1944, tens of thousands of Budapest Jews were murdered or sent on death marches before the city's liberation in early 1945.

Aftermath

Leading organisers, including László Endre and László Baky, were tried and executed after the war. Hungary has seen ongoing historical debate about the degree of Hungarian state responsibility, and memorials such as the Shoes on the Danube Bank in Budapest commemorate the victims.
