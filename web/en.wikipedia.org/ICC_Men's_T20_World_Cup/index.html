ICC Men's T20 World Cup

The ICC Men's T20 World Cup (formerly the ICC World Twenty20) is the Twenty20 International cricket tournament, organised by the International Cricket Council (ICC) since 2007. It generally takes place every two years and is contested by national men's teams in the Twenty20 format, in which each side bats for a maximum of twenty overs.

History

The first tournament was held in South Africa in 2007 and was won by India, who defeated Pakistan in a celebrated final at Johannesburg. The event's success confirmed Twenty20 cricket — only recently introduced — as a major international spectacle. Pakistan won the second edition in England in 2009, and England won the third in the West Indies in 2010.

The West Indies became the first team to win the title twice, triumphing in Sri Lanka in 2012 and again in India in 2016. Sri Lanka won the 2014 tournament in Bangladesh, Australia won the 2021 edition in the United Arab Emirates, and England claimed their second title in Australia in 2022, making them the first men's team to hold the ODI and T20 World Cups simultaneously.

The 2024 tournament, co-hosted by the West Indies and the United States, expanded to a record 20 teams. India defeated South Africa in the final in Barbados to win their second title, ending an 11-year wait for an ICC trophy.

Format

Recent editions have featured a group stage followed by a Super 8 (or Super 12) round, semi-finals and a final. Qualification for the tournament is determined by the ICC Men's T20I Team Rankings and by regional qualifying tournaments for associate member nations.
