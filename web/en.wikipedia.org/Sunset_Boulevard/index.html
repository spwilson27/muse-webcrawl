Sunset Boulevard

Sunset Boulevard is a boulevard in the central and western part of Los Angeles, California, extending about 22 miles (35 km) from downtown Los Angeles to the Pacific Ocean in Pacific Palisades. The street is an iconic part of Hollywood history and American popular culture, and lends its name to the celebrated 1950 film Sunset Boulevard directed by Billy Wilder.

Route

The boulevard begins near Figueroa Street in downtown Los Angeles and winds west through Echo Park, Silver Lake, Hollywood, West Hollywood, Beverly Hills, Holmby Hills, Bel-Air and Brentwood before ending at the Pacific Coast Highway. Its course follows the foothills of the Santa Monica Mountains.

The Sunset Strip

The portion of the boulevard running through West Hollywood, known as the Sunset Strip, became famous for its nightclubs, music venues, boutiques and giant billboards. Landmark venues include the Whisky a Go Go, the Roxy Theatre and the Viper Room, central to Los Angeles rock music from the 1960s onward.

In culture

Billy Wilder's film noir Sunset Boulevard (1950), starring Gloria Swanson as the faded silent-film star Norma Desmond, is one of the most acclaimed films ever made; its story of a struggling screenwriter drawn into Desmond's decaying mansion on the boulevard became a defining portrait of Hollywood. The film later inspired Andrew Lloyd Webber's stage musical of the same name.
