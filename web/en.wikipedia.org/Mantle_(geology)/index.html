Mantle (geology)

Thickness~2,900 km (Earth)
CompositionSilicate rock, rich in magnesium and iron

A mantle is a layer inside a planetary body bounded below by a core and above by a crust. Earth's mantle is a silicate rock layer about 2,900 kilometres thick, making up roughly 84% of the planet's volume and two-thirds of its mass.

Structure

The mantle is divided into the upper mantle (which includes the rigid lithospheric mantle and the ductile asthenosphere), a transition zone marked by phase changes in the mineral olivine at depths of about 410 and 660 kilometres, and the lower mantle extending to the core–mantle boundary at 2,890 kilometres depth.

Convection

Although solid, mantle rock flows very slowly over geological time. This convection drives plate tectonics at the surface, and plumes of hot material rising from deep in the mantle are thought to feed volcanic hotspots such as Hawaii and Iceland.

Composition

The mantle is composed mainly of magnesium- and iron-rich silicate minerals such as olivine and pyroxene; at lower-mantle pressures the dominant minerals are bridgmanite and ferropericlase.
