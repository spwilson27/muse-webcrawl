Rose Bowl (stadium)

The Rose Bowl is an outdoor athletic stadium in Pasadena, California, in the Arroyo Seco. Opened in 1922 and designed by Myron Hunt in a horseshoe form later enclosed into a bowl, it is a National Historic Landmark famed as the home of the Rose Bowl Game.

Location: Pasadena, California
Opened: 1922
Capacity: about 89,000 (historically over 100,000)
Tenants: UCLA Bruins football (since 1982)

Events

Beyond the annual New Year's college football game, the stadium hosted the 1932 and 1984 Olympic events, Super Bowl XI and four later Super Bowls, the 1994 FIFA World Cup Final and the 1999 Women's World Cup Final, plus the monthly Rose Bowl Flea Market. Its record crowd exceeded 106,000 for the 1973 Rose Bowl Game.

Setting

The stadium sits in Brookside Park against the San Gabriel Mountains, its rose-coloured sign an icon of American sport. See also Pasadena and Tournament of Roses Parade.
