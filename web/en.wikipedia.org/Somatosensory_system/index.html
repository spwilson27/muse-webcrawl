Somatosensory system


Mechanoreceptors in the human skin

The somatosensory system is the network of neural structures in the brain and body that produce the perception of touch, temperature, body position (proprioception), and pain. It is a subset of the sensory nervous system. The somatosensory system is a highly diverse sensory system composed of many different types of receptors and sensory neurons. It conveys information from the periphery of the body to the central nervous system, where it is processed to produce the experience of bodily sensation.

Receptors

Somatosensory receptors are classified by the stimuli to which they respond. Mechanoreceptors, including Meissner corpuscles, Pacinian corpuscles, Merkel discs, and Ruffini endings, respond to touch, pressure, and vibration. Thermoreceptors detect warmth and cold. Nociceptors respond to potentially damaging stimuli and give rise to the sensation of pain. Proprioceptors in muscles, tendons, and joints provide information about body position and movement.

Pathways

Two major ascending pathways carry somatosensory information to the brain. The dorsal column–medial lemniscus pathway carries fine touch, vibration, and proprioceptive signals; the spinothalamic tract carries pain and temperature signals. Both relay through the thalamus to the primary somatosensory cortex in the parietal lobe, where the body is represented in a topographic map known as the sensory homunculus.

Clinical significance

Damage to somatosensory pathways, for example from stroke, spinal cord injury, or peripheral neuropathy, can cause numbness, paraesthesia, chronic pain, and loss of proprioception.
