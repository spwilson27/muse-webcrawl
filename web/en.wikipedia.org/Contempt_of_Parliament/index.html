Contempt of Parliament

Contempt of Parliament is the offence of obstructing, impeding or insulting a parliament or its members in the performance of their duties, in jurisdictions modelled on the Westminster system. It is closely related to, but distinct from, breach of parliamentary privilege: privilege defines the rights and immunities, while contempt covers acts that violate them.

Scope

Conduct that may amount to contempt includes refusing to answer a committee's questions, giving false evidence, disobeying an order of the House, publishing confidential committee documents, interfering with witnesses, or attempting to bribe members. Each House is generally the judge of its own contempts and may punish by admonition, reprimand, fine (historically), or imprisonment for the remainder of the session.

Notable cases

In the United Kingdom, the power underpins the work of select committees; the House of Commons in December 2018 found the Government in contempt for withholding legal advice on the Brexit withdrawal agreement — the first time a government had been so found. Similar powers exist in Canada, Australia, India and other Commonwealth legislatures.

Contempt of Congress

The analogous doctrine in the United States is contempt of Congress, invoked when a person defies a congressional subpoena, and punishable through inherent, criminal or civil enforcement routes.
