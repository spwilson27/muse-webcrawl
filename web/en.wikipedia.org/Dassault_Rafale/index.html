The Dassault Rafale (literally "gust of wind") is a French twin-engine, canard delta wing, multirole fighter aircraft designed and built by Dassault Aviation. Equipped with a wide range of weapons, the Rafale is intended to perform air supremacy, interdiction, aerial reconnaissance, ground support, in-depth strike, anti-ship strike, and nuclear deterrence missions.

Development

France withdrew from the joint European fighter programme that became the Eurofighter Typhoon in 1985, preferring a smaller, carrier-capable aircraft wholly under national control. The Rafale A demonstrator flew in 1986; production variants followed, with the carrier-based Rafale M entering service with the French Navy in 2001 and the air-force Rafale B and C from 2006.

Design

The Rafale combines a delta wing with active close-coupled canards and a digital fly-by-wire system. Its SPECTRA electronic warfare suite, RBE2-AA active electronically scanned array radar (fitted to later standards), and sensor fusion support its "omnirole" concept. It carries the MICA and Meteor air-to-air missiles, Exocet anti-ship missiles, SCALP cruise missiles, and can deliver the ASMP-A nuclear missile.

Operational history and exports

French Rafales have flown combat missions over Afghanistan, Libya (2011), Mali, Iraq, and Syria. After years without export success, orders came from Egypt (2015), Qatar, India (36 aircraft, 2016, with further naval orders following), Greece, Croatia, the United Arab Emirates (80 aircraft, 2021), Indonesia, and Serbia, making the Rafale one of the most successful European fighters on the export market.
