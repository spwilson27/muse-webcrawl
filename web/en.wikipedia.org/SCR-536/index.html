The SCR-536 was a hand-held radio transceiver used by the United States Army during the Second World War. Manufactured by the Galvin Manufacturing Corporation (later Motorola), it was popularly known as the Handie-Talkie and is often credited as the first device widely called a "walkie-talkie".

Design

The SCR-536 was the first truly hand-held two-way radio: a self-contained unit weighing about 2.3 kg (5 lb) containing the transmitter, receiver and batteries in a waterproof housing. It operated in the 3.5–6.0 MHz AM band with an output power of roughly 0.36 watts, giving a range of about a mile on land and up to three miles over water. The telescoping antenna extended from the top, and the unit switched on when the antenna was pulled up. It had no power switch, squelch or external controls beyond channel selection by interchangeable coils and crystals.

Service

Introduced in 1941, the set was issued widely to infantry companies and platoons, and was carried ashore in amphibious landings including Normandy. Around 130,000 units were built by the end of the war. The heavier backpack SCR-300 is the set more properly nicknamed "walkie-talkie" by some, but the SCR-536's name endured in popular usage.
