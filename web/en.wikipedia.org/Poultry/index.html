Poultry

Poultry are domesticated birds kept by humans for their eggs, their meat or their feathers. These birds are most typically members of the superorder Galloanserae (fowl), especially the order Galliformes (which includes chickens, quails and turkeys).

The term also includes birds that are killed for their meat, such as the young of pigeons (known as squabs) but does not include similar wild birds hunted for sport or food and known as game. The word "poultry" comes from the French/Norman word poule, itself derived from the Latin word pullus, which means small animal.

Definition

Poultry is defined as domestic birds, including chickens, turkeys, ducks, and geese, raised for the production of eggs, meat, or feathers. The category also frequently includes pigeons, especially when referring to squab production. In classifications that draw a distinction between poultry and game birds, poultry refers to domesticated species while game birds are wild species hunted for food.

Types of poultry

The most widely kept poultry worldwide is the chicken, raised in the billions for both meat and eggs. Other important species include the domestic duck, the Muscovy duck, the domestic goose, the turkey, guinea fowl, and various species of quail. Chickens are commonly divided into layers, bred for egg production, and broilers, bred for meat.

History

The domestication of poultry took place around 8,000 years ago in Southeast Asia, where the red junglefowl was the principal ancestor of the modern chicken. From there, domesticated birds spread westward to India, the Middle East, Europe and Africa, and eastward across the Pacific. Turkeys were domesticated independently in Mesoamerica, and ducks were domesticated in both China and the Americas.

Production

Poultry farming is one of the largest branches of animal agriculture. The United States, China and Brazil are among the world's largest producers of poultry meat. Modern production ranges from small free-range flocks to intensive indoor systems housing tens of thousands of birds. Concerns about animal welfare, disease transmission such as avian influenza, and the use of antibiotics are major issues in the industry.
