Mediterranean climate

Mediterranean climate
Köppen classification: Csa, Csb (and Csc)
Regions: Mediterranean Basin; California; central Chile; Western Cape (South Africa); southwestern and southern Australia
Pattern: Dry summers, mild wet winters

A Mediterranean climate is a temperate climate type characterised by dry summers and mild, wet winters. In the Köppen classification it corresponds to the Csa (hot-summer) and Csb (warm-summer) categories. It takes its name from the lands around the Mediterranean Sea, where this climate is most extensive.

Distribution and causes

Mediterranean climates occur on the western sides of continents between roughly 30° and 45° latitude: the Mediterranean Basin, coastal California, central Chile, South Africa's Western Cape, and parts of southern and southwestern Australia. The pattern is driven by the seasonal migration of subtropical high-pressure belts: in summer the highs move poleward and suppress rainfall, while in winter the storm track of the mid-latitude westerlies returns.

Vegetation and life

These regions support distinctive sclerophyll shrublands — maquis and garrigue in the Mediterranean, chaparral in California, matorral in Chile, fynbos in South Africa, and mallee in Australia — and are global biodiversity hotspots. Agriculture features olives, grapes, citrus, and wheat adapted to the dry summer; wildfire is a natural and increasingly hazardous part of the ecosystem.

Climate change

Projections indicate Mediterranean-climate regions will generally become hotter and drier, with the Mediterranean Basin identified as a climate-change hotspot and heightened drought and fire risk in all five regions.
