National League (baseball)

Founded: 2 February 1876

Teams: 15

Most pennants: Los Angeles Dodgers franchise (25)

Full name: National League of Professional Baseball Clubs

The National League of Professional Baseball Clubs, known simply as the National League (NL), is the world's oldest extant professional team sports league. Founded in 1876, it is one of the two leagues that constitute Major League Baseball (MLB) in the United States and Canada, the other being the American League (AL). The National League comprises 15 teams divided into East, Central and West divisions. Its champion each season wins the National League pennant and advances to the World Series against the American League champion.

History

The National League was founded in 1876 by Chicago businessman William Hulbert, replacing the loosely organised National Association with a closed league of clubs bound by fixed schedules, player contracts and territorial exclusivity. Eight charter clubs began play in 1876; of them, the Chicago Cubs and Atlanta Braves franchises survive. The NL fought the rival American League in the "baseball war" of 1901–1903 before the two leagues agreed to coexist and meet in the World Series. Long known as the "Senior Circuit," the NL resisted the designated hitter rule — adopted by the AL in 1973 — for nearly five decades, requiring its pitchers to bat until MLB adopted a universal designated hitter in 2022.

Teams and play

Current NL clubs include the Los Angeles Dodgers, San Francisco Giants, St. Louis Cardinals, Chicago Cubs, New York Mets, Philadelphia Phillies and San Diego Padres. Since 1997 NL teams have played interleague games against AL opponents during the regular season. The league's postseason includes the Wild Card Series, Division Series and Championship Series.
