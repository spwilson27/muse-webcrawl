Joule
Symbol: J
Named after: James Prescott Joule
Definition: 1 kg·m²/s² (SI base units)

The joule (symbol: J) is the unit of energy in the International System of Units (SI). It is equal to the amount of work done when a force of one newton displaces a mass through a distance of one metre in the direction of that force.

Definition

One joule can also be defined as the work required to move an electric charge of one coulomb through a potential difference of one volt, or the energy dissipated as heat when a current of one ampere passes through a resistance of one ohm for one second. In SI base units, 1 J = 1 kg·m²·s⁻².

Named for Joule

The unit is named after English physicist James Prescott Joule (1818–1889), whose experiments established the mechanical equivalent of heat and helped found thermodynamics. It was adopted as an SI derived unit in 1960.

Conversions

One joule is approximately 0.239 calories, 2.78×10⁻⁷ kilowatt-hours, or the energy needed to lift a small apple one metre against Earth's gravity.
