Venosa (Lucano: Vënòsë) is a town and comune in the province of Potenza, in the southern Italian region of Basilicata, in the Vulture area. It is known as the birthplace of the Roman poet Horace.

History

The ancient Venusia was a Samnite town captured by Rome in 291 BC, when it became a Latin colony of some 20,000 settlers guarding the Appian Way. The Roman poet Quintus Horatius Flaccus — Horace — was born there in 65 BC. In the early Middle Ages the town passed among Lombards, Byzantines and Saracens; the Normans made it a stronghold, and Robert Guiscard's family, the Hautevilles, are buried in the Abbey of the Santissima Trinità. Manfred, son of Frederick II, died of wounds after the Battle of Benevento in 1266 and was initially buried near Venosa.

Main sights

Sights include the Aragonese castle built in 1470 by Duke Pirro del Balzo, the unfinished abbey church of the Santissima Trinità with the Hauteville tombs, extensive Jewish catacombs, and an archaeological park with Roman baths, an amphitheatre and the house traditionally identified as Horace's birthplace. The surrounding hills produce Aglianico del Vulture, one of southern Italy's most noted red wines. The town's later history forms part of the longer story of Italy told in Unification of Italy.
