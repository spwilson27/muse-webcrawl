Argument structure

In linguistics, argument structure is the set of syntactic arguments required by a predicate, especially a verb. Verbs differ in valency: intransitive verbs take one argument (the subject), transitive verbs take two (subject and object), and ditransitive verbs take three.

Thematic roles

Arguments bear semantic or thematic roles such as agent, patient, theme, experiencer, goal, and source. Theories of argument structure examine how these roles are linked to syntactic positions — the problem of linking — and how alternations such as the passive or the dative alternation rearrange them.

Approaches

Argument structure is studied in frameworks including generative grammar, lexical functional grammar, construction grammar, and lexical semantics. Phenomena such as argument-structure constructions in the work of Adele Goldberg argue that sentence patterns themselves carry meaning beyond the verb.
