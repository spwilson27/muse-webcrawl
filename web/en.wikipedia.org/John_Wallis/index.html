John Wallis (3 December 1616 – 8 November 1703) was an English clergyman and mathematician who is given partial credit for the development of infinitesimal calculus. He introduced the symbol ∞ for infinity and devised the Wallis product for π.

Life

Wallis was educated at Emmanuel College, Cambridge, and took holy orders. During the English Civil War he deciphered Royalist messages for Parliament. In 1649 he was appointed Savilian Professor of Geometry at the University of Oxford, a post he held for over fifty years, and he was one of the founders of the Royal Society.

Mathematics

His Arithmetica Infinitorum (1656) developed methods of indivisibles into a systematic treatment of infinite series and interpolation; in it he derived the infinite product for π/2 known as the Wallis product. Newton acknowledged Wallis's work as a key stimulus for his own development of calculus. Wallis also wrote on conic sections, algebra and the history of mathematics, and pioneered the teaching of the deaf, publishing an early treatise on speech and phonetics.

Legacy

Wallis was one of the most influential English mathematicians before Newton, and his notation and methods shaped the analytic mathematics of the following century.
