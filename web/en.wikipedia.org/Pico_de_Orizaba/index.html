Pico de Orizaba

Pico de Orizaba, also known by its Nahuatl name Citlaltépetl ("Star Mountain"), is an active stratovolcano, the highest mountain in Mexico and the third highest in North America, with an elevation of 5,636 metres (18,491 ft) above sea level. It lies on the border between the states of Veracruz and Puebla, within the Pico de Orizaba National Park.

Geology

The volcano is the youngest and largest of a chain of volcanoes in the eastern Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt. Its roughly symmetrical cone is capped by a crater about 300 metres wide, and nine glaciers — including the Gran Glaciar Norte, the largest in Mexico — descend its upper slopes, though they have retreated markedly in recent decades. The last major eruptions occurred in the 19th century, most recently in 1846; the peak is currently dormant.

Climbing

Pico de Orizaba is a popular high-altitude mountaineering objective. The standard route ascends the Jamapa Glacier from the Piedra Grande hut at about 4,260 metres and is technically straightforward but physically demanding due to altitude.
