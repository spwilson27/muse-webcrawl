Nebula Award

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nebula Awards annually recognize the best works of science fiction or fantasy published in the United States. They are organized and awarded by the Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association (SFWA), formerly the Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers of America. First given in 1966 for works published in 1965, the awards are presented at the annual SFWA Nebula Conference.

Categories

The principal categories are Best Novel, Novella, Novelette, Short Story, and (since 2018 as a full category) Best Game Writing; the Ray Bradbury Award for Outstanding Dramatic Presentation and the Andre Norton Award for Middle Grade and Young Adult Fiction are presented alongside, though technically distinct. Winners are chosen by vote of SFWA members, who are professional writers in the field.

Significance

Along with the reader-voted Hugo Awards, the Nebulas are among the most prestigious honours in the genre. Notable multiple winners include Ursula K. Le Guin, Isaac Asimov, Arthur C. Clarke, Larry Niven, Connie Willis, and N. K. Jemisin. The first Best Novel winner was Frank Herbert's Dune (1965, tied with Roger Zelazny's ...And Call Me Conrad).

The trophy

The award is a transparent block containing a glittering spiral nebula and gemstones representing planets; the design has remained essentially unchanged since 1966.
