Battle of Neerwinden (1793)

The Battle of Neerwinden was fought on 18 March 1793 during the Flanders campaign of the French Revolutionary Wars. The French army under General Charles François Dumouriez was defeated by the Coalition forces of Prince Josias of Saxe-Coburg-Saalfeld. The defeat ended Dumouriez's invasion of the Dutch Republic, forced the French to evacuate Belgium, and precipitated Dumouriez's defection to the Austrians.

Background

After his victory at Jemappes in November 1792, Dumouriez had occupied Belgium, but in February 1793 he invaded the Dutch Republic, leaving reduced forces to face the Austrian counter-offensive. Coburg's army recaptured Liège and besieged Maastricht; Dumouriez hastily returned south and, short of supplies and with his army demoralised, sought battle to restore the situation before Belgium was lost entirely.

The battle

The French, some 40,000 strong, faced roughly 39,000–43,000 Coalition troops around Neerwinden and the Little Gete river. The French right wing under Valence initially drove Coburg's left back from its villages, but the French centre and left under the young Duke of Chartres (the future King Louis-Philippe) and General Miranda were repulsed with heavy losses. Repeated Austrian cavalry charges, and the superior discipline of Coburg's infantry, gradually broke the French army, which retreated in disorder during the night.

Aftermath

French casualties and desertion reached about 5,000, and the army abandoned Belgium within days. Dumouriez, at odds with the Jacobin government in Paris and fearing the guillotine after his failure, arrested the Convention's commissioners sent to investigate him and defected to the Austrian camp on 5 April 1793—an act of treason that shocked revolutionary France and contributed to the establishment of the Committee of Public Safety.
