Air Commodore Sir Frank Whittle (1907–1996) was an English engineer and Royal Air Force officer credited with inventing the turbojet engine. His company Power Jets ran the first British jet engine, the Whittle W.1, in 1937, and his work led to the Gloster E.28/39, Britain's first jet aircraft, in 1941.

Early career

Whittle joined the RAF as an apprentice and was commissioned in 1928. In a 1928 thesis he argued that aircraft must fly high and fast, beyond piston engines' reach, and in 1930 he patented the turbojet concept. The Air Ministry initially showed little interest, and his patent lapsed in 1935.

Power Jets and the jet age

With private backing he founded Power Jets in 1936; its engine first ran on 12 April 1937. Hans von Ohain in Germany developed a similar engine independently, and his Heinkel He 178 flew in August 1939, before the Gloster E.28/39's first flight on 15 May 1941 with a Whittle engine. Whittle's designs powered the Gloster Meteor, the only Allied jet to see combat in the Second World War. He was knighted in 1948 and later emigrated to the United States, teaching at the US Naval Academy.
