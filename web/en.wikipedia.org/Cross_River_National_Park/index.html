Cross River National Park

Location: Cross River State, Nigeria
Area: about 4,000 km²
Established: 1991

Cross River National Park is a national park of Nigeria, located in Cross River State in the country's southeast, near the border with Cameroon. Established in 1991, it protects some of the last remaining large tracts of lowland tropical rainforest in Nigeria and is recognised as a biodiversity hotspot. The park consists of two separate sections: the Oban Division in the south and the Okwangwo Division in the north.

Lowland rainforest in Cross River National Park.

Wildlife

The park's forests are home to an exceptionally rich fauna, including forest elephants, chimpanzees, drills, leopards, and numerous monkey species. The Okwangwo Division is famous as the primary habitat of the Cross River gorilla (Gorilla gorilla diehli), the rarest of the gorilla subspecies, with only a few hundred individuals surviving in the hills along the Nigeria–Cameroon border. The region also supports an outstanding diversity of butterflies and birds.

Conservation

Threats to the park include illegal logging, poaching, encroachment by farming, and proposed road projects. Conservation work in and around the park is carried out by the Nigeria National Park Service with support from organisations such as the Wildlife Conservation Society, which runs ranger patrols, gorilla monitoring, and community programmes in the surrounding landscape, including the adjacent Afi Mountain Wildlife Sanctuary and Cameroon's Takamanda National Park.
