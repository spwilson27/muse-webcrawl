Sousaphone

ClassificationBrass (aerophone)
Invented1893, by J. W. Pepper for John Philip Sousa
KeyUsually BB♭
A sousaphone in a marching band

The sousaphone is a large brass instrument in the tuba family, designed to be easy to play while marching. Its tubing coils in a circle around the player's body, and its large, detachable bell points forward (or, in the original design, upward). It is named after the American bandmaster John Philip Sousa, who envisioned such an instrument for his concert band.

History

The first sousaphone was built in 1893 by the Philadelphia instrument maker J. W. Pepper at Sousa's request, as a concert instrument with an upward-pointing bell — nicknamed the "rain-catcher". Sousa wanted a bass voice that projected over the band rather than forward from the front, as with the helicon then common in marching bands. The C. G. Conn company later produced the marching form with a forward-facing bell, which became standard. Rain-catcher and marching sousaphones both remain in use; New Orleans brass bands favour the marching form.

Characteristics

Most sousaphones are pitched in BB♭ and have three piston valves; modern school instruments are often made of fibreglass to reduce weight, which can otherwise exceed 20 kg in brass. A distinctive tradition is the decoration of the bell — the University of Oklahoma's and Ohio State University's bands are famous for sousaphone "dotting the i", and many bands paint or plate their bells in school colours.
