Dragonfly (Titan space probe)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dragonfly is a planned NASA mission to send a robotic rotorcraft lander to the surface of Titan, the largest moon of Saturn. Selected in 2019 as the fourth mission in the New Frontiers programme, it will study Titan's prebiotic chemistry and habitability by flying between sampling sites. Launch is planned for no earlier than 2028, with arrival at Titan in the mid-2030s.

Mission

Developed and led by the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, Dragonfly exploits Titan's thick nitrogen atmosphere and low gravity: its eight rotors let the roughly 450-kg craft fly tens of kilometres at a time, covering far more ground than a wheeled rover could. It will land in the Shangri-La dune fields near the equator and eventually fly to the Selk impact crater, where liquid water may once have mixed with surface organics. Between flights it will drill and pneumatically transfer samples to a mass spectrometer, image the surface and sky, and measure seismic and meteorological conditions.

Power and instruments

Because sunlight at Titan is weak and hazy, Dragonfly is powered by a Multi-Mission Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator, which also recharges its batteries for flight. Its instruments include the Dragonfly Mass Spectrometer (DraMS), a gamma-ray and neutron spectrometer (DraGNS), cameras (DragonCam) and geophysics and meteorology sensors. The mission's goals are to characterise Titan's organic chemistry, search for chemical biosignatures, and understand the moon as an analogue of the early Earth.
