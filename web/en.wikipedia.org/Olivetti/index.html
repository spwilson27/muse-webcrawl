Olivetti S.p.A. is an Italian manufacturer of computers, tablets, smartphones, printers and other business products such as calculators and fax machines. Founded in 1908 in Ivrea by Camillo Olivetti, the company became world-famous for its typewriters and for a distinctive design culture that made its products icons of 20th-century industrial design.

History

Camillo Olivetti began production with the M1 typewriter in 1911. Under his son Adriano Olivetti, who led the company from the 1930s until his death in 1960, Olivetti combined advanced manufacturing with progressive social policies — building workers' housing, libraries, and cultural facilities in Ivrea — and commissioned leading architects and designers. Classic products include the Lettera 22 (1950) and Lettera 32 (1963) portable typewriters and the Valentine (1969), designed by Ettore Sottsass.

Olivetti moved into electronics with the Elea 9003 mainframe (1959) and the Programma 101 (1964), often called the first desktop personal computer. In the 1980s it was one of Europe's largest computer makers, but it struggled in the 1990s PC market. After a celebrated hostile takeover of Telecom Italia in 1999, Olivetti was merged into the Telecom Italia group in 2003, surviving as a brand focused on office products, IoT, and digital services.

Design legacy

Olivetti products are held in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York. The industrial archaeological complex of Ivrea, the company town shaped by Adriano Olivetti, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2018.
