Deep Thought (chess computer)

Deep Thought was a computer chess machine built at Carnegie Mellon University in the 1980s. It was the most successful chess computer of its era and the direct ancestor of IBM's Deep Blue, which defeated world champion Garry Kasparov in 1997. The name puns on "deep throat" and the fictional supercomputer Deep Thought in Douglas Adams's The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy.

History

Deep Thought grew out of the ChipTest project of Feng-hsiung Hsu, Thomas Anantharaman, and Murray Campbell. Its design centred on custom VLSI chess chips that combined move generation and position evaluation in hardware, allowing it to search roughly 500,000 positions per second in its later versions. In 1988 Deep Thought tied for first place at the ACM North American Computer Chess Championship and achieved a performance rating around 2550.

Matches against humans

In 1988 Deep Thought became one of the first computers to defeat a grandmaster in a regular tournament game, beating Bent Larsen — the first loss of a grandmaster to a computer under normal tournament conditions. It went on to defeat several strong players, including grandmaster Tony Miles, and in 1989 won the World Computer Chess Championship. In exhibition matches it lost decisively to Kasparov (1989) and to Anatoly Karpov (1990), showing that the best humans remained ahead.

Legacy

Hsu and Campbell moved to IBM, where the Deep Thought architecture was scaled up massively into Deep Blue. Deep Thought is thus a key step in the history of computer chess: it demonstrated that specialised hardware search, combined with a tuned evaluation function, could reach grandmaster strength.
