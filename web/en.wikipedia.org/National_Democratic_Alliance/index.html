The National Democratic Alliance (NDA) is a coalition of political parties in India led by the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP). It was formed in 1998 to contest the general election of that year and first came to power under Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee, whose government (1999–2004) was the first non-Congress coalition to complete a full term.

History

After a decade in opposition following 2004, the NDA returned to power in the 2014 general election under Narendra Modi, winning a sweeping majority in which the BJP alone secured 282 Lok Sabha seats. The alliance won again in 2019 with an increased majority. In the 2024 election the BJP fell short of a solo majority (240 seats), making Modi's third government dependent on allies such as the Telugu Desam Party and Janata Dal (United); the alliance as a whole won about 293 seats.

Composition

Membership has changed repeatedly as regional parties join and leave. Long-standing or notable partners have included the Shiv Sena, Akali Dal (which left in 2020), the JD(U), the TDP, the Lok Janshakti Party and the AIADMK at various times. The NDA is the principal rival of the Congress-led opposition, organised since 2023 as the INDIA alliance.
