Arcangelo Corelli (17 February 1653 – 8 January 1713) was an Italian composer and violinist of the Baroque era. His music was key in the development of the modern genres of sonata and concerto, in establishing the preeminence of the violin, and as the first coalescing of modern tonality and functional harmony.

Life

Corelli was born in Fusignano in the Papal States and studied violin in Bologna, joining its Philharmonic Academy at seventeen. By the late 1670s he was in Rome, where he spent the rest of his career under powerful patrons — notably Queen Christina of Sweden, Cardinal Pamphili, and Cardinal Ottoboni, in whose palace he lived from 1690. He led Rome's finest orchestras, and his disciplined ensemble playing set new standards. He amassed a notable fortune and art collection, and was buried in the Pantheon, an exceptional honour for a musician.

Works and influence

Corelli published only six opus numbers, each repeatedly reprinted across Europe: four sets of twelve trio sonatas (Opp. 1–4, mixing sonate da chiesa and da camera), the twelve violin sonatas Op. 5 (1700) — whose final work is the famous set of variations on "La Folia" — and the twelve concerti grossi Op. 6 (published posthumously, 1714), including the "Christmas Concerto" written for Christmas Eve 1690. His pupils, among them Francesco Geminiani and Pietro Locatelli, spread his style and violin technique across Europe; Handel, who met Corelli in Rome, and generations of later composers studied his works as models of clarity and balance.
