Oil reserves in Saudi Arabia

The oil reserves in Saudi Arabia are among the largest in the world, with proven reserves estimated at roughly 267 billion barrels — approximately 17 percent of the global total — second only to Venezuela. Most reserves lie in the Eastern Province, onshore and offshore in the Persian Gulf, and are concentrated in a relatively small number of very large fields. The reserves are managed by the state-owned company Saudi Aramco.

Major fields

Ghawar, discovered in 1948, is the world's largest conventional oil field and has produced more oil than any other. Other supergiant fields include Safaniya, the world's largest offshore field, Khurais, Shaybah, and Manifa. Saudi Arabia has historically acted as OPEC's swing producer, maintaining spare production capacity that gives it an outsized influence on world oil prices.

History

Oil was first discovered in commercial quantities at Dammam Well No. 7 in 1938 by the Arabian-American Oil Company. Production expanded rapidly after the Second World War, transforming the kingdom's economy and finances. Saudi reserves figures have been politically sensitive; the officially reported total rose sharply in the late 1980s and remained nearly flat for decades even as production continued, prompting periodic debate among analysts.
