RET proto-oncogene

The RET receptor tyrosine kinase

The RET proto-oncogene, located on chromosome 10 (10q11.2), encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase for members of the glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) family of extracellular signalling molecules. The gene's name, RET, derives from "rearranged during transfection", as its activation was first discovered in a rearranged DNA sequence from a human lymphoma cell line.

Function

RET signalling is essential for the development of the enteric nervous system, the kidneys, and spermatogenesis. Signalling requires a co-receptor of the GFRα family together with GDNF-family ligands.

Clinical significance

Activating germline mutations of RET cause multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN 2), comprising medullary thyroid carcinoma, phaeochromocytoma, and hyperparathyroidism; different mutations correlate with the subtypes MEN 2A, MEN 2B, and familial medullary thyroid carcinoma. Conversely, loss-of-function mutations cause Hirschsprung's disease, the congenital absence of enteric ganglia. Chromosomal rearrangements producing RET fusions occur in papillary thyroid carcinoma and non-small-cell lung cancer, and are targeted by selective RET inhibitors such as selpercatinib.
