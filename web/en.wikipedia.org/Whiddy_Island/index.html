Whiddy Island (Irish: Oileán Faoide) is an island near the head of Bantry Bay in County Cork, Ireland, roughly 5.6 km long and 2.4 km wide. Sheltered by the island, Bantry Bay is one of the finest deep-water anchorages in Europe, and Whiddy became the site of a major oil storage terminal.

History

The island was historically a place of fishing and farming and, during the First World War, hosted a United States naval air station whose seaplanes patrolled against U-boats in 1918. In the late 1960s Gulf Oil built a large oil terminal on Whiddy to receive crude oil from supertankers too large for other European ports.

Betelgeuse disaster

On 8 January 1979 the French tanker Betelgeuse exploded while discharging at the terminal jetty, killing 50 people — 42 French crew, seven Irish terminal staff, and one British diver who died later. The disaster, one of the worst in Irish history, led to a tribunal of inquiry and the long-term closure of the terminal as an import facility. The storage tanks were later refurbished, and the site now operates as an oil storage facility managed in connection with Ireland's strategic petroleum reserves.

Today

Whiddy has a small year-round population that grows in summer with visitors arriving by ferry from Bantry. The island has a pub, walking and cycling routes, and views across the bay to the Beara and Sheep's Head peninsulas.
