Isomorphism

In mathematics, an isomorphism is a structure-preserving mapping between two structures that can be reversed by an inverse mapping. Two isomorphic structures are essentially the same in the relevant sense, differing only in the names of their elements.

Examples

In algebra, a group isomorphism is a bijective homomorphism. In graph theory, a graph isomorphism maps vertices preserving adjacency. The logarithm is an isomorphism between positive real numbers under multiplication and reals under addition.

Category theory

In category theory, an isomorphism is a morphism with a two-sided inverse, generalising the idea across structures.

Other senses

The term is also used in chemistry, crystallography, biology and sociology for similar forms.
