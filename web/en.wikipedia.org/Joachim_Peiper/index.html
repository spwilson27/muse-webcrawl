Joachim Peiper

Joachim Peiper (30 January 1915 – 14 July 1976) was a German Waffen-SS officer and convicted war criminal. As an SS-Standartenführer commanding Kampfgruppe Peiper of the 1st SS Panzer Division Leibstandarte SS Adolf Hitler, he led the armoured spearhead of the German offensive in the Battle of the Bulge (December 1944), during which his men murdered American prisoners of war in the Malmedy massacre.


Peiper during the war

Career

Peiper joined the SS in 1933 and served as an adjutant to Heinrich Himmler before taking combat commands. On the Eastern Front he commanded a panzergrenadier battalion, earning a reputation for aggressive leadership and brutality; his unit was involved in atrocities including the burning of villages in Ukraine and the massacre at Boves in Italy in 1943.

Ardennes and the Malmedy massacre

On 17 December 1944, near Malmedy, Belgium, members of Kampfgruppe Peiper murdered 84 American prisoners of war. Peiper's column advanced deep behind American lines toward the Meuse but was stopped at Stoumont and La Gleize, and Peiper escaped on foot with the remnants of his force.

Trial and death

In 1946 an American military court at Dachau sentenced Peiper to death for his command responsibility; the sentence was commuted, and he was released in 1956. He later worked for Porsche and Volkswagen in France. He was killed in 1976 when his house in Traves, France, was firebombed after his identity became public.
