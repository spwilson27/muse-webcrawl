The Old City of Jerusalem (Hebrew: הָעִיר הָעַתִּיקָה; Arabic: البلدة القديمة) is a 0.9-square-kilometre walled area of East Jerusalem, one of the oldest continuously inhabited urban sites in the world and sacred to Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Surrounded by walls built under the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent in the sixteenth century, it is divided into four quarters: Muslim, Christian, Armenian and Jewish.

Holy sites

The Old City contains the Temple Mount (Haram al-Sharif) with the Dome of the Rock (691) and Al-Aqsa Mosque; the Western Wall, Judaism's holiest prayer site; and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, venerated by Christians as the site of Jesus's crucifixion and resurrection, shared among several denominations. The Via Dolorosa, traditional route of the Passion, winds through the Muslim and Christian quarters.

History

Jerusalem's walls have been destroyed and rebuilt many times — by Babylonians, Romans and Crusaders among others. The present walls follow roughly the lines of the Roman and medieval city. The Jewish Quarter was destroyed in 1948 and rebuilt after 1967. The Old City and its walls were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1981 and placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 1982. Its political status remains contested under international law.
