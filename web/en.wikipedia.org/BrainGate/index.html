BrainGate

BrainGate is a brain implant system — a brain–computer interface — developed originally by the company Cyberkinetics and pursued by researchers at Brown University and partner institutions. It uses a small array of about 100 electrodes implanted in the motor cortex to record neural signals associated with intended movement.

In landmark trials beginning in 2004, participants with tetraplegia, including Matthew Nagle, the first person to receive the system, were able to move a computer cursor, operate a television, and control a robotic arm by thought alone. Later studies enabled participants to type, to drink from a bottle using a robotic arm, and to regain forms of communication.

BrainGate is an investigational research system rather than an approved medical device, and its results have strongly influenced the wider field of neuroprosthetics.
