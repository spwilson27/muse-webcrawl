Financial inclusion

Financial inclusion is the availability and equality of opportunities to access financial services — including payments, savings, credit, insurance, and pensions — for all members of a population, particularly those historically underserved by formal finance. It is a major development-policy goal, promoted by the World Bank, the G20, and central banks worldwide.

State of access

According to the World Bank's Global Findex database, the share of adults worldwide with an account at a financial institution or mobile money provider rose from 51% in 2011 to 76% in 2021, driven largely by digital payments and mobile money. Nevertheless, roughly 1.4 billion adults remained unbanked as of 2021, disproportionately women, rural residents, and the poor. Gender gaps in account ownership persist in many developing economies.

Channels and innovations

Key enablers include mobile money (pioneered at scale by M-Pesa in Kenya from 2007), agent banking networks, microfinance institutions, digital ID systems enabling low-cost account opening, and interoperable instant payment systems such as India's UPI, layered on mass account programs like India's Jan Dhan Yojana (2014), which opened hundreds of millions of basic accounts.

Benefits and risks

Research associates account access with greater resilience to shocks, increased savings and investment, women's economic empowerment, and more efficient delivery of government benefits. Risks include over-indebtedness from easy digital credit, fraud, data privacy concerns, and dormant accounts that inflate headline statistics. Policy emphasis has shifted from access alone to "usage and quality" of services, consumer protection, and financial health.
