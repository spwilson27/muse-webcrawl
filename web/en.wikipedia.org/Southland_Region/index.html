Southland Region

Southland (Māori: Murihiku) is New Zealand's southernmost region. It consists mainly of the southwestern portion of the South Island and Stewart Island/Rakiura. The region covers over 3.1 million hectares and includes Fiordland National Park, the largest national park in the country. Its main city and seat is Invercargill, New Zealand's southernmost city.

Geography

The region stretches from the Fiordland fiords in the west — including Milford Sound and Doubtful Sound — across the fertile Southland Plains to the Catlins in the southeast. Major rivers include the Waiau, Aparima, Ōreti and Mataura. The climate is cool and temperate, with rainfall in Fiordland among the highest in the world.

History

Māori settled Murihiku from early in the Polynesian settlement of New Zealand, hunting moa and exploiting seasonal food resources. European sealers and whalers arrived in the early nineteenth century, and the port of Bluff became one of the country's oldest European settlements. Southland was briefly a separate province (1861–1870) before rejoining Otago, and became a region again under the local government reforms of 1989. The region is administered by Environment Southland (the Southland Regional Council).

Economy

The economy is based largely on agriculture — sheep, dairy and deer farming on the plains — along with forestry, fishing, and the Tiwai Point aluminium smelter near Bluff, long one of the region's largest employers. Tourism centred on Fiordland and Stewart Island is also significant. The population is around 100,000, concentrated in Invercargill and the town of Gore.
