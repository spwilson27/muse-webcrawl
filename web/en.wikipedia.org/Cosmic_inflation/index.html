Cosmic inflation

In physical cosmology, cosmic inflation is a theory of exponential expansion of space in the early universe, proposed by Alan Guth in 1980. The inflationary epoch is thought to have lasted from about 10⁻³⁶ to 10⁻³² seconds after the Big Bang.

Motivation

Inflation solves the horizon, flatness and monopole problems of the standard Big Bang model, and explains the origin of large-scale structure: quantum fluctuations stretched to cosmic scales became the seeds of galaxies, imprinted in the cosmic microwave background.

Models and evidence

Most models invoke a scalar field, the inflaton, slowly rolling down its potential. Measurements of the microwave background's near scale-invariant spectrum support inflation, though the theory's testability and multiverse implications (eternal inflation) remain debated.
