Regional rail

Regional rail is a category of passenger rail service that operates within and around a metropolitan region or between smaller cities and rural areas, typically with lower frequencies, longer station spacing and greater distances than urban rapid transit or commuter rail. Definitions vary between countries: in much of Europe regional trains (such as German Regionalbahn and Regional-Express services) form the backbone of local public transport, while in North America the term is often used for services between the suburban commuter belt and outlying regional centres.

Characteristics

Regional rail services usually run at clockface intervals of one or two hours, stop at most stations, use single- or double-deck multiple units or locomotive-hauled coaches, and accept integrated regional ticketing. Speeds and trip lengths are intermediate between stopping local trains and long-distance inter-city services.

Operators

In Germany, the federal states order regional services from operators including DB Regio and private competitors. In the United Kingdom regional franchises and concession operators run services outside London and the South East. In the United States, state-supported Amtrak routes function as regional rail in corridors such as the Pacific Northwest and the Midwest.
