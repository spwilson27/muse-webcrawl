Sąjūdis

Sąjūdis ("Movement"), officially the Reform Movement of Lithuania (Lithuanian: Lietuvos Persitvarkymo Sąjūdis), was a political organisation and mass movement that led the struggle for Lithuania's independence from the Soviet Union in the late 1980s and early 1990s. Established on 3 June 1988, it culminated in the restoration of Lithuanian statehood with the Act of 11 March 1990, making Lithuania the first Soviet republic to declare independence.

Origins

Sąjūdis emerged during Mikhail Gorbachev's glasnost and perestroika, initially presenting itself as a movement supporting reform. Its initiative group — largely intellectuals, academics, and artists gathered at the Lithuanian Academy of Sciences — elected a council (Seimas) chaired by musicologist Vytautas Landsbergis. Environmental campaigns, such as opposition to a third reactor at the Ignalina nuclear plant, helped it mobilise the public.

Road to independence

On 23 August 1989, the 50th anniversary of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, Sąjūdis organised the Baltic Way, a human chain of some two million people spanning 675 km across Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia. In the February 1990 elections to the Lithuanian Supreme Soviet, Sąjūdis-backed candidates won a majority, and on 11 March 1990 the newly elected parliament, led by Landsbergis, adopted the Act of the Re-Establishment of the State of Lithuania.

Aftermath

Soviet economic blockade and the violent crackdown of January 1991 followed, but international recognition came after the failed August 1991 Moscow coup. As normal party politics resumed, Sąjūdis lost cohesion; many of its figures joined new parties, and Landsbergis later co-founded the Homeland Union. Sąjūdis remains a symbol of Lithuania's "Singing Revolution".
