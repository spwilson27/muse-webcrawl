Karakum Desert

The Karakum Desert (Turkmen: Garagum, "black sand") is a large desert in Central Asia covering about 350,000 square kilometres (135,000 sq mi), roughly 70 percent of the territory of Turkmenistan. One of the world's great sand deserts, it lies east of the Caspian Sea, between the Amu Darya river and the Kopet Dag mountains.

Geography

The desert occupies the greater part of the Turan Lowland. Its surface is mostly sand in the central and southern parts, with clay takirs, salt flats and the eroded plateaus of the Zaunguz Karakum in the north. The Amu Darya flows along its northeastern edge, and the Murghab and Tejen rivers disappear into its sands from the south.

Climate

The Karakum has an extreme continental desert climate: summer temperatures regularly exceed 40 °C (104 °F) and can approach 50 °C, while winter temperatures fall below freezing. Rainfall, mostly in late winter and spring, averages only 100–150 mm per year.

Water and the Karakum Canal

The Karakum Canal, one of the largest irrigation canals in the world, carries water from the Amu Darya westward across the southern desert fringe for more than 1,300 km, irrigating cotton-growing oases around Ashgabat and beyond. Completed in stages between 1954 and 1988, it transformed the economy of southern Turkmenistan but contributed to the diversion of Amu Darya flow away from the Aral Sea.

Resources and wildlife

The desert contains significant deposits of natural gas and oil, developed since the Soviet era. Its most famous landmark is the Darvaza gas crater, a burning natural-gas pit in the central Karakum that has been alight since 1971. Despite its aridity, the desert supports saxaul shrubs, sand acacia, jerboas, goitered gazelles and, in spring, a brief bloom of ephemeral plants and tulips.
