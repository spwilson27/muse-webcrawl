Lower Saxony (German: Niedersachsen) is a German state in northwestern Germany. It is the second-largest state by land area, after Bavaria, and the fourth-largest by population. Its capital is Hanover, and other major cities include Brunswick, Oldenburg, Osnabrück, Wolfsburg and Göttingen.

History

The state was created after the Second World War, in 1946, from the merger of the former states of Hanover, Brunswick, Oldenburg and Schaumburg-Lippe within the British occupation zone.

Economy

Lower Saxony is a center of the automotive industry, home to Volkswagen, which is headquartered in Wolfsburg. Agriculture, food processing, shipbuilding along the North Sea coast, and tourism in the Harz mountains and on the East Frisian Islands are also important.

Geography

The state stretches from the North Sea coast and the Wadden Sea in the north to the Harz mountains in the southeast. Major rivers include the Weser, Ems, Aller and Elbe.
