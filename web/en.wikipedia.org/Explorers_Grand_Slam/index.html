Explorers Grand Slam

The slam combines the Seven Summits with both poles.

The Explorers Grand Slam (also called the Adventurers Grand Slam) is an adventurers' challenge consisting of summiting the highest mountain on each of the seven continents (the Seven Summits) and reaching both the North Pole and the South Pole.

Components

The Seven Summits are Everest (Asia), Aconcagua (South America), Denali (North America), Kilimanjaro (Africa), Elbrus (Europe), Vinson Massif (Antarctica), and either Kosciuszko (Australia) or Puncak Jaya/Carstensz Pyramid (Oceania), depending on the list used. The polar legs are full-length journeys—typically from the coast of Antarctica to the South Pole, and from a high-latitude starting point across the Arctic Ocean to the North Pole—though "last degree" journeys are sometimes counted in a separate category.

History

The concept was formalized in the 1990s as climbers who had completed the Seven Summits sought a further goal. David Hempleman-Adams is credited as the first person to complete the full Grand Slam, achieving the polar journeys and summits by 1998. Only a few dozen people have completed the full version with complete polar traverses; many more have completed versions using last-degree polar trips.

Variants

A related feat, the Three Poles Challenge, requires only Everest plus the two poles. Records are tracked by various adventure-statistics bodies, and definitions of what counts—especially for the North Pole, where shifting sea ice complicates verification—remain contested.
