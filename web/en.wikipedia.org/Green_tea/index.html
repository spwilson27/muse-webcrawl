Green tea

Green tea is a type of tea made from the leaves and buds of Camellia sinensis that have not undergone the withering and oxidation process used for oolong and black teas. Originating in China, it spread throughout East and Southeast Asia and is now produced and consumed worldwide.

Production

To prevent oxidation, freshly plucked leaves are heated soon after harvest—by pan-firing in China or steaming in Japan—then rolled and dried. Well-known varieties include Longjing (Dragon Well) and Biluochun from China, and sencha, gyokuro, and matcha from Japan; gunpowder tea and jasmine tea are also widely exported. Matcha is a powdered green tea whisked into hot water and used in the Japanese tea ceremony.

Composition and health

Green tea contains caffeine and catechins, notably epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), as well as the amino acid L-theanine. It has a long history in traditional medicine, and modern research has examined possible effects on metabolism and cardiovascular health, though evidence for most health claims remains modest. Brewing typically uses water below boiling (about 70–85 °C) to avoid bitterness from excessive extraction of tannins.
