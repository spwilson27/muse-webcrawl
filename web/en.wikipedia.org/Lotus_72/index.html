The Lotus 72 is a Formula One car designed by Colin Chapman and Maurice Philippe for the 1970 season. One of the most successful Formula One designs of all time, it won 20 Grands Prix, three Constructors' Championships and two Drivers' Championships across an unusually long six-season career.

Design

The 72 introduced a distinctive wedge-shaped nose, side-mounted radiators in place of a conventional front radiator, torsion-bar suspension and inboard front brakes — features that became standard across the sport. It was driven by Jochen Rindt, who won the 1970 World Championship posthumously, and by Emerson Fittipaldi, champion in 1972, as well as Ronnie Peterson and Jacky Ickx. In its famous black-and-gold John Player Special livery, the 72 became an icon of 1970s motor racing.
