Lady Godiva

Lady Godiva (died between 1066 and 1086), in Old English Godgifu ("gift of God"), was an Anglo-Saxon noblewoman, wife of Leofric, Earl of Mercia, and a generous benefactor of religious houses including Coventry. She is remembered chiefly for the legend that she rode naked through the streets of Coventry to persuade her husband to lift oppressive taxes on the townspeople.

According to the story, recorded by chroniclers from the 13th century, Godiva repeatedly pleaded with Leofric, who said he would relent only if she rode naked at midday. She did so, clothed only in her long hair, after ordering the townspeople to stay indoors; Leofric kept his word. A later addition tells of a tailor, "Peeping Tom", who looked and was struck blind. Historians treat the ride as legend, though Godiva herself is a well-documented historical figure.
