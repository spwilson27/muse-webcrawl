Belaya River

The Belaya (Russian: Бе́лая, "White"; Bashkir: Ағиҙел) is a river in Bashkortostan, Russia. It is a left tributary of the Kama and is 1,430 kilometres (890 mi) long, with a drainage basin of 142,000 square kilometres (55,000 sq mi). It is the largest tributary of the Kama by discharge.

Course

The Belaya rises on the slopes of the Iremel massif in the southern Ural Mountains and flows southwest, then northwest, through the republic of Bashkortostan. It passes the cities of Beloretsk, Sterlitamak and Ufa, the capital of Bashkortostan, before joining the Kama near the border with Tatarstan. Its main tributaries include the Ufa and Dyoma rivers.

Use

The river is an important waterway and water source for the region's petrochemical industry, and its lower course is regulated by reservoirs including the Yumaguzino Reservoir.
