Will and testament

A will or testament is a legal document that expresses a person's (testator) wishes as to how their property (estate) is to be distributed after their death and as to which person (executor) is to manage the property until its final distribution.

Terminology

Historically, "will" applied to real property and "testament" to personal property, but the terms are now used interchangeably. A person who dies without a valid will is said to have died intestate, and their estate is distributed under the rules of intestacy.

Requirements

Requirements vary by jurisdiction, but a valid will typically requires that the testator be of legal age and sound mind, that the will be in writing and signed, and that the signature be witnessed. Some jurisdictions recognise holographic (entirely handwritten) wills and, less commonly, oral or nuncupative wills.

Contents

A will usually appoints an executor, names beneficiaries and specific bequests, may create trusts (for example for minor children), appoints guardians, and revokes earlier wills. A codicil is a later document that amends an existing will.

Probate

After death, the will is submitted to a probate court, which validates it and supervises administration of the estate. Wills may be contested on grounds such as lack of capacity, undue influence or improper execution.
