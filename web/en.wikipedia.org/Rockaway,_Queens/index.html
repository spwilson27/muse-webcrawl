Rockaway, Queens

The Rockaways are a peninsula and neighbourhood in the borough of Queens in New York City, extending along the Atlantic Ocean on the south shore of Long Island. The area, named for a Lenape word often rendered as "sandy place", is bounded by Jamaica Bay to the north and the Atlantic to the south.

History

Once part of the town of Hempstead, the Rockaways were annexed to New York City in 1898. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries the peninsula became a popular seaside resort for New Yorkers, with amusement parks, hotels and bungalow colonies; the resort era declined after the Second World War as air travel and air conditioning changed holidays. The neighbourhoods include Far Rockaway, Arverne, Rockaway Beach, Belle Harbor and Breezy Point at the western tip.

Modern era

The Rockaways were severely damaged by Hurricane Sandy in 2012 and underwent extensive rebuilding of the boardwalk and beach defences. The area is served by the A train and the Rockaway Park Shuttle, and NYC Ferry began service in 2017; its surf breaks at Rockaway Beach are the city's main surfing destination.
