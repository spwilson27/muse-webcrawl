Pagoda

TypeTiered tower
ReligionsBuddhism, Taoism, Shinto
RegionsEast, South and Southeast Asia
Derived fromStupa

A pagoda is a tiered tower with multiple eaves, common in Asia and originating in ancient India. Pagodas are typically built for religious purposes, most often as part of Buddhist temple complexes, though they also appear in Taoist and Shinto contexts. The form evolved from the Indian stupa, combined with the multi-storey watchtowers of Han-dynasty China.

History

The pagoda developed in China by the first centuries CE, merging the stupa's role as a reliquary with indigenous tower architecture. It spread to Korea, Japan, Vietnam, and other parts of Asia along with Buddhism. The earliest Chinese pagodas were built of wood, later largely replaced by brick and stone for durability. Japanese five-storied pagodas incorporated a central pillar (shinbashira) that helped them withstand earthquakes.

Symbolism

Pagodas typically have an odd number of storeys, and their proportions often reduce gradually toward the top, symbolising the ascent toward heaven. They house sacred relics or scriptures and serve as landmarks for temples and towns.
