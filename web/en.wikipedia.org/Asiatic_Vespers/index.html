Asiatic Vespers

The Asiatic Vespers (also known as the Asiatic Eve or the Ephesian Vespers) was a carefully planned massacre of Romans and Latin-speaking peoples living in the cities of the Roman province of Asia and other parts of Anatolia in 88 BC. Carried out on the orders of Mithridates VI of Pontus at the opening of the First Mithridatic War, it involved the killing of some 80,000 people according to ancient sources.

Background

Resentment against Roman tax farmers and moneylenders in Asia, whose exactions under the publicani were notorious, made the province ripe for revolt. Mithridates, having overrun the province, sent secret orders to the satraps and city magistrates that on an appointed day all Romans and Italians — men, women, children and freedmen — were to be killed, their property confiscated, and anyone sheltering them punished.

The massacre

Killings took place across the province, including at Ephesus, Pergamon, Adramyttium, Caunus and Tralles. Many victims were dragged from sanctuaries where they had taken refuge. The scale of the massacre shocked Rome and hardened its determination to prosecute the war.

Consequences

The Vespers began the First Mithridatic War (89–85 BC), in which Sulla campaigned in Greece and Asia. The massacre remains one of the most notorious episodes of mass violence in the ancient world, and its name echoes the later Sicilian Vespers of 1282.
