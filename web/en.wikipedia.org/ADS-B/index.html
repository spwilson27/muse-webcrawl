ADS-B

Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast (ADS-B) is a surveillance technology in which an aircraft periodically broadcasts its identity, position, altitude and velocity, determined from satellite navigation (GNSS), so that air traffic control and other aircraft can track it. It is "dependent" because it relies on the aircraft's own navigation systems, and "automatic" because no pilot input is required.

Operation

ADS-B Out transmissions are broadcast on 1090 MHz (Mode S Extended Squitter) or, in the United States for aircraft below 18,000 feet, on 978 MHz (UAT). ADS-B In allows equipped aircraft to receive traffic (TIS-B) and weather (FIS-B) information. Ground stations relay the data into air traffic management systems, giving controllers radar-like coverage without radar — particularly valuable in mountainous, remote and oceanic regions.

Mandates and uses

The FAA required ADS-B Out in most controlled US airspace from 1 January 2020, and EASA imposed similar requirements in Europe. Space-based ADS-B, hosted on the Iridium NEXT satellite constellation by Aireon, extended surveillance to the entire globe from 2019. Receiver networks run by enthusiasts also feed public flight-tracking websites, making most commercial flights trackable in near real time.
