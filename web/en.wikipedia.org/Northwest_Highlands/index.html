The Northwest Highlands are a mountainous region of northern Scotland, lying northwest of the Great Glen Fault. The region includes the districts of Wester Ross, Sutherland, and parts of Caithness and Lochaber, and contains some of the oldest rocks in Europe — the Lewisian gneiss, up to three billion years old.

Geology

The landscape is divided by the Moine Thrust Belt, a major fault zone where ancient Moine schists were pushed westward over younger rocks during the Caledonian orogeny; the area's significance was established by the 19th-century "Highlands Controversy" and the work of Peach and Horne. West of the thrust lie isolated, steep-sided mountains of Torridonian sandstone — such as Suilven, Stac Pollaidh, and Liathach — rising from a glaciated "knock and lochan" terrain of bare gneiss. The region forms part of the North West Highlands Geopark, recognised by UNESCO.

Geography and settlement

The Northwest Highlands are among the most sparsely populated parts of Europe. The indented Atlantic coastline has numerous sea lochs, and settlements such as Ullapool, Lochinver, and Durness are small and widely scattered. The area suffered heavily during the Highland Clearances of the 18th and 19th centuries, when crofting tenants were removed for sheep farming. The North Coast 500 touring route now brings significant tourism.

Climate

The climate is oceanic and wet, strongly influenced by the Atlantic; low-lying western areas are mild for their latitude but among the wettest in Britain, while the mountains experience severe winter conditions.
