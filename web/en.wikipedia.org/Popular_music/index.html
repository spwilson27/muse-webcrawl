Popular music

Popular music is music with wide appeal that is typically distributed to large audiences through the music industry. These forms and styles can be enjoyed and performed by people with little or no musical training. It stands in contrast to both art music (such as Western classical music) and traditional or folk music transmitted orally within communities. The term originated in the 1880s in the United States, and "popular music" should not be confused with the narrower genre label pop music, though the two overlap.

Stylistic originsVaried; commercial song publishing, Tin Pan Alley
Cultural originsLate 19th century, United States and United Kingdom
Major formsPop, rock, hip hop, R&B, country, electronic dance music, jazz (early)

Characteristics

Musicologist Philip Tagg describes popular music as produced in industrial societies with commercial aims, designed for mass audiences, stored and distributed in non-written forms, and characterised by short-to-medium length songs in verse–chorus structures, prominent melody and hooks, and studio-produced "sound" as an expressive element. Scholars such as Richard Middleton emphasise that definitions are contested, since categories of popular, folk, and art music overlap and shift over time.

History

Commercial popular music grew with the sheet-music industry of Tin Pan Alley in the late 19th century, then was transformed by recording, radio, and film in the 20th century. Jazz and blues fed into swing, rhythm and blues, country, and in the 1950s rock and roll, which became the dominant idiom for decades, fragmenting into rock, soul, funk, disco, punk, metal, and many other styles. From the 1980s onward, hip hop rose from the Bronx to become, by the late 2010s, the most-consumed genre in the United States. Global industries such as K-pop and reggaetón, and the shift to streaming, have reshaped how popular music is produced, marketed, and measured.

Industry and criticism

The recording industry, charts such as the Billboard Hot 100, and awards such as the Grammys institutionalise popular music. Critics from Theodor Adorno onward have debated whether mass-produced music standardises taste or expresses genuine creativity and identity; academic study of popular music is now an established field with its own journals and associations (IASPM).
