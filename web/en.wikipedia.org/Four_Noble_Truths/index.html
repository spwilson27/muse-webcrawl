Four Noble Truths

Four Noble Truths
[Image: the Buddha delivering the first sermon at Sarnath]
Pali: cattāri ariyasaccāni
Sanskrit: catvāri āryasatyāni
Source: Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta
Tradition: Buddhism

The Four Noble Truths are "the truths of the Noble Ones", the truths or realities which are understood by the spiritually worthy. They are the central teaching of Buddhism, traditionally said to have been delivered by the Buddha in his first sermon, the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta ("Setting in Motion the Wheel of Dhamma"), at Sarnath after his enlightenment. The truths diagnose the human condition and prescribe its cure.

The four truths

  1. Duḥkha — there is suffering / unsatisfactoriness: life involves pain, ageing, sickness, death, and the impermanence of all pleasant states.
  2. Samudaya — suffering arises from craving (taṇhā): attachment to pleasures, to existence, and to non-existence drives the cycle of rebirth.
  3. Nirodha — suffering can cease: the complete fading away of craving brings liberation (nirvana).
  4. Magga — there is a path to the cessation of suffering: the Noble Eightfold Path.

The Noble Eightfold Path

The fourth truth sets out the Noble Eightfold Path: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration. These are grouped into the three trainings of wisdom, ethical conduct, and mental discipline.

Interpretations

The truths are often compared to a physician's method: identifying the illness, its cause, whether a cure exists, and the treatment. Different Buddhist schools vary in emphasis — the Theravada tradition treats full comprehension of the truths as the essence of enlightenment, while Mahayana traditions place them within a broader framework of emptiness and the bodhisattva path.
