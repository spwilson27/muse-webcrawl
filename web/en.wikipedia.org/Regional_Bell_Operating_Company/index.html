Regional Bell Operating Company

The Regional Bell Operating Companies (RBOCs) were the seven regional holding companies created by the 1982 consent decree that broke up the American Telephone and Telegraph Company's Bell System monopoly, effective 1 January 1984. Often called the "Baby Bells," they inherited AT&T's local exchange operations while AT&T retained long-distance service, manufacturing and Bell Laboratories.

The seven RBOCs

  • Ameritech — serving the Great Lakes and Midwestern states
  • Bell Atlantic — serving the Mid-Atlantic states
  • BellSouth — serving the Southeast
  • NYNEX — serving New York and New England
  • Pacific Telesis — serving California and Nevada
  • Southwestern Bell — serving the South Central states
  • US West — serving the Mountain and Pacific Northwest states

Consolidation

A wave of mergers after the Telecommunications Act of 1996 largely reassembled the Bell System. Southwestern Bell, renamed SBC Communications, acquired Pacific Telesis, Ameritech, and ultimately AT&T itself in 2005, adopting the AT&T name; it later absorbed BellSouth. Bell Atlantic merged with NYNEX and then GTE to form Verizon. US West was acquired by Qwest and then CenturyLink (now Lumen). By the 2010s the seven RBOCs had effectively consolidated into three incumbent local exchange carrier groups.
