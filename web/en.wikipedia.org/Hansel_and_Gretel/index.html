Hansel and Gretel is a German fairy tale collected by the Brothers Grimm and published in 1812 in Grimm's Fairy Tales. It tells of a brother and sister abandoned in a forest who fall into the hands of a cannibalistic witch living in a house made of gingerbread and sweets.

Plot

Hansel and Gretel are the children of a poor woodcutter. During a famine, their stepmother persuades their father to abandon them in the forest. Hansel first foils the plan by dropping pebbles to mark the way home, but on the second occasion he can only leave a trail of breadcrumbs, which birds eat. Lost, the children discover a house made of bread and cake, inhabited by a witch who fattens Hansel for the oven. Gretel tricks the witch, pushes her into her own oven, and the children escape with the witch's treasure and return home.

Origins and interpretation

The tale may preserve memories of the Great Famine of 1315–1317 and of medieval practices of child abandonment. Later editions of the Grimms' collection softened some elements, converting the children's mother into a stepmother.

Adaptations

The story inspired Engelbert Humperdinck's opera Hänsel und Gretel (1893), numerous films, ballets, and retellings, and remains one of the best-known fairy tales worldwide.
