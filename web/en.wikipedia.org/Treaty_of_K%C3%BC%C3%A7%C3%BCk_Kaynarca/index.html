Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca

Signed21 July 1774
LocationKüçük Kaynarca, Ottoman Empire (now Kaynardzha, Bulgaria)
PartiesRussian Empire; Ottoman Empire

The Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca (Turkish: Küçük Kaynarca Antlaşması) was signed on 21 July 1774 and ended the Russo-Turkish War of 1768–1774. It was one of the most humiliating treaties imposed on the Ottoman Empire and greatly expanded Russian influence in the Black Sea region.

Terms

The Ottoman Empire recognised the independence of the Crimean Khanate (which Russia annexed in 1783), ceded territory between the Dnieper and Southern Bug rivers, and granted Russia the ports of Kerch, Yenikale, and Kinburn. Russian merchant ships gained free passage through the Dardanelles, and Russia received a war indemnity. The treaty also gave Russia ambiguous rights to make representations on behalf of Orthodox Christians in the Ottoman Empire, a clause later used to justify Russian intervention, including in the events leading to the Crimean War.

Significance

The treaty marked the beginning of the Eastern Question — the long diplomatic struggle over the declining Ottoman Empire — and established Russia as a Black Sea naval power.
