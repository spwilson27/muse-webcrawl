Altyndepe

Altyndepe (Turkmen for "Golden Hill") is a Bronze Age archaeological site in southern Turkmenistan, near the foothills of the Kopet Dag mountains. It was one of the largest urban centres of the Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC), also known as the Oxus civilization, flourishing roughly between 3200 and 2400 BCE.

Excavations

The site was excavated by Soviet archaeologists, notably Vadim Masson, beginning in the 1960s and 1970s. The mound covers about 25 hectares and rises some 22 metres above the surrounding plain. Excavations revealed a monumental mud-brick complex including what excavators interpreted as a ziggurat-like stepped tower, elite residential quarters, craft workshops and rich burials.

Significance

Altyndepe shows evidence of early urbanism comparable to contemporary sites in Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley: planned streets, specialized pottery and metal production, seals, and long-distance trade in lapis lazuli, turquoise and ivory. Together with sites such as Namazga and Gonur Depe, it demonstrates that a complex, stratified society developed in Central Asia during the Bronze Age, interacting with Elam, Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley civilisation.
