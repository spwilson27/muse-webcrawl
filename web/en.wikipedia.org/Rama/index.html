Rama (Sanskrit: राम, romanized: Rāma) is a major deity in Hinduism, revered as the seventh avatar of the god Vishnu and as the hero of the ancient Sanskrit epic Ramayana. Born as the eldest son of King Dasharatha of Ayodhya in the kingdom of Kosala, he is celebrated as Maryada Purushottama — the "perfect man," an embodiment of righteousness (dharma), virtue and selfless duty.

Narrative

The Ramayana, traditionally attributed to the sage Valmiki, recounts Rama's life: his marriage to Sita, princess of Mithila; his fourteen-year exile in the forest, accepted to honour his father's promise to the queen Kaikeyi; Sita's abduction by Ravana, the demon king of Lanka; and the great war in which, aided by his brother Lakshmana, the devotee Hanuman and an army of vanaras, Rama slays Ravana and rescues Sita. His victorious return to Ayodhya and coronation are celebrated in the festival of Diwali, while his birth is marked by Ram Navami.

Worship and influence

Rama is a central figure of Vaishnavism and the bhakti movement; poets such as Tulsidas, whose Ramcharitmanas retold the epic in Awadhi, spread his devotion across northern India. Versions of his story permeate South and Southeast Asian culture, from the Thai Ramkien to Javanese and Khmer dance-drama. A grand temple at Ayodhya, his legendary birthplace, was consecrated in 2024. Rama's ideals of kingship and conduct — the just rule called Rama rajya — remain a touchstone in Indian political and moral discourse.
