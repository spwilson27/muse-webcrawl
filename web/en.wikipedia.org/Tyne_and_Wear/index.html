Tyne and Wear is a metropolitan and ceremonial county in North East England. It borders Northumberland to the north and County Durham to the south, and the North Sea to the east. The county was created in 1974 from parts of Northumberland and County Durham, and takes its name from the two rivers that run through it: the Tyne and the Wear.

Metropolitan boroughs

The county comprises five metropolitan boroughs: Newcastle upon Tyne, Gateshead, North Tyneside, South Tyneside, and Sunderland. Newcastle upon Tyne is the largest city and the county's traditional centre. The area is served by the Tyne and Wear Metro, one of the oldest light rail systems in the United Kingdom outside London.

History and economy

Tyne and Wear was historically a centre of coal mining, shipbuilding, and heavy engineering; the rivers Tyne and Wear launched a large share of the world's ships in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Though those industries declined in the later 20th century, the county has developed service, automotive (including the Nissan plant at Sunderland), and cultural sectors.
