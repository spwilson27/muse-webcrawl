Agro-terrorism

Agro-terrorism (also spelled agroterrorism) is the deliberate introduction of a disease agent, pest, or contaminant into an agricultural system — crops, livestock, or the food supply — in order to cause economic damage, social disruption, or fear. It is considered a form of bioterrorism directed at a nation's food production rather than directly at its population.

Rationale and targets

Agriculture is a large, geographically dispersed sector that is difficult to police. An outbreak of a contagious livestock disease such as foot-and-mouth disease can force the slaughter of millions of animals, trigger export bans, and cost billions of dollars, even with few or no human casualties. Crop pathogens such as wheat stem rust are also cited as potential agents.

History

State programs during the twentieth century researched anti-crop and anti-livestock weapons, though confirmed terrorist attacks on agriculture have been rare. The 1984 Rajneeshee salmonella contamination in Oregon targeted the food supply directly rather than farms. During the Second World War, several belligerents developed biological agents against crops and herds but did not deploy them at scale.

Preparedness

Governments maintain surveillance of animal and plant diseases, stockpile vaccines, and plan culling and quarantine responses. In the United States, agricultural defense involves the Department of Agriculture and the Department of Homeland Security; internationally, the World Organisation for Animal Health coordinates reporting of major livestock diseases.
