Muskellunge

The muskellunge (Esox masquinongy), often shortened to muskie or musky, is a species of large freshwater predatory fish native to North America. It is the largest member of the pike family, Esocidae, and a prized game fish famed for its elusiveness — it is nicknamed "the fish of ten thousand casts."

Muskellunge inhabit clear lakes and large rivers of the Great Lakes region, the St. Lawrence River, and the upper Mississippi drainage. They have an elongated, torpedo-shaped body, a duckbill-shaped snout and sharp teeth. Colour pattern varies: fish may be clear (uniform silvery-green), barred, or spotted. Adults commonly reach 70–120 cm, and the largest specimens exceed 30 kg.

Behaviour and angling

Muskellunge are solitary ambush predators that lie near weed beds and woody cover, striking at fish, waterfowl and even small mammals. They spawn in spring in shallow, vegetated water. A hybrid with the northern pike, the tiger muskellunge, is widely stocked. Because muskellunge grow slowly and reproduce late, fisheries management emphasizes catch-and-release and high minimum size limits.
