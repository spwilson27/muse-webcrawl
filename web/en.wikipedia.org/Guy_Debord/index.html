Guy Ernest Debord (28 December 1931 – 30 November 1994) was a French Marxist theorist, philosopher, filmmaker, critic of consumer capitalism, and a founding member of the Situationist International. He is best known as the author of The Society of the Spectacle (1967), a critique of the domination of social life by images and commodities in advanced capitalism.

Situationist International

After involvement with the Letterist movement of Isidore Isou, Debord co-founded the Situationist International in 1957. The group developed concepts such as the dérive (drifting through urban environments), détournement (the subversive reuse of existing cultural material), and psychogeography, and sought the revolutionary transformation of everyday life. The Situationists influenced the May 1968 uprising in France, whose slogans echoed Debord's writings.

The Society of the Spectacle

Published in 1967 as 221 numbered theses, The Society of the Spectacle argues that in modern capitalist societies authentic social life has been replaced by its representation — "the spectacle" — reducing people to passive consumers. Debord returned to the theme in Comments on the Society of the Spectacle (1988). He also made several experimental films, including Hurlements en faveur de Sade (1952).

Later life

The Situationist International dissolved in 1972. Debord spent his final years largely in seclusion in France and Italy, publishing sparingly, and took his own life in 1994. In 2009 the French government declared his archives a national treasure.
