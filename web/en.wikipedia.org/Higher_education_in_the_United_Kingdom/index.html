Higher education in the United Kingdom

Higher education in the United Kingdom is provided by more than 160 universities and other institutions with degree-awarding powers, including the ancient universities of Oxford and Cambridge and the large civic universities founded in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Education is a devolved matter, so funding and tuition arrangements differ between England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Structure

Undergraduate study typically lasts three years in England, Wales and Northern Ireland and four years in Scotland, leading to bachelor's degrees classified as first class, upper second, lower second or third class honours. Postgraduate provision includes taught master's degrees, usually one year, and research doctorates. Applications for most undergraduate courses are handled centrally by UCAS.

Funding and policy

Following the Robbins Report of 1963 the sector expanded greatly, and the Further and Higher Education Act 1992 ended the binary divide by granting university status to polytechnics. Tuition fees for domestic students were introduced in 1998 and raised substantially in England in 2012; Scottish students studying in Scotland pay no tuition. Quality and standards are overseen by bodies such as the Quality Assurance Agency and, in England, the Office for Students.
