Spawn (biology)

FieldBiology, ichthyology
DefinitionEggs and sperm released into water for fertilisation
RelatedRoe, fry, spawning bed

Spawn is the eggs and sperm released or deposited into water by aquatic animals. As a verb, to spawn refers to the process of releasing the eggs and sperm, and the act is called spawning. Most aquatic animals, except for aquatic mammals and reptiles, reproduce through the process of spawning.

Process

Spawn consists of the reproductive cells of many aquatic animals, some of which will become fertilised and produce offspring. The process typically involves females releasing ova (unfertilised eggs) into the water, often in large quantities, while males simultaneously or sequentially release spermatozoa (milt) to fertilise them.

Examples

Salmon migrate upstream to gravel spawning beds called redds, while many corals engage in mass broadcast spawning synchronised with lunar cycles. Fungi also produce structures called spawn, a term borrowed for the mycelial material used in mushroom cultivation.
