Jura wine

Region: Jura département, eastern France
Appellations: Arbois; Château-Chalon; Côtes du Jura; L'Étoile; Crémant du Jura; Macvin du Jura
Grape varieties: Chardonnay, Savagnin; Poulsard, Trousseau, Pinot noir
Signature wines: Vin jaune; vin de paille

Jura wine is produced in the French wine region of the Jura département, in the foothills of the Jura mountains between Burgundy and Switzerland. One of France's smallest and most distinctive wine regions, it is renowned for idiosyncratic traditional styles — above all the oxidative vin jaune — and for five grape varieties, three of them found almost nowhere else.

Grape varieties and styles

The region grows two white varieties, Chardonnay and the local Savagnin, and three reds: Poulsard (light and delicate), Trousseau, and Pinot noir. Wines range from fresh, Burgundian-style Chardonnays to the region's singular specialties. Vin jaune ("yellow wine") is made from Savagnin aged in barrel for six years and three months under a veil of yeast (voile), like fino sherry but unfortified; bottled in the 62-centilitre clavelin, the finest comes from Château-Chalon. Vin de paille ("straw wine") is a sweet wine from grapes dried for months before pressing, and Macvin du Jura is a fortified mistelle of grape juice and local marc brandy. A sparkling wine, Crémant du Jura, is also produced.

History

Vines have been grown in the Jura since Roman times; Arbois was among France's first appellations, granted AOC status in 1936. The scientist Louis Pasteur, a native of Arbois, conducted some of his foundational studies on fermentation and wine spoilage in his vineyard there. The region's small scale — some 2,000 hectares — and the fashion for its oxidative and "natural" styles have brought it growing international attention in the 21st century, pairing classically with the local Comté cheese and Bresse chicken.
