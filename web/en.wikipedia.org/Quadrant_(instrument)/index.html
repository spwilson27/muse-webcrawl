Quadrant (instrument)

A mariner's quadrant

A quadrant is an instrument used to measure angles up to 90°. Named from its shape — a quarter of a circle — it typically consists of a graduated arc of 90°, sighting vanes or a telescope, and a plumb line whose intersection with the scale gives the altitude of a celestial body or the angle of elevation of a target.

History and types

Quadrants were used in astronomy from antiquity: Ptolemy described large fixed mural quadrants for measuring star positions, and Islamic astronomers built monumental examples. Hand-held quadrants served navigators from the late Middle Ages: the mariner's quadrant, a simple quarter-circle with two sights and a plumb bob, measured the altitude of the sun or of Polaris to estimate latitude, but was hard to use on a rolling ship and was largely supplanted by the cross-staff, backstaff, and ultimately the octant (itself a quadrant's cousin, with an arc of 45°) and the sextant. Gunners and surveyors also used quadrants to set the elevation of artillery and to measure heights and distances.

Related instruments

The family of altitude-measuring instruments includes the astrolabe (from which the quadrant partly derives, the "quadrant" sometimes being one component of it), the octant, the sextant, and the quintant, all distinguished by the fraction of a circle spanned by their arcs.
