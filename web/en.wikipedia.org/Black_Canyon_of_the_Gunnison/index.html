Black Canyon of the Gunnison

The Black Canyon of the Gunnison is a deep, steep-walled gorge carved by the Gunnison River in western Colorado, United States. The canyon is the centrepiece of Black Canyon of the Gunnison National Park.

Geology

The river has cut through hard Precambrian gneiss and schist, producing walls up to 2,722 feet (830 m) deep that are in places nearly vertical. Some parts of the canyon receive only about half an hour of direct sunlight per day, giving rise to its name. The Painted Wall, streaked with pink pegmatite dikes, is the tallest cliff in Colorado.

History

The area was designated a national monument in 1933 and redesignated a national park in 1999.
