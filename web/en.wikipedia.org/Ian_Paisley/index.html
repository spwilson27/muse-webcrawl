Ian Paisley

Ian Richard Kyle Paisley, Baron Bannside (6 April 1926 – 12 September 2014) was a Northern Irish loyalist politician and Protestant religious leader. He founded the Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) in 1971 and led it for 37 years, becoming one of the most recognisable and controversial figures of the Troubles.

Early life and ministry

Paisley was born in Armagh and raised in Ballymena, County Antrim. Ordained in 1946, he co-founded the Free Presbyterian Church of Ulster in 1951 and served as its Moderator for many years. His fiery preaching and anti-Catholic, anti-ecumenical rhetoric made him both a devoted following and a divisive national figure.

Political career

Elected to Westminster as MP for North Antrim in 1970 — a seat he held until 2010 — Paisley campaigned vehemently against civil rights concessions and against the Sunningdale Agreement (1973) and Anglo-Irish Agreement (1985), famously declaring "Never! Never! Never!" to proposals for Irish government involvement. After the 1998 Good Friday Agreement he opposed it, yet the DUP became Northern Ireland's largest unionist party under his leadership. Following the 2006 St Andrews Agreement, Paisley made the historic decision to share power with Sinn Féin, and in May 2007 he was sworn in as First Minister of Northern Ireland alongside former IRA commander Martin McGuinness as deputy First Minister — the two becoming known, for their unexpectedly cordial working relationship, as the "Chuckle Brothers". He stepped down as First Minister and DUP leader in 2008 and was created a life peer as Baron Bannside in 2010.
