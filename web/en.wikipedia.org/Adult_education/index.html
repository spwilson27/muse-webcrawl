Adult education

Adult education, distinct from child education, is a practice in which adults engage in systematic and sustained self-educating activities in order to gain new forms of knowledge, skills, attitudes, or values. It can mean any form of learning adults engage in beyond traditional schooling, encompassing basic literacy to personal fulfillment as a lifelong learner.

Purposes

Adult education may be undertaken for vocational reasons, such as acquiring job skills or meeting licensing requirements; for personal development; for civic participation; or to complete formal qualifications such as a secondary school diploma or university degree.

Forms

Provision includes community colleges, universities' continuing education divisions, folk high schools, workplace training, evening classes, and online courses. Distance learning and massive open online courses (MOOCs) have expanded access in the 21st century.

Theory

The study of adult learning is often called andragogy, a term developed by Malcolm Knowles, who argued that adult learners are self-directed, bring experience as a learning resource, and are motivated by learning relevant to their social roles.
