Platyrrhini

Platyrrhini is a parvorder of primates comprising the New World monkeys — the monkeys native to Central and South America. The name, meaning "flat-nosed", refers to their broad noses with nostrils that open to the sides, contrasting with the narrow, downward-facing nostrils of the Old World monkeys and apes (Catarrhini).

Characteristics

New World monkeys are almost exclusively arboreal and span a wide size range, from the pygmy marmoset, the world's smallest monkey at about 100 grams, to woolly monkeys and muriquis approaching 10 kilograms. Many atelids possess fully prehensile tails that function as a fifth limb; no Old World primate has this adaptation. Nearly all are diurnal and live in social groups, though the owl monkeys are nocturnal.

Classification

Platyrrhini is usually divided into five families: Callitrichidae (marmosets and tamarins), Cebidae (capuchins and squirrel monkeys), Aotidae (night monkeys), Pitheciidae (titis, sakis, and uakaris), and Atelidae (howler, spider, woolly, and woolly spider monkeys).

Evolution

Fossil and molecular evidence indicates that platyrrhines descend from African primates that crossed the Atlantic Ocean — then narrower than today — probably by rafting on floating vegetation, roughly 40 million years ago. The oldest known New World monkey fossil, Perupithecus from Peru, dates to about 36 million years ago.
