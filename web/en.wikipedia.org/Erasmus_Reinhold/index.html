Erasmus Reinhold (22 October 1511 – 19 February 1553) was a German astronomer and mathematician, regarded as the most influential astronomical pedagogue of his generation. A professor at the University of Wittenberg, he was a leading member of the circle around Philipp Melanchthon that promoted the study of Copernican astronomy.

Work

Reinhold computed the Prutenic Tables (Tabulae Prutenicae, 1551), planetary tables based on the models of Nicolaus Copernicus's De revolutionibus and financed by Duke Albert of Prussia, after whom they were named. Although Reinhold valued Copernicus's mathematics, he stopped short of endorsing the physical reality of heliocentrism, treating it as a superior calculating device. The tables were more accurate than the older Alfonsine Tables and were used in the preparation of the Gregorian calendar reform of 1582.

He also catalogued over a thousand stars and wrote commentaries on Ptolemy and on Peurbach's Theoricae novae planetarum.
