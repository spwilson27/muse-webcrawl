Barnes Foundation

The Barnes Foundation is an American educational art institution and museum with one of the world's finest collections of post-impressionist and early modern paintings. It was founded in 1922 by Albert C. Barnes (1872–1951), a chemist who made his fortune developing the antiseptic Argyrol. Since May 2012 the main collection has been displayed in a building on Benjamin Franklin Parkway in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania; the original gallery in Merion, Pennsylvania, now houses archives and the horticulture programme.

Collection

The collection includes 181 works by Pierre-Auguste Renoir (the largest single collection of his work), 69 by Paul Cézanne, 59 by Henri Matisse, 46 by Pablo Picasso, 21 by Chaïm Soutine, 18 by Henri Rousseau, 16 by Amedeo Modigliani, and works by Van Gogh, Degas, Seurat, Monet, African art, and American decorative and folk art. Matisse's The Dance mural was commissioned for the Merion building. Paintings are displayed in dense, symmetrical "ensembles" with metalwork and furniture, exactly as Barnes arranged them — an idiosyncratic hang preserved in the Philadelphia building.

History and the move

Barnes's indenture required the collection to remain in Merion, unaltered and used for education. Decades of litigation over finances, access and the foundation's future led a court in 2004 to permit relocation to Philadelphia, a decision dramatised in the 2009 documentary The Art of the Steal. The new building, designed by Tod Williams and Billie Tsien, opened on 19 May 2012.
