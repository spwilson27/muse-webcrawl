Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease

Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease (CJD) is a rare, invariably fatal neurodegenerative disorder caused by prions — misfolded proteins that induce abnormal folding in normal proteins. It is the most common human prion disease, occurring in about one to two cases per million people per year worldwide.

Forms

Most cases (about 85 percent) are sporadic, arising without known cause. Smaller proportions are hereditary, caused by mutations in the PRNP gene, or iatrogenic, transmitted through medical procedures. Variant CJD (vCJD), identified in the 1990s, is linked to bovine spongiform encephalopathy ("mad cow disease") from contaminated beef.

Symptoms and course

Symptoms include rapidly progressive dementia, memory loss, personality changes, and movement problems such as myoclonus and ataxia. The sporadic form typically affects people around age 60 and progresses to death within months. There is no effective treatment or cure.
