Ulaanbaatar

Ulaanbaatar, previously spelled Ulan Bator, is the capital and largest city of Mongolia, home to roughly half of the country's population of about 3.3 million. Located in north-central Mongolia in the valley of the Tuul River, at an elevation of about 1,350 meters (4,430 ft), it is the coldest national capital in the world on average, with a severe continental climate and long, bitter winters.

History

The city began in 1639 as Örgöö (Urga), a mobile monastery town that moved dozens of times before settling permanently at its present site in 1778. As Urga it became Mongolia's religious center, the seat of the Jebtsundamba Khutuktu, and a hub of trade between Russia and China on the Tea Road. After Mongolia's independence from Qing China in 1911 and the establishment of the Mongolian People's Republic in 1924, the city was renamed Ulaanbaatar — "Red Hero" — in honor of the revolutionary Damdin Sükhbaatar. Soviet-era development replaced much of the ger districts' core with apartment blocks and monumental government buildings around Sükhbaatar Square.

The city today

Ulaanbaatar is Mongolia's political, economic, and cultural center, hosting the Government Palace, the National University of Mongolia, Gandantegchinlen Monastery with its great Avalokiteśvara statue, and the Zaisan Memorial. Rapid rural-to-urban migration since the 1990s has swelled the surrounding ger districts, home to a large share of the population, which lack central heating and contribute to the city's severe winter air pollution from coal smoke.
