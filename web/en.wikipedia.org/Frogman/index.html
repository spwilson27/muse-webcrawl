Frogman

A frogman is someone who is trained in scuba diving or swimming underwater in a tactical capacity that includes military, and in some European countries, police work. Such personnel are also known as combat divers or attack divers. Typically they use rebreathers that produce no exhaled bubbles, along with swimmer fins, for covert operations.

History

Military frogmen first became prominent during the Second World War. The Italian Decima MAS used manned torpedoes and limpet mines to attack British ships at Gibraltar and Alexandria. In response, Britain formed units such as the Royal Navy's frogmen and copied Italian techniques; Lionel Crabb became a noted British frogman. The United States' Underwater Demolition Teams, forerunners of the Navy SEALs, cleared beach obstacles in the Pacific and Normandy landings.

Roles

Frogmen conduct reconnaissance of beaches and harbours, underwater demolitions, sabotage of ships with limpet mines, infiltration from submarines or surface craft, and harbour defence against enemy divers. Police and civilian roles include search and recovery, underwater inspection, and rescue. Modern combat divers use closed-circuit rebreathers, dry suits, underwater navigation boards, and diver propulsion vehicles.
