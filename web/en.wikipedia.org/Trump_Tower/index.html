Trump Tower is a 58-storey mixed-use skyscraper at 721–725 Fifth Avenue, at the corner of East 56th Street, in Midtown Manhattan, New York City. Completed in 1982 and opened in 1983, it houses the headquarters of the Trump Organization and contained the personal penthouse residence of its developer, Donald Trump. The tower is one of the most recognisable buildings associated with Trump, and served as the setting for his announcement of his 2016 presidential campaign and as his campaign headquarters.

History

The site was formerly occupied by the flagship store of the department store chain Bonwit Teller. Trump acquired the site and its air rights, demolished the 1929 building—controversially destroying its Art Deco sculptural reliefs, which had been promised to the Metropolitan Museum of Art—and erected the tower in its place. To gain extra floor area, the design included a five-storey atrium open to the public.

Architecture

The tower is clad in dark bronze-tinted glass with a stepped, saw-tooth profile at its southwest corner. Inside, the atrium is lined with pink-orange Breccia Pernice marble, mirrors and brass, and features an 18-metre (60 ft) waterfall. The building contains luxury condominium residences above offices and ground-floor retail, which has included flagship stores for major brands.

Tenant history

Residents and tenants over the years have included celebrities, business figures and foreign dignitaries. Following Trump's election as president, the tower became a heavily secured location and a frequent site of protests and demonstrations.
