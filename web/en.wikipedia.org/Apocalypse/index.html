An apocalypse (from Ancient Greek apokálypsis, "a disclosure or revelation") is, in its original sense, a literary genre in which a supernatural being reveals hidden knowledge — especially about the end of the present age and the coming judgement — to a human recipient. In modern usage the word commonly denotes the end of the world itself, or any catastrophic event on a vast scale.

Apocalyptic literature

Apocalyptic writings flourished in Judaism of the Second Temple period and in early Christianity. The biblical Book of Daniel and the New Testament Book of Revelation (its full title is "The Revelation [Apocalypse] of Jesus Christ") are the canonical examples; others include 1 Enoch, 4 Ezra, and the War Scroll from Qumran. Typical features are visions mediated by angels, symbolic beasts and numbers, a periodised view of history, cosmic dualism between good and evil, and the expectation of resurrection and final judgement.

Related traditions

Comparable end-time expectations appear in many religions: the Jewish Messiah and World to Come; Christian millennialism and the Second Coming; Islamic signs of the Hour and the Mahdi; the Zoroastrian Frashokereti (final renovation), which influenced the Abrahamic traditions; the Hindu Kali Yuga; and the Norse Ragnarök.

Modern usage

Since the twentieth century "apocalypse" has been applied to scenarios such as nuclear war ("nuclear apocalypse"), pandemics, and climate catastrophe, and the term pervades popular culture — post-apocalyptic fiction explores civilisation after collapse. Secular movements predicting catastrophe, such as those surrounding the year 2012 (based on misreadings of the Maya calendar), have repeatedly failed to materialise.
