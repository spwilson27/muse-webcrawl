RMS Britannia

RMS Britannia
Owner: British and North American Royal Mail Steam Packet Company (Cunard)
Launched: 5 February 1840
Tonnage: 1,139 GRT
Fate: Sunk as target ship, 1880

RMS Britannia was an ocean liner of the British and North American Royal Mail Steam Packet Company, later known as Cunard Line. Launched in 1840, she inaugurated the company's regular transatlantic steam service between Liverpool and Halifax and Boston, marking the beginning of scheduled steamship crossings of the Atlantic.

Design and service

Britannia was a wooden paddle steamer of about 1,139 tons with a two-cylinder side-lever steam engine of about 740 horsepower driving paddle wheels, supplemented by sails. Her maiden voyage departed Liverpool on 4 July 1840, reaching Halifax in about 12 days and Boston shortly after. Charles Dickens sailed aboard her to America in January 1842 and described the cramped cabin in his American Notes.

Later career

In 1849 she was sold to the German Confederation's navy, where she served as the flagship Barbarossa before transfer to the Prussian Navy. She was sunk as a target ship in 1880.
