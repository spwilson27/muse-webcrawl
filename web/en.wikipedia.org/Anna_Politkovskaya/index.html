Anna Stepanovna Politkovskaya (30 August 1958 – 7 October 2006) was a Russian journalist and human rights activist known for her reporting on political events in Russia, particularly the Second Chechen War. Writing for the newspaper Novaya Gazeta, she documented abuses by Russian forces and Chechen fighters, earning international recognition and numerous awards.

Career

Politkovskaya was born in New York City, where her parents were Ukrainian diplomats at the United Nations. She studied journalism at Moscow State University, graduating in 1980, and worked for the newspaper Izvestia before joining Novaya Gazeta in 1999. From Chechnya she reported on hospital conditions, refugee camps and the disappearances of civilians, and she acted as a negotiator during the 2002 Moscow theatre hostage crisis. Her books include A Dirty War: A Russian Reporter in Chechnya and Putin's Russia (2004), a sharply critical account of Vladimir Putin's presidency.

Death

On 7 October 2006, Politkovskaya was shot dead in the lift of her Moscow apartment building. Her murder drew international condemnation and became emblematic of the dangers faced by journalists in Russia. Several men were convicted in connection with the killing in 2014, but the person who ordered it was never conclusively identified in court.

Legacy

The Anna Politkovskaya Award and a memorial prize administered by the Italian magazine Internazionale bear her name. Her reporting remains a key source on the Chechen wars and on civil liberties in post-Soviet Russia.
