Anmer Hall is a Georgian country house in the village of Anmer in Norfolk, England. It forms part of the Sandringham Estate and is the country residence of the Prince and Princess of Wales, William and Catherine, who received it as a wedding gift from Queen Elizabeth II.

History

The current red-brick house dates principally from the 18th century, though a house existed on the site earlier. The property passed through several private owners before being acquired for the Sandringham Estate. During the 20th century it was leased to tenants, including the Duke and Duchess of Kent in the 1970s and later the businessman Hugh van Cutsem.

Royal residence

Following their marriage in 2011, the then Duke and Duchess of Cambridge were given the use of Anmer Hall by the Queen. The house underwent substantial renovation, including a new roof and a glass-roofed conservatory extension, before the family moved in during 2015. It served as their principal family home while Prince William worked as an air ambulance pilot in Norfolk, and the family continued to use it as a country residence thereafter.

Architecture and grounds

The house has ten bedrooms and stands in grounds with gardens, a tennis court and outbuildings. It is not open to the public, and the surrounding estate land is privately managed as part of the wider Sandringham Estate.
