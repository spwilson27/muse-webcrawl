OriginSanta Cruz, California, 1881

The loganberry (Rubus × loganobaccus) is a hybrid of the North American blackberry (Rubus ursinus) and the European raspberry (Rubus idaeus), accidentally created in 1881 in Santa Cruz, California, by American judge and horticulturist James Harvey Logan.

Description

The loganberry plant is a vigorous, nearly thornless bramble. Its fruit is long, dark red to purple, and aggregates of drupelets like a blackberry but with the colour and flavour closer to a raspberry; the berries are rather tart and ripen over a long period in summer.

Cultivation and use

Loganberries are grown commercially in the Pacific Northwest of the United States and elsewhere, and are used in jams, pies, juices, and wines. The loganberry is itself a parent of later hybrids such as the boysenberry, tayberry, and marionberry.
