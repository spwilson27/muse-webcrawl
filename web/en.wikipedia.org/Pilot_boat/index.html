Pilot boat

A pilot boat is a type of boat used to transport maritime pilots between land and the inbound or outbound ships that they are piloting. Pilots board ships—traditionally by climbing a pilot ladder from the boat while underway—to guide them through hazardous or congested waters such as ports, harbours, and river mouths, where detailed local knowledge is essential.

Characteristics

Pilot boats are built for speed, seaworthiness, and the ability to operate alongside large vessels in rough conditions. Modern pilot launches are typically 15–20 metres long, self-righting, heavily fendered, and painted in conspicuous colors with the word "PILOT" displayed prominently. The transfer of the pilot between boat and ship, often in open sea and at night, is one of the most hazardous operations in shipping.

History

Pilotage is an ancient service; pilot boats originally were local working craft such as fishing boats that raced to reach incoming ships, since the first pilot to arrive got the job. Purpose-built pilot cutters and schooners developed in the 18th and 19th centuries in ports such as Boston and Bristol. In some modern ports pilots are transferred by helicopter instead of boat.
