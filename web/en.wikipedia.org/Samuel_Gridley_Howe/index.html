Samuel Gridley Howe (1801–1876) was an American physician, abolitionist, and advocate of education for the blind. He was the founding director of the Perkins Institution for the Blind (now Perkins School for the Blind) in Boston and is celebrated for his education of Laura Bridgman, the first deafblind person to receive a substantial education in English.

Early life and Greece

Howe studied medicine at Harvard Medical School. Inspired by Lord Byron, he travelled to Greece in 1824 to serve as a surgeon in the Greek War of Independence, later writing an account of the conflict.

Work for the blind

In 1832 Howe became the first director of the New England Institution for the Education of the Blind. He developed methods of raised-print reading and personally taught Laura Bridgman, demonstrating that deafblind people could be educated — a achievement that later inspired the education of Helen Keller.

Abolitionism

Howe was a committed abolitionist, a member of the "Secret Six" who funded John Brown, and co-founder of the abolitionist newspaper the Daily Commonwealth. He was married to the poet and activist Julia Ward Howe, author of "The Battle Hymn of the Republic".
