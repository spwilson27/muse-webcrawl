Pet Sematary

Pet Sematary is a 1983 horror novel by American author Stephen King. It was nominated for a World Fantasy Award for Best Novel in 1984. The title is a deliberate misspelling echoing the sign made by children at the animal burial ground that features in the story.

Plot

Dr. Louis Creed moves with his wife Rachel, their children Ellie and Gage, and Ellie's cat Church to a house in Ludlow, Maine. Behind the house lies a "Pet Sematary" where generations of children have buried their pets, and beyond it an ancient Mi'kmaq burial ground. When Church is killed by a truck, the elderly neighbour Jud Crandall shows Louis how animals buried in the far ground return to life — changed and malevolent. After Gage is killed on the same road, Louis, mad with grief, buries his son there despite Jud's warnings, with devastating consequences: "Sometimes, dead is better."

Background

King based the novel on his own time living in Orrington, Maine, beside a dangerous road where his daughter's cat was killed and buried in a real pet cemetery. He considered the finished manuscript too bleak and set it aside, publishing it only to fulfil a contract with Doubleday.

Adaptations

The novel was filmed in 1989, directed by Mary Lambert with a screenplay by King (who makes a cameo), featuring the Ramones' song "Pet Sematary"; a sequel followed in 1992. A second adaptation was released in 2019, starring Jason Clarke and John Lithgow, followed by the prequel Pet Sematary: Bloodlines (2023).
