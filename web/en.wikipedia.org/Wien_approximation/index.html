Wien approximation

The Wien approximation, also called Wien's distribution law, is an approximation to the spectrum of thermal radiation emitted by a black body. Proposed by Wilhelm Wien in the 1890s, it describes the short-wavelength side of the spectrum well but fails at long wavelengths.

Relation to Planck's law

Planck's law later gave the full black-body spectrum. Wien's formula follows from Planck's law when the photon energy is much larger than the thermal energy, allowing the “−1” term in the denominator to be neglected. In this high-frequency or low-temperature limit the spectral radiance falls exponentially with frequency.

Importance

The approximation was an important step toward quantum theory. Together with the Rayleigh–Jeans law, which works at long wavelengths, it showed that classical physics could not explain the whole black-body spectrum.
