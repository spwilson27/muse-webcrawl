Displacement (geometry)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In geometry and mechanics, a displacement is a vector whose length is the shortest distance from the initial to the final position of a point P undergoing motion. It quantifies both the distance and direction of the net change in position: if an object moves relative to a reference frame from position rinitial to position rfinal, its displacement is the difference of those position vectors, regardless of the path actually travelled.

Displacement versus distance

Displacement differs from distance travelled: distance is a scalar that accumulates along the whole trajectory, while displacement depends only on the endpoints. An object that completes a closed loop returns to its starting point and has zero displacement, even though the distance it travelled is positive. Dividing displacement by elapsed time gives velocity, whereas dividing distance by time gives speed.

Rigid bodies and derivatives

For a rigid body, motion can be decomposed into translation, described by the displacement of a reference point, and rotation, described by an angle of rotation. In continuum mechanics, the displacement field assigns a displacement vector to every material point of a body, and its spatial gradients determine the strain. Higher time derivatives of displacement include velocity, acceleration, jerk, snap, crackle and pop.
