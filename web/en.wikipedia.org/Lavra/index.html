Lavra

A lavra (Greek: λαύρα; Ukrainian/Russian: лавра) is a type of monastery in Eastern Orthodox and Eastern Catholic tradition consisting of a cluster of hermit cells or caves centred on a church and the residence of a superior. In modern usage it denotes a monastery of the highest rank.

History

The first lavras arose in 4th-century Palestine, notably those of St Chariton and St Sabbas (Mar Saba). In the Slavic world the title is reserved for a few great houses: the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra (founded 1051), the Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius at Sergiyev Posad, the Alexander Nevsky Lavra in St Petersburg, and the Pochaiv Lavra in Ukraine. The Great Lavra on Mount Athos, founded 963, is the oldest Athonite monastery.
