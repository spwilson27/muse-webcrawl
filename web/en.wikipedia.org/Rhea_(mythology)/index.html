Rhea (Ancient Greek: Ῥέα) is a Titaness in Greek mythology, daughter of the earth goddess Gaia and the sky god Uranus, and sister and wife of Cronus. She is the mother of the first generation of Olympian gods.

Mythology

Cronus, warned by a prophecy that he would be overthrown by his own child, swallowed each of Rhea's newborns in turn: Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades and Poseidon. When the sixth child, Zeus, was born, Rhea hid him in a cave on Mount Ida in Crete, where he was raised by nymphs and the goat Amalthea, and instead gave Cronus a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes, which he swallowed. When Zeus grew up he forced Cronus to disgorge his siblings, and together they overthrew the Titans in the Titanomachy.

Cult and associations

Rhea was associated with Crete, where Zeus was hidden, and her worship there involved ecstatic rites with cymbals and drums performed by her attendants, the Curetes, who clashed their weapons to drown out the infant's cries. In later tradition she was identified with the Phrygian mother goddess Cybele and with the Roman goddess Ops, and came to be regarded as a great mother of the gods. She is one of the Twelve Olympians' parent generation rather than an Olympian herself, though she was honoured on Olympus.
