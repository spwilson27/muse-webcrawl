The Turkic Khaganate (also Göktürk Khaganate) was a khaganate established by the Ashina clan of the Göktürks under Bumin Qaghan in 552. It was the first polity to use the name "Turk" (𐱅𐰇𐰼𐰜, Türük) politically, and it rapidly expanded across the Eurasian steppe, succeeding the Rouran Khaganate as the dominant power of Inner Asia.

Expansion

Bumin's brother Istämi expanded westward, defeating the Hephthalites in alliance with Sasanian Persia and establishing diplomatic and commercial relations with the Byzantine Empire along the Silk Road. At its greatest extent around 576–583, the khaganate stretched from Manchuria and the Greater Khingan range in the east to the Black Sea and the borders of Persia in the west — one of the largest contiguous empires in history.

Division and the Second Khaganate

Civil war after 583 split the khaganate into eastern and western halves. The Eastern Turks submitted to Tang China in 630, and the Western Khaganate fell by 657; for half a century the Turks were under Tang rule. In 682 Kutlug (Ilterish Qaghan) restored the empire as the Second Turkic Khaganate, which endured until 744, when it was overthrown by the Uyghurs.

Legacy

The khaganate left the earliest extensive texts in a Turkic language: the 8th-century Orkhon inscriptions (for Kül Tigin and Bilge Khagan), carved in runic Old Turkic script in Mongolia's Orkhon valley. The khaganate is foundational to the history and national identity of the Turkic peoples of Eurasia.
