Indian religions

Indian religions, sometimes also termed Dharmic religions or Indic religions, are the religions that originated in the Indian subcontinent. These religions, which include Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, and Sikhism, are also classified as Eastern religions. Although they are distinct traditions, they share concepts such as dharma, karma, samsara (reincarnation), moksha/nirvana (liberation), ahimsa (non-violence) and yoga.

History

The history of Indian religions begins with the religion of the Indus Valley Civilisation and the historical Vedic religion of the Iron Age Indo-Aryans. In the middle of the first millennium BCE, the shramana movement gave rise to Jainism and Buddhism, which challenged orthodox ritualism and offered paths of ascetic renunciation. Hinduism developed over the following centuries through the synthesis of Vedic religion with regional devotional (bhakti) traditions. Sikhism emerged in the Punjab in the 15th century CE with Guru Nanak.

Shared concepts

  • Dharma — duty, law, righteousness and the order of things.
  • Karma — action and its moral consequences across lives.
  • Samsara — the cycle of birth, death and rebirth.
  • Moksha / Nirvana — liberation from the cycle.
  • Ahimsa — non-violence toward all living beings.
  • Yoga and meditation — disciplines of body and mind aimed at realisation.

Demographics

According to the 2011 census of India, about 79.8% of India's population is Hindu, 1.7% Sikh, 0.7% Buddhist and 0.4% Jain, with most of the remainder following Islam and Christianity. Indian religions have spread worldwide through the Indian diaspora and, in the case of Buddhism, through two millennia of missionary and trade activity across Asia.
