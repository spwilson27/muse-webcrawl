Tim O'Reilly

Born1954, Cork, Ireland
OccupationPublisher, founder of O'Reilly Media

Tim O'Reilly (born 1954) is an Irish-American publisher, activist and venture capitalist, and the founder of O'Reilly Media, a computer book, magazine and conference publishing company. He popularised the terms "open source" and "Web 2.0" and has been a prominent advocate for open standards and collaborative technologies.

Career

O'Reilly founded his company in 1978 as a technical writing consultancy; it moved into publishing and produced best-selling manuals for Unix and later for internet technologies. Its 1993 Global Network Navigator was an early web portal. The 1998 Open Source Summit he hosted helped popularise the term "open source software".

Web 2.0

Through the Web 2.0 Conference, launched in 2004 with John Battelle, and his widely read 2005 essay "What Is Web 2.0", O'Reilly helped define the post-dot-com generation of participatory, data-driven web services. He has also campaigned on patent reform, government transparency ("Gov 2.0") and the societal effects of algorithmic platforms.
