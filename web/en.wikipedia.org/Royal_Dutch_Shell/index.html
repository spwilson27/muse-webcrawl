Royal Dutch Shell was an Anglo-Dutch multinational oil and gas company and one of the "supermajor" energy companies. For most of its existence it was structured as a dual-listed group combining Royal Dutch Petroleum of the Netherlands and the Shell Transport and Trading Company of Britain. In January 2022 the group simplified its structure, moved its headquarters and tax residence to London, and dropped "Royal Dutch" from its name, becoming simply Shell plc.

History

The group was created in 1907 when Royal Dutch Petroleum (founded 1890 to develop oil fields in Sumatra) merged its assets with the Shell Transport and Trading Company, founded by Marcus Samuel, whose shipping business had begun in seashells imported from the Far East — the origin of the name and the scallop-shell logo. Under the merger, interests were split 60:40 in favour of the Dutch partner, a ratio preserved for nearly a century.

Through the twentieth century Shell expanded worldwide in exploration, refining, petrochemicals and retail, becoming one of the Seven Sisters that dominated world oil. Notable episodes include the discovery of giant fields in Nigeria and the North Sea, the 1995 Brent Spar controversy with Greenpeace, and a 2004 scandal over the overstatement of proved reserves that led to executive resignations and fines.

Unification and renaming

In 2005 the twin parent companies were unified under a single holding company, Royal Dutch Shell plc, incorporated in England with headquarters in The Hague. In late 2021 the board proposed abandoning the dual share structure and Dutch headquarters entirely; shareholders approved, and on 24 January 2022 the company was renamed Shell plc.

Operations

At the time of the renaming the group employed around 86,000 people in more than 70 countries, with major businesses in upstream oil and gas, liquefied natural gas (greatly expanded by the 2016 acquisition of BG Group), refining and marketing, chemicals, and a growing power and renewables division.
