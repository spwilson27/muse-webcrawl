Ormen Lange (gas field)

LocationNorwegian Sea

Ormen Lange is a natural gas field on the Norwegian continental shelf in the Norwegian Sea, about 120 km northwest of Kristiansund. Discovered in 1997 by Norsk Hydro, it is one of Norway's largest gas fields and, at its development, the largest project in the Norwegian petroleum industry. The field is named after Ormen Lange ("the Long Serpent"), the flagship of the Viking king Olaf Tryggvason.

Development

The field lies in water about 850 metres deep, in an area scarred by the Storegga Slide, one of the largest known submarine landslides. Development used subsea wellheads tied back by pipelines to an onshore processing plant at Nyhamna on the island of Gossen in Aukra, Møre og Romsdal. Gas is conditioned at Nyhamna and exported through the Langeled pipeline — at 1,166 km, once the world's longest subsea pipeline — to Easington in England, supplying up to 20% of the United Kingdom's gas demand. Production began in October 2007.

Reserves and operation

Initial recoverable reserves were estimated at about 397 billion cubic metres of gas and 28.5 million cubic metres of condensate. Operation passed from Hydro to Shell in 2007; partners include Petoro, Equinor, Eni's Vår Energi and others. Compression projects, including a subsea compression pilot, have been undertaken to extend the field's life as reservoir pressure declines.
