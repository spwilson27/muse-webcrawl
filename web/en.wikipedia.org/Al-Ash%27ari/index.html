Al-Ash'ari

Abū al-Ḥasan ʿAlī ibn Ismāʿīl al-Ashʿarī (c. 874 – c. 936), commonly known as al-Ashʿarī, was an Iraqi Arab theologian who founded Ashʿarism, the theological school that became the dominant doctrine of Sunni Islam.

Life

Born in Basra, al-Ashʿarī was for much of his early life a follower of the Muʿtazilite school. Around the age of forty he publicly renounced Muʿtazilism and set out to defend traditionalist doctrine using the rational methods of kalām (dialectical theology).

Doctrine

Ashʿarism sought a middle position between Muʿtazilite rationalism and literalist traditionalism, holding, for example, that the Qurʾan is uncreated while affirming human acquisition (kasb) of acts created by God. His works include Maqālāt al-Islāmiyyīn, a survey of Islamic sects.
