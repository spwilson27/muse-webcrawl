Naval War College

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Naval War College (NWC) is the staff college and "home of thought" for the United States Navy, located at Naval Station Newport in Newport, Rhode Island. Founded in 1884, it is the oldest institution of its kind in continuous service in the world. The college educates and develops leaders, helps define the Navy's future, and advances naval warfare through research, analysis and wargaming.

History

The college was established on 6 October 1884 by General Order of Secretary of the Navy William E. Chandler, with Commodore Stephen B. Luce as its first president and Captain Alfred Thayer Mahan among its early faculty. Mahan's lectures there formed the basis of his influential work The Influence of Sea Power upon History, 1660–1783 (1890). The college's wargaming tradition, begun in the 1880s, was used in planning for both world wars.

Programmes

The NWC offers resident and distance-learning programmes through the College of Naval Warfare (senior level), the Command and Staff College (intermediate level), the Naval Command College for international officers (founded 1956), and the Naval Staff College. Graduates earn joint professional military education credit, and a Master of Arts in National Security and Strategic Studies or Defense and Strategic Studies.

Campus and research

The campus on Coasters Harbor Island includes Luce Hall (a National Historic Landmark), Pringle Hall, Mahan Hall, and Sims Hall, home of its wargaming centre. The college publishes the Naval War College Review, in print since 1948.
