Eric Paul Allman (born 1955) is an American computer programmer best known as the creator of sendmail, the first internet mail transfer agent, and of the syslog logging facility. His software was central to the operation of email on the early Internet and remains historically influential.

Career

While at the University of California, Berkeley, Allman worked on the INGRES database project and wrote delivermail (1979) for the ARPANET-era BSD Unix. As networks multiplied — ARPANET, UUCP, BITNET and others, each with different address formats — he rewrote it as sendmail (first shipped with BSD 4.1c in 1983), whose flexible configuration-file rewriting rules allowed it to route mail between heterogeneous networks. Sendmail came to handle the majority of Internet email.

He also created the syslog system-logging protocol and daemon, contributed the tset utility and the trek game to BSD, and co-founded Sendmail, Inc. in 1998 to commercialise the software. Allman has written extensively on the sendmail configuration language, notoriously complex, and on email architecture.

Recognition

Allman was inducted into the Internet Hall of Fame in 2014 in the Innovators category. He has been open about being gay throughout his career and has spoken about diversity in technology.
