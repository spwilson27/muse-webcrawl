Robert Smithson

Robert Smithson (January 2, 1938 – July 20, 1973) was an American artist known for sculpture and land art, most famously the Spiral Jetty (1970), a monumental earthwork of rock and earth extending into Utah's Great Salt Lake.

Work

Smithson began as a painter before turning to sculpture influenced by minimalism and geology. He developed the concepts of "site" and "nonsite" works — bins of material displaced from a landscape paired with maps and photographs. His essays, including "A Tour of the Monuments of Passaic, New Jersey" (1967) and "Entropy and the New Monuments", brought ideas of entropy, deep time, and industrial ruins into art criticism.

Death and legacy

Smithson died in a plane crash in Texas while surveying the site for his work Amarillo Ramp, which was completed posthumously. His writings and earthworks remain central to land art and contemporary sculpture.
