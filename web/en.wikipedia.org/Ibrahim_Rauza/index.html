Ibrahim Rauza

Location Bijapur, Karnataka, India
Built 17th century
Dynasty Adil Shahi

The Ibrahim Rauza is a tomb and mosque complex in Bijapur, Karnataka, India, built during the reign of Ibrahim Adil Shah II of the Adil Shahi dynasty in the early 17th century. It houses the tomb of Ibrahim Adil Shah II, his queen Taj Sultana, and other family members.

Architecture

The complex stands on a high plinth within a walled enclosure and consists of two principal buildings facing each other across a tank: the tomb and the mosque. The tomb is celebrated for its fine proportions, carved stonework, and a dome that later tradition has compared with the Taj Mahal at Agra, which it predates.

Inscriptions

The tomb is renowned for some of the most intricate carved inscriptions in the Deccan, including Quranic verses executed in raised calligraphy on the walls of the burial chamber.

Significance

The Ibrahim Rauza is considered one of the masterpieces of Adil Shahi architecture, noted for its symmetry, slender minarets, and delicate ornamentation.
