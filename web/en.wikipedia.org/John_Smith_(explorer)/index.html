John Smith (explorer)

Captain John Smith (c. January 1580 – 21 June 1631) was an English soldier, explorer, colonial governor, admiral of New England, and author. He played an important role in the establishment of the colony at Jamestown, Virginia, the first permanent English settlement in America, in the early 17th century. He was a leader of the Virginia Company during the colony's first two years and led expeditions exploring the Chesapeake Bay and the New England coast.

Early life and soldiering

Smith was born in Willoughby, Lincolnshire. After his father's death he became a soldier of fortune, fighting in the Netherlands and then in Hungary against the Ottoman Turks, where he claimed to have defeated three Turkish champions in single combat. Captured and enslaved in the Ottoman Empire, he escaped and made his way back to England by 1604.

Jamestown and Pocahontas

Smith sailed with the Virginia Company's expedition that founded Jamestown in 1607. Captured by Powhatan warriors in December 1607, he later wrote that Pocahontas, daughter of the paramount chief Powhatan, saved him from execution — an account whose accuracy historians still debate. As president of the colony's council from 1608, Smith imposed discipline with the rule that "he that will not work shall not eat," traded with Native peoples for food, and mapped the Chesapeake region. An injury from a gunpowder accident forced his return to England in 1609.

New England and later writings

In 1614 Smith explored and mapped the coast of Maine and Massachusetts Bay, naming the region "New England." His writings, including A Description of New England (1616) and The Generall Historie of Virginia, New-England, and the Summer Isles (1624), promoted English colonization of America. He died in London in 1631.
