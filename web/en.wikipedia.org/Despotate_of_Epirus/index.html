The Despotate of Epirus was one of the Greek successor states of the Byzantine Empire established in the aftermath of the Fourth Crusade in 1204. It claimed to be the legitimate successor of the Byzantine Empire, alongside the Empire of Nicaea and the Empire of Trebizond.

History

The state was founded around 1205 by Michael I Komnenos Doukas, a cousin of the Byzantine emperors Isaac II and Alexios III Angelos, who established his capital at Arta. Under his half-brother Theodore, Epirus expanded dramatically, capturing Thessalonica in 1224, where Theodore was crowned emperor. Epirote ambitions to retake Constantinople collapsed at the Battle of Klokotnitsa in 1230, when Theodore was defeated and captured by Ivan Asen II of Bulgaria.

After the Byzantine reconquest of Constantinople in 1261, Epirus survived as an autonomous despotate, often under Angevin, Serbian, or Italian influence. In the 14th and 15th centuries it was ruled successively by the Orsini, Serbian, and Tocco families, and fragmented between Arta and Ioannina. The last remnants fell to the Ottoman Empire in 1479.
