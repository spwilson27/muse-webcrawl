Aram Ilyich Khachaturian (6 June 1903 – 1 May 1978) was a Soviet Armenian composer and conductor, regarded as one of the leading composers of the Soviet Union and the most famous Armenian composer of the 20th century. His music blends Armenian folk melodies with the resources of the symphony orchestra, and is known for its vivid orchestration, rhythmic drive, and memorable melody.

Life and career

Born in Tiflis (now Tbilisi) to an Armenian family, Khachaturian moved to Moscow in 1921 and studied cello and composition, eventually teaching at the Moscow Conservatory. His Piano Concerto (1936) and Violin Concerto (1940) brought international recognition. Despite his official standing, he was denounced in the 1948 Zhdanov decree alongside Shostakovich and Prokofiev, though he was later restored to favour.

Works

His best-known works include the ballets Gayane (1942), which contains the famous "Sabre Dance", and Spartacus (1954); the Masquerade Suite (1944); three symphonies; concertos for piano, violin, and cello; and film and incidental music. The Adagio from Spartacus is widely known from its use in film and television.
