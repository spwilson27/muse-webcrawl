Upper Galilee

The Upper Galilee (Hebrew: הגליל העליון, HaGalil Ha'Elyon; Arabic: الجليل الأعلى, Al-Jalīl Al-A'la) is the northernmost region of the Galilee in northern Israel. It is a mountainous area bounded by the Mediterranean Sea to the west, Lebanon to the north, the Jordan Rift Valley and the Hula Valley to the east, and the Beit HaKerem Valley to the south, which separates it from the Lower Galilee.

Geography

The Upper Galilee is the more elevated and rugged of the Galilee's two sub-regions, with peaks rising above 1,000 metres, including Mount Meron (1,208 m), the highest point in Israel west of the Jordan River. The terrain consists of faulted limestone mountain blocks separated by deep valleys and gorges, such as Nahal Kziv and Nahal Amud, with a relatively high rainfall for the region that supports Mediterranean woodland and nature reserves.

Settlements

The principal towns include Safed (Tzfat), one of Judaism's four holy cities and the historical centre of Kabbalah, and Kiryat Shmona in the Hula Valley at the region's eastern edge. The area contains many kibbutzim, moshavim and community settlements, as well as Druze and Circassian villages such as Hurfeish and Rehaniya, and Arab towns including Fassuta and Mi'ilya.

History

The region was inhabited from prehistoric times and formed part of the territory allotted to the tribes of Naphtali and Asher in the Hebrew Bible. In the Second Temple period it was a centre of Jewish life, and after the destruction of the Temple many sages settled in its towns; Safed became a major centre of Jewish scholarship in the 16th century under the Ottoman Empire. During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War the region saw heavy fighting, including Operation Hiram, and many Arab villages were depopulated. The Upper Galilee has since remained strategically sensitive because of its position along the Lebanese border.
