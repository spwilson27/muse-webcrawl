The Battle of Brunete (6–25 July 1937) was fought during the Spanish Civil War west of Madrid. The Spanish Republican Army launched a major offensive to relieve pressure on the northern front and to encircle Nationalist forces besieging the capital; it ended with the Republicans holding only part of their gains at heavy cost.

The offensive

Some 50,000 Republican troops with substantial Soviet-supplied armour and air support attacked at dawn on 6 July, rapidly taking the village of Brunete. Units including Enrique Líster's 11th Division and the International Brigades advanced toward the Nationalist lines of communication, but delays allowed General Franco to rush in reinforcements, including the German Condor Legion, whose aircraft tipped the air balance.

Outcome

Nationalist counterattacks in mid-July retook most of the lost ground amid extreme summer heat; Brunete itself changed hands repeatedly and was left in ruins. Photographer Gerda Taro was fatally injured during the retreat. The offensive temporarily delayed Franco's northern campaign but failed to lift the threat to Madrid, and the Republic's best-equipped forces suffered severe losses.
