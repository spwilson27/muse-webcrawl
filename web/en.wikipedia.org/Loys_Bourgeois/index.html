Loys Bourgeois (French: [buʁʒwa]; c. 1510 – after 1550) was a French composer of the Renaissance and the principal early tune-smith of the Genevan Psalter, the metrical psalm book of the Calvinist Reformed churches. His psalm tunes — several still sung worldwide, most famously the melody known in English as the "Old 100th" — are among the most durable works of 16th-century music.

Life

Little is known of Bourgeois's life. Born in Paris around 1510, he was in Geneva by 1545, where the city council employed him to teach music and to compose and revise melodies for congregational psalm singing in John Calvin's church. He published his treatise Le droict chemin de musique (Geneva, 1550), the first French-language work to advocate replacing the traditional Guidonian hexachord syllables with a seven-syllable solmisation system (ba-ni-ma for the natural hexachord position) and a pioneering text on practical music theory.

In 1551 Bourgeois was briefly imprisoned by the Genevan authorities for altering established psalm tunes without permission — Calvin intervened for his release — and he left Geneva soon afterwards, returning to Paris; nothing further is known of him, and his date of death is unrecorded.

Music

Bourgeois supplied or edited many of the tunes in the early editions of the Genevan Psalter (notably the editions of 1547 and 1551), in which metrical French versions of the psalms by Clément Marot and Théodore de Bèze were set to single-line melodies for unaccompanied congregational singing. Among the tunes attributed to him are those for Psalms 42, 68 and 124 in the completed Psalter of 1562.

His most famous melody is the tune for Psalm 134 in the 1551 collection, later paired with Psalm 100 — hence "Old 100th" — and familiar to English-speaking congregations as the Doxology ("Praise God, from whom all blessings flow") and in Ralph Vaughan Williams's arrangement for the coronation of Elizabeth II. The harmonisation adapted from Bourgeois's melody also underlies the opening of "All people that on earth do dwell".
