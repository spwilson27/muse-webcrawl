Duke of Leinster

Duke of Leinster is a title in the Peerage of Ireland created in 1766 for James FitzGerald, 1st Duke of Leinster, head of the FitzGerald dynasty. It is the premier dukedom in the Peerage of Ireland, and the holder is styled the Premier Duke, Marquess and Earl of Ireland. The subsidiary titles held by the duke are Marquess of Kildare (1761), Earl of Kildare (1316), Earl of Offaly (1761), Viscount Leinster of Taplow (1747), Baron of Offaly and Baron Leinster of Taplow.

History

The FitzGeralds, of Cambro-Norman origin, had been Earls of Kildare since 1316 and were for centuries among the most powerful families in Ireland — "uncrowned kings of Ireland". James FitzGerald, 20th Earl of Kildare, was advanced first to Marquess of Kildare and then, in 1766, to Duke of Leinster, the first Irish dukedom not held by a member of the royal family. His wife was Emily FitzGerald, Duchess of Leinster, a sister of the celebrated Lennox sisters and mother of the revolutionary Lord Edward FitzGerald.

The family's principal seats were Carton House in County Kildare and Leinster House in Dublin; Leinster House, built for the family in 1745, was sold in 1815 and now houses the Oireachtas, the Irish parliament. Subsequent dukes were prominent in Irish and British public life, though the estates diminished over the 19th and 20th centuries. The dukedom continues in the FitzGerald family, the holders residing in England in recent generations.
