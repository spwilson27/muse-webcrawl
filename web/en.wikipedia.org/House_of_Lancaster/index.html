House of Lancaster

The House of Lancaster was the second of two cadet branches of the royal House of Plantagenet. The house was founded by John of Gaunt, third surviving son of King Edward III, who became Duke of Lancaster through his marriage to Blanche of Lancaster. Its members held the English throne from 1399 to 1461, and again from 1470 to 1471.

History

In 1399 John of Gaunt's son, Henry Bolingbroke, deposed his cousin Richard II and became King Henry IV. He was succeeded by his son Henry V, famous for his victory over France at the Battle of Agincourt in 1415, and then by his grandson Henry VI, who became king as an infant and later suffered bouts of mental illness.

Henry VI's weakness helped trigger the Wars of the Roses, a series of civil wars fought against the rival House of York. Henry was deposed in 1461 and briefly restored in 1470–71 before being deposed again and dying in the Tower of London. His son, Edward of Westminster, was killed at the Battle of Tewkesbury, ending the direct Lancastrian line. The Lancastrian claim was later taken up by Henry Tudor, who defeated Richard III at the Battle of Bosworth Field in 1485 and founded the Tudor dynasty as Henry VII.
