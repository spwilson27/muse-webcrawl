Britpop

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Years activec. 1992–1999 (peak 1994–1997)
OriginUnited Kingdom (London and Manchester)
Stylistic roots1960s British Invasion, glam rock, punk, Madchester, indie rock
Key actsBlur, Oasis, Pulp, Suede, Elastica, Supergrass, The Verve

Britpop was a mid-1990s British-based music culture and movement that emphasised British identity and produced bright, catchy guitar pop. Though largely viewed as a marketing and media phenomenon as much as a musical style, Britpop bands took their sound from the British guitar pop of the 1960s and from indie rock of the 1980s, reacting against the dominance of American grunge and domestic shoegaze. At its peak it was bound up with the broader "Cool Britannia" celebration of British culture, the Young British Artists, and the rise of New Labour.

Rise

The movement crystallised in 1993–94 with Suede's debut, Blur's Parklife (1994), and Oasis's Definitely Maybe (1994), then the fastest-selling debut album in UK history. Pulp's Different Class (1995) and hits by Elastica, Supergrass, and Sleeper broadened the scene. In August 1995 the media-hyped "Battle of Britpop" chart contest between Blur's "Country House" and Oasis's "Roll with It" — won by Blur — made headline news and signalled the genre's mainstream saturation.

Decline

Oasis's third album, Be Here Now (1997), was initially a sales phenomenon but quickly drew criticism for excess, and the band's popularity began to slide. Blur moved toward American alternative influences on Blur (1997), The Verve scored a major success with Urban Hymns (1997) but split in 1999, and Radiohead's OK Computer (1997) pointed British guitar music in a more experimental direction. By the end of the decade Britpop was over as a coherent movement, though its influence persisted in the post-Britpop generation of bands such as Coldplay and Travis, and the Blur–Oasis rivalry periodically revives in reunion tours.
