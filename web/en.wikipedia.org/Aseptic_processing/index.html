Aseptic processing

Aseptic processing is a processing technique wherein commercially thermally sterilized liquid products (typically food or pharmaceutical) are packaged into previously sterilized containers under sterile conditions to produce shelf-stable products that do not need refrigeration. Aseptic processing has almost completely replaced in-container sterilization of liquid foods, including milk, fruit juices, concentrates, cream, yogurt, salad dressings, liquid eggs, and ice cream mixes.

History

The technique grew out of the work of Olin Ball and others on heat penetration in the 1920s, and was commercialised for food packaging by the Tetra Pak company in the 1960s, whose aseptic cartons made long-life milk and juice widely available without refrigeration.

Process

The product is sterilised by ultra-high temperature (UHT) treatment — heating to roughly 135–150 °C for a few seconds — then cooled rapidly and filled into pre-sterilised containers in a sterile environment. Because sterilisation is brief, flavour and nutrient retention are better than in conventional canning.

Pharmaceutical use

In pharmaceuticals, aseptic processing is used to fill sterile drug products, vaccines and biologics that cannot withstand terminal sterilisation, requiring cleanroom environments graded under good manufacturing practice (GMP) and validated by media-fill simulations.
