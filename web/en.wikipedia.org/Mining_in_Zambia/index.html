Mining in Zambia

Mining in Zambia produces a substantial share of the country's exports and government revenue. Zambia is one of the world's major producers of copper, which accounts for roughly 70% of export earnings, and also mines cobalt, emeralds, gold, and other minerals. The industry is centred on the Copperbelt Province in the north.

History

Large-scale copper mining began under British colonial rule in the late 1920s, developed by companies such as Roan Selection Trust and Anglo American. After independence in 1964, the industry was progressively nationalised under President Kenneth Kaunda, culminating in the state conglomerate Zambia Consolidated Copper Mines (ZCCM) in 1982. Falling copper prices and underinvestment led to privatisation in the late 1990s and early 2000s, bringing foreign operators including First Quantum Minerals, Barrick, Glencore, and Chinese state firms.

Economic role and issues

Major mines include Kansanshi, Lumwana, and the historic Nkana and Mufulira operations. Recurrent policy disputes over mining royalties and windfall taxes, electricity shortages for smelters, and environmental damage — notably pollution around Kabwe, one of the world's most lead-contaminated towns — have shaped the sector. In the 2020s the government set a target of raising annual copper output to three million tonnes.
