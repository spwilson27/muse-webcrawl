Order of precedence

An order of precedence is a sequential hierarchy of nominal importance of persons. Most often it is used in the context of people by many organisations and governments, for very formal and state occasions, especially where diplomats are present. It can also be used in the context of decorations, medals and awards. Historically, the order of precedence had a more casual application, especially in the area of who was higher on a list or whose title or rank was considered superior.

Purpose

Orders of precedence determine such practical matters as the order in which officials are announced, seated at tables, greeted, or listed in official documents. They reduce disputes at ceremonies by providing an authoritative ranking in advance. Precedence may be determined by statute, royal warrant, custom, or the internal rules of an institution such as a university, a court, or a church.

Examples

In the United Kingdom, separate orders of precedence for England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland rank the sovereign, members of the royal family, archbishops, great officers of state, peers, and holders of orders of chivalry. In the United States, the order of precedence places the President first, followed by the Vice President, state governors, the Speaker of the House, the Chief Justice, and former presidents, with detailed rules for diplomats and military officers. In diplomatic relations, precedence among ambassadors is governed by the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, generally following the order in which envoys present their credentials. Orders of precedence also exist for orders, decorations, and medals, determining the sequence in which insignia and ribbons are worn.
