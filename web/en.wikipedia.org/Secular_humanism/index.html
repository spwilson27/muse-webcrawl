Secular humanism is a philosophy or life stance that embraces human reason, logic, secular ethics, and philosophical naturalism, while specifically rejecting religious dogma, supernaturalism, and superstition as the basis of morality and decision-making.

Secular humanists hold that knowledge of the world can be derived by observation, experimentation, and rational analysis, and that human beings are capable of being ethical and moral without religion or belief in a deity. The term "secular humanism" was popularised in the 20th century, drawing on earlier traditions of freethought, rationalism, and Renaissance humanism.

History

The roots of secular humanism can be traced to ancient Greek philosophy, the rationalism of the Enlightenment, and the freethought movement of the 19th century. In 1933, a group of American thinkers published the first Humanist Manifesto, which outlined a "religious humanism" centred on human welfare. Later manifestos, including Humanist Manifesto II (1973) and the Amsterdam Declaration (1952, revised 2002), shifted emphasis toward a explicitly non-theistic, secular framework.

Principles

  • Ethics based on human needs, interests, and consequences rather than divine command.
  • Commitment to science and the scientific method as the most reliable means of understanding the universe.
  • Support for democracy, human rights, and the separation of church and state.
  • Rejection of supernatural explanations in favour of naturalistic ones.

Organisations

Humanists International (founded 1952 as the International Humanist and Ethical Union) coordinates national humanist organisations worldwide. In the United States, the American Humanist Association has been a leading voice for secular humanism; in the United Kingdom, Humanists UK performs a similar role.

Criticism

Religious critics have sometimes characterised secular humanism itself as a religion or ideology, a framing humanists dispute. Others argue that a purely naturalistic worldview cannot ground objective moral values, a charge secular humanists answer by pointing to consequentialist and humanistic ethical traditions.
