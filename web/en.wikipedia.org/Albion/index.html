Albion

Albion is an alternative name for Great Britain, used poetically and in literary contexts. The name is among the oldest recorded for the island, attested in Greek sources from around the 6th century BC, where the island was called Albion (Ἀλβίων) alongside Ierne for Ireland.

Etymology

The name is commonly linked to the Latin albus ("white"), in reference to the white chalk cliffs of Dover visible from the European mainland, though it may derive instead from a Celtic root such as Proto-Celtic *albiyū, "upper world" (in contrast to the underworld), related to Welsh elfydd.

Usage

Albion persisted in learned and poetic usage through the Middle Ages and into modern literature, often with an archaic or romantic flavour. The phrase "perfidious Albion" (la perfide Albion), used in French and other languages, criticises alleged British diplomatic duplicity. William Blake made Albion a central figure of his prophetic mythology — the primordial giant man from whom humanity is divided. The name survives in place names and in sports clubs such as West Bromwich Albion and Brighton & Hove Albion.
