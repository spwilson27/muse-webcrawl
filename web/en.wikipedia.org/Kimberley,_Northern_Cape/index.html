Kimberley is the capital and largest city of the Northern Cape province of South Africa, located near the confluence of the Vaal and Orange rivers. It grew from the diamond rush that followed the discovery of diamonds in 1869–1871 and is famous for the Big Hole, one of the largest hand-dug excavations in the world.

History

The discovery of an 83.5-carat diamond, the Star of South Africa, in 1869 triggered a rush to the area; the richest finds came at the Colesberg Kopje, which became the Kimberley Mine. The town was named after John Wodehouse, 1st Earl of Kimberley, the British Colonial Secretary. Mining magnates such as Cecil Rhodes built their fortunes here, and Rhodes's De Beers Consolidated Mines, founded in 1888, came to dominate world diamond production. Kimberley was besieged during the Second Boer War (1899–1900).

Today the city is a provincial administrative centre, and the Big Hole and its museum are major tourist attractions.
