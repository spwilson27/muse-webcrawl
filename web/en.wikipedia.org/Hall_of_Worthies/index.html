Hall of Worthies

The Hall of Worthies (Korean: 집현전, Jiphyeonjeon) was a royal research institute established in 1420 by King Sejong of Joseon (Korea), located within Gyeongbokgung Palace in Seoul. Staffed with young scholars selected for their talent, the Hall researched Confucian classics, history, law and statecraft, and advised the king on policy and scholarship.

Hangul

The Hall of Worthies is most famous for its association with the creation of the Korean alphabet. Working under Sejong's direction — and with the king himself deeply involved — its scholars developed the phonetic script promulgated in 1443 and published in 1446 as the Hunminjeongeum ("The Correct/Proper Sounds for the Teaching of the People"). The script, now known as Hangul (Han'gŭl), was designed so that ordinary people could learn to read and write. Hall scholars also compiled works such as the Seokbo sangjeol, an early text printed in the new script.

Abolition

After Sejong's death the institution's influence declined. In 1456, King Sejo abolished the Hall after several of its scholars were implicated in a plot to restore the deposed King Danjong — the incident of the "Six Martyred Ministers". Its functions were later partly assumed by the Office of Royal Decrees and, in scholarship, by the Hongmun-gwan (Office of Special Advisors).

Legacy

The Hall of Worthies is remembered as a symbol of Sejong's scholarly kingship and of Korea's scientific and literary golden age; its image is associated with the 10,000-won banknote, which bears Sejong's portrait.
