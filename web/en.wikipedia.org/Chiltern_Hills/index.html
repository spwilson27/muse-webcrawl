Chiltern Hills

Highest pointHaddington Hill, 267 m (876 ft)
Length74 km (46 mi)

The Chiltern Hills are a chalk escarpment in England, stretching 74 km (46 mi) in a south-west to north-east diagonal from near Goring-on-Thames in Oxfordshire, through Buckinghamshire and Bedfordshire, to near Hitchin in Hertfordshire.

Geography and nature

The hills are characterised by steep chalk scarp slopes, dry valleys, and extensive beech woodlands, once used to supply timber for the furniture trade of High Wycombe. Much of the range is protected as an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (a National Landscape). The ancient Ridgeway and Icknield Way trackways traverse the hills, and red kites, reintroduced from 1989, are now a common sight.

Settlements

Towns and villages in and around the Chilterns include Henley-on-Thames, Amersham, Chesham, Berkhamsted, Tring, and Wendover. The area is largely within London's commuter belt and is crossed by the controversial HS2 railway.
