The double chevron logo recalls the helical gears that founded the company.

Citroën (French pronunciation: [sitʁɔɛn]) is a French automobile brand founded in 1919 by industrialist André Citroën. It is known for innovative engineering, distinctive styling and a string of landmark models including the Traction Avant, the 2CV, the DS and the Traction Avant. Since 2021 Citroën has been a brand of the multinational group Stellantis, formed from the merger of Groupe PSA and Fiat Chrysler Automobiles.

History

André Citroën began by manufacturing shell casings and helical-toothed gears during the First World War; the double-chevron logo derives from those gear teeth. In 1919 he launched the Type A, the first mass-produced car in Europe built using American-style production methods. Citroën became famous for bold marketing, including lighting the Eiffel Tower with its name from 1925 to 1934.

The 1934 Traction Avant pioneered front-wheel drive and unitary body construction in mass production, though its development costs bankrupted the company, which was taken over by Michelin. After the Second World War the utilitarian 2CV (1948) motorised rural France, and the futuristic DS (1955) introduced hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension. In 1976 Citroën merged with Peugeot to form PSA, and the brand later added volume models such as the Saxo, Xsara, C3 and C4 ranges.

Motorsport

Citroën has a strong rallying heritage: its World Rally Team won the World Rally Championship manufacturers' title eight times between 2003 and 2012, and Sébastien Loeb won nine consecutive drivers' championships with Citroën cars. The brand has also competed in touring car racing with success.
