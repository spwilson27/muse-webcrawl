The New York Yacht Club (NYYC) is a private social club and yacht club based in New York City and Newport, Rhode Island. Founded in 1844, it is one of the oldest and most prestigious yacht clubs in the world, and it played a central role in the history of the America's Cup.

History

The club was founded by John Cox Stevens and eight associates aboard Stevens's schooner Gimcrack in New York Harbor on July 30, 1844. In 1851, a syndicate of club members built the schooner America, which sailed to England and won the Royal Yacht Squadron's One Hundred Sovereigns Cup, a trophy that became known as the America's Cup. The cup was deeded to the NYYC, which successfully defended it 24 times from 1870 to 1980 — the longest winning streak in the history of sport — before losing to the Royal Perth Yacht Club's Australia II in 1983.

Clubhouses

The club's Manhattan clubhouse, a Beaux-Arts landmark at 37 West 44th Street designed by Whitney Warren, opened in 1901 and is famous for its Model Room, containing hundreds of ship models. In 1988 the club acquired Harbour Court, a waterfront estate in Newport, Rhode Island, which serves as its on-the-water clubhouse and hosts major regattas.

Activities

The NYYC organizes or hosts numerous sailing events, including the annual cruise, Race Week at Newport, and the Transatlantic Race. Its Invitational Cup is a leading international amateur keelboat competition. The club re-entered the America's Cup in the 2021 and 2024 cycles through its American Magic team.
