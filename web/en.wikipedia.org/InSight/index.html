InSight

InSight
Operator: NASA / JPL
Type: Mars lander
Launch: 5 May 2018
Landing: 26 November 2018
End of mission: December 2022

InSight (Interior Exploration using Seismic Investigations, Geodesy and Heat Transport) was a NASA robotic lander mission designed to study the deep interior of Mars. It landed in Elysium Planitia on 26 November 2018 after a journey of about 485 million kilometres.

Instruments

The lander carried SEIS, a highly sensitive seismometer that recorded hundreds of marsquakes, and HP3, a self-hammering heat-flow probe nicknamed "the mole", which struggled to burrow into the Martian soil. A radio experiment, RISE, tracked the planet's wobble to probe its core.

Findings

InSight's seismic data allowed scientists to estimate the thickness of the Martian crust, the size of its liquid core and the structure of its mantle, and it recorded the largest known marsquake, of magnitude 5.

End of mission

Dust gradually covered the lander's solar panels, reducing its power. NASA declared the mission over in December 2022 after two consecutive missed communications.
