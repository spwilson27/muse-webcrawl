Küstrin

Küstrin (Polish: Kostrzyn nad Odrą) is a town at the confluence of the Oder and Warta rivers in western Poland, historically a fortified Prussian town at the border between Brandenburg and the Neumark. Since 1945 the Oder has formed the German–Polish border, and the town is divided: the Polish city of Kostrzyn nad Odrą stands on the eastern bank, while the German municipality of Küstriner Vorland lies to the west.

History

Küstrin was fortified under Margrave John of Brandenburg-Küstrin in the 16th century and developed into one of Prussia's strongest fortresses. The young crown prince Frederick (later Frederick the Great) was imprisoned in the fortress after his failed flight attempt in 1730 and witnessed the execution of his friend Hans Hermann von Katte there.

Second World War

In early 1945 Küstrin was declared a fortress city and was almost entirely destroyed in heavy fighting as the Red Army established bridgeheads across the Oder before the advance on Berlin. The devastated old town on the eastern bank was never rebuilt and remains an open archaeological site within Kostrzyn, while the fortress bastions survive as a museum.
