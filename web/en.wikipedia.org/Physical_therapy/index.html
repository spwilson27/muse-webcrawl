Physical therapy

Specialty
Rehabilitation medicine
Practitioners
Physical therapists (PTs)
Focus
Movement, function, pain

Physical therapy (abbreviated PT), also known as physiotherapy, is a healthcare profession concerned with the assessment, diagnosis, and treatment of disorders of movement and function. Physical therapists help patients restore mobility, reduce pain, and prevent disability through exercise, manual therapy, education, and physical modalities.

History

The profession traces its roots to early practitioners such as Hippocrates and Galen, who advocated manual therapy and hydrotherapy. Modern physical therapy developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, expanding rapidly during the world wars and the polio epidemics, when rehabilitation of wounded soldiers and paralyzed children created urgent demand.

Practice areas

Clinical specializations include orthopedics, neurology, cardiopulmonary rehabilitation, sports medicine, geriatrics, pediatrics, and wound care. Treatment commonly involves therapeutic exercise, joint mobilization, gait training, and modalities such as heat, cold, ultrasound, and electrical stimulation.

Education and licensure

In the United States, physical therapists earn a Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT) degree and must pass a national licensure examination. In many other countries, physiotherapy is a bachelor's or master's level profession regulated by national boards.
