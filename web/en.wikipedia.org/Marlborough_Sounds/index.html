Marlborough Sounds
LocationNorth-eastern South Island, New Zealand
Main inletsQueen Charlotte Sound, Pelorus Sound, Kenepuru Sound
Main portPicton

The Marlborough Sounds are an extensive network of drowned river valleys at the northern end of the South Island of New Zealand. Formed by land subsiding while sea levels rose after the last ice age, the Sounds include Queen Charlotte Sound (Tōtaranui), Pelorus Sound (Te Hoiere) and the smaller Kenepuru Sound.

Geography

The labyrinth of peninsulas, bays and islands covers roughly 4,000 square kilometres of coastline despite spanning a much smaller area as the crow flies. The port town of Picton, at the head of Queen Charlotte Sound, is the South Island terminal for the Interislander ferries across Cook Strait to Wellington.

Economy and ecology

The sheltered waters support marine farms producing green-lipped mussels and king salmon, as well as tourism and recreational boating. The Sounds are home to dolphins, fur seals and the rare king shag. In Māori tradition the sounds were formed when the hero Kupe fought a giant octopus, Wheke.
