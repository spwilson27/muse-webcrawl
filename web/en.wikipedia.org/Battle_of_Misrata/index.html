Battle of Misrata

Tripoli Street, Misrata's main thoroughfare and the focus of the heaviest fighting, 2011.

Date18 February – 15 May 2011
LocationMisrata, Libya
ResultRebel victory
BelligerentsAnti-Gaddafi forces vs. Libyan Army loyalists

The Battle of Misrata was one of the longest and bloodiest engagements of the Libyan Civil War of 2011. Fought between February and May 2011, it pitted rebel forces holding the coastal city of Misrata — Libya's third-largest — against troops loyal to Muammar Gaddafi, who besieged the city for nearly three months.

Background

Protests against Gaddafi's rule reached Misrata in mid-February 2011 and quickly turned into an armed uprising. By late February the rebels controlled the city, making it the rebels' only major stronghold in western Libya after the government's recapture of Zawiya. Loyalist forces, including the Khamis Brigade, surrounded Misrata and began a siege in March.

The siege

Loyalist tanks, artillery and Grad rockets shelled the city daily, and snipers occupied tall buildings along Tripoli Street. The port — the city's only lifeline — was repeatedly attacked and mined, yet humanitarian ships continued to evacuate wounded civilians and deliver supplies. From 19 March, NATO aircraft enforcing UN Security Council Resolution 1973 struck loyalist armour and artillery around the city, gradually blunting the assault.

Rebel counteroffensive

In April and May the Misrata brigades, many of them improvised units of civilian volunteers, pushed the loyalists street by street toward the city limits. They cleared the Tripoli Street corridor, retook the port area, and on 11 May captured Misrata Airport. By 15 May the siege was effectively broken, and the front stabilised west of the city near Dafniya.

Aftermath

Misrata suffered the heaviest civilian toll of any Libyan city in the war, with deaths estimated in the high hundreds or more. The city's brigades later formed one of the most powerful components of the rebel military, playing a leading role in the advance on Tripoli in August 2011 and in the final capture of Sirte, where Gaddafi was killed.
