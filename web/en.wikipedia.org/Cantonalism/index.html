Cantonalism
Spanish political movement favouring a federation of autonomous cantons

Cantonalism (Spanish: cantonalismo) was a Spanish political movement of the 19th century that advocated the division of the state into quasi-independent cantons joined in a federation, inspired by the cantons of Switzerland.

Cantonal rebellion

In July 1873, during the First Spanish Republic, "intransigent" federal republicans rose in the Cantonal rebellion, proclaiming cantons in Cartagena, Valencia, Seville, Cádiz, Murcia, and many other cities. Most collapsed within weeks; only Cartagena, defended by the navy, resisted until January 1874.

Legacy

The failure of cantonalism discredited federal republicanism in Spain for a generation and prepared the way for the Restoration of the Bourbon monarchy in 1874.
