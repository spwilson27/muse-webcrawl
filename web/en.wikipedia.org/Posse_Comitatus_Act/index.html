The Posse Comitatus Act is a United States federal law (18 U.S.C. § 1385) signed on June 18, 1878, by President Rutherford B. Hayes. Its original intent was to end the use of federal troops to police state elections in the former Confederate states during Reconstruction. The Act limits the powers of the federal government in the use of federal military personnel to enforce domestic policies within the United States.

Provisions

The Act generally prohibits the use of the Army and Air Force as a domestic law enforcement agency ("posse comitatus") except where expressly authorized by the Constitution or by an act of Congress. By policy, the restriction has been extended to the Navy, Marine Corps and Space Force. The Coast Guard, which has explicit law-enforcement authority, and state National Guard forces under state control are not covered.

Exceptions

Important exceptions include the Insurrection Act, which permits the President to deploy troops domestically in certain circumstances, and statutes authorizing military support for counter-drug operations and disaster relief. The Act has rarely been the basis for criminal prosecutions but shapes policy on domestic military deployments.
