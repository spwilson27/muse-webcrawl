Politics of Japan

The politics of Japan take place in the framework of a unitary parliamentary constitutional monarchy. The Emperor is the head of state, defined by the 1947 Constitution as "the symbol of the State and of the unity of the people," while the Prime Minister is the head of government. Legislative power is vested in the National Diet, executive power in the Cabinet, and judicial power in the Supreme Court and lower courts.

Constitutional framework

The Constitution of Japan, promulgated in 1946 and effective from 3 May 1947, declares that sovereignty resides with the people. Article 9 renounces war and the maintenance of war potential, though Japan maintains the Self-Defense Forces. The Diet consists of the House of Representatives (465 members, four-year terms) and the House of Councillors (248 members, six-year terms with half elected every three years). The House of Representatives can override the upper house on budgets, treaties, and the designation of the prime minister.

Party politics

Japanese politics has been dominated for most of the post-war period by the conservative Liberal Democratic Party (LDP), which governed almost continuously from its founding in 1955 — the so-called "1955 System." The LDP's dominance was interrupted in 1993–1994 and again in 2009–2012, when the Democratic Party of Japan held power. The LDP governs in coalition with Komeito. Opposition parties include the Constitutional Democratic Party and the Japan Innovation Party. Politics within the LDP has traditionally been structured by factions (habatsu) and supported by personal support networks (kōenkai) and a powerful career bureaucracy.

Elections and governance

Members of the House of Representatives are elected by a parallel system of single-member districts and proportional representation blocks. Local government is organized into 47 prefectures and municipalities under the Local Autonomy Law. Japan is regularly rated a full democracy, noted for high voter turnout in its early post-war decades (declining since), clean elections, and peaceful transfers of power.
