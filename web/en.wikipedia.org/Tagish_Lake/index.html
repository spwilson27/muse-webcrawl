Tagish Lake

Location: Yukon and British Columbia, Canada
Length: ~100 km
Communities: Tagish, Carcross
Known for: Gold rush route; 2000 meteorite fall

Tagish Lake is a long, narrow lake in the far north of British Columbia and the southern Yukon, Canada, near the border with Alaska. Formed in a glacially carved valley, it is about 100 km (62 mi) long and consists of several arms, including Taku Arm, Windy Arm and Bennett Lake's connected waterways.

History

The lake takes its name from the Tagish people of the area. During the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897–98, stampeders crossed the lake system on their way between the coast at Skagway and the Yukon River headwaters; the North-West Mounted Police maintained a post at Tagish. The White Pass and Yukon Route railway later served the neighbouring community of Carcross.

The Tagish Lake meteorite

On 18 January 2000 a bright fireball exploded over the lake and scattered fragments of a rare carbonaceous chondrite meteorite across the frozen surface. Because the fragments were recovered quickly, kept frozen and little contaminated, the Tagish Lake meteorite became one of the most pristine and scientifically valuable meteorites ever studied, providing material rich in organic compounds from the early Solar System.
