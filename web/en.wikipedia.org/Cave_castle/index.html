Cave castle

A cave castle (German: Höhlenburg) is a type of medieval castle built into or in front of a natural cave or rock shelter. The cave forms part of the fortification: chambers within the rock provided storage, refuge and living space protected by a defensive wall closing off the cave mouth. Cave castles are found especially in the Alps, the Jura, southern France, the Balkans and the Karst of Slovenia.

Characteristics

Builders exploited caves high in cliff faces or at the heads of gorges, positions difficult to approach and easy to defend. Access was often by a narrow path, ladder or drawbridge. Behind an outer wall with arrow slits, the cave interior could contain cisterns, stores and stables, allowing a small garrison to withstand a siege. A related type, the rock castle, is hewn directly into living rock.

Examples

The best-known cave castle is Predjama Castle in Slovenia, built into a cavern beneath a 123-metre cliff and linked by legend to the knight Erazem Lueger, who was besieged there in the 1480s. Others include the castles of Luegstein and Kronmetz in the Alps, cave fortresses in the Ardèche, and the rock-cut strongholds of Cappadocia and Georgia, such as Vardzia — though the latter are monastic complexes rather than feudal castles.
