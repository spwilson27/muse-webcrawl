Environment Act 1995

The Environment Act 1995 (c. 25), the statute that established the Environment Agency and the Scottish Environment Protection Agency.

The Environment Act 1995 (c. 25) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom which created the Environment Agency for England and Wales and the Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA), bringing together a range of previously separate environmental regulatory functions. It received Royal Assent on 19 July 1995, and the new agencies took up their functions on 1 April 1996.

Background

Before the Act, pollution control, water management, waste regulation and fisheries in England and Wales were divided among Her Majesty's Inspectorate of Pollution, the National Rivers Authority, local waste regulation authorities and other bodies. The Act merged these into a single agency intended to provide integrated environmental protection. In Scotland, functions were gathered into SEPA from river purification boards, Her Majesty's Industrial Pollution Inspectorate and local authority waste functions.

Main provisions

  • Part I established the Environment Agency and SEPA, defined their general aims, and imposed on the Agency the principal aim of contributing towards sustainable development.
  • Part II addressed contaminated land, inserting a new regime into the Environmental Protection Act 1990 (Part IIA) identifying contaminated land and allocating remediation responsibility.
  • Part III covered National Parks, supplementing earlier legislation on their purposes and authorities.
  • Part IV dealt with air quality, requiring local authorities to review and assess air quality and paving the way for the national air quality strategy and local air quality management.
  • Part V contained miscellaneous and general provisions, including powers relating to hedgerows protection, mines and quarries, and fisheries.

Legacy

The Act remains the founding statute of the Environment Agency. Many of its functions have since been reshaped by devolution: in Wales, most Environment Agency functions passed to Natural Resources Wales in 2013, and in Scotland SEPA operates under the Scottish Government. The contaminated land and air quality regimes introduced by the Act continue in force, with amendments.
