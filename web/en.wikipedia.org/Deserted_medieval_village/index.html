Deserted medieval village

In the United Kingdom, a deserted medieval village (DMV) is a former settlement from the Middle Ages that was abandoned by its inhabitants and where no substantial dwellings remain, though earthworks such as house platforms, hollow ways and ridge and furrow often survive. More than 3,000 deserted medieval villages are known in England.

Causes of desertion

Villages were abandoned for many reasons: population decline after the Black Death in the 14th century; conversion of arable land to sheep pasture by landowners, sometimes involving deliberate clearance and enclosure; coastal erosion; changes in trade routes; and shrinkage rather than total desertion, leaving shrunken villages with a few houses.

Research

The systematic study of DMVs began with the work of Maurice Beresford in the 1950s, whose book The Lost Villages of England (1954) and excavations at Wharram Percy in Yorkshire established the field. Wharram Percy remains the best-known and most intensively investigated example, managed as a heritage site.

Survival and protection

Many sites survive as earthworks under permanent pasture and are protected as scheduled monuments. Fieldwalking, aerial photography and lidar have greatly increased the number of identified sites.
