The Staten Island Ferry is a passenger ferry service run by the New York City Department of Transportation across Upper New York Bay between the boroughs of Manhattan and Staten Island. The ferry's single route runs 5.2 miles (8.4 km) between Whitehall Terminal in Lower Manhattan and St. George Terminal on Staten Island, and the crossing takes about 25 minutes. The service operates 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, and has been fare-free since 1997.

History

Ferry services between Manhattan and Staten Island date to the early 18th century. Cornelius Vanderbilt began operating steam ferries on the route in the 1810s, and the service passed through several private operators before the City of New York took control in 1905 following a serious accident. The distinctive municipal-orange boats became an icon of the city.

Route and views

The crossing offers celebrated views of the Lower Manhattan skyline, the Statue of Liberty, Ellis Island and Governors Island, making the free ferry one of New York's most popular attractions for visitors as well as a vital commuter link. At rush hours boats depart every 15 minutes.

Fleet

The fleet includes several classes of vessel, among them the John F. Kennedy class (1965), the Samuel I. Newhouse and the large Barberi-class boats Andrew J. Barberi and Samuel I. Newhouse, and the newer Ollis-class ferries delivered from 2021, named for Staff Sergeant Michael H. Ollis.
