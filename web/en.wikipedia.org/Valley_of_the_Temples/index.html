Valley of the Temples

The Valley of the Temples (Italian: Valle dei Templi) is an archaeological site in Agrigento (ancient Greek Akragas), Sicily. It is one of the most outstanding examples of ancient Greek art and architecture of Magna Graecia, and is one of the main attractions of Sicily. The term "valley" is a misnomer, as the temples stand on a ridge outside the town of Agrigento.

History

Akragas was founded around 582–580 BC by Greek colonists from Gela. At its height in the fifth century BC the city was one of the most prosperous in the Greek world, and its wealthy citizens sponsored the construction of a series of monumental Doric temples along the southern ridge of the city. The city was sacked by Carthage in 406 BC, and although it recovered under Timoleon in the fourth century, it never regained its former glory.

The temples

  • Temple of Concordia — built c. 440–430 BC, one of the best-preserved Doric temples in the world, converted into a Christian church in the 6th century AD.
  • Temple of Juno (Hera Lacinia) — built c. 450 BC; partially burned during the Carthaginian siege.
  • Temple of Heracles — the oldest temple on the site, dating to the late 6th century BC.
  • Temple of Olympian Zeus — begun after the Battle of Himera (480 BC), one of the largest Doric temples ever attempted, left unfinished.
  • Temple of Castor and Pollux — its four standing columns, reassembled in the 19th century, are the symbol of Agrigento.
  • Temple of Vulcan and the Temple of Asclepius, the latter associated with healing cults.

Archaeological park

The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997. The regional archaeological park covers some 1,300 hectares and includes the Hellenistic-Roman quarter, necropolises, and the Garden of the Kolymbethra, an ancient hydraulic garden now restored by the Fondo Ambiente Italiano.
