Modern Language Association of America (MLA)

Founded: 1883

Headquarters: New York City, United States

Membership: about 25,000 in 100 countries

Publication: PMLA; MLA Handbook

The Modern Language Association of America, often referred to as the Modern Language Association (MLA), is the principal professional association in the United States for scholars of language and literature. The MLA aims to "strengthen the study and teaching of language and literature."

Founded in 1883, the MLA has more than 25,000 members in 100 countries, primarily academic scholars and teachers of English, modern languages, and other literary fields. Its offices are located in New York City.

Activities

The association publishes several journals, including PMLA (Publications of the Modern Language Association of America), one of the most prestigious journals in literary studies, and the MLA International Bibliography. It holds a large annual convention, typically in early January, featuring scholarly sessions and one of the main job markets for academic positions in languages and literature.

MLA style

The MLA is best known to students for MLA style, a system of citation and format rules documented in the MLA Handbook (first published in 1977; 9th edition 2021). MLA style is widely used in the humanities, especially in language and literary studies, and specifies a system of parenthetical in-text citations keyed to a "Works Cited" list, together with guidelines for document formatting.
