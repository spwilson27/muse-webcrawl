The River Shannon (Irish: Abha na Sionainne) is the longest river in Ireland at 360.5 km (224 mi), draining the Shannon River Basin, which covers about one fifth of the island's area. It flows generally southwards from the Shannon Pot in County Cavan through the midlands, passing through three great lakes — Lough Allen, Lough Ree and Lough Derg — and the cities of Athlone and Limerick, before turning west into the 113 km-long Shannon Estuary and the Atlantic Ocean.

Geography and navigation

Despite its length, the Shannon falls only about 18 m between its source and Limerick, making it eminently navigable; most of its course is suitable for leisure craft, and it connects with the Grand Canal and Royal Canal and, via the Shannon–Erne Waterway, with the Erne system in Northern Ireland. Viking longships penetrated the river in the 9th century, and monasteries such as Clonmacnoise grew on its banks.

Power and industry

The Shannon Scheme of the 1920s — Ireland's first great national development project — built the hydroelectric station at Ardnacrusha, which for a time supplied the great majority of the young Irish Free State's electricity. The estuary hosts the port of Shannon Foynes, Moneypoint power station and Shannon Airport, while the river is celebrated for angling, coarse and game fishing, and as a habitat for salmon, eels and the freshwater pearl mussel.

Name and folklore

Tradition derives the name from Sionann, a granddaughter of the sea god Manannán mac Lir, who sought the forbidden well of knowledge and was drowned when its waters rose to form the river. The Shannon appears in maps as early as Ptolemy's 2nd-century geography.
