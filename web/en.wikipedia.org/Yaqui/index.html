Yaqui

Yaqui (Yoeme)
RegionsSonora, Mexico; Arizona, United States
LanguageYaqui (Yoem Noki), Spanish, English
Language familyUto-Aztecan

The Yaqui, or Yoeme, are a Native American people of the Uto-Aztecan language family whose homeland lies in the valley of the Río Yaqui in the Mexican state of Sonora. Substantial Yaqui communities also live in Arizona in the United States, including the federally recognised Pascua Yaqui Tribe. The Yaqui language belongs to the Cahitan branch of Uto-Aztecan.

History

The Yaqui resisted Spanish conquest with notable success, defeating Spanish forces in 1533 and remaining largely independent through the colonial period. After Mexican independence, the Yaqui fought a long series of wars against the Mexican state during the 19th and early 20th centuries to defend their land and autonomy; many were deported to work on henequen plantations in Yucatán under the regime of Porfirio Díaz. Many fled north to Arizona during this period. Significant land rights were restored under President Lázaro Cárdenas in the 1930s.

Culture

Yaqui religious life blends Catholic influences from the Jesuit mission era with indigenous belief. The best-known ceremony is the Lenten and Holy Week cycle, featuring the pajko'ola and deer dancers; the deer dance, in which a dancer wearing a stuffed deer head imitates the movements of a deer, is an emblem of Yaqui identity. The traditional Yaqui homeland comprises eight sacred towns, or Eight Pueblos, along the Río Yaqui.
