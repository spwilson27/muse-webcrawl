Kythera (Greek: Κύθηρα), also spelled Cythera, is a Greek island lying opposite the south-eastern tip of the Peloponnese at the entrance to the Aegean Sea. It is traditionally listed among the Ionian Islands, although it lies far from the others.

History and mythology

In Greek mythology Kythera was a birthplace of Aphrodite, who was called "Cytherea"; the island was a major centre of her cult, with a famous temple described by Pausanias. Because of this association the island became a byword for love and desire, inspiring Watteau's painting The Embarkation for Cythera and Baudelaire's "Un voyage à Cythère".

Historically the island passed between Sparta, Athens, Rome, Byzantium, Venice (which held it for centuries as Cerigo) and the Ottoman Empire before joining the modern Greek state in 1864. The nearby islet is the site associated with the Antikythera wreck, found off neighbouring Antikythera island, which yielded the Antikythera mechanism.

Geography

The island is rugged and water-poor in the south, greener in the north, with the main town of Chora in the south, crowned by a Venetian castle, and the port of Agia Pelagia in the north. Heavy emigration, especially to Australia, has left a large Kytherian diaspora; the island's population swells in summer with returning emigrants and tourists.
