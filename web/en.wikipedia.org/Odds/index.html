Odds

In probability theory, odds provide a measure of the probability of a particular outcome. They are calculated as the ratio of the number of events that produce that outcome to the number that do not. Odds are commonly used in gambling and statistics.

Definition

If an event has probability p, its odds are p / (1 − p). Conversely, odds of a to b correspond to a probability of a / (a + b). For example, an event with probability 3/4 has odds of 3 to 1 "in favour", while the odds against it are 1 to 3.

Gambling usage

Bookmakers quote odds in formats such as fractional (e.g., 5/1), decimal (e.g., 6.0), and American moneyline (e.g., +500 or −200). The odds offered typically incorporate a margin (overround) so that the bookmaker profits regardless of the outcome. In parimutuel betting, odds are determined by the distribution of wagers among bettors.

Odds ratio

In statistics, the odds ratio is the ratio of the odds of an event in one group to the odds in another, widely used in logistic regression, case-control studies, and epidemiology to measure association between an exposure and an outcome.

Likelihood ratio and Bayesian statistics

Odds form a natural part of Bayes' theorem: posterior odds equal prior odds multiplied by the likelihood ratio (Bayes factor). Taking the logarithm gives log-odds (the logit), measured in bans or decibans in some information-theoretic traditions.
