Battle of Aquae Sextiae

The Battle of Aquae Sextiae took place in 102 BC near the Roman settlement of Aquae Sextiae (modern Aix-en-Provence) in southern Gaul. There the legions of the consul Gaius Marius decisively defeated the Teutones and Ambrones, two Germanic peoples migrating south, ending the threat they posed to Italy during the Cimbrian War.

Background

The Cimbri and Teutones had destroyed several Roman armies, most catastrophically at Arausio in 105 BC. Marius, elected consul repeatedly in defiance of custom, rebuilt the army: he trained his men rigorously, made them carry their own kit ("Marius's mules"), and had the local population dig a canal to secure his supply line while he refused battle until ready.

Battle

When the Ambrones attacked the Roman camp, Marius routed them with losses the sources put in the tens of thousands. Two days later the main Teuton host attacked uphill against the Roman position. Marius had hidden 3,000–4,000 men under Marcellus in woods behind the enemy; their sudden attack in the Teuton rear turned the battle into a massacre. King Teutobod was captured to grace Marius's triumph.

Aftermath

The following year Marius and Catulus destroyed the Cimbri at Vercellae, concluding the war. The victories made Marius the dominant figure in Rome and confirmed his military reforms as the foundation of the late-Republican army.
