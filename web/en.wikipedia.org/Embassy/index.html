Embassy

An embassy is the main location for the diplomatic mission of one country in another, generally in the capital city of the receiving state. It is headed by an ambassador, the highest-ranking diplomat, and serves as the centre for political relations, consular services, and the protection of the sending state's citizens abroad.

Functions and legal status

Under the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations of 1961, the premises of a diplomatic mission are inviolable: agents of the receiving state may not enter without the consent of the head of mission. Contrary to popular belief, embassy premises are not legally the sovereign territory of the sending state, though they enjoy extensive immunities. Embassies issue visas, assist nationals in emergencies, promote trade and culture, and report political developments to their home governments.

Related missions

Consulates in other cities handle primarily citizen services and visas; high commissions are embassies between Commonwealth countries; and some states maintain permanent missions to international organisations such as the United Nations.
