Patrick Pearse

Born: 10 November 1879, Dublin, Ireland
Died: 3 May 1916 (aged 36), Kilmainham Gaol, Dublin
Known for: Irish nationalism, the Easter Rising

Patrick Henry Pearse (Irish: Pádraig Anraí Mac Piarais; 10 November 1879 – 3 May 1916) was an Irish teacher, barrister, poet, writer, nationalist, and republican political activist. He was one of the leaders of the Easter Rising in 1916 and was proclaimed President of the Provisional Government of the Irish Republic in the Proclamation read outside the General Post Office in Dublin.

Patrick Pearse, teacher, writer, and leader of the Easter Rising.

Early life and the Gaelic revival

Pearse was born in Dublin to an English stonemason father and an Irish mother. As a young man he joined the Gaelic League and became editor of its newspaper, An Claidheamh Soluis, campaigning to revive the Irish language. In 1908 he founded St Enda's School (Scoil Éanna) in Rathfarnham, a bilingual boys' school intended to educate pupils in Irish culture and language, and later a girls' school, St Ita's. His writings — poems, plays, short stories, and essays such as "The Murder Machine" — established him as a leading voice of cultural nationalism.

The Easter Rising

Pearse joined the Irish Republican Brotherhood and the Irish Volunteers, for whom he delivered the famous graveside oration for Jeremiah O'Donovan Rossa in 1915, ending: "the fools, the fools, the fools! — they have left us our Fenian dead, and while Ireland holds these graves, Ireland unfree shall never be at peace." As a member of the secret Military Council he helped plan the Easter Rising, and on 24 April 1916 he read the Proclamation of the Republic outside the GPO. After six days of fighting he agreed to surrender to prevent further civilian deaths. He was court-martialled and shot by firing squad at Kilmainham Gaol on 3 May 1916, the first of the Rising's leaders to be executed.
