Battle of Stirling Bridge

Battle of Stirling Bridge
Date11 September 1297
LocationStirling Bridge, over the River Forth, Scotland
ResultScottish victory
BelligerentsKingdom of Scotland; Kingdom of England
CommandersWilliam Wallace, Andrew Moray (mortally wounded); John de Warenne, Hugh de Cressingham (killed)

The Battle of Stirling Bridge was fought on 11 September 1297 during the First War of Scottish Independence. The forces of William Wallace and Andrew Moray defeated the English army of the Earl of Surrey, John de Warenne, at the narrow wooden bridge over the River Forth near Stirling.

The Scots waited on the high ground of Abbey Craig while the English crossed the bridge two at a time. When roughly half the English force — some 5,400 men — had crossed, the Scots charged down and seized the bridgehead, trapping those across the river. The English vanguard was cut to pieces; the treasurer Hugh de Cressingham was killed (legend says the Scots flayed his skin), and Warenne, still on the far bank, ordered the bridge destroyed and retreated south. Andrew Moray died of his wounds shortly afterwards.

Significance

Stirling Bridge was the first great Scottish victory of the wars of independence and demolished the myth of English military invincibility. Wallace was made a Guardian of Scotland and knighted, and he raided deep into northern England before his defeat at Falkirk the following year. The Wallace Monument now overlooks the site from Abbey Craig.
