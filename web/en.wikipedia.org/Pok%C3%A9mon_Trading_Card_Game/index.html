Pokémon Trading Card Game

The Pokémon Trading Card Game (ポケモンカードゲーム, Pokemon Kādo Gēmu), abbreviated as PTCG or Pokémon TCG, is a collectible card game developed by Creatures Inc. and published by The Pokémon Company. Based on the Pokémon video game franchise, it was first published in October 1996 in Japan by Media Factory, and in North America by Wizards of the Coast beginning in 1999 before The Pokémon Company International took over North American publication in 2003. It is one of the best-selling trading card games in the world, with over 52 billion cards printed and sold worldwide.

Gameplay

Two players, called Trainers, build decks of 60 cards and battle using Pokémon cards. Each player has one Active Pokémon and may keep up to five on the Bench. Players attach Energy cards to their Pokémon so they can use attacks; when a Pokémon's damage equals or exceeds its Hit Points, it is Knocked Out and the opponent takes a Prize card. A player wins by taking all six of their Prize cards, by knocking out all of the opponent's Pokémon in play, or if the opponent cannot draw a card at the start of their turn.

Cards come in several types: Basic and evolved Pokémon (Stage 1 and Stage 2), Trainer cards (Items, Supporters, and Stadiums), and Energy cards (Basic and Special). Pokémon cards are aligned with types such as Grass, Fire, Water, Lightning, Psychic, Fighting, Darkness, Metal, Dragon, and Colorless, with Weakness and Resistance modifying damage.

History

The game debuted in Japan on 20 October 1996 with the Base Set, illustrated by artists including Mitsuhiro Arita and Ken Sugimori. Wizards of the Coast launched the English version in January 1999, riding the wave of "Pokémania"; the Base Set Charizard became one of the most valuable modern trading cards. After Nintendo and The Pokémon Company ended Wizards' license in 2003, publishing moved in-house, and the game has since released regular expansions synced with the video games, including sets for each new generation from Diamond and Pearl through Scarlet and Violet.

Competitive play

Play! Pokémon organizes League play, Regional and International Championships, and the annual Pokémon World Championships, first held in 2004. Formats include Standard, which rotates older expansions, and Expanded. The Pokémon TCG also exists digitally in Pokémon Trading Card Game Live, which replaced Pokémon Trading Card Game Online in 2023.

Collecting

Rare holographic and "secret rare" cards are highly collectible; a PSA Gem Mint 10 first-edition Charizard has sold for hundreds of thousands of dollars at auction, and a Pikachu Illustrator promo sold for over $5 million in 2021, making it the most expensive Pokémon card ever sold.
