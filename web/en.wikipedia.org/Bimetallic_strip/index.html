Bimetallic strip

A bimetallic strip is a strip consisting of two different metals bonded together, typically steel and copper or steel and brass, which have different coefficients of thermal expansion. When the temperature changes, the strip bends because one metal expands more than the other, converting a temperature change into mechanical displacement.

Principle

As the strip is heated, the metal with the higher expansion coefficient lengthens more, forcing the strip to curve toward the side of the lower-expansion metal. The amount of deflection is proportional to the temperature change, making the strip useful as both a sensor and an actuator.

Applications

Bimetallic strips are widely used in thermostats, where the bending strip opens or closes an electrical contact to control heating systems; in thermal circuit breakers and overload protectors; in thermometers; and in fire alarms. The principle was demonstrated in the 18th century by clockmaker John Harrison, who used bimetallic compensation in his marine chronometers.
