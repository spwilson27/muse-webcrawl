Ski pole

Ski poles are carried by skiers to improve balance, propulsion and timing. A pole consists of a shaft (of aluminium, fibreglass composite or carbon fibre), a grip with a wrist strap at the top, a small disc-shaped basket near the bottom to stop the pole sinking into the snow, and a hardened tip.

Uses

In alpine skiing, poles are planted beside the skier to initiate turns and provide rhythm and balance, and are used to push along flat ground. In cross-country skiing they are longer and central to propulsion, converting upper-body effort into forward motion in both classic and skating techniques. Pole length is chosen by discipline and skier height; racing cross-country poles may reach chin or nose height, while downhill poles are shorter so they do not interfere with the tuck.

History

Early skiers in Scandinavia used a single long staff. The two-pole technique developed with modern skiing in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; bamboo shafts gave way to steel, then aluminium and composites. Related equipment includes trekking poles used by hikers, adapted from ski poles.
