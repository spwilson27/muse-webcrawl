Kyiv

Kyiv, capital of Ukraine, on the Dnieper River.

Kyiv is the capital and largest city of Ukraine, located in the north-central part of the country on the Dnieper River. Its population is roughly 2.9 million (over 3 million in the metropolitan area), making it one of the largest cities in Europe. Kyiv is Ukraine's principal political, economic, educational, and cultural centre. The name is rendered from Ukrainian; the older Russian-derived spelling "Kiev" fell out of international use following Ukraine's independence and especially after 2022.

History

Kyiv is one of the oldest cities in Eastern Europe. By the ninth century it had become the centre of Kievan Rus', the great East Slavic state whose conversion to Orthodox Christianity in 988 under Volodymyr the Great shaped the region's culture. The Mongol invasion of 1240 destroyed the city, which later passed through Lithuanian, Polish, and Russian rule. Kyiv became a major industrial centre of the Russian Empire and then of the Ukrainian SSR. During the Second World War it suffered devastating occupation, including the Babyn Yar massacres of 1941. In 1991 it became the capital of independent Ukraine. The Euromaidan protests of 2013–14 were centred on its Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Independence Square). In February 2022 Russia invaded Ukraine; the Battle of Kyiv ended with Russian forces withdrawing from the region in early April 2022, and the city has since endured repeated missile and drone attacks.

Landmarks

The UNESCO-listed Saint Sophia Cathedral (11th century) and the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra monastery complex dominate the historic skyline, alongside St Volodymyr's Cathedral, the Golden Gate, Andriivskyi Descent, and the towering Mother Ukraine statue. Khreshchatyk is the city's main boulevard.

Transport and education

Kyiv has a three-line metro system (opened 1960), two international airports (Boryspil and Kyiv International), and is home to Taras Shevchenko National University and the Kyiv-Mohyla Academy, one of the oldest universities in Eastern Europe.
