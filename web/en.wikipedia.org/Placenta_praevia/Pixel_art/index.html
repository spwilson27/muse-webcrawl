Pixel art

Pixel art is a form of digital art, created through the use of software, where images are edited on the pixel level. The aesthetic is associated with graphics that came from 8-bit and 16-bit era computers and video game consoles, in addition to other limited systems such as LED displays and graphing calculators.

Most works of pixel art limit the palette of colours used, with some pieces employing only two colours. Because individual pixels are deliberately placed, pixel art occupies a middle ground between mosaic art and pointillism in earlier art forms.

Definition

Pixel artists debate what counts as true pixel art: the defining characteristic is that each pixel is placed intentionally with a high degree of control, rather than being the incidental byproduct of a filter or an automatic downscaling of a photograph. Low-resolution images created carelessly or converted automatically are often distinguished from pixel art proper.

History

The style developed out of the hardware constraints of early arcade games, home consoles, and personal computers. As high-resolution 3D graphics became standard, pixel art moved from necessity to stylistic choice, thriving in independent games, mobile games, and online art communities.

Techniques

Artists typically work with restricted palettes, manual anti-aliasing, and dithering patterns to simulate additional shades. Images are usually kept small so individual pixels remain visible; when enlarged, nearest-neighbour scaling preserves the crisp, blocky look.
