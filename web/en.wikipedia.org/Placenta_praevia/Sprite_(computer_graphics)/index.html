Sprite (computer graphics)

In computer graphics, a sprite is a two-dimensional bitmap that is integrated into a larger scene, most commonly in a 2D video game. Originally, the term referred to hardware-composited objects that could move independently of the background without redrawing it; modern usage covers any partly transparent 2D image or animation frame manipulated as a single unit.

History

Sprite hardware appeared in arcade systems and home computers of the late 1970s and 1980s, such as the Texas Instruments TMS9918 video chip, the Commodore 64, and the Nintendo Entertainment System. These systems imposed strict limits on the number, size, and colours of sprites per scanline, shaping the look of games of the era.

Animation

Animated characters are produced by cycling through a sequence of sprites stored in a sprite sheet (texture atlas). Transparency is achieved with a mask, an alpha channel, or a designated transparent colour, allowing rectangular images to appear as arbitrary shapes.

Modern usage

In contemporary game engines and web development, sprites are drawn as textured quads and used for 2D games, user-interface elements, and icon sets (CSS sprites). The aesthetic of limited-resolution sprites underlies pixel art.
