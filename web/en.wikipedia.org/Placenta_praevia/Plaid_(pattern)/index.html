Plaid (pattern)

Plaid refers to a checked pattern formed by bands of colour crossing at right angles. In Scottish usage the pattern itself is called tartan, while a plaid is a long piece of tartan cloth worn as a garment or thrown over the shoulder as part of traditional Highland dress. In North American English, "plaid" commonly denotes the pattern in any context.

Weaving

The pattern is produced by weaving dyed warp and weft threads in a simple twill, most often a 2/2 twill. The colour sequence (the sett) repeats across the cloth; where two colours overlap, they mix optically, producing the half-tone effect characteristic of plaids and tartans.

Cultural associations

Beyond Scottish clan tartans, plaid patterns are associated with flannel work shirts in North America, with rustic and outdoors clothing generally, and periodically with high fashion revivals. Certain setts, such as the beige check of Burberry, became trademarks in their own right.
