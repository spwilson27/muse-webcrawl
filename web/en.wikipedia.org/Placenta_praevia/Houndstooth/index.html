Houndstooth

Houndstooth (also called dogstooth, or pied-de-poule in French) is a duotone textile pattern characterised by broken checks or abstract four-pointed shapes, traditionally in black and white. The pattern is formed with a two-over, two-under twill weave using alternating groups of threads in two colours in both warp and weft.

History

Houndstooth originated in the Scottish Lowlands as a woven wool cloth worn by shepherds, where it functioned as a neutral district pattern not tied to any clan. In the 20th century it was adopted by high fashion: Christian Dior used it prominently from the late 1940s, and it became a signature pattern of houses such as Chanel and Alexander McQueen, appearing in suits, coats, and accessories at scales from fine "puppytooth" to oversized prints.

Construction

The weave uses four threads of the dark colour alternating with four threads of the light colour in both directions. The twill structure breaks the regular check of a pattern like gingham into jagged, star-like points. The same pattern can also be knitted or printed.
