Conjunctiva

The conjunctiva is a thin, transparent mucous membrane that lines the inside of the eyelids and covers the sclera (the white of the eye). It extends from the limbus, the border between the cornea and sclera, around the front of the eyeball and back into the folds (fornices) where it reflects onto the inner surface of the eyelids.

Structure

The conjunctiva is divided into the palpebral conjunctiva, lining the eyelids; the bulbar conjunctiva, covering the eyeball; and the fornices, the loose folds joining the two. Histologically it consists of non-keratinized stratified epithelium containing mucus-secreting goblet cells, overlying a vascular connective tissue layer. Its blood vessels are what become visibly engorged when the eye is irritated, producing "bloodshot" eyes.

Function

The conjunctiva helps lubricate the eye by producing mucus and part of the tear film, keeps the eyelid gliding smoothly over the globe, and provides immune surveillance and a physical barrier against microbes and foreign particles.

Disorders

Inflammation of the conjunctiva is called conjunctivitis ("pink eye"), commonly caused by viral or bacterial infection or by allergy. Other conditions include pterygium and pinguecula (growths associated with sun and wind exposure), subconjunctival haemorrhage, dry eye syndromes involving goblet-cell loss, and trachoma, a chlamydial infection that is a leading infectious cause of blindness worldwide.
