Trip hop
Origin: Bristol, England, early 1990s
Key acts: Massive Attack, Portishead, Tricky

Trip hop is a musical genre that originated in the early 1990s in the United Kingdom, especially Bristol. It has been described as a psychedelic fusion of hip hop and electronica with slow tempos and an atmospheric sound. The term was coined by the British music press to describe the experimental work of Bristol acts.

Characteristics

Trip hop tracks typically use downtempo breakbeats sampled from funk and jazz records, deep basslines, strings, Rhodes piano and melancholic female vocals or rapping. Landmark albums include Massive Attack's Blue Lines (1991) and Mezzanine (1998), Portishead's Dummy (1994) and Tricky's Maxinquaye (1995).

Influence

The genre influenced later downtempo and electronic music and was widely used in film, television and advertising; many of its leading artists distanced themselves from the label itself.
