Inti Raymi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Inti Raymi (Quechua for "Inti festival") is a traditional religious ceremony of the Inca Empire in honour of the sun god Inti, the most venerated deity of the Inca religion. It was the most important of the four great festivals of the empire and was celebrated at the winter solstice — around 24 June in the southern hemisphere — marking the Inca new year. After being banned under Spanish colonial rule, Inti Raymi was revived in the 1940s as a theatrical re-enactment and is today the second-largest festival in South America, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors to Cusco, Peru.

The Inca ceremony

According to the chronicler Garcilaso de la Vega, Inti Raymi was instituted by the emperor Pachacuti. The festival lasted about nine days (in its final form, longer) and took place after the maize harvest, when the sun stood farthest from the earth; the Inca fasted for three days and then greeted the rising sun in Cusco's main plaza, the Huacaypata. The Sapa Inca raised a golden goblet of chicha (maize beer) to the sun, poured offerings into channels said to carry them to the Coricancha temple, and presided over sacrifices of llamas whose entrails were used for divination. Celebrants from across the empire attended in their finest dress, with dancing, feasting and the carrying of sacred images (huacas).

Suppression and revival

The last imperial Inti Raymi was held in 1535; Viceroy Francisco de Toledo formally banned the festival in 1572 as a pagan rite contrary to the Catholic faith, though clandestine celebrations continued. In 1944, Faustino Espinoza Navarro and a group of Cusco artists staged a reconstruction based on Garcilaso's chronicle. The modern festival, held annually on 24 June, proceeds in three scenes: the Qoricancha gardens, Cusco's Plaza de Armas, and the culminating ceremony at the fortress of Sacsayhuamán, where an actor playing the Sapa Inca addresses the sun in Quechua. Related celebrations take place in Ecuador, where the festival merges with the feast of San Juan.
