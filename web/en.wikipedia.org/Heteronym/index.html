Heteronym

A heteronym is a word that has a different pronunciation and meaning from another word but the same spelling. The word "heteronym" has several related uses in linguistics and literature, and should not be confused with homonym, a broader category of words that sound or are spelled alike.

Definition (linguistics)Same spelling, different pronunciation and meaning
Exampleslead (metal / to guide), tear (rip / eye drop), wind (air / to coil)

In linguistics

In the linguistic sense, heteronyms are homographs that are not homophones: they are written identically but pronounced differently and have unrelated meanings. Examples in English include bass (a fish, rhyming with "class"; a low sound, rhyming with "base"), bow (to bend; a weapon), desert (arid land; to abandon), minute (a unit of time; tiny), polish (to shine) versus Polish (from Poland), present, produce, record, and row. Many English heteronyms arise because stress shifts distinguish noun/verb pairs, or because words from different origins converged in spelling.

In literature: Pessoa's heteronyms

The Portuguese poet Fernando Pessoa used "heteronym" for the fully fledged fictional authors he wrote as — not mere pseudonyms, but distinct personalities with their own biographies, styles, and worldviews. His major heteronyms include the pastoral classicist Alberto Caeiro, the futurist engineer Álvaro de Campos, and the neoclassicist Ricardo Reis, alongside the "semi-heteronym" Bernardo Soares, author of The Book of Disquiet. Pessoa left a trunk containing some 25,000 manuscript pages attributed to more than seventy such personae.

Other literary uses

The Danish philosopher Søren Kierkegaard published philosophical works under pseudonyms such as Johannes Climacus and Victor Eremita, a practice sometimes described in heteronymic terms because the pseudonymous authors express distinct standpoints with which Kierkegaard did not simply identify.
