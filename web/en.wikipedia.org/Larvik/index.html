Larvik is a town and municipality in Vestfold county, Norway, located on the Larviksfjorden at the mouth of the Lågen (Numedalslågen) river, about 105 kilometres southwest of Oslo. The town received its market charter in 1671 under Ulrik Frederik Gyldenløve, the Danish-Norwegian governor whose residence, the Herregården manor house (1677), still stands as a museum.

Economy and geology

Larvik is internationally known for larvikite, a coarse-grained iridescent syenite quarried around the town and used worldwide as a decorative facing stone on buildings and monuments; its blue schiller effect comes from feldspar crystals. The town is also the source of Farris, Norway's best-known bottled mineral water, drawn from springs in the Farris forest. Ferries connect Larvik with Hirtshals in Denmark, and the port handles timber, stone, and general cargo.

Notable people

Larvik was the birthplace and childhood home of explorer and ethnographer Thor Heyerdahl (1914–2002), leader of the Kon-Tiki expedition. The municipality also encompasses the coastal resort town of Stavern and Mølen, a vast pebble beach and ancient burial site at the end of the Raet moraine, protected as a geotope.
