Pharmacoeconomics

Pharmacoeconomics is the branch of health economics that evaluates the cost and consequences of pharmaceutical products and services. It compares the value of one drug or therapy to another to guide decisions by health systems, insurers, and governments about which treatments to fund.

Methods

Standard techniques include cost-minimisation analysis, cost-effectiveness analysis, cost-utility analysis — which expresses outcomes in quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) — and cost-benefit analysis. Results are often summarised as an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER), the extra cost per extra unit of health gained.

Applications

Health technology assessment bodies such as the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) in England use pharmacoeconomic evidence in reimbursement recommendations, so the discipline plays a central role in drug pricing and market access.
