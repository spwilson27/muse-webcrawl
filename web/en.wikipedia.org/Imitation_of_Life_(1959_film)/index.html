Imitation of Life is a 1959 American drama film directed by Douglas Sirk, produced by Ross Hunter and released by Universal International. It was Sirk's final Hollywood film and one of the biggest box-office successes of his career. It is the second film adaptation of Fannie Hurst's 1933 novel, following the 1934 version.

Plot

Aspiring actress Lora Meredith meets Annie Johnson, a Black widow, at Coney Island; Annie becomes her housekeeper and helps raise Lora's daughter Susie while Lora's career rises. Annie's light-skinned daughter Sarah Jane struggles painfully with her racial identity, passing for white and rejecting her mother, with tragic consequences.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial success. Juanita Moore and Susan Kohner received Academy Award nominations for their performances. In 2015 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant.
