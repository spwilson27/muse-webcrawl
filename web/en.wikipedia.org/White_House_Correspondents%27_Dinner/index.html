White House Correspondents' Dinner
First held: 1921
Location: Washington Hilton, Washington, D.C.
Organizer: White House Correspondents' Association
Frequency: Annual

The White House Correspondents' Dinner is an annual dinner held by the White House Correspondents' Association (WHCA) in Washington, D.C. Traditionally attended by the president and vice president of the United States, along with journalists, politicians, and celebrities, it raises scholarship funds and presents journalism awards — and is famous for the comedy performed there.

Tradition and notable moments

The evening features a comedian who roasts the president and the press, followed by the president's own humorous speech, often including self-deprecating jokes and videos. Memorable moments include Stephen Colbert's 2006 performance in character beside George W. Bush; Barack Obama's 2011 jokes about Donald Trump's birther claims, delivered shortly before the announcement of the raid that killed Osama bin Laden; and Obama's 2016 "mic drop." President Donald Trump declined to attend from 2017, and the dinner was cancelled in 2020 and 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic before returning in 2022. Critics periodically charge that the event displays overly cosy relations between the press and the powerful.
