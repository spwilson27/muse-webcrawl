Pulakeshin II

Pulakeshin II (reigned c. 610–642 CE), also spelled Pulakesin II, was the most famous ruler of the Chalukya dynasty of Vatapi (modern Badami) in the Deccan region of India. His reign marked the expansion of Chalukya power across much of peninsular India, and he is best remembered for successfully resisting the northward push of the powerful emperor Harsha of Kanauj.

Early life and accession

Pulakeshin was the son of Kirtivarman I. After the death of his uncle Mangalesha, who had attempted to pass the throne to his own son, Pulakeshin seized power around 610 CE. He then campaigned to consolidate the kingdom, subduing rebellious feudatories including the Kadambas, the Gangas, the Mauryas of the Konkan, and the Alupas.

Wars and conquests

Pulakeshin II extended Chalukya authority over the Latas and Gurjaras of Gujarat, the Malavas, and the kingdoms of Kosala and Kalinga. His most celebrated achievement was the defeat of Harsha Vardhana on the banks of the Narmada River around 618–619 CE, which halted Harsha's expansion into the Deccan and fixed the Narmada as the boundary between their empires. In the east, he annexed the territory of the Vishnukundinas and installed his younger brother Kubja Vishnuvardhana as viceroy, founding the Eastern Chalukya line. In the south he fought the Pallavas of Kanchipuram, defeating Mahendravarman I, though the conflict would outlast him.

Court and culture

The Aihole inscription of 634 CE, composed by his court poet Ravikirti, records his victories in ornate Sanskrit verse and is a key source for his reign. The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang visited the Chalukya kingdom and praised Pulakeshin's power and the prosperity of his subjects. His court maintained diplomatic contact as far away as Persia; a famous painting at Ajanta is often interpreted as depicting Persian envoys received at his court.

Death and legacy

Around 642 CE the Pallava king Narasimhavarman I invaded the Chalukya kingdom, captured and sacked Vatapi, and assumed the title Vatapikonda. Pulakeshin II appears to have died in this catastrophe, and Chalukya power was eclipsed for over a decade until Vikramaditya I restored it. Pulakeshin II is nonetheless regarded as one of the great kings of early medieval India and the architect of the first large Deccan empire.
