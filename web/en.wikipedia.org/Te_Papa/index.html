The Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa, commonly shortened to Te Papa, is New Zealand's national museum and national art gallery, located in Wellington. Its name translates roughly as "the place of treasures of this land". It is renowned for its bicultural philosophy, which places Māori culture and partnership between Māori and Pākehā at the centre of the institution.

History

Te Papa was created by the Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa Act 1992, merging the Dominion Museum (founded 1865 as the Colonial Museum) with the National Art Gallery. The new waterfront building on Cable Street, designed by Jasmax with strong input from Māori designers, opened on 14 February 1998. Entry to the permanent exhibitions is free, and the museum attracts well over a million visitors in most years.

Collections and exhibitions

Te Papa's collections span natural history (including a rare colossal squid specimen), Māori and Pacific taonga (treasures), New Zealand history, and art. Long-running exhibitions include Te Marae, a contemporary carved meeting house; Gallipoli: The Scale of Our War (2015), developed with Weta Workshop and notable for giant hyper-realistic figures of soldiers; and Te Taiao | Nature. The museum also leads the Karanga Aotearoa repatriation programme, which returns Māori and Moriori ancestral remains from overseas institutions.
