The Carnegie Endowment for International Peace (CEIP) is a nonpartisan international affairs think tank headquartered in Washington, D.C. Founded in 1910 by industrialist and philanthropist Andrew Carnegie with a gift of $10 million, it was dedicated to advancing cooperation between nations and promoting active international engagement by the United States.

History

Its first president was Elihu Root, former secretary of state and Nobel Peace laureate. The Endowment supported international law, helped foster the Hague tribunal system, and conducted studies on the causes and costs of war. In the 1990s, long-time president Morton Abramowitz and then Jessica Mathews broadened its agenda; under William J. Burns it recast itself as the first global think tank.

Global network

Beyond Washington, the Endowment has operated centers including the Carnegie Moscow Center (closed by order of the Russian government in 2022), Carnegie Europe in Brussels, centers in Beirut, Beijing, and New Delhi, and publishes Foreign Policy-associated research as well as the journal materials long associated with its scholars.

Research

Programs cover democracy and governance, nuclear policy, energy and climate, Asia, Russia and Eurasia, the Middle East, and technology and international affairs.
