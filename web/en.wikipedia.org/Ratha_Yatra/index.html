Ratha Yatra

Ratha Yatra (Odia: ରଥଯାତ୍ରା), also called the Car Festival, is a Hindu festival in which the deities Jagannath, his brother Balabhadra, and his sister Subhadra are drawn through the streets on enormous wooden chariots. The most famous celebration takes place at Puri, Odisha, where it has been held for centuries and draws over a million pilgrims.

The Puri festival

Each year new chariots — Nandighosa for Jagannath (45 feet tall, with 16 wheels), Taladhwaja for Balabhadra, and Darpadalana for Subhadra — are built from sacred neem wood. The deities travel from the Jagannath Temple to the Gundicha Temple, stay about a week, and then return in the Bahuda Yatra. Pulling the ropes is believed to confer great merit; the English word "juggernaut" derives from Jagannath and these unstoppable-seeming chariots.

Other celebrations

Ratha Yatras are held across India — notably at Mahesh, Ahmedabad, and Baripada — and worldwide, where ISKCON has staged the festival in cities such as London, New York, and Los Angeles since the late 1960s.
