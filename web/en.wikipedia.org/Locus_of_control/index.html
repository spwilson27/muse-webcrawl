Locus of control

Locus of control is a concept in personality psychology referring to the degree to which people believe they, as opposed to external forces, have control over the outcome of events in their lives.

Concept

Developed by Julian B. Rotter in 1954 within social learning theory, the construct distinguishes an internal locus — believing outcomes depend on one's own actions — from an external locus — attributing outcomes to luck, fate or powerful others.

Measurement

Rotter's Internal–External scale is a forced-choice questionnaire. Later scales separate dimensions such as chance and powerful others, and domain-specific versions exist for health and work.

Correlates

Internal locus is associated with better academic achievement, health behaviours and coping, while strong external beliefs correlate with stress and learned helplessness. The construct has been critiqued for oversimplifying beliefs and for cultural bias.
