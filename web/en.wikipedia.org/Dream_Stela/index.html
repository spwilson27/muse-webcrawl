Dream Stela

The Dream Stela, also known as the Sphinx Stela, is an epigraphic stele erected between the front paws of the Great Sphinx of Giza by the ancient Egyptian pharaoh Thutmose IV during the New Kingdom, in the first year of his reign, conventionally dated to 1401 BC.

Text

The partly damaged inscription tells how the young prince Thutmose, out hunting, rested at midday in the shadow of the Sphinx — then regarded as the god Horemakhet — and fell asleep. In a dream the god spoke to him, promising him the kingship of Egypt if he cleared away the sand that had engulfed the statue. The stela thus served as royal propaganda legitimising Thutmose's accession, which may not have followed the expected line of succession.

Description

The round-topped granite stela stands about 3.6 metres high. Its lunette shows Thutmose IV making offerings to the Sphinx on either side. When uncovered in modern times much of the text was already flaking away; early copies preserve passages now lost. The clearing of sand it records was the first of several restorations of the Sphinx, which was repeatedly buried by desert sand over the millennia.
