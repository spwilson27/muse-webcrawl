Gelon (died 478 BC), son of Deinomenes, was a Greek tyrant of Gela and Syracuse in Sicily. He first served as cavalry commander under Hippocrates, tyrant of Gela, and succeeded him in 491 BC. In 485 BC he seized Syracuse and transferred his seat of power there, repopulating and greatly enlarging the city with inhabitants from conquered neighbours, while leaving Gela to his brother Hiero.

Victory at Himera

Gelon is most famous for his decisive defeat of a great Carthaginian expedition under Hamilcar at the Battle of Himera in 480 BC — said by later tradition to have occurred on the same day as the Battle of Salamis. The victory made Syracuse the leading Greek power in Sicily, and Gelon was celebrated throughout the Greek world, dedicating offerings at Delphi and Olympia. Herodotus recounts that when mainland Greek envoys sought his help against Xerxes, Gelon offered a massive force but demanded command, and was refused.

Reputation

Unlike many tyrants, Gelon was remembered as a comparatively mild and capable ruler; the Syracusans honoured him after death with a hero's tomb, and later tradition treated him as one of the greatest of Sicilian Greeks.
