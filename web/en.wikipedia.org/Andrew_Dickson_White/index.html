Andrew Dickson White (7 November 1832 – 4 November 1918) was an American historian and educator who co-founded Cornell University with Ezra Cornell and served as its first president. He also served as a state senator, and as United States minister and ambassador to Germany and Russia.

Cornell University

After teaching history at the University of Michigan, White returned to New York and, as a state senator, worked with Ezra Cornell to charter a new university in Ithaca in 1865. As its first president (1866–1885), White championed coeducation, a broad curriculum joining the liberal arts with agriculture and engineering, and admission without regard to religion — the principle summed up in Ezra Cornell's aim of an institution "where any person can find instruction in any study."

Diplomatic service and scholarship

White served as US minister to Germany (1879–1881), minister to Russia (1892–1894), and ambassador to Germany (1897–1902), and he led the American delegation to the Hague Peace Conference of 1899. His best-known scholarly work, A History of the Warfare of Science with Theology in Christendom (1896), popularized the "conflict thesis" of the historical relationship between science and religion.

Legacy

White's home on the Cornell campus and his great book collection, now part of Cornell's library, remain central to the university he helped create.
