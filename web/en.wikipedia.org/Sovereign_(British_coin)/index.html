The sovereign is a British gold coin with a nominal value of one pound sterling. First issued in 1489 for Henry VII, the modern sovereign dates from the Great Recoinage of 1816 and was struck from 1817. It became the principal gold coin of the British Empire under the gold standard, minted at branch mints across the empire including Sydney, Melbourne, Ottawa, Bombay and Pretoria.

Design

The classic reverse shows Saint George slaying the dragon, designed by the Italian engraver Benedetto Pistrucci and first used in 1817. The obverse bears the portrait of the reigning monarch. Sovereigns are struck in 22-carat gold to a standard weight of 7.988 grams.

History

Regular circulation issues ceased after 1932 with the abandonment of the gold standard, but the Royal Mint continued proof and later bullion issues. Today sovereigns are sold as bullion and collectible coins, and in the United Kingdom they are exempt from capital gains tax because they remain legal tender. Fractional and multiple denominations include the half sovereign, double sovereign and quintuple sovereign.
