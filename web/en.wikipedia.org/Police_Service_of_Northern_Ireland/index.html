The Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) is the police service responsible for law enforcement and the prevention of crime within Northern Ireland. It was established in 2001 as the successor to the Royal Ulster Constabulary (RUC), as part of the policing reforms recommended by the Patten Report following the Good Friday Agreement.

Reform and composition

The Patten reforms introduced a new name, badge, and oversight structures including the Northern Ireland Policing Board and a Police Ombudsman. For a decade, recruitment followed a 50:50 policy of Catholic and non-Catholic officers to increase representation of the nationalist community, which had historically been underrepresented in the RUC.

Role

The PSNI polices a population of about 1.9 million people. Unlike most UK forces, its officers routinely carry firearms, reflecting the continuing threat from dissident paramilitary groups. The service motto is "Keeping People Safe".
