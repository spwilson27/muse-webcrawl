Mount Monadnock

Mount Monadnock, or Grand Monadnock, is a mountain in the towns of Jaffrey and Dublin in south-western New Hampshire, United States. Rising to 3,165 feet (965 m) and standing isolated above the surrounding landscape, it is the type example of a monadnock — an isolated residual hill of resistant rock — a geomorphological term derived from its name.

It is among the most frequently climbed mountains in the world, often said to be second only to Mount Fuji in Japan.

Geology and summit

Monadnock is formed of erosion-resistant schist and quartzite of the Littleton Formation. Its bare, rocky summit is not natural: in the early nineteenth century local farmers, fearing wolves, set fires that burned away the topsoil and vegetation, and wind and rain prevented its return, leaving the open ledges prized by hikers today.

On clear days the summit offers views of all six New England states, including the Boston skyline and Mount Washington.

History and culture

The name derives from an Abenaki word usually translated as 'mountain that stands alone'. The mountain was written about by Ralph Waldo Emerson and Henry David Thoreau, who climbed it several times, and it features in the work of H. P. Lovecraft (whose story 'The Whisperer in Darkness' references the area).

A network of about 40 miles of trails, including the popular White Dot and White Cross routes from Monadnock State Park, serves an estimated 80,000–125,000 hikers a year. The Metacomet-Monadnock Trail terminates (or begins) at its summit.
