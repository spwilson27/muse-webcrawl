Peronism

Peronism (Spanish: Peronismo), also known as Justicialism, is an Argentine political movement based on the ideas and legacy of President Juan Perón (1895–1974) and his second wife Eva Perón. It has been an influential force in Argentine politics since its introduction in the early 1940s, embodied chiefly by the Justicialist Party, and it has historically been opposed by anti-Peronism, a similarly durable current.

Ideology

Perón presented Justicialism as a "third position" between capitalism and communism, summarised in the "three flags" of political sovereignty, economic independence and social justice. In practice his governments (1946–1955) combined state-led industrialisation, expanded labour rights and social welfare, and an alliance with the trade-union movement and the descamisados ("shirtless ones", the working poor). Eva Perón's foundation distributed welfare on a vast scale, and women's suffrage was enacted in 1947.

History

Perón was overthrown by a military coup in 1955 and spent 18 years in exile while Peronism was banned, yet it survived as the identity of much of the labour movement and returned to power with his brief final presidency (1973–74). Since the return of democracy in 1983, Peronist or Peronist-led governments — under Carlos Menem, Néstor Kirchner, Cristina Fernández de Kirchner and Alberto Fernández — have alternated in power with non-Peronist administrations. The movement encompasses diverse factions, from Menem's market-oriented liberalism to the left-populist Kirchnerism, united mainly by loyalty to Perón's legacy.
