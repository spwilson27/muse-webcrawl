James Abercrombie (1706 – 23 April 1781) was a British Army general and commander-in-chief of forces in North America during the early part of the French and Indian War, best known for the disastrous British assault on Fort Ticonderoga in 1758.

Early career

Abercrombie entered the army as a youth and rose through staff appointments and political patronage, serving as aide-de-camp to senior commanders and sitting in Parliament for Banffshire.

North America

Appointed commander-in-chief in 1758, Abercrombie led an army of some 16,000 men against the French-held Fort Carillon (Ticonderoga). On 8 July 1758 he ordered repeated frontal assaults on entrenched French positions without waiting for his artillery; the attack failed with around 2,000 casualties, one of the bloodiest battles of the war. He was replaced by Jeffery Amherst later that year.

Later life

Despite the defeat, Abercrombie was promoted to lieutenant general in 1759 and full general in 1772. He died at Stirling in 1781.
