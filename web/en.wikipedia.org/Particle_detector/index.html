Particle detector

A particle detector, in experimental and applied particle physics, nuclear physics, and nuclear engineering, is a device used to detect and identify high-energy particles, such as those produced by nuclear decay, cosmic radiation, or reactions in a particle accelerator.

Principles

Most detectors exploit the ionization or excitation produced when a charged particle passes through matter. The resulting signals—electrical pulses, scintillation light, or visible tracks—are recorded and analysed to infer the particle's energy, momentum, trajectory, and identity.

Types

Historical detectors include cloud chambers, bubble chambers, photographic emulsions, and Geiger counters. Modern collider detectors are layered general-purpose instruments combining silicon trackers, calorimeters, and muon systems; examples include ATLAS and CMS at the Large Hadron Collider. Other designs include scintillation counters, Cherenkov detectors, drift chambers, and semiconductor detectors.

Applications

Beyond fundamental research, particle detection techniques are used in radiation monitoring, medical imaging, nuclear security, and geophysics, including muon tomography of large structures.
