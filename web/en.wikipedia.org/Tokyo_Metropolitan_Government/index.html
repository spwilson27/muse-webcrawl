Tokyo Metropolitan Government

The Tokyo Metropolitan Government is the government of Tokyo Metropolis, one of Japan's 47 prefectures but with a special structure reflecting its status as the capital and largest city. It administers the 23 special wards, 26 cities, 5 towns and 8 villages of the metropolis, home to roughly 14 million people.

Structure

The government is headed by the Governor of Tokyo, directly elected to a four-year term, and a unicameral legislature, the Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly, with 127 members. Its headquarters since 1991 is the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building in Nishi-Shinjuku, a twin-towered complex designed by architect Kenzō Tange and nicknamed "Tax Tower" for its cost.

History

The metropolis (to) was created in 1943 by merging the former Tokyo City with Tokyo Prefecture during wartime centralisation. After the Second World War the system's elected governorship was restored under the 1947 Local Autonomy Law. With an annual budget comparable to that of a mid-sized nation, the metropolitan government handles transport (including the Toei subway), waterworks, hospitals, and events such as the 2020 Summer Olympics.
