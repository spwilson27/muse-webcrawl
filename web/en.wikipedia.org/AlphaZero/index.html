AlphaZero

AlphaZero is a computer program developed by artificial intelligence research company DeepMind to master the games of chess, shogi and Go. This algorithm uses an approach similar to AlphaGo Zero. On December 5, 2017, the DeepMind team released a preprint introducing AlphaZero, which achieved superhuman performance in all three games within 24 hours of training by defeating the world-champion programs Stockfish, Elmo, and the three-day version of AlphaGo Zero in matches.

Method

AlphaZero starts from a blank slate: given only the rules of the game, it learns by playing games against itself using reinforcement learning, guided by Monte Carlo tree search. A single neural network architecture, with game-specific input encodings and move representations, is used for all three games — the program receives no game-specific knowledge beyond the basic rules, no opening books, and no endgame tablebases. This generality contrasted with specialised engines such as Stockfish, which embody decades of hand-tuned chess knowledge.

Results and reception

In the published results, in chess AlphaZero won a 1,000-game match against Stockfish without a single loss; it also decisively defeated Elmo in shogi and the three-day AlphaGo Zero in Go. Grandmasters praised AlphaZero's dynamic, sacrificial style of chess, and its games influenced human play. Critics noted questions about the match conditions, including the hardware and time controls given to Stockfish. The work, published in Science in 2018, was influential in establishing self-play reinforcement learning as a general technique, and the approach was later generalised by MuZero, which learns the rules of the environment as well.
