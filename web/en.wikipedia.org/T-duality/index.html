T-duality

T-duality (short for target-space duality) is an equivalence of two physical theories — in particular two string theories — compactified on circles of reciprocal radii. A string theory defined on a circle of radius R is equivalent to the same (or a different) theory on a circle of radius α′/R, where α′ is the string tension parameter. The duality exchanges momentum modes of the string with winding modes.

Overview

A closed string on a circle can wrap around the circle an integer number of times, giving rise to winding states whose energy grows with the radius. Quantised momentum states, by contrast, have energies inversely proportional to the radius. T-duality maps the full spectrum of the theory at radius R onto that at radius α′/R by interchanging these two families of states, showing that very small and very large compact dimensions can be physically indistinguishable in string theory.

Relations between string theories

T-duality relates the type IIA and type IIB superstring theories, and likewise the two heterotic string theories. Together with S-duality, it forms part of the web of dualities that motivated the proposal of M-theory as a unifying framework.

Mirror symmetry

Applied fibrewise to Calabi–Yau manifolds, T-duality underlies the SYZ approach to mirror symmetry, in which a Calabi–Yau manifold is viewed as a torus fibration and dualised along its fibres.
