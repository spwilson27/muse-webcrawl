Sežana

Country: Slovenia

Region: Littoral (Karst)

Status: Town and municipal seat

Sežana (pronounced [sɛˈʒaːna]; Italian: Sesana) is a town in the Slovenian Littoral region of western Slovenia, near the border with Italy. It is the seat of the Municipality of Sežana and an important centre of the Karst Plateau.

Geography

The town lies on the Karst Plateau close to the Italian border and the city of Trieste, to which it is connected by road and rail. The surrounding karst landscape is known for its limestone formations, caves and the Teran wine produced in local vineyards.

History

Sežana developed as a stop on routes between Trieste and the interior and grew with the arrival of the railway in the 19th century. After the First World War it belonged to Italy, and after the Second World War it became part of Yugoslavia and then independent Slovenia in 1991.

Sights

Nearby attractions include the Lipica stud farm, home of the Lipizzaner horses, just a few kilometres away, and the Škocjan Caves, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The town has a botanical garden established in the 19th century.
