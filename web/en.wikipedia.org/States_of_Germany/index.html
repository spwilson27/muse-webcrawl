States of Germany

The sixteen Länder of the Federal Republic of Germany

The Federal Republic of Germany is a federation of sixteen states, called Länder (singular Land). Each Land has its own constitution, parliament (Landtag), and government headed by a Minister President, and is represented at the federal level through the Bundesrat. The states hold principal responsibility for education, policing, culture, and local government under the Basic Law.

The sixteen states

The states are Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria (Bayern), Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen, Hamburg, Hesse (Hessen), Lower Saxony (Niedersachsen), Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, North Rhine-Westphalia (Nordrhein-Westfalen), Rhineland-Palatinate (Rheinland-Pfalz), Saarland, Saxony (Sachsen), Saxony-Anhalt (Sachsen-Anhalt), Schleswig-Holstein, and Thuringia (Thüringen). Berlin, Hamburg, and Bremen are city-states. Bavaria, Saxony, and Thuringia style themselves "Free States" (Freistaat), a historic title with no legal distinction.

History

Most western Länder were formed or reconstituted by the occupying powers after 1945 from historic German states such as Prussia, Bavaria, and Saxony. East Germany abolished its Länder in 1952 in favour of districts, but they were restored in 1990 just before reunification, bringing the total to sixteen. North Rhine-Westphalia is the most populous state (about 18 million), Bavaria the largest by area, and Bremen the smallest on both measures.

Government and finance

States administer most federal law, run the courts and universities, and share in tax revenue through a system of fiscal equalisation that redistributes funds from wealthier to poorer Länder — a perennial subject of political debate. State elections staggered through the federal term strongly shape national politics via the Bundesrat.
