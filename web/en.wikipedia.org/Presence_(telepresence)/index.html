Presence (telepresence)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

FieldMedia studies, human–computer interaction
DefinitionSubjective sense of "being there" in a mediated environment
Related termsTelepresence, immersion, social presence

Presence, in the study of mediated communication and virtual reality, is the subjective experience of being in one place or environment while physically situated in another, produced through technology. It is often summarised as the perceptual illusion of non-mediation: the user responds to the mediated environment as if it were real.

Definitions and types

Researchers distinguish several forms of presence. Spatial presence is the sense of being physically located in the virtual space. Social presence is the degree to which a medium conveys the sense of interacting with another real person. Self-presence concerns identification with a virtual body or avatar. The term "telepresence", popularised by Marvin Minsky in 1980, originally described remote operation of physical systems with a strong sense of being at the remote site.

Contributing factors

Presence is influenced by media properties—sensory richness, field of view, tracking fidelity, low latency and interactivity—and by user factors such as attention, involvement and prior experience. "Breaks in presence" occur when technical glitches or reminders of mediation disrupt the illusion.

Applications and research

Presence research informs the design of VR and AR systems, video conferencing, telemedicine, games and exposure therapies for phobias and PTSD, where a strong sense of presence helps patients confront feared situations. Measurement typically combines self-report questionnaires with behavioural and physiological indicators.
