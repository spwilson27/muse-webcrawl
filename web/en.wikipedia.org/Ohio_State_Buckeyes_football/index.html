Ohio State Buckeyes football

The Ohio State Buckeyes football team competes in American college football as a representative of Ohio State University. The Buckeyes compete in the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) and are founding members of the Big Ten Conference. Their home field is Ohio Stadium, one of the largest stadiums in the world, and their head coach as of the mid-2020s is Ryan Day.

History

Ohio State fielded its first football team in 1890. The program rose to national prominence under coaches such as John Wilce, Francis Schmidt, and Paul Brown, who led the Buckeyes to their first national championship in 1942. Woody Hayes coached from 1951 to 1978, winning five national championships and establishing the intense rivalry with Michigan known simply as "The Game". Later coaches included Earle Bruce, John Cooper, Jim Tressel (2002 national title), and Urban Meyer (2014 national title, won in the inaugural College Football Playoff).

Championships and honours

Ohio State claims nine national championships and has won numerous Big Ten titles. Buckeye players have won seven Heisman Trophies, including Archie Griffin, the only two-time winner (1974 and 1975). The program consistently ranks among the leaders in attendance, NFL draft selections, and all-time wins.

Traditions

Traditions include the dotting of the "i" in "Script Ohio" by The Ohio State University Marching Band, gold pants charms awarded for beating Michigan, the singing of "Carmen Ohio", and Buckeye leaves awarded on player helmets for exceptional plays.
