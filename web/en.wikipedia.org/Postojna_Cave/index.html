Postojna Cave

Postojna Cave (Slovene: Postojnska jama) is a 24-kilometre-long karst cave system near Postojna in southwestern Slovenia, carved by the Pivka River. It is the country's most famous show cave and one of the most visited tourist caves in Europe, with a recorded history of visits stretching back to the seventeenth century and over 38 million visitors in total.

History and features

Although signatures in the cave date to the 1200s, the great inner passages were discovered in 1818 by local lamplighter Luka Čeč, ahead of a visit by Emperor Francis I of Austria. In 1872 the cave installed the world's first underground railway for tourists; today's electric trains carry visitors several kilometres into the system. Highlights include the Great Mountain, the Russian Bridge, the brilliantly white "Brilliant" stalagmite, and the Concert Hall, said to hold 10,000 people.

Fauna

The cave is a biodiversity hotspot of subterranean life. Its most famous resident is the olm (Proteus anguinus), the blind, pale "human fish" salamander endemic to Dinaric karst caves; Postojna also yielded the first cave beetle ever described, the slenderneck beetle Leptodirus hochenwartii (1832). A vivarium displays cave animals to visitors.
