Gdańsk (Polish: [ɡdaj̆sk]; Kashubian: Gduńsk; German: Danzig) is a city on the Baltic coast of northern Poland. With a population of 486,022, it is the capital and largest city of the Pomeranian Voivodeship and the most prominent city in the geographical region of Pomerania. It is Poland's principal seaport and the country's fourth-largest metropolitan area. The city is at the southern edge of Gdańsk Bay, close to the city of Gdynia and the resort town of Sopot; with these it forms a metropolitan area called the Tricity (Trójmiasto), with a population of approximately 1.5 million.

History

Gdańsk was first mentioned in 997 and grew into an important trading city. It joined the Hanseatic League in the 14th century and, after the Thirteen Years' War, became part of the Kingdom of Poland in 1466, enjoying wide autonomy as a wealthy port city of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. In the Partitions of Poland it passed to Prussia, then became the Free City of Danzig under the League of Nations after World War I. The German demand for the city and the invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939, beginning with the assault on the Polish post office and Westerplatte, marked the start of World War II. After the war, Gdańsk was placed under Polish administration and rebuilt; its old town was extensively reconstructed. In 1980 the Lenin Shipyard strike led to the founding of the Solidarity movement, a key force in the fall of communism in Central Europe.

Economy

The Port of Gdańsk is the largest port in Poland and one of the largest on the Baltic Sea, handling cargo including containers, oil and bulk goods. The city is also a centre of amber trade — the surrounding coast is rich in amber deposits — as well as shipbuilding, petrochemicals, IT and tourism.

Landmarks

The Main Town, largely rebuilt after wartime destruction, contains the Long Market, the Main Town Hall, the Neptune's Fountain, St. Mary's Church (one of the largest brick churches in the world), the Gdańsk Crane over the Motława River, and the European Solidarity Centre.
