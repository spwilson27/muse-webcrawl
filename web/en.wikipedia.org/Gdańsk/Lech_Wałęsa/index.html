Lech Wałęsa

Lech Wałęsa (born 29 September 1943) is a Polish statesman, dissident and retired politician who co-founded and led Solidarity, the Soviet bloc's first independent trade union. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1983 and served as President of Poland from 1990 to 1995.

Early life and activism

Wałęsa was born in Popowo and trained as an electrician. From 1967 he worked at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk, where he took part in the 1970 protests in which workers were shot by the communist authorities. Dismissed in 1976 for his activism, he co-founded the Free Trade Unions of the Coast in 1978 and was repeatedly arrested.

Solidarity

In August 1980 Wałęsa climbed the fence of the striking Gdańsk Shipyard and led the occupation that produced the Gdańsk Agreement and the legal registration of Solidarity, which grew to some 10 million members. When the government declared martial law in December 1981 he was interned for nearly a year. Awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1983, he remained the symbolic leader of the underground movement, and in 1988–89 led the opposition at the Round Table Talks that paved the way to semi-free elections and the end of communist rule in Poland.

Presidency and later life

Wałęsa won the 1990 presidential election and oversaw Poland's transition to a market economy and withdrawal of Soviet troops, but lost the 1995 election to Aleksander Kwaśniewski amid political fragmentation. He has remained a public figure through the Lech Wałęsa Institute, though his legacy is debated because of allegations of 1970s collaboration with the security service, which he denies. Gdańsk's airport is named in his honour.
