Gdańsk Shipyard

The Gdańsk Shipyard (Polish: Stocznia Gdańska), formerly the Lenin Shipyard (Stocznia Gdańska im. Lenina), is a large shipyard in Gdańsk, Poland, famous as the birthplace of the Solidarity trade union movement in 1980.

History

The yard was built on the site of the former German Schichau-Werke and Danziger Werft shipyards, which were merged after 1945 under Polish state ownership. Renamed for Vladimir Lenin in 1967, it employed some 16,000 workers at its peak and built ships mainly for the Soviet Union and other Eastern Bloc customers.

Strikes and Solidarity

In December 1970, shipyard workers joined protests against food price rises; demonstrators were shot by the authorities, and the fallen are commemorated by the Monument of the Fallen Shipyard Workers, unveiled in 1980. On 14 August 1980 the yard struck again, sparked by the dismissal of crane operator Anna Walentynowicz. Under electrician Lech Wałęsa the strike committee issued 21 demands and, on 31 August 1980, signed the Gdańsk Agreement with the government, legalising independent trade unions and giving birth to Solidarity.

After 1989

Following the end of communism the yard struggled commercially, went through bankruptcy proceedings in 1996 and repeated restructuring, and survives on a reduced scale with ship repair, steel structures and offshore work. The historic grounds now also host the European Solidarity Centre, opened in 2014, and the shipyard area has been added to Poland's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status.
