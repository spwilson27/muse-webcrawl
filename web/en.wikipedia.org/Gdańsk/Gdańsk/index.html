Gdańsk

Gdańsk (Polish: [ɡdaɲsk]; German: Danzig) is a city on the Baltic coast of northern Poland. With a population of about 486,000, it is the capital and largest city of the Pomeranian Voivodeship and Poland's principal seaport. Together with Gdynia and Sopot it forms the Tricity metropolitan area.

History

First mentioned in 997 in the Life of Saint Adalbert of Prague, Gdańsk grew as a Slavic and later German-influenced trading port at the mouth of the Vistula. It belonged successively to the Polish Crown, the Teutonic Order, and again to Poland (1466–1793) as a wealthy autonomous member of the Hanseatic League, then to Prussia and Germany as Danzig, and between the world wars it was the Free City of Danzig under the League of Nations.

On 1 September 1939 the German battleship Schleswig-Holstein shelled the Polish garrison at Westerplatte in Gdańsk harbour — the first shots of the Second World War. The city was devastated in 1945, transferred to Poland, and its German population expelled; its historic centre was painstakingly rebuilt in the following decades.

Solidarity

In August 1980, strikes at the Gdańsk Shipyard led by Lech Wałęsa produced the Gdańsk Agreement and the founding of Solidarity, the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc — a movement that contributed decisively to the fall of communism in Eastern Europe in 1989.

Economy and sights

The Port of Gdańsk is the largest Polish seaport. Landmarks include the Long Market (Długi Targ) with Neptune's Fountain, St Mary's Church — among the largest brick churches in the world — the medieval Gdańsk Crane, and the European Solidarity Centre, opened in 2014 on the shipyard grounds.
