Martial law in Poland

Martial law in Poland (Polish: Stan wojenny w Polsce) refers to the period between 13 December 1981 and 22 July 1983, when the authoritarian communist government of the Polish People's Republic drastically restricted everyday life by introducing martial law and a military junta in an attempt to crush the Solidarity pro-democracy movement.

Declaration

In the early hours of 13 December 1981, General Wojciech Jaruzelski, head of government and of the Polish United Workers' Party, announced martial law in a televised address. Power passed to the Military Council of National Salvation (WRON). Army and ZOMO riot-police units occupied streets, telephones were cut, borders closed, and some 10,000 Solidarity activists — including Lech Wałęsa — were interned. Solidarity was suspended and formally delegalised in October 1982.

Repression

Strikes were broken by force, most bloodily at the Wujek coal mine on 16 December 1981, where nine miners were shot dead. Around 100 people died as a result of the crackdown and the underground resistance it drove. The United States and other Western governments imposed economic sanctions on Poland in response.

Aftermath

Martial law was suspended on 31 December 1982 and formally lifted on 22 July 1983, though many restrictive laws remained. The regime justified the move as preventing a Soviet invasion, a claim historians continue to debate. The failure to destroy Solidarity ultimately led to the Round Table Talks and the semi-free elections of June 1989. In 1992 the Sejm declared the imposition of martial law illegal, and Jaruzelski was later tried for his role, though proceedings ended without a verdict.
