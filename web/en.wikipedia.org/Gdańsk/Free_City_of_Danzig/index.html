The Free City of Danzig (German: Freie Stadt Danzig; Polish: Wolne Miasto Gdańsk) was a semi-autonomous city-state that existed between 1920 and 1939, consisting of the Baltic Sea port of Danzig (now Gdańsk, Poland) and nearly 200 nearby settlements. It was created under the Treaty of Versailles as a compromise between the competing claims of Germany and Poland.

Establishment

Neither fully German nor Polish territory, the Free City was placed under the protection of the League of Nations, with its foreign relations and customs largely handled by Poland, which also gained free use of the port. A High Commissioner of the League oversaw disputes. The city had its own elected parliament (the Volkstag), senate, constitution and currency, but the Polish minority and Poland's economic rights were constant sources of friction with the majority German population.

Rise of the Nazis and end

During the 1930s the local Nazi Party gained power and aligned the city ever more closely with Adolf Hitler's Germany, persecuting Jews and the Polish minority. On 1 September 1939 Germany attacked the Polish post office in Danzig and the Westerplatte depot, opening the Second World War; the Free City was formally annexed to the Reich the following day. After 1945 the city became Polish Gdańsk and its German population was expelled.
