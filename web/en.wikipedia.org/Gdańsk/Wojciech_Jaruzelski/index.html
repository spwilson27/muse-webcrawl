Wojciech Jaruzelski

Born6 July 1923 (Kurów, Poland)
Died25 May 2014 (Warsaw, Poland)
OfficesFirst Secretary of the Polish United Workers' Party (1981–89); President of Poland (1989–90)
Military servicePolish People's Army; Minister of Defence (1968–83)

Wojciech Witold Jaruzelski (6 July 1923 – 25 May 2014) was a Polish military officer, politician and de facto leader of the Polish People's Republic from 1981 to 1989. He imposed martial law in December 1981 to crush the Solidarity movement, and a decade later presided over the negotiated transition that ended communist rule in Poland.

Early life and military career

Jaruzelski was born into a landowning family in Kurów. After the Soviet invasion of eastern Poland in 1939 he was deported with his family to Siberia, where forced labour in the taiga damaged his eyes — the origin of the dark glasses he later habitually wore. He joined the Polish forces formed in the USSR in 1943 and fought his way to Berlin with the First Polish Army. After the war he rose through the ranks of the Polish People's Army, becoming its chief of staff and, in 1968, Minister of National Defence. Polish troops under his ministry participated in the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia that year.

Martial law and transition

Becoming prime minister in February 1981 and First Secretary of the ruling party in October, Jaruzelski confronted the mass Solidarity movement born at the Gdańsk shipyards. On 13 December 1981 he proclaimed martial law: Solidarity was banned, thousands were interned, and dozens killed. Jaruzelski later argued the move forestalled a Soviet invasion; critics called it a self-coup to preserve party rule. Martial law was lifted in 1983, but Jaruzelski remained in power, becoming head of state in 1985.

In 1989, amid economic crisis and renewed strikes, he agreed to the Round Table Talks with Solidarity, leading to the semi-free elections of 4 June 1989 and the formation of Tadeusz Mazowiecki's non-communist government. Jaruzelski served as President of Poland from 1989 until Lech Wałęsa succeeded him in December 1990.

Later life

Jaruzelski faced lengthy court proceedings over martial law and the 1970 coastal shootings; trials were repeatedly suspended because of his health. He died in Warsaw in 2014 and was buried with military honours at Powązki Military Cemetery, a decision that drew public protest.
