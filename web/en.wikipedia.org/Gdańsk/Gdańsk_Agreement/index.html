The Gdańsk Agreement is an accord reached between the Polish communist government and striking shipyard workers in Gdańsk on 31 August 1980. Conceding the workers' right to form free and independent trade unions, it made possible the creation of Solidarity (Solidarność) — the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc — and is regarded as a landmark in the eventual collapse of communism in Eastern Europe.

Background

In July 1980 the government raised food prices, sparking strikes across Poland. On 14 August workers at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk struck after the dismissal of crane operator and activist Anna Walentynowicz. Under the leadership of electrician Lech Wałęsa, the strike committee presented 21 demands, headed by acceptance of free trade unions independent of the ruling Polish United Workers' Party, the right to strike, freedom of expression and publication, and the release of political prisoners.

The agreement

After negotiations with a government commission led by Deputy Prime Minister Mieczysław Jagielski, the agreement was signed in the shipyard's health-and-safety hall on 31 August 1980, Wałęsa famously using a giant souvenir pen bearing the image of Pope John Paul II. The government accepted the 21 demands in principle, including independent unions, while the strikers recognised the leading role of the Party and Poland's existing alliances. Parallel accords were signed at Szczecin (30 August) and Jastrzębie (3 September).

Consequences

On 17 September 1980 the nationwide union Solidarity was founded, soon counting some ten million members. Sixteen months of relative freedom followed, until the declaration of martial law on 13 December 1981 suppressed the movement. The legacy of the Gdańsk Agreement, however, fed into the Round Table talks of 1989 and the first partly free elections in the Eastern bloc, accelerating the fall of communism in Europe.

The shipyard's Gate No. 2, the adjacent Monument to the Fallen Shipyard Workers, and the European Solidarity Centre (opened 2014) commemorate the events.
