Gdynia (Polish: [ˈɡdɨɲa]; German: Gdingen) is a city in northern Poland on the Baltic Sea coast. Together with neighbouring Gdańsk and Sopot it forms the Tricity metropolitan area. Gdynia is noted for its seaport and for its interwar modernist architecture.

History

For centuries Gdynia was a small fishing village. After the First World War, when Gdańsk became the Free City of Danzig and Poland's access to the sea was limited to a narrow corridor, the Polish government decided in 1921 to build a major seaport at Gdynia. Under engineer Tadeusz Wenda and minister Eugeniusz Kwiatkowski the port grew rapidly; by the late 1930s it was among the largest and most modern on the Baltic, and the village had become a city of over 100,000 people, famed for its white modernist buildings.

Occupied by Germany during the Second World War (as Gotenhafen), Gdynia returned to Poland in 1945. In December 1970, shipyard workers protesting in Gdynia were shot by security forces, an event commemorated by monuments near the shipyard. Today the port of Gdynia remains one of Poland's principal harbours, and the city hosts the annual Polish Film Festival.
