Telugu (తెలుగు) is a Dravidian language native to the Indian states of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, where it is the official language. It is the most widely spoken member of the Dravidian language family and among the most spoken languages in the world, with over 80 million native speakers. In 2008 the Government of India designated Telugu a classical language. Italian explorer Niccolò de' Conti reportedly called it the "Italian of the East" for its vowel-ending words and musicality.

History and literature

Telugu inscriptions appear from about the 6th century CE, and the script evolved from the Brahmi-derived Kadamba/Bhattiprolu scripts. The literary tradition is conventionally dated from Nannaya's 11th-century Telugu rendering of the Mahabharata, continued by Tikkana and Potana. The Vijayanagara emperor Krishnadevaraya, himself a Telugu poet, presided over a golden age of Telugu letters in the 16th century.

Script and grammar

Telugu is written in its own Brahmic script, an abugida with 56 characters including vowels and consonant-vowel syllables. It is agglutinative, with subject–object–verb word order, eight cases, and three grammatical genders. Telugu vocabulary draws heavily on Sanskrit loans alongside a native Dravidian core.
