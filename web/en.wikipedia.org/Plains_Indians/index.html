Plains Indians

Plains Indians or Indigenous peoples of the Great Plains and Canadian Prairies are the Native American tribes and First Nations peoples who have historically lived on the Interior Plains (the Great Plains and Canadian Prairies) of North America. Their historic nomadic culture of following the vast herds of American bison and later the horse culture became the archetypal image of Native Americans.

Peoples

Plains peoples include the Lakota, Dakota and Nakota (Sioux), Cheyenne, Arapaho, Crow, Comanche, Kiowa, Pawnee, Blackfeet, Mandan, Hidatsa, Arikara, and Plains Cree and Ojibwe, among others. They spoke languages from several families, including Siouan, Algonquian and Caddoan, and communicated across tribes with Plains Indian Sign Language.

Culture

From the 17th century, horses acquired from Spanish colonists transformed Plains life, enabling highly mobile bison hunting. The tipi served as a portable dwelling; the Sun Dance was a major ceremony for many tribes. Other groups, such as the Mandan and Pawnee, lived in semi-permanent earth-lodge villages and farmed corn, beans and squash alongside hunting.

History

Westward US expansion in the 19th century brought warfare, treaties, the near-extermination of the bison, and forced removal onto reservations. Famous conflicts include Red Cloud's War, the Great Sioux War of 1876 (with the Battle of the Little Bighorn), and the Wounded Knee massacre of 1890.
