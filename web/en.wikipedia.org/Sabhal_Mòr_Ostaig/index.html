Sabhal Mòr Ostaig (Scottish Gaelic, "the big barn of Ostaig") is a public higher- and further-education college on the Isle of Skye in north-west Scotland. It is the only college in Scotland providing education entirely through the medium of Scottish Gaelic, and it plays a central role in the language's revitalisation. The college is an academic partner of the University of the Highlands and Islands (UHI).

History

The college was founded in 1973 by the banker and Gaelic activist Sir Iain Noble, who bought the Ostaig farm steading on the Sleat peninsula and established it as a Gaelic educational trust. Early activities centred on short Gaelic courses and cultural events. From the 1980s the college developed full-time further education, and in the 1990s it introduced degree programmes validated through UHI's predecessors, initially in Gaelic language and culture, music, media and business.

Campus and teaching

The campus overlooks the Sound of Sleat toward the mainland mountains of Knoydart. Buildings include the converted steading at Àrainn Ostaig and the Àrainn Chaluim Chille campus opened in the 1990s, with student residences, a library of Gaelic material, lecture facilities and the headquarters of Gaelic media and publishing initiatives. Degree-level courses are taught wholly in Gaelic, and the college also runs distance-learning and short courses for adult learners worldwide.

Cultural role

Beyond formal education, Sabhal Mòr Ostaig hosts the national Gaelic cultural archive Tobar an Dualchais in partnership roles, supports Gaelic publishing and research — including the Soillse sociolinguistic research network — and is a focus of the Gaelic arts, hosting the Gaelic residency programme and supporting organisations based on site. Its existence is frequently cited as the single most important institutional achievement of the modern Gaelic revival.
