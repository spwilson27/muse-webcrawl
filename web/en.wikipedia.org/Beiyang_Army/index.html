Beiyang Army

Active1895–1928
CountryQing dynasty; Republic of China
FounderYuan Shikai
Peak strength~160,000 (early 1910s)

The Beiyang Army was a powerful, western-style military force created in late Qing-dynasty China and central to Chinese politics in the early Republic. It was established by Yuan Shikai from the Newly Created Army at Xiaozhan in 1895, and was named for the Beiyang (northern coastal) region.

Under Yuan Shikai

Trained by foreign — largely German — instructors and organised into six divisions, the Beiyang Army was the most modern formation in China by 1905. Its officer corps, educated at the Baoding Military Academy and in Japan, was personally loyal to Yuan, who used his military leverage to negotiate the abdication of the Qing in 1912 and become President of the Republic of China. After Yuan's failed attempt to make himself emperor in 1915–16, the army's senior commanders became the dominant political actors in the north.

Fragmentation

Following Yuan's death in 1916, the Beiyang officer corps split into rival Anhui, Zhili and Fengtian cliques whose struggles defined the Warlord Era. The cliques' armies were progressively defeated by the National Revolutionary Army during the Northern Expedition of 1926–28, ending the Beiyang government's rule.
