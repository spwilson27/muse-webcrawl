The Three Forms of Unity is a collective name for three confessional documents of the Reformed churches: the Belgic Confession (1561), the Heidelberg Catechism (1563), and the Canons of Dort (1618–1619). They are held as doctrinal standards by Dutch Reformed churches and by many continental Reformed and Presbyterian denominations around the world.

The documents

The Belgic Confession, written by Guido de Brès, sets out Reformed doctrine in 37 articles. The Heidelberg Catechism, written principally by Zacharias Ursinus and Caspar Olevianus, is a teaching catechism arranged for preaching over 52 Lord's Days. The Canons of Dort were adopted by the Synod of Dort (1618–19) in response to the Arminian controversy and are known for their five main points, later summarized in English-speaking Calvinism as the "five points" of Calvinism.

Use

Office-bearers in churches holding the Three Forms typically subscribe to them as faithfully summarizing Scripture, and the documents are used in catechetical instruction and doctrinal examination.
