Typhoid Mary

Real nameMary Mallon
BornSeptember 23, 1869, Cookstown, Ireland
DiedNovember 11, 1938, New York City
Known forFirst identified asymptomatic carrier of typhoid fever in the US

Mary Mallon (1869–1938), commonly known as Typhoid Mary, was an Irish-born cook believed to have infected between 47 and 53 people with typhoid fever while working in the New York City area. She was the first person in the United States identified as an asymptomatic carrier of Salmonella typhi: healthy herself, she shed the bacteria, probably from her gallbladder, and spread it through food she prepared.

Investigation and quarantine

Sanitary engineer George Soper traced a series of household outbreaks to Mallon in 1906–1907. She refused to believe she could spread a disease she had never suffered, resisted testing, and was forcibly quarantined on North Brother Island from 1907 to 1910. Released on condition she stop cooking, she later resumed work under an alias; after a 1915 outbreak at a maternity hospital, she was returned to the island, where she remained for the remaining 23 years of her life.

Legacy

"Typhoid Mary" has entered the language for anyone who spreads disease or misfortune. Her case remains a touchstone in debates about public health powers, individual liberty, stigma and the ethics of indefinite detention of healthy carriers.
