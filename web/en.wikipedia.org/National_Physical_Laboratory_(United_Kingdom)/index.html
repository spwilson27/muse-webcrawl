The National Physical Laboratory (NPL) is the national measurement standards laboratory of the United Kingdom. Based at Bushy House in Teddington, London, since its founding in 1900, it sets and maintains the UK's primary measurement standards and carries out research in metrology and applied physics.

History

NPL was founded in 1900 as Britain's counterpart to national standards institutes abroad. Its early work covered electrical standards, materials testing and aeronautics — much early British wind-tunnel research took place there. During the Second World War, NPL contributed to radar and operations research. In the 1940s and 1950s, Alan Turing worked at NPL on the design of the ACE computer, one of the earliest stored-program computer designs, and Donald Davies later developed the concept of packet switching there, foundational to the internet. Physicist Louis Essen built the first practical caesium atomic clock at NPL in 1955, enabling the redefinition of the second.

Work today

NPL maintains standards for the SI units in the UK, provides calibration and measurement services to industry, and conducts research in quantum technologies, advanced materials, environmental measurement and data science.
