Volcanism on Mars

Volcanism on Mars produced some of the largest volcanoes in the solar system. Because Mars lacks plate tectonics, magma sources remained fixed beneath the crust for billions of years, allowing shield volcanoes to grow to enormous size. The Tharsis region hosts Olympus Mons, about 21.9 km high and some 600 km wide, and the three Tharsis Montes; another volcanic province lies in Elysium.

History

Volcanic activity was greatest in the Noachian and Hesperian periods, roughly 4 to 3 billion years ago, though crater counts on some lava flows suggest eruptions may have continued until relatively recently in geological terms — possibly within the last few tens of millions of years. Pyroclastic deposits, lava channels and possible volcanic cones are observed across the planet. Volcanic outgassing contributed to Mars's early atmosphere and climate, and meteorites from Mars (shergottites) are largely volcanic rocks.
