Delilah

Delilah is a woman in the Hebrew Bible's Book of Judges (chapter 16), the lover of the Israelite hero Samson. Bribed by the lords of the Philistines, she discovers the secret of Samson's strength and betrays him to his enemies.

Biblical narrative

Samson loved Delilah in the valley of Sorek. The Philistine lords offered her eleven hundred pieces of silver from each of them to learn where his great strength lay. Three times she asked and Samson deceived her — with fresh bowstrings, new ropes, and weaving his locks into a loom — each time calling the Philistines upon him in vain. Persisting day after day, she finally drew the truth: no razor had touched his head, for he was a Nazirite from birth. While he slept on her lap his hair was shaved, his strength left him, and the Philistines seized, blinded and imprisoned him.

Reception

Delilah became a byword for the treacherous seductress, a counterpart to figures such as Salome and the femme fatale. She is the subject of Saint-Saëns' opera Samson and Delilah (1877) and of Cecil B. DeMille's film Samson and Delilah (1949), and her name survives in modern popular culture and song.
