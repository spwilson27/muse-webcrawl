Lough Neagh (from Irish Loch nEachach, "Eachaidh's lake") is a freshwater lake in Northern Ireland and the largest lake in the British Isles, with a surface area of about 392 km². Five of Northern Ireland's six counties border its shores. The lough supplies some 40 percent of Northern Ireland's drinking water and supports the largest wild eel fishery in Europe.

Geography and ecology

The lake is fed by the Upper River Bann, the Blackwater and other rivers, and drained northward to the Atlantic by the Lower Bann. It is shallow for its size and nutrient-rich; it is designated a Ramsar site and Special Protection Area for its wintering wildfowl. In recent decades it has suffered severe algal blooms from nutrient enrichment, which became a major environmental and political issue in 2023.

Legend

Irish mythology tells that the giant Fionn mac Cumhaill scooped up a clod of earth to throw at a Scottish rival, creating the lough — the clod landed in the Irish Sea to become the Isle of Man.
