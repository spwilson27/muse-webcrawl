Designated city (Japan)

A city designated by government ordinance (Japanese: 政令指定都市, seirei shitei toshi), also known simply as a designated city or government ordinance city, is a Japanese city that has a population greater than 500,000 and has been designated as such by an order of the Cabinet of Japan under the Local Autonomy Law.

Designated cities are delegated many of the functions normally performed by prefectural governments, including public education, social welfare, sanitation, business licensing, and urban planning. City governments are subdivided into wards (ku), each with its own administrative office performing local functions.

History

The first five designated cities — Osaka, Nagoya, Kyoto, Yokohama, and Kobe — were designated in 1956, followed by Kitakyushu in 1963. As of 2012, when Kumamoto became the twentieth, there are twenty designated cities, including Sapporo, Sendai, Saitama, Chiba, Kawasaki, Fukuoka, Hiroshima, Niigata, Shizuoka, Hamamatsu, Sakai, Okayama, and Sagamihara.

Requirements

To become a designated city, a city must in principle have a population of at least 500,000, though designation proceeds by cabinet order and typically follows sustained growth above approximately 700,000-1,000,000 residents, often achieved through municipal mergers.
