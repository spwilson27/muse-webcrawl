BRICS is an intergovernmental organisation of major emerging economies. The acronym was coined in 2001 by Goldman Sachs economist Jim O'Neill for Brazil, Russia, India and China; South Africa joined in 2010, making BRICS. In 2024 the grouping expanded to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran and the United Arab Emirates, with Indonesia becoming a full member in January 2025, alongside a tier of "partner countries".

History

The foreign ministers of the four original BRIC countries first met in 2006, and the first summit of heads of state was held in Yekaterinburg, Russia, in 2009. Summits have been held annually since, rotating among members. The 2023 Johannesburg summit invited six further states to join; Argentina declined after a change of government, while the others (except Saudi Arabia, whose status remained unclear) acceded in 2024.

Institutions and initiatives

BRICS created the New Development Bank (headquartered in Shanghai) and a Contingent Reserve Arrangement, both launched in 2014–15 as alternatives to the World Bank and IMF. Member statements regularly call for reform of global governance, greater use of local currencies in trade, and a more multipolar international order. Critics note the members' divergent political systems, economic rivalries (notably between India and China) and the group's limited institutionalisation compared with blocs such as the EU or G7.
