Japanese idol

A Japanese idol (アイドル, aidoru) is a type of entertainer marketed for image, attractiveness and personality in Japanese popular culture. Idols are primarily singers — often in groups — who also appear as television personalities, models and actors, and are typically teenagers or young adults trained by talent agencies.

History

The phenomenon took shape in the 1970s with singers such as Momoe Yamaguchi, and flourished in the 1980s "idol golden age" with acts like Seiko Matsuda and the group Onyanko Club. The 1990s saw the rise of Johnny & Associates' male groups such as SMAP, and the 2000s brought the large-scale "AKB48 model" pioneered by producer Yasushi Akimoto: theatre-based groups marketed on the concept of "idols you can meet", with handshake events and fan-elected rankings.

Industry and culture

Idols are produced by agencies that control their image, training and contracts; fans support them through merchandise, concerts and elections. The system has drawn criticism over strict dating bans, intense workloads and the treatment of minors. Underground (chika) idols perform in small venues, and virtual idols such as Hatsune Miku have extended the concept into digital media. The idol model has influenced the development of K-pop in South Korea.
