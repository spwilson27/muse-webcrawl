Dolby Atmos

Dolby Atmos is a surround sound technology developed by Dolby Laboratories. It expands existing surround sound by adding height channels, allowing sounds to be interpreted as three-dimensional objects with neither horizontal nor vertical limitations. Following the release of Atmos for the cinema market, a consumer version for home theaters was released.

Technology

Unlike channel-based systems such as Dolby Digital 5.1, Atmos treats each sound as an "audio object" with metadata describing its position and movement in three-dimensional space. A cinema Atmos system can support up to 128 simultaneous objects and 64 loudspeaker feeds, including overhead speakers; the renderer maps objects to whatever speakers are installed. Home systems typically use 5.1.2 or 7.1.4 configurations, the final number denoting height or ceiling speakers.

Adoption

Atmos debuted with Pixar's Brave in June 2012 and has since been adopted by thousands of cinemas, home theater receivers, soundbars, televisions, smartphones, streaming services, video games, and music releases marketed as spatial audio.
