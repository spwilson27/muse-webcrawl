The 1972 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XX Olympiad, were held from 26 August to 11 September 1972 in Munich, West Germany. Organizers intended the Games to showcase a democratic, optimistic Germany — the "Happy Games" — but they are remembered above all for the terrorist attack that claimed the lives of eleven Israeli athletes and coaches.

Munich massacre

On 5 September, eight members of the Palestinian group Black September broke into the Olympic Village and took members of the Israeli team hostage, demanding the release of prisoners held by Israel. A botched rescue attempt at Fürstenfeldbruck airfield ended with the deaths of all nine remaining hostages, two Israelis having been killed earlier, along with a West German policeman and five of the attackers. Competition was suspended for a day of mourning before IOC president Avery Brundage declared that "the Games must go on."

Sporting highlights

American swimmer Mark Spitz won seven gold medals, all in world-record time — a single-Games record that stood until Michael Phelps in 2008. Soviet gymnast Olga Korbut captivated audiences, while the Soviet men's basketball team controversially defeated the United States in the final, handing the Americans their first-ever Olympic basketball loss amid a disputed final three seconds. Finnish runner Lasse Virén won the 5,000 and 10,000 metres, recovering from a fall in the longer race.

The Soviet Union topped the medal table, ahead of the United States and East Germany.
