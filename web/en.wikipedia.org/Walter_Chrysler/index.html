Walter Percy Chrysler (April 2, 1875 – August 18, 1940) was an American businessman in railroads and in the automotive industry, and the founder of the Chrysler Corporation (now part of Stellantis). Born in Wamego, Kansas, and raised in Ellis, Kansas, Chrysler began work as a machinist's apprentice on the railroads, rising to become an executive in locomotive works before entering the automobile industry.

Career

His automotive career began with Buick, where he became president and dramatically improved production efficiency, and then with General Motors as a vice president. After leaving GM, he was brought in to rescue the Willys-Overland company and then the Maxwell Motor Company. In 1924 he introduced the first Chrysler automobile, the Chrysler Six, notable for its high-compression engine and four-wheel hydraulic brakes at a moderate price. The Chrysler Corporation was formally established on June 6, 1925.

In 1928 Chrysler expanded dramatically, founding the Plymouth and DeSoto brands and acquiring Dodge Brothers, making his company the second-largest automobile maker in the United States. That same year construction began on the Chrysler Building in New York City, briefly the tallest building in the world, which Chrysler financed personally. Time magazine named him its Man of the Year for 1928.

Legacy

Chrysler retired from day-to-day management in the 1930s and died in 1940 at his estate on Long Island. His autobiography, Life of an American Workman, was published in 1937. The company he founded remains one of the historic "Big Three" American automobile manufacturers.
