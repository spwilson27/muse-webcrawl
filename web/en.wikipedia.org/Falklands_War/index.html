Falklands War

The Falklands War (Spanish: Guerra de las Malvinas) was a ten-week undeclared war between Argentina and the United Kingdom in 1982 over two British dependent territories in the South Atlantic: the Falkland Islands and South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands. It began on 2 April 1982 when Argentine forces invaded and occupied the Falklands, and ended with the Argentine surrender on 14 June 1982, returning the islands to British control.

Course of the war

The ruling Argentine military junta under Leopoldo Galtieri ordered the invasion to assert Argentina's long-standing sovereignty claim and to rally domestic support. Britain, under Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher, dispatched a naval task force some 8,000 miles (13,000 km) to the South Atlantic. After British forces retook South Georgia, fighting intensified in May: the Argentine cruiser ARA General Belgrano was sunk by the submarine HMS Conqueror with the loss of 323 lives, and Argentine aircraft sank several British ships, including HMS Sheffield. British troops landed at San Carlos on 21 May, won the Battle of Goose Green, and advanced across East Falkland to capture the high ground around Stanley; the Argentine garrison surrendered on 14 June.

Aftermath

The war cost 649 Argentine and 255 British military personnel, and three Falkland Islanders, their lives. In Argentina, defeat discredited the junta, which fell and gave way to democratic elections in 1983. In Britain, victory greatly boosted the Thatcher government's popularity. Argentina maintains its sovereignty claim, while the islanders voted overwhelmingly in a 2013 referendum to remain a British Overseas Territory.
