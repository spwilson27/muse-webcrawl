José de Anchieta

José de Anchieta (19 March 1534 – 9 June 1597) was a Spanish Jesuit missionary in Brazil, a native of Tenerife in the Canary Islands. He is regarded as one of the founders of São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro and a foundational figure in Brazilian literature and education. He was canonized by Pope Francis in 2014.

Life

Anchieta studied in Coimbra, Portugal, and joined the Society of Jesus. In 1553 he sailed to Brazil as a novice, partly in hope that the climate would ease a spinal ailment. At the Piratininga plateau he helped Manuel da Nóbrega found the mission college from which the city of São Paulo grew (1554). He learned the Tupi language and wrote the first grammar of it, Arte de gramática da língua mais usada na costa do Brasil (1595).

Works and legacy

Anchieta wrote poems, plays and letters in Portuguese, Spanish, Latin and Tupi, including the epic poem De gestis Mendi de Saa. He acted as an interpreter and peace negotiator between the Portuguese and the Tamoio Confederation. He died at Reritiba (now Anchieta, Espírito Santo) in 1597. Because of his role he is sometimes called the "Apostle of Brazil".
